# Award No. 163/09 — Tar Foat Garmen (Cambodia) Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន តារ ហ្វោត ហ្គាមេន (ខេបបូឌា) អែល ធី ឌី) (Arbitration Council, 11 December 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Yu Sounti (employer-appointed), Tuon Si Phan (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhean (president) · Doc ID 140459

## 1. Facts
Tar Foat Garmen (Cambodia) Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន តារ ហ្វោត ហ្គាមេន (ខេបបូឌា) អែល ធី ឌី), on National Road 2 at Chak Angre Leu, Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employed some 1,823 workers and hosted seven unions. [Para 65][Para 66] On 5 October 2009 the company asked the labour inspectorate to suspend the contracts of all 1,823 workers for two months, from 8 October to 7 December 2009, because there were no orders; it shut the gates on 8 October and never obtained the inspector's authorisation. [Para 76][Para 83][Para 84] The complainant union, the Democratic Union of Khmer Garment Workers (សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា), pursued three demands after others were withdrawn, but two unions withdrew demand 2 and six withdrew demand 3. [Para 27][Para 107][Para 114][Para 140] A multi-ministry committee brokered an agreement on 3 December 2009 under which the company would pay 50% of the minimum wage plus USD 5 seniority and USD 6 living allowance, USD 36 per worker per month, and resume on 8 December; it did so. [Para 19][Para 105][Para 142]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay 100% of wages and other benefits for the two months during which it had no work to give, having suspended contracts without the labour inspector's authorisation? [Para 76][Para 116]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must terminate all the workers' contracts and pay compensation under Articles 75, 77, 89, 116 and 166 on the ground that it cannot continue to operate? [Para 139]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must identify a responsible person, that being unclear because its management has changed repeatedly? [Para 108]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — Democratic Union of Khmer Garment Workers (សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា):** because the suspension was never authorised by the labour inspector the company remained bound to pay full wages; the plant looked abandoned, with new machines and cloth moved to sister factories, so the workers distrusted the promise to reopen and wanted termination with statutory compensation; they also wanted a named, legally responsible manager. [Para 89][Para 94][Para 96][Para 108]
**Employer (respondent) — Tar Foat Garmen (Cambodia) Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន តារ ហ្វោត ហ្គាមេន (ខេបបូឌា) អែល ធី ឌី):** the closure flowed from the world economic crisis and loss of orders, the gates were not closed permanently, and it had already promised in its 17 October notice to reopen on 8 December and pay USD 12 per idle day; it could not pay 100%, and it had designated its manager as the responsible person by written authority. [Para 100][Para 101][Para 104][Para 152]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three demands were refused, no sum being awarded. [Para 165][Para 166][Para 167]
The panel first confirmed the company's representation: the authority signed by its manager on 24 November 2009 was valid, the workers did not contest it, and the delegation was not defective. [Para 73][Para 113][Para 160]
On Issue 1 the panel stated the governing rule in the workers' favour: under Articles 71 and 72(1) of the Labour Law a suspension must be notified to and supervised by the labour inspector, and where the procedure is not followed the employer owes 100% of wages even though no work is available. [Para 117][Para 118][Para 125][Para 127][Para 130] It nonetheless refused the claim on proof, because the 3 December agreement had split the workforce into those who accepted USD 36 and those who continued to demand 100%, and the union never filed the list of continuing claimants that the panel ordered by 11:30 on 11 December 2009; without names the panel could not say who was entitled. [Para 91][Para 131][Para 133][Para 137][Para 138]
On Issue 2 the panel found that the suspension had already ended, that the company had resumed operations and that most workers had returned; the continuing distrust was unsupported by any concrete fact or evidence, so the demand for termination and compensation under Articles 75, 77, 89, 116 and 166 failed. [Para 142][Para 143][Para 147][Para 148]
On Issue 3 the panel found that the company had identified its manager as the responsible person in the authority letters and in its reply, and that the workers had not disputed this, so there was no legal basis for the demand. [Para 152][Para 159][Para 163][Para 164]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer suspends contracts without the labour inspector's notification and supervision, it owes full wages for the idle period; but a collective claim for those wages still fails unless the union identifies, by name and by the deadline the panel sets, which workers did not accept the settlement the company offered. [Para 127][Para 130][Para 137][Para 138] A demand that the employer terminate contracts and pay severance fails once the suspension has ended and operations have resumed, mere distrust of the employer not being evidence that it cannot continue. [Para 143][Para 147]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the company holds the right under Article 2 of the Labour Law to appoint anyone to represent it in resolving a particular matter, so a demand for a clearer "responsible person" has no legal footing where a lawful appointment already exists. [Para 159][Para 163]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
OCR damage is heavy in the decision and signature blocks, where numerals and Latin fragments replace Khmer text and one arbitrator's name is partly lost. [Para 135][Para 169] The number of unions is given as seven in the facts but the invitation list accounts for five attending plus two absent, and one paragraph states six. [Para 26][Para 66] The workforce figure shifts between 1,823, about 6,000 formerly and about 2,000 now. [Para 65][Para 96] The panel twice refers to a second hearing on 9 November 2009 when the recorded second hearing was 9 December 2009, and the 3 December agreement is described as having been taken up at both. [Para 19][Para 90] Issued amounts are printed inconsistently — USD 8, then USD 10, then USD 12 per idle day, against USD 36 per month in the agreement. [Para 79][Para 101][Para 19]
