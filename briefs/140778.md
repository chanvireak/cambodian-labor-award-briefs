# Award No. 37/05-JDI — JD International Ltd and the Khmer Youth Union of JDI (Arbitration Council, 4 July 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (You Suonty, Liv Sovanna, chair Dr Sok Mathoeung) · Doc ID 140778

## 1. Facts
JD International Ltd ("JDI"), at No. 137, Street 1986, Sangkat Phnom Penh Thmey, Khan Dangkau, Phnom Penh, employs 52 workers. [Para 4] [Para 21] On 27 May 2005 the company notified the Khmer Youth Federation of Trade Union and the local Khmer Youth Union of JDI of the collective dismissal of the 23 workers at Machine A, following a machine breakdown and losses, and dismissed them on 30 May 2005. [Para 21] [Para 22] [Para 34] The Labour Inspector conciliated on 30 May and 3 June 2005 without agreement, and non-conciliation report No. 803 of 7 June 2005 brought the case to the panel, which heard it on 15 and 16 June 2005. [Para 10] [Para 11] The 22 workers other than the union vice-president, Mr P, accepted four of the five payments claimed; he alone demanded reinstatement. [Para 24] [Para 30]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the panel has jurisdiction over the dispute once 22 of the 23 dismissed workers have accepted the indemnities and only one union leader persists? [Para 43]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the dismissal of Mr P, vice-president of the local union, was legally proper where it formed part of a mass layoff? [Para 74]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that the employer dismissed the 23 workers unreasonably and owed indemnities under Articles 75, 89, 91, 116 and 166 of the Labour Law, and that the case of Mr P was separated from that of the 22 workers at the 30 May 2005 conciliation and remained alive, so that he must be reinstated. [Para 8] [Para 30] [Para 38]
**Employer (respondent):** that it had been losing profits, that the dismissal of all 23 was a mass layoff caused by a broken machine, that it offered the four payments under Articles 75, 89, 116 and 166, and that Mr P attended the 3 June 2005 conciliation without asking for reinstatement, which settled all 23. [Para 9] [Para 32] [Para 34] It later offered him the five payments claimed, which the union refused. [Para 35]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — JDI must reinstate Mr P from the date the award comes into effect and, within one month of the reinstatement offer, pay him full wages from the date of his dismissal until he is reinstated. [Para 89] [Para 91] [Para 92]
On Issue 1 the panel held that, although Chapter 12 Section 2 of the Labour Law gives it no express jurisdiction over individual disputes, it is the Labour Inspector and the Minister who classify a dispute before forwarding it, and the panel follows that classification absent particular reason not to, as in 10/03-Jacqsintex and 02/04 Cambodiana. [Para 46] [Para 47] It found the dispute collective under the three limbs of Article 302: it is between certain workers and an employer, it concerns relations between employer and union through the dismissal, and it could jeopardise the operation of the enterprise because 23 of 52 workers were dismissed and most union members supported Mr P. [Para 49] [Para 51] [Para 53] On jurisdiction over the reinstatement claim specifically, Article 312 and Article 33 of Prakas 99/04 confine the panel to issues in the non-conciliation report or to events arising afterwards that are a direct consequence of the dispute. [Para 58] [Para 59] The demand emerged only after the 22 workers accepted their indemnities, and the panel held it a direct consequence of the same mass layoff. [Para 63] [Para 70] [Para 72]
On Issue 2 the panel set out Article 95 of the Labour Law, which requires a layoff order based on professional qualifications, seniority and family burdens, notification of the Labour Inspector and a two-year rehiring priority. [Para 77] The Machine A dismissal was a reduction in the establishment's activity, but the layoff did not follow those procedures. [Para 78] [Para 79] As the 22 workers had accepted the layoff the panel made no order for them, while noting their right to claim reinstatement. [Para 80] For Mr P, Articles 282 and 293 of the Labour Law and Article 4 of Prakas 305/01 require the Labour Inspector's approval before dismissing a union leader, and that requirement applies inside a mass layoff as much as outside it. [Para 83] No approval was sought, so his dismissal was unlawful and reinstatement followed. [Para 85] [Para 86] [Para 87]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The dismissal of a union leader, including within a mass layoff, is unlawful unless the Labour Inspector has approved it in advance under Articles 282 and 293 of the Labour Law and Article 4 of Prakas 305/01; the remedy is reinstatement with full wages from the dismissal date until return to work. [Para 83] [Para 86] [Para 92] The panel defers to the Minister's and Labour Inspector's classification of a dispute as collective or individual, and a dispute carried by the union for a single remaining official still satisfies Article 302 where the dismissal concerns employer-union relations and could jeopardise the enterprise. [Para 47] [Para 53] A claim emerging after the non-conciliation minute is within jurisdiction only if it is a direct consequence of the same dispute. [Para 63] [Para 72] A mass layoff that ignores the Article 95 selection and notification requirements binds no worker who did not accept it, and workers who did accept keep their right to claim reinstatement. [Para 79] [Para 80]

**Obiter dicta:** that the 22 workers who accepted the four payments retain the right to claim reinstatement even though no order issued for them. [Para 80]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The conciliation attended by Mr P is dated 3 June 2005 in one paragraph and 3 May 2005 in the next, and the non-conciliation minute is variously placed at 3 May and 3 June 2005. [Para 31] [Para 32] [Para 64] The employer is called JD International Ltd on the face of the award, "JDI" throughout the text and "Cotton JDI" in the Ministry's documents. [Para 4] [Para 16] The report of 7 June 2005 and the Ministry's letter of request of 17 June 2005 are both numbered 803, and one passage cites Articles 78, 89 and 116 where the claim was under Articles 75, 89, 116 and 166. [Para 18] [Para 28] The district appears as both Khan Dangkau and Khan Russey Keo for the same address, and one paragraph refers to Articles 282 and 283 where the rest of the reasoning relies on 282 and 293. [Para 7] [Para 83] OCR runs words together throughout ("themployer", "withe", "athearing") and splits sentences across the numbered lines. [Para 24] [Para 58]
