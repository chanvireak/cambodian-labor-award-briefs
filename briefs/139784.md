# Award No. 337/14 — Jin Chen Yuan (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 31 December 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; Sin Kim Sean, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, chair appointed by both) · Doc ID 139784

## 1. Facts
Jin Chen Yuan (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. manufactures socks in the Sihanoukville special economic zone and employed about 130 workers. [Para 162][Para 163] On 21 November 2014 the union filed eleven collective demands; conciliation on 26 November settled seven, and the four left over reached the Council on 3 December 2014. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 15] A union election on 16 November 2014 made Ms S president and four others officers; on 24 November 2014 the employer dismissed her, eight colleagues stopped work demanding her return, and by 1 December 2014 all ten relationships had ended. [Para 213][Para 214][Para 282]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must accept back the ten named workers in their original roles and pay their wages from the date of dismissal until the date of return (the panel's issues 1 and 4, merged by agreement)? [Para 21][Para 221]

Issues 2 and 3 — the attendance bonus for short authorised leave and 200 per cent wages for night shift — were settled at the hearing and not decided. [Para 4][Para 5][Para 19]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the three elected leaders were dismissed although elected officers enjoy special protection and union activity is protected by Article 266; the employer had twice been notified of the election; the others only stopped work to secure their president's return. [Para 194][Para 195][Para 216]
**Employer (respondent):** it dismissed only Ms S, for refusing a day-shift assignment given to train new staff even after extra pay was offered and a warning issued on 17 November 2014; the other nine were not renewed because their fixed-duration contracts expired and they refused to work; night shift has been suspended for cost and quality reasons, so they cannot be taken back. [Para 181][Para 183][Para 189][Para 191]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer must accept back all ten workers and pay their wages from the date of dismissal to the date of return; no sum in riel or dollars is printed. [Para 329]

The panel first characterised each ending. [Para 221] Six workers had a last working day of 1 December 2014, the expiry date of their three-month fixed-duration contracts, so their relationship ended by non-renewal. [Para 223][Para 224] Two others held contracts running to 8 January 2015 and 3 January 2015 but were told on 1 December 2014 that they would not be renewed, which the panel held was a unilateral termination before expiry and therefore a dismissal. [Para 229] Mr D, whose contract ran to 1 December 2014, was refused entry from 26 November 2014 and the employer did not contest it; his ending was also a dismissal. [Para 234]

Special protection, the panel held, attaches only to dismissal and not to non-renewal, so only four of the ten were in scope. [Para 245][Para 246] Three of those four — the two whose contracts ran into January and Mr D — failed, because the local union had still not been registered with the Ministry of Labour, so the 30-day protection Prakas 305 gives founders and members from the date of application had never begun. [Para 249][Para 252][Para 253]

Ms S succeeded. The panel applied three cumulative conditions drawn from its earlier awards: that the worker is of a protected category, that the dismissal falls inside the protection period, and that the union notified the employer of the protected names by any means. [Para 259] She was elected president on 16 November 2014 and dismissed nine days later, inside the 45 days Prakas 305 article 3 guarantees to candidates after an election; the employer's refusal to sign for the union's two letters could not be converted into a failure to notify. [Para 271][Para 272][Para 275][Para 277] Article 293 required the employer to obtain the labour inspector's authorisation before dismissing her; the employer neither said it had applied nor produced any evidence of an application by the deadline for evidence, so the dismissal was procedurally unlawful and reinstatement with back pay followed. [Para 281][Para 284][Para 289]

For the eight remaining workers the panel asked whether their absence was a strike. [Para 316] Article 318 defines a strike by three elements — a group of workers, a stoppage of work, and the purpose of obtaining a settlement of demands — and the panel found all three present from 24 November 2014, when the group left the factory and refused to return until Ms S was reinstated. [Para 317][Para 320] Article 333 forbids any sanction imposed on account of participation in a strike, and the panel found such participation was the real reason for the non-renewals, the expiry date being no more than a pretext — visibly so for the two whose contracts still had a month to run. [Para 313][Para 323][Para 325] All eight were therefore reinstated with back pay. [Para 326][Para 328]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-duration contract allowed to run to its last day ends by non-renewal, which carries no reinstatement remedy; the same contract cut short before that day is a dismissal. [Para 229] A worker elected as a union officer is protected from dismissal for 45 days after the election, and an employer that dismisses one without first obtaining the labour inspector's authorisation under Article 293 must reinstate her and pay wages from the dismissal date; the union's duty to notify is discharged once its letters are delivered, however the employer receives them. [Para 275][Para 284] A group of workers who stop work to force a colleague's reinstatement is on strike within Article 318 even without notice, and ending their contracts because of that participation is a sanction prohibited by Article 333, whether the contracts had expired or not. [Para 320][Para 325]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that suspending night shift for cost and quality reasons could not justify refusing reinstatement [Para 191], and that separating union members from non-members at the 25 November 2014 meeting was not a legitimate reason to require them to stand apart [Para 305]; neither observation was necessary to the orders made.
