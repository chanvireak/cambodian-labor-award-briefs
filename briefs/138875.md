# Award No. 042/19 — Besin Bao (Cambodia) Factory Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 20 June 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Kong Phalla (president, appointed by the two) · Doc ID 138875

## 1. Facts
The respondent is a garment factory employing 250 workers. On 2 May 2019 five workers filed a collective complaint seeking improvement of working conditions on 15 points; conciliation on 24 May 2019 failed on 14 of them, and the file reached the Council on 28 May 2019. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 47] The employer was invited by letter delivered by hand at the factory and did not appear at the 10 June 2019 hearing, filing no documents. At the hearing the workers withdrew points 1, 2, 5 and 12, leaving ten. [Para 31][Para 39][Para 34]

## 2. Issues in dispute
3. Whether the employer must allow a worker to transfer to a third person the right to collect his or her own wages? [Para 95]
4. Whether the employer must organise an election of staff delegates (ប្រតិភូ បុគ្គលិក)? [Para 108]
6. Whether the employer is obliged to provide an on-duty doctor and adequate medicines to workers during working hours? [Para 130]
7. Whether the employer has applied overtime in accordance with the principle of voluntariness? [Para 149]
8. Whether the employer is obliged to provide hot water, cold water and hygienic drinking water to workers? [Para 157]
9. Whether the employer must calculate the annual-leave indemnity on the basis of the average of 12 months' wages? [Para 169]
10. Whether the employer must maintain wages while a worker is absent on sick leave supported by a medical certificate? [Para 178]
11. Whether the employer is obliged to let pregnant women leave work 15 minutes early? [Para 201]
13. Whether the employer must allow workers to take seven consecutive days of special leave at once? [Para 210]
14. Whether the employer must deduct 4,000 riel per month from the wages of union members and pay it to the union as dues? [Para 225]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer paid wages only to the worker himself, refusing even a written authority for a relative to collect them; there were no staff delegates and no doctor, only headache and muscle-rub ointment; overtime from 16:00 to 18:00 was in practice compulsory, three warnings leading to dismissal; only ordinary piped water was supplied; annual-leave pay used base wage only; sick leave on certificate was unpaid; pregnant women were released 5 minutes early; only 3 or 4 of the 7 special days were granted; and union dues deducted under written consents were not remitted. [Para 53][Para 57][Para 60][Para 63][Para 65][Para 67][Para 69][Para 72]
**Employer (respondent):** did not appear and put no contention on the record; no document was received from it. Its absence was nonetheless treated as no admission, the workers bearing the burden of proof on each assertion. [Para 31][Para 39][Para 103]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — four of the ten surviving demands were ordered: election of staff delegates; one on-duty nurse and one doctor present for at least 2 hours of working time plus adequate medicines per Prakas No. 330; annual-leave indemnity recomputed as (12 months' total wages ÷ 12) ÷ 26; 15 minutes' early release for women pregnant more than 3 months; and deduction of 4,000 riel per month from union members' wages for union dues. The remaining six demands were dismissed. [Para 246][Para 247][Para 248][Para 251][Para 253][Para 255]

The panel first held service regular — the date, place and contact numbers were in the invitation, handed to a company employee at the premises — so under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 it proceeded in absentia. [Para 75][Para 77][Para 78] It also satisfied itself that the single representative present, Mr H, held a valid mandate from the Department of Labour Disputes letter No. 009/19, there being no most-representative union at the factory. [Para 84][Para 90][Para 94]

On point 3 the panel read Article 113 of the Labour Law, following Award No. 274/13, as requiring direct payment to the worker unless the worker consents otherwise; but since the workers produced no evidence that the employer had refused a written authority, the claim failed on the burden of proof. [Para 98][Para 101][Para 106][Para 107] On point 4, Articles 3, 32 and 35 of the Law on Trade Unions and Articles 1 and 6 of Prakas No. 302 obliged an employer with 8 or more workers to organise the election within 45 days of request; the absence of delegates was uncontested, so the order followed. [Para 110][Para 112][Para 118][Para 122][Para 129] On point 6, Article 238 and Article 242 of the Labour Law plus Article 3 of Prakas No. 330 fixed staffing by headcount; for 250 workers the panel ordered one nurse and one doctor for at least 2 hours. [Para 130][Para 131][Para 137][Para 141] Adequate medicines were found lacking and ordered under Article 4 of Prakas No. 330. [Para 146][Para 147]

On point 7 the panel accepted Article 4 of Prakas No. 80 that overtime rests on consent and no discipline may follow refusal, but the allegation of three-warning coercion was unsupported and was dismissed. [Para 149][Para 152][Para 155][Para 156] On point 8, Article 1 of Prakas No. 054 requires only sufficient hygienic drinking water, not hot and cold water, and the claim of contamination was unproven. [Para 159][Para 161][Para 167][Para 168] On point 9, Articles 166 and 168 require an average of the preceding 12 months' wages; using base wage alone contravened that, so the panel prescribed the 12-month ÷ 12 ÷ 26 formula. [Para 172][Para 176][Para 177] On point 10, Articles 71(3) and 72 suspend the contract during certified sick leave, so wages are not owed by the employer; the worker's entitlement is the 70% daily allowance from the National Social Security Fund under Prakas No. 448 and No. 184. [Para 178][Para 182][Para 198][Para 200] On point 11, the Labour Law is silent, but Notification No. 294 of 27 September 2017 requires 15 minutes for women pregnant more than 3 months; the employer gave 5. [Para 201][Para 202][Para 208][Para 209] On point 13, Article 171 and Prakas No. 267 give the employer the right to decide the timing of the 7 special days, so a demand for 7 consecutive days failed. [Para 215][Para 222][Para 224] On point 14, Article 129 and Article 26 of the Law on Trade Unions permit deduction with written consent, and Article 4 of Prakas No. 300 requires 10 days' notice with consents attached; both were satisfied, so the deduction was ordered. [Para 225][Para 229][Para 238][Para 245]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand in a collective dispute succeeds only where the workers point to a specific provision — a Labour Law article, a ministerial prakas or a notification — imposing the duty claimed, and prove the facts triggering it; the employer's default does not reverse the burden, so an unsupported assertion fails even against an absent employer, while a duty fixed by a prakas or notification is enforced as written, including staffing ratios set by headcount. [Para 103][Para 137][Para 244]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that only a most-representative union holds exclusive negotiating rights under Article 54 of the Law on Trade Unions, and that absent one a department-recognised worker representative may conduct the dispute, were not necessary to any of the ten orders. [Para 82][Para 90] Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented, agreeing with the 12-month formula only for Article 167(2) and 168 payments and objecting to its wider use. [Para 277][Para 279]
