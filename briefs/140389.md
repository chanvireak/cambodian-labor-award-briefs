# Award No. 64/09 — Sinomax International (Cambodia) Garment Limited (Branch 2) (Arbitration Council, 17 March 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach for the employer, An Nan for the workers, Kong Phallack chairing) · Doc ID 140389

## 1. Facts
Sinomax International (Cambodia) Garment Limited (Branch 2) runs a garment factory at Village 2, Svay Rolum Commune, S'ang District, Kandal Province. [Para 11] The claimant, the Local Union of C.CAWDU (Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers Democratic Union), represents 750 workers there. [Para 1][Para 12]

Mr N began work on 12 April 2007 on a series of three-month fixed-duration contracts in the table cutting section at a main wage of US$ 65 a month, an average of US$ 90. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20] He was elected local union vice-president on 18 January 2008; on 28 March 2008 the company notified him that his contract expiring 12 April 2008 would not be renewed, and on 12 April 2008 that it was suspended pending the Labour Inspector's ruling. [Para 20][Para 21] A strike followed and, under an Arbitration Council order, he was reinstated on 24 April 2008; the company then terminated him. The five original demands went to conciliation, three were settled, and at the hearing the union withdrew the second of the two remaining, on resignation and severance, leaving the reinstatement claim. [Para 7][Para 10][Para 24]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must reinstate Mr N, vice-president of the local union, to his former position and pay back his wages and benefits from the date of suspension to the date of reinstatement? [Para 1][Para 13]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, Local Union of C.CAWDU):** the non-renewal was union discrimination, coming only weeks after Mr N's election, and aimed at a man who had become prominent in handling workplace disputes; the union also relied on the Labour Inspector and Ministry's referral of the demand as a collective dispute. [Para 15][Para 28][Para 34]

**Employer (respondent, Sinomax International (Cambodia) Garment Limited (Branch 2)):** the claim is an individual dispute — a letter from the head of the General Department of Labour says so — and Mr N's fixed-duration contract simply expired; an employer decides whether to renew, especially where the worker has committed misconduct three times and received two warnings. [Para 2][Para 15][Para 26]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the demand for reinstatement and back pay was rejected. [Para 76]

The panel began with jurisdiction. Article 302 of the Labour Law, as read in Award 45/07-Wilson, requires three conditions for a collective dispute: a dispute between an employer and a number of staff, on working conditions or the exercise or recognition of professional organisations' rights, capable of jeopardising the enterprise or social peace. All three were satisfied — the union represented the majority, the issue was a non-renewal of employment, and the 2008 strike had in fact disrupted operations. [Para 35][Para 37][Para 39][Para 40]

The employer's letter from the General Department of Labour did not displace that finding. Under Clause 11 of Prakas No. 099 and Article 312 of the Labour Law the panel decides independently, and the letter was addressed to the Kandal labour department for handling under individual-dispute procedure, not to the panel; it was evidence only, of no binding character. The Ministry's own non-conciliation report, referring the demand, was not displaced by it. [Para 43][Para 45][Para 46][Para 49]

The panel then considered res judicata, since Award 55-57/08-Sinomax had already dealt with the same parties and issue. Following Award 153/08-Hytex Garment, three conditions are required: same parties, same issue, and a final ruling. The first two were met but not the third — the earlier panel had declined to decide because the dismissal had not yet occurred and the dispute concerned future rights, so no factual decision had been made. Res judicata therefore did not apply. [Para 54][Para 57][Para 58][Para 60]

On the merits, Article 73 of the Labour Law provides that a fixed-duration contract normally terminates at its stated end date, and following Award 100/07-Hoya Cambodia the panel held that expiry ends the employment relationship automatically, so neither side can compel renewal without agreement. The employer therefore had the right to decline renewal, provided union discrimination was not the basis. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 68][Para 69]

On discrimination the claimant bears the burden, as in Awards 112/06-River Rich, 01/07-Supreme and 123/07-E Garment. The workers produced no evidence of the employer's dissatisfaction with union activity. The employer did rely on misconduct, though the panel found it unclear what misconduct was meant and whether it affected his work, and noted the company had not pleaded economic need to reduce staff. Still, absent evidence of anti-union motive and with the worker admitting some misconduct, the panel found the basis for the allegation insufficient. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 72][Para 73][Para 74]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-duration contract expires automatically at its end date, so the decision to renew belongs to the parties and cannot be compelled by an award; the panel will only intervene where non-renewal was grounded in union discrimination, which the claimant must prove. A prior award on the same parties and issue bars a second decision only if it also finally ruled on the facts — a refusal to decide because the dismissal had not yet occurred is not such a ruling. [Para 58][Para 68][Para 69]

**Obiter dicta:** that a letter from the General Department of Labour classifying a dispute as individual is evidence only and cannot bind the panel, which decides independently under Clause 11 of Prakas No. 099 and Article 312; and the panel's remark that the employer's own explanation of the misconduct was vague. [Para 45][Para 72]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is an unofficial English translation with the Evidence section omitted, so the evidentiary record survives only in summary. Its dates are internally impossible: the award is dated 17 March 2009 in the header, yet it records a non-conciliation report of 18 May 2009, a hearing on 1 June 2009, deadlines of 5 and 9 June 2009, and a state of affairs "as at 1 June 2009". [Para 1][Para 6][Para 8][Para 17] OCR drops the "h" throughout ("themployer", "athearing", "fixeduration") and mangles the earlier case reference, printed as "55-57/08", while the panel also writes "55-57/08--Sinomax" inconsistently. The reasoning repeats whole phrases, and Issue 2 appears in the demands but is recorded only as withdrawn, with no reasoning. The award prints the vice-president, union officers and employer's lawyers by full name, reduced here to initials and roles.
