# Award No. 43/13 and 51/13 — Asia Form Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 29 March 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambona, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Kong Phalha, chair) · Doc ID 139663

## 1. Facts
Asia Form Co., Ltd is a Phnom Penh design, training and graphics company (clipping and masking), registered in 2001, employing about 35 staff; it closed its clipping and masking section in 2012. [Para 77][Para 78][Para 79][Para 80] Eighteen staff, through the Cambodian Labour Union, claimed statutory termination payments after the section was shut and they were dismissed. [Para 81][Para 82] At the second hearing the claimants withdrew the annual-leave, medical-check and workbook demands and the rehiring demand, leaving only the termination payments for Mr H Noeun and 17 others. [Para 28][Para 116]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay statutory termination benefits — severance, moral damages, payment in lieu of notice and unused annual leave — to the 18 staff dismissed when the clipping and masking section closed? [Para 117]

The demands withdrawn at the hearing were: that all contracts be revised, that annual leave be 18 days a year and rise to 19 or 20, that all staff be sent for medical checks and given workbooks on termination, and that staff be rehired if the company reopens under any name. [Para 5][Para 6][Para 7][Para 116]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissals were made without valid reason and before the contracts expired, without any formal declaration of bankruptcy or closure, so the full statutory indemnities are owed. [Para 83][Para 101][Para 102]
**Employer (respondent):** it did not dismiss Mr H Noeun but simply did not renew his contract because the clipping and masking section had no work; orders fell 30% in April 2012 and by half in May 2012, and the training section closed in 2009 and clipping and masking in 2012. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 87][Para 88]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered severance, moral damages, payment in lieu of notice and unused annual leave for Mr H Noeun and the 17 others, calculated per contract length, and refused the moral-damages claim of the 14 longest-serving staff on the merits while awarding them half that sum for procedural failure; the non-binding award becomes final eight days after notification. [Para 227][Para 228][Para 229]

The panel first fixed the contract type. Under article 67(2) a fixed-duration contract may not exceed two years, may be renewed, and any total beyond two years converts it into an undetermined-duration contract; article 73(5) says the same where total duration exceeds the article 67(2) limit. [Para 117][Para 123][Para 129] Following award 10/03 Zacsintek, with ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982 cited as interpretative context, the panel took a two-month training period as probation, since the employer produced nothing to show it was training rather than employment, and found Mr H Noeun's total service to be 28 months 8 days — beyond two years — so his contract was of undetermined duration. [Para 95][Para 97][Para 129][Para 130][Para 131]

Ending that contract was therefore a dismissal, not a non-renewal. [Para 132] Article 74 permits it only for a reason concerning the worker's aptitude or conduct or the necessities of the enterprise, on written notice; the panel found the falling order volume a valid reason that the workers did not contest, but found no notice was given, the employer calling him in and dismissing him on the spot. [Para 133][Para 138][Para 139][Para 153] It then awarded: severance of 30 days' wages under article 89 for 2 years 4 months of service; one month's wages in lieu of notice under articles 75 and 77, service being over two but under five years; payment for unused annual leave under article 166; and the 8 days' wages of October 2012 still unpaid under article 116. [Para 143][Para 154][Para 159][Para 162] Moral damages under article 91 were refused because the reason for dismissal was valid. [Para 149][Para 150]

For the 17 others the panel split them by contract length. The three with under 24 months served on fixed-duration contracts that were ended early without agreement, so article 73 gave each moral damages measured by the wages due to the contract's end — 3 days, 2 days and 2 months 3 days respectively — plus termination indemnity of 5% of wages received under the same article. [Para 103][Para 185][Para 188][Para 189][Para 191] The 14 with over 24 months had undetermined-duration contracts by the same reasoning, and drew severance under article 89 at 15 or 45 days' wages according to length of service. [Para 192][Para 197] Their article 91 moral damages were refused because the closure of their whole section supplied a valid reason; but the panel found no evidence that the employer had notified the labour inspector of the collective dismissal as article 95 requires, so it awarded each of the 14 half the article 91 sum as damages for that procedural failure. [Para 180][Para 205][Para 206][Para 209][Para 211] It also awarded 12 days' wages in lieu of notice under article 77, the employer having given notice on 14 December 2012 and dismissed on 31 December 2012, and unused annual leave under article 166; final wages were already paid. [Para 215][Para 217][Para 222][Para 225]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive renewal of fixed-duration contracts so that total service exceeds two years converts the relationship into an undetermined-duration contract, and ending it is a dismissal that requires a valid reason and written notice, not a mere non-renewal. Where the employer has a valid economic reason but dismisses a whole section without notifying the labour inspector as article 95 requires, moral damages are payable at half the article 91 measure, even though full moral damages are refused. [Para 129][Para 132][Para 209][Para 211]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that Cambodian labour law leans towards undetermined-duration contracts because they give workers security and reward employer commitment, and that ILO Recommendation 166 is useful context though not binding, are general observations beyond the orders made. [Para 121][Para 122][Para 128]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- Mr H Noeun's total service is given as 28 months 8 days at [Para 142] but "about 27 months 8 days" at [Para 154]; both are said to exceed two years, so the conversion holds either way.
- The probation period is described as running from 1 June 2010 to 1 July 2010 yet called two months [Para 90], the employer dating it from "20 June 2010" [Para 91], and the employer says employment began 1 August 2010 [Para 93].
- The award is headed 43/13 and 51/13 but the hearing record at [Para 90] refers to case "42/13"; the company is printed as Asia Form, Asia Home, Esia Form and "Esia Home" throughout [Para 1][Para 41][Para 77].
- The closing pages are largely unrecoverable: the calculation tables at [Para 212] and the signature page at [Para 230] are machine noise, so the individual riel or dollar totals could not be reported — only the day counts printed in the reasons.
- The findings record the order list as truncated at [Para 4], so the first demand of case 43/13 is only partly legible.
