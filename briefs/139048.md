# Award No. 140/16 — International School of Phnom Penh (Arbitration Council, 1 August 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, chosen by the employer party; Sin Kim Sean, chosen by the worker party; Kong Phal, chair chosen by the two) · Doc ID 139048

## 1. Facts
The International School of Phnom Penh, founded in 1989, employs about 225 staff: 100 teachers, of whom 4 are Khmer (1 full-time, 3 part-time) and 96 foreign, and 125 support staff. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70] The dispute concerns the support staff only. [Para 71] The school has no local union but eight staff delegates, and 65 support staff thumbprinted an authority for five delegates to represent them. [Para 72][Para 75] The school maintains a written policy, No. 5.838 of 25 April 2012, giving a 5% annual salary increment plus the inflation rate, and had signed an agreement of 18 May and 9 June 2015 paying Khmer staff 7% of monthly salary into a pension for the 2015–2016 school year. [Para 86][Para 89][Para 188] Five demands were referred; one was settled at the hearing and one withdrawn, leaving two. [Para 19][Para 24][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
The panel's own numbering is kept:
2. Whether the employer must continue the annual 5% salary increment under policy No. 5.838 for the 2017–2018 school year, the employer having abolished the policy and substituted performance appraisal? [Para 152]
3. Whether the employer must continue paying Khmer support staff 7% of monthly salary on top of their seniority entitlement, as it does for foreign staff and as it did in 2015–2016? [Para 178][Para 194]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the 5% annual increment has applied every year under policy No. 5.838, and the school may not simply strike it out; the 7% payment is given to all foreign staff but to Khmer staff for one school year only, which is discriminatory, and the school promised to negotiate a replacement and has not done so. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 97][Para 98] No law forbids workers or staff delegates from bringing their own claim. [Para 80][Para 105]
**Employer (respondent):** the 5% policy was abolished entirely in May 2015 and replaced by appraisal-based increases; the 7% was a one-year additional benefit bought in exchange for the pension payout, given for 2015–2016 only, with negotiation to follow; and the panel has no jurisdiction because these are interest disputes, which only a most representative union may bring. [Para 91][Para 96][Para 99][Para 103][Para 77][Para 78]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to maintain the 5% annual increment under policy No. 5.838 for as long as no new policy replaces it, and the claim for the 7% payment in 2016–2017 was dismissed. [Para 192][Para 193][Para 194]

**Jurisdiction and standing.** Articles 309 and 312 of the Labour Law and Article 32 of Prakas No. 099 give the panel jurisdiction over the disputes recorded as non-conciliated; matters referred by the labour inspector or the Minister are presumed to be collective disputes unless the employer objects with supporting evidence, and an objecting employer carries the burden of proof. [Para 111][Para 116][Para 120][Para 121] The employer objected but produced no clear evidence of its own, arguing instead that the claims were interest disputes, so the objection to jurisdiction failed. [Para 122][Para 123] Article 312(2) and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 distinguish rights disputes, decided according to law from the law, a collective agreement or an agreement, from interest disputes, decided by equity. [Para 125][Para 127][Para 129][Para 130] Both surviving claims turned on the interpretation of a school policy and of a signed agreement, and were therefore rights disputes. [Para 132][Para 134][Para 135] No law bars workers from bringing a rights dispute in person, and Article 39 of the Constitution guarantees the right to complain; Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 permits representation by a lawyer or an authorised person, so the two delegates could act for the 65. [Para 137][Para 139][Para 148][Para 150][Para 151]

Issue 2. Policy No. 5.838 of 25 April 2012 gave a 5% increment plus inflation each school year. [Para 86][Para 90] The employer asserted the policy was abolished in May 2015 but produced no evidence of a valid abolition — only a copy with the clause struck through — and policy No. 2.710 requires staff participation in developing and changing school policy. [Para 91][Para 92][Para 168][Para 169] The panel held the old policy therefore remained in force. [Para 170][Para 171] It accepted that the employer may adopt a new annual-increment policy, provided it is lawful and reasonable and made with staff participation. [Para 172][Para 173] No new policy yet existed, so the period was one of negotiation, and since the employer had in fact paid the 5% for 2015–2016 and 2016–2017 the practice was to continue until a replacement appeared. [Para 174][Para 175][Para 176][Para 177]

Issue 3. The agreement of 18 May 2015 (workers) and 9 June 2015 (employer) is a contract formed by offer and acceptance under Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code, and an employer must perform the agreements it makes; the panel followed Award No. 271/14. [Para 179][Para 180][Para 182][Para 185] But the text of that agreement pays 7% of monthly salary for the school year running from 1 August 2015 to 31 July 2016 only, with negotiation for later years, and both parties acknowledged that reading. [Para 188][Para 189] The employer was therefore under no obligation to continue the payment for 2016–2017, and the claim was dismissed. [Para 190][Para 191][Para 194]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer cannot abolish an existing staff-benefit policy by unilateral deletion where its own policy on policy-making requires staff participation: the old policy stands until a new one is lawfully adopted, and the benefit must continue to be paid in the meantime. A time-limited agreement, by contrast, binds the employer only for the period its text covers; a promise to negotiate a successor creates no obligation to renew the payment. A claim that turns on the interpretation of such a policy or agreement is a rights dispute, which workers may bring in person through authorised delegates without any union. [Para 135][Para 150][Para 173][Para 189]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the current period is one of negotiation over a new increment policy, and that any new policy must be lawful, reasonable and made with staff participation, are guidance not necessary to the order. [Para 173][Para 174]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The referral is described as five demands [Para 19] but only four are set out [Para 2][Para 5][Para 9][Para 11]; the panel speaks of four non-conciliated points out of five demands without reconciling the two.
- The 7% demand is called issue 4 at [Para 133] and issue 3 at [Para 178] and [Para 194]; the operative order follows the latter.
- Attendance is given as two delegates and twelve workers at [Para 74] and [Para 149], but the list at [Para 74] names fourteen people, and the claimant group is 65 at [Para 72] though the complaint is said to come from 20 at [Para 18].
- Teacher numbers do not add up: 100 teachers of whom 4 are Khmer and 96 foreign [Para 68][Para 69], although 1 full-time and 3 part-time Khmer teachers plus 96 foreign would exceed 100.
- The award is dated 1 August 2016 [Para 1][Para 28] but the panel is said to have been constituted on "23 June 2022 2016" [Para 21], and Prakas No. 099 is dated "21 April 2019 2004" at [Para 127].
- Paragraph 6 is a stray fragment ("19 %.") and paragraph 193 garbles the policy numbers, printing "BBC 4.278" and "2012" where policy No. 5.838 is meant; the employer's jurisdictional objection is recorded twice in near-identical terms at [Para 4] and [Para 8].
