# Award No. 043/14 — Wai Alert E Pearl (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 4 April 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Liv Sovanna, Sok Matheung presiding) · Doc ID 139526

## 1. Facts
The employer, a garment factory of about 1,420 workers, agreed with three in-house unions on 4 January 2014 to terminate every worker's indefinite-term contract, pay 60 US dollars per year of service as severance, close seniority at 31 December 2013, and re-hire everyone on six-month fixed-term contracts from 1 January 2014. Workers endorsed the arrangement by thumbprint and took the money. The union federation then claimed the full statutory severance package for the terminated indefinite-term contracts. [Para 75][Para 78][Para 88][Para 120]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the severance package for terminated indefinite-term contracts, namely notice pay, dismissal compensation and moral damages under Articles 77, 89 and 91 of the Labour Law, on top of the 60 dollars per year already paid? [Para 96][Para 109]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the 60 dollar per year payment agreed in the minutes is not the severance the law requires; they do not seek to annul the agreement but ask the panel to raise the payment to the statutory level, computed as notice pay, dismissal compensation and moral damages. [Para 98][Para 100]
**Employer (respondent):** it had no wish to terminate anyone; it ended the indefinite-term contracts only at the unions' repeated request and after a thumbprint ballot, paid the agreed 60 dollars per year plus final wages and unused leave, and cannot pay more. [Para 104][Para 106][Para 118]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim for statutory severance was dismissed, but the clause extinguishing seniority and converting the workforce to fixed-term contracts was declared void; workers keep indefinite-term contracts and original seniority, must repay the 60 dollars per year they received, and the employer must restore the seniority payments it deducted from January 2014. [Para 149][Para 151][Para 154]
On representation the panel found the in-house National Union of Cambodian Workers was not yet registered with the Ministry, so it was not a lawful professional organisation and could not be treated as a party to the 3 October 2012 memorandum; the four workers who held written authority from 790 colleagues could nonetheless act for them, though an untitled, undated thumbprint list of a further group could not be accepted as a transfer of authority. [Para 28][Para 68][Para 71][Para 74]
On the severance claim the panel applied Articles 74 to 77 and 89: statutory notice pay, dismissal compensation and moral damages arise only where the employer terminates an indefinite-term contract by its own unilateral will. Here the termination followed the workers' own request and their acceptance of the employer's counter-offer, so a contract was formed under Articles 336, 337 and 342 of the Civil Code and no statutory package was owed. [Para 110][Para 123][Para 128][Para 129]
The panel then examined the agreement of its own motion. Under Article 357 of the Civil Code, an act contrary to a mandatory provision is void, and the parties must be restored to their prior position, each returning what it received. Because a change of contract type while the workers continued working for the same employer cannot extinguish accrued seniority, the clause wiping seniority was void from the outset; the workers were restored to indefinite-term contracts with unchanged seniority, and the 60 dollar payments had to be returned as unjust enrichment. [Para 139][Para 142][Para 145][Para 147][Para 148]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Statutory severance under Articles 77, 89 and 91 is owed only on the employer's unilateral termination of an indefinite-term contract; a termination agreed at the workers' request, even on the employer's counter-terms, is a consensual termination and attracts no such package. But an agreement that converts an existing indefinite-term workforce to fixed-term contracts and wipes accrued seniority is void, because seniority cannot be extinguished while the same workers continue working for the same employer; the parties are restored and payments made under the void clause must be returned. [Para 129][Para 139][Para 148]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that an internal union which has applied for but not obtained registration cannot be regarded as a lawful professional organisation or as a party to the industry memorandum, even where the federation it belongs to signed that memorandum. [Para 28][Para 30]
