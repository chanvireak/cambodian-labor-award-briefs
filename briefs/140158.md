# Award No. 145/12 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ដាយ៉ាំង (ខេមបូឌា) (Dayang (Cambodia) Co., Ltd) (Arbitration Council, 2 October 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-selected), Liv Sovanna (worker-selected, dissenting on Issue 4), Tang Tri (president) · Doc ID 140158

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ដាយ៉ាំង (ខេមបូឌា) is a garment factory on National Road 5 in Phnom Penh employing about 990 workers. [Para 55] Two unions represent the workforce, the Cambodia Workers' Union Federation and its factory branch, each with some 417 members, and neither is the most representative in the factory. [Para 56][Para 57] The five demands were conciliated on 16 July 2012 without agreement, and report 822 of 23 July 2012 reached the Secretariat on 24 July 2012. [Para 14][Para 15] Hearings were held on 6 August and 12 September 2012, and at the second hearing three demands (Issues 2, 3 and 5) were settled, leaving Issues 1 and 4 for decision. [Para 11][Para 12][Para 18] The parties had signed a memorandum of understanding of 28 September 2010 under which they agreed that the panel's decisions on rights disputes would be binding. [Para 19][Para 20]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must grant special leave when a worker's parent dies, preserving wages and other benefits without deducting the days from annual leave? [Para 58][Para 189]
2. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must reinstate the dismissed president of the factory union, on the ground that her dismissal did not follow legal procedure? [Para 64][Para 191]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** special leave on a parent's death is a necessity that the employer ought to grant as a favour, and it should not be set against annual leave; and the union president's contract was terminated unlawfully, because the employer neither followed the procedure for dismissing a union leader nor proved the theft it alleged, so she should return to work. [Para 58][Para 5][Para 64]
**Employer (respondent):** it grants seven days of special leave when a parent dies but deducts those days from annual leave, and it cannot go further; and it dismissed the union president for serious misconduct — taking a company shirt on 20 June 2012 — and for abandoning her job from that date, and it sought the labour department's authorisation on 9 July 2012. [Para 59][Para 62][Para 66][Para 67]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel refused the demand to preserve wages during special leave without any deduction, but ordered the employer to stop deducting special leave from a future year's annual leave, and it refused the demand to reinstate the union president; because the parties had agreed to be bound on rights disputes the award is immediately final on those points. [Para 189][Para 190][Para 191][Para 192]
On Issue 1 the panel held the demand to be a rights dispute and applied Article 171 of the Labour Law with Prakas 267 of 11 October 2001: the employer may grant up to seven days of paid special leave for family events, and may set those days against annual leave where the worker has not yet used it. [Para 123][Para 131][Para 136] It therefore found no obligation to preserve wages and benefits without any deduction and refused that part of the demand. [Para 134][Para 141] It also found the employer's practice of deducting special leave from the *following* year's annual leave unlawful, since Article 171(3) and Article 2 of Prakas 267 permit only a set-off against leave already accrued, or repayment; it ordered the practice stopped at once. [Para 135][Para 137][Para 140][Para 142]
On Issue 4 the panel first held the dispute to be one of rights, because the claim rested on Article 293 of the Labour Law. [Para 143][Para 144] Applying Article 83 and Article 10 of the internal regulations, it found on the security and packaging witnesses' evidence that the union president had taken a company shirt on 20 June 2012, which is serious misconduct. [Para 146][Para 148][Para 149][Para 150] It then found that the employer knew of the misconduct on 20 June but applied for authorisation to dismiss only on 9 July 2012, beyond the seven-day limit in Article 26, so it had forfeited the right to dismiss on that ground. [Para 162][Para 163][Para 164] It further found that the Article 293 request for the labour inspector's prior approval of a union leader's dismissal had gone unanswered for more than a month, which is deemed a refusal. [Para 166][Para 168] Nevertheless it held that the worker had been absent without leave for more than six days, which under Article 6 of the internal regulations amounts to abandonment, so the employment relationship ended on 28 June 2012 before the dismissal letter, and it refused reinstatement. [Para 179][Para 184][Para 185][Para 186][Para 188][Para 191]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may set paid special leave for a family death against annual leave already accrued, but may not deduct it from a future year's leave; the only permitted recovery where leave is exhausted is repayment. And while theft is serious misconduct, an employer that waits more than seven days after learning of it before seeking authorisation, and that dismisses a union leader without the labour inspector's prior approval, forfeits the right to dismiss on that ground. [Para 137][Para 164][Para 168]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that the worker's unauthorised absence exceeded six days and amounted to abandonment before the dismissal letter issued, and that a nurse or office worker is an employee rather than a manual worker, were not necessary to the refusal ordered on Issue 4 once the forfeiture findings were made. [Para 184][Para 186]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is internally contradictory on Issue 4: the reasons hold that the employer forfeited its right to dismiss and that the labour inspector's silence is deemed a refusal, yet the order refuses reinstatement because employment is said to have ended by abandonment before the dismissal letter. [Para 164][Para 168][Para 186][Para 191] The award date is printed as 2 October 2012 in the header but the reasons record that it was due on 13 July 2012 and extended only to 28 September 2012. [Para 1][Para 25] The company's name appears as ដាយ៉ាំង, ដាយ យ៉ាំង and ដាយ៉ាំង ខេមបូឌា ខូអិលធីឌី, and two similarly named unions are distinguished only by the addition of "federation". [Para 1][Para 56][Para 57] The worker at the centre of Issue 4 is a named individual and is identified here only by office; the employer's representatives are likewise named in the header and are referred to by role. [Para 1][Para 28] Several paragraphs of the reasoning are duplicated or overrun by Latin character noise, and the six-day abandonment finding is stated with two different end dates. [Para 179][Para 184][Para 216]

**Dissenting opinion:** the worker-selected arbitrator dissented on Issue 4, holding that the employer had decided to dismiss on 20 June, that the labour inspectorate's silence was a deemed refusal, that the dismissal letter of 29 June was served without authority, and that reinstatement should therefore be ordered. [Para 193][Para 206][Para 225][Para 249]
