# Award No. 18/04 — Sunway Hotel (Arbitration Council, 6 September 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mar Samborana (employer-chosen), An Nan (worker-chosen), Mathew Rendall (chair) · Doc ID 139302

## 1. Facts
Sunway Hotel, near Wat Phnom in Daun Penh District, collected a 10% service charge from 1997 until 1 February 2004, when it stopped and substituted a fixed US$30 per worker on top of basic salary [Para 19][Para 21]. Collective bargaining begun on 13 August 2003 broke down over that charge, and about 71 workers struck on 9 April 2004 [Para 18][Para 29]. The Council ordered them back to work without sanction on 12 April and again on 22 April; the hotel refused to comply and suspended 28 workers [Para 30][Para 33]. Negotiations later returned 25 of them, leaving three union leaders suspended [Para 50]. Both sides asked the Council to rule on those three and agreed the award would not be binding [Para 40].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must collect the service charge and share it with all hotel employees in compliance with the Labour Law? [Para 11]
2. Whether the employer must pay the employees it suspended during the arbitral proceedings, as of 12 April 2004? [Para 11]
3. Whether the 28 suspended workers must be reinstated? [Para 11]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the service charge had been shared equally, at between US$20 and US$50 a month, and the hotel's unilateral replacement of it with a flat US$30 breached the law; the workers demanded 75% of the charge with 25% retained by the employer [Para 20][Para 23]. They asked that the suspended workers return without punishment and receive their wages [Para 62].
**Employer (respondent):** it offered only a fixed sum, or at most 30% of the charge if collected, and then declined to appear at the hearing of 10 May 2004, so the case was heard in its absence [Para 24][Para 35][Para 36]. It treated the strike as illegal, opposed any extension of the 15-day period, and suspended the 28 pending a court decision [Para 53][Para 72].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must re-employ the three remaining workers immediately and pay them full salary and benefits from 13 April 2004 until they are re-employed, with no back pay for any who do not return within 48 hours of the award coming into force; the hotel's objection to the Council's orders was rejected; and the service charge issue was deferred until MoSALVY issues a Prakas [Para 82][Para 83][Para 84][Para 85].

On jurisdiction the panel noted that it had passed the 15-day period in Article 313 without the parties' agreement, the deadline having expired on 27 April 2004 [Para 51][Para 52]. Article 313 does not extinguish jurisdiction when the limit is exceeded, and the Council may extend where the claimant makes a reasonable request — which the workers did, to take part in conciliation [Para 54][Para 55]. The hotel's refusal to extend had a consequence it may not have intended: under Article 320 its employees thereby acquired the right to strike, yet they chose to ask for an award instead, so the request remained proper [Para 57][Para 58].

On the suspensions the panel relied on Rule 20 of the Prakas on the Arbitration Council, which prohibits any strike, lockout or aggravating act during proceedings; suspending the 28 workers, including their leaders, was such an aggravating act [Para 62][Para 63]. Its orders of 12 and 22 April rested on Rule 4.3 of the Procedural Rules and Article 34-D of the Prakas, are not open to objection under Article 40, and bind immediately [Para 66][Para 67][Para 68]. Since the hotel did not comply, it must pay in full from 13 April 2004 until the award [Para 69][Para 70].

On the punishment, the panel followed Case 28/04 (Raffles Grand Hotel d'Angkor): Article 71 permits suspension without pay only for its eleven listed reasons, and awaiting the outcome of an investigation is not one of them, though suspension for that purpose is permissible if wages are paid monthly under Article 116 [Para 76][Para 77]. Article 333 forbids punishing a worker for strike participation, and although Lida Garment (04/03) denies unlawful strikers the full Chapter XII protection, it does not strip all protection, and Article 27 requires proportionality [Para 78]. Dismissal is proportionate only for the serious misconduct described in Articles 337 and 330 — returning late after a court declares a strike illegal, or violence — and here no court had declared the strike illegal and no other misconduct was evidenced, so neither indefinite unpaid suspension nor dismissal was reasonable [Para 80][Para 81][Para 82].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Exceeding the 15-day period in Article 313 does not extinguish the Council's jurisdiction, and it may extend time on the claimant's reasonable request; but a respondent who refuses an extension cannot complain when its employees thereby gain the Article 320 right to strike [Para 54][Para 57]. A suspension imposed during arbitration is an aggravating act barred by Rule 20 and the Council's procedural orders are immediately binding and not open to objection, so wages run from the date the worker was kept out [Para 63][Para 68]. Suspension without pay while an investigation proceeds is not among Article 71's eleven grounds and wages must be paid monthly under Article 116; and indefinite suspension or dismissal for joining a strike never declared illegal offends Article 27 proportionality and Article 333 [Para 76][Para 78].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel clarified that a later court declaration that the April 2004 strike was illegal would not entitle the hotel to dismiss these workers, because Article 337 addresses failure to return to work after such a declaration, and that dismissal under it requires a final decision (saal krom chong kraoy) of the Municipal Court, not an emergency injunction (deka taam peak som) [Para 86][Para 87][Para 88].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award records that both sides agreed it "is not binding", yet its closing clause states that it becomes binding after 8 days absent opposition [Para 40][Para 88]. The chair is printed as "Mathew Rendall" in the panel composition and "Matthew Rendall" at the signature [Para 15][Para 88], and the general manager appears as both "Manfred Haeger" and "Manfred Hager" [Para 3][Para 56]. The strikers are "approximately 71" in one passage and "over 70" in the findings [Para 29][Para 45]. The service charge issue is listed as the first issue in dispute but the panel suspended it rather than deciding it, so the award leaves it open [Para 11][Para 60]. Several passages are corrupted by OCR duplication and dropped words — the Rule 4.3 quotation repeats and breaks off, and one reason line reads "suspend an employer" where an employee is meant [Para 66][Para 76].
