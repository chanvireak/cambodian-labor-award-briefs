# Award No. 024/18 — Textile Garment Hoeng Jing Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 27 July 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Yim Sothy (employer-appointed), Sin Kim Sean (worker-appointed), Pen Bunhea (president) · non-binding award · Doc ID 138903

## 1. Facts
Around 190 workers were employed at the Hoeng Jing garment factory in Phnom Penh; their union was registered with the Ministry of Labour on 25 May 2018 and three worker representatives were recognised by the Labour Disputes Department on 20 June 2018. [Para 1][Para 109][Para 110][Para 111]
The workers alleged that the factory had changed owners — the former director Fan Xiao Lin being replaced by Dai Zeng Jai, who claimed to have bought the enterprise — and demanded statutory compensation up to the date of the change. [Para 114][Para 115][Para 221]
Two named workers, Ms M (Mech Srey Touch) and Ms M (Mang Thak), were dismissed in May 2018, and a third, Ms T (Thou Somary), had her contract suspended on 50% wages after she refused a transfer from the "counting" (គិតបុង) section to a sewing assistant post. [Para 134][Para 140][Para 177][Para 187]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay statutory compensation up to the date the new owner took over? [Para 2][Para 211]
2. Whether the employer must facilitate maternity leave so that women workers receive the 70% maternity allowance from the National Social Security Fund? [Para 4]
3. Whether the employer must reinstate dismissed union members at their original posts and pay severance, wages and benefits for Ms M and Ms M from the date of dismissal? [Para 6][Para 226]
4. Whether the employer must pay wages and benefits for the period it suspended a union member's contract without authorisation from the labour inspector? [Para 8][Para 171]

Issue 2 was settled by agreement of the parties at the hearing and was not adjudicated. [Para 21][Para 22]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The company's director changed from Fan Xiao Lin to Dai Zeng Jai, who bought the enterprise, replaced the machines and changed the company seal; the contracts were re-signed with the new owner and the workers feared he would not pay their statutory termination entitlements, so those entitlements should be settled now. [Para 114][Para 115][Para 116][Para 118][Para 120]
**Employer (respondent):** The company has not changed director — Fan Xiao Lin remains the owner and Dai Zeng Jai is merely a general manager hired to supervise operations; the machinery was replaced only because it was old and productivity needed to rise, a point the workers themselves had raised. [Para 122][Para 123][Para 124]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issue 1 dismissed; Issue 2 settled by agreement; Issue 3 granted, with five heads of payment (payment in lieu of notice, dismissal indemnity, moral damages, unused annual leave, final wages) to Ms M and Ms M; Issue 4 granted, with 50% of wages and other benefits for Ms T for the suspension period. No global sum in riel or USD was computed on the face of the award, save that Ms T's base wage is printed as 180 USD per month plus a 25 USD allowance. [Para 173][Para 395][Para 396][Para 397]

On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 87(1) of the 1997 Labour Law: a change in the employer's legal situation — succession, sale, merger or conversion into a company — leaves contracts in force between the new employer and the existing workers. Following its earlier awards (No. 98/07 Sky Sino, No. 83/08 Le Grand Café, No. 100/08 Giant Rich), the panel held that even if an owner change occurred, the incoming employer must recognise the workers' seniority and benefits from their first day. Since work continued normally with no change of contract terms and no loss of benefit, no compensation was owed. [Para 211][Para 213][Para 215][Para 222][Para 223][Para 224]

On Issue 3 the panel dealt first with Ms M (Mech Srey Touch). Her half-day unauthorised absence on 4 May 2018, and her refusal to sign an acknowledgment, did not meet the Article 83(b) definition of serious misconduct; the internal rules (Prakas-approved 23 December 2014) prescribed graduated discipline — oral education, written warning, then dismissal — and the panel held dismissal disproportionate under Article 27. Article 74 requires a reasonable ground and Article 75 requires written notice, which was not given; the panel therefore awarded notice in lieu (Articles 75 and 77), dismissal indemnity at 15 days per year of service capped at six months (Article 89), moral damages equal to the dismissal indemnity (Article 91), unused annual leave (Articles 166 and 167) and final wages (Article 116). Average wage was to be computed on the last 12 months divided by 12, then by 26 for the daily rate. [Para 234][Para 245][Para 246][Para 247][Para 252][Para 262][Para 267][Para 275][Para 277][Para 287][Para 297]

For Ms M (Mang Thak) the panel accepted the workers' evidence that she started on 28 April 2016, rejected the employer's contradictory claim of 28 May 2016 for want of any supporting contract, and applied Article 67(2): successive fixed-term contracts totalling more than two years convert to an indefinite-term contract. Her total service of about 2 years and 10 days therefore made her a permanent employee, so non-renewal was in law a dismissal without a reasonable ground under Article 74, attracting the same five heads of payment. [Para 305][Para 316][Para 317][Para 330][Para 333][Para 342][Para 343][Para 349]

On Issue 4 the panel held that the employer could not unilaterally move Ms T from her counting post to a sewing assistant role, which carried lower benefits, without her agreement: Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the 2007 Civil Code require mutual consent to modify a contract. The transfer order being unlawful, the 50% wage suspension imposed for her refusal was likewise unlawful, and the employer was ordered to pay the withheld 50% and other benefits for the suspension period. [Para 382][Para 389][Para 391][Para 392][Para 394]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A change of owner or of the employer's legal status does not by itself entitle workers to immediate payment of termination entitlements, but the incoming employer inherits the obligation to recognise their accrued seniority and benefits under Article 87; and successive fixed-term contracts whose combined duration exceeds two years convert into an indefinite-term contract, so that their non-renewal is judged as a dismissal requiring a reasonable ground and written notice. [Para 222][Para 330]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the 1997 Labour Law leans towards indefinite-term contracts because job security benefits workers and long service encourages employer commitment, and that ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982, though non-binding, usefully informs Article 67. It also noted that only the most representative union holds exclusive rights to conclude collective agreements and to represent workers in collective disputes, and that the three recognised worker representatives could act because no most-representative union existed at the company. [Para 196][Para 206][Para 322][Para 328]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The purchase price of the enterprise is printed two ways: "ជិត ៨០$ (ប្រាំបី ម៉ឺន ដុល្លារ)" at Para 84 and "៨ ម៉ឺន ដុល្យារ" (80,000 USD) at Para 115. Ms M's total service is stated as "2 years 10 days" at Para 333 in the same sentence that prints "លើស ពី ២០ ឆ្នាំ" (more than 20 years), an evident OCR or drafting slip for two years. Para 335 dates the continued work to 19 May 2016 where the reasons elsewhere fix 19 May 2018. The case number appears as 024/18 in the header but 024/28 at Para 22. The factory's business registration is dated 05 December 2014 at Para 28 and 21 November 2014 at Para 90. The company name is rendered variously as Hoeng Jin, Hing Jing and Hoeag Jimmexule across the document, and the former director appears as Fan Xiao Lin, Fan Sav Ly and Fan Sao Lin.
