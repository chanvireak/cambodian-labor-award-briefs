# Award No. 044/16 — Zina Dai Dong (Cambodia) Electronic (Arbitration Council, 15 March 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Run Saray, chair; Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Vireak, appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 138991

## 1. Facts
Zina Dai Dong (Cambodia) Electronic (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្ស៊ិណា ដាយ ដុង (ខេមបូឌា) អេឡិចត្រូនិច), a manufacturer of telephone headset cable in Ang Snuol district, Kandal province, employed about 591 workers. [Para 32][Para 33] The Cambodian Industrial Garment Workers' Federation and its enterprise union, which held no registration certificate from the Ministry of Labour, filed a six-point complaint; three points were settled in conciliation and, of the three that remained, the workers withdrew point 1 at the hearing, leaving points 2 and 3. [Para 13][Para 18][Para 19][Para 34] The surviving demands were three cans of milk a month for women who had given birth and a transport allowance of 15 US dollars a month. [Para 4][Para 6][Para 40][Para 47]

## 2. Issues in dispute
2. Whether the employer must supply three cans of milk a month to women workers with new-born children, up to the age of 18 months? [Para 40][Para 66]
3. Whether the employer must pay a transport allowance of 15 US dollars a month to each worker? [Para 47][Para 82]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** after childbirth women workers are short of money and cannot buy milk for their children, and other companies do pay a milk allowance; although the company has a nursery and child-minders, workers cannot bring their children because the journey is difficult. [Para 41][Para 43][Para 44][Para 45] On transport, the company pays only 7 dollars a month while the fare costs up to 14 dollars, and they maintained the demand for 15 dollars even after the employer said it now pays 12. [Para 47][Para 50]
**Employer (respondent):** it already provides a nursery and child-minders, and beyond that the company is in economic difficulty with few buyers; it raised the transport allowance from 7 to 12 dollars a month from August 2014 and cannot pay 15. [Para 46][Para 48][Para 49]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both demands were declined and no sum in riel or dollars was awarded. [Para 89][Para 90]

Before the merits the panel had to settle who the claimants were. Article 268 of the Labour Law gives a professional organisation the rights the Law recognises only once it is registered with the ministry, and the enterprise union held no certificate. [Para 34][Para 51][Para 52][Para 56][Para 57] Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 nonetheless lets a party be represented by any person holding a written authorisation, and the federation held mandates from 32 workers dated 4 January 2016. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60] The employer produced its letter rejecting the complaint, listing 21 workers who had refused to support it; 18 of those were named in the mandate and three — Mr T, Mr K and Mr R — were not, and the workers did not contest this, so the panel found the claimants reduced to 14. [Para 38][Para 40][Para 61][Para 63][Para 64][Para 65]

On issue 2, the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rule, employment contract, agreement or past practice obliging the employer to supply three cans of milk a month, and classified the demand as one for a benefit above the law, hence an interest dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍). [Para 67][Para 68][Para 69] On issue 3 it made the same finding on the 15-dollar transport allowance. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 85]

Both being interest disputes, the panel applied Article 9(1) of Prakas No. 305 — only a most-representative union may negotiate a collective agreement binding all workers — together with Article 43 of Prakas No. 099, under which an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement for one year, and held that it may decide such a dispute only where the union bringing it is most representative, or where unions together represent more than half the workforce. [Para 71][Para 74][Para 75][Para 76] It followed its earlier awards in Cases 81/04, 98/04, 202/14, 02/11, 66/11 and 206/12. [Para 77][Para 78] Here the complainants were 14 individual workers, not a most-representative union, so the panel held they had no standing to bring an interest dispute and declined both demands. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 81][Para 86][Para 87][Para 88]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Standing to appear and standing to raise an interest dispute are different things: written mandates under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 let a union federation represent identified individual workers before the panel, but a claim for a benefit that no law, agreement, rule or practice grants can be decided only if the body bringing it is the most representative union in the enterprise, and a handful of mandated individuals is not. [Para 59][Para 65][Para 76][Para 79]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded without deciding anything on it that the company already ran a nursery with child-minders, which the workers said they could not use because of the journey — a point that would have mattered only had the milk demand been a rights claim. [Para 41][Para 45][Para 46]
