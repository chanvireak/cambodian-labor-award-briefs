# Award No. 89/09 — Pak Fat Garmen (Cambodia) Ltd and the unions of Tak Faat factory (Arbitration Council of Cambodia, 28 July 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Kav Tha កៅ ថា (employer-appointed), Tuon Si Phan ទួន ស៊ី ផាន់ (worker-appointed), Ang Eng Tong អាំង អេង ថុង (president) · Doc ID 140409

## 1. Facts
Pak Fat Garmen (Cambodia) Ltd, on National Road 2 in Chak Angre Leu, Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employed about 2,011 workers across three adjacent buildings — Nos 2, 3 and a five-storey No 5. [Para 1][Para 31][Para 33] After an 11 June 2009 agreement with all five factory unions, the company asked every worker to move from the ageing Buildings 2 and 3 into Building 5 while the two old buildings were repaired, promising to preserve all existing benefits including seniority; point 1 of that agreement recorded a USD 5 seniority bonus for May 2009, payable on 17 June 2009, against the workers' voluntary move from 12 June 2009, and the same terms were confirmed again on 3 July 2009. [Para 3][Para 38][Para 40]

The workers at first refused, saying they had lost confidence in the company because it had moved them from place to place several times after telling them each move was the last. [Para 4] By the hearing, however, all of them were working in Building 5 from 6 July 2009 and the USD 5 had been paid out by 11 July 2009. [Para 41][Para 42] The company's remaining complaint — that output had fallen because workers were not applying themselves — was not decided: the panel expressly issued no order on it and encouraged the parties to negotiate incentive pay instead. [Para 43][Para 66][Para 67] None of the five unions present at the hearing was the most representative union. [Para 32]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer's transfer of the whole workforce to Building 5 was made lawfully? [Para 48]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**ភាគីនិយោជក — Pak Fat Garmen (Cambodia) Ltd (applicant before the panel):** the move was required by the 11 June 2009 agreement with the unions because Buildings 2 and 3 are old and unsafe — rain enters through the roof, soaks the fabric and disrupts production, and buyers may stop ordering from an unsafe, unclean site; wages, seniority and all other benefits would be unchanged, and the new building is only 5 to 10 metres away. [Para 3][Para 33][Para 34][Para 37]

**ភាគីកម្មករនិយោជិត — the four unions appearing for the workers of Tak Faat factory (ពាក ហ្វាត), including the Democratic Apparel Workers' Union (សហភាព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ) and the factory workers' union:** they could not obey the transfer because the company had broken its word before, moving them repeatedly while promising each time that it was the last move, so they no longer trusted it; any fall in output came from new garment styles that cannot be sewn as fast as the old ones, not from unwillingness. [Para 4][Para 44][Para 62]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel held the transfer to Building 5 lawful and closed case No. 89/09 Tak Fat with no sum ordered, the relocation having already been completed on 6 July 2009. [Para 69][Para 70]

The panel began from Article 2, second paragraph, of the Labour Law: an enterprise may have several establishments, each employing a group of people working together at a place such as a factory or workshop, under the management of an employer. It read that provision, as it had in Awards 62/06, 108/06, 33/07, 106/07, 84/08 and 141/08, as conferring on the employer the right to organise and manage the company, provided the exercise is lawful and reasonable. [Para 48][Para 51][Para 52]

It then applied the test from Awards 17/03 and 18/03 (Hoeung Garmen): an employer may change the organisation of work so long as the change does not cut wages, does not move the workplace far from the original site, does not switch shifts from day to night or night to day, and does not affect essential professional skills — a change crossing those lines amounts to a breach of the contract and must follow termination procedure. Here both sides agreed that none of those lines was crossed. [Para 53][Para 54]

On the facts, the reason for the move was safety: Buildings 2 and 3 are dilapidated and could injure workers, photographs confirmed their condition, and the company had decided to repair both. The panel found a lawful right and a reasonable ground, and also found that the workers had voluntarily agreed to the move in the 11 June 2009 agreement and had in fact complied while the company paid the USD 5 seniority bonus, so both sides had performed the agreement. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57][Para 61]

Two further points were made without any order. The panel accepted that relocation can cause genuine difficulty for both sides, but said it has no role in resolving problems arising from the workers' loss of trust or their unwillingness to exert themselves; it observed that the employer has lawful options to rebuild confidence — incentive bonuses, a change in the wage method, extra benefits — and encouraged the company to consult worker representatives on any future transfer. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65][Para 67][Para 68]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may move its workforce to another building inside the same establishment, and need not follow termination procedure, where the change leaves wages, commuting distance, shift timing and professional skill untouched and rests on a safety reason plus a collective agreement that the workers performed. [Para 53][Para 57]

**Obiter dicta:** that a panel will not adjudicate a drop in productivity attributed to the workers' attitude, and that rebuilding trust after repeated relocations is a matter for the employer's own incentive and consultation measures. [Para 64][Para 65]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The plant's name is printed in at least three different Khmer forms across the face of the award, the findings and the operative order: the case title reads "Tak Faat" while the party box gives "Phak Faat Garmen (Cambodia) Ltd" (ភាក ហ្វាត ហ្គាមិន), and the two are used interchangeably for the same employer, so it appears under several transliterations. The claimant union is likewise printed once as the democratic apparel workers' union of the factory and elsewhere under its C.CAWDU abbreviation, and the five unions' names are given in inconsistent order. One passage describes the destination as a 59-storey building where the rest of the award says five storeys. Two document dates are printed twice with conflicting years, one of them a decade out. The five unions are listed in full with their officers' names and posts, which this brief does not reproduce; one union is recorded as absent. OCR loss is severe in the signature block, which carries stray digits and Latin fragments in place of the arbitrators' signatures, and the worker-appointed arbitrator's line is reduced to punctuation and numbers.
