# Award No. 037/19 — Dab Bil U N D (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 3 June 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Palla presiding; Ing Sothy appointed by the employer; Sin Kim Sean appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 138849

## 1. Facts
Dab Bil U N D (Cambodia) is a garment manufacturer at Steung Mean Chey, Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, which the panel records as having 1,529 workers at the date of the award. [Para 1][Para 56][Para 55] Its workforce struck for 3 months and 4 days: 948 workers joined the strike, 780 kept working and 72 left employment. [Para 61][Para 62] On 29 January 2019 the Labour Disputes Department received a complaint, made by telephone, raising two demands arising from that strike; conciliation left one demand unresolved, which reached the secretariat on 30 April 2019, and the panel heard it on 9 May 2019. [Para 9][Para 12][Para 13][Para 8] That remaining demand concerned the employer's dissolution of production Group 4 and the redistribution of its 61 members to other groups. [Para 60][Para 66][Para 67]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer has the right to reorganise the work and transfer the 61 workers who are members of Group 4 to work in other groups? [Para 60][Para 101][Para 128]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Group 4 had enough members to run its own line, and breaking it up after the strike was punishment because Group 4 had the largest number of strikers; some members were moved to pressing, serging and thread-cutting rather than their sewing skill, and they fear losing seniority, as happened in 2008 when the employer changed their workplace and their identity cards. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 73][Para 75][Para 77] At the hearing they argued the dispute was an individual one, because selection was by lottery and each contract is individual. [Para 85][Para 90]
**Employer (respondent):** the production line had to be reorganised after heavy strike losses, and this is its managerial prerogative under Articles 2 and 65 of the Labour Law, Article 64 of the Civil Code and article 3 of the internal rules; no wage was cut, no one was moved far away, no shift was reversed and no major change of skill was imposed, and workers were taught the new styles. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 80][Para 142] Transfer was by lottery precisely to avoid managers picking only the best workers for their own groups. [Para 82]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the workers' demand that the employer be ordered not to dissolve Group 4; the award is non-binding and becomes final 8 (eight) days after notification if no party objects to the Minister of Labour. [Para 159][Para 161][Para 162]
On jurisdiction, the panel held that Article 32 of Prakas No. 099 confines it to collective disputes, and applied the three conditions in Article 302 of the Labour Law as construed in Award No. 10/03 (Jackson Tech): a dispute between a number of workers and one or more employers, about working conditions, capable of disrupting the enterprise or social peace. [Para 91][Para 92][Para 97][Para 98][Para 99] All three were met — 61 workers, a working-conditions claim, and a strike that blocked the production line — and the employer did not contest jurisdiction. [Para 102][Para 105][Para 106][Para 109]
On the merits, the panel read Article 2 of the Labour Law as conferring the right to manage and direct the enterprise, exercisable only lawfully and reasonably, and followed its earlier awards setting four conditions for a lawful transfer: no wage cut, no relocation far away, no reversal of day and night shifts, and no substantially different skill. [Para 128][Para 130][Para 133][Para 135][Para 136] Both parties admitted the transfer touched neither wage, shift nor workplace, and the workers produced no particulars or evidence of the work in the receiving sections, so the fourth condition was not shown to be breached. [Para 137][Para 138][Para 140][Para 143][Para 144]
On the allegation of punishment, the panel applied the rule that the party asserting bears the burden of proof, found no evidence that the reorganisation was a reprisal for strike participation, and declined to decide the workers' fear of future loss of seniority because the Council resolves disputes that have arisen, not future ones. [Para 146][Para 147][Para 151][Para 154][Para 156][Para 158]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Article 2 of the Labour Law gives an employer the right to reorganise production and transfer workers within its own premises, and that right is exercised lawfully where the transfer does not cut wages, does not move workers far away, does not reverse day and night shifts and does not impose a substantially different skill; a worker who says the transfer is a reprisal for taking part in a strike must prove it, and an apprehension that seniority will be lost in future is not a dispute the panel will decide. [Para 133][Para 135][Para 147][Para 156]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that the employer closed Building B during the strike, ran Building A with eight groups only, and on the workers' return reopened Building B with five further groups, thirteen in all. [Para 83][Para 84]
