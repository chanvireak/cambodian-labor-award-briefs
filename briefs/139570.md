# Award No. 114/14 — Sukses Index Group (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 13 June 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Yu Soun Ty for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party — dissenting on issue 1, Mae Nimmith chair) · Doc ID 139570

## 1. Facts
Sukses Index Group (Cambodia) Ltd, a garment factory at Trapeang Chheu Neang, Ang Snuol, Kandal with about 600 workers, declined to renew the one-year fixed-duration contract of Mr V, a sewing worker since 05 January 2012 elected deputy president of the factory union on 15 September 2013. [Para 58][Para 60][Para 61] His contract expired on 30 April 2014 after notice on 11 April 2014; he had earlier held an unlimited-duration contract which the employer terminated with statutory compensation before he signed the fixed-term contract on 01 May 2013. [Para 84][Para 85][Para 122] The union also claimed wage deductions made on 10 February 2014 from three leaders who attended an ILO course without prior leave. [Para 86]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take Mr V back, reinstate him as deputy union president, stop discriminating against the union and pay back wages from the date of dismissal to the date of return? [Para 64][Para 117]
2. Whether Mr V's contract was of unlimited or fixed duration, and whether Article 67(2) bars him from signing a fixed-duration contract after more than two years' service? [Para 122][Para 144]
3. Whether the employer needed the labour inspector's authorisation before not renewing a fixed-duration contract held by a union leader? [Para 160]
4. Whether the non-renewal was an act of anti-union discrimination? [Para 167]
5. Whether the employer must repay the wages and allowances deducted for 10 February 2014 from the three union leaders? [Para 86][Para 194]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr V had never been at fault nor warned, had worked more than two years so his contract was of unlimited duration, and the employer refused to renew the contracts of exactly three union leaders — president, deputy president and activist — at once, which shows anti-union discrimination; the three had told the employer they were attending ILO training and had always been allowed to request leave afterwards. [Para 71][Para 73][Para 74][Para 97]
**Employer (respondent):** Article 73 gives it the right not to renew a fixed-duration contract; Mr V freely signed the one-year contract after receiving statutory compensation for the earlier unlimited-duration contract; the union was not registered at the time so there could be no discrimination; and the three leaders absented themselves without notice, contrary to Article 8(b)(1) of the internal regulation. [Para 70][Para 68][Para 69][Para 75][Para 90][Para 98]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed on all issues — the panel refused reinstatement and back wages and refused repayment of the 10 February 2014 deductions; the rights-dispute award becomes final immediately and applies to the parties only, under the Memorandum of Understanding of 03 October 2012. [Para 193][Para 227][Para 228][Para 229][Para 230]

On the union's standing the panel applied Article 268(2) of the Labour Law: where the Ministry does not respond within two months of a registration application, the organisation is deemed registered. [Para 104] The union held the Ministry's receipt dated 02 December 2013, more than two months before the 20 May 2014 hearing, so it had become a lawful professional organisation. [Para 105][Para 107][Para 111]

On the contract both parties agreed Mr V began on 05 January 2012 under an unlimited-duration contract, that the employer terminated it and paid statutory severance, and that he then signed a one-year fixed-duration contract on 01 May 2013. [Para 148][Para 149][Para 155] Reading Article 311 and Articles 336–337 of the Civil Code, the panel held no party can force another to accept a contract form or content not agreed, and a contract made under compulsion is void; here the change was by free agreement, so the fixed-duration contract was valid. [Para 135][Para 139][Para 143] Article 67(2), which caps a fixed-duration contract at two years, did not apply, because it governs successive fixed-duration renewals, not a one-off conversion. [Para 144][Para 150][Para 157]

On the labour inspector's authorisation, the panel read Article 293 and Article 4 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 as protecting the three union leaders only against dismissal (បញ្ឈប់), not against the natural expiry or non-renewal of a fixed-duration contract. [Para 160][Para 162][Para 163][Para 166]

On discrimination, the panel put the burden on the party alleging it and noted Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law forbid using union membership in decisions on discipline or termination. [Para 167][Para 168][Para 169] It had ordered the workers to file proof that notice of the elected leadership was sent to the employer by post; they filed nothing by the deadline, so the panel could not presume the employer knew the leadership composition and found no evidence of discrimination. [Para 176][Para 177][Para 178][Para 179]

On issue 4 the panel found the three leaders attended the ILO course on 10 February 2014 without asking permission or notifying administration, contrary to Article 8(b)(1) of the internal regulation, which requires seven days' advance application except in emergencies; absence without leave means no work was performed, so no wage is owed under Article 664 of the Civil Code and Articles 102 and 69 of the Labour Law. [Para 206][Para 207][Para 208][Para 215][Para 226]

**Dissent:** An Nan, the worker-party arbitrator, dissented on issue 1, reasoning that the periods of successive contracts must be added together and, once they exceed two years, the contract becomes one of unlimited duration under Article 67(2) and ILO Recommendation 166; the employer never sought the labour inspector's authorisation, so the termination was procedurally invalid and Mr V should be reinstated with back pay. [Para 232][Para 240][Para 247][Para 248]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The protection that requires an employer to obtain the labour inspector's authorisation before acting against a union leader applies to dismissal, not to the expiry or non-renewal of a fixed-duration contract, so non-renewal at the stated expiry date needs no authorisation. A worker who freely accepts conversion from an unlimited-duration contract to a fixed-duration one, after receiving the statutory severance for the former, cannot later claim the contract remained of unlimited duration, because Article 67(2) governs successive fixed-duration renewals and not such a conversion; and a union's claim of anti-union discrimination fails where it cannot prove the employer knew who its elected leaders were. [Para 150][Para 162][Para 164][Para 177]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's holding that the union became lawful by operation of Article 268(2) after two months of Ministry silence, and its reliance on Articles 336–337 of the Civil Code, were general statements of law that did not change the outcome, which turned on the absence of evidence of discrimination. [Para 104][Para 131][Para 135]
