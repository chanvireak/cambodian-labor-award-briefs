# Award No. 004/18 — Raffles Royal Hotel (Arbitration Council, 16 March 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Yu Soun Ty (employer-appointed), Sin Kim Sean (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (president) · Doc ID 138878

## 1. Facts
The respondent is a hotel employing 281 workers, party to a collective agreement of 23 June 2017 that was signed but never viséed by the Ministry of Labour. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 90] On 11 November 2017 the Asean Tourism Employees Union at Raffles Royal Hotel complained of five points; conciliation on 15 January 2018 failed on all five, which reached the Council on 18 January 2018. [Para 22][Para 24][Para 25] At the hearing the workers withdrew points 1, 4 and 5 (discrimination and interference in union affairs, the smoking ban, and the dismissal of two managers) and merged points 2 and 3, leaving one dispute: the transfer of the union adviser, Ms S, from the Elephant Bar to the Lobby Bar on 1 September 2017, and the treatment of female staff. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 89] The parties agreed the award would be binding. [Para 33]

## 2. Issues in dispute
The panel merged its points 2 and 3 into a single dispute and put two questions.
1. Whether the employer breached Article 6 of the collective agreement by transferring Ms S from the Elephant Bar to the Lobby Bar without her consent, and whether it must stop threatening, coercing and humiliating the 50 female staff at the workplace? [Para 152]
2. Whether the employer must transfer Ms S back to the Elephant Bar, and give her a defined shift, where she says the transfer was made out of spite and costs her tips and childcare time? [Para 165]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Article 6 requires the worker's consent before any transfer; Ms S was moved without consent, out of anger after she refused a drunken guest permission to smoke in the Elephant Bar, and the department head told her the union could not help her; she earned less in tips there and lost a fixed shift that let her care for her children. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 105][Para 108]
**Employer (respondent):** the move was a shift change within one department, not a transfer between departments, so no consent was required; transfers follow performance evaluation and business needs, four other staff were moved at the same time, and Ms S's evaluation showed she was not suited to the Elephant Bar. [Para 99][Para 111][Para 119][Para 126][Para 156]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand that the employer be ordered to stop breaching Article 6 and to transfer Ms S back to the Elephant Bar was dismissed, but the employer was ordered to fix her a single defined shift at the Lobby Bar starting at 2 p.m. each day, without rotation with other staff. [Para 212][Para 213][Para 215]

The panel first confirmed that the three named representatives held valid mandates under letter No. 019/17 of 11 December 2017 and that, with no most-representative union at the hotel, Prakas No. 362 allowed them to act. [Para 138][Para 146][Para 150] On Article 6 it read the clause as barring any move of a worker from one department to another without the worker's prior agreement, but found that the Elephant Bar and the Lobby Bar both sit inside the Food and Beverage Department under one head, so the move was a change of team, not a transfer between departments, and consent was not required. [Para 154][Para 157][Para 158][Para 159][Para 161][Para 162]

The panel rejected the motive alleged. Though the department head did reprimand and insult Ms S without hearing her after the 21 August 2017 incident with the guest, the claim that the transfer was driven by anger and spite rested on assertion alone, with no evidence to support it, and the panel noted the employer had already planned the move in June 2017, before that incident. [Para 108][Para 111][Para 166][Para 168][Para 175]

On the demand for a transfer back, the panel applied Article 2 of the Labour Law and its earlier line of awards: the employer may deploy and transfer workers within the enterprise provided the transfer is lawful and reasonable, which it treated as four conditions — no reduction in wages, no change of shift from day to night or night to day, no significant change of skill, and no move to a distant location. [Para 176][Para 180][Para 181][Para 182] Condition 3 and 4 were met, the work and skills being the same and both bars being within the same hotel; condition 1 was met because her wage was unchanged. [Para 183][Para 184][Para 186][Para 188] The tip argument failed: Article 9(ង) of the collective agreement requires pooled tips to be shared collectively, without defining the pooling, and the workers neither quantified what she had received before nor showed why the Lobby Bar yielded less, so no causal link to the transfer was established. [Para 195][Para 196][Para 192][Para 198] Condition 2, however, was not met: at the Elephant Bar she worked 3 p.m. to midnight, whereas at the Lobby Bar she was rotated across shifts starting at 6 a.m., 9 a.m. or 2 p.m. The panel therefore held the transfer lawful and reasonable in substance but required the employer to regularise a fixed 2 p.m. shift, which the employer had offered at the hearing, so that she could care for her children. [Para 199][Para 200][Para 203][Para 205][Para 208][Para 211]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A transfer within a single department, between outlets under one department head, is a change of team and not a transfer between departments, so the collective agreement's consent requirement does not apply; but a transfer that alters a worker's shift pattern engages the employer's duty to manage lawfully and reasonably under Article 2 of the Labour Law, and a panel will require the employer to restore a fixed shift even while upholding the transfer itself. [Para 157][Para 161][Para 202][Para 205]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the workers withdrew their allegations about the smoking ban and the dismissal of the two managers, and its observation that the section head's reprimand of Ms S was insulting, were not necessary to the order made. [Para 30][Para 166]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
OCR is very heavy: paragraphs 176–179 split Article 2 of the Labour Law across page breaks with intervening lines lost, paras 68, 74 and 77 each duplicate a heading with divergent spelling, and paras 56–64 duplicate the employer's exhibit list so that item numbers 18 and 20 appear twice. Internal contradictions were left as printed: the conciliation report is dated 18 January 2018 yet said to have reached the Secretariat on 18 January 2017 (para 25); the hotel's commercial registration is given as No. MOC-239738 of 25 October 1994 in the exhibit list (para 41) and as Inv. 001 E/1994 with new No. 00025164 of the same date at para 82; and the panel records one remaining issue while numbering it as points 2 and 3 (paras 31–32). The signature block at paras 217–218 lost the employer-appointed arbitrator's name and part of the president's, so the composition is taken from para 1. Paragraphs 192–194 are badly corrupted and the tip figures cannot be recovered.
