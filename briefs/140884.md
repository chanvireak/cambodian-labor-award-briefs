# Award No. 006/24 — ណាហ្គាភីស ខប ភើរេសិន (Arbitration Council, 10 June 2024)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៊ុង សុទ្ធី appointed by the employer party, អន ណន appointed by the worker party, ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា chair) · Doc ID 140884

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ណាហ្គាភីស ខប ភើរេសិន, a garment factory at Prek Treng village, Samrong Thom commune, Kien Svay district, Kandal, has operated since 2013 and employs 2,982 workers. [Para 1][Para 50][Para 56] On 3 April 2024 the workers filed seven demands; conciliation on 7 May 2024 settled four, and the labour dispute department referred two of the three remaining points — a nursery and breastfeeding room, and Social Security Fund contributions — to the labour inspectorate as questions of legal compliance. [Para 7][Para 8][Para 9][Para 11] Only the first demand reached the panel, and the workers asked it to decide that one alone. [Para 12][Para 18][Para 21]

That demand was for one midday meal a day, or 4,000 riel per person in lieu, for the 1,329 workers who signed the authorisation of 23 March 2024. [Para 54][Para 55] Both parties accepted that the company has never provided lunch or a lunch allowance since it opened, and that no promise, contract or agreement to do so exists. [Para 56][Para 57]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must provide one midday meal per working day, or pay 4,000 riel per worker in its place, to the 1,329 workers who demanded it? [Para 55][Para 74]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, តំណាង កម្មករ និយោជិត):** almost the whole workforce signed in support of the demand. [Para 60] Nearby factories, including ក្រុមហ៊ុន ជា ស៊ីណាត and ក្រុមហ៊ុន យី ដា, pay 2,000 riel a day and newer plants pay 3,000 riel, and roughly 70 per cent of factories nationwide provide a meal or an allowance. [Para 61][Para 67] The company has expanded from one building to three and hired up to 3,000 workers, so the pleaded losses are not credible, and the demand has been raised repeatedly since 2013–2014 without answer. [Para 62][Para 65][Para 66]

**Employer (respondent):** it has never given lunch or a lunch allowance and never promised one. [Para 56][Para 57] Orders have fallen and buyers now force low prices, so the company cannot absorb the cost and is instead trying to avoid suspending contracts. [Para 62][Para 69][Para 70] One factory cannot be compared with another; each sets its own benefits, and the neighbouring factories that pay have operated longer. [Para 72][Para 73]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider the demand for one midday meal or 4,000 riel per person for the 1,329 workers. [Para 94][Para 95]

The panel first recorded the parties' common ground that no meal or allowance had ever been given and that no contract, collective agreement or promise created one. [Para 74][Para 75] It then found no provision of the Labour Law, no regulation, no collective agreement, no agreement between the parties, no internal rule and no past practice obliging the employer to supply lunch or its cash equivalent. [Para 76] The demand was therefore one for an additional benefit, which made it a dispute over interests rather than rights. [Para 77][Para 78]

Article 96(2) of the Labour Law defines a collective agreement as a written agreement between an employer or employers' organisation and a union representing the workers; no such instrument existed here. [Para 79][Para 81] Under Article 54 (new) of the Law on Trade Unions, as amended in 2020, and Article 72 of that Law, the panel may decide a dispute over interests only where the claimant union is the most representative union in the enterprise or has the negotiating status of a bargaining council. [Para 82][Para 86][Para 87][Para 90] The company has a single union, សហជីព ស្មារតី កម្មករ ខ្មែរ, with 483 members, and there is no most-representative union at all. [Para 58][Para 59] The claimant was accordingly neither most representative nor otherwise qualified, so the panel declined to consider the claim. [Para 92][Para 93]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a demand seeks a benefit that no statute, regulation, collective agreement, internal rule or established practice provides, the panel classifies it as a dispute over interests and will decide it only if the claimant union is the most representative union under Article 54 (new) of the Law on Trade Unions or holds the negotiating status conferred by Article 72; a union with 483 members out of 2,982 workers, in an enterprise where no union has been certified most representative, has no standing to bring such a claim, and comparisons with the benefits paid by neighbouring factories do not create an obligation. [Para 76][Para 90][Para 59][Para 73]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's recital that an award on an interests dispute would operate as a collective agreement binding all workers and cost them their right to bargain and strike restates the policy behind Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099 rather than deciding anything in this case. [Para 88][Para 89]

- The factory is described as having grown to three buildings in one sentence and four in the next. [Para 62][Para 63]
- The number of workers behind the demand is 1,329 in the authorisation and in the operative order, while the union's membership is given as 483 and total employment as 2,982; the 1,329 figure was used. [Para 50][Para 54][Para 58]
- The three unsettled points are counted inconsistently: three at [Para 16], two referred to the inspectorate plus one to the panel at [Para 9]–[Para 12], and two described at [Para 19] as never referred. [Para 9][Para 16][Para 19]
