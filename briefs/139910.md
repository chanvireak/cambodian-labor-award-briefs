# Award No. 255/13 — Pou Yuen (Cambodia) Enterprise Bright (Arbitration Council, 27 December 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chhea presiding; Ing Sothy, employer-appointed, dissenting; An Non, worker-appointed) · Doc ID 139910

## 1. Facts
Pou Yuen (Cambodia) Enterprise Bright makes shoes on Street 271, Teuk Thla, Sen Sok, Phnom Penh, and employed about 3,550 workers. [Para 44][Para 45] Three unions under the Federation of Trade Unions of Workers stood as claimants, with 1,999 members between them. [Para 46][Para 52] Of ten demands, conciliation on 14 November 2013 settled six and left four: the base for overtime, the length of fixed-duration contracts, glue fumes in the production buildings, and rest breaks for workers who stand. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 61][Para 68][Para 73]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must calculate overtime on the basic wage plus the position allowance and other benefits, rather than on the basic wage alone? [Para 3][Para 53]
2. Whether the employer must conclude fixed-duration contracts of six months or longer whenever it renews a one- or three-month contract? [Para 5][Para 140]
3. Whether the employer must remove the smell of the glue used to close shoes from every production building? [Para 7][Para 170]
4. Whether the employer must give 15 minutes' rest every two hours, and chairs to sit on, to workers who stand at their stations? [Para 10][Para 186]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** a wage is not the basic wage alone; position, seniority, attendance and travel payments are all part of what they earn each month and must therefore enter the overtime rate. [Para 54][Para 55] Short contracts leave them insecure and exposed to non-renewal, and they asked for six months or more on each renewal. [Para 63][Para 64] There is no extraction equipment, workers have fainted in the water-pipe building, and glue stings their eyes. [Para 69]
**Employer (respondent):** the internal rules, approved by the Ministry, fix overtime on the basic wage; group leaders were told this when appointed and new workers are trained on it for one to three days. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58] On contracts it said workers wanted short terms when long ones were offered and long terms when short ones were offered, and that it acts lawfully. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67] It admitted the glue smell and said it was installing new extraction and repairing broken ducts, but could not say when. [Para 70][Para 71]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must compute overtime on basic wage plus position or skill allowance (for group leaders) plus seniority bonus, but not on the USD 10 regularity bonus or the travel and lodging allowance; it must conclude no six-month contract; it must remove the glue smell from every affected building within 01 month; it must provide sufficient chairs near the workstations, but need not give 15 minutes' rest every two hours. [Para 210][Para 211][Para 212][Para 213][Para 214][Para 215]

On issue 1 the panel held the claim a rights dispute, since it turns on Articles 139, 144 and 102 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 80 of 01 March 1999. [Para 86][Para 87] Article 139 new sets the 50% and 100% uplifts but does not say what the base is; Prakas 80 Article 5 says only "wage during normal work" without defining it. [Para 88][Para 90][Para 94] Reading Article 102 against Article 103 — the latter listing overtime, commission, bonuses and gratuities as wage heads — the panel held "wage during normal work" to mean the wage under Article 102, the pure wage for work done. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 100] Position allowance, paid monthly for performing group-leader duties and with no conditions attached, is therefore part of it, following awards 74/08 and 7813, and 107/13. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 105][Para 110] The seniority bonus it also included, expressly departing from award 111/08, on the ground that the bonus rewards experience and loyalty and so is extra remuneration for work. [Para 121][Para 123][Para 125][Para 126] The attendance bonus and the travel and lodging allowance it excluded, as incentives and support rather than payment for work. [Para 130][Para 133][Para 135] The employer's use of the basic wage alone was held an incorrect practice. [Para 137]

On issue 2 the panel held the claim a rights dispute turning on Article 65 of the Labour Law and Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code, and dismissed it: a contract arises from offer and acceptance, and neither party may impose a term the other has not agreed, so the workers could not force a six-month term, though the employer equally could not force a short one. [Para 140][Para 141][Para 156][Para 166][Para 167][Para 169]

On issue 3, Article 229 and Prakas No. 125 of 15 June 2001 require clean air and the absence of harmful or offensive smells; the employer admitted the smell and gave no date for the fix, which the panel found unreasonable since it could have asked its contractor. [Para 172][Para 174][Para 176][Para 180][Para 181] It allowed one month, as the employer had already begun. [Para 183][Para 184]

On issue 4, Prakas No. 053 of 10 February 2000 requires chairs near the workstation even where the work does not call for sitting, so that part was granted; but no article of the Labour Law requires a 15-minute break every two hours, and the employer's tolerance of absences for water was noted. [Para 189][Para 190][Para 196][Para 204][Para 208][Para 209]

**Dissent:** the employer-appointed arbitrator opposed the reasons on issue 1, holding that adding the position allowance and seniority bonus to the overtime base has no legal foundation and rests on equity, and that if that base is used for overtime it must equally be used for deductions; on issue 3 he agreed the smell must go but objected to a fixed one-month deadline, proposing that action begin within 3 days or 1 week. [Para 219][Para 224][Para 225][Para 226][Para 229]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
"Wage during normal work" in Article 139 of the Labour Law and Article 5 of Prakas No. 80 means the wage defined by Article 102 — everything paid as the price of the work itself — so a monthly position or skill allowance and a seniority bonus must be added to the basic wage before the 50% and 100% overtime uplifts are applied, while an attendance bonus and a travel and lodging allowance, being incentives and support rather than payment for work, must not. [Para 100][Para 109][Para 130][Para 135]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that it generally follows its earlier awards unless it gives reasons for departing, and used that licence to leave award 111/08 on the seniority bonus; it also observed that the employer's toleration of water breaks showed a sympathetic practice, though no legal duty to grant breaks existed. [Para 121][Para 123][Para 207]
