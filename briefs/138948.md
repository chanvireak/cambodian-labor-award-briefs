# Award No. 045/17 — PNP Sey A Thy Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 4 January 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mar Sambor Na (appointed by the employer), Liv Sovanna (appointed by the workers), Pen Bun Chhea (president) · Doc ID 138948

## 1. Facts
The respondent is a garment manufacturer employing approximately 149 workers, with a local union of the Cambodian Labour Union Federation (CUMW) registered on 8 May 2017. On 13 November 2017 the Provincial Department of Labour and Vocational Training issued letter No. 3059 authorising suspension of the contracts of 80 workers from 14 November to 13 December 2017; during the suspension the employer paid 50% of base wage plus a 100% seniority bonus. The workers claimed the suspension was unlawful and that full wages and benefits were owed, and separately claimed conversion of their contracts. [Para 47][Para 56][Para 116]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer's suspension of the workers' contracts was carried out lawfully under Article 71 of the Labour Law, so that the employer owes no 100% wage and benefit payment for the suspension period? [Para 54][Para 116]
2. Whether the employer must convert to undetermined-duration contracts the fixed-duration contracts of workers whose aggregate service exceeds two years? [Para 78][Para 135]

The claim as originally filed contained a third matter (wages for April and May 2017), which the workers withdrew at the hearing, leaving only issues 1 and 3 for determination. [Para 21][Para 22]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The suspension was invalid because the labour department came to the factory only once, the decision was taken after 4:00 pm outside working hours, and the employer acted without the agreement of the local union or the workers' delegate; the suspension cost them their benefits because they received only 50%. [Para 62][Para 64][Para 66]
**Employer (respondent):** The labour inspectorate investigated, recorded the facts properly and authorised suspension of 80 workers; the local union and the two worker delegates who had obtained the highest votes (2/3) attended the meeting and agreed, so the claim for 100% wages cannot be met. [Para 67][Para 69]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 1 dismissed; on issue 3 the panel ordered the employer to convert the fixed-duration contracts of 25 workers whose aggregate contract period exceeds two years into undetermined-duration contracts. No monetary sum was awarded. [Para 176][Para 177]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 71§11 of the Labour Law, which permits suspension of enterprise activity for serious material or economic difficulty for no more than two months, subject to supervision by the labour inspectorate, together with Article 72§1, under which a procedurally correct suspension relieves the employer of the duty to pay wages, while an irregular suspension obliges the employer to pay 100% of wages even where no work is provided. It followed its earlier awards (Nos 58/07, 08/07, 72/05, 22/05, 198/13, 160/14) holding that prior authorisation from the labour inspector is what makes a suspension lawful, found that letter No. 3059 supplied that authorisation for the full 14 November–13 December period, and held that any objection to the inspector's decision belonged before the labour inspector of the province rather than the panel. [Para 119][Para 122][Para 125][Para 131][Para 132]

On issue 3 the panel applied Article 67§2, under which a fixed-duration contract may not run beyond two years and renewal beyond that period converts it into an undetermined-duration contract, together with Article 73§5 and §6 and ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982. It held that paying 5% severance at each renewal does not preserve the fixed-duration character of the contract, and that the employer's practice of successive three-month contracts made the aggregate period the relevant measure. From the list supplied, one worker (badge No. 0557) had no recorded start date, so the panel determined the issue only for the remaining 25 workers. [Para 138][Para 157][Para 166][Para 172][Para 97]

Arbitrator Mar Sambor Na appended a dissenting opinion on issue 3, reasoning that the workers' concern was a fear of future non-renewal rather than an existing dispute, and that under Article 302 the Council resolves present disputes affecting actual industrial relations, not future ones. [Para 180][Para 185][Para 187]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of employment contracts is lawful, and discharges the employer from paying wages, only where the labour inspectorate has granted prior authorisation for a period not exceeding two months; absent that authorisation the employer owes 100% of wages even though no work was given. Successive fixed-duration contracts whose aggregate duration exceeds two years convert by operation of law into undetermined-duration contracts, and severance paid at each renewal does not prevent the conversion. [Para 124][Para 158][Para 172]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's preliminary survey of representation — that where no most-representative union exists, worker delegates recognised by the provincial labour department may represent the workers before the Council (Nos 171/16, 183/16, 188/16, 223/16, 237/16 followed) — was not necessary to either outcome. [Para 105][Para 110][Para 114]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Issue 1 is recited at [Para 54] and [Para 116] as concerning April and May 2017, while the claim at [Para 2] and the operative order at [Para 176] concern November and December 2017; both readings are printed and neither has been corrected here. [Para 100] inserts the words "year 2019" mid-sentence; [Para 132] prints "2023 2017" for the same date; [Para 134] prints "200 17"; and [Para 176] prints "1000 percent" where every other reference is 100%. The letter quoted at [Para 72]–[Para 73] carries untranslated OCR garbage ("whom work woments graving viver"), and [Para 134] contains a similar Latin-script intrusion. The employer's name appears in several slightly different Khmer spellings across the face of the award, and the workers' delegate list at [Para 69] is partially lost to OCR. No name has been expanded or invented.
