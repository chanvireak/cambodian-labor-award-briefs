# Award No. 086/19 — Khen Thaeren Apparel (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 2 October 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (In Sothy, appointed by the employer; Tun Si Phan, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, president), with a dissenting opinion by In Sothy on issue 3 · Doc ID 139217

## 1. Facts
Khen Thaeren Apparel (Cambodia) is a garment factory at Trapeang Thloeng, Chaom Chau, Por Sen Chey, Phnom Penh, employing 1,020 workers. [Para 1][Para 39] Workers filed eight claims; five were settled at conciliation on 26 August 2019 and three came before the panel. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 16] The disputed acts were the employer's transfer of trimming (កាត់ព្រុយ) work to sewing-line workers from 20 July 2019, the rate of the overtime meal allowance, and the base used to compute overtime pay. [Para 41][Para 57][Para 60]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay each sewing worker an incentive bonus of USD 20 per month on meeting the set sewing-and-trimming target, plus USD 20 per month for the extra trimming work? [Para 40][Para 65]
2. Whether the employer must pay a meal allowance for voluntary overtime of 2,000 riel per hour? [Para 57][Para 78]
3. Whether the employer must add the seniority bonus (ប្រាក់រង្វាន់អតីតភាពការងារ) to the base wage as the basis for calculating overtime pay? [Para 60][Para 106]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Since trimming was added, sewing workers cannot reach the target and lose the bonus they used to earn — the target fell from 50 to 40 shirts an hour and still was not met; and the seniority bonus is already folded into the base for administrative staff, section heads and mechanics, so excluding sewers discriminates against them. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45][Para 61][Para 62]
**Employer (respondent):** Paying the extra bonus would raise costs; article 69 sets only the framework of the work owed under the contract and does not require bonuses; the trimming added is minor and the piece rate was reduced; the meal allowance of 2,000 riel per day already meets the law; and the seniority bonus is deliberately shown separately for inspection purposes. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 52][Para 59][Para 63][Para 64]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must add the seniority bonus to the base wage for computing overtime pay [Para 133]; the USD 20 incentive bonuses are refused [Para 131]; the 2,000 riel hourly meal allowance is refused [Para 132].
On issue 1 the panel applied article 2 of the Labour Law, holding that it confirms the employer's right to organise and manage the enterprise where it acts lawfully and reasonably, and that a bonus not required by law may be designed unilaterally by the employer, following Awards 62/06, 108/06, 33/07, 119/09 and 24/05. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 70][Para 71] It found the workers had not objected to the trimming itself, had produced no evidence that their earnings fell, and that the employer still paid the incentive where the target was met; no provision obliged the employer to pay USD 20 for each head. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 77]
On issue 2 the panel read clause 2 of Notification 041/11 as requiring 2,000 riel per day, or a free meal, for voluntary overtime; the employer paid exactly that, and no law, collective agreement or practice created an hourly entitlement. [Para 78][Para 80][Para 81] The claim therefore exceeded the law and was an interest dispute, which the claimant union, not being the most representative union in the enterprise, could not bring. [Para 82][Para 97][Para 102][Para 104]
On issue 3 the panel worked through article 139 (new) of the amended Labour Law, article 5 of Prakas No. 80 and articles 102 and 103 of the Labour Law, noting none defined the wage base for overtime. [Para 106][Para 109][Para 111] It held "wage during normal work" means the wage regularly received on ordinary working days, not one contingent on output or targets [Para 117][Para 118], and that the seniority bonus under Notification 041/11 rewards experience and loyalty and forms part of that regular wage, following Award 255/13. [Para 122][Para 126][Para 127] The employer's practice of using the base wage alone was therefore wrong. [Para 128][Para 130]
Arbitrator In Sothy dissented on issue 3, holding that the majority's order rested on equity rather than legal basis, since the base wage exists to set a floor and to serve as the calculation base for other entitlements. [Para 141][Para 142]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The wage used to calculate overtime is the wage the worker regularly receives for normal work on ordinary days, which includes the statutory seniority bonus and is not confined to the minimum base wage; but a bonus the law does not create remains within the employer's managerial discretion as to its amount and conditions. [Para 118][Para 127]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that article 2 permits an employer to adopt bonus schemes beyond those the law requires, and that differing treatment between categories of staff does not by itself establish discrimination. [Para 71][Para 72]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award carries a dissenting opinion as an annex, so the outcome on issue 3 is by majority only. [Para 137] The closing date appears both as the lunar date "4th waxing of Assuj, B.E. 2567" and as 2 October 2019. [Para 143][Para 144] Stray years "2017" and "2016" intrude into the overtime-allowance and signature passages. [Para 57][Para 135] The company name is printed only in Khmer, the Latin form above being a transliteration.
