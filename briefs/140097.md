# Award No. 150/11-SCF — SCF (Cambodia) Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 12 January 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Kong Phalha, chair) · Doc ID 140097

## 1. Facts
SCF (Cambodia) Co Ltd, an animal-feed manufacturer at Chumkar Roth village, Vallis commune, Samrong Tong district, Kampong Speu, employed about 65 workers. The Chumroeun Kamkar Workers Union, with roughly 48 members and no registration certificate, filed a collective dispute carrying 11 demands. By the hearing the worker party pressed only the fate of four dismissed staff — Mr S, Mr V, Mr P and Mr L — and its claim that the dismissals were anti-union discrimination. [Para 1][Para 65][Para 66][Para 67][Para 68][Para 69]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the dismissals of the four workers were lawful or were discrimination against union activity? [Para 69][Para 86]
2. Whether the employer must reinstate the four or instead pay termination benefits, and on what wage base those benefits are computed? [Para 69][Para 71][Para 107]
3. Whether the panel has jurisdiction to order the employer to withdraw its court complaints against two of the four? [Para 118][Para 120]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The union was elected on 17 September 2011 and notified the employer on 4 October 2011; the four were dismissed on 10 and 17 October 2011 because of that activity. Mr S's resale of goods was assistance to customers when company stock ran out, and he had never previously been at fault. Severance must be computed on average actual monthly earnings, not the base wage. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 75][Para 84][Para 85][Para 86][Para 109]
**Employer (respondent):** Mr S committed serious misconduct — using the company car and sales staff for personal business and reselling vaccine to other customers for about $100 profit on 5 September 2011 — and Mr V left his work target without permission. It cannot take the four back. It is awaiting the Phnom Penh court's verdict on the illegal-strike charge and will comply with it. Severance is computed on base wage. [Para 70][Para 80][Para 81][Para 83][Para 88][Para 89][Para 95][Para 108]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Partly granted — the panel declined to order withdrawal of the court complaints, did not order reinstatement, and ordered the employer to pay each of the four workers severance, damages, notice pay, unused annual leave and final unpaid wages, computed on total actual earnings excluding the meal allowance. [Para 258][Para 259][Para 260][Para 261][Para 262]

On jurisdiction, the panel applied Chapter 12 Section 2 and Article 302 of the Labour Law, which confine its authority to collective labour disputes over working conditions and the exercise of rights conferred on recognised professional organisations. A demand that the employer withdraw a complaint already lodged with the Phnom Penh court was not such a dispute, so the panel declined to consider it. [Para 118][Para 120][Para 122][Para 123][Para 258]

On the wage base, Articles 102 and 103 define wage to include base wage plus benefits actually received, including commission, while the meal allowance is a benefit given to facilitate the worker's performance of the job and is not wage. The panel therefore took total actual monthly earnings less the meal allowance as the figure for every calculation. [Para 221][Para 224][Para 226][Para 227][Para 229][Para 232]

On severance, Article 73(k) paragraph 6 requires at least 5% of the wages paid during the contract. For Mr V, on a fixed-term contract from 1 July to 30 December 2011 at a base of $450 and actual average of $450 to $971, the panel found about 3 months 10 days of service and ordered severance at 5% of total wages. [Para 215][Para 218][Para 220][Para 231][Para 232]

On early termination, Article 73(k) paragraphs 1 to 3 entitle a worker whose fixed-term contract is ended by the employer alone to damages at least equal to the wages due to the end of the contract. The panel applied that to Mr V and to Mr S, whose indefinite contract was cut short on 10 October 2011. [Para 211][Para 213][Para 214][Para 216]

On annual leave, Articles 166 to 168 give 18 paid days a year, accrued at 1.5 days per month, and require payment for unused leave on termination at the average daily wage. Mr V had not shown how much leave remained, so the panel ordered payment of the unused balance. [Para 233][Para 234][Para 239][Para 240][Para 241][Para 242]

On final wages, Article 116 requires wages and indemnities to be paid within the statutory period after the last working day. Mr V had not received his last wage, so the employer was ordered to pay it. [Para 244][Para 245][Para 246][Para 247]

For Mr P and Mr L the panel found fixed-term contracts of the same kind as Mr V's, with a base of $200 and monthly earnings that varied with sales, so the same base and the same formula applied to them. [Para 250][Para 251][Para 252][Para 253][Para 254][Para 255][Para 256]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer ends a fixed-term contract before its term, the panel computes severance, damages, notice pay, annual leave and final wages on the worker's total actual monthly earnings during the contract, excluding the meal allowance, and not on the base wage printed in the contract; severance is at least 5% of those wages under Article 73(k) paragraph 6. [Para 226][Para 229][Para 232][Para 256]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel recorded that the union had not yet obtained its registration certificate and had not shown that it applied to register members after the election, but did not rest the outcome on either point. [Para 68][Para 76]
