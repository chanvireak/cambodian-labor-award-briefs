# Award No. 017/18 — In Kyung (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 19 June 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Kuy Neam (president) · Doc ID 138889

## 1. Facts
The company cuts and sews garments in Phnom Penh and employs about 1,050 workers. The Khmer Workers Spirit Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ស្មារតី កម្មករ ខ្មែរ) filed a collective dispute on 19 February 2018 asking for better working conditions; conciliation on 2 May 2018 left two of the points unresolved. The dispute concerns a Korean-speaking translator, Mr I, whom the workforce accuses of threatening and intimidating workers, and the wage base the company uses to compute the indemnity for unused annual leave. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 11][Para 12][Para 33][Para 34]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must dismiss Mr I, the translator, because he repeatedly used threatening and intimidating language towards workers? [Para 38][Para 80]
2. Whether, when a contract is terminated, the employer must compute the indemnity for unused annual leave on the average of total wages — the last 12 months for workers with 12 months' seniority or more, and the actual months worked for those with less — rather than on the base wage? [Para 56][Para 112]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr I discriminates against the union and interferes in its internal affairs, trying to dissolve it and blocking its leaders from resolving workers' problems; he told workers that if they joined the union the company would close its doors and they would lose their jobs, he threatened a union leader with "do you want to be a leg-crosser?", and he sued another leader for defamation; 500 workers asked for him to be dismissed. On the leave indemnity, Articles 166 and 168 require total wages — the last 12 months averaged and divided by 12 and then by 26 — to be the basis. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41][Para 42][Para 57][Para 58]
**Employer (respondent):** it can warn and instruct Mr I to correct his conduct but will not dismiss him, because no element shows a serious fault and he has not breached Article 83 of the Labour Law or the internal rules; he is only a translator, drives the director and inspects order when the director is absent, and the defamation suit is a personal matter between two individuals; on the indemnity it maintains its existing practice of using the base wage. [Para 3][Para 44][Para 49][Para 51][Para 53][Para 55][Para 59]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel refused to order Mr I's dismissal but ordered the employer to compute the annual-leave indemnity on total wages, by averaging the last 12 months for workers with 12 months' seniority or more and the actual months worked for those with less. [Para 111][Para 136][Para 137][Para 138]

On issue 1 the panel first established standing: there is no most representative union at the company, so under Article 54(1) of the Union Law, Prakas 362 of 29 August 2016 and its earlier awards, the three workers certified by the Labour Disputes Department could represent the workforce, and all three attended. [Para 62][Para 67][Para 74][Para 75][Para 78] On the merits, Articles 65 of the Labour Law and 664 and 311 of the Civil Code establish that an employment contract arises from the parties' mutual consent and only the parties to it can modify or dissolve it; a third party cannot, so the workers could not compel the employer to alter Mr I's contract. [Para 80][Para 83][Para 87][Para 88] Article 2 of the Labour Law gives the employer the right to organise and manage the enterprise, provided it acts lawfully and reasonably, and the panel had consistently held that it cannot order an employer to dismiss or transfer a worker at the claimant's demand unless the worker is shown to be a dangerous person who cannot be kept without creating real insecurity at work. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 95] Applying that test, the panel found the complaints amounted only to a union dispute and a defamation suit between two individuals, with no evidence that Mr I was dangerous or had caused concrete insecurity; even taking the threatening words to a union leader as proved — which the employer did not contest, and Mr I did not appear — they were not enough to conclude he could cause harm. [Para 100][Para 102][Para 103][Para 104] The claim was therefore dismissed, but the panel held that the employer must instruct Mr I and correct his inappropriate behaviour. [Para 109][Para 110][Para 111]

On issue 2, Article 167 requires payment of the leave indemnity where a contract ends before the worker has taken accrued leave, and Article 168 requires it to be at least the average of wages, bonuses, benefits and payments in kind over the 12 months before the leave date. [Para 112][Para 114] The panel had consistently read Article 168 to require total wages over the last 12 months divided by 12 and then by 26, and found the employer's base-wage calculation did not comply with that article. [Para 117][Para 121][Para 123] It noted Article 168 prescribes a method only for workers with 12 months' seniority or more, and that for shorter service the average of the wages actually earned in the months worked is the fairest basis, since the 12-month window exists to capture wage fluctuations. [Para 127][Para 128][Para 130] The order accordingly set out both methods. [Para 134][Para 135]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A panel will not order an employer to dismiss or transfer a co-worker at the workforce's request: Article 2 of the Labour Law leaves the organisation of the enterprise to the employer, and the only exception is proof that the person is dangerous and cannot be retained without creating concrete insecurity at work — allegations of union hostility, however corroborated, do not meet that threshold by themselves. By contrast, the indemnity for unused annual leave must be computed on the average of total wages, bonuses and benefits actually received, not on the base wage, and for workers with less than 12 months' service on the average of the months actually worked. [Para 95][Para 123][Para 133]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed, following Award 29/08 (Thai Rong), that improper or insulting language is inappropriate conduct that can disturb the workplace, and that the company should take suitable measures to stop it and foster a peaceful working environment — a recommendation carrying no order. [Para 106][Para 107]
