# Award No. 176/14 — 8 Star Sport Wear (Arbitration Council, 1 August 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Yu Soun Ty for the employer party, Tuon Si Phan for the worker party, Run Saray chair) · Doc ID 139629

## 1. Facts
8 Star Sport Wear, a garment factory in Steung Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, registered on 27 July 2006 and employing about 965 workers, was sued by two union federations over working conditions. [Para 1][Para 57][Para 58] Their complaint of 6 June 2014 went to conciliation on 25 June 2014, which settled 12 of 17 demands, left 4 unsettled and saw demand 14 withdrawn; the file reached the Council on 1 July 2014 and was heard on 14 July 2014. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 16][Para 18] The four surviving demands were a midday meal, a transport and accommodation allowance, a regular attendance bonus, and paid time off for pregnant workers' check-ups. [Para 68][Para 80][Para 90][Para 99]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must provide one midday meal a day, or 2,000 riel in lieu of it? [Para 68]
2. Whether the employer must pay a transport and accommodation allowance of USD 15 a month, being USD 8 on top of the USD 7 now paid? [Para 80][Para 81]
3. Whether the employer must pay a regular attendance bonus of USD 15 a month, being USD 5 on top of the USD 10 now paid? [Para 90][Para 91]
4. Whether the employer must allow pregnant workers one day a month to attend antenatal checks, without setting that day against their annual leave and while keeping their wage and other benefits unchanged? [Para 99][Para 100]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** some 40 to 50 per cent of garment factories in Cambodia already give a meal or 1,000 to 2,000 riel a day, and wages remain too low for living costs, so the employer should share its profits; the antenatal check is a Ministry of Health requirement, so the employer must not charge the day to annual leave. [Para 70][Para 71][Para 72][Para 102][Para 103]
**Employer (respondent):** the company has never provided a midday meal or meal money and asks only to apply the Labour Law; the Council already refused the meal and allowance claims in Award 52/12 and wages have just been raised, so it cannot pay more. [Para 77][Para 78][Para 79] The annual-leave deduction follows an agreement made three years earlier with another union, one without most-representative status. [Para 104][Para 105][Para 106]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer is ordered to stop setting annual leave against the days pregnant workers take for antenatal checks and, in place of any such deduction, to make no deduction from their wage and other benefits; all three money claims were refused, as was the claim to a paid monthly check-up day. [Para 185][Para 198][Para 199][Para 209] The rights-dispute order binds the parties at once under the memorandum of 3 October 2012; the rest becomes final 8 days after notification if no party objects to the Minister. [Para 200][Para 205][Para 211]

The panel first asked whether Award 52/12 of 29 March 2012, which had refused similar meal and allowance demands, barred reconsideration. It set out the three conditions for refusing a repeat claim — the same parties previously litigated the point, it was brought before the Council, and it was decided on the merits — and found none satisfied: that case was brought by a different union, the meal issue was never examined, and the earlier decision turned on other issues. [Para 116][Para 118][Para 121][Para 124][Para 127]

On classification, Article 312 of the Labour Law and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 direct the panel to decide rights disputes by law and interest disputes by equity. [Para 108][Para 110][Para 113][Para 114] For the meal, the allowance and the bonus the panel found no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice creating the duty, and therefore treated each as an interest dispute. [Para 128][Para 129][Para 152][Para 153][Para 162][Para 163]

An award on interests, however, replaces the collective agreement for one year and removes the workers' right to strike; so under Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 the panel will only decide such a claim if the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise, alone or together with others, with over half the workforce. [Para 134][Para 136][Para 137] The claimant union had about 300 members out of some 965 workers and was not most representative, so the panel refused to consider issues 1 to 3. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 140][Para 141][Para 142]

Issue 4 was split. Setting annual leave against antenatal days is governed by Article 170, which allows annual leave outside the Khmer New Year only by agreement between employer and worker, so the panel held the deduction unlawful without the workers' consent and ordered it stopped; the agreement relied on was with a union lacking representative status and the claim itself showed no consent. [Para 169][Para 170][Para 178][Para 180][Para 183][Para 184][Para 185] Keeping wage and benefits for the check-up day, by contrast, exceeds what the law requires, and the panel treated that part as an interest dispute and refused it for the same representativeness reason. [Para 186][Para 187][Para 193][Para 197][Para 198]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a benefit that no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or established practice provides is an interest dispute, and the panel will not decide it on the merits unless the union bringing it is the most representative in the enterprise, because an award on interests substitutes for the collective agreement for a year and extinguishes the right to strike; the same union may nonetheless obtain relief on a rights dispute, such as a demand that annual leave not be set against days taken for antenatal checks under Article 170, which is decided on law alone. [Para 137][Para 170][Para 184]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's explanation that res judicata exists to avoid conflicting outcomes on the same dispute and to bring disputes to a final decision, and its survey of Awards 45/07, 14/08, 95/09 and 52/12, are general statements about its own practice and were not needed for the orders made. [Para 117][Para 119]
