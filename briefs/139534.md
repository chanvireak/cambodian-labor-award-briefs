# Award No. 022/20 — Good Mirai Toys (Arbitration Council, 23 April 2020)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Virak for the worker party, Kong Phalla chair) · Doc ID 139534

## 1. Facts
Good Mirai Toys, a factory making garments and children's goods in Anlong Kong village, Khan Dangkao, Phnom Penh, employed about 274 workers, all of whom struck over 13 demands for better conditions. [Para 10][Para 48] On 27 March 2020 the Ministry received the complaint by telephone; conciliation settled one point, leaving two, and the report reached the Council on 30 March 2020. [Para 10][Para 11][Para 12] The Council issued Order No. 002/20 on 31 March 2020 suspending the strike, and the workers returned to their shifts on 1 April 2020. [Para 15][Para 16] At the hearing the workers withdrew their second point, leaving the reinstatement claim. [Para 20][Para 21]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must renew the contracts of the workers whose employment contracts it has terminated, so that they continue to work? [Para 3][Para 51]
2. Whether the employer must remove the named head of administration from the company? (withdrawn at the hearing) [Para 4][Para 20]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer must apply Instruction No. 050/19 of 17 May 2019 on contract types to convert long-serving workers to unspecified-duration contracts, and must suspend contracts under Instruction No. 014/20 on the COVID-19 textile slowdown; the employer cannot use the pandemic as a pretext to end employment. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 82]
**Employer (respondent):** it cannot meet the demand because orders collapsed — 30,032 dolls ordered for April and 4,918 for May, against a normal 110,000 to 120,000 a month — and the cause is lack of orders from COVID-19, not a shortage of raw materials; it has already agreed to convert the 44 workers with more than two years' service to unspecified-duration contracts, and will rehire the rest when work returns. [Para 55][Para 63][Para 64][Para 65][Para 66]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider the reinstatement claim; the award is of the non-binding type, becoming final after eight days if no party objects in writing to the Minister. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 89]

The panel framed its power first. Article 312(1) of the Labour Law and Article 33 of Prakas No. 099 confine it to the questions recorded in the non-conciliation minute, plus issues arising after that minute as a direct consequence of the dispute; earlier awards were followed on that reading. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 71][Para 72] That produced two separate reasons for declining the claim. First, the demand that Instruction No. 050/19 be applied to the 44 workers on unspecified-duration contracts does not appear in Report No. 347 Kb/Ak/Vk of 30 March 2020 at all, so the panel had no jurisdiction over it, even though it accepted that the instruction and Instruction No. 014/20 genuinely exist and cover COVID-19 disruption. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 84][Para 85] Second, on the facts as of the hearing date, none of the 64 workers given notice had yet lost their job: all were still working normally until their contracts expired in April 2020, so the termination had not occurred and the dispute was one about the future, which the panel will not decide. [Para 53][Para 59][Para 75][Para 76][Para 77]

The panel also recorded the parties' own settlement of part of the dispute: the 20 workers on fixed-duration contracts of under two years had accepted the 5 per cent termination indemnity, payment in lieu of unused annual leave and other benefits, with the employer's undertaking to re-employ them when work resumed, so their claim was not pressed. [Para 56][Para 79] On the 44 workers the employer confirmed they were still working under unspecified-duration contracts. [Para 67]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The panel decides only the questions recorded in the non-conciliation minute, and a demand raised for the first time at the hearing falls outside its jurisdiction unless it arose after the minute as a direct consequence of the recorded dispute — even where the legal instrument invoked exists and is applicable. A claim to continue or renew contracts that have been given notice but have not yet expired, where every affected worker is still working, is a dispute about the future and will not be arbitrated. [Para 70][Para 72][Para 77][Para 85]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's acceptance that Instruction No. 050/19 and the COVID-19 suspension instruction No. 014/20 are real and applicable [Para 84], and its observation that the employer's loss of orders was caused by COVID-19 rather than raw-material shortage [Para 65], are findings not needed for the jurisdictional dismissal.
