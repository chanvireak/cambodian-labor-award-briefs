# Award No. 244/15 — New Ark Khit Garment Factory Ltd (Arbitration Council, 25 September 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, An Non, chair Kong Phalla) · Doc ID 139431

## 1. Facts
New Ark Khit Garment Factory Ltd, a garment factory on National Road 4 in Ang Snuol district, Kandal province, employed about 4,300 workers. [Para 54] Its factory-level union (សហជីព សេរី ស្ត្រី កាត់ដេរ), registered with the Ministry on certificate 3267 of 27 January 2015, filed nine demands on working conditions after talks with management failed, and conciliation at the Kandal labour department on 01 September 2015 left all nine unsettled. [Para 36] [Para 44] Both parties attended the hearing of 10 September 2015 and agreed on what the factory currently pays; they disagreed only on whether more is owed. [Para 28] [Para 57]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a health allowance of US$8 per month? [Para 57]
2. Whether the employer must pay US$10 more per month in travel and accommodation allowance? [Para 60]
3. Whether the employer must pay US$10 more per month as a regular attendance bonus? [Para 65]
4. Whether the employer must pay a machine-skill allowance of US$10 to workers who can operate two or more machines? [Para 68]
5. Whether the employer must pay US$10 more per month towards the cost of milk? [Para 72]
6. Whether the employer must pay a meal allowance of 2,000 riel per hour of overtime? [Para 75]
7. Whether the employer must hold a Water Festival party for the workers? [Para 78]
8. Whether the employer must pay annual-leave compensation twice a year, at Pchum Ben and Khmer New Year, while the workers remain employed? [Para 82]
9. Whether the employer must grant special leave without deducting it from annual leave? [Para 87]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that the workers fall ill often and a health allowance would help them; that bus fares rose from US$12 to US$15 and room rent from US$25 to US$35 a month; that they work regularly without absence and should be rewarded; that workers able to run several machines are moved between lines and deserve a skill premium; that milk costs them US$30–40 a month; that an overtime meal allowance encourages overtime and they need to eat more; that neighbouring factories such as Tai Yang and Camwell hold Water Festival parties, and Khosmu and VF pay annual-leave compensation twice a year; and that special leave should not be set against annual leave because it is granted for bereavement, family illness or marriage. [Para 58] [Para 61] [Para 66] [Para 73] [Para 76] [Para 80] [Para 85] [Para 88]
**Employer (respondent):** that it has no capacity to pay: orders have fallen, wages have risen, buyers keep pressing prices down and revenue is low; that it applies the law and existing practice, which already provides US$7 travel and accommodation allowance, a US$10 attendance bonus, US$8 milk money and 2,000 riel a day for overtime meals, and a Water Festival party has never been held. [Para 59] [Para 60] [Para 78] [Para 86]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all nine claims were refused, and the union was told it had no standing to bring an interests dispute before the panel. [Para 178] [Para 186]

On issues 1 to 7 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, no collective agreement, no internal rule (registered 188/09) and no past practice creating any of the claimed entitlements, so each demand asked for a benefit above the statutory floor and was therefore an interests dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍) rather than a rights dispute. [Para 91] [Para 93] [Para 112] [Para 122] Where the law or a ministerial notification already fixed a benefit the panel checked compliance: Prakas 230 of 25 July 2012 sets US$7 travel and accommodation and a US$10 attendance bonus, and the employer was paying both; notification 041/11 of 07 March 2011 sets 2,000 riel a day for overtime meals, which was also paid. [Para 109] [Para 120] [Para 142]

The panel then treated standing as the decisive question. Under Article 96 of the Labour Law and Prakas 305 art 9(1), only the most representative union may require an employer to negotiate a collective agreement, and under Prakas 099 art 43(1) an award on an interests dispute replaces the collective agreement, binds every worker in the enterprise for one year and removes the right to strike over that demand. An interests claim may therefore be brought only by the most representative union, or by unions together representing more than half the workforce; this union was the claimant but was not the most representative in the factory, so it lacked standing and the panel declined to consider the seven demands on their merits. [Para 99] [Para 100] [Para 101] [Para 102] [Para 105] [Para 106]

On issue 8, Article 167(2)–(3) allows annual-leave compensation only where the contract is cut short before the worker has earned the leave, and any agreement by which a worker abandons or forgoes annual leave is void; the panel followed its earlier awards and held that leave not yet taken cannot be converted into cash while employment continues. [Para 159] [Para 160] [Para 161] [Para 165] [Para 166]

On issue 9, Article 171 and Prakas 267 arts 1–2 permit the employer to offset special leave against annual leave where the worker has not used all of it, and to require repayment where the worker already has; the factory's practice of deducting was therefore lawful and the demand failed. [Para 168] [Para 169] [Para 172] [Para 176] [Para 177]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a benefit the Labour Law does not require — with no collective agreement, internal rule or established practice behind it — is an interests dispute, and an interests dispute may be brought only by the union that is most representative in the enterprise, or by unions commanding more than half the workforce, because the resulting award displaces the collective agreement and binds every worker for a year; a minority union's claim must be refused for want of standing whatever its merits. Separately, unused annual leave is not convertible into money while the employment continues, and special leave may lawfully be set against annual leave. [Para 93] [Para 102] [Para 106] [Para 165] [Para 172]

**Obiter dicta:** that a collective agreement or arrangement under which a worker waives annual leave is void, and that an employer may require a worker who has already taken all annual leave to repay special leave taken. [Para 160] [Para 173]
