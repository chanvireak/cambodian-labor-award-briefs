# Award No. 26/04 — Cambodian Sportswear Mfg Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 May 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mr K Thach, employer-appointed; Mr T Siphann, worker-appointed; Mr A Eng Thong, chair) · Doc ID 139307

## 1. Facts
Cambodian Sportswear Mfg Co., Ltd. employs 5,140 workers. [Para 24] On 3 May 2004 almost 3,000 of them struck to demand better working conditions and payment of USD119 in piecework submitted by Ms H, and by 5 May the labour inspectors had reconciled nine of fourteen issues. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 27] Ms H had been given piecework from other departments by her Chinese supervisor once her own work ran out, and earned USD119, which the company refused to pay, alleging cheating. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 74][Para 75] The company also kept a group of "floating" workers on daily hire for one to six months. [Para 52][Para 76]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay Ms H the wages of USD119 she achieved in piecework and submitted to it? [Para 13][Para 14]
2. Whether the company must increase the piecework rate for finishing workers in the A1 and F6 buildings? [Para 14]
3. Whether the company must increase the monthly salary of finishing workers? [Para 14]
4. Whether the company must increase the wages of workers in the store, cleaning, material-giving and swimming-cloth departments? [Para 14]
5. Whether the casual or floating workers it employs must be treated as regular workers? [Para 14]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Ms H earned the USD119 because the Chinese supervisor added piecework from other departments; the more than 3,000 fingerprints authorised Mr S to negotiate on their behalf; and the floating workers had worked one to six months, eight to ten hours a day six days a week, with no leave except one week at Khmer New Year, having been promised USD45 but paid USD41, USD42 or USD43. [Para 21][Para 34][Para 53][Para 54][Para 55]
**Employer (respondent):** the union's present name differs from the registered one and the federation destroyed that union, so Mr S cannot represent anyone; Ms H worked slowly and was topped up to the minimum wage, USD119 was implausible, and at most USD10 or USD15 of foreign piecework was added; her claim is an individual dispute; there are only seven floating workers and the company will hire them full time if they meet the legal conditions. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 59][Para 61][Para 63][Para 66][Para 69][Para 70]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — no award is issued on the USD119 claim; the company must pay workers by achieved piecework without regard to whether the work matched their assigned roles; floating workers with two consecutive months at 21 days a month must be reclassified as regular, paid the minimum-wage shortfall, the USD5 monthly bonus and overtime over 48 hours a week from their first month; and the parties must negotiate a collective agreement within six months on issues 2 to 4. [Para 118][Para 119]

On representation, the panel held the union was formed by the employees and registered with MoSALVY, that a federation neither created it nor may destroy it, and that the fingerprints supported Mr S, who therefore had the right to represent the workers. [Para 84][Para 87][Para 89][Para 90]

On the piecework, Article 5 of the internal work rules recognises monthly, daily and piecework wages. [Para 91] The Chinese supervisor is management, whose duty is to monitor work for the employer, so a worker who completes tasks he assigned outside her normal role commits no misconduct. [Para 97][Para 98][Para 100] The company accepted that the piecework belonged to it, and so had to pay the USD119 unless it proved cheating, which it did not. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 104] The panel nonetheless declined to issue an award on that item because Ms H was absent without valid reason from the hearing and had written that she accepted the sum offered and resigned for health reasons. [Para 105][Para 106]

On the floating workers, Article 9 confines casual work to short, temporary, intermittent or seasonal tasks, Article 10 gives casuals the same rights as regular workers, and Article 166 treats 21 days a month as continuous service. [Para 107][Para 108][Para 109] These workers did 48 to 60 hours a week and more than 21 days a month, continuously except for the New Year week, so they were not casuals but regular workers entitled to the same benefits, including minimum wage. [Para 111][Para 112][Para 114][Para 115] Issues 2 to 4 were interest issues, which the panel sent to collective bargaining rather than deciding. [Para 118]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Workers labelled "floating" who in fact work 48 to 60 hours a week and more than 21 days a month continuously are regular workers under Articles 9, 10 and 166, entitled to minimum wage, the attendance bonus and overtime from their first month; and a worker who performs piecework assigned by a supervisor outside her normal role cannot be refused payment for it, the risk of that arrangement lying with the employer. [Para 97][Para 114][Para 115][Para 117]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's finding that Ms H had earned the USD119, coupled with its decision not to award it because of her absence and resignation letter, is an observation on evidence it did not need to make once it declined jurisdiction over that item. [Para 104][Para 106]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The bonus instrument is dated "27 November 2004", after this award of 22 May 2004 [Para 10].
- The award says conciliation succeeded on "one of three issues" [Para 15] while the case summary says "nine amongst fourteen issues were conciliated" [Para 27]; the issues list itself sets out five items and collapses items 2 and 3 into a single sentence [Para 14].
- Garbled repetitions: "the company's workers are not responsible for this kind of arrangement responsibility" [Para 100] and "Mrs. He Sorphorn'supervisor" [Para 27].
- The employee representatives are listed with duplicate names, "Kin Chhay" appearing as both third and fourth representative and the same names carrying both "Mr" and "Mrs" [Para 5][Para 6].
- The union's name is printed "PROSPERSOUS ... SPORSWEAR" in the title [Para 1].
- The award records that a letter from the company, sent one day after the hearing, stated Ms H' acceptance and resignation, but no copy of it is listed among the evidence [Para 21][Para 106].
