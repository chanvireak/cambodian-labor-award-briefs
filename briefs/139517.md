# Award No. 023/14 — Volume Co Mo (Cambodia) Inv (Arbitration Council, 11 March 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Mae Nimmith (Chair), An Vireak (worker party), Seng Vouch Hun (employer party) · Doc ID 139517

## 1. Facts
Volume Co Mo (Cambodia) Inv is a garment-cutting factory at Trapeang Thloeng village, Choam Chao commune, Posenchey district, Phnom Penh, employing about 118 workers [Para 48]. Its union, of the Solidarity Labour Federation of Cambodia, held only a receipt dated 9 December 2013 acknowledging its application to register, and the three workers elected as its leaders had all left the company by the hearing of 11 February 2014 [Para 49][Para 51]. The federation held written authorisations dated 20 November 2013, and the panel treated the federation, representing those workers, as the claimant [Para 54][Para 161]. Ten demands were referred after conciliation failed on all ten on 23 January 2014 [Para 18].

## 2. Issues in dispute
4. Whether the employer must allow workers to leave the factory premises during the midday break without seeking permission? [Para 57][Para 64]
7. Whether the employer must pay a meal allowance of 1,000 riel per hour for overtime worked from 4:00 to 6:00 p.m.? [Para 78]
8. Whether the employer must serve the midday meal on time, by 11:00 a.m., and in quantities sufficient as before the strikes? [Para 90][Para 91]
9. Whether the employer must let workers on all-night overtime rest inside the production building between 00:00 and 01:00? [Para 111]
10. Whether the employer must stop assigning workers to work outside their skill and contractual role? [Para 124]

Demands 1, 2, 5, 6 and part of 8 were withdrawn, and demand 3 settled by agreement [Para 23][Para 24].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the midday break is their own time, so leaving the premises is a matter of liberty and the section-head permission slip is unworkable — of ten who asked on 8 February 2014 only one was allowed out; the employer locks the gate and the security guard is the final gatekeeper [Para 58][Para 65][Para 70]. Overtime meal money of 1,000 riel an hour is owed, and the meal is late and now smaller than before the strikes [Para 81][Para 92][Para 106]. Outside there is no proper rest place — only a concrete platform under a roof, mosquitoes and cold — so workers should rest inside between 00:00 and 01:00 [Para 114][Para 117]. Packaging workers are sent to dig, lay bricks and lift steel [Para 127]. **Employer (respondent):** it does not lock the gate; the procedure exists to know who is outside if an accident happens, and it fears outside food causing illness [Para 59][Para 71][Para 73]. It gives a free lunch with three dishes, free rice and a dessert at 2:00 p.m. in place of the overtime meal allowance, an arrangement explained on recruitment and accepted by the workers, worth more than 2,000 riel [Para 79][Para 82][Para 88]. It denies restricting entry to protect property from staining and theft, and says the construction help is short-term and drawn from workers idle in their sections [Para 121][Para 133][Para 138].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must let workers leave the premises during the midday break without permission (4); the 1,000-riel meal allowance is refused (7); the midday meal must be served by 11:00 a.m. and in the pre-strike quantity (8); workers may not demand entry to the production building at night, but the employer must provide a suitable rest place with roof, walls, windows, space, mosquito control and lighting (9); and it must stop assigning workers outside their skill and role (10) [Para 271][Para 277].

*Characterisation.* Every surviving demand was held to be a dispute over rights, because each turned on the Labour Law, the internal work rules or Notification No. 041/11, not on a new benefit to be fixed by equity [Para 168][Para 176][Para 191]. It is binding at once under the memorandum of understanding of 3 October 2012 [Para 27][Para 278].

*Issue 4.* Article 137 and Article 4 of the internal rules of 13 August 2007 fix work at eight hours, 07:00–11:00 and 12:00–16:00, so the 11:00 to 12:00 break is not working time under the employer's management [Para 170][Para 172]. A procedure requiring each worker to obtain a section head's written permission before leaving during that hour restricts liberty in personal time, and the employer's managerial right under Article 2 extends only to measures that are lawful and reasonable [Para 186][Para 187][Para 188].

*Issue 7.* Point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 entitles a worker doing voluntary overtime to 2,000 riel a day or a free meal [Para 190]. Article 13 permits agreements more favourable than the statutory floor, and the panel found a standing oral agreement, acknowledged by both sides, substituting the free lunch and dessert for the overtime meal allowance, worth more than 2,000 riel; the employer had therefore discharged the obligation [Para 197][Para 199][Para 200].

*Issue 8.* The meal must be ready at 11:00, the word "on time" meaning at the fixed time; late service encroaches on rest and health, and the employer conceded delays caused by suppliers and traffic [Para 216][Para 218][Para 219]. On quantity, the employer did not contest the reduction and undertook to raise it if short, so it must restore the pre-strike quantity [Para 224][Para 225][Para 226].

*Issue 9.* Refusing entry to the production building was a lawful and reasonable exercise of management to protect property from staining and loss [Para 232][Para 233]. But Articles 229 and 230 require the establishment and its surroundings to meet health and safety standards, and the concrete platform outside, cold and mosquito-infested at midnight, does not; a one-hour break after a full night's work demands a proper place, so the employer must provide one rather than open the building [Para 245][Para 252][Para 253][Para 259].

*Issue 10.* Article 2's managerial right is bounded by legality and reasonableness; sending packaging workers to cut earth, lay bricks and lift steel, without agreement and without extra pay, was work of a materially different nature and heavier than their own [Para 262][Para 267][Para 268]. The employer's claim of a one- or two-week need drawing on idle staff did not cure the absence of consent [Para 138][Para 269].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A break that the Labour Law and the internal rules exclude from working time is the worker's own time, so an employer may not make exit from the premises conditional on a supervisor's permission: the managerial right under Article 2 reaches only measures that are lawful and reasonable, and controlling the gate is not among them. Conversely, an agreement more favourable than Notification No. 041/11 may lawfully substitute a free meal for the overtime meal allowance where both parties acknowledge it. An employer may close the production building during a night rest break to protect property, yet Article 229 still obliges it to provide a rest place that meets health and safety standards; and it may not move workers to materially different and heavier work without their agreement or extra pay [Para 188][Para 200][Para 253][Para 269].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel held that Article 268(2) of the Labour Law deemed the union registered once two months passed from the Ministry's receipt without a response, and that the claim of a Ministry suspension of registrations was unproven; but since no letter recognised its leaders, who had in any case left, only the workers who had given written authorisation were claimants before the panel [Para 143][Para 149][Para 158][Para 162].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The number of workers who authorised the federation is given as 39 in the findings and 390 in the reasoning [Para 54][Para 159].
- The company's name is spelled several ways in the Khmer text, and a 2006 Ministry letter records a change of registration number from "Co" to "Inv" [Para 34][Para 47].
- Numbers are corrupted at several points: the panel is said to have been formed on "05 February 2022 2014", and Prakas No. 099 is dated "21 April 2019 2004" [Para 20][Para 165].
- Demand 8 is split into two limbs with inconsistent numbering, and the withdrawal recorded at the hearing covers only the hygiene limb of the same demand [Para 24][Para 25].
- The final page is largely OCR debris, mixing stray English fragments with the signature block [Para 280].
