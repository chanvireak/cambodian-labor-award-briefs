# Award No. 215/14 — Ken Sport Shoe Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 3 September 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Yu Sony, appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna, appointed by the workers; Ang Eng Thong, president appointed by both) · Doc ID 139690

## 1. Facts
Ken Sport Shoe Co., Ltd. manufactures footwear at Chamkar Svay, Samaki Mean Chey district, Kampong Chhnang, with about 5,325 workers. [Para 47][Para 48] Three enterprise unions — the Free Workers Union, the Khmer Youth Union and the Cambodia Workers Union — are the claimants. [Para 49] On 31 July 2014 the provincial labour department received the workers' complaint and conciliation the same day failed on the single claim; the panel heard the case on 13 August 2014. [Para 8][Para 9][Para 7] The disputed act is the employer's refusal to move the head of section A1 (ID 2116) to another section after repeated complaints about his treatment of the workers under him. [Para 52][Para 96] The parties had signed a Memorandum of Understanding of 03 October 2012 on improving professional relations in the garment industry, and chose a non-binding award for interest disputes. [Para 15][Para 20]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must transfer the head of section A1 (ID 2116) to another section, as demanded by the workers? [Para 52][Para 143]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the section head is a bad supervisor — he refused leave to a seriously ill worker on 29 July 2014, swore at and threatened workers, made them line up 5 to 10 minutes before the bell, and took their photograph during a meeting; 577 workers thumbprinted a request that he leave building A immediately. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 63][Para 79]
**Employer (respondent):** moving, dismissing or disciplining a worker is the employer's managerial right under Article 2 of the Labour Law; the section head is a trained four-year asset whom it is hard to replace, the ill worker did not follow proper leave procedure, the employer had already educated and warned him, and it would not humiliate an employee by warning him publicly. [Para 97][Para 102][Para 103][Para 108]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to transfer the head of section A1, named Chin Vy, to another section; the order is binding between the parties under the Memorandum of Understanding of 03 October 2012. [Para 216][Para 217][Para 218]

The panel first classified the claim. A demand concerning the workers' employment contract falls under the Labour Law and the Civil Code, so it is a rights dispute, which the panel decides according to law rather than equity; it cited Award No. 145/12-DAI YANG and No. 201/14-IM & V (third branch) on the distinction. [Para 140][Para 141][Para 143][Para 145] On the contract point it applied Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 311 of the 2007 Civil Code: only the parties to a contract may create, vary or extinguish its obligations, so a third party cannot dissolve the employment contract between employer and worker — the panel cannot order dismissal at the union's request. [Para 148][Para 151][Para 153] Article 2 of the Labour Law gives the employer the right to organise and manage the enterprise so long as that right is exercised lawfully and reasonably, a reading the panel had already adopted in Award No. 62/06-KLIK SHU and No. 201/14-IM & V. [Para 159][Para 161][Para 167]

It then examined each act alleged. The delay in authorising leave on 29 July 2014 was explained by the need for a manager's signature and happened once only, so it did not show the section head to be a dangerous person. [Para 173][Para 174][Para 176] The photographing allegation was unsupported: the party asserting a fact bears the burden of proof, and the workers produced no evidence of what was photographed or how it was used to threaten them. [Para 179][Para 184][Para 185] The order to line up 5 to 10 minutes before the bell was an abuse of the workers' right to rest outside working hours, although the panel put it as a matter for an employer measure rather than a ground for transfer. [Para 189][Para 190][Para 191] The verbal abuse, however, was proved by four consistent witnesses and occurred both before and after the employer's education of 30 July 2014; Article 83(B)(4) of the Labour Law and Article 10(5) of the company's internal rules make insulting a worker serious misconduct warranting dismissal. [Para 192][Para 194][Para 200][Para 202][Para 205]

The panel accepted that disciplining a worker is the employer's prerogative, but found the employer had taken no effective measure to stop the conduct, so the decision was not made reasonably. [Para 206][Para 208] It noted the employer's difficulty in replacing a supervisor trained over four years, but held that psychological health is part of health and the employer must protect it, that the abuse was continuing and affected workers' dignity, and that transfer was a proportionate way to reduce the conflict. [Para 209][Para 213][Para 215]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union's demand that an employer transfer a supervisor is a rights dispute, and the employer's managerial prerogative under Article 2 of the Labour Law is respected unless the supervisor's proved conduct amounts to serious misconduct under Article 83 — here persistent verbal abuse that continued after the employer's own warning — and the employer has failed to take any effective disciplinary measure. In that case the panel may order a transfer to another section, but not dismissal, since only the parties to the employment contract may extinguish it. [Para 151][Para 159][Para 205][Para 208][Para 215]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that lining workers up before the bell breaches their right to rest, and that a public warning would humiliate the employee, did not by themselves justify the order. [Para 103][Para 190]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The union's most-representative certificate is recorded as valid only until 17 January 2014 [Para 50][Para 51], i.e. expired seven months before the hearing, yet the panel did not discuss standing; the point is left unresolved on the face of the award.
- The panel's operative paragraph names the supervisor as "ប្រធាន Lin ផ្នែក ឈ្មោះ ជិន វី" at [Para 216] — an untranslated "Lin" intrudes into the Khmer name; the same name appears as "ជិន វី" elsewhere [Para 52][Para 217].
- The award is dated 3 September 2014 in the header [Para 1] and the postponement from 26 August 2014 to that date is recorded [Para 22]; the hearing date is 13 August 2014 [Para 7], but the workers' evidence is dated "១៣ ខែធ្នូ សីហា ឆ្នាំ ២០១៤" — two months run together — at [Para 75].
- [Para 75] opens with an untranslated English OCR fragment ("an work prover the recording…") and [Para 219] ends with "momer Tokuland"; signature blocks at [Para 219][Para 220] are largely noise.
- [Para 183] repeats "ក្រុមប្រឹក្សា អាជ្ញាធរ ជាតិ … នៅក្នុង សំណុំរឿង នេះ កណ្តាល នៅក្នុង សំណុំរឿង ខាងលើ" — duplicated words make the sentence ungrammatical, though its sense (agreement with earlier awards) is clear.
