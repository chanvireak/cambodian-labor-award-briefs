# Award No. 110/09 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ប៊ី រី អី ភែរឹល (ខេមបូឌា) ("B.R. Apparel") (Arbitration Council, 8 September 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (ម៉ារ សំបូរ ណា, employer party; អាន វីរៈ, worker party; ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា chair) · Doc ID 140421

## 1. Facts
The employer, ក្រុមហ៊ុន ប៊ី រី អី ភែរឹល (ខេមបូឌា), began operating in April 2005 at Krasaing Phaem, Chom Chao, Dangkao, and employs about 3,000 workers. [Para 1][Para 35] Three unions exist in the factory and none is yet the most representative; the claimant is the local of សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព-សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ (KYFTU), with about 246 members whose dues the company deducted for July 2009. [Para 1][Para 36][Para 38] Of three demands, issue 3 was withdrawn at the hearing, leaving the weekly payment of the overtime meal allowance and the level of the regular attendance bonus. [Para 15][Para 20][Para 21]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay the overtime meal allowance once a week rather than monthly with wages? [Para 3][Para 39]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must raise the regular attendance bonus from US$5 to US$10 a month? [Para 5][Para 57]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** overtime runs from 16:00 to 18:00 Monday to Friday and the meal allowance is paid only with the monthly wage on the 6th, so workers cannot buy food for the overtime shift; nearby factories such as QMI pay it weekly; and the attendance bonus should be US$10 because production is strong and neighbouring employers pay US$6 to US$8. [Para 39][Para 41][Para 44][Para 59][Para 60]
**Employer (respondent):** the allowance has always been paid with the monthly wage since 2005, and the payroll is computerised, so paying two different ways is impossible; it already pays US$5 attendance bonus plus US$5 travel money, which exceeds the law; a survey of 50 workers showed most prefer a lump monthly payment. [Para 45][Para 47][Para 49][Para 61]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to pay the overtime meal allowance once a week to those workers who thumbprinted the claim; the claim to double the attendance bonus was refused for want of jurisdiction. [Para 116][Para 117]

On issue 1 the panel held that Article 116 requires at least two wage payments a month and fixes no date, and that Article 103 makes overtime pay part of wages. [Para 65][Para 67][Para 72] The meal allowance, however, is not part of overtime wages: it substitutes for a free meal which the employer owes under point 4 of Notice No. 017 of 18 July 2000 at 1,000 riel a day, and that instrument fixes no payment date. [Para 75][Para 79][Para 83] The purpose of the allowance being to let workers buy food for the overtime shift, the panel held it must be paid within a reasonable time and that weekly payment is reasonable, rejecting the computer-system objection as a matter the employer can change. [Para 85][Para 94][Para 96] It limited the order to workers who had thumbprinted the demand. [Para 56][Para 116]

On issue 2 the panel treated the demand for a further US$5 as a claim to a benefit above the statutory minimum, hence an interest dispute, and refused to consider it because the KYFTU is not the most representative union and an award would operate as a collective agreement for one year under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 105][Para 107][Para 111][Para 115]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An overtime meal allowance is not a component of overtime wages but the cash substitute for the free meal the employer owes under Notice No. 017 of 18 July 2000; although that instrument fixes no payment date, the allowance must be paid within a period that lets the worker buy food for the overtime shift, and one week satisfies that requirement even where the employer's payroll runs monthly. [Para 75][Para 83][Para 85][Para 96]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that changing the payroll system is feasible, and that most-representative status is a precondition for interest claims, are general observations. [Para 94][Para 111]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The employer's name is printed at least five different ways — "ប៊ី៖ អី តែ រឹល (ខេមបូឌា)", "ប៊ី រី អី ភែរី", "ប៊ឺ រី អី ភែរី" and "ប៊ី រី អី ភែរឹល" — so the registered form cannot be confirmed [Para 1][Para 25][Para 32].
- The arbitrator selected by the employer appears as "ម៉ារ សំបូរ ណា" in the composition list and only as initials in the signature block, and the chair is printed "ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា" here but "ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈាម" in the signature [Para 1][Para 119].
- From [Para 119] to the end the source is destroyed: the annexed list of workers' thumbprints and the second signature page survive only as fragments of Latin words, digits, years such as "2017" and punctuation, so the workers covered by the order cannot be identified from the text.
- The union is described both as a local of KYFTU and as "សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ" alone, and its membership is put at about 246 while the panel also records 300 workers surveyed or deducted for dues [Para 38][Para 47].
