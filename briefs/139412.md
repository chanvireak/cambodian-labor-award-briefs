# Award No. 115/15 — Te Hfu Am Brela (Tefu Am Brela) Co., Ltd., Kampong Cham (Arbitration Council, 12 June 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3 — Chhiv Phirum (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Nyan Somunnynt (chair, chosen by the two) · Doc ID 139412

## 1. Facts
The employer makes umbrellas, with Branch 1 on National Road 4 and Branch 2 in Kampong Cham, opened about 7 months before the award and employing about 820 workers. [Para 39][Para 41][Para 42] A union was formed at Branch 2 by election on 15 March 2015, and notice of the elected leadership was delivered to the employer through a company security guard on 21 March 2015. [Para 34][Para 61] Two QC workers, Ms K and Ms P, were the elected president and vice-president; both were removed from the workplace days later, Ms K being kept outside the factory from 30 March 2015 and Ms P told on 5 April 2015 that her contract would end on 16 April 2015. [Para 63][Para 67][Para 71][Para 79] Of 13 points raised in conciliation, 9 were settled and 4 (issues 3, 6, 12 and 13) came to the Council. [Para 12][Para 13]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must provide one mid-day meal, or 3,000 riel per day in lieu, to the workers? [Para 3][Para 49]
2. Whether the employer, in paying overtime, must include seniority pay, incentive pay and role allowance in the calculation? [Para 4][Para 53]
3. Whether the employer must take Ms K and Ms P back into work and repay their wages and other benefits from the date of dismissal until the date of return? [Para 5][Para 6]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the two QC workers asked for lunch or 3,000 riel per day because other factories in Phnom Penh and Kampong Cham provide it, while conceding the demand rests on no existing law or agreement; they claimed base wages of 128 US dollars plus 7 US dollars travel and accommodation per month, and asserted the dismissals were on account of their union activity, two other elected leaders having also been pushed out. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 54][Para 81]
**Employer (respondent):** Branch 2 had run only about 7 months with 13 buyers still absent, taking work from Branch 1, and would pay 1,000 riel per day for lunch only once direct orders arrived, at no known date; it does not include seniority, incentive or role pay in the overtime rate because no such payments exist at Branch 2, and said Ms K was excluded for causing disorder and Ms P simply reached the end of her fixed-term contract. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 59][Para 60][Para 82][Para 83]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the meal demand (issue 3) and the overtime-calculation demand (issue 6) were refused, while the employer was ordered to take Ms K and Ms P back into work and to repay their wages and other benefits from the date of dismissal until the date of return; the award is non-binding and becomes final 8 days after notification absent written objection to the Minister. [Para 174][Para 175][Para 176][Para 177]

On issue 3 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules or past practice obliging the employer to provide a meal or 3,000 riel. [Para 85] Because the demand exceeded the law it was a dispute of interest, and under Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 the Council may decide an interest dispute only where the union bringing it is the most representative in the enterprise, alone or together holding more than half the workforce; the panel also recalled Prakas No. 099 Article 43, under which such an award substitutes for a collective agreement and removes the right to strike on that subject for a year, and so requires that threshold. [Para 88][Para 92][Para 94][Para 95][Para 96][Para 97] It followed Award No. 169/11-Fortune TV, No. 02/11-Pou Yien, No. 66/11-Inhansung and No. 158/13 in declining an interest dispute brought by a union lacking that status and found the complainants here failed the test. [Para 98][Para 101][Para 102]

On issue 6 the panel applied Article 19 of Prakas No. 099, requiring written authorisation for anyone other than the party to appear, and found the group assistants whose pay was in issue were absent and never supplied fingerprints or written transfers; the two women present therefore had no standing to represent them, and the claim was refused on that procedural ground without reaching the merits. [Para 105][Para 107][Para 109][Para 110][Para 111]

On issues 12 and 13 the panel held both contracts to be of unfixed duration. Article 67 of the Labour Law requires a fixed-duration contract to be in writing with an express end date, failing which it becomes unfixed; the successive two-month contracts put before the panel bore no signature or thumbprint of either worker, and after the probation period the employer said nothing and let both continue working. [Para 119][Para 120][Para 121][Para 123][Para 125][Para 126][Para 127] Turning to union protection, the panel set out the three conditions from Award No. 50/05-Forsin and its successors — protected status, dismissal within the protected period, and notice of the protected names to the employer — and found all three met: the election of 15 March 2015 made them leaders under Article 293 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 305. [Para 130][Para 132][Para 149][Para 152][Para 158][Para 165] Ms P was dismissed on 16 April 2015, about 32 days after the election, and Ms K 25 days after it. [Para 154][Para 157] The notification letter of 21 March 2015 was received and signed for by the security guard, and the employer's plea that it never read the letter did not defeat receipt. [Para 160][Para 162] Dismissal without prior authorisation from the labour inspector was therefore void, and reinstatement with back pay followed. [Para 166][Para 171][Para 173]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a fixed-duration contract is not signed or thumbprinted by the worker and no notice of its termination is given, it converts to an unfixed-duration contract under Article 67; and a worker elected as a union leader who is dismissed inside the 45-day protected period without the labour inspector's prior authorisation is dismissed voidly, entitling her to reinstatement and to wages and benefits from dismissal to return. [Para 120][Para 125][Para 166][Para 173]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's reasoning that an interest award would extinguish the right to strike on the same subject for a year, offered to explain why the representativeness threshold matters, was not needed once the threshold was found unmet. [Para 96]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The dismissal date of Ms K is printed as 31 March 2015 in the facts and in the employer's account, but the panel's finding computes it as 9 April 2015 and 25 days after the election; both are reported and neither reconciled. [Para 71][Para 155][Para 157] The date on which the panel was constituted reads "១៩ ខែឧសភា ឆ្នាំ ២០២៣ ២០១៥" and Prakas No. 305 is dated "ឆ្នាំ ២០០១៩" in the reasons, each carrying a stray extra year. [Para 14][Para 170] The union-notification letter is cited as No. 059/15 in the evidence list and as No. 059/15 SK in the reasoning. [Para 33][Para 160] Two contract clauses are duplicated verbatim within single lines, and lines 91, 140 and 45 carry bare punctuation with no text. [Para 25][Para 75][Para 91] The signature block reduces the chair's name to "mammy 17770343" and the worker-appointed arbitrator's to digits, so both names are taken from the face of the award. [Para 178] The company name appears as "តេ ហ្វូ អាំ ប្រេឡឹ" and, in the provincial department's file, as "តេហ្វូ អាំ ប្រេឡា", which the panel itself notes. [Para 39]
