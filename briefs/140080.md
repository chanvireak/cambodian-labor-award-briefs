# Award No. 127/11-TASK — TASK (Trotrung ning Akpiwat Sahakum Krey kror) (Arbitration Council, 3 November 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry for the employer, Sin Kim Sean for the workers, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 140080

## 1. Facts
TASK is an NGO registered in 2004, successor to Servants to Asia's Urban Poor. Its executive director, Prom Pauv, dismissed three management staff — heads of the Health and Education programmes and the finance manager — on 21 July 2011, after they complained to the board about his conduct. Each had 5 to 10 years' service at a main wage of US$ 850 per month and held successively renewed fixed-duration contracts. [Para 24][Para 29][Para 34][Para 39]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the former staff are entitled to compensation in lieu of prior notice, indemnity for dismissal, payment in lieu of annual leave, outstanding wages, retirement payments, damages, compensation for defamation and certificates of employment? [Para 24]
2. Whether the employer's allegation of serious misconduct under Article 83 of the Labour Law is made out against the three dismissed staff? [Para 54]
3. Whether the demand for US$ 50,000 each in defamation compensation and a published letter of apology should be granted? [Para 123]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The three were dismissed without valid reason after they filed a good-faith complaint about the executive director's expenditure; the meeting of 19 July 2011 was called by a board member, not by them, and they deny divulging confidential information or organising a political demonstration. [Para 45][Para 48]
**Employer (respondent):** The three conspired with a board member to call an unauthorised meeting that confused staff, and divulged confidential information to SAUP staff, amounting to serious misconduct under Article 83 and Article 19 Chapter 3 of TASK's statute; TASK cannot afford the demands. [Para 5][Para 42][Para 43]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Partly granted — the panel ordered certificates of employment and payment of dismissal indemnity, damages equal to that indemnity, compensation in lieu of prior notice (three months' wages to Cheang Sarim and Net Chenda, two months to Sou Lalin), payment in lieu of annual leave, outstanding wages and retirement payments; the US$ 50,000 defamation claims and the apology letter were rejected. [Para 127]

The panel found no Article 19 in Chapter 3 of the statute the employer relied on, so the disciplinary basis asserted was unsupported. [Para 56] On Article 83, the evidence showed the 19 July meeting was called by a board member on his own initiative and the employer failed to prove the three staff were involved; nor was there evidence of incitement or political demonstration. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 66] Although the employer added a confidential-information ground at the hearing, the dismissal notices of 21 July 2011 cited only the meeting, and under Article 26 the employer had learned of the disclosure on 3 July 2011 — far outside the seven-day window — so the right to dismiss was renounced. [Para 70][Para 72][Para 75]

Because the contracts had been renewed beyond two years, Article 67 paragraph 2 converted them to undetermined duration, following Awards 36/06-Mondotex and 57/06-Evergreen. [Para 79][Para 80] Termination at will under Article 74 required prior notice and a valid reason; none was given, so Article 89 indemnity (15 days per year, capped at six months) applied — US$ 981.50 × 6 for Cheang Sarim and Sou Lalin and US$ 971.50 × 6 for Net Chenda. [Para 84][Para 88][Para 90] Article 91 damages were awarded equal to the indemnity since no proof of loss was offered. [Para 95][Para 96] Annual leave was recalculated under Article 166 rather than TASK's inferior statute, which Article 13 rendered unenforceable: 8.25 days, 22.25 days and 24.08 days respectively. [Para 104][Para 105][Para 108][Para 110][Para 112] Retirement payments under Section G of TASK's policy were confirmed, with Sou Lalin's US$ 5,700 loan to be repaid. [Para 115][Para 116] Certificates of employment were ordered under Article 93. [Para 120][Para 121] The defamation claims failed for want of a proper calculation and because Article 91 damages were sufficient reparation; the Labour Law provides no basis for an apology letter. [Para 123][Para 125][Para 126]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer's stated disciplinary ground cannot be located in its own statute, and the ground actually notified in the dismissal notice was not proven, the dismissal is without valid reason and Article 89 indemnity, Article 91 damages, Article 75 notice compensation and Article 167 leave payments follow. A ground raised for the first time at the hearing cannot rescue the dismissal, particularly where Article 26's seven-day period has expired. [Para 56][Para 70][Para 75]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that even if the alleged misconduct had been proven, Point L of TASK's policy did not provide for immediate dismissal for that type of offence. [Para 76]
