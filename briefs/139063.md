# Award No. 157/16 — Sky Line Apparel Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 4 August 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Tun Si Phan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, President) · Doc ID 139063

## 1. Facts
Sky Line Apparel Co., Ltd is a garment cut-and-sew factory in Kien Svay district, Kandal province, employing approximately 896 workers [Para 53][Para 54]. Ms V, a sewing-section worker and president of the Free Women Workers' Union branch at the company, was dismissed on 11 May 2016, three days after the union's founding election of 08 May 2016; the union notified the company and the provincial labour department of the elected leadership by letters of 09 May 2016 [Para 3][Para 59][Para 142]. She had worked since 07 September 2015 on successive fixed-duration three-month contracts, the last running from 07 May to 06 August 2016 [Para 56][Para 57]. She claims reinstatement with back pay and benefits; the company refuses [Para 3][Para 5].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. What type of employment contract did Ms V hold? [Para 115]
2. Did she receive the protection attaching to union leadership when she was dismissed? [Para 122]
3. Was her dismissal connected with her union activity (anti-union discrimination)? [Para 173]
4. Did the employer have the right to dismiss her? [Para 189]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Ms V had never committed any fault and was dismissed because she founded and presided over the union branch; on 09 May 2016 the head of administration asked her whether she had created a union and warned that she would "have problems", and on 11 May he had her card seized and had security drag her out [Para 61][Para 62][Para 67].

**Employer (respondent):** The dismissal had nothing to do with union activity; her performance-evaluation score was too low, so her fixed-duration contract was simply not renewed, and the termination letter cites failure to follow the team leader's instructions, talking during working hours and falling output [Para 72][Para 98][Para 102]. The company denies receiving the notification letters and denies that any union election took place [Para 87][Para 91].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Partly granted — the claim for reinstatement and back wages was dismissed, but the employer was ordered to pay her final unpaid wages, compensation for unused annual leave, the 5% end-of-contract indemnity, and damages (ប្រាក់ជម្ងឺចិត្ត) of at least the wages she would have earned to the end of the contract on 06 August 2016 [Para 215][Para 216][Para 217].

On issue 1 the panel preferred the company's own termination record, which states that her last fixed-duration contract ran to 06 August 2016, over its later assertion that the contract had expired on 07 May; she had in fact kept working until 11 May, so a fourth three-month contract from 07 May to 06 August 2016 existed [Para 117][Para 119][Para 120][Para 121].

On issue 2 the panel held that Article 11 of the Trade Union Law of 17 May 2016 confers rights and benefits only once the founders have registered with the Ministry, and the branch was unregistered, so as union president she could not represent members before the panel [Para 106][Para 110][Para 111]. Protection still arose, however, from Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, Article 3(3) and Article 4 of which extend the protection given to staff delegates to candidates in union leadership elections and to founders and members during the registration period [Para 124][Para 125][Para 129][Para 135]. Following Award No. 40/10 (Meng Yan), the panel drew three cumulative conditions: the worker must be of the protected category, the dismissal must fall inside the protection period, and the union must have notified the employer of the protected names by all reasonable means [Para 138][Para 139]. Conditions 1 and 2 were satisfied — she was an elected candidate and was dismissed three days after the election [Para 149][Para 150][Para 153]. Condition 3 was not: the panel accepted that the letter was posted on 10 May and reached the company on 17 May 2016, but since the dismissal occurred on 11 May the notification came too late [Para 165][Para 169][Para 170][Para 172]. The panel added that the postal clerk's failure to deliver was the postal service's responsibility and not the union's fault [Para 168].

On issue 3, the panel applied Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law, which forbid an employer from using union membership or activity as a ground for hiring, discipline or dismissal, and placed the burden of proving discrimination on the party alleging it [Para 173][Para 174][Para 177]. It weighed the two witnesses produced by the employer, who said they had not voted and did not know of the union, against Ms V's unsupported account of the 09 May meeting, and found she had produced no evidence capable of displacing the employer's denial [Para 92][Para 94][Para 185][Para 186][Para 188].

On issue 4, Article 73 of the Labour Law allows a fixed-duration contract to end at its term, and permits early unilateral termination by the employer only by agreement or for serious misconduct or force majeure; otherwise the worker is entitled to damages at least equal to the wages due to the end of the contract [Para 190][Para 193][Para 194][Para 195]. A failed performance evaluation is neither serious misconduct nor force majeure, so the employer owed compensation, together with the final wage under Article 116 and the leave and indemnity entitlements under Articles 166, 167 and 73(6) [Para 199][Para 204][Para 208][Para 214].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union leader whose union is not yet registered is protected against dismissal under Prakas No. 305 only where three cumulative conditions are met: she belongs to a protected category (founder, member during registration, or candidate in a leadership election), the dismissal falls within the protection period, and the union has notified the employer of the protected names by all reasonable means before the dismissal; a notice posted before but delivered after the dismissal does not satisfy the third condition [Para 139][Para 166][Para 170][Para 172]. Separately, terminating a fixed-duration contract before its term on the ground of a failed performance evaluation, absent serious misconduct or force majeure, obliges the employer to pay damages at least equal to the wages due to the contract's end, even where the employer genuinely intended not to renew [Para 194][Para 195][Para 199].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that it has no competence to review whether the conduct of a union election was itself proper, and declined the employer's invitation to do so [Para 147]; it also observed that delivery of a letter is the postal operator's duty and not the sender's fault, a remark that did not save the claim because of timing [Para 168].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company name is printed in more than eight different renderings across the award (ស្កាយ ឡាញ អ៊ីភែវីល, ស្កាយ ឡាញ អ៊ីភែរីល, ស្តាយ ឡាញ, ស្វាយ-ឡាញ, ស្កាយ ឡាញ់), including in the operative part [Para 1][Para 53][Para 216]. The conciliation report is numbered 1048 with the code "ភប/កណ" at Paras 8 and 49 but "កប/ភណ" at Para 13, which also dates it 24 June 2016 against 30 June 2016 elsewhere. The internal rules are dated "០៥ ខែធ្នូ ខែតុលា ឆ្នាំ ២០១៥" — two months in one phrase [Para 23]. The document-rebuttal deadline is given as 19 June 2016 although the hearing was on 13 July 2016 [Para 20]. The contract dates are internally inconsistent: Paras 117 and 118 give the final contract as 07 June to 08 August 2016, against 07 May to 06 August 2016 at Paras 57, 115 and 121, and the termination record at Para 98 prints a garbled string of contract periods. The union election is dated 08 May 2016 at Paras 142 and 150 but 06 May 2016 at Para 103. Paras 64, 76, 79, 82, 176, 181 and 217 are badly corrupted, with stray "2017" and "2016" tokens inserted into the reasoning and into citations of prior awards, several of which are illegible; the panel's signature block loses the worker-side arbitrator's name to OCR [Para 217].
