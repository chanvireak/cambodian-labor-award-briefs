# Award No. 159/08 — Four Season Garment & Wool Knitting Factory (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្វរ ធ្យូន ហ្គាមិ និង វូល លែន ណៃ ធីង ហ្វាក់ តូរី) (Arbitration Council, 30 January 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — employer-appointed កៅ ថា ច, worker-appointed ទួន ស៊ី ផាន់, president ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា · Doc ID 140581

## 1. Facts
Seventeen women in the "touch" (ប៉ះ) section of Four Season Garment & Wool Knitting Factory (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្វរ ធ្យូន ហ្គាមិ និង វូល លែន ណៃ ធីង ហ្វាក់ តូរី), a Kandal-province garment employer of 2,700 workers, were paid by the lot [Para 32]. Their union, the Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers' Democratic Unions (សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា — ស៊ី ខៅ ឌូ), complained after the company set a rate of USD 0.18 per lot for style PN 639 on 16 December 2008 [Para 33][Para 41]. The workers had run that style exclusively on 12, 13, 15 and 16 December 2008 before any rate was published, and from 17 December they took other styles without authorisation in protest [Para 40][Para 60]. Conciliation failed on one point and the file came to the Council on 31 December 2008 [Para 9][Para 13].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must raise the rate for style PN 639 by USD 0.05, from USD 0.18 to USD 0.23 per lot? [Para 68]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay the regular-attendance bonus of USD 5 for December 2008? [Para 69]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must allow workers to take other styles on their own initiative when negotiation over a style's rate has failed? [Para 70]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant union):** The rate of USD 0.18 is too low because a worker doing PN 639 exclusively can complete only five to nine lots a day and so cannot reach the guaranteed daily equivalent of USD 1.92; USD 0.23 per lot is needed [Para 42][Para 43]. The seventeen worked the whole of December so the USD 5 bonus is owed, and they took other styles only because a team leader told them to [Para 56][Para 62].

**Employer (respondent):** A worker who works hard on PN 639 can earn more than the base wage, and the protest meant the women were not working at full capacity, so their output cannot fix the rate [Para 44][Para 46][Para 47]. The bonus was withheld because they ignored assignments and the internal rules, and management may direct work as the production schedule requires [Para 63][Para 65].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three claims were rejected, including the demand for a higher lot rate on PN 639, the USD 5 attendance bonus for December 2008, and the right to choose styles unilaterally [Para 139][Para 140][Para 141].

Before the merits the panel examined its own jurisdiction under Article 312(1) of the Labour Law and Article 33 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, which confine it to the points in the non-conciliation record and their direct consequences [Para 71][Para 72][Para 73]. It held that the lot-rate claim, though not spelled out in the record, was a direct consequence of the dispute and had been pursued since the 17 December 2008 in-factory talks [Para 76][Para 77][Para 79]; that the bonus claim was also a consequence, because the deduction followed the protest [Para 85][Para 86][Para 87]; and that the third claim appeared in the record itself [Para 91][Para 92].

On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 108 of the Labour Law, which requires piece-rate pay to yield at least the guaranteed minimum wage for a worker of average skill working ordinary hours, together with point 1 of Notification No. 745 of 23 October 2006 setting the minimum at USD 50 a month after probation [Para 94][Para 95][Para 96]. It followed its earlier awards No. 03/05, No. 41/05, No. 44/06, No. 73/06 and No. 106/07 [Para 98]. Because workers mixed PN 639 with other styles on the sample days, the panel found the tables could not establish whether USD 0.18 fell below the minimum, and it declined to fix a rate on that evidence [Para 112][Para 113][Para 115]. It accepted that the company's practice of a three-day trial before publishing a rate was appropriate [Para 108].

On Issue 2 the panel applied point 3 of Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000, which gives a regular-attendance bonus of at least USD 5 a month, and read "regular" to require both the required number of days and compliance with assignments and internal rules [Para 120][Para 121][Para 125]. It found the internal rules, Article 2 point 11, treat working without following an assignment as non-attendance, so the women forfeited the bonus [Para 126][Para 127][Para 129].

On Issue 3 the panel applied Article 2 of the Labour Law, which places the workforce under the employer's management, and held that the employer's right to direct work is valid so long as it is exercised lawfully and reasonably [Para 131][Para 132][Para 135]. It followed Awards No. 17/03 and No. 18/03, No. 28/04, No. 20/06, No. 17/07, No. 116/07 and No. 47/08 [Para 133]. Workers may not take a style without authorisation whatever the state of the rate negotiation [Para 137].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A piece-rate claim under Article 108 fails where the claimants' own output records mix the disputed style with others, so that the panel cannot verify that a worker of average skill working ordinary hours would fall below the guaranteed minimum; and a regular-attendance bonus under Notification No. 017 is forfeited by a worker who, while present every working day, refuses the employer's assignment, because regularity includes obedience to lawful direction [Para 112][Para 125][Para 129].

**Obiter dicta:** The arbitrator appointed by the workers, ទួន ស៊ី ផាន់, appended a dissent on Issue 1, computing from the exclusively-worked days an average of seven lots a day over 26 working days, or 182 lots a month, which at USD 50 gives USD 0.27 a lot, and he would have ordered USD 0.23 as claimed [Para 143][Para 157][Para 159][Para 170].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The case name in the heading is truncated to a form that does not match the company name given in the body [Para 1]. The employer's name is printed in at least four different spellings across the award, and one report title renders it as a different word altogether [Para 17][Para 28][Para 33]. The two hearings are dated 09 and 16 January 2008 in an award issued on 30 January 2009, a year that the surrounding dates contradict [Para 1][Para 11]. The panel describes a three-day trial but lists four dates, 12, 13, 15 and 16 December 2008 [Para 40][Para 106]. Large parts of the reasons and the annex are corrupted by OCR, including duplicated phrases and lost digits [Para 167].
