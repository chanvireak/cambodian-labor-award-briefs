# Award No. 151/08 — Wil Sin Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. / ក្រុមហ៊ុន វីល សិន ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) ខូអិលធីឌី (Arbitration Council, 9 January 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — You Soun Ty (employer-appointed), Sin Kim Sean (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chea (president) · Doc ID 140571

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន វីល សិន ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) ខូអិលធីឌី (Wil Sin Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd.) is a garment factory at Prey Ta village, Sangkat Kab, Khan Dangkao, Phnom Penh, and appeared through its administration chief [Para 2]. The claimant is សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ ប្រចាំ រោងចក្រ (the Khmer Youth Union Federation at the factory), which filed on 18 November 2008; final conciliation on 25 November 2008 settled none of its seven demands [Para 16][Para 17]. On 16 December 2008 the second union, សហជីព ធម្មាធិបតេយ្យ, stated that it no longer pursued the file, leaving the Khmer Youth Union Federation as sole complainant [Para 19].

The seven demands, which the panel never reached, were: to dismiss two Chinese supervisors and the Chinese production manager; to raise the regular attendance bonus by USD 2 a month and pro-rate it by days absent; a 2,000 riel meal allowance for overtime worked from 16:00 to 18:00; USD 5 a month for housing or transport per worker; one additional payment a week; to raise seniority pay by USD 1 for each year; and to apply dismissals under the factory's internal rules without discrimination [Para 3][Para 5][Para 6][Para 7][Para 8][Para 9][Para 10]. The hearing was fixed for 26 December 2008 at 08:00; no union representative attended, and a telephone call to the secretariat said the union would not attend and asked that the case be closed [Para 15][Para 21].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1 — procedural) Whether all three conditions of Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 were satisfied, so that the file could be closed without examining the seven demands? [Para 37][Para 50]
2. (Issue 2 — procedural) Whether the panel nevertheless retained power to issue an order where the claimant union chose not to take part? [Para 51]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the Khmer Youth Union Federation and the workers pressed the seven demands listed above, but by telephone on the day of the hearing they declined to attend and asked the panel to close the file [Para 2][Para 21][Para 38].

**Employer (respondent):** ក្រុមហ៊ុន វីល សិន ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) ខូអិលធីឌី attended through its administration chief and asked the panel to apply the law [Para 22][Para 47]. It stated that the two Chinese supervisors had already been dismissed, and that the production manager could not be dismissed because he was not at fault as alleged [Para 4].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — case No. 151/08 was closed under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099, with no ruling on any of the seven demands [Para 58][Para 59].

Rule 4.7 of the Rules of Procedure annexed to Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 lets the panel either continue the arbitration in a party's absence or terminate it by issuing an order, on condition that the party was properly notified of the date, time and place [Para 32][Para 33]. Article 21 of the same Prakas, as interpreted in cases 16/07, 27/07, 95/08, 132/08 and 138/08, requires three conditions: proper notice, non-appearance at the hearing, and no valid reason for the absence [Para 35].

On the first condition the panel found that invitation letters No. 762 and No. 763 of 19 December 2008 had been issued and that the secretariat telephoned a reminder before the hearing, so notice was proved [Para 29][Para 30][Para 37][Para 40]. On the second, it read "appearance" in Article 21 as requiring both presence at the hearing and participation in the arbitration through its four stages — introduction and conflict explanation, explanation of procedure and definition of the issues, a fresh attempt at conciliation, and the hearing to the end — and found the union had done neither [Para 41][Para 42][Para 44][Para 45]. On the third, it found no reason of any kind had been given for the absence, so that condition too was met [Para 46][Para 48][Para 49]. It concluded that it held power to issue an order notwithstanding the union's non-participation [Para 51].

The panel then addressed the consequence for the claim: the union had been given a full opportunity to defend the workers' demands, had chosen to stay away, and had thereby given up the chance to present evidence, so that the claimant, which carries the burden of proof, had produced neither reasons nor evidence and its claim was treated as abandoned [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54][Para 55][Para 56].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the claimant party has been properly notified of the hearing and neither appears nor gives any valid reason, the panel may terminate the arbitration by an order closing the file, and may issue that order in the party's absence; non-appearance forgoes the opportunity to prove the demands, and the panel treats the claim as abandoned rather than deciding it on the merits [Para 44][Para 51][Para 56].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that staying away showed no sincere intention to have the dispute resolved, and its observation that the union had been afforded every opportunity to defend the claim, were characterisations rather than further grounds [Para 52][Para 57].
