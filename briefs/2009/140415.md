# Award No. 95/09 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន តាក ហ្វាត (ហ្គាមិន) (ខាំបូឌា) អែល អិលធីឌី ("Tak Faat") (Arbitration Council, 21 August 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៉ីង សុទ្ធី, employer party; វ៉ែន ពៅ, worker party; គង់ ផល្លៈ chair) · Doc ID 140415

## 1. Facts
The employer, ក្រុមហ៊ុន តាក ហ្វាត (ហ្គាមិន) (ខាំបូឌា) អែល អិលធីឌី, ran a factory on National Road 2, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, with about 2,035 workers. [Para 2][Para 70] The claimant is សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ ក្រុមហ៊ុន តាក ហ្វាត, a union of about 782 members that is not the most representative union in the factory. [Para 2][Para 71] Conciliation left eleven demands unresolved; at the hearing seven were settled and five (issues 1, 4, 5, 9 and 11) were withdrawn, so the panel decided only issues 2, 3, 6 and 10. [Para 31][Para 35][Para 36]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay US$220 a month into the health and safety fund from October 2008 until the date of payment? [Para 3][Para 72]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay severance and terminal benefits to the three woodworkers whose section it dissolved and who were moved to other sections without agreement? [Para 7][Para 83][Para 88]
3. (Issue 6) Whether the company must take back the employee delegate it suspended on 13 June 2009 and pay her wages from the date of suspension? [Para 13][Para 93][Para 185]
4. (Issue 10) Whether the company must pay full wages to the 231 workers of the finishing section for 15 to 30 June 2009, when it had no work for them? [Para 21][Para 225][Para 239]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the US$220 monthly contribution had been paid from November 2006 to October 2008 and was therefore a binding past practice; several of the 231 finishing workers had pressed their thumbprints for the June wages; the delegate was suspended without any prior warning or fault. [Para 77][Para 100][Para 99]
**Employer (respondent):** the fund payments were a discretionary benefit stopped because of the financial crisis, and the company had meanwhile registered and paid into the National Social Security Fund; the three woodworkers were moved, not dismissed; the delegate committed serious misconduct by selling food to other workers during working hours and the dismissal request was properly filed with the labour inspectorate. [Para 75][Para 96][Para 94][Para 186]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the fund claim was dismissed; the company was ordered to pay severance, notice compensation, unused annual leave and unpaid final wages to the three woodworkers, to reinstate the employee delegate with wages from the date of suspension, and to pay 100% of wages to the 231 finishing workers for 15 to 30 June 2009. [Para 240][Para 241][Para 242][Para 243]

On issue 2 the panel accepted that paying US$220 a month into the health and safety committee was a past practice within the meaning of Article 13 of the Labour Law, since it had run from November 2006 until October 2008. [Para 120][Para 79] It held, however, that a past practice may lawfully end where the employer's circumstances genuinely change, and it weighed the documented loss of orders against the National Social Security Fund contributions paid from November 2008. [Para 122][Para 131][Para 134]

On issue 3 the panel rejected the employer's res judicata objection: award 53/09 of 18 May 2009 involved the same parties but had not decided whether the three men were owed severance, so the same question could be decided. [Para 143][Para 149][Para 150] The transfer was unlawful for want of consent, the men had not abandoned their jobs, and the panel applied Articles 89, 75, 166 and 116 to award severance, notice, annual leave and final wages, while refusing moral damages under Article 91 because the company had never intended to dismiss them. [Para 164][Para 183]

On issue 6 the panel held that a delegate may only be dismissed with the labour inspector's authorisation under Article 293, that the inspectorate's silence beyond one month amounted to refusal, and that although Article 295 allowed suspension while the request was pending, a refused request made the suspension void — hence reinstatement with wages. [Para 199][Para 210][Para 222][Para 224]

On issue 10 the panel found that the company gave no notice of any suspension of the employment contract as Articles 71 and 72 require, and that once a suspension is irregular the employer owes 100% of wages even where no work is provided. [Para 227][Para 234][Para 238][Para 239]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A long-standing benefit paid at a fixed rate becomes a past practice that binds the employer, but it may be terminated where the employer shows a genuine change of circumstances of the kind the panel accepted here — an economic downturn coupled with the assumption of statutory social-security contributions — and where the workers did not object for eight months. Conversely, where the employer suspends work without the notice that Articles 71 and 72 require, the suspension is irregular and full wages remain payable. [Para 120][Para 131][Para 238]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that a refused dismissal request renders a pending suspension void, and its remark that past practices concerning production rhythms lie outside the doctrine, go beyond what the order required. [Para 118][Para 223]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award names the workers' delegate throughout; her name is not reproduced in this brief.
- The employer name is broken by scan debris at [Para 2], printed with a bracketed fragment between "តាក ហ្វាត" and "ហ្គាមិន", and the same company appears as "តាក ហ្វាត", "តាក ហ្គាត" and "ឆាក ហ្វាត" at [Para 42], [Para 44] and [Para 70].
- The arbitrator selected by the employer is printed "អ៉ីង សុទី្ល" in the signature block but "អ៉ីង សុទ្ធី" in the composition list; the worker-party arbitrator also appears as "វ៉ែន ពៅ វ៉ែន ពៅ" doubled at [Para 245].
- The statutory references are corrupted: "មាត្រា ៦៥៦០" [Para 113] and "ហត្រា ៨៩" for Article 89 [Para 88], and Article 332 is printed "មាត្រា ៣២២" [Para 108].
- Two different dates are given for the suspension: 13 June 2009 [Para 94] and 14 June 2009 [Para 216].
- The 231 workers in issue 10 are described as 500 finishing workers with 231 thumbprints at [Para 100] but as 231 throughout the order at [Para 239].
