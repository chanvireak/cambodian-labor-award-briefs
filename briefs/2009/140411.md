# Award No. 85/09 — Nan Kong Garmen (Cambodia) Co., Ltd and the Khmer Youth Union of Nan Kong factory (Arbitration Council of Cambodia, 24 August 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ly Tai Seng លី តៃ សេង (employer-appointed), Huon Di ហួន ឌី (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhean ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈាន (president) · Doc ID 140411

## 1. Facts
Nan Kong Garmen (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ណាន់ គ័ង ហ្គាមេន) employs about 1,350 workers at Damnak Thmey, Stueng Mean Chey, Phnom Penh. [Para 52] The Khmer Youth Union of the factory (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ) registered on 15 June 2009 and claims nearly 1,000 members; the company says no union existed there before. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 68] The union held a leadership election on 2 May 2009, notified the company through a federation officer on 7 May, and the guard at the gate refused the letter; after the union officers' names reached management the company let the president's six-month contract lapse on 25 May 2009 and dismissed the vice-president on 27 May 2009, three days after he was elected. [Para 58][Para 60][Para 73][Para 93][Para 244]

Of 24 demands in the non-conciliation report, eleven were settled at the hearing and one (Issue 21) was withdrawn, leaving nine for decision: union representation, the demand to dismiss an administrative officer (Ms L), removal of the landlord's security guards, 5% contract-end pay, holiday and Sunday meals, an overtime meal allowance, union-dues deduction, an extra attendance bonus, and pregnancy concessions. [Para 34][Para 35]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the refusal to renew the president's contract and the dismissal of the vice-president were anti-union discrimination? [Para 186]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must dismiss the administrative officer at the union's demand? [Para 251]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must remove all the landlord's security guards from the factory? [Para 267]
4. (Issue 4) Whether 5% contract-end pay is owed at each expiry of a fixed-duration contract? [Para 280]
5. (Issue 6) Whether the company owes a meal or 5,000 riel for work on holidays and Sundays? [Para 332]
6. (Issue 10) Whether the company owes a 500 riel per hour meal allowance on top of what it already pays for overtime? [Para 345]
7. (Issue 15) Whether the company must deduct 1,000 riel a month from members' wages as union dues? [Para 389]
8. (Issue 18) Whether the company owes a further USD 10 monthly attendance bonus? [Para 405]
9. (Issue 19) Whether pregnant workers must be released 15 minutes early and given one paid day a month for antenatal checks? [Para 413]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**ភាគីកម្មករនិយោជិត — Khmer Youth Union of Nan Kong factory (claimant):** the two union leaders were singled out because of their union posts — the president was told one day after his contract expired, once the election result had been passed to management, and no other worker was treated that way; the administrative officer had abused and threatened workers and refused to pass their complaints on; the landlord's guards insulted and obstructed workers; and the company should pay 5% at each contract expiry, a meal for holiday work, 500 riel an hour for overtime meals, dues deductions, a further USD 10 bonus and pregnancy concessions. [Para 58][Para 83][Para 116][Para 130][Para 155][Para 169][Para 177][Para 413]

**ភាគីនិយោជក — Nan Kong Garmen (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (respondent):** the president's contract simply reached its term, which ends the relationship; the vice-president was dismissed before his term for repeated breaches after five written warnings; the officer did nothing wrong and no worker ever complained to the company about her; the guards are the landlord's employees under contract; and the money demands exceed what the law requires — the company already pays 5 USD attendance bonus, 1,000 riel a day meal money for overtime, and gives only six-month contracts for business reasons. [Para 89][Para 94][Para 110][Para 136][Para 178][Para 266]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the company to take back the union president and vice-president and to pay the vice-president's wages and benefits from the date of dismissal until reinstatement, to convert successive fixed-duration contracts whose total exceeds two years into contracts of unspecified duration, and to pay 1,000 riel a day meal money whenever overtime is worked; every other claim was dismissed. [Para 420][Para 421][Para 425][Para 429]

On Issue 1 the panel held that a fixed-duration contract ends at its term under Article 73(k)(1), but that a decision not to renew taken because of union membership or activity contravenes Articles 12 and 279 and the Council's own reasoning in Award 10/03. The president's contract expired on 25 May 2009, yet the company summoned him on 26 May — after the union notified its election — and gave no reason beyond expiry, while other workers' contracts were renewed; the panel drew the inference of anti-union motive. [Para 187][Para 195][Para 199][Para 205][Para 216]

For the vice-president, the company relied on five warnings, but never produced the internal rules that are said to make three warnings gross misconduct; under Article 26(k)(2) the right to dismiss for gross misconduct lapses if not exercised within seven days, and the warnings all predated the renewed contract. The panel found the dismissal, timed with the president's non-renewal, to be discrimination and ordered reinstatement with back pay. [Para 223][Para 226][Para 230][Para 246][Para 247]

On Issues 2 and 3 the panel held that only the parties to a contract may terminate it, so workers cannot compel the employer to dismiss a colleague, and evidence must show the person is dangerous to workplace safety; the union produced none, describing no incident, date or name. The same failure of proof defeated the demand about the guards, who are engaged under the landlord's security contract. [Para 254][Para 261][Para 278]

On Issue 4 the panel held that 5% contract-end pay is not owed each time a fixed-duration contract expires while the employment relationship continues, but under Article 67(k)(2) a chain of renewals whose total exceeds two years converts the contract to one of unspecified duration, which it ordered. On Issues 6, 15 and 18 the panel refused the claims: a demand for holiday meals was a claim about work not yet assigned, the dues deduction had never been requested in writing, and no article of the Labour Law requires a further USD 10 bonus. [Para 341][Para 401][Para 409]

On Issue 10 the panel held that meal money is not part of the overtime wage but is a daily necessity the employer must supply, and Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 requires 1,000 riel a day or a free meal; that obligation was ordered, while the extra 500 riel an hour was refused as a claim for benefits above the statutory floor, which the union is not the most representative union and so cannot bring. [Para 352][Para 359][Para 377][Para 381][Para 385]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a union's existence is notified and its officers' names reach management shortly before a non-renewal and a dismissal, and the employer gives no reason beyond contract expiry while renewing others, the panel will infer anti-union discrimination and order reinstatement with back pay, because Article 12 and Article 279 forbid using union membership or activity in decisions on hiring and termination. [Para 199][Para 216][Para 246]

**Obiter dicta:** that the right to dismiss for gross misconduct lapses after seven days under Article 26(k)(2), and that claims for benefits above the statutory floor may be brought only by a union with most-representative status or more than half the workforce as members. [Para 230][Para 352]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The factory's name appears in at least five different Khmer spellings across the party box, the facts, the report references and the operative order, and one variation attaches the union to a differently named company. The party-designation lines are corrupted throughout, and one heading names the employer first while calling the workers "the claimant party". The nine union officers and committee members are printed in full along with the employer's lawyer and HR staff; none is reproduced here. OCR loss is severe at the end: one page of reasoned findings is reduced to fragments, and the signature block holds stray digits in place of signatures. One document date carries two conflicting years, one of them a decade out. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 418][Para 434]
