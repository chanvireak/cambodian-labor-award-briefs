# Award No. 156/09 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឃីង ឡែន ហ្គាមិន (King Land Garment) (Arbitration Council, 16 November 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: employer-appointed and worker-appointed arbitrators (names destroyed in OCR), president printed as ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា in the signature block · Doc ID 140448

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឃីង ឡែន ហ្គាមិន, a garment factory on National Road 2 in Chak Angre Krom, Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, operating since about 1996, employed roughly 780 workers and had three unions in the plant. [Para 1][Para 25][Para 26][Para 27]
The claimant was សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត, registered by certificate No. 398 of 26 December 2002 and claiming about 235 members; it had not asked the employer to check off dues but collected them by hand. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 31]
Three demands were referred: reinstatement with back pay of Ms N, a sewing deputy team leader and the union's factory president, who was removed on 17 August 2009; payment of at least the legal minimum wage to piece-rate sewers; and an end to anti-union discrimination. The workers merged the third demand into the first, so two issues were decided. [Para 2][Para 15][Para 33]
The employer did not attend the hearing despite being summoned, and no valid excuse was given, so the panel heard the case in its absence under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099/04. [Para 14][Para 58][Para 63]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issues 1 and 3) Whether the company's removal of Ms N, the factory's union president and a sewing deputy team leader, was lawful and free of anti-union discrimination, so that she must be reinstated with back pay? [Para 33][Para 65]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must top up the wages of piece-rate sewers to the legal minimum wage of 50 USD (45 USD for probationary workers) a month? [Para 79][Para 86]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត):** Ms N had never been at fault or warned, orally or in writing; the company suspended her contract three times without documents, paid 50 per cent for the last period and never paid the second half, then told her on 17 August 2009 that she was dismissed and had been replaced, and showed no labour-inspector authorisation; the removal was retaliation for helping a dismissed worker, and piecers regularly earned below the base wage. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38][Para 41][Para 42][Para 92]
**Employer (respondent, ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឃីង ឡែន ហ្គាមិន):** did not appear and filed no response, so the panel could obtain no reply to any of the workers' contentions and decided on the claimants' evidence alone. [Para 32][Para 45][Para 53]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to reinstate Ms N and to pay her wages and other benefits from the date of dismissal until her return, and dismissed the minimum-wage top-up claim. [Para 100][Para 101]
On issue 1 the panel applied Article 293 of the Labour Law, under which a worker representative or candidate may be dismissed only with the labour inspector's agreement, and Article 4 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, which extends that protection to three union leaders. The labour department's letter No. 840 of 2 September 2009 recognised Ms N as union president, and the company knew of her office, so she enjoyed the protection. [Para 65][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70]
The panel found that the company suspended her contract three times by word of mouth, without any written record, and then on 17 August 2009 dismissed her without filing any request for authorisation with the labour inspectorate as Article 293 requires. It therefore held the dismissal procedurally unlawful and ordered reinstatement with back pay, following awards 79/06, 74/08 and 107/08. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 77][Para 73]
On issue 2 the panel applied Article 108 of the Labour Law, which requires piece-rate pay to be calculated so that an averagely skilled worker earns at least the guaranteed minimum, Article 137 on working time, and Notification No. 745 of 23 October 2006 fixing 45 USD for probationary and 50 USD for full-rights workers, with the employer topping up any shortfall. It held the employer legally obliged to make up the difference. [Para 80][Para 81][Para 82][Para 86][Para 87][Para 88]
Nevertheless the claimants produced no list identifying which workers were underpaid, no pay slips and no table showing eight-hour, six-day work, despite an order to file that evidence by 11 November 2009; the panel therefore had no basis to quantify any shortfall and dismissed the claim, while restating that the employer remains bound to pay the minimum wage. [Para 91][Para 96][Para 98][Para 99][Para 102]
The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification unless a party objects. [Para 103]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union leader may be dismissed only with the labour inspector's agreement under Article 293 of the Labour Law; where the employer removes such a leader without seeking authorisation, the dismissal is unlawful and reinstatement with back pay follows, regardless of the reasons the employer gives. [Para 65][Para 71][Para 77]

**Obiter dicta:** piece-rate workers must still be guaranteed the minimum wage by top-up, but a panel will dismiss that claim where the union cannot name the underpaid workers or produce pay records. [Para 88][Para 98]

[Para 75][Para 1][Para 25][Para 26]
