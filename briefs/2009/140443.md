# Award No. 134/09 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ប្រូដឡែន ហ្គាមិន (Breaden Garment) (Arbitration Council, 12 October 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: តើ កៃ សេង (employer-appointed), លីវ សុវណ្ណឌី (worker-appointed), ប៉ែន ប៉ុន លោ (president) · Doc ID 140443

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ប្រូដឡែន ហ្គាមិន, a garment factory that moved to Damnak Thom, Stung Mean Chey, in 2007, employed about 875 workers. [Para 40][Para 41]
The claimant was សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ នៃ រោងចក្រ ប្រូដឡែន, registered on 7 August 2009 and claiming roughly 340 members, but not certified as the plant's most-representative union. [Para 33][Para 43][Para 148]
Twelve of the union's demands went unresolved at conciliation; the union then withdrew point 10, leaving eleven for decision, spanning wages during no-work periods, bonus and allowance payments, pregnant workers' rights, overtime, sick leave, medical staffing, a motorbike shelter, supervisory conduct, union dues and wage deductions for errors. [Para 22][Para 28][Para 29]
The employer did not attend the hearing and gave no valid excuse, so the panel heard the case in its absence under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099/04. [Para 26][Para 116][Para 121]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay 100 per cent of wages during short no-work periods where contracts were never lawfully suspended? [Para 122]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay a 4,000 riel meal allowance to workers who work on holidays or Sundays? [Para 137]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay 5 USD a month extra to workers able to operate two machines? [Para 156]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must top up piece-rate workers' wages to the 50 USD monthly minimum? [Para 168]
5. (Issue 5) Whether pregnant workers must be allowed to leave 15 minutes early from the first month and attend monthly check-ups without losing wages, regular attendance bonus or annual leave? [Para 182]
6. (Issue 6) Whether the employer must keep a nurse on duty at every working hour, stock adequate medicines and route sick workers through the company doctor first? [Para 217]
7. (Issue 7) Whether overtime must be organised on a voluntary basis only? [Para 236]
8. (Issue 8) Whether the employer must build a shelter for workers' motorbikes? [Para 244]
9. (Issue 9) Whether the employer must discipline team leaders who use abusive language towards workers? [Para 253]
10. (Issue 11) Whether the employer must deduct 1,000 riel per member per month from wages as union dues? [Para 265]
11. (Issue 12) Whether the employer may cut daily or hourly wages for minor errors? [Para 281]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ នៃ រោងចក្រ ប្រូដឡែន):** the employer sent workers home without notice when orders ran short and paid only 50 per cent; it required signatures and refusal-leave applications to force overtime; pregnant workers from the first month needed 15 minutes' early release and monthly check-ups; medicines were limited to fever tablets and team leaders shouted at and insulted workers. [Para 45][Para 47][Para 77][Para 90]
**Employer (respondent, ក្រុមហ៊ុន ប្រូដឡែន ហ្គាមិន):** did not appear and filed no substantive response, so none of the workers' contentions was contradicted; the panel recorded over and over that it could obtain no reply from the respondent. [Para 26][Para 53][Para 76]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered 100 per cent wages for short no-work periods, a top-up of piece-rate wages to 50 USD a month, a nurse on duty at all working hours with adequate medicines, the doctor-first sick-leave procedure, strictly voluntary overtime, disciplinary measures against abusive team leaders, and an end to unpaid overtime imposed as a penalty; it dismissed the meal allowance, two-machine premium, early-leave, motorbike shelter and union-dues claims. [Para 291][Para 292][Para 293][Para 294][Para 298][Para 301][Para 303][Para 304][Para 305]
On issue 1 the panel applied Articles 71 and 72 of the Labour Law: suspension is lawful only on serious economic or material difficulty, notified to and supervised by the labour inspector. The company instead applied its own 50 per cent rule with neither notice nor authorisation, so it owed full wages. [Para 124][Para 131][Para 134][Para 136]
On issue 4 it applied Article 108 (piece-rate pay must reach at least the guaranteed minimum) and Notification No. 745 of 23 October 2006 fixing 45 USD for probationary and 50 USD for full-rights workers, and ordered the top-up. [Para 168][Para 175][Para 180][Para 181]
On issue 5 it split the claim: an extra 15 minutes' early release beyond the existing 5 was refused for want of evidence, but a worker absent on an authorised antenatal day is not owed wages under Articles 71 and 72, while the regular attendance bonus may not be withheld for that day. [Para 186][Para 191][Para 196][Para 203][Para 216]
On issue 6 it applied Article 242 of the Labour Law and Joint Prakas No. 330 of 6 December 2000 requiring a nurse on duty at all hours, including overtime, Sundays and holidays, and adequate medicines. On issue 7 it applied Article 4 of Prakas No. 80 of 1999: overtime must be voluntary and no discipline may follow refusal. [Para 217][Para 219][Para 221][Para 237][Para 243]
On issues 2, 3 and 8 the panel found no law or agreement creating the claimed entitlement and classified them as disputes over interests, which it will not decide where the claimant union lacks most-representative status under Article 277. On issue 11 it reached the same result procedurally: Article 129(2) of the Labour Law and Article 5 of Prakas No. 305 let workers authorise check-off, but the union had filed no written authorisation, so the claim failed while the employer's duty to deduct revives once the formalities are met. On issue 12, Article 27 requires discipline proportionate to the fault, and unpaid overtime imposed for minor errors breached that. [Para 143][Para 160][Para 248][Para 278][Para 280][Para 288]
The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification. [Para 306]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that is not certified as most representative may still bring rights-based claims founded on the Labour Law, but it cannot obtain an award on a dispute over interests; and an employer that applies its own partial-wage rule without labour-inspector authorisation owes full wages for the whole no-work period. [Para 148][Para 251][Para 134]

**Obiter dicta:** a non-binding award in favour of a union without most-representative status would impose terms on all workers in the plant and is therefore declined, and the panel may proceed in the absence of a party properly summoned who shows no valid reason. [Para 152][Para 116]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
OCR damage is severe. A conciliation date is printed as 26 August 2019 rather than 2009, and the year recurs as 2019 elsewhere in the procedural recital. The demand count is unstable — fourteen, then twelve, then eleven — and the operative section re-uses "issue 5" for what is issue 9 in the reasons. [Para 22][Para 34][Para 303]
The company name appears in at least four forms (ប្រូដឡែន ហ្គាមិន, ប្រូវេឡែន ហ្កាមេន, ប្រូដឡែន ហ្គាបេន, ប្រូឡែន ហ្គាមិ), and the union's name is variously rendered. Individual workers, team leaders and shop stewards survive only as corrupted strings, so they are identified by section and role; the employer's representatives are named in the award but are referred to here by function. The president's name differs between the header and the signature block. [Para 1][Para 307]
