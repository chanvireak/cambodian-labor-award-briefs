# Award No. 31/09 — Mary Juice (Arbitration Council, 25 March 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun, Liv Sovanna, Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 140367

## 1. Facts
Mary Juice is a juice manufacturing enterprise established in 2003 on National Road 2, Chak Angre Krom Commune, Meanchey District, Phnom Penh, employing 50 workers in production, warehouse and marketing sections. [Para 12] The only union at the enterprise is the Local Union of the Cambodian Union Federation (CUF), which brings the claim but holds just 19 of the 50 workers as members and does not hold most representative status. [Para 13][Para 43] In January 2009 the union demanded a seniority bonus and a living allowance for production and warehouse workers with four to seven years of service, arguing that heavy juice-lifting work should attract the benefits paid in the garment, textile and footwear industry; the employer refused, conciliation failed, and the two demands reached the panel by non-conciliation report No. 153 KB/AK/VK of 2 March 2009. [Para 14]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the workers at Mary Juice are entitled to a seniority bonus because they work in the same field as workers in the garment, textile and footwear manufacturing industry? [Para 13]
2. (Issue 2) Whether those workers are entitled to a living allowance on the same comparison? [Para 46]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — Local Union of CUF:** workers with four to seven years' service claim the seniority bonus and living allowance that Ministry notifications give to garment, textile and footwear workers, saying that lifting and carrying juice is hard work in the same field. [Para 14][Para 23]
**Employer (respondent) — Mary Juice:** no law or regulation requires a manufacturing enterprise to pay either benefit, and it already provides superior benefits, with production and warehouse workers on US$ 50 per month plus a 20,000 riel living allowance and 20,000 riel attendance bonus, while marketing staff earn commission; it also denies that the demands came from the workers rather than from the union. [Para 21][Para 17][Para 23]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel declined to consider both demands, the seniority bonus and the living allowance, for want of standing. [Para 53][Para 54]

On entitlement, Point 5 of Notification No. 017 SKBY of 18 July 2000 sets seniority rewards built on US$ 2 for the first year and rising by US$ 1 a year to US$ 5 per month after four years, but the notification applies only to the garment, textile and footwear industry; neither the Labour Law, a labour contract, internal rules nor a collective bargaining agreement gives manufacturing workers the bonus. [Para 31][Para 33][Para 37] Point 1 of Notification No. 032/08/SCN of 17 April 2008 likewise confines the US$ 6 per month living allowance to garment and footwear factories, and these parties had no collective agreement and no contractual allowance. [Para 47][Para 50] The panel therefore treated both unmet demands as interests disputes, beyond what the law provides. [Para 39][Para 52]

Because the demands were interests disputes, standing decided the case. Under Article 268 only a registered professional organisation enjoys the Law's rights and benefits, and under Clause 5(1) of Prakas No. 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001 a union established in compliance with Article 268 may represent its members' interests. [Para 26][Para 27] The Local Union of CUF was registered on 1 October 2007, so the panel confined its consideration to union members. [Para 27] But an award settling an interests dispute takes the place of a collective bargaining agreement for one year under Clause 43 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY, and only a union holding most representative status under Article 277 can conclude such an agreement for all workers (Article 96(2)(b); Clause 9(1) of Prakas No. 305); following Award 101/08-GDM, the panel declines interests disputes brought by unions without that status. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43] With 19 members among 50 workers the union does not hold it, and an award would strip the parties of their right to industrial action for a year. [Para 44] The panel accordingly declined to consider both demands. [Para 45]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that does not hold most representative status under Article 277 of the Labour Law has no standing to bring an interests dispute — a demand for a benefit the law does not require — before the Arbitration Council, because an award on such a demand would stand in place of a collective bargaining agreement the union has no capacity to conclude. [Para 40][Para 43]

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that seniority and living-allowance entitlements are fixed by Ministry notification limited to garment, textile and footwear manufacturing rather than by the heaviness of the work performed. [Para 35][Para 36]
