# Award No. 120/09 — Rylight Apparel Souvenir Industrial Co., Ltd. and the Khmer Youth Union Federation / Khmer National Union (Arbitration Council, 7 October 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of three: Ly Tayseng (employer-appointed), Ven Vev (worker-appointed), Kong Phalla (president) · Doc ID 140433

## 1. Facts
Rylight Apparel Souvenir Industrial (ក្រុមហ៊ុន រីឡាយ អេប៊ល សូស អ៉ិន ដាស្ទ្រៀល), a garment factory at Kbal Damrey, Khan Dangkao, Phnom Penh employing about 1,200 workers, faced a collective dispute brought by the Khmer Youth Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ ហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ) together with the Khmer National Union of the factory over twelve demands to improve working conditions. [Para 71][Para 1]
Ministry conciliation settled two of the twelve points, leaving ten unresolved, which the Arbitration Council heard on 10 September 2009; during the hearing the parties settled three further points (issues 4, 6 and 9), and the panel decided the remaining seven. [Para 28][Para 33][Para 34]
Two demands were referred to but never reached: additional space for a dining hall and separate daycare and breastfeeding rooms, both of which the employer said were already provided or physically impossible. [Para 8][Para 18]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must allow pregnant workers one full day per month for antenatal health checks while preserving wages and the regular attendance bonus? [Para 78]
2. (Issue 2) Whether workers doing overtime on public holidays and Sundays are entitled to a 2,000 riel meal allowance per day? [Para 144]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must raise the base wage to USD 55 per month for workers able to operate three or more machines? [Para 159]
4. (Issue 5) Whether the company must open the front gate at 06:10 instead of 06:30 so workers can enter the compound? [Para 168]
5. (Issue 7) Whether the regular attendance bonus must be increased from USD 5 to USD 10 per month? [Para 182]
6. (Issue 8) Whether the company must stop assigning workers to clean along the production line? [Para 193]
7. (Issue 10) Whether the company may require workers to take leave, or deduct wages where no annual leave remains, when it conducts its annual stock count? [Para 210]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), through the Khmer Youth Union Federation and the Khmer National Union:** the half day the company allows for antenatal checks is insufficient because the hospital is far and queues are long; the meal allowance for holiday and Sunday overtime should be 2,000 riel because 1,000 riel does not cover a meal; skilled sewers who can run several machines deserve a USD 55 base wage; the gate should open at 06:10 so workers can eat before the shift; and the company should not deduct wages or force annual leave to be used during stocktaking. [Para 79][Para 81][Para 85][Para 91][Para 111]
**Employer (respondent), Rylight Apparel Souvenir Industrial:** it already grants half a day per month for antenatal checks on full pay and bonus, pays 1,000 riel or a free meal for overtime as Notice No. 017 requires, pays the legal minimum to skilled workers, opens the gate at 06:30 by agreement with the employee delegates and earlier on rainy days, and deducts only where a worker has no annual leave left. [Para 78][Para 82][Para 86][Para 93][Para 109]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — every demand was refused except issue 10, on which the panel ordered the employer to stop requiring workers to take leave, or deducting wages from those with no annual leave remaining, during the annual stock count; the order was declared non-binding and to become permanent eight days after notification absent objection. [Para 230][Para 236][Para 238]
On standing, the panel held that the Khmer Youth Union Federation had no registration certificate, so under Article 268 of the 1997 Labour Law it enjoyed none of the rights the law confers, including the right to represent members before the Council; the Khmer National Union was registered but was not the most representative union in the enterprise, so the panel examined the claims only for the 146 workers who were its members. [Para 121][Para 125][Para 129]
On issue 1 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law requiring a full day for antenatal checks, so the demand exceeded what the law guarantees and was an interests dispute; because the claiming union was not most representative, the panel declined to consider it. [Para 132][Para 142][Para 143]
Issue 2 was decided the same way: Notice No. 017 of 18 July 2000 entitles a worker doing overtime to 1,000 riel or a free meal, which the company paid, and the further 1,000 riel claimed was an interest beyond the legal minimum, which the panel refused to consider for want of a most representative claimant. [Para 147][Para 153][Para 157]
Issue 3 followed: Notice No. 745 of 23 October 2006 guarantees a USD 50 minimum wage and says nothing about skilled workers, no agreement provided USD 55, and the demand was again an interests claim that the panel declined. [Para 160][Para 162][Para 167]
On issue 5 the panel applied the employer's right under Article 2 of the Labour Law to manage the enterprise, found the 06:30 opening lawful and reasonable, noted the agreement with the nine employee delegates and the rain exception, and found no loss shown by the workers. [Para 175][Para 178][Para 181]
On issue 7 the panel read Notice No. 017 as setting a floor of USD 5, which the company paid, and found no law or agreement supporting USD 10; on issue 8 it found the fifteen minutes of cleaning fell inside working hours, rotated equally, and did not touch hours or pay, and that the agreement with the delegates was valid and unproven to be improper. [Para 186][Para 188][Para 203][Para 208]
On issue 10 the panel applied Article 170 of the Labour Law: annual leave is to be taken at the Khmer New Year unless the parties agree otherwise, and the employer must notify the labour inspector in advance; use of leave at stocktaking therefore required the workers' consent, which was absent. [Para 213][Para 218][Para 222]
The claim for repayment of past deductions was refused because the workers produced no list of affected workers, no amounts, and no dates of the stock counts. [Para 225][Para 229]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that is neither registered under Article 268 nor the most representative union in the enterprise cannot bring an interests dispute before the panel on behalf of the workforce, so demands that go beyond what the Labour Law or a prakas guarantees will be declined unless the claimant holds most-representative status; conversely, an employer may not substitute annual leave or a wage deduction for consent when it closes production for stocktaking. [Para 129][Para 155][Para 222]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that issuing an award on an interests demand would create a collective agreement binding all workers and would strip non-members of the right to strike over the same interest in future, which it treated as unjust. [Para 140]
