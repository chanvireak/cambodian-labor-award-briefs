# Award No. 10/09-New Wide — New Wide (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 19 February 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Kao Thach, Ann Vireak, Kong Phallack (Chair) · Doc ID 140354

## 1. Facts
New Wide (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd., a Phnom Penh garment factory, employs around 1400 workers [Para 25]. The claimant is the Local Union of C.CAWDU, which was not registered; 108 workers gave thumbprints in support [Para 26] [Para 27]. A complaint was lodged on 3 December 2008 and conciliation failed, leaving eight non-conciliated issues referred to the Secretariat on 28 January 2009 [Para 16] [Para 17]. At the hearing on 3 February 2009, issue 7 was resolved and issues 4, 5 and 6 were withdrawn; the panel therefore considered issues 1 and 2, combined at the workers' request, together with issue 8 [Para 20] [Para 21].

Issues 1 and 2 concerned E.L., a warehouse supervisor and Chinese translator on six-month fixed-duration contracts [Para 29] [Para 30]. His first contract ran from 29 August 2007 to 31 May 2008 and his renewed contract from 1 June 2008 to 30 November 2008; the employer notified non-renewal on 24 November 2008, which he signed on 29 November 2008 [Para 31]. He acknowledged at the hearing that he had not received final payment or severance pay equal to 5% of his wages for the second contract [Para 32].

Issue 8 concerned O.S., a K2 group leader earning US$ 90 per month, who sought US$ 100 per month, the senior group leader rate, under collective-bargaining minutes dated 18 November 2008 [Para 69] [Para 70] [Para 73].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must reinstate E.L. and reimburse his wages from termination until reinstatement [Para 3]?
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must refrain from discriminating against the union and restricting workers' freedom to join a union [Para 4]?
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay 1000 riel per hour for food and make overtime voluntary [Para 5]?
4. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must provide a truck for workers living far away when working overtime until 8:30 p.m. [Para 6]?
5. (Issue 5) Whether the employer must deduct the US$ 5 attendance bonus in proportion to days absent [Para 7]?
6. (Issue 6) Whether the employer must grant leaves of absence more easily based on actual facts [Para 8]?
7. (Issue 7) Whether the employer must provide two separate attendance-punching sites or a machine at each factory [Para 9]?
8. (Issue 8) Whether the employer must increase O.S.'s wage to US$ 100 per month [Para 10]?

## 3. Positions of the parties
The workers sought renewal of E.L.'s contract and wage reimbursement on the ground of union discrimination [Para 3] [Para 34]. They said E.L. had led a registered local union, resigned on 5 November 2008, and been elected president of the Local Union of C.CAWDU the same day, and that the employer refused the election notification [Para 36] [Para 37] [Para 40]. They alleged the administration manager threatened termination, renewed other workers' contracts including a replacement in E.L.'s post, and terminated the union vice-president [Para 47] [Para 50] [Para 52]. They said the alleged threat to a foreman was joking [Para 59].

The employer denied union discrimination [Para 35]. It said E.L. violated the internal rules and an agreement dated 13 August 2008 to transfer to the sample room, and that his contract had expired [Para 54] [Para 84]. It said it had paid his salary under that agreement and the workers did not oppose that claim [Para 55] [Para 56]. It relied on worker rejection letters and the Ministry's refusal to register the new union [Para 63] [Para 95].

On issue 8, the workers relied on the minutes' term of automatic promotion if no foreman supervised K2 by 1 December 2008 [Para 72] [Para 137]. The employer accepted the minutes but said a foreman was assigned on 21 November 2008 and that O.S. did not cooperate with him [Para 80] [Para 138].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted: the panel rejected the demands to renew E.L.'s contract and to increase O.S.'s wage to US$ 100 per month, but ordered the employer to pay E.L. severance pay equal to 5% of his wage for the second contract [Para 143] [Para 144] [Para 130].

On discrimination, the panel cited Article 12 of the Labour Law, which prohibits considering union membership or activity in hiring, discipline or termination decisions [Para 88]. It found the employer had renewed E.L.'s contract while he was a union president, but the workers had insufficient evidence that the second non-renewal was based on union leadership or founding a new union [Para 91] [Para 93]. The employer's stated reason was E.L.'s refusal to transfer to the sample room, which the workers did not refute, so non-renewal was not union discrimination [Para 96] [Para 98] [Para 99].

On notice, the panel held that a fixed-duration contract normally terminates at its specified ending date and neither party can compel renewal without agreement [Para 102] [Para 107]. Article 293 and Prakas No. 305 SKBY dated 22 November 2001 protect union leaders and candidates against dismissal, not contract expiry, which is distinct from dismissal [Para 108] [Para 109] [Para 120]. The 24 November 2008 notice of expiry and non-renewal was therefore lawful and proper [Para 125] [Para 127]. No reinstatement was due, but the unpaid 5% severance for the second contract was ordered [Para 128] [Para 129] [Para 130].

On issue 8, the panel read the minutes as requiring both cooperation with the new foreman and no foreman in the post by 1 December 2008 [Para 136]. It found a foreman was recruited on 21 November 2008 and that O.S. did not cooperate with him; the workers' response did not directly oppose that fact [Para 139] [Para 141]. The conditions were unmet and the wage claim was rejected [Para 142].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-duration contract ends on its stated date, and absent agreement or proven discrimination no party can compel renewal; protection for union leaders against dismissal does not convert lawful expiry into dismissal [Para 102] [Para 107] [Para 120]. A claimant alleging discriminatory non-renewal must prove union status or activity caused the decision; unrebutted lawful reasons defeat the claim [Para 88] [Para 93] [Para 98]. Where collective-bargaining minutes make promotion conditional on both cooperation and the absence of a foreman by a deadline, failure of either condition defeats automatic promotion [Para 136] [Para 141]. Severance pay equal to 5% of wage remains due when a fixed-term contract ends without renewal [Para 130].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel referred to earlier awards ordering reinstatement for union discrimination under Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law, but distinguished them on the evidence [Para 89]. Nothing here is binding precedent.

The English award omits the evidence section and directs readers to the Khmer version [Para 22] [Para 23]. It is an unofficial translation of the authoritative Khmer original [Para 2]. Chinese-script foreman interview records were declined as evidence [Para 79].
