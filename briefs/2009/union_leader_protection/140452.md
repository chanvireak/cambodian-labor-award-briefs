# Award No. 148/09 — FOP S Footwear (Cambodia) Co Ltd and the C.CAWDU union at the factory (Arbitration Council, 18 November 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (chair Mr Pen Bun Chhea; employer-party nominee Mr Chiv Phirom; worker-party nominee Mr An Non) · Doc ID 140452

## 1. Facts
FOP S Footwear (Cambodia) Co Ltd — printed in Khmer as ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្វូភី ស ហ្វូត វៀ (ខេមបូឌៀ) ខូ អិលធីឌី — makes shoe uppers and soles at Toul Sangke, Khan Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, with about 598 workers. [Para 1][Para 55] The claimant is the factory union of the C.CAWDU federation, សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ការងារ កម្មករ កម្ពុជា, registered on 19 June 2009. [Para 1][Para 43][Para 56] On 17 October 2009 workers struck after the company moved a sewing-line block chief to the warehouse; on 19 October the company suspended the union vice-president for serious misconduct under Article 83, and dismissed the block chief and a Chinese-language interpreter. [Para 168][Para 115][Para 131][Para 162] Conciliation on 20 October 2009 left all seven demands unresolved, and the report issued on 21 October 2009. [Para 19][Para 20] At the hearing the union added three demands absent from that report: removal of three named company staff, reinstatement of the interpreter, and prosecution over goods taken out over the fence during the strike. [Para 147][Para 148][Para 149]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must take back the block chief, the union vice-president and the interpreter at their original or equivalent positions? [Para 168]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay severance of 5 per cent to the workers, or whether that demand is already spent? [Para 133][Para 228]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the panel has power to order the company to comply with its earlier Award No. 124/09? [Para 139][Para 238]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — the factory union of សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ការងារ កម្មករ កម្ពុជា, C.CAWDU):** the three employees were dismissed without any prior warning or fault; the block chief's transfer was the trigger of the strike and so could not itself be serious misconduct; the interpreter was pushed into the crowd and never urged anyone to stop work. [Para 169][Para 128][Para 132] The 5 per cent severance is owed under the Labour Law, and the company should be made to honour Award No. 124/09. [Para 4][Para 5][Para 139]
**Employer (respondent — FOP S Footwear (Cambodia) Co Ltd):** the block chief refused a lawful transfer that preserved her post, pay and benefits; the interpreter incited the strike and led his section out on 17 October 2009; and the vice-president was suspended for serious misconduct and cannot return until the labour inspector rules. [Para 176][Para 177][Para 130][Para 202]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must take back the block chief at her original workplace or a suitable equivalent and must take back the union vice-president and the interpreter; the panel declined to reconsider the 5 per cent severance and declined to consider enforcing Award No. 124/09. [Para 245][Para 246][Para 247][Para 248][Para 249]

On jurisdiction over the three demands added at the hearing, the panel applied Article 312(1) of the Labour Law and Article 33 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, which confine it to the points in the non-conciliation record and to matters arising after it as direct consequences of that dispute. [Para 150][Para 151] It refused the demand to remove three named staff and the demand over stolen goods, but held it could hear the interpreter's dismissal because the dismissal letter, though dated 19 October, was handed over on 20 October, after conciliation had closed. [Para 154][Para 157][Para 160][Para 163][Para 167]

On the block chief, the panel found an indeterminate-duration contract and applied Article 74, which allows termination only with written notice and a reason going to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the needs of the enterprise. [Para 170][Para 171][Para 173] The transfer kept her title, wage and benefits but removed skills built over years, came with no training, and followed immediately on her argument with the section supervisor and the owner; the panel held it had no reasonable basis and that her refusal was not serious misconduct. [Para 180][Para 182][Para 183][Para 184][Para 185][Para 186] It added that the real reason for dismissal was her participation in the strike, which Articles 332 and 333 forbid as a ground of sanction. [Para 187][Para 193]

On the union vice-president, the panel held that Article 295 does not dispense with the Article 293 requirement that the employer seek the labour inspector's approval before suspending a staff delegate or union leader, and the company had never applied for approval to dismiss him. [Para 203][Para 209][Para 211][Para 212][Para 217] It therefore ordered his reinstatement. [Para 218]

On the interpreter, Article 83(5) treats incitement of other workers to serious misconduct as serious misconduct, but the panel followed its ruling in Case No. 20/05 that calling a strike is not serious misconduct unless the employer proves the worker incited violence. [Para 221][Para 222] The company relied on camera footage and on his walking out of his section, which the panel found proved no specific act of incitement; at most there was a defect in the form of the strike. [Para 223][Para 225][Para 226][Para 227]

On severance, the same parties had already had the same 5 per cent demand refused in Case No. 124/09 on 2 October 2009, so the panel applied res judicata and refused to decide it again. [Para 138][Para 229][Para 234][Para 235] On enforcement, Article 40 of Prakas No. 099 makes an award unenforceable once a party objects within eight calendar days, and the panel followed earlier rulings that it cannot compel compliance with its own award; it therefore declined the demand. [Para 238][Para 241][Para 242][Para 243]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand first raised at the hearing is within the panel's jurisdiction only if it is in the non-conciliation record or arises after it as a direct consequence of that dispute; a demand that the same parties already litigated and lost is barred by res judicata. A transfer that strips a long-serving worker of her acquired skill, without training and immediately after a dispute with management, lacks the reasonable basis Article 74 requires, and dismissal for taking part in a strike is unlawful under Articles 332 and 333 however irregular the strike. [Para 165][Para 185][Para 191][Para 234]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the strike's procedural defects may have cost the workers some statutory benefits during the strike, but that this did not justify dismissal. [Para 226]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company name is printed in several forms (ហ្វូភី ស ហ្វូត វៀ, ហ្វូកើស ហ្វូត វៀ, ហ្វូកឺស ហ្វូត វៀ), and the interpreter's name appears with three different initial consonants. [Para 1][Para 137][Para 148][Para 162] One company representative's name is printed once as សៀវ ទៀង and once as សៀវភៅ ទៀង, and a production manager's name is given in one place with a different spelling. [Para 147][Para 153] Prakas No. 099 is dated three incompatible ways in the same reasoning: 21 April 2004, "21 December April 2004" and "2019 2004". [Para 151][Para 154][Para 238] The non-conciliation report appears as No. 991 in the procedural section but as 424/09 in the evidence list. [Para 13][Para 33] Demands 4 to 7 in the conciliation list are never addressed in the reasons or the operative order, which covers only Issues 1 to 3. [Para 6][Para 9][Para 245]
