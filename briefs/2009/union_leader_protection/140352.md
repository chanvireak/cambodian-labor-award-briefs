# Award No. 06/09 — Sang Woo (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន សាង វ៉ូ (ខេមបូឌា) ខូ, អិលធីឌី) (Arbitration Council, 11 February 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — មាំរ សំបូរ ណា (employer-appointed), អន ណន (worker-appointed), សុខ មាំ ធឿង (president) · Doc ID 140352

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន សាង វ៉ូ (ខេមបូឌា) ខូ, អិលធីឌី is a garment factory at National Road 4, Samrong Tong district, Kampong Speu, employing about 2,520 workers and hosting two unions, of which the complainant — សហព័ន្ធសហជីពជាតិនៃឧស្សាហកម្មវាយនភ័ណ្ឌកាត់ដេរកម្ពុជា (NIFTUC) — claimed roughly 1,000 members but not majority status. [Para 34][Para 37] The union twice delivered lists of members consenting to a 1,000 riel monthly dues deduction, finally 1,169 thumbprints, which reached the employer through the provincial labour inspectorate on 17 December 2008. [Para 38][Para 43] The employer then summoned listed workers one by one and found only 31 confirming consent, 440 refusing, 97 already resigned and 10 duplicated card numbers. [Para 44][Para 58]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer is obliged to deduct 1,000 riel per month from the wages of union members and pay it over to the union under Article 129 of the Labour Law? [Para 38][Para 60]
2. (Issue 2) Whether summoning workers one by one to confirm their thumbprints, without a union representative present, amounts to interference in union affairs, and whether representation must be allowed? [Para 49][Para 100]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), the union សហព័ន្ធសហជីពជាតិនៃឧស្សាហកម្មវាយនភ័ណ្ឌកាត់ដេរកម្ពុជា (NIFTUC) and its members:** the members authorised the deduction in writing and by thumbprint, the employer had a statutory duty to implement it once the lists were delivered, and its refusal, followed by private interrogation of individual members — asking why they joined the union, whether the union or the job mattered more — frightened workers and interfered with union activity. [Para 38][Para 50][Para 53]

**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន សាង វ៉ូ (ខេមបូឌា) ខូ, អិលធីឌី:** it accepted the principle of the deduction but was entitled to verify each thumbprint because many workers had said they had only pressed a print to copy a colleague or to enrol in the union, not to authorise a wage deduction; it denied receiving the first list, denied asking about membership motives, and insisted that checking records was its management prerogative. [Para 39][Para 55][Para 56]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to deduct and remit dues for the 31 workers whose consent it had verified, immediately on the award taking effect, and to re-verify the disputed lists within 15 days; no damages were awarded. [Para 111][Para 112]

On the deduction, the panel set Article 129 of the Labour Law — which allows a worker to authorise a wage deduction for union dues, in writing and revocably at any time — alongside Article 281, which forbids the employer to deduct wages to fund the union or to pay dues on the worker's behalf. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 64] Read together the two provisions only appear contradictory; the panel held Article 281 protects workers from unilateral employer action and does not bar a deduction the worker has individually authorised, following Awards 05/03, 62/04, 94/04, 99/06 and 16/05. [Para 65][Para 67][Para 68] It also applied clause 5 of Prakas 305 of 2001, which requires at least 15 days between the written request and the payroll deduction. [Para 62]

On the contested lists, the panel held the request documents carried enough information to identify consenting members, so the employer owed a duty to deduct for every clear case, except workers who had resigned. [Para 79] But it accepted that where names, group codes or thumbprints were ambiguous the employer could summon the workers concerned, provided each worker might bring a shop-steward or union representative and the exercise stayed inside the 15-day window. [Para 102][Para 105] Because the 440 refusals had been recorded without any union representative present, those files had to be redone. [Para 108][Para 109]

On interference, the panel grounded the employer's right to manage on Article 2 of the Labour Law but held that the right must be exercised lawfully and reasonably. [Para 87][Para 96] It found no sufficient evidence that the interrogations happened as alleged, yet it ruled that questions touching why a worker joined a union — whether the union or the job mattered more, whether the company had done something wrong — would be improper conduct if asked, since Article 271 guarantees free choice of union membership. [Para 98][Para 100][Para 101]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union dues deduction under Article 129 requires an individual, written and revocable authorisation from each member; once that authorisation is delivered, the employer must deduct and remit it, and Article 281 is not a defence. Where the authorising documents are ambiguous, the employer may verify them worker by worker, but must let the worker bring a shop-steward or union representative, must complete the check within 15 days, and may not question the worker's motive for joining the union. [Para 68][Para 102][Para 100]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that worker authorisations may be withdrawn at any time by direct written notice to the employer, a point that did not affect the orders made. [Para 77]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer-appointed arbitrator's name is printed in two different forms, in the panel composition and again in the signature block, so one form is an OCR error. [Para 115] Paragraph 59 garbles the cross-reference to the two demands, printing a third "issue 222" that does not exist. [Para 59] The verification tally in paragraph 44 is arithmetically incomplete — 569 workers examined yielding 440 refusals, 31 consents, 97 resignations and 10 duplicate cards — and paragraph 106 restates the same categories with the counts detached from their labels. [Para 44][Para 106] The number of remaining unverified workers is given as 600 in the reasons and in the order without reconciling it against the 1,169 total. [Para 106][Para 112] Several quoted statutory provisions, including the text of Article 129, are partly destroyed, and the union's Khmer name is spelled inconsistently across the file. [Para 60][Para 1]
