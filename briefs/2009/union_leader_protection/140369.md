# Award No. 30/09 — Island Glory Industrial Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 27 March 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phyrum, An Nan, Ang Eng Thong) · Doc ID 140369

## 1. Facts
Island Glory Industrial Co., Ltd. is a Phnom Penh manufacturer at Tuol Sangke Village, Tuol Sangke Commune, Russei Keo District. [Para 2] The claimant is the Textile & Garment Workers' Federation of Cambodia (TGaFe), acting for Mr S, a mechanic paid US$ 85 per month who served a two-month probation from 19 August to 19 October 2008 and then a three-month fixed-duration contract running to 19 January 2009. [Para 17] A local union was founded at the factory in November 2008 with Mr S as secretary, and he recruited members during working hours; the employer was notified through a security guard on 8 December 2008, though the Ministry rejected the registration that day for lack of evidence of the election and accepted resubmitted documents on 13 January 2009. [Para 19][Para 22][Para 26] On 9 January 2009 the employer gave him notice that his contract would not be renewed; it expired on 19 January 2009 and he received US$ 107.84 in termination indemnity. [Para 24][Para 27]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the non-renewal of Mr S's fixed-duration contract was an act of union discrimination, so that the employer must reinstate him? [Para 33]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — Textile & Garment Workers' Federation of Cambodia (TGaFe):** the dismissal was union discrimination, shown by continuing recruitment advertisements after his contract ended, an informal meeting at which the union was asked whether it could change its leader, his success in protecting workers in four cases as a leader of under three months, rumours that he disrespected the foreman, and the employer's different treatment of the three unions — other unions were invited to meetings, TGaFe was not. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 29]
**Employer (respondent) — Island Glory Industrial Co., Ltd.:** there was no discrimination; his fixed-duration contract simply expired and was not renewed because the company must cut staff in the economic crisis, and he has already been paid termination benefits. [Para 3][Para 5][Para 30]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to reinstate Mr S. [Para 51]

Article 73 provides that a fixed-duration contract normally terminates at its stated end date, so the employment relationship ended on 19 January 2009 as a matter of contract. [Para 34][Para 35] That did not end the inquiry, because Articles 12 and 279 forbid an employer from taking union membership or union activity into account in hiring, discipline or termination, and in Award 10/03-Jacqsintex the panel held that a decision not to renew a fixed-duration contract falls within the same prohibition as a dismissal. [Para 38][Para 39] Following earlier awards, the burden of proving discrimination rests on the workers, and the panel tests it against the answers given at the hearing and the documentary evidence. [Para 40]

On that evidence the redundancy explanation failed. A fellow mechanic on probation had his contract renewed on 17 January 2009, two days before Mr S's contract expired and eight days after he was told it would not be renewed, and Mr S was the only mechanic not renewed. [Para 40][Para 41] The employer's recruitment advertisement stayed up until March 2009 and its explanation that a security guard failed to remove it was unconvincing, since the non-renewal fell in January and the employer admitted recruiting in other sections but offered nothing to show mechanics were not needed. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44] Its redundancy evidence was a document titled "list of workers resigning", a label too ambiguous to prove that his post had been abolished. [Para 45][Para 46] The employer knew of his union role from November 2008, when he recruited during working hours, whether or not the election notice reached it, and it gave no sufficient reason to exclude discrimination. [Para 47][Para 48] The panel therefore found the non-renewal was due to his union activities and ordered reinstatement, leaving the contract type to the parties' further discussion as they had agreed at the hearing. [Para 49][Para 50]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that declines to renew a fixed-duration contract bears the same prohibition as one that dismisses: where the worker's union activity is known and the stated economic reason is contradicted by continued recruitment and by the renewal of comparable contracts, the non-renewal is union discrimination and reinstatement follows, notwithstanding Article 73. [Para 39][Para 47]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that the union's notification of its leadership was never proven to have reached the employer, which did not affect the outcome because the employer knew of the activity in fact. [Para 22][Para 47]
