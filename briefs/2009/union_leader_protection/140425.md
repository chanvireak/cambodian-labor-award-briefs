# Award No. 112/09 — Yong Va Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd / ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉ុង វ៉ា អ៉ិន ធរ ស្ទ្រៀល (ខេមបូឌា) ខូ អិលធីឌី v Worker-Employee Union of Yong Va 2 (Arbitration Council, 23 September 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators · Doc ID 140425

## 1. Facts
The dispute is a collective one at the Yong Va 2 factory of Yong Va Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, which employed about 5,000 workers and hosted four unions; the claimant was the Worker-Employee Union (សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត) of that factory, organised through the Cambodia Workers' Union Federation [Para 34][Para 35][Para 36]. Conciliation at the Kandal provincial labour office settled 7 of 11 demands; the 4 unsettled demands reached the panel, and the union withdrew issue 3 at the hearing, leaving issues 1, 2 and 4 [Para 14][Para 15][Para 18][Para 19]. The hearing was held on 7 September 2009 [Para 13]. The panel sat as Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), the arbitrator appointed by the workers (name partly Latinised in the OCR) and Pen Bun Chhea as chair [Para 153].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must allow the claimant union's leaders to enter and leave through the front Gate 1, as the leaders of the other three unions do? [Para 40]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must repair the broken men's and women's toilets in Building 2 and guarantee sufficient water, working doors and soap? [Para 58][Para 106]
3. (Issue 4) Whether a worker holding a valid medical certificate may have sick leave requested on his or her behalf, instead of appearing in person at the front security post on the first certified day of illness? [Para 73][Para 123]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that letting other union leaders through Gate 1 while stopping the claimant's leaders is unequal treatment, amounting to discrimination; that the 10 July 2009 internal notice was issued without any agreement from the workers or their union; and that requiring a sick worker to come in person on the first certified day is unreasonable because such a worker needs rest [Para 56][Para 86][Para 87].
**Employer (respondent):** that the five other-union leaders who passed through Gate 1 did so by long custom and not by any right granted to them or their unions; that the new sick-leave procedure was adopted because absenteeism was disrupting the production line; and that it has a standing toilet-repair policy though it cannot promise a repair date [Para 51][Para 108][Para 124].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the Issue 1 demand was dismissed; the company was ordered to repair the Building 2 toilets and to guarantee sufficient water, doors and soap within 15 days of the award taking effect; and the employer was ordered to apply its internal rules on sick leave for genuinely ill workers holding valid medical certificates [Para 149][Para 150][Para 151].

On Issue 1 the panel worked from Article 23 of Decree-Law 38, under which a past practice may be used to interpret an unclear term, and from Award 95/09, which defines past practice as consistent, clear and stable conduct recognised and accepted by both sides [Para 93][Para 96][Para 98]. On the evidence the permission was personal, not corporate: named staff of the other unions and one former leader of the claimant union had passed through Gate 1, and the claimant itself conceded that those allowed through were named individuals [Para 101][Para 102]. Because the new leaders had never used the gate and the practice attached to persons rather than to union office, the refusal was not discrimination [Para 104][Para 105].

On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 229 of the Labour Law, requiring clean premises and sanitary working conditions, and Prakas No. 052 of 10 February 2000, whose Articles 1, 3 and 6 require usable toilets with water and soap, closing doors and daily cleaning [Para 110][Para 111][Para 113][Para 114]. The company did not contest that 3 men's and 4 to 5 women's toilets in Building 2 were broken, doorless or without water, or that women queued and some used the men's block [Para 115]. A standing repair policy that leaves defects unrepaired is not compliance with the Prakas [Para 119].

On Issue 4 the panel held that an employer may set leave procedures, including to protect its production line, but that any such procedure must be lawful and reasonable [Para 129][Para 130][Para 143]. The internal rules grant 12 paid sick days a year on production of a proper medical certificate and are silent on how the request is made [Para 140][Para 141]. Requiring a genuinely ill worker to attend the front security post in person on the first certified day is unreasonable, and the anti-fraud purpose is served by the certificate, not by who lodges it [Para 144][Para 147]. The employer was therefore ordered to apply its internal rules to genuinely ill workers with valid certificates [Para 148].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A past practice is binding only where the conduct is consistent, clear and accepted by both parties; a facility habitually extended to named individuals does not become a right of the union they belong to, so refusing the same facility to that union's other leaders is not discrimination [Para 98][Para 104]. The employer's right to regulate sick-leave requests is limited by legality and reasonableness, and a rule compelling a sick worker to attend in person on the first certified day fails that limit where a valid certificate already answers the employer's fraud concern [Para 144].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that an internal notice may clarify how an existing entitlement is obtained, and accepted that the company's concern about forged leave requests was legitimate in principle [Para 142][Para 146].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The header line survives only in fragments, with stray digits and symbols among the case number and date. The claimant federation's name is garbled in the party block, and the worker-appointed arbitrator's name is partly Latinised so the Khmer spelling cannot be confirmed. Individual names of union officials and of a former union leader are difficult to recover: the former leader's name appears in three different spellings across the file, and it is described here rather than reproduced. Prakas No. 052 is dated 10 February 2000 in three places but is printed as "10 February 2023 2000" once [Para 112]. The operative section states the Issue 2 order twice in succession [Para 150]. No material contradiction in the outcome was found.
