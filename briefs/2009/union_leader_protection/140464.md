# Award No. 168/09 — Tuek Thla Plaza III (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ទឹក ថ្លា ផ្លាហ្សា III) (Arbitration Council, 28 December 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — In Sothy (employer-appointed), On Non (worker-appointed), Kong Polla (president) · Doc ID 140464

## 1. Facts
Tuek Thla Plaza III (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ទឹក ថ្លា ផ្លាហ្សា III) runs a construction site, TP, at Tuek Thla, Sen Sok, Phnom Penh, with about 320 workers. [Para 42] The complainant, the National Cambodian Construction and Forestry Workers' Union (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ជាតិ កម្ពុជា កម្មករ សំណង់ និង ព្រៃឈើ), had about 180 members and was certified lawful by ministerial letter No. 144 of 30 October 2009. [Para 43][Para 21] A union election was held on 3 October 2009 with about 70 workers voting and eight candidates; the union notified the company, which signed receipt of the notice on 7 October 2009, and the ministry recognised the leadership on 30 October 2009. [Para 44][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50] The company then dismissed the union president on 24 October, the vice-president on 28 October and an activist who had stood as a candidate on 15 November 2009, in each case without seeking the labour inspector's authorisation. [Para 52][Para 61][Para 65][Para 69][Para 74] None of the three had a written contract; they were paid USD 7.5 or 4.5 a day, worked a full month without weekly or holiday rest, and were paid USD 1 an hour for overtime. [Para 53][Para 63][Para 70][Para 55] The conciliation of eleven demands left three unsettled. [Para 15][Para 16] The employer's lawyer attended the first hearing without a power of attorney and did not appear at the second, so the panel proceeded in the employer's absence. [Para 17][Para 19][Para 93][Para 94]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must take back the union president, the union vice-president and a union activist who had stood as an election candidate, and pay them wages from the date of dismissal? [Para 95]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must fix ordinary working time at eight hours a day while keeping wages and benefits unchanged? [Para 77][Para 127]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay double wages for work done on public holidays and Sundays? [Para 85][Para 139]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — National Cambodian Construction and Forestry Workers' Union (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ជាតិ កម្ពុជា កម្មករ សំណង់ និង ព្រៃឈើ):** the three were dismissed for forming and leading the union, one manager telling workers the company did not want a union and that they must ask permission to create one; work ran nine hours a day as the ordinary day, with overtime reaching to 9 p.m. or through the night, and Sunday and holiday work was paid at the ordinary daily rate, so double pay was owed. [Para 56][Para 64][Para 72][Para 78][Para 81][Para 86]
**Employer (respondent) — Tuek Thla Plaza III (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ទឹក ថ្លា ផ្លាហ្សា III):** it refused to take the three back and denied the claims; on the eight-hour demand it said it would consider wages and benefits if workers so wished, but it could not pay double for holiday work. It then withdrew from the proceedings for want of a valid power of attorney, so several claims went unanswered. [Para 3][Para 5][Para 7][Para 76][Para 88]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to reinstate all three with back pay from dismissal until return; to set ordinary working time at no more than eight hours a day; and to pay 200% of the ordinary daily wage for holiday and weekly-rest-day work; the demand to preserve other benefits was refused. [Para 148][Para 149][Para 150][Para 151][Para 152]
The panel first held it could hear the case in the employer's absence under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099, the employer having been properly summoned and given no valid excuse. [Para 90][Para 91][Para 93][Para 94]
On Issue 1, for the president and vice-president the panel applied Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 and Article 293(1) of the Labour Law: three union leaders enjoy protection, and dismissal requires the labour inspector's prior agreement, which was never sought although the ministry had recognised them and the company had been notified. [Para 97][Para 98][Para 100][Para 101][Para 102] For the activist, the panel applied the candidate protection in Article 3(3) of the same Prakas with its 45-day window, running here from 4 October to 17 November 2009, and found all three cumulative conditions met — he fell within the Prakas, the dismissal of 15 November fell inside the window, and the notice naming him reached the company on 7 October. [Para 106][Para 110][Para 113][Para 114][Para 118][Para 119][Para 123]
On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 137 of the Labour Law, which caps ordinary working time at eight hours a day and 48 a week, and found the company's nine-hour day unlawful; it refused to order preservation of benefits, however, because no evidence showed any benefit had been cut. [Para 130][Para 133][Para 134][Para 136][Para 138]
On Issue 3 the panel applied Article 139 as amended, Article 164 and Article 4 of Prakas No. 100 of 4 February 1999, and followed Award No. 101/08 in holding that holiday and weekly-rest work earns 200% of the ordinary wage in total; the company had paid only the ordinary rate. [Para 141][Para 143][Para 145][Para 147]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union leader or an election candidate dismissed without the labour inspector's prior agreement, or outside the 45-day window that runs from the poll, is dismissed unlawfully and must be reinstated with wages from the date of dismissal, provided the union had notified the employer of the names by any practicable means. [Para 98][Para 102][Para 119] Ordinary working time may not exceed eight hours a day or 48 a week, and work on public holidays or the weekly rest day is paid at 200% of the ordinary wage in total. [Para 130][Para 145][Para 147]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that a party which abandons its defence by failing to appear leaves the panel with only the claimant's account, and that it will nevertheless decide on that material. [Para 128]
