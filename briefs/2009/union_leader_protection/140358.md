# Award No. 13/09 — Chun Tich Thai Co., Ltd (Mithuna) (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ជួន តិច ថា យល៍ ខូ អិលធីឌី (មិថុនា)) (Arbitration Council, 23 February 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Chiv Phirom (employer-appointed), An Vireak (worker-appointed), Ing Phalla (chair) · Doc ID 140358

## 1. Facts
Chun Tich Thai Co., Ltd (Mithuna), a garment factory in Russey Keo, Phnom Penh employing about 4,800 workers, petitioned the Labour Disputes Department on 30 January 2009 for a ruling on its right to deduct, or decline to deduct, workers' wages to pay union dues. [Para 1][Para 67][Para 68] Two unions organise the plant: the Free Workers Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia at the Mithuna factory (2,508 members, founded 2000) and the Khmer Youth Union (1,228 members, founded 2003). [Para 69] Conciliation failed on the single non-conciliated point and the hearing was held on 11 February 2009. [Para 10][Para 8] For December 2008 and January 2009 the company deducted dues from 19 workers who had resigned from the Free Workers Union and kept the money inside the company rather than remitting it to the union, although each had signed a letter resigning from the union and asking that the monthly deduction stop. [Para 76] The company also asked the panel to confirm its general freedom to deduct or not deduct without friction with either union; the operative order does not answer that request. [Para 70][Para 107]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company may deduct workers' wages to pay union dues, and whether it must return dues deducted after the workers had written to resign from the union and stop the deduction? [Para 2][Para 10]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Chun Tich Thai Co., Ltd (Mithuna) (claimant):** it had always stopped deducting once a request arrived, but withheld the 19 workers' dues in this instance because it feared trouble with the Khmer Youth Union, and the letters had been collected in part by that union and delivered to the company's administration. [Para 72][Para 87]
**Free Workers Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia at the Mithuna factory and its 19 member workers (respondent):** the 19 letters are invalid because they carry neither the stamp nor the signature of the factory union president, as Article 27 of the union statute and past practice require, so the company could not treat them as effective revocations. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 81][Para 83]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the company to stop deducting monthly wages for union dues once a worker's written request to stop is received, and to return the deducted wages to the 19 workers according to the date of each resignation request; no sum was ordered paid to either union. [Para 107]

The panel read Article 129(2) of the Labour Law, which allows a worker to authorise in writing the deduction of wages to pay dues to the union of which he or she is a member, and permits that authorisation to be revoked at any time. [Para 93][Para 94] It held the article inapplicable to a request made by a union as a legal person; it governs the relationship between the individual worker and the company. [Para 95][Para 96] Its purpose is double: to protect workers from deductions contrary to their will and to protect the company from mistakes in deducting. [Para 98][Para 99] The panel therefore required each request document to be proper and clear, and held that where a worker has no wish to authorise a deduction, the employer must not continue it. [Para 100][Para 101] Once the company receives a letter expressing the worker's clear intention to stop, it must neither deduct nor retain the withheld share. [Para 103] On the facts the company had deducted and retained the dues of all 19 workers despite their letters, so it had used its right improperly. [Para 104][Para 105] The panel rejected the union's formality argument by implication: it treated the workers' own written revocation, not the union president's stamp, as the operative act, and it required repayment from the date of the resignation request. [Para 106]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A written authorisation to deduct union dues under Article 129(2) belongs to the individual worker and is revocable at any time; on receiving the worker's written revocation the employer must stop the deduction and return the amounts already withheld, and it may not hold those amounts in escrow to avoid friction between competing unions. [Para 97][Para 103][Para 106]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the company's concern about difficulties with the Khmer Youth Union could not justify retaining the deducted dues. [Para 87]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The table of 19 resignations at para 91 is badly scrambled: serial numbers, names and dates run out of order, at least two rows carry no name, and one row's date is reduced to a fragment. [Para 91] The letter list at paras 19–35 repeats numbers (8 and 9 each appear twice) and has one unnumbered entry. [Para 19][Para 32][Para 33] The number of non-conciliated points is printed as 01 in paras 10–11 but as 401 in para 14. [Para 10][Para 14] Para 89 dates one worker's request to 16 October 2007 while the table gives the same date and the letter list gives 16 October 2008 for a similarly named worker. [Para 89][Para 91] The company's Khmer name is printed with "យល៍" in the header and "យល" in its registration documents. [Para 1][Para 36] The award is dated 23 February 2009 in the header but para 108 says the parties selected it on 11 February 2009. [Para 108] The employer-side arbitrator appears as ឈីវ ភិរម្យ in the header and ឈីម ភិម្ស៍ in the signature block, and the chair as ឥង់ ផល្អៈ and គង់ ផល្ល. [Para 1][Para 109]
