# Award No. 122/09 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ាយ វី ភី (YVP) v Cambodia Labour Union Federation / សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា (CLUF) (Arbitration Council, 6 October 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators · Doc ID 140432

## 1. Facts
Case No. 122/09 concerns the factory of ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ាយ វី ភី at Sun Kanadia Building 5B, Sangkat Chom Chao, Khan Dangkao, Phnom Penh, which employed about 450 workers [Para 1][Para 32]. The claimant was សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា (CLUF) on behalf of the factory union, whose leadership election was held on 9 August 2009 with 20 participants [Para 1][Para 26][Para 37]. The union had applied to register on 13 August 2009 but had received no registration certificate by the hearing, and it produced no letters authorising it to act for the workforce; the panel therefore held it could act only for the three workers who were personally claimants and present [Para 33][Para 34][Para 35][Para 36][Para 89]. Of 12 demands, conciliation on 21 August 2009 settled 9 and left 3, on which the hearing of 10 September 2009 reached no settlement [Para 15][Para 16][Para 19][Para 20].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay 50 per cent of wages and allowances, averaged over the last 12 months, to workers taking maternity leave? [Para 3][Para 43][Para 153]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay the USD 5 monthly attendance bonus in full instead of deducting it in proportion to days of unauthorised absence? [Para 5][Para 47][Para 154]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must take back the union's president, vice-president and secretary and pay their wages and bonuses from the date of dismissal? [Para 7][Para 50][Para 152][Para 155]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា (CLUF):** that maternity pay should be half of total earnings, wages plus allowances, averaged over the preceding 12 months; that the USD 5 attendance bonus should no longer be docked for absence; and that the three elected leaders had been dismissed on 12 August 2009 without notice or warning, the president only because he left his workstation for five minutes to protest about a colleague injured by scissors [Para 43][Para 48][Para 50][Para 52].
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ាយ វី ភី:** that it already pays 50 per cent during maternity leave and will apply the law; that it pays the USD 5 bonus in accordance with Notification No. 017; and that the president was dismissed for serious misconduct in leaving his post, while the other two abandoned their jobs by not returning to work [Para 4][Para 6][Para 46][Para 53][Para 63].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issues 1 and 2 were dismissed, and the company was ordered to reinstate the union's president, vice-president and secretary and to pay their wages and other bonuses from 12 August 2009 until their return to work [Para 153][Para 154][Para 155].

On the preliminary question the panel applied Article 268 of the Labour Law, which confers the rights the law recognises on a professional organisation only once it is registered with the Ministry of Labour [Para 72][Para 75]. It noted the union's registration application was stamped as received on 13 August 2009 but no certificate had issued by the hearing, so the union did not yet hold the legal rights of a registered organisation [Para 76][Para 77][Para 78]. It added that Prakara 19 of Prakas No. 099 still lets an unregistered union appear, but only where it holds a written transfer of rights from the workers concerned, and none was produced [Para 81][Para 82][Para 84]. The panel accordingly confined the case to the three workers who filed and attended in person [Para 88][Para 89].

On Issue 1 the panel set out Articles 182 and 183 of the Labour Law, which give women 90 days' maternity leave and half pay including allowances, and Article 183's proviso limiting that right to women who have worked for the enterprise at least one year [Para 92][Para 93][Para 94]. It reviewed its earlier awards on calculating the average from the last 12 months [Para 95]. On the facts, however, the only claimants were three men, none of them a woman worker, so the maternity-pay claim could not apply to them and was dismissed [Para 96][Para 97][Para 98][Para 153].

On Issue 2 the panel relied on Notification No. 745 of 23 October 2006, which preserves the benefits in Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000, and on its earlier awards holding that the USD 5 attendance bonus rewards a full month worked without unauthorised absence [Para 101][Para 103]. It held that a day of unauthorised absence means the worker has not worked regularly as Notification No. 017 requires, so the bonus is not earned and may be deducted day by day [Para 105][Para 106][Para 107]. It rejected the demand to pay the bonus in full [Para 111][Para 154].

On Issue 3 the panel found that all three men had fixed-duration contracts and were candidates in the union election of 9 August 2009 [Para 113][Para 137]. It held that the president's five-minute absence to protest about an injured colleague was not serious misconduct and that dismissal was disproportionate, contrary to Articles 27 and 28 on disciplinary measures [Para 122][Para 123][Para 124]. It also found that the two others had not abandoned their jobs: the company's security staff escorted them out on 12 August 2009 and they were not allowed back, which showed an intention to dismiss them with the president [Para 125][Para 126]. Finally it applied Article 293 and Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, which protect union leaders and election candidates from dismissal for 45 days before and after the poll and require the labour inspector's approval [Para 127][Para 129][Para 130][Para 134]. All three conditions were met and no approval had been obtained, so the dismissals were unlawful [Para 136][Para 147][Para 148][Para 149][Para 150]. Reinstatement and back pay were ordered [Para 151][Para 152][Para 155].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An unregistered union cannot represent the workforce before the panel: under Article 268 it acquires the law's rights only on registration, and under Prakara 19 of Prakas No. 099 it may act for workers only with their written authority, so the case proceeds for the individual workers who filed and appeared [Para 75][Para 82][Para 89]. Dismissing a union leader or election candidate without the labour inspector's prior approval breaches Article 293 and Prakas No. 305, and a brief absence to protest a workplace injury is not serious misconduct justifying dismissal [Para 124][Para 134][Para 148].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that it would have applied the 12-month averaging method to a maternity claim brought by an eligible woman worker, since Article 183 limits that right to women with at least one year's service [Para 94][Para 95].

The three dismissed workers are named in full in the source; they are described here by office only.
