# Award No. 170/09-YVP — ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ាយ វី ភី ត្រេឌីង (YVP Trading) v សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា និង សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត នៃ ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ាយ វីភី (Arbitration Council, 29 December 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Panel: អាំង អេង ថុង (Chair), យូ សួន, លីវ សុវណ្ណា · Doc ID 140468

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ាយ វី ភី ត្រេឌីង (YVP Trading), a factory at Canadia Garden, Chom Chao, Dangkao, Phnom Penh with about 350 workers, is the respondent; the claimant is សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា and its factory union សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត នៃ ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ាយ វីភី [Para 25][Para 26]. Mr L, the union's factory president, began work on 18 May 2007 without a written contract [Para 28]. On 28 October 2009 the administration told him off for not covering his sewing machine at the end of the shift, a rule introduced while he was on a week's approved leave [Para 29][Para 30][Para 46]. On 19 November 2009 he argued with a team leader who had been criticising the team's work in abusive terms and who struck the table; he was dismissed that day and refused the wages tendered [Para 32][Para 33][Para 34][Para 35]. The labour department's 10 November 2009 letter had already recognised the union's new leadership, with Mr L as president [Para 20][Para 27].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether dismissal was a disciplinary penalty proportionate to the fault, namely the quarrel with the team leader and the uncovered machine? [Para 43][Para 45]
2. (Issue 1) Whether the dismissal of a registered union president complied with the procedure the Labour Law requires for protected workers? [Para 54][Para 61]

The five other demands in the 19 November 2009 complaint were settled at conciliation and did not come before the panel [Para 11].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា and the YVP factory union:** the company must take Mr L back at his original workplace and pay his wages and regular attendance bonus from the day of dismissal until he returns, because what happened was a misunderstanding rather than his fault and the case does not warrant the penalty of dismissal [Para 3][Para 36][Para 41]. When the union changed its leadership it notified the company on 28 September 2009 through a security guard, so the employer knew he was president [Para 38].
**Employer (respondent) — ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ាយ វី ភី ត្រេឌីង:** reinstatement is impossible because he quarrelled with his team leader three times and stirred conflict in the workplace, and at the moment of dismissal the employer did not know he was a union leader [Para 4][Para 37][Para 42].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to take Mr L back from the date the award takes effect and to pay his wages and regular attendance bonus from the date of dismissal until his return [Para 68][Para 69].
On the substantive question the panel applied Article 27 of the Labour Law, under which a disciplinary penalty must be proportionate to the weight of the fault, following Award 109/07-King Land (issue 27) [Para 43][Para 44]. It found that the failure to cover the machine could not count against him at all, since he had been on authorised leave when the rule changed and did not know of it [Para 46]. The quarrel was a mutual misunderstanding, regrettable but minor, and the employer never specified what kind of misconduct it considered serious enough to justify termination, nor did it produce the internal regulations it had been ordered to file by 17 December 2009 to show that its penalty followed them [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49]. No evidence showed he had behaved this way repeatedly towards other workers, so a warning or lesser sanction was the appropriate response [Para 50][Para 51][Para 52].
On the procedural question the panel found that he was a protected union leader: the labour department recognised the new leadership on 10 November 2009 and the union notified the company of the change on 28 September 2009 [Para 54][Para 55]. Article 293 of the Labour Law allows dismissal of a shop steward or candidate only with the Labour Inspector's authorisation, a rule extended to union leaders by Clause 4 of Prakas No. 305 SKBY dated 22 November 2001, and the panel had so held in Awards 79/06, 74/08, 107/08 and 03/09 [Para 56][Para 59][Para 61][Para 62]. Because the employer dismissed him without seeking the Inspector's agreement, the termination was procedurally unlawful and reinstatement with back pay followed [Para 66][Para 67][Para 68]. The order is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification [Para 70].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A disciplinary dismissal survives only if the penalty matches the weight of the fault under Article 27: where the misconduct is a single heated exchange with a supervisor, the employer carries no proven internal rule to the contrary, and no repetition is shown, dismissal is disproportionate and a warning is the lawful response [Para 52][Para 53]. Independently, a registered union leader may not be dismissed at all until the Labour Inspector has authorised it under Article 293, and the employer's ignorance of the leader's status is no answer once the union has notified it and the ministry has recognised the leadership [Para 60][Para 66][Para 67].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the quarrel, though minor, was conduct to be avoided so as to keep a working environment of tolerance, understanding and cooperation [Para 47].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The Khmer OCR is degraded. Paragraph 30 dissolves into stray numerals and a misread year in the middle of the worker's statement, so part of his account of the machine rule is lost [Para 30]. The security guard who is said to have received the union's notification letter is printed under two different names in two paragraphs, and one paragraph records the date of that letter as 28 September 2009 while the ministry's recognition is dated 10 November 2009 [Para 38][Para 55][Para 27]. The worker's name is printed in full and inconsistently, sometimes with its final consonant dropped; he is reduced to an initial here, as are the team leader, the security guard and the union officers [Para 41][Para 63][Para 65]. In the signature block the employer-chosen arbitrator's name is missing and the other two signatures are partly illegible, and the panel composition in the header carries a stray numeral after the employer-side arbitrator's name [Para 1][Para 71]. One line in the order section is garbled around the eight-day opposition period [Para 70].
