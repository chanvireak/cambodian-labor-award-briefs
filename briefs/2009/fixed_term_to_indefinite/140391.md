# Award No. 56/09 — Sun Tich PTE Ltd (Arbitration Council, 8 June 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ly Tai Seng (employer-appointed), An Virak (worker-appointed), Kong Phally (chair) · Doc ID 140391

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន សាន់ តិច ភីធី អ៉ី អិលធីឌី ("Sun Tich PTE Ltd") is a Phnom Penh factory whose workers are organised through សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត ក្រុមហ៊ុន សាន់ តិច [Para 1]. On 8 August 2008 the company announced that workers holding indefinite-duration contracts would be moved onto six-month fixed-duration contracts, with 15 days' wages per year of service paid in exchange; nine workers signed contracts running 1 October 2008 to 31 March 2009 and received notices of non-renewal between 23 and 25 March 2009 [Para 88][Para 22]. The nine include ordinary members, activists and union committee members, several with service dating to 1998–2004 [Para 54][Para 88]. Conciliation on their three claims failed and the file reached the Council on 4 May 2009; the hearing was held on 22 May 2009 [Para 10][Para 12].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must take the nine workers back into work on six-month fixed-duration contracts? [Para 2]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company discriminated against union members, both by ending the nine contracts and by moving three union members from clerk to production worker? [Para 4]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the nine are owed further sums on the ending of their indefinite-duration contracts — notice pay under Article 75, severance of 15 days per year under Article 89, or moral damages under Article 91? [Para 88]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the nine had indefinite-duration contracts which the company converted to fixed duration only to shed them; the non-renewals were union discrimination, and the company hired replacements into the warehouse and industrial-engineering sections after letting them go [Para 65]. Three union members were moved from clerk to production worker without prior notice, which they say was a demotion dressed as restructuring [Para 79].
**Employer (respondent):** the indefinite contracts were ended by agreement of both sides, with statutory compensation paid, because the factory needed economic restructuring under a LEAN programme [Para 3][Para 69]. The seven warehouse workers were selected by supervisors' performance assessments; the two industrial-engineering posts were cut from six to four to save cost, one post-holder earning a basic USD 165 a month; new hires merely filled resignations [Para 74][Para 76][Para 79].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issues 1 and 2 were dismissed; on issue 3 the company was ordered to pay severance (ប្រាក់បំណាច់បណ្តេញចេញ) under Article 89 to all nine according to length of service, capped at six months' wages and benefits, while moral damages and notice pay were refused [Para 166][Para 167]. The order is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification if no objection is filed [Para 168].

**Issue 1 and 2 — discrimination.** Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law forbid an employer to use union membership or union activity as the ground for hiring, disciplining or terminating; the panel read them as barring discrimination in hiring and dismissal alike [Para 97][Para 99]. The workers bore the burden of proving the discrimination alleged [Para 121]. The panel accepted the employer's explanation of the LEAN restructuring — a fact the workers conceded at the hearing — noted that no contradictory evidence was produced against the employer's documents, and found the workers had not shown how the discrimination operated [Para 111][Para 112][Para 114]. On the three transfers it likewise found no discrimination, since wages were unchanged and restructuring was admitted; the workers had not asked for their original posts to be restored, so that question was not decided [Para 129][Para 130].

**Issue 3 — payments on the ending of the indefinite contract.** The panel held that the change of contract type cut off the indefinite-duration relationship, and that this happened by the employer's will, whatever the form of the offer [Para 136][Para 137]. The 15 days' wages paid at the switch were, on the employer's own account, consideration for the conversion and not identified as either severance or damages, so the company had no intention of paying Article 89 severance then [Para 145][Para 147]. Article 89 requires severance of 15 days per year of service, capped at six months, where the employer alone ends the contract and no serious misconduct is alleged; all nine had more than twelve months' service and no misconduct was raised [Para 152][Para 153]. The panel therefore ordered severance for the nine, and refused moral damages because the conversion was not shown to be without valid reason, and refused notice pay because notice had been given before the conversion [Para 156][Para 160].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer proposes and a worker accepts the conversion of an indefinite-duration contract into a fixed-duration contract in return for 15 days' wages per year of service, the conversion nevertheless terminates the indefinite relationship by the employer's will, and the worker is entitled to Article 89 severance for that termination, capped at six months' wages and benefits, even though the replacement fixed-duration contract later ran to its term [Para 136][Para 153].

**Obiter dicta:** the claimant worker carries the burden of proving anti-union discrimination, and the panel will not infer it where the employer's restructuring is admitted and no contrary evidence is produced [Para 112][Para 121].
