# Award No. 49/09-Bloomtime — Bloomtime Embroidery Pte Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 18 May 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tayseng, Liv Sovanna, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 140382

## 1. Facts
Bloomtime Embroidery Pte Ltd. employs 800 workers at Trapang Thleung Village, Chom Chao Commune, Dangkor District, Phnom Penh; the claimant is the Worker Union at Bloomtime Embroidery Factory. Ten full-right and ten assistant worker delegates were elected on 31 May 2008, their mandate running to 31 May 2010. Two of them, a worker delegate and a union delegate, both on undetermined-duration contracts since 7 July 2005, were assigned to a month-long textile training course from 22 December 2008, refused it by letter, and did not attend work from 24 December 2008; the company treated them as having abandoned their posts on 31 December 2008. The union demanded that the employer stop recognising three of the elected delegates and reinstate the two, and at the hearing asked the panel to halt terminations of other workers' fixed-duration contracts, a request the panel declined to consider. [Para 18][Para 21][Para 27][Para 66]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must be ordered not to recognise three worker delegates elected on 31 May 2008, on the ground that they do not serve the workers' interests? [Para 14]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must reinstate the worker delegate and the union delegate to their former positions and pay their wages and benefits from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 18]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, Worker Union at Bloomtime Embroidery Factory):** the three delegates should not be recognised because they agreed to shift working hours and alter the employment contract without serving the workers' interests; and the two dismissed men did not abandon their posts, having been ordered to a course they did not need, having written to refuse it, and having emailed on 5 January 2009 that they were not quitting. [Para 14][Para 23][Para 20]

**Employer (respondent, Bloomtime Embroidery Pte Ltd.):** it has no legal authority to decide which delegates the workers recognise, since the quarrel is between the workers and their own delegates, though it will implement any lawful decision; and it did not dismiss the two men but applied point B-05 of clause 5 of its Internal Work Rules, under which absence for more than six consecutive days is serious misconduct and voluntary abandonment, so no wages or benefits are owed. [Para 16][Para 5]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected both the demand that the employer not recognise the three worker delegates and the demand that it reinstate the two delegates with wages and benefits; it also declined to consider the additional demand to suspend other workers' terminations. [Para 68][Para 69]

On issue 1 the panel began with jurisdiction. Article 302 of the Labour Law (1997) defines a collective dispute, and the panel read it as requiring three cumulative conditions: a dispute between employer and a group of workers, about working conditions or the rights and recognition of professional organisations, capable of disrupting the enterprise or social peace. This quarrel was between the workers and their own delegates, not about working conditions, and did not affect the factory's operation, so it was not a collective dispute. [Para 41][Para 43][Para 44]

Turning to the substance, the panel held that workers may dismiss a delegate who fails in his obligations, but only through the same procedure and form of voting used to elect delegates, with notice to the Labour Inspector within three days, and that an assistant delegate then takes over until a new mandate is elected. The workers had not identified which delegates they meant, had held no such vote, and could not end all mandates before expiry; the demand was rejected, following Award 106/06-Quick Sew and, on the timing of elections under Article 292, Awards 66/04-Winnent and 67/04. [Para 45][Para 49][Para 53]

On issue 2 the panel applied Article 2 paragraph 2, on the employer's right to direct and supervise the enterprise, as construed in Awards 39/07-San, 54/07-Yung Wah (Branch 1) and 47/08-Grandtex, and Article 69 paragraph 1, under which the worker must do the work assigned with due care. It found the training assignment reasonable, the workers' claim to already possess the skills insufficiently reasonable, the absence from 24 December 2008 established, and no evidence of any attempt to negotiate at the factory. Point B-05 of clause 5 of the Internal Work Rules therefore applied, and reinstatement was refused. [Para 57][Para 59][Para 62]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that the employer withdraw recognition from elected worker delegates is not a collective dispute under Article 302, because it opposes the workers to their own representatives rather than to the employer and does not touch working conditions or the operation of the enterprise; the lawful route is dismissal of a named delegate by the same vote that elected him, notified to the Labour Inspector within three days. [Para 43][Para 45]

**Obiter dicta:** an employer may lawfully direct workers to attend training to acquire further skills while maintaining their wages, and a worker who refuses a reasonable assignment and stays away for more than six consecutive days is treated as having abandoned employment. [Para 57][Para 62]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The text is an unofficial English translation with the article fused into the following word throughout ("themployer", "thevidence"), and several sentences duplicated or interrupted mid-clause, including the passage on voluntary abandonment, the summary of the three Article 302 conditions, and the citation list of prior awards. Prakas No. 286 on worker delegates is dated 5 November 2001 in one place and 5 November 2006 in another. The demand in issue 1 is printed in the non-conciliation report in a form that reads as an instruction to recognise the delegates rather than to withhold recognition, and the surrounding narrative is needed to read it correctly. The evidence section was omitted from the English version. Individual workers, delegates and the company director appear by name in the source and are described by role here.
