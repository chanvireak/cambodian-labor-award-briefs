# Award No. 11/09 — Gladpeer Garments Factory (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 20 February 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Chhiv Phyrum (employer-appointed), Ann Vireak (worker-appointed), Kong Phallack (chair) · Doc ID 140355

## 1. Facts
Gladpeer Garments Factory (Cambodia) Ltd, on National Road 4, Chom Chao Commune, Dangkor District, Phnom Penh, employs about 3,000 workers; the claimant is the local union of the Cambodia Labour Federation Union (CLUF), registered on 17 October 2001. [Para 11][Para 12] Some 1,000 workers have more than three years' service, about 100 more than six years and about 10 more than ten. [Para 12][Para 13] The employer's practice granted 18 days of annual leave for the first two years, 19 days from the third year and 20 days from the sixth year onwards. [Para 13] The union demanded an extra day of annual leave for every three years of service under Article 166 of the Labour Law, the employer answered that the law was unclear, and the single non-conciliated issue came to the panel after conciliation failed. [Para 2][Para 5][Para 13]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must grant an additional day of paid annual leave for every three years of service, computed from Article 166, paragraph four of the Labour Law? [Para 15][Para 22]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), the Local Union of the Cambodia Labour Federation Union (CLUF):** Article 166 obliges the employer to increase annual leave by seniority, one day for every three years of service, yet the employer caps entitlement at 20 days from the sixth year, so workers with six, nine and more years are under-credited; here 1,000 workers have passed three years of service. [Para 13][Para 15]

**Employer (respondent), Gladpeer Garments Factory (Cambodia) Ltd:** the Labour Law is not clear on the point, and its director already grants up to 20 days of annual leave even to workers with more than six years' service; it left the question to the arbitrators' judgment. [Para 2][Para 13][Para 14]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to provide paid annual leave in accordance with seniority, adding one day of paid leave for every three years of service. [Para 22]

The panel treated the case as one of statutory interpretation of Article 166. [Para 14] Paragraph one, it held, sets the base entitlement at one and a half working days per month of continuous service, that is 18 days a year; paragraph four then increases that length by one day per three years of service. [Para 16][Para 17] Following a line of earlier awards — 62/04-Ecent (issue 8), 68/05-Gold Lida, 81/07-Supreme (issue 2), 33/07-Goldfame (issue 5) and 23/08-M&V Branch 1 (issue 3) — the panel read the provision as conferring 19 days from the fourth year of employment, 20 days from the seventh and 21 days from the tenth. [Para 18][Para 19] It added a purposive reason: extra leave rewards and encourages seniority within the enterprise. [Para 21] It therefore held that the employer had no right to freeze entitlement at 20 days for workers of six years' service and more. [Para 21]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Article 166, paragraph four of the Labour Law operates automatically: annual leave is 18 days in the first three years and grows by one day for each completed three years of service, so a worker is entitled to 19 days from the fourth year, 20 from the seventh and 21 from the tenth; an employer's practice that caps leave at 20 days for all workers of six years or more is unlawful, and the workers' entitlement does not depend on a collective agreement being in force. [Para 17][Para 18][Para 21]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the same demand was under negotiation for the collective bargaining agreement then being concluded, but treated the statutory entitlement as independent of that negotiation. [Para 12]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
