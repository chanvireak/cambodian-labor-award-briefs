# Award No. 34/09 — Suntex Pte Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 6 April 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tayseng, Liv Sovanna, Kong Phallack) · Doc ID 140368

## 1. Facts
Suntex Pte Ltd. is a Phnom Penh garment manufacturer at Trapang Thloeng Village, Chom Chao Commune, Dangkor District, which began operating about ten years ago. [Para 15] Falling buyer numbers and rising production costs led it to cease night-shift operations; in August 2008 it reassigned workers from night to day shift, surveyed their views and offered three choices — convert to fixed-duration contracts with 15 days' wages per year of service, resign voluntarily with the same indemnity, or keep the contract and move to day shift. [Para 18][Para 21] The claimant workers ("Workers of Suntex Pte Ltd") say they were pushed out; the 22 additional workers who later joined brought the claimed total to 34, but the non-conciliation report and signed minutes name only 12. [Para 12][Para 32]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the 12 claimant workers are entitled to an additional indemnity for dismissal and related compensation because the employer's closure of the night shift amounted to an unlawful termination, or whether they resigned voluntarily and were paid what was agreed? [Para 14]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — Workers of Suntex Pte Ltd:** the employer terminated their contracts illegally by ceasing night work, leaving too little daytime work and suspending them, unilaterally imposing the options and downgrading undetermined-duration contracts to fixed ones; the English resignation letters were not understood by the signers, so they claim compensation in lieu of notice, dismissal indemnity, compensation equal to that indemnity and payment in lieu of annual leave under Articles 75, 76, 89, 90, 91, 167, 13 and 390 of the Labour Law. [Para 24][Para 28]
**Employer (respondent) — Suntex Pte Ltd.:** the workers all resigned and were already paid under their agreement; the three options were explained, the notification letter was in Khmer, a meeting with worker delegates preceded the choice, and there was no dismissal. [Para 3][Para 46][Para 49]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel rejected the demands for compensation in lieu of notice, indemnity for dismissal and compensation equal to the dismissal indemnity, but ordered the employer to pay the 12 workers compensation in lieu of annual leave they had not exhausted on the day they resigned. [Para 55]

The panel first fixed the parties. Article 312 of the Labour Law bars it from examining issues other than those in the non-conciliation report or matters directly consequent on them; that report named 12 workers, and those 12 had signed the conciliation minutes, so the 22 who authorised them later were not claimants. [Para 31][Para 32][Para 34]

On the merits, all contracts were of undetermined duration, which Article 74 allows either party to terminate at will on written prior notice. [Para 40][Para 42] The panel weighed the process rather than the label: the employer surveyed the workforce, issued a Khmer notification explaining its difficulties and the three options, and met the worker delegates, so the choice was free and informed; the 12 who signed English resignation letters received the agreed seniority indemnity of 15 days per year plus last wage. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 50] Signing demonstrated willingness to take the second option, so the resignations were voluntary and the dismissal-based claims failed. [Para 51][Para 52] Article 167 nonetheless preserves compensation in lieu of accrued paid leave on termination, following Award 153/08-Hytex Garment, so that order was made for the 12. [Para 53][Para 54]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer abolishes a shift for genuine economic reasons, discloses the reason in writing in Khmer and offers real alternatives that the workers choose after consultation with their delegates, the resulting resignations are voluntary and generate no dismissal indemnity; only accrued paid leave survives under Article 167 of the Labour Law. [Para 47][Para 53]

**Obiter dicta:** none identified beyond the standing analysis, which was necessary to the order. [Para 31]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel's worker headcount is printed inconsistently: the facts find that some claimants took option 1 while "another 32 workers chose option 2", and later that "the 34 workers chose the second option", although the operative order and the Article 312 analysis rest on 12. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 44] The hearing date is printed twice in the same line, and the evidence section was omitted from the English version. [Para 5][Para 11] OCR again drops letters at word joins ("themployer", "workerepresentative", "outling", "Traing"). [Para 2]
