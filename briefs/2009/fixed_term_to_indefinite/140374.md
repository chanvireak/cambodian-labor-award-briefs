# Award No. 39/09 — Quint Major Industrial Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 21 April 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach, Liv Sovanna, Kong Phallack) · Doc ID 140374

## 1. Facts
Quint Major Industrial Co., Ltd. is a garment manufacturer at Perk Village, Perk Commune, Angsnoul District, Kandal, employing about 1,400 workers; the claimant is the Local Union of the Cambodian Labor Union Federation (CLUF). [Para 17][Para 2] Mr C joined on 25 January 2007 on an undetermined-duration contract and held successive posts — head of sewing section A10, chief of the sample group, head of the sewing group in building 2, and from 1 March 2009 head of the cleaning group. [Para 18] He refused the cleaning post as not matching his skills, and was dismissed on 4 March 2009 with an offer of US$ 325.33 in final payments, which he refused. [Para 18][Para 25][Para 27] Of the seven demands, one was settled in conciliation and issues 3, 5 and 6 at the hearing, leaving issues 1, 2 and 4. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 15]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer lawfully dismissed Mr C, the second vice-president of the Local Union of CLUF, for refusing appointment as head of the cleaning group, and if not, to which position he must be reinstated? [Para 45]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the agreement of 23 October 2008 requiring a weekly skill-bonus competition remains binding on the employer? [Para 62]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must accept medical certificates from doctors outside the Kandal Referral Hospital as proof of illness for sick leave? [Para 73]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — Local Union of CLUF:** Mr C was the union's second vice-president and was moved to cleaning work that did not fit his skills while a colleague was said not to have returned from maternity leave; he had never been warned; the employer abandoned the agreed weekly skill competition the moment it was signed; and sick leave should be accepted on a certificate from any qualified doctor, as in the past. [Para 18][Para 23][Para 33][Para 41]
**Employer (respondent) — Quint Major Industrial Co., Ltd.:** it did not recognise Mr C as a union officer because the Ministry's letter No. 965 KB/AK/VK of 1 September 2008 lists only the president, vice-president and secretary; his reassignment was needed because the building 2 group head returned from maternity leave; the competition was replaced because buyers objected to Sunday working; and only Kandal Referral Hospital certificates are accepted after a forged certificate was found. [Para 18][Para 20][Para 34][Para 42]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to reinstate Mr C to a position requiring the same or similar skills with the same benefits, to hold the skill-bonus competition under the agreement, and to accept medical certificates from doctors recognised by the Ministry of Health. [Para 80][Para 81][Para 82]

On Issue 1, because his name was absent from the Ministry's acknowledged list of union officers, Mr C was an ordinary worker for dismissal purposes and had no special protection. [Para 46][Para 47] Clause 8 of the Internal Work Rules, registered at the Kandal Labour and Vocational Training Office on 10 May 2007, allows dismissal for breach only after a final written warning, and the employer gave none before dismissing him the day after his refusal, so the dismissal was inconsistent with its own rules and reinstatement followed. [Para 27][Para 47][Para 49][Para 50] The panel then fixed the post: Clause 5 requires compliance with appointments, and Article 2(2) with Awards 62/06-Quicksew, 108/06-Trinunggal Komara and 33/07-Goldfame gives the employer direction of the enterprise, but only lawfully and reasonably. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53] Moving a sewing-group head to cleaning work would let his skills deteriorate, and the panel held the employer should transfer a worker only to a section where the same or similar skills are used at the same wages and benefits. [Para 56][Para 58][Para 59]

On Issue 2, Article 22 of Decree No. 38 on Contracts and Other Liabilities of 28 October 1988 makes the 23 October 2008 agreement, signed at the Kandal Labour Office, binding, and Article 5 avoids only agreements that are illegal or contrary to public order. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 64] The switch from competition to speed-based selection was a unilateral change made without the workers' consent, which does not release the employer from the agreement, and the buyers' objection was asserted without concrete evidence. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 69][Para 70]

On Issue 4, Clause 4(K) of the Internal Work Rules requires a certificate from a "state hospital", read with Article 71(3) of the Labour Law on absence certified by a qualified doctor and Awards 102/07-Terratex and 72/08-Yung Wah (Branch 2). [Para 74][Para 77] State hospitals include urban, provincial, district and quarter supporting hospitals and commune and district health centres, and certificates from any doctor trained under the Ministry of Health framework and licensed to practise must be accepted, with an official seal for state hospitals and an operating patent attached for private ones. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 80]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that dismisses for breach of the Internal Work Rules without the final written warning those rules require acts unlawfully, and on reinstatement may not place the worker in a section where the skills built in the former post would atrophy; a collective agreement signed at the labour office binds until changed by consent, and sick leave must be accepted on any licensed doctor's certificate, not one named hospital's. [Para 49][Para 59][Para 65][Para 79]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that the buyers' objection to Sunday competitions was unsupported by concrete evidence, which mattered only because the employer pleaded it as justification for the unilateral change. [Para 69]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The dismissal payment is printed incoherently: a final wage of US$ 6.93, "three days' payment in lieu of one day of annual leave" of US$ 3.40, 30 days' notice at US$ 102, a US$ 3 seniority bonus and a US$ 6 incentive bonus are said to total US$ 325.33, which the components do not add up to. [Para 26][Para 27] The quotation of Article 71(3) repeats its last clause mid-sentence, and the issue list restarts its numbering so that "4", "5" and "6" appear twice while the panel hears issues 1, 2 and 4. [Para 75][Para 15][Para 5] The dismissed worker and the union's officers are printed with full personal names, which this brief does not reproduce. [Para 2] The evidence section was omitted from the English version, and OCR drops letters at word joins throughout ("themployer", "competion", "cleaningroup"). [Para 16][Para 3]
