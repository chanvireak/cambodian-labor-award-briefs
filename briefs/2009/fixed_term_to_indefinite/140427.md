# Award No. 118/09 — Shou Kong Shoe-Moulding Enterprise / សិប្បកម្ម ពុម្ព ស្បែកជើង (ស៊ូ ហ្វុង) v Cambodia Asia Trade Union Federation / សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្ពុជា អាស៊ី (Arbitration Council, 22 September 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tai Seng; Liv Sovann; Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 140427

## 1. Facts
Case No. 118/09 concerns the shoe-moulding enterprise សិប្បកម្ម ពុម្ព ស្បែកជើង (ស៊ូ ហ្វុង) at Chhak Chruk village, Sangkat Samrong Krom, Khan Dangkao, Phnom Penh, which has operated since 1999 and employed about 32 workers [Para 1][Para 31]. The claimant was សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្ពុជា អាស៊ី (Cambodia Asia Trade Union Federation) at Shou Kong, whose representatives were listed on the face of the award [Para 1][Para 32]. Of four demands, conciliation on 22 August 2009 left three unsettled, and the panel decided all three [Para 14][Para 15]. The hearing was held on 14 September 2009; the union attended and the employer was absent without explanation, so the panel proceeded in absentia [Para 12][Para 17][Para 53]. The award issued on 22 September 2009 [Para 1].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay the seniority bonus (ប្រាក់រង្វាន់អតីតភាពការងារ) for the period before July 2009 as well as from that month? [Para 33][Para 54]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay cash in lieu of the 9 annual-leave days it gave short of 18 days a year before July 2009? [Para 36][Para 74]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay a further USD 2 of the regular attendance bonus for the period before July 2009? [Para 42][Para 95]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្ពុជា អាស៊ី:** that since the enterprise opened in 1999 the company never paid the seniority bonus and paid only 3 days' attendance bonus and 9 days' annual leave, so it owes the arrears and, for leave, the difference of 9 days [Para 33][Para 38][Para 42]. The union based each claim on the Labour Law and asked for the shortfall to be paid at law [Para 35][Para 45].
**Employer (respondent), សិប្បកម្ម ពុម្ព ស្បែកជើង (ស៊ូ ហ្វុង):** that seniority pay, the 18 annual-leave days and the USD 5 attendance bonus would be applied only from July 2009, and that before then no seniority bonus or profit share was owed [Para 3][Para 5][Para 7]. The company did not appear at the hearing and filed no evidence [Para 18][Para 52].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to pay the seniority bonus and a further USD 2 attendance bonus for the last three years, from July 2006 to July 2009, and to give the annual leave days owed for the same period instead of cash; the demand for cash in lieu of annual leave was dismissed, and the company was ordered to stop requiring 30 full working days a month without Sunday or public-holiday rest [Para 108][Para 109][Para 110][Para 111][Para 112].

On procedure, the panel applied Prakara 21 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, following Awards 53/04, 63/04, 148/07 and 99/09, and held that where a party was duly invited, failed to appear and gave no valid reason, it may continue in that party's absence [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 53].

On Issue 1 the panel relied on Notification No. 745 of 23 October 2006, which preserves the benefits in Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000, and on point 5 of that notification, which sets the seniority bonus at USD 2 a month after one year, USD 3 after two years, USD 4 after three and USD 5 from the fourth year [Para 55][Para 56][Para 58][Para 60][Para 61]. It held that the company's failure to pay was contrary to the Labour Law [Para 66]. Because Article 120 of the Labour Law limits wage claims to three years, the panel ordered payment for July 2006 to July 2009 only, rejecting the union's claim back to 1999 [Para 67][Para 69][Para 72][Para 73].

On Issue 2 the panel read Article 166 of the Labour Law as entitling every worker to 18 paid annual-leave days a year, plus one extra day for every three years of service under Article 166(4) [Para 75][Para 77][Para 81]. It followed its earlier awards on that article [Para 79][Para 80]. But Article 167(3) voids any agreement to pay compensation in lieu of annual leave, so the claim for cash was dismissed and the remedy was leave in kind for the last three years, topped up to the total days each worker's seniority earned [Para 89][Para 92][Para 93][Para 94].

On Issue 3 the panel held that the USD 5 attendance bonus, read with the definition of "regular work" in Award 44/07, is earned by 26 working days a month where public holidays shorten the month [Para 97][Para 100][Para 101]. The company's practice of paying it only to workers who came 30 days without Sunday or holiday rest was contrary to the Labour Law, and it was ordered to stop it while paying the USD 2 shortfall for the three-year period [Para 102][Para 103][Para 106][Para 107].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A statutory or notificational bonus that the employer has simply never paid is still owed, but only within the three-year limit on wage claims in Article 120, so arrears run from three years before the claim and not from the start of employment [Para 67][Para 69][Para 72]. Annual leave may not be converted into money at the worker's request: the remedy for leave given short is additional leave, because Article 167(3) voids any agreement to pay in lieu of it [Para 89][Para 92].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that "regular work" means 26 days in a month where public holidays reduce the working days, which is why an attendance condition of 30 days was unlawful rather than merely harsh [Para 100][Para 102].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR is heavily damaged: the penalty and signature blocks are fragmentary, one line of the worker list at Para 33 survives as a run of Latin nonsense, and the numbered employee table there has unreadable names and dates, so the claimants are described by role only. Notification No. 745 is printed with three different reference suffixes [Para 55][Para 96], and Notification No. 017 is dated 18 July 2000 in the text but 18 July "200" in two places [Para 22][Para 56]. The operative order on Issue 3 states a two-year period while the reasoning and the panel's own order fix three years [Para 107][Para 112]. The award number in the header appears as a corrupt glyph string, and Issue 1 is printed with a garbled numeral in the panel's list of issues [Para 18].
