# Award No. 35/09 — 3 Q Garment Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 20 April 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phyrum, Ann Vireak, Ang Eng Thong) · Doc ID 140373

## 1. Facts
3 Q Garment Co., Ltd. is a Phnom Penh factory at Chom Chao Village, Chom Chao Commune, Dangkor District, employing 140 workers. [Para 12] The claimant is the Cambodian Workers Labor Federation Union (CWLFU), acting for 13 sewing workers of Group B on undetermined-duration contracts, after their section head Ms K — a sewing worker from 6 January 2007, promoted in July 2007 to head of the 36-worker section at US$ 146 per month — was demoted to ordinary sewer on 27 February 2009 for missing a 2,500-piece daily quota. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 26] The demotion followed her decision on 26 February to let the group go home at 5:15 p.m. after 2,300 pieces, which she said was to rest an exhausted group on a day when materials had run out. [Para 16][Para 19][Para 21] Group B then stopped work; most returned after the employer's announcement, but 13 refused, and the employer paid final wages and annual bonuses on 10 March 2009 and took their identity cards. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 56]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the 13 workers may require the employer to reinstate Ms K as head of the Group B sewing section? [Para 44]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the 13 workers are entitled to a dismissal indemnity under Articles 90 and 91 of the Labour Law because the employer's pressure forced them to stop working? [Para 57]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — Cambodian Workers Labor Federation Union (CWLFU):** Ms K was a good section head, the pressure of the quota and the demotion left the group unable to continue, and the employer in effect dismissed the 13 by collecting their wages, annual bonuses and identity cards; they will return if she is reinstated, and otherwise claim a dismissal indemnity. [Para 34][Para 36][Para 43]
**Employer (respondent) — 3 Q Garment Co., Ltd.:** it never dismissed anyone; Ms K lacked the ability for the role and Group B no longer needed a Cambodian head, so it supervised directly; the 13 simply refused to work, and those willing to follow directions were kept on. [Para 39][Para 41][Para 42]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected both the demand to reinstate Ms K as head of the Group B sewing section and the demand for a dismissal indemnity. [Para 64]

On Issue 1, the panel treated the claim as one third parties were making on someone else's contract. Article 65(1) of the Labour Law and Article 22 of Decree No. 38 on Contract and Other Responsibilities make a contract binding only on its parties, so only they may cancel, terminate, amend or renew it, and third parties cannot demand in their name without consent; the panel followed Awards 02/05-Bu Min, 88/06-Cambodia Apparel and 50/07-M & V (Branch 3). [Para 47][Para 49][Para 50] The 13 were not parties to the contract between Ms K and the employer, and she herself gave no reasons at the hearing to show the demotion was unreasonable. [Para 51][Para 52] Article 2(2) also reserves supervision and direction of the enterprise, including promotion decisions, to the employer exercised lawfully and reasonably (Awards 39/07-San, 54/07-Yung Wah, 47/08-Grandtex). [Para 52][Para 53]

On Issue 2, the workers accusing the employer of oppression carried the burden of proof (Awards 79/05-Evergreen, 77/08-Xing Tai, 101/08-GDM, 108/08-Hugo). [Para 55] The panel found that 23 of the 36 workers returned once the employer invited them back, leaving 13 who chose to stay away in protest at the demotion; apart from their dissatisfaction they produced no evidence of unfair treatment or repeated breach under Article 90. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58][Para 60] With no basis to find oppression or offences under Article 83 Part A, the panel held the stoppage voluntary and rejected the indemnity. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Workers who are not parties to a colleague's employment contract cannot demand her reinstatement or promotion on her behalf, and a work stoppage chosen to press that demand is a voluntary refusal to work that earns no dismissal indemnity absent evidence of the employer's unfair treatment. [Para 50][Para 62]

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that Group B had no further need for a Cambodian section head because the supervisor could perform the role himself. [Para 41]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The findings state that there is no union of employees at the factory, yet CWLFU appears throughout as the claimant party; both are reported as printed. [Para 13][Para 2] The account of 26 February is split and reordered mid-sentence across three numbered lines, so the time she released the group and the piece count are printed twice. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 18] One line merging the payslip distribution with the final payment is also cut mid-clause. [Para 36][Para 55] The section head and the union's officers are printed with full personal names, which this brief does not reproduce. [Para 2] The evidence section was omitted from the English version, and OCR drops letters at word joins ("themployer", "outling", "explaing"). [Para 11][Para 7]
