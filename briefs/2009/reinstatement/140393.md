# Award No. 63/09 — Global Apparel Limited (Arbitration Council, 9 June 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Chhiv Phirom (employer-appointed), Ven Peou (worker-appointed), Kong Phally (chair) · Doc ID 140393

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ក្លូប័ល អា ប៉ារែល ("Global Apparel Limited"), the garment factory formerly registered as Jusca Garments (Cambodia) Ltd, sits at Trapeang Toul village, Kandal province, and employs 910 workers [Para 34][Para 19]. The claimant is សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ នៃ រោងចក្រ ក្លូបល ("Democratic Union of Garment Workers at the Global factory"), registered 14 January 2008 [Para 25][Para 35]. In January 2009 the company moved its night-shift workers onto the day shift, and on 11 and 12 March 2009 it twice warned the union's factory president, Mr K S, about leaving his workstation, telling him no further overtime would be given [Para 40][Para 41]. Overtime for 1–12 March was paid; he claims two hours of overtime a day for 13–31 March were not [Para 36][Para 37]. The company closed the night shift on 9 May 2009 [Para 52].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay Mr K S overtime for 13 to 31 March 2009? [Para 36]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must take its former night-shift workers back into the night shift, as it previously promised? [Para 48]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr K S attended work and performed overtime of two hours a day from 13 to 31 March 2009 which the company has not paid; the company failed to arrange proper work for him after moving him from the night to the day shift, and had no work to give him [Para 37][Para 43]. The company hired outside contract workers for the night shift while refusing its own experienced night-shift staff, contrary to its earlier promise to take them back [Para 4][Para 48].
**Employer (respondent):** Mr K S did not work for the company during that period: he left his post in Group 37 of Building A for Building B, chatted with other workers and at times lay on their work tables, and he admitted this at the hearing [Para 39][Para 46][Para 63]. The night work was short-term contract work from 28 April to 9 May 2009 only, needing skilled outside workers because the production line was behind and no building was free in the day; the shift has now closed and the company would still take the old workers back if night work restarted [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both claims were refused, and no sum was ordered [Para 78][Para 79]. The order is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification if no objection is filed [Para 80].

**Issue 1 — overtime.** Clause 2(a)(1) of the company's internal rules requires a worker to follow instructions and answer for the tasks assigned, and Article 69(1) of the Labour Law requires the worker to put all his professional activity at the enterprise's disposal, to do the work he was hired for, personally and with care [Para 56][Para 57]. Following Award 49/09, the panel held that this duty binds a worker while he is under the employer's management and receiving wages from it [Para 59][Para 61]. On the facts it found he did not apply himself during the overtime periods: he repeatedly left his workstation, and he conceded the conduct and the two warnings [Para 62][Para 63]. Presence in the factory during overtime hours therefore did not create an obligation to pay, because he had not performed the duty of a worker under Article 69 [Para 64]. His contention that the company had no work for him was unsupported by any evidence, and the company was still recruiting to fill its production line [Para 65][Para 66].

**Issue 2 — reinstatement to the night shift.** The panel treated the claim as one about a future contingency: the workers asked that the former night-shift staff be taken back if night work resumed, and both sides accepted that no night work exists since 9 May 2009 [Para 70][Para 71][Para 72]. The Council exists to settle labour disputes that have arisen, not problems that have not yet arisen, as held in Awards 10/03, 14/06 and 36/09; nobody can predict future events [Para 73][Para 75]. Since the night shift had ceased and no concrete evidence of a live dispute was produced, the panel declined to consider the claim at all [Para 76][Para 77].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker who is present at the factory during overtime hours but does not perform the work assigned to him is not entitled to overtime pay, because Article 69(1) of the Labour Law conditions the wage on personal and careful performance of the work; and the panel will not decide a claim whose subject-matter depends on an event that has not yet occurred [Para 64][Para 76].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that the company maintained it would still re-engage the former night-shift workers if night operations resumed, and observed that the workers' concern was directed at that future possibility [Para 70][Para 71].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The first line of the file is largely destroyed, with a run of digits and stray Latin fragments before the Council's name, so the case number and date are legible only from the body [Para 1]. The employer is printed as ក្លូប័ល អា ប៉ារែល, ក្លូប័ល អាបាំ រែលី មីធីត, ក្លូប័ល អាបារ៉ែល and simply ក្លូបល; the certificate of incorporation annexed is for Jusca Garments (Cambodia) Ltd, and the internal rules relied on at Article 69 are those of a different name, ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្វាស្កា ហ្គាមេន, registered in 2004 [Para 1][Para 19][Para 22]. The worker's personal name appears in at least five different spellings across the findings, the claim and the reasoning, so he is identified here by initials only [Para 36][Para 43][Para 63]. The award says at one point that both parties accepted there is no night work, while the employer elsewhere describes a night contract that ran to 9 May 2009 [Para 52][Para 49]. Two of the six original claims were settled at conciliation and only two reached the panel [Para 12]. The signature block of the worker-appointed arbitrator and the chair is partly garbled [Para 81].
