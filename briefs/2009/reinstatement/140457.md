# Award No. 160/09 — Cosmo Textile Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខូស ម៉ូ តិច ស្តាយ ខូអិលធីឌី) (Arbitration Council, 21 December 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Yu Sounti (employer-appointed), Tuon Si Phan (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhean (president) · Doc ID 140457

## 1. Facts
Cosmo Textile Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខូស ម៉ូ តិច ស្តាយ ខូអិលធីឌី) opened at Svey Chrum village, Ang Snuol district, Kandal, on 15 June 2009 and employed 830 workers. The Khmer Youth Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធសហជីពយុវជនខ្មែរ) filed the complaint on behalf of the plant's union; it had a registration receipt dated 14 October 2009 but was not yet recognised by the Ministry. [Para 57][Para 59][Para 60] The union held a committee election on 11 October 2009 at a workers' rented house, electing Mr S as secretary; the company dismissed him on 16 October 2009, five days later, while he was still on a three-month probation as a cutter ending 20 October 2009. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 64][Para 125] A twelve-point collective dispute went to conciliation and failed in all twelve points; points 2–5 and 7–12 were settled at the hearing, point 6 was withdrawn by the workers, leaving Issue 1 and one additional claim. [Para 35][Para 39][Para 40][Para 41] Twelve demands never reached by the panel included overtime on a voluntary basis, conversion of probationers after two months, medical staff and medicine, travel allowance of USD 6 per month and demands against named supervisors. [Para 5][Para 7][Para 11][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must take Mr S back into normal work as secretary of the Khmer Youth Union Federation, his dismissal being alleged to be anti-union discrimination? [Para 112]
2. (Additional issue) Whether the company must take back the 250 workers it dismissed on 26 November 2009 after they refused to sign a letter promising to stop striking? [Para 147][Para 176]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — Khmer Youth Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធសហជីពយុវជនខ្មែរ):** Mr S was a candidate and elected secretary, so he enjoyed the protection of Prakas No. 305 and the dismissal five days after the poll was discrimination; the union notified the company of the election through its security guard and the Ministry the next day. [Para 112][Para 116][Para 127]
**Employer (respondent) — Cosmo Textile Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខូស ម៉ូ តិច ស្តាយ ខូអិលធីឌី):** the probation was ended because the section chief rated his work poor; the company never received notice of the election or of his candidacy; the 250 workers abandoned their jobs by seven consecutive absences under Article 6 of the internal rules and ignored court order No. 084. [Para 81][Para 71][Para 104][Para 159]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issue 1 dismissed; on the additional issue the panel ordered the company to take back the workers it dismissed for refusing to sign the no-strike letter. [Para 177][Para 178]
On Issue 1 the panel applied the three cumulative conditions it had drawn from Prakas No. 305 (22 November 2001) and earlier awards: the worker must fall within the Prakas, the dismissal must fall inside the protected period, and the union must have notified the employer of the protected names by any practicable means. [Para 114][Para 120][Para 122] Conditions one and two were met — he was an elected candidate and was dismissed 5 days after the poll, inside the 45-day window. [Para 124][Para 125][Para 126] Condition three failed: the federation official's account of handing the notice to an unnamed CSN security guard was unsupported by any receipt, log of refusal or identification, and the union never produced the notice to the panel despite an order to file it by 8 December 2009. [Para 129][Para 130][Para 131][Para 133] Without that third condition the panel held he had no protected status. [Para 134]
On discrimination the panel cited Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law and held the worker bears the burden of proving anti-union motive; the union offered no specific facts or evidence, so the dismissal was found unrelated to union activity. [Para 135][Para 137][Para 139][Para 143][Para 144][Para 145]
On the additional issue the panel first satisfied itself of jurisdiction over a claim not in the conciliation record, treating it as a direct consequence of the twelve-point dispute under Article 312. [Para 150][Para 155][Para 157] On the merits it applied Articles 332 and 333: workers recover their jobs when a strike ends, and no sanction may be imposed for taking part in a strike. [Para 161][Para 162] The signature condition was unlawful, and Article 6 of the internal rules on unauthorised absence could not be stretched to cover this case. [Para 173][Para 175]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker elected to union office gains the Prakas No. 305 protection only if the union has actually proved that it notified the employer of the candidate's name by some practicable means; an uncorroborated account of handing a notice to an unidentified guard does not discharge that burden, and the worker's own reinstatement claim then fails even where the timing of dismissal is suspicious. [Para 120][Para 132][Para 133] Separately, an employer may not make return to work after a lawful strike conditional on signing a promise never to strike again; such a condition is a sanction for striking and is void under Articles 332 and 333. [Para 172][Para 173]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that if the company wished to discipline workers who attended but refused to work, it should use its internal rules rather than impose a no-strike condition; it also noted that court order No. 084 imposed no signature requirement. [Para 170][Para 174]
