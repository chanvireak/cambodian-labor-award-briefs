# Award No. 04/09-E — E Garment Co., Ltd. and Cambodian Union Federation (CUF) Local Union (Arbitration Council, 16 February 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer, Liv Sovanna for the workers, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 140350

## 1. Facts
On 23 November 2008 forty-five security guards stationed at the E Garment Co., Ltd. factory were removed and replaced by guards from KRS Cambodia Security Co Ltd; thirty-seven of them continued the claim, represented by a CUF dispute resolution officer, on wages of US$ 70 to US$ 90 a month [Para 13][Para 14]. E Garment had signed two written security-service contracts with Grace Co., Ltd., on 1 July 2008 and 1 October 2008 [Para 20]. The guards sought reinstatement, failing which compensation under the Labour Law, and reimbursement of the sums they had paid to obtain their jobs and uniforms [Para 1]. The claim reached the panel through non-conciliation report No. 006/09 KB/KN dated 8 January 2009 [Para 4].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1A) Whether the 45 guards, or the 37 who continued the claim, were employed by E Garment Co., Ltd.? [Para 32]
2. (Issue 1B) Whether E Garment Co., Ltd. is responsible for reinstating them? [Para 44]
3. (Issue 1C) Whether they are entitled to have their wages back paid by E Garment Co., Ltd. from 23 November 2008 until reinstatement? [Para 53]
4. (Issue 2) Whether E Garment Co., Ltd. must reimburse the sums the guards paid to obtain their employment and uniforms? [Para 67]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), Cambodian Union Federation (CUF) Local Union of CUF:** the guards say they were recruited to work at the factory and were never told they would be working for Grace Co., Ltd.; they received their wages from E Garment Co., Ltd., through an administration officer of that company, and had no contract with Grace [Para 20]. They also say they paid US$ 50 to US$ 110 for the job itself and a further US$ 15 to US$ 50 for uniforms [Para 21].

**Employer (respondent), E Garment Co., Ltd.:** the 45 guards are not its employees but those of Grace Co., Ltd., which recruits, rosters, manages and pays them outside the factory under the two service contracts, so the company is not involved in this case and the issue lies between Grace and its workers [Para 17][Para 19][Para 24].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the demand for reinstatement, the demand for back pay of wages from 23 November 2008, and the demand that E Garment reimburse the sums paid for employment and uniforms [Para 81].

On Issue 1A the panel applied Articles 2 and 3 of the Labour Law, reading "employer" as the entity with authority to supervise and direct workers, and followed Arbitral Award 04/05-Eternity on the same point [Para 35][Para 36]. Because Grace recruited the guards, set their rosters, managed them and paid their wages, Grace was their employer, not E Garment [Para 36][Para 37]. The panel then held that the security contract was a labour-contracting arrangement within Article 45, following Arbitral Awards 47/06-Flying Dragon and 53/07-E Garment, so that under Article 47 Grace owed the duties of an ordinary employer [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41].

On Issue 1B the panel rejected reinstatement: the guards were Grace's workers and E Garment had ended the security-supply agreement, so it had no obligation to restore their positions at the factory [Para 46][Para 48]. On Issue 1C it accepted that Article 48 creates an indirect legal relationship between an entrepreneur and a sub-contractor's workers, but held that this relationship ended when the written security contract was terminated on 23 November 2008, so E Garment bore no liability for the resulting loss of benefits [Para 56][Para 64][Para 66].

On Issue 2 the panel cited Articles 44, 46, 126 and 127(2) of the Labour Law and Arbitral Awards 21/03-Loyal and 86/06-Kingsland for the proposition that pre-employment charges are unlawful [Para 68]. It found nonetheless that the money must be repaid by whoever received it, and on the evidence at the hearing the whole amount had passed to Grace's director, so only Grace was obligated to reimburse and the demand had been aimed at the wrong party [Para 69][Para 75][Para 76]. Jurisdictionally the panel held, under Article 312 of the Labour Law and Clause 33 of Prakas No. 099 dated 21 April 2004, that Grace could not be added as a party because the non-conciliation report named only E Garment [Para 49][Para 78][Para 79]. Arbitrator Liv Sovanna dissented in an annex, would have ordered E Garment to pay in lieu of notice and in lieu of annual leave to the 37 claimants, and would have rejected the rest [Para 116][Para 117].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A security company that recruits, rosters, manages and pays guards assigned to a factory under a written all-inclusive service contract is a labour contractor and, by Article 47 of the Labour Law, the employer of those guards, so the host enterprise is not liable to reinstate them [Para 41][Para 48]. The host enterprise's indirect relationship with such workers under Article 48 of the Labour Law lasts only while the service contract subsists, and ends with it [Para 65][Para 66]. Sums exacted as a condition of hiring are recoverable from the person who received them, and a panel cannot order a company to repay them when the non-conciliation report named a different party [Para 76][Para 79].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel advised the workers who wished to return to work, or to claim back pay, to meet Grace's director directly, and recorded Grace's statement that it had not terminated their contracts and would reinstate those who had not withdrawn their CVs [Para 49][Para 51][Para 79].
