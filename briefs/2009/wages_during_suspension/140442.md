# Award No. 133/09 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន តាក ហ្វាត ហ្គាមិន (ខាំបូឌា) អែល ធី ឌី (Tack Fat Gamicat (Cambodia) Ltd) (Arbitration Council, 23 October 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: រ៉ឹង សុទ្ធី (employer-appointed), មួន ស៊ី ផាន់ (worker-appointed), ប៉ែន ប៊ុន យោ (president) · Doc ID 140442

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន តាក ហ្វាត ហ្គាមិន (ខាំបូឌា) អែល ធី ឌី, a garment factory at Chark Angre Leu, Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employed about 1,820 workers and had seven unions inside the plant. [Para 3][Para 50][Para 51]
The claimant was សហជីព សេរីកម្មករ នៃ ព្រះរាជាណាចក្រ កម្ពុជា (Free Workers' Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia), which had applied for registration on 21 August 2009 but had not yet received its certificate. [Para 52][Para 53]
From June 2009 the factory lacked sufficient orders and rotated workers — two weeks of work at 100% wages followed by two weeks at home on 50% — a practice the employer said it had agreed with five of the plant's unions but not with the claimant. [Para 58][Para 61][Para 62]
Of eight demands, two (points 3 and 5) were settled at conciliation; points 1, 2 and 6 were related and the union agreed to merge them into a single dispute, leaving that merged point and point 4. [Para 22][Para 27][Para 28]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issues 1, 2 and 6) Whether the employer must pay wages at 100 per cent for periods when there was no work, because the employment contracts were never validly suspended? [Para 105]
2. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must re-pay the shortfall in June 2009 wages allegedly paid short to particular groups of workers? [Para 124]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, សហជីព សេរីកម្មករ នៃ ព្រះរាជាណាចក្រ កម្ពុជា):** the employer had suspended contracts for two months on a labour-inspector authorisation that then expired, went on paying only 50 per cent without seeking renewal, and so must pay full wages; June wages were also short, omitting seniority bonuses and regular attendance bonuses, and 793 workers had thumb-printed an authorisation for the union to act for them. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 71][Para 57]
**Employer (respondent, ក្រុមហ៊ុន តាក ហ្វាត ហ្គាមិន (ខាំបូឌា) អេល ធី ឌី):** orders fluctuated so full work could not be guaranteed; the 50 per cent rule for short lay-offs of one to three weeks was its standing practice, with contracts suspended through the inspectorate for longer periods; it admitted the June payroll had been short but said everything had been made good except 16, 17 and 19 June, when workers struck. [Para 7][Para 66][Para 82][Para 83]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the company to pay 100 per cent of wages to the workers for periods when it had no work to give because it had not lawfully suspended their contracts, and dismissed the June 2009 wage-shortfall claim. [Para 145][Para 146]
As a threshold matter the panel held that under Article 268 of the Labour Law a professional organisation acquires the rights the law confers only once registered, so the claimant union, still unregistered, could not itself represent members; but Article 19 of Prakas No. 099/04 lets a non-party act on written authority, and 793 workers had thumb-printed such an authority, so the panel took the case for those 793 only. [Para 90][Para 100][Para 103][Para 104]
On the merged points 1, 2 and 6, the panel applied Article 71 of the Labour Law, which permits suspension only on serious economic or material difficulty and requires the suspension to be placed under labour-inspector supervision. It found the order fluctuations a genuine material difficulty, but held that a lawful suspension requires notification to, and approval from, the labour inspector; the company had let the two-month authorisation lapse, continued on its own 50 per cent rule and never sought renewal, so the suspension was invalid. [Para 107][Para 113][Para 118][Para 120][Para 121]
On point 4, Article 118 of the Labour Law puts the burden of proving wage payment on the employer. Both sides filed payroll receipts and corrected payroll tables, but the workers never supplied a list naming who was underpaid, in which section, by how much and on what day, so the panel could not verify any individual claim and dismissed it, while recording that wage shortfalls remained the central problem requiring individual proof. [Para 129][Para 137][Para 142][Para 143][Para 144]
The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification unless a party objects. [Para 147]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may lawfully pay less than full wages during a work stoppage only if it has notified the labour inspector and obtained approval for the suspension of the contracts; where it lets an authorisation lapse and continues to apply its own partial-pay rule, the suspension is invalid and full wages are owed for the whole no-work period. [Para 118][Para 121][Para 122]

**Obiter dicta:** an unregistered union may still bring a collective dispute where the affected workers give it written authority, but only for those workers who did so; and a wage claim fails where the claimants cannot identify the underpaid individuals or amounts, even though the burden of proving payment lies on the employer. [Para 102][Para 140][Para 143]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
OCR damage is extensive. The date of the non-conciliation report is printed as 11 September 2009 in the procedural recital and as 14 September 2009 in the recital forwarding the file, and the same report number is given in two different forms. [Para 18][Para 23]
The award recites eight original demands but describes six unresolved points and a merged group of three, so the arithmetic of the demand list does not close. The company name appears in at least four OCR variants (តាក ហ្វាត, តាក ហ្គាត, តាក ឃ្លាត, តាក ហ្គាមិន), and the claimant union's name is variously rendered as សេរីកម្មករ and សេរិកម្មករ. [Para 22][Para 50]
Individual workers and shop stewards appear only under corrupted or duplicated spellings, so they are identified here by section and role. The worker-appointed arbitrator's name survives only in the signature block and the employer-appointed arbitrator's name is partly garbled there. [Para 148]
