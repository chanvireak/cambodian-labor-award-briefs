# Award No. 126/09 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន អំពែ រ័រ ហ្គាមិន (Amber Garment) and the Workers' Union of the factory (Arbitration Council, 6 October 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of three: ម៉ាឌ សំបូរ (employer-appointed), អរ ណវន (worker-appointed), ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈាម (president) · Doc ID 140438

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន អំពែ រ័រ ហ្គាមិន, a garment factory at Prek Samrong, Ta Khmao, Kandal province that began operating in 1997 and employs 701 workers, has two unions in the plant; the **សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត** of the factory is the claimant here. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38]
From 1 September 2009 the company gave no work at all in any section and did not say when work would resume; it required workers to attend and punch in daily while paying only 50% of wages and refusing them access to the production lines. [Para 40][Para 41]
Earlier, from 2 January to 23 March 2009, the company had suspended the contracts for three months with the labour inspector's written authorisation; it did not seek any such authorisation for the September stoppage. [Para 43][Para 44]
Conciliation at the Kandal labour office failed on all three points, and the company then failed to attend the hearing without excuse, so the panel proceeded in its absence under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 16][Para 25][Para 26][Para 63]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay 100% of wages for the period from 1 September 2009 in which it gave the workers no work? [Para 40]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay wages twice a month, no more than sixteen days apart, instead of once a month on the tenth of the following month? [Para 48]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay wages and other statutory compensation now, on the ground that it may be unable to continue operating? [Para 53]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), through the factory's Workers' Union:** the company has given no work in any section since 1 September 2009 and has not told them when work will resume, yet demands daily attendance and pays only half wages, so full wages are owed; wages should be paid twice monthly as Article 116 and Ministry Circular No. 1903 of 24 November 2008 require, for production-line workers though not office staff; and because they fear the factory may close without settling wages and severance, the company should pay out now. [Para 40][Para 45][Para 49][Para 51][Para 54]
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន អំពែ រ័រ ហ្គាមិន:** did not attend and filed no submission, so the only account of its position is what the workers reported of the conciliation, where it said it had no capacity to pay twice monthly and no capacity to pay compensation if it could not keep operating. [Para 7][Para 25][Para 61]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered 100% wages for all sections from 1 September 2009 until work resumes or a lawful suspension is authorised, and ordered wages paid twice a month at intervals of no more than sixteen days; it dismissed the demand for immediate payment of severance; the award is non-binding and becomes permanent eight days after notification. [Para 116][Para 117][Para 118][Para 119]
On issue 1 the panel applied Article 71(11) of the Labour Law, which permits suspension of the enterprise's activity for serious economic or material difficulty only for a period not exceeding two months and only under the supervision of the labour inspector, and Article 72(1), under which a lawful suspension relieves the employer of the wage obligation. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 77][Para 78]
It found that the company had neither applied for nor received the labour inspector's authorisation for the September stoppage, and had instead suspended the contracts unilaterally by requiring daily attendance at half pay; the suspension was therefore unlawful and full wages were owed from the first day without work until work resumes. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 84][Para 85]
On issue 2 the panel applied Article 116, which requires wages to be paid at least twice a month with no more than sixteen days between payments, and Circular No. 1903 of 24 November 2008, which permits a collective agreement to adopt a different schedule only if it is registered with the Ministry. [Para 88][Para 91][Para 92]
It then distinguished "worker" (កម្មករ) from "employee" (និយោជិត) under Articles 5 and 6, held that the 701 production-line workers are workers who perform manual work under the employer's direction, and concluded that they are entitled to two payments a month, while office staff receive one. [Para 95][Para 97][Para 101][Para 102]
On issue 3 the panel found no evidence that the company had decided to close: it was only preparing a relocation from Ta Khmao to Ang Snuol district, and the removal of three or four machines and the conditions attached to the August payroll showed preparation to move, not closure. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 113]
Because no closure had occurred and the workers had shown nothing more than anxiety about the future, the demand was one for a future event and was declined. [Para 109][Para 112][Para 115]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that stops giving work without the labour inspector's prior authorisation under Article 71(11) cannot treat the stoppage as a lawful suspension, and owes 100% of wages from the first day without work until work resumes; and where the statutory twice-monthly payment rule applies, production-line workers, as distinct from office employees, must be paid at intervals of no more than sixteen days unless a registered collective agreement provides otherwise. [Para 83][Para 93][Para 101]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that suspicion created by the removal of machines and by conditions attached to the payroll could not substitute for evidence of an actual decision to close the factory. [Para 108]
