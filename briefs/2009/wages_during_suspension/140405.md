# Award No. 83/09-សាន់ វើល ដ៏ — ក្រុមហ៊ុន សាន់ វើល ដ៏ ហ្គាបេន (Arbitration Council, 5 August 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (លី តៃ សេង ថា for the employer, លីវ សុវណ្ណា for the workers, chair ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈាន) · Doc ID 140405

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន សាន់ វើល ដ៏ ហ្គាបេន ("Sun World Garment") began operating in 2007 at Trapeang Thloeng village, Chom Chao, Dangkao district, Phnom Penh, with about 750 workers, some 85 per cent of them women. [Para 33] The claimant is សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ (the Khmer Youth Union Federation) at the factory, whose local union registered on 7 April 2009. [Para 25][Para 35] Of 10 demands, 2 were not conciliated: a nursery and breastfeeding room, and one day a month of paid health check for pregnant workers. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 53] The panel sat twice and also went to the factory on 25 July 2009, where the company admitted the panel but refused entry to the union. [Para 10][Para 11][Para 51][Para 77]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must build a nursery and breastfeeding room for its workers' children and set a clear schedule for their use? [Para 40][Para 64]
2. (Issue 2) Whether pregnant workers are entitled to one day a month to attend a health check with their wages, and separately their regular attendance bonus, preserved? [Para 53][Para 83]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the members of សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ argued that the factory has neither a nursery nor a breastfeeding room, or that any space provided is cramped and improper, and that a pregnant worker needs one day a month for antenatal checks and vaccination, so neither her wage nor her attendance bonus should be cut. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 53][Para 59]
**Employer (respondent):** Sun World Garment said it had already fitted out a nursery and breastfeeding room, announced on 1 May 2008, with rubber matting, pictures, toys and one staff member to watch the children, that no worker had ever asked to use it, and that it grants two or three hours for checks but cannot preserve wages and bonuses because it says no law requires it. [Para 40][Para 43][Para 46][Para 58][Para 84]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the company to arrange a nursery and breastfeeding room that are proper and adequate to protect the health of mothers and infants once the award takes effect, dismissed the claim for wages during the health-check day, and ordered the attendance bonus to be pro-rated by the number of days of authorised absence. [Para 111][Para 112][Para 113]

On Issue 1, Article 186 of the Labour Law requires any enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a breastfeeding room in or near the workplace and a nursery, and where it cannot run a nursery for children over 18 months it must pay for outside care. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 66][Para 67] The panel relied on Awards 103/08, 115/08 and 75/09 for the duty to provide both facilities and, using its investigative power under clause 24 of Prakas No. 099, summoned the parties for an on-site inspection on 25 July 2009. [Para 68][Para 72][Para 75][Para 76] Because the company let the panel in but shut the union out, the panel declined to inspect at all, holding that an inspection without the claimant union present would look like collusion with the employer, and that a company sincerely asserting it had built a compliant nursery should let the union in. [Para 78][Para 80][Para 81] Having thus been unable to verify either the existence or the adequacy of the facilities, the panel ordered proper arrangement rather than finding the claim satisfied. [Para 82]

On Issue 2, Articles 71(a)(6) and 72 suspend the contract, and with it the wage obligation, while a worker is absent with the employer's permission, so the panel dismissed the demand for wages on the check-up day, following Award 119/08. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 90][Para 92][Para 93] The attendance bonus is different: Article 103 treats bonuses as part of wages, and Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 does not define how many days count as regular attendance, so the panel held the bonus must be paid for the days worked and cut only in proportion to authorised absences. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 104][Para 106][Para 110]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An enterprise with at least 100 women must maintain both a breastfeeding room and a nursery, and a panel invited to inspect will not do so over the objection of the claimant union, so an employer that blocks the union's participation cannot prove compliance and will be ordered to put adequate facilities in place; a pregnant worker's authorised day off for a check-up suspends her wage claim but only reduces, pro rata, her attendance bonus. [Para 66][Para 81][Para 92][Para 110]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that only about 20 workers were pregnant at the time and that one or two a day asked for leave, a scale the company could easily accommodate. [Para 55]
