# Award No. 123/09 — Suty Vey Co., Ltd. (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ូត វ៉េ) and the Khmer Youth Union Federation (Arbitration Council, 28 September 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of three: Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Aing Eng Tong (president) · Doc ID 140434

## 1. Facts
Suty Vey Co., Ltd. (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ូត វ៉េ), a factory at Tuol Sangkae, Khan Russey Keo, Phnom Penh with about 600 workers, stopped giving work from 25 July 2009 and then suspended operations from 1 August to 30 September 2009. [Para 22][Para 29]
The workers, represented by the Khmer Youth Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ), demanded a clear statement whether the factory would reopen or close, payment of statutory benefits if it closed, and full wages and benefits for the idle period. [Para 26][Para 32]
Conciliation failed on both points and the Council heard the case on 9 September 2009; the employer, though properly summoned, did not appear and gave no reason, and the panel decided to proceed in its absence under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 16][Para 44][Para 48]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must state clearly to the workers whether it will continue or close operations, and pay statutory benefits if it closes? [Para 49]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay 100% of wages and all other benefits for the period in which it gave the workers no work, from 25 July 2009 onward? [Para 63]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), through the Khmer Youth Union Federation:** the company never answered their request for a statement on its operations, withheld information, and stayed away from the hearing, so they feared it would close without paying severance and other benefits; for the idle period the company should pay full wages plus the USD 6 seniority payment, the USD 5 attendance bonus and statutory allowances, and it should not have charged the shutdown against their annual leave, which they had protested. [Para 28][Para 34][Para 35][Para 65]
**Employer (respondent), Suty Vey Co., Ltd.:** did not appear and filed no submission, so the only account of its practice before the panel was the workers' evidence that it announced the two-month stoppage orally on payday, 5 August 2009, on the ground that there was no work, and that it deducted annual leave for 25–31 July. [Para 16][Para 38][Para 68]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel dismissed issue 1, ordered the employer to restore the annual leave it deducted for 25–31 July 2009, and ordered it to pay 100% of wages and other benefits for the suspension from 1 August to 30 September 2009, which the labour inspector had not authorised; the order is non-binding and becomes permanent eight days after notification. [Para 99][Para 101][Para 102][Para 104]
Preliminarily, the panel held that where a party has been properly summoned and fails to appear without a valid reason, the Council continues on the evidence before it, following Awards 53/04, 63/04, 148/07 and 99/09. [Para 45][Para 46]
On issue 1 the panel found no evidence that the company had decided or announced a closure, that the demand rested only on anxiety created by the temporary suspension, and that it was therefore a demand for the future; since no one can predict future events, the panel declined to consider it. [Para 52][Para 58][Para 61]
On issue 2 the panel applied Article 170 of the Labour Law: annual leave is taken at Khmer New Year unless the parties agree otherwise, and the employer must notify the labour inspector of any agreed alternative; using annual leave to cover a work shortage without the workers' consent was therefore improper, and the leave had to be restored. [Para 69][Para 73][Para 75][Para 76]
For the suspension itself the panel applied Articles 71(11) and 72(1): a suspension caused by serious economic or material difficulty must be placed under the supervision of the labour inspector, and a lawful suspension relieves the employer of paying wages, but an unlawful one does not. [Para 78][Para 81][Para 83][Para 84]
Because the employer was absent and produced no authorisation from the labour inspector, and the workers showed that the stoppage was announced only orally, the panel found the suspension unauthorised and ordered full wages and benefits for the whole period. [Para 87][Para 91][Para 92]
Arbitrator Ing Sothy agreed with the outcome but dissented on the union's standing, reasoning that the Khmer Youth Union Federation sought to represent individual workers rather than a registered local union and had not produced its statutes when ordered to, so representation was unproven. [Para 106][Para 115][Para 116]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer suspends operations without the prior authorisation and supervision of the labour inspector that Articles 71(11) and 72(1) require, the suspension does not extinguish the wage obligation and the employer owes 100% of wages and benefits for the suspended period; equally, annual leave may not be consumed to cover a work shortage absent the workers' agreement and notice to the inspector. [Para 83][Para 85][Para 75]

**Obiter dicta:** a demand that the employer declare whether it will close in future is a demand for the future that the panel will decline, because no one can foresee events, and anxiety alone is not a legal basis. [Para 55][Para 58]
