# Award No. 125/09 — វីនខេប ខប ភើរេសិន (Win Kheb Kheb Pheresen) (Arbitration Council, 09 October 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · employer-appointed arbitrator and worker-appointed arbitrator named in Khmer, chair not printed · Doc ID 140436

## 1. Facts
The dispute arose at a Phnom Penh garment factory employing about 600 workers; the complainant union had about 380 members and was registered on 14 December 2007 [Para 34] [Para 35] [Para 36]. The panel's jurisdiction came from Chapter 12 of the 1997 Labor Law (Articles 309–317), Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, and Prakas No. 190 of 02 September 2009 [Para 7]. Conciliation before the Ministry of Labour failed on two of twelve demands, and the non-conciliation report No. 846 was sent to the Secretariat on 04 September 2009 [Para 14] [Para 15] [Para 9]. The first issue concerned 28 group-4 workers dismissed on 28 March 2009, who held indefinite-term contracts and had received only final wages and unused annual leave pay [Para 37] [Para 38]. The employer said the workers stopped work for a morning on 28 March 2009 after a leave request for one worker was refused, returned their cards, and were dismissed [Para 45] [Para 48] [Para 49]. The employer treated the stoppage as an illegal strike causing production and delivery loss [Para 51]. The second issue concerned deduction of a USD 7 monthly attendance bonus for days of sick leave supported by state medical certificates [Para 66]. The hearing was held on 16 September 2009; both parties appeared [Para 11] [Para 17]. No witnesses or experts testified; the panel relied on party documents [Para 19].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the dismissal of the 28 group-4 workers on 28 March 2009 was for a valid reason and, if not, what statutory payments were owed [Para 37] [Para 77] [Para 104]?
2. (Issue 1) Whether the one-morning work stoppage was a strike under Article 318, and whether procedural irregularity made it a serious fault permitting immediate dismissal [Para 86] [Para 91]?
3. (Issue 2) Whether the employer had to deduct the USD 7 attendance bonus proportionally for days of authorised sick leave supported by a valid state medical certificate [Para 66] [Para 134] [Para 158]?
4. (Issue 2) Whether the employer's practice of deducting the whole bonus after one-and-a-half days' absence, even for authorised sick leave, was lawful [Para 153] [Para 154]?

## 3. Positions of the parties
The union sought dismissal severance, pay in lieu of notice, moral damages, and other statutory benefits for the 28 workers, contending they had not committed serious misconduct and had received no prior notice [Para 39] [Para 77]. It argued the attendance bonus should be paid pro rata for sick leave certified by a state doctor, citing Prakas No. 016/03 as support [Para 70] [Para 71]. The employer maintained the morning stoppage was an unlawful strike without proper notice, breaching internal rules and the employment contract and cited Articles 331 and 336 of the Labor Law, and justified immediate dismissal for serious fault because it disrupted production and delayed deliveries [Para 61] [Para 63] [Para 85] [Para 90]. It said the leave request for one worker was suspicious because she had been seen dressing for a party [Para 52]. On the bonus, the employer said the USD 7 monthly payment was for regular attendance only, exceeded the legal minimum, and could be forfeited for any absence, even authorised sick leave [Para 66] [Para 73] [Para 76].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted. The panel ordered the company to pay dismissal severance, pay in lieu of notice, and moral damages to the 28 dismissed workers, and to deduct the attendance bonus proportionately for authorised sick leave supported by a valid state medical certificate; no riel or USD totals were printed in the operative order [Para 159] [Para 160].

On dismissal, the panel found the contracts were indefinite and termination required a valid reason and prior notice [Para 79] [Para 82]. It held the morning stoppage met the elements of a strike under Article 318 [Para 86] [Para 88] [Para 89]. Although the strike did not follow statutory procedure, the employer had not obtained a decision of the competent authority declaring the strike unlawful under Article 337 [Para 91] [Para 92]. The panel therefore held that procedural irregularity alone could not be treated as serious misconduct justifying dismissal [Para 93] [Para 98]. It relied on Article 332, providing that workers recover their jobs after a strike, and Article 333, prohibiting employer sanctions for strike participation [Para 99] [Para 100] [Para 103]. Because the dismissal lacked valid reason, severance under Article 89 and notice compensation under Articles 75 and 77 were payable [Para 106] [Para 112] [Para 115] [Para 116]. Unused annual leave and final wages were not further considered because already paid [Para 119] [Para 120] [Para 123] [Para 124]. Moral damages were awarded under Article 91 because termination without valid reason opened that remedy [Para 126] [Para 131].

On the bonus, the panel treated it as part of wages under Article 103, and noted that certified sickness suspends the employment contract under Article 71(3), with consequences governed by Article 72 [Para 143] [Para 145] [Para 146]. The company deducted USD 2 for one sick day and the entire USD 7 bonus for one-and-a-half days, even where leave was authorised [Para 153] [Para 154] [Para 156]. The panel found that practice incorrect and ordered pro-rata deduction, noting the employer already paid USD 7 against the USD 5 minimum in Notice No. 017 of 18 July 2000 [Para 157] [Para 158] [Para 139].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A strike that is procedurally irregular is not, without a competent authority's declaration of illegality under Article 337, serious misconduct justifying dismissal, and an employer may not punish strike participation by dismissal, contrary to Articles 332 and 333 of the Labor Law [Para 92] [Para 93] [Para 98] [Para 100] [Para 103]. Where dismissal lacks valid reason, statutory severance, notice compensation, and moral damages follow [Para 106] [Para 115] [Para 126]. An attendance bonus forming part of wages must be deducted proportionately, not wholly forfeited, for authorised sick leave supported by a proper medical certificate [Para 143] [Para 152] [Para 158].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel approved earlier awards describing strike action as a right conferred by the Labor Law and noting that Notice No. 017 does not specify how many days of attendance earn the bonus [Para 94] [Para 97] [Para 141] [Para 142].
