# Award No. 41/09-ASD — ASD (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 22 April 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, An Nan, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 140375

## 1. Facts
ASD (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. employs 850 workers at Angkeo Village, Kantork Commune, Angsnoul District, Kandal Province. The claimant is the Local Union of the Cambodia Federation Voices' of Worker Union (CFWU) at the same factory. Nine collective demands were conciliated and eight were not; at the hearing the parties settled issues 1, 4, 5 and 6, leaving issues 2 and 8 (the transfer, suspension and reinstatement of the local union secretary, Mr C), issue 3 (production targets) and issue 7 (the overtime meal allowance) for the panel to decide. [Para 13][Para 11][Para 12]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issues 2 and 8) Whether the employer must reinstate Mr C to his previous work location rather than the new one near the accounting office, and cease all threats and restraints on the workers' rights? [Para 16]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must determine the production target in proportion to an eight-hour working day? [Para 31]
3. (Issue 7) Whether the employer must pay the 1000 riel meal allowance for overtime from 6:00 to 8:30 p.m. regularly each day? [Para 64]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, CFWU local union):** Mr C was moved and then suspended because he was the local union secretary and could not meet other workers at the new spot; the employer threatened to call the police to arrest him; targets calibrated to 10 or 12 hours force overtime beyond the legal eight-hour day; and the meal allowance was paid up to three or four days late a week and sometimes not at all. [Para 24][Para 27][Para 34][Para 40]

**Employer (respondent, ASD (Cambodia) Co., Ltd.):** the transfer moved Mr C ten metres with no change in wage, hours or skills, and he remained a mechanic; it agreed to reinstate him, but at the new location near the accounting office; it never called the police, only threatened to have steel measured; targets are fixed by the production section; and allowance payments were late but never by more than one week. [Para 21][Para 26][Para 29][Para 36]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel declined to consider reinstatement to the previous location, rejected the demand that the employer cease threats and restraints, and rejected the demand for daily payment of the meal allowance; it ordered the employer to set the production target in proportion to an eight-hour working day so that overtime is voluntary. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 72]

Issues 2 and 8 turned on jurisdiction, not merits. Article 312 of the Labour Law (1997) confines the panel to issues in the non-conciliation report or matters arising as a direct consequence of the dispute. The transfer and suspension of Mr C arose before the report of 14 March 2009 and were not recorded in it, so the panel declined to consider reinstatement to the old location, following Awards 153/08-Hytex, 06/08-Kingsland, 14/07-Supreme, 42/07-South Bay and 29/05-Kang Ning. On the threats, the workers produced no concrete evidence beyond the remark about measuring steel, and the panel applied its standing rule that the claimant bears the burden of proof (79/05-Evergreen, 77/08-Xing Tai, 101/08-GDM, 108/08-Hugo International). [Para 48][Para 51][Para 52]

Issue 3 was decided on Articles 108 and 137 of the Labour Law and clause 4 of Prakas No. 80 on overtime work of 1 March 1999, which make overtime voluntary and forbid coercion or discipline of workers who decline it. A target set for 10 or 12 hours makes the extra hours a condition of finishing the task, which is coercion in substance. Following Award 14/03-Chu Hsing, the panel held that the employer may set targets but only ones a worker of usual ability can meet within eight hours with reasonable rest. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61]

Issue 7 failed on characterisation. Under Article 103 of the Labour Law, wage does not include benefits granted exclusively to help the worker do the job, and the additional 1000 riel for overtime to 8:30 p.m. is such a benefit. No law governs its timing and there is no written agreement on it, so the demand for daily payment was rejected while the employer's undertaking to pay it stood. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 69]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may fix production targets, but a target that can be met only by working beyond eight hours is unlawful, because it converts voluntary overtime into a condition of earning the ordinary task wage; such a target must be recalibrated to eight hours unless the workers voluntarily agree to more. [Para 61][Para 62]

**Obiter dicta:** the employer's right under Article 2 of the Labour Law to direct and supervise the enterprise must be exercised lawfully and reasonably, and the meal allowance is a job benefit rather than a wage component. [Para 57][Para 67]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The document is an unofficial English translation of the Khmer original in which the definite article is fused into the following word throughout ("themployer", "theight", "thevidence"), and the evidence section was omitted from the English version. Issue 4 is recorded as agreed on 25 March 2009 although the hearing took place on 24 March 2009. The local union secretary's name appears in full in the source and is reduced to an initial here.
