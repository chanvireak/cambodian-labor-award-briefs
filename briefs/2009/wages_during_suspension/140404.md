# Award No. 84/09-Su Tong Fang — Su Tong Fang (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 22 July 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer, An Nan for the workers, chair Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 140404

## 1. Facts
Su Tong Fang (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. employs about 2,274 workers at Toul Sangke village, Russei Keo district, Phnom Penh. [Para 10] Facing a fall in purchase orders and a flooded site needing repairs, it suspended the workers' contracts for two months from 1 July to 31 August 2009 and, by a minute of 19 June 2009, agreed with the local unions of KYFTU and FTUWKC and the group leaders to pay US$ 20 in lieu of accommodation. [Para 12][Para 14][Para 16] The Federation Union of Development of Workers' Rights (FUDWR) then claimed a US$ 6 monthly living allowance for the suspended months; its local union was not yet registered, but 69 workers thumbprinted a written authorisation. [Para 10][Para 22][Para 35]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether FUDWR may bring this demand on behalf of all workers in the factory? [Para 31][Para 33]
2. (Issue 1) Whether the workers whose contracts were suspended from 1 July to 31 August 2009 are entitled to the US$ 6 monthly living allowance? [Para 32][Para 36]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** FUDWR argued that the US$ 6 living allowance is a monthly allowance mandated by the Royal Government to sustain workers' living conditions, so Su Tong Fang (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. must pay it without being asked, and that it owed the allowance to all workers, not only those who sought its help. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 26]
**Employer (respondent):** the company said the workers accepted US$ 20 instead of the accommodation it used to provide, that the 19 June 2009 meeting discussed only the suspension and never the living allowance, that the workers raised the allowance two days later, and that it had never been notified of FUDWR's existence. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 19][Para 26]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay the monthly US$ 6 living allowance to the 69 workers on the list of names for the two-month suspension from 1 July to 31 August 2009. [Para 40]

On representation, the panel applied Clause 19 of Prakas No. 099, which allows a party to be represented by any person expressly authorised in writing, and Award 136/08-Supertex; since the local union was unregistered it could not represent the workers, but the federation held a letter of 9 July 2009 bearing the thumbprints of 69 workers. [Para 34][Para 35] It therefore allowed FUDWR to act for those 69 only, after removing one duplicate entry from the list of 70 and giving the employer until 15 July 2009 to object, which it did not do. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24][Para 35]

On the merits, Point 1 of Notification No. 032/08/SCN of 17 April 2008 requires employers in garment and footwear establishments to pay a US$ 6 monthly living allowance that is not part of the main wage, and the minutes of 5 May 2008 provide that beyond the first month every worker receives the full US$ 6 "without any conditions". [Para 36][Para 37] Following Award 44/09-South Bay, the panel read "without any conditions" to mean that the allowance is owed whether or not the suspension was lawful, so the US$ 20 paid in lieu of accommodation did not discharge it. [Para 39][Para 40]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The US$ 6 living allowance under Notification No. 032/08/SCN is payable for every month a worker remains employed, without any condition, so an employer that suspends contracts and pays a separate sum in lieu of accommodation must still pay the allowance for the suspended months; and an unregistered local union cannot represent workers, but its federation may do so on written authorisation from each claimant. [Para 35][Para 37][Para 40]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the employer had never been notified of the local union because that union was not registered. [Para 11][Para 20]
