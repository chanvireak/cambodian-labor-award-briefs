# Award No. 43/09-Hugo International — Hugo International Investment Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 7 April 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry, Ann Vireak, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 140376

## 1. Facts
Hugo International Investment Co., Ltd. employed about 550 workers at Kbal Damrey Village, Dangkor District, Phnom Penh, of whom 445 remained at the hearing; 418 claimant workers authorised the Khmer Youth Trade Union Federation (KYFTU) and the Federation Union Development of Workers Rights (FUDWR) to represent them. Work ran short from January 2009 and the employer suspended the workforce three times: 1 to 15 January at 50% wages without Labour Inspector permission; 15 January to 15 March on a US$13 monthly living allowance, which the Inspector permitted; and 16 March to 16 May 2009 under a 6 March agreement with eight worker delegates, which the Inspector did not authorise. [Para 14][Para 19][Para 21][Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the workers' protest in front of the factory was a strike, so that the panel could not hear the case? [Para 29]
2. (Issue 1) Whether the third work suspension, from 16 March to 16 May 2009, was lawful? [Para 36]
3. (Issue 1) Whether the workers are entitled to full wages for the period of that suspension? [Para 37]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, 418 workers represented by KYFTU and FUDWR):** the suspension must end and full wages be paid, because Article 71(11) permits suspension only once and only under the control of the Labour Inspector; the 6 March agreement was never discussed with the workers by their delegates; and the gathering outside the factory was not a strike since the employer had no work for them to do. [Para 28][Para 25]

**Employer (respondent, Hugo International Investment Co., Ltd.):** the factory is still operating but has no purchase orders, so the suspension must continue for a period and it will provide US$13 per month to workers who present themselves twice a week; the protest was a strike and the panel therefore could not sit that day; and its representative's authorisation was valid, as a shareholders' letter of 30 March 2009 confirmed his power to supervise and direct the enterprise. [Para 6][Para 25][Para 17]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to cease the work suspension immediately and to provide the workers with full wages for the duration of the two-month unlawful suspension. [Para 45]

On the strike objection, Article 318 of the Labour Law (1997) defines a strike as a concerted work stoppage within an enterprise to obtain satisfaction of demands as a condition of return to work, which the panel read as three cumulative conditions. The protest failed the first: with no work available there was no stoppage, so there was no strike, and the hearing could proceed as planned despite the line of authority (28/06-GDM, 34/06-Goldfame, 97/07-Suit Way, 126/07-Heng Yu, 39/08-Zhong Yov) that a strike suspends the panel's ability to hear. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 32][Para 34]

On lawfulness, Article 71(11) allows suspension where the enterprise faces serious economic or material difficulty, caps it at two months, and requires it to be under the control of the Labour Inspector. The panel treated Inspector authorisation as a precondition, not a formality, and held that a bilateral agreement with worker delegates cannot supply what the law reserves to the Inspector; it therefore found the third suspension unlawful, following Awards 22/05, 46/05 and 49/05-Ocean and 59/05-Tack Fat. [Para 38][Para 40][Para 41][Para 42]

On wages, the panel applied its prior holdings (21/03-Loyal, 01/04-New Point (Branch 3), 60/04-United Arts) that an unlawful suspension entitles workers to full wages for its duration, and ordered payment accordingly. [Para 44]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of the labour contract under Article 71(11) is lawful only where the Labour Inspector has authorised it; the employer's agreement with worker delegates cannot cure the absence of that authorisation, and an unauthorised suspension obliges the employer to pay full wages for the whole period of the suspension. [Para 41][Para 44]

**Obiter dicta:** a protest held when the employer has no work available is not a concerted work stoppage and so falls outside Article 318, and the workers' objection to the authority of the employer's representative was answered by the shareholders' letter confirming his power to supervise and direct the enterprise. [Para 32][Para 18]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The text is an unofficial English translation with the definite article fused into the following word throughout ("themployer", "thevidence") and "letter" rendered as "detter". Sentences are duplicated at the referral of the non-conciliated issue and in the finding on the workforce count, where the figure of 550 appears twice mid-sentence. The award relies on a shareholders' letter dated 30 March 2009 at a hearing held on 23 March 2009, a date the text does not reconcile. Names of workers and employer representatives appear in full in the source and are not reproduced here.
