# Award No. 91/09 — Siem Reap International Airlines and the food-service workers' unions (Arbitration Council of Cambodia, 3 August 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Seng Vouch Hun សេង វួច ហ៊ុន (employer-appointed), On Non អន ណន (worker-appointed), Ang Eng Tong អាំង អេង ថុង (president) · Doc ID 140410

## 1. Facts
Siem Reap International Airlines (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អាកាសចរ សៀមរាប អន្តរជាតិ) is based at Kok Dong, Teuk Vil, Siem Reap, with about 100 employees, and ran its own catering service to feed its own passengers. [Para 40][Para 49] On 14 November 2008 the European Commission banned its flights and the airline suspended operations; on 4 December 2008 the Civil Aviation Secretariat suspended its air operator certificate. [Para 22][Para 50][Para 51] Catering nonetheless kept working by selling meals to Bangkok Airways until that airline stopped buying in June 2009 and opened its own catering. [Para 52]

On 4 May 2009 the company notified the 75 catering workers that the service was closing and their contracts would end on 1 June 2009. [Para 55] On 28 May 2009 those workers received final May salary, contract compensation, an additional allowance, pay in lieu of notice and unused annual leave — but no Article 91 moral damages. [Para 57][Para 58] The claim as filed sought moral damages for 75 workers; at the hearing the union reduced it to 59 workers with two or more years' service, dropping the 16 with shorter service because they had been properly notified. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the workers are entitled to moral damages under Article 91 for the termination of their contracts? [Para 71]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer had a valid reason for terminating the contracts, given that notice was shorter than the statutory period? [Para 78]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**ភាគីកម្មករនិយោជិត — Cambodia Tourism and Services Workers' Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត វិស័យ ទេសចរណ៍ និង សេវាកម្ម កម្ពុជា) and the Siem Reap International Airlines catering union (claimants):** Article 91 obliges the employer to pay moral damages because it ended the contracts by its own unilateral will without valid reason, giving less than the one month's notice Article 75 requires to workers with two to eleven years' service; that short notice is itself the wrong, so acceptance of the severance package was only provisional and the union wrote to claim the damages separately. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 59][Para 66]

**ភាគីនិយោជក — Siem Reap International Airlines (respondent):** the closure was justified — the European ban and the suspension of its licence stopped the flights, and the loss of its only catering customer ended the work — so the terminations had a valid reason; in any event it had already paid wages in lieu of the notice period it could not give, so nothing further is owed, and the workers signed that they had received all statutory entitlements and claimed nothing more. [Para 48][Para 60][Para 65]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the claim for Article 91 moral damages for the 59 workers whose contracts the company terminated, and ordered no payment. [Para 94][Para 95]

Article 91 gives a right to moral damages where a contract is terminated by the unilateral will of one party without valid reason, and the panel stressed that this head of damages must not be confused with pay in lieu of notice or with Article 89 severance. [Para 71][Para 72]

The panel then separated the two questions the parties had run together. A short notice period is not the same thing as the absence of a valid reason: for short notice the remedy is the wages and benefits for the days not observed, under Article 77, and the panel found the company had already paid that in full and the workers had acknowledged receiving it. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 64][Para 92]

On valid reason, the panel followed its earlier interpretation that a contract of unspecified duration may be terminated at the employer's will only for a reason connected to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the necessities of the undertaking. It found the necessity here genuine: the flights were suspended, the licence withdrawn, and the sole catering customer had stopped ordering. [Para 84][Para 85][Para 86] The workers did not dispute those grounds. [Para 87][Para 88] The panel therefore held the closure of the catering service to rest on a valid reason, and dismissed the moral-damages claim for all 59. [Para 93][Para 94]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Moral damages under Article 91 are available only where the termination itself lacked a valid reason; an employer that closes a service for a genuine necessity of the undertaking, and pays wages and benefits for the notice period it did not observe, owes nothing under Article 91, because short notice is remedied by Article 77 pay in lieu and not by damages. [Para 62][Para 92][Para 93]

**Obiter dicta:** that pay in lieu of notice exists to let a worker rest from work in order to look for new employment, and that a settlement receipt signed by workers does not bar a claim the law gives them, since an agreement contrary to law is void. [Para 67][Para 91]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The claimant headcount is printed as 75 in the non-conciliation report and in the award's statement of the claim but as 59 in the reasoning and the order; the reduction is explained at the hearing, yet the two figures sit side by side without reconciliation. The number of unresolved conciliation points is printed three different ways, including a three-digit form that cannot be right. The company's name is spelled at least four ways across the face of the award and the document list, some of them unrecognisable. A director whose name appears only in Latin script is truncated by OCR to a surname fragment and an initial, and is not reproduced here. OCR damage is heavy: the party-designation lines, the evidence list's numbering and the signature block are all corrupted, and the president's name is printed differently in the header and at the signature. [Para 9][Para 42][Para 68][Para 94][Para 97]
