# Award No. 161/09 — Sebbakam Prek Treang (សិប្បកម្ម ព្រែក ត្រែង) (Arbitration Council, 9 December 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Kao Thach (employer-appointed), Vaen Pov (worker-appointed), Kong Polla (president) · Doc ID 140458

## 1. Facts
Sebbakam Prek Treang (សិប្បកម្ម ព្រែក ត្រែង), a handicraft workshop at Prek Treang village, Sitbo commune, Sa'ang district, Kandal, employed about 1,200 workers and was not yet registered with the Ministry of Commerce. Two unions operated there; the complainant was the Democratic Union of Khmer Garment Workers (សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ, "Si Khou Dou"), which had filed for registration on 25 October 2009 and was still awaiting its certificate. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38][Para 68][Para 73] Of eight demands carried to conciliation on 11 November 2009, three were settled and five failed; at the hearing the workers withdrew Issues 3 and 4 as already covered by agreements, leaving Issues 1, 2 and 5. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 25][Para 26] The employer's two invited representatives attended without any written authority and then withdrew, so the panel proceeded in their absence. [Para 23][Para 58][Para 60][Para 67]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must recognise medical certificates stamped by a clinic or health centre, and not only those issued by state institutions? [Para 84]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must reissue worker identity cards bearing the correct company name, worker name, ID number, section and first date of employment, as promised in the 23 June 2009 agreement? [Para 98]
3. (Issue 5) Whether the company must recalculate and repay August 2009 wages that it computed incorrectly? [Para 108]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — Democratic Union of Khmer Garment Workers (សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ, "Si Khou Dou"):** the company had always refused certificates from private clinics or health centres, contrary to the 19 June 2009 agreement to grant leave on a doctor's prescription, and had refused maternity leave to a worker holding a Svey Pral health centre certificate; it had also never issued correct ID cards despite the 23 June 2009 promise, and had underpaid August wages. [Para 42][Para 44][Para 49][Para 52]
**Employer (respondent) — Sebbakam Prek Treang (សិប្បកម្ម ព្រែក ត្រែង):** absent and unrepresented at the hearing; in its written defence it accepted only medical certificates bearing a state-institution stamp and denied any ability to change the company name on the cards or to pay extra rice money. [Para 3][Para 6][Para 10][Para 12][Para 27]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to recognise properly certified medical certificates from state and private hospitals; to issue new worker cards with the required particulars; and to pay USD 1.49 in August 2009 wage arrears to one worker, the second worker's wage claim being dismissed. [Para 131][Para 132][Para 133][Para 134]
Threshold: the panel first asked whether the union could represent its members although unregistered. Under Article 268 of the Labour Law a professional organisation acquires the rights the Law recognises only once registered, and the union had only filed on 25 October 2009; the panel therefore confined itself to the three workers who appeared in person, there being no written authority from the roughly 1,000 claimants. [Para 69][Para 75][Para 77][Para 80][Para 82][Para 83]
On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 71(3) of the Labour Law, which suspends the contract during absence certified by an official doctor, and read "official doctor" as one trained at a Ministry of Health-recognised school holding a valid diploma and practice licence; it also relied on the 19 June 2009 agreement that the company would grant leave per medical prescription, so certificates from provincial, district, khan referral hospitals and commune or sangkat health centres must all be accepted. [Para 86][Para 88][Para 92][Para 93][Para 94]
On Issue 2 the panel held that an employer's managerial right under Article 2 of the Labour Law includes issuing cards, but that right must be exercised lawfully and reasonably, and both parties are bound by the 23 June 2009 agreement promising new cards within two months; since the company had issued cards that omitted the agreed particulars, it was ordered to reissue them. [Para 102][Para 104][Para 106][Para 107]
On Issue 5 the panel applied Article 118, which puts the burden of proving wage payment on the employer; the employer was absent, one worker proved entitlement of USD 65.26 against USD 63.77 paid, a shortfall of USD 1.49, while the other showed only wage components and neither his total received nor the shortfall, so his claim was dismissed for want of evidence. [Para 109][Para 121][Para 123][Para 125][Para 126]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An unregistered union has no standing to bring its members' collective dispute: until the registration certificate issues, the panel will hear only the individual workers who appear in person, and a union's assertion that it represents a thousand workers without written authority from each adds nothing. [Para 75][Para 77][Para 83] Where an employer has signed an agreement to honour doctors' prescriptions, "official doctor" means any practitioner licensed by the Ministry of Health, and the employer cannot narrow it to state institutions alone. [Para 92][Para 93] On a wage shortfall the employer bears the burden of proof; a worker who proves what was due and what was paid recovers the difference, while one who proves neither fails even though the claim is otherwise plausible. [Para 109][Para 126]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that wage disputes are the central problem it must resolve, but only where a worker supplies clear facts and sufficient evidence. [Para 127]
