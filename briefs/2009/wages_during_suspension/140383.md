# Award No. 54/09 — USA Fully Field (Cambodia) Garments Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 30 April 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tayseng, Huon Chundy, Pen Bunchhea chairing) · Doc ID 140383

## 1. Facts
USA Fully Field (Cambodia) Garments Co., Ltd., operating since 1999 with about 508 workers, suspended 42 workers of its fray-trimming section on half wages from 19 March to 20 April 2009, saying they had not taken responsibility for their tasks and had cut production. The Free Trade Union of Workers of the Kingdom of Cambodia (FTUWKC) local union, with 130 members at the factory, demanded the remaining 50% of wages because work in the section continued and was performed by ironing-section workers. Management also sought to reorganise the production line for long-term stability, a demand the panel never reached. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 2][Para 3]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay the remaining 50% of the wages of the 24 fray-trimming workers it placed on compulsory half-paid leave from 19 March to 20 April 2009? [Para 17]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, FTUWKC local union):** the suspension was not agreed; there was still work to finish in the section and ironing-section workers were doing it, so half wages were unjustified; only some of the suspended workers had caused the productivity drop. [Para 19][Para 21][Para 26]
**Employer (respondent, USA Fully Field (Cambodia) Garments Co., Ltd.):** the section's workers ignored instructions and did not take responsibility, production fell and market demand was missed; management therefore had to reform the line, and the 50% payment followed the established practice of leave at half pay. [Para 22][Para 24][Para 29]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to pay the remaining 50% of wages to the 24 fray-trimming workers placed on leave from 19 March to 20 April 2009. [Para 51]

Before the merits the panel narrowed the claimant class: on the evidence filed on 24 and 27 April 2009 it found 14 union members and 10 non-members who had sought the union's assistance, and confined its order to those 24. [Para 18][Para 19]

On the substance the panel treated the compulsory leave as a disciplinary penalty and tested it against Article 71(7) of the Labour Law (1997), which permits suspension of a labour contract for "temporary layoff of a worker for valid reasons in accordance with internal [work rules]". It held that an employer cannot suspend a contract as a disciplinary measure unless its internal work rules allow that sanction, following Arbitral Awards 54/04-Cambodia Sportswear (issue 2) and 70/04-Hanna Cambodia (issue 1). [Para 38][Para 39]

Applying that rule, the panel found the employer's reason — failure to take responsibility, falling production — was genuine enough to be misconduct, and Clause 10 of the Internal Work Rules dated 6 March 1999 does provide graduated penalties: a warning noted in the personal file for minor misconduct, a warning letter for medium misconduct, and termination for serious misconduct. But the employer imposed none of these; it only asked the workers to take leave. The suspension was thus a disciplinary measure absent from the internal work rules and non-compliant with Article 71(7). [Para 43][Para 47][Para 49]

The panel then disposed of Article 72(1), under which suspension of a contract affects only the main obligations of work and wage: because a suspension made without compliance with the law never takes effect, Article 72(1) could not be used to authorise half pay. The act of placing the workers on leave was therefore invalid, and full wages for the period were owed. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 51]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A labour contract may be suspended only for a reason the Labour Law and the employer's own internal work rules permit; where compulsory leave at reduced pay is used as a disciplinary sanction that the internal work rules do not list, no valid suspension occurs, Article 72(1) does not operate, and the employer owes the withheld portion of wages for the whole period. [Para 38][Para 41][Para 51]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that the workers' failure to take responsibility for their tasks "can be considered misconduct", and that the employer was entitled to discipline under its internal work rules, had it actually applied Clause 10. [Para 47][Para 48]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is an unofficial English translation of the Khmer original and is heavily OCR-damaged: "the employer" is printed throughout as "themployer", "a hearing" as "athearing", and the Evidence section is expressly omitted from the English version, so the panel's evidentiary reasoning survives only in summary. The worker count is given as 43 in the procedural background drawn from the Ministry report but as 42 in the panel's own facts. The non-conciliation report number appears both as "No. 286 KBIAKIVK" and "No. 286 KB/AK/VK". The schedule of 24 claimants repeats identity numbers — two workers are both listed as ID 3126 and two as ID 3129 — and the award prints each worker's full name and ID number, which this brief reduces to initials and roles.
