# Award No. 53/09 — Tack Fat Garment (Cambodia) Ltd. / ក្រុមហ៊ុន តាក ហ្វាត ហ្គាមិន (ខាំបូឌា) អែល.ធី.ឌី (Arbitration Council, 18 May 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry for the employer, Tuon Si Phan for the workers, Kong Polla chairing) · Doc ID 140386

## 1. Facts
Tack Fat Garment (Cambodia) Ltd. (ក្រុមហ៊ុន តាក ហ្វាត ហ្គាមិន (ខាំបូឌា) អែល.ធី.ឌី) employed about 2,400 workers and was itself in a state of suspended contracts for want of orders. [Para 42][Para 43] The claimant, the Cambodian Apparel Workers' Democratic Union Federation (សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា) through its Tack Fat factory local, put two demands: repayment of a US$5 attendance bonus withheld from two workers after unauthorised absences on 12 December 2008, and reinstatement of three carpenters. [Para 3][Para 5][Para 44]

Because production had fallen, the company dissolved its carpentry section on 14 January 2009 and reassigned the three carpenters to the boiler section at unchanged wages; they refused, and after three written warnings the company treated them as having abandoned their jobs. [Para 62][Para 66][Para 72]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer may deduct the US$5 regular attendance bonus from workers who were absent from the workplace without permission? [Para 89]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the transfer of the three carpenters from the carpentry section to the boiler section was lawful, whether they in fact abandoned their jobs, and whether the employer must take them back in the carpentry section? [Para 108]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, Cambodian Apparel Workers' Democratic Union Federation, Tack Fat local):** both workers admitted the absence and accepted the wage deduction, but not the loss of the US$5 bonus; the 2004 agreement covers lateness caused by traffic jams, whereas here they came to work and merely did not enter the section. They denied abandoning their jobs, saying the period was one of dispute resolution over the reassignment, and asked for severance if reinstatement were refused. [Para 48][Para 51][Para 71][Para 74]

**Employer (respondent, Tack Fat Garment (Cambodia) Ltd.):** the 2004 agreement preserves the bonus only for lateness exceeding five minutes a month for reasons such as traffic, and not entering the workplace is lateness, not absence with permission; the carpentry section no longer exists, the three were offered other work at the same wage and refused it, so they abandoned their posts under Clause 6 of the Internal Work Rules. [Para 50][Para 52][Para 70][Para 73]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both demands were rejected; the panel ordered the parties to negotiate in good faith to find work acceptable to both, failing which the three carpenters may claim statutory severance. [Para 135][Para 136][Para 137][Para 138]

On Issue 1 the panel held the 2004 agreement inapplicable because it addresses lateness of a few minutes caused by traffic, not walking out of the workplace for an hour or more. It turned to Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000, point 3, which gives a bonus of at least US$5 to workers who work the full number of days required in a month, preserved by point 3 of Notification No. 745 of 23 October 2006, and read it, consistently with Awards 62/04-Eisin, 15/05-Wing Tai 2, 62/07-Hung My, 143/08-Cham Teak Tea Y, 152/08-Wilson and 159/08-Favour, as an incentive for a full month worked without unjustified absence. [Para 88][Para 91][Para 92][Para 94][Para 95]

Clause 2 of the Internal Work Rules of 23 December 1998 requires punctual attendance and obedience to supervisors, and Award 159/08 treats punctuality as covering both the required number of days and compliance with instructions. The two workers were absent without permission from 12:00 to about 13:30 and to 14:53 on 12 December 2008, so they had no right to the bonus. [Para 97][Para 99][Para 101][Para 104]

On Issue 2 the panel accepted that an employer may reorganise and transfer staff under Article 2 of the Labour Law, but only lawfully and reasonably: no wage cut, no move to a distant site, no day-to-night shift swap, and no transfer to work far removed from the worker's skill, as held in Awards 39/07-San, 54/07-Young and 47/08-Grand Tech and in Awards 17/03 and 18/03-Hou Heng. [Para 115][Para 118][Para 120]

It found the move to the boiler section — hauling firewood — destroyed the carpenters' skills, offered worse conditions and more physical fatigue, so the transfer was not lawful and the three had no duty to accept it; they therefore did not abandon their employment. Reinstatement in the carpentry section was nevertheless refused, because the section had genuinely closed for lack of orders and no law obliges an employer to keep a section open. [Para 112][Para 113][Para 121][Para 124][Para 130]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The US$5 regular attendance bonus under Notification No. 017 is earned only by a worker who works the full required days of the month and obeys the employer's instructions; unauthorised absence from the workplace forfeits it even where the worker attended the site. Separately, a transfer that removes a worker from his trade to unskilled heavy work without his agreement is unlawful, so refusal to obey it is not abandonment — but the panel will not order reinstatement into a section the employer has genuinely closed, and sends the parties to negotiate, with severance as the fallback. [Para 94][Para 121][Para 130][Para 137]

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that, for work to be effective, employers should generally assign workers to work matching their skills and knowledge; and that the absence of any intention on either side to end the employment relationship kept it alive despite appearances. [Para 114][Para 132]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The Khmer text is badly OCR-damaged throughout: the opening page is a line of stray digits and diacritics, the closing signature block dissolves into Latin gibberish, and clock times are corrupted — one absence ends at "hour 130" where 13:30 is meant, another passage reads "10 122:00". Internal dates conflict: the transfer to the boiler section is dated 15 January 2009 in the facts but 16 January 2009 later in the same reasons, and one witness account puts the abandonment on 6 or 7 February 2009 without the panel settling it. The award prints the three carpenters and the two bonus claimants by full name, reduced here to initials, and one of the same workers is spelled two different ways within the judgment.
