# Award No. 73/09 — Global Apparel Limited (Arbitration Council, 10 July 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — កៅ ថា ច (employer-appointed), លីវ សុវណ្ណា (worker-appointed), គង់ ផល្លៈ (chair) · Doc ID 140397

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ក្លូប័ល អា ប៉ារែល ("Global Apparel Limited"), the Kandal factory formerly registered as Jusca Garments (Cambodia) Ltd, employs about 2,500 workers [Para 73][Para 45]. Three unions organise the workforce — សហជីព ខ្មែរ with 410 members, សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព សាមគ្គីភាព with 100 and សហជីព ឯករាជ្យ និង ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ with 300 — and both sides accept that none holds most-representative status in the factory [Para 74][Para 75][Para 78]. Of 22 demands, 11 were settled at conciliation on 28 May 2009 and 11 were referred; at the hearing the workers withdrew issues 8, 9, 10 and 11, leaving seven [Para 30][Para 34][Para 35]. The company is registered separately from PCS Co. in Tuek Thla although both have the same owner [Para 79].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must shorten Saturday working hours and release workers at 2:15 p.m.? [Para 80]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must add 400 riel to the meal allowance of workers who work overtime? [Para 87]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay an incentive bonus to the modern-market section as it does to the sewing section? [Para 91]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must reinstate Mr S S, a union secretary, to his post? [Para 100]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the company must add a third hot-water tank to each building and repair the hand-washing machines? [Para 115]
6. (Issue 6) Whether the company must pay a technical allowance to all mechanics? [Para 122]
7. (Issue 7) Whether the company must open the east gate to motorbikes as it does the west gate? [Para 129]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the factory belongs to the same owner as PCS Co. in Tuek Thla, so it should release at 2:15 p.m. on Saturdays as that plant does, and the 1,600 riel meal money no longer buys a meal since prices and fuel rose [Para 81][Para 87]. The modern-market section was denied the incentive bonus that sewing received, and all sections struck on 5 and 6 February 2009 over late January wages [Para 93][Para 94]. Mr S S was absent because the manager refused his leave request, and the east gate restriction causes congestion that leaves no time to eat at midday [Para 107][Para 136].
**Employer (respondent):** eight hours a day is what the law and the internal rules require, and a 2:15 p.m. finish would make the factory uncompetitive [Para 83]. It pays 1,600 riel where Notification No. 017 requires only 1,000 [Para 89]. The bonus policy is conditional on not striking, and sewing bonuses reflected output, not strike participation [Para 96][Para 97]. Mr S S abandoned his post after 20 days' absence, and the provincial labour department authorised his termination [Para 101][Para 112]. Hot and cold water are both provided in sufficient quantity, and motorbikes must use the main gate for security [Para 120][Para 131].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all seven claims were refused or declined, and nothing was ordered [Para 227][Para 233]. The order is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification [Para 234].

**Issues 1 and 2 — interests disputes.** Articles 137 and 138 cap full-time work at eight hours a day and 48 a week and leave the arrangement of hours to the enterprise; the company works eight hours, so it complies [Para 140][Para 145][Para 146]. A Saturday of seven hours would exceed what the law requires and is therefore a claim about benefits, not rights; the same is true of the extra 400 riel, since the company already pays 1,600 riel against the 1,000 riel fixed by point 4 of Notification No. 017 [Para 150][Para 151][Para 167][Para 168]. An interests dispute may be brought only by a union with most-representative status, which none of the three has, so both were declined [Para 152][Para 157][Para 172]. The two companies are separate legal persons and each may set its own hours [Para 148][Para 149].
**Issue 3 — strike and bonus.** No statute provides for an incentive bonus, but the company's own policy conditions it on the recipient not striking [Para 177][Para 174]. Under Article 332 the contract is suspended during a strike and no wages are due, so the bonus, one of the conditions of which is abstention from strike, was not earned [Para 183][Para 186].
**Issue 4 — reinstatement.** The union could not state the type or duration of Mr S S's contract, when he was dismissed, when he returned to protest, or why leave was refused; with the claimant absent, the panel found no facts on which to decide and rejected the claim for want of evidence (Awards 101/07, 10/09, 158/08) [Para 189][Para 190][Para 193].
**Issues 5, 6 and 7.** Prakas No. 054 of 10 February 2000 requires sufficient hygienic drinking water, which the employer provides hot and cold; the demand for a third tank had no legal basis [Para 196][Para 201]. The technical allowance has no basis in law, agreement or past practice and is an interests dispute, so it was declined [Para 205][Para 207][Para 211]. The employer's right to manage under Article 2 extends to choosing which gates workers use, and the restriction was reasonable for security; the panel nonetheless held the employer obliged to ease the congestion by posting security agents or negotiating with the landowner (Awards 17/03, 47/08) [Para 218][Para 221][Para 224].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that exceeds the minimum the Labour Law guarantees — shorter hours than the statutory day, a meal allowance above Notification No. 017, or an allowance with no legal or contractual basis — is an interests dispute that only a union holding most-representative status may bring, and where no union holds that status the panel will decline to consider it [Para 151][Para 168][Para 207].

**Obiter dicta:** an incentive bonus may lawfully be made conditional on not taking strike action, because Article 332 suspends the contract and wages during a strike; and where a security rule causes congestion at the gate, the employer must mitigate it even though the rule itself is lawful [Para 186][Para 224].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The file is a Khmer original with heavy OCR loss: whole passages are reduced to Latin fragments and digit strings ("and , and the to the control"), including one evidence entry and the closing signature block, so the employer-appointed arbitrator's name is partly destroyed [Para 49][Para 235]. The employer's name is printed at least six ways — ក្លូប័ល អា ប៉ារែល, ក្លូប័ល អាបាំ រែល, ក្លូប័ល អាបារ៉ែល, ក្លូប័ល អា ប៉ារ៉ែ លីមីធីត, ក្អប័ល អា ប៉ារែល and ក្លូប័ល អា ប៉ារែលីមីធីត [Para 1][Para 48][Para 51]. The internal rules relied on are those of ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្វាស្កា ហ្គាមេន, registered in 2004 under a different name, and the employer could not produce the correspondence by which the labour inspector approved their continued use [Para 38][Para 104][Para 106]. Union membership totals 810 against 2,500 workers while the three unions' figures are separately stated [Para 75][Para 157]. The procedural summary says 11 of 22 demands were settled, then that four further issues were withdrawn, leaving seven, which is consistent only if two of the referred points were duplicates [Para 30][Para 34].
