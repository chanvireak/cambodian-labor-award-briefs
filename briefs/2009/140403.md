# Award No. 82/09-ស៊ាវគិញ — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ាវ គិញ (អឺម អេហ្វ យឺ) (Arbitration Council, 23 July 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (ឈីវ ភិរម្យ for the employer, លឹវ សុវណ្ណា for the workers, chair ញាន សុមុន្ទិន្ទ) · Doc ID 140403

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ាវ គិញ (អឺម អេហ្វ យឺ) ("Seav Kinh") operates at Khva village, Dangkao district, Phnom Penh, with about 420 workers. [Para 35] Mr S had worked there since May 2000 in the cutting section on an undetermined-duration contract at USD 70 a month, and in 2007 was elected deputy president of the Khmer Youth Union at the factory and also served as staff delegate. [Para 39][Para 40] On 20 June 2009 an argument broke out between him and leaders of a rival union, and the company suspended the contracts of three men — Mr S, a worker and a staff delegate — pending the panel's decision. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 49] The two other men withdrew their claims after resigning, leaving only Mr S's demand. [Para 13][Para 37] Of 13 demands, 12 were settled in conciliation. [Para 10][Para 11]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer's suspension of Mr S's employment contract from 20 June 2009 was lawful, so that he must be allowed to resume his normal work as deputy union president and staff delegate? [Para 39][Para 56]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ and សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ, the two unions at ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ាវ គិញ, argued that the quarrel on 20 June 2009 was not serious misconduct, that Mr S had never been warned, that he kept receiving his wages and lost no benefit, and that he should be restored to normal service. [Para 42][Para 51][Para 52][Para 54]
**Employer (respondent):** Seav Kinh said it suspended the three contracts only to await the panel's decision, because it feared reprisals between the two unions that would disrupt the production line, and it conceded that the suspension was not for serious misconduct and that it had not filed a request to dismiss Mr S with the labour inspector. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 47][Para 50]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the company to end the suspension of Mr S's employment contract and to allow him to resume normal work from the date of the award. [Para 65][Para 66]

The panel first narrowed the dispute: the case concerned a suspension, not a dismissal, of Mr S, and the panel would decide only the lawfulness of that suspension. [Para 55] Article 71(a), paragraph 7 of the Labour Law permits an employer to suspend a contract for "temporary layoff for a valid reason in accordance with the internal regulations", and earlier awards, notably 54/04, read that provision as allowing suspension only as a disciplinary measure where the internal regulations provide for it. [Para 56][Para 59] Here the company neither asserted that its internal regulations mention suspension nor produced them to the panel, so Article 71(a)(7) could not be applied. [Para 46][Para 60][Para 62] The panel further found that the employer itself denied any serious misconduct, and that a suspension with no fixed duration cannot be proportionate to the weight of an offence as Article 27 requires when serious misconduct is not accepted. [Para 63][Para 64] The suspension was therefore unlawful and had to be lifted. [Para 65]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may suspend an employment contract for disciplinary reasons under Article 71(a), paragraph 7 of the Labour Law only where its internal regulations provide for suspension and it produces those regulations to the panel; a suspension of indefinite duration imposed where the employer does not allege serious misconduct is unlawful, and the worker must be restored to normal work from the date of the award. [Para 60][Para 64][Para 66]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that Mr S continued to receive his wages and lost no benefit during the suspension, a fact that did not cure the illegality of the measure. [Para 51]
