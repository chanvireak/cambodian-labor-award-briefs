# Award No. 159/09 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ថប វើល ដ៏ ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) អិលធីឌី v. សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ នៃ រោងចក្រ ថប វើល ដ៏
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia, Case No. 159/09, award dated 8 December 2009, made under Article 313 of the Labour Law; hearing held 27 November 2009.

## 1. Facts

The employer, ក្រុមហ៊ុន ថប វើល ដ៏ ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) អិលធីឌី, is a garment factory at Chrey Ampil village, Kbal Koh commune, Kien Svay district, Kandal province [Para 1]. It began operating in 1999 and transferred its shares and registered new articles of incorporation by letter No. 1689/06 dated 27 April 2006 [Para 47]. It currently employs 788 workers, 34 men and 754 women [Para 48]. The claimant union is the C.CAWDU local at the factory, formally registered by certificate No. 1685 dated 23 July 2009 [Para 49][Para 50]. The panel was constituted with Kau Thach appointed by the employer, Sin Kim Sean appointed by the workers, and Pen Bunhea as chair appointed by both arbitrators [Para 1].

Ten demands were conciliated by the Kandal provincial labour office on 26 October 2009, where three were settled and seven remained unresolved [Para 20][Para 21]. The unresolved file reached the Council secretariat on 16 November 2009 and the hearing was fixed for 27 November 2009 [Para 21][Para 22][Para 28]. At the hearing the parties settled issues 1, 2, 3, 5, 6 and 7, leaving only issue 4 for decision [Para 24][Para 25]. The settled demands — full wages during work shortages, permission for pregnant workers to leave 15 minutes early, conversion of probationary workers after two months, extra seating at the cutting tables, sick-leave documentation, and adequate fans at the sewing stations — are therefore not decided in this award [Para 3][Para 6][Para 10][Para 12][Para 15]. The remaining demand was that the company pay 50% of wages for the 90 days of maternity leave before the worker goes on leave, together with an additional month of leave granted in practice [Para 25][Para 26]. No witnesses or experts gave evidence beyond the parties [Para 29].

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. (Issue 4) Whether the company must pay 50% of wages for the 90 days of maternity leave before the worker takes that leave, rather than paying one month at a time during the leave [Para 25]?

## 3. Positions of the parties

The employer, ក្រុមហ៊ុន ថប វើល ដ៏ ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) អិលធីឌី, through its administration and accounting representatives, accepted that it grants an extra month of leave in practice after the three-month maternity period, and said the workers had agreed to drop any further claim on that point [Para 26]. It maintained that it cannot pay the whole 90 days in advance and asked to continue paying monthly as it always had [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58]. It offered a workaround: a worker who finds it difficult to collect the money may transfer the right to a relative or friend to collect on her behalf [Para 56].

The union, សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ នៃ រោងចក្រ ថប វើល ដ៏, represented by its president, vice-president, a committee member and its secretary, argued that the women want the money in hand before giving birth so they have savings to spend during confinement [Para 53]. It pointed out that the workers are poor and live far from the factory, which makes coming back each month to collect wages difficult [Para 54]. It relied on the Labour Law's requirement that the employer pay wages before the leave day, and maintained its demand unchanged after the company's offer [Para 55][Para 59].

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** The single remaining issue is granted. The company is ordered to pay 50% of wages for the 90 days of maternity leave before the worker begins that leave [Para 75][Para 76].

The panel began from Article 183 of the Labour Law, which entitles a woman on maternity leave to half her wage, including allowances, paid by the employer, and which voids any agreement to the contrary [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63]. It noted the one-year service condition in that article [Para 64], and read the text as an obligation to pay half pay to women on maternity leave [Para 65]. It then observed that Article 183 says nothing about the timing of payment [Para 66], so it turned to Article 115(3), which forbids paying wages on a day the worker is entitled to rest and requires payment one day earlier where pay day falls on a rest day [Para 67][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70]. Applying that rule, the panel held that maternity pay must be handed over before the leave starts [Para 71]. It found support in its own earlier awards — No. 57/06 (Evergreen), No. 97/06 (Chiv Mex) and No. 70/09 (Double U) — which had reached the same conclusion [Para 72][Para 73]. On the facts, the company had been paying one month at a time and had not paid the 90 days in advance [Para 74]. The panel therefore ordered payment of 50% of the 90 days before the leave, and declared the award non-binding, becoming final eight days after notification if no party objects in writing to the Minister of Labour [Para 75][Para 77].

## 5. Ratio decidendi

Maternity pay under Article 183 is half the wage including allowances, and Article 115(3) fixes its timing: because a worker on maternity leave is on rest, the half pay for the whole 90 days must be paid before the leave begins, and a practice of paying it month by month during the leave is unlawful [Para 61][Para 67][Para 71][Para 74]. An employer's offer to let a worker nominate someone else to collect the money does not cure the breach, since the statutory rule is about when payment is made, not who collects it [Para 56][Para 70].
