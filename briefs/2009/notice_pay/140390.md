# Award No. 65/09 — Nugget Textile (Pte) Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 11 June 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tayseng for the employer, Ven Pov for the workers, Pen Bunchhea chairing) · Doc ID 140390

## 1. Facts
Nugget Textile (Pte) Ltd., operating since September 2007 at Trea Village, Stung Meanchey Commune, Meanchey District, Phnom Penh, employed about 671 workers. [Para 28] The claimant, the Local Union of KYFTU (Khmer Youth Trade Union Federation), claimed to represent 266 members, evidenced by a check-off list, a figure the employer accepted. [Para 2][Para 28][Para 29]

Eleven demands went to conciliation; after the final session on 18 May 2009 four remained, and at the hearing issue 3, on termination payments, was settled and issue 4, on a 5,000 riel meal allowance for Sunday and holiday work, was withdrawn. [Para 6][Para 10][Para 13] The company rents its premises, says it has no space for a day-care centre, provides a nursing room, and used to pay US$5 a month in lieu until the workers disputed it and the global financial crisis made it unaffordable. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 33][Para 34]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must build a day-care centre and nursing room for female workers? [Para 2][Para 49]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must renew the three-month fixed-duration contracts of pregnant workers that expired in May 2009? [Para 3][Para 58]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, Local Union of KYFTU):** the Labour Law obliges the company to provide a day-care centre and nursing room so mothers can breastfeed, and the workers deny knowing of any nursing room; pregnant workers' contracts were never renewed — the company told two of them, one a sewing team leader, to take unpaid maternity leave and return after the birth — while the work continued and new workers were hired, so the non-renewals were discriminatory. They do not seek renewal for all pregnant workers, only those whose contracts expired in May 2009. [Para 32][Para 34][Para 36][Para 38][Para 41]

**Employer (respondent, Nugget Textile (Pte) Ltd.):** rented premises leave no room for a day-care centre, the US$5 payment in lieu is now unaffordable in the global financial crisis, it has provided a nursing room; and it does not renew contracts where workers have misconduct or poor evaluations, applying the same standard to everyone — renewals follow the needs of the production line, which the crisis has reduced. [Para 30][Para 33][Para 44][Para 45][Para 47][Para 48]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — on Issue 1 the employer was ordered to provide a day-care centre and nursing room for female workers, with the alternative of paying the fees of an external day-care centre for children over 18 months; on Issue 2 the demand for renewal of pregnant workers' contracts was rejected. [Para 56][Para 64][Para 65]

Before the merits the panel dealt with a representation objection. The workers argued that only the enterprise owner could bind the company under Clause 10 of the statute registered on 3 May 2007, so the head of administration could not represent it. The panel held that Clause 9 places management with a president who is not the owner, that the Internal Work Rules had been registered by the president with the worker delegates' agreement, and that the head of administration held a full authorisation from him. [Para 15][Para 21][Para 23][Para 26]

On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 186 of the Labour Law, which requires any enterprise employing at least one hundred women to set up a nursing room and a day-care centre in or near the premises, and, where children over 18 months cannot be accommodated, to pay the fees of an external day-care centre. Awards 63/04-Shine Well, 68/04-City New, 103/08-Vivatino and 115/08-Top One were followed. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 52]

The panel said the provision's purpose is to let mothers breastfeed for the first six months without formula, in line with government policy on infant safety, as recognised in Awards 77/08-Xing Tai, 103/08-Vivatino and 115/08-Top One. A cash payment in lieu — even one the workers had accepted, which they had not — would not meet that purpose or discharge the obligation. The employer must therefore notify the workers of the nursing room's location and build a day-care centre, or pay external fees. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 56]

On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 73 of the Labour Law and Award 100/07-Hoya Cambodia: a fixed-duration contract expires automatically at its stated date, ending the obligations of both sides, so neither party can compel renewal. The employer may therefore decide whether to renew, provided discrimination is not the basis. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 61][Para 62]

On discrimination the claimant workers must show concrete evidence, as in Awards 79/05-Evergreen, 90/06-Evergreen, 99/06-South Bay, 123/07-E Garment and 77/08-Xing Tai. Here the union gave no figures — how many pregnant workers were not renewed, how many replacements were hired — and no documents, while the employer attributed non-renewals to production needs. The demand was therefore rejected for want of evidence. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 64]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer with at least one hundred female workers must provide a nursing room and a day-care centre, and a monthly cash allowance in lieu does not discharge that statutory duty even if the workers agree to it; the only lawful substitute is paying the fees of an external day-care centre for children over 18 months. A fixed-duration contract, by contrast, expires automatically, and a claim that pregnant workers were singled out in non-renewal fails unless the union produces concrete evidence of numbers and replacements. [Para 54][Para 56][Para 61][Para 63]

**Obiter dicta:** that the management of an enterprise rests with its president rather than its owner, so a representative authorised by the president may bind the company at a hearing notwithstanding a statute reserving decisions to the owner. [Para 20][Para 21]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
This is an unofficial English translation carrying no Khmer script at all, so the parties' names appear only in Latin transliteration and the original spelling cannot be checked — a name defect in itself. The Evidence section is omitted, and OCR drops the "h" throughout ("themployer", "athearing", "cano", "cris"), truncates the employer's telephone number and runs sentences together, so that Issue 1's heading and Issue 2's facts merge in one paragraph. Two contradictions survive: the authorisation to represent the company is attributed to one named president at one point and to a differently named president later, and the panel adopts the second without acknowledging the first; and the reasons state that the employer terminated "the two workers" on 31 May 2009 without identifying them. [Para 48] The award prints the enterprise owner, the company president, several union officers and two pregnant workers by full name, all reduced here to roles and initials.
