# Award No. 28/09 — Yakjin (Cambodia) Inc. (Arbitration Council, 19 March 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phallack presiding; Chhiv Phyrum, employer nominee; Ven Pov, worker nominee) · Doc ID 140366

## 1. Facts
Yakjin (Cambodia) Inc., at Tasek village, Kambol commune, Angsnoul district, Kandal province, faced fourteen demands from the Local Union of the Khmer Youth Trade Union Federation (KYFTU): voluntary overtime free of coercion; accurate monthly wage calculation; a US$2 increase in the attendance bonus to US$7 a month; payment in lieu of annual leave twice a year at Khmer New Year and Pchum Ben; better conditions in the day-care centre; one day a month off with pay for pregnant workers' medical checks; clean toilets and sufficient clean water; proper conduct from group leaders and supervisors; an increase in the main wage; deduction of 1,000 riel in union dues from each member's wage; a US$5 monthly accommodation or transport allowance; the production building door opened half an hour earlier at noon; weekly payment of the weekly and attendance bonuses; and seven days of special leave on top of 18 days of annual leave. [Para 2][Para 3][Para 4][Para 5][Para 6][Para 7][Para 9][Para 10][Para 11][Para 12][Para 13][Para 14][Para 15] Conciliation on 20 February 2009 settled nothing, and all fourteen issues reached the Secretariat on 25 February 2009. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20] The worker party was properly notified but did not attend the hearing of 6 March 2009; the employer attended as summoned. [Para 21][Para 22] The panel therefore made no finding of fact on any issue. [Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether, the worker party having been properly notified and having stayed away without reason while the employer appeared, the panel should close the case by award under Clause 21 of Prakas No. 099 and Rule 4.7 of the Procedural Rules? [Para 26][Para 36]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the KYFTU local union at Yakjin (Cambodia) Inc. brought the fourteen demands listed above on 6 February 2009 and then neither attended the hearing nor appointed any representative to present them. [Para 2][Para 18][Para 32]
**Employer (respondent):** the company rejected every one of the fourteen demands at conciliation and attended the hearing as summoned. [Para 3][Para 4][Para 5][Para 6][Para 22]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel closed Case 28/09-Yakjin, leaving all fourteen demands undecided. [Para 36][Para 37]
Clause 21 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 allows the panel, where a party duly invited fails to appear without good cause, either to proceed in its absence or to terminate the arbitration by award, and Rule 4.7 of the Annex to the same Prakas is to the same effect, subject to satisfaction that notification was proper. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 28]
The panel applied the three conditions derived from Awards 30/05-Maurea, 16/07-Lotus, 27/07-M & V (Branch 3), 95/08-Yung Wah, 132/08-GHG, 138/08-Marlyn, 151/08-Wilson Garment and 09/09-Nimbus Textile: proper notification, non-appearance, and absence of reasons. [Para 27][Para 28]
The Secretariat sent written notice in its regular form and confirmed the date, time and venue by telephone, so the first condition was met. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 31][Para 32] The worker party did not attend and authorised no representative under Clauses 19 and 20 of the Prakas, so the second was met. [Para 32][Para 33] It gave the Secretariat no reason for its absence, so the third was met. [Para 34][Para 35]
The panel did not reach the merits of any demand, including overtime, wage calculation, bonuses, leave, the day-care centre, pregnant workers, sanitation, supervisor conduct, union dues or allowances. [Para 25][Para 36]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where one party, duly notified, fails to appear without explanation while the other appears, the panel may terminate the arbitration by award once notification, non-appearance and absence of reasons are each established, and it will do so rather than adjudicate the absent party's demands on the record alone, leaving those demands undecided. [Para 28][Para 35][Para 36]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the employer had rejected all fourteen demands at conciliation and that the complaint was dated 6 February 2009, neither fact being needed once the three conditions were met. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 25]

[Para 18] The list of demands duplicates and skips numbering, so fourteen issues are spread over fifteen numbered entries, and the fourth demand's answer runs into the fifth demand's text.
