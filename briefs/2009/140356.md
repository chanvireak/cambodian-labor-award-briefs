# Award No. 15/09 — Charm Textile Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 3 March 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ly Tayseng (employer-appointed), Huon Chundy (worker-appointed), Pen Bunchhea (chair) · Doc ID 140356

## 1. Facts
Charm Textile Co., Ltd, at Bonla Saet Village, Russei Keo District, Phnom Penh, employed about 596 workers in ironing, sewing, folding, cutting and storage; the claimant is the local union of the Free Trade Union of Workers of the Kingdom of Cambodia (FTUWKC), which claimed about 400 members but holds no certificate of most representative status. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 16] On 27 November 2008 the employer wrote to the Ministry of Labour asking permission to suspend all contracts for two months, 28 November 2008 to 27 January 2009, because buyers had withdrawn their orders after a strike; without waiting for a reply it announced the suspension, locked the gate, and required workers to punch in once a week on Thursdays, treating absentees as having abandoned their jobs. [Para 19][Para 28][Para 54] It later reported 417 workers to the Labour Inspector as having abandoned employment. [Para 30] Three demands — suspension pay, reinstatement of about 300 workers with seniority preserved, and severance in the alternative — went unresolved through conciliation. [Para 8][Para 10]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Preliminary issue) Whether the claimant union may bring the dispute on behalf of all 596 workers, given that it holds no most representative status and the authorising thumbprints are undated, untitled and partly duplicated? [Para 32][Para 40]
2. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay full wages and benefits for the two months of suspension, having suspended the contracts without the Labour Inspector's permission? [Para 43][Para 57]
3. (Issues 2 and 3) Whether the workers abandoned their employment or were dismissed by the employer, and what termination payments follow? [Para 59][Para 70]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), the Local Union of the Free Trade Union of Workers of the Kingdom of Cambodia (FTUWKC):** the contracts were suspended without the Labour Inspector's permission, so full wages and benefits are owed for both months; the roughly 300 absent workers held undetermined-duration contracts, never resigned, and must be reinstated with seniority intact or, failing that, paid severance under the Labour Law. [Para 19][Para 25][Para 26]

**Employer (respondent), Charm Textile Co., Ltd:** the buyers withdrew all orders after the workers struck without notice, leaving no work; it duly requested permission, gave the US$ 10 monthly accommodation allowance to the 140 who attended, and considers the 417 who never punched in to have abandoned their employment, so it will neither reinstate them nor terminate them. [Para 21][Para 29][Para 32]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to pay full wages and benefits for 28 November 2008 to 27 January 2009, and to pay dismissal indemnity, damages, notice pay, unused-leave compensation and outstanding wages to union members. [Para 86][Para 87]

On representation, the panel applied Articles 268 and 266 of the Labour Law, holding that a registered local union represents its members, and Clause 19 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, which lets a party be represented by any person expressly authorised in writing, so non-members could be covered only on proper authorisation. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 37] The documents filed on 18 February 2009 bore neither title nor date identifying them as an authorisation and contained duplicated thumbprints, so the panel found them vague and invalid and confined the award to union members. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 42]

On Issue 1, the panel applied Article 71(11), permitting suspension for up to two months in serious economic difficulty, and held that the suspension must be under the Labour Inspector's control, meaning notified to and approved by that officer, following Awards 22/05-Ocean, 72/05-North Gaiety, 27/08-Archid and 105/04-United Eternity. [Para 43][Para 45][Para 49][Para 50] It found the economic reason sound and within two months but the process unlawful, because the employer acted on 27 November 2008 without awaiting any response, and so became liable for full wages under Article 72(1), as in Awards 21/03-Loyal, 60/06-New Max, 74/07-Global Apparels and 53/08-Yung Wah. [Para 47][Para 52][Para 53][Para 57]

On Issues 2 and 3, the panel read Article 74 as requiring resignation to be clear and voluntary, and Article 72(1) as removing the duty to work during a lawful suspension; the demand that workers punch in weekly therefore had no legal basis, and it was the employer, not the workers, that terminated the contracts. [Para 69][Para 70] Dismissal indemnity under Article 89, damages equal to that indemnity under Article 91 — distinguishing 84/08-Trinunggal Komara, where valid reasons existed — notice pay under Articles 75 and 77, leave compensation under Article 167 and final wages under Article 116 followed. [Para 71][Para 79][Para 85]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A contract suspension under Article 71(11) is lawful only if the Labour Inspector has been notified and has approved it; an employer that suspends immediately on its own request and locks the gate owes full wages for the whole suspension. During a lawful suspension the worker owes no attendance, so a demand to punch in weekly cannot convert absence into abandonment; termination effected that way is a dismissal by the employer, attracting dismissal indemnity, damages under Article 91, notice pay, leave compensation and final wages, and an award can extend only to union members where the authorising thumbprints are undated, untitled or duplicated. [Para 49][Para 70][Para 42]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded the employer's claim that the 140 attending workers were willing to work at a nearby factory, an arrangement that had no bearing on the orders. [Para 29]
