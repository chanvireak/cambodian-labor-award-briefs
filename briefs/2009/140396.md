# Award No. 74/09 — Fortune Garment & Woolen Knitting Factory Ltd (Arbitration Council, 13 July 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ly Tayseng (employer-appointed), An Nan (worker-appointed), Pen Bunchhea (chair) · Doc ID 140396

## 1. Facts
Fortune Garment & Woolen Knitting Factory Ltd, at Sitbo village, S'ang district, Kandal province, employs about 2,700 workers [Para 27]. The claimant is the Local Union of the Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Worker Democratic Union (C.CAWDU), which holds most-representative status under certificate No. 002/08 of 8 January 2008 [Para 28]. Of twelve demands raised in April 2009, two were settled at the first conciliation, five more were resolved at the hearing, and four remained: clothing for each worker, two demands connected to two union activists, and the March 2009 attendance bonus [Para 18][Para 22][Para 23]. The employer had suspended work for one month from 1 February 2009 and notified resumption by posting on an information board; some workers returned late and lost the bonus [Para 46][Para 48].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must provide each worker with a set of clothing free of charge under Prakas No. 307? [Para 28]
2. (Issue 9) Whether the employer must pay the March 2009 attendance bonus to workers who returned to work later than the date in its notification? [Para 38]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Prakas No. 307 requires a free set of clothing for every worker, and the employer has never provided one since the Prakas took effect on 14 December 2007 [Para 29][Para 58]. The attendance bonus should follow clause 2 of the 7 December 2007 agreement, which deducts US$ 1 per day of permitted leave rather than the whole US$ 5, because the late return followed a suspension the employer notified only by posting a board the workers did not read [Para 39][Para 44][Para 47]. Issues 3 and 4 concern two union activists and should be treated as collective [Para 31].
**Employer (respondent):** few factories give clothing, Prakas No. 307 is not aimed at garment factories, and the economic slump means it cannot afford uniforms for 2,700 workers [Para 30][Para 31]. Clause 2 of the agreement covers leave with permission, not a late return after suspension; it notified the suspension on 23 January 2009 through team leaders and supervisors, and about 80% returned on schedule [Para 44][Para 49][Para 51]. Issues 3 and 4 are individual disputes for another procedure [Para 32].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to provide a set of clothing to the workers in accordance with Prakas No. 307 of 14 December 2007; issues 3 and 4 were declined for want of jurisdiction; and the attendance-bonus claim was rejected [Para 81].

**Issue 1 — clothing.** Clause 6 of Prakas No. 307 requires an employer to provide at least two sets of clothing a year free of charge, the first within 15 days of engagement, each set containing a skirt or trousers and a shirt in the right size [Para 54][Para 55][Para 57]. The employer had provided none since the Prakas entered into force, and its inability to pay was no answer: a Prakas of the Ministry of Labour is legally binding and must be strictly implemented whether or not the employer can afford it [Para 58][Para 60].
**Issues 3 and 4 — jurisdiction.** The panel observed that these two entries in the non-conciliation report looked unlike the other issues and stated no content; the conciliation officer had treated both as individual disputes, and the report and minutes recorded only that the workers wanted them included because the two people are union activists [Para 35][Para 37][Para 61]. Under Article 312(1) the Council has no duty to examine issues other than those in the non-conciliation report or matters arising directly from it; the panel found the Ministry did not intend to refer these two as collective disputes and that it therefore had no jurisdiction [Para 62][Para 67][Para 68].
**Issue 9 — attendance bonus.** Point 3 of Notification No. 745 and point 3 of Notification No. 017 make the US$ 5 bonus an incentive for regular attendance over the month's working days; the notifications do not define "regular", and Award 44/07 reads it as the month's working days, allowing for permitted holidays [Para 68][Para 69][Para 71]. Clause 2 of the 7 December 2007 agreement scales deductions for absence with permission, and does not reach a late return in breach of the 21 February 2009 resumption notice [Para 77][Para 78]. The workers conceded that those who returned late had not worked the full month — 20 of 100 in the packing section — so they were not entitled (Awards 94/04, 08/07) [Para 79][Para 81].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A Prakas of the Ministry of Labour binds the employer independently of its financial capacity, so an employer that has never supplied the sets of clothing required by clause 6 of Prakas No. 307 must supply them even during an economic downturn; and the attendance bonus is earned by attending the month's working days, so a worker who returns late after a suspension, in breach of the resumption notice, forfeits it [Para 57][Para 60][Para 78].

**Obiter dicta:** the Council will not assume jurisdiction over an issue that the conciliation report lists but that the conciliation officer treated as an individual dispute and that carries no stated content, since Article 312(1) confines it to the issues actually referred [Para 62][Para 67].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
This is an unofficial English translation and the evidence section was omitted, so the documents behind the findings cannot be checked [Para 25]. The translation systematically drops a leading letter ("themployer", "istbo", "admission"), including inside quotations of Prakas No. 307 and Article 312 [Para 54][Para 62]. The procedural summary is inconsistent: it says two issues were resolved at the first session and ten referred, then that twelve were raised and that issues 2, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 10 were resolved at the hearing, leaving four, while the list of demands runs to twelve numbered items [Para 18][Para 21][Para 22]. The two activists' names are spelled differently in the demand list and in the jurisdiction finding, and the award's own summary of their demand appears only in that later passage [Para 2][Para 66]. The non-conciliation report is dated 5 June 2009 in the reasons and 9 June 2009 in the procedural summary [Para 16][Para 32].
