# Award No. 116/09 — Xing Tai Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd / ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ីន តាយ ហ្គាបេន (ខេមបូឌា) ខូអិលធីឌី v Khmer Youth Union Federation / សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ (Arbitration Council, 28 September 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៊ឹង សុទ្ធី; អាន វីរៈ; គង់ ផល្លះ chair) · Doc ID 140429

## 1. Facts
Case No. 116/09 concerns the garment factory of ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ីន តាយ ហ្គាបេន (ខេមបូឌា) ខូអិលធីឌី (Xing Tai Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd) in Phnom Penh, which reported about 732 workers, some 600 of them women, while the company put the figure at 700 [Para 76][Para 77]. The claimant was សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ (Khmer Youth Union Federation) at the plant, with about 200 members although only 84 had dues deducted, so the panel found it was not the most representative union in the enterprise [Para 78][Para 79]. Conciliation on 12 August 2009 settled 3 of 13 demands, and the union withdrew two issues at the hearing, leaving issues 1 to 7 and 9 to be decided [Para 30][Para 35][Para 36]. The hearing was held on 8 September 2009 and both sides attended [Para 28][Para 33].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must restore the annual leave days it deducted while there was no work and required workers to take leave? [Para 3][Para 139]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay USD 1 for every 2 overtime hours and 500 riel an hour as an overtime meal allowance? [Para 5][Para 151]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay a 5,000 riel meal allowance for overtime worked on public holidays and Sundays? [Para 7][Para 174]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must pay a share of annual profits equal to one month's wage? [Para 9][Para 186]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the company must install a television in the workers' canteen? [Para 11][Para 193]
6. (Issue 6) Whether wages must be paid on the 5th of each month instead of the 10th? [Para 13][Para 202]
7. (Issue 7) Whether the company must provide transport home when overtime runs to 20:30? [Para 15][Para 222]
8. (Issue 9) Whether the company must arrange special health checks every six months at its own cost? [Para 20][Para 236]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ:** that the company had cut annual leave days by making workers take leave whenever there was no work, so the days must be restored; that overtime and holiday work was underpaid, that a meal allowance of 5,000 riel was owed, that the company should share profits, provide a television, pay wages on the 5th, run six-monthly health checks and provide transport after late overtime [Para 3][Para 122][Para 131][Para 136].
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ីន តាយ ហ្គាបេន (ខេមបូឌា) ខូអិលធីឌី:** that leave had been taken by agreement recorded with workers' thumbprints, that it already paid 150 per cent for overtime and 1,000 riel of meal money, that it was applying Notification No. 017 rather than the higher sums demanded, and that paying wages on the 10th was a reasonable practice [Para 4][Para 110][Para 113][Para 219].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to add back the annual leave days it had deducted and to provide safe transport home when overtime ends at 20:30; the demands for overtime top-up, meal allowances, profit share, a television, payment on the 5th and six-monthly health checks were refused [Para 249][Para 250][Para 251][Para 252][Para 253][Para 254][Para 255][Para 256][Para 257].

On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 170 of the Labour Law, under which annual leave is taken at Khmer New Year unless another time is agreed and the labour inspector is notified in advance [Para 140][Para 141]. It followed earlier awards reading that article the same way [Para 142][Para 143]. On the facts the company had imposed leave during work stoppages, taking thumbprints even where workers objected, and had never notified the inspector, so the practice was unlawful and had to stop [Para 144][Para 145]. Annual leave cannot be converted into money except on termination of the contract under Article 168, so the remedy was to add the deducted days back to each worker's balance for those the employer did not dispute [Para 149][Para 150].

On Issue 7 the panel held that night work is the 11-hour span containing 22:00 to 05:00, which it placed at 19:00 to 06:00 [Para 224][Para 227][Para 232]. Prakas No. 080 of 1 March 1999 requires an employer whose workers leave at night to provide safe transport to their homes [Para 223][Para 233]. The obligation was therefore ordered for workers finishing overtime at 20:30 [Para 234][Para 235].

On Issues 2, 3, 4, 5 and 9 the panel found the demands exceeded the statutory floor and were interest disputes, which it declined to hear because the claimant union was not the most representative [Para 158][Para 163][Para 182][Para 189][Para 197]. On the merits it also found that Prakas No. 080 sets overtime at 150 per cent, that Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 provides 2,000 riel of meal money a day for overtime, that Article 103 defines but does not compel a profit share, that no law requires a television, and that Article 247 leaves the periodicity of special health checks to a Prakas that has never been issued [Para 152][Para 170][Para 187][Para 194][Para 237][Para 243].

On Issue 6 the panel read Article 116(2) as requiring payment at least once a month while leaving the date to the employer, and found the 10th, with payment moved to the 9th when the 10th falls on a Sunday, to be reasonable [Para 205][Para 214][Para 215][Para 219].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Annual leave may be scheduled outside Khmer New Year only by agreement notified in advance to the labour inspector; an employer that imposes leave during work stoppages without that step acts unlawfully, and because leave may not be commuted to cash before termination the remedy is to restore the days [Para 143][Para 149][Para 150]. Night work is the 11-hour period running from 19:00 to 06:00, and an employer must provide safe transport home for workers leaving at night [Para 232][Para 234].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that a claim for benefits above what the law and Notification No. 017 provide becomes an interest dispute, which it will not decide for a union lacking most-representative status [Para 157][Para 181].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award date survives only as "28" with the month replaced by a stray word, so the month cannot be read from the face of the award. The annex listing the workers claiming restored leave, and the pages following the signatures, are destroyed by OCR: they consist of digits, initials and Latin fragments from which no names or dates can be recovered, so the claimants are described by role. Two amounts are printed inconsistently: Notification No. 017 is said to give 1,000 riel and also 2,000 riel of meal money a day [Para 170][Para 171]. The withdrawn issue numbers are garbled while the panel lists issues 1 to 7 and 9 as the ones considered [Para 35][Para 36]. The name of the claimant union's representative appears in several spellings across the file.
