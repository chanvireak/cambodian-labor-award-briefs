# Award No. 131/09 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន មេដក្រេស ថេក ស្ថាយ (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. and the Free Workers' Union of the factory (Arbitration Council, 15 October 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of three: ម៉ៅ សំបូរណយ (employer-appointed), An Virak (worker-appointed), Pen Bun (president) · Doc ID 140439

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន មេដក្រេស ថេក ស្ថាយ (Cambodia) Co., Ltd., a factory in Kandal province that began operating on 2 January 2009 and employs about 710 workers, has two unions: the **សហជីព សេរីកម្មករ**, the Free Workers' Union, registered on 17 July 2009, and the **សហជីព រួបរួមការងារ កម្ពុជា**, which holds the certificate of most-representative status valid until 25 September 2011. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 32]
Eleven demands went to conciliation, eight were settled, and three remained: music speakers in each production section, retention of the USD 5 monthly accommodation allowance during short authorised absences, and conversion of six-month fixed-term contracts into two-year contracts. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 37][Para 46][Para 55]
The Free Workers' Union, with only about 50 to 70 members and no most-representative certificate, is the claimant; both parties attended the hearing on 29 September 2009 and conciliation failed again on all three points. [Para 15][Para 33][Para 34]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must install music speakers in each section so as to reduce workers' fatigue during working hours? [Para 37]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must keep the USD 5 monthly accommodation allowance unchanged when a worker takes one to three days of authorised personal leave? [Para 46]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must replace its six-month fixed-term contracts with two-year fixed-term contracts? [Para 55]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), through the Free Workers' Union:** speakers playing music would lift the atmosphere, relieve boredom and fatigue and encourage work, and other factories in the sector already do it; workers who take one to three days of authorised personal leave should not lose the USD 5 housing allowance because they pay their landlords monthly whether or not they sleep there; and six-month contracts should become two-year contracts to protect union members from the anxiety of not knowing whether their contracts will be renewed. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 50][Para 57]
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន មេដក្រេស ថេក ស្ថាយ (Cambodia) Co., Ltd.:** speakers cannot be installed on a production floor because some workers like music and some do not, and irritation or distraction would hurt output and quality; the USD 5 housing allowance is a voluntary incentive on top of the statutory USD 5 attendance bonus, paid to those who attend 26 days, and it is reduced by USD 1, USD 3 or USD 5 for one, two or three days' absence unless the absence is certified sick leave or annual leave; and its survey in April and May 2009 showed most workers preferred six-month contracts precisely because they receive the 5% end-of-contract payment every six months. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 46][Para 52][Para 58][Para 59]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused all three demands and declared its order non-binding, to become permanent eight days after notification absent objection. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 104][Para 105]
On issue 1 the panel found that no law, prakas, notice, circular or other rule requires an employer to install music speakers, so the demand went beyond what the law guarantees and was an interests dispute. [Para 62][Para 63]
Applying its consistent practice, it held that only a union with most-representative status may bring an interests dispute, by reference to Article 277 of the Labour Law and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099, which makes an award on interests effective as a collective agreement for one year. [Para 64][Para 66][Para 67][Para 69]
It found that the Free Workers' Union had only 50 to 70 members and no most-representative certificate, while the United Labour Union of Cambodia held that status, so the claimant could not bring the demand; the panel followed Awards 81/04, 48/09 and others in refusing to consider it. [Para 74][Para 76][Para 79][Para 80]
On issue 2 the panel applied Article 2 of the Labour Law, under which the employer directs and manages the enterprise so long as it acts lawfully and reasonably, and found the USD 5 housing allowance to be a discretionary incentive above the statutory attendance bonus of USD 5 under Notice No. 017 of 18 July 2000. [Para 83][Para 86][Para 88]
Since the allowance was neither required by law nor fixed by any agreement, and the reductions for short absences were a condition of the employer's own scheme, the demand was refused. [Para 88][Para 89]
On issue 3 the panel applied Article 65 of the Labour Law, which defines the employment contract, and Articles 1 and 22 of the Civil Code (Law No. 38), under which a contract is formed by the parties' mutual agreement and cannot be imposed on an unwilling party. [Para 92][Para 94][Para 97]
Because the employer did not consent and the demand would force it to accept a contract term it had not agreed, the panel refused to order conversion; the survey it examined showed 50 workers for and 50 against open-ended contracts. [Para 61][Para 98][Para 99][Para 101]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a workplace amenity or benefit that no law requires is an interests dispute, which only the union holding most-representative status may bring before the panel, and an award on such a demand would bind the whole workforce as a collective agreement for a year; separately, the panel cannot rewrite the term of a fixed-term contract where the employer withholds consent, because a contract rests on mutual agreement. [Para 67][Para 71][Para 97][Para 100]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the USD 5 housing allowance is a voluntary payment more generous than the law requires, given in order to encourage regular attendance, and that generosity of this kind is not a legal obligation. [Para 52][Para 88]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
OCR damage is severe and the award is only partly recoverable. The employer's name appears in more than eight different Khmer spellings and the Latin name is unrecoverable; the arbitrators' names differ between the header and the signature block, where the employer-side arbitrator is printed with a variant surname. [Para 1][Para 105] The accommodation allowance is printed as "USD 5", "5 6 dollars" and other variants, the conciliation report number is given as 350/09 and as a garbled variant, and the cover page records the hearing as 25 September while the procedure section records 29 September. [Para 2][Para 10][Para 12][Para 49] The operative section survives only in fragments, with the issue 2 order repeated in broken form. [Para 103][Para 104] Individual workers and union officials are referred to by role only and their names are not reproduced here.
