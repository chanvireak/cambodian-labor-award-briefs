# Award No. 22/09 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ក្លូប័ល អាបាំ រែលី មីធីត (Global Apparels Limited) (Arbitration Council, 10 March 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bunchhea presiding; Chhiv Phyrum, employer nominee; Tuon Siphann, worker nominee) · Doc ID 140364

## 1. Facts
Global Apparels Limited (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ក្លូប័ល អាបាំ រែលី មីធីត), formerly JUSCA Garments (Cambodia) Ltd, has operated at Trapeang Toul village, Kambol commune, Ang Snuol district, Kandal province since 2003 and employs over 3,000 workers, of whom some 300 worked the night shift. [Para 1][Para 25][Para 45][Para 46] Seven unions exist in the factory; the claimant is the CCAWDU democratic garment workers' union of the factory (សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព-សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ នៃ រោងចក្រ ក្លូប័ល អាបាំ រែល), with 253 of those night-shift workers among its members, and it is not the most representative union there. [Para 1][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49] Three demands went to conciliation: keep the night shift, continue the contracts of 260 workers and convert fixed-duration contracts into indefinite ones, and pay night-shift workers moved to the day shift an extra US$30 a month. [Para 2][Para 4][Para 6] At the hearing the union withdrew issue 2 and the parties agreed to merge issues 1 and 3. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20] On 7 February 2009 the company closed the night shift altogether and moved all 300 night-shift workers, who all hold indefinite-duration contracts, to the day shift, citing collapsed orders in the global financial crisis. [Para 55][Para 57]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issues 1 and 3) Whether the company's closure of the night shift and transfer of the 300 night-shift workers to the day shift was lawful and reasonable, and whether those workers may claim an extra US$30 a month on top of their day-shift wages? [Para 72][Para 77][Para 106]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** night-shift work paid about US$130 a month against US$60 to US$80 on the day shift, the difference supported homes and rented rooms, and the contracts name two shifts; they would accept the transfer only with US$30 a month added, invoking Article 13 of the Labour Law on conditions more favourable than the law. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67][Para 71]
**Employer (respondent):** the factory's own internal rules put shift allocation in management's hands, clause 6 of every contract allows the company to change hours and shifts when necessary, the transfer was a response to a genuine fall in orders, and the 2007 precedent saw about 302 workers moved without a claim. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61][Para 64]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the demand that the night shift be kept, and declined to consider the demand for an extra US$30 a month. [Para 105][Para 137][Para 138]
Article 2 paragraph 2 of the Labour Law gives the employer the right to organise and manage the enterprise, including changing shifts, provided the change is lawful and reasonable; the panel followed Awards 62/06-Klick Su, 108/06-Trung Gal Vumara, 33/07-Gold Fame, 106/07-IM & V III, 84/08-Trung Gal Vumara, 08/09-Global Apparel, 54/06-Bright Sky and 53/08-Yong Wa I. [Para 80][Para 82][Para 85]
On reasonableness, the panel found the global crisis and the drop in orders real and acknowledged by both sides, and noted the night shift had only ever existed to absorb periods of heavy orders. [Para 86][Para 87][Para 88][Para 90][Para 92] It also relied on Article 65 and Article 22 of Kret 38 — the contract is the law of the parties — and on clause 6, by which the workers had already agreed that shifts follow the company's decision when necessary. [Para 93][Para 96][Para 102][Para 103]
On the US$30, the panel held that lower day-shift pay is not a lost benefit: Article 144 (new) fixes night work at 130 percent of the day rate, so the wage follows the shift, and Article 106 requires equal pay only for equal work. [Para 108][Para 109][Para 112][Para 117][Para 118][Para 120]
Because the sum appears neither in the Labour Law nor in any agreement, the claim was an interest dispute, and since the union is not most representative — an award would replace the collective agreement for a year and bind all 3,000 workers — the panel declined to consider it. [Para 123][Para 126][Para 129][Para 130][Para 132][Para 136]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may abolish a shift and move workers holding indefinite-duration contracts to another shift where the contract itself provides that shifts follow the company's decision when necessary and the change answers a genuine collapse in orders; wages then follow the shift under Article 144 (new), so no benefit is lost, and a demand for an extra allowance to offset the change is an interest dispute the panel will not hear from a union that is not most representative. [Para 92][Para 103][Para 117][Para 130]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the company had moved about 302 night-shift workers to the day shift in September 2007 without any claim, and accepted the union's explanation that the workers had not understood their position at the time. [Para 61][Para 63]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The case is named "ក្លូប័ល អា ប៉ារែល" on the face, and the company is printed as "ក្លូប័ល អាបាំ រែលី មីធីត", "ក្លូប័ល អា ប៉ារែលី មីធីត" and "ក្លូប័ល អា ប៉ារែល" in the body. [Para 1][Para 41][Para 42][Para 45] The chair is printed "ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា" in the panel list, and the worker nominee appears as "ទួន ស៊ី ផាន់" in the panel list but "នួន ស៊ី ផាន់" at the signature. [Para 1][Para 140] The evidence schedule restarts numbering, printing two items as "១-" and omitting item 13. [Para 33][Para 41] Paragraph 139 repeats the eight-day objection clause twice in one sentence, and the US$30 figure is printed both as "ចំនួន៣០" and "ចំនួន ៣០". [Para 6][Para 73][Para 139] Employer representatives are named in the header but their list is interleaved with the parties' positions, so no representative is named here. [Para 1][Para 2]
