# Award No. 93/09 — NagaWorld Ltd and the Khmer Workers' Rights Union at NagaWorld (Arbitration Council of Cambodia, 6 August 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Eng Sothy អ៊ឹង សុទ្ធី (employer-appointed), An Virak អាន វីរៈ (worker-appointed), Kong Phally គង់ ផល្លៈ (president) · Doc ID 140412

## 1. Facts
The NagaWorld Ltd branch (សាខា ក្រុមហ៊ុន ណាហ្គាវើល ដ៏ លីមីធីត) at the Hun Sen Park, Chamkar Morn, Phnom Penh, employs about 3,100 workers. [Para 1][Para 39] The Khmer Workers' Rights Union of NagaWorld resort (សហជីព ទ្រង់ សិទ្ធិ ការងារ បុគ្គលិក កម្មករ ខ្មែរ នៃ មណ្ឌល កំសាន្ត ណាហ្គា), founded in 2000 with about 950 members, brought the case through the Cambodia Tourism and Services Workers' Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត វិស័យ ទេសចរណ៍ និង សេវាកម្ម កម្ពុជា); neither is the most representative union at the site. [Para 1][Para 40][Para 41]

Both sides agree the company deducted 2,000 riel a month from members' wages for the union from late 2006 until it stopped in March 2009. [Para 42] The union's members asked for the deduction to resume because paying dues directly was difficult, and they signed or thumbprinted their consent. [Para 2][Para 48] The single point referred was whether the company must resume the deduction; the file also holds a list of 42 workers supporting an earlier, separate demand for the return of union leaders and activists, which this award does not decide. [Para 10][Para 33]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must resume deducting 2,000 riel a month from members' wages and pay it to the union? [Para 43]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**ភាគីកម្មករនិយោជិត — Khmer Workers' Rights Union of NagaWorld (សហជីព ទ្រង់ សិទ្ធិ ការងារ បុគ្គលិក កម្មករ ខ្មែរ នៃ មណ្ឌល កំសាន្ត ណាហ្គា) and the Cambodia Tourism and Services Workers' Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត វិស័យ ទេសចរណ៍ និង សេវាកម្ម កម្ពុជា), claimants:** Article 129 of the Labour Law and paragraph 5 of Prakas No. 305 require the employer to honour a member's written authorisation to deduct dues, and the company did so for two years, so it should continue; since March 2009 the union has collected directly from about 590 members rather than leave them unpaid, and the members have again asked the company to deduct. [Para 1][Para 43][Para 47][Para 48]

**ភាគីនិយោជក — NagaWorld Ltd (respondent):** it cannot resume because it has moved to new payroll software that cannot perform the deduction, because the union keeps suing the company, and because Article 281 forbids employers from deducting wages for union dues. [Para 3][Para 44]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel refused the demand that the company resume the existing 2,000 riel deduction as a standing arrangement, but ordered that the company must deduct dues and remit them to the union whenever the union satisfies Article 129(2) and paragraph 5 of Prakas No. 305. [Para 78][Para 79]

The panel read Article 129(2) as permitting a worker to authorise deduction of union dues in writing, an authorisation revocable at any time, and paragraph 5 of Prakas No. 305 as requiring the request to be made in writing at least 15 days in advance, whereupon the employer must act on it. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58] It followed its earlier awards holding that a union seeking deduction must give the employer a document sufficient in law — a list of names bearing each worker's signature or thumbprint, or individual written consents — and that the employer must then deduct monthly and hand the money to the union. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 66]

Those formalities were not met here. The panel had ordered both sides to file the 2006 authorisations and any fresh request by 24 July 2009; the union filed nothing, and could not show the name list, the members' requests or the written authorisation of any member. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 73][Para 75] Its claim therefore failed, not on the merits of the principle, but for want of the documents the law requires. [Para 74][Para 76]

The employer's own reasons were rejected as unlawful: neither resentment at being sued nor a change of payroll software is a legal ground for refusing a statutory duty, and Article 281 was read, as in earlier awards, as protecting workers from employer interference in union affairs rather than as a bar on deduction made at the members' own written request. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 71]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer must deduct union dues from a member's wage and remit them to the union once the union produces the written authorisations and name list required by Article 129(2) and paragraph 5 of Prakas No. 305; without those documents the claim fails, and an employer's administrative convenience or dislike of litigation is no defence once they exist. [Para 66][Para 71][Para 79]

**Obiter dicta:** that Article 281 aims to stop employers from interfering in or capturing a union, not to prevent a deduction the workers themselves request in writing. [Para 67]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The monthly figure is printed as 2,000 riel in the facts, the reasoning and the order, but as 200 riel in the summary paragraph that precedes the order, a tenfold difference within three pages. The union's name is given in at least three Khmer variants and once with a different word order, and the company appears both as a branch and as the parent limited company. One document in the file is dated with its month printed twice, and another bears a case number that is not this one. The employee lists and the foreign settlement documents name dozens of individuals in full, including two foreign managers; none is reproduced here. OCR damage is heavy in the party designations and the signature block, and the employer-appointed arbitrator's name is printed twice with different diacritics. [Para 30][Para 42][Para 76][Para 78][Para 81]
