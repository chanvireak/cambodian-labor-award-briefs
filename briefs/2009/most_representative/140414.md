# Award No. 92/09 — Joon Tech Thai (Mithona) Co., Ltd v Khmer Youth Union of Joon Tech Thai (Mithona) Company
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia (ក្រុមប្រឹក្សា អាជ្ញា កណ្តាល), Phnom Penh Centre, Sothearos Blvd, Sangkat Tonle Basak, Khan Chamkar Morn, Phnom Penh; award dated 13 August 2009, hearing 24 July 2009 at 14:00. Panel: Eng Sothy `អ៊ីង សុទ្ធី` (employer-appointed), Tuon Si Phan `ទូន ស៊ី ផាន់` (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea `ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា` (president). Doc ID 140414.

## 1. Facts

The employer is Joon Tech Thai (Mithona) Co., Ltd (`ក្រុមហ៊ុន ជូន តិច ថា យល៍ (មិថុនា)`), on Federation of Russia Blvd, Sangkat Tuek Thla, Khan Sen Sok, Phnom Penh. It began in 1997 and employed about 4,800 workers, 4,650 of them women [Para 40][Para 41]. Two unions operate there: the Khmer Youth Union of Joon Tech Thai (Mithona) Company, with about 1,200 members, and the Free Workers Union, with about 2,300; neither held most-representative status [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45].

The Khmer Youth Union filed a complaint on 4 June 2009 seeking better conditions [Para 17]. Conciliation on 3 July 2009 left all five demands unsettled [Para 18]. Non-conciliation report No. 566 K.B/A.K/V.K was dated 13 July 2009 and reached the secretariat on 15 July 2009 [Para 14][Para 19]. Invitations issued 16 July 2009 brought both parties to the hearing, where conciliation again failed [Para 20][Para 22]. The five referred demands were the only matters in issue: a 500 riel per hour meal allowance for overtime, an additional USD 5 monthly attendance bonus, an additional USD 1 seniority bonus for each year beyond five years, a profit share equal to one month's base wage per year with arrears, and enlargement of the factory daycare [Para 2][Para 46].

The company runs two shifts, 06:15–14:15 and 14:15–22:15, and overtime is voluntary [Para 46][Para 47]. It pays a flat 1,000 riel meal allowance per day of overtime regardless of hours, with most overtime running 22:15 to 05:00 [Para 49][Para 50]. It pays a USD 5 monthly attendance bonus under Notification No. 017 and seniority pay of USD 2 in year one rising USD 1 in each of years two to four, with no increment after five years [Para 61][Para 70][Para 127]. The daycare is a room 4 by 9.5 metres with four windows, a door, two wall fans, six beds and a toilet, used by 10 to 20 children in the morning and 5 to 10 in the afternoon [Para 81][Para 82].

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay a meal allowance of 500 riel per hour of overtime, in addition to the flat 1,000 riel per day it already pays [Para 1]?
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay an additional USD 5 per month as a regular attendance bonus [Para 1]?
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay an additional USD 1 of seniority bonus for every year of service beyond five years [Para 1]?
4. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must pay workers a profit share equal to one month's base wage for each year, including past years in which nothing was paid [Para 1]?
5. (Issue 5) Whether the employer must enlarge the daycare to meet the standards of the Ministry of Labour's prakas [Para 1]?

## 3. Positions of the parties

The Khmer Youth Union argued the 1,000 riel daily allowance is inadequate and that 500 riel per hour would encourage voluntary overtime, [Para 51][Para 53]. It read Notification No. 017 as setting a minimum USD 5 attendance bonus that workers may ask to have increased, and argued the seniority scale cannot sensibly stop at year five when workers may serve 20 or 30 years [Para 63][Para 64][Para 68][Para 69]. It asserted the company earns annual profits from workers' effort, evidenced by overtime running to 05:00 and the opening of another branch [Para 71][Para 73][Para 75]. It maintained the daycare is cramped and badly arranged, that workers fear their children will fall ill there, and that it should be enlarged  [Para 85][Para 86][Para 87].

The company replied that it cannot pay 500 riel per hour and applies Notification No. 017, paying 1,000 riel per day without regard to hours [Para 54]. It noted the global economic crisis had cut order prices and that it had already folded seniority pay and a living allowance into base wages while paying overtime at 200 per cent [Para 55][Para 56]. It said it cannot raise the attendance bonus or seniority pay and has no capacity to share profits, asking the panel to decide that last point [Para 65][Para 77]. It described the daycare as spacious, well ventilated and convenient, said it would consider enlarging it if more children were sent [Para 70][Para 81][Para 88].

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** All five demands were rejected. The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Minister of Labour [Para 170][Para 175].

On Issue 1 the panel held that point 4 of Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 fixes the meal allowance at 1,000 riel "per day", not per hour, and the company complies by paying 1,000 riel whatever the hours [Para 91][Para 92][Para 98]. No article of the Labour Law and no written agreement requires 500 riel per hour, so the demand exceeds what the law provides and is an interest dispute [Para 99][Para 100][Para 101]. Because the claimant union lacks most-representative status, it has no legal right to bring an interest dispute to the Council, and the panel declined to consider the demand [Para 103][Para 113][Para 114].

On Issue 2 the panel found that point 3 of Notification No. 745 of 23 October 2006 preserved the benefits under points 3 to 6 of Notification No. 017, and that no Labour Law provision requires an additional USD 5 monthly attendance bonus or any written agreement to that effect [Para 117][Para 122][Para 123]. The demand was an interest claim beyond the law [Para 124].

On Issue 3 the panel noted that point 5 of Notification No. 017 stops at four years' service and says nothing about five years and beyond, so it does not require a further USD 1 each year, and no agreement or practice supports the claim [Para 135][Para 136][Para 137]. The demand was an interest claim beyond the law [Para 138].

On Issue 4 the panel observed that the workers produced no report, document or evidence showing the company made a profit, while the company asserted losses from the global downturn [Para 74][Para 76]. The demand was rejected [Para 173].

On Issue 5 the panel found the company had already set up a daycare for employees' children and that Article 187 of the Labour Law leaves detailed hygiene standards to a ministerial prakas which the Ministry had not yet issued [Para 159][Para 161][Para 162]. Workers identified no specific number of children wishing to enrol and offered no evidence that the existing daycare harms children's health [Para 165][Para 166]. The panel rejected the demand [Para 167][Para 169].

## 5. Ratio decidendi

A demand beyond the floor set by a ministry notification, with no article or written agreement behind it, is an interest dispute rather than a rights dispute [Para 101][Para 104]. A union without most-representative certification has no standing to bring an interest dispute to the Arbitration Council [Para 108][Para 113][Para 114]. Where a notification fixes an allowance "per day", the Council will not convert it into an hourly entitlement [Para 92]. Claims for profit share, expanded daycare or higher bonuses fail where the claimant produces no documentary evidence of profit, enrolment numbers or health risk [Para 166][Para 173].

## 6. Source defects disclosed

The source is a Khmer scan with substantial OCR corruption: the party box and company name are rendered inconsistently, appearing as `ជូន តិច ថា យល៍ (មិថុនា)` in the heading, `ក្រុមហ៊ុន មិថុនា` in the document list and `ក្រុមហ៊ុន Lin` in the Issue 2 reasoning, so the English transliteration in the title is an approximation. The numeral "5" in the attendance-bonus demand appears as "៥៦០" in one line and "៥" elsewhere [Para 4][Para 61]. The non-conciliation report number appears as 566 and the hearing-invitation numbers as 449 and 450 with inconsistent dates [Para 14][Para 38][Para 39]. Article references and the names of cited precedents are partly garbled. The panel's reasoning on Issue 1 is internally repetitive and contains a duplicated paragraph [Para 106].
