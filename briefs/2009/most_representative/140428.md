# Award No. 117/09 — Thai Bun Rong Cement Co., Ltd / ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ីម៉ង់ត៍ ថៃ ប៊ុន រ៉ុង v Democratic Workers' Union / សហជីព កម្មករ ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ (Arbitration Council, 23 September 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (គល់ វឌ្ឍនា; អាន វីរៈ; តាំង ទ្រី chair) · Doc ID 140428

## 1. Facts
Case No. 117/09 concerns the cement plant of ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ីម៉ង់ត៍ ថៃ ប៊ុន រ៉ុង (Thai Bun Rong Cement Co., Ltd) at L'Ang village, Kampot province, which employed about 500 workers [Para 2][Para 42]. The claimant was សហជីព កម្មករ ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ (Democratic Workers' Union) of the plant, registered under certificate No. 1617 of 11 February 2009 and represented before the panel by the Cambodia Labour Union, to which it had transferred its rights [Para 29][Para 43][Para 44]. Of 12 demands, conciliation on 14 August 2009 settled 9 and left 3 for the panel, which decided four numbered issues [Para 16][Para 17]. The hearing was set for 7 September 2009; the union attended, the employer did not, and the panel proceeded in absentia [Para 13][Para 19][Para 93].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must reinstate the dismissed union secretary and pay his wages from the date of dismissal until reinstatement? [Para 45][Para 94]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must reinstate the 9 security guards and 2 workers it refused to take back? [Para 63][Para 146]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay a USD 5 regular-attendance bonus and a USD 6 living allowance each month? [Para 74][Para 161]
4. (Issue 4) Whether Sunday work must be paid at 200 per cent of the ordinary wage rather than the 1.5 times the company offered? [Para 82][Para 192]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សហជីព កម្មករ ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ:** that the company seriously violated freedom of association on 1 June 2009 by dismissing the union secretary, a popular organiser, without valid reason, without a written contract and without the labour inspector's approval [Para 59][Para 68]. It also sought reinstatement and back pay for 14 guards and 3 workers, and 200 per cent pay for Sunday work [Para 147][Para 192].
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ីម៉ង់ត៍ ថៃ ប៊ុន រ៉ុង:** that the secretary was dismissed for failing to respect the internal rules, intimidating other workers and obstructing its instructions; that the guards were let go after extensive theft and the matter was already settled; and that it had agreed to pay only 1.5 times for Sunday work [Para 52][Para 148][Para 151][Para 192].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to reinstate the union secretary and pay his wages from 1 June 2009 until reinstatement, and to pay 200 per cent of the ordinary wage for Sunday work; the claims for the guards and workers and for the USD 5 and USD 6 allowances were dismissed [Para 200][Para 202][Para 203][Para 204][Para 205].

On Issue 1 the panel first noted that the plant's internal rules had never been signed by a workers' representative, endorsed by the labour inspector, or disseminated as required, so a dismissal resting on them could not be lawful [Para 56][Para 106][Para 107]. It then held that under Article 82 and Article 83(4) of the Labour Law an employer may dismiss summarily for serious misconduct such as threatening or intimidating other workers, but must show what rule was breached and how serious the breach was [Para 100][Para 101][Para 108][Para 109]. The company showed neither, and its inquiry minutes were drawn up two months after the dismissal, outside the time limits in Article 26 [Para 117][Para 118][Para 120]. Finally, because the dismissed man was a union leader whose registration the ministry had notified to the company, Article 293 required the labour inspector's prior approval, which was never sought [Para 129][Para 130][Para 140][Para 143]. The panel therefore ordered reinstatement and back pay [Para 145].

On Issue 2 the panel found the evidence inadequate: the union never specified what was stolen, when, or how serious the guards' conduct was, nor the grounds on which the three workers were dismissed [Para 158][Para 159]. Only 14 guards and 3 workers had actually transferred their rights to the union, and for those the claim still failed for want of proof [Para 155][Para 160].

On Issue 3 the panel held that the USD 5 attendance bonus in Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 applies only to the textile, garment and footwear sectors, not to cement, and that neither the Labour Law nor any collective agreement required it here [Para 165][Para 166][Para 167]. The USD 6 living allowance in Notification No. 032/08 of 17 April 2008 is likewise confined to textile, garment and footwear establishments [Para 183][Para 187]. Both demands exceeded what the law provides, so they were interest disputes, which the panel declined to hear because the claimant union was not the most representative in the enterprise under Article 277 [Para 174][Para 176][Para 182][Para 191].

On Issue 4 the panel applied Article 139 (new) of the amended Labour Law, under which work on the weekly rest day carries an additional 100 per cent, giving 200 per cent in total [Para 193][Para 194][Para 195]. Sundays were the plant's weekly rest day and were paid at the ordinary rate, so the practice was unlawful [Para 196][Para 198][Para 199].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A dismissal of a registered union leader is unlawful unless the labour inspector's prior approval was obtained, and a dismissal for misconduct is unlawful where the internal rules relied on were never endorsed by the inspector or disseminated and the employer cannot identify the rule broken or its gravity within the statutory time limit [Para 108][Para 113][Para 140]. Sector-specific ministerial notifications on attendance bonuses and living allowances do not extend to establishments outside the sectors they name, and a union that is not the most representative cannot bring an interest dispute over benefits above the statutory floor [Para 166][Para 182].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that paying only 1.5 times for weekly rest-day work was agreed at conciliation but could not displace the statutory 200 per cent rate [Para 195][Para 198].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR is badly damaged in several places: the party details, the list of guards and workers, and the signature block are fragmentary, with runs of Latin nonsense where a line was lost [Para 26][Para 38][Para 47][Para 207]. Individual names in the operative and reasoning sections appear in three or four different spellings, and the three workers listed at Para 72 are named in full; they are described here by role only. The worker-appointed arbitrator's name is printed in two different forms between the face of the award and the signatures [Para 2][Para 207]. The conciliation report states 3 unsettled demands while the panel numbers four issues [Para 16][Para 17]. The employer's letter is dated "3 September 2019 2009" [Para 99], and the number of guards is given as 9 in the issue heading but 30 and 14 elsewhere [Para 63][Para 67][Para 155].
