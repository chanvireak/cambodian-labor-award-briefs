# Award No. 165/09 — Thap Van Garmen Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ថប វ័ន ហ្គាមិន លីមីតធិ) (Arbitration Council, 22 December 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — In Sothy (employer-appointed, dissenting), Vaen Pov (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhean (president) · Doc ID 140460

## 1. Facts
Thap Van Garmen Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ថប វ័ន ហ្គាមិន លីមីតធិ), at Kambol village, Svey Pak, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, opened in late 1997 and employed 612 workers under three unions. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40] The complainant, the Khmer Youth Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ), was not yet registered, holding only a receipt for its application of 26 October 2009, but 204 workers had signed written authorisations in its favour. [Para 42][Para 93][Para 94] Of fourteen demands, nine were settled in conciliation on 18 November 2009 and five came to the panel. [Para 20][Para 21] The five concerned sick-leave bonus, ante-natal check-up time, periodic special health checks, a share of profits and compensation should the factory relocate or change owner. [Para 43][Para 50][Para 63][Para 73][Para 77]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must keep paying the regular attendance bonus, in full, to workers who are absent on sick leave certified by a state doctor? [Para 43]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must allow pregnant workers one full paid day a month to attend ante-natal checks, with wages and attendance bonus preserved? [Para 50]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must arrange a special medical check for all serving workers every three months at its own cost, given the dust, chemicals and a fainting incident? [Para 63]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must pay each worker a share of profits equal to one month's wage each year? [Para 73]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the company must now pay severance in advance against the possibility that it may later relocate or change owner? [Para 77]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — Khmer Youth Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ):** a worker with a proper state medical certificate should keep both wage and the USD 5 attendance bonus, and the bonus is unfair because it has never been raised in ten years of service; 1.5 hours a month is too little for a pregnant worker to reach a distant hospital and queue; 16 workers fainted from cloth dust on 13 November 2009 and no special check has ever been arranged; the factory has steady orders and profits; and workers fear the owner will abscond as neighbouring factories have. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 53][Para 64][Para 65][Para 73][Para 78]
**Employer (respondent) — Thap Van Garmen Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ថប វ័ន ហ្គាមិន លីមីតធិ):** wages are already preserved for sick workers holding state certificates, but the attendance bonus is earned only by turning up regularly; 1.5 hours a month is sufficient and blood tests are allowed as needed; it sent the 16 fainting workers to hospital at its own cost and covers all medical expenses during working hours; it cannot pay a profit share; and it has no plan to relocate or change owner. [Para 43][Para 51][Para 69][Para 70][Para 75][Para 80]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issue 1 succeeded in part, the company being ordered to reduce the regular attendance bonus in proportion to the number of days of certified sick leave rather than forfeiting it entirely; Issues 2, 3, 4 and 5 were refused. [Para 156][Para 157][Para 158][Para 159][Para 160]
Threshold: under Article 268 of the Labour Law an unregistered union has no rights, but under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 it may still appear and represent workers who have authorised it in writing, so the panel confined the case to the 204 authorising workers. [Para 83][Para 92][Para 93][Para 94]
On Issue 1 the panel held that under Articles 71(3) and 72(1) a certified sick absence suspends the contract, so neither side owes performance; but the attendance bonus is part of wages under Article 103, and stripping it entirely for a single certified sick day would be unjust to the worker while paying it in full would be unjust to the employer, so it must be pro-rated to the days of leave. [Para 105][Para 108][Para 110][Para 111][Para 112][Para 115]
On Issue 2 the claim went beyond anything the Labour Law or any agreement requires; it was therefore an interest dispute, and the panel declines interest disputes where the claimant union is not the most representative in the enterprise, which the Khmer Youth Union Federation was found not to be. [Para 119][Para 120][Para 122][Para 126][Para 127][Para 131]
On Issue 3 the panel read Article 247 as leaving the frequency of special medical checks to a Prakas that has never been issued, found no law or agreement fixing a three-month cycle, treated the demand as an interest dispute and refused to consider it. [Para 133][Para 134][Para 139][Para 140][Para 142]
On Issue 4 Article 103 merely defines the components of wages and does not oblige an employer to distribute profits, so that demand too was an interest dispute and was not considered. [Para 146][Para 147][Para 149]
On Issue 5 the panel held it cannot decide problems that have not yet arisen, since nobody can predict whether or when the company will move or change owner. [Para 152][Para 154][Para 155]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A certified sick absence suspends the contract but does not destroy the attendance bonus, which forms part of wages: the employer must reduce it in proportion to the days of certified sick leave, giving neither a full forfeiture nor full payment. [Para 110][Para 115][Para 116] Where a demand exceeds what the Labour Law or a collective agreement grants, it is an interest dispute which the panel will not decide at the request of a union that is not the most representative in the enterprise. [Para 120][Para 122][Para 127]

**Obiter dicta:** the employer-appointed arbitrator dissented from Issue 1, arguing that the workers never asked for pro-rating, that the attendance bonus is conditional on regular attendance, and that the panel should have referred the interpretation to the competent authority instead of devising a proportional formula. [Para 191][Para 192][Para 196]
