# Award No. 29/09 — Fortune Garment (Woven & Knit) Factory Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 21 July 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៊ុក រឹ, អន ណន, កុយ នាម) · Doc ID 140371

## 1. Facts
The employer is ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្វ័រ ធ្យូន ហ្គាមែន វូលែន នីត ធីង ហ្វាក់ គូរី អិលធីឌី (Fortune Garment Woven & Knit Factory Co., Ltd.) at Sitbo Village, Sitbo Commune, S'ang District, Kandal, employing about 2,700 workers. [Para 104] The claimant is the local union of សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា (C.CAWDU), which holds a most-representative-status certificate for the factory, No. 002/08 of 8 January 2008. [Para 105] Bargaining on a collective agreement opened in June 2008 on a proposal of over 100 points; 38 went to conciliation, six were settled there and five more with third-party help, leaving 27 for the panel. [Para 106][Para 72] The demands still refused include a furnished union room, use of the canteen on two days' notice, a 5% cap on floating workers, notice before subcontracting, a wage-evaluation committee, lighter work for pregnant workers, 1,000 riel an hour for overtime meals and 19 days' annual leave. [Para 4][Para 8][Para 20]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether, the 27 residual demands being interest disputes the panel would not fix itself, the employer bargained in good faith and must be ordered to conclude a collective bargaining agreement with the union holding most representative status? [Para 125][Para 157]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — local union of សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា (C.CAWDU):** the employer meets every demand by saying it cannot be granted, offers no reason and refuses to disclose the financial, order and export figures the union needs in order to judge what the company can afford. [Para 123][Para 121]
**Employer (respondent) — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្វ័រ ធ្យូន ហ្គាមែន វូលែន នីត ធីង ហ្វាក់ គូរី អិលធីឌី (Fortune Garment Woven & Knit Factory Co., Ltd.):** it has conceded as far as it can and the 27 surviving points ask more than the Labour Law imposes; the information sought is the company's confidential business. [Para 124][Para 119]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to bargain in good faith with the union so as to conclude a collective bargaining agreement within three months after the award becomes enforceable; it fixed none of the 27 demands itself. [Para 157]

The panel first classified the dispute: the great majority of the points sent to it were interest disputes, not rights disputes. [Para 127] A union can bring an interest dispute only if it holds most representative status, which Article 277 of the Labour Law conditions on registration and the other statutory criteria, and here the C.CAWDU local union held the certificate. [Para 129][Para 130][Para 131] It then weighed Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, under which an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement for one year: an award on these points would bind all 2,700 workers and cost those not in the union their right to strike over them, an unfairness the panel declined to create. [Para 132][Para 133]

That led to the fairness route in Article 312, as interpreted in Award 114/08-Vitex: the council applies equity only where the workers show that bargaining has been exhausted and that the claim is reasonable and within the employer's means. [Para 135][Para 137][Para 138] Prakas No. 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001, Article 11, sets the bargaining duties — an orderly procedure, reasoned concessions and objections, facilities for union representatives and disclosure of all information relevant to the negotiation. [Para 141][Para 143][Para 144][Para 145] The employer made no concessions and no reasoned objections to the evidence requests, refused the union's letters of 13 and 26 June 2009 seeking financial data, orders and export volumes for 2007, 2008 and the first half of 2009, and never asked for confidentiality although it was open to it to do so. [Para 147][Para 153][Para 155] The panel treated that conduct, alongside its bare assertion that it could give no more, as bad faith. [Para 156] It therefore ordered genuine bargaining within a fixed period rather than substituting its own terms. [Para 157]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that answers a most-representative union's demands with a bare refusal, offers no reasoned counter-proposals and withholds the financial and order information needed to test its claimed inability to pay has failed the duty to bargain in good faith, and the council will order bargaining within a fixed period instead of imposing the terms of an agreement. [Para 146][Para 156]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that issuing an award on the interest points would substitute for the collective agreement for a year and strip non-members of their right to strike over those points, which is why it left the terms to the parties. [Para 133]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The signature page is badly corrupted: a block of Latin characters and numbers replaced the arbitrators' signatures, and one arbitrator's name is cut to a single word. [Para 159] The employer's name is printed in several different Khmer spellings across the header, the findings and the evidence list. [Para 76][Para 78] Hearing dates conflict — the second hearing is 12 June 2009 in the hearing list but 26 June 2009 in the procedural narrative. [Para 62][Para 116] The residual count is also printed inconsistently: 11 points are said to have been withdrawn, while the employer and the panel both work from 27 remaining. [Para 148][Para 124] The union's representatives are printed with full personal names, which this brief does not reproduce. [Para 4]
