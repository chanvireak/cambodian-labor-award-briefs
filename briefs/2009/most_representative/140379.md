# Award No. 42/09-River Rich Textile — River Rich Textile Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 30 April 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach, Ven Pov, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 140379

## 1. Facts
River Rich Textile Ltd., established in March 2004, employs about 2,274 workers at Prek Ta Pring Village, S'ang District, Kandal Province, of whom roughly 8% own motorbikes and 10% bicycles. The claimant is the Local Union of the Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Worker Democratic Unions (C.CAWDU), which holds most-representative status under certificate No. 1190 of 30 July 2007 and claims 1,550 members through dues deducted from wages. Four demands went to conciliation on 12 March 2009; none was resolved, and issue 1 (wages of skilled workers) was withdrawn at the hearing, leaving issues 2, 3 and 4. [Para 17][Para 18][Para 12][Para 14]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must take responsibility for the loss of workers' bicycles and motorbikes from the factory? [Para 18]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the workers are entitled to reimbursement of medical check fees under the Labour Law, and for how many years back they may claim? [Para 58]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must pay full wages when there is no work in the first and second weeks of the month, and 50% of wages when there is none in the third and fourth weeks? [Para 74]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, C.CAWDU Local Union):** the employer must answer for vehicles stolen from a lot without a guard, fourteen bicycles having been lost in 2008; it must reimburse 10,100 to 12,000 riel of medical check fees per worker from 2004 onward; and it must pay full wages for the first two workless weeks and 50% for the last two, because it cannot substitute annual leave for periods when it has no work. [Para 18][Para 25][Para 37]

**Employer (respondent, River Rich Textile Ltd.):** it raised the lot fence after negotiating with the workers on 18 November 2008 and has posted a guard for thirty minutes before lunch and before the end of the day since December 2008, so it will not accept liability, especially as some workers leave vehicles unlocked or keys in them; it has paid for medical checks since August 2008 and will not fund pre-2004 recruits who had to bring certificates to be hired; and it seldom lacks work, the annual leave schedule being set to its production flow. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 24][Para 30]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel rejected the demand on stolen vehicles, ordered reimbursement of 10,100 to 12,000 riel of medical check fees to 142 workers who paid for checks between 5 March 2006 and August 2008, ordered full wages whenever there is no work for one or two weeks unless a lawful suspension is implemented, and declined to consider the 50% demand for future long shortages. [Para 96][Para 73]

Issue 2 failed for want of a legal ground. No provision of the Labour Law, a labour contract, the Internal Work Rules or a collective agreement makes the employer liable for stolen vehicles, so the demand was an interests dispute. The panel noted that only an MRS union may bring such a dispute, and that this union qualifies under Article 96(2)(b) of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305, but decided the claim on equity under Article 312 paragraph 2 and rejected it: the fence had been raised, a guard posted, theft complaints had stopped, and the workers had brought no reliable evidence. [Para 44][Para 49][Para 50][Para 52][Para 57]

Issue 3 succeeded in part. Article 247(c) of the Labour Law obliges the employer to pay for pre-employment and periodic medical exams even without an implementing Prakas, following Awards 05/06-WND, 56/07-Golden Crown, 98/07-Sky Sino, 40/08 and 52/08-Supreme Garment and 136/08-Supertex. Article 120 sets a three-year limitation running from the contract date or the date the expense was incurred (61/07-M & V (Branch 3), 93/07-Global Footwear, 50/06-WND); counted back from the complaint of 5 March 2009, that fixed the window at 5 March 2006 to August 2008, when the employer began paying. Of 223 workers in the window, only the 142 who supplied thumbprints were compensated. [Para 59][Para 62][Para 67][Para 72][Para 73]

Issue 4 also succeeded in part. Article 166 entitles every worker to paid annual leave and Article 170 requires its timing to be agreed, so the employer could not direct workers to take extra days deducted from their remaining annual leave. Articles 71(11) and 72(1) allow suspension of the wage obligation, but only under the Labour Inspector's control; the employer notified the Inspector yet never obtained permission, so it had to pay full wages for workless weeks. Exercising its power under Clause 34 of Prakas No. 99 to grant any just civil remedy, the panel ordered that for the future, and declined the 50% demand as too speculative to decide. [Para 81][Para 82][Para 89][Para 94][Para 100]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that keeps workers at home without work but does not obtain the Labour Inspector's authorisation under Article 71(11) must pay full wages for the idle weeks, and it may not discharge that obligation by directing workers to consume annual leave; reimbursement of medical check fees reaches back three years from the date of complaint, not to the start of employment. [Para 90][Para 82][Para 67]

**Obiter dicta:** only a union holding most-representative status may bring an interests dispute before the panel, and a claim about future work shortages affecting unidentified workers is too speculative to decide. [Para 49][Para 100]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The text is an unofficial English translation with the article fused into the following word ("themployer", "thearing", "forerolution") and several sentences duplicated or truncated mid-word, including one passage running "135/07-Wilson … reasons for decision" twice. The hearing is dated 3 April 2009 in the heading, yet the summons is said to have fixed 26 April 2009, a date after the hearing, and the postponement is recorded as "3 April 2008". The certificate of most-representative status appears as No. 1190 KBNK and No. 1190 KBIVK in different places. The list of the 142 compensated workers is attached only to the Khmer award. Names of union officers and employer representatives appear in full in the source and are not reproduced here.
