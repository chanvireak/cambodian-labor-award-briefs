# Award No. 75/09-ធ្នូប៊ល ហ្វូត វែរ — ក្រុមហ៊ុន គ្លូប៊ល ហ្វូត វែរ អ៉ិន ធើល៍ ខប (Arbitration Council, 6 July 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (ឈីវ ភិរម្យ for the employer, អន ណន for the workers, chair រុន សារ៉ាយ) · Doc ID 140400

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន គ្លូប៊ល ហ្វូត វែរ អ៉ិន ធើល៍ ខប ("Global Footwear Industries Corp") makes footwear at Ang Snuol village, Peuk commune, Ang Snuol district, Kandal, with about 1,100 workers, of whom roughly 730 were parties to this dispute. [Para 35][Para 36] They were represented by the federation សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព លើកស្ទួយ សិទ្ធិ កម្មករ, which held no certificate of most-representative status. [Para 37] Of 14 collective demands, 10 were settled in conciliation on 1 June 2009 and 4 were referred to the panel. [Para 16][Para 17]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company may require overtime beyond two hours a day, and whether the workers are owed 2,000 riel meal money for each overtime period? [Para 38][Para 83]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company's practice of setting production quotas per hour is unlawful or causes unfair warning to workers who do not meet them? [Para 56][Para 113]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must stop assigning sewing workers to scrub bricks and sweep the floor at the end of the shift? [Para 69][Para 132]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must grant a one-hour breastfeeding break until the child reaches 18 months and pay USD 20 a month for outside daycare until the child is 5 years old? [Para 76][Para 143]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the federation សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព លើកស្ទួយ សិទ្ធិ កម្មករ argued that overtime past two hours breaches the Labour Law and Prakas No. 80, that consent is not genuinely free when the employer presses for it, that quotas are raised from 10 to 15, 17 or 20 pairs an hour and rankings are given by managerial favour, and that cleaning is the job of hygiene staff. [Para 52][Para 57][Para 66][Para 72]
**Employer (respondent):** Global Footwear said overtime is voluntary and thumbprinted, that work is only heavy since April 2009 and staff may leave at 4:00 p.m., that quotas bring rank and a USD 2 monthly allowance at each of 10 ranks, that warnings were never issued, and that it already gives a one-hour break for one year plus USD 6 to mothers. [Para 49][Para 63][Para 79][Para 59]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to arrange working time so that no worker works more than 10 hours in one day, rejected the 2,000 riel meal-money claim, dismissed Issues 2 and 3, declined to consider the 18-month breastfeeding break, and ordered the parties to agree a fair rate, or pay actual invoices, for outside daycare. [Para 171][Para 172][Para 173][Para 174][Para 175][Para 176]

On Issue 1(a), the panel read Article 4 of Prakas No. 80 (overtime rests on voluntariness, and no employer may coerce or discipline a worker who declines) with Article 140 of the Labour Law, and found that no provision fixes a maximum number of overtime hours as such; Article 140 only caps daily working time, including overtime, at 10 hours. It therefore held the employer may require overtime but must keep the day within that ceiling. [Para 86][Para 92][Para 93][Para 95] On Issue 1(b), Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 sets meal money at 1,000 riel a day, and the union conceded no agreement ever provided 2,000 riel; the demand exceeded the notification, so the panel declined to consider it. [Para 97][Para 99][Para 101][Para 112]

On Issue 2, the panel applied Article 2 of the Labour Law and its own earlier awards (24/05, 18/08, 20/06, 108/06, 116/07, 10/03) holding that an employer may direct human resources and set output or incentive levels lawfully and reasonably; it found no discrimination, no loss of benefit and no evidence of any actual warning, so the quota system was not a breach of the employment contract. [Para 116][Para 118][Para 121][Para 123][Para 129]

On Issue 3, the panel found each worker sweeps only 10 to 15 minutes at the end of the shift, about three times a month, on rotation within a group of 35 to 45, and that no agreement governed the practice; the arrangement was not excessive and no evidence showed it was unreasonable. [Para 133][Para 134][Para 140][Para 141]

On Issue 4(a), Articles 184 and 185 give a one-hour daily breastfeeding break for one year only, so a demand to extend it to 18 months went beyond the law and was an interest claim; because the union lacked most-representative status and the claim would bind all workers, the panel declined to consider it. [Para 143][Para 147][Para 152][Para 154] On Issue 4(b), Article 186 requires the employer to meet the cost of outside daycare where no nursery exists; following Award 133/07, the panel held the employer may choose between a nursery and paying actual invoices, set 36 months as the reasonable outer limit, and rejected the demand to age 5. [Para 156][Para 158][Para 169][Para 178]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may require voluntary overtime up to the 10-hour daily ceiling of Article 140 of the Labour Law even though no text fixes an overtime maximum, but it may not pay less than the meal allowance set by Notification No. 017, and a demand above it is an interest claim the panel will not decide where the referring union lacks most-representative status; the statutory breastfeeding hour runs for one year, and daycare support where no nursery exists is discharged by a nursery or by actual invoices, not beyond 36 months. [Para 93][Para 95][Para 101][Para 106][Para 169]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that production changes are ordinary in the Cambodian garment industry and that an employer's discretion over incentive pay follows the circumstances. [Para 119][Para 120]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company's name is printed in at least four different forms across the award, including a version beginning "ធ្នូប៊ល" in the case name and a bracketed variant in the ministry report; the case name and the party name therefore do not match on the face of the document. [Para 1][Para 23][Para 31] One reasoning line contains stray Latin text where a worker's name was rendered by OCR. [Para 108] The dispositive section repeats the heading for Issue 2 three times within a single line, and several lines are broken mid-sentence. [Para 173] The signature block is garbled, with the worker-side arbitrator's name reduced to fragments of digits and strokes, though the panel is named in the header. [Para 180]
