# Award No. 18/09 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន តេរ៉ាតិ ផ្សះ ក្លឹត ទិញ និង ហ្គាមិន អ៉ីន ធី ណា ស្យូណាល់ ហ្វាក់ ថូរី អិលធីឌី (Tera Tech Cloth Dyeing and Garment International Factory Co., Ltd) (Arbitration Council, 11 March 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kuy Neam presiding; Mar Samborna, employer nominee; An Vireak, worker nominee) · Doc ID 140361

## 1. Facts
Tera Tech Cloth Dyeing and Garment International Factory Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន តេរ៉ាតិ ផ្សះ ក្លឹត ទិញ និង ហ្គាមិន អ៉ីន ធី ណា ស្យូណាល់ ហ្វាក់ ថូរី អិលធីឌី), on National Road 2, Chak Angre Krom, Meanchey district, Phnom Penh, employs about 2,490 workers and hosts three unions. [Para 2][Para 46][Para 47] The claimant, the Seri Kamkar union of the Tera Tech factory (សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ នៃ រោងចក្រ តេរ៉ាតិច្ស) with about 360 members, is not the most representative union there. [Para 2][Para 49] Twelve women in the dyeing section hold indefinite-duration contracts; that section has 17 workers, twelve women and five men. [Para 51][Para 52] By letters of 19 and 21 January 2009 the company ordered all twelve women moved out — six to attaching, three to labelling, three to sorting or ironing — keeping wages, seniority and bonuses. [Para 53][Para 58][Para 77] Conciliation of 21 January 2009 left this single issue unresolved. [Para 12][Para 13]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company, under its management right in Article 2 paragraph 2 of the Labour Law, may reassign the twelve women from the dyeing section to other sections by the letters of 19 and 21 January 2009, or whether their contracts confine them to dyeing? [Para 69][Para 75]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** their employment contracts name the dyeing section as their workplace, repeated transfers leave them without any genuine skill, and Article 69 does not let the employer move them; they wish to work in dyeing until they retire and invoked Article 13 to preserve conditions better than the law. [Para 54][Para 56][Para 66][Para 67][Para 69]
**Employer (respondent):** there was no bad intent; the transfer rebalances a production chain hit by the global financial crisis, places the women where there are no chemicals and no heavy drums of dye of 10 to 50 kilograms, and preserves wages, seniority, bonuses and the training needed for the new sections. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 59][Para 77]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the demand that the company not transfer the twelve women out of dyeing was refused, and the company was ordered to maintain their working conditions in the new sections, including wages, seniority and bonuses. [Para 92][Para 93][Para 94]
The panel held that Article 2 paragraph 2 of the Labour Law gives the employer the right to organise and manage the enterprise, including reassigning workers, but only where the transfer is lawful and reasonable. [Para 72][Para 74] It followed its earlier awards 62/06-Klick Su, 108/06-Trung Gal Vumara, 33/07-Gold Fame, 106/07-IM & V III, 84/08-Trung Gal Vumara and 08/09-Global Apparel on that point. [Para 73][Para 81]
On reasonableness, the panel weighed the company's account that orders had fallen and the chain had to be rebalanced cheaply against the workers' account that they had always worked in dyeing and wanted to stay. [Para 75][Para 82][Para 83] It noted both sides accepted that the dyeing section is the physically harder one, that no benefit would be lost, that the new sections have equivalent conditions, and that the women had been trained for them. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 77] The reasons for the transfer therefore outweighed the objections. [Para 85]
Article 69 was held beside the point: it governs the worker's duty to give all professional activity to the enterprise and not to compete with it, and says nothing about reassignment; no clause of the contracts or the internal rules forbids a transfer either. [Para 87][Para 89][Para 90][Para 91]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may reassign a worker to another section under its management right in Article 2 paragraph 2 of the Labour Law without the worker's consent, where the transfer is driven by production needs such as a collapse in orders, the contract and internal rules contain no promise of a fixed section, benefits and seniority are preserved, and the new section's conditions are no worse; Article 69 confers no right to refuse. [Para 74][Para 85][Para 90][Para 91]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the dyeing section requires physical strength and is harder than the others, and that the company planned to replace the women there with men who can run two machines and lift drums of up to 50 kilograms — reasoning that supported but did not decide the outcome. [Para 59][Para 61][Para 79]
