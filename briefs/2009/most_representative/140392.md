# Award No. 60/09 — Cambodia Airport Management Services (CAMS) (Arbitration Council, 10 June 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mar Somborna (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Pen Bunhea (chair) · Doc ID 140392

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូឌា អ៊ែរ ផត មែនី ជ្ជមែន សឺវីស ("Cambodia Airport Management Services", CAMS) provides ground and public services at Phnom Penh International Airport and employs about 700 staff [Para 50]. The claimant is សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត ឯករាជ្យ ព្រលានយន្តហោះ អន្តរជាតិ ("Independent Union of Phnom Penh International Airport"), the only union in the company, with 170 members; the claim was brought for 188 of the roughly 230 ground-service staff [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53]. Union and company signed collective agreement No. 067 of 21 March 2008, valid three years and applied by both sides [Para 55]. From 1 April 2009 the company introduced a roster requiring work until 24:30, paying only the 200% night-shift rate for 22:30–24:30 instead of the 400% overtime rate previously paid, without discussing it with the workers [Para 59][Para 60][Para 64].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must schedule day and night shifts separately, so that a day shift does not run into the night shift? [Para 4]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must abolish ten-hour daily shifts and treat hours above eight a day as overtime? [Para 6][Para 22]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** day and night work must be separated because a shift running past 24:30 exposes them to health risks and to danger on the way home, and because the new roster cut the earnings they used to receive; the transport and accident cover the company cites are inadequate for the risk [Para 70][Para 73][Para 94]. Eight hours is the legal working day, so anything beyond it must be counted and paid as overtime [Para 75][Para 76].
**Employer (respondent):** clause (b) of the employment contract and the internal rules, together with the 21 March 2008 collective agreement, give management the right to build flexible rosters, because airline flight schedules change constantly; schedules are posted five days ahead as the agreement provides [Para 66][Para 68]. The 200% night rate the company pays exceeds the 130% required by law, and neither the Labour Law nor the agreement requires day and night shifts to be split [Para 67][Para 68].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the operative part refuses both claims and orders nothing else [Para 161][Para 162]; the order is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification if no objection is filed [Para 163]. The panel's findings on clause 7.8 of the collective agreement and on the employer's notice, transport and time-recording duties sit in the reasons, not in the operative part [Para 156][Para 157].

**Issue 1 — separate shifts.** Article 2 and Article 138 of the Labour Law leave the arrangement of working time to the employer; following Award 11/05 and Award 62/06, the panel held the employer may set and change rosters provided the arrangement is lawful and reasonable [Para 85][Para 89][Para 90]. Article 144 (new) defines night work as 22:00 to 05:00, and nothing in the law or in clause 7.12 of the agreement forbids a roster that crosses from day into night [Para 105][Para 109][Para 107]. The workers' health and safety concern is genuine and the panel said the employer must meet it, but by strengthening workplace safety and transport rather than by splitting the shifts [Para 98][Para 99][Para 100].

**Issue 2 — ten-hour shifts.** Article 137 caps full-time work at eight hours a day and 48 hours a week, but Article 141 and Prakas 143 of 10 June 2002 allow hours to be averaged over twelve consecutive weeks, subject to a daily maximum of ten hours, a weekly average of 48, prior agreement with the most representative union and the shop stewards, and seven days' notice to the labour inspectorate [Para 116][Para 119][Para 126]. Clauses 7.2, 7.6 and 7.7 of the collective agreement set shifts of four to ten hours and a 40–48 hour week, and the panel verified against the roster that no worker exceeded 48 hours in a week [Para 113][Para 114][Para 131]. The claim therefore had no legal basis and was refused [Para 158][Para 160]. Clause 7.8, which allowed publication only five days ahead, fell short of the seven-day requirement and was declared void [Para 155][Para 156]. The worker-appointed arbitrator dissented on this issue, holding that Prakas 143 requires the shop stewards' consent in addition to the union's, which the employer had not shown [Para 186][Para 187].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may roster work across the boundary between day and night and may set shifts of four to ten hours without breaching Article 137, where a registered collective agreement so provides and the twelve-week average stays within 48 hours a week; but that flexibility is conditional on prior agreement with the most representative union and the shop stewards and on seven days' notice to the labour inspectorate, and a clause permitting shorter notice is void [Para 126][Para 156].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that health, security and safety of workers travelling home after a night shift remain the employer's responsibility whatever the roster, and that the parties may continue to negotiate improved transport and hygiene protection [Para 99][Para 100].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's name is printed in at least four forms across the document — ខេមបូឌា អ៊ែរ ផត មែនី ជ្ជមែន សឺវីស, សីវីស, បែនី ជ្ជមែន សិវីស and the initials CAMS — and in two places the reasons substitute the placeholders "ក្រុមហ៊ុន A" and "សហគ្រាស H" for the company's name [Para 28][Para 50][Para 154]. The night shift's end is given as 22:30, as 23:55 and as 24:30 in different passages, and the roster's start as 05:30 [Para 56][Para 58][Para 59]. Two of the three claims were merged into issue 2 by agreement at the hearing, so the file records three claims but two issues [Para 22]. The panel's orders on transport, time clocks and seven-day notice appear only in the reasons and not in the operative part, which refuses both claims and says nothing of them [Para 150][Para 161]. The Prakas 143 conditions are cited under inconsistent article numbering, and the worker-appointed arbitrator's dissent ends in a garbled signature line [Para 105][Para 188][Para 203].
