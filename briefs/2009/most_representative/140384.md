# Award No. 47/09 — Branch of NagaWorld Ltd. / សាខា ក្រុមហ៊ុន ណាហ្គាវើល ដ៏ លីមីធីត (Arbitration Council, 7 May 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer, Ven Peou for the workers, Kong Polla chairing) · Doc ID 140384

## 1. Facts
The Phnom Penh casino and resort branch of NagaWorld Ltd. (សាខា ក្រុមហ៊ុន ណាហ្គាវើល ដ៏ លីមីធីត) employed about 3,000 workers, roughly 1,600 of them in the casino section. [Para 62][Para 64] The claimant, the Cambodia Tourism and Service Workers Union Federation (CTSWF, សហព័ន្ធ ហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត វិស័យ ទេសចរណ៏ និង សេវាកម្ម កម្ពុជា) together with the Khmer Employees' Rights Union at Naga Resort, held about 955 members but no most-representative certificate. [Para 1][Para 65]

On 26 February 2009 the company terminated 14 union leaders, staff representatives and activists, citing the economic crisis and falling visitor numbers, and paid each a final settlement. [Para 111][Para 188] Five demands went to conciliation on 19 March 2009; the first, on night-shift wages, was settled, and the remaining four were referred. [Para 19][Para 21]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay every worker an annual bonus equal to one month's wages? [Para 70]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must raise wages by 10% a year? [Para 79]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must pay equal base wages to Khmer and foreign workers of the same rank and professional skill? [Para 84]
4. (Issue 5) Whether the employer must take back the 14 terminated union leaders, staff representatives and activists? [Para 91]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, CTSWF and the Khmer Employees' Rights Union at Naga Resort):** a one-month annual bonus had been paid once, on 1 May 2008; revenue rose from USD 144 million in 2007 to USD 193.5 million in 2008, so a 10% annual increase was affordable; a Khmer Pit Supervisor with 1996 seniority earned a USD 350 base wage while two foreign Pit Supervisors hired in 2007 earned up to USD 1,000; and the 14 terminations were forced, with security escorts, cards confiscated and February wages bundled with severance. [Para 70][Para 75][Para 85][Para 188][Para 200]

**Employer (respondent, Branch of NagaWorld Ltd.):** the offer of a bonus depended on revenue and management's decision, and the economic crisis and falling player numbers made any increase or bonus impossible; it did not explain the wage gap between Khmer and foreign Pit Supervisors; and the 14 had signed termination agreements and received their money, so they could not be reinstated. [Para 73][Para 82][Para 90][Para 111]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issues 2, 3 and 5 were dismissed without consideration of the merits, and on Issue 4 the employer was ordered to pay equal base wages to Khmer and foreign workers of the same rank in the same department. [Para 215][Para 216][Para 217][Para 218]

On Issue 2 the panel read Clause 5(4) of the Internal Work Rules of 7 April 2008, which makes the annual incentive bonus dependent on the company's revenue and management's decision and fixes no one-month entitlement. No law, contract or collective agreement supplied that entitlement, so the demand was a benefit claim, beyond what the law grants. [Para 144][Para 145][Para 147]

On Issue 3 the panel found no labour regulation, contract, internal rule or collective agreement providing a 10% annual increase, and noted the parties themselves conceded there was no legal basis; it dismissed the demand as a benefit claim. [Para 162][Para 166]

On Issue 4 the panel cited Article 1 of the Labour Law, which covers all contracts performed in Cambodia whatever the parties' nationality, and Article 12, which forbids an employer to use race or nationality as a ground of decision, together with Article 106 requiring equal wages for work of equal conditions, skill and output. It noted that differences in skill or output may justify different wages, but here the employer never explained why Khmer and foreign Pit Supervisors in the same Casino Operation Department were paid USD 350 and USD 1,000, so the claim was accepted. [Para 171][Para 175][Para 180][Para 182][Para 186]

On Issue 5 the panel found the 26 February terminations were not directly coerced, but that confiscating identity cards and withholding February wages unless severance was accepted amounted to psychological pressure, and the employer's lawyer did not contest it. It nevertheless declined to order reinstatement: under Article 19 of Decree-Law No. 38 on Contracts, only the injured party may seek nullity, and the workers had asked for reinstatement rather than annulment of the 27 February and 2 March agreements. They must first have those agreements annulled. [Para 202][Para 206][Para 212][Para 214]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that exceeds what statute, contract, internal rules or collective agreement grant is a benefit claim that the panel will not decide on its merits. Adjudication of such claims also presupposes that the claimant union holds most-representative status; and where workers seek reinstatement after signing termination agreements they say were coerced, the panel will not look behind the agreements until the injured party has sought their annulment under Article 19 of Decree-Law No. 38. [Para 149][Para 156][Para 212]

**Obiter dicta:** the finding that withholding February wages and identity cards to obtain signatures was psychological pressure, though the workers had not pleaded annulment; and the observation that issuing an award on a benefit claim would substitute for a collective agreement and strip other workers of the right to strike over it. [Para 157][Para 208]

Several paragraphs carry untranslated Latin garbage where the original Khmer was lost — one passage on most-representative status (line 151) and one on Article 277 (line 152) are unreadable — and line 172 introduces a stray "Law 137" that fits no citation. The foreign Pit Supervisor's base wage is printed as "1 0 dollars" at line 87 and as USD 1,000 at line 183, and the reading "up to USD 1,000" has been used. The schedule of 14 workers is printed with full names and identity-card numbers, reduced here to roles, and the award embeds the Issue 5 heading inside a paragraph rather than giving it its own numbered line.
