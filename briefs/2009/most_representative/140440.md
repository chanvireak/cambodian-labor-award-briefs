# Award No. 129/09 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ាយ ថិច ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) ខូ អិលធីឌី (Vay Tech Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd.) and the Khmer Youth Union of the factory (Arbitration Council, 13 October 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of three: Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Suong Sophal (worker-appointed), Kong Phalla (president) · Doc ID 140440

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ាយ ថិច ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) ខូ អិលធីឌី, a garment factory at Trapeang Thleung, Choam Chao, Phnom Penh with about 970 workers, of whom roughly 90% are women, faced ten demands from the **សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ** (Khmer Youth Union Federation) through its factory union. [Para 22][Para 47][Para 48][Para 103]
Conciliation failed on all ten points; at the hearing of 25 September 2009 the parties settled seven, leaving issue 1 (removal of the company's director, Mr D), issue 3 (abolition of the cards used to enter the toilets) and issue 6 (milk powder for mothers of children over eighteen months). [Para 17][Para 21][Para 22]
The director, Mr D, did not attend to answer the demand against him, and the union's deputy president, Ms H, and its president, Mr M, gave evidence about the incidents of 7 September 2009. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 54][Para 68]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must dismiss its director, Mr D, because he used violence against Ms H and had outsiders assault Mr M on 7 September 2009? [Para 51]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the company must abolish the practice of requiring a card from the team leader in order to enter the toilets? [Para 92]
3. (Issue 6) Whether the company must supply two cans of milk powder a month, worth about USD 20, to women with children over eighteen months old? [Para 102]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), through the Khmer Youth Union Federation and its factory union:** the director seized Ms H's hand and twisted it while she was collecting thumbprints on the demand petition, then had a group of men in plain clothes detain her in his office for hours and refuse her the toilet, and a similar group detained and injured Mr M; the card system humiliates workers by making even a toilet visit subject to the team leader's permission; and the company should provide two cans of milk powder monthly to mothers whose children are over eighteen months. [Para 58][Para 63][Para 68][Para 72][Para 94][Para 109]
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ាយ ថិច ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) ខូ អិលធីឌី:** the director cannot be dismissed because he is the head of the company and used no violence and hired no outsiders; Ms H was collecting thumbprints during working hours against the internal rules, so he merely took the paper from her hand; security staff only asked Mr M to sit and wait for the dispute to be resolved; the card system was reintroduced because queues at the toilets disrupted the production line, and cards are not required for pregnant workers or team leaders; and it already pays USD 8 a month, and cannot afford more. [Para 78][Para 88][Para 89][Para 96][Para 99][Para 110]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel refused to order the director's dismissal but ordered him to stop using violence against workers, and dismissed issues 3 and 6; the award is non-binding and becomes permanent eight days after notification. [Para 176][Para 177][Para 178][Para 179][Para 180]
On issue 1 the panel applied Article 65 of the Labour Law, under which an employment contract binds only its parties and is governed by ordinary contract law, and Article 42 of Decree No. 38 of 28 October 1988, under which a contract is the law of the parties and may be varied only by consent. [Para 114][Para 116][Para 119]
It held that hiring and dismissing staff is the exclusive right of the employer, and that a worker cannot compel the removal of a manager; it followed Awards 14/03, 87/04, 116/07 and 54/08 in refusing the dismissal. [Para 121][Para 122]
The panel nevertheless found, on the undisputed facts, that the director had seized Ms H's hand to take the paper away even though collecting thumbprints in working hours breached the internal rules, and that this was no way to apply discipline: an employer may discipline under the internal rules or the law but may not use violence. [Para 123][Para 125][Para 126]
It applied Article 83, which lists assault as serious misconduct, and found the grabbing of the hand not serious enough to qualify, and no evidence of repetition. [Para 130][Para 131][Para 132]
Invoking Article 34 of Prakas No. 099, which lets the panel order a party to cease unlawful conduct, it ordered Mr D to stop using violence in resolving labour disputes and to change his management approach, while declining to dismiss him. [Para 136][Para 140]
On issue 3 the panel applied Article 2 of the Labour Law to uphold the employer's right to organise and manage the enterprise, including the flow of workers to the toilets, and found the card system lawful and reasonable: 46 toilets serve 940 workers, twenty more are under construction, and 67 cards cover 37 teams, with pregnant workers and team leaders exempt. [Para 143][Para 146][Para 149][Para 152]
The panel found no impact on health or dignity beyond the workers' dislike of the arrangement. [Para 151][Para 154]
On issue 6 the panel applied Article 186, which requires a daycare room for enterprises with at least 100 women workers and lets an employer without facilities pay for a child's place in an outside nursery; since the law requires no milk powder, the demand exceeded the legal obligation and was an interests claim. [Para 155][Para 160][Para 163][Para 164]
Because the Khmer Youth Union of the factory was not the most representative union, it could not bring an interests claim, and the demand was declined; the panel noted the USD 8 monthly payment was not contrary to the law. [Para 167][Para 169][Para 174][Para 175]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The panel cannot order an employer to dismiss a manager, because hiring and dismissal are the employer's own contractual rights, but it may order the manager to stop unlawful conduct: violence is never a permissible means of resolving a labour dispute, and discipline must be imposed under the internal rules or the Labour Law, so a manager who assaults a worker will be ordered to desist even though he keeps his post. [Para 121][Para 126][Para 140]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the payment of USD 8 a month to mothers of children over eighteen months was more favourable than the law requires and did not conflict with Article 186. [Para 162]
