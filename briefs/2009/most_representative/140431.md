# Award No. 119/09 — SL Garment Processing (Cambodia) Ltd / ក្រុមហ៊ុន SL Garment Processing (Cambodia) v Cambodia Labour Union Federation / សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា (CLUF) (Arbitration Council, 25 September 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun; Tuon Siphan; Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 140431

## 1. Facts
Case No. 119/09 concerns the garment factory of ក្រុមហ៊ុន SL Garment Processing (Cambodia) at Stung Meanchey, Khan Meanchey, Phnom Penh, which began operating in 1997 and whose workforce had fallen from about 3,500 to between 3,100 and 3,200 [Para 1][Para 33][Para 34]. The claimant was សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា (CLUF), the union at the SL factory, registered with about 600 members; the plant had three unions and none was the most representative [Para 2][Para 35][Para 37]. Of 11 demands, conciliation on 7 August 2009 settled 5 and left 6 [Para 24][Para 25]. At the hearing on 8 September 2009 the parties settled issues 3 and 6 and the union withdrew issue 2, leaving issues 1, 4 and 5, which the union merged into two claims [Para 22][Para 26][Para 27][Para 28][Para 29].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay 200 per cent of the ordinary wage to newly recruited workers on the night shift, as it does for long-serving workers? [Para 38][Para 121]
2. (Issues 4 and 5) Whether the company must keep the piece rates it paid before the end of 2007 — USD 0.25 per unit for the dyeing team, USD 0.15 for the front-and-back stitching team and USD 0.80 for the hemming team — instead of the lower rates it introduced? [Para 45][Para 96][Para 122]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា (CLUF):** that the company runs three shifts, the third from 22:00 to 06:00, and works night shifts in almost every section, so new recruits should get the same 200 per cent as long-serving staff because their wages are low and prices of goods and rent have risen [Para 38][Para 39][Para 43]. On piece rates, that the company should restore the rates paid before the end of 2007, when it cut them, so workers again receive an incentive above the quota [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58].
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន SL Garment Processing (Cambodia):** that it pays 130 per cent to new recruits as the amended Labour Law requires, while retaining 200 per cent for long-serving workers engaged before the July 2007 amendment [Para 40][Para 41][Para 42]. On piece rates, that it may set output targets and adjust the incentive to market conditions, since order prices had fallen in the world economic crisis, and that base wages were already paid according to law [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both remaining claims were refused: no order was made for 200 per cent night-shift pay for new recruits, and no order was made restoring the pre-2008 piece rates [Para 95][Para 116][Para 121][Para 122].

On Issue 1 the panel first noted that the plant's three unions had not filed jointly, only the SL factory union was the claimant, and no union was most representative [Para 92][Para 93]. Applying Prakas No. 305 of 22 December 2001 and its earlier awards, it held that a union without most-representative status cannot bring an interest dispute to the Council [Para 87][Para 88][Para 90][Para 91][Para 94]. Because the Labour Law as amended in July 2007 gives 130 per cent for night work and the company already paid that, the claim for 200 per cent went beyond the statutory floor and was an interest dispute, so the panel declined to consider it [Para 40][Para 95][Para 121].

On Issues 4 and 5 the panel treated the question as whether the company was bound to keep the old rates or entitled to change them [Para 97]. It held that Article 2 of the Labour Law gives the employer the right to manage and direct the enterprise within the law and reasonably [Para 100]. It followed its earlier awards to the effect that changing production targets and the incentive attached to them is not in itself a breach of the employment contract [Para 102][Para 103][Para 105]. It found that the rates had been cut from the end of 2007, that workers had not objected at the time, and that the new scheme had run for nearly two years, so it had become the company's ordinary practice and the workers had accepted it [Para 110][Para 112][Para 113][Para 114][Para 115]. The claims were therefore refused [Para 116][Para 122].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the law as amended sets a night-work premium of 130 per cent and the employer pays it, a demand for 200 per cent is a claim above the statutory floor and an interest dispute that a union lacking most-representative status cannot bring to the Council [Para 87][Para 94][Para 95]. An employer may adjust piece rates and output-linked incentives within its management right, and a revised scheme applied without objection for close to two years becomes the ordinary practice of the enterprise [Para 100][Para 114][Para 115].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that an employer intending to change an incentive should still notify workers in advance and state its reasons, so that the change is fair and transparent [Para 117][Para 120].
