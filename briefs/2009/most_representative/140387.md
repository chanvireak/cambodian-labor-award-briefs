# Award No. 48/09 — Rousing Garment Co., Ltd. / ក្រុមហ៊ុន រូស៊ីង ហ្គាម៉ែន ខូ អំល ធី ឌី (Arbitration Council, 18 May 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer, Liv Sovanna for the workers, Pen Bunchia chairing) · Doc ID 140387

## 1. Facts
Rousing Garment Co., Ltd. (ក្រុមហ៊ុន រូស៊ីង ហ្គាម៉ែន ខូ អំល ធី ឌី), operating since 1997 with about 4,300 workers, faced 19 demands from the Union for Freedom at Rousing Company (សហជីព ដើម្បី សេរីភាព នៃ ក្រុមហ៊ុន រ៉ូស៊ីង), registered on 12 January 2009 and affiliated to the Cambodian Asian Trade Union Confederation. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 52]

At the two hearings 13 of the 19 demands were settled by agreement, leaving six: reinstatement of two union officers, a larger attendance bonus, a seniority increment, a meal allowance for overtime, a company nursery, and 300% holiday pay. [Para 30][Para 31] The company had suspended the contract of its union vice-president, Ms M, on 28 February 2009 for selling noodles inside the factory during working hours, and the Labour Disputes Department authorised her termination on 13 March 2009. [Para 79][Para 81] A rival union at the factory held the most-representative certificate, valid to 11 February 2011. [Para 49][Para 178]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must reinstate the union vice-president, Ms M, and the union activist, Ms Y? [Para 75]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must raise the US$5 monthly attendance bonus to US$10? [Para 97]
3. (Issue 5) Whether the employer must add US$1 a year to the seniority bonus of workers with five or more years' service? [Para 101]
4. (Issue 11) Whether the employer must pay a 1,500 riel meal allowance for two hours' overtime, and 1,500 riel per hour beyond that? [Para 107]
5. (Issue 18) Whether the employer must build a nursery or pay US$20 a month instead? [Para 112]
6. (Issue 19) Whether the employer must restore holiday pay at 300%, as paid from 2001 to December 2008? [Para 120]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, Union for Freedom at Rousing Company):** the suspensions of Ms M and Ms Y were anti-union discrimination, coming within a month of the union's creation and after a series of other union officers were terminated; the US$5 bonus, the seniority increment and the meal allowance should rise because market prices had risen; a nursery was required; and the 300% holiday rate had been applied since 2001. [Para 86][Para 98][Para 103][Para 108][Para 241]

**Employer (respondent, Rousing Garment Co., Ltd.):** Ms M was terminated for a disciplinary offence, not for union activity; the attendance bonus, seniority increment and meal allowance already met Notification No. 017 and the law; it had built a nursery before but no worker used it; and the 200% holiday rate in the contracts and internal rules was restored after an accounting error, with the workers raising no objection for over three months. [Para 83][Para 100][Para 110][Para 123][Para 259]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issues 1, 2, 5, 11 and 19 were refused, and on Issue 18 the employer was ordered to set up a nursery in the factory, with childcare at its expense in an outside nursery where it cannot take children over 18 months. [Para 269][Para 270][Para 271][Para 273][Para 274][Para 276]

On Issue 1 the panel did not reach the merits of Ms M's case. Under Article 293 of the Labour Law a union leader's dismissal requires the labour inspector's authorisation, with a two-month appeal to the Minister; the Department had authorised it on 13 March 2009 and Ms M had not appealed by the second hearing on 22 April 2009, so the question lay in the appeal procedure and not with the panel. Award 149/08-Cambo Handsome was followed. [Para 139][Para 143][Para 151][Para 153] Ms Y's demand was refused because she had resigned and collected her final pay, never appeared at either hearing, and the union could produce no evidence for her. [Para 161][Para 164]

Issues 2 and 5 were refused as benefit claims. The US$5 attendance bonus is the minimum set by point 3 of Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000, preserved by Notification No. 745 of 23 October 2006, and the employer was paying it. [Para 167][Para 170] Point 5 of Notification No. 017 stops at four years' service and requires no further increment, and no law, agreement or past practice supplied one. [Para 195][Para 198] Both demands exceeded the law, and the union lacked most-representative status, which the panel treats as a precondition for benefit claims; the certified union was the other one at the factory. [Para 175][Para 178][Para 185]

Issue 11 failed on the same ground: point 4 of Notification No. 017 fixes the meal allowance at 1,000 riel a day or a free meal, which was paid, and no instrument provided 1,500 riel. [Para 205][Para 208]

Issue 18 succeeded. Article 186 of the Labour Law requires an enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a nursing room and a nursery, and where children over 18 months cannot be accommodated the employer must pay for an outside nursery on production of invoices, as held in Awards 63/04-Saovil and 115/08-Top World. A cash payment of US$5 a month did not discharge that duty. [Para 223][Para 226][Para 237][Para 239]

On Issue 19 the majority held that a 300% rate paid from 2001 to December 2008 and changed by notice of 15 November 2008 became a binding oral agreement under Article 65 of the Labour Law, but that over three months' silence made it a new agreement at 200%, and that Article 164 and Prakas No. 10 of 4 February 1999 entitle a worker to 200% only, so 300% was a benefit claim. [Para 248][Para 254][Para 261][Para 264][Para 268]

**Dissent:** the annexed opinion of arbitrator Liv Sovanna argues that no rule makes silence consent, that Article 120 gives a three-year limitation for wage claims, that the union filed within a month and eleven days of registration, and that the employer stays bound to 300% until a new agreement or a new contract. [Para 320][Para 325][Para 326]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand above what statute, notification, agreement or established practice grants is a benefit claim that the panel will not decide, and a union without a most-representative certificate cannot bring one at all. The dismissal of a union leader must first run the Article 293 route of the labour inspector's authorisation and the two-month appeal to the Minister before the panel can look at it. An employer's statutory duty to provide a nursery is not discharged by a small monthly cash payment. [Para 153][Para 185][Para 237]

**Obiter dicta:** the majority's view that prolonged failure to object to a reduced holiday rate converts it into a new binding agreement — a point the annexed opinion disputes as contrary to Articles 65 and 120 of the Labour Law. [Para 261][Para 321]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The Khmer text is severely OCR-damaged: the first page and every page header dissolve into digits and diacritics, telephone and fax fields repeat numbers at random, and several passages insert untranslated Latin strings where Khmer was lost. The employer's name is printed both as រូស៊ីង and រ៉ូស៊ីង, and the claimant is described at different points as the Union for Freedom at Rousing Company and as the Cambodian Workers' Union at the Rousing factory, which are different bodies. The annexed dissenting opinion is written partly in the panel's own voice, so the boundary between the majority's reasoning on Issue 19 and the dissent is blurred, and Issue 19's reasoning recites a 300% entitlement while the order dismisses the 300% demand — a contradiction unless the annex is read as dissent. The award prints the two union officers and several supporting workers by full name, reduced here to initials.
