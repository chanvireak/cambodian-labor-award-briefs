# Award No. 03/09 — Xing Tai Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 6 February 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Ann Vireak, Kong Phallack presiding) · Doc ID 140347

## 1. Facts
The respondent is Xing Tai Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd., Northbridge Street, Tuol Thla, Russei Keo, Phnom Penh, employing about 750 workers. The claimant is the Local Union of the Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers Democratic Union (C.CAWDU), with about 350 members and no most-representative status. Of twelve demands, three were settled in conciliation and, at the hearing, the workers withdrew two more and the employer agreed to install one additional punch-in machine, leaving six issues for decision. The panel also declined to consider two further demands — pro-rata deduction of the bonus for unauthorised absence and full wage for leave taken for personal commitments — as interest disputes the union had no standing to bring. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 26][Para 21][Para 22][Para 119][Para 128]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the dismissal of the local union's vice-president for refusing a work assignment was lawful, when the employer notified the Labour Inspector but never obtained authorisation to dismiss him? [Para 27]
2. (Issue 2) Whether a worker's severe headache, treated by an outside doctor during working hours at a cost of 60,000 riel, was a work-related accident the employer must reimburse? [Para 38]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer's refusal to raise the local union secretary's wage by USD 20, and to give him the overtime given to other mechanics, was union discrimination? [Para 42]
4. (Issue 5) Whether the employer must pay the whole maternity benefit — 50 per cent of 90 days' wages and perquisites — before the leave begins rather than partly on return? [Para 59]
5. (Issue 8) Whether the USD 7 monthly attendance bonus must be reduced in proportion to days of authorised absence instead of being forfeited entirely? [Para 62]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, C.CAWDU Local Union):** the vice-president was deliberately overloaded and then dismissed on a pretext because he helped workers resolve their problems; the quality-control worker's illness arose at work; the secretary's rejected wage requests and withheld overtime were retaliation for his union role; the full maternity payment should be handed over before leave because mothers face heavy costs; and the attendance bonus is part of wages, so it should be cut only in proportion to days absent. [Para 36][Para 46][Para 60][Para 64]
**Employer (respondent):** the vice-president refused to perform the piecework-recording job assigned to him, slammed a table and used improper language, so it dismissed him for serious misconduct after two written warnings; the headache was the worker's own illness; the secretary's poor performance, slow repairs and wasteful use of spare parts explain the refused increase, and overtime is its management right; it pays two months before leave and one on return under an existing agreement; and the bonus exists to reward regular attendance. [Para 4][Para 49][Para 51][Para 65]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must reinstate the local union vice-president from the date the award takes effect with back pay from dismissal to reinstatement, pay 50 per cent of 90 days' wages and perquisites before maternity leave begins, and deduct the USD 7 attendance bonus pro rata for authorised absence; the 60,000 riel medical claim and the USD 20 wage increase were rejected. [Para 128]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 293 of the Labour Law and clause 4 of Prakas No. 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001, which extends shop-steward protection to the three union leaders: dismissal requires the Labour Inspector's authorisation, and the employer's letter of 26 November 2008 was mere notification, followed by dismissal before any authorisation arrived, so the procedure was unlawful; it followed Awards 79/06-Woosu, 74/08-Generation International and 107/08-Seratex. [Para 72][Para 74][Para 75][Para 76]

On issue 2 the panel read Article 248 of the Labour Law, as interpreted in Award 85/07-Vivatino Design, to cover accidents at work or in working hours, direct commuting accidents, and occupational illness; a headache with no evidence of any workplace cause fell outside all three, so the claim failed for want of proof. [Para 80][Para 81][Para 82]

On issue 3 the panel first rejected the employer's argument that the demand was an individual dispute, since the Ministry had referred it as collective and the employer produced no evidence to the contrary; on the merits, clause 5 of the Internal Work Rules makes increases depend on performance, discipline and the employer's capacity, the secretary admitted he slackened after his request was refused, and the workers showed no concrete evidence of union discrimination. [Para 90][Para 92][Para 94][Para 96]

On issue 5 the panel applied Articles 182 and 183 — 90 days' leave and half wage including perquisites after one year's uninterrupted service — together with Article 115(3), under which payment falling on a day off is made the day before; following Awards 57/06-Evergreen, 97/06-New Max Garment and 08/08-Hytex it ordered the whole benefit paid before the leave starts, and noted that workers with under a year's service get the leave but not the pay. [Para 98][Para 99][Para 102][Para 103]

On issue 8 the panel held the bonus is part of wage under Article 103, and that authorised absence suspends the contract under Articles 71(6) and 72(1), so the employer may deduct pro rata; it followed Awards 57/07-Seratex, 106/07-M & V and 107/08-Seratex. Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented on this point, arguing that a bonus is conditional and may be withheld but not divided. [Para 107][Para 111][Para 130]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union leader may be dismissed only after the Labour Inspector has authorised it; a letter informing the Inspector of an intended dismissal is not a request for authorisation, and a dismissal carried out before authorisation is issued is unlawful whatever the underlying misconduct. [Para 75][Para 76]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's discussion of why an award on an interest dispute would replace a collective agreement for one year and bar industrial action was not necessary to its refusals, which rested on the union's lack of most-representative status. [Para 116][Para 118]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
This is an unofficial English translation of a Khmer original, and its OCR is damaged: the definite article is consistently fused into the following word ("themployer", "thearing", "thevidence"), several dates and the wage figure in issue 3 are lost, and the evidence section is omitted by the translator. Individual names of the union vice-president, the union secretary and the injured worker are printed in full in the source and are reduced to roles here. Membership is given as about 350 in the facts and 375 of 700 workers in the reasons, against 750 workers elsewhere. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 118]
