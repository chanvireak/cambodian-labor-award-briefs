# Award No. 38/09 — AIA Garment Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 8 April 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (You Suonty, Tuon Siphann, Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 140372

## 1. Facts
AIA Garment Co., Ltd. operates on Street 253, Toeuk Laak Village, Toeuk Laak III Commune, Tuol Kork District, Phnom Penh, and has run since 1997 with about 300 workers. [Para 14][Para 15] The only union is the Local Union of the Khmer Youth Trade Union Federation (KYFTU), with roughly 140 members and no most representative status; 49 non-members authorised it to pursue the termination-payment demand on their behalf. [Para 15] From January 2009 the employer had intermittent work, sometimes sending workers home on arrival, paid full wages only on days worked and half on the others, then suspended work from 1 March to 17 March and 17 March to 18 April 2009 while offering US$ 8; the workers say the Labour Inspector was never notified or asked to approve. [Para 17][Para 21][Para 48] The employer did not attend the hearing and gave no reason. [Para 9][Para 33] Of the nine non-conciliated demands, issues 2, 5, 6, 7 and 9 — meal allowance for Sunday and holiday overtime, closing or relocation payments, a US$ 3 attendance-bonus increase, a standby physician and medicine, and a day-care and nursing room — were withdrawn, leaving issues 1, 3, 4 and 8. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 3]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer lawfully suspended the labour contracts, so that it owes full wages with back pay for the days without work from January to April 2009? [Para 38]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay termination payments under the Labour Law should it close the factory? [Para 50]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must stop removing sewing machines and other equipment from the factory during the suspension? [Para 56]
4. (Issue 8) Whether the employer was entitled to deduct the US$ 5 attendance bonus for December 2008 from three workers? [Para 66]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — Local Union of the Khmer Youth Trade Union Federation (KYFTU):** the suspensions were unlawful because the employer neither informed nor obtained approval from the Labour Inspector, so full wages are owed for every day without work, including the March and April periods paid at US$ 8; the equipment removals threaten their return to work; and the December attendance bonus was earned because the three workers attended regularly that month. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 27][Para 33]
**Employer (respondent) — AIA Garment Co., Ltd.:** no position was put; the employer was absent from the conciliation and from the hearing, made no response to any issue and gave no reason for its non-attendance. [Para 9][Para 23][Para 36]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay additional wages amounting to full wages for the days without work from January to April 2009, declined to consider the termination-payment demand, rejected the equipment demand, and ordered back payment of the US$ 5 December 2008 attendance bonus to the three named workers. [Para 66]

The panel first held it could proceed in the employer's absence: Clause 21 of Prakas No. 99/04 allows the panel to continue where a duly invited party fails to appear without good cause, following Awards 53/04-Kuong Hong, 63/04-Shine Well and 06/08-Kingsland. [Para 35][Para 37]

On Issue 1, Article 71(11) permits suspension in serious economic or material difficulty for no more than two months and under the Labour Inspector's control, and Article 72(1) relieves the employer of wage payment only for a lawful suspension. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41] The employer notified nobody, so the suspension was unlawful and full wages were due for each day without work; the panel followed its consistent line in Awards 21/03-Loyal, 46/04-M & A, 60/06-New Max, 74/07-Global Apparels, 28/08-FineGis and 53/08-Yung Wah (Branch 1) that an employer without work which does not lawfully suspend must pay in full. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 48] The March and April suspensions at US$ 8 were unlawful on the same basis. [Para 49][Para 50]

On Issue 3, the panel found the demand conditional on a closure that had not happened: the senior administration officer had said in early March 2009 that the company could not continue, but the director told the workers the next day that the factory would not close and only the contracts would be suspended; a claim about an unpredictable future event cannot be decided. [Para 51][Para 53][Para 54][Para 55]

On Issue 4, Article 2 and the Awards 62/06-Quicksew and 108/06-Trinunggal Komara give an employer the right to supervise and direct the enterprise, including its assets, exercised lawfully and reasonably; 13 machines removed against a workforce of 300 was a small number, and the workers offered no evidence of a coming shortage. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 61][Para 63]

On Issue 8, Point 3 of Notification No. 745 KKBV of 23 October 2006 preserves the benefits in Point 3 of Notification No. 017 SKBY of 18 July 2000, the attendance bonus for regular attendance; the three workers had attended regularly in December 2008, and the panel therefore held the deduction contrary to law under Clause 34 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY. [Para 66]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that has no work for its staff but does not suspend the contracts through the Labour Inspector under Article 71(11) of the Labour Law must pay full wages for the idle days, whatever partial payment it chooses to make; and an attendance bonus already earned for regular attendance cannot be deducted for a later protest. [Para 43][Para 48][Para 66]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that 13 machines removed from a plant of 300 workers is too small a number to found a fear of future shortage. [Para 63]
