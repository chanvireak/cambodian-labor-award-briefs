# Award No. 135/09 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្វ័រ ធ្យូន (Four D' Town) (Arbitration Council, 23 October 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: ហៅ សំបូរ ណា (employer-appointed), (worker-appointed arbitrator, name lost to OCR), គង់ ផល្លា (president) · Doc ID 140441

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្វ័រ ធ្យូន, a garment factory employing about 1,850 workers, was respondent to a collective claim brought by សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា (CCAWDU), the union holding most-representative status at the factory by certificate of 8 January 2008. [Para 77][Para 78]
Thirteen demands were tabled; twelve of them (points 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12 and 13) were collective-bargaining demands previously litigated in case 29/09, in which the panel had ordered the employer to negotiate a collective agreement (អនុសញ្ញារួម) in good faith within three months. [Para 79][Para 81]
The thirteenth claim in dispute, point 5, was separate: reinstatement of four named workers — a team leader in the cutting section, a worker in the fabric-inspection section, one in packing and one in the mobile section — with back pay from dismissal to re-engagement. [Para 90][Para 120]
The prior order 29/09 issued on 21 July 2009 became binding on 29 July 2009, no party having objected within eight days, and its three-month negotiation window ran to 29 October 2009. [Para 82][Para 84]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 5) Whether the dismissal of the four workers named in point 5 gives rise to a collective labour dispute within the panel's jurisdiction, so that their reinstatement with back pay can be ordered? [Para 122]
2. (Points 1–4, 6–13) Whether the employer must continue negotiating the twelve collective demands with the union so as to conclude a collective agreement by 29 October 2009? [Para 119]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, CCAWDU):** the employer had not honoured order 29/09, roughly five negotiation sessions had failed, and the union demanded reinstatement of the four workers at their original posts with back pay from dismissal to re-engagement; the strike arose because the employer refused the demands and the union could not hold the workers back. [Para 85][Para 88][Para 90]
**Employer (respondent, ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្វ័រ ធ្យូន):** the six planned negotiation rounds had reached only three when the workers struck early; each of the four dismissals had already been resolved under the individual-dispute procedure at the Kandal provincial department of labour, two of them by award 74/09, so the matter was not a collective dispute; and the twelve remaining demands belong to the ongoing bargaining. [Para 87][Para 108][Para 134]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered both parties to continue negotiating the twelve collective demands (points 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12 and 13) in order to conclude a collective agreement, with the three-month window running until 29 October 2009, and rejected the point 5 claim for reinstatement and back pay. [Para 138][Para 139]
On the twelve bargaining points, the panel applied Prakas No. 099/04 on the Arbitration Council, Articles 40 and 41: an award becomes final and binding where no party objects within eight calendar days of notification. Order 29/09 having issued on 21 July 2009, no objection having been filed by the eighth day, the panel held it binding on both sides from 29 July 2009 and directed the parties to keep negotiating in good faith until 29 October 2009. [Para 110][Para 112][Para 114][Para 118]
On point 5, the panel first asked whether it had jurisdiction, noting that in principle it follows the labour inspector's and the Ministry's characterisation of a dispute as individual or collective absent a clear reason to depart from it. Applying Article 302 of the Labour Law, it found condition (a) satisfied because the claim was filed by the union, and condition (b) satisfied because the subject was working conditions and employment relations; condition (c) failed, because four dismissals at different times for different reasons had not been shown to obstruct the enterprise or disturb social peace. [Para 123][Para 130][Para 131][Para 132]
The panel added that two of the four had already had their reinstatement claim refused in award 74/09, and that the other two had been processed as individual disputes by the provincial department, so the four-worker claim was not a collective dispute and was not to be considered. The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification if no party objects. [Para 134][Para 136][Para 137][Para 140]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union's claim on behalf of several dismissed workers is a collective labour dispute only if all three Article 302 conditions are met; dismissals occurring at different times, for different reasons, without demonstrated disruption to the enterprise or to social peace, fail condition (c) and fall outside the panel's collective-dispute jurisdiction, whatever the union's representative status. [Para 129][Para 132]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that it will ordinarily defer to the labour inspector's and Ministry's classification of a dispute as individual or collective unless there is a clear contrary reason, and its remark that the parties had not yet exhausted their own six-round negotiation schedule. [Para 117][Para 123]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
OCR damage is heavy throughout. The header date (23 October 2009) conflicts with the reasons, which print the date of order 29/09 twice — as 21 July 2009 at one point and as "21 May July 2009" at another. [Para 1][Para 82][Para 113]
The negotiation deadline is printed as 29 October 2009 in the reasoning and as 29 October 2019 in the order-quotation, an evident digit loss. The number of demands is stated as thirteen, then as twelve, then as an unreadable "14" figure in the recital of the order. [Para 116][Para 119][Para 79]
Worker and union-official names are corrupted to the point that the same individual appears under two or three different spellings, and several are reduced to fragments; they are therefore given here by role only. The worker-appointed arbitrator's name survives only as unreadable characters, and the employer-appointed arbitrator's name is partly garbled in the signature block. The company name itself appears in at least four OCR variants (ហ្វ័រ ធ្យូន, ហ្វ័រ ឱ្យ ន, ហ្គីវ ឱ្យ ន). [Para 141][Para 142]
