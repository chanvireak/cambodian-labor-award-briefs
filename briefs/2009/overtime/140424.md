# Award No. 113/09 — ASD (Cambodia) Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 23 September 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, An Lean, presiding) · Doc ID 140424

## 1. Facts
The respondent is ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេស ឌី (ASD) (Cambodia) Co Ltd, a garment factory on National Road 4, Ang Snuol district, Kandal, employing about 710 workers; the claimant is the National Federation of Unions of the Textile, Garment and Apparel Industry of Cambodia (NIFTUC). In early April 2009 the parties agreed orally to swap days so that the whole workforce rested on 17 April 2009, a working day, and made the day up on 1 May 2009, International Labour Day, with no deduction for 17 April and no holiday premium for 1 May. Conciliation failed on both demands and the case came to the panel with two claims; the second, for a television and VCD player in the canteen, was withdrawn by the union at the hearing and is not decided here. [Para 12][Para 17][Para 31]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the April 2009 day-swap agreement extends to the seven workers hired after 17 April 2009, so that the employer need not pay them wage for 1 May 2009 or the holiday premium for work done that day? [Para 48][Para 49]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the claim is simply one for what the law allows: 200 per cent wage for those who worked overtime on 1 May 2009 and an undisturbed day's wage for those who rested, because the seven workers hired after 17 April never took part in any swap and never rested on 17 April. [Para 30][Para 46]
**Employer (respondent):** the employer did for these workers exactly what it did for the majority, applying the day-swap agreement concluded in early April 2009; it accepts that in fact wages were deducted from those absent on 1 May and no premium was paid to those who came. [Para 36][Para 47]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must pay one day's wage to each of the 2 workers whose wage it deducted, and one additional day's wage to each of the 5 workers who volunteered to work on 1 May 2009. [Para 64][Para 65]

The panel applied Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 22 of Decree No. 38 dated 28 October 1988: a contract has effect only between the parties to it, so only they may terminate or vary the employment relationship it creates, a rule it had already followed in Award No. 54/08. [Para 52][Para 53] On the facts the oral swap was made before 17 April 2009 between the employer and the workers then employed; the seven hired afterwards neither joined the agreement nor rested on 17 April, so the swap could not be set against them and they owed the employer no make-up day on 1 May. [Para 54][Para 55]

Turning to the money, the panel read Article 4 of Prakas No. 10 of 4 February 1999, Articles 161 and 162 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 214 of 23 October 2008: a worker who works on a paid public holiday earns one further day's wage on top of what he already receives, and rest on such a holiday may not reduce monthly, fortnightly or weekly wage, so the employer's deductions from the two absentees were unlawful and the five who worked were owed the extra day. [Para 57][Para 59][Para 62][Para 63] Evidence on headcount was taken from the union's list of 15 September 2009, which named eight claimants; one was excluded because he entered on 28 May 2009, after the holiday, leaving seven, of whom two suffered a deduction and five worked. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 42][Para 44]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A collective arrangement that exchanges a working day for a public holiday binds only the workers who were parties to it; a worker recruited after the exchanged rest day cannot be made to make it up, and any work he does on the substituted public holiday is payable at the statutory holiday rate on top of his normal wage. [Para 54][Para 59]

**Obiter dicta:** none identified.
