# Award No. 124/09 — Focus Footwear (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្វូកឺ ស ហ្វូត វៀ (ខេមបូឌៀ) ខូ អិលធីឌី) (Arbitration Council, 2 October 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of three: Ouk Ri (employer-appointed), An Virak (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhean (president) · Doc ID 140435

## 1. Facts
Focus Footwear (Cambodia) Co., Ltd., a shoe factory operating since 1997 at Tuol Sangkae, Khan Russey Keo, Phnom Penh with about 548 workers, faced twelve demands from the **សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ការងារ កម្មករ កម្ពុជា** (Cambodia Workers' Labour Union Federation), a registered union with about 282 members in the plant. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 48]
Conciliation left eight demands unresolved; at the hearing the parties settled issues 1 and 5, and the union withdrew issue 8 by letter of 22 September 2009, leaving issues 2, 3, 4, 6 and 7 for decision. [Para 23][Para 27][Para 28][Para 29]
The disputes concerned daily briefings held before the shift, a 5% end-of-contract payment, severance for workers who resign, the USD 5 attendance bonus, and advance payment of maternity pay. [Para 49][Para 64][Para 80][Para 86][Para 95]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the company must stop holding workers' meetings outside working hours, between 06:45 and 07:00 or 06:50 and 07:00, or pay overtime for that time? [Para 49]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay 5% of wages as an end-of-contract indemnity to each worker when a fixed-term contract reaches its end? [Para 64]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the company must pay workers who resign of their own accord the same benefits as workers it dismisses? [Para 80]
4. (Issue 6) Whether the USD 5 attendance bonus must be paid in full, or only pro-rated, to workers who take two days of authorised personal leave in a month? [Para 86]
5. (Issue 7) Whether the company must advance the 50% maternity wage for the full 90 days before a woman's maternity leave begins? [Para 95]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), through the Cambodia Workers' Labour Union Federation:** briefings should be held inside working hours so that every worker hears the instructions, and if they are held outside those hours the time should be paid as overtime; workers who have renewed fixed-term contracts for years and now hold open-ended contracts should receive the 5% indemnity for the earlier fixed terms; long-serving workers who resign deserve severance; the attendance bonus should be reduced in proportion to authorised leave rather than forfeited entirely; and maternity pay should be paid in one sum before leave because travel to collect it monthly is costly and a woman recovering from childbirth should not have to travel. [Para 61][Para 69][Para 83][Para 89][Para 99]
**Employer (respondent), Focus Footwear (Cambodia) Co., Ltd.:** the 06:50 meeting lasts only five to ten minutes, attendance is voluntary, no penalty is imposed on absentees and food is allowed; the 5% indemnity is payable only when a contract is actually terminated, not when it is renewed or converted to an open-ended contract; it applies whatever the law requires; it may deduct the bonus on authorised absence because the bonus requires 26 full days; and paying 90 days of maternity pay in advance is impossible because of its accounting system. [Para 52][Para 76][Para 85][Para 92][Para 104]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the daily briefings to be held within working hours from the date the award takes effect, with overtime pay at the statutory rate if they are held outside those hours; ordered the attendance bonus to be pro-rated to the days of authorised absence rather than forfeited; ordered 50% maternity pay for 90 days to be advanced to women before their leave begins; and dismissed issues 3 and 4. [Para 176][Para 177][Para 180][Para 181][Para 178][Para 179]
On issue 2 the panel applied Article 2 of the Labour Law, which gives the employer the right to organise and direct the enterprise, and held that daily instruction meetings are a lawful exercise of that right. [Para 107][Para 112][Para 113]
It then applied Article 137, capping normal hours at eight a day and forty-eight a week, and Article 139 on overtime rates of 50% and 100%, and Prakas No. 80 of 1999, which requires overtime to be voluntary and forbids pressure or discipline against those who decline; because absence from a briefing leaves a worker uninformed about the day's work, attendance is not truly voluntary, so briefing time outside hours is overtime. [Para 114][Para 120][Para 124][Para 125][Para 127]
On issue 3 the panel applied Article 73 of the Labour Law and followed Award 60/06 (Numek Gaming): the 5% indemnity is owed only where a fixed-term contract ends and the employment relationship is actually severed, and where the contract is renewed or converted into an open-ended one there is no severance, so the demand failed. [Para 133][Para 139][Para 141][Para 143]
On issue 4 the panel found the demand concerned resignations that had not yet occurred and could not be assessed, and dismissed it as a demand for the future. [Para 148][Para 150][Para 151]
On issue 6 the panel read Notice No. 745 of 23 October 2006, which preserves the benefits of Notice No. 017, and Notice No. 017 of 18 July 2000, which guarantees at least USD 5 to a worker who works the regular number of days in a month; since neither text says whether authorised absence forfeits the bonus, the panel followed Awards 57/07 and 143/08 and ordered pro-rating. [Para 155][Para 156][Para 157][Para 160][Para 162]
On issue 7 the panel applied Articles 182 and 183, which give 90 days' maternity leave on half pay including allowances, and Article 115, which forbids paying wages on a rest day and requires payment the day before; it followed Awards 57/06, 97/06, 08/08, 114/08 and 139/08 in holding that maternity pay must be advanced before leave begins. [Para 163][Para 165][Para 170][Para 171][Para 173][Para 175]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that calls a briefing outside working hours must treat the time as overtime and pay the statutory rate, because attendance at a briefing that conveys the day's instructions cannot be genuinely voluntary; and maternity pay must be advanced in full before leave begins, since Article 115 forbids payment on a rest day and requires payment the preceding day. [Para 125][Para 127][Para 170][Para 175]

**Obiter dicta:** none identified.

## 6. Source defects disclosed

[Para 130][Para 131][Para 172] The panel's composition is legible only from the header, because the signature block prints one arbitrator's name in fragments and another with stray Latin characters. [Para 183] The company is also referred to in the English transliteration "Focus Footwear (Cambodia) Co., Ltd." while the Khmer registry certificate gives a differently spelled trade name.
