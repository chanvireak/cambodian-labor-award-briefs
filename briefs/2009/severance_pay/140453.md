# Award No. 154/09 — វិទ្យុ អាស៊ី សេរី (Radio Free Asia) and four members of its Cambodian staff (Arbitration Council, 16 December 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (chair គង់ ផល្លៈ; employer nominee យូ សួន ទី; worker nominee ហួន ជុន ឌី) · Doc ID 140453

## 1. Facts
វិទ្យុ អាស៊ី សេរី (Radio Free Asia) has broadcast into Cambodia since 1997 and keeps about 20 staff in Phnom Penh. [Para 71][Para 72] Four of them had their contracts ended within three days in September 2009: a news editor and editorial adviser engaged in 1997 and terminated on 26 September, a news editor engaged in 2006 and terminated on 25 September, a web publisher engaged in 2005 and terminated on 25 September, and a reporter engaged in 2006 and paid per article, terminated on 24 September. [Para 86][Para 87][Para 138][Para 141][Para 167][Para 168][Para 189][Para 190] Each held a written contract describing the engagement as a service contract, with monthly pay fixed at USD 1,750, USD 1,200 and USD 1,400 for the first three, and per-article rates of USD 50 or USD 100 for the reporter. [Para 116][Para 156][Para 181][Para 199] Conciliation at the Ministry of Labour on 30 October 2009 failed on a single demand: payment of the benefits in Articles 75, 89, 91, 116, 166 and 167 of the Labour Law. [Para 2][Para 10]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether an employment relationship existed between the broadcaster and each of the four claimants? [Para 221]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the dispute is a collective labour dispute within Article 302 of the Labour Law? [Para 280][Para 288]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the termination of the three employees was lawful, and what benefits they are owed? [Para 297][Para 326]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — the four staff of វិទ្យុ អាស៊ី សេរី):** they worked under the broadcaster's direction, on schedules fixed by its senior editors, with pay set monthly, leave requiring permission, and disciplinary warnings issued by email; the terminations were reprisals for their protest at the dismissal of their colleague and they are owed notice pay, severance, unused annual leave and moral damages. [Para 103][Para 112][Para 158][Para 244][Para 323]
**Employer (respondent — វិទ្យុ អាស៊ី សេរី):** the four were independent contractors under enterprise contracts governed by Decree No. 38 of 1988, not employees; there is no union at the broadcaster, the dispute is not collective, the contracts ended in a restructuring forced by the economic crisis and two months' pay was paid as a goodwill gesture. [Para 84][Para 85][Para 210][Para 212][Para 216]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the broadcaster must pay notice pay, severance, unused annual leave and moral damages to the three employees, while the claim of the fourth claimant is dismissed for want of an employment relationship. [Para 355][Para 357][Para 358]
The panel began with Articles 1, 2 and 3 of the Labour Law, which apply only where a contract of employment exists, and with its earlier awards in Cases No. 04/05 and 53/07, which identify the indicators of an employer's control: recruitment, wage-setting, hours, leave, discipline and termination. [Para 221][Para 229][Para 234][Para 235] No single indicator is decisive; the test is how the parties actually behaved. [Para 234][Para 236]
On the news editor and editorial adviser it found recruitment by the broadcaster's deputy director, work supervised by named senior editors, a schedule issued by the broadcaster, a fixed monthly wage unaffected by output, office attendance by 09:00, leave requiring permission, and an email warning issued for breaching the programme rules; that control made him an employee, and the same finding was made for the second news editor and the web publisher. [Para 237][Para 242][Para 244][Para 246][Para 251][Para 253][Para 254][Para 258][Para 260][Para 262]
On the reporter, by contrast, the panel found that she controlled her own programme and working methods, produced articles as her own work product without prior editorial control beyond conformity with requirements, was paid per article rather than by time, was free to take other work, and was never promoted, demoted or disciplined; there was therefore no employment relationship and her claim was refused. [Para 266][Para 267][Para 268][Para 269][Para 271][Para 276][Para 278]
On the collective character of the dispute, Article 302 requires that it arise between a group of workers and their employer, concern working conditions, and be capable of disrupting the enterprise or public peace; all three conditions were met by the three employees and the disruption of broadcasts. [Para 288][Para 290][Para 293][Para 294][Para 295][Para 296]
On termination, the panel applied Articles 332 and 333: a strike is a right, and participation in it — even irregular — is not a ground for sanction, so the dismissal of the first claimant for joining the protest was unjustified. [Para 312][Para 315][Para 319] For the other two, the broadcaster gave restructuring as its reason but never explained why restructuring required ending their contracts, and both were dismissed days after protesting about their colleague's fate, so their dismissals also lacked a valid reason. [Para 322][Para 323][Para 324][Para 325] The panel then awarded notice pay under Articles 75 and 77 — three months for the longest-serving employee and one month for the other two — severance of 15 days per year of service under Article 89, payment for unused annual leave under Article 166, and moral damages equal to the severance under Article 91. [Para 327][Para 331][Para 335][Para 336][Para 337][Para 348][Para 354][Para 357]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Whether a contract is one of employment turns on the employer's actual control — over recruitment, schedule, wage, leave, discipline and termination — not on the label the parties give the contract; a contributor who controls her own working methods, is paid per item, may work elsewhere and is never disciplined is not an employee. [Para 234][Para 249][Para 271] Dismissal for taking part in a strike is unlawful under Articles 332 and 333, and an employer that pleads restructuring must explain why it required the particular dismissals. [Para 319][Para 322][Para 324]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the labour inspector and the Minister normally decide whether a dispute is collective and that the panel will follow that decision absent a clear reason to depart from it. [Para 281]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
An annex to the award carries a written objection from the employer-nominated arbitrator, who argued that the first claimant committed serious misconduct under Article 83(3) by burning tyres and should receive nothing; that dissent is not the award's reasoning and is reported separately here. [Para 360][Para 361][Para 370][Para 376] One passage puts the number of staff at "43" where the evidence shows four claimants and about 20 staff in all. [Para 72][Para 304] The award date on the face is 16 December 2009, while the objection is dated the same day and the conciliation report 30 October 2009. [Para 1][Para 67][Para 377] The chair's name is printed as គង់ ផល្លៈ, គង់ ផល្ល and គង់ ផលៈ, and one sentence dates an episode to the year 2016. [Para 1][Para 359][Para 337] The four claimants are named in full in the reasons and appear here as Mr A, Mr H, Mr T and Ms S. [Para 76][Para 357]
