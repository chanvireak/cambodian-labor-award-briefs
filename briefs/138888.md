# Award No. 016/18 — Cheng Shi Xin Manufacturing (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 14 June 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ouk Ri (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Kong Phalha (president) · Doc ID 138888

## 1. Facts
The company, a garment manufacturer in Takeo province employing about 850 workers, engaged Mr S on a two-month probation contract from 13 December 2017 and then a three-month fixed-duration contract running 13 February to 12 May 2018. Mr S was elected president of the Amnach Youth Union at the company on 21 April 2018; notice of the result was posted to the employer on 23 April 2018 and refused, the postal service returning it on 4 May 2018. On 12 May 2018, the contract's expiry date, the employer told him it would not be renewed and offered statutory severance of 1,188.36 USD in total, which he refused. [Para 1][Para 42][Para 49][Para 50][Para 51][Para 65]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take Mr S back into work in the sewing section, attendance number 1149, because the decision not to renew his contract was discrimination against a union leader? [Para 45][Para 92][Para 107]
2. Whether the employer must pay a midday meal allowance of 2,000 riel per day to the workers? [Para 72][Para 147]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the non-renewal had no cause — Mr S had never committed a fault or received a warning — and was union discrimination, because he had just been elected president of the Amnach Youth Union at the company after the election of 21 April 2018; the meal allowance is requested because wages are insufficient and neighbouring factories such as Y Cheng pay it. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 73][Para 74]
**Employer (respondent):** the contract simply reached its term and the company was entitled to end it, paying 5% indemnity plus the other statutory sums; it also declined to renew four other workers in April, Mr S was often absent and too slow for his line chief to manage, and it did not know he had been elected because it refused the postal notice; it renewed another union leader, Mr H, on 19 May 2018, which it would not have done had it been discriminating. [Para 2][Para 55][Para 57][Para 58][Para 59][Para 75]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed in full — the panel refused to order the employer to reinstate Mr S, and refused to consider the meal-allowance claim; no sum was awarded beyond the 1,188.36 USD the employer had already offered. [Para 146][Para 166][Para 169][Para 170]

On issue 1 the panel began with standing: no most representative union exists at the company, so it applied Article 54(1) of the Union Law and Prakas 362 of 29 August 2016 to hold that a worker certified by the Takeo Labour Department may represent the workforce, and that Mr S, who held such a certificate and appeared at the hearing, could bring the case. [Para 76][Para 80][Para 88][Para 90] On the merits, Article 73(1) of the Labour Law provides that a fixed-duration contract expires of its own accord when the term is reached; the panel followed its earlier awards to hold that expiry ends the employment relationship automatically and that renewal is a matter for the parties, neither being able to force the other. [Para 93][Para 95][Para 97][Para 98] It therefore treated the case as a non-renewal, not a dismissal, so the rules protecting workers from dismissal did not apply. [Para 103][Para 104] Turning to the discrimination allegation, the panel held that the worker bears the burden of proving union discrimination, and weighed the postal receipts against the employer's evidence. [Para 121][Para 125] It accepted that the refusal of the postal notice and the postal service's confirmation meant the employer must be taken to have known of the election result, but found that knowledge alone was not enough to infer discrimination. [Para 132][Para 135][Para 136] The employer's consistent explanation — other workers not renewed, absenteeism, slow output, and the renewal of another union leader, Mr H, who had also been elected an officer — was not contradicted by the workers, and the claim failed on the evidence. [Para 137][Para 140][Para 141][Para 142][Para 143]

On issue 2 the panel found no statutory provision, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice obliging the employer to pay 2,000 riel a day, so the demand was an interest dispute rather than a rights dispute. [Para 148][Para 149][Para 150] Because an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement and binds all workers for a year under Article 43(1) of Prakas 099, the panel may decide one only where the union bringing it is most representative or otherwise entitled under Articles 54 and 72 of the Union Law; this union was neither, so the claim was not considered. [Para 160][Para 163][Para 164][Para 166]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-duration contract that reaches its term expires automatically, and the employer's decision not to renew is not a dismissal — so the dismissal protections do not apply and no reinstatement can be ordered; to succeed on an allegation that the non-renewal was union discrimination the worker must prove it, and proof that the employer knew of the worker's union office is not by itself proof of discrimination. A panel will also not decide an interest claim brought by a union that is not most representative in the enterprise. [Para 95][Para 103][Para 105][Para 136][Para 164]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel reasoned at length that service by post is effective even where the recipient refuses delivery, invoking Article 246 of the Civil Procedure Code and its earlier awards, although the point did not decide the case because the employer prevailed anyway. [Para 128][Para 129][Para 132]
