# Award No. 115/10 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ជី-ហ្វម៉ូស (ខេមបូឌា) and សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត រោងចក្រ ជី-ហ្វម៉ូស (Arbitration Council, 30 November 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, An Nan, Kong Phallack) · Doc ID 140321

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ជី-ហ្វម៉ូស (ខេមបូឌា), a factory at Kampoul, Ang Snuol, Kandal, with about 1,000 workers, faced eighteen demands on working conditions, of which twelve were settled at the hearing, leaving Issues 4, 5, 7, 8, 14 and 18. [Para 25][Para 29][Para 30][Para 41]
The claimant is សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត រោងចក្រ ជី-ហ្វម៉ូស, which the panel found does not hold most-representative status. [Para 42][Para 43]
The live demands were pro rata deduction of the USD 5 attendance bonus, a monthly pregnancy check-up day, maternity pay for three months in advance, one-year contracts, sick leave on a private clinic certificate, and repayment of union dues deducted after a worker withdrew consent. [Para 44][Para 49][Para 53][Para 61][Para 66][Para 72]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 4) Whether the company must deduct the USD 5 attendance bonus only in proportion to the days of leave the employer authorised, rather than in full? [Para 44][Para 80]
2. (Issue 5) Whether the company must allow a pregnant worker one paid day a month to attend an antenatal check-up? [Para 49][Para 102]
3. (Issue 7) Whether maternity pay of 50% of wages and allowances for the whole three months must be paid before maternity leave begins? [Para 53][Para 117]
4. (Issue 8) Whether the company must replace its three-month fixed-duration contracts with one-year contracts? [Para 61][Para 140]
5. (Issue 14) Whether the company must keep wages and the attendance bonus for sick leave certified by an officially recognised private doctor? [Para 66][Para 153]
6. (Issue 18) Whether the company must stop deducting union dues from workers who have withdrawn consent, and refund earlier deductions? [Para 72][Para 168]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា and the ជី-ហ្វម៉ូស factory union, through Mr K and Ms S:** the bonus must be cut only for the days actually taken; 1,000 riel no longer covers a meal and television campaigns urge monthly check-ups; maternity pay should be a lump sum before birth because travel after delivery is hard and relatives spend money collected on their behalf; three-month contracts leave workers insecure; private clinics licensed by the Ministry of Health issue valid certificates; and deductions must stop once a worker objects in writing. [Para 50][Para 56][Para 57][Para 64][Para 68][Para 71][Para 78][Para 79]
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន ជី-ហ្វម៉ូស, through Ms W, its lawyer Mr H and accounting and administrative staff:** the company follows Notification No. 017 and its 30 December 2009 agreement giving four check-ups of half a day; maternity pay is monthly as the law allows; the Ministry of Labour confirmed contracts of three months, six months or a year; only State hospital certificates are accepted because private ones can be bought; and dues can stop only if the old union asks. [Para 46][Para 51][Para 59][Para 65][Para 69][Para 74]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issues 4, 7 and 14 are granted in the workers' favour, Issue 18 is granted in part (stop the deductions, no refund), and Issues 5 and 8 are rejected. [Para 186][Para 187][Para 188][Para 189][Para 190][Para 191]
On Issue 4 the panel treated the USD 5 bonus as part of wages under Article 103, and an authorised absence as suspending the contract under Articles 71(6) and 72, so no wage is earned for those days and the employer may deduct — but only pro rata to the days absent, not the whole bonus, as prior awards (57/07, 106/07, 128/08) require. [Para 83][Para 89][Para 92][Para 95][Para 96][Para 101]
Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented on that issue, reasoning from the dictionary meaning of "bonus" that the giver sets the conditions and may withhold it entirely. [Para 196][Para 202][Para 213]
On Issue 5 the panel held that no provision requires a full day a month, the 2009 agreement governs, and the demand was a claim above the law; because this union is not the most representative union, an interest claim could not be entertained. [Para 104][Para 106][Para 115]
On Issue 7 the panel applied Articles 182 and 183 (90 days' leave, half pay including allowances after one year's service) with Article 115(3), which forbids paying wages on a rest day and requires payment the day before; maternity pay must therefore be paid before the leave starts, following Awards 70/09, 60/10 and 107/10. [Para 120][Para 121][Para 131][Para 137][Para 139]
On Issue 8 the panel held that under Article 65 and Articles 1 and 22 of Decree No. 38 KR.Ch contracts rest on consent, so the panel, as a third party, cannot impose a one-year term the employer refuses. [Para 142][Para 147][Para 149][Para 151][Para 152]
On Issue 14 the panel read Article 71(3) as suspending the contract for certified illness, and defined an official doctor as one trained at a recognised school and licensed by the Ministry of Health — including a private clinic doctor. Refusing such certificates was unlawful, though the company may discipline a worker who buys a false one. [Para 154][Para 158][Para 161][Para 164][Para 165][Para 167]
On Issue 18 the panel applied Article 129(2): consent to deduct dues must be in writing and may be withdrawn at any time, so the employer had to stop; but the workers proved no amount of past deductions, so no refund was ordered. [Para 168][Para 170][Para 173][Para 178][Para 182][Para 183]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The USD 5 attendance bonus is wages, so an authorised absence suspends the contract and the employer may reduce the bonus only in proportion to the days absent; withholding it in full for authorised leave is unlawful. [Para 89][Para 95][Para 101]
Deduction of union dues depends on the worker's written consent, which may be revoked at any time, so the employer must stop deducting on the worker's own objection and cannot insist that only the union may authorise termination. [Para 170][Para 173][Para 178]

**Obiter dicta:** A licensed private-clinic doctor is an "official doctor" for Article 71(3), while a worker who buys a false certificate may be disciplined under the internal rules. [Para 161][Para 165]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company's name is printed in at least four different forms across the file (with a hyphen, a quotation mark, an underscore, and a doubled "m"), and the case name in the header differs from the party block; the form used above is the one in the party block. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 30]
The employer-chosen arbitrator's name appears in four spellings, including two in the signature block and dissent annex, and the worker-chosen arbitrator gains an extra element in the signature block; the panel composition follows the face of the award. [Para 1][Para 194][Para 196]
Numbers are unstable: the maternity demand carries a stray figure before "50 per cent", the complaint line carries a stray "20", and the union is described as having no most-representative status while the panel nonetheless decides three of its claims on the merits as statutory entitlements. [Para 8][Para 24][Para 43][Para 106]
The 30 December 2009 agreement is cited as the company's authority throughout but its text survives only as a quotation at line 52. [Para 33][Para 52]
