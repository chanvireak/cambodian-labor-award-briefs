# Award No. 356/14 — Ever Green E Feril (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 January 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, chosen by the employer party; An Non, chosen by the worker party, dissenting; Tang Tri, chair chosen by the two) · Doc ID 139795

## 1. Facts
Ever Green E Feril (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a garment factory at Sangkat Tuek Thla, Khan Sen Sok, Phnom Penh, employing about 1,700 workers. [Para 48][Para 49] A total of 142 of them transferred their rights to the Federation of Trade Union of Khmer Workers' Spirit (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ស្មារតី កម្មករ ខ្មែរ), which the Ministry of Labour had not yet registered at the date of the award. [Para 50][Para 53][Para 54] Of twelve demands put forward, conciliation on 19 December 2014 settled five and left seven; at the hearing of 9 January 2015 the parties settled three more — issues 5, 6 and 7 — leaving four for decision. [Para 22][Para 27][Para 28]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of 4,000 riel per day? [Para 55][Para 83]
2. Whether the employer must raise the overtime meal allowance by 1,000 riel, to 2,000 riel per hour? [Para 61][Para 103]
3. Whether the employer must raise the regular attendance bonus from 10 to 15 US dollars a month, and whether it must pay that bonus pro rata for days of absence permitted by the employer? [Para 66][Para 111][Para 120]
4. Whether the employer must raise the travel and accommodation allowance from 7 to 15 US dollars a month? [Para 76][Para 158]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the price of food, rent, water and electricity has risen and other factories pay more, so the lunch allowance should be 4,000 riel a day, the overtime meal allowance 2,000 riel an hour, the attendance bonus 15 US dollars and the travel allowance 15 US dollars a month; the pro-rata deduction of the attendance bonus is unjust because a worker who obtains permission is treated the same as one absent without it. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 64][Para 72][Para 73][Para 79][Para 80]

**Employer (respondent):** it cannot meet the demands; it already pays above the Labour Law, having raised the 100 US dollar base wage by 5 US dollars to 105 US dollars (a figure also used to compute overtime), and pays 2,000 riel of overtime meal allowance, a 10 US dollar attendance bonus, 7 US dollars of travel and accommodation allowance and additional production and team bonuses. [Para 59][Para 61][Para 77][Para 82] The two-stage deduction of 5 US dollars for absence between days 1 and 15 and 5 US dollars for absence between days 16 and 30 is its established practice. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 152]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider all four remaining demands for want of standing, without reaching their merits, and the award was declared non-binding subject to the eight-day objection period. [Para 166][Para 168][Para 172]

On the first, second and fourth demands the panel reasoned in the same way: it found no provision of the Labour Law, no collective agreement, internal regulation, employment contract or past practice obliging the employer to pay 4,000 riel of daily lunch allowance, 2,000 riel per hour of overtime meal allowance or 15 US dollars of travel allowance, so each demand sought a benefit above the statutory floor and was an interests dispute rather than a rights dispute. [Para 84][Para 85][Para 106][Para 161][Para 162]

On the third demand the panel split the question. As to the increase from 10 to 15 US dollars it found no legal or contractual basis and classified the claim as an interests dispute. [Para 114][Para 116] As to the pro-rata deduction it applied Prakas No. 230 K.B/S.Ch.N. of 25 July 2012, which requires the worker to have worked the full number of working days in the month "without absence" to earn at least 10 US dollars, and read that wording as making attendance a condition of the bonus; a worker absent with the employer's permission but without pay has not met the condition. [Para 111][Para 124][Para 135][Para 147] It distinguished the older Prakas No. 041 of 7 March 2011, which did not contain the words "without absence", held that the 2012 text deliberately tightened the condition, and declined to read it down. [Para 125][Para 129][Para 131][Para 132] It also held the company's two-stage 5 US dollar deduction to be a reasonable practice because it caps the loss at 5 US dollars whatever the number of permitted absences in each half of the month. [Para 154][Para 155]

Having classified every demand as an interests dispute, the panel turned to standing and held that it could decide such a dispute only where the claimant is the most representative union in the enterprise, or several unions together represent more than half the workers: Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 give the most representative union the right to request a collective agreement, and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 provides that an award on an interests dispute replaces the collective agreement for one year, which would cost the workers their right to strike on those interests. [Para 87][Para 90][Para 92][Para 93][Para 94][Para 95] Because the claimant union was unregistered and not the most representative, it lacked the legal capacity to bring an interests dispute, and the panel followed its earlier awards in cases No. 81/04, No. 98/04, No. 169/11, No. 228/13 and No. 172/14 in refusing to consider the demands. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 100][Para 101][Para 102]

Arbitrator An Non dissented on the third issue only, arguing that the majority relied on a single award (No. 181/12) while seven other awards had read the 2012 Prakas as not treating absence with permission as a failure of the condition, and that the panel should have ordered the pro-rata deduction. [Para 174][Para 177][Para 178][Para 179][Para 181]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that is neither the most representative union in the enterprise nor part of a group of unions representing more than half the workers has no standing to bring an interests dispute to the Arbitration Council; because an award on interests replaces the collective agreement for a year and extinguishes the right to strike on those matters, the panel will refuse to consider the demand at all rather than assess whether the benefit claimed is fair. [Para 94][Para 95][Para 101] Separately, the attendance bonus under point 2 of Prakas No. 230 of 25 July 2012 is conditional on attendance on every working day of the month, so absence with the employer's permission but without pay does not entitle the worker to a pro-rata bonus. [Para 135][Para 147][Para 156]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the company's two-stage 5 US dollar deduction is a reasonable and worker-favourable practice were not necessary to a decision refusing to consider the demand at all. [Para 154][Para 155]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award is dated 22 January 2015 on its face and in the recital of the parties' agreement to postpone from 16 January 2015, but the signature block is dated 21 January 2015 at Phnom Penh [Para 1][Para 31][Para 182]; both dates are reported as printed.
- The company name appears as "អេវើរ ហ្គ្រីន អី ភែរីល", "អេវើរ ហ្គ្រីន អី ភែរឹល" and shortened to "អេវើរ ហ្គ្រីន" [Para 1][Para 32][Para 48]; one reference to the earlier award in case No. 81/04 prints it as "អេវឿហ្គីន" [Para 96].
- The date on which the panel was constituted reads "25 December 2022, 2021, 2014" [Para 24] and stray years intrude at [Para 1] and [Para 166]; the operative period is 2014–2015.
- Amounts are printed inconsistently: the overtime meal allowance is 2,000 riel "per day" at [Para 61] and [Para 104] but "per hour" at [Para 103] and [Para 110]; the attendance bonus is 10 US dollars "per month" but 15 US dollars "per day" in the conciliation summary at [Para 6].
- The reasoning repeats whole sentences at [Para 41] and [Para 47], and the operative paragraphs at [Para 169]–[Para 171] are broken by OCR loss; the order was taken from [Para 168].
- The worker-party arbitrator's and chair's signatures at [Para 173] are lost to OCR ("mon", "uping the Sz"), so the composition rests on [Para 1] and the dissent annex.
