# Award No. 042/16 — Victoria Angkor Kom Penhny Ltd (Victoria Angkor Hotel) (Arbitration Council, 31 March 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Chiv Phirom (employer-selected), An Vira (worker-selected), Pen Bun Chha (president) · Doc ID 138994

## 1. Facts
About 72 employees of the Victoria Angkor Hotel in Siem Reap, a hotel and restaurant business employing roughly 217 staff, struck from 29 December 2015 to 6 January 2016 over working conditions, after a secret ballot on 16 December 2015 and notice to the employer and the provincial labour office on 17 December [Para 66][Para 67][Para 89][Para 121]. The Siem Reap Court of First Instance issued a protection order on 5 January 2016 requiring the strike to stop, and the workers ended it, presenting themselves on 7 January [Para 91][Para 96]. The employer conditioned their return on thumbprinting new terms, sent them home, and later announced that anyone not re-registering between 21 and 29 January 2016 would be treated as having abandoned employment; the panel treated the workers as dismissed on 29 January 2016 [Para 93][Para 108][Para 145].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate all striking staff to their original posts and preserve their wages and other benefits from the date of dismissal until return to work? [Para 3]
2. Whether the employer must preserve the wages and service charge of the strikers for the period 29 December 2015 to 6 January 2016? [Para 5]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer must take back all 62 unconditionally, because they had agreed to resume normal work under the court's protection order and the employer instead imposed undisclosed new conditions before allowing them to work [Para 77][Para 78][Para 79]; the strike was lawful, having completed the Arbitration Council procedure, been approved by secret ballot and been notified in advance [Para 118]; the employer recruited replacements during the strike and paid them a service charge, which obliges it to pay the strikers as well [Para 117][Para 119].
**Employer (respondent):** it never dismissed anyone; the workers left their posts of their own accord, and under the internal rules and Article 83 of the Labour Law eight days' absence is abandonment of employment [Para 81][Para 82]; it cannot reinstate the 62 or preserve their wages, but will pay severance according to the Labour Law [Para 80]; wages and service charge cannot be paid because no court had yet ruled the strike lawful [Para 123]; no replacements were recruited [Para 124].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement and strike-period pay were refused, but the employer was ordered to pay the 6 fixed-term workers their Article 73(3) damages, Article 73(6) severance, accrued annual leave and final wages, and the 54 indefinite-term workers their notice compensation under Articles 75 and 77, Article 89 dismissal damages, accrued annual leave, Article 91 moral damages and final wages; the award fixes formulas, not sums [Para 265][Para 266][Para 270][Para 223][Para 275].

The panel first resolved who was before it, excluding workers who had not transferred representation and a claimant not named in the transfer letter, leaving 60 [Para 73][Para 76][Para 129]. On the character of the cessation it rejected the employer's abandonment theory: neither the internal rules nor Article 83 establishes a leave-taking mechanism, they only list conduct that may lead to termination [Para 141][Para 142]. The workers had not received the 21–29 January window for re-registration, so they could not be treated as having walked away; the termination was by the employer on 29 January 2016 [Para 143][Para 144][Para 145].

On the contract type, the panel applied Article 67 read with Article 73: fixed-duration contracts cannot exceed two years and longer ones become indefinite [Para 146][Para 148][Para 160]. On the evidence, 6 of the 60 held contracts of two years or less and 54 held longer ones, so the majority were indefinite-term [Para 162][Para 163].

For the 6 fixed-term workers the panel held the employer could let the term expire, but owed damages of at least the wages due to the contract's end under Article 73(3), five per cent severance under Article 73(6), outstanding annual leave under Articles 166–167, and final wages under Article 116 [Para 170][Para 175][Para 180][Para 182]. For the 54 indefinite-term workers, the employer had given no proper reason under Article 74 and did not show any notice, so notice compensation under Articles 75 and 77, Article 89 dismissal damages, unused leave, Article 91 moral damages and final wages followed [Para 190][Para 191][Para 203][Para 209][Para 220].

On issue 2 the panel held that a strike suspends the contract under Article 332 and Article 72, so no wage is owed for strike days [Para 233][Para 236][Para 237]. Service charge under Article 134 goes only to staff who actually served customers; the strikers served none, and their claim that replacements were hired was unsupported because the promised list was never filed by the 14 March 2016 deadline [Para 260][Para 262][Para 263][Para 248][Para 250].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where workers obey a court order ending a strike and present themselves for work, but the employer makes their resumption conditional on accepting new terms notified to nobody and later declares them to have abandoned their posts, the cessation is a dismissal effected by the employer, and it must satisfy Article 74 and carry the statutory consequences of notice, dismissal damages and severance rather than the penalties for abandonment [Para 144][Para 145][Para 191].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that had the employer in fact recruited replacements during the strike, Article 334 would have obliged it to pay the strikers' wages for the strike period — a rule it applied in the abstract only, since the claim was unproven [Para 238][Para 239]. It also noted that the labour inspectorate, not the employer, decides whether protected workers may be dismissed [Para 167][Para 168].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The claimant count is printed three ways: 62 at the hearing, 61 on the membership list, and 60 after exclusions, with the reconciling sentence corrupted to "ត្រូវ JO នាក់" so the number dropped cannot be read [Para 71][Para 87][Para 128][Para 129]. The employer's counsel is recorded as requiring attendance on "7 January 2015" where the sequence plainly requires 2016 [Para 100], and one notice is dated "29 December 2015 to 06 December January 2016", carrying two months [Para 31]. A long paragraph on Article 73(6) repeats itself almost verbatim, and several arbitral award numbers cited as authority are unrecognisable [Para 173][Para 186]. The annexes listing the 60 claimants are badly damaged by OCR: duplicated rows, merged columns and dates truncated mid-string, and one annex is headed with a different transliteration of the company [Para 87][Para 279][Para 280]. The award date was extended by agreement from 15 to 31 March 2016 [Para 21].
