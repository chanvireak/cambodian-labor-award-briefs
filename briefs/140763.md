# Award No. 13/05 — Tack Fat Co., Ltd (Employer party) v C.CAWDU of Tack Fat Factory (Employee party) (Arbitration Council, 20 April 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Arbitrators: Ms Seng Vuoch Hun (employer party), Mr Tuon Siphann (worker party), Mr Kong Phallak (chair) · Doc ID 140763

## 1. Facts
Tack Fat Garment Co., Ltd., at #1159 National road No. 2, Sangkat Chak Angrealeu, Khan Meanchey, Phnom Penh, employs approximately 6,000 workers [Para 13]. On 28 January 2005 the Khan Meanchey labour inspectorate received a complaint covering 14 points, and eight were conciliated by 16 February 2005 [Para 13][Para 14]. Six non-conciliated issues were referred to the Arbitration Council on 15 March 2005 [Para 15]. At the first hearing the parties removed issue 5 as already conciliated, and issues 2 and 4 were conciliated, leaving issues 1, 3 and 6 [Para 15][Para 17][Para 21]. A second hearing on 6 April 2005 found no agreement on those three [Para 19][Para 20]. Five unions operate in the factory and none, including C.CAWDU of Tack Fat Factory, has most representative status [Para 21]. From 1996 to early 2001 the company deducted US$2 per month from each worker's wages and provided lunch in exchange; the arrangement later ended [Para 22][Para 23]. Carpenters served one month of probation and 39 mechanics and 30 electricians one and a half months, all at US$40 per month [Para 27][Para 28]. The medical check fees claimed total 10,100 riel [Para 30].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay 1,000 riel for each worker's lunch, or restore the past lunch policy [Para 2][Para 22]?
2. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay a technical bonus of US$15 per month to workers in the electrical, mechanical and carpentry sections [Para 4][Para 28]?
3. (Issue 6) Whether the company must reimburse 10,100 riel in medical check fees to each worker who paid, and increase the workers' basic wage [Para 7][Para 48][Para 51]?

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), C.CAWDU of Tack Fat Factory:** the lunch allowance of 1,000 riel should be paid as in the past, and the company stopped preparing meals because workers complained and wanted better, more sanitary food [Para 2][Para 25]. Electricians, carpenters and mechanics proved their skills through probation yet never received a technical bonus, while translators receive US$20 to US$30 [Para 28][Para 29]. Workers say some paid for their own medical checks, some borrowed from the company, and some were sent for a second check because the company lost their documents [Para 30].
**Employer (respondent), Tack Fat Co., Ltd:** it stopped providing lunch because there was an agreement with the workers, and a 2004 renegotiation was delayed six months at the General Manager's request without result [Para 24][Para 26]. It denies paying translators a technical bonus and says it gave only a US$5 monthly allowance to some workers [Para 30]. It has never paid medical check fees, treating a pre-employment check as each worker's own responsibility [Para 30]. It has no wage-increase policy and did not raise wages because since 2004 the economic situation worsened with fewer buyers [Para 34][Para 35].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel rejected the demand for lunch or 1,000 riel, rejected the US$15 technical bonus, ordered the employer to pay 10,100 riel to every worker who paid for their own medical check by the end of May 2005, and rejected the demand to raise basic wages [Para 52][Para 53].
On Issue 1, the panel found the lunch arrangement rested on an agreement between the parties which both agreed had terminated, so its benefits and obligations were no longer effective [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39]. No law requires the employer to pay 1,000 riel for lunch, and the demand seeks benefits beyond statutory entitlement, making it an interests dispute [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41]. Article 43 of Prakas 99/04 makes an award on an interests dispute stand in place of a collective bargaining agreement for one year and bind all workers, so the panel examined the union's representative status [Para 42]. Article 277 of the Labour Law 1997 and Article 6 of Prakas 305 dated 22 November 2001 require membership of more than half of all workers plus registration and other conditions [Para 43]. The panel found C.CAWDU of Tack Fat did not qualify and, following prior awards, had no right to an award on an interests dispute; the parties should keep negotiating [Para 43][Para 44][Para 46].
On Issue 3, the panel found no evidence or legal basis requiring technical bonuses and treated the demand as an interests dispute, so it declined to consider it [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48].
On Issue 6, the panel applied Article 247(a) and 247(c) of the Labour Law and Prakas 9/94 of 19 January 1994, whose Article 7 places the cost of the employee's medical examination on the owner of the enterprise [Para 48][Para 49]. It noted that recent cases have questioned whether Prakas 9/94 remains relevant, but that in those cases the panel still ordered reimbursement of 10,100 riel [Para 51]. It therefore ordered payment of 10,100 riel to each worker who paid for their own check [Para 51][Para 53]. The wage-rise demand lacked evidence or legal basis and was likewise an interests dispute the panel declined to consider [Para 51][Para 52].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer must reimburse workers the cost of their medical examinations under Article 247 of the Labour Law and Article 7 of Prakas 9/94, and the doubt cast on that Prakas in later awards does not displace the employer's liability [Para 49][Para 51]. A demand for a benefit the law does not require is an interests dispute, and a union lacking most representative status under Article 277 cannot obtain an award that would operate as a collective bargaining agreement for all workers [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel suggested that continued negotiation between the parties was one of many options capable of benefiting both sides [Para 44][Para 45].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR is heavily corrupted, rendering "the employee" as "thempoyee", "the employer" as "thempoyer", "at the" as "athe", "to" as "o" and "and" as "and" fused to following words; several sentences repeat themselves mid-line. The factory address reads "Sangkat Chakangrekleu" in the header [Para 2] and "Chak Angrealeu" in the case summary [Para 13]. The union's registration letter and certificate are both dated 8 August 2005, months after the 20 April 2005 award [Para 13]. Prakas 9/94 is printed "Prakas 9194" in the reasoning [Para 49] and the Labour Law is cited as "Article 247-a" and "247-c" where (A) and (C) are meant [Para 48]. The issue list merges the reinstatement demand into the name of a worker, losing the sentence break [Para 4][Para 5], and paragraph 48 prints "Article 5 of this Prakas [provides]" twice [Para 48].
