# Award No. 004/22 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន សាន់ នីខន (ខេមបូឌា) អឹភែរឹល ឯ.ក (Arbitration Council, 24 March 2022)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · In Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (president) · Doc ID 140857

## 1. Facts
The respondent, ក្រុមហ៊ុន សាន់ នីខន (ខេមបូឌា) អឹភែរឹល ឯ.ក, is a garment factory employing 800 workers [Para 55]. It carries on the business of ក្រុមហ៊ុន ផ្លេក ថា ម (ខេមបូឌា) អឹភែរឹល លីមីធីត, which had operated since 2011 and whose name was changed to the respondent's from 1 January 2022, the workers staying at their machines throughout [Para 58][Para 63][Para 64]. On 14 January 2022 the worker party filed a five-point demand [Para 14]; conciliation on 15 February 2022 settled two points and the three remaining points reached the Council on 23 February 2022 [Para 15][Para 16]. All three concern what survives the change of employer: seniority, the labour book, and the type of contract [Para 24].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the new employer must preserve the workers' seniority from their first day of work with ក្រុមហ៊ុន ផ្លេក ថា ម? [Para 57][Para 152]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must record seniority from that first day when issuing the new-format labour book through the automated system? [Para 103][Para 172]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must continue to apply the contracts of unlimited duration the workers held before? [Para 119][Para 190]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The ក្រុមហ៊ុន សាន់ នីខន (ខេមបូឌា) អឹភែរឹល ឯ.ក worker party argued the old employer paid only the seniority back-pay due under Article 89-new and never settled the other termination entitlements, so the transfer did not break continuity [Para 65]. It added that the new employer still pays the seniority bonus — USD 9 a month in one example — and that a three-month fixed-duration contract would strip the benefits attached to the old contracts [Para 67][Para 129].

**Employer (respondent):** Seniority could not be preserved because the old employer had already paid wages and benefits in full, and it pointed to a payment list of 31 December 2021 covering seniority, annual leave, final wages and travel costs [Para 4][Para 70]. Recording seniority in the labour book is the Ministry's task, not the employer's, and old books without an inspector's stamp could not be carried into the automated system [Para 180][Para 181]. Converting to fixed-duration contracts was its management right, with conversion to unlimited duration after two years and three months [Para 134][Para 135].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the new employer was ordered to preserve seniority from the first day with the old company, to record that date in the new-format labour books, and to continue applying the workers' contracts of unlimited duration [Para 198][Para 199][Para 200]

On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 87-new of the Labour Law, under which a change in the employer's legal status — succession, sale, merger or incorporation — leaves contracts in force between the new employer and the workers of the old enterprise [Para 152][Para 153], following awards Nos 045/18 and 023/19 [Para 154]. Because the party asserting a fact bears the burden of proof [Para 157], the panel examined the meeting minute of 3 November 2021 and found no clause saying the payments were made to end the contracts or cut off seniority [Para 168]. The employer's evidence therefore did not support its claim that the entitlements had been paid to terminate [Para 169], and Article 87-new obliged it to preserve seniority [Para 170].

On Issue 2 the panel read Articles 32, 34 and 37 of the Labour Law and clause 4 of Prakas No. 197 of 20 August 2014 together: every worker must hold a labour book in which the employer records the date of engagement, wages, increases and successive contracts, and must take it to the labour inspector for endorsement [Para 172][Para 173][Para 175][Para 178][Para 179]. That recording duty is one of the new employer's obligations when it continues the old contracts [Para 183], so the seniority date had to be entered in the new-format book [Para 184].

On Issue 3 the panel held that under Article 13 of the Labour Law the statute's provisions are rules of public order and any arrangement contrary to them is void [Para 191][Para 192]. Since the new employer owed continuation of the old contracts, the 3 November 2021 agreement to convert unlimited-duration contracts into fixed-duration ones could not be applied [Para 195][Para 196], and the employer was ordered to keep applying them [Para 197].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a business changes hands or name but the workforce and the work continue, Article 87-new carries the existing contracts — including their unlimited duration — to the new employer, and seniority runs from the first day with the predecessor unless the predecessor actually paid the termination entitlements that would break continuity [Para 153][Para 168][Para 170]. An agreement converting unlimited-duration contracts into fixed-duration ones is void under Article 13 because it defeats those mandatory provisions [Para 192][Para 196].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that a worker's seniority entitlement is computed from the social-security record rather than from the stamp in the labour book [Para 111].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The date on which the panel was constituted is printed as "25 February 2023 2022" [Para 17], one contract term appears as "44" months in the reasoning though the parties spoke of three months [Para 187], and the passage listing the labour-book endorsement duty is badly corrupted, repeating itself mid-sentence [Para 179]. Several lists give individual workers' full names and first-employment dates; those individuals are referred to here only by role [Para 56][Para 94][Para 119]. A stray company-name fragment and a page number interrupt the signature block [Para 202].
