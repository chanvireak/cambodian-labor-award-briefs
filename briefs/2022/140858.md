# Award No. 002/22 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ត្រី ប៉ូស អ៉ិនធើណេសិន ណល ឯ.ក (Tripos International Co., Ltd.) (Arbitration Council, 1 March 2022)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of three — សេង វួច ហ៊ុន (employer-appointed), ទួន ស៊ី ផាន់ (worker-appointed), ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា (president) · Doc ID 140858

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ត្រី ប៉ូស អ៉ិនធើណេសិន ណល ឯ.ក manufactures footwear in Kampong Chhnang province with 3,371 workers. [Para 62] On 3 January 2022 the workers filed eight demands; conciliation on 19 January 2022 settled four, and one of the rest — overtime for the third-dose vaccination day of 18 December 2021 — went to the labour inspector as outside the panel's competence. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 15] The three remaining points reached the Council on 25 January 2022 under report No. 064. [Para 16] At the hearing of 9 February 2022 the parties settled issues 1 and 2, so only issue 3 was decided. [Para 22][Para 23] Fifteen workers' thumbprints supported the claim. [Para 105]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must stop holding meetings of workers before the 7:00 am start of working hours? [Para 108]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer calls meetings before working hours for every team, some daily and others nearly daily for five to ten minutes, although the internal rules of ក្រុមហ៊ុន ត្រី ប៉ូស អ៉ិនធើណេសិន ណល ឯ.ក fix the start of work at 7:00 am, and workers who arrive late for a meeting are scolded in language they cannot accept. [Para 66][Para 72][Para 75]
**Employer (respondent):** only 4 or 5 of the company's 161 teams meet before hours, mainly the sample team, which needs technical briefing; there is no policy of pre-work meetings, they occur only when necessary and never lead to discipline, and the practice dates from the company's opening. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 82][Para 85]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to stop holding meetings before working hours; the order is non-binding and becomes final 8 days after notification. [Para 139][Para 140][Para 141]

The panel first settled who could bring the claim: no union at the company held most-representative status, so under Article 16 of Prakas No. 303 and Article 1 of Prakas No. 362 the recognised worker delegates represented the 15 claimants. [Para 93][Para 95][Para 101]

On the merits, Article 2(2) of the Labour Law gives the employer the right to organise and manage the enterprise, but only lawfully and reasonably. [Para 110][Para 111] Article 137 caps normal working time at eight hours a day and 48 hours a week, so time worked beyond the fixed schedule is overtime; Article 139-new and Article 4 of Prakas No. 80 of 1 March 1999 make overtime voluntary and forbid discipline of a worker who declines it. [Para 114][Para 118][Para 124] Meetings held from 6:50 or 6:55 until 7:00 were therefore overtime, and the panel found no overtime payment had been made and no consent given. [Para 135][Para 137] Following Award No. 124/09 and Award No. 150/14, it held the practice contrary to Articles 137 and 139-new and to Prakas No. 80. [Para 130][Para 126][Para 138]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Attendance at a meeting the employer convenes before the contractual start of work is overtime, whatever the meeting is called: the management right to organise meetings displaces neither the eight-hour limit nor the voluntariness requirement, so an employer that neither pays for the time nor obtains consent must stop convening workers early. [Para 111][Para 118][Para 135][Para 138]

**Obiter dicta:** only 4 or 5 of 161 teams actually met before hours, and meetings held inside working hours remain open to the employer. [Para 79][Para 74]
