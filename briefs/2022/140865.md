# Award No. 014/22 — Gress Fol (Cambodia) Tech Style Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 23 May 2022)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Eing Sothy for the employer, An Non for the workers, Kong Phalla presiding) · Doc ID 140865

## 1. Facts
Gress Fol (Cambodia) Tech Style (ហ្គ្រេស ហ្វូល (ខេមបូឌា) ថេក ស្ថាយ) is a garment factory in Treang district, Takéo province, employing 1,033 workers. [Para 47] Twenty workers petitioned the Labour Disputes Department on 7 March 2022 with eight demands on working conditions, mostly about piece rates (តម្លៃឡូត៍) and wage top-ups. [Para 15] Conciliation on 25 March 2022 settled four of them, leaving Issues 1, 2, 6 and 7 for the panel. [Para 23][Para 24]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must raise piece rates by 3.84 per cent, the 2022 minimum-wage increase rate? [Para 48]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must top up piece-rate workers' pay to the minimum wage whenever output falls short of it? [Para 75]
3. (Issue 6) Whether the attendance bonus must be deducted pro rata for days of lawful leave taken with the employer's permission? [Para 85]
4. (Issue 7) Whether the employer must pay a 4,000 riel daily lunch allowance? [Para 90]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), through the union (សហជីពប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ នៃ ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្គ្រេស ហ្វូល (ខេមបូឌា) ថេកស្តាយ):** piece rates have not risen since 2015 even as the minimum wage rose each year, and some rates were cut, so sewers cannot earn the minimum; the attendance bonus is docked when lawful leave is taken. [Para 61][Para 87]
**Employer (respondent), Gress Fol (Cambodia) Tech Style (ហ្គ្រេស ហ្វូល (ខេមបូឌា) ថេក ស្ថាយ):** every piece rate was negotiated with section heads, workers accepted the rates by working to them, and COVID-19 has removed the orders that once let them reach the minimum; deducting for absenteeism keeps attendance regular. [Para 68][Para 71][Para 74]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to top up piece-rate workers to the minimum wage, while the 3.84 per cent increase, the bonus deduction claim and the 4,000 riel lunch allowance were all refused. [Para 194][Para 195][Para 196]
On Issue 1, the panel read Article 2 and Article 137 of the Labour Law with Article 7(1) of the Minimum Wage Law and Prakas No. 264/21: the employer may set and vary piece rates, but must set them so an average-skilled worker working normally earns the minimum wage. Because the workers showed no figure for how many fell short, the panel could not find the rates too low as such, and Prakas No. 264/21 imposes a duty to top up, not a duty to raise rates by any percentage. [Para 95][Para 99][Para 112]
On Issue 2, Article 7(2) of the Minimum Wage Law and Article 2 of Prakas No. 264/21 require the employer to add the shortfall up to the minimum, 192 USD for probationary and 194 USD for full-rights workers per month. The employer admitted it had not done so for two years, so the claim succeeded. [Para 115][Para 120][Para 123]
On Issue 6, the panel followed the Full Bench decision of 3 October 2017 on Notification No. 230: attendance bonus is earned by presence on all working days, counting days off that are a legal right, a contractual right, or a legal duty, and an employer may not use the bonus to block exercise of a statutory right. The claim to a pro-rata deduction was therefore refused. [Para 125][Para 129][Para 143]
On Issue 7, no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice required a lunch allowance, so the demand was a demand for better than the law — an interest dispute. Under Article 54 (new) of the Union Law, Article 72 of that law and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099, only a most-representative union may bring an interest dispute, and this claimant was not one. [Para 173][Para 187][Para 190]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A minimum-wage Prakas obliges an employer to top up piece-rate earnings to the statutory floor; it does not oblige the employer to raise piece rates by the percentage by which the minimum wage rose, and an arbitration panel will not fix rates for want of evidence of how many workers fall below the floor. [Para 112][Para 113]

**Obiter dicta:** a most-representative union's award on an interest dispute takes the place of a collective agreement for one year under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099, which is why a union lacking that status cannot obtain such an award. [Para 185][Para 186]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel's constitution is printed as "1 April 2021 2022" [Para 18]. The arbitrators are named differently in the header and the signature block: the workers' arbitrator appears as An Non at the front and as a variant spelling at the back, and the chair's name differs by one final letter [Para 198]. Prakas No. 264/21 is dated 28 September 2022 in one citation and 28 September 2021 in another [Para 102][Para 118]. The lunch claim is printed as 4,000 riel a day in the demand but as a typographically corrupted form of the same figure in the reasoning [Para 90][Para 172]. Worker names in the pay samples survive only as fragments, so claimants are described by role [Para 77][Para 79].
