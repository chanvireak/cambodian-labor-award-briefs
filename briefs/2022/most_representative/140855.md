# Award No. 001/22 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេ ផ្លាស់ អេ ស៊ូ (ខេមបូឌា) (Arbitration Council, 9 February 2022)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · In Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (president) · Doc ID 140855

## 1. Facts
The employer, ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេ ផ្លាស់ អេ ស៊ូ (ខេមបូឌា), manufactures footwear and shoe components and employed 1,222 workers at the date of the award [Para 41]. On 7 December 2021 the workers' representatives filed a seven-point demand to improve working conditions [Para 17]; conciliation on 30 December 2021 settled three points, and the four unsettled points reached the Arbitration Council on 4 January 2022 through report No. 865 [Para 19]. At the hearing the parties agreed on Issue 1, leaving Issues 2, 3 and 4 for decision: the number and opening times of the factory gates at the morning entry, at the midday meal break, and a USD 5 monthly incentive payment [Para 25][Para 26]. The claims were brought by the collective worker party of a union with 81 members that was not the most representative union in the enterprise [Para 75].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer is obliged to open both factory entrance gates for the morning entry from 6:00 am as it previously did? [Para 44][Para 80]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the employer is obliged to open both gates for workers leaving at the midday meal break? [Para 61][Para 98]
3. (Issue 4) Whether all sewing-section and cutting-section workers with two or more years' seniority are entitled to a USD 5 monthly incentive payment? [Para 69][Para 107]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេ ផ្លាស់ អេ ស៊ូ (ខេមបូឌា) workers arrive from 6:00 am and must stand waiting outside until 6:30, causing congestion on the road and insults from passers-by, and the same congestion occurs when they leave for the midday meal [Para 48][Para 64]. Workers on the flow line already receive USD 5 a month after two years, so the sewing and cutting sections should receive the same [Para 70].

**Employer (respondent):** The small gate is kept for emergencies only, and one gate opens at 6:30 because the security shift changes and guards must spray alcohol and check temperatures [Para 50][Para 51]. There is no congestion: workers arrive around 6:40 and are all inside by 6:58, as its video shows [Para 55][Para 91]. No policy ever granted a USD 5 incentive and the company raised base wages by USD 2 under the Ministry's Prakas instead [Para 76][Para 77].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three claims of the worker party were rejected, with no money ordered [Para 127][Para 126]

On Issue 2 the panel held that Article 2 of the Labour Law of 1997 leaves the employer the right to organise and manage the enterprise so long as that right is exercised lawfully and reasonably [Para 80][Para 81], following awards Nos 62/06, 108/06, 33/07, 119/09, 156/13 and 201/14 [Para 82]. Because the party asserting a fact bears the burden of proof [Para 85], and because the worker party produced no evidence of congestion while the employer's video showed none before 6:30 am [Para 88][Para 91], the panel found clause 9 of the internal rules — requiring workers to use the gate the company designates — confirmed the employer's right to fix a single gate and its opening hour [Para 93][Para 94]. That arrangement was lawful and reasonable and did not affect workers' rights, so no obligation to open both gates at 6:00 am arose [Para 95][Para 96].

Issue 3 was decided on the same ground: applying the management right and the burden of proof, the panel found no evidence of congestion or prejudice at the midday exit, and held the employer owed no duty to open both gates for the meal break [Para 98][Para 101][Para 102][Para 103].

On Issue 4 the panel found no provision of the labour law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice obliging the employer to pay USD 5 a month, and characterised the demand as one exceeding the law, hence an interest dispute [Para 107][Para 108]. Under Article 96(2) of the Labour Law a collective agreement is a written agreement between an employer and a union representing the workers [Para 109][Para 111]; under new Article 54 of the Union Law only a most representative union holds the exclusive right to bargain or to resolve collective labour disputes [Para 112][Para 114], and Article 72 of the Union Law gives bargaining-council status where no union is most representative [Para 117]. Clause 1 of Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 makes an award on an interest dispute a substitute collective agreement binding all workers for one year [Para 119][Para 120], so only a most representative union or one with the statutory standing may bring such a claim [Para 121]. The claimant union was not of that character [Para 124], and the panel therefore declined to consider the claim [Para 125].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer's Article 2 right to manage the enterprise authorises it to designate which gates workers use and when those gates open, and such an arrangement stands unless the claimant proves by evidence that it is unlawful, unreasonable or actually prejudices workers' rights [Para 95][Para 88]; a demand for a benefit above what the labour law, a collective agreement, internal rules or established practice provides is an interest dispute that the panel will not consider at the instance of a union that is neither most representative nor otherwise qualified as a bargaining agent [Para 108][Para 121][Para 124].

**Obiter dicta:** none identified [Para 26]
