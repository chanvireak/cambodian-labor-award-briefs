# Award No. 038/21 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ថៃ ប៊ុន រ៉ុង អ៉ិន ដាស ស្ទ្រី (ស៊ី ខេ) ខម ភេនី លីមីធីត (Thai Bunrong Industry (C.K.) Co., Ltd.) (Arbitration Council, 19 January 2022)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of three — អ៊ុក រី (employer-appointed), ទួន ស៊ី ផាន់ (worker-appointed), ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា (president) · Doc ID 140854

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ថៃ ប៊ុន រ៉ុង អ៉ិន ដាស ស្ទ្រី (ស៊ី ខេ) ខម ភេនី លីមីធីត is a construction company in Kampot province employing 107 workers. [Para 62] On 11 October 2021 the workers filed four demands, one an individual dispute and three collective; conciliation on 10 November 2021 settled none of them, and report No. 208/21 of 23 November 2021 reached the Council on 30 November 2021. [Para 11][Para 12][Para 13] The complaint carried 81 thumbprints, but after checking the employer's objection to 20 names the panel found only 64 workers were genuine claimants. [Para 137][Para 143] The three remaining demands were heard on 20 December 2021. [Para 10]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must grant paid public holidays, paid sick leave, paid annual leave and paid special leave under Article 169 of the Labour Law to all the workers? [Para 145]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must add 27 USD to each worker's monthly wage, raising it to 197 USD? [Para 168]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must switch from calculating wages by the day to calculating them by the month? [Para 191]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** casual workers enjoy the same statutory rights as permanent ones, so the company cannot plead seasonal hiring; the 170 USD paid for 26 days does not cover food and travel, and since 2019 rises have gone to some workers and not to most union members; day-rate calculation itself destroys paid leave. [Para 146][Para 99][Para 107][Para 118]
**Employer (respondent):** the claimants were engaged casually and paid only for days actually worked, so Article 169 does not apply to them; wages are 26,200 riel and 27,500 riel a day, paid as a monthly total, and the higher rate rewards good performance, with 17 union members among those raised, so there is no discrimination. [Para 85][Para 145][Para 109][Para 114]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to grant paid holidays, paid sick leave, paid annual leave and paid special leave under Article 169; the 197 USD wage demand and the daily-to-monthly conversion were both rejected; the order is non-binding and becomes final 8 days after notification. [Para 203][Para 204][Para 205][Para 206]

On issue 1 the panel read Articles 9 and 10 of the Labour Law, under which casual and permanent workers carry the same rights and duties, and refused to classify the claimants either way. [Para 150][Para 155][Para 149] Following the jurisprudence of Award No. 97/08 it held that labels do not defeat statutory entitlement, and ordered payment of holidays, sick leave, annual leave and special leave under Article 169. [Para 162][Para 163][Para 167]

On issue 2 the panel found no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice obliging an increase of 27 USD on 170 USD, which made the claim an interest dispute rather than a rights dispute. [Para 173][Para 174] Under Article 96(2) of the Labour Law, Article 54-new of the Trade Union Law, Article 72 of the Trade Union Law and Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099, an award on an interest dispute substitutes for a collective agreement and binds every worker, so only a most-representative union may bring one. [Para 182][Para 183][Para 188] The claimant union being no more than one of several base unions, the panel declined to consider the demand. [Para 189][Para 190]

On issue 3 the panel held that Article 11 of the Labour Law lets the employer pay monthly, daily or hourly, at intervals no longer than 15 days or one month, and that Article 2 confers management authority exercised lawfully and reasonably. [Para 193][Para 197] Daily calculation did not by itself deprive the workers of leave, and the panel left the choice with the employer. [Para 200][Para 202]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that is not the most representative in the enterprise cannot bring an interest dispute: the panel will decline to consider a wage-increase demand that rests on no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or established practice. [Para 185][Para 182]

Seasonal or day-rate engagement is no answer to a claim for paid leave, because casual and permanent workers hold identical statutory entitlements and the panel need not classify them to enforce Article 169. [Para 163][Para 165][Para 167]

Choosing between monthly, daily and hourly wage calculation is a management prerogative that stands unless it is exercised unlawfully or unreasonably and impairs a statutory entitlement. [Para 197][Para 200]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel expressly declined to decide whether the 64 claimants were permanent or casual workers. [Para 149]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The wage is printed as 170 USD in the reasoning but appears once as ១៧០០០ ដុល្លារ and once as ១ ១៩៧, and the daily rate appears as both 26,200 and 27,500 riel. [Para 168][Para 169][Para 171][Para 109] The postponement clause dates the order "២២ ខែធ្នូ ឆ្នាំ ២០២៣ ២០២១", giving two years. [Para 23] The company name recurs in several OCR variants (អ៉ិន ដាស្ទ្រី, អ៊ិន ដាស ស្ទ្រីល, ខេមភេនី), and the registration number appears as both MOC-66325286 and 00019385. [Para 1][Para 61][Para 31][Para 32] The president's signature line is truncated to ប៉ែន ប៊ុន. [Para 208] Names of the three worker representatives, the employer's lawyers and the payroll officer are reduced to initials here, as is the individual worker named in the withdrawn personal dispute.
