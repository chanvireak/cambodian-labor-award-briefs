# Award No. 044/21 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេហ្វ.ភី.ហ្សិត.ហ្វើនី ឈ័រ (ខេមបូឌា) ឯ.ក (Arbitration Council, 8 February 2022)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Yu Soun Ty (employer-appointed), Sin Kim Sean (worker-appointed), Kong Phalla (president) · Doc ID 140856

## 1. Facts
The employer, ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេហ្វ.ភី.ហ្សិត.ហ្វើនី ឈ័រ (ខេមបូឌា) ឯ.ក, manufactures furniture components and employed 800 workers [Para 56]. On 1 December 2021 the worker party filed a 22-point demand [Para 17]; conciliation on 22 December 2021 settled 13 points, leaving 9 points that reached the Council on 29 December 2021 through report No. 843 [Para 19][Para 22]. At the hearing the parties agreed Issues 1, 3, 4 and 9, the worker party withdrew Issue 5, and Issues 2, 6, 7 and 8 remained for decision [Para 29][Para 30][Para 31]. The employer did not attend the conciliation stage and gave no reason for its absence [Para 11].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer is obliged to stop calling workers to meetings before working hours and hold them within working time instead? [Para 59][Para 100]
2. (Issue 6) Whether the employer must reimburse the wages and other benefits of union leaders who attended conciliation at the Ministry, and stop deducting wages and bonuses for future attendance? [Para 74][Para 130]
3. (Issue 7) Whether the employer is obliged to arrange food sellers inside the company compound as before? [Para 81][Para 160]
4. (Issue 8) Whether the employer must stop holding daily group meetings and stop requiring workers to stand straight with both hands behind their backs? [Para 87][Para 169]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេហ្វ.ភី.ហ្សិត.ហ្វើនី ឈ័រ (ខេមបូឌា) ឯ.ក worker party said the employer calls a 15-minute standing meeting every morning before work and another at 12:10 or 12:15, leaving no time to eat, and that lateness is punished by forced exercise or a warning [Para 60][Para 62][Para 63]. Union leaders attended conciliation on proper invitation letters as part of their normal duties, food sellers are 250 metres away, and standing with hands behind the back treats workers like prisoners [Para 75][Para 86][Para 90].

**Employer (respondent):** The meetings assign work and maintain safety, and without them workers scan in late; the company offered to cut the meeting from 15 minutes to 5 or 10 [Para 66][Para 67][Para 70]. It did not know which workers were union leaders, food sellers inside would create rubbish and fail customer audits, and the daily meeting summarises results and allocates the next day's work [Para 76][Para 83][Para 92].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to stop holding meetings before working hours and to reimburse the wages and benefits of two union leaders who attended conciliation, while the claims on future deductions, food sellers and standing posture were refused [Para 188][Para 189][Para 190][Para 191][Para 193]

On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 2 of the Labour Law of 1997, under which the employer may manage the enterprise only lawfully and reasonably [Para 100][Para 101], following awards Nos 62/06, 33/07, 119/09, 33/13, 188/14, 336/15 and 091/16 [Para 102]. Article 137 caps normal hours at 8 a day and 48 a week [Para 103], Article 139-new requires 50% and 100% overtime rates [Para 110], and clause 4 of Prakas No. 80 of 1 March 1999 makes overtime voluntary and bars discipline for refusing it [Para 114]. On award No. 124/09 the panel held a meeting held outside working hours is overtime that must be paid [Para 120], and the internal rules fix the working day at 7:00–11:00 and 12:30–16:30 [Para 123]. Because the 15-minute pre-shift meetings pushed workers past 8 hours, were not urgent or exceptional work, went unpaid and were not voluntary, they were unlawful and had to stop [Para 124][Para 125][Para 126][Para 128].

On Issue 6 the panel applied Article 306 of the Labour Law, which requires parties to attend every conciliation meeting [Para 130], Prakas No. 099 Article 20 and Article 14, which protect representatives from detriment for attending [Para 134][Para 136][Para 137], and Article 34 of the same Prakas, which allows an order to pay compensation [Para 147], following award No. 036/20 [Para 148]. It ordered reimbursement of wages and benefits for the two leaders who attended on 15 July and 22 December 2021 [Para 150][Para 151], but refused to order an end to future deductions because no dispute had yet arisen [Para 155][Para 158][Para 159].

On Issue 7 the panel found no law, collective agreement, internal rule or practice requiring food sellers inside the compound, held the 250-metre distance was not a real obstacle, and treated the refusal as a reasonable exercise of the management right [Para 161][Para 165][Para 167][Para 168]. On Issue 8 it held in-hours meetings are part of the work owed to the employer and dismissed the claim for want of any evidence of prejudice, finding the standing posture was part of an exercise routine applied to all workers [Para 173][Para 185][Para 186][Para 187].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A meeting that the employer requires workers to attend outside the hours fixed by Article 137 and the internal rules is overtime, and is lawful only if it addresses genuinely urgent or exceptional work, is paid at the statutory rate and rests on the workers' consent; meetings held within working hours remain within the management right unless the claimant proves they breach the law, the internal rules or workers' rights [Para 124][Para 125][Para 173][Para 178]. A union representative summoned to conciliation may not have wages or benefits deducted for attending, and the panel will order reimbursement of what was withheld [Para 137][Para 150].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that no dispute over future deductions had yet arisen, so the claim for an injunction against future conduct was premature rather than wrong on the merits [Para 155][Para 158].
