# Award No. 32/05 — HS ENT Co., Ltd and the Khmer Youth Federation of Trade Union (Arbitration Council, 17 June 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, Tuon Siphann, chair Dr Sok Mathoeung) · Doc ID 140777

## 1. Facts
HS ENT Co., Ltd, at Thnaut Phdem village, Bekchan commune, Ang Snoul district, Kandal province, washes cloth and employs 740 workers in three buildings. [Para 31] [Para 32] [Para 92] On 9 May 2005 the provincial vocational training office received a call about a strike at the factory; conciliation held there on 12 May 2005 failed on three demands, which reached the panel under non-conciliation report No. 88/MoVLT of 24 May 2005 and were heard on 1 June 2005. [Para 33] [Para 35] [Para 18] [Para 22] On 5 May 2005 the company met the workers' representatives and announced the closure of Building B for lack of work, and on 6 May 2005 dismissed all 131 workers in that building, among them the KYFTU president, vice-president and secretary, paying severance on 18 May 2005 without seeking the Labour Inspector's authorisation. [Para 59] [Para 58] [Para 50]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must reinstate the three local union leaders whose contracts were terminated with the 131 Building B workers? [Para 15] [Para 50]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay an additional US$500 to each of the 27 workers on top of the severance already paid? [Para 15] [Para 59]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must give the laid-off workers priority for reinstatement if work becomes available? [Para 16]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that the company carried out a mass layoff while following the procedure for individual termination, without valid reason and without prior notice, although it was still recruiting, and that a GMAC representative incited the dismissals; that the 131 Building B workers were all KYFTU members and the three leaders were protected; and that the 27 workers were owed US$500 each for seniority, family burden and unemployment under Article 95 of the Labour Law. [Para 39] [Para 40] [Para 43] [Para 62] [Para 89]
**Employer (respondent):** that Building B was closed because there was no work, that it did not object to union activity in the company, and that it calculated and paid notice, dismissal indemnity, damages, last wages and unused annual leave for all 131 workers under Articles 75, 89, 91, 116 and 166 of the Labour Law. [Para 37] [Para 64] [Para 65] It said it would announce publicly if Building B reopened and asked the 131 workers to leave clear addresses. [Para 45]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must reinstate the three KYFTU leaders immediately from the date of the award, pay them wages from 6 May 2005 onward, and accept repayment of the severance they received on 18 May 2005; the claim for an extra US$500 for the 27 workers was rejected; and the company must give all Building B workers dismissed on 6 May 2005 priority for reinstatement to the same positions within two years. [Para 103] [Para 104]
On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 293 of the Labour Law, which allows dismissal of a shop steward or candidate only after authorisation from the Labour Inspector, together with Articles 282 and 295 and Prakas 305/01, which protect union leaders. [Para 70] [Para 73] The company conceded it never asked the Ministry or the Kandal Labour Inspector for permission and instead closed the building by its own decision. [Para 75] Because no authorisation was obtained the three leaders remained employed in law, so reinstatement with back pay from 6 May 2005 was ordered, against repayment of the termination payments they had received. [Para 80]
On Issue 2 the panel held that the closure of part of the operation for lack of work was a valid reason under Article 74, so no damages were payable under Article 91, and it checked the payments against Articles 75, 89, 91, 116 and 166 on its own table: the two workers whose figures were evidenced received US$312 and US$277 against entitlements of about US$317 and US$268, so the company paid at or above the law. [Para 82] [Para 88] It held that the additional seniority in Article 95 merely classifies workers for selection in a mass layoff and creates no entitlement to an extra US$500 for family burden or unemployment. [Para 90] [Para 92]
On Issue 3 the panel found Article 95 permits dismissal for a reduction of work and that the written notice to the workers' representatives required by paragraph 3 was given by the minute of 5 May 2005. [Para 95] [Para 96] [Para 100] As the whole building closed with no worker retained, no selection by skill, seniority or family burden was needed, but paragraph 5 gives every dismissed worker two years' priority for the same position. [Para 99] [Para 102] The panel also found that five workers transferred from Building A to Building B a month before the dismissal should be reinstated to their former work in Building A, but issued no order on that point because the demand was absent from the non-conciliation report and was not a direct consequence of the dispute under Article 312 and Article 33 of Prakas 99/04. [Para 101] [Para 102]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union leader protected by Articles 282, 293 and 295 cannot be dismissed without prior authorisation from the Labour Inspector; absent it the dismissal is ineffective, the leader remains employed and must be reinstated with wages from the dismissal date, repaying the termination payments already received. [Para 73] [Para 80] A closure of an entire building for lack of work is a valid reason under Article 74, defeats a claim for damages under Article 91 and for extra seniority money, and requires no selection among workers, provided the representatives were notified in writing; Article 95 seniority classifies workers for selection rather than paying compensation. [Para 82] [Para 90] [Para 96] Dismissed workers nonetheless hold a two-year priority to be rehired to the same positions under Article 95 paragraph 5. [Para 102] A demand first raised at the hearing and not a direct consequence of the non-conciliation dispute falls outside the panel's jurisdiction. [Para 102]

**Obiter dicta:** that reinstating the five workers moved from Building A a month earlier would show the employer's good intentions and answer doubts about its motives, though no order issued. [Para 101]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The non-conciliation report is dated 24 May 2005 but said to have been submitted to the Secretariat on 25 May 2004, a year that cannot be right and is reported as printed. [Para 18] The demand is described as covering 28 workers in the findings but 27 at the hearing, one worker having withdrawn. [Para 59] [Para 60] The three union leaders' names are spelled inconsistently across the findings and reasons, one variant introducing a different family name, and the two evidenced workers appear under three spellings each; initials are used here. [Para 55] [Para 66] [Para 67] [Para 97] Building B is printed variously as Building "2, B" and Building B, and the Prakas on the Arbitration Council is cited as "99704" for 99/04. [Para 16] [Para 25] The final operative passage of the reasons is cut off mid-sentence by the scan. [Para 102]
