# Award No. 021/17 — King Fresh In Das Striel Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 25 April 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Kong Phalla, chair appointed by both) · Doc ID 138925

## 1. Facts
The company is a garment manufacturer employing about 1,206 workers [Para 43][Para 44]. For three consecutive years up to and including 2016 the employer gave each worker a Khmer New Year gift consisting of one sarong and 20,000 riel [Para 54][Para 101]. In 2017 the employer did not give the gift, and instead allowed one day's rest on 12 April 2017 at half wages with other benefits preserved [Para 55][Para 102]. Two local unions were registered at the factory, with 370 and 147 members respectively, and there was no most-representative union [Para 45][Para 50][Para 51]. Conciliation on 29 March 2017 left two points unresolved [Para 10][Para 11].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must give each worker one sarong and 20,000 riel for Khmer New Year as in previous years? [Para 2][Para 19]
2. Whether the 12 April 2017 rest day should be paid at 100 per cent of wages? [Para 18]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** although no collective agreement or written instrument required a New Year gift, the employer had given it for three consecutive years and had promised orally to continue; the workers therefore did not accept the employer's unilateral change and demanded full wages for 12 April 2017 [Para 57][Para 58][Para 61].
**Employer (respondent):** it is no longer able to provide the gift as before; it never promised to give it every year, the gift having followed an annual discussion, and it replaced it with a rest day on 12 April 2017 at half wages with all other benefits preserved [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the claim that the employer provide one sarong and 20,000 riel per worker for Khmer New Year; no sum was ordered [Para 141][Para 142].

On standing, the panel found no most-representative union at the company, so Article 54(1) of the Trade Union Law gave no union exclusive representation [Para 45][Para 67][Para 70]. Following Awards 180/16 and 171/16, it held that where no most-representative union exists the workers may represent themselves or mandate a representative, but under Article 19 of Prakas 099 a representative must hold written authorisation from the party [Para 71][Para 72][Para 81][Para 93]. The three workers designated by the Kandal provincial department of labour letter No. 003 did not appear at the hearing; six other workers did appear but held no written authorisation from all 1,206 workers [Para 79][Para 80][Para 83][Para 95]. Under Article 11 of the Trade Union Law, the leaders of the two registered unions could represent only their own members — 370 and 147, a total of 517 [Para 87][Para 88][Para 97][Para 98][Para 99]. The case therefore proceeded for those 517 members only [Para 100].

On the merits, the panel treated the three consecutive years of gifting as past practice (ការអនុវត្តកន្លងមក), applying Article 65 of the Labour Law and its earlier awards defining the doctrine as conduct repeated regularly and consistently and accepted by both sides [Para 104][Para 111][Para 113][Para 114][Para 119]. It held that past practice normally obliges the parties to continue it, but may be lawfully ended where the parties agree to a replacement practice or the underlying circumstances change [Para 120][Para 121][Para 122]. It then found that in 2017 the parties had agreed that the workers would rest on 12 April 2017 at 100 per cent wages and full benefits, with the employer deducting half a day of annual leave, and that this benefit was given expressly in place of the gift [Para 131][Para 133][Para 139]. Having already received that replacement, the workers' further demand for the gift was unreasonable, and the panel concluded they had accepted the new practice replacing the old [Para 134][Para 135][Para 140].

Issue 2 was settled at the hearing: the workers received 100 per cent wages and all benefits for 12 April 2017, the employer deducting half a day of annual leave, so only the gift claim remained [Para 18][Para 19][Para 63].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A benefit granted regularly and consistently over successive years, and accepted by both sides, becomes a past practice the employer is bound to continue; but it may be ended lawfully where the parties agree on a replacement practice or the original circumstances change, and once the workers have actually received a benefit given expressly in substitution, a panel will not also award them the discontinued one [Para 121][Para 130][Para 134][Para 140].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's analysis of standing — that without a most-representative union a representative needs written authorisation, and that registered union leaders may represent only their own members — was directed to who was properly before it rather than to the substance of the gift claim [Para 93][Para 95][Para 99]. Its observation that rest-day entitlements and production-schedule arrangements are not the kind of practice that crystallises into an enforceable past practice was also not applied to the outcome [Para 116].

Paragraph 40 garbles the hearing-invitation dates. The employer's account of the 12 April arrangement at paragraph 3 (half wages, then 100 per cent on 13 April) differs from the arrangement recorded at paragraphs 18, 63 and 131 (100 per cent on 12 April with half a day of annual leave deducted); the panel decided on the latter. Paragraph 47 records that the three department-designated representatives claimed to be union leaders yet did not attend the hearing. The award date, 25 April 2017, is consistent in the header and in paragraph 21 [Para 1][Para 21].
