# Award No. 171/13 — Bou Vhear Garmen Fek Thery (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 16 September 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phalla presiding; Ing Sothy appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna appointed by the workers, dissenting in part) · Doc ID 139854

## 1. Facts
Bou Vhear Garmen Fek Thery (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (បូវ ឃើរ ហ្គាមេន ហ្វេក ធើរី) is a garment factory at Pong Toek, Peuk commune, Ang Snuol, Kandal, registered on 8 October 2012 with 716 workers. [Para 51][Para 52] Eleven demands went to conciliation on 14 August 2013 and none was settled; at the hearing of 28 August 2013 the claimants withdrew issues 1, 2 (in part), 3, 8, 9 and 10, leaving issues 2 and 7 (merged), 4, 5, 6 and 11. [Para 15][Para 24][Para 25] Ms C was hired on 18 March 2013 on probation and then a three-month contract to 31 July 2013, and was elected union president at elections on 18 and 22 July 2013. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 73] On 23 July 2013, the day after the second election, the employer told her it would not renew her contract; on 24 July security refused her entry. [Para 64][Para 67][Para 68] Notices of the elected candidates sent on 19, 20 and 23 July 2013 were refused at the gate and by post; the union's registration receipt issued on 15 August 2013. [Para 72][Para 75][Para 76]

## 2. Issues in dispute
2. Whether the employer must take Ms C back into work, she having been dismissed a day after her election as union president? [Para 60][Para 103]
4. Whether the employer must hear the workers' reasons before walking away or deciding when they ask for leave for genuine necessity? [Para 78][Para 150]
5. Whether the workers are entitled to wages and the regular attendance bonus for the days on which they struck? [Para 83][Para 165]
6. Whether the employer must maintain wages and the regular attendance bonus while a worker is on sick leave supported by a proper medical certificate? [Para 86][Para 176]
11. Whether the employer must give a mask a day to every worker? [Para 92][Para 218]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Ms C had never been at fault; she was a union leader dismissed without reason and the employer never sought the labour inspector's authorisation; the union notified the employer of its elected candidates three times and the employer refused the letters; the company also cut all wages and the attendance bonus for strike days and for sick leave, and gave no masks although cloth dust blows into workers' noses. [Para 64][Para 66][Para 86][Para 220]
**Employer (respondent):** it reserved its right not to answer every point because the local union was not yet registered; it produced its registered internal rules, its announcements on the meal allowance and working conditions, photographs of protective equipment and a list of workers who received masks. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 30][Para 38]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 2 the employer must take Ms C back into work; issue 4 the employer must hear the workers' reasons before leaving or deciding on a request for leave for genuine necessity; issue 5 refused; issue 6 not considered; issue 11 refused. The award is non-binding and becomes final 8 (eight) days after notification. [Para 228][Para 229][Para 230][Para 231][Para 233][Para 234]

On issue 2, Article 293 of the Labour Law allows a staff delegate or candidate to be dismissed only with the labour inspector's agreement, and Article 3(3) of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 extends that protection to every candidate in a union leadership election, for 45 days before the poll and 45 days after it for those not elected. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 105] The panel followed award 40/10 (Meng Yan), holding that an elected candidate's protection cannot simply end at the poll, and award 154/13 against the same employer, holding that elected candidates are protected from the election until the union is registered and thereafter as leaders; without that protection an elected candidate would risk discriminatory dismissal, contrary to Articles 266, 12 and 279. [Para 118][Para 121][Para 124][Para 125] She was elected president twice, dismissed on 23 July 2013 within the protected period, and notice was given by three letters the employer refused; nothing showed the labour inspector's prior authorisation, so the dismissal was unlawful and she must be reinstated. [Para 141][Para 143][Para 147][Para 148][Para 149]

On issue 4, Article 2 gives the employer power to organise work, including leave procedures, but only so far as lawful and reasonable; the panel held an employer must consider whether a worker's absence is genuinely necessary and hear both sides, and ordered it to do so. [Para 155][Para 158][Para 164]

On issue 5, Article 332(1) suspends the labour contract during a strike, so no work is given and no wages paid, and Article 334 obliges the employer to pay strikers only where it hired replacements; no such allegation was made, so the demand was refused, following awards 27/10 (Hut In Brother) and 178/12 (Dae Koro). [Para 165][Para 167][Para 169][Para 175] On issue 11, Article 229 requires workplaces to be kept clean and to provide the conditions necessary for workers' health; masks were issued in the sewing, finishing and packing sections and the 61 claimants work in sewing. [Para 218][Para 222][Para 225]

On issue 6, the panel found no statute, agreement, internal rule or practice requiring wages or the attendance bonus to be maintained during certified sick leave, held the demand to exceed the law and so an interest dispute, and declined to consider it because the claimant federation, authorised by 61 of 716 workers, was not the most representative union. [Para 196][Para 204][Para 207][Para 208][Para 217]

**Dissent (Liv Sovanna, appointed by the workers), on issue 6 only:** Article 4 of the company's registered internal rules gives full wages for the first month of sick leave on a proper medical certificate and 60 per cent in the second and third months; under Article 13(2) a provision more favourable than the law prevails, so the employer must apply it, and the majority wrongly confined it to long-term illness. [Para 243][Para 251][Para 253][Para 256] On the bonus he accepted that a worker absent through illness should not receive the full month's bonus, but held that cutting it entirely defeats the purpose of an attendance incentive and contradicts Article 72 of the Constitution; it should be cut pro rata to the days of sick leave. [Para 273][Para 276][Para 278]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A candidate elected to union leadership is protected against dismissal from the day of the election until the union is registered, and thereafter as a union leader; a dismissal during that period without the labour inspector's prior authorisation is unlawful and the panel will order reinstatement. [Para 121][Para 143][Para 149] Notice of the protected candidates given by any available means, including letters refused at the gate and by post, satisfies the notification condition. [Para 141] An employer exercising its power to organise work must still hear a worker's reasons before refusing leave sought for genuine necessity. [Para 163][Para 164] A demand above what statute, agreement or practice requires is an interest dispute, which the panel will not decide unless the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise. [Para 196][Para 204][Para 208]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that listening to both sides is ordinary practice in state institutions, private bodies and factories alike and a principal means of avoiding misunderstanding. [Para 162][Para 163]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel's constitution sentence is incomplete, saying only that the panel "was constituted and on 19 August 2013". [Para 21] The award describes two union elections but at para 130 says "24 times", and refers to "3 candidates" where four or five are described. [Para 130][Para 134][Para 137] The employer's name varies in Khmer between បូវ ឃើរ ហ្គាមេន ហ្វេក ធើរី and បូវយើរ ហ្គា មេន; its registration number is Inv.1637 E/2012 of 8 October 2012 at para 51 but certificate No. 6268 of 12 October 2012 at para 28. [Para 2][Para 28][Para 51] Issue 6 is one demand in the reasoning but two in the operative order, which repeats itself, and the dissent repeats whole passages of the majority's reasoning verbatim. [Para 231][Para 241][Para 250] The worker's name is printed in full throughout; she is not named here. [Para 62]
