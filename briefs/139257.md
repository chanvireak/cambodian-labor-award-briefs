# Award No. 189/15 — Wai Xin 2 (Arbitration Council, 12 August 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuch Hun for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Ang Eng Phong chair) · Doc ID 139257

## 1. Facts
The company sews and weaves sweaters at Koh Thom district, Kandal, and employed 316 workers; it began operating in May 2015 and paid monthly wages on the 11th. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 42] The Khmer Youth Voice Union Federation held mandates from 20 workers and had held a factory election on 10 July 2015, though the local union was not yet registered with the Ministry. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39] Of 16 demands conciliated on 14 July 2015, 12 were settled and four referred; two of the four were withdrawn at the hearing, leaving the payday demand alone. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 22][Para 23] The employer did not attend the hearing of 31 July 2015, notwithstanding written summons and telephone attempts. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 52][Para 53]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay monthly wages to the workers on the 7th of every month? [Para 58]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** wages should be paid on the 7th because workers face daily living costs and microfinance instalments falling due before the 11th; two affiliated companies under the same owner pay between the 7th and the 9th. [Para 46][Para 47]
**Employer (respondent):** it did not appear, but stated in the conciliation report that it disagreed and pays on the 11th of each month, and may pay earlier where the 11th falls on a public holiday or Sunday. [Para 43][Para 44]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand for payment on the 7th was rejected, but the employer was ordered to pay wages no later than the 10th of each month, and earlier where that date falls on a rest day; the award is non-binding. [Para 90][Para 91][Para 92][Para 93][Para 94]

The panel first dealt with the employer's absence. Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 allows it to continue in a party's absence, and it found the summons properly served on the company director, with the company's administration refusing to accept the letter, and telephone contact attempted without success on the hearing day. [Para 50][Para 52][Para 53][Para 54] It therefore proceeded on the workers' evidence, holding that the employer had abandoned its right to answer and to object to documents. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 59][Para 60]

On the merits it applied Article 116(1), which requires wages to be paid at least twice a month with no more than 16 days between payments, and Article 116(2), which requires at least one payment a month for employees. [Para 67][Para 68] It held that the twice-monthly rule cannot apply to garment, textile and footwear workers, because the attendance bonus can only be calculated once a month by reference to a worker's presence, and because wages vary with overtime and piece-rate output, so the employer cannot close the payroll immediately at month end. [Para 75][Para 77][Para 79] Reading Article 2(2), it held the employer does hold the right to fix the payday, but only within the limit set by Article 116, and it disagreed with earlier awards treating the payday as entirely within managerial discretion. [Para 84][Para 86][Para 87][Para 89]

It then weighed the 7th against practicality: no official data fixes payday in the sector, but on the panel's observation of cases before the Council payday there never exceeded the 10th; 10 days after month end is a reasonable period to total wages, bonuses, overtime and piece-rate pay, and the workers produced no evidence of a concrete harm from payment on the 11th. [Para 78][Para 81][Para 82][Para 83] It rejected the 7th and set the 10th as the ceiling. [Para 90][Para 92] Arbitrator Seng Vuch Hun dissented in part, agreeing that the 7th should be rejected but objecting that the panel had created a 10-day standard no party claimed, unsupported by official data and unexplained as to why 10 days rather than another period. [Para 103][Para 107][Para 108][Para 119]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The twice-monthly payment rule in Article 116(1) does not apply to garment, textile and footwear workers, whose attendance bonus can only be computed monthly; the employer may therefore fix a single monthly payday, but only within the statutory limit, and wages must be paid no later than 10 days after the end of the month — earlier where that day is a rest day. [Para 77][Para 89][Para 92]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's statement that in cases before the Council paydays in the sector never exceeded the 10th rests on no official data and is an observation rather than a finding; the dissent's view that the panel should have stopped at rejecting the 7th is opinion. [Para 78][Para 119]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The dissent's appendix cites the award as dated "26 December August 2015" while the face and the signature page give 12 August 2015 [Para 96][Para 100][Para 120].
- The chair is named អាំង អេង ផុង on the face but អាំង អេង ថ្ងុំង in the signature block [Para 1][Para 95].
- Conciliation report No. 1177/15 is dated 20 July 2015 where first cited and 14 July 2015 where cited again [Para 11][Para 16][Para 29].
- The company is printed as វ៉ៃ ស៊ិន ២, វ៉ៃ ស៊ីន ២ and វ៉ៃ ស៊ិន; no Latin-script form appears in the text, so the heading uses the catalogue title, Wai Xin 2 [Para 11][Para 16][Para 36][Para 39].
- The panel is said to have been constituted on "23 July 2019 2015" [Para 17], and the party heading on the face reads "២- តាតី កម្មករ និយោជិត" instead of ភាគី [Para 1].
- The number of claimants is given as 20 mandated workers and then as 23 complainants, without explanation [Para 39][Para 41].
- The workers asserted a practice of paying on the 11th and the panel accepted it, yet the order caps payment at the 10th, so the award does not say whether the 11th had in fact been applied [Para 43][Para 60][Para 92].
- The quotations of Article 116 are partly garbled, and the passage on the twice-monthly rule is repeated in two versions within the reasons [Para 67][Para 72].
