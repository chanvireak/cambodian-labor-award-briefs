# Award No. 001/26 — Yak Jin (Cambodia) Inc (Arbitration Council, 2 March 2026)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phalla presiding; Ouk Ry appointed by the employer; An Non appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 140896

## 1. Facts
The employer, ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉ាក ជីន (ខេមបូឌា) អ៉ីន — Yak Jin (Cambodia) Inc — is a garment-cutting factory employing 2,708 workers, 2,126 of them women. [Para 83] On 6 December 2023 the company met the workers' representatives and union leaders and agreed to suspend employment contracts every Saturday, paying 50% of base wage for each suspended day. [Para 102][Para 118] The company then cut nine Saturday working days — 30 December 2023, 2, 13, 20 and 27 January 2024, and 3, 10, 17 and 24 February 2024 — without topping the 50% up to a full wage. [Para 101] The claimant group, ភាគី តំណាង កម្មករ និយោជិត ក្នុង ការដោះស្រាយ វិវាទ ការងារ រួម នៃ ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉ាក ជីន (ខេមបូឌា) អ៉ីន, brought two demands; conciliation on 16 January 2026 failed and the file reached the Council on 23 January 2026. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 15]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay a further 50% of wages, to complete 100%, for the nine Saturdays on which it reduced working days, to 131 workers? [Para 160]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the workers may claim wages for the ordinary working days of 12 and 13 April 2022 that were swapped for the paid public holidays of 14 and 19 May 2022? [Para 179]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), តំណាង កម្មករ និយោជិត នៃ ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉ាក ជីន (ខេមបូឌា) អ៉ីន:** they never agreed to the Saturday suspensions, received only 50% of wage for the nine days, and are owed the remaining 50% for 146 workers. [Para 109] On the day swap they worked 14 and 19 May 2022, which are paid public holidays, and were never paid for doing so in place of 12 and 13 April 2022. [Para 132][Para 133]
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉ាក ជីន (ខេមបូឌា) អ៉ីន:** it acted on the 6 December 2023 suspension agreement approved by majority vote of the representatives and union leaders, and only 144 workers appear on the fingerprint list. [Para 112][Para 113] The Article 120-new three-year limitation bars the wage claim, which is why it refuses to pay. [Para 6][Para 136]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer ordered to pay a further 50% of wages to 131 workers for the nine Saturdays; the day-swap claim dismissed as time-barred. [Para 192][Para 193]
Before the merits the panel fixed the claimant group, holding under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 that a representative needs written authority, so it counted only workers whose names and fingerprints were on the claim and whom the employer accepted as valid — 131 of the 146 claimed. [Para 142][Para 144][Para 156][Para 157] A duplicate name reduced the 144 fingerprints to 143, and the employer recognised 131. [Para 145][Para 146][Para 154]
On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 71(11) and Article 72 of the Labour Law: a suspension of enterprise activity must sit under labour-inspector supervision, and the employer must notify and obtain prior authorisation. [Para 160][Para 163] Following Award 58/07, a properly authorised suspension frees the employer from paying wages, but an unlawful one obliges it to pay 100% of wages and benefits even without work. [Para 168][Para 169] Here no authorisation letter was ever requested for the nine Saturdays, so the suspension was invalid and the missing 50% is owed. [Para 174][Para 175][Para 176]
On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 120-new, which extinguishes wage claims three years after the date payment fell due, and found the employer paid the swapped-day wages on 10 June 2022. [Para 179][Para 184] The claim was lodged on 19 December 2025 and received on 25 December 2025, past three years, so the right had lapsed. [Para 186][Para 187][Para 188]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Cutting working days while paying half wage is a suspension of the employment contract, and it is lawful only if the employer notified and obtained prior authorisation from the labour inspector; without that, the employer owes the full 100% of wages for the days not worked. [Para 163][Para 169][Para 175] A wage claim is extinguished three years after the date the wage fell due, counted from the day the employer actually paid, not from the day the work was performed. [Para 179][Para 184][Para 187]

**Obiter dicta:** A suspension agreement approved by a majority of representatives and union leaders does not dispense with the inspector's authorisation. [Para 112][Para 175]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The claimant count is printed four ways: 146 claimed, 144 fingerprinted, 143 after one duplicate name, and 131 accepted. [Para 101][Para 145][Para 146][Para 157]
- Individual claimants are named in full with employee numbers in the reasons and in the employer's evidence summaries, contrary to the Council's usual practice of initials. [Para 145][Para 147]
- The date on which the employer filed its evidence is printed as 17 February 2026 in one place and as 17 December February 2026 in another. [Para 89][Para 147]
- The worker-appointed arbitrator's name differs between the header and the signature block, and the signature block carries OCR debris. [Para 1][Para 195]
- The recital of Article 120-new and the Prakas 099 provision at the start of Issue 2 is partly garbled, with stray article numbers interleaved. [Para 179]
