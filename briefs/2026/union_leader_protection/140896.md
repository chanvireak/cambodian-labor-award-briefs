# Award No. 001/26 — Yak Jin (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, March 2, 2026)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Uk Rith, An Non, Kong Phalla (president) · Doc ID 140896

## 1. Facts
The employer is a garment manufacturer employing 2,708 workers, of whom 2,126 were women. [Para 86] The Labour Disputes Department received the workers' petition on 25 December 2025 seeking relief on two points; conciliation on 16 January 2026 failed, and the unresolved points were referred to the Council on 23 January 2026 with the report of 22 January 2026. [Para 14] [Para 15] [Para 16] A panel was formed on 27 January 2026, both sides attended the 10 February 2026 hearing, further conciliation failed, and the parties chose a non-binding award. [Para 17] [Para 19] [Para 20] [Para 22] Evidence deadlines were 17 and 19 February 2026, and issuance was extended to 2 March 2026. [Para 23] [Para 24] The record comprised party documents and ministry reports, with no outside witnesses or experts. [Para 25] [Para 80] [Para 81] The workers claimed for 146 people; the fingerprinted petition listed 144 entries including one duplicate, leaving 143, and the employer ultimately recognised 131 proper claimants. [Para 145] [Para 150] [Para 151] [Para 160] The employer also challenged the union leadership's authority, while the workers relied on a registered union and a 20 November 2025 request for recognition of new leaders. [Para 3] [Para 88] [Para 89]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether, after limiting the claimant class to 131 workers, the employer had to pay an additional 50% of wages to complete 100% wage payment for the nine Saturday work-reduction days of 30 December 2023, 2, 13, 20 and 27 January 2024 and 3, 10, 17 and 24 February 2024? [Para 103] [Para 163] [Para 167]
2. (Issue 2) Whether, for the same 131 claimants, the workers could recover wages for the swap of the normal working days of 12 and 13 April 2022 to the paid holidays of 14 and 19 May 2022? [Para 133] [Para 186]

## 3. Positions of the parties
The employer said the existing union leadership's term had expired and new leaders had sought recognition, so those leaders could not act for the workers; it relied on majority support and the 6 December 2023 suspension agreement. [Para 3] [Para 4] [Para 5] On Issue 1, the workers said the labour-inspectorate letter of 25 December 2023 was not permission to suspend contracts, so the employer owed the additional 50% to complete 100% wages for the nine Saturdays. [Para 113] [Para 114] The employer answered that it followed the 6 December 2023 agreement because there was no time to agree with each worker, and had already paid 50% to 220 workers on 10 November 2025 under Award No. 012/24. [Para 111] [Para 115] The workers replied that the 146 claimants had not received that payment. [Para 112] On Issue 2, the workers said the 146 claimants worked on the paid holidays without receiving the corresponding pay; the employer said wages were paid on 10 June 2022 and that Article 120-new of the Labour Law barred a claim after three years. [Para 135] [Para 137] [Para 141] The workers argued Article 120-new applied only after termination of the employment contract. [Para 139] [Para 140] The employer also cited prior payments to 233 and 147 workers under earlier awards, while the workers said these claimants had never previously claimed. [Para 136] [Para 138] The employer disputed claimant identity, and the workers did not object to its evidence. [Para 146] [Para 161]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Partly granted. The panel ordered the employer to pay the additional 50% to complete 100% wages for the nine Saturdays to 131 workers, and dismissed the holiday-swap claim for those 131 workers; no riel or USD sum is printed in the award. [Para 200] [Para 201]
Under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099, written authority was required for representation, and the panel followed its earlier awards. [Para 147] [Para 148] [Para 149] It reduced the 144 petition entries to 143 after removing one duplicate, then accepted the employer's 131 proper claimants because the workers neither filed evidence nor objected. [Para 151] [Para 160] [Para 161] [Para 163] For Issue 1, Article 71 of the Labour Law permits suspension of enterprise activity only for serious economic or material difficulty, under labour-inspectorate supervision and for no more than two months, while Article 72 relieves the wage obligation only for a lawful suspension. [Para 167] [Para 168] [Para 171] [Para 172] The inspectorate's letter required individual agreements or, failing agreement, a lawful suspension under the Labour Law; the employer instead relied on majority approval and never applied for permission to suspend. [Para 109] [Para 110] [Para 129] [Para 130] [Para 178] [Para 180] The panel therefore treated the Saturday reductions as unlawful and ordered the additional 50% for the nine days to the 131 workers. [Para 182] [Para 183] For Issue 2, Article 120-new sets a three-year period running from the payday, and the panel rejected the workers' argument that it applied only after contract termination. [Para 187] [Para 189] [Para 193] [Para 194] Wages for the swapped holiday work were paid on 10 June 2022, but the petition was received on 25 December 2025, so the claim was time-barred. [Para 191] [Para 195] [Para 196] [Para 197] The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notice absent written objection to the Minister. [Para 202]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that reduces workdays without prior labour-inspectorate authorisation and a lawful suspension has not shifted the wage risk; it must maintain full wages, and any partial payment leaves the balance payable. [Para 170] [Para 175] [Para 182] A wage claim under Article 120-new of the Labour Law runs from payday and is lost after three years, including for currently employed workers. [Para 187] [Para 193] [Para 194] [Para 196] Under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099, a representative needs written authority, and unchallenged employer records may fix the claimant class. [Para 147] [Para 148] [Para 161] [Para 163] These are reasons of an arbitration panel, not binding precedent. **Obiter dicta:** the panel endorsed its earlier interpretations on representation, suspension and limitation, but those references create no precedent. [Para 149] [Para 176] [Para 190]
