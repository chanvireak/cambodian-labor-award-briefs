# Award No. 010/26 — Son Suy Sing Industrial (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 3 September 2026)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Sengl Ruoy Hun, Liv Sovanna, Pen Bunchhea · Doc ID 162369

## 1. Facts
The company manufactures sports and general-use bags and employs 3,031 workers, 2,254 of whom are women [Para 48]. On 6 July 2026 the workers’ union filed a complaint with the Labour Dispute Department raising seven points; four remained unsettled after conciliation [Para 19][Para 20]. Those four points were: provision of lunch, a $10 allowance for WH2 warehouse workers, clean drinking water and an end to harassment, and establishment of a nursery under Article 186 [Para 1][Para 4][Para 6][Para 11]. At the arbitration hearing on 17 August 2026 the parties reached agreement on the first three points, leaving only the nursery claim in dispute [Para 24][Para 25]. The parties chose a non‑binding award [Para 26].

## 2. Issues in dispute
Only Issue 4 remained after the hearing [Para 24]. The panel therefore considered:
4. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must comply with Article 186 of the Labour Law by establishing a nursery for children over 18 months, and if it cannot, pay the costs of an external nursery according to actual invoices? [Para 11][Para 52][Para 64]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers** relied on Article 186, arguing that an enterprise with more than 100 female workers must provide a nursery [Para 11][Para 55]. Because the company has 2,254 female workers, they demanded that a nursery be set up or, if not possible, the employer must pay for external nursery care as invoiced [Para 11][Para 56].

**Employer** acknowledged the legal obligation but stated it could not immediately build a nursery because of insufficient space and difficulty finding qualified caregivers [Para 58][Para 59]. It committed to try to build one by the end of 2026 [Para 59][Para 69]. Since April 2026 the employer had been paying $5 per month to each female worker with a child aged 18‑36 months as a substitute [Para 12][Para 54]. The employer consulted the ILO, which advised the cash payment as a temporary measure given the space constraints [Para 63]. It also conceded that the Labour Law does not allow an allowance in place of a nursery [Para 62]. If a nursery were built, the $5 payment would stop [Para 60].

## 4. Award and reasoning
Article 186 requires an enterprise that employs at least 100 female workers to set up a nursery and a breastfeeding room; where on‑site establishment is impossible, female workers may place their children in an external nursery at the employer’s expense [Para 64]. The panel followed its earlier decisions that read the provision identically [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67]. The company employed 2,254 women, well above the threshold [Para 68][Para 71]. Although the employer cited space and staffing difficulties, no nursery had yet been provided [Para 68]. The panel held that the employer was obliged to comply with Article 186 [Para 70][Para 72]. It therefore ordered the employer to set up a nursery [Para 73]. If the employer is unable to do so, female workers with children older than 18 months may enrol them in an outside nursery at the employer’s cost, based on actual invoices [Para 72][Para 75].

**Outcome:** Granted. The employer shall establish a nursery; failing which, female workers may place their children in an external nursery at the employer’s expense according to actual invoices.

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Under Article 186 of the Labour Law an enterprise with at least 100 female workers must provide a nursery on‑site or, if that is not feasible, pay for external childcare at actual cost. A cash allowance is not a lawful substitute for this duty. [Para 64][Para 72][Para 62][Para 67]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the employer’s $5 monthly payment, though intended as a temporary measure, did not satisfy the requirements of Article 186 [Para 62][Para 72].
