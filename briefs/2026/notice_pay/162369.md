# Award No. 010/26 វ.រ — Sun Suying In Das Steel (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 3 September 2026)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Seng Touch Hun (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (president) · Doc ID 162369

## 1. Facts
The employer, Sun Suying In Das Steel (Cambodia), manufactures bags and employs 3,031 workers, of whom 2,254 are women [Para 48]. On 6 July 2026 the labour dispute department received a complaint from the workers' representatives raising seven points on working conditions [Para 19]. Conciliation on 27 July 2026 settled three points and left four unsettled [Para 20]; the four unsettled points were forwarded to the Arbitration Council on 3 August 2026 [Para 21], and the panel was constituted on 5 August 2026 [Para 22]. At the hearing the parties reached agreement on issues 1, 2 and 3, leaving only issue 4 for the panel to decide [Para 24] [Para 25]. Issue 4 concerns childcare: the company employs 245 women with children aged 18 to 36 months [Para 52], has not established a nursery [Para 53], and since 1 April 2026 has paid USD 5 per month in lieu of a nursery [Para 54]. The employer said it lacked space and qualified carers [Para 58], would try to comply by the end of 2026 [Para 59], and would stop the USD 5 payment if it built a nursery [Para 60]. The award is non-binding [Para 26] [Para 76].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 4) Whether the employer is obliged under Article 186 of the Labour Law to build a nursery and a breastfeeding room for the children of women workers [Para 52] [Para 64]?
2. Whether, where the enterprise cannot itself accommodate children over 18 months, the employer must pay the actual invoices of an outside nursery used by the woman worker [Para 64] [Para 72]?
3. Whether the employer's payment of USD 5 per month in lieu of a nursery discharges that statutory obligation [Para 54] [Para 62]?

## 3. Positions of the parties
The workers' representatives relied on Article 186, which they said requires any employer with 100 or more women workers to provide a nursery [Para 55]; they asked the employer to comply so that they could work with peace of mind [Para 56], and noted that 200 union-member women alone have small children [Para 57]. The employer's representatives said the company could not comply because it has no adequate space and it is difficult to find carers meeting the required standard [Para 58]; the employer would try to set up a lawful nursery by the end of 2026 [Para 59]; if a nursery is built the USD 5 monthly payment will cease [Para 60]; and time is needed to survey affected workers to avoid protests [Para 61]. The employer acknowledged that the law does not require cash in lieu of a nursery [Para 62], and said it paid the USD 5 on ILO advice given the space constraints [Para 63]. Both sides accepted the figures of 2,254 women workers and 245 children aged 18 to 36 months [Para 52] [Para 68].

## 4. Award and reasoning
The panel set out the text of Article 186 of the Labour Law [Para 64] and aligned itself with earlier awards holding that a company must provide a breastfeeding room and nursery, and that where in-house care for children over 18 months is impossible the mother may use an outside nursery at the employer's expense against actual invoices [Para 65] [Para 66] [Para 67]. On the facts, the panel found that the employer employs 2,254 women [Para 71] and has not yet established a nursery [Para 68] [Para 71]. It held that the employer is under a duty, once it employs at least 100 women, to build a nursery and provide a breastfeeding room [Para 70], and that if it cannot do so, women with children over 18 months may place them in an outside nursery with the employer bearing the actual invoiced cost [Para 72]. The panel therefore ordered the employer to establish a nursery for the women workers [Para 73] [Para 74], and, in the event it cannot, permitted mothers of children over 18 months to send them to any nursery with the employer paying the care costs on actual invoices [Para 75]. The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification if no party objects in writing to the Minister [Para 76].

**Outcome:** Granted — the employer was ordered to set up a nursery for the women workers [Para 74]; failing that, mothers of children over 18 months may use any outside nursery with the employer paying the actual invoices [Para 75]. No sum in riel or USD was fixed, and the USD 5 monthly allowance was neither awarded nor expressly abolished [Para 54] [Para 60].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that employs at least 100 women is in breach of Article 186 for as long as no nursery and breastfeeding room exist, and the size of the workforce, not the employer's financial or space constraints, triggers the duty [Para 70] [Para 71]. A voluntary monthly cash allowance is not a substitute for that duty, since the law does not authorise payment in lieu [Para 62] [Para 72]. Where in-house provision is genuinely impossible, the duty converts into an obligation to reimburse the actual invoiced cost of an outside nursery chosen by the mother [Para 72] [Para 75].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel recorded the employer's reliance on ILO advice to pay USD 5 per month instead of building a nursery, but did not treat that advice as discharging the statutory duty [Para 63].
