# Award No. 010/26 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន សុន ស៊ុយ ស៊ីង អ៊ិន ដាស ស្ទៀល (ខេមបូឌា) (Arbitration Council, ថ្ងៃទី ៣ ខែកញ្ញា ឆ្នាំ ២០២៦)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · សេង រួច ហ៊ុន (employer-appointed), លីវ សុវណ្ណា (worker-appointed), ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា (president) · Doc ID 162369

## 1. Facts
The employer, ក្រុមហ៊ុន សុន ស៊ុយ ស៊ីង អ៊ិន ដាស ស្ទៀល (ខេមបូឌា), is based in Chbar Mon, Kampong Speu province [Para 1]. It manufactures bags and employed 3,031 workers, including 2,254 women [Para 48]. Its enterprise union was registered with the Ministry of Labour and Vocational Training [Para 38]. The workers' demand carried 1,036 fingerprints [Para 51]. On 6 July 2026 the workers filed a claim on seven working-condition points [Para 19]. Conciliation on 27 July 2026 settled three points and left four unresolved [Para 20]. The unresolved points reached the Arbitration Council on 3 August 2026 [Para 21], and the panel was formed on 5 August 2026 [Para 22]. The hearing was held on 17 August 2026 on the 18th floor of the NSSF building in Phnom Penh [Para 17][Para 18]. The employer appeared through a company adviser and the head of administration; the worker side appeared through federation officials and enterprise union officers [Para 1]. At the hearing the panel mediated further: issues 1, 2 and 3 were agreed, leaving only issue 4 [Para 24]. The parties elected a non-binding award [Para 26]. Evidence closed on 19 August 2026 and objections on 21 August 2026 [Para 27]. The parties agreed to postpone the award to 3 September 2026 [Para 28]. Both sides accept that 245 women workers have children aged 18 to 36 months [Para 52]. The employer has arranged no nursery [Para 53]. Since 1 April 2026 it has paid USD 5 per month per eligible woman worker in lieu [Para 54].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must comply with Article 186 of the Labour Law by arranging a nursery and breastfeeding room and, if the enterprise cannot take children over 18 months directly, whether women workers may place those children in any nursery with the employer paying the care costs on actual invoices [Para 64]?

## 3. Positions of the parties
The worker representatives asked the employer to apply Article 186, under which an enterprise employing at least 100 women must arrange a nursery [Para 55]. They noted the 2,254 women workers and the 245 women with children aged 18 to 36 months, and asked for a nursery so they could work with peace of mind [Para 52][Para 56]. They added that about 200 union-member women have small children [Para 57].

The employer said it could not yet provide a nursery because it lacked space and could not find caregivers meeting the standard [Para 58]. It would try to arrange one, lawfully, by the end of 2026 [Para 59]. If it built a nursery it would stop the USD 5 monthly payment [Para 60]. It acknowledged that the Labour Law does not require cash in lieu of a nursery [Para 62]. It said it had consulted the ILO, which recommended cash because of the limited space [Para 63]. It wanted time to survey workers with small children first [Para 61].

## 4. Award and reasoning
The panel set out Article 186 of the Labour Law 1997: an enterprise with at least 100 women or children must arrange a breastfeeding room and a nursery; if it cannot take children over 18 months, women workers may send them to any nursery and the employer pays the care costs on actual invoices [Para 64]. The panel adopted the same interpretation as in earlier Council awards [Para 67]. It found that the employer employs 2,254 women workers but has arranged no nursery [Para 71]. It therefore held that the employer has a duty under Article 186 to arrange or build a nursery [Para 70][Para 72]. The employer's own admission that the law does not require a cash substitute confirmed that the USD 5 payment is not compliance [Para 62]. The panel ordered the employer to arrange a nursery [Para 73]. If it cannot, women workers with children over 18 months may place them in any nursery and the employer must pay the actual invoices [Para 75]. No party witnesses or experts were heard [Para 29]. No fixed riel or USD sum was ordered; the alternative remedy is actual invoices [Para 75]. The award is non-binding and becomes final after eight days without written objection to the Minister of Labour [Para 76].

**Outcome:** granted — the employer is ordered to arrange a nursery for the workers; if it cannot, it must pay the actual invoice costs of external nursery care for children over 18 months [Para 74][Para 75].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that employs at least 100 women workers must arrange a nursery and breastfeeding room under Article 186 of the Labour Law [Para 70]. If it cannot take children over 18 months, it must pay the actual invoice costs of any external nursery those workers use [Para 75]. Payment of a monthly cash allowance is not a statutory substitute and does not discharge that duty [Para 62][Para 72].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted the employer's ILO-supported cash arrangement but still ordered performance of the Article 186 duty [Para 63][Para 72].
