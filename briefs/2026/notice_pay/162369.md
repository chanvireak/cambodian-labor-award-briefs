# Award No. 010/26 — Sun Shui Shing Industrial (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 3 September 2026)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bunchea presiding; Seng Ruch Hun, Liv Sovanna) · Doc ID 162369

## 1. Facts
Sun Shui Shing Industrial (Cambodia), at Chbar Mon, Kampong Speu, makes bags and employs 3,031 workers, 2,254 of them women. [Para 48][Para 1] On 6 July 2026 the workers filed seven demands; conciliation on 27 July 2026 settled three and left four, and at the hearing of 17 August 2026 the parties agreed three of those four, leaving only the nursery claim. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 24][Para 25] The employer has no nursery for children over 18 months and, since 1 April 2026, has paid USD 5 a month to women with children aged 18 to 36 months in place of one. [Para 53][Para 54]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must keep paying the USD 10 a month lunch allowance and restore the midday meal? [Para 2][Para 3]
2. Whether the employer must pay an additional USD 10 per worker to the WH2 warehouse loading and unloading group, as promised for April 2026? [Para 4][Para 5]
3. Whether the employer must supply clean drinking water daily, have the water tanks inspected and cleaned, and stop driving workers away from the water point? [Para 6][Para 9]
4. Whether Article 186 of the Labour Law obliges the employer to provide a nursery, and where it cannot accommodate children over 18 months, whether women workers may place their children in any nursery at the employer's expense against actual invoices? [Para 12][Para 52][Para 64]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the law requires any enterprise with at least 100 women workers to provide a nursery; this employer has 2,254 women, 245 of them with children aged 18 to 36 months, and pays USD 5 a month instead of building one, which is no substitute; they want a lawful nursery so they can work with peace of mind. [Para 52][Para 55][Para 56] They also pressed the water, food and warehousing claims, and offered to accept bottled water temporarily while the water system is fixed. [Para 4][Para 7]
**Employer (respondent):** there is not enough space and qualified child-minders are hard to find; the company will try to build a compliant nursery by the end of 2026, and will stop the USD 5 payment once it does; it accepts the law does not authorise payment in lieu, but says the ILO advised cash instead of a nursery because of the site constraints. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60][Para 62][Para 63] On the other claims it said the midday meal was withdrawn because of economic difficulty and fuel costs, that the water is tested every three months and certified safe, and that workers were told to return to work because they stood talking and ate at the water point during working hours. [Para 2][Para 8][Para 9]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer is ordered to arrange a nursery for the women workers, and where it cannot, women with children over 18 months may place them in any nursery with the employer paying the maintenance cost against actual invoices; the award is non-binding and becomes final 8 (eight) calendar days after notification if no party objects to the Minister of Labour. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 75][Para 76]
Issues 1 to 3 were disposed of by the parties' agreement at the hearing, so the panel reasoned only on issue 4. [Para 24][Para 25] It read Article 186 of the Labour Law of 1997 as requiring an establishment with at least one hundred women workers, whatever their age, to provide a nursing room and a nursery, and as allowing a woman, where the enterprise cannot accommodate a child over eighteen months, to place the child in any nursery at the employer's expense on actual invoices. [Para 64][Para 70] It followed its own earlier awards on the same article — 63/04, 103/08, 81/09, 91/13, 212/14, 235/15, 014/17 and 035/22 — which ordered the company to build a nursery and permitted an outside nursery at the employer's cost only as a fallback. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67]
On the facts, both parties admitted 2,254 women workers and 245 women with children aged 18 to 36 months, and the employer admitted no nursery had been set up. [Para 68][Para 71] The panel therefore held that the statutory duty was engaged and unsatisfied, and that a monthly USD 5 payment, which the employer itself said the law does not provide for and which the ILO had suggested only because of space limits, does not discharge it. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 72] The employer's undertaking to build by the end of 2026 and its complaint about space and child-minders went to the timing of compliance, not to the existence of the duty. [Para 58][Para 69]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that employs at least 100 women workers must actually provide a nursing room and a nursery under Article 186; a monthly cash allowance paid in place of a nursery does not discharge that duty, however long it has been paid, because the Act provides for money only where the employer cannot accommodate children over 18 months and a woman chooses an outside nursery, and then only against actual invoices. [Para 64][Para 70][Para 72]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that the parties had settled issues 1 to 3 at the hearing and that it would therefore examine issue 4 alone, and noted the employer's statement that it would withdraw the USD 5 payment if it built a nursery. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 60]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer-appointed arbitrator is printed "Seng Ruch Hun" in the panel list and "Seng Touch Hun" in the signature block; the award does not reconcile the two, and both spellings are reproduced here rather than resolved. [Para 1][Para 77] Para 74 sets the age threshold for an outside nursery at "over 107 months", against "over 18 months" everywhere else in the award. [Para 74][Para 72][Para 75] The Minister's letter recognising the union's leadership is dated 30 November 2026 at para 39 — after the award — and 30 November 2023 at para 50. [Para 39][Para 50] The company name varies across the document (Steel/Steam variants, and "In Das"/"Sin Das"/"Un Das"), and the conciliation report No. 620 of 31 July 2026 is said to have reached the secretariat in "August 2026" at para 16 but on 3 August 2026 at para 21. [Para 16][Para 21][Para 48] The list of issues in the header at para 1 is truncated by the OCR, and individual workers, union officials and employer representatives appear only under partial OCR names; they are not reproduced here. [Para 1][Para 50]
