# Award No. 244/16 — Lou Thai (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 4 January 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Virak, appointed by the workers; Kuy Neam, chair) · Doc ID 139398

## 1. Facts
Lou Thai (Cambodia) Co., Ltd cuts and sews garments at Svay Toeu, Svay Rieng city, and employed about 1,951 workers. [Para 1][Para 31][Para 32] On 8 December 2016 two workers fought inside the factory; both were taken to the administration office, one signed a warning and the other, Mr H, refused, and the employer let the other worker go home but kept Mr H at the office until noon. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 44][Para 45] The workforce struck on 9 December 2016 over that handling, and struck again from 12 to 15 December 2016 to demand wages for 9 December; the employer paid no wages or benefits for any of those days. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40][Para 41] Six workers were recognised as the complainant representatives. [Para 33][Para 68]

## 2. Issues in dispute
Four demands were conciliated, three settled, and one remained. [Para 13][Para 14]
1. Whether the employer must pay wages and other benefits to the workers who struck on 9 December 2016 and from 12 to 15 December 2016? [Para 35][Para 69]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer caused the strike — it allowed the worker who started the fight to go home while detaining the worker who was assaulted, which provoked the workforce — so it should bear the wage cost of the strike days; the employer also failed to release Mr H from the administration office and refused to hand the aggressor over to the workers for referral to the authorities. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 46][Para 49][Para 50]
**Employer (respondent):** under Article 332 the contract is suspended during a strike and no wages or seniority accrue; the workers had promised on 23 November 2016 not to strike again unlawfully and that if they did, no wages or benefits would be paid; Mr H alone refused to acknowledge the warning, the fight was reported to the authorities, and the workers gave no advance notice of the strike. [Para 4][Para 5][Para 6][Para 47][Para 48][Para 51][Para 52]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the claim for wages and other benefits for 9 December and 12 to 15 December 2016. [Para 79][Para 80]

The panel began with Article 332(1) of the Labour Law, which provides that the employment contract is suspended while a strike is in progress and that during the strike no work is given and no wages are paid. [Para 69][Para 70] It read that provision together with Article 72(1), under which suspension extinguishes the employer's wage obligation, and followed its own earlier reasoning in Award No. 27/10 (Hat In The Bay) that on any day a worker strikes — whether the strike is procedurally lawful or unlawful — the employer is not required to pay wages or bonuses for that day. [Para 72][Para 74][Para 75] It reaffirmed that line in this award and in the earlier awards it cited. [Para 76]

The workers' argument that the employer provoked the strike was therefore beside the point: the panel accepted that the strike was called because of the employer's handling of the 8 December fight, and that the workers believed the employer was the author of the dispute, but held that this did not alter the statutory consequence that the contract was suspended on the strike days and the wage obligation with it. [Para 77][Para 78] The claim failed in its entirety. [Para 79]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
During the days on which workers strike, the employment contract is suspended by operation of Article 332(1) and the employer owes no wages, bonuses or other benefits for those days under Article 72(1), whether the strike was procedurally lawful or unlawful and whether or not the employer's conduct provoked it; the lawfulness of the strike is not a precondition to the wage exemption. [Para 74][Para 76][Para 78]

**Obiter dicta:** because the factory has no most-representative union, the panel applied Articles 54 and 11 of the Labour Union Law and Article 1 of Prakas No. 362 to hold that the six workers recognised by the Svay Rieng provincial labour office could represent the complainants. [Para 58][Para 64][Para 65][Para 66] The award is non-binding and becomes final if no objection is filed within eight days. [Para 20][Para 81]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The date of the award is printed two ways: page 1 gives "4 January 2016" [Para 1], while paragraph 21 and the closing orders give 4 January 2017 [Para 21][Para 82]. The Council itself issued a corrigendum in the same file correcting page 1 from 2016 to 2017 without altering the substance, so the operative date is 4 January 2017 [Para 82][Para 83]. Year digits elsewhere are corrupted in the same way — "2019" and "2029" appear inside 2016 phrases [Para 69][Para 79]. Paragraph 27 cites a labour office letter concerning "ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ូ (ខេមបូឌា)", a different company name evidently carried over from another file [Para 27], and paragraph 66 repeats the province as "Kaev province, Kaev province, Svay Rieng". The complainant count is unstable, reading "2 out of 6 workers" at [Para 68]. The closing signature page is largely destroyed, leaving Latin OCR noise in place of the arbitrators' names and the Council's address block [Para 87][Para 88]. No monetary amounts are printed.
