# Award No. 244/13 — Dewhirst (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 5 December 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy appointed by the employer party, An Virak appointed by the worker party, Kong Phallha chair appointed by both) · Doc ID 139897

## 1. Facts
The Federation of Khmer Youth Voice Union, for workers at Dewhirst (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, asked the panel to order the company to execute the earlier Award No. 188/13 between the same parties, and carried forward eleven demands from the unsuccessful conciliation. [Para 19][Para 2] Nine of the eleven had already been decided in Award No. 188/13 of 30 September 2013, leaving only its issues 2 and 3 as new. [Para 27][Para 52] When the panel explained this at the hearing of 5 December 2013, the workers' representatives withdrew by leaving the hearing; the employer accepted the explanation. [Para 24][Para 54] What remained was purely procedural: whether to continue or to terminate. [Para 55]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the nine demands already decided between the same parties in Award No. 188/13 could be reopened by a second reference? [Para 52][Para 75]
2. Whether the panel had to terminate the arbitration once the claimant, properly invited, withdrew its claim and left the hearing without a valid reason? [Para 55][Para 66]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** all eleven demands should be heard and decided afresh, including those already decided in Award No. 188/13; the workers said they did not understand why the Labour Dispute Department had sent the file to the Council a second time, and then withdrew their right to continue while asking the panel to proceed "according to law". [Para 54][Para 80]
**Employer (respondent):** accepted the panel's explanation that the nine previously decided issues could not be re-heard, and left the procedural consequence to the panel. [Para 54]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** withdrawn — the panel ordered the termination (បញ្ចប់) of the arbitration proceedings in case No. 244/13; no relief was ordered on any of the eleven demands. [Para 90][Para 91]

On the first issue the panel applied the principle of res judicata (អាជ្ញាអស់ជំនុំ): where the same disputed points are brought by the same parties before a tribunal that has already ruled on them, the tribunal has discretion not to take them up again unless a legal provision requires otherwise. [Para 75] The arbitration procedure contains no such provision, so the discretion stood unqualified, and the panel followed its own reasoning in Award No. 81/11-GJH, issue 1. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 78] It accordingly confirmed that only issues 2 and 3 remained to be heard. [Para 79]

On the second issue the panel worked from Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004: Article 18 obliges the panel to invite the parties to present their claims in person and to file the necessary documents, Article 20 obliges the parties to attend every meeting convened, and Article 21 and Rule 4.7 of the annexed procedural rules allow the panel either to continue in the absence of a party or to close the arbitration by order. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57][Para 58][Para 59] It read those provisions as establishing a practice that runs in opposite directions: an unexplained absence of the respondent leads the panel to continue and hear the case in absentia, because the claimant must still prove its case; an unexplained absence of the claimant leads to termination, because a claimant that does not appear forfeits the chance to give the reasons and evidence that support its demand. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65] Termination therefore required three cumulative conditions: the parties were properly invited, the claimant did not appear before the panel, and the claimant gave no valid reason. [Para 66]

Condition one was satisfied: invitation letter No. 1243 of 20 November 2013 went to the Federation and the worker representatives with the date, place and contact numbers of the hearing, and the workers in fact attended. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 70][Para 71] Condition two was satisfied on the panel's reading of "appearance": it means both presence at the hearing and participation through to its close, which the panel illustrated by the four stages of an arbitration hearing — introduction and conflict explanation, explanation of procedure and identification of the issues, attempted conciliation, and hearing to judgment. [Para 72][Para 73] Having been told that only issues 2 and 3 would be heard, the workers declared that they abandoned their right to take part and walked out, so they did not participate to the end. [Para 81][Para 82][Para 83] Condition three was also satisfied: their only stated reason was the panel's refusal to re-decide all eleven issues, and a demand to reopen decided issues is itself contrary to res judicata and so cannot be a valid reason. [Para 84][Para 85][Para 86]

Having found all three conditions met, and having recorded that the workers were given sufficient opportunity and time to defend the claims, the panel closed the file. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 90]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a claimant union is properly invited, appears, and then abandons the hearing before the proceedings close without a valid reason, the panel will terminate the arbitration instead of deciding the claims in the claimant's absence: the burden of giving reasons and evidence rests on the claimant, and non-appearance forfeits it. Non-appearance of a respondent, by contrast, does not stop the case. A demand that the panel re-decide issues already decided between the same parties is not a valid reason, because it offends res judicata, which the panel may invoke of its own motion where no provision of the arbitration procedure requires reopening. [Para 64][Para 66][Para 77][Para 84]

**Obiter dicta:** the four-stage description of an arbitration hearing, and the observation that the workers had no intention of continuing to resolve the dispute with their employer through the legal process, are general remarks that were not needed to satisfy the three conditions. [Para 73][Para 89]
