# Award No. 117/19 — Thien Sung Garmen (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 January 2020)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-nominated), Liv Sovanna (worker-nominated, dissenting on issue 3), Pen Bun Chhea (president) · Doc ID 139284

## 1. Facts
Thien Sung Garmen (Cambodia) Co., Ltd sews garments and employed 1,182 workers when the award issued. [Para 74][Para 75] In November 2019 the workforce lodged 13 demands to improve working conditions; the Labour Dispute Department's conciliation of 18 December 2019 settled none of the nine points it handled, and the file reached the Council on 27 December 2019. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 22] At the hearing the workers withdrew four demands (issues 2, 7, 8 and 9) and the parties agreed issue 6 by consent, leaving issues 1, 3, 4 and 5 for decision. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 34] The award is non-binding. [Para 35][Para 152]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is entitled to refuse permission for union leaders to carry out union work during the midday meal break inside the production building? [Para 115]
3. Whether the employer is obliged to pay wages twice a month on the 20th of the month being worked and the 5th of the following month, as Prakas No. 442 requires? [Para 121]
4. Whether the employer is obliged to remove the step (កំណល់) that production workers must climb onto while security staff check them before they leave work? [Para 130]
5. Whether the employer has the right to set high production-volume bonus targets? [Para 140]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Union work at midday does not touch working time, and the canteen is too cramped and too many workers eat at home for leaders to reach members there. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 81] Wages in fact came on the 28th and then the 8th or 10th, not on the dates Prakas No. 442 fixes. [Para 92][Para 93] The step is slippery and workers are stiff after long hours, so they risk falling. [Para 103][Para 104] Bonus targets climb day by day — 1,048, 1,123, 1,198, 1,272 units — and workers who fall short are blamed and refused overtime. [Para 111][Para 112]
**Employer (respondent):** Under Article 2 of the Labour Law the employer controls use of the production building; thumb-printing union papers there soiled garments and left no time to resume at 12:00. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 84][Para 118] Wages are already paid twice monthly on a schedule notified a year in advance and accepted by the other unions. [Para 91][Para 94] The step was cut from 30cm to 20cm, anti-slip rubber fitted, and no worker has been hurt in two to three years of use. [Para 101][Para 106][Para 107][Para 108] Targets are fixed by industrial engineers from headcount, hours and machine speed. [Para 113]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all four remaining claims were rejected and nothing was ordered payable. [Para 148][Para 149][Para 150][Para 151]

On issue 1 the panel read Article 66 of the Trade Union Law 2016 as making access for union representatives conditional: the employer must not refuse it except where access would obstruct the ordinary operation of the enterprise. [Para 115][Para 116] Both sides accepted that the employer opens the production building at 11:45 for workers to return to their places for a 12:00 restart, and the panel found on the quality-control report and photographs that thumb-printing union papers inside the building had in fact soiled goods. [Para 76][Para 118] Because union activity was already allowed elsewhere on the premises during the break, the refusal was confined to the production building and so fell within the exception; the claim was rejected. [Para 117][Para 119][Para 120]

On issue 3 the panel held that Article 116 of the Labour Law and Article 1 of Prakas No. 442 impose a duty to pay twice a month, in the second and fourth weeks, but do not fix the 20th and the 5th. [Para 121][Para 122][Para 123] The evidence showed two payments each month — 28 November and 8 December, 28 December and 10 January — and the workers advanced no legal basis showing that practice breached the Prakas. [Para 126][Para 128] The claim for payment on the 20th and the 5th was therefore rejected, following Award No. 085/19-Jin Yi Garmen (Cambodia). [Para 125][Para 129]

On issue 4 the panel applied the rule that the party asserting a fact bears the burden of proof, as held in the awards it listed. [Para 133][Para 134] The workers offered no specific evidence that the step caused falls, while the employer had lowered it, fitted anti-slip rubber and offered to add handrails, which the workers declined. [Para 136][Para 137] The arrangement was lawful and reasonable, and the claim was rejected. [Para 138][Para 139]

On issue 5 the panel held the production bonus to be a separate incentive payment, not part of the base wage, and one the Labour Law does not require. [Para 143][Para 145] Following Award No. 24/05-Way Well, it held that an employer may unilaterally set bonus schemes to encourage higher output in less time, and that setting targets breaches no worker right. [Para 140][Para 141][Para 147]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer's duty under Article 66 of the Trade Union Law 2016 not to refuse union representatives access to the enterprise is not absolute: where access to a specific area — here the production building during the midday break — has in fact disrupted production, and union activity remains available elsewhere on the premises, the employer may refuse access to that area alone. [Para 116][Para 119] Separately, the twice-monthly wage obligation is satisfied by two payments falling in the second and fourth weeks; it does not entitle workers to insist on the 20th and the 5th, and an incentive bonus outside the Labour Law may be conditioned on output targets the employer sets. [Para 123][Para 145]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that the employer had offered to fit handrails to the step, an offer the workers did not accept, and treated that willingness as evidence of reasonable management rather than as a ground of decision. [Para 137] It also observed that an employer may pay on Saturdays because no overtime is worked then. [Para 95]
