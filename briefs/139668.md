# Award No. 52/13 — Van Co Industry Striel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 10 April 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mr Ing Sothy (chosen by the employer), Mr Liv Sovanna (chosen by the workers), chair Mr Pen Bun Chea · Doc ID 139668

## 1. Facts
Van Co Industry Striel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a garment factory at Trachak village, Bek Chan commune, Ang Snuol district, Kandal, employed 2,218 workers. [Para 48] The Khmer Workers' Spirit Union at the company, registered under certificate No. 2795 of 4 March 2013 with about 300 members, brought 10 demands; conciliation on 27 February 2013 failed on 8 points, and at the hearing the wage demand was settled by agreement to apply the Ministry's announcement of 21 March 2013 raising the base wage to US$80 a month, leaving 7. [Para 36][Para 49][Para 50][Para 24][Para 28][Para 52][Para 53] Both parties chose a non-binding award. [Para 14]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise the transport and accommodation allowance from US$10 to US$15 a month? [Para 54][Para 101]
2. Whether the employer must raise the attendance bonus from US$10 to US$15 a month? [Para 60][Para 115]
3. Whether the employer must provide one midday meal a day or pay 5,000 riel a day in lieu? [Para 66][Para 123]
4. Whether the employer must pay a skills allowance of US$10, US$15 or US$20 a month according to whether the worker can operate one, two or three needles? [Para 70][Para 129]
5. Whether the employer must pay 5,000 riel a day in meal allowance for work on public holidays, and 300% of the normal wage plus 5,000 riel a day for work on Sundays? [Para 76][Para 135]
6. Whether the employer must pay a position or technical allowance of US$50 a month to workers assisting team leaders? [Para 87][Para 178]
7. Whether the employer must pay a US$20 monthly milk allowance to women workers with children aged between 18 months and 3 years, instead of the US$10 paid? [Para 97][Para 184]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Transport is expensive because most workers live far from the factory and wages are too low for daily living; other neighbouring factories such as "Barry Apple" already pay 2,000 riel a day towards lunch; skilled workers deserve an incentive payment, as do workers who carry the responsibilities of a team leader; and US$10 does not buy enough milk for a child. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 63][Para 67][Para 71][Para 90][Para 98]
**Employer (respondent):** It has no capacity to grant any of the demands; it already pays a US$65 base wage to experienced sewers and US$61 to new ones, a US$10 attendance bonus, US$10 transport allowance and US$10 milk allowance, which meets or exceeds the law and Ministry Notification No. 230, and it applies the law on holiday and Sunday pay. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 64][Para 65][Para 74][Para 75][Para 104][Para 159]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — every remaining demand is refused; the award is non-binding until no party objects in writing within 08 days. [Para 191][Para 192][Para 199][Para 200]

The panel's method was uniform. Each claim was classified as an interest dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍) because no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement or agreement between the parties obliged the employer to pay the amount claimed, and each demand exceeded what the employer already paid or what the law required. [Para 105][Para 119][Para 125][Para 131][Para 174][Para 180][Para 186] It then applied the rule that an interest award replaces the collective agreement for a year and removes other workers' right to strike on that subject, so it may decide such a dispute only where the claimant union is representative or several unions together represent over half the workforce; this union held no certificate of most-representative status, and it followed its earlier awards No. 169/11, No. 02/11 and No. 66/11 in declining jurisdiction. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 108][Para 109][Para 110][Para 112][Para 113]

On the transport allowance the panel found the employer's US$10 already exceeded the US$7 required by point 1 of Notification No. 230 K.B/S.J.N. of 25 July 2012. [Para 103][Para 104] On the attendance bonus it found the US$10 paid satisfied point 2 of the same notification. [Para 117][Para 118] On holiday and Sunday work it distinguished the two: work on a public holiday is not overtime but ordinary work for which Article 164 and Prakas No. 10 of 4 February 1999 give one day's extra wage, and the employer's additional 2,000 riel meal allowance was already better than the law; work on Sunday, by contrast, falls outside the 48-hour week under Article 137 and so is overtime under Article 139, and the employer's 200% wage plus 2,000 riel met the 100% increase the article requires. [Para 138][Para 143][Para 145][Para 146][Para 152][Para 156][Para 164][Para 172][Para 173] On the meal, skills, position and milk allowances it found no legal or contractual basis and no practice to build on. [Para 125][Para 131][Para 180][Para 186]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that exceeds a statutory minimum the employer already satisfies, or that rests on no law, collective agreement or agreement, is an interest dispute; the panel will not arbitrate it unless the claimant union holds most-representative status or the unions together represent more than half the workforce, because an interest award would displace the collective agreement and suspend others' right to strike. Work on a public holiday is ordinary work carrying one extra day's wage, while work on the weekly rest day is overtime attracting the statutory increase. [Para 109][Para 146][Para 172]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that the employer's practice of adding a 2,000 riel meal allowance to holiday and Sunday pay is a benefit above the law, and that no legal provision requires an employer to supply a midday meal. [Para 125][Para 152]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The procedural summary states 8 unresolved points out of 10 demands and then records point 1 as settled at the hearing, leaving 7. [Para 24][Para 28] The attendance bonus is printed as US$10 a month at paras 61 and 118 but as "10 riel" a week at para 62, and the skills demand appears as "US$10" at para 70 and as "US$1000" at para 129; all are reproduced as printed, and the brief follows the figures used in the order. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 118][Para 129] The company's name is printed in two Khmer transliterations in the heading and body ("Van Co Industry Striel", "Van Co In Das Striel"); the face of the award is followed here. [Para 1][Para 112] Para 26 repeats "ឆ្នាំ" twice, and para 24 records the conciliation of 27 February 2013 while para 20 dates the non-conciliation report 6 March 2013. [Para 20][Para 24][Para 26] Individual workers and union officials are recorded only by name and are not reproduced here.
