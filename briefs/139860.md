# Award No. 180/13 — Booy Cher Garment Factory (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 23 September 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, chosen by the employer party; Aa Vireak, chosen by the worker party; Mae Nimmit, chair) · Doc ID 139860

## 1. Facts
Booy Cher Garment Factory (Cambodia) Co Ltd, a garment factory at Pong Tuek village, Baek Chan commune, Ang Snuol district, Kandal, registered as Inv.1637 E/2012, employed about 716 workers. [Para 56][Para 57] Of eight demands one was settled at the hearing, leaving seven. [Para 21][Para 22] The union was not yet registered, holding only the Ministry's receipt for its application of 13 August 2013, and represented 41 of the workers. [Para 25][Para 59][Para 62] The employer dismissed Ms S, a sewer on a two-month probation, on 23 July 2013 while the probation was still running, and the union says the dismissal punished her for organising. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 201]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back Ms S and pay her wages from the dismissal to her return? [Para 63]
2. Whether the employer must give each worker a water bottle? [Para 224]
3. Whether the employer must keep sufficient medicine for workers? [Para 240]
4. Whether the employer must pay a separate skills allowance to workers who can sew on three machines? [Para 258]
5. Whether the employer must apply overtime on a voluntary basis? [Para 277]
6. Whether the employer must pay half the maternity allowance in advance, calculated on the average wage? [Para 304]
7. Whether the employer must deduct the attendance bonus only in proportion to authorised days of leave? [Para 314]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Ms S was dismissed without cause two days after the union election and after the employer learned she was organising; the employer coerced Ms N into overtime and threatened her with resignation; the factory's medicines ran out and sick workers were given only medicated oil. [Para 84][Para 85][Para 91][Para 133][Para 134][Para 156]
**Employer (respondent):** Ms S was still on probation and was reported as slow, careless and disrespectful; overtime is offered on signed volunteer sheets and nobody is forced; medicines are bought on request and recorded daily. [Para 96][Para 105][Para 168][Para 169][Para 248]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must keep sufficient medicine and provide hygienic drinking glasses for workers who have no bottle; the reinstatement, bottle, skills-allowance, overtime, maternity and bonus claims were dismissed. [Para 335][Para 336][Para 337][Para 338][Para 339][Para 340]

Issue 1: Article 68 caps probation at three months, two for specialist workers and one for ordinary workers, and Article 82 removes the notice requirement during probation; earlier awards held that no reason need be given for dismissing a probationer. [Para 195][Para 196][Para 197][Para 198] Ms S's probation ran from 27 May to 26 July 2013 and she was dismissed on 23 July, inside it, so no reason was required. [Para 201][Para 202] The discrimination allegation failed: the alleged admission by an administrator was raised only in the union's written summary, not at the hearing, no other evidence supported it, and the three elected union leaders kept working normally. [Para 216][Para 217][Para 218][Para 222][Para 223]

Issue 2: Prakas No. 054 of 10 February 2000 requires adequate clean drinking water and, at Article 4, clean glasses or other hygienic means for workers to drink from — not a personal bottle for each worker. [Para 224][Para 225][Para 226][Para 233] Because the employer had distributed bottles only to groups 1 to 12 of 30, the panel ordered it to provide glasses or another hygienic means for those still without. [Para 230][Para 237][Para 238][Para 239]

Issue 3: Prakas No. 330 of 6 December 2000 requires an enterprise with 50 or more workers to maintain an infirmary stocked as its annex prescribes, and the panel held the employer bears the burden of proving sufficiency. [Para 240][Para 242][Para 243][Para 249] The purchase requests and invoices covered May and July and the daily records only June to August, which did not establish that medicines were adequate in May. [Para 250][Para 251][Para 252] The employer was ordered to stock medicines to the annex. [Para 257]

Issue 4: nothing in the law, a collective agreement, the internal rules or past practice requires a skills allowance beyond what is paid; the employer already pays base wages of USD 80, 83, 86 and 90 by skill, above the legal minimum, so the claim was an interest dispute. [Para 258][Para 260][Para 261][Para 262] The union represented 41 of 716 workers and was not the most representative, so the panel declined to arbitrate it. [Para 274][Para 275][Para 276]

Issue 5: Article 4 of Prakas No. 080 of 1 March 1999 forbids compelling or disciplining workers who refuse overtime, and the panel found the employer's practice — a volunteer sheet circulated at about 2:30 or 3 p.m. which only volunteers sign — complied. [Para 277][Para 281][Para 282][Para 283] The single incident involving Ms N was not shown to be coercion: the warning she received also concerned poor work, she worked overtime as usual afterwards, and no disciplinary measure followed. [Para 294][Para 296][Para 298][Para 303]

Issues 6 and 7: the maternity claim concerned a future event, and the panel exists to decide disputes that have arisen, not hypothetical ones, so it was refused. [Para 307][Para 311][Para 312] The attendance-bonus claim failed on the evidence: the July 2013 payroll showed a 26-day month, USD 10.58 paid for 25 days worked and USD 9.52 for 22.5 days, which is the pro-rata deduction the union was asking for. [Para 324][Para 326][Para 327][Para 329][Para 332]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker dismissed within a lawful probation period is not owed a reason, and an anti-union dismissal will not be found on an allegation that first appears in the union's written summary and is contradicted by the continued employment of the other elected leaders. [Para 198][Para 202][Para 216][Para 222] Prakas No. 054 requires hygienic drinking vessels, not an individual bottle for every worker, so an employer that supplies clean glasses discharges the duty. [Para 226][Para 233] Where the employer must prove it stocked medicines to the Prakas No. 330 annex, purchase requests and invoices that do not cover the disputed month fall short. [Para 249][Para 252] The panel will not decide a claim that depends on an event that has not yet occurred. [Para 307][Para 311]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's recommendation that the employer set up a monitoring mechanism so that no supervisor pressures workers who decline overtime, and that workers report any pressure, is guidance beyond the order dismissing the claim. [Para 301][Para 302]
