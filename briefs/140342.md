# Award No. 136/10 — Va Yang Garment (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 27 December 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Ven Pov, worker-appointed; Kong Phalla, presiding) · Doc ID 140342

## 1. Facts
Va Yang Garment (Cambodia) (វ៉ា យ៉ាង ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា)), a factory of about 432 workers at Trapeang Chhouk, Sen Sok, Phnom Penh, faced seven demands from the Workers' Rights Union Federation (សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព សិទ្ធិ ប្រយោជន៍ កម្មករ) and its Va Yang factory union. [Para 39][Para 2] Conciliation settled three of the seven; four remained, and the union withdrew issue 1 on work clothing at the hearing, leaving three. [Para 16][Para 20] Neither union in the plant held most-representative status. [Para 42]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether a further USD 1 seniority bonus must be paid from the fifth year of service and each year thereafter? [Para 43]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the company must arrange special health examinations for all workers every six months or every year at its own cost? [Para 51]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the company must distribute a share of its profits to workers equal to each worker's final wage for the year? [Para 60]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — Workers' Rights Union Federation and its Va Yang factory union):** the longer a worker serves the more skilled she becomes, so a USD 1 annual increment above the fifth year was owed beyond the USD 5 ceiling; special health checks were needed to guard against cloth poisoning, since workers had fainted in 2006 and 2007, and the employer should bear the cost; and because the law lists a share of profits as part of wages, the company should distribute one. [Para 43][Para 51][Para 60]
**Employer (respondent — Va Yang Garment (Cambodia), represented by its administrative head):** it applied Notification No. 017, which stops the seniority bonus at USD 5, and it could not pay more; it had no capacity to fund periodic special examinations, had joined the National Social Security Fund, which bears workplace-accident costs, and had never received such a request; and as a small subcontracting factory it had no capacity to share profits. [Para 44][Para 55][Para 56][Para 61]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three remaining demands were refused, each because the panel classified it as an interest dispute and declined to consider it. [Para 108][Para 109][Para 110]

On issue 2 the panel set out point 3 of Notification No. 745 of 23 October 2006, which preserves points 3 to 6 of Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000, and then point 5 of Notification No. 017 itself: USD 2 a month after one year, USD 3 after two, USD 4 after three and USD 5 after four. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 68] It read the schedule as capped at USD 5 from the fourth year and as saying nothing about any further increment after the fifth, so the claim for an extra dollar a year rested on no instrument at all. [Para 70][Para 71] That made the demand a claim for a benefit beyond the law, an interest dispute. [Para 73][Para 74]

On issue 3 the panel examined Article 247 of the Labour Law, which requires the ministry responsible for labour to issue a prakas fixing the intervals for pre-employment, periodic and special health examinations, and which puts the cost of those examinations on the employer. [Para 89][Para 91][Para 92] It found that no prakas had in fact fixed any interval for special examinations. [Para 93] The panel accepted that when an examination takes place the employer pays, but since neither law nor agreement obliged the company to arrange one every six months or every year, the demand was again one of interests. [Para 97][Para 98][Para 99][Para 100]

On issue 4 the panel quoted Article 103, which lists a share in profits among the components of wages, and applied its settled reading that the article is a definition of wages and not a duty to distribute profits at year end. [Para 103][Para 104] It noted that no law, collective agreement, contract or internal rule required the company to do so, and that both sides accepted this. [Para 62][Para 105]

Each of the three was then disposed of on the same jurisdictional ground. Because Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 makes an award in an interest dispute stand as a collective agreement for a year and bind every worker in the factory, only a union certified as most representative under Article 277 may bring such a case; this union had no certificate. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 82][Para 87][Para 88] The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification. [Para 111]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the law fixes a benefit ceiling — a capped seniority bonus, or a health-examination duty left to a prakas that has never fixed an interval — a demand to go beyond it is an interest dispute, which the Council will not hear from a union without most-representative certification, because its award would bind every worker as a collective agreement for a year. [Para 70][Para 93][Para 82]

**Obiter dicta:** Article 103 defines the components of wages, including a share of profits, without obliging any employer to distribute profits; and the employer bears the cost of a special health examination whenever one is actually carried out. [Para 104][Para 97]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The membership figures cannot all be true: the union is recorded as having about 147 members, then as about 600 members "among about 147 workers in the factory", while the workforce is elsewhere given as 432. [Para 41][Para 84][Para 39] The award never reconciles these and the reasoning rests on the union being a minority.

The employer's name appears in at least four forms — វ៉ា យ៉ាង ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) in the party block, វា យ៉ាង in the evidence list, វ៉ា យ៉ាង ហ្គុ មេន in the findings, and the shortened វ៉ា យ៉ាង throughout. [Para 1][Para 28][Para 39]

The jurisdiction paragraph is corrupted, reading "ជំពូក ទី ១២ ផ្នែក ៤ ទៅ ទូទាំ រ(ខ)" where the standard formula is Part 2(b). [Para 10] The conciliation report is dated 6 December 2010 but recorded as reaching the secretariat on 8 December. [Para 12] The signature block carries scan debris after the presiding arbitrator's name. [Para 112]
