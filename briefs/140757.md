# Award No. 04/05 — Eternity Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 7 February 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Hem H. Naryth, Tuon Siphann, Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 140757

## 1. Facts
Eternity Garment Co., Ltd employs 600 workers at building 646, National Road 2, Chak Angre Krom commune, Mean Chey district, Phnom Penh [Para 16]. The Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers Democratic Union (C.CAWDU) complained to the Mean Chey labour inspector in December 2004, and conciliation on 31 December 2004 resolved two of the five issues raised, leaving three for the Council on a non-conciliation report dated 5 January 2005 [Para 17][Para 18][Para 19]. Of those three, the pay-slip demand was settled at the Council hearing and the demand about the 1–2 November 2004 blackout was withdrawn by letter dated 1 February 2005, so only the third issue was decided [Para 22][Para 28][Para 29]. That issue concerned the company's 16 security guards — 12 men and 4 women, of whom 10 lodged the complaint — who worked two twelve-hour shifts, 7:00 a.m. to 7:00 p.m. and 7:00 p.m. to 7:00 a.m., and had never received annual leave [Para 30][Para 32][Para 38].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 3) Whether the security guards are entitled to paid annual leave and to the seniority bonus on the same basis as the company's other employees? [Para 6][Para 29]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** C.CAWDU, for the 10 guards under the head of security, argued that the guards had never been granted annual leave or a seniority bonus and asked that both be given [Para 39]. **Employer (respondent):** Eternity Garment Co., Ltd argued that at recruitment the guards were told they would work under the company's direction for 12 hours a day without annual leave or any benefit other than wages, and that this term governed the relationship [Para 63].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to arrange and provide annual leave to the 10 security guards who claimed it, counting from the start of their employment, and to pay them seniority bonuses with seniority counted from 18 August 2000 or, for later hires, from their start date [Para 73][Para 74].
The panel first asked whether the guards were workers at all. Reading Articles 2 and 3 of the Labour Law together, it found they were: the company recruited them, fixed their wages and their 12-hour hours, approved their leave, punished them and alone had the right to dismiss them, and 10 of the 16 worked for the company and nowhere else [Para 41][Para 46][Para 50][Para 51]. No written contract was signed, but the panel held that an oral contract bound the parties under Article 65, which allows a labour contract to be written or verbal and formed according to local custom [Para 53][Para 54][Para 61].
On annual leave, Article 166 entitles every worker to one and a half working days of paid leave per month of continuous service [Para 61]. The company's term withholding that leave gives less than the law, so Article 13 abrogates it as null and void [Para 65][Para 66]. The panel held that Article 167(4) did not assist the employer, because it applies where leave is deferred by agreement, whereas here the employer simply refused it [Para 66][Para 67]. It followed MS International Case 27/04 and ordered compensation for leave from the date of employment [Para 68].
On the seniority bonus, Article 5 of Notification 017 of 18 July 2000 grants a monthly bonus of US$2 after one year rising to US$5 after four years, calculated from 1 August 2000, in textile, garment and footwear enterprises [Para 69][Para 70]. The panel found the guards covered on three grounds: they fall under the Labour Law, they work in a garment factory, and beyond patrolling they count trousers and clothes like production workers [Para 70].
Finally, the panel observed that Articles 137 to 148 require eight hours a day, 48 hours a week and six days a week, and that the company's 12-hour, seven-day schedule exceeds them, but it declined to decide that question because neither party had raised it, merely noting that the employer should reschedule [Para 71][Para 72][Para 73].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A security guard recruited, paid, rostered, disciplined and dismissible by a garment factory works under that factory's direction and management, and so is a worker under Articles 2 and 3 of the Labour Law; an oral term withholding statutory annual leave is void under Article 13, and because such a guard works inside the garment factory and performs production tasks, the seniority bonus under Notification 017 extends to him or her [Para 51][Para 66][Para 70].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that the guards' 12-hour, seven-day roster breaches Articles 137 to 148 and should be rescheduled was expressly not decided, the issue not having been raised by either party [Para 72][Para 73].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The complaint date is printed as both "December 06 2004" and "December 303 2004" in the same paragraph [Para 17]. The first hearing is given as 18 January 2005 at 8:00 a.m. in one place and at 14h00 in another [Para 12][Para 21]. The award says both "at least 10 of the 16" guards worked only for the company and refers later to "the 10 security guards", without stating which six were excluded [Para 49][Para 73]. Article 166 is quoted with a 21-day monthly average rule that belongs to a different provision [Para 63]. OCR systematically compresses words ("thecurity", "themployer"), and the panel's names are split across numbered lines with the "chosen by" labels misaligned [Para 9][Para 11].
