# Award No. 70/09 — G.W. Enterprise Limited (Arbitration Council, 9 July 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ouk Ry (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Pen Bunchhea (chair) · Doc ID 140394

## 1. Facts
G.W. Enterprise Limited, formerly G.T. Ltd, has traded at Damnak Thom village, Meanchey district, Phnom Penh since 1998 under the same management and employs about 700 workers [Para 2][Para 18][Para 20]. The claimant is the Local Union of the Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU), registered with the Ministry of Labour on 7 April 2009 [Para 3][Para 51]. Of ten demands advanced in May 2009, six were settled at conciliation and four were referred to the Council on 2 June 2009: medical check fees, advance payment of maternity wages, a meal allowance for Sunday and holiday work, and an increase in the seniority bonus [Para 11][Para 12][Para 3]. A first hearing was adjourned at the employer's request because it was arranging wage payments, and the hearing was held on 16 June 2009 [Para 13][Para 14].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must reimburse the 10,100 or 12,000 riel medical check fees paid by new and veteran workers? [Para 20]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay the maternity payment equal to 50% of 90 days' wages before the worker commences maternity leave? [Para 29]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay a 4,000 riel meal allowance for work on Sundays and public holidays? [Para 33]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must increase by US$ 1 the seniority bonus of workers with more than five years' service? [Para 39]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer has never reimbursed medical checks, so both new and veteran workers should be repaid what they spent [Para 27]. Female workers need the whole 50% of 90 days' wages before leave begins, to meet childbirth costs and because their homes are far from the factory; the Labour Law requires advance payment [Para 31]. Work on Sundays and holidays is overtime and merits a 4,000 riel meal allowance, and workers with five to ten years' service deserve a US$ 1 increase because the Labour Law scales the bonus by seniority [Para 39][Para 42].
**Employer (respondent):** applicants take a written test, work three to seven days, then bear their own medical check although the job advertisement requires a fitness certificate [Para 21][Para 22]. It cannot afford reimbursement, though it will comply if the law so requires [Para 24][Para 26]. Maternity wages are paid at 50% monthly, on the 10th, to workers with a year's service, and paying 90 days in advance is unaffordable [Para 29][Para 32]. It already pays double wages for Sunday and holiday work and currently has no such work because the global financial crisis cut its buyers [Para 34][Para 36]. Its US$ 5 bonus matches Notification No. 017, whose ceiling is US$ 5 [Para 7][Para 44].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to reimburse the medical fees of Local Union members whose claims fall within the three-year limitation period, counted from the date the contract was signed or from the date the expense was incurred, and to pay the maternity payment of 50% of 90 days' wages before the leave begins; the meal allowance and seniority-bonus demands were declined [Para 91]. The award is non-binding and becomes binding eight days after notification [Para 91].

**Standing.** Under Article 268 of the Labour Law and clause 5 of Prakas No. 305, a professional organisation enjoys the rights recognised by law once registered; the Local Union of KYFTU was registered on 7 April 2009, so the Council would decide the demands made for its members only [Para 46][Para 48][Para 51].

**Issue 1 — medical fees.** Article 247(a) and (c) of the Labour Law and clause 2(d) of Inter-ministerial Prakas No. 1191 of 21 November 2006, which fixes the fee at 12,000 riel per Cambodian worker, place the cost of pre-employment medical checks on the employer, consistently with Awards 02/03, 19/04, 59/05, 05/06, 98/07 and 52/08 [Para 54][Para 55]. Because the check is a condition of employment, the claim arises from the contract and Article 120 gives it a three-year limitation; following Award 93/07, the period runs from signature or commencement where the check preceded work, and otherwise from the date the fee was incurred [Para 57][Para 58][Para 61]. Evidence the union filed a day late was excluded [Para 27][Para 28].
**Issue 2 — maternity payment.** Article 183 entitles a woman on maternity leave to half her wage and perquisites, but sets no payment date; Article 115(3) forbids payment on a day off and requires it a day earlier [Para 63][Para 65]. Following Awards 57/06 and 97/06, the panel held the 50% payment must be made before the leave commences, not monthly during it [Para 68][Para 69].
**Issues 3 and 4 — declined.** The meal allowance was a claim about work that does not presently exist, and the Council does not deal with problems that have not arisen (Awards 10/03, 14/06, 141/08) [Para 72][Para 73][Para 75]. The seniority increase went beyond Notification No. 017 and No. 745, which stop at US$ 5 for four years, making it an interests dispute; the Council declines interests disputes from a union without most-representative status (Awards 101/08, 03/09) [Para 83][Para 85][Para 90].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The cost of a worker's pre-employment medical check is the employer's, and the three-year limitation on the claim runs from signature of the contract or the date the expense was incurred; maternity pay of half of 90 days' wages must be handed over before the leave begins, since Article 115 forbids payment on a day off [Para 63][Para 65][Para 61].

**Obiter dicta:** the employer's inability to afford the payments does not displace an obligation the Labour Law and Prakas No. 1191 impose, and demands going beyond the law's ceiling become interests disputes that a union without most-representative status cannot bring [Para 26][Para 85].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
This is an unofficial English translation of the Khmer original, and the evidence section was omitted from it altogether, so the documents the panel weighed cannot be checked [Para 17]. The employer's name survives only as the initials G.W., formerly G.T., so its full registered name is not recoverable from this file [Para 2][Para 18]. The text drops the leading letter of many words throughout — "themployer", "asstipulated", "foregistration" — which obscures quoted statutory language [Para 25][Para 46]. Issue 1 states the check fee as either 10,100 or 12,000 riel in the same sentence, while the Prakas is applied at 12,000 riel [Para 20][Para 55]. Passages quoting Prakas No. 305 are duplicated and broken across three separate sentences [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51]. The seniority table in the facts is scrambled and two of the ten original demands are numbered inconsistently in the procedural summary [Para 44][Para 11].
