# Award No. 80/13 — 8 Star Sportwear Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 23 May 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer, An Nan for the workers, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 139685

## 1. Facts
8 Star Sportwear is a garment manufacturer at Steung Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, with 961 workers. Its local union of the Cambodian Workers of Economic Union Federation (CWEF) holds registration certificate No. 2422 dated 12 March 2012 but no Most Representative Status. Of five demands raised in March 2013, two were settled in conciliation, leaving three: a US$3 increase to the accommodation and transport allowance (from US$7 to US$10), a 2,000 riel daily payment in lieu of lunch, and a 2,000 riel meal allowance for workers who volunteer to work on Sundays. The company works Monday to Saturday, 7:00–11:00 a.m. and 12:00–4:00 p.m., and Sunday work was still occurring in the weeks before the hearing. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 25][Para 35][Para 41]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must add US$3 to the accommodation and transportation allowance, raising it to US$10 per month? [Para 26]
2. Whether the employer must pay 2,000 riel per day in lieu of lunch to each worker? [Para 31]
3. Whether the employer must pay a 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance to workers who volunteer to work on Sundays? [Para 35]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Neighbouring factories pay US$10, US$12 and US$15 for accommodation and transport and provide free lunch or a daily lunch allowance; wages are too low to cover daily expenses, so matching those benefits would keep workers motivated. Sunday work exceeds 48 hours a week and is therefore overtime, entitling them to the same 2,000 riel meal allowance paid for weekday overtime. [Para 27][Para 33][Para 38]
**Employer (respondent):** The US$7 allowance already complies with Ministry of Labour notification, which sets no minimum and cannot require parity with better-off factories; lunch is not provided and is not owed; Sunday work is weekly-rest work paid at 200 per cent, a different legal construction from overtime, and the company will stop scheduling Sunday work rather than pay a meal allowance for it. [Para 28][Para 29][Para 34][Para 39][Para 40]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer is ordered to pay a 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance per day to workers who volunteer to work on Sundays (Part I, rights dispute, binding); the panel declined to consider the US$3 allowance increase and the 2,000 riel lunch payment (Part II, interests disputes, binding eight days after notification absent written opposition to the Minister of Labour). [Para 76][Para 77][Para 78]

**Classification.** The panel set out the dividing line from Article 312 paragraph 2 of the Labour Law — the Council decides by law disputes over the interpretation and enforcement of laws, regulations or collective agreements, and in equity all others — together with Clause 43 of Prakas No. 099, under which an award settling an interests dispute replaces a collective agreement for one year. Following Awards 05/11-M&V1, 13/11-Gold Kamvimex, 14/11-GHG, 31/11-Quint Major and 62/11-Ocean Garment, rights disputes concern entitlements already stated in law, an agreement or a collective agreement and are resolved by law; interests disputes concern demands stated in none of these and are resolved in equity. [Para 46][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50]

**Issue 1 — accommodation and transport.** Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 requires a US$7 accommodation and transport allowance, which the employer pays, so it has discharged that obligation; nothing in the Labour Law, an agreement, a collective agreement or the internal rules requires a further US$3. The demand was therefore an interests dispute. Because an interests award substitutes for a collective agreement and binds all workers, including non-members, and removes the right to strike on the matters it covers, only a union with MRS — or one representing more than half the workforce — may bring such a case; following 81/04-Evergreen, 98/04-Great Union, 02/11-Pou Yuen, 66/11-In Han Sung and 169/11-Fortune Teo, the panel declined to consider it because this union holds no MRS certificate. [Para 51][Para 53][Para 54][Para 57][Para 58][Para 59]

**Issue 2 — lunch payment.** No provision of the Labour Law, any agreement, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice obliges the employer to provide lunch or 2,000 riel per day for it, so the demand was likewise an interests dispute and was declined on the same MRS ground. [Para 60][Para 61]

**Issue 3 — Sunday meal allowance.** Here the panel found a rights dispute, because Point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 provides that workers who volunteer to work overtime at the employer's request receive 2,000 riel per day for meals or a free meal. Article 137 caps work at eight hours a day and 48 hours a week, and the company already works 48 hours Monday to Saturday; following 114/08-Whitex and 52/12-Star Sportwear, Sunday work therefore exceeds regular hours and is overtime, whatever the employer calls it. The allowance is payable regardless of the number of overtime hours worked. The employer conceded Sunday work continued in the weeks before the hearing and could not guarantee it would stop, and did not deny that the cutting section worked on Sunday, so the practice was established and the obligation breached. [Para 65][Para 69][Para 71][Para 72][Para 74]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Work performed on Sunday by workers already employed 48 hours from Monday to Saturday is overtime, so the 2,000 riel daily meal allowance in Point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 is owed to volunteers regardless of hours worked; and a union without Most Representative Status, or without more than half the workforce as members, cannot bring an interests dispute, because an interests award replaces the collective agreement and binds non-members. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 58]

**Obiter dicta:** The observations that the employer would stop scheduling Sunday work rather than pay the allowance, and that the panel could not rely on any future guarantee either way, did not affect the order, which rests on the practice actually found. [Para 42][Para 44]
