# Award No. 081/19 — Khmer Electricity Call Power (Arbitration Council, 16 September 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mr S Vouch Hun for the employer party, Mr An Non for the worker party, Mr R Saray chair) · Doc ID 139160

## 1. Facts
Khmer Electricity Call Power generates electricity at Street 217, Sangkat Veal Vong, Khan 7 Makara, Phnom Penh, and employed 55 workers. [Para 1][Para 32][Para 33] The workers, represented by their collective-bargaining representatives, filed five demands to improve working conditions: correct public-holiday leave, 130% pay for night shifts, use of annual leave at their convenience, an end to discrimination against union leaders, and termination with severance for Mr C, absent on sick leave for about ten months with hypertension. [Para 2][Para 3][Para 4][Para 5][Para 6][Para 11] The workers withdrew demand 4 at the hearing, leaving issues 1, 2, 3 and 5. [Para 19][Para 20] The employer did not appear; its accountant, Mr M, attended but stated he held no written authority to represent it. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 62]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the workers are entitled to rest on public holidays, with wages, without having to apply for permission and without having their wages deducted? [Para 65]
2. Whether workers performing the 11 p.m. to 7 a.m. shift are entitled to 130% of the daytime wage for the whole shift? [Para 81]
3. Whether workers are entitled to use their annual leave at a time of their own convenience? [Para 91]
4. Whether the employer is obliged to terminate the contract of Mr C and pay severance? [Para 106]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** before 2018 the employer paid 100% of wages on top of the normal wage to operators who worked public holidays, but from 2018 it threatened wage deductions against those who applied for holiday leave, so workers no longer dared to rest. [Para 36][Para 38][Para 39] Machine operators work three rotating shifts including 11 p.m. to 7 a.m., about 20 workers doing eight night shifts per month, and are paid the same as day workers despite Article 144 requiring 130%. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 44][Para 45] Since 2018 the employer has refused annual leave at times of the workers' choosing and has deducted wages when leave was used, as in the three days deducted from Mr N while he was treated for illness in Thailand. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 51] Mr C has been absent about ten months and is unlikely to return, so the contract should be terminated with statutory severance. [Para 55][Para 56]
**Employer (respondent):** absent, and therefore advanced no position before the panel; its accountant expressly disclaimed authority to represent it. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 63]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 1 dismissed, issue 2 dismissed, issue 5 dismissed, and on issue 3 the employer was ordered to review and permit the 20 workers to use their annual leave when they have personal business, in accordance with the conditions of the company's operations and the spirit of the Labour Law. [Para 127][Para 128][Para 129][Para 130]

Threshold: applying Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, and following Awards No. 53/04, 63/04, 148/07 and 161/15, the panel held it could proceed where a properly summoned party fails to appear without good reason; since Mr M held no written authority, the employer was treated as absent and the case decided on the workers' evidence. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 63][Para 64]

On issue 1, Article 164 and Article 4 of Prakas No. 10 SKBY of 4 February 1999 give a worker who works on a public holiday a further sum equal to a normal day's wage, i.e. 200% in total, and the panel found the employer's double-payment practice consistent with that. [Para 65][Para 67][Para 69][Para 73][Para 74] Requiring an application in advance was a permissible administrative arrangement, but requiring it in order to deduct wages from workers who did not come in on a holiday was not. [Para 75][Para 76] Because no specific worker was shown to have suffered such a deduction, the panel declined to consider the demand. [Para 77][Para 78][Para 79]

On issue 2, Article 144 of the amended Labour Law defines night work as at least eleven consecutive hours including 22:00 to 05:00, and night work is paid at 130% of the daytime wage. [Para 81][Para 82][Para 84][Para 85] The workers sought 130% for the entire 11 p.m.–7 a.m. shift, but produced no evidence of any entitlement beyond the statutory night hours, so the demand had no legal basis and was dismissed. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 89][Para 90]

On issue 3, Article 170 requires annual leave to be granted at the Khmer New Year unless otherwise agreed, and allows an employer to fix the remainder where leave exceeds fifteen days. [Para 91][Para 93][Para 94] Following Awards No. 21/05, 81/07, 123/09, 53/04, 45/05, 181/11 and 03/13, the panel held the right is real but not absolute: the employer may weigh the effect on its operations, but it may not impose unpaid leave or schedule leave for periods when it has no work, and it produced no internal rule or contrary evidence. [Para 95][Para 98][Para 99][Para 103][Para 104][Para 105]

On issue 5, Article 71(3) makes absence on duly certified illness a suspension of the contract, capped at six months and extendable until a replacement is found, and Article 72(1) suspends the principal obligations. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 108][Para 109] Termination on expiry of that period is a right of the employer, not an obligation, and under Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code no party can be forced into a contract it has not agreed to; the panel therefore could not order termination or severance. [Para 111][Para 112][Para 113][Para 121][Para 123][Para 126]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Statutory entitlements are paid for the hours the law defines, not for the shift as a whole: night premium is owed at 130% for work between 22:00 and 05:00 only, and a claim for the entire 11 p.m.–7 a.m. shift fails for want of legal basis. Conversely, an employer may require advance notice of public-holiday absence but may not attach a wage deduction to it, and may schedule annual leave by reference to operational needs only where a genuine agreement or the fifteen-day rule under Article 170 allows it. A contract suspended by certified illness cannot be converted into a termination at the worker's demand, since termination remains the employer's right. [Para 76][Para 84][Para 86][Para 99][Para 111][Para 123]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that Article 71(3) is intended to protect the worker's job security during sick leave, and that the employer's failure to produce an internal rule counted against it, were general observations rather than grounds of decision. [Para 103][Para 110]
