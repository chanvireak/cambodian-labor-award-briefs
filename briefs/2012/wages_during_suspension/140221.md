# Order No. 227/12 — Global Footwear International Co (Arbitration Council, 23 November 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mr Hem Hunarith for the employer party, Mr An Nan for the worker party, Mr Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 140221

## 1. Facts
The Federation of Unions Promoting Workers' Rights brought a collective dispute at Global Footwear International, a factory at Ang Snuol, Kandal, over four demands: a base wage of USD 70 a month, an extra USD 5 on the existing attendance premium, an extra USD 5 for transport or housing, and no deduction of the daily wage or other bonuses during a strike. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 7][Para 10] Conciliation at the Kandal provincial labour department on 13 November 2012 settled none of the four, and the report No. 849 of 14 November 2012 reached the Council the next day; the workers were already on strike. [Para 17][Para 18][Para 19] By Order No. 031/12 of 15 November 2012 the panel required the strike to be suspended at once and all strikers to return to their shifts from the morning of 16 November 2012. [Para 20] The workers then reported that the company would not let them clock in, and they carried on striking. [Para 21][Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the panel should continue the arbitration when the workers keep striking during the proceedings? [Para 57][Para 66]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** they maintained the strike even though the employer's position was to let them return to work, and they said so at the meeting of 22 November 2012. [Para 23][Para 24]
**Employer (respondent):** through counsel it filed an authority letter of 15 November 2012, a request that the Council order the workers to suspend the strike, and a letter of 16 November 2012 complaining that the Council's order had not been complied with; it also produced wage and payroll records. [Para 25][Para 27][Para 32]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel decided not to continue the arbitration in case No. 227/12, leaving the four demands undecided. [Para 67][Para 68]

Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 requires the parties to suspend every dispute measure, including a strike or lockout, while the panel is seized of the matter, and to attend all meetings it convenes. [Para 57][Para 58] The panel had already construed that article, in earlier orders, as barring it from proceeding whenever the worker party is on strike, and it applied that line to Order No. 04/04-IMSI of 29 January 2004, the second order of 22 April 2004 in case 18/04 (the Kambodiana, Raffles Le Royal, Raffles Grand d'Angkor, Sunway, InterContinental and Sofitel Angkor hotels) at points 9 and 11, the second order of 24 December 2005 in case 77/06 at point 4, and case 110/11 (Simano). [Para 59][Para 60][Para 62][Para 63][Para 64][Para 65]

The panel found that the workers had kept striking in disregard of Order No. 031/12 and had shown no genuine intention of submitting the dispute to arbitration, so it declined to continue. [Para 66][Para 67] No hearing was held, and the panel therefore made no findings of fact on the four demands, taking into account only the company's documents and the Ministry file; the worker party filed nothing. [Para 52][Para 56]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A panel will not hear a collective dispute while the worker party is on strike contrary to an order suspending the strike under Article 20 of Prakas No. 099, and will terminate the arbitration instead; the continuing strike is treated as showing that the party does not intend to have the dispute arbitrated, so the demands are left undecided rather than dismissed on their merits, and the Council keeps the power to end proceedings where a party breaches its order. [Para 57][Para 63][Para 66][Para 67]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's statement that the parties must attend every meeting it convenes, and its quotation of the earlier ruling that the Council may take up the same dispute again once the strike ends, are general procedural observations and do not qualify the termination. [Para 58][Para 61]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The company's offer on the third demand is printed as "៣ ឆ្នាំ ដុល្លារ" — "3 years dollars" — so the sum offered for transport or housing cannot be stated with confidence; only the USD 3 figure elsewhere in the demand summary is reliable. [Para 8]
- The arbitrator appointed by the employer appears as "ហែម ហូណារិទ្ធ" on the face of the order and as "ហែម ហួ ណារិទ្ធ" in the signature block; the variant is not reconcilable from the text. [Para 1][Para 68]
- The evidence list repeats items 3 and 4 in garbled duplicate lines, and the conciliation minute date is cut to "ឆ្នាំ ៥-" — the year is lost. [Para 27][Para 28]
- The employer's name is printed in at least three different Khmer spellings across the order and in Latin script as Global Footwear. [Para 1][Para 26][Para 68]
- Stray Latin fragments ("The", "D", "Chan", "I VOA /ទំ") interrupt the quotes at the start of the reasons, and one counsel's name is cut to a bare surname initial. [Para 27][Para 60][Para 66]
- Individual workers named in the evidence schedule are recorded here by role only, and one worker's name is printed with two spellings. [Para 33][Para 35]
