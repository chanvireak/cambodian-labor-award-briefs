# Award No. 43/12 — Goldfame Enterprise Britex (Int'l) Nit Thar Limited (Arbitration Council, 30 April 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Nhan Somonnith, chair) · Doc ID 139982

## 1. Facts
Goldfame Enterprise Britex (Int'l) Nit Thar Limited operates a garment factory in Kandal Province with 5,300 workers. The Democratic Union of Garment Workers brought a collective dispute covering two claims: the dismissal of union activist Khveak Thum, a checker in the mobile group, and the employer's alleged breach of the 2010 collective agreement. The employer suspended Khveak Thum on 12 October 2011 and dismissed her for alleged serious misconduct — deliberately falsifying her time card — relying on a letter of agreement from the Kandal Provincial Department of Labour and Vocational Training. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 144][Para 155][Para 218]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the suspension and dismissal of Khveak Thum were lawful, and whether she should be reinstated with back wages from the date of suspension to the date of reinstatement? [Para 142][Para 143]
2. Whether the employer breached the 2010 collective agreement, specifically point 2(1) of Article 1 (priority re-employment of former workers) and point 2(7) (seniority bonus for workers with more than five years' service). [Para 142][Para 291][Para 300]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The union argued that the suspension and dismissal violated the Labour Law and were motivated by anti-union discrimination against an activist. It contended that the Department of Labour's letter was itself unlawful and therefore invalid and unenforceable. It sought reinstatement and back pay from the date of suspension. [Para 145][Para 146][Para 143]
**Employer (respondent):** The employer maintained that the dismissal rested on serious misconduct — intentional time-card fraud — and pointed to the provincial Department of Labour's letter of agreement as validation. It also argued that the 90 dismissed workers had signed mutual agreements terminating their fixed-duration contracts freely, and that its seniority-bonus practice conformed to the collective agreement. [Para 144][Para 148][Para 279][Para 312]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** The panel ordered the employer to reinstate Khveak Thum and to pay her back wages of US$ 62.50 per month (US$ 61 base wage plus US$ 1.50 seniority bonus) from her last pay day before the unlawful dismissal until the date of reinstatement, plus overtime pay for hours actually worked but not yet paid. It ordered the employer to review its application of the priority-re-employment clause and, when recruiting for the relevant sections, to notify the union, give former workers a reasonable opportunity to respond, and give priority to eligible former workers. It rejected the seniority-bonus claim. [Para 314][Para 315][Para 316][Para 317]

The panel first examined whether the Department of Labour had authority to issue the letter approving the dismissal. Reading Articles 343–348 of the Labour Law, it found that labour inspectors and provincial departments may conciliate and record failed conciliation, but have no power to authorise a dismissal or issue a legally binding decision. A letter issued beyond that authority is void, so the panel did not need to assess the letter's procedural regularity. [Para 184][Para 185][Para 186][Para 187]

Because the dispute had not been conciliated and was referred as a collective dispute, the panel held it had full authority to decide whether the worker's conduct amounted to serious misconduct. [Para 188][Para 189]

On the merits, Article 83 of the Labour Law lists theft, embezzlement and breach of trust as serious misconduct, but the employer used the word "cheating" (kengbanlom), which is not one of the three statutory terms; the panel nonetheless read the allegation as one of breach of trust. [Para 190][Para 193][Para 194][Para 195]

The employer alleged that Khveak Thum deliberately falsified her time card. The panel found that the employer bears the burden of proving intent. The employer conceded that she did not punch the card herself, produced no evidence that she knew the card had been punched at 21:00, and had no clear rule on how many days' notice a worker must give for overtime. She had asked her section chief for permission to leave at 20:00 to study Korean; permission was refused and the chief neither signed her card nor explained the overtime procedure. Following the factory's practice of punching through group representatives, she left her card with her group representative. She did not benefit from the erroneous punch because her October 2011 wages had not yet been paid. The panel therefore found no serious misconduct, no embezzlement or breach of trust, and no intent to cheat the company. [Para 206][Para 207][Para 209][Para 210][Para 211][Para 212][Para 213][Para 218][Para 219][Para 220][Para 222]

Having found no serious misconduct, the panel held it unnecessary to decide the union's separate allegation of anti-union discrimination. [Para 223]

On point 2(1), the panel interpreted the clause as requiring the employer to give former workers priority over new recruits when it needs additional labour in the sections where those workers were employed, but not to rehire every former worker regardless of section requirements; where eligible former workers exceed demand, the employer must select among them in accordance with the Labour Law. [Para 282][Para 283][Para 284][Para 285]

On point 2(7), the panel read "from the fifth year onward" to cover all workers with more than five years' service, including those with five, six, seven or eight years. It found that the employer's practice of paying US$ 1 per month in the first year and US$ 5 for workers with over five years' service matched Article 3(c) of the collective agreement, and rejected the claim. [Para 311][Para 312][Para 313]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A provincial Department of Labour has no statutory authority to authorise or approve a dismissal; a letter purporting to do so is void and cannot validate the employer's decision. [Para 185][Para 187]

An employer alleging serious misconduct by intentional time-card fraud bears the burden of proving the worker's intent and knowledge; absent clear rules and evidence, an erroneous punch made by a group representative is not serious misconduct. [Para 209][Para 211][Para 220][Para 222]

Where a collective agreement gives former workers priority for re-employment, the obligation is section-specific and conditional on labour demand; it does not create an absolute right to reinstatement for every former worker. [Para 282][Para 283][Para 284]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the employer should instruct workers directly and in writing on time-card procedure during overtime and should make the punching system easy to use without anxiety; this was a recommendation, not a binding order. [Para 221]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The source is a Khmer-language award with substantial OCR noise: repeated fused words, stray Latin characters and page numbers embedded mid-sentence, and occasional garbled numerals. The worker name appears variously as Khveak Thum, Khvae k Thum and Khve k Thum. The back-wage figure is printed as "62,50" dollars, which the text itself explains as US$ 61 base wage plus US$ 1.50 seniority bonus. The annex listing 90 former workers is heavily corrupted and cannot be reliably reproduced. The award date and panel composition are clear.
