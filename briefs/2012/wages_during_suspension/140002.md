# Award No. 80/12 — S.H. International (Arbitration Council, 4 May 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer, Liv Sovanna for the workers, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 140002

## 1. Facts
S.H. International operates a garment factory at Ang Keo village, Chom Chao, Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh. Two free trade unions represented the workforce in a collective dispute over nine demands, including the removal of four named administrative staff and two team leaders, travel and accommodation allowances, a higher attendance bonus, a base wage of USD 7,000 per month, and the treatment of wages and bonuses during sick, special and annual leave. Conciliation before the Ministry of Labour failed and the non-settlement report No. 432 K.B/AK/VK, dated 23 April 2012, was forwarded to the Council. The employer's president attended the pre-hearing meeting; the workers' representatives did not. [Para 1][Para 22][Para 1][Para 2]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer should dismiss the two named administrative staff? [Para 2]
2. Whether the employer should dismiss the two named team leaders and a third named employee? [Para 4]
3. Whether the employer should pay a travel allowance of USD 1,000 per person per month? [Para 6]
4. Whether the employer should pay an accommodation allowance of USD 10 per person per month? [Para 8]
5. Whether the employer should pay a regular attendance bonus of USD 10 per person per month? [Para 10]
6. Whether the employer should raise the base wage to USD 7,000 per person per month? [Para 12]
7. Whether the employer should refrain from deducting wages and the attendance bonus when a worker is sick with a valid medical certificate? [Para 14]
8. Whether the employer should be barred from taking the medical certificate before the worker applies for sick leave? [Para 16]
9. Whether the employer should refrain from deducting wages and the attendance bonus when a worker takes special leave or annual leave? [Para 18]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the nine demands stood unresolved after conciliation; the union also asked the Council to order the workforce to suspend its strike while the arbitration proceeded. [Para 2][Para 32]
**Employer (respondent):** the company cannot dismiss the named staff because they committed no fault; it has no capacity to pay the travel and accommodation allowances; it already pays the attendance bonus at USD 7 per month under Prakas No. 041/11 SVN of 7 March 2011; it pays wages according to the Labour Law; and it keeps wages intact during certified sick leave while deducting the bonus only for days actually absent. [Para 3][Para 7][Para 11][Para 13][Para 15]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel discontinued the arbitration in case No. 80/12-S.H. International and made no order on any of the nine demands. [Para 51][Para 52]
The panel began from its own jurisdiction under Chapter 12, Section 2(B) (Articles 309 to 317) of the 1997 Labour Law, Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council of 21 April 2004 and Prakas No. 136 on the appointment of arbitrators of 7 June 2011 (ninth term), and recorded that conciliation had been attempted under Chapter 12, Section 2(A) without agreement. [Para 20][Para 21]
Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 requires disputing parties to suspend every dispute measure — strike or lockout — and any other measure capable of aggravating the dispute while the panel is seized, and to attend every meeting to which the panel summons them. [Para 41][Para 42]
The panel followed its own prior interpretation of that article: in case No. 04/04-IM SI it issued a notice on 29 January 2004 declining to resolve or order on a collective dispute because the parties had no right to strike during the proceedings, and in cases No. 28/06-GDM and No. 34/06-Gold Fame it closed the files because the workers kept striking despite two orders to return to work. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47]
It also relied on cases No. 110/11-Simanou and No. 59/12-Vin Sing Thich, where arbitration was discontinued for the same reason. [Para 48]
On the facts, the workers continued their strike during the proceedings, disregarded the panel's order No. 011/12 K.B/AK of 25 April 2012, did not return to work and did not attend the pre-hearing meeting of 27 April 2012. Because the panel never held a hearing it expressly made no factual findings on the disputed points. [Para 40][Para 50]
The panel noted that the workers themselves had asked it to order the suspension of the strike, and that the Phnom Penh Court of First Instance had already ordered the workforce to stop the strike immediately by order No. C8 "ឈ" of 26 April 2012. [Para 32][Para 33]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a workforce continues a strike during the arbitration of its collective dispute, in defiance of the panel's order to suspend dispute measures and return to work, the panel will discontinue the arbitration instead of ruling on the demands, whatever their merits. [Para 49][Para 51]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that it had not conducted a hearing and therefore recorded no findings of fact on the issues in the non-settlement report. [Para 40]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The document is a Khmer-language order; the English rendering of the employer's name is unstable ("S.H. International", "អេស អេក អ៉ីនធើណេសិនណល", "អេស អេក អាសយដ្ឋាន"), and the union names are partly garbled. The accommodation demand prints a stray digit in the period ("ក្នុង ៤ មួយ ខែ"), and the wage demand is printed as USD 7,000 per month without a riel equivalent. Paragraph 19 is badly corrupted ("AN", "អាវុធ", "Ji"). The prior case numbers are inconsistently transliterated ("04/04-អ៊ិម អេស អាយ", "វីនស៊ីងថិច"), and the court order number is printed as "C8 "ឈ"" without a full docket. Individual names are given only in Khmer and are redacted here.
