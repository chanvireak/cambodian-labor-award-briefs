# Award No. 75/12 — Hong Ing (Arbitration Council, 3 May 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Yu Soun Ty, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Aing Eng Thong, chair) · Doc ID 139995

## 1. Facts
Hong Ing (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហុង អ៉ីង), a garment factory operating since 2011 with about 120 workers, pays a monthly attendance bonus of USD 13. [Para 23][Para 30] It deducts the whole bonus when a worker is absent without leave, and also deducts the whole bonus where a worker takes two days' certified sick leave, even with the employer's permission. [Para 32][Para 33] The Cambodian Labour Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ពលកម្ម កម្ពុជា), which had no local union at the factory, was authorised in writing by 100 workers to represent them. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 28] The workers demanded that the attendance bonus be deducted only pro rata for days of certified sick leave. [Para 30]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the Federation has standing to represent the 100 workers before the Arbitration Council? [Para 40]
2. Whether the employer may deduct the attendance bonus pro rata for days a worker is absent on certified sick leave? [Para 46]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The attendance bonus is part of wages under Article 103 of the Labour Law, so when a worker is ill with a proper medical certificate the employer may deduct only the proportionate amount for the days missed, not the whole USD 13. [Para 37][Para 39]
**Employer (respondent):** The attendance bonus is paid above what the law requires, and deducting it discourages absenteeism; the employer cannot pay the bonus for days not worked. [Para 36]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must deduct the attendance bonus pro rata for days absent on certified sick leave, not in full. [Para 61][Para 62]

On standing, the panel applied Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 (21 April 2004), which allows a dispute party to be represented by another person holding written authorisation. [Para 40][Para 41] It found the Federation held written authorisations from 100 workers and that the employer did not object to the thumbprints, so the Federation had lawful standing. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44]

On the merits, the panel noted that Notice No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 requires an attendance bonus of at least USD 7 per month, and that the employer's USD 13 exceeded that minimum. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48] It held the attendance bonus is a form of wage under Article 103. [Para 49][Para 51] Under Article 71(3) of the Labour Law, absence due to illness certified by an official doctor suspends the employment contract; under Article 72(1), suspension relieves both the duty to work and the duty to pay wages, absent a contrary provision. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54][Para 56] The employer therefore need not pay the bonus for certified sick days, but it may not deduct the entire monthly bonus; it may deduct only pro rata for those days. [Para 57][Para 58] The panel followed its earlier awards Nos 62/07 and 10/12. [Para 59][Para 60]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus is part of wages under Article 103 of the Labour Law. Because certified sick leave suspends the employment contract under Articles 71(3) and 72(1), the employer owes no bonus for those days, but may deduct only the proportionate amount attributable to them, not the whole monthly bonus. [Para 51][Para 58]

**Obiter dicta:** The employer's stated policy aims — rewarding regular attendance and discouraging excessive absence — were not treated as a justification for full deduction; the panel measured the deduction against the suspension rules rather than the employer's purpose. [Para 36][Para 58]

## 6. Source defects disclosed

[Para 1][Para 25] Paragraph 34 contains the garbled token "Leagu" where the sense requires "ឈឺ" (sick) during working hours. [Para 34] Paragraph 40 misnumbers the Prakas provision as "ប្រការ ១៩" while the surrounding text and the award's reasoning rely on Article 19 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 40] The worker count shifts between about 120 employees and the 100 who signed authorisations. [Para 23][Para 28] Several individual names survive only as initials or corrupted strings, and the date of the attendance-bonus notice appears in more than one form. [Para 46]
