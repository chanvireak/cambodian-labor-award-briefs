# Award No. 67/12 — Star Knitting & Garment Factory Ltd (Arbitration Council, 20 April 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sombouna, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Run Saray, chair) · Doc ID 139993

## 1. Facts
Star Knitting & Garment Factory Ltd, a garment factory employing about 4,388 workers, suspended the employment contracts of 395 workers from late October to 26 December 2011 for lack of orders and financial difficulty, and promised to restart production on 1 March 2012 with workers returning that day. [Para 28][Para 32][Para 35] Only 230 returned; 158 did not come back at all. [Para 36][Para 37] The workers' union claimed 50% of total wages for those 158 for 1 March–2 April 2012 and a 5% end-of-contract indemnity. [Para 30][Para 41] The employer did not appear at the hearing. [Para 14][Para 49]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay wages equal to 50% of total wages to the 158 workers who did not report for work from 1 March 2012 to 2 April 2012? [Para 30][Para 52]
2. Whether the employer must pay the 5% end-of-fixed-duration-contract indemnity on 2 April 2012 when the employment relationship had not been severed? [Para 41][Para 62]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The 158 workers never returned after the suspension ended, so the employer owes them 50% of their total wages for the period, and the 5% indemnity fell due when their fixed-duration contracts reached their term on 2 April 2012. [Para 30][Para 41][Para 42]
**Employer (respondent):** Absent from the hearing without valid excuse; it had treated the 158 workers as having abandoned their employment. [Para 40][Para 49]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both claims rejected; the award is a non-binding order that becomes final eight days after notification absent objection. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 84]

On issue 1, the panel applied Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 664(1) of the Civil Code: an employment contract arises from the reciprocal promise of work and wages. Because the 158 workers did not return to the factory after the suspension was lifted, they did not perform the promised work, so no contract and no wage obligation arose between them and the employer from 1 March 2012. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 59][Para 60] The panel therefore rejected the 50% wage claim. [Para 61]

On issue 2, the panel read Article 73 of the Labour Law (5% indemnity on expiry of a fixed-duration contract) and found the phrase "on termination of the contract" ambiguous. [Para 64][Para 66][Para 68][Para 69] Following prior awards 60/06 and 76/06, it held the 5% indemnity is payable only when the fixed-duration contract ends and the employment relationship is actually severed; where the contract is renewed or the relationship continues, the indemnity is not owed. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 75][Para 76] Since the workers had not resigned and the employer had neither closed the company nor cut the employment relationship, the relationship subsisted, so the 5% claim was rejected. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 81]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The 5% indemnity under Article 73 of the Labour Law is payable only when a fixed-duration contract expires and the employment relationship is actually severed; continuation or renewal of the relationship defeats the claim, even where the employer has no work to assign. [Para 72][Para 75][Para 77]

**Obiter dicta:** The employer's unilateral characterization of the 158 workers as having abandoned their employment did not itself determine the wage claim; the panel reasoned from the absence of performed work rather than from abandonment. [Para 40][Para 59]
