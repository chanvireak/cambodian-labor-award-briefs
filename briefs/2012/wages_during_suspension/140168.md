# Award No. 162/12 — Osei Ang Garment (Arbitration Council, 20 August 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tai Seng for the employer, An Non for the workers, Pen Bunhea presiding) · Doc ID 140168

## 1. Facts

Workers at Osei Ang Garment, a garment factory in Phnom Penh, struck from 11 August 2012 demanding that the company dismiss Mr F, its deputy production manager, whom they accused of pressuring workers, threatening them, throwing clothing at their heads during working hours, and withholding overtime from women who refused to give him their phone numbers [Para 2][Para 12]. The Labour Disputes Department received the complaint by telephone and conciliation ended on 16 August 2012 with the single demand unresolved [Para 9][Para 10]. At the employer's request the panel issued Order No. 022/12 on 16 August 2012 suspending the strike immediately and requiring all strikers to return to their shifts from the morning of 17 August 2012 [Para 13]. The workers did not comply, and the panel convened a pre-hearing meeting on 17 August 2012 [Para 14][Para 15][Para 16]. The merits of the demand against Mr F were never reached; the panel recorded that it made no findings of fact on the issue sent to it [Para 21].

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. (Issue 1) Whether the panel should continue the arbitration proceedings at all, given that the workers kept striking after it had ordered the strike suspended? [Para 23][Para 27][Para 33]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** The workers maintained their demand that the company remove Mr F from his post as deputy production manager, alleging systematic mistreatment of the workforce, and they refused to end the strike while the case was pending [Para 2][Para 16].

**Employer (respondent):** The employer said it could not remove Mr F because he had committed no fault and there was no evidence supporting the workers' accusations [Para 3]. It asked the panel to proceed with the arbitration, which is why the panel issued the return-to-work order [Para 13].

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to continue the arbitration in case No. 162/12, closing the file without any ruling on the demand against Mr F [Para 35].

The panel framed its question as whether to continue the proceedings at all [Para 23]. It relied on Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council, which requires the parties to suspend every dispute measure — strike or lockout under Article 318 of the Labour Law, or any other measure capable of aggravating the dispute — while the arbitration panel is sitting, and requires them to attend all meetings to which the panel summons them [Para 23][Para 25][Para 26]. It noted its consistent practice, applied in Award No. 04/04 (IM SI) and in Awards No. 28/06 (G.E.M.) and No. 34/06 (Gold Fame), of refusing to act where workers keep striking during the proceedings, and of closing cases on that ground [Para 27][Para 28][Para 29]. The same result was reached in Awards No. 110/11 (Simano) and No. 59/12 (Win Sing Tech) [Para 30][Para 31].

Applying that practice, the panel found the workers had not agreed to stop the strike during the arbitration and had not respected Order No. 022/12 of 16 August 2012, either by continuing to strike or by returning to work [Para 32]. It considered that terminating the proceedings would leave the parties free to exercise their lawful rights through means outside the Arbitration Council [Para 33]. It therefore decided not to continue [Para 34][Para 35].

## 5. Ratio decidendi

Where workers continue a strike, or otherwise maintain a dispute measure, after the Arbitration Council has ordered the dispute measures suspended for the duration of the arbitration, the panel will decline to continue the proceedings and will close the file, leaving the parties to pursue their lawful rights outside the arbitration procedure; a panel does not adjudicate a demand that the parties have removed from its reach by their own conduct. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 32][Para 33]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observation that the parties remain at liberty to resolve the dispute by other lawful means is not a finding on the merits and implies no view on whether the demand against Mr F was well founded [Para 33].

## 6. Source defects disclosed

The scan is short and heavily corrupted. Paragraph 34 is largely unrecoverable, containing a run of unrelated words about a company, finances and "12000" before the case name reappears [Para 34]. Latin fragments ("Sin", "Ball", "The", "Li", "Lin") break the Khmer text at several points, and the signature block is reduced to stray characters and digits [Para 14][Para 27][Para 31][Para 36]. The award is internally inconsistent on whether the workers returned to work: paragraph 14 says they did not enter work as ordered, while paragraph 32 says they continued the strike while also coming in to work [Para 14][Para 32]. The employer's managers and the fourteen named worker representatives are printed in full in the party lists and have been reduced to initials and roles here [Para 1][Para 2]. The source contains no paragraph stating the type of award, so whether it is binding or non-binding is not recorded in this document.
