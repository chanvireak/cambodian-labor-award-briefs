# Award No. 194/12 — An Full Garment (Arbitration Council, 23 October 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Kong Phallha, chair) · Doc ID 140191

## 1. Facts
Workers at An Full Garment, a factory in Samrong Tong district, Kampong Speu, put five demands through the Union Federation of Cambodia: a lunch allowance of USD 15 a month, double pay for Sunday work, a base wage of USD 70, no deduction of pay for rest days, and full pay when the company has no work for them [Para 2][Para 3][Para 4][Para 5][Para 6]. Conciliation failed on all five points and the file reached the Council on 27 September 2012 [Para 9][Para 14]. The workers had struck since 17 September 2012 and remained on strike after the referral [Para 15]. The panel issued Order No. 029/12 on 1 October 2012, suspending the strike and requiring everyone back at work from 2 October 2012 [Para 16][Para 34]. Neither party filed any evidence, and the employer did not attend the pre-hearing meeting of 4 October 2012 or the hearing [Para 19][Para 20].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the panel should continue the arbitration at all, given that the workers kept striking after it had ordered the strike suspended under Prakas No. 099? [Para 26][Para 29]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** They had stopped striking in compliance with Order No. 029/12, but the employer refused to let the strikers back into the workplace, treating them as having abandoned their jobs, and did not attend the meeting called to take information [Para 18][Para 37].
**Employer (respondent):** It appeared at neither the pre-hearing meeting nor the hearing and filed no documents, having said during conciliation that it could not meet the union and the workers because those workers had already abandoned their employment [Para 12][Para 19][Para 20].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel decided not to proceed and terminated the arbitration of case No. 194/12, closing it by award [Para 40][Para 41].

The panel framed its own question as whether the arbitration should continue, and answered it under Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, which requires the parties to suspend every dispute measure, including a strike, while the panel is seized, and to attend all meetings to which they are summoned [Para 26][Para 27]. It noted its consistent practice in Awards No. 28/06 (GDM), No. 34/06 (Gold), No. 110/11 (Simano) and No. 59/12 (Win Sing Tech): the panel will not continue where the workers carry on striking after an order to stop [Para 29][Para 31].

On the facts, the strike had begun on 17 September 2012 and had not ended, and Order No. 029/12 had required a return to work from the morning of 2 October 2012 [Para 15][Para 16]. The panel also recorded that the employer's refusal to readmit the strikers, and its failure to attend the pre-hearing meeting of 4 October 2012, were measures taken in disregard of its order which aggravated the dispute during the proceedings [Para 39]. It drew no consequence in the employer's favour from that finding, but neither did it let the workers' non-compliance pass.

Because no evidence was produced on either side, the panel made no finding on any of the five demands and expressly stated it could not do so, the more so as it never held a hearing on the merits [Para 25][Para 20]. The award therefore carries no ruling on the lunch allowance, Sunday pay, base wage, rest-day deductions or no-work pay that the workers had claimed [Para 2][Para 6].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the claimant workers continue a strike after the panel has ordered it suspended under Article 20 of Prakas No. 099, the panel will decline to exercise its jurisdiction and will terminate the arbitration by award, leaving the demands undecided on the merits [Para 29][Para 40][Para 41].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that an employer who refuses to readmit strikers who have complied, and who stays away from the hearings, itself disregards the panel's order and aggravates the dispute — a finding that carried no remedial order here [Para 39].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel could find no facts at all because no hearing was held and no document was filed, so the entire factual account rests on the conciliation report and the parties' letters [Para 20][Para 25]. The demand list is duplicated and cut mid-sentence where two letters are run together, and the company name is printed in several different forms across the header, the body and the order. The strike order is cited as No. 029/12 with the date printed once as 1 October 2012 and once garbled with a doubled year, the conciliation report is numbered 1271/12 and dated 27 September 2012 while the file also records it as 1271/12 with a different prefix, and the union's demands are numbered inconsistently between the report and the schedule.
