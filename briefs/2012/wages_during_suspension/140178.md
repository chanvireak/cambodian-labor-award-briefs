# Award No. 178/12 — DecoRo (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 2 October 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambor Na for the employer, Liv Sovanna for the workers, Pen Bunhea presiding) · Doc ID 140178

## 1. Facts
DecoRo (Cambodia) is a furniture factory in Kampong Speu employing 120 workers. [Para 51][Para 54] On 1 July 2012 the workers voted to form a union affiliated to the Cambodia Labour Movement Development Federation, and on 5 July they notified the employer of the result. [Para 57][Para 64] From 10 to 17 July 2012 between 30 and 40 workers struck without prior notice. [Para 71] On 17 July the employer terminated 16 workers, citing unlawful strike, intimidation of other workers and failure to return after two notices. [Para 84][Para 151] The union's demands were five; demand 4 was withdrawn and demands 1–3 merged, leaving Issues 1–3 and Issue 5, of which the panel heard evidence for only eight workers. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 114]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1, incorporating Issues 2 and 3) Whether the employer discriminated against the union when it terminated the contracts of the eight striking workers, such that they must be reinstated with back pay and benefits? [Para 118]
2. (Issue 5) Whether the demand that the employer repay money workers paid to gain entry to the recruitment test is a collective labour dispute within the panel's jurisdiction? [Para 182]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the union was lawfully created before the strike, so the terminations were anti-union reprisals; the employer had reassigned cutter-section workers to cleaning, refused the union notification letter and told workers no union would be allowed in the factory. [Para 125][Para 66]
**Employer (respondent):** the strike was procedurally unlawful for want of the seven-day notice and of prior conciliation; only 5 of the 16 gave written authority to the federation, and two notices with a seven-day deadline were ignored. [Para 129][Para 130][Para 150] It denied the payments claim: the sums went to a security guard and his wife in their personal capacity and the company had no fee policy. [Para 98][Para 104]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must reinstate the eight workers and pay back pay from the date of termination to the date of reinstatement; the Issue 5 repayment claim was dismissed for want of jurisdiction. [Para 195][Para 196]
On discrimination the panel applied Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law and its own consistent line that the party alleging discrimination carries the burden of proof. [Para 118][Para 119][Para 120] The workers filed no evidence by the deadline, and the panel reasoned that an employer set on discrimination would not have issued two return-to-work notices with a seven-day grace period. [Para 130][Para 131] It therefore found no anti-union motive behind the eight terminations. [Para 132]
The panel then turned to whether the employer could order strikers back. Under Articles 319, 320, 324, 336 and 337 only a court may declare a strike unlawful and order return within 48 hours; while a strike is running the employer has no such power. [Para 144][Para 145][Para 155][Para 156] The notices here were framed as orders carrying a deadline whose breach was treated as abandonment — a condition the panel held invalid and no basis for a finding of serious misconduct. [Para 154] Terminating strikers instead of using the statutory procedure was itself a punishment prohibited by Article 333 and void. [Para 158]
On back pay the panel split the period. Article 332 suspends the contract during a strike, so no wages are owed for the strike days, and although the employer hired ten replacements contrary to Article 334, that right was forfeited because the strike itself was procedurally unlawful. [Para 163][Para 165][Para 173] For the period after the unlawful terminations the employer owed back pay, having had no right to terminate. [Para 174]
On Issue 5 the panel applied Article 302 and its three cumulative conditions for a collective dispute; all three failed, the payee acting personally and the matter touching neither working conditions nor industrial peace. [Para 183][Para 186][Para 188][Para 190][Para 193] Arbitrator Mar Sambor Na dissented, holding the terminations a lawful exercise of management rights warranting severance rather than reinstatement. [Para 225][Para 226]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may invite strikers to return to work, but a notice that fixes a deadline and treats non-return as abandonment is an invalid condition; dismissal of strikers for taking part in a strike, lawful or not, is a punishment prohibited by Article 333, so reinstatement and back pay from the date of dismissal follow, while wages for the strike days themselves are not owed under Article 332. [Para 154][Para 158][Para 163][Para 174]

**Obiter dicta:** only a court, not the panel, may declare a strike unlawful and fix the 48-hour return period. [Para 145][Para 155] The panel also preferred the employer's later summary of headcount (120) over the older conciliation report (125). [Para 54]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR is heavily corrupted: stray Latin fragments ("Allan", "Dail", "Mare", "Fill", "Sing", "Band", "Shan", "Sand", "She", "Chen") are spliced into Khmer sentences, and the panel composition is partly garbled at the signature block. [Para 7][Para 118][Para 152][Para 190] Several evidential lists are duplicated verbatim, and one worker's name appears in two different spellings across the record; names of workers, the security guard and his wife are reduced to initials here, and the guard's name is not reproduced at all. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 85][Para 86]
Internal contradictions were left standing: the panel states the terminated group both as 8 and as 80 in the same question, the strike is described as 30–40 workers, 16 dismissals and 4–5 claimants, and the cleaning reassignment is given as 2–3 days in the positions and 4 days in the reasoning. [Para 118][Para 71][Para 60][Para 126] The award is dated 2 October 2012 in both the header and the closing line, but the reasons refer to a 30 September conciliation minute and a 4 September non-conciliation report. [Para 48][Para 227]
