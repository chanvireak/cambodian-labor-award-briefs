# Award No. 31/12 — B & N Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 9 March 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phallack chair; Ing Sothy, Liv Sovanna) · Doc ID 139955

## 1. Facts
B & N Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd runs a garment factory at Tuol Sangke, Russei Keo, Phnom Penh, employing 720 workers; the claimant is the local union of the Free Trade Union of Workers of the Kingdom of Cambodia, which holds a most-representative certificate dated 27 October 2010 effective to 10 October 2012. [Para 20][Para 21] Of 13 demands, three were withdrawn and the panel treated the remainder as 14 issues; conciliation on 10 February 2012 resolved none, and non-conciliation report No. 171 reached the Secretariat on 15 February 2012 for a hearing on 29 February 2012. [Para 8][Para 9][Para 11][Para 12] The employer was duly summoned but did not appear and gave no reason, so the panel proceeded in its absence under clause 21 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 10][Para 55][Para 56]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1(a). Whether pregnant workers may leave 15 minutes before the end of the working day? [Para 21][Para 67]
1(b). Whether pregnant workers may take two days a month off with wages and benefits maintained for medical checks? [Para 25][Para 75]
2. Whether workers may take annual leave for one day before and after Khmer New Year, and before and after Pchum Ben, and for personal commitments? [Para 28][Para 90]
4(a). Whether wages must be paid by 4:00 p.m. on payday? [Para 31][Para 104]
4(b). Whether underpaid or wrongly deducted wages must be paid within a week of payday? [Para 32][Para 109]
5. Whether the employer must provide masks during working hours? [Para 33][Para 115]
6. Whether the employer must provide clean toilets, drained to an acceptable standard? [Para 34][Para 121]
7. Whether the employer must place a doctor on stand-by during working hours? [Para 37][Para 129]
8. Whether the employer must place a clock at the card-punching machine and stop changing the time? [Para 38][Para 137]
9(a). Whether overtime on Sundays and public holidays must be paid at 250 per cent plus a 2,000 riel meal allowance? [Para 42][Para 148]
9(b). Whether only USD 0.25 may be deducted from the attendance bonus for each day of unauthorised leave? [Para 47][Para 167]
10. Whether the employer must enforce the behaviour standards for security guards agreed on 14 July 2008? [Para 48][Para 177]
12. Whether the employer must provide a proper place for posting information? [Para 48][Para 184]
13. Whether the employer must bear the cost of treatment for work-related accidents? [Para 50][Para 188]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** they sought each item above, arguing that the crowd leaving at the gate endangers pregnancies, that public hospitals are too busy for a half-day check, that fares rise at festivals so leave should start early, that the clock at the punch machine is moved to shorten the lunch break, that the security guards use bad language contrary to the 2008 agreement, and that the employer has failed to pay treatment costs and to report accidents to the National Social Security Fund. [Para 23][Para 27][Para 29][Para 42][Para 50][Para 52]
**Employer (respondent):** absent and unrepresented at the hearing, so it advanced no case; the award records only its existing practices — five minutes' early release for pregnant workers, half a day a month for checks, no annual leave taken, wages paid after 4:00 p.m., double pay for Sundays, and the whole USD 7 bonus forfeited on any leave. [Para 2][Para 10][Para 24][Para 25][Para 45][Para 48]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — many rights claims succeed; the panel split its order into a binding Part I (rights) and a non-binding Part II (interests). [Para 196][Para 202][Para 203][Para 208]
The panel first classified each demand as a rights dispute, with a basis in the law, an agreement or a collective agreement, or an interests dispute decided in equity; only a union holding most-representative status may bring the latter, because such an award becomes a one-year collective agreement. [Para 65][Para 69][Para 70]
On 1(a) there was no legal basis, and since pregnant workers already leave five minutes early and can thereby avoid the crush, the demand failed; on 1(b) the panel ordered one day a month with pay deducted for the extra half day, holding two days more than a check requires. [Para 67][Para 73][Para 74][Para 85][Para 86] On issue 2, Article 170 makes Khmer New Year leave a right, so one day before and after was ordered for workers with a year's service; Pchum Ben leave was granted in equity following award 81/07; leave for personal commitments was refused because Article 170 requires an agreement reported to the labour inspector. [Para 91][Para 92][Para 98][Para 100][Para 101]
Issue 4(a) succeeded on Articles 115(3) and 137: wages must be paid during working hours, here by 4:00 p.m. [Para 104][Para 106][Para 108] On 4(b) the one-week demand was refused, since equity cannot fall below the statute, but the employer was ordered to pay wages in full on payday. [Para 113][Para 114] Issue 5 succeeded on Article 229 — two masks a week; issue 6 on Article 229 and Prakas No. 052, ordering a compliant sewage system; issue 7 on Prakas No. 330, requiring two nurses and a doctor for three hours a shift for 601 to 900 employees; issue 8 on Articles 137 and 139, ordering a visible clock and no arbitrary change. [Para 119][Para 122][Para 128][Para 134][Para 135][Para 146]
On 9(a) the 250 per cent claim was refused as an interest above the law, but the 2,000 riel Sunday allowance was ordered under point 2 of Notification No. 041 of 7 March 2011; the same allowance for public holidays was refused because holiday work is not overtime. [Para 151][Para 159][Para 166] On 9(b) the demand failed: unauthorised leave means the worker has not attended the month's working days, so no bonus is earned at all. [Para 174][Para 175][Para 176] Issue 10 succeeded on the 14 July 2008 tripartite agreement; issue 12 in equity; and issue 13 was declined because no accident had yet given rise to a live dispute. [Para 182][Para 186][Para 196]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Annual leave is a right under Article 170, and an employer that gives pay in lieu instead of allowing it must grant one day before and one day after Khmer New Year to workers with a year's service; leave at other times requires an agreement reported to the labour inspector. [Para 91][Para 92][Para 100] The attendance bonus is earned by attending the month's working days, so where the absence is unauthorised no bonus is due at all and there is nothing to reduce proportionally — unlike authorised absence, which is treated as attendance. [Para 173][Para 174][Para 176]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that an interests award stands as a collective agreement for a year and removes non-members' right to strike on the same demand, and that it may proceed where a duly summoned party fails to appear without good cause. [Para 55][Para 69]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The English text is an unofficial translation with pervasive OCR word-joins — "themployer", "athearing", "annualeave", "meallowance", "remaing" — and quotation marks are frequently lost, including in Article 102 and Article 13. [Para 1][Para 109][Para 112] The award is dated 9 March 2012 in the header while the hearing was 29 February 2012 and the conciliation sessions are given as both 10 and 13 February 2012. [Para 1][Para 7][Para 8] The record says 13 demands were conciliated but also that there were 14 issues, and the numbering skips issues 3, 5(a) and 11 without restating the list, so the panel's own count cannot be reconciled. [Para 8][Para 11][Para 12] The operative part mislabels sub-items ("(11)", "(111)") and para 196 reproduces a duplicate sentence about NSSF members. [Para 196][Para 204][Para 206] The evidence section was omitted from the English version, so the findings rest on assertions recorded rather than documents. [Para 19][Para 20]
