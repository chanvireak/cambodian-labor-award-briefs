# Award No. 83/12 — Top World Garment (Cambodia) Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 29 May 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer, An Nan for the workers, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 140003

## 1. Facts
Top World Garment (Cambodia) Ltd. runs a garment factory at Chroy Ampil village, Kbal Kaoh commune, Kean Svay district, Kandal province, employing 830 workers, 792 of them women. The local union of C.CAWDU, registered under certificate No. 1685 KBNK of 23 July 2009, represented 412 workers. On 24 April 2012 at 5:50 p.m. the sewing supervisor hit a worker, Ms S, on the head three times with a child's denim skirt carrying sewn-in underwear, in front of up to 300 workers, after the worker refused to re-sew the skirt and asked that the supervisor be consulted first. The workers demanded her dismissal; the employer refused and offered compensation instead. [Para 1][Para 19][Para 21][Para 25][Para 28]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must dismiss the sewing supervisor Van Liza (Wang Ming Xia), who hit the head of Ms S with a skirt in front of other workers? [Para 19]
2. Whether, if the employer refuses to dismiss her, the employer must pay Ms S US$ 500 compensation and maintain the workers' daily wages and attendance bonuses for the day they went on strike? [Para 20]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the supervisor committed an act of violence and serious misconduct under clause 10 of the Internal Work Rules; the blow was deliberate, humiliating and witnessed by hundreds of workers, and the employer must dismiss her. [Para 21][Para 22]
**Employer (respondent):** the worker suffered no injury, was not hospitalised and did not lose consciousness; the supervisor acted unintentionally and is a kind person, so the act is not serious misconduct; the company preferred to deal with it internally by verbal warning. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 32][Para 34]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to dismiss the sewing supervisor Van Liza (Wang Ming Xia), and declared the award on this rights dispute binding and enforceable under the MoU of 28 September 2010. [Para 81][Para 82]
The panel first classified the demand as a rights dispute, because it concerns an entitlement arising from the employment contract and the law, applying its earlier rulings in Awards 05/11-M & V (Branch 1) and 13/11-Gold Kamvimex. [Para 48][Para 49]
On the facts, the employer acknowledged at the hearing and in its first statement of 4 May 2012 that the supervisor hit the worker's head with the skirt, but its second statement of 8 May 2012 said she merely waved it "as a joke" for educational purposes; the panel found the later statement contradicted the earlier acknowledgement and refused to consider it, treating the hearing acknowledgement as fact because both parties could respond to it. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 37]
The panel also held that the skirt had underwear sewn inside, since the employer did not refute the workers' assertion and, as the factory made the garment, could have produced evidence; it disregarded the witness thumbprints as unnecessary once the act was admitted. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40][Para 41]
It rejected the workers' added claim of dizziness as unsupported by specific evidence. [Para 42][Para 43]
Applying point 4 of part B of Article 83 of the Labour Law and point 5 of clause 10 of the Internal Work Rules, the panel held that hitting a worker's head with a skirt was deliberate violence amounting to serious misconduct warranting immediate dismissal. [Para 63][Para 51][Para 55]
It then addressed whether the workers could demand dismissal: under Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 311 of the Civil Code only the contracting parties may terminate a contract, so third parties cannot, following Award 116/10-Whitex. But the panel applied its consistent line of awards — 14/03-Chu Hsing, 17/03 and 18/03-Ho Hing, 87/04-Noble Apparel, 116/07-Grace Sun, 54/08-Zhong Yov, 129/09-Whitex Garment and 123/10-June Textile — allowing workers to demand dismissal where the employee is dangerous and keeping them could harm the workplace. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67]
It found the supervisor dangerous: a supervisor must not strike workers' heads, the head needs special protection, and an aggressive supervisor will repeat the act against other workers; the employer had confirmed it would neither warn her in writing nor reach an agreement to prevent recurrence. [Para 69][Para 72][Para 77][Para 78][Para 79]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Workers may demand the dismissal of a fellow employee only where that person is shown to be dangerous and keeping them could harm the workplace; a supervisor who deliberately strikes a worker's head in the presence of the workforce meets that test, and an employer that declines to discipline her has failed its disciplinary duty. [Para 67][Para 79]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that helmet laws show the head requires special protection, and its remark that the supervisor's act also damaged the employment relationship and the worker's dignity. [Para 73][Para 79]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The English translation drops the letters "t" and "h" throughout, printing "themployer", "thearing", "skirto hithead" and "attorney"-type clusters, so several sentences had to be reconstructed from context. The header of the document describes it as the "Dissenting opinion by Arbitrator Ing Sothy", yet the body is the panel's reasoned award and it carries the signatures of all three arbitrators; both readings are reported and neither is corrected here. The evidence section was omitted from the English version and the reader is referred to the Khmer original. [Para 1][Para 17][Para 18][Para 82]
