# Award No. 173/11 — Jin Ying (Cambodia) Knitting Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 10 January 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Pen Bun Chhea (president), Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Tuon Siphan (worker-appointed) · Doc ID 140114

## 1. Facts
Jin Ying (Cambodia) Knitting Co., Ltd. began operating in late 2009 and now employs roughly 200 workers, of whom about 70 belong to the claiming union. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 30] On 25 October 2011 the Cambodian Workers' Union Federation filed a collective claim listing nine demands; conciliation on 9 November failed on all nine, and the file reached the Council on 15 December 2011. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 17] At the hearing the workers withdrew issues 3 to 9 and pressed issues 1 and 2 plus one new demand. [Para 20][Para 21] The employer had been properly summoned and did not appear, giving no reason, so the panel proceeded in its absence under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 19][Para 56][Para 59][Para 60]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must provide a medical room staffed by a doctor or nurse together with sufficient medicines (panel's issue 1)? [Para 31][Para 61]
2. Whether the company must keep the whole USD 7 attendance bonus when a worker is absent with the employer's authorisation (panel's issue 2)? [Para 78][Para 80]
3. Whether the company must repay the USD 7 bonus and USD 1.1 half-day wage deducted from a union officer for attending the Ministry's conciliation and the Council hearing (the additional issue)? [Para 47][Para 86][Para 99]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The company long had no medical room for workers who fell ill, then converted a five- or six-person security room into one, but the room has no doctor and no medicines; administration staff refuse to issue any medicine, saying they do not know what the worker has, so workers with headaches or dizziness go and have "wind" scraped under a tree or near the toilets, and go outside for treatment when seriously ill. [Para 31][Para 32][Para 35][Para 36][Para 37][Para 38] The company does authorise family emergencies such as a sibling's wedding or a death, yet deducts the full USD 7 attendance bonus anyway, so the bonus should be preserved when leave is authorised. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45] A union vice-president who took half a day's authorised leave to brief the Ministry on 2 November 2011 lost USD 7 and USD 1.1, and should be repaid and not have her December pay cut for attending the 9 December conciliation and the 30 December hearing. [Para 47][Para 49][Para 54]

**Employer (respondent):** Absent from the hearing without explanation; no documents were filed by the employer and no response was made to any of the workers' contentions. [Para 40][Para 46][Para 55]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must appoint one mid-level doctor or nurse and keep sufficient medicines and medical supplies; the demand to keep the entire USD 7 bonus was refused, but the company must stop deducting the whole bonus and pro-rate it for authorised days off; and the company must repay USD 7 plus USD 1.1 to the union officer and not cut her December pay; the award is non-binding on issue 2 and becomes final eight days after notification unless objected to. [Para 113][Para 115][Para 116][Para 117][Para 118]

On issue 1, the panel applied Article 238 of the Labour Law, which requires enterprises to provide first aid for their workers, and Article 242 paragraphs 3 and 4, which require a nurse on permanent duty day and night and sufficient dressings and medicines. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 64] It also applied Article 3 of Joint Prakas No. 08 SKaK/94 of 19 January 1994, requiring one mid-level doctor and one nurse for 50 to 200 staff and one doctor with two nurses from 400 staff upward, and Article 5 on the contents of the first-aid box. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 69] Finding that the company has a medical room but neither a permanent medical officer nor any medicines, the panel ordered compliance for its roughly 200 workers, noting that lack of prompt medicine harms health and that sending workers outside costs them time and money and reduces productivity. [Para 71][Para 74][Para 75][Para 77]

On issue 2, the panel read point 1 of Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 — USD 7 for workers attending the required number of working days — and found it silent on authorised absences. [Para 80][Para 81] Following Awards No. 136/11 and No. 154/11, it held the employer may deduct only pro rata for days of absence it has authorised, and must not deduct the whole bonus in those cases. [Para 82][Para 83] Because the workers claimed retention of the full USD 7 and not pro-rating, that claim was refused while the pro-rata order was made. [Para 84][Para 85]

On the additional issue, the panel first asked whether it had jurisdiction over a demand absent from the conciliation record. [Para 89][Para 90] Article 312 of the Labour Law and Article 33 of Prakas No. 099 limit it to the recorded issues and their direct consequences, but earlier awards accepted claims arising after the record as direct consequences. [Para 91][Para 92][Para 94] Attending the Ministry and the hearing is participation in the conciliation and arbitration procedure, so the deduction was a direct consequence. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 98] Article 306 of the Labour Law, Article 10 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 and Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 protect party representatives from wage and bonus deductions while conciliating, so the company's practice was unlawful. [Para 99][Para 102][Para 103][Para 104][Para 108]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A garment enterprise must staff its infirmary to the level fixed by Joint Prakas No. 08 SKaK/94 for its headcount and keep it stocked; a room without a medical officer or medicines does not satisfy Articles 238 and 242. [Para 65][Para 77] An attendance bonus under Notification No. 041/11 may not be forfeited in full for absence the employer has itself authorised; only a pro-rata deduction is permitted. [Para 82] A worker who is a union officer and attends conciliation or an arbitration hearing suffers no deduction of wages, bonus or benefits for doing so, and a claim for that deduction is a direct consequence of the recorded dispute even if raised later. [Para 96][Para 102][Para 108]

**Obiter dicta:** The employer-appointed arbitrator dissented on issue 2, arguing that a bonus is conditional, that its giver decides whether the condition is met, and that ordering a pro-rata deduction the workers never claimed exceeds the panel's role. [Para 123][Para 130][Para 138]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The company is printed as "Jin Ying", "Jin Ing" and "Jin Ei" in different paragraphs; treated as one entity. [Para 1][Para 28]
- The signature page is badly corrupted, with stray Latin fragments ("muntonically", "Muchallezz7") in place of signatures. [Para 119]
- The claim record is internally contradictory on the medical room: the workers say there was none, then that one was created from a security room and later had its sign removed. [Para 31][Para 32][Para 33]
- The issue numbering is discontinuous because issues 3 to 9 were withdrawn at the hearing. [Para 20]
- Stray figures from another document ("2017", "២០") appear inside the reasons and the dissent, and the operative paragraph prints "ចំនួន ២ ៧ដុល្លារ" with a stray numeral between the words. [Para 112][Para 122]
- The dissent refers to "all 20 days" of work in the month where the notification speaks of the number of working days in the month, without fixing that number. [Para 140]
- The union officer whose pay was deducted is named in full in the source; she is reduced to "Ms O" throughout.
