# Award No. 211/12 — Song Sin Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 23 November 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Mar Sambor Na, An Non, Ang Eng Thong (president) · Doc ID 140206

## 1. Facts
The employer is a garment cutting and sewing company employing about 250 workers [Para 34]. The complainant is a federation of trade unions acting for workers at the factory; the factory-level union had sought registration on 19 September 2012, and the federation held authority letters transferred from 27 workers dated 10 September 2012 [Para 36]. The Labour Disputes Department received the petition on 18 September 2012 and conducted conciliation, with a final session on 8 October 2012 [Para 12]. Only two of the original demands remained unresolved, and the failure report No. 1200 dated 11 October 2012 was sent to the Arbitration Council secretariat on 22 October 2012 [Para 13]. The hearing was held at the Council's Phnom Penh premises on 6 November 2012, with both parties present [Para 10]. The panel tried conciliation again without success and sought further evidence from the parties [Para 16]. The parties agreed to postpone the award from 15 November to 23 November 2012 [Para 17], and jointly chose a non-binding order [Para 18]. The record contained no outside witnesses or experts [Para 19]. Evidence included the employer's internal rules, negotiation and conciliation minutes with other unions, the ministry report, union registration and authority papers, and hearing invitations [Para 20]. The employer was represented by its head of administration; the workers by the federation president and union officials [Para 1].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer had to deduct the attendance bonus proportionally to the number of days of authorised unpaid absence for personal affairs, rather than deducting USD 3 for one day's permitted absence and the whole bonus for two or more days, where the leave was permitted but unpaid and distinct from annual and special leave [Para 40]?
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer had to continue paying a 1,000-riel daily travel allowance for overtime from 16:00 to 18:00 after Ministry notice No. 230 introduced a USD 7 per month travel and accommodation allowance effective 1 September 2012 [Para 47]?

## 3. Positions of the parties
The workers stated that the employer cut USD 3 from the attendance bonus for one permitted day of absence and cut the whole bonus for two or more permitted days [Para 41]. They argued that permitted absences should reduce the bonus only in proportion to days taken, that forfeiting the whole bonus removed workers' motivation to return, and that the small bonus should be treated like a basic-wage deduction [Para 42]. The employer refused, saying the Ministry notice linked the bonus to regular attendance, that the policy encouraged punctuality, and that urgent absences were accommodated through flexible paid annual leave without any bonus deduction and without prior notice [Para 43]. On the travel allowance, the workers relied on an agreement under which the employer paid 1,000 riel per day for overtime from 16:00 to 18:00, argued this was a benefit more favourable than the law that could not be withdrawn, and cited the Ministry notice's requirement that better benefits be preserved [Para 44]. The employer said it stopped the daily allowance when notice No. 230 took effect on 1 September 2012, replacing it with USD 7 per month [Para 47]. It relied on a clause in its 28 June 2012 agreement with another union permitting it to follow any later government decision [Para 49], and noted the 29 June 2012 conciliation minutes with the other union did not record that clause even though it had been discussed [Para 52]. Neither other union made any claim on this issue [Para 54].

## 4. Award and reasoning
On Issue 1, the panel noted that notice No. 041 had been repealed and replaced by notice No. 230 dated 25 July 2012, effective 1 September 2012 [Para 64]. The new notice grants a minimum bonus of 10 USD per month to workers who attend regularly "without absence" [Para 66], wording absent from the old notice [Para 67]. The panel held that once fixed by the notice, the bonus ceased to be a discretionary management tool and became a general binding rule for textile, garment and footwear employers, so its regime must follow the wage regime of Article 103 of the 1997 Labour Law [Para 75]. Interpreting "absence", the panel read it as unauthorised absence, not every physical non-attendance [Para 84]. Authorised unpaid leave is an agreement in which the worker accepts no wage and the employer accepts the absence without discipline and keeps the job open [Para 85]. It is a pre-agreed reduction of that worker's working days in the month, not unauthorised absence [Para 90]. Reasoning from the monthly wage assumption of 8 hours per day, 6 days per week and 208 hours per month, the panel held it reasonable that a worker working fewer hours receive proportionate pay, and that paying the full bonus despite agreed unpaid leave would be unfair because the worker had already agreed to no wage that day [Para 93]. It therefore ordered a proportional deduction [Para 95]. On Issue 2, the panel applied Articles 311 and 309 of the Civil Code: a contract binds only its parties, who alone may amend, rescind or enforce it, and a third party cannot compel performance [Para 97]. The 1,000-riel daily allowance arose from the 29 June 2012 conciliation minutes between the employer and another union [Para 103], and the complainant federation was not a party to that agreement, so it cannot force performance [Para 104]. Separately, the USD 7 monthly allowance under the notice is more favourable than the old 1,000-riel daily payment, so the employer has no obligation to pay the daily amount on top of it unless it wishes to [Para 115]. The claim was rejected [Para 117].
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered pro rata deduction of the attendance bonus for authorised unpaid personal-leave days [Para 118] and rejected the claim for the 1,000-riel daily travel allowance [Para 119]; the non-binding award becomes final after 8 days absent written objection to the Minister [Para 120].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A regularity bonus fixed by Ministry notice follows the wage regime, so an employer may reduce it in proportion to days of authorised unpaid leave, which is a negotiated reduction of working days rather than unauthorised absence [Para 95]. A union that is not a party to a collective agreement cannot compel the employer to perform its terms for that union's members [Para 105]. Where a later statutory allowance is more favourable than an earlier voluntary payment, the employer satisfies its obligation by paying the statutory allowance alone [Para 116].
**Obiter dicta:** The panel declined to interpret the consequences of absences outside the authorised-unpaid-leave case before it [Para 79].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
OCR damage appears in the header and several body lines, with garbled numerals and mixed Khmer-Latin text [Para 1]. The record states ten original demands in one place and twelve in another [Para 12]. The negotiation minutes are dated 28 June 2012 in one passage and 26 June 2012 in another [Para 55]. The same minutes describe a company-supported travel allowance of 5,000 riel per week [Para 55], while the 29 June conciliation minutes record 1,000 riel per day [Para 58]. The employer representative's name is spelled inconsistently across the text.
