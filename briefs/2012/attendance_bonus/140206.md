# Award No. 211/12 — Sung Sin Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 23 November 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ang Eng Tong chair; Mar Samborna appointed by the employer; An Non appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 140206

## 1. Facts
Sung Sin Co Ltd is a garment factory in Phnom Penh employing about 250 workers. [Para 1][Para 34][Para 35] The Federation of Unions for Cambodian Workers filed a complaint on 18 September 2012 seeking improvement of several working conditions; final conciliation on 8 October 2012 left 2 of 10 demands unresolved, and the file reached the Council's secretariat on 22 October 2012. [Para 11][Para 12][Para 13] Both parties attended the hearing of 6 November 2012, and the parties agreed to extend the deadline for the award from 15 November to 23 November 2012. [Para 15][Para 17] The two unresolved demands were the pro-rata deduction of the attendance bonus for days of authorized unpaid leave, and the restoration of a 1,000 riel daily travel allowance for overtime worked between 16:00 and 18:00. [Para 2][Para 4][Para 39][Para 44]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must deduct the attendance bonus in proportion to the number of days on which the worker took authorized unpaid leave, rather than withdrawing the whole bonus? [Para 39][Para 59]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay a 1,000 riel daily travel allowance to every worker who worked overtime from 16:00 to 18:00, in addition to the 7 USD monthly travel and accommodation allowance set by Notification No. 230? [Para 44][Para 96][Para 114]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the attendance bonus is small, so pro-rata deduction is the fair analogue of the pro-rata deduction of base wage; workers who take authorized leave in good faith lose the incentive to return to work when the whole bonus is withdrawn; and Notification No. 230 preserves any benefit more favourable than the law. [Para 42][Para 45][Para 96]
**Employer (respondent):** the bonus is a discretionary management tool meant to reward regular attendance and cannot be granted in full where the worker was absent; the notification's scheme of preserving benefits more favourable than the law supports the company's practice, and it cannot pay both the 1,000 riel and the 7 USD. [Para 3][Para 43][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issue 1 was decided in the workers' favour, the employer being ordered to deduct the attendance bonus in proportion to the days of authorized unpaid leave; Issue 2 was dismissed. [Para 95][Para 117][Para 118]

On Issue 1, the panel first noted that the attendance bonus in paragraph 2 of Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012, which took effect on 1 September 2012, had ceased to be a mere instrument of management and had become a general legal standard binding all employers in the textile, garment and footwear sector. [Para 74][Para 75] The bonus therefore follows the legal regime of wages under Article 103 of the Labour Law. [Para 75] Reading the words "coming to work regularly for the number of working days in each month, without absence", the panel held that "absence" means unlawful absence, and that unpaid leave authorized by the employer is not absence. [Para 84][Para 85][Para 91] Since monthly wages are paid as a lump sum on the assumption of 8 hours a day over 6 days a week, a worker who works fewer hours fairly receives wages proportionate to the hours worked, and it would be unjust to the employer to keep the whole bonus. [Para 92][Para 93][Para 94] The panel accordingly ordered pro-rata deduction only. [Para 95]

On Issue 2, the panel found that the workers of this federation were not parties to the June 2012 agreement between the company and a different union, and as third parties to that contract they could neither modify nor enforce it, so the 1,000 riel clause could not be invoked by them. [Para 97][Para 100][Para 101][Para 102][Para 105] It then compared the two entitlements: 7 USD a month under Notification No. 230 against 1,000 riel a day paid only when overtime was worked. [Para 108][Para 113][Para 115] The monthly 7 USD was the more favourable benefit, so the employer owed nothing extra and had already discharged its obligation under the notification. [Para 115][Para 116][Para 117]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus fixed by ministerial notification is a wage standard, not a discretionary gift: where a worker takes unpaid leave authorized by the employer, the employer must reduce the bonus in proportion to the days absent, because authorized leave is not "absence" and because the worker's wage is itself pro-rated to hours worked. A contractual travel allowance agreed with one union cannot be enforced by workers of another union who were not parties to it, and where a later ministerial notification sets a higher monthly travel and accommodation allowance, the employer discharges its obligation by paying that higher amount. [Para 75][Para 84][Para 101][Para 115]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel declined to interpret the notification's silence on the consequences of unlawful absence, saying it would not decide points not raised by the case before it. [Para 78][Para 79]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The extension of the award deadline is printed with a doubled year, "២ ២០១២", so the date cannot be read exactly. [Para 17] The notification is cited as No. 230 in some passages and as No. 230/12 in others, and its issue date appears as 25 July 2012 while the preceding notification is given as No. 041 of 7 March 2011 and later as No. 041/11. [Para 47][Para 59][Para 67][Para 74][Para 108] The agreement of 28 June 2012 is attributed in one passage to a solidarity union and in another to a Khmer children's union, and the conciliation record of 29 June 2012 is described as containing both a 5,000 riel weekly travel allowance and a 1,000 riel daily allowance. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 55][Para 58] Two passages in the Issue 2 reasoning are garbled beyond recovery, including one where the panel states its conclusion on the meaning of the notification. [Para 110][Para 114] Name defects: the employer's administrative chief and the union's president, vice-president and federation officers are named in full in the appearances list — this brief identifies them by role only; the factory's transfer-of-authority letter also names an individual in Latin script. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 19] The signature block is partly illegible for all three arbitrators. [Para 121]
