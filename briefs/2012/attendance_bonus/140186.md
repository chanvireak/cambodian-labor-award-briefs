# Award No. 186/12 — Meng Yan Garment Manufacture & Wet Process Sing Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 October 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, An Nan, Tang Tri as chair) · Doc ID 140186

## 1. Facts

Meng Yan is a laundry and ironing company of about 500 workers in Svay Rolum commune, Saang district, Kandal province, and describes itself as a supporting enterprise to the garment sector [Para 78][Para 79][Para 88]. The Khmer Youth Voice Union, registered at the company on 28 June 2010, filed nine demands through the provincial labour department, conciliation on 3 September 2012 settled none of them, and the file reached the Council on 13 September 2012 [Para 17][Para 18][Para 19][Para 59]. At the hearing on 26 September 2012 the parties settled six of the nine demands — Issues 3, 4, 5, 6, 8 and 9 — leaving Issues 1, 2 and 7 [Para 22]. Two of those were decided on the merits; Issue 7, on the proportional attendance bonus, was recorded but never reached: the panel found no clear legal basis and expressly declined to consider it [Para 157][Para 158]. As a preliminary point the panel held that the union's leadership mandate had expired on 28 June 2012 and no new leadership had been registered, so its officers could not represent members, but the four workers who attended could act for themselves under Article 19 of Prakas 099, and the panel decided the demands for those four only [Para 117][Para 120][Para 124][Para 126].

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. (Issue 1) Whether Meng Yan, a laundry and ironing enterprise classed as a supporting industry, falls within Notification No. 206 of 13 December 2011 so that it must pay the USD 5 monthly health allowance for January to April 2012? [Para 135][Para 137]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must install two hot-water tanks, where it already supplies drinking water and a company clinic from which workers can obtain hot water? [Para 142][Para 151]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** Notification No. 206 creates the right to USD 5 a month from 1 January 2012 for all workers, and the company's payment from May 2012 proves the obligation while leaving January to April unpaid [Para 83][Para 85]. On water, workers fall ill with colds and need hot water; on the bonus, the attendance bonus is part of wages under Article 103 and Notification No. 230 requires it to be paid in proportion to the days taken as lawful leave [Para 99][Para 110].

**Employer (respondent):** it is a laundry, ironing and printing business within the three supporting categories, not a textile, garment or footwear factory, so Notification No. 206 does not bind it, and the USD 5 paid since May 2012 is voluntary and was given only to avoid strikes disrupting production [Para 88][Para 89][Para 98]. Drinking water is adequate and hot water is available at the clinic; there is no duty to provide hot-water tanks [Para 100][Para 102]. On the bonus it applies its established practice of deducting USD 2, USD 4 or the whole bonus according to days absent [Para 106][Para 107][Para 109][Para 112].

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** dismissed — both decided demands were rejected: no order to pay the USD 5 health allowance for January to April 2012, and no order to install two hot-water tanks; the attendance-bonus demand was declined without decision [Para 141][Para 152][Para 161].

**Issue 1.** The panel read Notification No. 206 of 13 December 2011, which fixes USD 5 a month for workers in the textile, garment and footwear industries from 1 January 2012, together with letter No. 3891 of the Ministry of Economy and Finance and letter No. 005 of the professional relations working group [Para 128][Para 130][Para 131]. Those instruments identify three supporting categories only — ironing, printing and embroidery — and state that the tax-relief extension was never granted to supporting industries, the USD 5 allowance being tied to that package [Para 135][Para 136]. The panel found nothing in law classifying a laundry of this kind with the textile and garment sector, so the notification did not cover it, and the voluntary payment since May 2012 was an improvement on the legal minimum that the employer might continue [Para 137][Para 138][Para 140]. The claim was rejected [Para 141].

**Issue 2.** Under Prakas No. 054 of 10 February 2000 the employer must supply sufficient safe drinking water, hot or cold, kept in covered containers with a tap [Para 142][Para 143][Para 144]. Following Awards 73/09-Global Apparel and 143/09-Sino Phea, the panel treated that as an employer duty, found the company supplied water adequately and that workers could obtain hot water at the clinic without difficulty, and held there was no duty to add hot-water tanks [Para 145][Para 147][Para 149][Para 151]. The claim was rejected [Para 152].

## 5. Ratio decidendi

A ministerial notification fixing a monthly health allowance for the textile, garment and footwear industries binds only the industries it names; an enterprise shown to be in a supporting category outside them owes the allowance only if some other legal instrument extends it, and its voluntary payment above the legal minimum creates no precedent obligation for earlier months [Para 136][Para 137][Para 140]. Prakas No. 054 is satisfied by adequate safe drinking water, so a request for hot-water tanks fails where hot water is otherwise accessible [Para 144][Para 151]. A union whose leadership mandate has expired and whose new leadership has not been registered cannot represent its members before the panel [Para 120].

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that paying the allowance is more advantageous than the legal minimum, and that the employer may therefore continue it, was not necessary to rejecting the claim [Para 140].
