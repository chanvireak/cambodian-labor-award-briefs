# Award No. 208/12 — Osei Ang Gamean Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 14 December 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chhea chair; Mar Samborna appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 140203

## 1. Facts
The respondent is a garment factory employing roughly 2,300 workers and hosting two unions; the complaining union, the Cambodia Workers' Union of the Osei Ang factory, is the smaller with over 100 members. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 61] A strike ran from 11 to 30 August 2012. [Para 74] Six women workers who did not join it claim full wages and the full 10 USD attendance bonus for 16, 17 and 18 August, saying strikers prevented them from completing their hours. [Para 62][Para 68][Para 69] The union's complaint of 21 September put six demands; conciliation on 5 October left five unresolved, and at the hearing four of those five were settled, leaving only Issue 1. [Para 19][Para 23][Para 24] The panel also refused to consider a list of 143 claimants filed on 23 November, after the 16 November deadline it had set. [Para 63][Para 65]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the six workers who attended work during the strike are entitled to full wages and to the full 10 USD monthly attendance bonus for August 2012, or only to pay for the hours they actually worked? [Para 97][Para 102]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** they came to the factory on 16, 17 and 18 August and would have worked full eight-hour days but for strikers who cut threads and blocked them at their machines; the lost time was no fault of theirs, so full wages and the full 10 USD bonus were due. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 75]
**Employer (respondent):** the line manager did not initial their attendance sheets because they were not working — some sat idle at the machines, some slept — and the attendance bonus is never paid to a worker who does not complete full hours on a working day. [Para 70][Para 72][Para 73][Para 76]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay wages for the hours actually worked on 16, 17 and 18 August 2012 (7, 8 and 8 hours for one worker; 5 hours, 3 hours 30 minutes and 4 hours for the other five) and was permitted to reduce the attendance bonus of the six in proportion to the days on which they did not work full hours. [Para 145][Para 146][Para 147]

The panel first held the claim to be a rights dispute, since it rested on wages under the 1997 Labour Law and on the attendance bonus fixed by ministry Notification No. 041 KBSChJN of 7 March 2011, and a rights dispute is a decision that binds at once rather than a bargaining claim. [Para 97][Para 100][Para 148]

On standing, the panel had ordered a signed list of claimants by 16 November; the union produced one only at the second hearing, naming 143 workers, so the panel declined to consider it and confined itself to the six who appeared and argued their claim. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65][Para 67]

On wages, Article 102 defines wages as remuneration for work done or to be done; the August attendance sheets carried no "A" or "A leave" marking, so there was no recorded absence, but work sheets were produced for only three of the six and two of those covered July, a month not in dispute. [Para 93][Para 95][Para 102][Para 106][Para 108] Where the sheets were silent the panel adopted the workers' own account of their hours. [Para 116][Para 121][Para 125] It then held that hours lost while strikers disrupted the line were not payable, because disruption by fellow employees inside the plant was something the employer could neither control nor foresee. [Para 127][Para 128][Para 129]

On the bonus, Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 set a 10 USD minimum but took effect only on 1 September 2012, so August fell under the older 7 USD floor of Notification No. 041; the company's 10 USD practice already exceeded the law. [Para 130][Para 132][Para 133][Para 135] Because the bonus rewards regular full attendance, the employer could reduce it for each day the worker sat at the machine without working full hours. [Para 140][Para 143][Para 144]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker who attends on a strike day but works only part of it is entitled to wages for the hours actually worked and no more; time lost to disruption by striking colleagues inside the plant is not chargeable to the employer, who can neither prevent such entry nor predict it. An attendance bonus conditioned on regular full attendance may be reduced day by day where the worker was present but did not work the full hours. [Para 102][Para 128][Para 143]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's comparison of the company's 10 USD practice with the 7 USD statutory floor and the 10 USD floor due from 1 September 2012 was not needed to decide August, and the panel noted the company's payment was better than the law required. [Para 132][Para 135]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award states the procedural history twice in inconsistent terms: five of six demands unresolved at conciliation, then four of those five settled at the hearing, leaving one. [Para 19][Para 23] The recital of the second worker's hours differs between passages — eight hours on 17 August appears in the findings but is dropped in the reasons and restored in the order. [Para 96][Para 113][Para 145] Several document lists and evidence entries are duplicated or half-overwritten by OCR, including the July and August work sheets. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 95] Name defects: the employer's two expatriate managers are rendered inconsistently in Latin script across the appearances list — not reproduced here; the six claimants are identified in this brief by employee number and group only, and the shop-steward and union officers appear by role. [Para 1][Para 66] The date of the parties' memorandum of understanding prints in the operative paragraph as "2012" with no day or month. [Para 148]
