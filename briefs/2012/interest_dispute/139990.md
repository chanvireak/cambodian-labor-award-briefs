# Award No. 63/12 — Great On Near Tech Style Factory Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 April 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (You Soun Ty, employer-appointed; Toun Si Phan, worker-appointed; Aing Eng Thong, chair) · Doc ID 139990

## 1. Facts

Great On Near Tech Style Factory Ltd is a garment, embroidery and printing factory at Village 04, Svay Rolum Commune, Sa'ang District, Kandal, employing 1,848 workers. Six workers in the "mok pruy" (garment inspection) section, each with 14 years' service, refused to thumbprint an agreement the employer drafted on disciplinary measures for quality defects. Conciliation at the Ministry of Labour failed on 13 March 2012 and the single unresolved issue came to the panel; the parties agreed at the 9 April 2012 hearing to a binding award. [Para 1][Para 36][Para 38][Para 48][Para 51]

The disputed measure read: on a first serious quality defect the worker is "educated"; on a second, a written warning; three written warnings constitute serious misconduct. The employer had already sent the six workers for a 15-day eye examination from 2 to 17 February 2012 on 50% wages, and four of the ten workers in the section had signed. [Para 10][Para 11][Para 48][Para 61]

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. Whether the employer may require the six inspection workers to sign the disciplinary-measures agreement, and whether the refusal to sign is a rights dispute or an interest dispute? [Para 40][Para 62]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (respondent):** They accept being warned in accordance with procedure and their own fault, but ask the employer to forgo dismissal; they refuse to sign because they fear dismissal, four workers having signed before and been dismissed on a fault; re-work is already done without extra pay as long-standing practice. [Para 40][Para 52][Para 55][Para 56]

**Employer (claimant):** Recurring quality defects cost orders, since buyers cancel on a third defect and late shipments must go by air; the agreement is meant to reduce faults, and the eye examination was to establish whether the workers needed treatment or glasses, not to withhold wages. [Para 42][Para 57][Para 58][Para 60][Para 61]

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejects the employer's demand that the workers sign the disciplinary-measures agreement, and makes no order for costs or compensation. [Para 79][Para 81]

The panel first classified the demand. A rights dispute (វិវាទអំពីសិទ្ធិ) concerns a right contained in law, a contract, a collective agreement or a convention; this demand turned on the 1997 Labour Law and the 2007 Civil Code, so the panel found it a rights dispute. [Para 62][Para 65]

On the substance, Article 311 of the Civil Code provides that a contract is the meeting of wills of two or more persons to create, modify or extinguish an obligation. The panel found no meeting of wills here: the workers never consented to the disciplinary measure the employer proposed, and no party may compel another to agree to what it does not accept. [Para 66][Para 68][Para 69]

The panel then held that a disciplinary measure is part of the employer's right to direct and manage the enterprise under Article 2 of the Labour Law, which the employer may create unilaterally provided it is lawful and reasonable; such a measure is not a contract and creates no obligation on either party. The demand was therefore refused as a request for consent to something the employer could issue on its own. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 76][Para 77]

The panel expressly declined to examine whether the measure was lawful and reasonable, because its legality was not the subject of the complaint. It noted only that the workers retain the right to protest lawfully if a disciplinary measure is unlawful or unreasonable. [Para 75][Para 78]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

An employer's disciplinary scale is an exercise of the management prerogative under Article 2 of the Labour Law, not a contract: it binds nobody by consent and needs none, so a demand that workers sign it is not a demand the panel can grant, and the panel will not review its lawfulness where legality is not pleaded. [Para 74][Para 77][Para 78]

**Obiter dicta:** Workers may lawfully protest a disciplinary measure that is unlawful or unreasonable, and the employer's management right is itself limited to what is lawful and reasonable. [Para 73][Para 75]
