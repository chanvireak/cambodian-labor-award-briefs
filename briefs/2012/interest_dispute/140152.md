# Award No. 137/12 — DaYang (Cambodia) Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 10 August 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Run Saray (president) · Doc ID 140152

## 1. Facts
DaYang (Cambodia) Ltd. is a garment factory on National Road 5, Sangkat Srey Keo, Khan Srey Keo, Phnom Penh, employing about 990 workers, and appeared through its administration chief, Mr P, a company adviser, Mr C, and an administration officer, Mr C [Para 1][Para 46]. The Cambodian Workers' Union Federation filed on 11 June 2012; final conciliation on 6 July 2012 settled 3 of 11 demands, leaving 8 before the Council [Para 23][Para 25][Para 26]. At the hearing of 20 July 2012 four more were settled, leaving issues 2, 5, 6 and 8 [Para 29]. The two applicant unions hold registration certificates and together have about 413 members, which is not a majority of the workforce [Para 47][Para 48].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer has an obligation to pay 2,000 riel for the midday meal taken at 11:30? [Para 49][Para 72]
2. (Issue 5) Whether the employer must pay 2,000 riel for meals when workers work on public holidays? [Para 54][Para 86]
3. (Issue 6) Whether the employer must add USD 8, raising the infant-milk allowance from USD 7 to USD 15 a month? [Para 60][Para 102]
4. (Issue 8) Whether the workers may require the employer to terminate their indefinite-duration contracts, pay 80 per cent severance back to 2003 and sign new contracts? [Para 65][Para 119]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** a lunch allowance of 2,000 riel would encourage the workforce and match what other factories such as SL, Yangtai, Sporttex and Evergreen pay [Para 50][Para 52]. Working on a public holiday is overtime, so the 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance should apply then too [Para 55][Para 56]. Milk at USD 7 no longer covers the market price, so USD 15 is needed [Para 61]. Members have worked since 2003 and should receive 80 per cent severance together with fresh contracts [Para 66].

**Employer (respondent):** it has no capacity to pay a lunch allowance or the extra milk money and asks to keep the existing arrangements [Para 53][Para 62]; the USD 7 milk payment rests on an agreement the workers themselves acknowledge [Para 63]. On holidays it already pays 1,000 riel for meals and 300 per cent of normal wages, and it has no intention of dismissing anyone, paying indefinite-term severance only on transfer or closure [Para 54][Para 69][Para 71].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all four demands were refused, three of them without any examination on the merits [Para 140][Para 144].

On the midday meal the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, no agreement and no collective agreement fixing 2,000 riel, so the claim went beyond the law and was a dispute over interests [Para 72][Para 73]. It then set out the consequence: an interest award becomes a collective agreement binding every worker in the factory for a year, so only a union with majority representativeness may bring such a claim, and it found these unions were not representative [Para 77][Para 81][Para 83][Para 84].

The public-holiday meal claim was treated the same way, and the panel added two reasons of its own: earlier awards hold that working on a public holiday is not overtime, and Ministry Notice No. 041 on the 2,000 riel overtime meal therefore cannot be extended to holidays [Para 87][Para 90][Para 91][Para 99].

On the milk allowance the panel had to deal with res judicata, because the same point was decided in case 12/11-DaYang [Para 103][Para 104]. It held that the three conditions for barring a repeat claim are identity of the disputing party, the same issue, and a complete decision, and found the first unmet because the earlier complainant was the federation acting on transfers from 366 workers, whereas here the complainants are the two plant unions; the bar therefore did not apply [Para 107][Para 110][Para 112][Para 113]. It also observed that awards 88/11 and 89/11 were never registered as a collective agreement, so they could not bind through that route [Para 95][Para 96][Para 97]. On the merits the demand was still an interest claim, because it exceeded the USD 7 fixed by agreement, and it was refused [Para 115][Para 116][Para 118].

The severance demand was the one rights claim. Reading Article 74 of the Labour Law with Articles 311 and 65 of the Civil Code, the panel held that only the parties to the contract may create, vary or extinguish its obligations, so workers cannot force an employer to terminate their contracts; the employer said it had no intention of doing so [Para 122][Para 128][Para 129][Para 132]. The claim was therefore dismissed on the law [Para 138][Para 139].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a demand exceeds what the law, an agreement or a collective agreement gives, it is a dispute over interests, and a union holding well under half the workforce cannot bring it before the panel [Para 73][Para 84]. A previous award bars a repeat claim only where the disputing party, the issue and the decision are all identical, and an award that was never registered as a collective agreement cannot bar through that route [Para 107][Para 110][Para 97]. Workers cannot compel an employer to dissolve their indefinite-duration contracts in order to trigger severance, since only the contracting parties may extinguish a contract [Para 129][Para 133].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that holiday work is not overtime, and that Notice No. 041 therefore cannot be read into holiday meal payments, were offered in support of a claim already disposed of as an interest dispute [Para 87][Para 91].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The earlier DaYang awards are dated inconsistently: 15 August 2011 in the reasoning and 15 November 2011 in the res judicata analysis [Para 58][Para 96]. The two union registration certificates are dated 23 and 17 February 2012 in the findings but 2011 in the documentary list [Para 39][Para 40][Para 47]. The milk figure is printed three ways — "47 dollars 7 dollars", USD 7 and USD 15 — and the reasoning once asks whether the employer must raise it by "80 dollars" [Para 60][Para 61][Para 115]. OCR has dropped clauses at lines 49, 54, 65 and 132, where stray Latin words ("L", "Band", "Will", "Stand") interrupt the Khmer, and the operative section repeats itself [Para 143]. Individual workers and union officers appear only by role.
