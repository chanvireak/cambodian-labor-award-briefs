# Award No. 155/12 — Global Apparel Limited (Arbitration Council, 5 September 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Vira (worker-appointed), Tang Try (chair)

## 1. Facts

The respondent is a garment factory employing roughly 2,235 workers at Trapeang Krasang, Chom Chao, Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh [Para 51]. It trades as Global Apparel Limited, having been registered earlier as Chas Ska Garment (Cambodia) Ltd; the change of name and restatement of articles took effect in 2007, and the company says the legal personality and workforce were unchanged [Para 59][Para 70].

There is no local (enterprise-level) union at the factory. The Cambodia Union Confederation, a confederation, brought the case after 573 workers gave it written authority to represent them in conciliation and arbitration [Para 24][Para 55]. Three of those 573 had supplied names but no thumbprint, so the panel counted 570 [Para 56][Para 57]. The union's own registration receipt for a factory-level union was only issued on 9 August 2012, and the employer did not contest the list of 570 [Para 54][Para 58].

Conciliation at the Ministry's labour dispute department failed on all five demands on 25 July 2012, and the unresolved points were referred to the Council on 6 August 2012 [Para 19][Para 20]. A hearing was held on 16 August 2012 [Para 17]. The parties elected a non-binding award, and agreed to extend the deadline for its issue to 5 September 2012 [Para 26][Para 27].

One demand was recorded but never reached: the workers originally asked that the date of first employment on their cards be corrected, and withdrew that request at the hearing, leaving only the claim for termination with statutory payments [Para 60]. The panel therefore made no ruling on the card dates. Separately, 326 workers signed the severance claim list, of whom 28 gave a name without a thumbprint, leaving 298 whose claim the panel considered [Para 66][Para 67][Para 68].

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. (Issue 1) Whether the 2007 change of company name entitled the workers to have their contracts terminated with payment of final wages, accrued annual leave, notice pay, dismissal damages and medical costs, or obliged the employer to correct the first-employment date on their cards, as the workers argued at [Para 59] and [Para 60]?
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer should raise the monthly travel allowance by USD 9, to a total of USD 15 [Para 75]?
3. (Issue 3) Whether overtime worked on Sundays and public holidays should be paid at four times the normal rate rather than twice [Para 80]?
4. (Issue 4) Whether the meal allowance for overtime between 15:00 and 17:00 should be increased by 2,000 riel on top of each 4,000 riel paid [Para 86]?
5. (Issue 5) Whether the employer discriminated against the 14 elected employee delegates by dealing with other worker representatives instead of them [Para 90][Para 92]?

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimants):** the 2007 change of name was never notified to them, so the employer should terminate the indeterminate contracts and pay the five statutory items, or at least record the true start date on their cards [Para 59][Para 64]. Travel money of USD 6 a month is not enough to rent a room or a vehicle, so it should rise to USD 15 [Para 75][Para 76]. Sunday and holiday work is done on their rest days and with no food seller in front of the factory, and anyone who refuses such overtime is denied further overtime, so it should be paid at ×4 [Para 82][Para 83]. They also cited 36 workers who worked Sunday 8 July 2012 [Para 84]. The 2,000 riel meal allowance no longer buys a meal [Para 88]. On discrimination, discussions should always be with the employee delegates and not with ad hoc representatives [Para 90].

**Employer (respondent):** the name change did not alter the legal person, seniority or benefits, and each worker's card and attendance record still show the true first date of employment [Para 70][Para 73]. The 570 workers did not object to the employer's account [Para 74]. Travel allowance has been USD 6 since April 2012 and the company cannot afford more [Para 77][Para 78]. Holiday overtime is paid at ×2 plus 1, and Sunday overtime has not been required since April 2012 [Para 80][Para 81]. The meal claim is unaffordable [Para 89]. There is no discrimination: the company cooperates with delegates, but in one case 200 workers refused to deal through their delegates, and no delegate has ever sent a complaint [Para 93][Para 95][Para 96][Para 97].

## 4. Award and reasoning

On representation, the panel first satisfied itself that the confederation could act. Reading article 19 of Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council, it held that a party may be represented by another person holding written authority, and that the 570 written authorisations met that test [Para 100][Para 101][Para 102][Para 103]. This disposed of the employer's standing objection without deciding the merits.

On Issue 1, the panel found the change of name was lawfully recorded, that the Ministry of Commerce accepted the new name and articles in 2007, and that the company remained fully liable for claims arising from the change [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63]. Because the employer retained seniority and benefits, and the workers did not contest that, the panel rejected the severance claim [Para 73][Para 74][Para 157].

On Issue 2, the panel treated the travel allowance as a matter of interest rather than right, and, applying its usual approach to interest disputes, declined to consider the claim for an extra USD 9 [Para 158]. The same reasoning was applied to Issue 4, the meal allowance, which the panel found was not required by any law, collective agreement or other instrument [Para 141][Para 142][Para 144][Para 160].

On Issue 3, the panel rejected the ×4 claim, and also declined to consider the union's evidence about 36 workers on Sunday 8 July 2012, because that union was not a party to the case [Para 85][Para 140][Para 159].

On Issue 5, the panel found the workers had produced no specific facts of discrimination, noted that article 297 lets workers complain directly to the employer without going through delegates, and rejected the claim [Para 151][Para 152][Para 154][Para 155][Para 156][Para 161].

**Outcome:** all five demands rejected. The award is non-binding and becomes final if no party objects in writing within eight days of notification [Para 162].

## 5. Ratio decidendi

A change of company name that preserves the legal person, seniority and benefits does not trigger termination and severance; the successor keeps the liabilities but the contracts continue [Para 61][Para 73]. Claims that are purely about improving conditions — travel and meal allowances — are interest disputes, which the Council declines to adjudicate unless a law, collective agreement or other instrument requires the benefit [Para 158][Para 160]. Evidence tendered by a union that is not a party to the reference will not be considered [Para 159]. A discrimination complaint fails where the complainant offers no specific facts, and article 297 permits workers to approach the employer directly, so direct dealing is not itself discrimination [Para 152][Para 155]. Written authority from each worker satisfies the representation requirement for a confederation acting for a group [Para 101][Para 103].

## 6. Source defects disclosed

The OCR of this award is badly corrupted. Header and address blocks are repeated three times at the start, and page numbers and scanner artefacts are embedded mid-sentence throughout [Para 1][Para 2][Para 3][Para 16][Para 86][Para 163]. Latin fragments appear inside Khmer sentences, sometimes replacing words [Para 27][Para 40][Para 45][Para 99][Para 101][Para 142]. The company name is spelled several different ways in the same document [Para 1][Para 51][Para 59][Para 61]. The list of documents received is duplicated with different wording [Para 45][Para 46][Para 49][Para 50]. The signature block is garbled and includes Latin text where arbitrator names should be [Para 163]. Substantively, the award records at line 26 that the parties chose a non-binding award, yet the document list says the parties agreed on a binding award on rights disputes [Para 26][Para 49], and the panel never resolves that contradiction. The panel also does not address the employer's own statement that worker cards are replaced annually since 2010, which sits awkwardly with its finding that first-employment dates are accurately recorded [Para 71][Para 73].
