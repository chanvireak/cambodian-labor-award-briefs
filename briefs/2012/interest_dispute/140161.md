# Award No. 153/12 — Kbal Koh Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 23 August 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), On Non (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (chair) · Doc ID 140161

## 1. Facts
Kbal Koh Garment Co., Ltd has operated a garment factory in Steung Mean Chey, Phnom Penh since 2001 with about 975 workers [Para 47][Para 48]. The CAWDU-affiliated union at the factory, with 170 members, filed 17 demands on working conditions on 27 June 2012; conciliation on 27 July 2012 left seven unsettled, and those seven were referred to the panel [Para 21][Para 22][Para 50]. At the hearing of 13 August 2012 the parties reached binding agreement on Issues 1, 2, 5 and 7, and the workers withdrew Issues 4 and 6, leaving only Issue 3 for adjudication [Para 26][Para 27]. Because both sides had signed a memorandum of understanding of 28 September 2010, the panel divided its decision into rights and interest disputes, the rights portion being binding on the parties and the interest portion non-binding [Para 28][Para 29].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay a USD 20 monthly milk allowance to women who have just given birth, from birth until the child is 18 months old? [Para 51]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the allowance should run from birth to 18 months because mothers of small children are few and face real hardship; other factories pay USD 17 a month or two tins of milk powder, citing Yong Va as an example [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53].

**Employer (respondent):** it has no capacity to pay; it has already built a nursery and a breastfeeding room and gives mothers one hour a day (30 minutes in the morning, 30 in the afternoon); no law requires a milk allowance, and paying one would undercut the policy of encouraging breastfeeding [Para 54][Para 55][Para 56][Para 57].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider the USD 20 monthly milk allowance claim [Para 70][Para 71].

The panel first classified the demand. Following its earlier awards No. 05/11 (Im Ve, Branch 1) and No. 13/11 (Gold Kamvy Meat), it defined a rights dispute as one concerning an entitlement set by law, a contract or a collective agreement; finding no Labour Law provision, collective agreement or contract fixing USD 20 a month for mothers of children up to 18 months, it held the demand to be an interest dispute [Para 58][Para 61][Para 62].

It then applied its standing rule for interest disputes: Prakas No. 099 article 43 makes such an award a collective agreement binding on all workers in the enterprise for one year, which would strip non-members of the right to strike over the same subject, so only a union with majority representativeness may bring such a claim [Para 64][Para 65][Para 66]. Following Award No. 169/11 (Four Fortune) and Awards No. 02/11 (Pou Yien) and No. 66/11 (In Hansung), it found this union, with 170 members against roughly 975 workers, lacked that status and refused to decide the demand on the merits [Para 67][Para 68][Para 70]. The refusal was issued as a non-binding interest award, final after eight days absent written objection to the Minister [Para 72].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a benefit that no law, contract or collective agreement grants is an interest dispute, and the panel will decline it on the merits unless the union bringing it represents more than half the enterprise's workforce — because an interest award operates as a collective agreement binding all workers, including those who did not claim it [Para 61][Para 66][Para 68].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted, without deciding on it, that providing milk money would work against the policy of promoting breastfeeding, and recorded the existing nursery, breastfeeding room and one hour of nursing time as the employer's current practice [Para 55][Para 57].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Line 69 of the source is badly corrupted: a block of unrelated Khmer words has been dropped into the middle of the sentence on the union's legal capacity, so that reasoning survives only in part and the sentence has been reconstructed from its opening and closing clauses [Para 69]. Six further demands are recorded in the file without reasons, since they were settled or withdrawn at the hearing rather than decided [Para 26]. The demand text prints the milk allowance as "២ ២០" (two, twenty) dollars, a doubled numeral left by OCR, and the operative section repeats USD 20 [Para 6][Para 71]. Worker and representative names appear in inconsistent Khmer spellings and one representative's name is partly lost in the signature block, which also carries stray Latin-script fragments; the brief therefore names no individuals [Para 73].
