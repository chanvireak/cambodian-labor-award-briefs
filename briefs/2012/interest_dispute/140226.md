# Award No. 230/12 — Im Vay Garment International (Kampong Chhnang) (Arbitration Council, 24 December 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kaov Thach; Tuon Siphan; Pen Bunhea presiding) · Doc ID 140226

## 1. Facts
Im Vay Garment International, Kampong Chhnang branch, is a garment manufacturer employing about 4,000 workers [Para 47]. The Free Workers' Union of the Im & Vay weaving factory, registered in 2011, put forward 20 demands to improve working conditions; conciliation at the provincial labour department failed on 5 points, which were referred to the Council [Para 15][Para 48]. At the hearing the workers withdrew issues 2, 4 and 5, leaving issues 1 and 3 for decision [Para 19][Para 20]. The parties had signed a memorandum of understanding of 3 October 2012 making decisions on rights disputes binding between them [Para 22], and chose a non-binding award for interest disputes [Para 25].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the USD 5 monthly health-care allowance must be added to the basic wage so that it enters the calculation of the 5% end-of-contract indemnity for fixed-duration contracts of 3 or 6 months? [Para 49]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the employer may deduct the USD 10 monthly regular attendance bonus pro rata to the days of special leave it had itself authorised? [Para 58]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Article 73 of the Labour Law requires all allowances received for the work to be added together, so the health-care allowance forms part of the wage on which the 5% indemnity is computed; Notification No. 206 of 13 December 2011 contradicts that article [Para 52][Para 56]. On issue 3, past practice gave the attendance bonus pro rata to actual attendance, and the employer's own notices confirm this [Para 60][Para 63].
**Employer (respondent):** Notification No. 206, point 1, states expressly that the USD 5 health-care allowance is not consolidated into the basic wage, and Article 103 does not list it among wage components [Para 53][Para 55][Para 81]. On issue 3, Notification No. 230 conditions the bonus on regular attendance "without absence", so an absent worker earns no bonus [Para 68].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim to fold the USD 5 health-care allowance into the basic wage was dismissed [Para 135], while on issue 3 the panel ordered the employer to deduct the regular attendance bonus only pro rata to the days of special leave authorised by the employer [Para 136]. The section 1 decision binds the parties once, by virtue of the memorandum of understanding [Para 137].

On issue 1 the panel first classified the claim as a rights dispute, because it turned on entitlements written in the Labour Law and in a ministerial notification rather than on a new benefit [Para 73]. Reading Article 73, it held that the end-of-contract indemnity is proportionate to the wage and to the duration of the contract, with a floor of 5% of the wages the worker received during the contract [Para 75][Para 76][Para 78]. The remaining question was whether the health-care allowance is part of "wage". Point 1 of Notification No. 206 states that the allowance "is not added so as to become a wage proper (basic wage)", and Article 103 of the 1997 Labour Law does not list it among wage components [Para 80][Para 82]. The panel therefore concluded that the allowance falls outside the indemnity calculation and dismissed the demand [Para 83][Para 85].

On issue 3 the panel again found a rights dispute, the claim resting on Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 [Para 89]. It noted that the employer had invoked Award No. 209/12 as authority for stopping the pro-rata deduction, but read that award as declining to review enforcement of Award No. 163/12 for want of jurisdiction, not as overruling it [Para 94][Para 95]. Notification No. 230 replaced Notification No. 041 and added the words "without absence" from 1 September 2012 [Para 98][Para 99]. The panel found no authoritative interpretation of that phrase [Para 104][Para 105]: the absences defined by law — annual, special, sick, maternity, public holiday and weekly rest — do not permit any deduction, so the bonus of USD 10 is payable in full for them [Para 106][Para 107], while other authorised special leave is not defined by law and must be assessed case by case [Para 108][Para 109]. Where the employer has already authorised leave, the worker cannot be treated as absent [Para 117]. The attendance bonus is nonetheless a wage component as a sum paid as a bonus under Article 103 [Para 125][Para 127]; authorised leave suspends the contract under Articles 71 and 72, so no wage and no bonus is owed for those days [Para 128][Para 131][Para 132] — but the employer may deduct only the share corresponding to the leave days, not the whole bonus [Para 133][Para 134].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A bonus fixed by ministerial notification as a general rule for a whole industry, rather than left to the employer's discretion, is a wage component; authorised special leave therefore counts as attendance for eligibility, but the bonus accrues only in proportion to days actually worked, and an employer may deduct no more than the days of leave it authorised. [Para 101][Para 127][Para 133]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the employer arbitrator's reading of Award No. 209/12 confused a refusal to exercise jurisdiction over enforcement with a rejection of the underlying order [Para 95]. The employer arbitrator appended a dissent, arguing that a worker absent even with permission cannot satisfy "without absence" and should lose the bonus for those days [Para 148][Para 149].
