# Award No. 205/12 — Sou Tong Fang Group Ying Kan (Cambodia) Garment Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 14 November 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chiv Phirom, appointed by the employer; An Nan, appointed by the workers; Kong Phalha, chair) · Doc ID 140197

## 1. Facts
The company is a garment factory at Tuol Sangke, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, registered in 1997 and employing about 620 workers. [Para 40][Para 41] Ms L had worked there since 28 November 1999 as a panel checker in the sewing section on an indefinite-term contract. [Para 49][Para 50] The employer dismissed her on 31 August 2012 under Article 10 of its registered internal rules, after a series of written warnings for checking the wrong trousers, bringing food into the factory and a scuffle with a security guard; it paid her last month's wage of 87 USD and her annual leave but no other severance. [Para 47][Para 51][Para 55][Para 68]

The federation filed seven demands, of which six — voluntary overtime, a demand that the company stop deducting wages for the union, toilet hygiene, forced labour, verbal abuse by section managers, and dismissal of sick workers holding medical certificates — were settled at the hearing of 26 October 2012. [Para 15][Para 17] The panel therefore decided Issue 1 alone, and both parties agreed the award would be binding even though the case concerned both rights and interests. [Para 18][Para 20][Para 39] At the hearing Ms L dropped her claim for reinstatement and asked only for statutory severance. [Para 45]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer dismissed Ms L lawfully under the 1997 Labour Law, and if the dismissal was not for serious misconduct, what severance it owes her? [Para 76][Para 91]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** through the federation, Ms L no longer sought reinstatement but claimed the statutory termination payments, since the employer had given her only her final wage and annual leave and had ended the contract on the day it notified her. [Para 45][Para 48][Para 98]
**Employer (respondent):** the dismissal was made under Article 10 of the internal rules, which allow termination where a worker repeats the same minor fault three times; she had been warned repeatedly, and it had already paid her final wage and leave entitlement. [Para 51][Para 75][Para 86]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the dismissal itself was upheld, but the employer was ordered to pay three months' wages and benefits in lieu of notice, six months' wages and allowances as dismissal compensation, payment for unused annual leave, and the outstanding final wage; the claim for moral damages under Article 91 was dismissed. [Para 121][Para 123][Para 124]

On the lawfulness of the dismissal the panel applied Article 74, which lets an employer terminate an indefinite-term contract at will provided it gives notice stating a reason going to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the needs of the enterprise. [Para 77][Para 78] It then read Article 10 of the internal rules, registered with the labour inspectorate under No. 030 of 25 March 1999, which escalates from oral education to written warning to suspension to dismissal after a third repetition of a minor fault. [Para 68][Para 80][Para 85] The panel accepted that Ms L had been warned many times over the same minor faults, that she admitted the January, May and August incidents, and that the employer therefore had a suitable reason to dismiss her. [Para 87][Para 89][Para 90]

The panel separated the reason for dismissal from the procedure that follows it. Because the ground was repeated minor misconduct and not the serious misconduct listed in Article 83, the notice obligation in Articles 75, 77 and 82 still applied, and the employer had dismissed her on the day it notified her. [Para 91][Para 95][Para 96][Para 98] Her service from 28 November 1999 to 31 August 2012 exceeded ten years, so Article 75 set the notice period at three months, and Article 77 required wages and benefits for that unserved period. [Para 100][Para 101] Because she had more than twelve months' service, Article 89 gave dismissal compensation at fifteen days' wages and allowances per year, capped at six months, and the panel awarded the cap. [Para 101][Para 103] Article 166 entitled her to cash for annual leave she had not taken, and Article 116 required the final wage and all allowances within 48 hours, which had still not been paid. [Para 107][Para 109][Para 117][Para 120]

Moral damages were refused: Article 91 allows them only where a contract is terminated without proper reason, and the panel had already found a proper reason, so the worker had no entitlement. [Para 110][Para 115][Para 116]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A registered internal rule that permits dismissal after three repetitions of the same minor fault supplies the "suitable reason" Article 74 requires, so the dismissal is lawful on the merits; but where the ground is minor misconduct rather than the serious misconduct listed in Article 83, the employer still owes the full statutory consequences of a termination without the required notice — notice-in-lieu under Article 77, dismissal compensation under Article 89 up to the six-month ceiling, unused annual leave under Article 166 and the final wage under Article 116. Moral damages under Article 91 remain unavailable once a proper reason is found. [Para 90][Para 95][Para 99][Para 103][Para 116]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's approval of the parties' agreement to accept a binding award covering both rights and interests disputes, and its remark that the six settled demands fell away, are procedural observations that do not affect the orders. [Para 18][Para 20]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The worker's identity number is printed with a hyphen throughout as "5-68" and appears in the warning letters only in that form, so the warnings can be matched to her solely by the number rather than by name.
- The internal rules are dated 25 March 1999 in the reasons but the registration certificate is cited as 25 March 199%) in the evidence list, a corrupted year that cannot be read as printed. [Para 24][Para 68]
- The employer appears under at least four transliterations of its name in the style of cause, the registration certificate and the statutes, including "Sou Tong Fang Group Ying Kan", "Sou Tong Fang Gamint", "Sou Tong Fang Gruob Ieng Khang" and "Sou Tong Fang Faing". [Para 22][Para 23][Para 40]
- The number of faults relied on oscillates between three and four: the reasons describe four incidents, the internal rule speaks of three repetitions, and the panel's conclusion refers to "three or more". [Para 51][Para 85][Para 90]
- The Article 74 and Article 77 quotations are interrupted by embedded Latin-script OCR debris and stray words such as "The" and "Sang" at the head of several paragraphs. [Para 76][Para 78][Para 91]
- The award date appears only as 14 November 2012 in the header; the hearing was 26 October 2012, and the conciliation report is dated 10 October 2012 but transmitted on 18 October 2012. [Para 1][Para 11][Para 13]
