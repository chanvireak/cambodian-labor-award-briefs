# Award No. 144/12 — E Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 27 September 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Tuon Si Phan (worker-appointed), Run Saray (chair) · Doc ID 140160

## 1. Facts
E Garment Co., Ltd is a garment and apparel factory at Sway Rolum, Kandal, employing about 3,800 workers, with a CAWDU-affiliated union on site [Para 211]. In June 2012 the union filed ten demands on working conditions; conciliation settled two, and the remaining eight were referred to the panel on 20 July 2012 [Para 26]. Before the merits the panel examined the union's standing and found that the second-term leadership had expired on 27 July 2012 and the third-term leadership had never been recognised by the Ministry, so only the thirteen workers actually present could pursue claims [Para 283][Para 292]. A separate unresolved strand — the demand that 33 activists be rehired under the 2 May 2009 agreement — was recorded but no claimant appeared for it [Para 397].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must supply at least two sets of work clothing per worker per year? [Para 216]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must raise the regular attendance bonus to USD 12 per month? [Para 221]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay a USD 15 monthly travel and accommodation allowance? [Para 225]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must pay a 10 riel meal allowance for overtime worked from 4 p.m. to 7 a.m.? [Para 229]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the dismissal of the eight union leaders was lawful, and whether they must be reinstated with back pay? [Para 235]
6. (Issue 6) Whether the employer must grant seven days' special leave on full pay without deducting it from annual leave? [Para 258]
7. (Issue 7) Whether the employer must pay a USD 20 monthly milk allowance to women who have given birth? [Para 263]
8. (Issue 8) Whether the employer must reinstate the 33 workers named in point 2 of the 2 May 2009 agreement? [Para 397]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Prakas 307 article 6 obliges the employer to give two sets of clothing free each year, and wages are too low to buy them; other factories do better on bonuses, travel and meal money; the eight leaders were dismissed without the procedure the Labour Law requires, and the 2009 agreement is valid because its signatory had authority to bind the company [Para 218][Para 223][Para 235][Para 271].

**Employer (respondent):** it has no capacity to meet the demands; it will pay what Ministry Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 requires (USD 10 attendance bonus, USD 7 travel) from 1 September 2012; the 2009 agreement is invalid for want of signature authority, company seal and registration, and was extracted under pressure from the international union and the buyer [Para 220][Para 222][Para 267].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — clothing ordered; the bonus, travel, meal and milk claims refused; two union leaders reinstated with compensation equal to three months' base wages; the employer ordered to stop deducting special leave from a future year's annual leave [Para 400][Para 405][Para 408].

On Issue 1 the panel applied Prakas 307 article 6 (14 December 2007) and followed its own Award No. 74/09 (Four Twin), holding the obligation unconditional, and ordered two sets of clothing a year [Para 298][Para 299].

Issues 2, 3, 4 and 7 were treated together as interest disputes. On each the panel found no statute, Prakas, notification, collective agreement or individual contract fixing the sum claimed, so the demand exceeded what the law grants and the dispute was one of interest [Para 302][Para 303][Para 318][Para 323]. It then held that an interest dispute may only be brought by a union with majority representativeness in the enterprise, which this union lacked, and declined to decide those demands [Para 312][Para 315][Para 316].

On Issue 5 the panel split the eight leaders. Two were protected union leaders, and article 293 of the Labour Law requires the labour inspector's prior approval for their dismissal; the employer dismissed them on 1 August 2010 and obtained approval only on 19 August 2010, so the dismissal was void under Prakas 305 article 4 and reinstatement followed [Para 333][Para 348][Para 352][Para 353]. Back pay was refused because the claimants gave no reason for delay since 2010; the panel substituted compensation of three months' base wages under Prakas 099 article 34 [Para 358][Para 362]. The other two were ordinary workers, not protected representatives, so article 293 did not apply, and their dismissal for serious misconduct in a factory fight was lawful under articles 83 and 82 [Para 364][Para 371][Para 372].

On Issue 6 the panel read article 171 with Prakas 267 (11 October 2001): special leave may be deducted from unused annual leave, but may not be charged against a future year's entitlement; it refused the wage-retention claim and ordered the practice stopped [Para 379][Para 382][Para 389][Para 391].

Issue 8 was refused for want of any claimant present or written authority from the 33 workers [Para 398][Para 399].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union whose leadership registration has lapsed cannot carry an interest dispute as the enterprise's representative union, and the panel will decline those demands; but individual workers present at the hearing may still pursue their own individual claims without written authority [Para 288][Para 290][Para 315]. Where dismissal of a protected union leader precedes the labour inspector's approval, it is void and reinstatement is ordered, with compensation in lieu of back pay where the claim was left dormant without explanation [Para 349][Para 352][Para 358].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed generally that it orders back pay from dismissal to reinstatement whenever a dismissal is procedurally defective or lacks a proper reason, and that this was displaced only by the claimants' unexplained delay [Para 356]. One arbitrator appended a dissent, arguing the employer's dismissal procedure was correct under articles 26, 293 and 295 [Para 414][Para 422].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The operative section mislabels Issue 3: it refuses an "attendance bonus of USD 15 per month" where the reasons refused a USD 15 travel and accommodation allowance, so the dispositive text and the reasoning name different benefits [Para 317][Para 402]. Several worker names appear under two or three different Khmer spellings between the evidence schedule and the findings, indicating OCR or transliteration drift; the brief therefore identifies individuals by role only. Page markers and running headers are embedded mid-sentence in many lines, and the dissent's citation of article 26 is partly garbled [Para 241][Para 422]. The award date is consistent at 27 September 2012 in both header and signature block [Para 424].
