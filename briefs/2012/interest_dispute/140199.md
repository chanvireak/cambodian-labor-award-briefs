# Award No. 204/12 — Cambo Chotap (Ltd) Garment (Arbitration Council, 7 December 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Nan, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · Doc ID 140199

## 1. Facts
Cambo Chotap (Ltd) Garment, the employer party, is a garment factory at Kantaok, Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh, employing about 2,200 workers [Para 1], with a registered collective agreement of 25 September 2012 that fixes a monthly attendance bonus of 10 USD. [Para 39][Para 51][Para 52][Para 183] On the claimant side the Khmer Workers' Power Union Federation and six other federations, later five, filed thirteen demands after a strike [Para 1], and at the hearings of 25 October and 7 November 2012 six of the twelve unresolved points were settled, leaving Issues 1, 3, 4, 5, 11 and 12 for decision. [Para 21][Para 26][Para 27] Only five of the seven federations appeared, and none of their factory-level unions held a registration certificate. [Para 28][Para 30][Para 55]

The strike ran from 28 September to 4 October 2012 and was called without prior notice to the employer and without exhausting conciliation. [Para 101][Para 106][Para 244]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must raise the basic wage by 10 USD, to 71 USD for probationary workers and 76 USD for full-status workers? [Para 57][Para 116]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay a further 2,000 riel meal allowance for voluntary overtime worked from 4 to 6 p.m.? [Para 63][Para 133]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the attendance bonus must be deducted only in proportion to the days of absence that the employer lawfully authorised? [Para 66][Para 142]
4. (Issue 5) Whether the employer must provide one midday meal or a meal allowance of 4,000 riel per day? [Para 82][Para 207]
5. (Issue 11) Whether the overtime meal allowance must be paid weekly rather than monthly? [Para 99][Para 213]
6. (Issue 12) Whether the workers are entitled to their wages and attendance bonus for the days of the strike? [Para 101][Para 231]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** wages no longer cover daily living as prices rise and Article 104 requires a decent wage; 2,000 riel does not buy a meal; the company deducts the whole attendance bonus for a single authorised absence; and weekly payment of the overtime meal allowance is needed because families borrow money at month-end. [Para 59][Para 64][Para 68][Para 99]
**Employer (respondent):** it already pays above the legal minimum and grants benefits better than the law — a 13 USD attendance bonus, 10 USD travel and lodging, and 3 USD monthly meal support — and any increase must await a decision on the majority of workers' wishes; the strike was unlawful and no wages are owed for it. [Para 60][Para 70][Para 107][Para 234]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must deduct the attendance bonus only in proportion to the authorised days of absence and must pay the overtime meal allowance weekly; the wage increase, the extra 2,000 riel, the 4,000 riel midday meal and the strike wages were refused. [Para 249][Para 251][Para 248][Para 250][Para 252]

On the wage claims the panel found that the employer already paid 61 USD to probationary and 66 USD to full-status workers, above the 56 and 61 USD set by Ministry Notification No. 049/10, so the demand exceeded what the law guaranteed and was an interest dispute. [Para 116][Para 118][Para 119][Para 120] Because none of the claimant unions held a registration certificate, they lacked the representative status Article 277 requires for bringing interest disputes, and the panel declined to consider Issues 1, 3 and 5. [Para 124][Para 128][Para 131][Para 132][Para 207][Para 208]

On Issue 4 the panel worked through Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 and the collective agreement, distinguishing absences that the law itself excuses — annual, special, sick, maternity, public-holiday and weekly rest — from other authorised absences. [Para 147][Para 152][Para 153] Applying Articles 71(6) and 72, it held that an authorised absence suspends the contract and the wage, so the employer owes no bonus for those days but may not strip the bonus entirely; the deduction must be proportional to the days absent. [Para 177][Para 178][Para 179][Para 180][Para 181] It fixed the monthly bonus at 13 USD, the more favourable of the practice and the 10 USD in the agreement, under Article 13. [Para 185][Para 195]

On Issue 11 the panel found that the notification requires meal money of 2,000 riel per overtime day but is silent on timing, and that a reasonable interval must let the worker actually buy food for the overtime session; given the workers' stated need, weekly payment was ordered. [Para 213][Para 214][Para 218][Para 228][Para 229]

On Issue 12 the panel applied Article 332, under which the contract is suspended on strike days and no wage is payable, and Article 324 and Article 320(4), which require seven days' notice and prior exhaustion of conciliation. [Para 231][Para 240][Para 241] The strike met neither requirement, so wages were not owed and, because the strikers did not attend work regularly, the attendance bonus was also lost. [Para 242][Para 244][Para 245][Para 246]

Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented on Issue 4, reasoning that a bonus is a discretionary reward whose owner is the employer, that the condition is full attendance, and that a worker who does not meet the condition cannot claim a proportionate share. [Para 256][Para 264][Para 272][Para 274]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus that the law treats as part of wages may be reduced only in proportion to the days of absence the employer has authorised, because an authorised absence suspends the contract under Articles 71 and 72 and removes the wage for those days without extinguishing the rest of the entitlement; the employer may not forfeit the whole monthly bonus for one or two authorised days. Conversely, an unlawful strike suspends the contract under Article 332 and forfeits both wages and the attendance bonus, since the workers did not attend work regularly. [Para 178][Para 180][Para 245]

**Obiter dicta:** the dissent's dictionary survey of the word "bonus" and its hypothetical of counting to a thousand for a reward are illustrations of the discretionary nature of a bonus, not findings binding on the panel. [Para 257][Para 265]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The operative clause on Issue 12 dates the strike "from 28 November to 04 October 2012", while the facts and reasons put it from 28 September to 4 October; the award is internally inconsistent on the start date. [Para 244][Para 252]
- The number of claimant federations shifts between seven and five, and the list of names in the reasoning omits two of the seven named at the head of the award. [Para 21][Para 53][Para 54]
- Only five of the seven federations attended, yet the facts section says five federations are the claimants without explaining which two dropped out. [Para 28][Para 53]
- The attendance bonus is printed as 10 USD in the collective agreement, 13 USD in company practice, and 10 USD in Notification No. 230; the panel's choice of 13 USD leaves three figures in the record. [Para 147][Para 183][Para 195]
- The employer's name appears as "Cambo Chotap", "Cambo Chhotap" and "Cambo Chotab" within the same document, including the style of cause and the registration certificate. [Para 1][Para 36][Para 51]
- Long passages are replaced by Latin-script debris, and the operative paragraph on Issue 5 repeats itself mid-sentence. [Para 3][Para 250]
