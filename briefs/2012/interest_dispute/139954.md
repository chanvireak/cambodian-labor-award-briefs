# Award No. 32/12 — Global Apparels Limited (Arbitration Council, 14 March 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phallack chair; Ing Sothy, Liv Sovanna) · Doc ID 139954

## 1. Facts
Global Apparels Limited, at Trapang Tuol Village, Kambol Commune, Angsnuol District, Kandal Province, employs 2,570 workers; the claimant is the local union of the Workers' Union Federation (WUF). [Para 15] A complaint of 13 February 2012 went to the Kandal Department of Labour Disputes, conciliation on 13 February 2012 resolved two of four issues, and non-conciliation report No. 180 KB/RK/VK of 15 February 2012 reached the Secretariat on 17 February 2012; the hearing was on 1 March 2012. [Para 5][Para 6] Two demands survived: the transfer of Mr Y out of the shipping section, and a meal allowance with overtime pay for overtime worked. [Para 2][Para 3]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must transfer Mr Y from the shipping section? [Para 2][Para 22]
2. Whether the employer must provide a meal allowance and pay overtime for overtime work? [Para 3][Para 29]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the workers demanded that the employer transfer Mr Y out of the shipping section, and that it provide a meal allowance and overtime pay for overtime work. [Para 2][Para 3] At the hearing the workers stated that the 13 claimant workers in the shipping section had resigned from their positions. [Para 18][Para 29]
**Employer (respondent):** the employer refused to transfer Mr Y, and on the meal and overtime demand said only that it would discuss the matter with the director first. [Para 2][Para 3] It appeared at the hearing and agreed to binding arbitration of both disputes. [Para 7][Para 12]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider the transfer demand and rejected the meal allowance and overtime demand; the award is binding, final and immediately enforceable because the parties chose binding arbitration. [Para 31]
The panel first classified both demands as rights disputes, applying its established definition of a rights dispute as one concerning entitlements in the law, an agreement or a collective agreement, and finding that these concerned entitlements in the Labour Law. [Para 20][Para 21]
On the transfer demand, the panel noted that the same issue on the same facts had already been brought before it and decided in Award No. 175/11-Global Apparels of 24 January 2012, which rejected the demand. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 22] It acknowledged that no law imposes res judicata on the arbitral process and that it therefore has discretion whether to reopen an issue. [Para 25][Para 26] It then set out the three conditions on which it will decline a demand — identical disputant parties, the same issue already brought, and a prior ruling on the facts — and found all three met, the claimant workers being the same party as in 175/11. [Para 26][Para 28] Consistency with the earlier ruling required it to decline the demand. [Para 28]
On the meal and overtime demand, the panel found that the 13 claimant workers in the shipping section had resigned, so that no claimant workers remained in the case, and rejected the demand. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 29][Para 30]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Although no statute binds the arbitral process to res judicata, the panel will decline to reconsider a demand where the identical disputant parties were involved in the previous dispute, the same issue was already brought before it, and it has ruled on the facts; the purpose is to avoid conflicting results on one issue and to close the dispute with a final decision at first instance. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 28] A demand also falls away where every claimant worker named in it has resigned, leaving no claimant in the case. [Para 19][Para 30]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that, under the Memorandum of Understanding on Improving Industrial Relations in the Garment Industry of 28 September 2010, rights disputes go to binding arbitration while interest disputes may go to non-binding arbitration and be objected to, and that in this case the parties chose binding arbitration for both disputes. [Para 9][Para 10][Para 11][Para 12]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award date is 14 March 2012 in the header but the decision is dated 1 March 2012, the hearing date. [Para 1][Para 31] The decision and order labels the two surviving demands "Issue 3" and "Issue 6", although the issues in dispute are numbered 1 and 2 and the record elsewhere speaks of four issues, two of which were resolved. [Para 6][Para 31] The English text is an unofficial translation with systematic OCR word-joins throughout — "themployer", "meallowance", "athearing", "remaing", "outling", "wit he" — and the opening quotation mark of "res judicata" is lost at para 24. [Para 1][Para 15][Para 24] The evidence section was omitted from the English version, so the panel's factual findings cannot be checked against the underlying documents. [Para 14][Para 15] The non-conciliation report is dated 15 February 2012 but the last conciliation session is given as 13 February 2012, and the complaint and the session share that date. [Para 5][Para 6]
