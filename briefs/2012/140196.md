# Award No. 200/12 — Master & Freng (Cambodia) Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 15 November 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Nan, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · Doc ID 140196

## 1. Facts
Master & Freng (Cambodia) Co Ltd is a garment factory at Ang Snuol, Kandal, employing about 778 workers and operating since 21 March 2011. [Para 90][Para 91] A union local of the Cambodia Democratic Garment Workers Federation (C.CAWDU) held its leadership election, notified the employer of the result on 11 July 2012 and applied for registration on 16 July 2012; it was still unregistered at the second hearing on 25 October 2012. [Para 110][Para 112][Para 228] Between 4 and 9 August 2012 the employer dismissed five workers who were union members, and the federation, holding written authority from 147 workers, sought their reinstatement with wages from dismissal to return and an order ending anti-union discrimination. [Para 93][Para 101][Para 200] Two further claimants named at conciliation told the hearing they no longer pursued their claims, and the panel did not decide them. [Para 101]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must take back the five dismissed workers and pay them wages from the date of dismissal to the date of their return? [Para 101][Para 200]
2. (Issue 2) Whether those dismissals amounted to anti-union discrimination prohibited by Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law? [Para 200][Para 201]

The panel recorded that the two issues were related and that the workers had agreed to merge them, and it decided them together. [Para 20][Para 103]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** all five were union members or candidates, the dismissals were acts of anti-union discrimination, the plant-closure and discipline reasons were pretexts, and the union president was dismissed without the labour inspector's authorisation. [Para 138][Para 166][Para 172]
**Employer (respondent):** each dismissal rested on its own cause — insubordination and incitement by the president, refusal to re-sew garments by the candidate, incompetence during probation, and violence with a broom against a laundry bin — and it produced three unsigned warning letters and three unsigned probation contracts. [Para 123][Para 163][Para 183][Para 188][Para 140]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to reinstate Ms N, Mr K, Mr R and Mr S with wages from dismissal to return; Mr K was ordered to repay the 203.80 USD severance he had received, keeping his final month's wage; the claim for Ms S was dismissed. [Para 311][Para 312][Para 313][Para 314][Para 316]

On discrimination the panel quoted Articles 12 and 279 and held that the workers bore the burden of proving that union membership or activity motivated the dismissals; the panel reviewed the individual files and found no evidence tying any of the five dismissals to the union, so Issue 2 failed. [Para 201][Para 203][Para 207][Para 209][Para 210]

On protection, the panel applied Article 293 and Prakas No. 305 and restated the three conditions it had used in earlier awards: the worker must fall within the Prakas, the dismissal must fall inside the protected period, and the employer must have been notified of the protected person's name. [Para 213][Para 218][Para 221] All five satisfied the first two conditions because the registration application of 16 July 2012 was still pending; only Ms N and Mr K were shown to have been named to the employer, through the election-result letter signed for on 11 July 2012, so only those two held protected status. [Para 226][Para 229][Para 238][Para 241]

Ms N, the elected union president, was dismissed on 9 August 2012 without any request to the labour inspector, which the panel held contrary to Article 293 and Prakas No. 305, and ordered her reinstated with back wages. [Para 243][Para 244][Para 245] Mr K held a fixed-term contract from 1 July to 30 September 2012 and was dismissed on 9 August; the panel found that his acceptance of 203.80 USD severance about half a month later was not a valid agreed termination under Article 73 because it was not made in the presence of the labour inspector, so the termination was unilateral and required prior authorisation. [Para 253][Para 256][Para 258][Para 262] For Mr R the panel set aside the unsigned probation contract, found an indefinite-term contract, held that beating a broom against a laundry bin was not the serious misconduct listed in Article 83(4) and that a warning should have sufficed, and found no valid reason notified under Article 74. [Para 189][Para 198][Para 276][Para 278][Para 280] For Mr S it likewise found an indefinite-term contract and held the dismissal void for want of notice and of any reason going to his aptitude or conduct. [Para 283][Para 285][Para 287]

Ms S was different: the panel held that Article 65 permits an oral contract, that an unsigned probation contract is therefore not fatal, that her 65 USD wage identified her as a probationer, and that under Articles 68 and 82 an employer may end a probation at will without notice, so she had no right to reinstatement. [Para 298][Para 300][Para 306][Para 308][Para 310]

Arbitrator Ing Sothy appended a dissent, reasoning that both Ms N and Mr K were fixed-term workers already compensated under Article 73(3), but that because they enjoyed Article 293 protection the employer should have put the terminations to the labour inspector; if the inspector approves they stand, and if not a new contract should be created. [Para 320][Para 323][Para 325]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Protection against dismissal under Article 293 and Prakas No. 305 is conditional on the employer having been notified of the protected person's name: where a union applies for registration but the employer is told only of the candidates elected, workers whose names were never communicated remain unprotected, and their dismissal is not unlawful merely for lack of the labour inspector's authorisation. Acceptance of severance money after a dismissal does not turn a unilateral termination of a fixed-term contract into the agreed termination that Article 73 requires, because that agreement must be made in writing before the labour inspector. [Para 238][Para 241][Para 256]

**Obiter dicta:** the remarks that a factory's former administration never told the new management of the union, and that the union was still assembling seniority evidence for the Ministry, play no part in the orders; the general statement that probation exists to let the employer judge the worker is likewise illustrative only. [Para 116][Para 118][Para 305]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The dismissed worker Mr K is written four different ways across the file, including one form in which the middle element is rendered "Vann" and another in which it is "Sovann"; the panel resolved it by adopting the spelling in his labour contract. [Para 96][Para 97]
- The employer's name appears as "Master & Freng", "Master & Faeng" and "Master & Greng" within the same document, including in the style of cause. [Para 28][Para 62]
- The union dated its leadership election to 2 July 2012, but the panel rejected that for lack of evidence and adopted 11 July 2012 from the election-result letter. [Para 106][Para 109][Para 110]
- Ms N's monthly wage is printed as 220 USD while her contract runs "01 June 2012 to 01 September 2012", a three-month span stated with a single day as an endpoint. [Para 119][Para 120]
- Ms S's base wage is claimed at 70 USD by the workers and 65 USD by the employer; the panel preferred 65 USD from the payroll voucher, so both figures survive in the text. [Para 177][Para 178][Para 180]
- Long stretches of the reasons are corrupted to Latin-script gibberish, e.g. a full paragraph after the Article 293 discussion and fragments embedded in the evidence list; the surrounding Khmer carries the meaning. [Para 217][Para 73]
- The panel composition is printed twice with the arbitrators' names split from their signatures, and the chair's name appears in a signature block mangled beyond legibility. [Para 318]
