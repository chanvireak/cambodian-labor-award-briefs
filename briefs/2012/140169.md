# Award No. 161/12 — Horise In Dash Strile Khob (Arbitration Council, 21 August 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer, Tuon Siphan for the workers, Pen Bunhea presiding) · Doc ID 140169

## 1. Facts

Workers at Horise In Dash Strile Khob, a Phnom Penh garment factory whose workforce had fallen from almost 2,000 to just over 100, struck from 10 August 2012, represented by the Khmer Democratic Workers' Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ ខ្មែរ) [Para 2][Para 6][Para 23]. Their first demand was that the company state plainly whether it still had work at all, so that they could decide what to do; the remaining eleven demands alleged continuous pressure on workers to resign without legal compensation, dismantling of teams, cuts in wages and skill allowances, wages paid several times a month, unpaid rest days, and thumbprinting of blank papers [Para 3][Para 7][Para 8][Para 9][Para 11]. Conciliation on 13 August 2012 failed on all 12 of 12 demands and the report reached the Council on 16 August 2012 [Para 21][Para 22]. At the employer's request the panel issued Order No. 021/12 on 16 August 2012 suspending the strike and requiring all strikers back on their shifts from the morning of 17 August 2012 [Para 23]. The workers did neither: they kept striking and did not attend the pre-hearing meeting of 17 August 2012, giving no reason for their absence [Para 24][Para 26][Para 40]. The panel held no hearing and made no findings of fact on any of the twelve demands [Para 31][Para 32].

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. (Issue 1) Whether the panel should continue the arbitration at all, where the workers kept striking after the panel had ordered the strike suspended and stayed away from the pre-hearing meeting? [Para 33][Para 42]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** The workers demanded that the person with ultimate decision-making authority, as named in the company's articles, come forward in person, because past settlements negotiated with company representatives had produced agreement on paper but no implementation [Para 5][Para 13]. They asked the company to confirm whether it had work and the term of its premises lease, and alleged abuse of workers in the forms listed in Article 90 of the Labour Law [Para 3][Para 4][Para 6].

**Employer (respondent):** The company representatives who appeared said they had been properly delegated, held valid written authorisation, and had power to decide [Para 14]. The employer attended the pre-hearing meeting on time, whereas the workers' side did not appear at all [Para 26][Para 41].

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to continue the arbitration in case No. 161/12, closing the file without any ruling on the twelve demands [Para 43].

The panel put the question to itself as whether to continue the proceedings [Para 33]. Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council requires parties to suspend all dispute measures — strike or lockout under Article 318 of the Labour Law, or any other measure capable of aggravating the dispute — during the panel's work, and to attend every meeting to which the panel summons them [Para 33][Para 34]. The panel recalled its settled reading of that Article: it will not continue an arbitration while workers remain on strike, as applied in Award No. 04/04 (IM SI), where the panel declared it could neither resolve nor issue an award on a collective dispute whose parties had no right to strike during arbitration [Para 35][Para 36]. The same course was taken in Awards No. 28/06 (G.D.E.M.) and No. 34/06 (Gold Fame), where cases were closed after two orders to return to work were ignored, and in Awards No. 110/11 (Simano) and No. 59/12 (Win Sing Tech) [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39].

On the facts, the panel found the workers had continued the strike through the arbitration, had not complied with Order No. 021/12, and had failed to attend the pre-hearing meeting of 17 August 2012 without explaining the absence, while the employer had attended on time [Para 40][Para 41]. It concluded that the arbitration should not continue [Para 42].

## 5. Ratio decidendi

A panel will not continue, and will close, an arbitration where the party that brought the collective dispute maintains a strike during the proceedings in defiance of the panel's order to suspend all dispute measures, and fails to attend the meetings the panel convenes; Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 makes suspension of dispute measures and attendance a condition of the procedure, not a courtesy. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 40][Para 42]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observation that the employer's delegates carried valid written authority to decide is a procedural aside, since the point was never adjudicated and the file was closed on other grounds [Para 14].

## 6. Source defects disclosed

The OCR is poor throughout: whole clauses are replaced by meaningless runs of Latin and English words, notably at the point where Order No. 021/12 is described, and the signature block survives only as stray characters [Para 23][Para 44]. The award contradicts itself on whether the workers returned to work as ordered — paragraph 24 says they did not enter work, paragraph 40 says they continued to strike while also coming in to work [Para 24][Para 40]. The company's name is printed in at least five variants and the conciliation minutes are dated 08 August 2012 although the conciliation itself is recorded as 13 August 2012 [Para 2][Para 21][Para 28]. Named managers and individual workers appear in the party lists and have been reduced to roles and initials here [Para 2].
