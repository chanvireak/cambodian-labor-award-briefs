# Award No. 26/12 — N.C.X Co., Ltd (Honda motorcycle assembly) (Arbitration Council, 20 April 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bunchhea presiding; Kao Thach, Sin Kim San) · Doc ID 139953

## 1. Facts
N.C.X Co., Ltd, trading as the Honda motorcycle assembly company at Trapeang Thleung, Chom Chao, Dangkao, Phnom Penh, registered in 1998, assembles motorcycles from parts imported from Thailand and employs 665 workers. [Para 100][Para 101] On 16 December 2011 it notified the reduction of 20 workers with effect from 17 December 2011, giving lack of raw materials after the Thai floods as the reason; four of those dismissed were the president, secretary, treasurer and first adviser of the Democratic Workers' Union, formed at a 8 December 2011 election. [Para 107][Para 108][Para 135][Para 255] Of eight demands, two were settled at the hearing (issues 5 and 8) and two were merged into issue 1, leaving four. [Para 21][Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the dismissal of the four union officers on 17 December 2011 was union discrimination and procedurally lawful, and whether they must be reinstated with back pay? [Para 104][Para 225]
3. Whether the employer must pay a seniority bonus not previously paid, and continue paying it? [Para 213][Para 303]
4. Whether the employer must pay 100 per cent of wages whenever production is suspended and there is no work? [Para 216][Para 317]
6. Whether the employer must pay an attendance bonus of USD 7 a month? [Para 223][Para 342]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the four were dismissed because they were union leaders, days after the election and after the notification of the result had been delivered to the company's gate; they should be reinstated with back pay from the date of dismissal. [Para 226][Para 232][Para 259] They also argued the plant's conditions are harder than a garment factory's, so a seniority bonus is due, that the employer owes full wages whenever it suspends production, and that a USD 7 attendance bonus is warranted because the work is comparable to garment work. [Para 213][Para 216][Para 223]
**Employer (respondent):** this was a collective layoff caused by the production halt for want of raw materials, carried out under Article 95 by dismissing first those rated least able and then those with least seniority, with no connection to union activity. [Para 227][Para 111][Para 242] It denied that a security guard named in the union's receipt slip was ever its employee, denied receiving the election notification, and said Notice No. 041/11 covers only textiles, garments and footwear, not vehicle assembly. [Para 224][Para 260][Para 305]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the four workers are reinstated with back pay from the date of dismissal; the panel declined jurisdiction over the fifth, Mr O; issues 3 and 6 were refused, and issue 4 was refused as to future suspensions but the employer was ordered to stop unlawful suspensions immediately. The award is non-binding and becomes final 8 days after notification. [Para 349][Para 350][Para 351][Para 353][Para 355]
On issue 1 the panel first applied the burden of proof: the party alleging discrimination carries it, and the union produced no specific evidence that the dismissals were retaliatory. [Para 228][Para 231][Para 234] It then tested the layoff against Article 95, which requires dismissal by professional ability and then seniority; the employer relied on three consecutive "C" ratings, but the three rating dates were not consecutive, and it produced originals only for the last, offering no reason for the omission. [Para 236][Para 243][Para 244][Para 245] The panel therefore refused to accept the ratings and found Article 95 not complied with. [Para 247][Para 248]
It then applied Article 293 and clause 4 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001: union founders and members are protected as workers' delegates from the moment the registration application is filed until 30 days after registration. [Para 249][Para 250][Para 251] All three conditions were met — the four were union officers, they were dismissed on 17 December 2011 while the union held only registration receipts of 14 December 2011 and 10 February 2012, and notification had been given. [Para 255][Para 256][Para 257][Para 281] On the notification the panel weighed the conflicting evidence of the Phoenix security company's operations head and his assistant, both testifying for one institution, and preferred the 11 December 2011 duty roster bearing a name with no ID number and no signature, over the later letter denying employment. [Para 266][Para 276][Para 279][Para 280] No inspector approval having been sought, the dismissals were unlawful and reinstatement with back pay followed. [Para 283][Para 285]
Mr O's case was outside jurisdiction: Article 312 confines the panel to the issues in the non-conciliation record, his name was not in the list of 20, and the name list was not attached to the original complaint. [Para 287][Para 296][Para 300][Para 302] Issues 3 and 6 failed because Notice No. 041/11 expressly covers only weaving, garment and footwear workers, and this is a vehicle assembly plant; both were interest disputes and the union, not yet registered, lacked standing. [Para 305][Para 307][Para 314][Para 315] Issue 4 concerned suspensions that had not yet occurred, so there was no present dispute, but under clause 34 of Prakas No. 099 the panel ordered the employer to cease unlawful suspensions at once. [Para 333][Para 340][Para 341]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a layoff rests on professional-ability rankings, the employer must prove the ranking with original evaluation documents for each period relied on; ratings that are not the last three consecutive assessments, and for which no originals are produced without explanation, cannot satisfy Article 95. [Para 244][Para 245][Para 248] Separately, a union's founders and officers are protected as workers' delegates from the date the registration application is filed, so a dismissal effected during that window without the labour inspector's prior agreement is unlawful regardless of the economic reason for it. [Para 250][Para 251][Para 283]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that a claim directed at suspensions that may occur in the future raises no existing dispute, since no one can predict future events, and that its power under Prakas No. 099 includes ordering an immediate end to unlawful conduct. [Para 333][Para 334][Para 340]
