# Award No. 58/12 — Dic Knitwear Limited (Arbitration Council, 19 April 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Tuon Si Phan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · Doc ID 139984

## 1. Facts
Dic Knitwear Limited (ឌីក នើធី នីត ធើរ) is a garment factory at Prek Ta Pring village, Setbo commune, Sa'ang district, Kandal Province. The claimant is the Democratic Union of Garment Workers of Cambodia (សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា) and its factory-level union. [Para 1][Para 3]

The dispute began when workers in the cutting and finishing sections refused to follow management's work assignments and went on strike, alleging that the company had not negotiated piece rates for lot 3009 before production. The Provincial Department of Labour and Vocational Training conciliated on 5 March 2012; both of the two claims remained unresolved and were referred to the Arbitration Council on 13 March 2012. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 14]

A strike was in progress when the file reached the Council secretariat. The Council issued Order No. 008/12 on 13 March 2012 requiring workers to return to their shifts on 14 March 2012; they complied and normal arbitration resumed. The hearing was held on 26 March 2012. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must negotiate the piece rate for lot 3009 with the cutting and finishing sections before production begins? [Para 3]
2. Whether the company must raise the piece rate for lot PN 1203012A in the weaving section from US$ 0.55 to US$ 0.59 per garment (US$ 6.60 to US$ 7.08 per lot)? [Para 5][Para 6]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The cutting and finishing workers demanded that the company negotiate the lot price for series 3009 first, and only then start production. The weaving workers demanded US$ 0.59 per garment for lot PN 1203012A, i.e. US$ 7.08 per lot, on the ground that they are all skilled at this work. [Para 3][Para 5][Para 91]

**Employer (respondent):** The company refused to negotiate first and asked workers to perform the work first, with labour-productivity discussion to follow. It asked to keep the existing rate of US$ 0.55 per garment, i.e. US$ 6.60 per lot in size XS. [Para 4][Para 6]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** The panel ordered the employer to notify the cutting and finishing workers of the piece rate for series 3009 after the workers had completed three days of work. It rejected the weaving section's demand to raise the rate for lot PN 1203012A to US$ 0.59 per garment. The award is binding and becomes final immediately, in accordance with the parties' agreement at the hearing. [Para 100][Para 101][Para 102]

On the applicable law, Article 108 of the Labour Law provides that piecework and output-based work must be paid so that a worker of average skill working normal hours receives at least the guaranteed minimum wage. Point 3 of Notification No. 049/10 of 9 July 2010 provides that piece-rate workers are paid according to actual output, and that if output falls below the minimum the employer must top up to US$ 56 per month for probationary workers and US$ 61 per month for full-rights workers. [Para 81][Para 82][Para 83][Para 84]

The panel also relied on its earlier awards holding that Cambodian employers may set or change piece rates, but must do so in a way that lets an average-skill worker doing normal work earn the minimum wage. [Para 85][Para 87]

On the evidence, the January and February 2012 payrolls showed that 5.17% of weaving pieceworkers working normal hours without overtime earned a base wage below US$ 61 per month, while 94.83% earned more than US$ 61. For those who earned below the minimum, the employer had topped up their pay to the minimum. [Para 88][Para 89]

The panel therefore found that the employer's rate-setting for the weaving section was lawful and reasonable, because it enabled the majority of workers to receive wages above the statutory minimum. It rejected the claim for US$ 0.59 per garment. [Para 97][Para 98][Para 99]

The panel added that, to keep piece-rate setting accurate and lawful, the employer must arrange a trial period of appropriate length, as explained under issue 1, to ensure that an average-skill worker doing normal work can earn the legal minimum wage. [Para 90]

On issue 1, the panel treated the employer's right to manage and direct the enterprise under Article 2 of the Labour Law as lawful and reasonable in character, and ordered notification of the series 3009 rate after three days of work, which reconciles the employer's management right with the workers' need for a rate fixed before they are committed to a long production run. [Para 92][Para 95][Para 100]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may lawfully set piece rates provided that an average-skill worker doing normal hours earns at least the minimum wage, and provided that any shortfall is topped up to US$ 56 or US$ 61 per month. [Para 81][Para 84][Para 85]

A piece rate is lawful and reasonable where the payroll evidence shows the great majority of workers earn above the minimum and the rest are topped up; a demand above such a rate is an interest claim the Council will reject. [Para 88][Para 98][Para 99]

Where the employer's management right is exercised lawfully and reasonably, the Council will still require the piece rate for a new series to be notified to workers within a short trial period rather than left open indefinitely. [Para 95][Para 100]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel recommended that the employer organise a proper trial period whenever it sets a new lot price, so that the rate is accurate and consistent with Article 108. [Para 90]
