# Award No. 103/12 — Se Piya Garment Co (Arbitration Council, 5 July 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouchhun, employer-selected; An Virak, worker-selected; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · Doc ID 140133

## 1. Facts
Se Piya, a Phnom Penh garment factory employing about 200 workers, dismissed three union leaders on 5 April 2012, three weeks after the union's founding election of 15 March 2012 in which they were elected president, vice-president and secretary. [Para 28][Para 38][Para 39] Two of them were still on three-month probationary contracts; the third had completed probation and stayed on without any written contract. [Para 32][Para 36][Para 37] The employer said the factory had run out of work and had dismissed about 50 workers in all; the union said the three were singled out for their union roles. [Para 51][Para 53]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take the three union leaders back into normal work? [Para 56]
2. Whether the employer must stop discriminating against the union? [Para 4]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the three were probationary only in name, were careless and wandered about during working hours according to the employer, and were dismissed because they had formed a union; the employer asked them on the day of dismissal whether "probationary workers can form a union" and told them to go and fetch their federation president. [Para 3][Para 47][Para 48]
**Employer (respondent):** the factory genuinely had no work — it is a new plant and dismissed about 50 workers — and it never received the election notice, since guards have no authority to accept letters for the company and only the accountant or administration may do so. [Para 45][Para 51][Para 54]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement of all three was refused, but the employer was ordered to pay the one worker on an indefinite contract his pay in lieu of notice (seven days), his unused annual leave and his final wages; the dismissal indemnity and moral damages were refused. [Para 115][Para 116][Para 117]

The panel took the two conditions of protection from Article 3(3) of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001: the dismissal must fall inside the protected window, and the union must have notified the employer of the candidacies by all available means. [Para 60][Para 57] The first was met — the election was on 15 March and the dismissals on 5 April, inside the 45-day period after the poll. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 70] The second was not. The union attempted notification three times: at the factory gate on 21 March, where the guard refused it; on 23 March, where the accountant read the letter, tore the envelope and handed it back; and by ordinary post on 3 May, which was both unofficial and after the dismissals. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 42][Para 43] The panel found no note by the deliverer recording the date and hour of refusal, and held the union had not proved it used all available means, so the three acquired no special protection. [Para 74][Para 75][Para 76]

The panel then examined the terminations on ordinary grounds. For the two probationary workers, no reason needed to be given during the trial period, so their dismissal was lawful. [Para 78][Para 81] For the third, the expired probation and the absence of any written contract converted the relationship into an indefinite contract under Articles 67(1) and 76(7), so Article 74 applied and a valid reason tied to capability, conduct or the needs of the enterprise was required. [Para 83][Para 85][Para 86][Para 87] The panel accepted the operational reason: the company had dismissed about 50 of only 200 workers, which it would not have done had work existed. [Para 94][Para 95] The termination was therefore lawful, but notice, leave and final wages remained due; the dismissal indemnity was refused because seniority was under six months, and moral damages because the reason was valid. [Para 96][Para 103][Para 108][Para 112]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Protection of elected union leaders under Prakas No. 305 requires both that the dismissal fall inside the protected window and that the union have notified the employer of the candidacies by all available means; attempts that leave no contemporaneous written record of delivery or refusal — a guard's oral refusal, a torn envelope, an unregistered postal letter sent after the dismissal — do not establish notification, and the leaders are then dismissible on ordinary grounds. [Para 60][Para 74][Para 76] Where probation expires and the worker keeps working without a written contract, the contract becomes indefinite, and termination then requires a valid reason under Article 74 plus notice, leave and final wages. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 101][Para 108]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that probationary workers need not be given a reason for dismissal, and that 50 dismissals out of 200 workers is itself evidence of a genuine lack of work, are general remarks on proof rather than holdings on the union's discrimination complaint. [Para 78][Para 94]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award states that the factory union held no registration certificate from the Ministry at [Para 30], yet the panel elsewhere treats it as a base union entitled to the Prakas No. 305 protection at [Para 57] and [Para 67]. The worker on the indefinite contract is named in two forms in the source, with the family name printed once as "ហ៊ុន" and once as "ហ៊ុយ" at [Para 82]; he is reduced to an initial here. The non-conciliation report is numbered 566 and dated 31 May 2012 at [Para 8] but 30 May 2012 at [Para 13]. The conciliation minute is dated 24 May 2012 at [Para 23] and again at [Para 25] with corrupted text. The panel also records the employer's assertion that it dismissed 50 workers while the file contains no list of them. [Para 51] The three dismissed leaders and the union's executive are individuals and are reduced to roles and initials. [Para 2]
