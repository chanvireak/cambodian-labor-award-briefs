# Award No. 129/12 — Tai Yang Enterprises Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 July 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Nan, worker-appointed; Pen Bunchhea, president) · Doc ID 140145

## 1. Facts
Tai Yang Enterprises Co., Ltd runs a garment factory in Angsnuol district, Kandal province, with about 3,400 workers; C.CAWDU was the claimant [Para 23][Para 24]. Of the 472 workers said to have authorised the union on 10 July 2012, the panel verified only 471 names with identity numbers, start dates and thumbprints, and it considered the demand of those 471 only [Para 25][Para 26][Para 28]. The complaint of 29 June 2012 raised six demands; two were settled in conciliation and four were referred by non-conciliation report 526/12 of 3 July 2012; at the hearing of 13 July 2012 issues 2 to 4 (allowances and the attendance-bonus top-up) were withdrawn, leaving issue 1 [Para 7][Para 9][Para 10][Para 21]. The workers struck, ignored interim order 018/12 directing them back to work from 5 July 2012, and returned only on 10 July 2012 [Para 10][Para 15]. Their contracts are of undetermined duration and the employer had never terminated them [Para 28][Para 46].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the 471 workers are entitled to require the employer to terminate their employment contracts and pay termination compensation (outstanding wages, unused annual leave, pay in lieu of notice, indemnity for dismissal and damages), the employer being unwilling to terminate? [Para 28][Para 42]
2. Whether the 471 workers are entitled to require the employer to maintain their seniority with Tainan Enterprises (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a separate company established on the same premises? [Para 28][Para 49]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** they asked the panel to order termination of the 471 contracts with the five heads of payment, and to preserve their seniority with Tainan Enterprises (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, because the establishment of that company on Tai Yang's premises affected their morale [Para 28][Para 29]. They acknowledged that their benefits and identity cards were unchanged and that Tai Yang and Tainan are two separate entities, and asked the panel to decide on the principles of equity [Para 29][Para 30].

**Employer (respondent):** its head of administration and the assistant to the general manager attended the hearing but had no written authorisation from the company, saying they had been too busy to have one signed and promising to file it later, which they never did [Para 18][Para 19][Para 31]. Because they were unauthorised, the panel recorded that their counter-arguments would not be considered and ruled in the employer's absence [Para 21][Para 30][Para 41].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the demand for termination payments for the 471 workers and declined to consider the seniority demand; nothing was ordered to be paid [Para 56][Para 57].
On representation, the panel applied clause 19 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, which allows a party to appear in person or through a person expressly authorised in writing, and followed awards 71/08-River Rich and 161/09-Prek Treng holding that an officer without a written mandate cannot represent the employer [Para 32][Para 36][Para 40]. It therefore determined that neither of the two company officers present was authorised and decided the case in the employer's absence [Para 41].
On the termination payment, the panel relied on Article 311 of the Civil Code, under which a contract is the matching of intentions of two or more parties to create, change or extinguish an obligation, and Article 65 paragraph 1 of the Labour Law, which establishes the working relationship between worker and employer [Para 42][Para 44]. Since an employment obligation can be extinguished only by the intention of both contracting parties, and the workers' contracts had not been terminated by the employer and the employer did not wish to terminate them, the workers could not compel termination and therefore could not claim termination pay; severance is ordered where the employer terminates without valid reason [Para 45][Para 47][Para 48].
On seniority, the panel found Tainan Enterprises (Cambodia) Co., Ltd to be an entity separate from Tai Yang, which alone contracted with the 471 workers; Tainan was a third party to the dispute and the panel would not rule on a demand directed at it [Para 49][Para 50][Para 53][Para 55]. The award is non-binding and becomes binding eight days after notification unless a party lodges written opposition with the Minister [Para 57].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Workers cannot force their own dismissal: because an employment contract is extinguished only by the concurring intention of employer and worker, a demand that the employer terminate the contracts and pay severance fails where the employer has not terminated and does not wish to, and severance arises only on an employer's termination without valid reason [Para 44][Para 47][Para 48]. Likewise a demand to preserve seniority with a different company is not decided, that company being a third party and not a disputant [Para 53][Para 55].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's holding that an officer who attends without a written mandate cannot bind the employer, and that his statements will not be weighed, was stated as a general procedural rule drawn from Prakas 099 and two prior awards, although the outcome on issue 1 did not turn on any disputed fact [Para 32][Para 40].
