# Award No. 181/11 — Nak Gettech Sway (PTI) Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 6 February 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Pen Bun Chhea (president), Seng Vouch Hun (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed) · Doc ID 140117

## 1. Facts
Nak Gettech Sway (PTI) Ltd. is a garment manufacturer operating since 2007 with about 300 workers. [Para 30][Para 31] On 19 October 2011 the Cambodian Workers' Economic Union Federation gave notice of a strike over three demands; conciliation on 15 December 2011 failed on all three, and the file reached the Council on 26 December 2011. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 14] A strike had already taken place from 4 to 8 August 2011 and ended with a written agreement of 8 August 2011, whose clause 2 allowed the company to recover strike-day wages and bonuses by deducting annual leave in 2012. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 40] The federation produced no union registration certificate and no written delegation from the workers, so only one claimant attended and the panel limited the case to that worker. [Para 35][Para 61][Para 64]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must refrain from deducting, in 2011, the annual leave of the workers who struck from 4 to 8 August 2011, and instead take that deduction from 2012 leave as the 8 August 2011 agreement provides? [Para 37][Para 65]
2. Whether the company must pay 100 per cent of wages while it has no work for the workers, and must stop substituting annual leave for those idle days (the panel's issues 2 and 3, consolidated)? [Para 43][Para 72]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The 8 August 2011 agreement is clear that strike-day wages are retained by the company but recovered from 2012 annual leave, so the deduction the company took from 2011 leave was a breach; the panel should order the 4 days restored. [Para 42][Para 66][Para 69] When orders dry up the company sends workers home on 50 per cent wages or on annual leave without asking them, although it is the company's own fault that there is no work, and workers want to keep their annual leave for Pchum Ben and Khmer New Year; on days without work the company never notified or sought authorisation from the labour inspector. [Para 44][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 51]

**Employer (respondent):** When there are no orders for one or two days it sends workers home using their annual leave, and where a worker has no annual leave left it pays 50 per cent of wages; this is done after notifying the workers and with their prior agreement. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the company must deduct the strike days from 2012 annual leave and restore the 4 days of 2011 annual leave it took, and must pay 100 per cent of wages during periods when it has no work and stop substituting annual leave for those days; the award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent written objection. [Para 100][Para 101][Para 102][Para 103][Para 104]

Before the merits, the panel addressed representation. [Para 52] Article 268 of the Labour Law makes registration with the Ministry of Labour the condition for a professional organisation to enjoy the rights the law recognises, and Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 permits representation by another person only on written authorisation. [Para 53][Para 56][Para 57] Earlier awards require either a union registration certificate or written delegations from the complaining workers. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60] Neither existed here, so the union had no standing and the panel considered only the single claimant present at the hearing. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63][Para 64]

On issue 1, the panel read clauses 1 and 2 of the 8 August 2011 agreement and found their content and meaning to be that the company recovers the 4 strike days (4 to 8 August 2011) from annual leave in 2012. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 68] Because the company instead cut the claimant's 2011 annual leave, that act was wrong, and it was ordered both to take the deduction in 2012 and to restore the 4 days removed from 2011. [Para 69][Para 70]

On the consolidated issues 2 and 3, the panel applied Article 71(2) of the Labour Law on suspension of enterprise activity for economic or material difficulty, which requires the suspension to be placed under labour-inspection supervision, and Article 72(1) on the effects of suspension. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 78] A company that follows the statutory suspension procedure owes no wages, but one that does not must pay 100 per cent of wages even with no work to give. [Para 79][Para 80] It found no notification to, or authorisation from, the labour inspector, and held the 50 per cent payment and annual-leave substitution unlawful. [Para 82][Para 83]

On the annual-leave question, Article 170 provides that annual leave is granted at Khmer New Year unless otherwise agreed, and that where the leave exceeds 15 days the employer may fix the remainder — but only for leave of that length, and with prior notice to the inspector and the workers' agreement. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 87][Para 88][Para 91] Earlier awards hold that an employer may not use a worker's annual leave to cover idle periods. [Para 92][Para 95][Para 98] The substitution was accordingly prohibited. [Para 99]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a settlement agreement fixes the year in which strike-day wages are to be recovered from annual leave, the employer may not take that deduction from an earlier year, and the leave wrongly removed must be restored. [Para 68][Para 70] An employer that sends workers home for want of orders without notifying and obtaining the labour inspector's supervision of a suspension under Article 71(2) must pay full wages under Article 72(1), and may not offset those idle days against annual leave. [Para 80][Para 83][Para 98] A union that can produce neither a registration certificate nor written delegations from the complaining workers has no standing, and the panel will hear only the workers who appear in person. [Para 60][Para 64]

**Obiter dicta:** none identified.
