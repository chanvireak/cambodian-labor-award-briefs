# Award No. 193/12 — Tropicana Resort & Casino (Arbitration Council, 21 November 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun, An Nan, Tan Try as chair) · Doc ID 140188

## 1. Facts

Tropicana Resort & Casino operates a hotel and entertainment club at Poipet in Banteay Meanchey province with about 893 workers, and the local union of the Cambodian Tourism and Service Workers Federation, registered on 23 August 2011, is the claimant [Para 18][Para 20]. On 16 August 2012 the union led a strike over eighteen bases of demand, including reinstatement of three workers and payment of their back wages from dismissal to reinstatement [Para 5]. Conciliation resolved thirteen of twenty-one issues, then five more, leaving three, and on 17 September 2012 the employer wrote to the Ministry refusing to reinstate the three [Para 6][Para 7][Para 8]. The third of the three issues was settled by agreement at the hearing, so the panel decided only the first two [Para 14][Para 15]. Three hearings were held — 10 October 2012, 14 November 2012 (postponed at the employer's request because its witness was in Thailand) and 16 November 2012 — and at the last the employer brought no witness and no evidence, having promised to do so [Para 9][Para 10][Para 11][Para 14].

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer was obliged to reinstate the local union president and pay his back wages, where it dismissed him without the authorisation of the Labour Inspector required by Article 293 of the Labour Law? [Para 58][Para 62]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer had a valid reason to dismiss the union activist for alleged gambling misconduct, and if not, what termination payments were owed once it maintained its refusal to reinstate him? [Para 77][Para 84]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** the president was dismissed because of his involvement in the complaint over the activist's dismissal, and he was called into the CCTV room twice on that account; the three warning letters relied on were never given to him and record only accusations beside a signature; the activist committed no misconduct, there are no internal work rules, and his dismissal followed a coerced confession extracted under threats against his wife and relatives [Para 22][Para 27][Para 29][Para 30][Para 34][Para 38].

**Employer (respondent):** the president was dismissed for misconduct after three written warnings, dated 12 April, 17 April and 1 May 2011, which he signed; the activist conspired with a patron to help him win at gambling, and the company holds a CCTV tape, witnesses and the patron's signed confession letter to prove it [Para 24][Para 25][Para 26][Para 42][Para 43][Para 44].

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to reinstate the local union president with back pay of base wages from the date of dismissal to the date of reinstatement, and, the employer having refused to reinstate the activist, ordered one month's wages in lieu of notice, dismissal indemnity of 60 days' wages and benefits, payment for unused annual leave and damages equal to the indemnity [Para 66][Para 95].

**Issue 1.** Article 293 of the Labour Law permits dismissal of a shop steward or candidate only after authorisation from the Labour Inspector, and Clause 4 paragraph 2 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 extends that protection to three union leaders, so the local union president enjoyed it [Para 58][Para 59]. Following Award 190/12-Long Lead, the panel held that dismissal without that authorisation is a procedural violation, and the employer admitted at the second hearing that it had not sought authorisation [Para 60][Para 62]. Because the procedure had failed, the panel did not need to examine the reasons for dismissal [Para 64]. It ordered reinstatement and back pay of base wages only, the sum the workers had specifically claimed [Para 22][Para 65]. It also noted that the provincial department had already directed reinstatement by letter No. 044 of 20 April 2012 and rejected the employer's appeal on 31 July 2012 for breach of Article 293 [Para 31][Para 32][Para 33].

**Issue 2.** The activist was not a union leader but an activist, and the panel held that the special protection of Article 293 reaches only union leaders, not activists or members [Para 69][Para 70][Para 71]. His engagement began in 2008 on a verbal contract, and under Article 67 paragraphs 1 and 7 a contract of fixed duration must be in writing with a precise finishing date or it becomes one of undetermined duration, which was his case [Para 72][Para 73]. Article 74 then required a valid reason relating to his aptitude or behaviour [Para 74][Para 75][Para 76][Para 77]. The employer asserted serious misconduct but produced no CCTV tape and no witness, its documents in foreign languages were not considered, and the confession letter it did file was undated and objected to [Para 45][Para 46][Para 78][Para 79]. Having failed to prove the misconduct, the employer had no legal basis to dismiss, and reinstatement was ordered [Para 81][Para 82][Para 83]. Because the employer persisted in refusing reinstatement, the panel awarded one month's notice pay for service of three years nine months, 60 days' indemnity under Article 89, unused annual leave under Article 166, and damages under Article 91 equal to the indemnity; outstanding wages had already been paid, satisfying Article 116 [Para 85][Para 87][Para 91][Para 94][Para 95].

## 5. Ratio decidendi

A union leader within the protection of Article 293 and Prakas No. 305 cannot be dismissed without prior authorisation from the Labour Inspector, and where that authorisation is missing the dismissal is unlawful whatever its merits, so reinstatement and back pay of the wages claimed follow without any inquiry into the alleged misconduct [Para 58][Para 64][Para 65]. A union activist outside that protection still holds a contract of undetermined duration where the engagement was verbal, so dismissal requires a proven valid reason; an employer that asserts serious misconduct but files no recording, calls no witness and relies on an undated, disputed confession fails to discharge its burden, and on refusing reinstatement owes notice pay, indemnity, leave pay and damages equal to the indemnity [Para 70][Para 80][Para 94].

**Obiter dicta:** the observations that the activist's own account used the word fiancee rather than wife, and that the employer said it did not call the police because his wedding was near, were credibility remarks that did not decide either issue [Para 48][Para 51].

## 6. Source defects disclosed

The English text is an unofficial translation whose OCR drops initial letters throughout — "themployer", "thearing", "sue" for issue, "traing" for training — and duplicates whole clauses at paragraphs 4, 5 and 8, so several sentences appear twice [Para 4][Para 5][Para 8]. The evidence section was omitted from the English version altogether, leaving the documentary record described only indirectly [Para 16][Para 17]. The president's starting date is printed two ways: 5 December 2000 in the workers' claim and 31 January 2001 on his application form, the panel adopting the later date [Para 20][Para 21]. One employee witness is identified only by a first name and the patron by full name; the award also lists workers and union officers in full. This brief reduces all of them to roles.
