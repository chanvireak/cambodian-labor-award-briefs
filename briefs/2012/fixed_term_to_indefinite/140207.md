# Award No. 213/12 — Tai Yang Enterprise (Arbitration Council, 13 December 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Tang Tri chair; Ing Sothy appointed by the employer; An Non appointed by the workers, dissenting) · Doc ID 140207

## 1. Facts
Tai Yang Enterprise Co Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន តៃ យ៉ាង អ៉ិន ធើ ប្រាយ), also printed as Tai Yien, Tai Nan and Khem Well, is a garment factory in Ang Snoul district, Kandal, employing about 4,000 workers. [Para 1][Para 35] The claimant is the Cambodian Union Confederation (សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្ពុជា), acting for the 53 workers of the factory. [Para 1][Para 36][Para 37] A strike began on 25 June 2012 and continued until 23 August 2012; on 26 June 2012 the Kandal Provincial Court issued injunction order No. 22 DKSa protecting the factory, and the workers acknowledged at the hearing that they knew of it. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46][Para 81] The employer posted repeated announcements calling the workers back, the last setting 20 August 2012 as the final date, after which absentees would be treated as having abandoned their jobs; more than 1,000 strikers returned. [Para 62][Para 68][Para 82] The 53 workers who did not return, all on indefinite contracts, claim reinstatement or, failing that, statutory severance; the panel refused to consider their unsigned list of 49 names and proceeded on the 53 named in the hearing. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must reinstate the 53 workers on indefinite contracts, or instead pay them statutory severance and related terminal entitlements? [Para 37][Para 73]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers, through the Cambodian Union Confederation (claimant):** they had a right to strike and complied with the notice procedure; they did not abandon their posts but were refused by the employer even after the Minister of Social Affairs wrote asking that they be taken back; the employer therefore owes either reinstatement or full statutory compensation. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 55][Para 58]
**Employer (respondent):** a court injunction was in force and the company announced repeatedly, with a final deadline of 20 August 2012, that anyone not returning would be treated as having abandoned employment; only 53 held out, so their contracts ended by their own abandonment and no severance is due. [Para 56][Para 60][Para 62][Para 82]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel held that the 53 workers had abandoned their employment, that their contracts were terminated on 20 August 2012, and that the employer owed them neither reinstatement nor severance. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 87][Para 88]

The panel began from Article 337 of the Labour Law, which reserves to the labour court the power to decide whether a strike is lawful and provides that, where a strike is declared unlawful, the strikers must return to work within forty-eight hours of that declaration, failing which they commit serious misconduct. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 75] It found that the workers knew of injunction order No. 22 of 26 June 2012 and nonetheless continued the strike for nearly two months, until 23 August 2012. [Para 80][Para 81]

It then turned to the employer's announcements. The final announcement fixed 20 August 2012 as the last day for return, and the workers did not dispute that it was issued, nor that they stayed away afterwards; the panel noted that the employer could not state how many announcements were made or give their dates and had not filed them as evidence, and that it nevertheless accepted the fact of the final announcement because the workers conceded it. [Para 62][Para 64][Para 65][Para 69][Para 83] On that basis it concluded that the 53 had abandoned their employment, that the employment relationship ended on 20 August 2012, and dismissed the claim in its entirety. [Para 84][Para 85][Para 86][Para 87]

The workers' arbitrator dissented. In his view the record showed the workers did not abandon their posts: they struck until 23 August and then decided to return after the Minister's letter of 20 August 2012, but the employer refused to take them back. [Para 92][Para 93][Para 96][Para 97] He considered the employer had failed to prove the misconduct it alleged, since it never produced the text of the injunction or of its announcements, and that under Articles 74, 91, 75 and 166 the workers were entitled at least to severance, moral damages, payment in lieu of notice and unused annual leave. [Para 99][Para 100][Para 110][Para 112][Para 123][Para 132][Para 142]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Workers who continue a strike after a court injunction and after the employer's final announced return date, without valid reason, are treated as having abandoned their employment; the panel will dismiss a claim for reinstatement or severance in that situation, because the employment relationship ended on the announced deadline by the workers' own conduct rather than by any dismissal by the employer. [Para 81][Para 85][Para 86]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the employer's failure to produce the text of the injunction or of its announcements did not matter, because the workers themselves admitted knowing of both. [Para 65][Para 83]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The issue is printed as "ចំណុច វិវាទ ទី៖" with no numeral, so the panel's own issue numbering is lost and this brief numbers it 1. [Para 37][Para 73] The claimant group is given as 53 workers throughout, but the list they filed contains only 49 names and is unsigned, so the true number of claimants cannot be verified. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39] The ministry letter is dated 17 August 2012 in the recital and in the letter itself, but the workers said at the hearing that it issued on 19 August and that they received it on 20 or 22 August. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 52] The same letter is described in one passage as ordering the employer to take the workers back and in another as merely requesting it. [Para 51][Para 54] The 48-hour period in Article 337 is corrupted in the text as "រឹស សិច ប្រាំបី ម៉ោង". [Para 74] The company appears under four spellings (Tai Yang, Tai Yien, Tai Nan, Khem Well) and the case name itself varies. [Para 1][Para 35] Name defects: the employer's general manager, the union president and eleven women workers are named in full in the source — this brief identifies them by role; the arbitrators' signatures are largely illegible. [Para 1][Para 4][Para 90]
