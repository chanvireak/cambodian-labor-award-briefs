# Award No. 36/12 — Yung Wah Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 March 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bunchhea chair; Mar Samborana, Liv Sovanna) · Doc ID 139956

## 1. Facts
Yung Wah Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd runs a garment factory at Thmey Village, Ta Khmao, Kandal Province, employing 4,372 workers, with seven unions in the plant; the claimant is the local union of the Labor Federation Union of Cambodia (LFUC). [Para 17][Para 18][Para 19] A complaint of 27 December 2011 went to the Kandal labour dispute office; the last conciliation session on 3 February 2012 resolved one of six issues and three more were settled at the hearing of 7 March 2012, leaving issue 1 (sick-leave certification) and issue 5 (the dismissal of Ms L). [Para 9][Para 11][Para 13][Para 14] Ms L, a washing-section worker with ID no. 6078 on an indefinite contract since 14 October 2005, was dismissed on 22 December 2011 for serious misconduct towards a co-worker, Ms V, after an incident at 12:30 p.m. on 21 December 2011. [Para 28][Para 32][Para 42]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must maintain wages and benefits for sick leave certified by any doctor recognised by the state, or only by doctors at Chey Chamnak and Calmette hospitals? [Para 2][Para 20][Para 44]
5. Whether the employer must reinstate Ms L, dismissed on 22 December 2011 for serious misconduct, or pay her termination benefits? [Para 4][Para 25][Para 27]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the two named hospitals are far from their homes and from the places where they fall ill, so limiting certification to them denies sick pay in practice; they asked that any state-recognised doctor's certificate suffice. [Para 21] On Ms L, they said she was dismissed without valid reason and as a result of union discrimination, and that she should be reinstated or paid termination benefits under the Labour Law. [Para 26][Para 27]
**Employer (respondent):** it limits certification to those two public hospitals because they follow a proper procedure, whereas private hospitals issue fake certificates, and because 100 to 200 workers take sick leave a day and unrestricted leave would disrupt the production line. [Para 24] It refused reinstatement or termination pay because Ms L committed misconduct by scratching Ms V's eyes and pulling her hair during working hours. [Para 28]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the sick-leave demand was rejected but the employer was ordered to deduct the attendance bonus proportionally; reinstatement was refused, and the employer was ordered to pay compensation in lieu of notice, a dismissal indemnity of 90 days' wages and benefits, and damages of the same amount. The award is non-binding and becomes binding eight days after notification unless a party objects to the Minister. [Para 75][Para 76]
On issue 1 the panel applied Article 71(3) and Article 72(1): absence for illness certified by a qualified doctor suspends the contract, so the worker need not work and the employer need not pay. [Para 46][Para 47] Since the employer's practice of maintaining wages for two hospitals already exceeds the statute, and Article 13(2) permits benefits superior to the law, the panel refused to extend the wage benefit. [Para 49][Para 50] On the attendance bonus it held that the bonus is a gratuity and therefore part of wages under Article 103, so the employer may deduct it only in proportion to the days of certified sick leave, and it ordered that deduction for certificates from any public or private hospital recognised by the Ministry of Health. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 55]
On issue 5 the panel applied the burden of proof rule: the claimant must prove misconduct, and the employer had accepted Ms V's account without investigating, never checked for injury to her eyes, and offered three written testimonies that described only hair-pulling and covered eyes, one of them treating the incident as joking. [Para 57][Para 59][Para 60][Para 61] Serious misconduct under clause 10(C) of the Internal Work Rules means the acts in Article 83, and on this evidence the dismissal was not substantiated. [Para 57][Para 62] Because the employer wanted to end the relationship and gave no prior notice, Article 75(4) required two months' notice for six years' service, and Article 77 requires compensation in lieu; Article 89(2) requires 15 days' wages per year, capped at six months, hence 90 days; and Article 91 entitles her to damages equal to the dismissal indemnity without proof of loss. [Para 65][Para 68][Para 70][Para 71][Para 75]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that voluntarily pays wages during certified sick leave may confine that benefit to certificates from named hospitals, because Article 13(2) allows benefits above the law and the law itself requires no wages during suspension — but the attendance bonus is wages under Article 103 and may be deducted only in proportion to the days of certified sick leave, certified by any doctor the Ministry of Health recognises, public or private. [Para 46][Para 49][Para 50][Para 53][Para 55] Where the employer dismisses for serious misconduct without proving the acts alleged and without prior notice, it owes compensation in lieu of notice and the statutory dismissal indemnity, not reinstatement. [Para 61][Para 65][Para 68]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted the employer's two reasons for restricting certification — the integrity of public-hospital certificates and the 100 to 200 daily sick absences that would disrupt production — without treating either as decisive on the bonus question. [Para 24]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The working hours are printed "6:30 a.m. to 10:30 a.m. and 11:15 p.m. to 3:00 p.m.", the afternoon session evidently intended as a.m. [Para 28] The dismissed worker's name appears as Leang Srouymuoy, Srouymouy, Sourmouy and "Leane Srouymouy", and the co-worker as both Ven Maly and Van Maly. [Para 28][Para 35][Para 70] The company is styled "Yung Wah IIndustrial" at para 1 and Yung Wah Industrial elsewhere. [Para 1][Para 17] Article 83 is quoted with a stray "194." before the text of point 4. [Para 57] The award was due on 14 March 2012 and issued on 22 March 2012 after the parties agreed an extension. [Para 14][Para 1] The English text is an unofficial translation with pervasive word-joins ("themployer", "sick deave", "attendance bonus"), duplicated sentences at paras 43 and 46, and the evidence section omitted, so the written testimonies relied on cannot be checked. [Para 15][Para 43][Para 46]
