# Award No. 16/12 — Vision Glory Knitting (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 24 February 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Kong Phallack (chair) · Doc ID 139941

## 1. Facts
Vision Glory Knitting (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. runs a garment factory at Dangkor, Phnom Penh, with 560 workers and two unions, the Local Union of the Cambodian Labour Union Federation (CLUF) and the Workers' Benefits Union [Para 18]. Of the claims filed on 30 December 2011, two survived conciliation on 18 January 2012: reinstatement of Mr S (name redacted), the local union president, and Mr K (name redacted), its vice-president, whose fixed-duration contracts ended in December 2011; and the date for repaying a medical check-up fee [Para 6][Para 8][Para 2][Para 3]. The fee issue was settled at the hearing of 3 February 2012, leaving reinstatement, which the panel treated as a rights dispute and decided by binding award under the MoU of 28 September 2010 [Para 10][Para 11][Para 44][Para 65].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer was obliged to reinstate the union president and vice-president, whose fixed-duration contracts it did not renew on 23 December 2011? [Para 42]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer had to fix a date for repaying the medical check-up fee? [Para 3]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** both men began probationary work in the washing section on 21 April 2011 and held contracts running from 21 September to 21 December 2011; the non-renewal was union discrimination, coming the day after they were re-elected on 22 December 2011, and neither had ever been warned [Para 20][Para 31][Para 34][Para 36][Para 38]. They accepted the employer's account of the facts but argued that, once elected, they reasonably refused tasks outside their core duties [Para 32][Para 33].
**Employer (respondent):** the true contract dates were 23 September to 23 December 2011, the originals having been altered with correction pen; it did not renew because the two refused directions on tasks such as installing sewing machines and construction work after becoming union leaders, although previously diligent; it did not renew other workers in December 2011 either, and would have acted earlier had it intended discrimination [Para 22][Para 24][Para 27][Para 30][Para 39][Para 40].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted (Issue 1) — the employer was ordered to reinstate Mr S and Mr K; no sum in riel or USD was awarded. Issue 2 had already been resolved by agreement [Para 64][Para 65][Para 10].

The panel first classified the dispute, holding that a demand for reinstatement grounded in the Labour Law is a rights dispute, so the award is binding and enforceable under the MoU [Para 44][Para 65]. It then fixed the contract type. Under Art. 67(7) a fixed-duration contract must be in writing, failing which it becomes a contract of undetermined duration [Para 44][Para 45]. The parties disputed the end date — 21 December on the workers' account, 23 December on the employer's — and the employer alone held the originals, which it had admittedly altered with correction pen [Para 47][Para 52]. The panel ordered production by 8 February 2012 and the employer did not comply [Para 49]. Applying its consistent rule that the claimant bears the burden of proof but that a party withholding the best evidence cannot rely on the gap, the panel found the employer had insufficient evidence and accepted the workers' dates (Awards 79/05 Ever Green, 101/08 GDM, 148/11 Dai Young) [Para 50][Para 54][Para 55].

It followed that the two were working on 22 and 23 December 2011 under contracts of undetermined duration, and that the 23 December 2011 non-renewal was in substance a dismissal requiring the procedure of the Labour Law [Para 55].

On that procedure, Art. 293 permits dismissal of a shop steward, or a candidate for shop steward, only with the Labour Inspector's authorisation, and clause 3(3) of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 extends the same protection to a worker who runs for union leadership, from 45 days before the election to 45 days after it, provided the employer was duly informed by any reliable means [Para 57][Para 58][Para 59]. The panel applied the three conditions it derived in Awards 71/09 Hytex and 09/10 Chung Hao [Para 61]. Both men were candidates and were elected on 22 December 2011; the dismissal on 23 December 2011 fell inside the 45-day window; and the union notified the employer of the election on 23 December 2011 [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63]. The employer never sought the Labour Inspector's authorisation, so the dismissal of protected union leaders was procedurally unlawful and reinstatement followed [Para 63][Para 64].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer holds the original fixed-duration contracts, has altered their dates, and fails to produce them when ordered, the panel will accept the workers' version of the dates; work performed after that end date under no written fixed-duration contract converts the relationship to one of undetermined duration under Art. 67(7), so a later "non-renewal" is a dismissal [Para 54][Para 55]. A union leader elected within the preceding 45 days may be dismissed only with the Labour Inspector's prior authorisation under Art. 293, and a dismissal without it is unlawful, entitling the leader to reinstatement [Para 63][Para 64].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that on contract expiry the employer's practice is to pay 5% severance and pay in lieu of annual leave, and that neither man had received severance, annual-leave payment or December 2011 wages, and that the dismissal occurred without Labour Inspector authorisation — observations not reflected in the order, which was confined to reinstatement [Para 41][Para 64]. Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented, holding the non-renewal lawful under Art. 73 because the contracts had genuinely expired and double pay had been given for the gap day, while accepting that Art. 293 authorisation was still required [Para 69][Para 70][Para 71].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Two dates for referral of the case appear: the non-conciliation report is dated 27 January 2012 and was submitted on 30 January 2012, but the reasons say the two issues were referred to the Secretariat "on 30 May 2012", after the award date [Para 6][Para 9]. Paragraph 38 dates the dismissal and the notification of election results to 23 October 2011, while the findings elsewhere place both on 23 December 2011 [Para 38][Para 27][Para 63]. Mr K is described as vice-president in the party list and in the issue statement but as secretary in the factual narrative [Para 2][Para 20][Para 42].
