# Award No. 165/12 — Jin Yi Garment (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 19 September 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tai Seng for the employer, An Non for the workers, Pen Bunhea presiding) · Doc ID 140171

## 1. Facts

Jin Yi Garment (Cambodia) Ltd, also trading as Long Piseu, is a garment factory in Samaki Meanchey district, Kampong Chhnang province, which began operations in August 2012 and employed 487 workers [Para 36][Para 37]. Two federations were the claimants: the Cambodia Democratic Union Federation and the Cambodia National Heritage Union Federation [Para 38]. The Democratic federation held a leadership election on 5 August 2012, at which worker A was elected union president and worker B secretary, but the union had not yet been registered [Para 39][Para 42]. On 9 August 2012 the union delivered notice of the two office-holders to the employer through the security desk at about 1:00–2:00 p.m.; the employer admits receiving the notice at about 10:00 a.m. that day [Para 43][Para 44]. By about 3:30 p.m. the same afternoon the employer had dismissed both women [Para 43][Para 51]. Conciliation on 15 August 2012 failed on the single outstanding demand, and the case came to the panel on 28 August 2012 [Para 5][Para 8][Para 7]. The parties elected a non-binding award [Para 13].

The single demand was that the employer take both workers back into normal employment [Para 2][Para 41]. The union argued the dismissals were union discrimination [Para 41]. The employer argued the two were still within their probation period and that their work was poor, and that other probationary workers had also been let go [Para 45][Para 46]. The panel found that worker A had been hired on 5 July 2012 for the warehouse, had signed only a job application and never a written contract, and was told neither that the engagement was probationary nor how long it would last [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50]. Worker B applied on 8 August 2012, began work at 10:00 a.m. that day and worked until 9 August 2012 [Para 90]. Worker A's net monthly wage was 61 US dollars and she was not paid after dismissal [Para 52][Para 53].

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer's dismissal of the two union office-holders on 9 August 2012 was lawful, and whether they must therefore be reinstated to normal employment? [Para 41][Para 132]

No other issue was recorded as decided; the panel framed the case around this single demand and answered it in the operative order [Para 132].

## 3. Positions of the parties

The workers' side maintained that the dismissals were motivated by union discrimination, coming hours after the union notified the employer of its newly elected leadership [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43]. It asked for both to be reinstated to normal work [Para 41].

The employer denied any link to union activity [Para 45]. It said the two were probationary workers whose work was slow and that the company had dismissed other probationary workers in the same period [Para 46]. It added that the deputy union president and the leadership of the other federation were not dismissed [Para 47]. On worker A specifically, the employer said at the hearing that she was an ordinary worker on a one-month probation [Para 54].

## 4. Award and reasoning

The panel first classified each employment relationship. Article 65 allows a labour contract to be written or oral, and Article 66 allows engagement by time or by task, for a fixed or indefinite term [Para 84][Para 85][Para 86]. From this the panel drew the rule that a contract made orally, without written form, is an indefinite-term contract [Para 86]. Worker A had signed only an application form, never a contract, and worked on from 5 to 9 August 2012 with no written agreement [Para 49][Para 50][Para 87]. Her relationship was therefore indefinite-term, and her dismissal was not the end of a probationary contract but a dismissal during an indefinite-term contract [Para 88][Para 89].

Worker B stood differently. She applied on 8 August, worked from 10:00 a.m. that day and was dismissed on 9 August [Para 90]. The employer gave two inconsistent accounts — that she was a probationary worker, and that management had not yet decided to take her on and was allowing two or three days to test her ability [Para 92][Para 93]. The panel read the two-to-three-day trial as a probation period, consistent with the company's internal rules requiring one month's probation for unskilled workers [Para 95][Para 96]. As a warehouse worker she was unskilled, so her probation was one month, and she was dismissed within it [Para 97][Para 98][Para 99].

That did not end the matter, because both were union leaders [Para 100]. Article 293 requires the labour inspector's agreement before a staff delegate or candidate is dismissed, and Prakas 305 of 22 November 2001 extends that protection to union leadership candidates [Para 100][Para 101]. The panel identified three conditions for protection: the worker must be a protected person, the dismissal must fall within the protection period, and the union must notify the employer of the candidacy by any practicable means [Para 104]. On the first, both were elected office-holders, so both were protected [Para 105][Para 106]. On the second, the dismissal fell within the protection period, since the protection runs at least 45 days before and after the election for candidates who are not elected [Para 108][Para 109][Para 112][Para 116][Para 117]. On the third, the union's notice reached the employer on 9 August 2012 and the employer admitted receipt, so notification by a practicable means was complete [Para 119][Para 120][Para 121][Para 122].

Having found both protected, the panel asked whether the dismissals followed legal procedure [Para 124]. Article 293 requires the employer to seek the labour inspector's prior authorisation, and the employer had not done so [Para 124][Para 125]. The dismissal was therefore procedurally unlawful [Para 126][Para 127]. Following its earlier awards, the panel ordered the employer to take both workers back into normal employment [Para 128][Para 130][Para 131].

**Outcome:** The employer was ordered to reinstate worker A and worker B to normal employment [Para 132].

## 5. Ratio decidendi

An oral contract of employment, made without written form, is an indefinite-term contract; a worker engaged that way cannot be treated as a probationary hire whose contract simply expired [Para 86][Para 88]. A union office-holder elected in a leadership election is a protected person under Article 293 and Prakas 305, and the protection covers the period around the election whether or not the candidate was elected [Para 101][Para 106][Para 109]. Notification of the candidacy by any practicable means — including delivery through the employer's security desk — satisfies the union's duty, and the employer's admitted receipt closes the point [Para 103][Para 121]. A dismissal of a protected worker without the labour inspector's prior authorisation is unlawful, and the remedy is reinstatement [Para 126][Para 127][Para 131].
