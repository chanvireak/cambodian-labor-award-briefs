# Order No. 229/12 — AZIADEE (Arbitration Council, 26 December 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mr You Soun Ty for the employer party, Mr An Nan for the worker party, Mr Aing Eng Tong chair; Mr You Soun Ty added a dissenting opinion) · Doc ID 140224

## 1. Facts
AZIADEE is a hair-cutting, hairdressing and beauty establishment in Boeung Keng Kang I, Phnom Penh, operating since 2007 with 13 staff and no Internal Work Rules; the claimants are five massage staff, engaged orally with a base wage of USD 50–70 a month, USD 1 an hour and a USD 20 meal allowance. [Para 30][Para 32][Para 33][Para 58][Para 66] On 17 October 2012 they asked the cashier for two or three hours off from about noon to join the funeral procession of the late King, were refused, went anyway and asked for their wages to be docked; on 18 October 2012 the employer dismissed all five on the spot without notice. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43][Para 44][Para 47][Para 97] One of the five, Ms C, had been trained and referred to the establishment by an NGO, "For the Smile of Children", which the employer says makes her that organisation's worker rather than its own. [Para 38][Para 67][Para 74] The union had one of five demands conciliated away; three unresolved demands reached the Council under report No. 2080 of 12 November 2012. [Para 13][Para 14]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether Ms C is a worker of the employer at all, given that an NGO trained and referred her? [Para 71][Para 72]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the five claimants are casual (floating) workers or permanent workers? [Para 78][Para 80]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the five are entitled to payment in lieu of notice, dismissal damages, moral damages, unused annual leave and final wages? [Para 92][Para 107]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** they asked the cashier for a few hours off, were refused, went to the procession out of national mourning, and told the cashier to dock their wages, believing the penalty for absence was USD 20 as after Pchum Ben; the employer then dismissed them without notice or payment. [Para 42][Para 45][Para 46][Para 55]
**Employer (respondent):** only eight massage staff remained that day including the five, so releasing them would have left three and harmed the business; other establishments displayed the late King's portrait for staff to pay respects instead; no government instruction granted leave on 17 October; and the five are floating staff, with Ms C supplied by an NGO, so no statutory severance is owed. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 51][Para 56][Para 79]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to pay each of the five the severance package under the Labour Law, being payment in lieu of notice, dismissal damages, unused annual leave, moral damages and final wages; the order is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Minister. [Para 145][Para 146]

On Issue 1 the panel applied the Article 3 definition of a worker — a person who contracts to work for remuneration under the direction and management of another — and the Award 130/08-Fu Hing checklist (who recruits, who pays, who fixes hours, who grants leave, who handles serious misconduct, who ends the contract), also citing 53/07-Igaman and 04/05-Intercontinental. [Para 72][Para 73] It found the employer recruited Ms C, paid her, set her hours, granted leave and dismissed her, while the NGO only advised and trained, so she was the employer's worker and the employer owed her severance like the others. [Para 74][Para 75][Para 76][Para 77]

On Issue 2, Article 9 distinguishes permanent work, which is regular and of a permanent character, from occasional work that is short, intermittent or seasonal; the five worked regularly each month for base wage, hourly pay and meal allowance, so they were permanent workers, and the panel added that even occasional workers are owed statutory severance on dismissal. [Para 80][Para 81][Para 83][Para 85][Para 86] Article 67 requires a fixed-duration contract to be in writing with a stated end date, failing which it becomes of undetermined duration, and none of the five had a written contract. [Para 87][Para 89][Para 90][Para 91]

On Issue 3, Article 74 allows unilateral termination only with written notice and a valid reason; the panel found no internal rules and no legal provision making a single unauthorised afternoon's absence serious misconduct, so discipline must still be proportionate under Article 27. [Para 94][Para 103][Para 104][Para 105][Para 107] The right response was a warning, oral or written, before any dismissal. [Para 113] The dismissal therefore lacked a valid reason, which grounds moral damages equal to the dismissal damages under Article 91, the workers being relieved of proving loss. [Para 112][Para 136][Para 138][Para 141] Notice pay followed Article 75 and 77: one month for the two with over two years' service, 15 days for the three with six months to two years. [Para 120][Para 124][Para 125] Dismissal damages followed Article 89: 60, 7, 45, 30 and 7 days respectively. [Para 126][Para 130] Unused annual leave was owed under Article 166, and final wages and allowances within 48 hours under Article 116. [Para 134][Para 143][Para 144] The panel disregarded the fact that the workers telephoned radio stations, since that happened after the dismissal. [Para 114][Para 117]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker's status follows the reality of direction and management, not the identity of whoever trained or referred her, so an employer that recruits, pays, sets hours, grants leave and dismisses cannot escape severance by pointing to an NGO that supplied the worker; and an unauthorised absence of a few hours to attend a national mourning event, where no internal rule or statutory provision makes it serious misconduct, will not justify immediate dismissal, which is disproportionate under Article 27 and exposes the employer to moral damages equal to the dismissal damages under Article 91. [Para 76][Para 104][Para 112][Para 141]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that telephoning radio stations was not the best way to resolve a dispute, while expressly not counting it against the workers because it followed the dismissal, is a general observation on conduct. [Para 114][Para 117]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The arbitrator appointed by the employer filed a dissent (annex to the order) disputing only the moral damages, and the order nonetheless records a unanimous signature block; the dissent is dated the same day. [Para 147][Para 149][Para 152][Para 153]
- The chair's name appears as "អាំង អេង ថុង" on the face of the order and in a corrupted form in the signature block. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 147]
- The NGO's name and the paragraph describing its role are badly corrupted by OCR, with stray Latin words embedded, so the referral relationship is reconstructed from the surrounding findings only. [Para 67][Para 68]
- One claimant's name is printed inconsistently across the order and the establishment's address is partly garbled; workers are recorded here by role and initial. [Para 2][Para 34][Para 83]
- One sentence of Latin OCR noise interrupts the reasoning on Article 116, and the paragraph on Article 116 allows 48 hours while the article quoted says 48 hours in words — the two figures only coincide if read as hours, which the text supports. [Para 143][Para 142][Para 144]
