# Award No. 19/12 — IMPA International Security (Arbitration Council, 13 March 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun for the employer party, Sin Kim San for the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 139948

## 1. Facts
IMPA International Security is a security-services company employing about 3,000 guards; the claimant is the union of the company's workers, registered on 11 January 2012 and recognised by the Ministry of Labour. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 29] Of 16 demands, 12 remained unresolved after conciliation and were referred on 1 February 2012; the panel held two hearings and the parties settled five issues and withdrew three, leaving eight for decision. [Para 16][Para 21][Para 24][Para 85][Para 97][Para 109]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate the union's elected president, dismissed on 2 December 2011 while its registration application was pending? [Para 2][Para 44]
3. Whether the employer must stop deducting US$ 20 from a guard's wage for each day of unauthorised absence? [Para 74][Para 168]
4. Whether the employer must restore payment of monthly wages before the 10th of each month to supervisory guards? [Para 79][Para 173]
5. Whether the employer must pay the statutory termination benefits to a guard dismissed on 24 August 2011 after 15 years' service? [Para 85][Para 193]
8. Whether overtime must be voluntary and paid at 150% of the normal rate? [Para 229][Para 237]
11. Whether the employer must reinstate the union secretary, reassigned and then suspended after it sought authorisation to dismiss him? [Para 111][Para 243]
12. Whether the employer must arrange and pay for the health check of every guard? [Para 125][Para 256]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the president was dismissed for his union activity, the employer producing no evidence of the three allegations and no warning; guards lose US$ 20 for a single day's absence, a fine rather than a wage adjustment; supervisory guards were always paid before the 10th until the payroll bank changed without notice or agreement; the 15-year guard was owed the Article 75, 85, 91, 166 and 167 benefits; overtime was imposed without notice. [Para 51][Para 69][Para 75][Para 94][Para 230]
**Employer (respondent):** the president was dismissed for leaving early, circulating the company's ID codes and losing clients, and the authorisation request is still with the Ministry; the US$ 20 deduction follows a written instruction with a period to object; the change of bank and pay date came from head office abroad; working two jobs was a disciplinary offence but not serious misconduct, and the guard did not seek reinstatement; overtime is set by management. [Para 71][Para 78][Para 83][Para 90][Para 199][Para 233]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** largely granted — reinstatement of the president; the US$ 20 daily deduction stopped and internal discipline substituted; the pre-10th pay date restored for supervisory guards; termination benefits for the long-serving guard (three months' notice pay, 90 days' dismissal indemnity, final wages, annual leave) but no damages; overtime made voluntary and paid at 150%; the union secretary reinstated in his former post and wage; health checks arranged and paid by the employer. [Para 272][Para 273][Para 275][Para 276][Para 278][Para 279][Para 280]

On issue 1 the panel set out Article 293 and Clause 4 of Prakas No. 305: dismissal of a shop steward or candidate requires the Labour Inspector's authorisation, and founders and members are protected during a registration application. [Para 130][Para 131][Para 132] Protection depends on three conditions — that the worker is one the Prakas covers, that the dismissal fell inside the protected period, and that the union had notified the employer of the names — and the third failed because the union never told the employer of the election or of the protected persons. [Para 137][Para 144][Para 145][Para 148] The panel nonetheless held that the president held a contract of undetermined duration, so Article 74 applied: the employer needed a valid reason and written notice, produced no evidence identifying the dates, recipients or clients behind its three allegations, issued no written or oral warning, and did not show the misconduct reached a disciplinary threshold in the internal rules. [Para 149][Para 153][Para 157][Para 158][Para 159] Reinstatement followed. [Para 167]

On issue 3 the panel held Article 28 of the Labour Law prohibits monetary penalties and double discipline for one fault, defining a monetary penalty as any measure reducing the wage below what the work should earn, and ordered the deduction stopped with the internal disciplinary system used instead. [Para 169][Para 170][Para 171][Para 172] On issue 4 it read Article 116(2) as requiring payment at least once a month without fixing a day, leaving the date to management prerogative; but where the employer moved the payroll bank and the pay date from before the 10th to the 15th for supervisors only, without clear notice, agreement or any reason given at the hearing, the prerogative had not been exercised reasonably, and the original date was restored. [Para 175][Para 180][Para 183][Para 190][Para 191][Para 192]

On issue 5, Article 75 required three months' notice after more than ten years' service and Article 77 compensation in lieu where none was given, which the panel fixed at three months' wages and benefits; [Para 200][Para 203][Para 205] Article 89, at 15 days per year of service capped at six months, produced 90 days for 15 years; [Para 207][Para 210][Para 213] Article 91 damages were refused because working two jobs gave a valid reason, the employer itself conceding it was not serious misconduct; [Para 218][Para 219] final wages and annual leave had already been paid and were confirmed due. [Para 221][Para 222][Para 227][Para 228]

On issue 8 the panel held overtime voluntary under Clause 4 of Prakas No. 80 of 1 March 1999, which forbids compulsion or discipline of a worker who declines, and required the 150% rate under the amended Article 139 and Clause 5 of that Prakas, which the employer had not applied. [Para 231][Para 234][Para 237][Para 238][Para 240][Para 242] On issue 11 the employer sought authorisation to dismiss the union secretary on 28 December 2011 and had no reply by the hearing; under Article 293 the Inspector must decide within one month and silence is a refusal, so reinstatement in his former post at his former wage was ordered. [Para 243][Para 245][Para 250][Para 253][Para 255] On issue 12, Articles 247(a) and (c) with Clause 2(d) of inter-ministerial Prakas No. 1191 of 21 November 2006 put the cost of the health check on the employer, at 12,000 riel per worker, whether the check precedes or follows recruitment; the social-security argument was irrelevant to that obligation. [Para 257][Para 259][Para 264][Para 265][Para 271]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union leader who cannot satisfy the third Prakas No. 305 condition, because the union never notified the employer who its protected officers were, loses the Article 293 protection but keeps the ordinary protection of Article 74: a dismissal resting on allegations for which no evidence, warning or disciplinary step is produced is without valid reason, and reinstatement follows. Separately, deducting a fixed sum from wages for each day of absence is a prohibited monetary penalty under Article 28, and overtime is voluntary and payable at 150%. [Para 145][Para 159][Para 167][Para 171][Para 231]

**Obiter dicta:** the discussion of when a Labour Inspector's silence becomes a refusal, and the panel's observation that a one-month period runs from receipt of the request, are general statements on Article 293 not needed for the order on issue 1. [Para 135][Para 245][Para 250]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The non-conciliation report carried 16 demands but only 12 were referred [Para 21]; the decision treats seven of them and numbers them 1, 3, 4, 5, 8, 11 and 12, with no text for 2, 6, 7, 9 or 10, so the numbering cannot be reconciled with the original list.
- The employer is recorded as absent without reason at the first hearing [Para 13], while the panel elsewhere finds both parties present at the hearings [Para 23].
- The health-check sum appears as "1,2000 riel" and as "12.000 riel" in successive paragraphs [Para 265][Para 268], and the statutory figure is 12,000 riel [Para 259]; the order carries no figure at all [Para 280].
- The employer's name shifts between "IMPA International" [Para 1] and "IMPA International Cutting" [Para 28], and the evidence list repeats whole entries verbatim with duplicated numbering [Para 30][Para 32][Para 37].
