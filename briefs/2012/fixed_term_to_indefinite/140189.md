# Award No. 190/12 — Long Lead (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 24 October 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun, Liv Sovanna, Ang Eng Tong as chair) · Doc ID 140189

## 1. Facts

Long Lead (Cambodia) is a shoe factory at Chamkar Svay village, Sethey commune, Samaki Meanchey district, Kampong Chhnang province, employing 1,341 workers, and the Khmer Youth Union at the factory is the claimant [Para 61][Para 62][Para 64][Para 65]. The union complained on 19 September 2012 that the company had refused to take back its local president and put him to normal work; conciliation on 20 September 2012 settled nothing, and the file reached the Council on 21 September 2012 [Para 8][Para 9][Para 10]. The single hearing was held on 3 October 2012 with both parties present [Para 7][Para 12]. The award was due on 18 October 2012 and was extended by agreement to 24 October 2012 [Para 18]. The worker had been engaged on successive fixed-duration contracts from 20 April 2010, renewed in April 2011, April 2012 and finally for five months to 20 September 2012, when the company declined to renew [Para 70][Para 71][Para 72][Para 76][Para 77]. The union had been registered on 12 July 2010 and its new leadership was recognised by the Ministry by letter No. 866 of 9 August 2012 [Para 66][Para 115].

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer was obliged to reinstate the local union president and pay him back wages from dismissal to reinstatement, where his successive fixed-duration contracts had run beyond two years and he was a protected union leader dismissed without the Labour Inspector's authorisation? [Para 69][Para 95][Para 113]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** the company failed to follow the procedure in Article 73 of the Labour Law and the separate procedure for dismissing union leaders; the president had worked continuously from April 2010 through successive contracts, had been the local union president since mid-2010, and should be taken back with back pay [Para 70][Para 89][Para 90].

**Employer (respondent):** this was a non-renewal of a fixed-duration contract, not a dismissal; it gave advance notice, paid a 5% end-of-contract indemnity at the end of each fixed term before renewing, and on 16 September 2012 tendered final wages, seniority pay, health allowance, travel and accommodation allowances, function allowance, overtime, leave pay and the end-of-contract indemnity, all of which the worker refused [Para 82][Para 84][Para 85][Para 86][Para 87][Para 88].

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to take the local union president back into work and to pay him back wages from the date of dismissal until the date it takes him back [Para 121][Para 123].

The panel first classified the claim as one over rights, because it turned on the type of employment contract, and therefore decided it by law rather than by equity [Para 91][Para 94]. It then posed the question whether the contract was of fixed or undetermined duration [Para 95]. Article 67(2) caps a fixed-duration contract at two years and permits renewal only while the maximum total stays within two years, and any practice contrary to that rule converts the contract into one without a precise term [Para 96][Para 97][Para 98]. Article 73(5) adds that where no notice is given the contract continues for an equal term, or is treated as of undetermined duration once the total exceeds the Article 67 limit [Para 99][Para 103]. Following the reasoning in Award 10/03-Jacksinter and drawing on ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982, the panel read the reference to total duration as referring to the overall maximum, not to each renewal, and held that renewal beyond two years converts the contract [Para 100][Para 104][Para 105][Para 108]. The worker's successive contracts ran from 20 April 2010 and, measured to 20 September 2012, totalled two years and three months; the employer also never issued a fresh recruitment notice requiring him to reapply [Para 109][Para 110][Para 111]. The panel therefore found the contract one of undetermined duration, so the non-renewal was a dismissal [Para 112].

As local union president of a union registered in July 2010 and with its new leadership recognised in August 2012, the worker held the special protection of Article 293 of the Labour Law and Clause 4 paragraph 2 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, which extends to three union leaders the rule that no dismissal may occur without the Labour Inspector's prior authorisation [Para 113][Para 114][Para 115][Para 116]. The employer's letter No. 708 of 7 September 2012 notified the termination of ninety fixed-duration contracts without naming the workers, and no authorisation was obtained; the panel found the procedure violated [Para 117][Para 118][Para 119]. It ordered reinstatement and back pay [Para 120][Para 121].

## 5. Ratio decidendi

Successive fixed-duration contracts whose combined duration passes two years convert into a contract of undetermined duration under Articles 67(2) and 73(5), and the two-year limit is measured against the total of all renewals, not against each renewal; the employer's refusal to renew is then a dismissal, not the expiry of a term [Para 98][Para 104][Para 112]. A union leader dismissed while enjoying the protection of Article 293 and Prakas No. 305 must be reinstated with back pay if the Labour Inspector's prior authorisation was never sought, and a notice that lists ninety unnamed workers does not supply that authorisation [Para 114][Para 117][Para 119].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that undetermined-duration contracts promote job security for workers and commitment for employers, and that ILO Recommendation No. 166 is persuasive though not binding, were context for the interpretation rather than part of the ruling [Para 102][Para 107].
