# Award No. 173/12 — King Fashion Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 10 October 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tai Seng, Liv Sovanna, Kong Phallha presiding) · Doc ID 140177

## 1. Facts
King Fashion Industrial, a garment factory at Damnak Ampil, Ang Snuol district, Kandal, employed 1,005 workers and dismissed four of them — Mr S, Mr H, Mr So and Mr K — on 24 August 2012 [Para 100][Para 107]. On 23 August 2012 a union held an election beside the factory; the four were elected president, vice-president, secretary and treasurer [Para 109][Para 110]. They say they served notice of the result on the employer at about 2:00 pm on 24 August, hours after the dismissals [Para 112][Para 159]. Conciliation at the Kandal provincial department of labour failed on a single issue, reinstatement of the four [Para 13][Para 108], and the union was still unregistered when the file reached the Council [Para 102]. The employer's allegation that the union's election minutes were forged [Para 76] was recorded but not decided: the panel disposed of the discrimination question on the employer's state of knowledge, so authenticity never became material [Para 162].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the dismissal of the four workers was anti-union discrimination contrary to Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law? [Para 153]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer terminated the contracts of the four in accordance with legal procedure? [Para 163]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissals were discrimination against union leaders, coming immediately after the election; they refused the severance tendered because what they want is their jobs back [Para 108][Para 152][Para 119].
**Employer (respondent):** the dismissals had nothing to do with the election, of which the company was unaware when it acted at about 10:30 am on 24 August; the contracts were ended lawfully with wages, 5% severance, unused annual leave and remaining contract pay; Mr S and Mr H performed poorly and drank alcohol, and Mr K incited a strike that disrupted production [Para 114][Para 137][Para 140][Para 174].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the reinstatement claims of Mr S and Mr So were dismissed [Para 176][Para 225]; the employer was ordered to take back Mr H in his former post [Para 204] and Mr K [Para 224][Para 226]; Mr H must repay the 5% severance he had already received [Para 209].
On Issue 1 the panel applied Articles 12 and 279, which forbid an employer to use union membership or activity as a ground of dismissal [Para 153][Para 154]. The election was held at about 11:25 am on 23 August and notice given at about 2:00 pm on 24 August, whereas the dismissals occurred at about 10:30 am that morning [Para 109][Para 159][Para 160]. The panel therefore held that the employer did not know the four had been elected, so the dismissals could not be discrimination on union grounds [Para 162].
On Issue 2 the panel split the four by contract type. Mr S and Mr So held successive six-month fixed-term contracts, the last expiring on 1 September and 11 November 2012 respectively [Para 171]. Under Article 73 the employer could end them early and owed severance proportional to wages and contract length, at least 5% where the collective agreement is silent [Para 169][Para 170]; it paid final wages, unused annual leave, 5% severance and the wages remaining to the contract end [Para 174], so the panel found the procedure lawful and dismissed those claims [Para 175][Para 176].
Mr H's contracts ran from 2 August 2010 in successive six-month terms, an aggregate beyond two years [Para 177][Para 189]. Reading Article 67(2) with Article 73(5), the panel held that renewals whose total exceeds two years convert the relationship into an undetermined-duration contract [Para 183][Para 188]. The employer had accused him of poor performance and drinking but had never warned him orally or in writing, as the internal rules require for light or medium misconduct before dismissal [Para 195][Para 200][Para 201]. The panel found the disciplinary process did not comply with those rules [Para 202] and invoked Article 34 of Prakas 99 to order reinstatement [Para 203].
Mr K held an undetermined-duration contract and was dismissed for serious misconduct under Article 83(5), incitement [Para 211][Para 215]. The panel noted that the Article reaches incitement to serious misconduct, and that Article 330 treats violence during a strike as serious misconduct [Para 216][Para 217]; earlier awards hold that calling a strike is not itself serious misconduct absent evidence of incitement to violence [Para 218]. Disruption was proven, but no violence was shown [Para 221][Para 222], so the charge failed and reinstatement was ordered [Para 223][Para 224].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive fixed-term contracts whose aggregate duration exceeds two years become a contract of undetermined duration by operation of Article 67(2), and a dismissal for alleged light or medium misconduct without the warnings the internal rules prescribe is unlawful, reinstatement following under Article 73 and Prakas 99 [Para 188][Para 202][Para 203]. Separately, a dismissal cannot be anti-union discrimination where the employer acted before knowing of the election or of the workers' union offices [Para 162], and incitement is not serious misconduct under Article 83(5) unless it is incitement to serious misconduct such as violence [Para 218][Para 223].

**Obiter dicta:** ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982 is not binding but useful context for reading Article 67 [Para 185][Para 187]; the worker who gains an undetermined-duration contract must return the 5% severance paid at each earlier contract end, by any method the parties agree [Para 209].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The Khmer OCR is noisy and duplicates whole lines, for example the notice letters to Mr So and Mr H [Para 26][Para 27] and the union congress minutes [Para 89]. Stray Latin fragments ("Mani", "Apple", "Billin") interrupt the reasons [Para 14][Para 203][Para 213], and the case caption gives the employer in Khmer transliteration while the title renders it "King Fashion Industrial" [Para 1][Para 99]. One worker's surname is spelled three different ways across the non-settlement report, his job application and contracts, and his birth certificate, the panel adopting the contract spelling [Para 104][Para 106]; the award elsewhere gives two spellings of the same surname within one paragraph [Para 107].
