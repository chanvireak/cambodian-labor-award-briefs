# Order No. 131/12 — Master & Freng (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 17 August 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — You Soun Ty (employer-appointed), An Virak (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (president) · Doc ID 140148

## 1. Facts
Master & Freng (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. operates at Village 3, Peuk Commune, Ang Snuol District, Kandal, and appeared through its general affairs chief, Mr C, and an administration officer, Mr O [Para 1]. A federation of cooperative unions for Cambodia filed a collective dispute on 26 June 2012 seeking improvement of a package of working conditions; conciliation by the Kandal provincial labour department settled 11 of 18 demands and left 7 unresolved [Para 21][Para 22]. The unresolved points reached the Arbitration Council on 6 July 2012 through non-conciliation report No. 522/12 [Para 18][Para 23]. Hearings set for 13 and 19 July 2012 were postponed at the employer's request with the workers' agreement, and the federation then failed to attend the hearing of 10 August 2012 at 2 p.m. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 26].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the panel should continue the arbitration at all, given that the claimant federation did not appear at the hearing of 10 August 2012? [Para 44][Para 41]

The panel recorded seven further demands — contract length, attendance bonus, transport allowance, midday meal, maternity pay, overtime meal allowance and bonus deductions (paras 2-15). Having declined to continue the case, it decided none of them; they are set out in §1.

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the federation demanded contracts running six months to two years instead of short renewals, a USD 18 attendance bonus, USD 15 transport, USD 15 midday meal, full pay across 90 days of maternity leave, and 3,000 riel for overtime meals [Para 2][Para 4][Para 6][Para 8][Para 10][Para 12]. It did not appear at the final hearing and explained only that its official had mistaken the time, asking the panel to proceed under its own procedure [Para 26][Para 57].

**Employer (respondent):** the company applied its existing three-month renewal practice and offered USD 7 attendance bonus and USD 5 transport under the Prakas, said it had no capacity to fund the midday meal, asked that maternity pay follow the Labour Law, offered 2,000 riel for overtime meals, and asked that bonus deductions follow the Prakas [Para 3][Para 5][Para 7][Para 9][Para 11][Para 13][Para 15]. It attended on the date fixed [Para 27][Para 58].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to continue arbitration in case No. 131/12 and treated the complaint as abandoned; no order was made on any of the seven demands [Para 63][Para 65].

On the merits the panel recorded that it made no factual findings on the seven disputed points, precisely because the claimant federation never attended [Para 41]. It turned instead to Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council, which allows the panel either to continue in a party's absence or to close the case by order [Para 44]. Reading Article 21 together with Rule 4.7 of the Council's procedural rules — which requires the panel, on either course, to be satisfied that the parties were properly notified of the date, time and place — the panel treated notification as the threshold question [Para 45][Para 46].

Following its earlier awards in cases 16/07-Lotus, 27/07-IM & V3, 138/08-Malin & 2 and 11/11-Chhun Tech, the panel applied three cumulative conditions for closing a file in a party's absence: proper invitation, non-appearance, and absence of a valid excuse [Para 47][Para 48]. The first was satisfied: the secretariat telephoned and wrote to the claimant by letter No. 776 of 2 August 2012, and telephoned again on the hearing day, when the claimant answered that it would not come [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51]. The second was satisfied because "appearance before the panel" means both presence at the hearing and participation through all four stages — introduction and conflict-of-interest explanation, explanation of procedure and issues, optional renewed conciliation, and the hearing itself — none of which the claimant attended [Para 53][Para 54][Para 55]. The third was satisfied because confusing the hearing time is not a valid excuse [Para 57][Para 59]. With all three conditions met, the panel held the claimant to have abandoned its complaint and closed the file [Para 61][Para 63].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claimant that has been properly summoned, then neither appears nor shows a valid excuse, is deemed to have abandoned its complaint, and the panel may terminate the arbitration without any ruling on the merits; "appearance" requires participation across every stage of the hearing, not mere presence [Para 55][Para 63].

**Obiter dicta:** the four-stage description of an arbitration hearing, and the remark that the Council keeps power to issue an order even where a claimant stays away, were not necessary to the three-condition test [Para 54][Para 62].
