# Award No. 101/12 — Dae Myoung Cham Peni Ltd (Angkor National Museum) (Arbitration Council, 20 September 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouchhun, employer-selected; An Non, worker-selected; Kong Phalla, chair) · Doc ID 140134

## 1. Facts
Dae Myoung Cham Peni Ltd, a company whose business is the construction and operation of the Angkor National Museum in Siem Reap, has run the museum since 2007 and employs about 46 staff. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 89][Para 90] The claimant is the Cambodian Tourism and Service Workers' Union Federation. [Para 91] Seven museum officers — gallery guards and customer-service staff, one of them the union's vice-president — had worked there for years without written contracts, under the day-to-day direction of two civil servants of the Ministry of Culture. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 101][Para 116] After a December 2011 appraisal the company concluded they were not civil servants, asked them to sign contracts, and terminated them on 18 April 2012 with effect from 18 May 2012 when they refused to sign a draft that still described them as gallery rather than service officers. [Para 98][Para 104][Para 118][Para 132]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate the seven and pay them wages and allowances from the date of dismissal to the date of return? [Para 92][Para 160]
2. Whether the employer must repay the USD 20 it deducted from one officer's February 2012 wages and resume his normal salary increments? [Para 196]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, Cambodian Tourism and Service Workers' Union Federation):** the refusal to sign was not a rejection of the employer's managerial authority, because the contract had been amended under the 9 April 2012 agreement and the only outstanding point was the job title; the company then dismissed them for union membership, and the terminated staff were told by the Ministry to keep working. [Para 122][Para 127][Para 162]
**Employer (respondent):** the seven committed serious misconduct by refusing to sign a contract that conformed to the 9 April agreement, showing they did not wish to keep working; they also neglected their duties, slept in the gallery during working hours, refused training and overtime, and the USD 20 was merely recovery of an overpayment caused by an administrative mistake. [Para 104][Para 107][Para 111][Para 146][Para 201]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to take the seven back into work from the date the award becomes final, and refused both the back-wage claim for the seven and the whole of the wage-deduction and salary-increment claim. [Para 214][Para 216][Para 217]

On reinstatement the panel first asked whether the refusal to sign amounted to rejecting the employer's right to manage or to an intention to abandon the job. [Para 162] Article 65 defines the employment contract and Articles 336 and 337 of the Civil Code require a matching offer and acceptance; the panel found offer and acceptance had not yet met, so the oral indefinite contract continued in force and the refusal to sign a new instrument was not itself a breach. [Para 166][Para 170][Para 173] It noted that the employer had in fact amended the contract to follow the 9 April agreement, and that the demand to change the job title was a new request outside that agreement. [Para 163][Para 165]

The panel then tested the misconduct alleged. Article 69(1) obliges the worker to do the job personally and with care, and Article 83(b) treats refusal to perform the contract as misconduct. [Para 175][Para 178] But Article 26(1) requires the employer to act on misconduct within 15 days of learning of it, and the employer admitted it had never imposed any disciplinary measure, preferring to give the staff time to reform. [Para 177][Para 114] No evidence at all was produced for the sleeping, absenteeism, training or overtime allegations, and the panel held they were unproven. [Para 113][Para 182][Para 184] Under Article 74 an indefinite contract may be terminated, but only for a reason relating to the worker's capability or conduct or to the needs of the enterprise; none was established, so the terminations were unlawful and reinstatement followed. [Para 187][Para 189][Para 190]

Back wages were nevertheless refused: between 19 May and 18 September 2012 the seven worked at the company, at the Ministry's request, and were paid normally, so they had lost nothing. [Para 192][Para 193][Para 194]

On the USD 20, the panel held Article 28 — which forbids double punishment and defines a pecuniary penalty as any measure reducing wages due for work done — was not engaged, because the January payment of USD 150 instead of USD 130 was an administrative error and the deduction merely recovered the excess. [Para 196][Para 197][Para 199][Para 200] It also rejected the anti-union discrimination complaint: Articles 12 and 279 forbid using union membership in decisions on pay and discipline, but the workers bore the burden of proof and produced nothing specific. [Para 205][Para 206][Para 211][Para 213]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker's refusal to sign a written contract that departs from what the parties had already agreed is not insubordination and does not end an existing oral indefinite contract; to terminate that contract the employer must prove the misconduct alleged and must have applied the statutory disciplinary procedure within 15 days of learning of it, failing which the termination is unlawful under Article 74 and the panel orders reinstatement. [Para 165][Para 170][Para 177][Para 189][Para 190] Recovering an amount paid to a worker by administrative error is not a pecuniary penalty under Article 28, and a discrimination complaint unsupported by evidence fails because the worker carries the burden. [Para 199][Para 211]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the workers were "temporarily" employed between May and September 2012 at the Ministry's request, and that the company retains the right to require contracts in future, are context for the back-wage refusal rather than holdings. [Para 131][Para 193]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is dated 20 September 2012 in the header, although the panel records that it was to issue on 25 June 2012 and was postponed by agreement of the parties. [Para 18] The employer of record is Dae Myoung Cham Peni Ltd, but the concession contract exhibited is between the Ministry of Culture and a different company, Vilailuck International Holding, so the chain by which the employer acquired operating rights is not explained. [Para 51][Para 88] The seven officers are named in full throughout the evidence schedule at [Para 93]; they are reduced to roles and initials here, and their salary table gives monthly wages for all seven. [Para 97] The termination date is given as 18 April 2012 with effect 18 May 2012 at [Para 118] and again at [Para 132], while the same workers are said to have worked and been paid from 19 May 2012, which cannot both be literally true. [Para 130][Para 192] Several paragraphs are duplicated verbatim in the source, including the reasoning on jurisdiction and the operative order. [Para 140][Para 215]
