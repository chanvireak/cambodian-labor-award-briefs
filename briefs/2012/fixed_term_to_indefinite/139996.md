# Award No. 64/12 — Dik Nitha Nit Ther Co Ltd (Dignity Knitter) (Arbitration Council, 10 May 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sombouna, employer-appointed; Sin Kim Sean, worker-appointed; Kong Phalla, chair) · Doc ID 139996

## 1. Facts
Dik Nitha Nit Ther Co Ltd is a garment factory cutting, sewing, printing and pressing socks, sleeves, hoods and scarves, employing 1,898 workers. [Para 70][Para 71] Its in-house union, the Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers' Democratic Union (C.CAWDU), brought 16 demands covering 1,200 members. [Para 72] Conciliation settled 6 demands and the panel settled one more (issue 3); the parties withdrew the remainder, leaving issues 1–2 and 4. [Para 29][Para 33][Para 34][Para 35] About 25% of the workforce holds fixed-duration contracts of one, two or three months, and 75% hold undetermined-duration contracts. [Para 74][Para 77]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay monthly wages to all fixed-duration workers on the 8th of each month, as it does for undetermined-duration workers? [Para 73][Para 118]
2. Whether the employer must pay the meal allowance for overtime twice a month to fixed-duration workers, as it does for undetermined-duration workers? [Para 73][Para 126]
3. Whether the employer must let all workers in the sock/footwear building leave and enter through the main factory gate, as they did before? [Para 93][Para 138]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Workers all labour for one company, so pay should be equal; Article 116 of the Labour Law and the 27 February 2010 agreement require wages between the 8th and 10th of the following month, and workers must pay rent and transport on the 8th. [Para 80][Para 81][Para 82][Para 83] The meal allowance is needed to buy food when working overtime. [Para 84][Para 86] The 1.5-metre side gate is too narrow, workers jostle and have scraped against motorbike exhaust pipes, and the split hampers union activity. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 104][Para 107]

**Employer (respondent):** Paying all 1,898 workers on one day is impractical because wages are calculated both monthly and by output, and payroll packing cannot be ready in time. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 89] The side gate was created in early 2010 to reduce congestion at the main gate and to control order, card checks and early departures; scrapes were never reported and the 28 October 2011 quarrel was a personal matter. [Para 99][Para 104][Para 110][Para 112]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay wages to all fixed-duration workers between the 8th and 10th of the following month and to pay the overtime meal allowance twice a month; the gate claim was dismissed. [Para 124][Para 137][Para 162]

On issues 1–2, the panel found both were rights disputes because they concerned the application of an agreement provided for by law. [Para 113][Para 116] Clause 2 of the agreement of 27 February 2010, registered as No. 103/10 K.B.V. on 3 March 2010, bound the employer to pay monthly wages once a month between the 8th and 10th of the following month, without distinguishing contract type. [Para 90][Para 92][Para 118][Para 123] Reading Article 311 of the Civil Code, the panel held the agreement embodied the parties' consent and overrode the employer's payroll-difficulty argument. [Para 120][Para 121][Para 122] It therefore ordered payment within that window for every fixed-duration worker. [Para 124]

On the meal allowance, Notification No. 041/11 K.B./S.Ch.N. of 7 March 2011 required 2,000 riel per day or one free meal for voluntary overtime at the employer's request. [Para 128] Following awards 47/07 Chung Fai, 79/09 and 85/09, the panel held the free meal must be given on each day of overtime, and the allowance exists so workers can buy food during overtime; paying it twice a month still serves that purpose. [Para 129][Para 132][Para 134][Para 136] It ordered the allowance twice a month. [Para 137]

On issue 4, the panel held the employer's right to direct and manage the undertaking under Article 2 of the Labour Law includes designating exit and entry gates. [Para 138][Para 141][Para 143] The main gate is 4 metres wide and the side gate 1.5 metres; the side gate serves 250–300 sock-building workers while about 1,200 use the main gate. [Para 146][Para 147][Para 148][Para 101] Sending all workers through the main gate would worsen congestion, and workers had not shown any exhaust-pipe scrape. [Para 104][Para 152][Para 153] The panel dismissed the claim. [Para 159][Para 162]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A collective agreement registered with the Ministry of Labour that fixes a payday binds the employer for all workers, whatever their contract duration, and administrative difficulty in payroll calculation is no defence to paying within the agreed window. [Para 118][Para 122][Para 123] An employer's statutory right to direct and manage the undertaking includes allocating which gate workers use, and a safety objection fails unless the risk is actually evidenced. [Para 141][Para 143][Para 153]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the split gate was introduced to control card checks and early departures, and that the 28 October 2011 quarrel was a personal dispute rather than a labour accident. [Para 110][Para 112][Para 155]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award date is 10 May 2012 in the header but the reasons say the award was due on 11 April 2012 and postponed by agreement to 11 May 2012. [Para 1][Para 36] Paragraph 34 describes the withdrawn issues 1–2 as the 5% contract indemnity and unused annual leave, while paragraphs 35 and 73 describe issues 1–2 as the payday and meal-allowance claims; the numbering is inconsistent. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 73] The company name is transliterated variously as Dik Nitha Nit Ther, Dik Niti Nit Ther and Duk Net Nhit Ther. [Para 44][Para 63] Paragraph 56 gives the same letter number as dated both 2019 and 2009. [Para 56] The section on the parties' evidence is heavily garbled by OCR. [Para 42][Para 63][Para 64]
