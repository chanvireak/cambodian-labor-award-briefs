# Award No. 164/11 — Good People (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 1 February 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (I. Sothy, employer-appointed; A. Vireak, worker-appointed; P. Bunchhea, chair) · non-binding award (unofficial English translation) · Doc ID 140104

## 1. Facts
Good People (Cambodia) Ltd runs an undergarment factory at Dangkor that began operating in June 2011 with 360 workers and no union. The claim was brought by the Cambodian Workers of Economic Union Federation (CWEUF) on behalf of six workers who started on verbal contracts on 17 and 18 May 2011, were trained for about three months, then signed two-month probationary contracts running to 16 and 17 October 2011; on 15 October 2011 the company gave them termination notices and ended their contracts. The workers had struck from 6 to 11 October 2011 for a USD 3 attendance-bonus rise and a USD 4 wage rise, and they say they were dismissed for representing the others in the negotiations. Twelve demands were referred; eleven were settled at the hearings, leaving only the reinstatement claim. [Para 1][Para 5][Para 17][Para 27][Para 30][Para 36][Para 41][Para 47]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the four workers who neither attended nor authorised a representative may have their reinstatement claim considered at all? [Para 54][Para 55]
2. Whether the company must reinstate the six workers who were present, their probationary contracts having been terminated two days before the dates on which they were due to expire? [Para 46][Para 66]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The probationary contracts ran to 16 and 17 October 2011, yet the company terminated them on 15 October, before expiry; the six had worked three months without any misconduct and had been chosen to represent the workforce in the strike negotiations, so the early termination was discrimination for union-type activity and they should be reinstated. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49]
**Employer (respondent):** It trains workers on a verbal contract for three months before offering a probationary contract, to familiarise them with the work; the contracts had expired, so no prior notice and no reasons were required, and it declined to renew because of poor performance and talking during working hours. It maintained its refusal to reinstate. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 49][Para 57]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the demand that the company reinstate the six workers, Ms C S, Ms P M, Ms V L, Ms S D, Ms S S and Ms I S. [Para 66][Para 67]

On the threshold question the panel applied Clause 19 of Prakas No. 099, under which a party may appear in person or through a person expressly authorised in writing, following Award No. 140/11 (Global Apparel). Four of the ten workers named in the non-conciliation report — Mr S R, Ms K C, Mr A N and Ms V N — did not appear and had authorised nobody, so only the six who attended were considered. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54][Para 55]

On the merits the panel first asked whether an employer may train workers for three months before signing a probationary contract. Article 68 sets the maximum probationary periods — three months for regular employees, two for specialised workers and one for non-specialised workers — and states the purpose of probation, but says nothing about when the contract must be signed or when probationary work must begin. The panel found no provision of the Labour Law or any other law forbidding a training period before the probationary contract, and noted that the workers knew of and accepted both the training and the two-month contracts. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 64][Para 65]

On termination the panel held that Article 82 releases the parties from the obligation to give prior notice in the cases it lists, and that a two-month probationary contract which has run its term falls within it; it also held, following Award No. 27/03 (Standard), that no provision requires an employer to give reasons for ending a probationary contract. It therefore rejected the reinstatement demand, without deciding whether the two-day gap between the notice and the stated expiry dates, or the workers' role in the strike negotiations, altered the analysis. [Para 66][Para 67]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may lawfully put workers through a period of training on a verbal contract before signing a probationary contract, because Article 68 fixes only the maximum length and the purpose of probation and not the point at which it must begin; and when a probationary contract has run its term the employer may end it without prior notice under Article 82 and without stating any reason. [Para 62][Para 64][Para 66][Para 67]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that, as a matter of practice, work on a probationary basis normally happens at the beginning of the employment relationship, and that the workers had accepted the training arrangement and the contracts that followed it. [Para 63][Para 65]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
This is an unofficial English translation of a Khmer original. The text is systematically corrupted by a dropped "t" ("themployer", "athearing", "raing"), which occasionally makes sentences ambiguous. The award records ten workers as demanding reinstatement but considers only six, so the outcome covers fewer people than the demand. The termination is dated 15 October 2011 while the contracts are said to expire on 16 and 17 October 2011, a discrepancy the panel does not resolve. The file also lists, among the employer's exhibits, agreements in which two of the dismissed workers undertook not to complain to the Ministry, which the award does not discuss. The award date was extended by agreement from 29 December 2011 to 1 February 2012. [Para 17][Para 22][Para 28][Para 41][Para 46]
