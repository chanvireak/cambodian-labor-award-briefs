# Award No. 78/12 — Dignity Knitter Limited (Arbitration Council, 18 May 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Nan, worker-appointed; Kong Phallack, chair) · Doc ID 139998

## 1. Facts
Dignity Knitter Limited runs a garment factory at Prek Ta Pring village, Sethbo commune, S'ang district, Kandal, employing about 1,898 workers. [Para 25] The claimant is the Local Union of C.CAWDU, which represents 900 workers and was registered on 30 July 2007. [Para 26][Para 27] The union filed 10 demands on 28 March 2012; conciliation resolved two, and eight went to the Council. [Para 11][Para 12] A further conciliation resolved issue 5, the parties combined issues 1 and 3, and the Council combined issues 6, 7 and 8. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 16] The award due date was extended by agreement from 4 May to 18 May 2012. [Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute
2. Whether the employer must let group leaders register workers' attendance at their workstations? [Para 2][Para 61]
1. Whether the employer must pay wages for checking and fixing shirts returned after the quality-control section has checked them under the AQL standard (combined with issue 3)? [Para 2][Para 84]
4. Whether the employer must reinstate Sat Sreysor and Hoeun Chanthy and pay their wages from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 3][Para 97]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Groups of 100, 80 or 30 workers register attendance and completed pieces at the group leaders' desks near the exit gate after work, and workers who cannot push through the crowd wait 10 to 15 minutes. [Para 27][Para 30] They would accept registration during working hours, but want it moved to the workstations. [Para 29] Rechecking returned shirts happens about five times a month and takes a morning to two days; because identity codes are removed before the AQL check, the employer cannot tell who erred, so the work should be paid. [Para 43][Para 45] The two dismissed workers were punished disproportionately, and the dismissals were retaliation for the 15 March 2012 strike and for complaining on behalf of the section. [Para 50][Para 53]
**Employer (respondent):** Registration takes under 10 minutes, crowding is caused by workers not queueing, and sending group leaders to workstations wastes their time and the workers' time. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 34] It says rechecking is a joint responsibility, buyers require identity codes to be removed, and paying for rechecks would encourage careless work and add electricity, water, transport and late-delivery costs. [Para 46][Para 48][Para 49] It says the dismissals are an individual dispute, justified by misconduct and insubordination after warnings, and that the Department of Labour authorised Hoeun Chanthy's dismissal. [Para 51][Para 55][Para 56]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** the demand on attendance registration was rejected but the employer was ordered to finish registration within 10 minutes after work; the employer was ordered to pay fixing workers for rechecking shirts returned after the AQL check; and the Council declined to consider the reinstatement demand. [Para 111]

On issue 2, the Council treated attendance management as a rights dispute because it has a basis in the Labour Law. [Para 62][Para 63] Reading the second paragraph of Article 2, and awards 17/07 Charm Textile and 116/07 Grace Sun, it held registering attendance falls within the employer's right to direct and supervise, exercised lawfully and reasonably. [Para 64][Para 65] Working hours run 7:00–11:00 and 12:30–4:30, and registration starts at 4:30 and takes 10 to 15 minutes. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 77] Applying Articles 137 and 139 and award 27/12 Ying Dong, the Council held that time is unpaid overtime and that the direction is neither reasonable nor lawful; the workers' counter-demand was also unreasonable. [Para 70][Para 71][Para 78][Para 80] The employer was ordered to add staff or use attendance lists and to finish within 10 minutes. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 83]

On issues 1 and 3, the Council held the fixing workers discharge their obligation at stage 1, and their responsibility is relieved at stage 2 when group leaders pass the shirts. [Para 92][Para 93] Rechecking after the AQL stage 3 is a new requirement, and under Article 664 of the Civil Code, which forms an employment contract by services in exchange for pay, the employer must pay for it. [Para 95][Para 96]

On issue 4, the Council applied Article 302 and award 10/03 Jacqsintex: conditions A and B were met, but C was not, because the two were dismissed on 16 March 2012, one day after the strike, and the strike did not aim at their reinstatement. [Para 99][Para 100][Para 105] Sat Sreysor was not shown to be a union member, and Hoeun Chanthy's delegate mandate had expired on 16 January 2012. [Para 105][Para 107][Para 108] No disruption to the enterprise or social peace was shown as at the hearing, so the dispute was not collective and the Council had no jurisdiction. [Para 109][Para 110]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer's managerial right to direct attendance must be exercised lawfully and reasonably, and requiring workers to queue 10 to 15 minutes after working hours to register is unpaid overtime contrary to Articles 137 and 139. [Para 64][Para 78][Para 80] A fixing worker's responsibility ends when group leaders pass the shirts at stage 2, so rechecking after the AQL stage 3 is new work that must be paid under Article 664 of the Civil Code. [Para 93][Para 95][Para 96] A dismissal of two workers is not a collective dispute where the strike did not aim at their reinstatement and no disruption to the enterprise is shown. [Para 105][Para 109]

**Obiter dicta:** The Council observed that the employer should post more staff or provide attendance lists to control queues, and that paying for rechecks would not necessarily encourage carelessness. [Para 79][Para 49] It also declined to consider issues 6 to 8, the employer's supervisory directions, because that right is already set by the Labour Law and no specific facts were presented. [Para 60]
