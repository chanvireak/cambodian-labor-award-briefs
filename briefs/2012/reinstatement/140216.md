# Award No. 226/12 — Kem Tekang Sport Tech Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 13 December 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chea, president) · Doc ID 140216

## 1. Facts
Kem Tekang Sport Tech Co., Ltd. — also printed as Kem Tekang I-Mech Sport Tech — is a garment and sleeve manufacturer in Kandal province employing about 610 workers [Para 32]. The Khmer Union Federation's factory union registered on 11 October 2012 with about 430 members, of whom the employer was checking off dues for 150 in October 2012 [Para 33][Para 34]. Conciliation at the Kandal provincial labour department failed on 6 of 13 demands, and the file reached the panel on 8 November 2012 [Para 13][Para 14][Para 15]. At the hearing of 23 November 2012 five further issues (1, 2, 4, 5 and 6) settled, leaving only Issue 3 to be decided, in a non-binding award [Para 16][Para 17][Para 18]. The settled demands included reinstatement of one worker as team leader, a monthly 10 USD travel allowance, a nursery or milk allowance, and 50 per cent wages during maternity leave [Para 2][Para 3][Para 6][Para 7].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must buy and issue one pair of shoes to each worker each year to wear inside the workplace, where the employer requires clean shoes at entry and workers may otherwise work barefoot on the production line? [Para 35][Para 49][Para 53]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** One pair of work shoes per worker per year is needed because working without shoes exposes workers to electric shock and other hazards; a worker was in fact shocked in March 2012, and only about 20 per cent of the workforce owns clean personal shoes, so the rest work in bare feet on the line [Para 42][Para 39][Para 48].

**Employer (respondent):** The company cannot afford the demand [Para 51]. It does not require workers to remove soiled shoes and work barefoot — the company provides a shoe rack and a space to change, workers may clean and re-wear their own shoes, and the notice instructing shoes to be placed in a box will be reworded to say shoes must be cleaned before entry; the March 2012 shock was during start-up and caused no serious injury [Para 46][Para 52][Para 43][Para 44].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand that the employer buy and give each worker one pair of shoes a year is dismissed, but the employer is ordered to provide sufficient shoe-cleaning materials (cloth, water, soap) and enough time to clean soiled shoes, and to take no unsuitable measure that would affect workers' rights arising from that cleaning [Para 67][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70].

The panel framed the question as whether the employer must supply one pair of shoes per worker per year [Para 53]. Relying on the employer's right to organise and manage the enterprise under Article 2 of the Labour Law as previously interpreted, the panel held that the right must be exercised lawfully and reasonably [Para 55][Para 57]. On the facts it found the workers' safety concern genuine in principle — barefoot work exposes workers to electric shock and other dangers — but noted that no specific worker was shown to have been injured by electric shock to date, and there was no clear evidence of how many workers actually worked barefoot [Para 58][Para 60][Para 62]. The employer's own practice of providing a changing area, allowing workers to clean and re-wear their shoes, and re-wording the box notice was accepted as reasonable, so the management decision stood [Para 63][Para 64]. Because the safety risk was nonetheless real, the panel attached two conditions: sufficient cleaning materials and adequate time, with no retaliatory or otherwise unsuitable measure against workers who take that time [Para 65][Para 66].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that even though no worker had been shocked recently, barefoot work could expose workers to electric shock and other hazards, and that the employer must guarantee nobody works barefoot — an obligation stated more broadly than the order itself [Para 60][Para 61].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer's right under Article 2 of the Labour Law to organise and manage the enterprise permits it to require clean shoes at the workplace entrance and to decline to supply footwear, provided the arrangement is lawful and reasonable; where workers must clean their own shoes to comply, the employer must supply the cleaning materials and the working time that make the requirement practicable, and must not penalise workers for using them [Para 57][Para 65][Para 66].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award date is printed as 13 December 2012 in the header, yet the parties' agreed extension runs to 14 December 2012 — a one-day internal contradiction [Para 1][Para 19]. The company name appears in at least four OCR variants (Kem Tekang Sport Tech, Kem Tekang I-Mech Sport Tech, Kem Tekang Spot Tek, Kem Ta Kang Spot Tek), and its commercial registration is dated 12 September 2011 while the statutes are dated 16 August 2011 [Para 21][Para 23][Para 32]. Two named workers and their roles appear in the demand list and are withheld here [Para 2][Para 5]. Latin OCR debris interrupts the case citations at [Para 56], and the panel's own finding that it "could not find" barefoot work is inconsistent with its acceptance of the safety risk [Para 61][Para 60].
