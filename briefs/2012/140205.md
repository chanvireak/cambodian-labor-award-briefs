# Award No. 212/12 — Sin Fang (Cambodia) Garment Manufacturing (Arbitration Council, 21 November 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ang Eng Tong chair; Ing Sothy appointed by the employer; An Vireak appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 140205

## 1. Facts
The respondent is a garment factory employing about 400 workers, where the Khmer Workers' Movement Union had been set up twice and was registered on its second attempt. [Para 27][Para 28] Its president and vice-president — two women in the sewing section, engaged on 10 May 2011 and 5 May 2010 respectively — were each dismissed twice: on 7 July and 3 July 2012, taken back on 20 August, dismissed again on 21 August, and taken back on 1 November 2012. [Para 50][Para 53][Para 56][Para 60][Para 61] The union complained of anti-union discrimination and claimed reinstatement plus back pay; conciliation failed on both points, and the employer did not appear at the hearing, which proceeded in its absence under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 11][Para 14][Para 15][Para 16]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer discriminated against the Khmer Workers' Movement Union, as shown by the dismissal of its leaders and activists and by the reassignment of the two leaders to a separate room? [Para 29][Para 73]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay back wages from the dates of dismissal, and wages for the single day of 20 August 2012 on which both women worked after being taken back? [Para 46][Para 48][Para 109]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** discrimination took four forms — the dismissal of the president and vice-president, their reassignment to a separate room and from sewing garments to sewing samples after 1 November, the dismissal of activists and members after the union notified its founding election, and the earlier dismissal of the whole leadership when the union was first formed; a rights officer was also dismissed on 30 September without cause, and the employer said openly that the two could not work because it feared a strike. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 32][Para 33][Para 34][Para 35][Para 66]
**Employer (respondent):** absent from both conciliation and hearing, and filing no evidence; the panel therefore accepted the workers' account as true but only so far as their own evidence carried it. [Para 4][Para 14][Para 17][Para 19]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the discrimination claim and both back-pay claims were dismissed, but the employer was ordered to pay one day's wages, for 20 August 2012, to each of the two union leaders. [Para 131][Para 132][Para 133]

On discrimination, the panel applied Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law, which forbid an employer to use union membership or activity as the ground for discipline, dismissal, assignment of work, grading or pay. [Para 73][Para 74] Following earlier awards, it held that the party making the accusation bears the burden of proving it, and that the panel weighs the explanations given at hearing against the documentary evidence. [Para 75][Para 77] The union had been ordered to file the founding documents and a list of dismissed activists and members; it filed nothing by the 6 November deadline, so the panel found no specific evidence of discrimination. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 82][Para 83] It also found that the union's rights officer had not been dismissed and had good relations with the employer, and that the dismissal of the two leaders, followed by their reinstatement, did not by itself establish the charge. [Para 84][Para 85][Para 86]

On back pay, the panel took each leader separately. The vice-president had served more than two years, so her successive fixed-duration contracts became an indefinite contract under Article 67, terminable under Article 74 only with notice and a valid reason; but no evidence established the circumstances of her 3 July dismissal, so the claim failed on proof rather than on law. [Para 87][Para 93][Para 95][Para 96][Para 102][Para 104][Para 107] The president's last fixed-duration contract ran to 10 July 2012 and she had already been paid her severance and leave entitlement on 7 July, so there was no basis to award back pay. [Para 121][Para 122][Para 123][Para 125] Both women had, however, actually worked on 20 August 2012 before being dismissed again the next day, and Article 102 obliges the employer to pay wages for work done, so one day's wage was ordered for each. [Para 113][Para 114][Para 116][Para 127][Para 128][Para 130]

Reinstatement, the other half of Issue 2, was not decided: both women had been taken back on 1 November 2012, so the demand had been met. [Para 46][Para 47]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union alleging anti-union discrimination must prove it: the panel will not infer discrimination from the timing of dismissals or from the reinstatement that follows, and where the claimant files neither the union's founding documents nor a list of the workers said to have been victimised, the claim fails. Separately, wages accrue for each day on which work was actually performed, even where the worker was dismissed the following day and the surrounding termination is unproven. [Para 75][Para 85][Para 114]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's treatment of Article 67 — that successive fixed-duration contracts running beyond two years convert into an indefinite contract — was set out at length but did not decide the case, the claim failing for want of evidence about the dismissal. [Para 87][Para 93][Para 104]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The union's registration is dated three ways: 3 August 2012 where the certificate is listed, 23 August 2012 in the findings, and 3 August again in the chronology, while the founding election is given as 7 May in one passage and 7 June 2012 in another. [Para 22][Para 28][Para 33][Para 42][Para 43][Para 63] The paragraph recording the evidence deadline and the number of documentary exhibits is corrupted, leaving "4" attached to the wrong noun twice. [Para 44][Para 70] Name defects: the union's two leaders, its rights officer and activist are named in full throughout the source — this brief identifies them by office only, and the pay records are referred to by employee number. [Para 31][Para 34][Para 50][Para 56] The employer's representatives are named but never appeared, so no attendance record exists to check them against. [Para 1][Para 14] The signature block is partly illegible for all three arbitrators. [Para 135]
