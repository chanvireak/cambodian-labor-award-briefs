# Award No. 188/11 — Sun Vael Su Company (Arbitration Council, 2 February 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · three-member panel (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Virak, worker-appointed; Ang Eng Thong, president) · Doc ID 140123 · individual workers, union officials and company staff identified by role only

## 1. Facts
Sun Vael Su is a garment factory at Trapeang Thleung, Dangkao, Phnom Penh, operating since October 2007 with about 1,300 workers [Para 31][Para 32]. Three factory unions, all affiliated to the same federation, brought seven demands; six were settled in conciliation on 22 December 2011 and the panel settled the survivors down to one: whether members may wear their union badge inside the factory [Para 9][Para 10][Para 33][Para 34]. The three unions together claim about 721 members; two hold registration certificates of 20 April 2011 and 8 July 2011, and the third never sent its certificate to the Council [Para 35][Para 36][Para 37][Para 38]. The company issues and requires its own identity card, and permits no other badge to be worn with it during working hours [Para 39][Para 57].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must allow union members to wear their union badge into the factory alongside the company identity card? [Para 2][Para 39]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The badge identifies which union a worker belongs to and lets the union intervene quickly when a member is injured at work or has trouble travelling between home and the factory; members are happy to wear it and it is worn behind the company card, so it harms no one [Para 40][Para 41][Para 42]. Members wore union badges routinely from the first union's creation without difficulty until a dispute with administration in June or July 2011, after which the company forbade it [Para 44][Para 45]. They do not dispute that administration has since called in and warned members who wore the badge [Para 47][Para 48].
**Employer (respondent):** The factory has several unions, so permitting badges would create disorder, and no law, regulation or internal rule provides for a union badge [Para 43]. It denies having ever allowed union badges inside and denies that unions have any entry into the company [Para 46].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the claim and confirmed that the company may require workers to wear only its own identity card, with no other badge worn alongside it during working hours [Para 63][Para 64]

The panel first classified the demand, holding that it is a rights dispute because it touches the employer's power of management written in Article 2 of the Labour Law [Para 49][Para 52]. Reading Article 2(2) as it had in Awards 17/07 and 116/07, it held that an employer may organise and direct the enterprise so long as it acts reasonably [Para 53][Para 56]. On the facts it found that the company admits only its own card at the gate and inside the premises, and that it refuses union badges because workers may belong to more than one union, so that allowing one badge would invite every other union to demand the same [Para 57]. The panel accepted that reasoning: a union membership badge is not a company pass and has no connection with the relationship between the employer and the worker, so prohibiting it is a reasonable exercise of management authority [Para 59][Para 60]. It added that the workers had pointed to no provision of law and no agreement conferring the right they claimed [Para 61]. The demand accordingly had no proper legal basis and was dismissed; the order was declared binding at once under the memorandum of understanding of 28 September 2010 [Para 62][Para 65].


## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim that workers be allowed to wear a union badge inside the plant succeeds only if a law, a collective agreement or an internal rule confers it; in the absence of such a provision the refusal is a reasonable exercise of the employer's management authority under Article 2, because a union badge is not a company pass and is unconnected with the employment relationship, so the panel will dismiss the demand [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remark that workers may join more than one union, and its observation that other unions would demand equal treatment if one badge were allowed, explain the finding of reasonableness but were not the ground of decision [Para 57].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel states that three unions brought the complaint, but the face of the award lists four worker-side entities, and one of the three never supplied its registration certificate, so its standing is not established on the record [Para 1][Para 33][Para 38]. The non-conciliation report is numbered 1458 and dated 28 December 2011 in one place and 27 December 2011 in another [Para 6][Para 10]. The document recording the parties' agreement to a binding award is dated "13 January" with the month printed in corrupted characters [Para 30]. The employer's representative is spelled "Jonhson Chong" on the face and "Johnson Chong" in the authority letter [Para 1][Para 23]. The worker-side party is twice described as "the employee party" where the employer party is meant [Para 3][Para 46]. Article 2 of the Labour Law is broken across three fragments, one of which is a single character, so the quoted text cannot be read in full [Para 53][Para 54][Para 55]. The heading of the issue section omits its number [Para 39].
