# Award No. 140/12 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូឌា អ៊ែរ ផត មែនីជ្ជមែន សឺវីស (Cambodia Airports Management Services, CAMS) (Arbitration Council, 7 September 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ly Tai Seng (employer-selected), Liv Sovanna (worker-selected), Kong Phalla (president) · Doc ID 140155

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូឌា អ៊ែរ ផត មែនីជ្ជមែន សឺវីស (CAMS, Cambodia Airports Management Services) runs Phnom Penh International Airport on behalf of the state as a public-service company, with 818 employees, 82 percent of them on shift work, and a collective bargaining agreement registered on 12 July 2011 for a three-year term. [Para 78][Para 82] The claimant is the Independent Union of Phnom Penh International Airport Workers (សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត ឯករាជ្យ ព្រលានយន្តហោះ អន្តរជាតិ). [Para 80] From 1 January 2012 it applied a new wage-and-grading system, notified to each employee by letter of 27 February 2012 giving the new job title, wage and bonus, and stating that the contract terms and job-description duties were unchanged. [Para 85][Para 87][Para 88] The union's four demands went through conciliation on 11 July 2012 with no agreement on any of them, and both parties attended the hearing of 7 August 2012. [Para 19][Para 24] The workers merged demands 1 and 2, so the panel decided three issues, on the claims of 217 employees who thumbprinted the first, 43 Cargo employees on the second and 51 on the third. [Para 27][Para 114][Para 125][Para 151]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issues 1 & 2) Whether the employer was entitled to replace its employees' existing job titles with the new titles and categories of the 2012 system, or must it restore the former titles? [Para 83][Para 152]
2. (Issue 3) Whether employees holding the same job title must receive the same wage and benefits, where only 15 of 43 Cargo staff received an increase under the multi-skill scheme? [Para 116][Para 182][Para 184]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the employer discriminated in the check-in section by sending only 15 of 52 staff to Air France training and then choosing from among them? [Para 141][Para 194]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant union, សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត ឯករាជ្យ ព្រលានយន្តហោះ អន្តរជាតិ):** the re-titling altered their employment contracts without consent, added duties — one driver said he was given more loading work after becoming a Handling Agent A2 — and was unfair because only some employees received a wage increase, 9 of 16 loaders, 9 of 16 team leaders and 1 of 26 drivers; identical titles therefore should carry identical pay, and the check-in training opportunity was distributed without equality. [Para 98][Para 99][Para 100][Para 116]
**Employer (respondent):** renaming titles is part of its right to organise; the change touched no contractual duty, role, wage or benefit, and any employee unwilling to do work outside their skill is not required to do it and is not sanctioned; titles are graded, so one title spans four levels with different job descriptions and different pay; and the number of trainees was set by Air France, with CAMS choosing from among those trained. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 108][Para 110][Para 139][Para 145]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three issues were dismissed, the fourth demand being refused consideration for lack of specificity, and the non-binding award becomes final eight days after notification absent written objection to the Minister of Labour. [Para 202][Para 203][Para 204][Para 205]
On Issues 1 and 2 the panel applied Article 2 of the Labour Law and Article 5 of the collective agreement, both of which reserve to the employer the management of its commercial operations and the choice of methods and means, provided the right is exercised lawfully and reasonably. [Para 160][Para 162] It found the title change confined to the Operations division and verified that duties, responsibilities, wages and benefits were preserved, that no added function had been imposed, and that the single driver's account of heavier loading was not shown to be a duty the employer required; the change was therefore lawful and reasonable, and no unfairness arose because employees who were not upgraded kept their contract terms while those upgraded moved to new grades and job descriptions. [Para 177][Para 178][Para 179][Para 180]
On Issue 3 the panel treated the demand as one under Article 106 and again required the three equalities of working conditions, professional competence and results. [Para 184][Para 185] Because each new title is internally divided into graded levels — Handling Agent into A1 to A4 — with distinct job descriptions, skill requirements and responsibility, the panel held that a shared title did not establish equal conditions, equal competence or equal results. [Para 186][Para 187][Para 188][Para 189][Para 191]
On Issue 4 the panel quoted Article 12, which bars an employer from using race, colour, sex, religion, political opinion, national or social origin or union membership as a ground for decisions on the allocation of work or vocational training. [Para 194] It found the trainee number and the final selection set by Air France, but the choice of which 15 of 52 employees to send made by CAMS, and it observed that CAMS ought to state its selection criteria clearly and transparently. [Para 195][Para 197] Because the workers never identified which kind of discrimination was alleged, nor the form it took, the panel found the claim had no specificity to adjudicate and declined to consider it. [Para 199][Para 200][Para 201]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer renames job titles and re-grades pay under its lawful managerial right while preserving duties, responsibilities, wages and benefits, the panel will not order the former titles restored; and identity of job title alone does not satisfy Article 106, because a single title may be internally graded into levels carrying different job descriptions, skills and pay. [Para 162][Para 180][Para 187][Para 192]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel added that CAMS should make the criteria for choosing employees for Air France training clear and transparent, so that all 52 check-in staff could see an equal opportunity — a recommendation not necessary to its refusal to consider the claim. [Para 197][Para 198]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR collapses the old-to-new title conversion table into an unreadable run of paired names, and the Article 106 discussion at para 185 is partly replaced by Latin character noise, leaving that passage to be reconstructed from the parallel reasoning on Issue 3's conditions. [Para 92][Para 185] The employer's representative list numbers two entries "1" and attributes two different posts to one representative, so the two roles cannot be matched to persons. [Para 1] The company is printed under three spellings and abbreviated throughout as CAMS, and the CBA registration number appears as 299 in the findings while the 2010 leadership-recognition letter carries a different number. [Para 78][Para 82] Claimant counts are stated inconsistently — 217 signatures, then 43 Cargo staff, then 51 — and the panel reconciled them itself. [Para 114][Para 125][Para 151] Individual employees are not reproduced here; the drivers and loaders whose evidence is described are identified only by role. [Para 99][Para 120]
