# Award No. 136/12 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ចំរុះ សិប្បករ អង្គរ (Artisans Angkor) (Arbitration Council, 30 August 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-selected), Liv Sovanna (worker-selected), Kong Phalla (president) · Doc ID 140154

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ចំរុះ សិប្បករ អង្គរ (Artisans Angkor), a mixed-economy company at Siem Reap with state, private and artisan shareholders, runs wood-carving, rattan and silk workshops and employed about 1,303 artisans. [Para 154][Para 155] Its artisans' union, registered in 2007 with third-term leaders recognised on 23 August 2011, filed a collective dispute over four demands on 26 June 2012; the Siem Reap labour department conciliated all four on 3 July 2012 and recorded no agreement on any of them. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 15][Para 157] Both parties attended the hearing of 30 July 2012, and by agreement the award date was moved from 30 July to 30 August 2012. [Para 18][Para 21] Only the 34 piece-rate wood-carving artisans pursued the claims; the panel found no dispute had yet arisen in the rattan and silk sections and that no representative of those sections attended, so those sections fell outside the award. [Para 159][Para 161][Para 251]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether piece-rate carvers must be paid the same as the fixed-salary "sample" artisans under Article 106 of the Labour Law, by raising the standard hours of new figures or raising their own piece rate? [Para 204]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer coerced carvers who refused the new figures by issuing first written warnings and by withholding the standard hours of those figures? [Para 232]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer owes wages for periods in which carvers had no work to do, where they had declined to produce the figures in the monthly production plan? [Para 252]
4. (Issue 4) Whether, if standard hours cannot be agreed, the employer must instead pay piece-rate carvers a base wage of USD 150 to 245 a month as it does the sample artisans? [Para 260]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Article 106 entitles them to equal pay where skill, competence, hours and piece rate are the same, and they carve the same figures as the sample group; the sample artisans are paid USD 150 to 245 a month while their own rate of USD 0.38 to 0.65 per standard hour yields less, so the new standard hours are set too low and the employer is coercing them through warnings and withheld hours. [Para 204][Para 208][Para 244]
**Employer (respondent):** the standard hours were computed through a mechanism agreed and practised for years and cannot simply be raised; the sample artisans work under a different regime, with no piece rate, a monthly salary, direct supervision and a separate workshop, so none of the Article 106 conditions is met; it never cut hours, it merely declined to pay hours for figures not produced; and a base-salary scheme was tried once and could not deliver sufficient output. [Para 206][Para 207][Para 245][Para 261]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all four demands were dismissed, and the award, being non-binding, becomes final eight days after notification if no party objects in writing to the Minister of Labour through the Council's Secretariat. [Para 381][Para 384][Para 385]
On Issue 1 the panel read Article 106 as requiring three equalities — equal working conditions, equal professional competence and equal results — and broke "equal conditions" into four elements: the same work, the same production process, the same working environment and the same tools. [Para 263][Para 264][Para 265] It found the first two elements unmet: sample artisans produce to customer order in a dedicated sample workshop under human-resources supervision, while piece-rate artisans work to the monthly plan, so the roles merely resemble each other. [Para 266][Para 267] The competence limb failed for want of evidence, because the claimants refused to carve the disputed figures and so produced no comparable output; the results limb failed on the same ground. [Para 282][Para 284][Para 290]
On Issue 2 the panel held that Article 2 of the Labour Law and Article 22 of the internal regulations give the employer the right to organise and manage the production line where that is lawful and reasonable, and that a first written warning is the lighter sanction prescribed for a minor fault. [Para 302][Para 313][Para 322] Since the sanction imposed was milder than the fault warranted, it was an exercise of managerial right, not coercion. [Para 321][Para 323]
On Issue 3 the panel applied Article 108, which guarantees the minimum wage to piece workers whose output falls short despite their having worked, and held it inapplicable where the shortfall came from refusal to work at all. [Para 334][Para 335][Para 336] On the evidence it found the employer withheld nothing. [Para 329]
On Issue 4 the panel relied on Articles 65 and 69 of the Labour Law and Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code: a contract arises from offer and acceptance, no party may impose its type on another, and the employer chooses the wage system. [Para 358][Para 366][Para 377]
On the evidence the panel also fixed the standard hours for the large elephant figure at 11 hours 15 minutes, preferring the employer's figure to the workers' 11 hours 30 minutes. [Para 194][Para 195][Para 196]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim by piece-rate artisans for parity with fixed-salary sample artisans under Article 106 fails where the comparison group works in a separate sample workshop under direct supervision on a different process, and where the claimants' refusal to perform the disputed work leaves the panel without the output data needed to compare competence and results; a first written warning for refusing the production plan is a lawful exercise of managerial authority, not coercion, and the panel will not convert a freely accepted piece-rate contract into a monthly salary at the claimant's demand. [Para 265][Para 284][Para 322][Para 366]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that the claim covered 34 artisans rather than the 35 the employer said it employed, because only 34 warning letters had been issued and received; and it noted the employer's account that its older demonstration method had been practised with the artisans for a long time before the 2012 special workshop replaced it. [Para 250][Para 251][Para 180]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR is heavily damaged in the evidentiary lists and in the wage-comparison table, where figures, percentages and amounts run together across lines and cannot be reconciled; one passage of the claimant's argument survives as a run of Latin characters rather than Khmer. [Para 220][Para 244][Para 288] Page numbers and stray words are embedded mid-sentence throughout, and several listed documents appear twice under different numbers. [Para 150][Para 151][Para 147][Para 148] The artisan count is printed as 34 in the findings and 35 in the employer's statement, a discrepancy the panel itself addressed. [Para 161][Para 250] The company name is printed with a broken space in the union's registered name and with at least two variant spellings of the workshop name. [Para 156][Para 202] Individual workers are not reproduced here; one worker representative's name is printed in three differing forms across the header, the union-leadership paragraph and the evidence list, so the office held by that person cannot be reliably assigned. [Para 1][Para 157][Para 198]
