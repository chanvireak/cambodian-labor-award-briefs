# Award No. 10/12 — Jung Fai Knitwear FTY Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 24 February 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Kong Phalla (president) · Doc ID 139939

## 1. Facts
Jung Fai Knitwear FTY Co., Ltd. is a garment factory at Chak Angre Krom, Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employing about 500 workers, of whom the Khmer Workers' Rights Union at the factory claimed some 168 members [Para 61][Para 149]. On 27 November 2011 the union federation filed a 27-point claim to improve working conditions; conciliation on 5 January 2012 failed and the case reached the panel on 17 January 2012 [Para 34][Para 35][Para 36]. At the hearing the parties settled 16 points and the workers withdrew 4, leaving 7 for decision — canteen space, attendance-bonus deductions, the bonus rate, transport allowance, sick-leave bonus, antenatal health checks and special leave [Para 40][Para 41].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 8) Whether the employer is obliged to restore the staff canteen to its former size, so that workers need not eat near chemical storage? [Para 132]
2. (Issue 9) Whether the employer may forfeit the whole monthly attendance bonus when a worker takes authorised leave, or only a sum proportional to the days absent? [Para 153]
3. (Issue 11) Whether the employer must raise the attendance bonus from USD 7 to USD 10 a month? [Para 180]
4. (Issue 12) Whether the employer must pay a USD 3 monthly transport allowance? [Para 190]
5. (Issue 20) Whether a worker absent on sick leave with a proper medical certificate keeps the attendance bonus, in whole or in proportion to the days absent? [Para 195]
6. (Issue 23) Whether a pregnant worker from her third month is entitled to one paid day a month for antenatal checks? [Para 211]
7. (Issue 26) Whether the employer may deduct wages for days of special leave taken out of annual leave and pay them back only in December? [Para 216]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the canteen was cut back in December 2011 for business use, so some 200 users crowd a shed built for about 100; attendance bonus should be lost at USD 3 for one day and USD 6 for two rather than in full; the rate should match other factories at USD 10; bus fares have risen; antenatal checks need more than the one hour allowed; sick leave with a certificate should not cost the bonus; and deferring special-leave wages to December deprives them of monthly income [Para 81][Para 85][Para 91][Para 94][Para 112][Para 119][Para 127].
**Employer (respondent):** there is no vacant space in the factory to enlarge the canteen; Notice No. 041/11 gives USD 7 only to workers attending the full number of working days, so any absence forfeits it; the company cannot fund transport because it already buses workers home when overtime runs to 9 pm; it already allows one hour a month for antenatal checks with a hospital next to the factory; and the December pay-back of special leave follows the workers' own request [Para 83][Para 87][Para 92][Para 95][Para 122][Para 129].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issues 9, 20 and 26 ordered as declarations of entitlement (no sum in riel or USD awarded); Issues 8, 11, 12 and 23 dismissed [Para 230][Para 232][Para 235][Para 237].

On the canteen the panel found no statute, collective agreement or practice imposing a duty to build or enlarge a dining hall; a claim going beyond the law is an interest dispute, and the panel declines interest disputes where the claimant union is not the most representative in the factory (Labour Law Art. 277; Prakas No. 099 Art. 43) [Para 135][Para 141][Para 145]. The union held 168 of 500 workers and produced no certificate of most-representativeness, so the claim was refused [Para 149][Para 150][Para 151].

On Issue 9 the panel read clause 1 of Notice No. 041/11 as promising USD 7 to workers attending the required working days, without defining them; following Award No. 112/11 (Yong Wati) it treated "regular working days" as the days the employer requires or the law obliges the worker to serve [Para 153][Para 155]. Authorised absence suspends the contract under Arts. 71(k)(6) and 72, so neither wages nor bonus are earned for those days — but the employer may not forfeit the month's bonus in full, only a sum in proportion to the days absent [Para 163][Para 166][Para 168]. The workers' proposed USD 3 / USD 6 scale was refused, since Art. 13 makes labour-law entitlements public order and the panel cannot award less than the notice provides [Para 173][Para 178][Para 179].

Issue 11 failed because the employer was already paying the USD 7 the notice requires, and no collective agreement or agreement promised USD 10; the extra USD 3 was an interest claim [Para 182][Para 185][Para 187][Para 189]. Issue 12 failed for the same reason: no legal rule and no agreement obliged the employer to pay USD 3 a month for transport [Para 190][Para 191][Para 193][Para 194]. Issue 23 failed because neither law nor agreement granted a full day a month for antenatal checks, making it another interest claim [Para 211][Para 212][Para 213][Para 215].

On Issue 20 the panel applied Arts. 71(k)(3) and 72: absence certified by an official doctor suspends the contract, so the bonus is not earned for those days, but the employer may deduct only in proportion to the sick days, as ordered in Award No. 62/07 (Hong Meur) [Para 201][Para 203][Para 206][Para 207][Para 208]. On Issue 26 it held that annual leave under Art. 166 is paid leave and that Arts. 171 and 117 require wages to be paid on time; withholding special-leave wages until December was improper, and the employer was ordered to pay them monthly [Para 216][Para 221][Para 224][Para 227][Para 229].

Arbitrator Ing Sothy appended a dissent on Issues 9 and 20, arguing that a bonus is a discretionary payment whose conditions the giver alone sets, so the panel should not have rewritten the employer's scheme [Para 243][Para 251].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A monthly attendance bonus payable under an employer's notice is wages within Art. 103; where a worker's absence is authorised by law or by the employer, the contract is suspended for those days, so the employer may withhold the bonus only in proportion to the days absent and may never forfeit the whole month's bonus [Para 162][Para 168][Para 207]. A claim for more generous terms than the labour law or the employer's own notice provides is an interest dispute, which the panel will decline to hear where the union bringing it cannot show it is the most representative in the enterprise [Para 141][Para 186].

**Obiter dicta:** the canteen claim also failed because the workers did not show that eating outside the canteen harmed their health or hygiene [Para 140]; the panel observed generally that a collective agreement binds all workers in the factory and that strike rights cannot be used to reopen one before it expires [Para 148]; and it noted that a hospital stands beside the factory and about ten workers a month give birth [Para 122].
