# Award No. 172/12 — Alim (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, Phnom Penh (Arbitration Council, 14 September 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phallaph presiding; Ouk Ry, employer nominee; An Non, worker nominee) · Doc ID 140174

## 1. Facts
Alim (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a garment factory at Trapeang Thleung, Chom Chao, Pur Senchey, Phnom Penh, began operating in July 2011 and employed about 500 workers, the complainant being the Khmer Workers' Union within the Khmer Workers' Federation. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 68] A strike over twelve demands was notified on 24 August 2012, the labour dispute department conciliated that day and failed on all twelve, and non-conciliation report No. 975 Kb/Ak/Vk was forwarded on 27 August 2012. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 22] At the hearing of 30 August 2012 nine demands were settled (Issues 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 9, 10, 11 and 12), Issue 7 was withdrawn, and the panel was to decide Issues 1 and 6 only; the workers then withdrew Issue 1 as well, over the employer's objection, so no demand for reinstatement was ever decided. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 75][Para 105] A second conciliation record of 29 August 2012 had provided that all workers return to work, and a Phnom Penh court injunction of 28 August 2012 ordered the strike ended while excepting workers whose contracts the company had terminated. [Para 76][Para 85]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the complainant union may withdraw its demand that Mr E, the union president, be taken back into normal work, where the employer objects to the withdrawal and says his probationary contract was lawfully terminated? [Para 75][Para 104][Para 106]
2. (Issue 6) Whether the employer must pay wages no later than the 10th day of the following month, rather than on the Saturday falling between the 6th and the 12th as it does, so that workers can meet rent due on the 10th? [Para 96][Para 113][Para 115]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the union president should be taken back into normal work, since the second conciliation record promised that all workers would return and he is a worker too — he did in fact return on 30 August 2012; and wages should be paid by the 10th because their landlords require the rent on that day. [Para 75][Para 78][Para 79][Para 97][Para 121]
**Employer (respondent):** his probationary contract of two months was lawfully terminated on 23 August 2012 after repeated warnings, with all sums paid and receipted, so the phrase "all workers" in the second conciliation record must be read with the court injunction, which excepts workers whose contracts were terminated; payment on a Saturday is chosen because goods ship only on Fridays, Saturdays carry no overtime and workers go home at 4 p.m., and the workers have no actual complaint about monthly payment. [Para 80][Para 81][Para 85][Para 95][Para 100][Para 103][Para 118][Para 122]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay wages no later than the 10th day of the following month, and Issue 1 was not considered at all because the workers had withdrawn it. [Para 131][Para 132][Para 133]
On Issue 1 the panel asked only whether a complainant may withdraw a demand against the respondent's opposition, and answered it by following Award 20/07-Star Night Thing: where the complainant withdraws, the subject matter of the case no longer exists, so there is no complaint and no award, and the panel has no power to decide a dispute that has been withdrawn. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 108][Para 109] It added that an employer has no right to prevent a withdrawal, since the workers were the ones who lodged the complaint with the Ministry and the subject matter falls away once they stop pursuing it. [Para 110][Para 111][Para 112] The employer's argument that the second conciliation record bound it to reinstate him, read together with the injunction's exception for terminated workers, was therefore never reached on the merits. [Para 85][Para 88][Para 90]
On Issue 6 the panel found that paying once a month after the work has been performed satisfies the employer's legal obligation, so the mere monthly cycle was not a breach. [Para 118][Para 119] It then weighed the employer's reasons for choosing the Saturday between the 6th and 12th — Friday-only shipping, no Saturday overtime, and the safety of workers leaving at 4 p.m. — against the workers' need to have wages in hand when rent falls due on the 10th, and held that the employer must not let the burden of its own shipping obligations fall on the workers' payday or their livelihood. [Para 122][Para 125][Para 126][Para 129] The employer's fear of liability for wages lost during working hours was found not to be made out, since such loss is not its responsibility. [Para 127][Para 128] Ten days after the work is performed was held a reasonable period for the employer to arrange payment, and the employer was ordered to pay by the 10th of the following month. [Para 130][Para 131][Para 133] The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Minister. [Para 134]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A complainant may withdraw a demand at any stage, even over the respondent's objection, and once withdrawn the subject matter is gone: the panel has no jurisdiction to decide it and will issue no order on it, the employer having no standing to insist on a ruling. [Para 108][Para 110][Para 111]
Paying wages once a month discharges the legal obligation to pay, but the day chosen must answer to the workers' need rather than only to the employer's shipping schedule; wages are to be paid within a reasonable period after the work is done, which the panel fixed at no later than the 10th of the following month. [Para 119][Para 126][Para 129][Para 130][Para 131]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that a court injunction ordering a strike ended does not by itself oblige the employer to reinstate a worker whose contract it had already terminated, the injunction excepting exactly that case — a point it did not need, having declined to decide the reinstatement demand. [Para 85][Para 90][Para 95]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The worker at the centre of Issue 1 is named in full throughout the award as a worker and union president; he is referred to here as Mr E, and the numerous repetitions of his name — including in the exhibit list for three separate written warnings — are not reproduced. [Para 2][Para 31][Para 32][Para 33][Para 75] The company name appears variously as "Alim (Cambodia)", "Alin (Cambodia)" and with an unexplained corporate suffix in the conciliation records, and one exhibit list numbers the same document twice. [Para 1][Para 53][Para 55] Paragraph 115 is corrupted by an untranslated insertion ("whichowever write 2007") inside the quotation of Article 2, and paragraphs 116 and 117 survive only as fragments, so the definition quoted there cannot be relied on. [Para 115][Para 116][Para 117] Paragraphs 108 to 112 and 122 to 124 repeat themselves, paragraph 130 breaks into a duplicated and partly garbled sentence, and the panel is twice called a "national" council. [Para 108][Para 112][Para 130] The settled demands are listed as nine and the enumeration does support that count, but Issue 1 is described both as withdrawn and as not considered, and the demand to reinstate is dated to a contract said to run from 31 July to 31 September 2012, a month that does not exist. [Para 26][Para 81][Para 111][Para 132]
