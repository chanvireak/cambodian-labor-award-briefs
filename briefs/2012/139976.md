# Award No. 45/12 — Pou Yuen (Cambodia) Enterprise Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 6 July 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, An Nan; chair Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 139976

## 1. Facts
Pou Yuen (Cambodia) Enterprise Ltd. runs a footwear factory with 2,950 workers. Three WUF-affiliated unions — the Workers Union, the Workers' Spirit Union and the Workers' Rights Union — filed a 20-issue collective demand on working conditions. Eight issues were settled in conciliation on 21 February 2012 and seven more at the hearing of 19 March 2012, leaving issues 2, 4, 7, 9 and 11 for the panel: accommodation allowance, attendance bonus, maternity pay, drinking water and wages during work stoppages. [Para 14][Para 16][Para 17][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay an additional monthly US$ 3 accommodation allowance on top of the existing US$ 4? [Para 28]
2. Whether the employer must pay an additional monthly US$ 6 attendance bonus on top of the existing US$ 9? [Para 30]
3. Whether the employer must provide a maternity payment equal to three months' wages and benefits calculated on average wages over the previous 12 months? [Para 31]
4. Whether the employer must provide two large bottles of drinking water each month, the workers' original demand having been for hygienic drinking glasses? [Para 35][Para 37]
5. Whether the employer must pay full wages when workers take fewer than 13 days off for lack of work, and half wages when they take 13 days or more? [Para 40]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the allowances are needed because consumer prices, rent, transport and utility bills have risen; the attendance bonus would reward service and regular attendance; maternity pay should track average wages over 12 months and be paid before leave, since travel from the provinces and weak health make collection difficult; there was no water or glasses outside the infirmary; and the employer must find work for them. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 33][Para 39][Para 42]
**Employer (respondent):** it cannot afford the allowance increases; its US$ 9 attendance bonus already exceeds the US$ 7 statutory minimum; maternity pay excludes overtime because workers are not technically employed during leave, and is paid monthly on pay day; it supplies sufficient water and workers must look after their own health; and the February 2012 notification on short-time work was signed by the unions and worker delegates. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 34][Para 39][Para 41]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 7: maternity payment of 50% of wages and benefits, computed by dividing wages earned in the previous 12 months by 12, halving the result and multiplying by 3, payable at least one day before leave begins; issue 9: demand for two bottles of water rejected, but the employer ordered to supply hygienic glasses or other drinking vessels; issue 11: full wages ordered while no work is available. Issues 2 and 4 rejected as interests disputes, in a non-binding Part II award. [Para 94][Para 95][Para 96][Para 97]

The panel first classified the issues, following Awards 05/11-M&V 1, 13/11-Gold Kamvimex, 14/11-GHG and 37/11-ASD: a rights dispute concerns entitlements in the law, an agreement or a collective agreement. Issues 7, 9 and 11 had a basis in the Labour Law and were rights disputes decided on law; issues 2 and 4 had no basis in the contracts or any agreement and were interests disputes decided on equity. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 61]

Because an award on an interests dispute replaces a collective agreement and binds all workers, non-members losing their right to strike, the panel held under Clause 43 of Prakas No. 099 that only a most-representative-status union or a group representing over half the workforce may bring one (Awards 81/04-Evergreen, 98/04-Great Union, 169/11-Fortune Teo, 02/11-Pou Yuen, 66/11-In Han Sung). [Para 46][Para 48][Para 49] The three unions were unregistered, but their 1,540 members were 52.20% of 2,950 workers, so together they had standing under Award 19/08&20/08-Quint Major Industrial 2. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54][Para 55]

On equity, the US$ 4 allowance already exceeded the statutory minimum and Award 27/12-Ying Dong held that rising rent and transport costs are not a matter of the employer's management, so the extra US$ 3 was refused. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 60] The US$ 9 attendance bonus exceeded the US$ 7 required by Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011, and the workers gave insufficient grounds, so the extra US$ 6 was refused. [Para 61][Para 63][Para 64]

On maternity pay, Articles 182(1) and 183(1) fix ninety days at half wage including perquisites but are silent on timing. Article 115(3) requires wages to be paid before a day off, and Article 103 treats money paid during maternity leave as wage (Award 154/11-B&N), so Article 115 applies to maternity payment (Awards 06/08-Kingsland, 97/06-New Max, 91/07-JK, 58/11-United Apparel, 81/11-GHG). Monthly payment of half of selected allowances violated Article 115. [Para 67][Para 70][Para 71][Para 72][Para 73]

On water, Clauses 1 and 4 of Prakas No. 054 require sufficient hygienic drink and drinking vessels. Water supply was sufficient, but glasses were kept only at the infirmary, which contravened Clause 4. [Para 75][Para 78][Para 81][Para 83]

On short-time work, Article 71(11) permits suspension only for serious economic difficulty, under Labour Inspector control, and Awards 136/08-Supertex and 51/09-Yung Wah 1&2 require notification so the Inspector can verify justification. The employer never notified the Inspector, relying instead on a signed notification offering 50% of daily wages. [Para 84][Para 86][Para 89] Under Article 13(11) the Labour Law is public order, so a unilateral or agreed rule below it is void; full wages were therefore ordered. [Para 91][Para 92][Para 93]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A statutory entitlement may not be reduced by an agreement, however widely signed: where an employer suspends work for lack of orders without notifying the Labour Inspector, the suspension is unlawful and full wages are owed, and a document signed by worker delegates cannot convert that into a lawful 50%-wage arrangement. [Para 89][Para 92][Para 93]

**Obiter dicta:** that a maternity payment is wage for Article 115 purposes and so must be paid before leave begins; that rising rent and transport costs are not the employer's management concern; and that competing unions may aggregate members to cross the 50% threshold for an interests dispute. [Para 57][Para 70][Para 73]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The English version omits the evidence section entirely, deferring to the Khmer original. The case number prints as "45112-Pou Yuen", presumably 45/12. The header dates the award 6 July 2012 while para 22 records an extension of the due date for issuing it to 6 April 2012; both are reproduced as printed. In Part II the attendance-bonus issue is printed "Issue 43" instead of Issue 4. The translation drops the letter "t" throughout ("themployer", "righto", "equivalento"), and several passages repeat or truncate, notably the quoted Clause 4 of Prakas No. 054 and the 1 February 2012 notification at para 90, whose opening words are garbled.
