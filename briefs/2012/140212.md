# Award No. 218/12 — Nhu Ark Khit Garment Factory Ltd (Arbitration Council, 4 December 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3 (Seng Vouch Hun for the employer, An Virak for the workers, Kuy Nam presiding) · Doc ID 140212

## 1. Facts
Nhu Ark Khit Garment Factory Ltd operates at Trapeang Village, Peuk Commune, Ang Snuol, Kandal, and employs roughly 3,000 workers. The union federation filed nine demands in September 2012; conciliation on 5 October 2012 failed on eight of them, and at the hearing the union withdrew Issue 8 (a USD 10 monthly skills allowance), leaving seven. The parties had signed a memorandum of understanding on 3 October 2012 and, in this case, both chose a non-binding award on the interest disputes. The panel divided the seven issues into rights disputes (Issues 2 and 3) and interest disputes (Issues 1, 4, 5, 6 and 7), and decided the latter group on representation grounds without reaching their merits. [Para 1][Para 27][Para 30][Para 33][Para 37]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must play music for workers during working hours and install two more televisions and loudspeakers in the canteen? [Para 119]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must widen the western gate so that workers can enter and leave more easily? [Para 82]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must deal with theft of company clothing inside the factory instead of referring workers to the authorities? [Para 90]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must provide transport or USD 3 a day in travel costs to workers who work on public holidays and Sundays? [Para 100]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the employer must add USD 3 a month to the existing USD 7 travel allowance? [Para 106]
6. (Issue 6) Whether the employer must pay a USD 10 a month housing allowance? [Para 111]
7. (Issue 7) Whether the employer must pay a 2,000 riel meal allowance for the midday break? [Para 116]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the two canteen televisions sit in the middle of the room so workers at the far end cannot see them, and the music is played at unclear volume with songs they dislike; the gate is only 3 metres wide and jams at the midday break; the company refers workers who take one or two garments to the authorities, where they are held for hundreds of dollars, when dismissal would suffice; and neighbouring factories such as QM and Met Met pay USD 10 travel, USD 10 housing and USD 13 in meal money. [Para 76][Para 78][Para 85][Para 92][Para 108][Para 117]
**Employer (respondent):** the company cannot meet the demands; the congestion comes from street vendors outside the gate, not from the gate itself, and the western gate is 3.6 metres wide; the Labour Law requires theft to be reported, and leniency would let every worker steal a garment; and the extra allowances are beyond its capacity. [Para 80][Para 83][Para 86][Para 93][Para 96][Para 103][Para 110]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the claims on Issues 2 and 3 were dismissed as rights disputes and the claims on Issues 1, 4, 5, 6 and 7 were refused consideration as interest disputes; no payment was ordered. [Para 182][Para 183][Para 185]
Issue 2: both sides accepted that congestion occurs at the midday break because food sellers crowd the entrance, and the panel found it unproven that widening the gate would remove the jam; the employer's evidence that no worker had been squeezed at the gate, except by the bus carrying workers, was not rebutted. The claim was dismissed. [Para 86][Para 87][Para 88]
Issue 3: the internal rules of April 2009, registered as No. 188/09, provide in Article 10 that deliberate and proven misconduct is disciplined according to its gravity and that serious misconduct is dealt with under the Labour Law, with referral to the competent authority where necessary. The panel held that the employer was applying its own rules and the law, and that the union's demand to keep every theft case inside the factory had no legal basis. [Para 98][Para 99]
Interest issues: the panel noted that the music, television, transport, travel, housing and meal demands appear in no law, collective agreement or prior employer practice, so each is a demand for benefits above the law and therefore an interest dispute. Following its established approach, it refused to consider an interest dispute where the union bringing it lacks most-representative status in the factory. The union of Nhu Ark Khit had not obtained that status, so it could not lawfully represent all the workers in an interest dispute; Issues 1, 4, 5, 6 and 7 were therefore refused, without any finding on their merits. [Para 122][Para 123][Para 125][Para 174][Para 175]
The panel split its award into two parts: the rights-dispute decisions, which become final immediately and bind the parties under the memorandum of understanding of 3 October 2012, and the interest-dispute decisions, which become final eight days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Minister. [Para 33][Para 184][Para 190]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a union that is not the most representative in the factory brings a demand for a benefit that appears in no law, collective agreement or established practice, the panel will classify it as an interest dispute and refuse to consider it, leaving the merits untouched; a demand that the employer refrain from reporting proven theft to the authorities is not an arbitrable claim, because the employer is applying its registered internal rules and the Labour Law. [Para 122][Para 174][Para 99]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that widening the gate would not certainly cure the congestion, since the crowd of outside food sellers, not the gate, causes it. [Para 87]
