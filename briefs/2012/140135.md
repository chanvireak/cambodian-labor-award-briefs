# Award No. 102/12 — For Thyun Thiv Garment Factory Co (Arbitration Council, 6 July 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-selected, dissenting; Liv Sovanna, worker-selected; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · Doc ID 140135

## 1. Facts
The employer, a Phnom Penh garment factory whose Khmer name is transliterated above and which employs about 478 workers, let the two-month fixed-term contract of the union's elected president expire on 28 February 2012, having given him notice on 18 February. [Para 141][Para 151][Para 217] The union had been registered on 12 March 2012 with roughly 200 members, and its leadership notice reached the company on 3 January 2012. [Para 143][Para 228] Of the 11 collective demands, five were unresolved; the check-off claim was settled at the hearing, leaving four for decision, of which only the reinstatement claim succeeded. [Para 19][Para 23][Para 248]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay 2,000 riel meal money to workers who volunteer to work on public holidays? [Para 191]
2. Whether the employer must reinstate the dismissed union president and pay him back wages? [Para 214]
4. Whether the employer must pay USD 15 a month as a milk allowance for newborn children up to 18 months? [Para 240]
5. Whether the employer must pay a skills allowance of USD 5, USD 10 or USD 15 a month according to the number of machines a worker can operate? [Para 244]

(The panel's issue 3, the 1,000 riel union check-off, was settled by agreement at the hearing and was not decided. [Para 23])

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** work on a public holiday is a form of overtime and so attracts the 2,000 riel meal allowance that is otherwise paid for overtime; the president was the only stitcher in his section not renewed although he was the most skilled, and the company then hired an untrained worker to do his job, which shows the "no work" explanation is false. [Para 146][Para 224][Para 230]
**Employer (respondent):** it pays 300 per cent of the statutory rate for holiday work and holiday work is not overtime because total hours stay under 48 a week; the contract simply ran to its term, the stitching section had little work, and non-renewal is the employer's right; the milk and skills allowances have no legal basis and the company cannot afford them. [Para 3][Para 148][Para 152][Para 175][Para 185]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to take the dismissed union president back into work, but refused to order back wages, refused the holiday meal allowance, and refused both the milk allowance and the skills allowance. [Para 238][Para 239][Para 251][Para 252][Para 253]

On the meal claim the panel held, following Article 164 of the Labour Law and Articles 2 and 4 of Prakas No. 210 of 4 February 1999, that holiday work earns one additional day's ordinary wage rather than an overtime rate, so long as total hours stay within 48 a week; the 2,000 riel allowance is therefore not owed. [Para 194][Para 196][Para 200][Para 202] Because the claim exceeded what the law gives, it was an interest dispute, and only a most representative union may bring one; this union was not, so the claim was refused on that ground as well. [Para 205][Para 212][Para 213]

On reinstatement the panel split the question in two. First, was the non-renewal itself regular? Article 73(5) requires ten days' notice where a fixed-term contract exceeds one month, and the notice of 18 February for a contract expiring on 28 February satisfied it. [Para 214][Para 216][Para 219] Second, was it discriminatory? The panel weighed the direct and circumstantial evidence: the union had notified the candidacies on 3 January 2012 and the employer admitted it knew the president held that office; the employer had renewed all twelve other stitchers in the section and refused only him; and on 18 May 2012 it recruited a worker with no stitching skill to perform exactly his former tasks. [Para 228][Para 229][Para 230][Para 232] The panel held this proved anti-union discrimination contrary to Articles 12 and 279, and that the assertion of a contractual right not to renew was no answer. [Para 233][Para 234][Para 235] Reinstatement was ordered; back wages were refused because the case concerned the expiry of a fixed-term contract, not a dismissal. [Para 238][Para 239]

The milk and skills claims failed because no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement or party agreement supported them, which made them interest claims that this union lacked standing to bring. [Para 240][Para 241][Para 244][Para 245]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that declines to renew a fixed-term contract at its expiry acts lawfully if it gives the ten days' notice required by Article 73(5); but where the union has notified the employer of the worker's candidacy, the employer knew of it, every comparable worker was renewed and a replacement was recruited for the same tasks, the non-renewal is unlawful anti-union discrimination under Articles 12 and 279, and the remedy is reinstatement — without back wages, because the contract expired by its term rather than being terminated. [Para 219][Para 229][Para 232][Para 235][Para 239]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that the employer had given the union no answer on the milk and skills demands, and its observation that the union's registration post-dated the dismissals, are context rather than grounds of the order. [Para 143][Para 185]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer-selected arbitrator filed a signed dissent against the reinstatement order, arguing that non-renewal at expiry is a contractual right and that Articles 282, 293 and Prakas No. 305 should first have been complied with; the award is therefore not unanimous. [Para 257][Para 258][Para 261] The union's registration certificate is dated 12 March 2012 at [Para 143] but the ministerial confirmation letter is dated 12 March 2011 at [Para 128]. The check-off claim is still listed as a live issue at [Para 6] although the panel records it as settled at [Para 23]. Five unresolved points are said to exist at [Para 19] but only four are decided. The Latin form of the company name in the title is a transliteration of the Khmer name printed on the face of the award. The dismissed president and the other named workers and union officers are individuals and are reduced to roles and initials here; the replacement hired in May 2012 is named only as "a worker" at [Para 230].
