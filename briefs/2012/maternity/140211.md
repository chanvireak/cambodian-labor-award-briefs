# Award No. 214/12 — Tai Yang Enterprises Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 16 November 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3 (Chhiv Phirom, An Non, Tang Tri presiding) · Doc ID 140211

## 1. Facts
Tai Yang Enterprises is a garment company at Bek Chan, Ang Snuol, Kandal, employing about 4,000 workers. The claimant federation filed seven demands; by the hearing four (Issues 2, 5, 6 and 7) had been withdrawn, leaving three for decision. The employer did not appear and gave no reason, so the panel proceeded in its absence under Art 21 of Prakas 099, deciding on the workers' evidence alone. The union had no registration certificate, only a receipt for its application, and the transfer-of-rights documents for the 155 workers said to be represented were never filed; only three claimants attended, so the panel considered the claim for those three only. [Para 17][Para 18][Para 31][Para 34][Para 56][Para 57][Para 64]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must raise the regular attendance bonus to the full USD 15 a month claimed, instead of the USD 11 it now pays? [Para 35]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must stop reprimanding and questioning workers excessively when they request occasional leave? [Para 44]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must deduct the attendance bonus in proportion to the days of permitted unpaid leave, rather than forfeiting the whole month's bonus? [Para 50]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** since 2009 the employer paid a USD 7 monthly bonus plus USD 1 for each week of full attendance, giving USD 11; in July 2012 it absorbed the weekly USD 4 into the USD 7 and then began forfeiting the whole USD 11 for a single day of permitted personal leave, whereas the old practice deducted only USD 1 for the affected week; supervisors also refuse sick leave and use improper language, one team leader in particular. [Para 37][Para 42][Para 44][Para 52]
**Employer (respondent):** absent at the hearing and filed no documents or written submission, so no position on the merits was before the panel beyond the documented practice of paying USD 11 a month. [Para 17][Para 23][Para 53]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand for a USD 15 attendance bonus was dismissed, the demand on reprimands was dismissed, and on Issue 4 the employer was ordered to reduce the attendance bonus only in proportion to days of permitted unpaid leave. [Para 129][Para 130][Para 131]
Attendance bonus (Issue 1): the panel noted the USD 11 paid already exceeded the USD 10 minimum fixed by point 2 of Notification 230 of 25 July 2012. The weekly USD 1 element was a discretionary reward, and under Art 2 of the Labour Law the employer may manage the enterprise provided its decisions are lawful and reasonable; the change had been announced by loudspeaker and the consolidation of the two elements was consistent with the notification, so the refusal to restore the weekly payment was lawful and reasonable. [Para 73][Para 77][Para 80]
Reprimands (Issue 3): the party making an allegation bears the burden of proof (Award 101/08). The workers gave no date and named no worker affected, and conceded that treatment varied with each supervisor's character, with some supervisors making leave easy; only one team leader was identified, so the panel held the information should be raised with the company's administration or the employer rather than treated as a proven breach. [Para 32][Para 84][Para 88][Para 89]
Bonus and leave (Issue 4): the panel reasoned that the attendance bonus in Notification 230 has ceased to be a mere management tool and has become a general legal norm binding all textile, garment and footwear employers, so its regime follows wages under Art 103. In ordinary workplace usage "absence" means unauthorised absence, not being present without permission; the Labour Law distinguishes annual, sick, special, maternity, holiday and weekly leave precisely because each has different consequences. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 113][Para 115][Para 117]
Consequence: unpaid leave granted on request is a prior agreement reducing that month's working days, so it cannot be treated as absence; but paying the full bonus for a day the worker agreed to forgo would be unjust to the employer, since the worker has already accepted losing wages for that day. The order was therefore pro-rata deduction, not full payment. [Para 118][Para 123][Para 127][Para 128]
President Tang Tri dissented on Issue 4: the notification carries two suspensive conditions ("present on the working days of each month" and "without absence"), so under Art 325 of the Civil Code the employer's duty never arises unless both are met, and reading "absence" as unauthorised absence substitutes the panel's assumption for the parties' will; personal leave without urgency should not earn the bonus. [Para 134][Para 138][Para 151][Para 174]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer grants unpaid leave on request, the days forgone are fixed by prior agreement and are not "absence" within the attendance-bonus notification, so the bonus may be reduced in proportion to those days but may not be forfeited for the whole month; an employer's decision to consolidate or withdraw a discretionary weekly bonus element remains lawful and reasonable where the total paid still exceeds the notified minimum. [Para 117][Para 127][Para 80]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that reported mistreatment by a single supervisor is a matter for the company's internal administration rather than for an award, and that it would not interpret parts of the notification unrelated to the case. [Para 89][Para 111]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The date of the complaint is printed as 27 November 2012 [Para 13], which falls after the hearing of 6 November and after the award date, and contradicts the conciliation report of 22 October; both dates are reported, not reconciled. The panel records that three claimants attended while describing "four names" [Para 62]. The company is called Tai Yang Enterprises in the heading but "Tai Yien" in two of the Ministry documents [Para 23], and the employer is recorded both as absent and as having appeared [Para 17]. Worker representatives and the named team leader are reduced to roles; the team leader's name survives in the source. One arbitrator's name is spelled two ways across the signature pages.
