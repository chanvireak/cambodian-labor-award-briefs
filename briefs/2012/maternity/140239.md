# Award No. 93/12 — Long Lead (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 19 June 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (president) · Doc ID 140239

## 1. Facts
Long Lead (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. operates at Chamkar Svay village, Sethey commune, Samaki Mean Chey district, Kampong Chhnang, employing 1,400 workers, of whom the employer says 90 per cent are women [Para 1][Para 37][Para 43]. The Khmer Youth Union Federation, registered on 12 July 2010 with 612 members, filed eight demands on 11 April 2012; conciliation on 18 May 2012 settled seven, leaving one before the panel, which heard it on 30 May 2012 [Para 7][Para 9][Para 10][Para 39][Para 40]. The demand as filed was for two one-kilogram tins of milk powder a month plus USD 20 baby-care money; at the hearing the workers dropped the milk powder and asked instead for a nursing room and nursery [Para 41]. The company already has a nursing room, but it does not meet the standard, and the four women who have returned from maternity leave have not used it [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must set up a nursing room (បន្ទប់បំបៅគោ) and nursery (ទារកដ្ឋាន) in or near the factory, or failing that pay USD 20 a month toward childcare? [Para 41][Para 50][Para 53]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the room and nursery are a legal duty the employer must discharge, and the factory sits far from any town with no childcare nearby [Para 42][Para 60]. If a room truly cannot be arranged, USD 20 a month for child-minding would do; but a receipts requirement is unworkable because grandparents, not nurseries, look after the children [Para 47][Para 48].

**Employer (respondent):** it does not agree to the demand and leaves the matter to the panel [Para 49]. It points out that it has a nursing room already, though not yet to standard, that only four women have returned from maternity leave, and that no one has used the room to date [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to arrange a nursing room and nursery inside or near the company, and, until a nursery exists, to let mothers place their children anywhere with the employer meeting the actual cost [Para 67][Para 69][Para 70].

The panel first classified the claim. Following its own earlier reasoning in Awards 05/11-IM WE and 13/11-Gol Camivich, it defined a rights dispute as one turning on an entitlement written into a law, an agreement or a collective agreement, and held that Article 186 of the Labour Law supplies exactly such an entitlement [Para 50][Para 51][Para 54]. That article requires any enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a nursing room in or near the workplace and a nursery as well [Para 55]. Because the claim was characterised as a rights dispute, the decision became immediately final and binding on the parties through the Memorandum of Understanding of 28 September 2010, rather than provisional as an interest award would be [Para 14][Para 16][Para 71].

On the substance the panel accepted that Article 186 binds the employer, and that the existing room fell short of the requirement [Para 58][Para 62]. It then tested the alternative the workers had offered. Award 81/09-Vorn Reach had allowed an employer to discharge the duty by reimbursing an outside nursery against receipts, and the panel endorsed that reading in principle [Para 56][Para 57]. It held, however, that the outside-nursery route could not work in this case, because the company is remote from any town and there is no nursery nearby to send a child to [Para 63][Para 64]. Article 186 therefore required performance in kind: a room and nursery in or near the factory, with the interim solution that mothers may place children anywhere while the employer pays the actual cost shown on receipts [Para 65][Para 66][Para 68].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a nursing room and nursery rests on Article 186 of the Labour Law and is therefore a dispute over rights, not interests, so the resulting award binds the parties at once and cannot be reopened as a bargaining claim [Para 54][Para 71]. Where an employer cannot satisfy Article 186 by reimbursing an outside nursery because none exists near a remote site, the duty must be performed in kind on or beside the premises, and until then the employer bears the actual documented cost of whatever childcare the mother arranges [Para 64][Para 66].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's endorsement of Award 81/09-Vorn Reach, under which receipts from an outside nursery suffice, was not applied here but was left as the general rule for factories with nurseries nearby [Para 57].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award prints its issue heading without any numeral, so "(Issue 1)" is this brief's own label for the single issue referred and not the panel's numbering [Para 41][Para 50][Para 69]. The company name shifts between Long Lead, Long Li D and Long Ly across the text [Para 37][Para 60]. Three letters are addressed to the Kampong Speu labour department although the factory and the conciliation are in Kampong Chhnang, and the file elsewhere records a request made to the Kampong Chhnang department, leaving the correct province unresolved [Para 27][Para 32][Para 8]. The operative order is printed twice almost verbatim and the reasoning repeats its own sentences at lines 59 and 65-70 [Para 65][Para 67][Para 68]. The signature block is corrupted by stray Latin fragments, and paragraph 72, which should carry the interest-dispute order, is empty [Para 72]. Individual union officers and workers appear only by role.
