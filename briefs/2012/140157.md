# Award No. 147/12 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ដេខូរ៉ូ (ខេមបូឌា) (Dekoro (Cambodia)) (Arbitration Council, 22 August 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ly Tai Seng (employer-selected), Liv Sovanna (worker-selected), Kong Phalla (president) · Doc ID 140157

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ដេខូរ៉ូ (ខេមបូឌា) began manufacturing furniture fittings in November 2011 at Tramang Kong, Samrong Tong, Kampong Speu, and employs about 120 workers, of whom only some 25 are women. [Para 54][Para 55] The Federation of Union Development Movement of Cambodia (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព អភិវឌ្ឍន៍ ចលនាការងារ កម្ពុជា) filed for the 45 workers affected, three of them representing the group; the union held only a receipt for its registration application dated 9 July 2012 and had not yet been registered. [Para 42][Para 56][Para 57][Para 58] The union was not the most representative union in the factory. [Para 58] Conciliation on 7 July 2012 failed because the employer did not attend, and the report of non-conciliation of 20 July 2012 reached the Secretariat on 24 July 2012 listing 12 unresolved demands. [Para 16][Para 21][Para 22] At the hearing of 6 August 2012 the parties merged demands 1 and 2, and 11 and 12, and settled five points (7, 8, 9, 10 and 11 & 12), leaving five demands for decision. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 33] The award date was moved by agreement from 15 to 22 August 2012. [Para 34]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issues 1 & 2) Whether the employer must raise the base wage by USD 6 and the regular-attendance bonus by USD 5 a month, and pay every section the same quality-and-quantity bonus of USD 25 a month? [Para 59][Para 82]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay a transport allowance of USD 10 per worker per month? [Para 68][Para 129]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must pay a milk allowance of USD 15 per month in place of the nursery it has not built? [Para 71][Para 114]
4. (Issue 5) Whether the employer must let pregnant workers go home 15 minutes before the end of the shift? [Para 76][Para 120]
5. (Issue 6) Whether the employer must roof the bicycle and motorbike parking area? [Para 78][Para 126]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant union):** the base wage of USD 61 and the attendance bonus of USD 7 are too low for the workers' living costs and other factories pay more; the quality-and-quantity bonus is unequal because the leather-sewing section receives USD 25 while every other section receives USD 15, although the sections work interdependently; other factories pay transport money; the company has no nursery so USD 15 a month should replace it; pregnant workers need 15 minutes to leave without being jostled; and the parking area has no roof, so the sun ages their vehicles. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 68][Para 73][Para 77][Para 78]
**Employer (respondent):** the current wage and attendance bonus follow the Ministry of Labour's notification; work differs in difficulty between the six sections, so the USD 25 bonus is confined to the leather-sewing section as an exercise of managerial discretion; there is no agreement and no legal duty to pay transport money, and the company provides parking with a padlock and accepts liability for loss; Article 186 of the Labour Law requires a nursery only where 100 or more women are employed; and it offers 5 minutes, not 15, for pregnant workers to leave. [Para 61][Para 65][Para 66][Para 69][Para 75][Para 76][Para 80]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider all five remaining demands (Issues 1 & 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6), leaving the five points settled at the hearing to stand, and the non-binding award became final after eight days absent written objection. [Para 127][Para 128][Para 129][Para 130][Para 131][Para 132][Para 133]
On Issues 1 & 2 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, no collective agreement and no agreement between the parties requiring a base wage of USD 67 or an attendance bonus of USD 12, and noted the parties had reached no agreement on the demand. [Para 67][Para 82] It classified the claim as an interest dispute. [Para 83] Following its usual approach, it held that for an interest dispute it will examine whether the union is the most representative, because only such a union may lawfully conclude a collective agreement and bring an interest dispute to the panel. [Para 84][Para 117] It cited Article 43 of Prakas 099 of 21 April 2004, under which an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement for one year from the date it becomes final. [Para 85] Because this union was not registered and was not the most representative, it lacked the legal standing and the panel refused to consider the claim. [Para 112][Para 118][Para 127]
On Issue 3 the panel found no law, collective agreement or party agreement requiring USD 10 a month for transport, classified the demand as an interest dispute, repeated the representativeness test, and refused to consider it on the same ground. [Para 70][Para 122][Para 123][Para 124][Para 129]
On Issue 4 the panel found no law, collective agreement or agreement requiring the USD 15 milk allowance, noted the company has no nursery and about 20 women, and refused to consider the demand as an interest dispute for the same lack of a registered, most-representative union. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 114]
On Issue 5 the panel found no law, collective agreement or agreement granting 15 minutes, noted the employer already allows 5 minutes and that no pregnant worker had been shown to have suffered from jostling, classified the demand as an interest dispute, and refused to consider it. [Para 115][Para 116][Para 120]
On Issue 6 the panel found no law, collective agreement or agreement requiring a roof over the parking area, classified the demand as an interest dispute and refused to consider it. [Para 121][Para 122][Para 126]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a wage increase, allowance or facility that no article of the Labour Law, no collective agreement and no party agreement requires is an interest dispute, and the panel will not consider it unless the union bringing it is registered and is the most representative union in the enterprise — the status that carries the legal right to conclude a collective agreement and to refer an interest dispute. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 118][Para 124]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the six production sections do different work and that the company's parking area is guarded by a padlock with the employer accepting liability for loss — neither observation was needed to refuse the demands. [Para 61][Para 80]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The base wage demanded is printed as USD 76 in the summary of the 12 demands but as USD 67 in the findings and in the operative section, and the panel reasoned on USD 67 without noting the discrepancy. [Para 2][Para 59][Para 82] The company is "ដេខូរ៉ូ (ខេមបូឌា)" in the header but "ដេខូរ៉ូ ខេមបូឌា ខូអិលធីឌី" in the findings, and the conciliation report number is printed as "ដេខ ១០២៧/១២" where elsewhere it is "លេខ ១០២៧/១២". [Para 1][Para 50][Para 54] The employer's representative is named three different ways across the header and the evidence list, so the office cannot be matched to the name; the name is not reproduced here. [Para 1][Para 35] Latin OCR noise is embedded in the reasoning at the representativeness test and in the operative section, and the word "Apple" interrupts the wage demand. [Para 82][Para 84][Para 118][Para 129] The Milk-allowance discussion opens without its issue heading and the number of women is printed as about 25 in the findings and about 20 in the issue text. [Para 55][Para 71] Individual workers and union officials are identified only by role or initial here. [Para 2][Para 57]
