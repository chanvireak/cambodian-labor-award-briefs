# Award No. 51/12 — Sabrina (Cambodia) Garment MFG Corp (Arbitration Council, 27 March 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Liv Sovanna; Ang Eng Thong chairing) · Doc ID 140235

## 1. Facts
A garment factory in Kompong Speu employing 5,000 workers dismissed Mr G, a worker in the ironing section on an undetermined-duration contract since 19 December 2006 and secretary of the local FTUWKC union elected on 28 January 2012. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 34] On 20 February 2012 the employer agreed with the C.CAWDU union — the most representative union at the plant — and worker representatives to move 32 ironing workers to the sample section; Mr G alone refused, because the new wage was never stated and the sample work was harder. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 26][Para 32] He was dismissed on 22 February 2012 for refusing a direction, and the employer had not sought Labour Inspector authorisation. [Para 33][Para 60][Para 63] The award is an unofficial English translation of the Khmer original and is binding as a rights dispute under the 28 September 2010 MoU. [Para 1][Para 9][Para 67]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must reinstate the dismissed union leader, having dismissed him for refusing a transfer whose new wage was never disclosed? [Para 2][Para 22][Para 36]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), the local FTUWKC union:** Mr G was elected secretary on 28 January 2012, the union notified the employer of its leadership on 2 February 2012 in person and again by post on 6 February 2012, and the dismissal of a protected union leader without Labour Inspector authorisation was unlawful. [Para 17][Para 34][Para 35][Para 70]
**Employer (respondent):** Mr G was dismissed for refusing to follow a lawful direction to move to the sample section; at the time of dismissal he was treated as an ordinary worker, the union's notification was never received, and the company only learned of his union role at the hearing. [Para 33][Para 70][Para 73]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to reinstate Mr G to the sample section, to maintain his wages and benefits, and to retain a work responsibility at least at the level of his previous responsibility in the ironing section. [Para 64][Para 66]

The panel first classified the demand as a rights dispute, because termination of an employment contract has a basis in the Labour Law. [Para 37][Para 38] It then applied Article 74: an undetermined-duration contract may be terminated at will, but only with written notice and only for a valid reason relating to the worker's aptitude or behaviour and the needs of the enterprise. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 42]

On whether the refusal was misconduct, the panel read Article 2's definition of an enterprise, as it had in earlier awards, to give the employer a right to direct and supervise the company provided that it is exercised lawfully and reasonably. [Para 43] Article 112(a) requires the employer to inform workers precisely of the wage terms before assigning them to a job or whenever those terms change. [Para 44] Here the employer transferred Mr G and changed his wage without ever stating the exact figure, telling him only that it would rise to the head of section's wage and that he should move first. [Para 28][Para 30][Para 45][Para 46] The panel held that a worker has the right to know the wage for specific tasks so as to judge whether the work is commensurate, and that requiring a move to a harder section without disclosing the wage was unreasonable and inconsistent with Article 112(a). [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49] Refusal to move in those circumstances was therefore not a refusal of a direction amounting to misconduct, and there was no valid reason to dismiss. [Para 51][Para 52]

The panel then applied Article 293 and clause 3 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, under which a union leadership candidate enjoys shop-steward protection for 45 days before and, if not elected, 45 days after the election, provided the employer is notified by reliable means. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 55] It found all three Hytex conditions met: Mr G was a candidate and was elected, the dismissal fell within 45 days of the election, and the union notified the employer on 2 and 6 February 2012 by reliable means despite the employer's denial. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58] Because no authorisation had been requested from the Labour Inspector before dismissal, the panel refused to weigh the request the employer produced only afterwards. [Para 60][Para 62][Para 63]

Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented in part, agreeing that Mr G should be reinstated from 23 February 2012 until the Labour Inspector rules, but holding that the decision whether to dismiss a union leader thereafter lies exclusively with the Labour Inspector. [Para 76][Para 77]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer's direction to move a worker to another section on a changed wage is not a lawful and reasonable exercise of managerial authority unless the worker is told the new wage before the move; a refusal to move in the absence of that information is not misconduct and cannot justify dismissal. [Para 47][Para 51][Para 52]
Where a union has notified the employer of its elected leaders by reliable means, dismissal of one of them without prior Labour Inspector authorisation under Article 293 is unlawful and requires reinstatement. [Para 58][Para 63][Para 64]

**Obiter dicta:** That protection for an elected candidate lasts at least 45 days after the election or may extend to the date the union applies for registration, and the majority's view that a later authorisation request is inadmissible to cure the defect. [Para 58][Para 62]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
This is an unofficial English translation, so the Khmer original governs; the OCR systematically drops "th" from word onsets, producing forms such as "themployer" and "athearing" throughout. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 7] All worker and union names are printed in full and I have reduced them to initials. [Para 2][Para 22] The commencement year is printed as 19 December 2012 with 2006 supplied in brackets, an apparent OCR repair. [Para 22] The hearing date is 12 March 2012 at one point but "the hearing on 14 March 2012" later. [Para 5][Para 59] The union's postal notification is dated 6 February 2012 in the findings but 6 March 2012 in the dissent, where the receipt is said to bear 5 March 2012. [Para 35][Para 71] The dissent's conclusion is self-contradictory, stating both that the Labour Inspector will not order dismissal and that he will approve it on the same premise. [Para 77]
