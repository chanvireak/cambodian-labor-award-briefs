# Award No. 224/12 — Joo Yu In Das Stri (Arbitration Council, 13 December 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Tuon Si Phan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chea, president) · Doc ID 140217

## 1. Facts
Joo Yu In Das Stri — also printed as Joo Yu Industry — is a footwear manufacturer in Sen Sok, Phnom Penh, employing about 200 workers [Para 40][Para 100][Para 101]. The Federation of Unions for Workers' Rights brought the complaint of 26 September 2012, relying on a rights-transfer letter of 20 September 2012 bearing three workers' thumbprints and a list of 68 workers [Para 15][Para 42]. Conciliation on 1 November 2012 failed on all eight demands, and the file reached the panel on 6 November 2012 [Para 16][Para 17]. At the hearing of 16 November 2012 issues 3, 4/1, 5, 7 and 8 settled, issues 1 and 2 were withdrawn, leaving only Issue 4/2 (meal allowance for overtime, paid weekly instead of fortnightly) and Issue 6 (a permanent nurse and adequate medicines) for decision in a non-binding award [Para 20][Para 21][Para 22]. The award date was extended by agreement from 4 December to 14 December 2012 [Para 23][Para 24].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 4/2) Whether workers are entitled to be paid the overtime meal allowance once a week rather than once a fortnight, where the employer pays on the 1st and the 16th or 17th of each month? [Para 43][Para 63][Para 65]
2. (Issue 6) Whether the employer must station a nurse on duty during all working hours and provide sufficient medicines, where the company has about 200 workers and keeps only basic remedies? [Para 55][Para 90][Para 104]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The meal allowance must come weekly because workers need that money each week to buy food for the overtime hours they work, and a fortnightly payment defeats its purpose [Para 45][Para 64][Para 84]. On health care, the employer should station a nurse on duty to protect workers' health; the union only learned at the hearing of 16 November 2012 that the administration hands out medicines [Para 59][Para 60][Para 108].

**Employer (respondent):** Weekly payment is impossible because of heavy export volumes and lack of time to process it; the company originally paid weekly but changed the practice over a year ago, and workers agreed to the change [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49]. It denied any agreement at the 14 April 2012 meeting and said there is no written record of one [Para 52][Para 53]. It cannot afford a full-time doctor, though it says medicines are already sufficient [Para 61][Para 62].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted in part — the panel ordered the employer to pay the overtime meal allowance once a week to workers doing overtime, and ordered it to station one nurse on duty plus a doctor or mid-level nurse present for at least two hours during working hours, and to supply medicines sufficient under Prakas No. 330 [Para 111][Para 112][Para 113].

On Issue 4/2 the panel held, following Awards 85/09 and 110/09, that the meal allowance is not part of the overtime wage: it substitutes for a free meal, and the employer that does not provide the meal must pay 2,000 riel a day under Notice No. 041 [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67][Para 72]. Notice 041 does not fix the payment date, but Award 76/11 requires a free meal to be given to workers doing overtime, and payment in lieu must be made within a reasonable period ensuring the worker can buy food for the overtime worked, whether daily, weekly or monthly [Para 73][Para 77][Para 80]. The panel noted the change of practice had run over a year, but found the workers had put no evidence of the alleged 14 April 2012 agreement by the deadline of 20 November 2012 [Para 50][Para 54]. Weighing the purpose of the Labour Law, it concluded the allowance is due weekly so workers can buy food for overtime, and ordered accordingly [Para 87][Para 88][Para 89].

On Issue 6 the panel applied Article 238 (first aid must be organised), Article 239 §1 (the occupational health team is led by a doctor) and Article 242 §1 (enterprises with at least 50 workers must have a permanent infirmary, staffed by a doctor and one or more nurses in proportion to the workforce) [Para 90][Para 91][Para 92]. Prakas No. 330 of 6 December 2000 fixes the ratio: for 50–300 workers, one nurse permanently on duty and a doctor or mid-level nurse for two hours per 8-hour shift, with minimum furniture, equipment and a medicines list in its annex [Para 97][Para 118][Para 119]. Though the employer kept remedies for headache, stomach ache, cough and wound cleaning, the panel found them insufficient under the Prakas and found no doctor on the premises [Para 106][Para 109][Para 110].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the timing of the meal allowance depends on workers' necessity and that a worker paid weekly or monthly can still plan food purchases, so the obligation is framed by need rather than by a fixed statutory timetable [Para 77][Para 81][Para 82].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The overtime meal allowance is compensation for a meal, not part of the overtime wage, so an employer who does not provide the meal owes 2,000 riel a day under Notice 041 and must pay it within a period that lets the worker buy food for the overtime worked — weekly where the workers show that need; and an enterprise of 50 to 300 workers must maintain a permanent infirmary with a nurse on duty and a doctor or mid-level nurse present at least two hours per shift, equipped to the Prakas 330 standard [Para 67][Para 77][Para 103][Para 118].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award date is printed as 13 December 2012 in the header although the parties' extension runs to 14 December 2012 [Para 1][Para 24]. The employer's name appears in several OCR forms (Joo Yu In Das Stri, Joo Yu Industry, Joo Yu), and the non-conciliation report is dated 6 November in one place and 5 November in another [Para 12][Para 17][Para 37][Para 40]. Three workers who thumbprinted the rights-transfer letter are named in full and are withheld here [Para 42]. The panel's reasoning is internally inconsistent: at [Para 77] it holds that payment need only fall within a reasonable period and not weekly, yet at [Para 88] it finds a weekly entitlement and orders it [Para 89]. Pages 11–12 reproduce Prakas No. 330 and its annex in full, including French pharmaceutical lists, so the appendix is regulation text rather than award text [Para 115][Para 130].
