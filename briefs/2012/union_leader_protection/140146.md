# Award No. 125/12 — Tae Young (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 08 August 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed, dissenting; An Non, worker-appointed; Pen Bunchhea, president) · Doc ID 140146

## 1. Facts
Tae Young (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a garment factory in Angsnuol district, Kandal province, employing about 700 workers [Para 59]. The Khmer Youth Voice Union at the company, registered on 11 January 2012 with 44 members, was the claimant [Para 60][Para 61]. Its complaint of 10 May 2012 carried 18 demands; conciliation settled 12 of them, the workers withdrew 4, and 2 remained for arbitration — the dismissal of Ms S, the local union secretary, and the payment of full wages during work suspensions [Para 27][Para 31][Para 32]. Ms S was hired on 14 July 2011 on a two-month probation contract and then on successive six-month fixed-duration contracts [Para 62][Para 63]. The employer dismissed her on 17 January 2012 for fighting a hygiene-section worker inside the factory, paid her severance, and did not seek the labour inspector's authorisation [Para 79][Para 80][Para 102]. Two hearings were held, on 05 and 31 July 2012 [Para 24][Para 25].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the dismissal of Ms S, the union secretary, was lawful and, if not, whether the employer must reinstate her and pay her wages and allowances from the date of dismissal until her return? [Para 62][Para 90]
2. Whether the employer must pay base wages at 100 per cent during periods when it suspended contracts without the labour inspector's authorisation, and pay arrears to the workers whose contracts were suspended? [Para 81][Para 110]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Ms S did not fight; there was only a quarrel with a hygiene worker, and her dismissal was discrimination against a union officer, no fault being proved against her [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74]. She had reported the union leadership to the company administration on 26 November 2011 and the employer did not deny it [Para 75][Para 76]. On suspensions, the employer suspended contracts for 3 to 5 days without the inspector's permission, paid only 50 per cent, and the lack of work was the employer's own problem which should not touch wages; a list of 30 affected workers was filed on 10 July 2012 [Para 82][Para 84][Para 85][Para 87].

**Employer (respondent):** the union president's own testimony established that a fight did take place in the factory yard on 13 January 2012, so dismissal followed serious misconduct, and the company also dismissed the other worker involved [Para 70][Para 71][Para 77][Para 78]. It paid severance which Ms S never collected [Para 79]. For short suspensions it does not seek authorisation, only for long ones [Para 86], and it objected that the workers' list of names was neither accurate nor clear [Para 89].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to take Ms S back and pay her wages and allowances from the dismissal date until she resumes work, while her fellow workers' claim for 100 per cent wages during past suspensions was refused; the employer was separately ordered to stop immediately any suspension not compliant with the Labour Law [Para 131][Para 132].
On issue 1 the panel held that Article 293 of the Labour Law requires the labour inspector's agreement before dismissing a staff delegate or candidate, and that Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 extends that protection to three union leaders under Articles 282 and 293; as local union secretary Ms S enjoyed the same protection as a delegate [Para 90][Para 95][Para 96]. Article 295 allows the enterprise head to stand an employee down at once in a serious-misconduct case while awaiting the inspector's decision, but the employer took no such step [Para 100][Para 106]. The panel accepted that the fight occurred and was serious misconduct [Para 103], yet because the employer never sought prior authorisation and never stood her down pending a decision, the dismissal was procedurally unlawful [Para 105][Para 107]; under Article 34 of Prakas 099 it ordered reinstatement and payment of wages and allowances until her return [Para 108][Para 109].
On issue 14 the panel accepted the legal premise: Article 71(11) permits suspension only for serious economic or material difficulty, for no more than two months, under the labour inspector's supervision, and Article 72 exempts the employer from wages only where the suspension was lawful; the suspensions of 3 to 5 days were never notified to the inspector, so full wages were owed [Para 110][Para 112][Para 116][Para 119][Para 121]. It nevertheless refused the arrears because the claimants bore the burden of proof and supplied only a list of 30 names with no dates, durations or amounts, on which no award could be based [Para 122][Para 124][Para 126][Para 127]. It did order the unlawful suspension practice stopped at once [Para 129][Para 130].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union leader's dismissal is unlawful, whatever the misconduct, unless the employer first obtains the labour inspector's agreement; where Article 295 permits an immediate stand-down pending that decision, failure to use it leaves the dismissal without procedural foundation, and reinstatement with wages and allowances from the dismissal date follows [Para 96][Para 106][Para 109]. A claim for wages withheld during unlawful suspensions still fails in its money part where the claimants prove only a bare list of names, with no dates, durations or amounts, since the burden of proof lies on them — though the panel may order the unlawful practice to cease [Para 122][Para 126][Para 129].

**Obiter dicta:** the employer-appointed arbitrator recorded a dissent, appended to the award, that the company had acted under Articles 73 and 83 and that non-renewal of a fixed-duration contract is the contracting party's right, so he would have refused reinstatement [Para 138][Para 139][Para 140].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award announces 18 demands but the recount of conciliation adds up only because 12 settled, 4 were withdrawn and 2 remained, while the numbered list runs to 18 items of uneven subject matter [Para 27][Para 31][Para 32]. The non-conciliation report is dated 19 June 2012 although the final conciliation session is dated 08 June 2012 [Para 22][Para 27]. Ms S's last fixed-duration contract is described in three incompatible ways: as running from 03 February to 03 August 2012, from 14 September 2011 to 14 March 2012, and as having ended with a dismissal on 17 January 2012 [Para 64][Para 65][Para 67][Para 69]. Her Khmer name is OCR'd in several spellings across the file, and the panel's own text refers to her inconsistently; she is reduced here to an initial [Para 90][Para 107][Para 131]. The award was due on 16 July 2012 and postponed by agreement to 08 August 2012 [Para 33]. Stray Latin fragments ("San", "The", "SA", "Li") appear at the start of sentences, and the employer's officers and the union's officers are identified only by role here [Para 115][Para 122][Para 128].
