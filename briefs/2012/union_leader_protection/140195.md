# Award No. 199/12 — Tai Yang (Arbitration Council, 12 November 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Socheath, employer-appointed; An Virak, worker-appointed; Pen Bunhea, chair) · rights dispute made binding by agreement · Doc ID 140195

## 1. Facts
Tai Yang is a garment factory in Kandal province [Para 1]. The Democratic Union of Khmer Garment Workers at the factory elected its leadership at a ballot on 6 June 2012 and notified the company of the result on 7 June 2012 [Para 96][Para 24]. The company then dismissed eight union members: the president Ms K, the vice-president Ms R, the secretary Ms C, and five others — two advisers, two activists and the treasurer [Para 48][Para 52][Para 100]. Six of the women were dismissed on 18 July 2012, the adviser Ms S on 25 July 2012 and the treasurer Ms B on 30 August 2012 [Para 52][Para 70][Para 81]. The union demanded their reinstatement, back pay and compensation [Para 48]. Conciliation failed on 3 October 2012 and the parties agreed on 7 November 2012 to make the rights part of the dispute binding when the award issued [Para 6][Para 43].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must take back the eight dismissed workers — that is, whether each was a protected union candidate dismissed without the labour inspector's prior authorisation? [Para 48][Para 152]
2. (Issue 1, compensation limb) Whether the five workers found not to be protected are entitled to compensation for dismissal without a valid reason? [Para 196][Para 210]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** All eight were dismissed because of their union membership and activity, the leaders within days of the ballot and the others after they joined the union, and none of the dismissals had the labour inspector's authorisation [Para 49][Para 50][Para 152].
**Employer (respondent):** The dismissals were lawful and unconnected with the union; the workers were dismissed for the needs of the business and, in the case of the leaders, after the company had lost work and reorganised its lines [Para 120][Para 145][Para 152].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to take back the three elected leaders, the president, vice-president and secretary, from the date the award became final, and ordered compensation for the five other workers; the reinstatement claims of those five were rejected [Para 195][Para 213][Para 246][Para 247].

The panel set out Article 293, requiring the labour inspector's agreement before dismissing a staff delegate or a candidate, and Article 3 (Ka) of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, which protects candidates for 45 days before the ballot and 45 days after it for those not elected [Para 152][Para 155][Para 156][Para 168]. It noted that the Prakas does not in terms cover candidates who win a leadership post, and held that a winning candidate is protected from the date of the ballot until the union is lawfully registered [Para 167][Para 168][Para 193].

On the three leaders, the panel found that the ballot was held on 6 June 2012, that the company was notified by letters No. 155/12 of 5 June and No. 159/12 of 7 June 2012, and that the dismissal of six of the women on 18 July 2012 fell within the protected period [Para 23][Para 24][Para 52]. No authorisation had been sought or obtained, so those dismissals were void and reinstatement was ordered [Para 193][Para 195].

The panel then found that the other five — Ms S, Ms C, Ms J, Ms K and Ms B — were neither candidates nor elected leaders, so they fell outside the protection of Article 293 [Para 188][Para 212]. It nevertheless found that the company had no valid reason for dismissing them and no evidence of misconduct, so the dismissals were unlawful; because the employer said at the hearing that it would not take them back, the panel ordered compensation instead of reinstatement [Para 206][Para 207][Para 209].

For those five unprotected workers the panel went on to assess compensation. It found the women held unlimited-duration contracts with seniority ranging from four years ten months to twelve years, and that the company had given no valid reason for the dismissals [Para 196][Para 218][Para 222]. It awarded notice pay of one to three months according to seniority, a dismissal indemnity of 75 to 180 days of wages, payment for unused annual leave for the last three years, and moral damages equal to the dismissal indemnity, with last wages already paid [Para 213][Para 246][Para 247].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A candidate who wins a union leadership post is protected against dismissal under Article 293 from the date of the ballot, probationary or fixed-term status aside, and a dismissal without the labour inspector's prior authorisation is void and requires reinstatement; a worker outside that protection may still be dismissed for no valid reason, but the employer then owes notice pay, dismissal indemnity, unused annual leave and moral damages [Para 168][Para 193][Para 212][Para 213].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that Prakas No. 305 is silent on elected candidates, a gap it filled by reference to the anti-discrimination provisions [Para 167][Para 193].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The remedy table is badly damaged: the columns setting out the riel and dollar amounts for each worker are garbled, so the award records the entitlement in days and months but the money figures cannot be read reliably [Para 247]. The dismissal date of Ms S is given as 25 July 2012 in the reasons but the surrounding narrative places the events on 24 July 2012, and the treasurer's dismissal is dated 30 August 2012 in one place and August 2012 without a day in another [Para 81][Para 210]. The document list is printed twice over, with the same conciliation report entered under two slightly different titles, and one date in it carries the year "2023" where 2012 is meant [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38]. Sentences are duplicated in the demand section, and the workers' names are printed in more than one spelling, so they are given as initials or roles here. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 48]
