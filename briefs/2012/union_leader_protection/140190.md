# Award No. 191/12 — Alim (Cambodia) Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 25 October 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Socheath, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Kong Phallha, chair) · non-binding award · Doc ID 140190

## 1. Facts
Alim (Cambodia) Co Ltd is a garment factory that began operating in March 2012 with 500 employees [Para 62][Para 63]. Mr E was hired on 31 July 2012 on a two-month probation contract due to end on 31 September 2012, at USD 68 net per month plus a regularity bonus of USD 10, a health allowance of USD 5 and USD 5 for travel and accommodation [Para 71][Para 72][Para 73][Para 74]. He was elected president of the Khmer Workers' Union at the factory on 13 August 2012 and was dismissed on 17 September 2012 [Para 97][Para 80]. A first dismissal on 23 August 2012 was withdrawn by the workers at the hearing of 30 August 2012 and the panel declined to consider it [Para 75][Para 77].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer was obliged to take Mr E back in his original post with the same duties and other benefits — that is, whether the dismissal of a probationary worker who had been elected union president was lawful? [Para 71][Para 102]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The dismissal was anti-union discrimination, made once the company learned he had founded and won the union presidency; he committed no misconduct, was never warned orally or in writing, and never signed the three warning letters produced [Para 4][Para 85][Para 87]. The union notified the election result on 13 August and again by post on 14 August, the letter coming back marked "Refused" [Para 100][Para 101].
**Employer (respondent):** He was a probationary worker dismissed for failing to follow his supervisors' instructions during working hours and for stopping other workers from doing overtime; three written warnings were issued, and severance for the second dismissal was paid though he did not collect it [Para 80][Para 81][Para 84]. It denied knowing of or receiving the union notice, saying the new administration had only started on 14 August 2012 [Para 6][Para 93][Para 101].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to take Mr E back at his original workplace, keeping the same role, duties and other benefits [Para 141][Para 142].

On the probation point the panel followed its Awards No. 27/03 (Spen Diet) and No. 12/10 (Blue Som Sen Jury) in holding that an employer need not give reasons for dismissing a worker during the probation period, so the absence of a stated valid reason was not by itself decisive [Para 104][Para 105][Para 107]. Article 293 nonetheless provides that any dismissal of a staff delegate or of a candidate may be made only with the agreement of the labour inspector, and the panel read it as drawing no distinction between probationary and permanent workers [Para 109][Para 110].

The panel then applied Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, Article 4 of which protects founders and voluntary members from the date the union files for registration and for 30 days after registration [Para 111][Para 112]. It noted that the Prakas does not expressly deal with candidates who win a leadership post, and held that a winning candidate is protected from the day of the ballot, since the opposite result would defeat the prohibition on anti-union discrimination in Articles 12 and 279 [Para 118][Para 119][Para 120].

Applying the three conditions it had set in earlier awards — protected status, dismissal inside the protection window, and notice of the protected names to the employer — the panel found each met: Mr E was elected on 13 August 2012, the registration receipt was dated 21 August 2012, and the dismissal fell while registration was pending [Para 123][Para 126][Para 127]. On notice, the panel held that sending the names by post, which the employer refused, satisfied the duty to inform by any reliable means [Para 129][Para 130][Para 131]. The employer never requested the labour inspector's authorisation before dismissing him [Para 136]. Under Article 4 of the Prakas the dismissal was therefore void, and reinstatement followed [Para 138][Para 139][Para 140].

The arbitrator appointed by the employer filed a dissenting opinion in an annex, arguing that Mr E did not meet the candidacy conditions of Article 269 — he was 21 and had about two months' seniority — and so fell outside the protection the majority applied [Para 145][Para 146][Para 147].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker elected to a union leadership post is protected against dismissal under Article 293 from the date of the ballot, probationary status notwithstanding, and a dismissal effected without the labour inspector's prior authorisation is void and calls for reinstatement in the same role with the same benefits; notice of the protected names given by any reliable means is enough, and an employer's refusal of a registered letter does not defeat it [Para 119][Para 130][Para 137][Para 141].

**Obiter dicta:** that probationary workers may be dismissed without a stated reason, and that Prakas No. 305 is silent on elected candidates — a gap the panel filled by reference to Articles 12 and 279 [Para 107][Para 118][Para 119].
