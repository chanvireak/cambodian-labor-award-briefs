# Award No. 197/12 — Master Piece (Arbitration Council, 12 November 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mao Khemara, employer-appointed; Sok Panhavuth, worker-appointed; Kong Phallha, chair) · Doc ID 140194

## 1. Facts
Master Piece is a garment factory with about 327 workers [Para 104]. The Khmer Workers' Spirit Federation had received a transfer of rights from 90 workers and filed 13 demands against the company [Para 105][Para 130]. Conciliation settled 9 of them, the panel merged issues 4 and 5, and three issues remained: 4/5, 6 and 10 [Para 25][Para 26]. Mr N was hired on 17 June 2012 in the packaging unit on a two-month probation contract, then given a three-month fixed-term contract from 17 September to 17 December 2012 [Para 111][Para 112]. He was elected president of the union at the ballot of 19 September 2012 and the company dismissed him on 20 September 2012 [Para 113][Para 114]. Three other named workers, Mr P, Mr K and Mr Y, also had dismissal claims, and the workers had struck for seven days [Para 106][Para 145].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 4/5) Whether the employer must take back Mr N as union president and the three other named workers in their normal work? [Para 106][Para 154]
2. (Issue 6) Whether the employer must dismiss the supervisor Ms N? [Para 138][Para 172]
3. (Issue 10) Whether the workers who took part in the seven-day strike are entitled to wages and benefits for the strike days? [Para 145][Para 181]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The four dismissals were discrimination against union leaders and activists, done without a valid reason and without the labour inspector's authorisation [Para 127][Para 150]. The supervisor Ms N loaded workers with unreasonable amounts of work and insulted them, so she should be dismissed [Para 141][Para 142].
**Employer (respondent):** Mr N was dismissed for incompetence in packing, for campaigning for the union during working hours, and for working only about two hours on 4 October 2012 after being readmitted [Para 116][Para 117][Para 119]. The dismissals were not anti-union because about 30 other workers were dismissed at the same time, and strikers are not entitled to wages for the days they did not work [Para 89][Para 149].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to take Mr N back in his normal job; the demand to dismiss the supervisor was rejected; and the demand for wages and benefits over the strike days was rejected [Para 194][Para 195][Para 196].

On issue 4/5, the panel set out Article 293, under which a staff delegate or a candidate may be dismissed only with the labour inspector's agreement, and Article 3 (Ka) of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, which protects candidates for 45 days before the ballot and, for those not elected, 45 days after it, and protects elected leaders until the union is properly registered [Para 154][Para 155][Para 156][Para 160]. It found that Mr N was elected on 19 September 2012, that the union notified the employer of the result the next day and the administration assistant received the letter, and that he was dismissed on 20 September 2012, inside the protection window [Para 163][Para 164][Para 168]. No authorisation was requested from or given by the labour inspector, so the dismissal was procedurally unlawful and reinstatement followed [Para 169][Para 170][Para 194]. The panel decided the point on that ground and did not need to establish an anti-union motive.

On issue 6, the panel held that the party making an allegation must prove it, and that no worker came forward to say she had heard the supervisor insult anyone or that the workload she set was unlawful [Para 173][Para 174][Para 178][Para 179]. The demand was rejected for want of evidence [Para 180].

On issue 10, the panel applied Article 332(1), under which the employment contract is suspended during a lawful strike and wages are not paid, and Article 334, which forbids the employer from using replacement workers and, where replacements are used, obliges it to pay the strikers' wages [Para 182][Para 183]. Following its Awards No. 27/10 (Hat Enterprise) and No. 178/12 (Decoro), it held that strike-day wages are payable only where replacement hiring is proved [Para 188]. It ordered the workers to file a list of the replacements used, and nothing had been filed by 19 October 2012, so the claim failed [Para 191][Para 192][Para 193].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker elected as a union leader is protected against dismissal from the date of the ballot, and a dismissal effected without the labour inspector's prior authorisation is unlawful whatever reason the employer gives, so the remedy is reinstatement in the normal job [Para 165][Para 170][Para 194]. Strike-day wages, by contrast, are payable only where the employer is shown to have hired replacements during the strike, the burden of that proof resting on the workers [Para 188][Para 193].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that Mr P, Mr K and Mr Y had withdrawn their reinstatement claims by the time of the hearing, Mr P having accepted the benefits paid to him, so only Mr N's case was decided on the merits [Para 107][Para 109].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is internally inconsistent on dates: the conciliation report is dated 27 September 2012 in one place and 28 September 2012 in another, the award states it was to issue on 24 October 2012 but carries 12 November 2012 and a two-week extension, and the strike is given as seven days without start and end dates [Para 17][Para 22][Para 27][Para 145]. The 13 demands are printed twice in overlapping lists with different numbering, so the mapping from demand to issue number cannot be checked. Several figures are doubled in the OCR — the wage of 10 dollars, the count of 45 workers electing the union — and the supervisor's name appears with and without its final consonant. The four workers' names are printed in more than one spelling, so they are given as initials here. [Para 130][Para 145][Para 154]
