# Ruling No. 127/12 — Tai Yien (Arbitration Council, 05 July 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Sin Kim Sean, worker-appointed; Pen Bunchhea, president) · Doc ID 140147

## 1. Facts
Tai Yien operates a factory in Bek Chan commune, Angsnuol district, Kandal province [Para 1]. The claimant was the Cambodia Trade Union Confederation, and the complaint of 20 June 2012 asked the company to improve a set of working conditions; conciliation on 25 June 2012 settled none of the five demands, which were forwarded by non-conciliation report 522/12 of 26 June 2012 [Para 13][Para 14][Para 15]. The five demands were payment of benefits on the change of the company's name, a $15 monthly travel and lodging allowance, a $15 monthly attendance-bonus increase, a $15 monthly milk allowance, and an end to deducting position and technical allowances whenever a worker or union leader is absent three days or more [Para 3][Para 4][Para 5][Para 6][Para 7]. The workers had struck since 25 June 2012 and continued after the case reached the Council [Para 16]. Order 017/12 of 29 June 2012 required the strike to be suspended at once and all strikers to return to their shifts from the afternoon of 29 June 2012 [Para 17]. They did not return [Para 18], and at the pre-hearing conference of 03 July 2012 their representatives said the strike would not end until the employer answered the demands [Para 19][Para 22]. No hearing was held and the panel found no facts on the demands [Para 29].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the panel should continue the arbitration when the workers kept striking, and kept failing to return to work, after order 017/12 required them to return? [Para 30][Para 37]

The five demands themselves — benefits on the change of company name, the $15 travel and lodging allowance, the $15 attendance-bonus increase, the $15 milk allowance and the ban on deducting position and technical allowances for absences of three days or more — were never reached, the panel finding no facts on them because no hearing was held [Para 3][Para 7][Para 29].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the strike would not end and the workers would not return until the employer responded to their demands; through their ten representatives they maintained all five claims as recorded in the non-conciliation report [Para 22][Para 3]. No documents were filed on their side and no witnesses were called [Para 25].

**Employer (respondent):** the company's general manager and a general manager's assistant attended, and the employer said it welcomed dialogue with the strikers but that the strikers had to return to work first [Para 1][Para 23]. It filed no documents either [Para 25].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** withdrawn — the panel ordered that the arbitration in case 127/12 not continue, leaving all five demands undecided and ordering nothing [Para 38][Para 39].
The panel framed its question as whether arbitration should continue at all [Para 30], and read Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 as requiring the parties to suspend every dispute action during the panel's proceedings — strike or lock-out under Article 318 of the Labour Law, or any other measure that could aggravate the dispute — and to attend every meeting to which the panel summons them [Para 30][Para 31].
It followed its earlier interpretation that arbitration will not continue while the worker party keeps striking [Para 32], citing notification 04/04 of 29 January 2004 and awards 28/06, 34/06, 110/11 and 59/12, in which files were closed or proceedings discontinued for the same reason [Para 33][Para 34][Para 35][Para 36].
On the facts, it found that the workers continued their strike throughout the proceedings and disobeyed order 017/12 of 29 June 2012, both by continuing to strike and by failing to return to work [Para 37]. Because no hearing took place it recorded that it had found no facts on the disputed issues and had received no documents from either party [Para 25][Para 29]. It therefore decided not to continue the arbitration [Para 38][Para 39]. No type of award clause and no eight-day objection period appear on the face of the ruling [Para 39].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the worker party keeps striking after the case has been referred and after the panel has ordered the strike suspended, the panel will not continue the arbitration: Article 20 of Prakas 099 obliges the parties to suspend strikes, lock-outs and any aggravating measure and to attend the panel's meetings, and continuing the strike removes the basis on which the panel proceeds [Para 30][Para 32][Para 37]. The consequence is procedural closure of the file rather than adjudication, so the demands stay undecided [Para 34][Para 38].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's statements that the employer welcomed dialogue but required a return to work first, and that the parties were asked at the pre-hearing conference to stay calm, are recorded context that played no part in the decision [Para 21][Para 23].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
No facts were found and no hearing held, so section 4 rests entirely on the procedural findings and the demands are described only as the ministry recorded them [Para 29]. The company's name is printed in two forms in the Khmer text (តៃ យៀន and តែ យៀន), and the file's own title renders it in Latin as Tai Yang [Para 1][Para 10]. Demand 5 is garbled in the original: it refers to position and technical allowances being used for payment whenever workers or four union leaders are absent three days or more, and the sense is reconstructed [Para 7]. Odd Latin fragments ("Shan", "Lea", "Vi", "The") are injected at the start of several sentences by OCR [Para 9][Para 18][Para 36][Para 38]. The ten worker representatives are listed by full name on the face of the ruling and are not reproduced here [Para 3].
