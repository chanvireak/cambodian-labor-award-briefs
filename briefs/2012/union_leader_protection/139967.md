# Award No. 39/12 — King Way Man Newfashion Garment Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 30 March 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (កៅ ថា ច, អន ណន, ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា chairing) · Doc ID 139967

## 1. Facts
King Way Man Newfashion Garment Co., Ltd. of Prek Treng village, Sitbo commune, Sa'ang district, Kandal, is a garment manufacturer employing 1,200 workers [Para 47]. The CCAWDU union was the claimant; it was not yet registered, and 25 workers had thumbprinted an authorisation for the federation to represent them [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50]. Of four disputed issues, issues 2 and 3 were settled in conciliation and issues 1 and 4 were combined into one: the demand that the employer take back Ms C, elected secretary of the CCAWDU union at the company, and pay her back wages from the date of termination until her return [Para 22][Para 51]. Ms C began work on 5 May 2011 as a sewing worker in the "phong" section on a two-month probationary contract averaging US$ 175.73 per month; the contract expired on 4 July 2011 and the employer did not renew it [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 54]. The union election was held on 11 June 2011 with 15 candidates, and the results were notified to the employer on 17 June 2011 [Para 56].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issues 1 and 4, combined) Whether the employer must take Ms C back into work and pay her back wages from the day it stopped her until the day she returns? [Para 51][Para 64]
2. Whether the employer's decision not to renew her probationary contract amounted to union discrimination? [Para 72][Para 81]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** After the union notified the election result on 17 June 2011 the company president began finding fault with Ms C and ended her probationary contract at once; her wage of US$ 175.73 a month shows she performed well, so the employer should not have ended the contract, and the real cause was union discrimination [Para 57][Para 58][Para 71][Para 82].
**Employer (respondent):** There was no discrimination at any time; the probation was not renewed because the group leader's and administration's evaluations rated her skills average but her conduct poor — she did not follow the employer's instructions during working hours and often argued with her supervisors, so she failed the probation assessment [Para 59][Para 60]. It admitted receiving the election notification but said it had already objected to the Labour Inspector because the successful candidates were ineligible, the union president not being on the company's worker list and the secretary being under the age required for union leadership [Para 61].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the demand that the employer take Ms C back and pay back wages from the end of her contract until her return; the award is binding, both parties having agreed on 15 March 2012 to accept a binding award on all issues [Para 91][Para 92].
The panel first stated the test: the employer may lawfully decline to renew a probationary contract where it considers that the worker cannot meet the conditions for serving it [Para 70]. It relied on Article 68 of the Labour Law, which caps a probationary contract at three months generally, two months for skilled workers and one month for ordinary workers, and on Article 82, which dispenses the parties from prior notice in the case of a probation or internship specified in the contract [Para 65][Para 66]. It followed Arbitral Award 27/03-Standard, where the panel held that an employer need not give reasons for ending a probationary contract, and agreed with that ruling [Para 67][Para 68].
On the facts, Ms C's two-month probation ran to 4 July 2011 and simply was not renewed at expiry, within the lawful maximum, so the employer was entitled to let it lapse [Para 69][Para 70].
On discrimination, the panel applied Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law, which forbid an employer to use union membership or union activity as a ground for hiring, disciplining or terminating a worker, and repeated that the workers bear the burden of proving discrimination [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74][Para 77]. Weighing the evidence, it found that the non-renewal flowed from the employer's dissatisfaction with Ms C's attitude — her arguments with supervisors and refusal to follow instructions — more than from any assessment of her skill, which was rated only average [Para 83][Para 84]. It also found it significant that the union vice-president and the other election candidates were still working for the company, and that no other union leader or candidate had been dismissed; that absence of a pattern showed there was no union discrimination [Para 85]. It added that a probationary period exists precisely so that both sides may observe and assess whether the relationship should continue, and the employer retains the right to decide not to renew at its expiry [Para 86][Para 87][Para 89]. The demand for reinstatement and back wages was therefore rejected [Para 90][Para 91].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that lets a probationary contract run to its stated expiry and declines to renew it exercises a right the Labour Law gives it, needs no prior notice under Article 82 and need give no reason, so the panel will not order reinstatement even where the worker had recently been elected a union officer [Para 67][Para 68][Para 89][Para 90].
That right is still bounded by Articles 12 and 279: where the workers prove that the decision not to renew was taken because of union membership or activity, the panel will intervene; a bare temporal link between an election notification and the decision, unsupported by any pattern of dismissing union leaders, does not discharge the workers' burden [Para 74][Para 77][Para 85].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that a probationary period serves both parties as an opportunity to observe and assess whether to continue the employment relationship, a rationale wider than the facts required [Para 86][Para 87].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The Khmer text is heavily degraded by OCR and by machine substitution: Latin fragments ("Live", "John", "maddement") replace Khmer words, and the worker's name is printed in at least five different spellings (ឆ្លូញ, ឆ្លួញ, ឆូញ, ឆ្អូញ, ឆ្មូញ), so it is rendered here as Ms C rather than settled [Para 13][Para 23][Para 51][Para 64][Para 91]. Dates are corrupted: the employer's postponement letter is dated "២៧ ខ្មែរ កុម្ភៈ ឆ្នាំ ២០១២" (27 with an intrusive word, February 2012) and the supervisor's letter is dated "១៩ ខែធ្នូ មីនា ឆ្នាំ ២០១២" (19 with two months, December and March, 2012) [Para 23][Para 32]. The union is called "សហជីពប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ" in the parties' section but "សម្ព័ន្ធសហជីពប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ" elsewhere, i.e. local union and federation are used interchangeably [Para 1][Para 48]. The panel writes at Para 86 that a probationary contract "is a period of one year", contradicting Article 68's maximum of three months set out at Para 65 — an internal contradiction in the reasons. The employer's objection letter is referred to both by number KW 2011/1001 and by the Labour Inspectorate route without a file number [Para 31][Para 61].
