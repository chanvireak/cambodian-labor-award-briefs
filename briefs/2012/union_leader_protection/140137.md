# Award No. 109/12 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្គ្រេត អន នើរ ថេកស្តាយ លោក ធើរី លីមីធីត (Arbitration Council, 25 June 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · ម៉ារ សំបូរ ណា, ទួន ស៊ី ផាន់, chair ញាន សុមុន្និន្ទ · Doc ID 140137

## 1. Facts
The employer is a Kandal province garment factory cutting, sewing, knitting, printing and finishing clothing, socks, gloves and towels [Para 28]. The Khmer Workers' Pride Union Federation filed a complaint on 2 April 2012 with the Kandal Provincial Labour Office seeking improved working conditions [Para 9]. Ministry conciliation on 11 May 2012 settled four of five demands, leaving one unresolved [Para 11]. The unresolved dispute was referred to the Arbitration Council by non-conciliation report No. 449/12 dated 31 May 2012, received by the secretariat on 6 June 2012 [Para 12]. The sole remaining claim was that the employer implement the Council's order in Case No. 40/12 dated 16 March 2012 [Para 3]. In that earlier case the panel had ordered the employer to notify workers of lot prices for each series after seven days of work [Para 37]. The employer objected to that order by letter of 20 March 2012, lodged with the secretariat on 21 March 2012; the secretariat notified the union president on 22 March 2012 [Para 38]. The employer's objection was validly made, rendering the Case No. 40/12 order non-binding [Para 31]. The hearing was held on 13 June 2012 at 08:30 a.m.; both parties attended, conciliation failed, and the parties chose a non-binding order [Para 17]. The same federation and company were parties in Case No. 40/12, and the union did not claim any worker in this case was outside the earlier case [Para 29]. No witnesses, experts or worker documents were submitted; the panel relied on the employer's documents (authorization letter, the prior order, company statutes, internal regulations) and ministry and secretariat documents [Para 18].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the panel should order the employer to implement point 2 of Case No. 40/12 dated 16 March 2012, requiring notice of lot prices series by series after seven days of work [Para 37]?
2. Whether the panel should decide again on a dispute already decided between the same parties, given the res judicata principle [Para 48]?
3. Whether the panel has power to compel a party to comply with a non-binding order that has been validly objected to [Para 46]?
4. Whether the three res judicata conditions — prior participation of the same parties, the same dispute previously brought, and a final decision on the merits — are met [Para 51]?

## 3. Positions of the parties
The union federation, as claimant, demanded that the employer implement the Case No. 40/12 order of 16 March 2012 [Para 35]. At the hearing the workers' side specified the demand as notice to workers of lot prices for each series after seven days of work, in line with that order [Para 33]. The federation did not claim that any worker in this case had been excluded from the earlier case [Para 30]. The employer refused to implement the order [Para 34]. Its position was that it had already objected to Case No. 40/12 in accordance with the law, so that order was not binding [Para 31]. It relied on Prakas No. 099 dated 21 April 2004, under which a party may object to a non-binding order within eight days of notice, and on the principle that an objected-to non-binding order cannot be enforced [Para 40]. The employer also invoked res judicata, arguing the same dispute between the same parties had already been decided [Para 48]. Both sides agreed to a non-binding order in the present case [Para 17].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider the workers' claim that the employer implement point 2 of Case No. 40/12 dated 16 March 2012, and ordered no payment in riel or USD [Para 59].

The panel's jurisdiction rested on Chapter 12, Part 2(b) (Articles 309–317) of the 1997 Labour Law, Prakas No. 099 dated 21 April 2004, its procedural rules, and Prakas No. 121 dated 7 June 2012 [Para 4]. It first asked whether it should decide the same dispute again, given that it had already done so on 16 March 2012 in Case No. 40/12 [Para 37]. Under Article 40 of Prakas No. 099, a party may object to a non-binding order within eight calendar days of notice, and a timely objection means the order cannot be implemented [Para 40]. The employer objected on 20 March 2012 and lodged the objection on 21 March 2012 [Para 38]. The parties in Case No. 40/12 had chosen a non-binding order, which they were entitled to object to [Para 45]. The panel therefore could not force the employer to implement an order to which it had validly objected [Para 47]. Separately, applying res judicata, the panel adopted the three conditions from Case No. 42/07: the same parties previously litigated the issue, the same dispute was previously brought, and the panel decided it on the merits in full [Para 51]. All three were met: the federation had raised the same lot-price demand against the same employer in Case No. 40/12, which the panel had decided in full by ordering notice of lot prices after seven days [Para 53]. The present demand was substantively the same as point 2 of Case No. 40/12, and the panel has no power to compel compliance with a non-binding order [Para 54]. No decision on the merits could assist the parties, so the panel declined to reconsider the dispute [Para 56] and dismissed the claim [Para 58]. The present order is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notice if no party objects in writing to the Minister [Para 60].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the same parties bring a dispute the panel has already decided on the merits in full, the panel may refuse to reconsider it, in order to avoid contradictory outcomes and to give the dispute a single final resolution [Para 51]. A non-binding order validly objected to under Article 40 of Prakas No. 099 within eight days of notice cannot be implemented, and the panel has no power to compel compliance with it [Para 40]. This panel's reasoning is not binding precedent. **Obiter dicta:** the panel referred to earlier res judicata reasoning in Case No. 81/11 and Case No. 42/07 [Para 50].
