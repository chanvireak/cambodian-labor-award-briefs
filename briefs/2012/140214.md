# Award No. 220/12 — Cambo Han Sam L.T.D. 3 (Arbitration Council, 12 December 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Taing Try, president) · Doc ID 140214

## 1. Facts
Cambo Han Sam L.T.D. 3 is a garment factory employing about 1,400 workers, registered in commerce in 2012 [Para 39][Para 40]. The Federation of Movement Worker Unions, registered in July 2012, filed a complaint on 26 September 2012 seeking improved working conditions; conciliation resolved 2 of 10 demands and the two unresolved matters were referred to the panel on 30 October 2012 [Para 12][Para 13][Para 29][Para 42]. Only the two unresolved demands were heard: the regular-attendance bonus (ប្រាក់រង្វាន់ធ្វើការទៀងទាត់) and the deduction of 1,000 riel monthly union dues from 254 members [Para 43][Para 52]. A further claim for damages of 1,000 riel per month per union member had been recorded in the non-conciliation report but was not pressed in argument [Para 5].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay the regular-attendance bonus in full for days a worker was absent on leave that was lawfully authorised, unpaid, by the employer, given that Notice No. 230 K.B/S.J.N of 25 July 2012 makes "without absence" a condition of the bonus? [Para 43][Para 65]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must deduct 1,000 riel per month in union dues from the wages of 254 workers said to be members of the Movement Worker Union? [Para 52][Para 103]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The bonus must be pro-rated to the number of days lawfully taken off without pay, following Awards 136/11, 154/11, 169/11, 171/11, 173/11 and 177/11; most such leave was taken to care for sick children or work the fields, and the employer's unilateral deductions would deter workers from taking lawful leave [Para 44][Para 45][Para 49]. On dues, 254 workers signed letters resigning the Khmer union, applying to join the Movement union and authorising the deductions, which were sent to the employer on 19 July 2012 [Para 53][Para 111].

**Employer (respondent):** Paying the bonus in full despite absence would make absence habitual and defeat the bonus's purpose of encouraging punctuality and productivity; the company's own practice, applied since September 2012, is already more generous than the law [Para 48][Para 50][Para 51]. On dues, of the 254 names some had left, some were duplicated, some had no attendance number in the company system (only 216 verified), and no resignation letters had been received from the Khmer union [Para 54][Para 55][Para 58].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issue 1 dismissed; on Issue 2 the panel refused the claim as pleaded but ordered the employer to deduct 1,000 riel per month from each worker still in service whose name and attendance number are correctly and specifically stated in the membership letters [Para 102][Para 114][Para 116].

On Issue 1 the panel read point 2 of Notice No. 230 (which replaced Notice No. 041) and found that the added words "without absence" express a condition precedent to the bonus: a worker must attend the full number of working days in the month, and leave granted by the employer does not count as performance [Para 69][Para 74][Para 77]. Because the notice is the source of the entitlement, the panel held it could not follow the earlier awards that had pro-rated the bonus for authorised unpaid leave, and it declined to accept the workers' premise [Para 72][Para 78]. Following Award 181/12 (New Orion), the panel treated the attendance bonus as a conditional grant that an employer may withhold where the condition is unmet [Para 79][Para 81]. It further held that a lawful-authorised absence and the payment of the bonus are separate matters: authorisation regularises the leave administratively, while the bonus depends on satisfying point 2 [Para 89][Para 94][Para 98]. The employer's actual practice — 9 USD for one day's absence, 7 USD where the second absent day was under four hours, and total forfeiture beyond that — was found to be better than the statutory minimum of 10 USD and therefore permissible [Para 99][Para 100].

On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 129 (new), paragraph 2, of the Labour Law: dues may be deducted only where the worker is a member and has authorised the deduction in writing, revocably at any time [Para 103][Para 104]. Following Award 74/11 (Fortune Million Footwear), written consent protects the worker's real intention and shields the employer from deducting against that intention [Para 106][Para 109]. Findings of fact: the union sent 254 names on 19 July 2012 with resignations and authorisations, but the employer produced no list identifying which were departed or duplicated at the evidence deadline, so the bare assertion of 216 verified names could not defeat the union's list as a whole [Para 111][Para 113].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted the general practice of respecting earlier awards unless new legal grounds or good reason justify departure, and expressly declined to read point 2 of Notice 230 as remaining ambiguous [Para 71][Para 76].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a bonus is created by ministerial notice and that notice conditions entitlement on attendance "without absence", a worker granted unpaid leave does not satisfy the condition, and the arbitrator must apply the notice as written rather than pro-rate the bonus as earlier awards did; while union dues may be deducted only from workers still in service whose identity is specifically verified and who have given written, revocable authorisation [Para 77][Para 114].

The award is a scan with heavy OCR damage: stray Latin fragments and clipped sentences interrupt the reasons (for example at [Para 68], [Para 86] and [Para 96]), and one reasoning paragraph is reduced to a stray "ល ។" [Para 86]. The case number and the company name appear in two spellings (Cambo Han Sam / Khembo Han Sam Sam) and the company's commercial registration is printed as "Inv.1086 E/2006" dated 1 June 2012 while a company statute is dated September 2010 [Para 39][Para 21][Para 41].
