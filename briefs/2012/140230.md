# Award No. 250/12 — Global A Parallel Ltd (Arbitration Council, 25 January 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy; Oun Non; Pen Bunhea presiding) · Doc ID 140230

## 1. Facts
Global A Parallel Ltd is a garment manufacturer in Phnom Penh employing 2,223 workers [Para 40][Para 41]. The claimant is the Workers' Development Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព អភិវឌ្ឍន៍ កម្មករ), which has no factory-level local at the company; it holds written transfer-of-rights letters from only 42 workers [Para 42][Para 43][Para 86]. Fourteen demands were conciliated, six of them unresolved [Para 21][Para 22]; at the hearing four of those six were settled, leaving issues 3 and 6 [Para 25][Para 26]. The award was issued on 25 January 2013 and the parties chose a non-binding award [Para 29].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay the 2,000 riel daily productivity incentive bonus to workers who meet their work target within 8 hours, rather than requiring 10 hours of work? [Para 44][Para 55]
2. (Issue 6) Whether the employer must issue payslips one day before payday so that workers can check their wages in advance? [Para 104]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The company had paid the 2,000 riel incentive for 8 hours of work since 2003, a practice running for eight years and nine months, and then changed the rule in September 2012 so that no bonus was paid for 8 hours even when the target was met; at the hearing they reduced the claim to 1,000 riel [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54][Para 55]. Payslips in advance would let them challenge errors before payday instead of waiting for a late adjustment [Para 77][Para 79].
**Employer (respondent):** The bonus requires a full 10 hours of work, not 8 [Para 46][Para 90]. The company has never issued payslips before payday; it pays on the 7th, allows complaints from the 7th to the 15th, and repays by the 21st or 22nd once accounting has re-checked [Para 73][Para 74][Para 75][Para 81].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim for a 1,000 riel bonus at 8 hours was refused, and the employer was ordered to continue paying the 2,000 riel incentive to workers who meet the target within 8 hours [Para 126][Para 127]; the payslip claim was refused [Para 128]; and the employer was ordered to settle miscalculated wages within a reasonable period [Para 129].

The panel first fixed who it could hear. Under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 a party may be represented by another person only with written authorisation, so with a local union absent and mandates from 42 workers, the federation could represent those 42 alone [Para 83][Para 84][Para 86][Para 87].

On issue 3 the panel ordered the employer to produce written evidence or witnesses for its claim that 8 hours never earned the bonus; none was produced by the deadline [Para 59][Para 60]. It inferred that the 2,000 riel paid for 8 hours was company money, not a foreman's private gift as the employer had suggested through its team leaders [Para 58][Para 61]. The 2008 productivity-bonus guideline produced by the employer sets targets by group grade but says nothing in writing about the 2,000 riel practice [Para 63][Para 69][Para 70]. Article 2 of the Labour Law gives the employer the right to manage, but only through measures that are lawful and reasonable [Para 92][Para 95][Para 97]; a sudden change with no stated reason, affecting workers' earnings and potentially the production chain, is not reasonable [Para 98][Para 100][Para 101]. The panel therefore refused the workers' own revised figure, since setting a new bonus level is itself an exercise of the employer's prerogative, and instead ordered continuation of the existing 2,000 riel payment for 8-hour target days [Para 102][Para 103].

On issue 6, Article 112(b) requires the employer to make wages clear and easy to understand whenever they change, but it fixes no time for issuing a payslip, and no other article requires one before payday [Para 106][Para 108][Para 109][Para 110]. The claim was therefore an interest dispute [Para 111], and an interest award replaces the collective agreement for a year and binds all workers, so only a most-representative union may bring one; this federation had no local and no certificate of representative status [Para 113][Para 115][Para 118][Para 119][Para 120]. The panel nevertheless found that a repayment lag of up to two weeks affects workers' livelihoods after they have already performed the work, and ordered settlement of miscalculated wages within a reasonable period [Para 121][Para 124][Para 125].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A bonus paid consistently over years, and proved by the employer's own admissions rather than by any written scheme, forms part of the terms of employment; the employer's prerogative to change it extends only to changes that are lawful and reasonable, so an unexplained cut that removes the benefit will not be upheld. A federation holding written mandates from named workers may represent those workers only. [Para 61][Para 87][Para 101]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that the workers' revised demand for 1,000 riel would itself have altered the employer's bonus policy, which the employer is entitled to set [Para 102]. The award is non-binding and becomes final if neither party objects within eight days [Para 130].
