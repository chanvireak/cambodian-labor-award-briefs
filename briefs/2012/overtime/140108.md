# Award No. 167/11 — Hong Va (Cambodia) Garment Manufacturing Ltd (Arbitration Council, 13 January 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Uok Ry, employer-appointed; An Virak, worker-appointed; Aing Eng Thong, chair) · Doc ID 140108

## 1. Facts
The Democratic Development Union at Hong Va (Cambodia) Garment Manufacturing Ltd, a garment plant operating three sites known as Hong Va 1, 2 and 3 under a single commercial registration, filed a claim on 18 November 2011 that overtime pay be issued twice a month instead of once; conciliation on 1 December 2011 failed and the single point came to the Council under non-conciliation report No. 1386 of 9 December 2011. [Para 1][Para 6][Para 8][Para 58]

Hong Va 3, the newest building, opened in June 2010 and employs about 850 workers, of whom roughly 401 belong to the claimant union; the employer has paid overtime only once a month, together with the monthly wage, since that building opened, while Hong Va 1 pays overtime twice a month and Hong Va 2 once. A collective agreement of 2009 applying to all three sites provides at clause 2 that monthly wages are to be paid once a month no later than the 7th of the following month. [Para 62][Para 64][Para 67][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70]

The union relied on a minute of 22 June 2011 recording that Hong Va 3 would apply the same conditions as Hong Va 1, but that minute in fact records only an agreement on "sy bong" sewing and on extending the "bong" price period from one week to ten days. The employer challenged 82 of the 401 signatories as no longer employed or not employed at Hong Va; the union asserted the discrepancies were spelling errors but named none, so the panel counted only 319 claimants. [Para 77][Para 78][Para 85][Para 88][Para 91][Para 97]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the demand that overtime be paid twice a month is a dispute over rights or over interests? [Para 92][Para 95]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to pay overtime twice a month to the 319 claimant workers at Hong Va 3? [Para 99][Para 128]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** workers are poor, some borrow at interest to cover the month, and paying twice a month would ease that burden; Hong Va 1 already pays twice a month although the three sites belong to one employer, and the employer had promised in a minute that Hong Va 3 would apply Hong Va 1's conditions, so all should be treated alike. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 75][Para 76][Para 79]

**Employer (respondent):** paying twice a month is a heavy burden, the company lacks staff to arrange it, and its accounting system is set to pay wages and overtime together, so it cannot pay overtime separately on request; the double payment at Hong Va 1 was a discretionary incentive to attract overtime work, which the company does not wish to extend to the other buildings. [Para 3][Para 80][Para 81][Para 82]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay overtime twice a month to the 319 workers identified as claimants at Hong Va 3. [Para 128][Para 129]

The panel first classified the demand. Following its earlier awards, it held a rights dispute to be one concerning an entitlement set out in a law, agreement or collective convention, and found that this demand turned on the manner in which wages are paid under the Labour Law, so it was a rights dispute, decided with binding effect because the parties had chosen a binding award. [Para 92][Para 93][Para 95][Para 18]

Article 116 requires wages to be paid at least twice a month with no more than sixteen days between payments, and Circular No. 1903 of 24 November 2008 repeats that employers must pay twice a month at intervals of at most sixteen days; the panel read this as an obligation owed to each worker, not a target. Article 103 lists overtime pay among the constituents of wages, so overtime is part of the wage and must follow the same twice-monthly cycle. [Para 99][Para 101][Para 104][Para 105][Para 106]

The employer's reliance on clause 2 of the collective agreement did not assist it: Article 13 makes the Labour Law's provisions mandatory unless it expressly provides otherwise, and any rule created unilaterally or by agreement that falls below the law is void. Clause 2, allowing one payment a month, therefore gave the workers less than the statutory minimum and could not be applied. [Para 120][Para 121][Para 122][Para 123]

On the evidence the panel found that Hong Va 1's practice of paying twice a month conformed to Article 116, and that the employer's explanation—wanting to encourage overtime attendance and to prevent workers holding back piecework on ordinary days to be paid on Sundays—was an administrative convenience that could not justify departing from the statute; it also noted the company had offered no evidence beyond its own statements. [Para 125][Para 126][Para 127]

Because the union could not identify which of the challenged names were misspelled, the panel declined to decide for the full 401 and confined the order to the 319 it could verify, a point it made twice in the reasoning and once in the operative order. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 128]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Overtime pay is part of wages under Article 103, so it must be paid on the twice-monthly cycle Article 116 imposes, at intervals of no more than sixteen days; a collective agreement clause permitting monthly payment falls below that mandatory minimum, is void under Article 13, and cannot be relied on by an employer, nor can administrative convenience justify the shortfall. [Para 104][Para 106][Para 116][Para 122]

**Obiter dicta:** that a difference in payment practice between buildings of a single registered employer is a matter of equal treatment rather than of law, and that the panel would in principle treat identically situated workers alike. [Para 75][Para 76]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The header dates the award 13 January 2012, but the reasons record that the parties agreed at the hearing to postpone the award to "13 December 2012", and the signature block is largely destroyed, leaving only fragments ("powns", "<unk>") where the chair's signature should be and "Amvice Leal-2" beside the worker-appointed arbitrator. The award names the 2009 collective agreement as dated 31 January 2009 in the reasons but 4 February 2009 in the evidence list; the employer is printed as "Hong Va (Cambodia) Garment Manufacturing Ltd" in the header and in several variant spellings in the annexes, and the site is written "Hong Va 3" and "Hong Va ៣" interchangeably. The two annexed name lists, which the outcome depends on, are OCR-corrupted to the point that many rows carry broken letters and truncated employee numbers, and the file's last pages repeat fragments of the reasons. Individual names in those lists and in the signature block are reduced here to roles or initials. [Para 1][Para 19][Para 129][Para 24][Para 69][Para 130][Para 134][Para 58]
