# Award No. 57/12 — Cambodia Airport Management Services (Siem Reap International Airport) (Arbitration Council, 19 April 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sophanna, employer-appointed; Tuon Siphan, worker-appointed; Pen Bunchhea, chair) · Doc ID 139987

## 1. Facts
The Tourism and Service Workers Federation of Cambodia filed a claim on behalf of employees of Cambodia Airport Management Services (CAMS), which operates Siem Reap International Airport and employs 453 staff. Conciliation at the Ministry of Labour failed on 9 demands; 5 were settled and 2 more were agreed to need no award, leaving issues 2, 5, 6 and 9 for the panel. The disputed acts are the employer's 2011 termination of its free-treatment agreement with Ly Srey Vina clinic, its refusal to grant a compensatory day off when a public holiday falls on a non-Sunday weekly rest day, its use of outsourced contract labour, and its non-renewal of a fixed-term worker's contract. [Para 1][Para 20][Para 26][Para 99]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to continue providing free medical treatment through Ly Srey Vina clinic to workers and their families? [Para 154]
2. Whether the employer must grant one additional day of rest when a paid public holiday falls on a worker's weekly rest day that is not Sunday, and whether workers are owed compensation for the three years the employer failed to do so? [Para 170][Para 188]
3. Whether the employer must stop hiring contract staff from outside companies such as Quarry? [Para 124][Para 200]
4. Whether the employer must take back Mr H, an active union member whose fixed-term contract was not renewed? [Para 139][Para 220]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Ly Srey Vina was hygienic, professional and preferred by most staff, and the employer — a profitable company — should restore it or provide comparable private clinics; a public holiday colliding with a non-Sunday rest day must attract a replacement day, with three years' arrears; outsourcing to Quarry pays substandard wages and denies job security; and Mr H's non-renewal was union discrimination, since other workers' contracts were renewed. [Para 106][Para 116][Para 130][Para 152]

**Employer (respondent):** Choosing and changing health providers is its managerial prerogative and it still funds free treatment at state-recognised hospitals and clinics; weekly rest is fixed on Sunday, so no replacement day is owed; contracting out is a lawful management right under Article 2 of the Labour Law and clause 5 of the collective agreement; and Mr H's contract simply expired, with non-renewal justified by his filling in leave forms for others without authorisation. [Para 109][Para 117][Para 135][Para 146]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to give one additional day of rest whenever a paid public holiday falls on a worker's non-Sunday weekly rest day, but dismissed the claim for three years' arrears and dismissed the claims on the clinic, on outsourcing and on reinstatement. [Para 242][Para 243][Para 244]

On issue 2, the panel held that an employer's right to manage under Article 2 of the Labour Law covers the choice of health provider, and that the 2011 ending of the Ly Srey Vina agreement was lawful and proper; because the employer continued free treatment at other recognised hospitals and clinics, there was no breach and the demand was refused. [Para 167][Para 169]

On issue 5, the panel read Articles 147 and 148 of the Labour Law together: weekly rest is normally Sunday but may be scheduled on another day where the enterprise cannot release all staff at once. It followed the wording of Article 162 and Article 2 of the 2012 sub-decree on the annual holiday calendar, and held that a public holiday falling on a worker's non-Sunday rest day must still attract one compensatory day, as for Sunday. It therefore ordered the extra day prospectively. The arrears claim failed on evidence: the workers filed their supporting documents on 30 March 2012, after the 27 March deadline, and the panel declined to consider late evidence that the employer had no chance to rebut; the documents covered only 2009 anyway. [Para 178][Para 184][Para 187][Para 191][Para 193][Para 196]

On issue 6, the panel reasoned that the outsourcing contract between CAMS and Quarry was a contract formed by the will of those two parties, and that the workers were third parties who could not alter or dissolve it; overtime is voluntary and no provision obliges the employer to supply it. Contracting out for ancillary or short-notice services was within the employer's right to organise operations and did not touch the core rights of existing staff. [Para 209][Para 210][Para 212][Para 217]

On issue 9, the panel held that a fixed-term contract ends automatically at its expiry under Article 73; Mr H's last contract ran to 31 January 2012 and he was given 18 days' notice of non-renewal. Renewal is a matter for the parties, and the employer may decline it unless it acts on discriminatory grounds. The panel found the workers had not discharged the burden of proving union discrimination, since Mr H's conduct in helping colleagues gave no reason for animosity and no evidence of union activity was produced. [Para 221][Para 225][Para 227][Para 240]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a paid public holiday falls on a worker's weekly rest day, and that rest day is scheduled on a non-Sunday day under Article 148, the employer must still give one compensatory day of rest; but arrears for past failures are recoverable only on specific evidence of the days and sums claimed, filed within the panel's deadline. [Para 184][Para 196]

**Obiter dicta:** That overtime is voluntary and the employer is never obliged to offer it; and that a third party to a commercial contract cannot vary or dissolve it. [Para 212][Para 210]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The case number appears as "57/12" in the header while the file is styled a 2012 award; the OCR repeatedly renders the employer as "A Cambodia" and "Khmer", and the contractor as both "Quarry" and "Khu Ri". Dates are garbled in places ("30 December March 2012", "3 years 201 of the last"), and the sub-decree is dated both 2011 and "2019 2012".
