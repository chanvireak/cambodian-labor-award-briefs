# Award No. 160/12 — Khembo Yun Fung Garment (Best One) (Arbitration Council, 14 September 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambona for the employer, An Virak for the workers, Kong Phallak presiding) · Doc ID 140167

## 1. Facts

Khembo Yun Fung Garment (formerly Best One), a garment company registered on 10 July 2012 and employing 300 workers, faced a collective dispute lodged by the CCAWDU union, itself registered on 12 July 2012 [Para 54][Para 56]. Conciliation on 7 August 2012 left 2 of 13 demands unresolved, and at the hearing of 27 August 2012 the parties settled one of them — Issue 2, on conversion of fixed-term contracts — leaving only Issue 1 for adjudication [Para 13][Para 17][Para 18]. That issue concerned Ms B, a sewer in Team 1 earning between 80 and 120 US dollars a month, who had joined on 3 November 2011 and was also secretary of the factory union [Para 57][Para 59][Para 61]. She was removed from the factory on 4 August 2012 after a dispute with the factory director; the employer treated the removal as the non-renewal of an expired three-month fixed-term contract [Para 3][Para 79][Para 80].

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must reinstate the union secretary it removed from work on 4 August 2012, given that her last fixed-term contract had expired on 3 August 2012? [Para 57][Para 103][Para 124]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** Ms B said she had an oral contract from 3 November 2011 and that the employer stopped her on 4 August 2012 without prior notice because it was dissatisfied with her [Para 57][Para 58]. She denied signing or thumbprinting any of the three written contracts produced by the employer, denied the fingerprints were hers, and said she was the only one of 24 workers in her team to be dismissed [Para 60][Para 64][Para 68].

**Employer (respondent):** The employer produced three original fixed-term contracts of three months each, all thumbprinted, and said it simply declined to renew the last one, which expired on 3 August 2012 [Para 63][Para 64][Para 66][Para 3]. It said it had dismissed her for disrespecting the factory director — slapping a desk on 30 July 2012 hard enough to switch off a computer — in breach of clauses 3 and 9 of the contract, and that she refused to apologise [Para 70][Para 83][Para 84][Para 85]. It conceded it had not notified the labour inspector [Para 87].

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to take Ms B back into work [Para 124].

The panel first had to characterise the contract. It held it had no competence to examine whether the fingerprints on the three originals were genuine or forged, and that under prior awards the party making an assertion bears the burden of proof [Para 91][Para 94][Para 95]. Because the employer produced three original contracts and the worker offered nothing beyond a denial, the panel presumed the three fixed-term contracts valid [Para 96][Para 97][Para 98].

The contract nonetheless expired on 3 August 2012, and the employer let Ms B work again on 4 August — the day after expiry — and called her in again that day to be told her contract would not be renewed [Para 103][Para 105][Para 106]. Under Article 73(1) a fixed-term contract ends at its term, but performance continuing after expiry amounts to tacit renewal for a term equal to the original, so her contract ran automatically to 3 February 2013 [Para 102][Para 107][Para 108]. The panel therefore recharacterised the 4 August act: it was not a non-renewal at expiry but a termination before the term ended, that is a dismissal [Para 109][Para 110].

That dismissal engaged Article 293 of the Labour Law, under which a staff delegate or candidate may be dismissed only with the labour inspector's consent, and Article 4(2) of Prakas No. 305 on the protection of three union leaders per enterprise [Para 112][Para 113]. The panel found Ms B was one of those three protected leaders and that the employer never sought the inspector's authorisation [Para 114][Para 118]. Following its earlier awards on the same point, it held the dismissal procedurally unlawful and, under Article 4(4) of the Prakas, void [Para 116][Para 119][Para 121][Para 122].

## 5. Ratio decidendi

Where an employer permits a worker to keep working after the stated expiry date of a fixed-term contract, the contract is renewed tacitly for a term equal to the original, so a later removal during that renewed term is a dismissal and not a lawful non-renewal; and where the worker is a protected union leader under Article 293 of the Labour Law, a dismissal effected without the labour inspector's prior authorisation is void and the worker must be reinstated. [Para 107][Para 112][Para 122]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed, as a general matter of contracting practice, that employers normally keep every worker's contract on file and should hand one copy to the worker at each signing, to avoid disputes of this kind [Para 99][Para 100]. This duty was not the basis of the order.
