# Award No. 146/12 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន វីង ស្តារ ស៊ូ (Ving Star Shoes) (Arbitration Council, 29 August 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-selected), Liv Sovanna (worker-selected), Pen Bun Chhea (president) · Doc ID 140159

## 1. Facts
The employer, ក្រុមហ៊ុន វីង ស្តារ ស៊ូ (Ving Star Shoes), is a shoe and garment factory at Kong Pisey, Kampong Speu, operating from February 2012. [Para 51] The conciliation report recorded about 1,650 workers; at the hearing the employer said recruitment had raised the workforce to roughly 4,000. [Para 52][Para 53] Three factory unions — សហជីព ការងារ ដើម្បី កម្ពុជា, សហជីព ស្មារតី កម្មករ ខ្មែរ and សហជីព សម្លេង យុវជន ខ្មែរ — brought the dispute jointly and asked សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ការងារ ដើម្បី កម្ពុជា to file for them, but the panel found no valid written transfer of representation and treated the three unions themselves as the worker party. [Para 23][Para 25] None of the three is the most representative union; that status belongs to សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា រោងចក្រ ស្បែកជើង វីង ស្តារ, certified on 19 July 2012. [Para 56][Para 57] The complaint was lodged on 28 June 2012 and conciliated on 6 July 2012, when 11 points were settled and nine were not; report 1022/12 of 20 July reached the Secretariat on 24 July. [Para 16][Para 18] Both parties attended the hearing of 3 August 2012, where one more point was settled, leaving eight: Issues 6, 9, 12, 13, 15, 16, 20 and 22. [Para 26][Para 27] The award issued on 29 August 2012, the date having been extended by agreement. [Para 28]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 6) Whether the employer must provide one free mid-day meal, or 4,000 riel in allowance, to every worker? [Para 58]
2. (Issue 9) Whether the employer must raise the basic wage of full-status workers from 66 to 85 US dollars a month? [Para 68]
3. (Issue 12) Whether the employer must preserve wages, the attendance bonus and the transport allowance during sick leave of more than three days supported by a recognised doctor's certificate? [Para 73]
4. (Issue 13) Whether the employer must pay the overtime meal allowance once a week rather than once a month? [Para 83]
5. (Issue 15) Whether the employer must raise the attendance bonus to 13 dollars a month from the date Notice 230/12 takes effect? [Para 95]
6. (Issue 16) Whether the employer must pay 1,500 riel an hour in meal allowance for overtime from 6 p.m., and provide transport home? [Para 103]
7. (Issue 20) Whether the employer must pay a 10-dollar monthly cooperation allowance to each worker who accepts its overtime requests? [Para 110]
8. (Issue 22) Whether the employer must pay a skills allowance of 5 dollars per machine, up to 15 dollars for three machines? [Para 112]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers:** living standards are low and the basic wage small, so a free meal or 4,000 riel is needed; the factory is remote, with few sellers charging 4,000 to 5,000 riel; 85 dollars is needed to support a family; wages and bonuses should continue during certified sick leave, since the employer deducts them even where a certificate exists; the overtime meal allowance should be weekly as promised; the attendance bonus should rise to 13 dollars; overtime meal money should be 1,500 riel an hour after 6 p.m. with transport home, because the site is isolated and 8 p.m. is night; and cooperation and per-machine skills allowances would reward effort. [Para 60][Para 62][Para 70][Para 76][Para 84][Para 106][Para 113]
**Employer:** it cannot meet the meal demand but is building a canteen for food sellers; it pays 66 dollars to full-status workers, above the 61 dollars set by Notice 049/10, and cannot pay 85; it preserves wages and bonuses for three days of certified sick leave only; it has no internal regulations; it cannot pay overtime meal money weekly for 4,000 workers for lack of staff and computer systems; it already pays a 10-dollar monthly transport allowance and 7,000 riel to each driver who waits, so no vehicle is required; it pays 8 dollars' attendance bonus, 10 dollars from July 2012; and it has no per-machine skills scheme. [Para 66][Para 74][Para 82][Para 93][Para 96][Para 108][Para 114]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — seven of the eight demands were refused without consideration on the merits, and on Issue 13 the panel ordered the employer to pay the overtime meal allowance once a week. [Para 214][Para 219]
The panel held Issues 6, 9, 15, 16, 20 and 22 to be interest disputes, because no provision of the Labour Law, no collective agreement and no agreement between the parties fixed the benefit claimed, and the claims exceeded what the law and the Ministry's notices required. [Para 116][Para 131][Para 149][Para 186][Para 207][Para 211] Applying Article 43 of Prakas 099 and its own jurisprudence, it held that on an interest dispute only the most representative union may seize it, and that the three claimant unions together were not the most representative in the factory. [Para 119][Para 121][Para 126] It therefore refused those demands without reaching their merits. [Para 127][Para 181][Para 209]
On Issue 12 it added that Articles 71(3) and 72(1) suspend the contract for certified illness and with it the duty to pay wages, that the attendance bonus counts as part of wages, and that neither it nor the transport allowance is owed for sick days. [Para 139][Para 143][Para 151] On Issue 15 the claim concerned a bonus payable after Notice 230/12 took effect, a future dispute the Council cannot decide. [Para 180] On Issue 16 it held that under Article 144 and Prakas 80 of 1999 night runs from 22:00 to 05:00, that overtime here ends at 7 p.m., and that the transport duty in Article 6 of Prakas 80 therefore never arises; the voluntary 10-dollar allowance is better than the law requires. [Para 196][Para 203][Para 205]
On Issue 13 the panel held that Notice 041/11 entitles a worker who volunteers for overtime to 2,000 riel a day in meal money or a free meal but fixes no time for payment; because the money exists so the worker can buy food on the overtime day, payment must come at an interval that makes that possible, and weekly payment is reasonable and necessary. [Para 153][Para 157][Para 168] It ordered weekly payment accordingly. [Para 169]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that exceeds what the Labour Law, a Prakas or a collective agreement already secures is an interest dispute, and only the union most representative in the enterprise — or unions whose combined membership exceeds half the workforce — may bring it to arbitration; minority unions, even acting jointly, cannot. [Para 124][Para 126] The statutory overtime meal allowance must be paid often enough that the worker can buy food on the overtime day, so weekly payment is required even though the Prakas fixes no period.

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that certified sick leave suspends the wage obligation under Articles 71(3) and 72(1), that night begins at 22:00, and that transport is owed only where work ends between 22:00 and 05:00, were unnecessary to the outcome. [Para 139][Para 203]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Seven of the eight demands were never decided on their merits, so the substance of those claims is left unresolved and only Issue 13 produced an operative order. [Para 215][Para 224] The reasoning treats Issues 6 and 9 as barred for lack of representativity yet gives substantive reasons on Issues 12, 15 and 16, yielding two inconsistent grounds for refusal. [Para 126][Para 139][Para 181] The workforce figure shifts from 1,650 to about 4,000 across the record. [Para 52][Para 53] The employer's admission that it has no internal regulations is recorded but never acted on. [Para 82] Company, union and federation names vary between paragraphs and are partly corrupted by Latin character noise, and the operative section repeats the same refusal. [Para 125][Para 216] The award is dated 29 August 2012 although the reasons record a due date of 15 August. [Para 1][Para 28] Workers, union officers and the employer's representatives are named individually in the source and are identified here by role only. [Para 2]
