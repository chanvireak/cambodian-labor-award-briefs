# Award No. 179/12 — SL Garment Processing (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 28 September 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ung Sothi for the employer, Liv Sovanna for the workers, Pen Bunhea presiding) · Doc ID 140179

## 1. Facts
The respondent, SL Garment Processing (Cambodia), is a laundry and garment-processing company operating since 1997 with about 4,500 workers; the claimant is the Cambodia Workers' Union Federation and the SL factory workers' union, which is the enterprise's own union. [Para 1][Para 55][Para 56] The enterprise union was registered on 30 December 2005 and its third-term leadership was recognised on 18 July 2012. [Para 47][Para 58] The federation behind the claim had 455 members and was not the most representative organisation in the plant. [Para 59] The union filed 11 demands on 6 August 2012; conciliation on 29 August produced five unresolved points, of which the union then withdrew Issues 2 and 5, leaving Issues 1, 3 and 4. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 24][Para 25] A rights/benefits split was applied because the parties had signed the 28 September 2010 Memorandum of Understanding on industrial relations. [Para 26]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether newly recruited night-shift workers are entitled to 200 per cent of the day wage, as the union demands, or only the 130 per cent fixed by the amended Article 144? [Para 82]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay a health-care allowance of USD 10 per worker per month where no law, collective agreement or practice establishes that amount? [Para 98]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must pay 3,000 riel meal money for Sunday overtime in addition to the free meal it already provides? [Para 112]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** old night-shift workers already receive 200 per cent, so paying new recruits 130 per cent is unequal treatment of workers doing identical night work, which is also damaging to health; they sought equalisation rather than a legal ruling. [Para 60][Para 62][Para 63] On health they argued exposure to chemicals justified USD 10 monthly, and on meals that Sunday overtime deserved recognition beyond the free meal. [Para 68][Para 72]
**Employer (respondent):** it applies Article 144 as amended — workers hired before the amendment keep 200 per cent while those hired after receive 130 per cent — and cannot meet the other demands because of its budget. [Para 64][Para 5][Para 73] On health it accepted that chemical exposure exists but said protective equipment is supplied, and it already gives a free meal for all overtime including Sundays. [Para 69][Para 114]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three claims were refused; the Issue 1 ruling is binding at once under the 2010 Memorandum of Understanding, while the Issue 3 and 4 rulings become final after eight days if no written objection is filed with the Minister. [Para 120][Para 121][Para 124]
The panel first separated rights disputes, decided according to law, from interest disputes, decided in equity, applying Articles 312 and 96 and Prakas 099, and held that only a most-representative union may bring an interest dispute. [Para 75][Para 80][Para 81][Para 107]
On Issue 1 the panel held night-shift pay is governed by Article 144 as amended and Prakas 185, which fix 130 per cent for night work between 22:00 and 05:00, making the demand a rights dispute. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 85] It then applied res judicata: the same parties had litigated the same issue in Award 119/09-SL of 25 September 2009, that award decided it fully, and no new evidence was produced, so the panel declined to reconsider the 200 per cent demand. [Para 86][Para 95][Para 96][Para 97] The panel noted it retains discretion to reopen where genuinely new evidence is accepted, but found none here. [Para 93]
On Issue 3 the panel found no law, collective agreement or agreement fixing USD 10, so the claim was an interest dispute; since the claiming union was not most representative in the enterprise, the panel declined to consider it. [Para 98][Para 99][Para 109][Para 110][Para 111]
On Issue 4 the panel applied point 2 of Prakas 041/11, which grants either 2,000 riel or a free meal for voluntary overtime; the employer chose the free meal, which the panel held already satisfied the rule, and the 3,000 riel demand therefore went beyond the legal standard and was also an interest claim. [Para 113][Para 115][Para 116][Para 119][Para 123]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the same parties bring the same demand that a previous award has already decided on the merits, the panel will decline to reconsider it unless genuinely new evidence is produced. [Para 87][Para 90][Para 93][Para 97] A demand for an amount that no law, collective agreement or practice fixes is an interest dispute, which the panel will not entertain unless it is brought by a union holding most-representative status in the enterprise. [Para 81][Para 107][Para 110]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that an interest award becomes a collective agreement binding all workers in the company for one year, which would deprive non-members of the right to strike over the same interest demand. [Para 105][Para 106]
