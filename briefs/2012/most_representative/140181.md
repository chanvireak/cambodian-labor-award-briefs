# Award No. 181/12 — New Orion (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 October 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ri for the employer, Liv Sovanna for the workers, Kong Phalla presiding) · Doc ID 140181

## 1. Facts
New Orion (Cambodia) Garment is a shoe and garment factory with about 1,500 workers, employing three unions; the claimant is the Cambodia Workers' Union Federation and its enterprise union, registered since 2002 and not the most representative in the plant. [Para 69][Para 72][Para 74][Para 76] The federation held written authority from the 39 workers it represented. [Para 3][Para 73] Of 11 non-conciliated demands, Issues 5 and 10(1) were settled at the hearing and the union later merged Issues 8 and 9, leaving Issues 1–4, 6, 7, 8–9, 10(2) and 11 for decision under the rights/benefits split required by the 28 September 2010 Memorandum of Understanding. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 28][Para 29]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must raise the travel and housing allowance from the USD 9 it pays to the USD 15 demanded? [Para 157]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the regular attendance bonus must be raised from the USD 13 actually paid to the USD 15 demanded? [Para 174]
3. (Issue 3) Whether a worker who takes personal leave authorised by the employer keeps the attendance bonus pro rata for the days absent? [Para 180]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must pay 2,000 riel meal money for work performed on public holidays? [Para 197]
5. (Issue 6) Whether wages and the attendance bonus must be preserved for sick leave certified by any state-licensed private hospital, not only the two the employer recognises? [Para 204]
6. (Issue 7) Whether the employer may require a village or commune chief's letter before a worker in year two or later uses annual leave? [Para 223]
7. (Issues 8 and 9) Whether cutting the attendance bonus of the union secretary amounted to anti-union discrimination, and whether the employer may withdraw a bonus it had been paying? [Para 240][Para 242]
8. (Issue 10, part 2) Whether a worker who is 45 minutes late in a month keeps the full attendance bonus? [Para 253]
9. (Issue 11) Whether the employer may warn a worker whose punch failed but whose supervisor signed for attendance? [Para 261]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** transport and rent costs have risen so the allowance must rise; the bonus should follow the general wage increase; authorised personal leave is not absence; holidays are rest days, so work then is overtime deserving meal money; leave in year two should not need a village chief's letter because obtaining one is difficult; and the bonus cut followed the secretary's union candidacy. [Para 77][Para 80][Para 102][Para 110][Para 125][Para 197]
**Employer (respondent):** it cannot afford the increases; holiday work is not overtime because the shift is only eight hours and meal money is paid only beyond that; sick leave is paid on certificates from two named private hospitals, which exceeds the law; and the bonus cut followed a supervisor scoring exercise required by a buyer, in which the secretary ranked 11th and about 100 workers lost the bonus. [Para 79][Para 85][Para 93][Para 94][Para 119][Para 121][Para 244]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — only Issue 7 succeeded; the employer must stop requiring a village or commune letter for annual leave taken from the second year of service, and every other claim was dismissed. [Para 283][Para 280][Para 288]
The panel classified each demand as a rights dispute, decided by law, or an interest dispute, decided in equity, and held that only a most-representative union may bring an interest claim. [Para 155][Para 156][Para 169][Para 171]
Issues 1, 2, 4 and 6 failed as interest disputes: the employer already paid USD 9 against the USD 7 fixed by Notice 230, and USD 13 against the USD 10 minimum, so the demands exceeded the legal floor and the union lacked representative status to pursue them. [Para 157][Para 159][Para 174][Para 175][Para 200][Para 213] Holiday work was held not to be overtime, following Award 115/08. [Para 197][Para 199]
Issue 3 was a rights dispute. The panel held the bonus conditional on regular attendance, that authorising leave is an exercise of management's own discretion under Article 2, and that permission to be absent does not entitle the worker to the bonus pro rata; the claim was dismissed. [Para 181][Para 184][Para 193][Para 195]
On Issue 7 the panel applied Articles 166 and 167: a worker acquires annual leave after one year, and once the employer voluntarily allows leave from the first year that is a benefit exceeding the law, but it may not attach a village-chief condition to leave earned from year two. [Para 223][Para 227][Para 235][Para 238]
Issues 8 and 9 failed on the evidence: about 100 workers lost the bonus under the same scoring, the union president and vice-president were among those rewarded, and the secretary had signed the deduction agreement. [Para 245][Para 247][Para 248] Issue 10(2) failed because 45 minutes of lateness is not regular attendance, and Issue 11 because warnings for a missed punch were the mildest step under Article 10 of the internal rules. [Para 257][Para 259][Para 277][Para 278]
Arbitrator Liv Sovanna dissented on Issue 3, holding the long-standing interpretation to be pro-rata deduction and finding the 11 July 2012 Labour Advisory Committee statement inconsistent with Notice 230's added words. [Para 292][Para 307][Para 317]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for an amount above the statutory floor fixed by ministerial notice is an interest dispute, which the panel will not decide unless the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise. [Para 161][Para 172] An attendance bonus is earned only by actual regular attendance, so leave the employer authorises does not carry the bonus pro rata. [Para 193][Para 195] Where the employer grants annual leave from the first year as a benefit beyond the law, it may attach conditions to that extra benefit but not to leave earned by statute from the second year. [Para 235][Para 238]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted an interest award becomes a collective agreement binding all workers for one year, which would remove others' right to strike over the same demand. [Para 168]
