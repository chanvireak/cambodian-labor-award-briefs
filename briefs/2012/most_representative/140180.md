# Award No. 180/12 — San Luen Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 1 November 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom for the employer, An Non for the workers, Kong Phalla presiding) · Doc ID 140180

## 1. Facts
San Luen Garment is a garment factory registered on 25 October 2011 with about 550 workers; the claimant is the Khmer Youth Voice Union Federation and its enterprise union. [Para 1][Para 48][Para 49] A union committee was elected on 1 July 2012 by 26 workers, the federation notified the employer on 4 July, and registration was applied for on 10 July; 39 workers gave the federation written authority to represent them. [Para 42][Para 82][Para 83][Para 51] The employer objected to registration on 25 July, arguing the elected leaders lacked one year's seniority, and dismissed the union president on 31 July and the vice-president on 8 August without seeking the labour inspector's approval. [Para 90][Para 169][Para 176] Of 14 demands, five remained unresolved; Issues 12 and 14 were merged, leaving Issues 2, 7, 12–14 and 13 for decision. [Para 22][Para 26][Para 62]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the regular attendance bonus of USD 10 a month must be paid pro rata for days the worker was absent on unpaid leave authorised by the employer? [Para 109]
2. (Issue 7) Whether the employer must provide a free midday meal or 4,000 riel a day to all workers? [Para 140]
3. (Issues 12 and 14) Whether the dismissal of the union president and vice-president was lawful, they having been dismissed while the union's registration application was pending? [Para 156][Para 162]
4. (Issue 13) Whether the employer must abolish Saturday afternoon overtime and release workers at 4 pm? [Para 185]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the attendance bonus is part of wages, so absence on leave the employer itself authorised should not forfeit the whole month's bonus; other factories pay a midday meal allowance; Saturday afternoons should be free as they are for civil servants; and the two dismissals were anti-union reprisals. [Para 56][Para 59][Para 67][Para 99]
**Employer (respondent):** the bonus is a gratuity conditional on regular attendance and it cannot fund a meal or a shorter week; the dismissals rested on poor performance, indiscipline and probation status, and were unrelated to union activity. [Para 57][Para 61][Para 66][Para 93] It conceded it had not sought the labour inspector's authorisation before either dismissal. [Para 68][Para 81]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must pay the attendance bonus pro rata for authorised unpaid leave, and must reinstate both union leaders with back pay from dismissal to reinstatement; the meal allowance and Saturday-overtime claims were dismissed. [Para 199][Para 201][Para 200][Para 202]
The panel first satisfied itself that the federation held valid written authority from the 39 workers, applying Article 19 of Prakas 099. [Para 105][Para 107][Para 108]
On Issue 2 the panel found that Notice 041/11 had been repealed and replaced by Notice 230 of 25 July 2012, which added the words "without absence" and raised the bonus to USD 10. [Para 114][Para 116] It held the bonus had become a binding entitlement rather than a discretionary management tool, and that "absence" must be read so as to preserve fairness; leave the employer has authorised, including annual, sick and special leave, is not absence. [Para 124][Para 126][Para 134][Para 136] It therefore ordered pro-rata deduction by day. [Para 139]
On Issue 7 no law, collective agreement or practice fixed a free meal or 4,000 riel, so the claim was an interest dispute, and the claiming union, not yet certified and not most representative, could not bring it. [Para 140][Para 141][Para 153][Para 154][Para 155]
On Issues 12 and 14 the panel applied Article 4 of Prakas 305 and Article 293 of the Labour Law: a worker who is a union founder or member during a pending registration is protected as an employee representative, and dismissal requires the labour inspector's prior approval. [Para 156][Para 157][Para 158] All three conditions were met — the two were union members, the dismissals fell inside the protected window, and the employer knew their names from the 25 July objection. [Para 162][Para 170][Para 173] Both dismissals were therefore procedurally unlawful and, under Article 34 of Prakas 099, the remedy was reinstatement with back pay. [Para 177][Para 180][Para 184]
On Issue 13 the panel held overtime allocation is part of management's right under Article 2 of the Labour Law, exercised lawfully and reasonably, since overtime was voluntary and workers could leave at 4 pm. [Para 188][Para 192][Para 196] Presiding arbitrator Kong Phalla dissented on Issue 2, holding the bonus conditional and forfeitable for any absence. [Para 206][Para 220][Para 229]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker who is a founder or member of a union whose registration is pending is protected like an employee representative, so dismissal without the labour inspector's prior authorisation is unlawful and the remedy is reinstatement with back pay from the date of dismissal. [Para 156][Para 158][Para 177][Para 184] A bonus that the employer has converted by notice into a stated entitlement is no longer a discretionary gratuity, and "absence" in its conditions does not include leave the employer itself authorised. [Para 124][Para 136]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted it will follow its earlier awards unless a new legal basis appears, and found such a basis here in the new wording of Notice 230. [Para 233][Para 234]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR is badly degraded in places: Latin fragments ("She", "Nation", "Sana", "Apple", "Stank", "Fill", "Ling", "Sing", "Land", "Cham", "Life") break Khmer sentences, several paragraphs are duplicated verbatim, and the chair's dissenting annex repeats itself. [Para 17][Para 24][Para 124][Para 148][Para 192][Para 206] Article 293 is printed twice in the same line, and one operative paragraph opens with a stray word before naming the issue. [Para 158][Para 201]
The award is internally inconsistent on numbers and dates: the conciliation report is dated both 6 September and 4 September, and the decision records "four" unresolved points while listing five. [Para 18][Para 23][Para 26] The attendance bonus is described as 7 dollars in Notice 041/11 and 10 dollars in Notice 230, and the panel's own order gives the dismissed workers' names while the head of the file names them differently. [Para 109][Para 116][Para 1] Workers, the union president and vice-president are reduced to initials here; the employer company is named as it appears on the face of the award.
