# Award No. 223/12 — 8 Star Sport Wear Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 7 December 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chea, president) · Doc ID 140218

## 1. Facts
8 Star Sport Wear Ltd. — also printed as 8 Star Spot Wear — is a garment factory in Vattanac Industrial Park, Phnom Penh, operating since 2006 with about 920 workers [Para 38][Para 39]. The factory's Economic Workers' Union of Cambodia registered on 12 March 2012; a separate Free Workers' Union holds the certificate of most-representative status, valid to 16 March 2013 [Para 41][Para 29][Para 57]. Conciliation on 25 October 2012 settled one of five demands, leaving four; the panel heard the case on 15 November 2012 and issued a non-binding award on the interests issues and a binding one on the rights issues, under the 3 October 2012 Memorandum of Understanding [Para 16][Para 20][Para 21][Para 24][Para 37]. The award date was extended from 30 November to 7 December 2012 [Para 25].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay the overtime meal allowance weekly, where it pays monthly on the 7th on the ground that the workforce asked for monthly payment about three years earlier? [Para 43][Para 102][Para 104]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay the regular-attendance bonus pro rata for days a worker takes occasional authorised leave for personal business, where Notice No. 230 of 25 July 2012 conditions the bonus on attendance "without absence"? [Para 60][Para 126][Para 131]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must build a roof over the workers' bicycle parking area, where it has fitted only a green mesh net? [Para 76][Para 167]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the arbitrator has jurisdiction over a demand that the employer stop including the overtime meal allowance and the annual-leave indemnity in wages subject to income tax? [Para 83][Para 180]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The meal allowance must come weekly because workers lack money for daily living, must borrow at interest, and the new tax deductions have cut their take-home pay [Para 43][Para 44][Para 117]. On the bonus, workers who obtain the employer's written permission to be absent are in the position of workers the employer has sent outside the factory, so a pro-rated bonus should follow [Para 61][Para 62]. On parking, about 100 bicycles stand in the sun and rain, which ages them and makes them unsafe to ride; the net cuts heat but does not stop rain [Para 79][Para 78][Para 82].

**Employer (respondent):** The monthly practice was adopted at the workers' own request about three years ago under an agreement with the most-representative union; the collective agreement contains no meal-allowance clause, the law fixes no interval, and weekly payment disrupts monthly accounting [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 56][Para 52]. The bonus practice is more generous than the law: 5 USD for the first half of the month and 5 USD for the second, so a worker absent in the first half still receives 5 USD [Para 63][Para 72]. It deducts tax on all payments because they are income [Para 92][Para 93].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay the overtime meal allowance weekly and to deduct the attendance bonus pro rata to the days of authorised personal leave; the bicycle-roof demand and the tax demand were refused, the latter for want of jurisdiction [Para 193][Para 194][Para 195][Para 197].

On Issue 1 the panel held the claim to be a rights dispute because Notice No. 041/11 point 2 gives 2,000 riel a day or a free meal to any worker doing overtime at the employer's request, without capping hours [Para 102][Para 103][Para 104]. The notice does not fix a payment date; the panel held payment must fall within a reasonable period that lets the worker buy food for the overtime, and that the collective agreement contains no meal-allowance clause and the parties never agreed a weekly cycle [Para 105][Para 108][Para 122]. Weighing the administrative burden of weekly payment against the workers' daily subsistence needs, it found the latter weightier and ordered weekly payment [Para 123][Para 124][Para 125].

On Issue 2 the panel first classified the claim as a rights dispute because the bonus is set by Notice No. 230 [Para 126][Para 130]. It found that the notice's expression "regular attendance" was not clarified by the competent Labour Advisory Committee and that it would not read "without absence" as covering lawful or unlawful absence alike [Para 135][Para 136]. Absences flowing from annual leave, special leave, sick leave, maternity leave, public holidays and weekly rest do not justify any deduction, so the bonus stays at 10 USD [Para 137][Para 138]. Authorised personal leave, however, suspends the employment contract under Articles 71 and 72, so no wage and no bonus is earned for those days, and the employer may deduct pro rata only [Para 159][Para 162][Para 163][Para 165].

On Issue 3 the panel found no law, collective agreement or agreement requiring a bicycle-park roof, making it an interests dispute; as the applicant union holds no most-representative certificate, the panel refused to consider it, following Awards 169/11, 147/12, 02/11 and 66/11 [Para 168][Para 169][Para 173][Para 174][Para 175][Para 179].

On Issue 4 the panel applied Article 302 and the three cumulative conditions for a collective labour dispute [Para 182][Para 183]. Because the employer had merely withheld tax as the tax law requires and the obligation to pay income tax runs between the worker and the State, represented by the Ministry of Economy and Finance, the first condition failed and the panel declined jurisdiction without reaching the other two [Para 185][Para 186][Para 187][Para 192].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted a non-binding interests award becomes a collective agreement binding all workers in the enterprise for one year, removing others' right to strike on that issue [Para 172]. It also observed that weekly payment genuinely burdens the employer's administration [Para 123].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Authorised personal leave suspends the employment contract, so it earns neither wage nor attendance bonus, and an employer that pays the bonus under a notice conditioned on "without absence" may deduct pro rata to those days but may not forfeit the whole bonus; and an arbitrator has no jurisdiction over a demand about the incidence of income tax, because that obligation runs between the worker and the State rather than between worker and employer [Para 165][Para 186].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The pages are heavily OCR-damaged, with Latin debris cutting the reasoning at [Para 144], [Para 166] and [Para 180]. The employer appears as 8 Star Sport Wear, 8 Star Spot Wear and 8 Star Skot Wear, and the operative reasoning at [Para 166] is reduced to unrecognisable fragments, so the exact formula ordered for the pro-rated bonus must be read from the decision at [Para 194] [Para 38][Para 50]. Individual names of union officers and of a worker cited on tax are printed in full and are withheld here [Para 2][Para 91]. Two internal tensions are recorded rather than resolved: at [Para 108] the panel holds that payment need only be within a reasonable period and not weekly, yet at [Para 124] it orders weekly payment; and having said at [Para 140] that it could not interpret "without absence" and would decide case by case, it lays down a general pro-rata rule at [Para 165]. Arbitrator Ing Sothy filed a dissent on Issue 2, reproducing dictionary definitions of "bonus" and arguing the bonus is a conditional gift whose conditions the employer as grantor sets, so no bonus is earned where attendance is incomplete [Para 200][Para 207][Para 219].
