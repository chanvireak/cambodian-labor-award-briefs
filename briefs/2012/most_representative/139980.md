# Award No. 46/12 — Thap Van Garment Cambodia Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 22 March 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, An Nan; chair Run Saray) · Doc ID 139980

## 1. Facts
Thap Van Garment Cambodia Co., Ltd. is a garment factory employing about 730 workers. The Workers' Movement Union at the factory, with 240 members and new leadership recognised by the Ministry of Labour on 9 March 2012, brought five demands, of which one was settled by agreement at the hearing. The factory infirmary measures 7 m² (2 m by 3.5 m) against a regulatory minimum of 20 m², and the employer paid annual leave as a monthly cash sum instead of granting actual leave. [Para 39][Para 41][Para 42][Para 44][Para 49][Para 65]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must provide an adequately sized infirmary, of at least 20 m², for the workers? [Para 44][Para 45]
2. Whether the employer must keep a vehicle on standby for workers who faint or collapse? [Para 48]
3. Whether the employer must allow pregnant workers one day per month to attend antenatal checks while preserving their daily wage and other benefits? [Para 51]
4. Whether the employer must pay a meal allowance of 1,000 riel per hour for overtime worked from 18:30 to 20:30? [Para 58][Para 95]
5. Whether the employer must grant annual leave as the Labour Law requires and stop paying it out in cash each month? [Para 64][Para 101]

Issue 2 was resolved by agreement at the hearing — the employer undertaking to use every means to send workers who fall ill or faint to hospital — and was not decided. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** a 7 m² infirmary leaves no space for a worker to lie down, so a room of at least 20 m² is needed; two hours a month is not enough for an antenatal check because the hospital is about 5 km away and queues last until the afternoon; the 500 riel per hour paid for late overtime is below what the law requires and wages are too low to save; and annual leave must be usable as rest rather than converted into monthly cash, since workers cannot take time off when they have family matters. [Para 45][Para 53][Para 54][Para 55][Para 61][Para 67][Para 68]
**Employer (respondent):** it does not deny the infirmary is small but has no space to extend it; two hours per month is sufficient for antenatal checks and it cannot grant more; it cannot meet the meal allowance demand; and its practice of paying 1.5 days of annual leave in cash each month is the established company rule. [Para 47][Para 57][Para 63][Para 65][Para 69]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 1: the employer ordered to provide an infirmary with a floor area of at least 20 m²; issue 5: the employer ordered to allow workers with one year's service or more to take annual leave and to stop the monthly cash payment in lieu; issues 3 and 4 rejected for want of standing. The award is non-binding, the parties having chosen non-binding arbitration. [Para 24][Para 119][Para 120][Para 122][Para 123][Para 124]

On the infirmary, Article 238 of the Labour Law requires every enterprise covered by Article 1 to provide first-aid and treatment facilities for its workers. Article 1 of Prakas No. 330 SKBY of 6 December 2000 requires an enterprise with 50 or more workers to maintain a permanent infirmary, and Article 2 requires a floor area appropriate to the workforce but at least 20 m². The panel measured the existing room at 7 m² for some 720 workers and found this below the minimum, ordering the room to be enlarged. Shortage of premises was no answer: the obligation is fixed by the Prakas. [Para 70][Para 71][Para 72][Para 73][Para 74][Para 76][Para 77]

On the antenatal claim, the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, any agreement, or any collective agreement granting a paid day each month, and it followed Award 17/07-Charm Textile, which held that beyond weekly rest, annual leave, special leave and sick leave the law imposes no further duty to release pregnant workers for checks. The demand therefore sought a benefit superior to the law and was an interests dispute. Because the union held no certificate of most representative status, and an interests award would replace a collective agreement binding all workers while stripping non-members of the right to strike, the panel declined to consider it. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 80][Para 85][Para 89][Para 92][Para 93][Para 94]

The meal allowance fell the same way. The employer paid 2,000 riel for overtime from 16:00 to 18:00, consistent with the law, but only 500 riel per hour for the 18:30 to 20:30 stretch; the panel found no legal or contractual provision requiring 1,000 riel per hour, classified the demand as an interests dispute, and declined it for the same lack of representative status. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 95][Para 96][Para 97][Para 98][Para 100]

On annual leave, Article 166(1) entitles every worker to paid annual leave of one and a half working days per month of continuous service, eighteen days a year, and Article 167(1) makes the right exercisable after one year's service. Article 166(4) adds one day for each three years of seniority. The company's internal rules, clause 4, themselves provide 18 days plus one day after three years, so they conform to the law; the practice did not. The employer paid 1.5 days in cash every month and never allowed the leave to be taken, which the panel held contrary to the Labour Law: annual leave is a right the law confers so that workers can rest when they need to. Following Award 56/06-Borik, the panel noted that the law does not permit arrangements that substitute compensation for the exercise or waiver of annual leave. It ordered the employer to grant leave to workers with a year's service and to cease the monthly cash payments. [Para 101][Para 104][Para 106][Para 109][Para 111][Para 113][Para 114][Para 116][Para 117][Para 118]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Annual leave is a right to actual rest, not a deferred cash entitlement: an employer that pays leave out month by month and never lets workers take it contravenes Articles 166 and 167 of the Labour Law, and must instead grant leave to workers who have completed a year of service and stop the cash substitution. [Para 104][Para 113][Para 116][Para 118]

**Obiter dicta:** that beyond the statutory leave categories the law imposes no duty to give pregnant workers an extra paid day each month for antenatal checks; and that shortage of space does not excuse an infirmary smaller than the 20 m² fixed by Prakas No. 330. [Para 73][Para 79]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The Khmer original is badly OCR-damaged: the company's registered suffix reads "អី ម អេហូជី ខូ អិលធីឌី", which cannot be reliably transliterated, so the party is named here as printed on the face of the award minus that suffix. The workforce is given as 730 at para 39 and about 720 at para 74. Para 19 records five of ten demands resolved in conciliation, while only five demands appear in the non-conciliation report and five were referred. Paragraphs 119 to 123 duplicate their own headings mid-sentence, and the signature block for the chair arbitrator is corrupted. On issue 4 the workers' figure is 10 riel per hour at para 8 but 1,000 riel per hour at paras 58 and 95; the reasoned text and the order use 1,000 riel. The library record romanises the employer as "Thab Von", where this brief follows ថប វ័ន as "Thap Van".
