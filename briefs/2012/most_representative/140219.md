# Award No. 225/12 — Gret An Neur Tech Style Fek Tery Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 14 December 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambor Na, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Taing Try, president) · Doc ID 140219

## 1. Facts
Gret An Neur Tech Style Fek Tery Co., Ltd. — also printed as Gret An Neur Tech Style and, in its registration documents, as Winner Knitting — is a garment factory in Sa'ang district, Kandal, employing about 1,806 workers [Para 26][Para 47]. Its factory union of the Democratic Garment Workers' Union holds the most-representative certificate No. 129, dated 17 August 2012 [Para 36][Para 49]. Conciliation on 30 October 2012 failed on both demands; the panel heard the case on 22 November 2012 and issued a binding award on rights issues and a non-binding one on interests, under the 3 October 2012 Memorandum of Understanding [Para 13][Para 18][Para 19][Para 22]. The award date was extended from 6 December to 14 December 2012 [Para 23]. Two workers feature: a staff delegate dismissed on 13 June 2012 and a packing worker and union activist transferred to weaving on 22 September 2012 [Para 52][Para 100].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must reinstate the dismissed staff delegate and pay her back wages, where it dismissed her for a serious fault under Article 83 without first seeking the labour inspector's authorisation required by Article 293? [Para 50][Para 121][Para 123]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer harassed the packing worker and union activist by transferring him to weaving at a lower wage, and must stop that harassment? [Para 76][Para 115][Para 133]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The delegate had served since 18 March 1998, was a fourth-term staff delegate, and was dismissed without notice on 13 June 2012 for an alleged Article 83 fault; as a delegate she could not be dismissed without the labour inspector's prior approval, and the employer's pressure on her to sign a resignation showed bad faith [Para 50][Para 51][Para 74][Para 75]. The activist was moved from packing (piece-rate, 290–350 USD a month) to weaving on a 61 USD monthly base wage, losing half his income after he questioned the compulsory transfer of packers to another company and joined a piece-rate protest [Para 109][Para 110][Para 115].

**Employer (respondent):** The delegate had other workers punch her card for a half-day's absence and admitted it in writing, which is exploitation of the company and a serious fault [Para 53][Para 54][Para 58]. The activist repeatedly packed wrong quantities; he was warned orally twice, signed a warning letter promising not to repeat the error within one month, breached it on 19 September 2012, and was moved because the company lost confidence in him — a lawful disciplinary measure, not harassment [Para 82][Para 100][Para 105][Para 150].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to reinstate the delegate and pay her wages from the date of dismissal until her return; the harassment claim and the related request to transfer the activist back to packing were refused [Para 132][Para 164][Para 165][Para 166][Para 168].

On Issue 1 the panel first classified the claim as a rights dispute because Article 293 governs [Para 118][Para 121]. It held that it had no competence to examine the merits of the delegate's fault, since she was a delegate, and turned to the procedural question [Para 122][Para 123]. Article 293 requires the employer to obtain the labour inspector's agreement before dismissing any staff delegate or candidate; the employer dismissed her on 13 June 2012 without asking [Para 123][Para 124][Para 125]. The dismissal was therefore procedurally unlawful, and the panel noted the correct course was to suspend the contract temporarily and then seek authorisation [Para 126][Para 127]. Under Prakas 099 Article 34 the panel may order reinstatement of a dismissed worker and immediate payment, so it ordered reinstatement plus wages for the whole period out of work [Para 128][Para 130][Para 131].

On Issue 2 the panel treated the claim as a rights dispute because it concerned disciplinary measures under the internal regulations [Para 136]. It found the two oral warnings to be lawful disciplinary measures for faulty packing, and the written warning of 18 August 2012 — signed and thumbprinted by the worker, in which he accepted transfer to any post in the factory if he erred again — to be a proportionate measure [Para 140][Para 143][Para 150]. The employer having shown repeated over- and under-packing and a breach of that undertaking, the panel held the employer entitled to discipline him and found no specific evidence of harassment; the party making an allegation bears the burden of proof [Para 151][Para 152][Para 153][Para 155]. The further request to move him back to packing was refused for want of jurisdiction, because it did not appear in the non-conciliation record and was not a direct consequence of the dispute, the transfer having occurred on 22 September 2012, before that record of 7 November 2012 [Para 157][Para 160][Para 162][Para 163].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that even where a serious fault appears, a delegate's fault cannot be adjudicated in the arbitration [Para 122]. It also stated that the employer's right to organise under Article 2 must be exercised lawfully and reasonably [Para 188].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A staff delegate cannot be dismissed without the labour inspector's prior agreement under Article 293, and a dismissal made without it is procedurally unlawful whatever the merits of the alleged fault, entitling the delegate to reinstatement and wages from the dismissal date to the date of return; whereas a transfer following oral warnings and a signed written undertaking, imposed for repeated packing errors, is a proportionate disciplinary measure and not harassment [Para 125][Para 132][Para 150].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is heavily OCR-damaged, with Latin fragments breaking the reasoning at [Para 151] and [Para 163], and the operative paragraph is split across [Para 166] and [Para 167] ("from the date the employer dismissed her until her return"). The employer is named in at least four forms (Gret An Neur Tech Style Fek Tery, Gret An Neur Tech Style, Gret An Neur Chek Style, Gret An Neur), while its internal regulations and union registration are issued to "Winner Knitting Factory Ltd" and a "Winner" factory respectively — a name carried over from before the change [Para 26][Para 37][Para 42][Para 113]. Both individual workers, their attendance numbers and the factory-level union officers appear in full throughout the document, including in quoted warning letters; they are withheld here and should be redacted at source [Para 3][Para 5][Para 28][Para 106]. Arbitrator An Non dissented on Issue 2, finding that a transfer cutting a worker's wage by half after years in one post was an unreasonable use of managerial power and amounted to harassment, and that the majority's reasoning contradicted itself by first finding no evidence and then declining jurisdiction [Para 173][Para 180][Para 185][Para 190].
