# Award No. 096/12 — Yong Va Industry (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, Branch 1 (Arbitration Council, 20 June 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chhea presiding; Mar Sambor Na appointed by the employer, Liv Sovanna appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 140128

## 1. Facts
Yong Va Industry (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, Branch 1, a garment factory of about 1,900 workers at Ta Khmau, Kandal, was registered in March 1998 and has six unions, none of which is the most representative [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38][Para 39]. The Cambodia Economic Workers' Union at the factory, with about 200 members, filed for conciliation on 3 April 2012; on 25 April 2012 six of eleven demands were settled and five were referred by report No. 421/12 K.B/KN of 2 May 2012 [Para 17][Para 18][Para 19][Para 41]. At the hearing of 30 May 2012 one more point (point 3) was settled, leaving points 1, 2, 4 and 5 for decision [Para 22][Para 23]. The award was due on 16 June 2012 and was issued on 20 June 2012 by agreement [Para 24].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay a bonus of 4,000 riel a day to the workers in the QI, QC and QA sections who inspect the full quota of shirts set by the company? [Para 43]
2. Whether the company must deduct the USD 7 attendance bonus only in proportion to days of absence authorised by the employer, and keep the full bonus where a worker is late by no more than 30 minutes in a month? [Para 53]
4. Whether the company must pay 1,200 riel a day as a midday meal allowance, in addition to the 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance? [Para 59]
5. Whether the company must pay USD 15 a month in housing and travel allowance to each worker? [Para 62]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — Cambodia Economic Workers' Union at Yong Va I):** the company sets daily quotas of 300 to 400 shirts for QI and chain-based quotas for the other two sections, yet never pays an incentive when the quota is met and instead reprimands workers or takes their thumbprint when it is not; the attendance bonus is forfeited entirely for any absence, authorised or not, and only five minutes of lateness a month is tolerated; midday meals are not covered; and rent has risen from USD 15 to USD 40 and transport from USD 15 to USD 18 a month [Para 44][Para 45][Para 47][Para 48][Para 55][Para 56][Para 62][Para 63].

**Employer (respondent — Yong Va Industry (Cambodia) Co., Ltd):** a quota-based incentive would make workers chase volume and neglect quality; quotas were set only for QI, not for QC and QA, and only to equalise workloads; it denies ever disciplining or taking thumbprints for missed quotas; it pays 1,200 riel a day for lunch when there is no overtime and 2,000 riel overtime meal money when there is; and it cannot pay the housing and travel claims [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51][Para 52][Para 60][Para 64].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must reduce the USD 7 attendance bonus in proportion to the number of days of absence authorised by the employer, but may not forfeit it in full; the claims for the 4,000 riel quota bonus, for the full bonus despite 30 minutes of lateness a month, for the 1,200 riel midday meal on top of overtime meal money, and for USD 15 a month in housing and travel were refused [Para 121][Para 122][Para 123][Para 124][Para 125].

On point 1 the panel found no law, collective convention or agreement requiring a quota bonus and classified the claim as an interest dispute [Para 65][Para 66]. It restated its rule that it declines interest disputes where the claiming union is not the most representative, since Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 turns an award on interests into a collective convention for one year binding all workers, and Article 277 governs that status [Para 67][Para 69][Para 70][Para 71]. This union was not the most representative and had only about 200 members out of 1,900, so the claim was refused [Para 74][Para 75]. On point 2 the panel split the claim in two. On authorised absence it held that Notice No. 041/11 K.B/S.J.N of 7 March 2011 sets a USD 7 minimum for regular attendance, that Article 103 makes bonuses part of wages, and that an absence authorised under Article 71(6) suspends the contract under Article 72(1) and so carries no wage for that day — hence the employer may deduct proportionately but not in full, following awards No. 136/11 and No. 154/11 [Para 78][Para 84][Para 86][Para 87][Para 88][Para 92][Para 95][Para 96]. On lateness it held that fixing working hours is part of the employer's right to manage the enterprise under Article 2, that the schedule was not contested, and that arriving late without prior permission means the worker did not attend regularly as Notice 041/11 requires, so no bonus is due for that month [Para 101][Para 103][Para 106][Para 108][Para 109][Para 110]. Points 4 and 5 failed on the same interest-dispute reasoning, no legal or contractual entitlement for a separate meal allowance or a housing and travel allowance having been shown [Para 113][Para 114][Para 117][Para 118][Para 120].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus that forms part of wages may be reduced only in proportion to days of absence authorised by the employer, never forfeited in full; but a worker who arrives late without prior permission has not attended regularly for the month, and no attendance bonus is owed. A demand for a production incentive, a meal allowance or a housing allowance that no law, convention or agreement provides is an interest dispute the panel will not hear unless the claiming union is the most representative in the enterprise [Para 92][Para 109][Para 67].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that setting working hours is one of the employer's management rights and must be exercised lawfully and reasonably, without deciding any particular hour to be unreasonable [Para 101][Para 105].
