# Award No. 166/12 — Pou Yien (Cambodia) Enterprise, Phnom Penh (Arbitration Council, 10 September 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chhea presiding; Ly Tai Seng, employer nominee; Hun Chundy, worker nominee) · Doc ID 140173

## 1. Facts
Pou Yien is a shoe manufacturer that began operating in 2010 at Tuek Thla, Sen Sok, Phnom Penh, employing 3,027 workers and hosting eight registered unions, none of which was the most representative; three of them, all affiliated to the Cambodian Workers' Trade Union Federation, brought this collective dispute jointly with 2,180 members between them. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 39][Para 40][Para 41][Para 46] Following a strike notice of 15 August 2012, the labour dispute department conciliated on 17 August 2012 and failed on all eight demands, reporting non-conciliation No. 943 Kb/Ak/Vk dated 20 August 2012. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 17][Para 33] At the hearing of 30 August 2012 both sides appeared and the panel settled three demands and recorded the withdrawal of four (Issues 5, 6, 7 and 8), leaving only Issue 3 for decision, and the parties agreed the award would be binding on all issues. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 21][Para 26] The workers also raised at the hearing a demand absent from the non-conciliation record — that a second gate at Building A be opened to ease access to food — which the panel treated separately as a question of jurisdiction. [Para 96][Para 97]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must provide one free midday meal to every worker, where no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement or agreement between the parties requires it? [Para 47][Para 65]
2. (Additional demand) Whether the panel has jurisdiction to order the employer to reopen the Building A gate, that demand having been raised for the first time at the hearing and not appearing in the non-conciliation record? [Para 96][Para 97][Para 104]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the meal is needed because food sold at the factory gate runs out before the workers of Building A — the building farther from the sellers — can reach it, so they return to work without eating or eat dessert instead; other factories, including American Kaing New Ellen, Indian factories and factories in the Bavet area, already provide a meal, and if the employer will not, it should open a second gate at Building A. [Para 47][Para 50][Para 51][Para 53][Para 55]
**Employer (respondent):** nothing in the law obliges it to feed the workforce; it will provide the meal only if the law requires it, and in any case the factory already provides an eating area and some workers bring food from home. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61][Para 88][Para 89]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the demand for one free midday meal for every worker, and held it had no jurisdiction over the additional demand to reopen the Building A gate; the award is binding on the parties from the date of issue. [Para 95][Para 104][Para 105][Para 106]
On Issue 3 the panel first classified the demand and found no provision of the Labour Law, no collective agreement and no agreement between the parties requiring a midday meal, so the demand was one about interests and not about rights. [Para 65][Para 66] It then tested standing, since no union among the eight was most representative in the enterprise; under Article 9(A) paragraph 2 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 several unions may combine to table a collective agreement where together they represent the majority of the workforce, and the panel accepted that the three claimant unions, with 2,180 members equal to 72.01 per cent of 3,027 workers, met that test. [Para 77][Para 78][Para 81][Para 84][Para 85] On the merits it applied the equity power in Article 312 paragraph 2 of the Labour Law and found the true cause of the missed meals to be that the sellers could not supply enough food for so large a workforce — a situation outside the employer's control — so that requiring 3,027 free meals a day would be a heavy cost and, on equity, unreasonable. [Para 86][Para 91][Para 92][Para 93] It nevertheless held the workers' complaint reasonable in substance, since they need a meal to have the energy to work, and directed the employer to raise the supply problem with the midday sellers at the Building B gate and encourage them to increase the quantity of rice available so that Building A workers can eat adequately. [Para 90][Para 94]
On the additional demand the panel applied Article 312 paragraph 1 of the Labour Law and Article 33 of Prakas No. 099, which confine its jurisdiction to the issues stated in the non-conciliation record and to issues arising after that record as a direct consequence of the dispute. [Para 97][Para 98] It framed two conditions — whether the gate was closed after the record, and whether the closure was a direct consequence of the original dispute — and found on the evidence that the Building A gate had been closed since 2010, when the site was still the LA factory, and so long before the non-conciliation record of 20 August 2012 and unrelated to the midday meal demand. [Para 99][Para 100][Para 101][Para 102][Para 103] Neither condition being met, it declined jurisdiction. [Para 104][Para 105]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a benefit the Labour Law does not grant and no agreement provides is a dispute about interests, which a panel will only entertain where the claimant union is most representative or, in an enterprise that has none, where several unions together represent a majority of the workforce; and even then it will refuse a claim whose cost is heavy and whose cause lies outside the employer's control, preferring a practicable direction to the employer. [Para 78][Para 81][Para 85][Para 91][Para 92][Para 94]
A demand first raised at the hearing and not in the non-conciliation record falls outside jurisdiction unless the facts it concerns arose after that record and are a direct consequence of the recorded dispute. [Para 97][Para 98][Para 102][Para 104]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that an interest award substitutes for the collective agreement for one year from the date it becomes final, and that this could deprive other workers of the right to strike over interests in future — a remark unnecessary to a decision that refused the demand. [Para 72][Para 73]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The list of demands disposed of at the hearing is cut mid-enumeration, so the third of the three settled demands is identified only by a broken fragment and its number cannot be recovered; four were withdrawn (Issues 5 to 8) and Issue 3 alone was decided, yet the arithmetic of eight demands, three settled and four withdrawn leaves one unaccounted for. [Para 20][Para 21] Paragraphs 75 and 76 are a duplicated and truncated run that repeats the panel's agreement with prior awards without stating it, and paragraph 64 repeats its own opening clause. [Para 64][Para 75][Para 76] Several award citations in the reasons are corrupted — the numbers are run together with the party names and one is printed as "0807" in two different cases — so those precedents cannot be verified from this text. [Para 71][Para 74][Para 82] The employer's name appears as "Pou Yien", "Phou Yien" and "To Yien" across the same document, its representatives are listed only by role and given name and are not reproduced here, and the panel's signature block carries OCR debris where signatures were. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 107] One sentence of the reasoning states that the demand arose because supply at the Building B gate was insufficient while an earlier passage places the difficulty at Building A, and both readings are left standing. [Para 50][Para 86]
