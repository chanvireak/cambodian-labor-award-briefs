# Award No. 183/12 — Conpress Holdings (Cambodia) Garment Factory Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 1 November 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel: Ouk Ry (employer-appointed), Huon Chundy (worker-appointed), Pen Bunchea (chair) · Doc ID 140182

## 1. Facts
Conpress Holdings (Cambodia) Garment Factory Co., Ltd ("Khon Bras Hol Ding") is a garment factory employing about 720 workers [Para 66]. On 28 August 2012 the Labour Disputes Department received a complaint about a strike over working conditions [Para 27]. Conciliation on 3 September 2012 left 9 of 24 demands unresolved [Para 28], and the file reached the Arbitration Council secretariat on 11 September 2012 [Para 29]. At the hearings the parties settled Issues 1 (fans and heat measurement) and 4 (seniority bonus during leave); the workers withdrew Issue 3 (wage recalculation) [Para 32][Para 33]. The panel therefore decided Issues 2, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9 only [Para 34].
The triggering dismissal was that of Ms S, a sewing worker on a fixed-duration contract, terminated on 25 August 2012 after she had been collecting thumbprints for a factory union the previous day [Para 85][Para 87]. A second worker, Ms P, had already accepted a severance payment and did not appear at either hearing [Para 80][Para 131].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the dismissal of Ms S was tainted by anti-union discrimination and whether she must be reinstated? [Para 138][Para 139]
2. (Issue 5) Whether workers have the right to speak on the telephone during working hours? [Para 168][Para 169]
3. (Issue 6) Whether the employer must pay a USD 1 travel allowance and a 2,000 riel meal allowance for voluntary work on public holidays or Sundays? [Para 180]
4. (Issue 7) Whether the employer must replace the company's security provider? [Para 118][Para 214]
5. (Issue 8) Whether the employer must pay a 2,000 riel daily midday meal allowance? [Para 126][Para 236]
6. (Issue 9) Whether the employer must pay a USD 15 monthly milk allowance for 18 months? [Para 129][Para 240]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Ms S was dismissed because she was gathering thumbprints to form a union, not for any misconduct; she had never been warned, and she refused the severance payment and pressed for reinstatement [Para 86][Para 87][Para 96]. The union also argued that telephone use is necessary for urgent family matters and does not affect output because earphones can be used [Para 103].
**Employer (respondent):** The dismissal followed a Chinese sewing-section head's assessment that Ms S incited other workers without justification, and the employer paid severance rather than reinstate her [Para 89][Para 91][Para 4]. It said the security guards are Royal Gendarmerie (PM) personnel under a separate contract, that internal rules and the employment contract bar personal business and telephone use in working hours, and that the additional allowances are not payable [Para 105][Para 124][Para 127][Para 130].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement was refused, but the employer was ordered to pay Ms S her last wage, remaining annual leave, 5% contract-termination indemnity and wages through 18 November 2012; the USD 1 travel claim was refused while a 2,000 riel meal allowance was ordered for voluntary Sunday overtime; the telephone, security, midday meal and milk claims were refused [Para 244][Para 245][Para 247][Para 249][Para 250][Para 251][Para 252].
On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 12 and Article 279 of the Labour Law, which prohibit using union membership or activity as a ground for discipline or termination [Para 139][Para 140]. It found the workers had not shown how the employer learned of the thumbprint campaign, and the union's leadership list of 8 September 2012 named 19 workers but not Ms S; the discrimination allegation therefore failed [Para 149][Para 150][Para 151]. Turning to the termination itself, the panel held that a fixed-duration contract may be cut short only by agreement or for serious misconduct [Para 157]. The employer produced no evidence of the alleged incitement and had not supplied the assessor's written evaluation by the 10 October deadline, so the dismissal was a unilateral act by the employer [Para 93][Para 163][Para 164]. Under Article 73 the worker was entitled to compensation measured by wages to the contractual end date, plus the 5% indemnity where the collective agreement is silent [Para 165][Para 160]. The panel refused reinstatement but ordered payment of final wages, unused annual leave, the 5% indemnity and wages to 18 November 2012 [Para 166][Para 167].
On Issue 5 the panel read Article 2 of the internal rules and clause 7(H) of the employment contract as barring personal business and telephone use during working hours, and held that telephone calls are personal business [Para 106][Para 107][Para 171]. It nonetheless encouraged the employer to show understanding for genuine emergencies [Para 177].
On Issue 6 the panel treated the travel allowance as a new benefit, an interest dispute, and refused to consider it because the claimant union was not the most representative in the enterprise [Para 181][Para 191][Para 192]. It distinguished Sunday work: because the normal week runs 48 hours from Monday to Saturday, Sunday work exceeds ordinary hours and is overtime, so the 2,000 riel meal allowance under Notice 041/11 applies [Para 206][Para 211][Para 212][Para 213]. Holiday work, by contrast, is not overtime and attracts no meal allowance [Para 201][Para 204][Para 205].
On Issue 7 the panel held the security guards' contract was a separate employment relationship to which the workers were third parties, and that workers may not vary it absent proof the guards were dangerous [Para 227][Para 228][Para 229]. The four incidents alleged did not affect workers' safety, although the panel called the guards' conduct improper and urged corrective measures [Para 232][Para 233][Para 234].
On Issues 8 and 9 the panel found no law, collective agreement or practice creating the midday meal or milk allowances, classified both as interest disputes, and declined to consider them because the union had 77 authorising members and no registration certificate, so it lacked standing to bring an interest dispute [Para 236][Para 190][Para 191][Para 193][Para 243].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that ends a fixed-duration contract before its term, without proven serious misconduct and without the documentary evidence it was ordered to produce, must pay the worker wages to the contractual end date plus the statutory 5% termination indemnity, even where reinstatement is refused [Para 157][Para 165][Para 166]. Separately, a union that is not the most representative in the enterprise, and that holds no registration certificate, cannot bring an interest dispute asking the panel to create new benefits [Para 187][Para 191][Para 192].
**Obiter dicta:** Sunday work is overtime because the ordinary week is 48 hours Monday to Saturday, whereas public-holiday work is not overtime [Para 206][Para 201]. The panel also observed that the security guards' behaviour, though not unsafe, was improper and should be corrected by the employer [Para 233][Para 234].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award date is printed as 1 November 2012 although the panel recorded that the award was to issue on 3 October 2012 and was postponed by agreement of the parties; both dates appear and are consistent only on that reading [Para 1][Para 35]. The dismissed worker's name is rendered two different ways in the same award — the conciliation report and the national identity card and contract spell it differently — and the panel expressly chose the identity-card spelling as official; the variance is a name defect I have not reproduced [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74]. Of 78 authorisation letters, one bore a clear thumbprint but no written name, so the panel considered only 77 workers [Para 70][Para 71]. The OCR text is heavily corrupted, with stray Latin tokens ("She", "CA", "Mani", "Soutta", "Mark", "Bell", "Sall", "Seri", "Aust", "Sang", "The") spliced into Khmer sentences, and several amounts appear in inconsistent form (the milk claim is stated as both USD 12 and USD 15, and the holiday meal claim as both 3,000 and 2,000 riel) [Para 17][Para 109][Para 11][Para 180]. The signature block is partly illegible [Para 254].
