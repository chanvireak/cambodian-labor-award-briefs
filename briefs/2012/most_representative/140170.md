# Award No. 158/12 — Horus Industrial Corp (Hori S In Dash Strile Khob) (Arbitration Council, 21 September 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer, Tuon Siphan for the workers, Pen Bunhea presiding) · Doc ID 140170

## 1. Facts

Horus Industrial Corp, a garment factory operating since 1992 and employing 513 workers, faced a collective dispute filed on 24 July 2012 by the Khmer Democratic Workers' Union Federation [Para 27][Para 97]. Conciliation on 3 August 2012 left 9 of 12 demands unresolved; the panel held hearings on 16 August and 4 September 2012 and the parties agreed to postpone the award to 21 September 2012 [Para 28][Para 30][Para 40]. Only 107 of the 513 workers had thumbprinted an authorisation for the union to represent them [Para 100]. At the hearing the workers withdrew Issues 2 and 3 and reached agreement on Issue 9, leaving six issues for decision [Para 33][Para 34]. The union held only a receipt for its registration application, not a registration certificate, so the panel found it was not yet recognised as a legally constituted professional organisation [Para 35][Para 38].

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must raise the basic monthly wage from 61 to 75 US dollars, where the claimant union lacked registration at the time of the claim? [Para 101][Para 193][Para 201]
2. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must pay a meal allowance of 1,000 riel per hour whenever overtime exceeds two hours? [Para 106][Para 206][Para 210]
3. (Issue 5) Whether the employer must preserve the function allowance of five team leaders moved to the sample section, and repay function and skill allowances withheld from 45 workers? [Para 112][Para 211][Para 236]
4. (Issue 6) Whether the employer must repay the wages it deducted from 12 workers as penalties? [Para 144][Para 255][Para 261]
5. (Issue 7) Whether the employer must stop transferring workers between functions and posts at will? [Para 155][Para 262][Para 267]
6. (Issue 8) Whether the employer must offer overtime to every worker who volunteers, rather than selecting only fast sewers? [Para 181][Para 268][Para 281]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** The workers sought a 75 dollar basic wage because prices had risen and new factories were advertising 80 to 85 dollars, and argued this would also motivate the workforce [Para 103][Para 104]. They asked for a 1,000 riel hourly meal allowance beyond three hours of overtime [Para 106], for the function allowance of five team leaders to be kept and withheld allowances repaid to 45 workers [Para 112][Para 113], and for an end to deductions made for not swiping cards, talking on the phone, eating snacks or chatting [Para 144].

**Employer (respondent):** The employer said it applied the law and paid the statutory minimum of 61 dollars [Para 105][Para 192]. It said it already paid 2,000 riel for overtime from 4 to 6 pm and a further 1,000 riel beyond 6 pm, and would keep overtime to two or three hours [Para 108][Para 109][Para 111]. It argued that function allowance follows the actual post held, that team numbers had dropped from eight to four, and that the 40 workers' so-called skill allowance was in fact a productivity bonus payable only at 80% of target [Para 125][Para 121][Para 137][Para 138].

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** partly granted — Issues 1, 4 and 8 were declined; on Issue 5 the employer must reassign the five team leaders to suitable posts and keep their allowance calculation intact while repaying their actual losses; on Issue 6 the employer must repay the wages deducted from 12 workers as penalties; and on Issue 7 it must adopt a clear transfer procedure [Para 282][Para 284][Para 287][Para 288].

On Issue 1 the panel held that a demand above the statutory minimum wage is an interest dispute, and that under Article 277 of the Labour Law and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 a union may bring such a claim only if it is registered and most representative [Para 194][Para 198][Para 199]. The claimant had no registration certificate and represented only 107 of 513 workers, so the panel refused to consider the wage claim [Para 200][Para 201][Para 202].

On Issue 4 the panel found the employer already complied with Point 2 of Notification No. 041/11, which sets a 2,000 riel meal allowance per day of overtime; the additional 1,000 riel per hour was also an interest demand and was refused on the same ground [Para 203][Para 207][Para 208][Para 210].

On Issue 5 the panel held that under Article 2 of the Labour Law an employer may organise work as it sees fit, but only lawfully and reasonably [Para 215][Para 218]. Moving five team leaders to the sample section cut their function allowance, contrary to the first condition that wages not be reduced, so the transfer was unreasonable [Para 221][Para 222][Para 223]. It ordered reassignment to suitable posts with the allowance calculation preserved [Para 224]. On the 45 workers, the panel found under Article 118 that the employer had discharged its evidential burden with payroll tables, and that the claimants never identified which month or how much was withheld, so that claim failed [Para 227][Para 234][Para 235][Para 241].

On Issue 6 the panel applied Article 28, which forbids monetary penalties, and the internal rules, which require oral then written warnings rather than deductions [Para 243][Para 248][Para 253]. It ordered repayment to the 12 workers, while allowing the employer under Article 128 to recover the sums three of them had borrowed [Para 255][Para 259][Para 261].

On Issue 7 the panel held the employer may promote or demote but must base transfers on law or internal rules and must not cut wages or benefits unless the worker is at fault; one day's oral notice was insufficient [Para 264][Para 266][Para 267]. On Issue 8 it held overtime rests on the voluntary principle in Article 4 of Prakas No. 80 and Article 139 of the Labour Law, that the employer selects volunteers by productivity, and that allocating overtime by need is not discrimination [Para 268][Para 271][Para 277][Para 280].

## 5. Ratio decidendi

An employer's managerial right to reassign or transfer workers is lawful only where it does not reduce the wage, allowance or benefit the worker actually received, unless the worker is at fault; and a function allowance tied to the post held may be recalculated only through a lawful and reasonable reorganisation, not a one-day oral notice. Separately, deductions from wages as discipline are void under Article 28, though genuine advances may be recovered by instalments under Article 128. [Para 218][Para 223][Para 253][Para 259]

**Obiter dicta:** A demand above the statutory minimum wage, or for a meal allowance exceeding the Prakas rate, is an interest dispute that a panel will not reach unless the claimant union is registered and most representative [Para 195][Para 208].

## 6. Source defects disclosed

The scan is long and unevenly OCR'd: Latin fragments ("Shane", "Shell", "Soul", "Tram", "Ling", "York") interrupt the Khmer text and several clauses end mid-sentence [Para 40][Para 192][Para 249][Para 262]. Paragraphs 45 and 46 duplicate the same internal-rules entry with different spellings, and the company appears as Horus Industrial Corp, Hori S In Dash Strile Khob and several Khmer variants [Para 45][Para 46][Para 97]. The award notes that the union's registration certificate was never produced, so its legal personality is described only by inference [Para 37][Para 38]. Individual workers and employer representatives are named in full in the body, including in the function-allowance table, and are reduced to initials and roles here [Para 115][Para 157]. The award is expressly non-binding [Para 290].
