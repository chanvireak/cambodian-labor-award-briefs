# Award No. 170/11 — IM Way International Garment Ltd (Kampong Chhnang branch) (Arbitration Council, 12 January 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, employer-appointed; An Virak, worker-appointed; Kong Phalha, chair) · heard as non-binding award · Doc ID 140110

## 1. Facts
The Free Workers' Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia at the Kampong Chhnang branch of IM Way International Garment Ltd, a plant of about 4,652 workers with nine unions and roughly 600 members in the claimant union, filed sixteen demands on 30 November 2011; conciliation on 8 December 2011 resolved all but four, which came to the Council under non-conciliation report No. 753 of that date. [Para 8][Para 11][Para 12][Para 37][Para 38]

The four surviving demands were: a clear rule on deduction of the USD 7 monthly attendance bonus for late arrival and for sick or personal leave, with deduction in three stages rather than in full; company liability for two motorbikes stolen inside the factory on 15 and 25 November 2011; abolition of overtime between 9pm and 11pm; and abolition of short-term contracts. [Para 2][Para 3][Para 4][Para 5][Para 39][Para 52][Para 62][Para 65]

The employer pays the bonus only to workers who arrive on time and are not absent, deducting the whole USD 7 for any sick or personal leave even where leave was authorised, and its internal rules at clauses 2 and 4 fix the 7.30am start and the duty to respect working hours; motorbikes are parked in an area under CCTV with a posted notice that the company does not answer for members' property; overtime is voluntary and paid at the statutory rate. [Para 41][Para 44][Para 47][Para 53][Para 54][Para 56][Para 63]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must fix a clear rule for deducting the attendance bonus, preserving the bonus for late arrival and cutting it in three stages for sick and personal leave? [Para 39][Para 73]
2. Whether the company must compensate two workers whose motorbikes were stolen from the factory compound? [Para 52][Para 121]
3. Whether the company must abolish overtime from 9pm to 11pm? [Para 62][Para 125]
4. Whether the company must abolish short-term employment contracts? [Para 65][Para 136]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** a worker who arrives late has still attended, so losing the whole bonus is wrong, and the company should state how many minutes of lateness are tolerated; the company should pay for the stolen motorbikes because the machines were parked inside its compound; overtime should stop at 7pm because refusal has been met with threats not to renew contracts; and short-term contracts leave workers insecure, exposed to non-renewal whenever they decline overtime or make a small mistake. [Para 42][Para 49][Para 59][Para 64][Para 69]

**Employer (respondent):** the bonus is paid to those who come regularly by the number of working days, as Notification No. 041/11 requires, and the applicant's staged deduction falls below that standard; the company only provides parking, displays a notice and a card disclaiming liability, and both thefts were reported to the police; overtime is voluntary and unpaid coercion is absent; short-term contracts respond to the production chain and cannot be eliminated. [Para 43][Para 48][Para 51][Para 60][Para 67][Para 68]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all four surviving claims were refused: the staged-deduction rule, the motorbike compensation, the abolition of 9pm–11pm overtime, and the abolition of short-term contracts. The panel nonetheless recorded that overtime must remain voluntary and that the attendance bonus should not be lost in full where leave was authorised. [Para 167][Para 168][Para 171][Para 172]

On issue 1 the panel divided the demand. As to lateness, it held that neither Notification No. 041/11 nor the internal rules require the bonus to survive late arrival, and that the earlier award it followed denied the bonus to workers who do not arrive on time; the claim therefore exceeded what the law provides, was an interest dispute, and could not be decided because the claimant union had no certificate of most-representative status. [Para 79][Para 81][Para 85][Para 86][Para 87][Para 95][Para 96]

As to sick and personal leave the panel took the opposite view on character: Notification No. 041/11 does not address absence at all, so the gap had to be filled, and it followed its own earlier awards in holding that a worker whose leave the employer authorised should not forfeit the entire bonus, the employer being entitled to deduct in proportion to the days absent. Because the union's three-stage scale (USD 2, 2 and 3) would give the workers less than that standard, Article 13 made it void, and the claim was refused on that ground. [Para 100][Para 101][Para 103][Para 114][Para 115][Para 116][Para 121]

On issue 2 the panel first asked whether the demand was a collective labour dispute at all under Article 302, and answered no: lost motorbikes concern neither working conditions nor the exercise of union rights nor recognition of a union, and could not disrupt the enterprise or public order. It added that no provision of the Labour Law or any other rule makes an employer liable for workers' property, and that the workers had shown no duty of custody, the company having provided parking, cameras and a notice disclaiming responsibility while the thefts were reported to the police. [Para 121][Para 122][Para 123][Para 124]

On issue 3 the panel found the demand a rights dispute, since it concerned overtime governed by Prakas No. 80 of 1 March 1999, Article 4 of which forbids an employer to compel overtime or to discipline a worker who declines it. On the facts it found no present compulsion: the workers themselves accepted that the earlier threats of non-renewal had ceased, so the employer was applying the voluntary principle, and the claim to abolish the evening shift was refused while the principle was restated. [Para 125][Para 127][Para 128][Para 132][Para 133][Para 135]

On issue 4 the panel treated the demand as a rights dispute but one that ran against the law's own logic: under Article 65 of the Labour Law and Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code a contract arises from matched offer and acceptance, and neither party may force a term on the other. The workers could not compel long contracts, but equally the company could not compel a short one against their will, and a contract freely agreed binds until its expiry. The demand to abolish short-term contracts was therefore refused. [Para 136][Para 138][Para 143][Para 149][Para 161][Para 164][Para 165][Para 166]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus may be withheld for late arrival, because no law guarantees it to a worker who does not come on time, but where the employer has authorised sick or personal leave the bonus may only be reduced in proportion to the days absent, and any agreed scale that would leave the worker with less is void under Article 13; an employer that merely provides parking, with cameras and a notice, owes no duty to compensate workers for stolen vehicles, and a demand touching neither conditions of work nor union rights is not a collective labour dispute at all. [Para 85][Para 103][Para 121][Para 122][Para 123]

**Obiter dicta:** that overtime must rest on the voluntary principle and that neither party may impose a contract duration on the other. [Para 128][Para 163]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is internally inconsistent on the attendance-bonus sub-issue: the reasoning states that the employer must deduct only in proportion to days absent and that the workers should not lose the bonus in full, yet the operative order contains no such direction and instead refuses the claim outright; the operative order has been followed. The union's name appears as both the Free Workers' Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia and a union "of IM and Way", the plant is spelled "IM Way", "Im and Way" and "Im Way" in different places, the number of members is given as 600 while the demands are said to come from the whole workforce, and the signature block is partly destroyed, with fragments where the worker-appointed arbitrator signed. Two sentences are duplicated wholesale in paragraphs 119 and 130, and the reasoning's paragraph numbering repeats. Individual workers, union officers and employer representatives are reduced here to roles or initials. [Para 1][Para 38][Para 114][Para 167][Para 174][Para 119][Para 130]
