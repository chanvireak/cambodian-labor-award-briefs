# Award No. 089/12 — C.P. Cambodia Cutting Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 25 June 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phalla presiding; Seng Vouch Hun appointed by the employer, An Virak appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 140127

## 1. Facts
C.P. Cambodia Cutting Co., Ltd, an animal-feed, food and pig-farming enterprise operating since 1996 with about 995 workers, faced 14 demands from the Cambodia Asia Union at the company, which is not the most representative union there [Para 70][Para 71][Para 72]. Conciliation on 9 April 2012 settled two points, and the file was referred by report No. 363/12 K.B/KN of 3 May 2012 [Para 32][Para 33]. At the hearing of 21 May 2012 three more points were settled (points 1, 5 and 7), point 5 being withdrawn by the union and point 7 agreed with a motorcycle shed to be built from October 2012, and point 1 being left to the parties' own agreement on payment for unused annual leave [Para 36][Para 73][Para 90][Para 96]. That left the wage, allowance, bonus, bonus-deduction and leave claims for decision [Para 37].

## 2. Issues in dispute
2. Whether the company must provide a base wage of 400,000 riel a month to workers? [Para 75]
3. Whether the company must pay a travel allowance of 80 a month to workers living far from the company, and a housing allowance of 80 a month to workers living near it? [Para 80]
4. Whether the company must add 150 to the medical allowance, on top of the 150 already granted to workers each year? [Para 87]
6. Whether the company must raise the seniority payment by USD 1 a month for each year of service, on top of the USD 5 already paid? [Para 92]
8. Whether the company must pay severance in lieu of unused annual leave for the years served from first entry up to 2012? [Para 98]
9. Whether the company must provide one free midday meal to all staff and workers? [Para 101]
10. Whether the company must stop deducting the hours of an absence from the overtime hours already computed at the premium rate? [Para 103]
11. Whether the company must pay the USD 5 monthly health-care subsidy announced by the Prime Minister to workers outside the garment and footwear sector? [Para 111]
12. (Issue 12) Whether the company must increase the annual leave entitlement beyond the statutory minimum? [Para 114][Para 207]
12. Whether the company must increase paid annual leave in step with seniority? [Para 114]

The panel recorded twelve issues and settled issues 1, 5 and 7 [Para 36]; the nine below are the ones it decided.

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — Cambodia Asia Union at C.P. Cambodia):** garment workers have a statutory base wage while they have none, so they ask for 400,000 riel; water and rent have risen since the company withdrew in-factory housing and paid only USD 20 a month instead; workers fall ill more than office staff, who get up to 3,000 a year in medical cover; they want seniority pay like the garment sector; and the company gives only 18 to 19 days' leave even after ten years [Para 75][Para 78][Para 81][Para 87][Para 93][Para 115].

**Employer (respondent — C.P. Cambodia Cutting Co., Ltd):** it has three wage schemes — a fixed 340,000 to 390,000 for staff, a daily 10,000 for workers with 500 to 2,000 added at its discretion, and tonnage rates of 2,400 and 1,850 riel — and applies the old policy; it pays 1,500,000 riel a year in medical costs and cannot add more; the USD 5 is for garment and footwear factories only; and its internal rule gives 18 days, plus one day at three years [Para 75][Para 76][Para 77][Para 89][Para 113][Para 117][Para 118].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to stop deducting days of absence from overtime hours already computed at the premium rate and to make its wage calculations clear, and to grant one extra day of paid annual leave for every three years of service, that is 19 days in years 4 to 6, 20 days in years 7 to 9 and 21 days in years 10 to 12. The base-wage, travel and housing, medical, seniority, severance-in-lieu, midday-meal and USD 5 health-subsidy claims were refused [Para 199][Para 200][Para 201][Para 202][Para 203][Para 204][Para 205][Para 206][Para 207].

The panel disposed of the wage, allowance and subsidy claims on one ground. No law or regulation fixes a base wage in this sector, and there was no collective agreement or convention, so each was a demand above the legal minimum and therefore an interest dispute [Para 119][Para 120][Para 121][Para 122]. It repeated its standing rule that it declines interest disputes where the claiming union is not the most representative, because Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 makes such an award a collective convention binding every worker for a year, and Article 277 fixes the conditions for that status; this union did not hold it [Para 123][Para 125][Para 126][Para 127][Para 128]. The medical, seniority and meal claims fell on the same reasoning once no legal or contractual entitlement was found [Para 136][Para 144][Para 145][Para 165]. On severance for unused leave the panel went further: Article 166 guarantees paid annual leave, Article 167(2) allows payment in lieu only when the contract ends, and Article 167(3) voids any arrangement to pay in lieu or waive leave during the employment, so employees still serving could not be paid out, and it followed awards No. 94/04-ETN and No. 45/05-BNI in refusing [Para 149][Para 151][Para 152][Para 155][Para 157][Para 162]. On point 10 the panel found the employer did deduct absence hours, but from overtime hours already weighted at 1.5 and 2 rather than from actual hours — a worker's 143.5 actual hours appearing as 198.5 in the payroll — and since the basic monthly wage was not reduced the wage was not in fact lost; nevertheless Article 112 requires the employer to make wage conditions clear and easy to understand, so it ordered the practice stopped and the calculations corrected [Para 171][Para 172][Para 173][Para 175][Para 178][Para 179]. On point 12, Article 166(4) adds one working day for each three years of service and internal rule 4.6 says the same; the panel held that adding only one day regardless of seniority breached both, and ordered the step scale [Para 189][Para 191][Para 196][Para 197].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim for a wage, allowance or bonus that no law, collective agreement or internal rule provides is an interest dispute, and the panel will not hear it unless the union bringing it is the most representative in the enterprise, because its award would become a collective convention binding all workers for a year. Separately, annual leave is a guaranteed entitlement that may be bought out only when the contract ends; an employer's practice of granting a single extra day regardless of seniority breaches Article 166(4) and must follow the one-day-per-three-years scale [Para 123][Para 127][Para 155][Para 196].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that no worker's wage was in fact lost through the overtime deduction, and that the practice's real vice was the confusion it caused, not the sum [Para 175].
