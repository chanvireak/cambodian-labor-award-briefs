# Award No. 176/12 — For Tyun Gament garment factory, Kandal province (Arbitration Council, 5 October 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Aeng Thong presiding; Ing Sothy, employer nominee; An Non, worker nominee) · Doc ID 140176

## 1. Facts

The employer is a garment exporting enterprise at Prek Khsev village, Kandal province, which the panel records as employing about 1,200 workers according to the conciliation report, but adopts 1,400 on the employer's statement at the hearing [Para 68]. The claimant is the CCAWDU union at the factory, with 787 members, which holds no certificate of most-representative status: its only certificate was issued on 8 January 2008 and had expired by the hearing date [Para 69][Para 70].

The Ministry of Labour received the union's complaint on 28 June 2012 [Para 13]. Conciliation on 16 August 2012 produced agreement on two of five demands and disagreement on three [Para 14]. The non-conciliation report No. 671/12 of 3 September 2012 reached the Secretariat on 4 September 2012 [Para 10][Para 15]. Hearings were held on 12 September and 1 October 2012 [Para 12][Para 13].

The parties had signed a memorandum of understanding dated 28 September 2010 on improving industrial relations in the garment industry, under which awards on rights disputes are binding and awards on interest disputes are not [Para 21][Para 22][Para 23]. They agreed to extend the award deadline from 26 September to 5 October 2012 [Para 27]. Of the five original demands, one — Issue 1, alleging discrimination against pregnant women through termination of their fixed-duration contracts, seeking reinstatement and back pay — was settled at conciliation and never reached the panel [Para 2][Para 19]. The panel considered only Issues 2 and 3 [Para 20]. The demand for "other benefits" at 100% during sickness was left unresolved because the claimant never specified what those benefits were [Para 145].

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay the full seniority bonus and the full US$5 monthly health-care allowance for the month in which a woman works only part of the month because her maternity leave begins mid-month, rather than a pro-rata amount by days worked? [Para 71][Para 90]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must maintain 100% of wages and other benefits while a worker is absent sick on a certificate issued by a private clinic or private doctor recognised by the government? [Para 77][Para 128]

## 3. Positions of the parties

On Issue 2 the workers demanded that the employer stop reducing seniority and stop pro-rating the regular attendance payment for women on maternity leave, arguing that a woman who works fewer than the full days of a month only because her maternity leave has fallen due should still receive 100% of the seniority bonus and health-care allowance for that month [Para 71]. The employer refused, saying the bonus and allowance are computed by reference to the days actually worked before the leave began, and that on return the worker is paid pro rata to month-end so that nothing is lost [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74][Para 75].

On Issue 3 the workers demanded full wages and benefits during certified sick leave [Para 77][Para 144]. The employer said it maintains wages and allowances only where a state hospital certificate is produced; where the certificate comes from a private clinic or doctor it deducts wages pro rata and the regular attendance bonus under company agreement [Para 79][Para 80]. It explained its distrust by past cases of workers obtaining improper certificates from private clinics [Para 81]. The parties did not dispute certificates from state hospitals, only those from private clinics and doctors [Para 78].

## 4. Award and reasoning

The panel classified both remaining demands as interest disputes, not rights disputes, and declined to consider them.

Its framework: article 312(2) of the Labour Law requires the Council to resolve rights disputes by law and all other disputes by equity [Para 84][Para 87]. A rights dispute is one concerning an entitlement set out in a law, agreement or collective agreement; an interest dispute is one where no such instrument provides the entitlement, and it is decided by equity [Para 88][Para 89].

On Issue 2, the law grants women 90 days' maternity leave with half wages and half allowances [Para 90][Para 91][Para 92]; the panel found the employer had complied with that provision [Para 93]. The demand for 100% for the whole month therefore exceeded what the law gives, which made it an interest dispute [Para 107][Para 108]. The same analysis disposed of the health-care allowance [Para 124][Para 125][Para 127].

On Issue 3, the panel held that sick leave on a valid certificate suspends the employment contract under articles 71(3) and 72, so neither side owes performance and the employer owes no wages absent a contrary rule [Para 128][Para 129][Para 130][Para 131]. No law, internal rule or agreement required wage retention on a private certificate, so the demand again exceeded the law and was an interest dispute [Para 141]. The "other benefits" component was unquantified and could not be decided without a defined subject matter [Para 145][Para 146].

The decisive obstacle was representativeness. Because the 2010 MOU makes rights awards binding but interest awards non-binding [Para 22], and because an interest award becomes a collective agreement binding all workers and stripping non-members of the right to strike over the same subject [Para 110][Para 111], the panel held it may decide an interest dispute only where the claimant union is representative, or several unions together represent more than half the workforce [Para 112]. It followed earlier awards refusing to hear an interest dispute from a union without most-representative status [Para 113][Para 114]. The claimant union held no valid certificate [Para 115], so the panel refused to consider the interest demands [Para 116][Para 117].

**Outcome:** No award on rights; Issue 2 refused (full seniority bonus and US$5 health-care allowance for the partial maternity month) [Para 148]; Issue 3 refused (wage and benefit retention on private-clinic sick certificates) [Para 149]. The interest-dispute rulings become final eight days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Minister [Para 150].

## 5. Ratio decidendi

An award on an interest dispute operates as a collective agreement binding every worker in the enterprise and extinguishes the strike right of non-members, so the Council will decline to decide an interest dispute unless the claimant union holds most-representative status or the unions together represent more than half the workforce. A demand that exceeds what statute, internal rule or agreement grants is an interest dispute, not a rights dispute, and is decided by equity rather than law. "Official doctor" means a graduate of one of the four medical schools holding a valid diploma and a Ministry of Health practice licence [Para 135].
