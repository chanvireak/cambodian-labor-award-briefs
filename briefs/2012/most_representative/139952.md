# Award No. 28/12 — Al Super Industry (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 7 March 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Aing Eng Thong presiding; Ing Sothy, An Non) · Doc ID 139952

## 1. Facts
Al Super Industry (Cambodia) is a garment factory employing about 1,500 workers, of whom 258 joined the claim brought by the Cambodian Workers' Spirit Union federation, a union not yet registered. [Para 50][Para 52][Para 53] Mr K (Khoun Ly) had worked there since 20 April 2006 on an indefinite contract as chief cutter at the cutting table and as a workers' delegate; the company suspended him on 29 December 2011 and asked the labour inspector on 30 December 2011 for permission to dismiss him. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 56][Para 57] Of ten demands, nine were unsettled at conciliation on 10 February 2012 and one more was settled at the hearing, leaving eight. [Para 27][Para 31]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take Mr K back and preserve his benefits, given that the labour inspector approved his dismissal only on 17 February 2012, more than a month after the request? [Para 54][Para 105]
2. Whether the employer must raise the attendance bonus from USD 7 to USD 10 a month? [Para 73][Para 123]
3. Whether the employer may deduct the whole USD 7 attendance bonus when a worker is absent on an authorised day or on certified sick leave, or only pro rata? [Para 77][Para 137]
4. Whether the employer must pay USD 10 a month for lodging and travel? [Para 80][Para 156]
5. Whether the employer must pay a monthly allowance of USD 10 to team leaders and USD 5 to all workers? [Para 82][Para 161]
7. Whether the employer must play an extra 30 minutes of music in the morning and 30 minutes in the afternoon? [Para 92][Para 169]
8. Whether the employer must hold a party with prizes on International Women's Day, 8 March? [Para 95][Para 175]
9. Whether the employer must arrange special health checks twice a year? [Para 99][Para 180]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissal of Mr K was union discrimination, coming two days after the 27 December 2011 election at which he was elected union president and after the company's security guards refused the notification letter; they also said USD 7 was not enough to cover their costs, that raising it to USD 10 would reduce absences and encourage overtime, and that fares had risen from USD 8 to USD 11 and from USD 10 to USD 13 a month. [Para 67][Para 74][Para 75][Para 80] They denied none of the employer's account of Mr K's absences. [Para 66]
**Employer (respondent):** Mr K was absent without leave for 4 days in June 2011, 3 days in July and 1 day in August, slept in the cloth store on 11 August 2011, quarrelled with and threatened team leaders on 29 December 2011, and ignored written warnings; the company says it never received notice that he had become union president. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 62][Para 64][Para 70] It could not raise the bonus or pay travel and lodging because orders were low, and the union had no agreement on any of it. [Para 76][Para 81]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 1 and issue 3 in the workers' favour; issues 2, 4, 5, 7, 8 and 9 refused, most for want of jurisdiction; issue 6 was disposed of by agreement. The award is non-binding and becomes final 8 (eight) days after notification if no party objects to the Minister of Labour. [Para 187][Para 188][Para 189][Para 195]
On issue 1 the panel applied Article 293 of the Labour Law: a workers' delegate may be dismissed only with the labour inspector's agreement, the inspector must answer within one month, and silence is a refusal. [Para 105][Para 112][Para 113] The request went in on 30 December 2011 and the approval came on 17 February 2012, 17 days late, so the panel treated the request as refused and ordered reinstatement once the award takes effect, unless the employer appeals to the Minister and keeps the suspension alive. [Para 116][Para 117][Para 118][Para 122] It followed award 76/04. [Para 114]
On issue 3 the panel held the attendance bonus is wages under Article 103, and that authorised absence or certified sick leave suspends the contract under Articles 71(6) and 72(1), so only the days absent may be deducted; it followed awards 136/11 and 154/11. [Para 143][Para 145][Para 149][Para 152][Para 154]
Issue 2 failed on standing rather than merit: the claim for USD 10 exceeds the USD 7 the notice guarantees and so raises an interest dispute, and the union holds no registration certificate, hence no most-representative status, and cannot bring an interest dispute at all. [Para 125][Para 128][Para 135] Issues 4, 5, 7, 8 and 9 were refused on the same ground — the panel found no statute, collective agreement or agreement of the parties providing for them. [Para 157][Para 163][Para 171][Para 176][Para 184]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The one-month period in Article 293 is mandatory: an inspector's agreement to dismiss a workers' delegate that issues after that month is deemed a refusal, and the employer must reinstate the delegate unless it appeals to the Minister. [Para 112][Para 117][Para 118] A union that is not registered, and so holds no most-representative certificate, cannot bring an interest dispute before the panel at all, whatever the merits of its demand. [Para 128][Para 130][Para 135]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel explained that an award on interests stands in place of a collective agreement for one year and removes the right to strike over the same demands, and it noted that the employer may appeal to the Minister and continue the suspension for up to two months more. [Para 121][Para 132][Para 134]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Arbitrator Ing Sothy again appended a dissent on issue 3, reasoning from dictionary and French doctrinal definitions that a bonus is conditional and its conditions belong to the giver, so a worker who does not meet them receives nothing; it is printed with the award and not reconciled with the majority. [Para 196][Para 198][Para 205][Para 213] Para 125 states the statutory attendance bonus as "two dollars" where para 123 and the operative order say USD 7. [Para 123][Para 125][Para 189] The date of the dismissal request appears as "22 30 December 2011" at para 115 against 30 December 2011 elsewhere. [Para 36][Para 115] The company's Latin name is not printed anywhere on the face of the award, which gives only the Khmer transliteration reproduced above. [Para 1][Para 49] Stray OCR strings ("Nix247", "Xinhua", "Jus") interrupt paras 20, 21 and 32, and the list of demands in the header omits numbering for several items. [Para 20][Para 32] Individual workers and union officers appear only as partial OCR names and are not reproduced here. [Para 1][Para 2]
