# Award No. 091/12 — Tai Yang Enterprises Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 12 June 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phalla presiding; Chhiv Phirom appointed by the employer, An Non appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 140126

## 1. Facts
Tai Yang Enterprises Co., Ltd, a garment factory of about 4,000 workers in Ang Snuol district, Kampong Speu, registered in 1997 and re-registered on 1 July 2011, faced 14 demands from the Khmer Workers' Spirit Federation, which organised about 1,600 of the workers [Para 1][Para 74][Para 78][Para 82]. Conciliation on 7 May 2012 left 14 points unresolved and the file was referred by report No. 384/12 K.B/KN of 8 May 2012 [Para 21][Para 22]. At the hearing of 24 May 2012 four points were settled (points 4, 5, 8 and 10) and the union dropped four more (points 11 to 14), leaving six: points 1 and 2 read together, and points 3, 6, 7 and 9 [Para 25][Para 26]. Two unions existed in the factory and the Free Workers' Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia, not the claimant, was the most representative [Para 79][Para 80].

## 2. Issues in dispute
Point 1 was heard together with point 2:
1. Whether the company must pay USD 15 a month in housing allowance and USD 15 a month in travel allowance to every worker? [Para 83]
3. Whether the company must pay the five termination packages and preserve seniority because it transferred to Tai Nan, or, if it did not transfer, moral damages equal to the last annual wage multiplied by each worker's years of service? [Para 86]
6. Whether the company must raise the milk allowance for women who have given birth from USD 8 to USD 15 a month? [Para 105]
7. Whether the company must extend the early departure of pregnant women from five minutes to fifteen minutes before the end of the shift? [Para 111]
9. Whether the company must raise the regular attendance bonus from USD 7 to USD 10 a month? [Para 121]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — Khmer Workers' Spirit Federation):** the cost of goods has risen, so rent and transport have risen with it, and USD 5 a month in total is not enough; the company moved to Tai Nan on 15 February 2010 and owed the five termination packages with seniority preserved, failing which moral damages; a can of milk costs USD 11 and a baby needs six a month; pregnant women walking in crowded exits risk their safety; and a higher attendance bonus would discourage absence [Para 84][Para 88][Para 106][Para 112][Para 122].

**Employer (respondent — Tai Yang Enterprises Co., Ltd):** it has paid USD 5 a month in housing and travel since May; the company never transferred to Tai Nan; it already pays USD 8 a month for milk and the law requires no more; it allows five minutes and would create a separate exit for pregnant women; and it cannot raise the bonus [Para 85][Para 96][Para 107][Para 116][Para 118][Para 123].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — every one of the six remaining claims was refused: the USD 15 housing and USD 15 travel allowances, the termination packages and moral damages, the milk allowance increase, the additional ten minutes for pregnant women, and the attendance bonus increase [Para 174][Para 175][Para 176][Para 177][Para 178].

On points 1 and 2 the panel found no law, collective convention, agreement or internal rule obliging the employer to pay USD 15 plus USD 15, and the practice was USD 5 in total; the claim therefore went beyond what the law requires and was an interest dispute [Para 126][Para 127][Para 128]. The panel set out its standing rule that it will decline an interest dispute where the claiming union is not the most representative in the factory, because Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 turns such an award into a collective convention binding all workers for one year, removing others' right to strike on the same subject; Article 277 of the Labour Law and the most-representative certificate govern that status [Para 129][Para 131][Para 133][Para 136]. Since the claimant had about 1,600 members against a factory of 4,000 and the other union held the certificate valid to 11 April 2013, the claim was refused [Para 80][Para 137].

On point 3 the panel first had to decide whether the company had moved at all, because both alternatives in the claim depended on it [Para 139]. The union filed nothing showing a transfer, only documents showing Tai Nan's own business registration, and the panel held that the opening of Tai Nan's business was a separate commercial arrangement and not proof of transfer [Para 141][Para 142][Para 143]. The company's registration had merely been re-filed on 1 July 2011, and its NSSF occupational-risk contributions for 3,246 workers were paid in April 2012, so its legal position was unchanged and the workers remained its employees [Para 144][Para 145][Para 146]. Article 91(1) allows moral damages only where one party terminates the contract without valid reason, and here the contracts were running normally, so nothing was owed [Para 148][Para 149][Para 150][Para 151]. On the milk allowance, no law, convention or agreement required more than USD 8, so the panel treated it as an interest dispute and refused it on the same ground [Para 157][Para 158][Para 160]. The pregnancy claim failed likewise, no text requiring fifteen minutes being shown [Para 161][Para 162][Para 164]. On the attendance bonus, Notice No. 041/11 K.B/S.J.N of 7 March 2011 requires USD 7 and the employer was already paying USD 7, so it had complied [Para 165][Para 166][Para 168].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a benefit that no law, collective convention, agreement or internal rule provides is an interest dispute, and the panel will decline to hear it unless the union bringing it is the most representative in the enterprise, because an award on an interest dispute becomes a collective convention binding every worker for a year and so removes the right of non-members to strike for the same thing. Where a claim is pleaded in the alternative on the premise that the employer changed corporate identity, the claimant must prove the change; a new company's registration is not proof that the old one dissolved [Para 129][Para 133][Para 143].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the employer offered a separate exit lane for pregnant women, and that no accident had yet occurred on exit, but did not rest its decision on either point [Para 117][Para 118].
