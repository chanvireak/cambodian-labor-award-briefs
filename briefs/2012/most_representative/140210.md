# Award No. 216/12 — Wing Star Shoes (Arbitration Council, 22 November 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3 (Ing Sothy, An Non, Sok Matherng presiding) · Doc ID 140210

## 1. Facts
Wing Star Shoes is a shoe-sewing company at Kong Pisey, Kampong Speu, which began operating in February 2012 and employs about 7,000 workers. The claimant union was registered in August 2012 with 180 members, while a rival union at the plant holds most-representative status. Of five demands, three were resolved in conciliation and two reached the panel: whether the monthly attendance bonus and the travel and housing allowance may be reduced in proportion to days of absence. On 25 July 2012 the Ministry issued Notification 230, effective 1 September 2012, replacing Notification 041 of 7 March 2011 and raising the minimum attendance bonus from USD 7 to USD 10 a month. The employer had paid the bonus of USD 10 since July 2012 and the travel allowance of USD 10 since February 2012, against a legal minimum of USD 7. [Para 1][Para 22][Para 41][Para 46][Para 61]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must reduce the regular attendance bonus in proportion to the days a worker is absent with the employer's lawful permission, rather than forfeiting the whole bonus? [Para 46]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must keep paying the USD 10 travel and housing allowance in full, without pro-rata deduction, when a worker is absent? [Para 56]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer divides the USD 10 bonus by three and forfeits the whole amount once a worker is absent three days with permission; where a worker has served a full month, losing the entire bonus for one day's permitted absence is plainly unjust, and earlier awards (136/11, 154/11, 169/11) ordered pro-rata deduction. Notification 230 does not require deduction of the travel allowance at all. [Para 47][Para 51][Para 52][Para 59]
**Employer (respondent):** Notification 230 requires attendance "without absence" for the USD 10, so an absent worker has no entitlement; the plant's own practice of paying USD 10 instead of the legal USD 7 is already more generous than the notification, and the allowance is divided by 26 days because the extra USD 3 was meant to encourage attendance. [Para 53][Para 55][Para 58][Para 61]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered that the attendance bonus be reduced only in proportion to the days of unpaid leave taken with the employer's permission, and that the employer continue paying the USD 10 travel and housing allowance without any deduction when a worker is absent. [Para 117][Para 118]
Issue 1: the panel noted that Notification 230 added the words "without absence" (ដោយគ្មានអវត្តមាន), which Notification 041 did not contain, and that the notification nowhere states the consequences of absence. It held that although a bonus is normally a managerial tool within the employer's discretion, the USD 10 attendance bonus in Notification 230 has ceased to be an instrument of management and has become a general legal norm binding every employer in the textile, garment and footwear sector, so its legal regime must follow that of wages under Art 103 of the 1997 Labour Law. [Para 72][Para 77][Para 78]
Meaning of absence: in ordinary workplace usage "absence" means unauthorised absence — not being present without the employer's or its representative's permission — and the various forms of leave in the Labour Law (annual, sick, special, maternity, holiday, weekly) are named separately precisely because each has different consequences. The panel therefore read "absence" in Notification 230 as "unauthorised absence". [Para 84][Para 86][Para 88]
Unpaid permitted leave: leave granted on request without pay is a prior agreement between the worker and employer reducing the working days of that month, and it cannot be treated as absence or unlawful absence; paid leave, by contrast, carries wages so that a worker performing fewer hours is paid proportionally. Paying the full bonus for a day the worker agreed to forgo would be unjust to the employer, so deduction pro rata is the correct measure. [Para 89][Para 95][Para 99][Para 100]
Issue 2: point 1 of Notification 230 fixes the travel and housing allowance at USD 7 a month and attaches no attendance condition, and point 4 applies only to newly hired workers who have not yet completed a month. Since the employer has paid USD 10 since February 2012, it must maintain that amount and may not divide it by 26 days when a worker is absent. [Para 101][Para 111][Para 115][Para 116]
Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented on Issue 1, reasoning that a bonus is by definition conditional and that the employer, as the grantor of the bonus, decides whether the condition has been met, so a worker absent even with permission cannot claim it. [Para 121][Para 139]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The minimum attendance bonus fixed by ministerial notification is a general legal norm and therefore wages, not a discretionary reward; "absence" in it means unauthorised absence, so permitted unpaid leave, being a prior agreement reducing the month's working days, may reduce the bonus pro rata but cannot forfeit it. An allowance fixed by notification without an attendance condition may not be reduced for absence at all. [Para 77][Para 88][Para 111]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel declined to interpret the notification beyond the case before it, saying it would not rule on points unrelated to the dispute. [Para 82]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The arbitrator appointed by the employer is printed as Ing Sothy on the face of the award but as a different spelling of the same name in the signature block [Para 120]; the dissent appendix is signed in the first form. The court address in the hearing notice omits a digit of the street number ("Street 32" for 592 corner 327) [Para 8]. Union officials and worker representatives are named in full and reduced here to roles. The company appears as Wing Star Shoes, Wing Star Shoe factory and Wing Star Star Shoes at different points, and the notification number switches between 230 and 041 in the reasons. Faded OCR fragments interrupt paragraphs 17, 35 and 38.
