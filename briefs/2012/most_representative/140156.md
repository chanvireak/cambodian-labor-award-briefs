# Award No. 143/12 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ម៉ាស្ទ័រ & ហ្វ្រែង (ខេមបូឌា) ខូអិធី ឌី (Master & Frank (Cambodia) C.T.D.) (Arbitration Council, 17 August 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-selected), Tun Si Phan (worker-selected), Vun Saray (president) · Doc ID 140156

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ម៉ាស្ទ័រ & ហ្វ្រែង (ខេមបូឌា) ខូអិធី ឌី is a garment factory in Kandal province sewing medical clothing, employing about 540 workers. [Para 66] The Cambodian Democratic Union Federation of Garment Workers brought the dispute on 138 written mandates; its factory-level union had some 150 members but had only a receipt for its registration application dated 16 July 2012, not a certificate. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 70] Of the 16 demands, the Kandal labour department settled four at conciliation on 18 July 2012 and referred the remaining twelve, and at the hearing the parties settled two more and the workers withdrew five, leaving five demands for decision. [Para 33][Para 37][Para 38] Both parties attended the hearing of 2 August 2012, having met beforehand on 23 July 2012. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 36]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of 4,000 riel per worker per day? [Para 71][Para 209]
2. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must pay USD 1.5 per day as meal money to the two clinic staff, as it does for office staff? [Para 75][Para 210]
3. (Issue 7) Whether the employer must keep the full regular-attendance bonus when a worker takes sick leave supported by a medical certificate? [Para 100][Para 211]
4. (Issue 8) Whether the employer must pay USD 20 a month as a milk allowance in place of the nursery it has already built? [Para 103][Para 212]
5. (Issue 12) Whether the employer or the worker must bear the cost of the separate pair of shoes the employer requires to be worn inside the production room? [Para 107][Para 196]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** wages are too low and prices rising, and other factories in the area pay meal money — USD 3,000 riel at one, 1,500 at another, 1,800, 3,000 and 2,000 riel at others; the clinic staff are employees like office staff and used to receive the USD 1.5; the employer unlawfully deducts the attendance bonus for certified sick leave; and workers cannot afford the separate indoor shoes the employer compels them to buy. [Para 72][Para 81][Para 101][Para 110]
**Employer (respondent):** no law, Prakas or collective agreement requires meal money, milk money or the USD 1.5 for clinic staff; the USD 1.5 is paid only to the 16 office staff and nurses are not office staff because they work in a separate room; the attendance bonus is a wage that may be reduced pro rata for sick-leave days; and the indoor shoes are required by the buyer for hygiene. [Para 73][Para 90][Para 92][Para 102][Para 112]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — on Issue 12 the panel ordered the employer to supply each worker with one pair of shoes to wear inside the production room; Issues 3, 4 and 8 were refused consideration and Issue 7 dismissed, with the non-binding award becoming final after eight days absent written objection. [Para 208][Para 209][Para 210][Para 211][Para 212][Para 213][Para 214]
On Issues 3 and 8 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, no Prakas, no collective agreement and no agreement between the parties requiring either the 4,000 riel lunch allowance or the USD 20 milk money. [Para 115][Para 189] Because the claims went beyond what the law grants, it classified them as interest disputes, and held that it will only decide such a dispute where the union bringing it is the most representative in the factory. [Para 117][Para 118][Para 119] Applying Article 277 of the Labour Law and Article 43 of Prakas 099, it found this union was not yet registered and therefore lacked the standing to bring an interest dispute, so it declined to consider both claims. [Para 123][Para 127][Para 128][Para 129]
On Issue 4 the panel distinguished workers from employees under Articles 5 and 6 of the Labour Law, held that a nurse works with knowledge rather than manual labour and is therefore an employee, and accepted that "employee" generally means the same as office staff. [Para 141][Para 146][Para 147] It could find no legal basis for the USD 1.5 meal money and no evidence the employer had actually paid it in May 2012, and it noted the union held no written mandate from the second clinic staff member, who did not appear. [Para 151][Para 152][Para 133][Para 135] The claim was beyond the law and, as an interest dispute, refused consideration. [Para 158][Para 160]
On Issue 7 the panel treated the regular-attendance bonus as a wage under Article 103 of the Labour Law, read with notification No. 041/11 requiring at least USD 7 a month for regular attendance, and followed its earlier awards on the same point. [Para 169][Para 170][Para 171][Para 177] It held that sick leave authorised by the employer reduces the number of days worked, so the bonus may be cut pro rata to those days; there is no duty to pay it in full. [Para 175][Para 176][Para 180][Para 181]
On Issue 12 the panel applied Article 2(2) of the Labour Law and held that requiring a separate pair of indoor shoes is a genuine work requirement imposed at the buyer's request for hygiene. [Para 196][Para 199][Para 201] But making workers pay for that requirement was not a reasonable exercise of managerial authority, because a worker should not bear costs in order to perform duties for the employer's benefit. [Para 203][Para 204] It ordered one pair per worker and left the replacement interval to the employer. [Para 207][Para 208]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer imposes, for a customer's hygiene standard, a requirement that workers wear a separate pair of shoes inside the production room, the employer must supply that pair: shifting the cost of a requirement imposed for the employer's operational benefit onto the worker is not a reasonable exercise of managerial authority under Article 2(2). By contrast, a claim for an allowance that no law, Prakas or agreement requires is an interest dispute that the panel will not decide unless the claimant union is registered and most representative under Article 277. [Para 199][Para 203][Para 204][Para 128]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the nurses and office staff work in separate rooms, and that office staff are those who speak a foreign language — observations about the workplace that it did not need in order to refuse the meal-money claim. [Para 92][Para 93][Para 140]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award states at para 65 that the parties agreed to make the award binding on rights issues, yet the closing clause declares the award non-binding — a contradiction the source leaves unresolved. [Para 65][Para 214] Paragraph 39 is overrun by unrelated OCR text naming other proceedings and a 2016 date, so the extension of the award date to 17 August 2012 can only be read from the header. [Para 39] The union and federation names are printed in at least four variant forms across the header, the findings and the evidence lists. [Para 1][Para 67][Para 127] The presiding arbitrator's surname is printed one way in the panel line and another in the signature block. [Para 1][Para 215] Two clinic staff are named in the body but only one is reproduced here; the panel itself declined to decide the claim of the second, who neither appeared nor gave a written mandate. [Para 133][Para 134][Para 135] Individual workers are referred to by role only. [Para 99][Para 110]
