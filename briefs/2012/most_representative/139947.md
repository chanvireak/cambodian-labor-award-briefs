# Award No. 22/12 — Win Shingtex (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 5 March 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Run Saray chair) · Doc ID 139947 · unofficial English translation of the Khmer original

## 1. Facts
Win Shingtex (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. runs a garment factory employing 1,134 workers; the local union of C.CAWDU, representing 757 workers, holds a certificate of most representative status and is the claimant. [Para 19] A cleaner began work on 3 September 2009 on a two-month probationary contract and was then given consecutive three-month fixed-duration contracts, each renewal taking effect immediately, until her final contract expired on 31 January 2012, when the employer told her it would not be renewed; total service was 2 years and 4 months on an average wage of US$ 80. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 55] At each expiry the employer paid 5% of the wages earned in that period plus unused annual leave. [Para 19]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate the cleaner, or failing reinstatement pay her the termination benefits of an undetermined duration contract? [Para 2][Para 44]
2. Whether the employer must dismiss the head of administration and the administration assistant? [Para 4][Para 79]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the successive contracts exceeded two years in total, so the contract was one of undetermined duration; the non-renewal was union discrimination, coming after the administration assistant pressed her on the morning of 31 January 2012 to sign a different union's membership card and after another worker's contract was renewed when she agreed to join that union. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 31][Para 32]
**Employer (respondent):** the contract was not renewed because of absences — 12 days unauthorised in November 2011, five in December including half days, and three in January 2012 — and poor performance, and it denies union discrimination; as to the two staff, they are competent, have improved the administration, and it will educate rather than dismiss them. [Para 23][Para 25][Para 33][Para 38][Para 39][Para 40]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — on issue 1 the panel held the contract to be of undetermined duration and ordered the employer to pay the termination package of such a contract: outstanding wages if not yet received, dismissal indemnity of 30 days' wages, and payment in lieu of unused annual leave, with the cleaner to repay the 5% severance she received over her 2 years and 4 months; on issue 2 the demand was rejected. [Para 96]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 67(2), which caps a fixed-duration contract at two years and allows renewal only so long as the renewals do not surpass that maximum, any violation converting the contract into one of undetermined duration. [Para 46][Para 48] Following award 10/03-Jacqsintex, it read the two-year limit as a cap on total duration rather than on each renewal, a reading reinforced by Article 73(5) and by paragraph 3 of ILO Recommendation 166 of 1982, which discourages fixed-duration contracts for long-term employment. [Para 48][Para 51][Para 53] Her contracts ran from 3 September 2009 to 31 January 2012, so the contract converted. [Para 55][Para 56]

That conversion then fixed the termination entitlements. Article 74 lets either party terminate an undetermined duration contract at will but requires written notice and a valid reason relating to aptitude or behaviour or the needs of the enterprise, so the absences and poor performance were a reason but not a basis for damages. [Para 57][Para 60][Para 65][Para 66] Article 89 gave a dismissal indemnity, fixed by the panel at 30 days' wages; [Para 61][Para 63] Article 91 damages were refused because the employer had a valid reason; [Para 65][Para 67] Articles 75 and 77 required one month's notice for service between two and five years, which was not given, so compensation in lieu of notice of one month's wages was owed; [Para 67][Para 69][Para 70] Articles 166 and 167 entitled her to payment in lieu of unused annual leave; [Para 71][Para 73][Para 74] Article 116 required final wages within 48 hours. [Para 74] Because the contract had never lawfully been fixed-duration, the 5% severance paid at each purported expiry had to be returned, leaving her no worse off overall. [Para 77][Para 79]

On issue 2 the panel repeated the Article 65 and Civil Code 664 and 311 analysis: the two staff have separate contracts with the employer, the workers are third parties, and dismissal could only be compelled on proof that the men were dangerous and that retaining them would harm the workplace. [Para 84][Para 87][Para 89] The complaints — a physician no longer permanently on standby, unhelpfulness in negotiations, one of them using the administration office for his own union — did not meet that test, and the employer had undertaken to educate. [Para 91][Para 94][Para 95]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Consecutive fixed-duration contracts whose total length exceeds two years convert into a contract of undetermined duration by operation of Articles 67(2) and 73(5); the employer then owes the full termination package for such a contract — notice compensation, dismissal indemnity and payment in lieu of unused annual leave — and must recover the severance it paid at each purported expiry, since those expiries were legally ineffective. A valid reason for not renewing bars damages but not the indemnity. [Para 48][Para 51][Para 63][Para 70][Para 79]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that ILO Recommendation 166 is not binding but is a useful aid to interpreting Article 67, and its general preference for undetermined duration contracts as a matter of employment security, are interpretive observations rather than operative reasons. [Para 50][Para 52][Para 53]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The face of the award announces a "Dissenting opinion by Arbitrator Ing Sothy" [Para 1], but no dissent is set out anywhere in the translated text and the decision is recorded as unanimous; the dissent's content is unavailable.
- The reasons award one month's wages as compensation in lieu of notice [Para 70], and the analysis of Article 75 supports it [Para 67][Para 69], but the operative order at [Para 96] lists only outstanding wages, the 30-day dismissal indemnity and annual leave, omitting notice pay. The omission is reported, not repaired.
- The employer pays "5% of the wages" at each contract expiry [Para 19], a figure that corresponds to no provision cited in the award, and the repayment order at [Para 79] assumes those sums were received in full; no amounts are computed.
- The evidence section is omitted from the English version with a direction to the Khmer original [Para 18][Para 19].
- The translation drops the letter "t" throughout ("themployer", "fixeduration", "totalength"), and the internal work rules quotation at [Para 26] breaks off mid-category ("considered or six consecutive days").
