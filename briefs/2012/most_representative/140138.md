# Award No. 104/12 — Hai Yun Garmin (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 2 July 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Aing Aeng Thong presiding; Chhiv Phirom, An Non) · Doc ID 140138

## 1. Facts
Hai Yun Garmin is a garment factory at Damnak Thom, Stung Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, with about 300 workers. [Para 35] A strike over 21 demands produced three unsettled claims: the deduction of the monthly regular attendance bonus, a $15 housing or transport allowance, and the fate of three dismissed workers who had stood as candidates in a union election. [Para 16][Para 17] The employer pays a regular attendance bonus — $10 a month, raised to $12 from 25 May 2012 — under Notice No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011, which guarantees at least $7 to workers who attend every working day of the month. [Para 41][Para 86][Para 109] The three workers, respectively machine chief, warehouse chief and mechanic, gave notice of their candidacy at 09:40 on 17 May 2012; the accountant read the notice but refused to sign for it, and that afternoon security barred two of them from the factory. [Para 57][Para 64][Para 65][Para 74][Para 75]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer may deduct the whole regular attendance bonus from a worker who is absent with the employer's lawful permission, and whether it must leave that bonus intact where the absence is without permission? [Para 41][Para 79][Para 86][Para 109]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to pay each worker $15 a month as a housing or transport allowance? [Para 53][Para 117]
3. Whether the employer must take back the three dismissed workers, who were candidates for the union leadership, in their former posts with their former pay and with back wages? [Para 57][Para 126]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the bonus is wages under Article 103, so the internal rules on sick leave bar any deduction there; for permitted absence the deduction should at most be pro rata, low pay making such leave necessary; other factories already pay the $15 allowance; the three were candidates dismissed on 17 May and must return with back pay. [Para 42][Para 49][Para 53][Para 54][Para 62]

**Employer (respondent):** retaining the bonus during sick leave is its settled practice and will continue; but it cannot keep it for permitted or unauthorized absence, because the $10 paid already exceeds what the law requires; it cannot afford the $15; and it dismissed the three on 18 May 2012 for leading a strike that caused heavy losses. [Para 43][Para 51][Para 56][Para 63]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — deduction for absence with the employer's permission is limited to a pro-rata amount (the claim that no deduction at all be made is refused); the employer may deduct the whole bonus for unauthorized absence; the $15 allowance was not considered; and the employer must reinstate the three dismissed workers with back wages from 18 May 2012 and full pay for 17 May 2012. [Para 106][Para 116][Para 125][Para 155][Para 157][Para 159]

On standing, the factory union had not been elected or registered and could not act itself, but Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 lets a party appear through a person holding its written authorisation, and 268 of roughly 300 workers had thumbprinted authorisations for the federation. [Para 37][Para 79][Para 80][Para 82][Para 83]

On issue 1, the panel held the bonus to be a wage: Article 103 includes sums paid as a bonus, and Notice No. 041/11 was issued as a reward to workers. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 95] Since the notice does not define the working days of a month, the panel adopted Award No. 112/11: the reference is to the days the employer requires work or the law obliges the worker to serve. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 92] Leave lawfully granted suspends the contract under Articles 71(6) and 72, so no wage is due for those days and therefore no bonus — but only in proportion to the days absent. [Para 96][Para 98][Para 99][Para 101][Para 104][Para 106] Unauthorized absence is different: the bonus exists to encourage full attendance, and a worker absent without permission has no entitlement at all, so deducting the whole bonus for that month is lawful. [Para 110][Para 112][Para 116] The parties agreed at the hearing to keep the bonus during certified sick leave, so the panel did not rule on it. [Para 44][Para 45]

On issue 2, no provision of the Labour Law, no collective agreement and no contract obliged the employer to pay $15, which made the demand an interests dispute rather than a rights dispute. [Para 117][Para 118] Only a union that is the most representative in the enterprise may conclude a collective agreement or bring an interests dispute, and the factory union here was neither elected nor registered; the panel therefore declined to consider the claim. [Para 119][Para 120][Para 122][Para 123][Para 124][Para 125]

On issue 3, the protection of Article 293 — dismissal of a candidate only with the labour inspector's consent, extended to union candidates by Article 4 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 for 45 days either side of the election — did not apply, because the union never fixed an election date and the panel could not tell whether the dismissal fell inside the protected period. [Para 126][Para 127][Para 130][Para 132][Para 133][Para 134] The dismissal was nonetheless unlawful: Articles 12 and 279 forbid using union membership or activity as the ground for discipline or termination, and the employer admitted telling the three it would dismiss them if they went ahead, which showed its intention, anger notwithstanding; Article 333 voids any sanction for taking part in a strike. [Para 135][Para 136][Para 142][Para 144][Para 146][Para 147] Two were barred on returning from lunch and so dismissed on 18 May, the third at about 12:15 on 17 May; the employer did not contest that 17 May was not a working day from the afternoon shift, hence full pay for that day and back wages from 18 May. [Para 149][Para 151][Para 152][Para 153]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus is a wage but one earned by attendance: lawfully granted leave suspends the contract, no wage is due for those days, and the bonus may be deducted only in proportion to them; unauthorized absence forfeits the whole bonus. Dismissing workers who campaigned for a union is unlawful under Articles 12, 279 and 333 even where the 45-day protection of Article 293 does not attach because no election date was fixed, and reinstatement with back wages follows. An interests claim lies only for a representative, registered union. [Para 101][Para 112][Para 133][Para 146][Para 124]

**Obiter dicta:** the remarks that the bonus encourages hard work and that Cambodian workers do not take leave without need are explanatory colour, as is the observation that an interests award displaces the collective agreement for a year under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 50][Para 46][Para 120]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The conciliation date conflicts internally: the reasons record conciliation on 25 December 2011, while the non-conciliation report and the minutes are dated 31 May 2012 and 25 May 2012 respectively. [Para 17][Para 18][Para 32]
- The third worker's name gains an extra element in the operative paragraph, appearing as a three-part name there and a two-part name in the reasons; the initials used in this brief are keyed to the form used in the reasons. [Para 57][Para 154]
- The bonus is described three ways — at least $7 under Notice No. 041/11, $10 actually paid, and $12 from 25 May 2012 — and the panel never reconciles them. [Para 41][Para 51][Para 86]
- The list of worker representatives breaks off at two names on the first page and resumes at three to seven on the next, so the delegation is incomplete on the face of the award. [Para 2][Para 4]
- Latin debris interrupts the Khmer text throughout ("State", "Space", "Li", "New", "Man", "Mail", "Sill", "Apple"), and page numbers split words. [Para 12][Para 19][Para 65][Para 79][Para 92][Para 96][Para 121][Para 146]
- The signature block is corrupted: the chair's name is preceded by a stray numeral and the signature line shows "tractic". [Para 161]
- Sentences are repeated verbatim by OCR at [Para 65], [Para 113] and [Para 160]; no witnesses or experts gave evidence outside the parties. [Para 22]
