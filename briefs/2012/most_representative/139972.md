# Award No. 42/12 — Inkyung Wondang Apparel Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 20 March 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 139972

## 1. Facts
Inkyung Wondang Apparel Co., Ltd. runs a garment factory at Chamka Oulek Village, Kakab Commune, Pursenchey District, Phnom Penh, employing 1,400 workers; the claimant is the Local Union of CFLU, which represents 428 workers and holds no most representative status (MRS). [Para 17][Para 19] Ten demands were referred by report No. 200 KB/RK/VK dated 23 February 2012; at the hearing the workers withdrew issues 3, 8 and 10, leaving seven unresolved. [Para 2][Para 15] The employer was absent "due to a prior engagement", the workers asked the panel to proceed in absentia, and the employer filed evidence and documents on its practice on 9 March 2012, to which the workers made no objection. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 21][Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must maintain wages and attendance bonuses when workers take sick leave and supply a proper doctor's certificate? [Para 2]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must provide a 2,000 riel meal allowance when workers perform overtime on Sundays? [Para 2]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must allow workers to take leave for a personal commitment? [Para 2]
4. (Issues 5 and 7) Whether the employer must refrain from forcing workers to work overtime and from scheduling overtime on pay day? [Para 3][Para 5]
5. (Issue 6) Whether the employer must pay monthly wages by 4:00 p.m. and compensate any delay in payment? [Para 3][Para 4]
6. (Issue 9) Whether the employer must instruct supervisors and group leaders not to verbally abuse workers? [Para 5]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer reduces daily wages and the US$ 7 attendance bonus during authorised sick leave; it pays no 2,000 riel meal allowance for Sunday overtime although a Prakas requires one; leave for personal commitments is refused or rebuked; the employer rebukes workers who decline overtime; wages are paid at 4:00 p.m. so some arrive home late; and a group 15 leader called them "You slacker!" and "You are blind!". [Para 24][Para 32][Para 35][Para 42][Para 53][Para 55][Para 60][Para 61]
**Employer (respondent):** through its written evidence it maintains daily wages but deducts the attendance bonus in proportion to days worked, pays a total overtime payment of US$ 5.50 including US$ 0.82 (3,200 riel) meal allowance, requires a signed request form before overtime, pays wages from 3:30 p.m. to 4:00 p.m. on the 10th, and does not tolerate abusive behaviour. [Para 26][Para 34][Para 45][Para 59][Para 62]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — a non-binding award ordering the employer to continue its practice of maintaining wages during authorised sick leave, to maintain the US$ 0.82 (3,200 riel) Sunday overtime meal allowance, to continue facilitating personal leave, to maintain voluntary overtime, to maintain payment of wages during working hours, and to instruct the group 15 supervisor not to use abusive language; the demand for the full attendance bonus was declined. [Para 123][Para 124][Para 125]

The panel first held that it could proceed in the employer's absence under Clause 21 of Prakas No. 099, following 148/07-Pay Her and 99/09-Kingsland, and that Clause 25 let it receive the documents the employer filed after the hearing. [Para 63][Para 65] On issue 1 it found the employer had not in fact deducted wages for authorised sick leave, so that part of the demand was already met. [Para 66][Para 67] The attendance bonus, being at least US$ 7 under point 1 of Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011, is a gratuity and therefore wage under Article 103; absence certified by a qualified doctor suspends the contract under Article 71(3) and Article 72(1), so no bonus is earned for those days, but the employer may only deduct proportionately, as ordered in 62/07-Hong Mei and 110/12-Chung Fai. [Para 70][Para 71][Para 73][Para 74][Para 76][Para 77] A demand for the full bonus exceeds the law, making it an interests dispute, which the panel will not hear from a union without MRS under Article 277 and Clause 43 of Prakas No. 099; the union represented 428 of 1,400 workers and held no certificate. [Para 80][Para 82][Para 83] On issue 2, point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 requires either a 2,000 riel allowance or a free meal, and the US$ 0.82 actually paid equals 3,200 riel, so the employer was ordered to maintain that practice, which is more favourable than the notification. [Para 86][Para 89][Para 90] On issue 4 the panel held that leave is subject to management prerogative under Article 2 paragraph 2, following 37/07-JRB and 99/09-Kingsland, that the workers must use the employer's one-day advance form, but that refusal is unreasonable where enough workers remain to fill the assembly line. [Para 92][Para 93][Para 96][Para 97] On issues 5 and 7, Clause 4 of Prakas No. 80 of 1 March 1999 forbids coercion over overtime, following 118/07-Ja Ding and 107/08-Seratex; since a signature is required before overtime and no coerced worker was identified, the employer had complied. [Para 98][Para 102][Para 104] On issue 6, Articles 115(3) and 137 require wages to be paid during working hours, which the payment window of 3:30 p.m. to 4:00 p.m. satisfies. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 110][Para 111] On issue 9 the panel found abuse of a worker over a slight mistake inappropriate and ordered the employer to instruct the group 15 supervisor accordingly. [Para 119][Para 120][Para 121]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus that a notification fixes at a minimum monthly sum is a gratuity and so part of wages under Article 103; because authorised sick leave supported by a qualified doctor's certificate suspends the contract under Articles 71(3) and 72(1), the employer owes no bonus for the days absent but may deduct only in proportion to them, and a demand for the whole bonus is an interests dispute that the panel will decline from a union without MRS. [Para 70][Para 76][Para 77][Para 80]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's advice that workers who are in fact coerced may file a complaint, and its view that the employer should discipline workers rather than let a supervisor abuse them, are guidance going beyond the orders made. [Para 103][Para 120]
