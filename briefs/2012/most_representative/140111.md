# Award No. 169/11 — Four Toon Thiew Garment Factory Ltd (Arbitration Council, 18 January 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed — dissenting; Tuon Si Phan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · Doc ID 140111

## 1. Facts
The Khmer Youth Voice Federation and its local union at Four Toon Thiew Garment Factory Ltd, a plant of about 400 workers with roughly 50 union members, filed twelve demands on 18 November 2011; conciliation on 5 December left seven unresolved, and one (hot and cold water and fish sauce) was settled at the hearing, leaving six. [Para 18][Para 22][Para 28][Para 44][Para 46]

The six were: proportional rather than total deduction of the USD 7 attendance bonus for one to three days of authorised personal leave; hygiene workers' right to overtime; equal pay with piece-rate workers; conversion of fixed-term contracts older than two years into indefinite ones; a meal or 4,000 riel for Sunday overtime; and payment of the whole 50% maternity wage and allowances before leave begins. The union holds no certificate of most-representative status. [Para 48][Para 53][Para 71][Para 79][Para 86][Para 92]

The company deducts the entire USD 7 bonus for any personal leave, even authorised leave; pays maternity benefit month by month; pays overtime at the statutory rate with no meal; and engages piece-rate workers supplied by a contractor. [Para 50][Para 60][Para 86][Para 205]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must reduce the attendance bonus proportionally, and not in full, when a worker takes one to three days of personal leave authorised by the employer? [Para 94][Para 112]
2. Whether the union may claim that the six hygiene workers be allowed to work overtime like production workers? [Para 120][Para 121]
3. Whether the company must pay all ordinary workers the same wage as piece-rate workers? [Para 130][Para 133]
4. Whether the company must convert fixed-term contracts of more than two years into indefinite contracts? [Para 142][Para 160]
5. Whether the company must provide a meal or 4,000 riel for overtime worked on Sundays? [Para 168][Para 182]
6. Whether the company must pay the full 50% maternity wage and allowances for the 90 days at least one day before maternity leave begins? [Para 192][Para 195]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** a worker absent a day or two on authorised business still attended most of the month, so losing the whole bonus is unfair; the six hygiene workers do more cleaning when production runs overtime and should share the extra hours; piece-rate workers sew the same garments and should not be paid more; after two years a contract should become indefinite, or seniority is lost; Sunday work merits a meal; and maternity pay should be paid in one sum before the worker stops work. [Para 49][Para 57][Para 73][Para 87]

**Employer (respondent):** the bonus is paid as the internal rules and Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 provide; hygiene workers get overtime whenever there is extra work; piece-rate workers are paid and supervised by the contractor and receive no attendance or seniority payments, so the situations differ; contracts never exceed two years and on expiry the company pays the 5% termination payment and carries seniority forward; no law requires a meal for Sunday overtime; and maternity pay cannot be paid in one sum, though a woman may authorise a relative to collect it. [Para 52][Para 5][Para 68][Para 88]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the three-stage deduction was refused, but the employer must reduce the attendance bonus in proportion to days of authorised personal leave; must pay 2,000 riel or provide one free meal for Sunday overtime; and must pay the full 50% wage and allowances for the 90-day maternity leave at least one day before leave begins. The claims on hygiene workers' overtime, equal pay with piece-rate workers, and conversion of contracts were refused. [Para 209][Para 213][Para 214][Para 215]

On issue 1 the panel held the attendance bonus to be a wage, since Article 103 lists sums paid as a reward among the constituents of wages, and read Notification No. 041/11 as guaranteeing USD 7 to workers who attend the month's working days. It distinguished three situations: full attendance, where the bonus is paid in full; absence on statutory paid leave such as public holidays and annual leave under Articles 161 and 166, which does not reduce the bonus; and absence on leave the employer authorised outside those entitlements, where the bonus is earned only for days worked and may be reduced in proportion and no more. The union's scale of USD 2, 2 and 3 would leave the worker worse off and was void under Article 13. [Para 103][Para 108][Para 110][Para 113][Para 119]

On issue 2 the panel never reached the merits: under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 a union may act for workers only with their written authorisation, and the six hygiene workers were not members, did not attend and gave none. [Para 121][Para 122][Para 126]

On issue 3 the panel applied Article 106, which requires equal pay only where conditions of work, professional skill and output are all equal. Ordinary workers put in eight hours a day under the company's direction and are paid by the company; piece-rate workers put in ten hours, are paid by the contractor out of a lump sum and are supervised by its staff. One condition failed. [Para 131][Para 133][Para 140]

On issue 4 the panel set out the law on contract types under Articles 65 and 66 and the Civil Code on offer and acceptance, holding that no party may impose a contract form or duration on the other and that conversion after two years is not automatic. The union produced no list of the workers concerned, no contracts and no evidence of seniority by the deadline of 3 January 2012. [Para 142][Para 149][Para 161][Para 163][Para 167]

On issue 5 the panel found Sunday work to be overtime, since it exceeds the normal day and week under Article 137, and held that point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 entitles a worker who volunteers for overtime at the employer's request to 2,000 riel a day or one free meal, without any hour threshold. As the company had never paid it, the panel ordered payment at that statutory rate and refused the higher 4,000 riel claim as an interest demand beyond the law. [Para 170][Para 176][Para 182][Para 191]

On issue 6 the panel found maternity benefit to be part of wages under Article 103 and held Article 115 applicable: wages must not be paid on a rest day, and if the due date falls on one, payment must be made the day before. Monthly payment during the leave breached it, so the employer must hand over the whole 50% for the 90 days at least one day before the leave starts. [Para 196][Para 201][Para 208]

Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented on issue 1: he agreed the union's staged scale should be refused, but objected that the panel then ordered a proportional deduction nobody had claimed, and he treated the bonus as a discretionary reward whose conditions belong to the employer. [Para 222][Para 223][Para 230][Para 237]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus is a wage, so it must be paid in full where absence is on statutory paid leave and may be reduced only in proportion to days of authorised personal leave not worked; and a union may not bring a claim for workers who are not members, did not appear and gave no written authority. [Para 106][Para 110][Para 122][Para 125]

**Obiter dicta:** that equal pay under Article 106 presupposes identical conditions of work, skill and output, so a contractor's lump-sum workers are not comparable to the client's own employees. [Para 136][Para 140]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award names the company three ways — "Four Toon Thiew", "For Thyeun Thiev" and variants reversing the word order — and prints the union both as a federation and as a local union. The reasoning records the conciliation reference as 1389 and as 1384 and misdates the conciliation minute as 5 November rather than 5 December 2011. The evidence list and operative order contain garbled fragments ("Company Leard Marker"), and the signature block is largely destroyed, with "cackbam" where the chair signed; the dissent's citations are corrupted. Individuals are reduced to roles or initials. [Para 1][Para 18][Para 23][Para 126][Para 217]
