# Award No. 27/12 — Ying Dong Shoes Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 15 March 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bunchea presiding; Ing Sothy, Liv Sovanna) · Doc ID 139951

## 1. Facts
Ying Dong (យីង ដុង) is a shoe factory at Borey Sen Monorom, Chom Chao, Dangkao, Phnom Penh, employing about 1,300 workers, 1,240 of them women, and the Cambodian Workers' Union at the factory holds the most-representative certificate of 5 November 2010, valid to 5 November 2012. [Para 56][Para 85][Para 131] On 9 January 2012 the federation filed 15 demands; conciliation on 1 February 2012 settled 3, and at the hearing of 22 February 2012 the parties settled 4 more (demands 1, 2, 7 and 10), leaving eight for the panel. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 38] The parties had signed the Memorandum of Understanding of 28 September 2010, so the panel split the award into rights disputes (binding at once) and interest disputes (non-binding). [Para 40][Para 41][Para 42]

## 2. Issues in dispute
3. Whether the employer may deduct the whole monthly attendance bonus when a worker is absent on one lawfully authorised day, or only pro rata to the days absent? [Para 59][Para 102]
4. Whether the employer must add a Khmer administrator alongside the Chinese administrative head? [Para 63][Para 124]
5. Whether the employer must pay USD 15 a month per worker for lodging and travel? [Para 72][Para 140]
6. Whether the employer must provide a nursing room and nursery under Article 186, and pending construction must it pay USD 10 a month for March, April and May 2012? [Para 81][Para 84]
8. Whether the USD 5 monthly health allowance must be added into the base wage? [Para 86][Para 166]
9. Whether the employer must pay a further USD 5 a month attendance bonus on top of the USD 7 already paid? [Para 88][Para 175]
11. Whether the employer must respect the stated start and finish times and pay overtime for the 10 minutes a day workers are required to arrive early? [Para 92][Para 184]
12. Whether the employer must pay 50 per cent of wages for the three months before maternity leave, ahead of the leave? [Para 99][Para 200]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the attendance bonus is wages, and losing the whole USD 7 for a half day, even with the employer's permission, discourages them; they also sought USD 15 each a month for lodging and travel because rents and fares have risen and they work Sundays and holidays when asked; a Khmer administrator is needed because the Chinese head does not understand Khmer custom or law and insults workers; the health allowance should swell the base wage so overtime is computed on it; and maternity pay should be released before the leave begins. [Para 60][Para 72][Para 73][Para 86][Para 133][Para 200]
**Employer (respondent):** the bonus goes only to workers who come regularly and never stop, the company cannot add USD 15 a month, the Chinese administrator has lived and worked in Cambodia 13 years and knows Khmer law, and it had no evidence of the alleged insults; it is building a nursery to open on 1 June 2012; the health allowance is expressly not part of the base wage; it already pays USD 7 bonus plus USD 5 cooperation money; and it provides 11 vans plus 2 more for overtime travel. [Para 62][Para 68][Para 69][Para 76][Para 82][Para 90][Para 146][Para 171][Para 181]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issues 3, 6, 11 and 12 in the workers' favour; issues 4, 5, 8 and 9 dismissed. [Para 213][Para 216][Para 220][Para 222]
Issue 3 was classed as a rights dispute because clause 1 of Ministry Notice No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 guarantees at least USD 7 a month to workers who attend the working days of the month, and Article 103 of the Labour Law counts bonuses as wages. [Para 105][Para 106][Para 113] Reading Article 71(6) and Article 72(1) together with its earlier awards 136/11, 158/11, 28/12, 112/11, 03/12 and 68/11, the panel held that authorised absence suspends the contract, so the employer may deduct the bonus only in proportion to the days of authorised absence, not in full. [Para 117][Para 120][Para 122][Para 123]
Issue 6 turned on Article 186: an enterprise with at least 100 women workers must provide a nursing room and a nursery, and where it cannot take children over 18 months a mother may place the child elsewhere at the employer's expense against actual invoices. [Para 154][Para 155][Para 160] Because the company is still building and had none in operation, the panel ordered a room in or near the factory until the new nursery opens, plus payment of outside invoices for children aged 18 months and over, relying on award 81/09. [Para 83][Para 161][Para 164][Para 165] It refused the USD 10 a month bridging allowance as having no legal basis. [Para 163]
Issues 4, 5 and 9 were interest disputes for which no statute, collective agreement or agreement of the parties provided: hiring staff is the employer's prerogative, rent and fares are outside its control, and the bonus already paid met the notice. [Para 125][Para 138][Para 147][Para 150][Para 178][Para 182] Issue 8 was a rights dispute but failed on the text: clause 1 of Notice No. 206 of 13 December 2011 says the USD 5 health allowance is not added into the base wage. [Para 168][Para 171][Para 174]
Issue 11 succeeded on Articles 137 and 139: the panel found the team meetings were held at 6:50 and 12:20, outside the 7:00–11:30 and 12:30–16:00 hours, using 20 minutes of the workers' time, and ordered the employer to respect the working hours. [Para 188][Para 194][Para 196][Para 199] Issue 12 succeeded on Articles 182, 183, 115 and 103: 90 days' maternity leave at 50 per cent of wages and allowances must be paid before the leave begins, following awards 139/08 and 109/09. [Para 203][Para 208][Para 209][Para 212]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Because an authorised absence suspends the employment contract under Article 71(6) and Article 72(1), the attendance bonus — which is wages under Article 103 — may be reduced only in proportion to the days of authorised absence; an employer may not forfeit the whole month's bonus, and the same proportional logic bars it from paying less than the statutory minimum for the days actually worked. [Para 105][Para 117][Para 120] Maternity pay of 50 per cent for 90 days is earned before the leave and must be paid in advance, not in monthly instalments during it. [Para 205][Para 209][Para 212]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel explained at length that only a union holding most-representative status may bring an interest dispute, since an award on interests substitutes for a collective agreement and removes others' right to strike over it — observations that did not decide any of the eight issues. [Para 127][Para 129][Para 130]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Arbitrator Ing Sothy filed an appended dissent on issue 3, arguing from dictionary definitions of "bonus" that a bonus is conditional and its conditions belong to the giver, so a worker who does not meet them forfeits it; the majority reasoning and the dissent are both on the face of the award. [Para 224][Para 226][Para 232][Para 242] The opening date of the nursery is printed as "1 September June 2012" at para 161 and as 1 June 2012 at para 82. [Para 82][Para 161] The non-conciliation report is No. 145 dated 9 February 2012 at paras 30 and 51 but 8 February 2012 at para 35. [Para 30][Para 35][Para 51] Article 186 is quoted with OCR debris ("ក្មេង … ២០១៧") where the text should read one hundred women workers, and Article 137 is garbled into "fifty-eight hours a week" at para 188 against forty-eight at para 191. [Para 154][Para 188][Para 191] The employer's USD 5 cooperation payment appears as "USD 56 (five)" at para 181, and paras 76 and 109 carry untranslatable OCR strings ("AKANA", "World Chammer Champions…"). [Para 76][Para 109][Para 181] The claim on nurseries is stated as USD 15 a month for 24 months in the demand list but as USD 10 a month for three months at the hearing. [Para 12][Para 84] Individual workers, union officers and the employer's representatives are reduced to partial OCR names and are not reproduced here. [Para 2]
