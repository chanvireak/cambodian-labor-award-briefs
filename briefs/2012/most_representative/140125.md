# Award No. 189/11 — Van Sing Workshop Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 2 February 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phalla presiding; Ing Sothy appointed by the employer, An Non appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 140125

## 1. Facts
Van Sing Workshop Co., Ltd, which began operating in July 2010 and employs 565 workers, was met with 13 demands from the C.CAWDU union at the company, rising out of a strike; after conciliation 13 points were referred by report No. 1475 K.B/AK of 30 December 2011 [Para 1][Para 17][Para 50]. At the hearing of 10 January 2012 the union withdrew five of them (points 3, 4, 6, 9 and 10), leaving eight to be decided [Para 25]. The company union had 382 members but was not the most representative union in the enterprise [Para 53]. Three union leaders had been suspended without a fixed term on 17 December 2011, and no notice of the suspension was sent to the labour inspector [Para 74].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay a seniority bonus to workers who have served one year or more? [Para 54]
2. Whether the company must organise elections of employee delegates? [Para 60]
5. Whether workers may use their annual leave entitlement to cover special (compassionate) leave? [Para 63]
7. Whether the company must recognise medical certificates from private clinics and keep paying wages during sick leave? [Para 66]
8. Whether the company must take back the three suspended union leaders and pay them back wages from the date of suspension? [Para 71]
11. Whether the company must pay a 3,000 riel allowance for each overtime hour worked from 6 p.m. onwards? [Para 83]
12. Whether the attendance bonus must be reduced in proportion to days of authorised absence rather than forfeited entirely? [Para 87]
13. Whether the company must maintain wages and the attendance bonus for the two workers injured at work? [Para 90]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — C.CAWDU):** seniority bonus was never paid and is owed from first entry into service; the absence of employee delegates makes disputes hard to resolve and pushes workers into strikes; annual leave should cover compassionate absences; private clinic certificates are quicker and closer than public hospitals; the three leaders were suspended for union activity; overtime pay after 6 p.m. does not cover meals and travel; the attendance bonus is forfeited for a single absent morning; and two injured workers had wages and bonuses cut [Para 54][Para 60][Para 64][Para 67][Para 79][Para 84][Para 88][Para 91].

**Employer (respondent — Van Sing Workshop Co., Ltd):** it did not answer the seniority, delegate, annual-leave, overtime, bonus and injury claims, asking the panel to decide the delegate question [Para 59][Para 62][Para 65][Para 86][Para 90][Para 95]. On the union leaders it said one had knocked over cartons of goods, showed photographs of the fallen cartons and produced only an unsigned draft letter to the Ministry [Para 75][Para 76][Para 80]. It argued that where a worker is absent more than four days after a work accident the wages fall on the National Social Security Fund [Para 95].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must pay the seniority bonus to all workers with one year or more of service counted from July 2010, including the 66 workers listed in the appendix, serving and departed; organise elections of full and deputy employee delegates; charge special leave against remaining annual leave; recognise private clinic certificates meeting the stated conditions; and reinstate the three union leaders with back pay from the date of suspension to the date of return. The 3,000 riel overtime allowance, wage retention during sick leave and the accident-related claims were refused [Para 220][Para 221][Para 222][Para 223][Para 224][Para 225][Para 226][Para 227][Para 229].

On seniority bonus, the panel applied Notice No. 041/11 K.B/S.J.N of 7 March 2011, which gives workers with one year or more a monthly bonus on a scale and applies from 1 March 2011 [Para 96][Para 98]. Because the workshop opened in July 2010, all workers reaching one year by the date of claim qualified; the panel ordered payment to 66 workers, not the 138 originally claimed [Para 102][Para 104]. On delegates, Article 283 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 286 SKBY require every enterprise with eight or more workers to hold an election of full and deputy employee delegates, at the employer's cost [Para 105][Para 107][Para 109][Para 110]. On special leave, Article 171 and Prakas No. 267 SKBY allow up to seven paid days a year for family events, deductible from annual leave [Para 114][Para 116][Para 118]. On sick leave, Article 71(3) speaks of a "official doctor" without distinguishing public from private, so a certificate from a private clinic with a Ministry of Health-recognised diploma and practice licence must be accepted; but Article 72(1) suspends the wage obligation during suspension of the contract, so no wages are owed for the sick absence [Para 123][Para 125][Para 129][Para 131][Para 135]. On the union leaders, the employer bore the burden of proving misconduct, produced photographs showing fallen cartons but no specific damaged property, and failed to file its internal record by the 16 January 2012 deadline; the suspension was also indefinite, which Article 71(7) does not permit, so it followed award No. 45/05-BNI and ordered reinstatement with back pay [Para 140][Para 144][Para 147][Para 152][Para 154][Para 167][Para 169]. On the overtime allowance, the panel found no law, agreement or collective convention providing it, classified the claim as an interest dispute, and refused to hear it because the union was not the most representative in the enterprise [Para 172][Para 173][Para 179][Para 181]. On the attendance bonus, Article 103 makes bonuses part of wages, so the bonus must be paid for days actually worked and only pro-rated for authorised absences [Para 189][Para 191][Para 193][Para 196]. On the two injured workers, no accident record or medical report was filed by the deadline, so the claim failed on the burden of proof [Para 214][Para 216][Para 219].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an award turns on a claim of misconduct justifying suspension, the employer carries the burden and must produce both the evidence of the specific damage and its internal rules within the deadline set by the panel; a suspension left open-ended, with no term and no notice to the labour inspector, is unlawful and requires reinstatement with back pay. Where a wage claim has no basis in law, agreement or collective convention, it is an interest dispute that the panel will not hear unless the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise [Para 140][Para 152][Para 173][Para 179].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the absence of employee delegates makes strikes more likely, which is why it ordered elections immediately [Para 112].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR is heavily damaged in the appendix and signature pages: the list of 66 workers is a jumble of names, identity numbers, dates and dollar figures, and the panel's own text at one point orders payment to "19" workers where the operative order says 66 [Para 103][Para 104][Para 231][Para 232]. The procedural section states 13 non-conciliated points were referred and then that "07" were referred, and the hearing paragraph says 13 points were conciliated rather than eight [Para 21][Para 22][Para 25]. The employer is printed variously as Van Sing Co., Ltd, Van Sing Workshop and "Vai Sin Factory", and the union as both the federation and the company union [Para 1][Para 29][Para 46]. The award date is 2 February 2012 in the header and at the foot, consistent, but the panel sat on 10 January 2012 and extended the deadline by agreement [Para 19][Para 27][Para 260]. Arbitrator Ing Sothy filed a dissenting opinion on points 8 and 12, arguing that Article 72(1) bars back pay during suspension and that the attendance bonus is discretionary; that dissent is part of the document but not of the majority award [Para 234][Para 235][Para 257].
