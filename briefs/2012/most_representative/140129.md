# Award No. 100/12 — Yong Va Industry (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, Branch 1 (Arbitration Council, 20 June 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kuy Neam presiding; Seng Vouch Hun appointed by the employer, An Non appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 140129

## 1. Facts
Yong Va Industry (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, Branch 1, is a garment factory at Ta Khmau, Kandal, employing about 2,000 workers, of whom roughly 35 per cent are paid monthly and 65 per cent by output [Para 28][Para 29][Para 35]. The Cambodia Economic Workers' Union at the factory, with about 200 members, brought three demands — a wage increase for the assistant, mobile and hygiene sections, a wage increase for mechanics and electricians, and the reinstatement of an administrative employee (name redacted) — and conciliation on 29 May 2012 left all three unresolved by report No. 439/12 K.B/KN of the same date [Para 3][Para 4][Para 5][Para 13][Para 14][Para 30]. At the hearing of 8 June 2012 the union withdrew point 3, leaving points 1 and 2 [Para 17][Para 18]. The union is not the most representative in the factory [Para 31].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must raise the base wage of workers in the assistant (mobile), hygiene and support sections to USD 75 a month? [Para 32]
2. Whether the company must add USD 30 a month to the base wage of the mechanics and the electrician, on top of the USD 86 they now receive? [Para 47]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — Cambodia Economic Workers' Union at Yong Va I):** the assistant, mobile and hygiene workers are in truth a single group, sweeping in teams and sometimes helping to cut shirts or trim for other teams, or carrying shirts to the sewers, scanning bundles and opening plaques; they work as hard as the sewing lines but earn only about USD 100 to 110 a month all told, against USD 130 to 180, and sometimes USD 300, for sewers [Para 32][Para 33][Para 34][Para 39][Para 40]. The 16 mechanics and the single electrician are skilled, each mechanic keeping about 200 machines running, so they deserve USD 30 more, or failing a rise, an incentive of the same amount [Para 47][Para 49][Para 52].

**Employer (respondent — Yong Va Industry (Cambodia) Co., Ltd):** it has no capacity to raise wages as demanded; it is the employer that decides which workers are paid monthly and which by output; about 25 per cent of output-paid workers already earn roughly what monthly-paid workers earn, so parity cannot be fixed; and any worker seeking a rise may ask the section head, who assesses the request and sends it to administration [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43][Para 44][Para 45][Para 50]. It added that it does raise wages through section heads where the head considers a rise warranted [Para 51].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both remaining claims were refused, the panel declining to order any increase to USD 75 a month or any addition of USD 30 a month [Para 73][Para 74].

On point 1 the panel applied Notice No. 049/10 K.B/S.J.N of 9 July 2010, which guarantees a minimum wage of USD 61 a month for workers in the garment sector generally [Para 54][Para 55]. It found that the assistant (mobile) and hygiene workers were already paid that USD 61 minimum, and that no provision of the Labour Law raises it from USD 61 to USD 75, nor was there any agreement or collective convention providing the increase [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58]. The claim was therefore one for more than the law secures, that is an interest dispute [Para 59]. The panel then applied its standing rule that it declines interest disputes where the claiming union is not the most representative in the factory, because such an award would operate as a collective convention for one year, and it noted that this union had 200 members out of 2,000 [Para 60][Para 62][Para 63][Para 64]. On point 2 it found the mechanics and electrician already on a base of USD 86 a month, with the claim taking them to USD 116, and no agreement or collective convention requiring the USD 30; the claim was again an interest dispute and was refused on the same ground, the panel expressly cross-referring to its reasoning on point 1 [Para 65][Para 67][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70][Para 71][Para 72].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A wage claim that exceeds the minimum secured by the applicable notice, and that no agreement or collective convention supports, is an interest dispute; the panel will not arbitrate it where the claiming union is not the most representative in the enterprise, since an award on interests would bind every worker as a collective convention for a year. Paying the statutory minimum discharges the employer's legal obligation even where workers in other sections earn considerably more [Para 59][Para 60][Para 64].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded the employer's account that wages may be raised individually on a section head's assessment, without making any order about that route [Para 45][Para 51].
