# Award No. 175/11 — Global Apparel Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 24 January 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Run Saray (president), Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Virak (worker-appointed) · Doc ID 140112

## 1. Facts
Global Apparel Co., Ltd. (also printed "Global Apparel" and linked to JI Textiles) is a garment manufacturer employing approximately 2,581 workers, of whom some 400 belong to the plant union. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44] On 2 October 2011 the union filed a collective claim for improved working conditions; conciliation on 9 December 2011 settled six of nine demands and left three unresolved, which reached the Council on 22 December 2011. [Para 13][Para 14] The parties had signed a memorandum of understanding dated 28 September 2010 on improving professional relations in the garment industry, under which the parties agreed that rights-dispute awards would bind them. [Para 19][Para 20]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the workers' demand that the company stop assigning evaluation scores to workers should be granted? [Para 46]
2. Whether the company should be ordered to transfer the head of the shipping section out of his post? [Para 60]
3. Whether the company must discuss overtime work with the union whenever it requires work beyond normal hours? [Para 69]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Scoring is a form of threat and coercion over workers and the union, used as a pretext for easy dismissal, since a team leader who dislikes a worker can fail him outright and cost him his job; the system should therefore be abolished. [Para 54][Para 55] The shipping-section head is difficult to work with, has driven two staff to resign, calls subordinates "crazy" and throws documents at them, and refuses overtime that would cost the company extra wages; nineteen staff petitioned for his removal. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63][Para 64] Overtime beyond two hours a day should be discussed with the union, which represents and protects the workers, and team leaders can pressure workers who refuse. [Para 69][Para 70]

**Employer (respondent):** Evaluation is not coercion but an assessment of conduct and work quality under the internal rules and the Labour Law, and any worker who believes a score is unfair may complain to the administration, which investigates before deciding not to renew. [Para 3][Para 57] The head cannot be transferred because he has brought the company many benefits, though he will be advised to behave properly toward staff during working hours. [Para 5][Para 68] Overtime is voluntary, never forced, and the law does not require consultation with any one union where the plant has nine. [Para 71][Para 73][Para 74]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three claims were refused and no compensation was ordered; the rights-dispute rulings take effect immediately and the interest-dispute ruling becomes final eight days after notification absent written objection. [Para 121][Para 123][Para 124][Para 125]

On issue 1, the panel first classified the claim. A rights dispute concerns entitlements set by law, agreement or collective convention, and because the evaluation governs whether a fixed-duration contract is renewed, the demand was held to be a rights dispute. [Para 76][Para 78] On the merits, however, the panel found no concrete dispute: the workers who complained had never failed an evaluation and had always had their contracts renewed, so the claim rested only on a fear that a team leader might one day fail them. [Para 82] Following Award No. 10/03-Jacksintik, the panel held it exists to resolve labour conflicts, not problems that have not yet arisen, since no one can predict which right will be breached, when, in which section, or to how many workers. [Para 84][Para 85] The claim was therefore not entertained. [Para 87]

On issue 2, the panel applied Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 664 of the Civil Code, which define the employment contract as arising from the mutual consent of the parties, read with Article 311 of the Civil Code on the formation of contracts. [Para 89][Para 91][Para 94] Because the shipping head has his own separate contract with the employer, the petitioning workers are third parties who cannot modify or dissolve it; following earlier awards, a worker may only demand such a transfer on proof that the person is dangerous and cannot be kept in the factory without creating real insecurity. [Para 95][Para 97][Para 99][Para 101] The panel accepted that some of his conduct was genuinely inappropriate — improper words and gestures toward subordinates, which affect morale and workplace relations — but held that the employer may respond through internal rules by oral or written warning, and found no proof that he was dangerous in the required sense. [Para 105][Para 106][Para 107]

On issue 3, the panel classified the demand as an interest dispute, since no article of the Labour Law requires the employer to consult the union about overtime and the parties had no agreement on the point. [Para 109] In interest disputes the panel considers the most-representative status of the union bringing the claim and has refused to hear such disputes where the union lacks it. [Para 110][Para 111] Article 277 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 require registration and the conditions for that status, and an interest award itself stands as a collective convention for one year. [Para 114][Para 115][Para 116] The plant union had not been recognised as most representative by the Ministry of Labour, so it lacked the legal capacity to bring an interest claim on behalf of all workers. [Para 118][Para 119][Para 120]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that the employer abandon an evaluation system governing contract renewal is a rights dispute, but the panel will not adjudicate it in the absence of an identified worker actually harmed; anticipatory challenges to events that may never occur are not entertained. [Para 78][Para 84] A worker cannot compel the employer to move a supervisor who holds his own contract with the employer, because non-parties cannot vary that contract; only evidence that the supervisor is dangerous and cannot be retained will support such an order. [Para 95][Para 101] An interest dispute may be brought only by a union holding most-representative status; one without it cannot litigate demands on behalf of the whole workforce. [Para 113][Para 120]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that a supervisor's insulting language and throwing of documents harms morale and workplace relations, and that the employer may discipline him under internal rules by oral or written warning if the behaviour continues — observations offered as guidance, not as orders. [Para 105][Para 106]
