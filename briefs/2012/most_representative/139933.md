# Award No. 03/12-Camwell — Camwell MFG Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 January 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Tuon Siphann, chair Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 139933

## 1. Facts
Camwell MFG Co., Ltd operates a garment factory employing 1,500 workers. [Para 17] [Para 18] The claimant is the Local Union of CLUF, holding registration certificate No. 2360 dated 3 January 2012, claiming to represent 200 workers and holding no certificate of most representative status (MRS). [Para 19] [Para 20] [Para 21] On 14 December 2011 CLUF filed seven demands; conciliation failed and non-conciliation report No. 052/11 KB/KN dated 3 January 2012 referred all seven to the Council, which resolved issues 1, 2 and 4 at the hearing of 16 January 2012, leaving issues 3, 5, 6 and 7. [Para 4] [Para 8] Both parties having signed the MoU of 28 September 2010, the panel divided the remainder into rights and interests disputes, the parties choosing non-binding arbitration for the latter. [Para 9] [Para 11]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the 2,000 riel meal allowance each Saturday rather than monthly? [Para 22]
2. Whether the employer must provide a monthly US$ 10 transportation and accommodation allowance to each worker? [Para 28]
3. Whether the employer must deduct from the attendance bonus in proportion to the number of days of authorised leave taken? [Para 31]
4. Whether the employer must provide an additional monthly US$ 5 attendance bonus on top of the existing US$ 7 bonus? [Para 33]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that overtime meal money is needed each day to buy food and avoid borrowing, that other factories pay the allowance weekly, that other factories supply transport or housing help and a larger attendance bonus, and that earlier awards require proportionate deduction for authorised leave. [Para 24] [Para 29] [Para 32] [Para 35]
**Employer (respondent):** that its practice has stood since 1998 and that five buildings served by five administration staff leave it short of human resources, that it has never paid a transport or accommodation allowance, that it already deducts US$ 2, US$ 4 and the whole bonus for one, two and three days' leave, and that it pays the statutory US$ 7. [Para 25] [Para 27] [Para 29] [Para 32] [Para 71]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — as rights disputes the panel ordered the employer to pay the overtime meal allowance on Saturday of each week and to deduct from the attendance bonus in proportion to authorised leave taken, both binding; as interests disputes it declined to consider the US$ 10 transport and accommodation allowance and the additional US$ 5 bonus, that part being non-binding. [Para 75] [Para 76]

The panel first fixed the divide: under Article 312 of the Labour Law and Clause 43 of Prakas No. 099 dated 21 April 2004, a rights dispute concerns entitlements in the law, an agreement or a collective agreement, while an interests dispute has no such basis and is settled in equity. [Para 39] [Para 40] [Para 42]
On issue 3 it held the demand concerned only the timing of payment, which Point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 dated 7 March 2011 mandates, making it a rights dispute; following Award 47/07-Chung Fai and Awards 79/07-Terratex and 85/09-Nan Kuang, the 2,000 riel allowance substitutes for a free meal and must be paid daily, so the employer must arrange weekly payment despite its staffing difficulty. [Para 47] [Para 48] [Para 51] [Para 52]
On issue 5 it found no basis in law, contract or collective agreement, hence an interests dispute; because the union held no MRS it lacked standing to bring a collective interests dispute, following Awards 81/04-Evergreen and 98/04-Great Union and the later awards declining such cases. [Para 53] [Para 55] [Para 56]
On issue 6 it held the attendance bonus a gratuity and therefore part of wage under Article 103; public holidays and annual leave under Articles 161 and 166 preserve the full bonus, but authorised leave, arising from the worker's request and the employer's agreement and stipulated in no law, is the exception, so the bonus is reduced in proportion. [Para 62] [Para 64] [Para 65] [Para 66] [Para 67]
On issue 7 it found the demand to exceed the statutory US$ 7 minimum and declined it as an interests dispute for the same lack of MRS. [Para 71] [Para 73] Arbitrator Ing Sothy recorded a dissent on issue 6 only, annexed under Clause 37 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 79]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union holding no certificate of most representative status has no standing to bring an interests dispute — a demand with no basis in law, contract or collective agreement — and the panel will decline to decide it, while the same union may still obtain a binding award on a rights dispute on the same facts. An overtime meal allowance under Notification No. 041/11 is an alternative to a free meal and must therefore be paid for each day of overtime, not accumulated to payday. Where an attendance bonus is a component of wage, leave imposed by law preserves it in full, but authorised leave, resting on the parties' agreement, permits proportionate deduction. [Para 42] [Para 56] [Para 49] [Para 65]

**Obiter dicta:** that an award settling an interests dispute becomes a one-year collective agreement applicable to all workers at the enterprise, waiving non-members' right to strike in later interests disputes. [Para 55]
