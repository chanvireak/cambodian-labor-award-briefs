# Award No. 171/11 — Sing Lik M (Cambodia) Garment Factory (Arbitration Council, 5 January 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed — dissenting; An Non, worker-appointed; Kong Phalha, chair) · heard as non-binding award · Doc ID 140109

## 1. Facts
The Federation of Unions for Labour Rights and its local union at Sing Lik M (Cambodia) Garment Factory, a plant of about 300 workers, filed twelve demands on 7 December 2011; conciliation failed and the file reached the Council under non-conciliation report No. 1394 of 13 December 2011. At the hearing of 23 December 2011 nine points were settled or withdrawn, leaving three: the reinstatement of Mr S V H, the union president; a monthly day off for antenatal checks; and retention of the attendance bonus during certified sick leave. [Para 19][Para 21][Para 24][Para 25][Para 42]

Mr S V H began work on 29 July 2011 on a two-month probation contract and, after it expired, was kept on without any written contract; he was paid USD 135 a month as a finishing supervisor. He was elected union president on 23 October 2011, and the union posted notice of the elected leadership to the employer on 24 October 2011 and applied for registration the same day. The employer says it never received the notice, and on 3 December 2011 dismissed him by telephone for being lazy. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47][Para 54][Para 56][Para 58][Para 60]

On 12 December 2011 the employer signed an agreement with the workers, without any union taking part, granting pregnant workers one day a month for checks from the fifth month and thirty to sixty minutes before that, and cutting the attendance bonus by USD 2, 3 and 2 for the first three days of certified sick leave. [Para 82][Para 90][Para 91]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back Mr S V H, a union leader, and pay his wages from the dismissal to his return? [Para 45][Para 98]
2. Whether the employer must give pregnant workers one full day a month for antenatal checks while preserving wage and the attendance bonus? [Para 76][Para 141]
3. Whether the employer must keep the whole attendance bonus when a worker takes sick leave supported by a proper certificate? [Para 156][Para 169]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the president was dismissed for his union role, having never been warned, and the union had notified his election to the employer by post and to the Ministry; pregnant women need a full day each month from the start of pregnancy, as the Ministry of Health advises, and losing the whole USD 7 attendance bonus for one day of genuine illness is unjust. [Para 79][Para 92][Para 94][Para 110]

**Employer (respondent):** the factory only began operating in 2011 and cannot grant the antenatal day; it applies the 12 December 2011 agreement, under which pregnant workers get one day a month from five months and a shorter allowance before that; the dismissal was for laziness, and no authorisation from the labour inspector was sought because the company did not know he was a union leader. [Para 60][Para 81][Para 84][Para 97][Para 129]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must take back Mr S V H and pay his wages from the dismissal until his return; the antenatal-day claim was refused; the claim to retain the whole attendance bonus was refused, and the employer was instead ordered to reduce the bonus in proportion to the days actually worked by a worker on properly certified sick leave. [Para 171][Para 172][Para 173][Para 174]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 293, read with Article 4 of Prakas No. 305: a union founder or member is protected as an employee delegate from the date of the registration application, and protection runs for thirty days after registration, but only if three conditions are met — the worker falls within the Prakas, the dismissal occurred while protection applied, and the union told the employer the names of protected persons by any workable means. [Para 98][Para 102][Para 103][Para 105]

The first two conditions were satisfied: he was a founder who applied for registration on 24 October 2011, and he was dismissed on 3 December 2011 while that application was pending; the union's notice of 24 October 2011 was also held to be timely. On the third, however, the panel put the burden on the union to prove it had actually posted the notice, and the union produced nothing by the 27 December 2011 deadline, so the panel presumed the employer never received it and found the protection unavailable. [Para 107][Para 108][Para 110][Para 112][Para 115][Para 116][Para 121]

That did not save the dismissal. Because no contract was issued after probation, Article 74 governed and the relationship was of indefinite duration; the employer may terminate on its own will but must give written notice stating a valid reason going to the worker's capacity, conduct or the needs of the enterprise. The reason given — laziness — was unsupported: no dates, no count of occurrences, no effect on output, no prior warning, and no internal rule making laziness a ground for dismissal. The dismissal was therefore unlawful, and under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 the panel ordered reinstatement with arrears rather than compensation. [Para 122][Para 126][Para 127][Para 131][Para 132][Para 133][Para 139][Para 140]

On issue 9 the panel found that the employer already gave more than the law requires — a full day a month from the fifth month, a shorter allowance before — and that no law, agreement or collective convention granted a full day from the start of pregnancy. The claim thus exceeded the statutory floor and was an interest dispute; because the union held no certificate of most-representative status, the panel declined to decide it. [Para 143][Para 144][Para 145][Para 148][Para 153][Para 154]

On issue 12 the panel reasoned that the attendance bonus exists to reward regular attendance, that Notification No. 041/11 was not meant to punish a worker who stays away with a valid medical certificate, and that forfeiting all USD 7 for one certified day off is unjust to the worker — but that paying the full bonus for days not worked is equally unjust to the employer. It therefore fixed a middle course, pro-rating the bonus to days worked and declining to strike the deduction down entirely. [Para 157][Para 163][Para 164][Para 165][Para 166][Para 169][Para 170]

Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented on issues 1 and 12: he treated the dismissal as the exercise of the employer's contractual right under Article 74, argued that laziness and a workplace fire caused by the worker's negligence justified termination, and read the bonus as a discretionary reward whose conditions belong to the employer. [Para 178][Para 182][Para 185][Para 198][Para 205]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union's statutory protection for its elected leaders depends on the union proving it notified the employer of their names; where it cannot, the leader is an ordinary indefinite-term employee, and a dismissal for an asserted fault that the employer cannot date, quantify or support with warnings or an internal rule lacks the valid reason Article 74 requires, so reinstatement with arrears follows; an attendance bonus may be reduced pro rata for certified sick leave but not forfeited in full. [Para 105][Para 115][Para 127][Para 133][Para 166]

**Obiter dicta:** that the employer's practice of a full antenatal day from the fifth month exceeds what the law requires, and that a payment labelled a reward stays discretionary as to its conditions. [Para 143][Para 198]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is internally inconsistent on dates: it records the dismissal as 3 December 2011 in the reasoning but 3 October 2011 in one passage, which would place it before the 23 October 2011 election, and it gives the registration application as 24 October in one place and 26 October in another. The employer is named "Sing Lik M (Cambodia) Garment Factory" in the header and in several variant spellings thereafter, and the plant's own union is described as registered on 13 December 2011 while the notice relied on is dated 24 October 2011. Arbitrator names are printed inconsistently ("Ing Sothy" and "Ing Sothy" with diacritics), the signature block is largely destroyed, and the dissent's dictionary citations are garbled. Individual workers and employer representatives are reduced to initials here. [Para 1][Para 108][Para 120][Para 107][Para 121][Para 31][Para 44][Para 176]
