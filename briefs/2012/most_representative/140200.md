# Award No. 203/12 — Sino Garment Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 6 November 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chiv Phirom, appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna, appointed by the workers; Kong Phalha, chair) · Doc ID 140200

## 1. Facts
Sino Garment Co Ltd, the employer party, runs a cut-and-sew factory at Prek Samrong, Takhmao, Kandal [Para 1], employing about 320 workers, of whom 45 are men and 275 women, with no nursery, no breastfeeding room and no on-site infirmary. [Para 36][Para 41][Para 99] On the claimant side were the federation named in the style of cause and its local at the factory, which received a registration certificate on 11 October 2012 and counted 104 members. [Para 1][Para 37][Para 38] The federation filed fourteen demands on 27 August 2012, conciliation on 21 September 2012 failed on all fourteen, and the panel heard the case on 24 October 2012. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 24]

The employer's administrative officer attended and claimed to represent the company but produced no written authority to do so, so the panel decided the case in the employer's absence and issued a non-binding award. [Para 25][Para 26] The employer filed no documents at all. [Para 27]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must build a nursery and a breastfeeding room? [Para 40][Para 93]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must hold an election of worker representatives? [Para 43][Para 104]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer has discriminated against union members? [Para 46][Para 119]
4. (Issue 4) Whether workers may leave the factory to meet visiting relatives? [Para 48][Para 125]
5. (Issue 5) Whether full wages are owed when the employer has no work to give? [Para 50][Para 136]
6. (Issue 6) Whether the employer must adopt internal rules? [Para 54][Para 148]
7. (Issue 7) Whether the employer must pay cash in lieu of untaken annual leave? [Para 56][Para 153]
8. (Issue 8) Whether wages and allowances must be preserved during sick leave beyond three days? [Para 60][Para 170]
9. (Issue 9) Whether the employer must deduct 1,000 riel a month in union dues? [Para 64][Para 185]
10. (Issue 10) Whether the attendance bonus must be reduced in proportion to authorised unpaid absence? [Para 66][Para 194]
11. (Issue 11) Whether the employer must pay for workers' medical examinations? [Para 69][Para 232]
12. (Issue 12) Whether fixed-term contracts running over two years become indefinite-term? [Para 71][Para 240]
13. (Issue 13) Whether the employer must provide an infirmary and a duty doctor? [Para 73][Para 255]
14. (Issue 14) Whether payslips must be issued three days before payday? [Para 75][Para 272]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — the Hope Workers' Union Federation and its factory local:** the fourteen demands were argued from Article 186, Article 242, Article 292 and Articles 166 to 167 of the Labour Law, from Notification No. 230 on the attendance bonus, and from the practice of deducting union dues; the union said it had submitted fingerprint letters to the employer's security staff, who refused them. [Para 42][Para 55][Para 65][Para 191]
**Employer (respondent) — Sino Garment Co Ltd:** absent. Its administrative officer attended without written authority, filed no evidence, and the panel therefore recorded no case for the company on any issue. [Para 25][Para 27]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to build a nursery and breastfeeding room, hold a worker-representative election, adopt internal rules, deduct union dues on proper documents, reduce the attendance bonus pro rata, pay for medical examinations, and provide an infirmary with a duty doctor and payslips three days early; the demands on discrimination, leaving the premises, full pay during work shortages, cash for annual leave, sick-leave pay beyond three days and conversion of fixed-term contracts were dismissed or refused. [Para 283][Para 285][Para 289][Para 294][Para 295][Para 296][Para 298][Para 299]

On the nursery the panel applied Article 186, which requires any enterprise employing at least one hundred women to provide a breastfeeding room and a nursery, or, where no nursery is possible, to pay the cost of an outside one on production of receipts. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 100][Para 101] On the representative election it applied Article 292 and Prakas No. 286, holding that any enterprise with eight or more workers must elect representatives and that the election is the employer's duty. [Para 104][Para 108][Para 112][Para 117]

On discrimination the panel quoted Article 12 and found the evidence insufficient to conclude that union membership motivated any decision, so the claim was refused. [Para 119][Para 123][Para 124] On the two demands it treated as interest disputes — leaving the premises to meet visitors, and preservation of wages and benefits during sick leave beyond three days — it held the union lacked a most-representative certificate, so it declined to consider them. [Para 126][Para 178][Para 179]

The attendance bonus produced the longest reasoning. The panel found that Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012, replacing Notification No. 041/11, added the words "without absence" to the ten-dollar bonus and thereby ceased to be a management tool, becoming a general legal norm binding on all textile, garment and footwear employers. [Para 199][Para 201][Para 209][Para 210] Reading "absence" as unlawful absence, and treating authorised unpaid leave as an agreement under which both sides give up the wage and the attendance for those days, it ordered a pro-rata reduction rather than total forfeiture. [Para 220][Para 221][Para 227][Para 231]

On the fixed-term contracts the panel held that a contract renewed past two years in total becomes indefinite, but that the party asserting this must prove it, and the workers had not shown which or how many contracts exceeded two years. [Para 250][Para 252][Para 253][Para 254]

The chair dissented on Issue 10, reasoning that the bonus is a discretionary reward whose condition is full attendance, so a worker who does not meet the condition cannot claim a proportional share. [Para 306][Para 308][Para 316][Para 324]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Once a Ministry notification fixes an attendance bonus for a whole sector, the bonus is governed by the wage regime of Article 103 rather than by the employer's discretion: "absence" in that notification means unlawful absence only, so authorised unpaid leave reduces the bonus in proportion to the days taken instead of forfeiting it entirely. A union that has registered but cannot show it is the most representative in the factory may not bring interest disputes at all, while rights disputes under Article 186 (nursery), Article 242 (infirmary) and Article 292 (representative election) proceed regardless of registration. [Para 209][Para 210][Para 231][Para 134][Para 100][Para 269]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that an indefinite-term contract encourages worker commitment and that ILO Recommendation No. 166 is persuasive but not binding illustrate Article 67 without deciding anything, since Issue 12 failed on proof. [Para 245][Para 249][Para 252]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The dissent on Issue 10 is attributed in its body to the chair, but the appendix heading at the end of the file attributes it to the worker-appointed arbitrator; the record does not resolve which arbitrator wrote it. [Para 301][Para 332]
- The employer is printed as "Sino Garment", "Sino Gamint" and "Sino Gament" in different places, including the style of cause and the fact section. [Para 1][Para 28][Para 36]
- The employer's representative is named in three different transliterations within the same award, and the panel's reasons alternate between them when discussing his authority. [Para 25][Para 79][Para 88]
- Page numbers and stray years are embedded inside sentences, e.g. "2017" and "-១៣-" inside the Article 71 quotation, and "-២៣-" inside the Article 67 quotation, breaking the text at those points. [Para 136][Para 184][Para 240]
- The award describes the union as holding a registration certificate of 11 October 2012 while also finding that it holds no certificate of most-representative status, without explaining the difference in terms the parties could follow. [Para 38][Para 178]
- Several operative paragraphs are duplicated verbatim in the reasoning, so the nursery order appears twice with slightly different wording ("Li" inserted mid-sentence in one). [Para 102][Para 283]
- The heading of Issue 5 refers to a suspension of activity of no more than two months, but the reasoning never establishes whether the employer applied for or obtained the labour inspector's approval, and the panel dismissed the claim for want of evidence on that very point. [Para 136][Para 145][Para 146]
