# Award No. 210/12 — Thong Chav Enterprise (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 19 December 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3 (Ing Sothy, Liv Sovanna, Pen Bunchhea presiding) · Doc ID 140208

## 1. Facts
Workers of the Al Super Enterprise (Cambodia) Ltd garment factory, represented by two factory unions, claimed unpaid annual-leave compensation for 2011 and January–June 2012 together with a package of improved conditions. In June 2012 the employer terminated their indefinite contracts and on 11 August 2012 paid USD 100 to each worker as a lump sum said to cover severance, seniority and remaining leave; on 2 July 2012 the workers had signed new fixed-duration contracts with Thong Chav Enterprise (Cambodia), recording in voluntary letters that prior seniority was broken. Conciliation left seven of fourteen demands unresolved; at the hearing the water-tank demand (Issue 3) was settled by agreement, leaving the rest for the panel. [Para 1][Para 23][Para 26][Para 113][Para 117]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay the outstanding annual-leave compensation for 2011 and for January–June 2012? [Para 112]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer may deduct the regular attendance bonus pro rata to days of authorised personal leave, and whether it must retain the whole bonus where a worker is up to 30 minutes late in a month? [Para 179]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must keep wages and the attendance bonus intact during sick leave certified by a medical certificate? [Para 233]
4. (Issue 5) Whether the minimum daily incentive bonus must be raised to 3,000 riel? [Para 237]
5. (Issue 6) Whether pregnant workers must receive one day a month for antenatal checks on full pay and bonus? [Para 244]
6. (Issue 7) Whether the meal allowance for overtime must be raised to 3,000 riel for 16:00–18:00 and 5,000 riel for 18:00–20:00? [Para 250]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Arts 166 and 167 give a right to untaken leave paid on termination, and the USD 100 did not include it; the attendance bonus is wages, so absence on authorised leave cannot forfeit it; and the factory's practice on drinking water, pregnancy checks and overtime meals fell below legal minima and below comparators. [Para 119][Para 145][Para 157][Para 161]
**Employer (respondent):** the USD 100 settled severance, seniority and remaining leave; Thong Chav is a separate company with different owners from Al Super, so it owes nothing on the old contracts; current practice already exceeds the law; and it has no capacity to pay more. [Para 115][Para 130][Para 149][Para 167]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issue 1 was dismissed for want of evidence; on Issue 2 the panel ordered that the attendance bonus be deducted only pro rata to days of authorised personal absence, and dismissed the 30-minute lateness claim; Issues 4, 5, 6 and 7 were dismissed. [Para 260][Para 261][Para 262]
Issue 1: the panel accepted that Arts 166 and 167 of the 1997 Labour Law give a worker paid annual leave and compensation for leave untaken when the contract ends, and that the employer had terminated in June 2012. It nevertheless dismissed the claim because the workers never filed a list identifying which workers still had leave owing, so no sum could be fixed, and it applied the rule that the party making the allegation bears the burden of proof, following Award 115/10. [Para 170][Para 174][Para 176]
Issue 2: Notification 230 of 25 July 2012 replaced notification 041 and set a minimum attendance bonus of USD 10 a month for workers present on every working day, with the words "without absence" added from 1 September 2012. The panel held the bonus to be a reward and so part of wages under Art 103; absence on authorised leave suspends the contract under Arts 71(6) and 72, so no wage and no bonus accrue for those days. The employer could therefore deduct pro rata to days absent but not forfeit the whole monthly bonus. [Para 183][Para 207][Para 214][Para 216][Para 217]
Lateness: no law, collective agreement or practice obliged the employer to keep the bonus for cumulative lateness up to 30 minutes, and the factory's tolerance of under 15 minutes already exceeded the legal floor, so this limb was an interest claim. [Para 219][Para 222]
Issues 4–7: each was characterised as an interest dispute. Under Art 43 of Prakas 099 an award in an interest dispute stands in place of a collective agreement for one year and binds all workers, so the panel may decide only where the claimant union holds most-representative status, meaning more than half the workforce; the two unions here did not establish that, and the demands were dismissed without reaching their merits. [Para 225][Para 230][Para 231]
Arbitrator Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer, appended a dissent on Issue 2, reasoning that a bonus conditioned on full attendance cannot be claimed by a worker who did not meet the condition. [Para 268][Para 285]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer grants unpaid personal leave, the absence suspends the employment contract, and the regular attendance bonus — being a reward and therefore wages — accrues only for days worked, so the employer may reduce it proportionately to the days of absence but may not withhold the entire monthly bonus. [Para 214][Para 216]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that no competent body had yet interpreted the words "without absence" in Notification 230, and declined to read them as covering both authorised and unauthorised absence. [Para 186]
