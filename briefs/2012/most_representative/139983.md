# Award No. 52/12 — 8 Star Sport Wear (Arbitration Council, 29 March 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Run Saray, chair) · Doc ID 139983

## 1. Facts
8 Star Sport Wear is a garment factory at Wat Phnom, Steung Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employing about 927 workers. The claimant is the Federation of Union of Khmer Youth Voice (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព សម្លេង យុវជន ខ្មែរ), registered with the Ministry of Labour on 16 June 2011; the panel found it was not the most representative union. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46][Para 47]

Conciliation on 23 February 2012 failed on six of the thirteen claims, and the unresolved points were referred to the Arbitration Council on 2 March 2012. At the hearing the parties settled the nursery claim (point 4), so the panel considered points 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 24][Para 78][Para 79]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay 100% of wages to workers for periods when it had no work for them, and pay the shortfall retroactively? [Para 48]
2. Whether the employer must pay the statutory overtime rate of [(1 x 2) + 1] and a 5,000 riel food allowance when workers voluntarily work overtime on Sundays and public holidays? [Para 61][Para 62]
3. Whether maternity leave pay must be calculated on the average of the 12 months before leave and paid for the full three months before the worker stops work? [Para 70]
4. Whether the employer must pay a monthly housing or transport allowance of US$ 20 per worker? [Para 80]
5. Whether pregnant workers from one and a half months must be allowed one paid monthly health check without deduction from annual leave, and to leave 15 minutes early? [Para 84][Para 198]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The union argued that the employer's failure to provide work was its own fault under Article 83(a)(5) of the Labour Law, so full wages were owed. It sought 5,000 riel food money for Sunday and holiday overtime, maternity pay based on the 12-month average paid in advance, US$ 20 monthly housing or transport allowance, and paid monthly pregnancy check-ups with 15 minutes' early departure. [Para 48][Para 58][Para 62][Para 70][Para 80][Para 84]

**Employer (respondent):** The employer argued that not all workers were sewing-machine operators, so Article 83(a)(5) did not apply; that it paid under point 4 of the collective agreement of 5 November 2010, giving 50% of base wage for stoppages under six days; that Sunday work was not overtime because it was only eight hours; and that no law or agreement required housing, transport or the disputed pregnancy benefits. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 57][Para 59][Para 60][Para 65][Para 81][Para 86]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Partly granted. The panel ordered the employer to pay 100% wages to 52 named workers for the no-work periods, because it had not lawfully suspended their contracts; to pay a 2,000 riel daily food allowance to workers who voluntarily worked Sunday overtime; and to pay 50% of wages and benefits for 90 days of maternity leave, calculated on the average of the last 12 months and paid at least one day before leave began. It rejected the 5,000 riel Sunday and holiday food claims, the US$ 20 housing or transport allowance, and the pregnancy check-up and early-departure claims. [Para 217][Para 219][Para 220][Para 221][Para 222][Para 223][Para 224][Para 225]

On issue 1, the panel held that Article 71 of the Labour Law permits suspension of contracts only in defined cases, including serious economic difficulty, and only for up to two months, subject to labour inspection. Article 72(1) provides that suspension removes the duty to pay wages only where the law is followed. Where the employer suspends work without notifying or obtaining the inspection service's view, it must pay 100% of wages. [Para 92][Para 93][Para 98][Para 99][Para 100]

The panel found the employer had no work for periods of three or four days and did not notify or seek authorisation from the labour inspector, relying instead on point 4 of the collective agreement. It held that point 4, which provides only 50% of base wage for stoppages under six days, contravenes Article 13 of the Labour Law, which makes labour law provisions mandatory and void inconsistent agreements. The company's practice was therefore unlawful. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 106][Para 107][Para 108][Para 109]

The union listed 68 workers; 52 specified the days they did not work (18 for 14–16 February 2012, 34 for 15–17 February), a list the employer did not dispute. The panel disregarded the 16 whose days were unspecified and ordered 100% wages for the 52. [Para 115][Para 116][Para 117][Para 118][Para 119][Para 120][Para 121]

On issue 2, voluntary Sunday work beyond normal hours is overtime under Article 137. Since the employer never paid the food allowance for Sunday overtime, the panel ordered 2,000 riel per day under Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011. The 5,000 riel claim exceeded the law and was an interest dispute; the panel declined to decide it because the claimant union was not the most representative. [Para 126][Para 127][Para 128][Para 129][Para 130][Para 131][Para 133][Para 134][Para 135][Para 136]

For public holidays, the panel followed prior awards holding that holiday work is not overtime but attracts a compensatory payment equal to a normal day's wage under Article 164 and Prakas No. 10 of 4 February 1999. The 5,000 riel claim was likewise an interest dispute and was declined. [Para 152][Para 153][Para 154][Para 156][Para 158]

On issue 3, the panel held that maternity pay is 50% of wages including benefits under Articles 182 and 183, but that Article 115 requires wages to be paid before the leave day, so the three months' maternity pay must be paid at least one day before leave starts, computed on the average of the last 12 months. [Para 159][Para 160][Para 167][Para 172][Para 174][Para 175][Para 176][Para 177]

On issue 4, res judicata did not apply because the earlier case (No. 142/10) was brought by a different union. On the merits, no law, agreement or settlement required a US$ 20 housing or transport payment; the claim was an interest dispute and was declined. [Para 185][Para 186][Para 190][Para 193][Para 194][Para 195][Para 196][Para 198]

On issue 5, the panel found no law or agreement required paid time off for monthly pregnancy check-ups without deducting annual leave, or 15 minutes' early departure; both exceeded the law and were declined as interest disputes. [Para 205][Para 206][Para 212][Para 213][Para 216]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that lays workers off without the notification and inspection required by Articles 71–72 of the Labour Law must pay full wages for the no-work period; a collective agreement clause providing less is void under Article 13. [Para 99][Para 100][Para 108][Para 109]

Voluntary work on Sunday beyond the normal weekly hours is overtime, and the employer owes the food allowance set by Notification No. 041/11; a demand above that statutory rate is an interest dispute. [Para 127][Para 129][Para 133][Para 134]

Work on a public holiday is not overtime but carries a compensatory payment equal to one normal day's wage. [Para 152][Para 154]

Maternity pay of 50% for 90 days must be calculated on the average of the 12 months before leave and paid at least one day before the leave begins, because Article 115 requires payment before rest days. [Para 167][Para 174][Para 175]

Where a claim exceeds what law, agreement or practice provides, it is an interest dispute the Council will decline if the claimant union is not the most representative. [Para 135][Para 141]

**Obiter dicta:** Res judicata requires identity of parties, subject matter and a final decision; a different claimant union defeats the first condition. [Para 182][Para 185][Para 186]
