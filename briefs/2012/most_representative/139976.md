# Award No. 45112-Pou Yuen — Pou Yuen (Cambodia) Enterprise Ltd (Arbitration Council, 6 July 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Ing Sothy, An Nan and Pen Bunchhea · Doc ID 139976

## 1. Facts
Pou Yuen (Cambodia) Enterprise Ltd operated a footwear factory employing 2,950 workers. [Para 25] The claimants were the Workers Union, the Workers' Spirit Union and the Workers' Right Union, affiliates of WUF, together representing 1,540 workers (52.20% of the workforce). [Para 26] [Para 27] A conciliation attempt failed, and 12 non-conciliated issues were referred to the Secretariat by non-conciliation report No. 220 KB/RK/VK dated 28 February 2012. [Para 12] [Para 15] At the hearing on 19 March 2012, seven further issues were resolved, leaving Issues 2, 4, 7, 9 and 11 unresolved. [Para 16] [Para 17] The parties were signatories to the MoU of 28 September 2010, under which rights disputes were binding and interests disputes non-binding. [Para 18] [Para 19] [Para 21]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay an additional monthly US$3 accommodation allowance on top of the existing US$4 allowance? [Para 28]
2. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must pay an additional monthly US$6 attendance bonus on top of the existing US$9 bonus? [Para 30]
3. (Issue 7) Whether the employer must pay maternity compensation equal to three months' wages and benefits calculated on average wages over the previous 12 months? [Para 31]
4. (Issue 9) Whether the employer must provide two large bottles of drinking water monthly, after the workers changed their original demand for drinking glasses? [Para 35] [Para 36] [Para 37]
5. (Issue 11) Whether the employer must pay full wages when work is unavailable for less than 13 days and half wages for 13 days or more? [Para 40]
6. Which issues are rights disputes and which are interests disputes under the MoU and the Labour Law? [Para 44] [Para 45]

## 3. Positions of the parties
The workers sought the accommodation increase because of rising consumer prices, rent, transport and utility costs; the employer said it could not afford it. [Para 29] [Para 30] The workers said the attendance bonus increase would reward service and regular attendance; the employer refused, saying its US$9 bonus already exceeded the legal requirement. [Para 30] [Para 31] On maternity pay, the workers relied on travel difficulties, ill health, transport costs and maternity expenses; the employer said workers were not technically employed during leave and excluded half of overtime from its calculation. [Para 33] [Para 34] On drinking water, the workers said many lacked glasses and borrowed from colleagues; the employer said workers were responsible for their own health and needed their own glasses. [Para 39] On work unavailability, the employer relied on a notification signed by itself, union representatives and worker delegates providing 50% of daily wages; the workers argued the employer must provide work. [Para 41] [Para 42] The employer did not inform the Labour Inspector of the unavailability of work. [Para 43]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered a maternity payment calculated as 50% of average monthly wages over the previous 12 months multiplied by three, payable at least one day before leave; hygienic glasses or other drinking vessels; and full wages when work was unavailable because the employer had not followed the legal suspension procedure. [Para 73] [Para 94] [Para 95] [Para 96] It rejected the additional US$3 accommodation allowance, the additional US$6 attendance bonus, and the demand for two monthly bottles of drinking water. [Para 60] [Para 64] [Para 83] [Para 97] The panel classified Issues 7, 9 and 11 as rights disputes because they had a basis in the Labour Law, and Issues 2 and 4 as interests disputes lacking a contractual or statutory basis. [Para 44] [Para 45] For interests disputes, it held that an award takes the place of a collective agreement, so only a most-representative-status union or unions representing over half the workers could bring them. [Para 46] [Para 48] [Para 50] Although the three unions were not registered, they represented 52.20% of the 2,950 workers and could proceed together. [Para 49] [Para 54] [Para 55] On equity, the panel found the existing benefits already exceeded legal minima and declined to impose extra costs without law or agreement. [Para 57] [Para 58] [Para 61] [Para 63] On maternity, Articles 182 and 183 of the Labour Law gave 90 days' leave and half wages including perquisites, while Article 103 treated maternity payments as wages and Article 115 required advance payment; the employer's monthly partial payment violated Article 115. [Para 67] [Para 68] [Para 70] [Para 72] On water, Clause 1 of Prakas No. 054 on Supply of Hygienic Drinks dated 10 February 2000 required sufficient hygienic drinks and Clause 4 required glasses or drinking vessels; the employer had sufficient water but glasses only at the infirmary, contrary to Clause 1. [Para 75] [Para 76] [Para 78] [Para 81] On suspension, Article 71 paragraph 11 required Labour Inspector control and Article 72 meant wages remained payable unless the legal suspension procedure was followed. [Para 84] [Para 85] [Para 88] [Para 89] The 50% notification conflicted with Article 13 of the Labour Law and was void. [Para 91] [Para 92] [Para 93] Part I was a binding award; Part II was non-binding for eight days after notification, subject to written opposition to the Minister of Labour. [Para 96] [Para 97]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A dispute is a rights dispute when the claimed entitlement has a basis in law, an agreement or a collective agreement; absent such a basis, it is an interests dispute. [Para 44] [Para 45] An interests dispute award may bind all workers as a collective agreement, so the claimant union must hold most representative status or be a collection of unions representing a majority. [Para 46] [Para 48] [Para 54] Maternity pay is wage for Article 115 of the Labour Law purposes and must be paid at least one day before leave, calculated from the previous 12 months' average wages and benefits. [Para 70] [Para 73] A lawful suspension requires notice to and control by the Labour Inspector; without it, full wages remain due, and a unilateral lower-benefit notification is void under Article 13. [Para 85] [Para 88] [Para 92]

**Obiter dicta:** Increased rent and transport costs do not by themselves concern the management of the employer where the benefit already exceeds legal requirements. [Para 57] [Para 58]
