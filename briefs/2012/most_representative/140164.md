# Award No. 154/12 — SD (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 August 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Chiv Phirom (employer-appointed), On Non (worker-appointed), Aing Eng Thong (chair) · Doc ID 140164

## 1. Facts
SD (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a garment factory at Kantok, Phnom Penh employing about 620 workers [Para 45]. The NIFTUC-affiliated union at the factory filed fifteen demands on working conditions; three remained unsettled after conciliation on 24 July 2012, and at the hearing of 10 August 2012 Issue 3 — a request for the panel to explain the procedure for establishing an occupational health and safety committee — was resolved by agreement, leaving Issues 1 and 2 [Para 15][Para 19][Para 20]. The union had 494 members of whom 255 paid dues, and the panel found it was not yet the most representative union in the enterprise [Para 47][Para 48]. Both parties elected a binding award on all issues at the hearing, so the outcome takes effect immediately [Para 26][Para 43][Para 77].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay a USD 10 monthly travel allowance? [Para 49][Para 58]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the basic wage must be raised from USD 61 to USD 66 a month for every worker? [Para 54][Para 72]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** other factories provide the travel benefit, and the workers' own cost survey shows an average of USD 10 a month spent on transport; workers have been at the company since 1998, a neighbouring factory pays a USD 71 basic wage, and USD 61 is too low for a decent living [Para 49][Para 51][Para 55].

**Employer (respondent):** it has no capacity to meet either demand; it says it already pays a USD 7 travel allowance under Ministry Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012, and that the existing basic wage is USD 61 [Para 4][Para 6][Para 53].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider both the USD 10 travel allowance and the increase of the basic wage from USD 61 to USD 66 a month [Para 76][Para 77].

On Issue 1 the panel accepted the factual premise. Drawing on the workers' evidence filed on 14 August 2012, it found that workers at one comparator factory receive USD 5 and at another USD 10 a month for travel, and that the eleven-worker cost survey showed an average transport outlay of USD 10 a month, with the employer currently paying nothing [Para 50][Para 51][Para 52]. It nevertheless found no provision of the Labour Law, no collective agreement and no contract fixing a USD 10 travel allowance, classified the demand as an interest dispute under its Awards No. 31/11 (Khlin Thom Mejeur Indas Striel) and No. 62/11 (Oseang), and refused it [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60].

On Issue 2 the panel recorded the comparators — USD 68.28 at one factory, USD 68.67 at another and USD 61 at a third — but found no legal or contractual duty on the employer to raise the basic wage, so the demand was again an interest dispute decided on the same reasoning [Para 56][Para 72][Para 73].

Having classified both demands as interest disputes, the panel applied its standing rule: under article 96 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 305 article 9 only the most representative union may bargain collectively, and under Prakas No. 099 article 43 an interest award operates as a collective agreement binding every worker in the enterprise for a year, which would cost non-members their right to strike [Para 61][Para 65][Para 66]. Following Award No. 169/11 (Four Fortune), it held this union lacked the required standing and refused to decide either demand on the merits [Para 68][Para 69][Para 70].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Evidence that comparable employers pay a benefit, and that the benefit matches the workers' actual cost, does not convert the claim into a rights dispute: absent a statute, collective agreement or contract, the panel will not award it unless the claimant union represents more than half the workforce [Para 58][Para 66][Para 70].

**Obiter dicta:** none identified beyond the panel's observation that an interest award binds non-members and removes their right to strike on the same subject in future [Para 66].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The signature block is effectively destroyed: the arbitrator names are replaced by stray Latin fragments and an unknown-token marker, so the panel's composition could only be taken from the header [Para 1][Para 78]. Stray words and duplicated clauses interrupt the reasoning at several points, and one sentence repeats itself where a page header was merged into the text [Para 25][Para 64][Para 69]. The award also contradicts itself on travel money: the employer's own statement says it pays USD 7 a month under Notification No. 230, while the panel's facts state the employer pays nothing at all [Para 4][Para 52]. The employer is named inconsistently ("SD", "ASD", "SD Cambodia" and "A S D"), and the wage comparator table appears twice in slightly different form [Para 1][Para 31][Para 56]. Individual workers, union officers and employer representatives are named in full in the source; the brief names none of them.
