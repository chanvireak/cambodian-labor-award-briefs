# Award No. 206/12 — We Li (Cambodia) Garment Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 November 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; Vong Vanna, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · Doc ID 140198

## 1. Facts
We Li (Cambodia) Garment is a garment factory at Kakab, Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh, employing about 600 workers. [Para 76] A local of the Labour Union Federation for Cambodia had notified an election and applied for registration, receiving only a receipt for its file on 19 October 2012 and no registration certificate. [Para 77][Para 57] The federation, mandated by 26 workers, filed twelve demands on working conditions; the panel treated the claim as brought for 25 workers because one name on the mandate could not be read. [Para 78][Para 79]

Nine of the twelve demands were settled at the hearings of 26 October and 6 November 2012, leaving Issues 8, 9 and 10, and the parties agreed to a non-binding award. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 30] A supplementary demand for the reinstatement of a dismissed worker, Mr C, was withdrawn in writing on 15 November 2012 and the panel declined to consider it. [Para 107][Para 108][Para 109]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 8) Whether the workers may require the employer to replace three-month fixed-term contracts with six-month fixed-term contracts after probation ends? [Para 81][Para 111]
2. (Issue 9) Whether Sunday overtime and public-holiday wages must be paid together with the monthly salary, and whether those amounts must be included in the base for the 5% end-of-contract indemnity? [Para 92][Para 131]
3. (Issue 10) Whether the employer must pay team leaders a monthly function allowance of 50 USD? [Para 101][Para 145]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** three-month contracts expose them to easy termination at each expiry and the company should use six-month terms after probation; Sunday and holiday overtime paid weekly depresses the 5% severance because it is left out of the wage base; and team leaders carry heavy duties that justify a 50 USD allowance. [Para 87][Para 96][Para 106]
**Employer (respondent):** it keeps three-month terms because contract length follows the volume of orders and short terms let workers leave without notice; weekly payment of Sunday and holiday overtime is the long-standing practice the majority of workers want; and no law or agreement provides a function allowance. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 91][Para 99][Para 100][Para 105]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claims under Issues 8 and 10 were dismissed and refused consideration, but the employer was ordered to include Sunday overtime and public-holiday wages in the wage base used to compute the 5% end-of-contract indemnity for fixed-term contracts. [Para 157][Para 158][Para 159][Para 160]

On Issue 8 the panel applied Article 65 of the Labour Law, under which a labour contract is governed by the general law of contract and may take whatever form the parties agree, and Articles 664, 311, 336 and 337 of the 2007 Civil Code, which make a contract the meeting of an offer and an acceptance. [Para 111][Para 113][Para 117][Para 119][Para 121] Following earlier awards, it held that neither party may force a contract form or content on the other, and that the panel as a third party cannot impose terms the parties never accepted, so the demand for six-month contracts was refused. [Para 123][Para 124][Para 126][Para 127][Para 129]

On Issue 9 the panel split the demand. Article 103 lists overtime pay as a component of wages and Article 116 requires wages to be paid at least twice a month, but the panel read those provisions as fixing minimum frequency and not as requiring Sunday and holiday overtime to be consolidated into the monthly run; payment weekly, four times a month, therefore complied, and that part was dismissed. [Para 132][Para 134][Para 136][Para 137][Para 139] The second part succeeded: Article 103 makes overtime part of wages and Article 73 (sixth paragraph) sets the end-of-contract indemnity for fixed-term contracts at no less than 5% of the wages paid during the contract where no collective agreement fixes it, so Sunday overtime and holiday wages must be counted in that base. [Para 140][Para 142][Para 143][Para 144]

On Issue 10 the panel held that no law, collective agreement or practice granted a function allowance, which made the demand an interest dispute rather than a rights dispute. [Para 146][Para 147] Following a line of earlier awards, it held that a union may bring an interest dispute only where it is the most representative union in the factory, which under Article 277 requires registration; this local held no certificate, so the panel refused to consider the claim. [Para 149][Para 152][Para 154][Para 155][Para 156]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Overtime pay is part of "wages" under Article 103, so when the statutory 5% end-of-contract indemnity for a fixed-term contract is calculated under Article 73, Sunday overtime and public-holiday wages earned during the contract must be added to the base even though the employer pays them weekly rather than monthly. Conversely, no worker may compel an employer to adopt a longer contract term, because contract form rests on mutual consent under Article 65 and the Civil Code, and a union that has applied for but not obtained registration cannot bring an interest dispute, since Article 277 makes registration a condition of representative status. [Para 142][Para 144][Para 123][Para 155]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the weekly payment of Sunday and holiday overtime reflects the preference of the majority of workers, and its remark that contract length tracks the volume of orders, explain the employer's practice but are not part of the orders. [Para 90][Para 99]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The federation's mandate names 26 workers but the award proceeds for 25, the panel explaining that one worker's name could not be read clearly on the document; the missing worker is unidentified. [Para 78][Para 79]
- The employer is printed as "We Li", "Wei Li", "Ve Li" and "Weli" across the style of cause, the registration certificate and the evidence list. [Para 1][Para 31][Para 32]
- The chair's name in the signature block is corrupted into "ថៃ@ ប៊ុន ណា" plus stray Latin characters, while the header names him differently; the signature page cannot be relied on for the composition. [Para 1][Para 162]
- The panel is described in one place as the "Arbitration Authority Council" and elsewhere as a third party to the contract, an inconsistency in self-description. [Para 124][Para 153]
- Large parts of the Article 664 Civil Code quotation and of the Issue 10 reasoning are replaced by Latin-script gibberish, including the phrase "sonding oner monous to the popure introvers". [Para 113][Para 125][Para 102]
- The 5% indemnity order is printed twice in overlapping fragments at the operative paragraph, and the figure of 50 USD is repeated with a stray "1999" inserted in the middle of the sentence. [Para 145][Para 159]
