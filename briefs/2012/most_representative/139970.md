# Award No. 41/12 — Magnate Footwear Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 10 April 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana for the employer party, Tuon Siphann for the worker party, Run Saray chair) · Doc ID 139970

## 1. Facts
Magnate Footwear Co., Ltd. is a shoe manufacturer at Trapeang Krassaing Commune, Pursenchey District, Phnom Penh, employing 3,000 workers; the claimant is the Local Union of CLUF, which had not been formally registered and held only an acknowledgement receipt of its application dated 2 February 2012, an objection the employer did not take. [Para 1][Para 22][Para 23][Para 24] The employer conceded 13 of the 16 demands, leaving three non-conciliated issues, and at the hearing the workers added a demand that five workers be reinstated after they refused transfer when a gluing section was disbanded on 10 February 2012. [Para 10][Para 12][Para 44] Both parties are signatories to the Memorandum of Understanding on Improving Industrial Relations in the Garment Industry dated 28 September 2010, so the panel split the issues into rights and interests disputes. [Para 15]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must provide a monthly accommodation and transportation allowance, revised at the hearing to US$ 10 transportation and US$ 7 accommodation per worker? [Para 2][Para 25]
2. Whether the employer must allow the three union leaders to use a handheld transceiver to receive information from the employer? [Para 3]
3. Whether the employer must allow a half-hour dinner break with wages and benefits maintained during overtime, revised at the hearing to overtime worked from 6:00 p.m. to 8:00 p.m.? [Para 4][Para 38]
4. Whether the panel must consider the additional demand that the employer reinstate the five workers who refused transfer to the new branch? [Para 42][Para 73]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** consumer prices have risen, they each spend US$ 10 a month on transport, and other factories such as Berry and Nextoo already provide these benefits; a transceiver is cheaper than a mobile phone and lets the union reach local unions at other factories; overtime from 1:00 p.m. onwards leaves them exhausted. [Para 25][Para 27][Para 30][Para 31][Para 38]
**Employer (respondent):** it cannot afford the allowance unless the law requires it; a transceiver would disturb other workers and affect the production line, and two announcements of 1 March 2011 and 1 January 2012 prohibit it; it will grant the half-hour break but will not pay for it and requires make-up work. [Para 3][Para 28][Para 31][Para 34][Para 40][Para 41]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — Part I (binding award on rights): issue 2 rejected and the additional demand declined; Part II (non-binding award on interests): issues 1 and 3 declined for want of representative status. [Para 86][Para 87]

On issue 1 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, a collective agreement or any agreement between the parties requiring the allowance, which made it an interests dispute; an award on an interests dispute takes the place of a collective bargaining agreement for one year under Clause 43 of Prakas No. 099, so the panel will only hear such a claim from a union holding most representative status (MRS) or representing more than half the workforce, following 81/04-Ever Green, 98/04-Great Union, 169/11-Fortune Teo, 02/11-Pou Yuen and 66/11-In Han Sung; this union had no registration certificate and so no standing to claim a benefit for all 3,000 workers. [Para 49][Para 52][Para 54][Para 55] On issue 2 the panel treated the demand as a rights dispute within the employer's management prerogatives under Article 2 paragraph 2 of the Labour Law, and held the prohibition reasonable because use of transceivers during working hours would affect the production line, consistently with 17/07-Charm Textile and 116/07-Grace Sun. [Para 56][Para 58][Para 65][Para 66] On issue 3 it again found no legal or agreed basis, and considered the employer's position reasonable because the workers perform no service during the break and so are not owed wages for it. [Para 68][Para 71] On the additional demand the panel applied Article 312 of the Labour Law, which confines it to the non-conciliation report and to events that are a direct consequence of the current dispute; the strike notice of 14 February 2012 preceded the five workers' refusal to transfer on 16 February 2012, and the workers had asked CLUF for help on 1 February 2012, so the reinstatement demand was a separate issue and was declined. [Para 74][Para 77][Para 82][Para 83][Para 85]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that has no basis in the Labour Law, an agreement or a collective agreement is an interests dispute, and because an award on an interests dispute operates for one year as a collective agreement binding every worker in the factory, the panel will decline it where the claimant union holds neither a certificate of registration nor most representative status — the employer's failure to object to the union's standing does not cure the defect. [Para 49][Para 52][Para 55]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that non-members lose their right to strike on future interests disputes and that the use of handheld transceivers is subject to reasonable management prerogative are general observations on representation and supervision, not findings required to reject the three demands. [Para 53][Para 65]
