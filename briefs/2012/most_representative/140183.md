# Award No. 182/12 — Top Form (Cambodia) Co., Ltd
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel: Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Virak (worker-appointed), Pen Bunchea (chair) · award dated 10 October 2012 · Doc ID 140183

## 1. Facts

Top Form (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a garment factory that began operating in mid-September 2011 and employs about 620 workers [Para 59] [Para 60]. The claimant is the Federation of Trade Unions for Workers' Interests, acting for the workforce [Para 61].

A strike complaint reached the Labour Disputes Department on 6 September 2012, listing 16 demands [Para 24]. Conciliation on 7 September 2012 resolved none of them [Para 25], and the file was forwarded to the Arbitration Council on 10 September 2012 [Para 26]. The Council held hearings on 19 September and 8 October 2012; the workers did not appear at the second hearing [Para 22] [Para 23] [Para 27].

At the first hearing the parties settled Issues 4, 5, 6, 9, 10, 12, 13, 14, 15 and 16 by agreement, and the claimant withdrew Issue 8 [Para 28] [Para 29]. The panel therefore set out to decide Issues 1, 2, 3, 7 and 11 [Para 30]. In the event it decided only Issues 1, 2 and 3; on Issues 7 (medical costs for commuting accidents) and 11 (wage retention during certified sick leave) it recorded the employer's explanation that it already preserved wages, bonus and travel payments on production of an NSSF-recognised hospital certificate, and declined to order anything [Para 193] [Para 194] [Para 195] [Para 196]. Those two demands were recorded but never ruled on.

Two threshold matters were resolved. Of 251 authorisation letters, 13 bore unclear thumbprints, so only 238 workers were considered [Para 66] [Para 67]. The employer objected that the federation was unregistered at the company and that only lawyers may represent parties; the panel held that Article 19 of Prakas 099/04 allows representation by any person with written authority, and the federation's authorisations were valid [Para 148] [Para 152] [Para 155].

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay a 4,000 riel overtime meal allowance to workers who work overtime until 18:00? [Para 156]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must stop withholding tax from workers' wages? [Para 172]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must stop deducting the regular attendance bonus when a worker is occasionally late? [Para 185]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** The employer pays only 2,000 riel meal money for overtime from 16:00 to 18:00, but other factories pay 4,000 riel; food prices have risen and workers leaving at 18:00 find the market closed [Para 72] [Para 74] [Para 75]. On tax, the employer deducts from base wage, bonus and benefits for anyone earning from USD 130 a month, without explanation [Para 80] [Para 82] [Para 86]. On lateness, delays come from traffic, broken transport or punctured tyres, yet more than five minutes' delay is treated as absence and cuts both wage and bonus [Para 92] [Para 94] [Para 95].

**Employer (respondent):** Notice 041/11 fixes the overtime meal allowance at 2,000 riel, and there is no legal or agreed basis for 4,000 [Para 157] [Para 158]. Tax is withheld at the rates set by the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the tax law, applying the definition of wages in Article 103 of the Labour Law; the employer cannot lawfully stop [Para 89] [Para 90] [Para 91]. On punctuality, the employer does not deduct the bonus for lateness of up to five minutes a day and up to 30 minutes a month, which is more generous than the legal minimum; it deducts only where lateness exceeds the 15-minute tolerance in each half-month period [Para 103] [Para 104] [Para 110].

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** dismissed — all three decided claims were refused, Issue 2 for want of jurisdiction [Para 197] [Para 198] [Para 199].

On Issue 1 the panel found that the employer pays 2,000 riel per day for overtime worked from 16:00 to 18:00, as required by point 2 of Notice 041/11, and that no law, collective agreement or practice supported 4,000 riel [Para 156] [Para 157] [Para 158]. It characterised the higher claim as an interest dispute and, because the claimant federation was not the most representative union in the enterprise and was not registered there, declined to consider it [Para 159] [Para 169] [Para 170] [Para 171].

On Issue 2 the panel asked whether a demand that the employer stop withholding tax is a collective labour dispute at all [Para 172]. Article 302 defines such a dispute as one between employer and workers over working conditions, the exercise of professional-organisation rights, recognition of a professional organisation, or employer-worker relations, capable of disrupting the enterprise or public peace [Para 173]. The panel found none of the three conditions met: the withholding obligation runs between the worker and the State, represented by the Ministry of Economy and Finance, not between worker and employer [Para 175] [Para 176] [Para 177]; it does not concern working conditions or professional-organisation rights [Para 178] [Para 179]; and no evidence showed it threatened production or public order [Para 180] [Para 181]. The claim was therefore outside jurisdiction [Para 182] [Para 183] [Para 184].

On Issue 3 the panel read point 2 of Notice 230 of 25 July 2012, which entitles a worker who attends on all the working days of a month without absence to a bonus of at least USD 10 [Para 185]. It held that "attending regularly on the working days of the month" includes attending at the hours fixed by the company, so a worker who arrives late has not attended regularly and is not entitled to the bonus [Para 186] [Para 187]. It then examined the employer's actual practice: no deduction for lateness of up to five minutes a day and up to 30 minutes a month, with the 30 minutes split into 15-minute halves [Para 188] [Para 189]. It called that practice considerate and refused the claim [Para 190] [Para 191] [Para 192] [Para 199].

## 5. Ratio decidendi

Three rules emerge. First, a demand that the employer cease withholding tax is not a collective labour dispute within Article 302, because the obligation is owed by the worker to the State rather than arising between worker and employer, so the panel has no jurisdiction [Para 173] [Para 176] [Para 183]. Second, where a Prakas or notice fixes a minimum entitlement such as the 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance, a claim for a larger sum is an interest dispute that only a most-representative, registered union may bring [Para 158] [Para 159] [Para 170]. Third, an attendance bonus conditioned on regular attendance at the hours fixed by the employer is not earned by a worker who arrives late, but a tolerance practice more generous than the legal minimum will be upheld [Para 186] [Para 187] [Para 190].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the employer's tolerance of five minutes' lateness a day was considerate of workers' transport difficulties [Para 190]. It also noted that the employer had already agreed to preserve wages and bonus during certified sick leave and for commuting accidents, so no order was needed [Para 194] [Para 196].

## 6. Source defects disclosed

The final eleven lines of the file are OCR wreckage: strings of digits, Latin fragments and disconnected Khmer syllables that cannot be read as sentences, and the signature block and annexed authorisation list are lost [Para 201] [Para 202] [Para 207] [Para 211]. The employer's name is a name defect — the award renders it three different ways on its face, including variant spellings of the same enterprise, so the file is unreliable on the corporate name [Para 1] [Para 59] [Para 64]. The award date is given as 10 October 2012 in the header, while the reasons record that it was to issue on 3 October 2012 and was postponed by agreement, which is reconcilable but leaves the operative date ambiguous [Para 1] [Para 31]. The demand recorded as Issue 8 appears in the claim list as USD 7 travel and accommodation plus a USD 10 bonus, and the same figures are repeated when the claim is withdrawn, so the numbering of the surviving issues shifts between paragraphs [Para 9] [Para 124].
