# Award No. 177/11 — Kwai Yang (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 19 January 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Pen Bun Chhea (president), Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed) · Doc ID 140116

## 1. Facts
Kwai Yang (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. is a garment factory in Ang Snuol district, Kandal province, employing about 550 workers producing winter clothing on irregular orders. [Para 46][Para 65] On 21 November 2011 the provincial labour department received a claim of 15 demands from the workers' friendship union federation; conciliation settled eight and left seven unresolved, which reached the Council on 22 December 2011. [Para 17][Para 18] A strike broke out during the proceedings and the panel issued Order No. 017/11 KBA on 3 January 2012 requiring an immediate return to work, which was obeyed. [Para 24][Para 25] At the hearing issues 5 and 7 settled, issues 2 and 3 were withdrawn, leaving issues 1, 4 and 6; the federation held written delegations from only 43 workers. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 23][Para 47]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pro-rate the attendance bonus by the number of days of family-related leave it has authorised, rather than deducting a flat amount per day (panel's issue 1)? [Para 48][Para 68]
2. Whether the employer must pay a USD 10 monthly transport allowance to workers who do not live in the company's free housing (panel's issue 4)? [Para 57][Para 89]
3. Whether the employer must convert three-month fixed-duration contracts into one-year contracts (panel's issue 6)? [Para 63][Para 100]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Under the company's Notification of 15 November 2011 the attendance bonus is USD 12 (USD 7 attendance plus USD 5 incentive), and a worker who takes one authorised day off loses USD 2 of it even though the leave was lawfully granted; the bonus should be cut in proportion to the days taken. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 54][Para 56] Workers who must travel home rather than stay in company housing spend money on fares while housed workers spend nothing, which is unequal, so USD 10 a month should be paid. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 60] Contracts of three months should become one-year contracts because workers worry about job security and confidence. [Para 63][Para 64]

**Employer (respondent):** The notification is the applicable rule: full USD 12 for a month with no absence, a USD 2 deduction for an authorised day off, and forfeiture of the whole bonus for unauthorised absence. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 55] The company built four houses for workers free of charge and encourages everyone to live there to save time and money, so no transport allowance is owed. [Para 5][Para 62] Contracts are a matter of agreement, and because orders for winter jackets are irregular the company cannot commit to long fixed terms; every three-month contract is paid with monthly wages, a 5 per cent indemnity and annual leave. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pro-rate the attendance bonus by the number of authorised days off, while the transport-allowance and contract-conversion claims were both dismissed; the award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent written objection. [Para 120][Para 121][Para 122][Para 123]

On issue 1, the panel noted that the new notification, like Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000, is silent on lawful absence, and that earlier awards therefore ordered pro-rating rather than total forfeiture of the attendance bonus. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 71][Para 72] Following Awards No. 112/11-Yong Va I and No. 136/11-Cambodia Hen Sam, it held that a worker with valid authorisation should not lose the whole bonus, only the days actually taken. [Para 74][Para 75] It then applied Article 13 of the Labour Law, under which labour-law provisions are public order and any rule giving a worker less is void: clause I(3)(a) of the 15 November 2011 notification, deducting a flat USD 2 per authorised day, fell below that standard and could not be applied. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 85][Para 86][Para 88]

On issue 4, the panel classified the demand as an interest dispute, since the allowance appears in no law, agreement or collective convention and exceeds what the law provides. [Para 89] In interest disputes it considers whether the claimant union has most-representative status and has refused to hear such disputes where it does not. [Para 90][Para 91] Only 43 workers had delegated to the federation and no plant union with most-representative status was a party, so the claim could not be brought. [Para 92][Para 98][Para 99]

On issue 6, the panel applied Article 65 of the Labour Law and Articles 664, 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code: a contract exists only where offer and acceptance match, and neither party may impose terms the other has not accepted. [Para 101][Para 103][Para 106][Para 108][Para 110] Here the workers offered a one-year term and the employer did not accept, and the employer offered three months and the workers did not accept, so no contract of either length arises and the claim failed. [Para 117][Para 118][Para 119]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a bonus notification is silent on lawful absence, an attendance bonus must be cut in proportion to the authorised days taken, not forfeited or flat-rated; and because Article 13 makes labour-law standards public order, a company rule that deducts more than that proportion is void and will not be applied. [Para 71][Para 84][Para 88] A monthly transport allowance found in no law or agreement is an interest dispute, which a federation holding delegations from only a minority of the workforce cannot bring. [Para 89][Para 98] Contract duration cannot be imposed by either side: absent matching offer and acceptance there is no contract term to enforce. [Para 110][Para 119]

**Obiter dicta:** The employer-appointed arbitrator dissented on issue 1, reasoning from dictionary and French labour-law definitions that a bonus is conditional and that its giver decides whether the condition has been met, so a pro-rata order substitutes the panel's judgment for the employer's. [Para 132][Para 134][Para 141]
