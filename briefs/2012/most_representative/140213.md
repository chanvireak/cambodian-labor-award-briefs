# Award No. 221/12 — Wah Yang Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 20 December 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3 (Ing Sothy for the employer, An Non for the workers, Kong Phalha presiding) · Doc ID 140213

## 1. Facts
Wah Yang Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a garment factory at Trapeang Chhuk, Tuek Thla, Sen Sok, Phnom Penh, employing about 320 workers, of whom roughly 95 per cent are women. The Cambodian Workers' Movement Federation and its factory union are the most representative union at the plant, certified by letter No. 044/11 of 10 May 2011 valid to 10 May 2013. The union filed eleven demands; conciliation failed on all eleven, but at the hearing the parties settled nine of them (Issues 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10 and 11), leaving only Issue 3 (a nursery or USD 15 a month milk allowance for mothers who have given birth) and Issue 8 (pro-rata deduction of the attendance bonus). [Para 1][Para 26][Para 43][Para 45][Para 46]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must build a nursery or pay USD 15 a month in milk allowance to women who have returned from maternity leave? [Para 46]
2. (Issue 8) Whether the employer must reduce the regular attendance bonus in proportion to days of absence taken with the employer's permission? [Para 50]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** there is no nursery on site and mothers need either a crèche or USD 15 a month towards milk; and the employer forfeits the whole USD 12 monthly attendance bonus when a worker takes even one day of permitted personal leave, whereas the panel in Awards 136/11, 154/11, 169/11, 171/11 and 216/12 ordered pro-rata deduction. [Para 46][Para 51][Para 53]
**Employer (respondent):** it is preparing to build a nursery but cannot say when it will be finished; and it will continue its existing practice of forfeiting the entire attendance bonus whenever a worker is absent on personal leave, even where that leave was authorised. [Para 49][Para 56]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to provide a nursery within a reasonable period and, until then, to pay the actual invoiced cost of an outside nursery for any worker who uses one; the demand for pro-rata deduction of the attendance bonus was dismissed. [Para 65][Para 92]
Issue 3: Art 186 of the 1997 Labour Law requires any enterprise employing at least 100 women to arrange a nursing room and a nursery, and where the enterprise cannot accommodate children over 18 months, the mother may place the child in an outside nursery at the employer's expense. The panel found the factory had 320 workers, about 95 per cent of them women, and had no nursery; that the employer said it was building one but could not give a date; and that the statutory duty was therefore unmet. It ordered a nursery within a reasonable period and, in the meantime, reimbursement of the real cost of an outside nursery, following Award 81/09. [Para 57][Para 62][Para 63][Para 64][Para 65]
Issue 8: the attendance bonus is fixed by point 2 of Notification 230 of 25 July 2012, which replaced Notification 041/11 and added the words "without absence". The panel held that the new wording makes the bonus conditional, that a worker absent on personal leave — even with the employer's permission and without pay — has not attended on the working days of that month, and that such a worker therefore does not satisfy the condition. It expressly departed from its earlier awards (112/11, 136/11, 154/11) because those rested on the repealed notification, and dismissed the demand for pro-rata deduction. Leave that the Labour Law itself requires to be treated as attendance — annual, special, sick with a medical certificate, maternity, public holiday and weekly rest — was distinguished, and the panel held that only those forms of leave preserve the bonus. [Para 66][Para 70][Para 73][Para 74][Para 81][Para 82][Para 89]
Dissent: arbitrator An Non dissented on Issue 8, reasoning that reading "absence" as unlawful absence is an assumption not drawn from the Khmer dictionary or from any authoritative source, that the majority's reading empties the phrase of its protective purpose, and that the earlier awards should have been followed. [Para 96][Para 101][Para 103][Para 108]
Effect: the decision is a non-binding award which becomes final eight days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Minister. [Para 93]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer with at least 100 women workers must provide a nursery, and until it does so it must pay the actual cost of an outside nursery for any worker who needs one; but where the attendance-bonus notification conditions the bonus on attendance "without absence", permitted unpaid personal leave breaks the condition, and the employer may forfeit the whole monthly bonus rather than deduct it pro rata. [Para 64][Para 64][Para 89]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the earlier line of awards ordering pro-rata deduction could no longer be followed because it had been built on a notification that had since been repealed. [Para 74]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is an order of a non-binding character, and the text says both that nine of eleven issues were settled at the hearing [Para 25] and that conciliation had failed on all eleven [Para 20]; the two statements are reconcilable only if "settled" refers to the hearing, which is not spelled out. Page markers from the scan ("-៣-", "-១០-") intrude mid-sentence, and paragraph 91 is corrupted by OCR into a string of numbers and Latin fragments. The union's factory president, vice-president and the four federation officials who attended are named in full and are reduced here to roles. One worker's age is printed as "95 per cent" in a sentence about the workforce, evidently a stray decimal point [Para 48]. The presiding arbitrator's name appears as Kong Phalha in the composition and in a different transliteration in the signature block [Para 94].
