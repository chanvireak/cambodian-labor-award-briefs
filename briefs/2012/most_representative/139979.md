# Award No. 48/12 — Hor Thyun Thiv Garment Factory Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 10 April 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Tuon Siphann; chair Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 139979

## 1. Facts
Hor Thyun Thiv Garment Factory Co., Ltd. is a registered garment factory employing 413 workers, with two unions: the Khmer Youth Voice Union and the Cambodia Workers' Union, the claimant, which had about 150 members and was still awaiting its registration certificate on a receipt dated 20 January 2012. Mr S, a sewing worker and union secretary, worked from 5 August 2010 under successive fixed duration contracts, the last running 1 August 2011 to 31 January 2012, when the employer declined to renew it and offered final payments totalling US$ 95.32. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57][Para 58][Para 59][Para 76][Para 79]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the overtime meal allowance once a week, every Saturday, rather than every two weeks? [Para 60]
2. Whether the employer must provide travel and accommodation allowance of US$ 10 per month per worker? [Para 68]
3. Whether the employer must pay 1,000 riel per hour as meal allowance when workers volunteer for overtime from 10 p.m. to 6 a.m.? [Para 70]
4. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr S as union secretary with back pay from the date of termination, the union also demanding that contracts not be terminated without clear reason and without discrimination? [Para 73]

Demands 5 and 6, on termination without clear reason and on non-discrimination, were joined to issue 4 at the hearing. [Para 24][Para 25]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the overtime meal allowance is needed weekly because workers buy their daily meals with it and send money home, and cannot wait two weeks; the US$ 10 travel and accommodation allowance is justified because goods have become dearer and other factories pay US$ 13 and US$ 5; the night-shift meal allowance is needed because workers require food and a drink to stay awake; and Mr S's contract had always been renewed until he became union secretary, after which the employer hired replacements. [Para 61][Para 68][Para 70][Para 81][Para 82]
**Employer (respondent):** it has paid the overtime meal allowance fortnightly since the factory opened in 2009 under a written agreement of 21 January 2012; it cannot afford the travel allowance; the night shift already receives a meal worth 3,000 riel, double wages and a paid rest day plus 2,500 riel meal money; and Mr S's contract simply reached its term, as happened to other sewing workers before and after him. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 69][Para 71][Para 84]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all four demands were rejected, including reinstatement and back pay for Mr S, and the additional claim concerning Mr T was not considered. Nothing was ordered. [Para 161][Para 162][Para 163]

On issue 1, the panel treated the fortnightly payment as resting on the 21 January 2012 agreement and therefore as a rights dispute, but the workers had been ordered to file the list of workers actually claiming the weekly payment by 19 March 2012 and had not done so. Without a list the panel found no identified claimants and rejected the demand. [Para 97][Para 98][Para 100][Para 102][Para 103]

On issues 2 and 3, the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement or agreement obliging the employer to pay either benefit, so both were interests disputes. An interests award replaces a collective agreement and binds all workers, non-members losing the right to strike, so only a union with most representative status, or several unions together representing over half the workforce, may bring one (Awards 81/04-Evergreen, 98/04-Great Union, 169/11-Pou Yuen, 02/11-Pou Yuen, 66/11-In Han Sung). The claimant union had not yet received its registration certificate at all. It therefore lacked standing, and the panel declined to consider both demands. [Para 105][Para 106][Para 108][Para 111][Para 112][Para 114][Para 118]

On issue 4, Article 73 provides that a fixed duration contract expires by itself when its term falls due, ending the obligations of both sides at once. The panel held that declining to renew at expiry is the employer's decision not to hire, not a dismissal, so the termination was lawful in form and the question became whether it was tainted by discrimination under Articles 12 and 279. Following Award 10/03-Jacqsintex and Awards 28/07-DF and 123/07-Um Garment, the panel confirmed that non-renewal can amount to prohibited discrimination, but that the party alleging it bears the burden of proof. [Para 128][Para 130][Para 133][Para 137][Para 139][Para 142][Para 145]

The union's case rested on the sequence of events: contracts renewed until Mr S became secretary, then not renewed, with new workers hired. The panel found this insufficient on its own. No specific evidence of discrimination was filed by the 19 March 2012 deadline, and the employer showed that two other sewing workers' contracts also went unrenewed in January 2012 while 15 new sewing workers were recruited, and two more in February against 25 recruits — a pattern inconsistent with targeting the union. The claim was therefore rejected. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 90][Para 147][Para 149][Para 150][Para 152]

The additional claim for Mr T, whose contract expired on 28 February 2012 after the conciliation record of 23 February 2012, required the panel to apply Article 312: it may consider only matters in the non-conciliation record or arising after it as a direct consequence of that dispute. Because Mr T's non-renewal came later and was not a direct consequence of Mr S's case, it was not considered. [Para 155][Para 156][Para 157][Para 159][Para 163]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Non-renewal of a fixed duration contract at its expiry is not a dismissal, and a union alleging that such non-renewal is anti-union discrimination must prove it: the bare coincidence that a worker held union office at the moment his contract ended, coupled with replacement hiring, does not discharge the worker party's burden of proof. [Para 133][Para 142][Para 147][Para 152]

**Obiter dicta:** that an interests dispute may be brought by several unions aggregating more than half the workforce even absent a single most-representative union; and that a claim arising after the conciliation record is admissible only if it is a direct consequence of the recorded dispute. [Para 110][Para 159]
