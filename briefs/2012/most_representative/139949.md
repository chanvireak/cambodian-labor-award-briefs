# Award No. 24/12 — New Arch Kid Garment Factory Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 6 March 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 139949

## 1. Facts
New Arch Kid Garment Factory is a garment and embroidery factory in Ang Snuol district, Kandal, employing about 3,500 workers; it moved there from Phnom Penh in December 2008. [Para 49][Para 53] The claimant union has 1,318 members and no certificate of most representative status. [Para 50] Sixteen demands were lodged; the parties settled or withdrew five at the hearing, leaving eleven for decision, eight of which turn on the law. [Para 28][Para 29]

## 2. Issues in dispute
2. Whether the employer must give each worker US$ 10 a month for transport or accommodation? [Para 3][Para 53]
3. Whether the employer must raise the monthly attendance bonus from US$ 7 to US$ 15, being US$ 8 more? [Para 4][Para 61]
4. Whether the employer must pay a skill bonus of US$ 5, US$ 3 and US$ 2 to workers who can operate three, two and one machine? [Para 5][Para 67]
5. Whether the employer must raise the minimum wage of every section's assistant by US$ 5? [Para 6][Para 73]
9. Whether the employer must add 1,500 riel to the meal allowance for overtime worked from 18:00 to 20:00? [Para 10][Para 86]
10. Whether the employer must move the drinking-water tank away from the toilets? [Para 11][Para 90]
11. Whether the employer must install two hot-and-cold water dispensers in each building? [Para 12][Para 95]
12. Whether the employer must let shop stewards carry out their duties in every building? [Para 13][Para 98]
13. Whether the employer must obtain the shop stewards' agreement before requiring overtime until dawn? [Para 14][Para 103]
14. Whether the employer must equalise the wages of team leaders across all teams and sections? [Para 15][Para 106]
16. Whether the employer must repay the 5% of the US$ 30 that workers borrowed? [Para 17][Para 192]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** all workers serve the one factory under one manager but only about ten who agreed to move to the new site in 2008 receive US$ 5 of housing money, so the benefit should be equal; the $7 attendance bonus should become $15; skilled operators deserve a technical bonus; assistants in building C already got $5 more, so all should; the 18:00–20:00 shift earns only 500 riel of meal money against 2,000 riel earlier; the water tank sits about three metres from the toilets and smells; cold and hot water should be available in each building; shop stewards should reach workers in every building without the building head's permission; the employer never seeks their agreement for overtime until dawn. [Para 57][Para 62][Para 67][Para 75][Para 86][Para 91][Para 95][Para 99][Para 102][Para 105]
**Employer (respondent):** the $5 housing money was a goodwill gesture to workers of the old factory who helped during the move, there is no policy or means to extend it, and five trucks already carry 300 to 400 workers free; the $7 bonus follows the Ministry's instruction; no funds exist for technical or wage increases; the tank has a cover, a lock and a guard and there is no hygiene problem; water is available in the medical room; it never barred stewards from the buildings, it asks only that they notify the building head; it accepts that supervisors' pay now comes on the 15th. [Para 54][Para 58][Para 59][Para 65][Para 93][Para 96][Para 100][Para 101]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the water tank must be moved away from the toilets, the employer must obtain the shop stewards' agreement before overtime until dawn, and it must repay the 5% of the US$ 30 loan; the remaining eight demands were declined. [Para 196][Para 201][Para 204][Para 206]

The panel disposed of eight demands on a single ground. Each was found to have no basis in the Labour Law, a Prakas, a collective agreement or the individual contract, so each was a demand above what the law provides, hence an interests dispute. [Para 116][Para 129][Para 134][Para 139][Para 145][Para 188] An interests award would take the place of a collective agreement for one year under Clause 43 of Prakas No. 099 and bind all workers, so only an MRS union, or unions representing more than half the workforce, may bring such a dispute; this union holds no MRS certificate, so the panel declined to consider the transport, attendance, skill, assistant-wage, meal-allowance, dispenser, building-access and team-leader demands. [Para 123][Para 124][Para 126][Para 127]

On the water tank the panel applied Clause 3 of Prakas No. 054 on hygienic drinking water, which requires the tank to stand near the workplace but leaves the precise place to the internal rules, and Article 229(1) of the Labour Law, which obliges every establishment to keep the workplace clean and healthy. [Para 149][Para 151][Para 153][Para 154] The internal rules say nothing on the point, and the panel found as fact that the tank stands beside toilets whose smell reaches it, so the employer had not secured the hygiene the law requires; it rejected the argument that cleaning measures would suffice, since water for drinking must not be sited next to toilets at all, and ordered the tank moved to the front near the production-line entrance or to any hygienic place near the workplace. [Para 152][Para 155][Para 156][Para 160][Para 161]

On the dispensers the panel read Clause 1 of Prakas No. 054 as requiring sufficient drinking water, not a set number or capacity of containers, and found the employer already supplied water to requirement and in the medical room; the demand was one of convenience only, and the employer's fear of spillage in the production buildings was reasonable, so it was refused. [Para 162][Para 163][Para 164][Para 167][Para 169]

On issue 13 the panel relied on Clause 2 of Prakas No. 80, which requires a written request for overtime to be accompanied by a statement of agreement from every shop steward of the enterprise; the employer had not sought that agreement before overtime until dawn, and was ordered to do so. [Para 185][Para 186][Para 187] On issue 16 it found that the US$ 30 borrowed before the 2011 Pchum Ben festival had already been recovered from wages, that no debt remained, and that the 5% deducted on top must be repaid. [Para 193][Para 194][Para 195]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a benefit that no law, Prakas, collective agreement or contract provides is an interests dispute, and because an interests award operates as a one-year collective agreement binding the whole workforce, a union without most representative status has no standing to bring it, whatever its merit. By contrast, siting a drinking-water tank beside a toilet breaches Article 229(1) and Clause 3 of Prakas No. 054 even if the toilets are kept clean, because the law requires water near the workplace and hygienically placed, and the employer's internal rules cannot fill that gap. [Para 123][Para 126][Para 156][Para 160]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that the employer's voluntary US$ 5 payment to workers who stayed through the 2008 move was a goodwill gesture, capable of being extended but not required, is an observation on the equities and not a rule. [Para 59][Para 163]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The reasons jump from issue 5 to issue 9 and from 14 to 16 [Para 139][Para 145][Para 192], and issues 6 and 7 appear only in the settled list [Para 28]; issue 7 was withdrawn rather than agreed, so the record conflates settlement and withdrawal.
- The attendance-bonus discussion is dated "February 2023" at [Para 128] in an award of 6 March 2012; the year is impossible and is reported as printed.
- The opening party block contains machine-garbled tokens ("&lt;unk&gt;") and a run of repeated nonsense syllables in place of the union's address [Para 1]; the registrar's data is unrecoverable there.
- The union's appeal letters and the Ministry's report name the employer inconsistently, including "ញូវ អាពយុ គូ" [Para 45], against "ញូ អាក ឃីត ហ្គាមេន ហ្វេក ធើរី" on the face of the award [Para 1].
- Issue 16's reasoning and order are internally inconsistent: the panel finds no debt remains and no further payment is due [Para 193], then orders the employer to repay 5% of the US$ 30 [Para 195][Para 206].
