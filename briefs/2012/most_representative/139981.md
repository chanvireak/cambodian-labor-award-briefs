# Award No. 50/12-New Archid — New Archid Garment Factory Limited (Arbitration Council, 25 April 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun, Tuon Siphann, Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 139981

## 1. Facts
New Archid Garment Factory Limited operates a garment factory in Kandal Province employing approximately 3,400 workers. The Khmer Workers Pride Federation Union (KWPFU) Local Union, representing 200 workers, brought a collective dispute after conciliation resolved five of 18 issues. The remaining 13 issues concerned working conditions, union rights, and wages. [Para 1][Para 4][Para 20][Para 24]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer should provide a transportation allowance to workers from Kompong Speu province by taxi, as it does for Phnom Penh workers? [Para 9][Para 25]
2. Whether the employer should provide a free meal when overtime work is requested from 11:00 p.m.? [Para 10][Para 29]
3. Whether the employer should refrain from deducting union contribution fees from wages upon request? [Para 11][Para 32]
4. Whether the employer should provide a monthly US$ 10 bonus to assistants to group leaders? [Para 12][Para 34]
5. Whether the employer should immediately rectify underpaid wages? [Para 12][Para 39]
6. Whether the employer should set sewing work amounts proportionate to working hours? [Para 12][Para 44]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The workers demanded a transportation allowance for Kompong Speu workers because their base wage of US$ 61 is insufficient and the employer provides taxis for Phnom Penh staff. They also sought a free meal for late overtime, cessation of union fee deductions for 177 workers who revoked authorisation, a US$ 10 bonus for group leader assistants, immediate rectification of underpaid wages, and proportional sewing targets. [Para 26][Para 29][Para 32][Para 35][Para 40][Para 46]
**Employer (respondent):** The employer refused the transportation allowance, stating the taxis for Phnom Penh staff were contractual benefits and it cannot afford the demand. It refused the free meal, noting occasional late overtime and next-day time off. It continued deducting union fees because workers did not request in person. It refused the bonus, citing performance-based pay. It stated wage rectification required the director's signature from abroad, and that production targets were set by buyers' evaluations. [Para 27][Para 30][Para 33][Para 38][Para 41][Para 56]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to refrain from deducting union contribution fees from the wages of 177 workers who requested cessation, and to rectify underpaid wages on pay day. It declined to consider the transportation allowance and US$ 10 bonus as interests disputes, declined the free meal demand, and rejected the proportional work demand. [Para 90][Para 91][Para 92]
The panel found the transportation allowance demand was not supported by the Labour Law, an agreement, or a collective agreement, making it an interests dispute; because the union lacked most representative status (MRS), the panel declined to consider it. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61][Para 62][Para 63]
The free meal demand was also an interests dispute with no legal or contractual basis, so the panel declined it. [Para 63][Para 64]
On union fee deductions, Article 129 paragraph 2 of the Labour Law permits workers to revoke authorisation at any time; the employer's practice of requiring in-person requests contravened this, so the panel ordered cessation for the 177 workers who had submitted written requests. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70]
The US$ 10 bonus demand was an interests dispute with no legal or contractual basis, so the panel declined to consider it. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74]
On underpaid wages, Article 102 requires full remuneration on pay day; the panel found the period between pay slip distribution (10:30 a.m.) and payment (2:30 p.m.) sufficient for rectification, and ordered the employer to rectify underpayments on pay day. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 80][Para 83]
On production targets, the panel applied prior awards holding that employers have discretion to set targets and that claimants bear the burden of proof; the workers failed to provide evidence that targets were unreasonable, so the panel rejected the demand. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 89][Para 90]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union without most representative status lacks legal standing to bring an interests dispute concerning collective interests before the Arbitration Council; such a dispute will be declined. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63]
An employer's practice requiring workers to submit in-person requests to cease union fee deductions contravenes Article 129 of the Labour Law; written revocation must be honoured. [Para 68][Para 69]
Under Article 102, wages must be paid in full on pay day; administrative constraints such as a director's availability do not excuse delayed rectification. [Para 77][Para 83]
An employer has discretion to set production targets and incentive bonuses; a claimant alleging targets are unreasonable bears the burden of proof. [Para 87][Para 89]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that late-night overtime occurred occasionally and workers received next-day time off and an overtime allowance, but these observations did not alter the outcome on the free meal demand. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 32]
