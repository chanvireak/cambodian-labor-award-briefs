# Award No. 185/12 — Win Sing Tech (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 26 October 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Venh Pov, Pen Bunhea as chair) · Doc ID 140187

## 1. Facts

Win Sing Tech (Cambodia) is a garment factory in Choam Chao, Phnom Penh, employing about 860 workers, and the Democratic Union of Khmer Workers in the Garment Industry is the claimant [Para 44][Para 45]. The union filed three demands on 8 August 2012: severance calculated on a twelve-month average wage, an increase of the base wage for Khmer section chiefs to USD 300 and for Khmer team leaders to USD 250 a month, and proportional payment of the attendance bonus when a worker takes necessary leave [Para 3][Para 5][Para 7]. Conciliation on 11 September 2012 settled none of them, and the file reached the Council on 13 September 2012 [Para 15][Para 16]. At the hearing of 27 September 2012 the parties reached agreement on Issue 1 — the severance basis — so the panel decided only Issues 2 and 3 [Para 18][Para 19]. Because both parties had signed the memorandum of understanding of 28 September 2010 on professional relations in the garment industry, the panel split its decision between disputes over rights, which bind the parties, and disputes over interests, which do not [Para 20][Para 21][Para 24]. No witnesses were heard [Para 26].

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. (Issue 2) Whether Article 106 of the Labour Law obliged the employer to raise the base wage of Khmer section chiefs to USD 300 and of Khmer team leaders to USD 250 a month, matching what Chinese section chiefs and team leaders are paid? [Para 48][Para 95]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the employer had to pay the USD 8 attendance bonus in proportion to the days a worker took as authorised necessary leave, under Notification No. 041/11? [Para 73][Para 122]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** the company withdrew its Chinese section chiefs and team leaders and left Khmer staff in charge with only a token wage increase, even though the Khmer staff carry the same responsibilities, teach workers without needing an interpreter, and in some months produce the same output; equal work therefore requires equal pay under Article 106 [Para 49][Para 50][Para 58][Para 61].

**Employer (respondent):** base wages are set individually by reference to each person's actual capacity, and the Khmer chiefs and leaders do not manage as well as the Chinese ones — for instance they cannot keep workers from leaving the floor for the toilet — so the company cannot meet the demand [Para 51][Para 71][Para 72]. On the bonus it applies its established practice, which it says is more generous than the law [Para 85].

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected both demands it decided, on the base wage at USD 300 and USD 250 and on the proportional attendance bonus, and recorded the Issue 1 agreement as binding and immediately enforceable [Para 121][Para 134][Para 135][Para 137].

**Issue 2.** The panel treated the claim as one of rights, because it was founded on Article 106 [Para 97]. It read Article 106 as requiring three cumulative conditions — equal conditions of work, equal professional skill, and equal results — and adopted the four-element reading of "equal conditions" from Award 136/12-Silpakar Angkor: the same work in substance, the same process, the same working environment and the same equipment [Para 95][Para 96][Para 98][Para 101]. The workers had proved none of them: they asserted identity of role but did not describe how the Khmer chiefs' responsibility had grown or what the Chinese chiefs did differently [Para 56][Para 105]. On skill, the panel found no evidence permitting comparison, since the only point made was that Chinese chiefs teach through an interpreter while Khmer chiefs do not [Para 109][Para 110][Para 112]. On results, the comparison the workers produced did not state which month or year it covered, and the panel found the outputs were in any event unequal [Para 115][Para 116][Para 118]. With all three conditions unmet, no obligation arose and the demand was rejected [Para 119][Para 120][Para 121].

**Issue 3.** Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 fixed a minimum attendance bonus of USD 7 a month; Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 raised it to USD 10 and expressly repealed the earlier notification, with effect from 1 September 2012 [Para 123][Para 124][Para 125][Para 126]. The claim concerned deduction of the USD 8 bonus in September 2012, a period already governed by the repealing notification, so the panel held it had no legal basis on which to rule and rejected the demand [Para 132][Para 133][Para 134].

## 5. Ratio decidendi

Article 106 does not equalise pay between holders of the same job title: a claimant must establish equal conditions of work, equal professional skill and equal results, and the absence of any one of them defeats the claim, so an assertion that two posts bear the same name, unsupported by evidence of what each post does and produces, fails [Para 96][Para 105][Para 119]. A demand founded on a ministerial notification that has been repealed before the period in dispute cannot be adjudicated, even if the same benefit is re-fixed at a higher rate by the repealing instrument [Para 133].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that Notification No. 230 set the bonus at a higher figure than its predecessor, and that comparisons of output should identify the month and year, were observations that did not affect the result [Para 124][Para 115].
