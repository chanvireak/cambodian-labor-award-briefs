# Award No. 70/12 — Cambodia Brewery Limited (Arbitration Council, 10 May 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouchhun, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Aing Eng Thong, chair) · Doc ID 139994

## 1. Facts
Cambodia Brewery Limited, a brewer and distributor operating since 1995 with 844 workers (306 staff and 538 beer promoters), dismissed Mr M Bunn (a draught-equipment technician employed since 1 July 1997) and Mr Ri Yasi (a sales representative employed since 3 May 2010) on 18 January 2012 for serious misconduct. [Para 43][Para 45][Para 57][Para 59] Both had used company vehicles outside working hours, driven while intoxicated and caused traffic accidents, damaging company property. [Para 61][Para 64][Para 66] Of eight claims filed, one remained unresolved: the workers' demand that the two be reinstated with back pay, or compensated. [Para 10][Para 48] Both parties chose a non-binding award. [Para 15]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the dismissal of two employees for serious misconduct constitutes a collective labour dispute over which the Arbitration Council has jurisdiction? [Para 48][Para 93]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The dispute is collective because the Ministry of Labour referred it and resolved it together with the eight other claims; the dismissals were wrongful, and the two should be reinstated with back pay or paid statutory severance, including $230 USD in car-repair costs advanced by Mr M Bunn. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 56]
**Employer (respondent):** The dispute is not collective: it arises from individual serious misconduct by each man, and dismissal was necessary to deter other workers; the men also deceived six former colleagues into thumbprinting support for the added claims. [Para 50][Para 53][Para 54]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel declined to consider the claim for reinstatement or statutory severance. [Para 105][Para 106][Para 107]

The panel applied Article 302 of the Labour Law, which defines a collective labour dispute, and the three-condition test from Award 10/03: (a) a dispute between one or more employers and a number of their workers; (b) concerning working conditions, the exercise of professional-organisation rights, or employer-worker relations; and (c) capable of disrupting the enterprise's operations or harming social peace. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 95] Conditions (a) and (b) were satisfied because the dispute involved multiple workers and arose from the employment relationship in which the two men were hired and then dismissed for alleged serious misconduct. [Para 98][Para 99]

Condition (c), however, failed. Though several months had passed since the dismissals, no worker came forward to support the two men, and there was no disruption to the factory's production chain. [Para 100][Para 101] Even the six former colleagues who had once supported Mr M Bunn had done so only to help him find other work, not to support reinstatement. [Para 102] The panel therefore held the dispute was not a collective labour dispute and that it had no jurisdiction. [Para 103][Para 104]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A dismissal of individual workers for serious misconduct is not a collective labour dispute within Article 302 of the Labour Law unless it is capable of disrupting the enterprise's operations or harming social peace; absent such disruption, the Arbitration Council has no jurisdiction. [Para 95][Para 103][Para 105]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the employer's evidence included a recording in which the two men appeared intoxicated while driving, and that the six colleagues had withdrawn their support after saying Mr M Bunn misled them into thumbprinting. [Para 80][Para 83]
