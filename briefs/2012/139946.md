# Award No. 21/12 — Global Apparels Limited (Arbitration Council, 6 March 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 139946 · unofficial English translation of the Khmer original

## 1. Facts
Global Apparels Limited runs a garment factory employing 2,526 workers; the claimants are six unions and the workers' representatives. [Para 20] Conciliation on 8 February 2012 left four issues unresolved and they were referred the same day. [Para 10][Para 13] The employer, not the workers, is a signatory to the Memorandum of Understanding on Improving Industrial Relations in the Garment Industry of 28 September 2010, and the parties chose binding arbitration for rights disputes and non-binding arbitration for interests disputes. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 18]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must dismiss a staff member of the human resources section whom the workers accuse of verbal abuse and threats? [Para 21]
2. Whether the employer must pay a monthly US$ 10 accommodation allowance to each worker? [Para 30]
3. Whether the employer must replace the security guards involved in the 3 February 2012 incident with a union leader? [Para 34][Para 71]
4. Whether the employer must allow union leaders to use handheld transceivers inside the factory? [Para 42]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the HR staff member called them "You stupid idiot — do not talk to me!" and threatened to report to the administration office anyone who returned late from the toilet; four security guards seized a union president's transceiver by force and dislocated her arms, and no discipline followed; rent and transport costs have risen and 24 of the federation's 27 factories pay an accommodation allowance; administration staff use transceivers, so union leaders should too. [Para 24][Para 32][Para 36][Para 98]
**Employer (respondent):** no complaint about the HR staff member was ever raised before, he denies the allegations, and it will discipline him if misconduct is proved but will not dismiss him; it cannot afford the allowance and there is no agreement providing it; the guards are supplied by MPA Security Company and will be warned; the transceiver ban protects discipline and working hours and permission from the Ministry of Interior would be needed. [Para 25][Para 29][Para 32][Para 42][Para 98]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 1 rejected, issue 2 declined as an interests dispute, issue 3 granted with an order to replace the four security guards named in the award, issue 4 rejected. Part I is a binding rights award; Part II is non-binding and becomes binding eight days after notification absent written opposition. [Para 95][Para 101][Para 102]

On issue 1 the panel reasoned from Articles 65 of the Labour Law and 664 and 311 of the Civil Code: an employment contract is formed by the matching intentions of the employer and the worker, and only the parties to it may terminate or modify its obligations. [Para 53] The claimant workers are third parties to the HR staff member's contract and cannot compel his dismissal unless they prove he is a dangerous person whose retention would harm the workplace, the test applied in awards 54/08-Zhong Yov and 124/10-June Textile. [Para 54][Para 58] The abuse alleged was not supported by any fact showing dangerousness, and the employer's own disciplinary powers were the proper response, so the demand was rejected. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61]

On issue 2 the panel found no basis in the internal rules, a collective agreement or the law, which ordinarily makes the demand an interests dispute. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65] Clause 43 of Prakas No. 099 means an interests award becomes a one-year collective agreement binding all workers, so only an MRS union, or unions representing over half the workforce, may bring such a dispute. [Para 66][Para 68] No claimant union held MRS, none proved its membership figures, and the Prakas No. 305 Clause 9 route was therefore closed, so the panel declined to consider the demand. [Para 69][Para 70]

On issue 3 the same contract analysis applied, but the panel went on to the dangerousness question and answered it differently: taking a transceiver from a union leader by force risked injury and workplace disharmony, the guards should have used a more appropriate approach, the employer had taken no disciplinary action since the incident, and it did not produce the MPA Security Company contract it was ordered to file. [Para 84][Para 89][Para 92][Para 94] Retention of the four guards may therefore harm the workplace, and replacement was ordered. [Para 95]

On issue 4, Article 2(2) of the Labour Law was read, consistently with awards 17/07-Charm Textile and 116/07-Grace Sun, as giving the employer the right to supervise the enterprise lawfully and reasonably; the ban responded to lost working hours, and the workers cited no law or agreement conferring the right they claimed. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 99][Para 100]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Workers are third parties to the contracts of fellow employees and may not demand a dismissal unless they prove the person is dangerous and that retention would cause specific harm to the workplace; where security guards have used force against a union leader and the employer has taken no disciplinary step and withheld the security-service contract, retention is itself the proved harm and replacement will be ordered. A demand with no basis in law, agreement or internal rules is an interests dispute that only an MRS union, or unions representing over half the workforce, may bring. [Para 54][Para 68][Para 94][Para 95]

**Obiter dicta:** the observations that the guards should have adopted a more appropriate approach and that the employer must take measures to improve the security and safety of its workers are guidance not necessary to the order. [Para 89][Para 91]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The last conciliation session is dated 18 May 2012 [Para 12], after both the hearing of 13 February 2012 [Para 10] and the award date of 6 March 2012; the same paragraph also says three issues were resolved there although four were referred. The 18 May date is impossible and is reported, not corrected.
- The parties are said to have extended the due date for the award to 6 February 2012 [Para 18], which precedes the hearing and coincides with the award date; the reason is unexplained.
- The evidence section is omitted from the English version with a note to consult the Khmer original [Para 19], so the documentary record could not be checked.
- The number of guards shifts between five [Para 34] and four [Para 71]; the operative order names four.
- The translation drops the letter "t" throughout ("themployer", "athearing", "took" for "too"), and the list of precedent awards at [Para 70] is garbled with "84/07-Yung Wah 2" repeated four times. The demand figure appears as "US$ 10" at [Para 30] and as "100" at [Para 3].
