# Award No. 82/12 — Food Glory (Cambodia) Manufacturing Ltd (Arbitration Council, 31 May 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Kol Vuthana, An Nan, Kong Phallack (chair) · Doc ID 140001

## 1. Facts
Food Glory (Cambodia) Manufacturing Ltd, a garment factory at Damnak Thom, Stung Mean Chey, Phnom Penh employing about 600 workers and three unions, faced 18 demands lodged on 20 April 2012 by the Food Glory union of the Federation of Trade Unions for Workers' Rights [Para 1][Para 19][Para 49]. Conciliation on 20 April 2012 settled 12 of the 18; the six remaining went to the Arbitration Council by non-conciliation report No. 444 K.B/AK/VK dated 25 April 2012 [Para 20][Para 21]. At the hearing of 8 May 2012 Issues 1, 5 and 6 were resolved or withdrawn, leaving Issues 2, 3 and 4 on overtime meal breaks and transport allowances [Para 24][Para 78][Para 82].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer should deduct the regular attendance bonus (7 USD divided by 3 days) for actual absences? [Para 53]
2. Whether the employer should give a half-hour (the workers say 15-minute) meal break counted as working time and an extra 1,000 riel meal allowance for overtime after 18:00? [Para 4][Para 58]
3. Whether the employer should raise the transport allowance from 7 to 10 USD per month? [Para 66]
4. Whether the employer should maintain the transport allowance during sick and other leave? [Para 70]
5. Whether the employer should pay a 10 USD monthly housing allowance? [Para 77]
6. Whether the employer should reinstate the union president? [Para 82]
Issues 1, 5 and 6 were resolved at the hearing; the panel decided Issues 2 to 4 [Para 24][Para 78][Para 83].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The meal break during overtime after 18:00 should be counted as working time and an extra 1,000 riel paid; transport should rise to 10 USD because actual travel costs 15 USD a month, and the allowance should survive sick and other leave because the employer's notice is not law [Para 58][Para 63][Para 67][Para 71].
**Employer (respondent):** It applies its notices of 18 July 2011, 16 January 2012 and 21 April 2012; it cannot pay more because profits are low and it has just raised transport from 5 to 7 USD in January 2012; the deduction practice for absences was agreed with the staff delegates [Para 3][Para 12][Para 68][Para 69][Para 73].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel rejected the fixed 15-minute paid break and the extra 1,000 riel, but ordered the employer to continue allowing a meal break counted as working time, with no fixed duration, for overtime after 18:00; the transport increase to 10 USD and maintenance of the allowance during leave were dismissed [Para 132][Para 133][Para 134][Para 135].
On Issue 2(a), the panel found no law, collective agreement or agreement requiring a paid meal break during overtime, but held the employer's existing practice — a break counted as working time with no fixed limit — more favourable than the law and more favourable than the 15 minutes claimed, so it ordered that practice continued rather than the fixed break demanded [Para 90][Para 97][Para 100]. On Issue 2(b), Article 13 of the Labour Law and the principle that more favourable practices are not void under Article 95 were applied, but no instrument required an extra 1,000 riel on top of the 1,000 already paid for overtime from 18:00 to 20:00, and the employer's two-tier meal payments (2,000 riel for 16:00–18:00, 1,000 riel for 18:00–20:00) were left undisturbed [Para 101][Para 102][Para 103][Para 117]. Because the claim was an interest dispute, the panel also examined the union's representativeness and found it had no registration certificate and no most-representative status under Article 277, so it declined to consider the extra allowance [Para 105][Para 110][Para 115][Para 117]. On Issue 3, the panel held the 7 USD transport allowance was a benefit above the law, that the employer had recently raised it from 5 to 7 USD, and that no law or agreement required 10 USD, so the claim was rejected and the workers bound by the notice's conditions [Para 66][Para 69][Para 118][Para 120][Para 129]. On Issue 4, the same reasoning applied: the transport allowance is not statutory, and the employer's notice governing deductions for absence stands, so the claim to preserve it during sick or other leave was rejected [Para 70][Para 122][Para 127][Para 130].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a benefit exceeds what the Labour Law requires — a meal break counted as working time during overtime, or a transport allowance — the panel will not order an increase or extension that no law, collective agreement or agreement requires, and the workers must take the benefit on the conditions the employer has attached to it [Para 97][Para 120][Para 129]. A union without registration and most-representative status cannot sustain an interest claim for extra allowances [Para 115][Para 117].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that even though 155 of the 230 members had evidenced their support, the union's lack of registration prevented it from representing them on the allowance claim [Para 86][Para 115].
