# Award No. 34/12 — Sin Chang Sin (Cambodia) Garment Factory (Arbitration Council, 19 March 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chiv Phirom, Tuon Si Phan, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 139960

## 1. Facts
The employer runs a garment factory that had been operating for about one year at Tuol Sangkae, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, with roughly 750 workers; the claimant is the Cambodian Workers' Trade Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ដើម្បី កម្មករ កម្ពុជា) and its local union, registered on 8 August 2011. [Para 1][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49] The union filed 15 demands; conciliation failed on all 15, and at the hearing the workers withdrew points 1, 2, 5 and 14 and merged points 12 and 15. [Para 24][Para 31][Para 33] The employer did not attend and gave no reason, so the panel heard the case in its absence. [Para 27][Para 50][Para 95][Para 101]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must provide hot water in the factory clinic? [Para 51]
2. Whether the company must make the toilets hygienic and supply soap at each one? [Para 55]
3. Whether the company must pay the overtime meal allowance once a week, every Saturday? [Para 58]
4. Whether the company must pay wages in full by 4:00 p.m. on payday? [Para 63]
5. Whether the company must let pregnant workers leave 10 minutes before the end of the shift? [Para 68]
6. Whether pregnant workers are entitled to one day a month for antenatal checks with their other benefits preserved? [Para 74]
7. Whether the company must pay three months' maternity pay in advance? [Para 77]
8. Whether the company must keep the factory yard clean and hygienic? [Para 79]
9. Whether overtime must remain voluntary, free of compulsion and of warnings against those who decline? [Para 89]
10. Whether the company must reimburse workers who paid for their own health checks? [Para 84]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Pregnant workers need hot water when unwell, the ten toilets beside the sewing building are dirty and have no soap, the meal allowance is sometimes paid late, wages come after 4:00 p.m. on payday, the company warns workers who refuse overtime that they will lose their jobs, and it withholds the 8 USD attendance bonus from workers who take antenatal leave. [Para 52][Para 56][Para 60][Para 74][Para 76][Para 91]
**Employer (respondent):** No position was recorded; the company did not appear at the hearing and did not explain its absence. [Para 50][Para 95][Para 99]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered hygienic toilets with sufficient soap, weekly overtime meal allowance paid every Saturday, wages paid by 4:00 p.m., proportionate only deduction of the attendance bonus for antenatal days, 50% of wages and allowances for 90 days' maternity leave paid at least one day before the leave began, a clean yard, and an immediate stop to verbal warnings over overtime; it dismissed the hot-water, ten-minute early-leave and health-check reimbursement claims. [Para 236][Para 237][Para 238][Para 239][Para 240][Para 241][Para 242][Para 243][Para 244][Para 245]
The panel first held that Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 permits a hearing in the absence of a duly invited party who gives no valid reason, following Awards 53/04, 63/04, 148/07, 99/09 and 173/11. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 101]
On hot water, Prakas No. 054 SKBY requires hygienic drinking water near the workplace but does not specify hot water, and the union could not establish whether hot water existed in the clinic; since the claimant bears the burden, the claim failed. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 106][Para 109][Para 112] On toilets, Article 229 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 052 SKBY require clean washrooms with sufficient soap, following Award 31/12-B&N, so the order was made. [Para 113][Para 116][Para 118][Para 122]
The meal allowance was owed under point 2 of the company's notice No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011, and the panel applied its earlier holdings (47/07-Chung Fai, 79/07-Terratex, 85/09-Nan Kuang) that the allowance serves to buy food for the overtime actually worked, so it must be paid weekly on Saturdays. [Para 125][Para 127][Para 130][Para 132] On wages, Article 115(3) forbids payment on a rest day and Article 137 caps the working day at eight hours, so wages were ordered paid before 4:00 p.m. [Para 134][Para 138][Para 142]
The antenatal claim succeeded in part: the right to one day a month for checks was recognised, but because an authorised absence suspends the contract under Article 71(6) and Article 72(1), the employer may withhold the daily wage while deducting the 8 USD attendance bonus only in proportion to the days absent. [Para 163][Para 179][Para 180][Para 184][Para 189] On maternity pay, Article 183 requires 50% of wages and allowances for 90 days and Article 115(3) treats that sum as wages, so it must be paid at least one day before the leave starts. [Para 190][Para 191][Para 197][Para 204][Para 205]
The ten-minute early-leave claim was refused without reaching the merits: no law, convention or agreement grants it, so it is an interest dispute, and a union that is not the most representative in the enterprise cannot bring one. [Para 143][Para 144][Para 149][Para 150][Para 151] The health-check reimbursement claim failed because the union never filed the list of workers who had paid, leaving the panel without evidence of who or how many. [Para 221][Para 227][Para 228] Finally, Prakas No. 80 SKBY makes overtime voluntary, so the verbal warnings were unlawful and had to stop immediately. [Para 229][Para 233][Para 234]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer authorises an absence, the contract is suspended: the daily wage may be withheld, but a regular-attendance bonus, being wages under Article 103, may be reduced only in proportion to the days absent, never forfeited in full. Maternity pay under Article 183 is wages for Article 115(3) purposes and must therefore be paid at least one day before the leave begins. A claim unsupported by any list identifying the claimants or the amounts paid fails for want of evidence, and a union that is not the most representative in the enterprise cannot bring an interest dispute at all. [Para 103][Para 150][Para 184][Para 205][Para 227]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remarks that hygienic drinking water must be sited near the workplace under Prakas No. 054, and that a panel may proceed in the absence of a party who was properly invited and gives no reason. [Para 96][Para 105]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is Khmer only and the company name is printed both in Khmer and, once, in Latin script as Xing Chang Xin, while the case name on the face reads 34/12-Sin Chang Sin; the title above follows the case name. [Para 1][Para 35] The signature block spells the employer-side arbitrator's name differently from the face of the award (Chiv Phirom against ឈឺ វ ភិរម្យ). [Para 1][Para 248] Article 247-2 of the Labour Law is quoted in truncated form, the health-check fee appears as 12,000 riel in the Prakas text but as 1,2000 riel and 1,2000 in the panel's application, and the attendance-bonus figures are 7 USD in the notice and 8 USD in practice. [Para 164][Para 214][Para 215][Para 219]
