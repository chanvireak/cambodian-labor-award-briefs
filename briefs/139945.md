# Award No. 17/12 — BIS Glory (Cambodia) Manufacturing (Arbitration Council, 22 February 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phireak for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 139945

## 1. Facts
BIS Glory (Cambodia) Manufacturing is a garment factory employing 1,100 workers, 490 of whom belong to the two claimant unions. [Para 43][Para 45] Nineteen demands were conciliated on 16 January 2012; sixteen were settled and the three unresolved ones were referred to the panel on 30 January 2012. [Para 14][Para 15] At the hearing of 8 February 2012 the parties settled the meal-allowance and short-work issues, leaving only the basis for calculating the annual-leave indemnity. [Para 18][Para 19]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must give 2,000 riel or one meal to workers who work overtime on public holidays and Sundays? [Para 2]
2. Whether the annual-leave indemnity must be calculated on the total wage of the 12 months before termination rather than on the base wage? [Para 4][Para 46]
3. Whether the employer must pay 100% of the base wage when there is no work for fewer than 13 days, and 50% beyond 13 days? [Para 6]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer's base-wage method understates the indemnity; the total wage earned in the last 12 months before termination is the lawful basis for the calculation. [Para 47]
**Employer (respondent):** the company pays the annual-leave indemnity on the base wage when each contract ends, and that method has always been accepted by both sides; it refused the demand. [Para 46][Para 51][Para 52]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted on issue 2 — the employer must use the total wage of the 12 months preceding termination as the basis of the annual-leave indemnity, that total divided by twelve and then by 26 (the number of working days in a month) to reach the average daily wage. [Para 68][Para 69] The award is binding at once as a rights award under the Memorandum of Understanding of 28 September 2010. [Para 70]

The panel first tested its own jurisdiction to treat the demand as a rights dispute, adopting its earlier definition that a rights dispute concerns entitlements established by law, an agreement or a collective agreement. [Para 55][Para 56] Because the manner of paying the annual-leave indemnity on termination is fixed by the Labour Law, the demand is a rights dispute whatever the parties' practice had been. [Para 58] Article 168 of the Labour Law requires the employer to pay, before the leave, an allowance at least equal to the average wage, bonuses, allowances and benefits over the twelve months preceding the leave, including the value of payments in kind and excluding reimbursement of expenses already advanced. [Para 59][Para 61] The panel read that article as directing the employer, on termination, to take the average of wage, bonuses, allowances and benefits across the preceding twelve months. [Para 61] The employer instead used the base wage alone, and the panel held that practice inconsistent with Article 168 and with its earlier awards 27/04-M&S and 98/07-Fortune Garment, which divide the total wage of the last twelve months to find the monthly average. [Para 62][Para 64][Para 66][Para 67] The internal work rules prescribe 8 hours a day and 48 hours a week with Sunday off, so workers work 6 days a week and 26 days a month, which supplies the divisor. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 63]

The panel did not quantify any sum: no worker-by-worker figures were before it, and the workers could not say how many colleagues had been paid on the base-wage basis. [Para 48][Para 49]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
On termination the annual-leave indemnity under Article 168 is computed on the average of the total remuneration actually earned — wage, bonuses, allowances and benefits in kind — over the twelve months before the leave, divided by twelve months and by 26 working days; an employer's settled practice of using only the base wage does not displace that method, and the parties' acquiescence cannot reduce a statutory entitlement. [Para 59][Para 61][Para 67][Para 68]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's division of awards into rights and interests disputes, and its statement that a rights award becomes final immediately under the 28 September 2010 MoU while an interests award waits eight days for objection, are procedural observations that do not affect the order. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 71]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The employer's name is printed three ways: "ភិស គ្គួរី" in the party block [Para 1], "ភិស ក្លូរី" in the findings [Para 43] and "ភិស ធ្នូរី" in the VAT certificate entry [Para 33]. The form used on the face of the award is kept here.
- The conciliation minute is dated 16 January 2011 in the employer's evidence list [Para 37] and 16 January 2012 in the Ministry's list [Para 39]; the 2012 date matches the rest of the file and the 2011 entry appears to be an error.
- The parties' positions contradict each other inside the record: the employer is reported both as denying that any such case had arisen [Para 50] and as describing the base-wage method as its standing practice [Para 46], while the panel states that both parties accepted that method [Para 51] although the workers had expressly disputed it [Para 47].
- The order at [Para 68] repeats the same clause three times with bracketed fragments and describes the month as "26 days in one month February"; the divisor of 26 is independently supported at [Para 54].
- Amounts and dates in the header block are partly illegible, and a stray "2017" sits in the worker-party address [Para 1]; the signature block carries unexplained digit strings for two arbitrators [Para 72].
