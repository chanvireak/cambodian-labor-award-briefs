# Award No. 020/18 — Sun Ya Khiel Memorial Hospital (Arbitration Council, 10 July 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (You Soun Thy for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 138824

## 1. Facts
Sun Ya Khiel Memorial Hospital, a Kampot-province hospital employing about 104 staff, faced a collective dispute raised by two worker delegates over eight demands; four were settled at conciliation on 10 May 2018 and four were referred to the Council, which heard the case on 14 June 2018. [Para 1][Para 10][Para 11][Para 49] The hospital has no most-representative union (ភាពជាតំណាងបំផុត), so the panel first had to decide whether the two delegates could represent the workers at all. [Para 52][Para 106][Para 122] The four unresolved demands concerned 5% termination pay on a two-year fixed duration contract (FDC), aggregation of seniority, the two-year cap on FDCs, and rest on public holidays for out-patient (OPD) doctors. [Para 2][Para 3][Para 4][Para 5]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay termination compensation of 5 per cent when a doctor's two-year fixed duration contract expires and the employer continues with an undetermined duration contract (UDC)? [Para 124][Para 53]
2. Whether the employer must aggregate the seniority of workers whose undetermined duration contract was later changed back into a fixed duration contract? [Para 136][Para 62]
3. Whether the employer must conclude fixed duration contracts of no more than two years? [Para 159][Para 72]
4. Whether the employer must allow doctors working in the out-patient department to rest on public holidays in accordance with the Prakas of the Ministry in charge of labour? [Para 169][Para 93]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer signed successive two-year FDCs and then a UDC, which wiped out two years of seniority; a doctor's contract ran for three years and a doctor leaving before three years had to repay USD 100 a month in training costs, which coerced doctors into staying; OPD doctors should rest on public holidays and 2017 practice was to close OPD on those days. [Para 54][Para 73][Para 79][Para 94]
**Employer (respondent):** the hospital, set up in 2012, signed one- or two-year FDCs with inexperienced doctors and then continued them on UDCs without any break; it paid USD 200 extra on conversion and one month's wage on resignation, exceeding the Labour Law; no doctor ever had a three-year FDC; the three-year term in trainee contracts is a training-repayment condition, not the contract term. [Para 56][Para 59][Para 61][Para 77][Para 80][Para 161][Para 165]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issues 1, 2 and 3 were dismissed; on issue 4 the panel ordered the employer to allow doctors to rest on public holidays and to organise holiday work only with the doctors' voluntary consent. [Para 183][Para 184][Para 185][Para 186]

On representation the panel held, under Article 54(1) of the Trade Union Law of 17 May 2016, that only a most-representative union has the exclusive right to bring and settle a collective dispute, but where no such union exists workers may represent themselves or mandate another person; Prakas No. 362 of 29 August 2016 empowers the Labour Disputes Department and provincial Departments to recognise worker representatives, and the Kampot Department had recognised the two delegates. [Para 106][Para 110][Para 116][Para 122]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 73(6) and (7) of the 1997 Labour Law: termination compensation is owed when the FDC ends and the employment relationship is severed, and where a UDC genuinely replaces an FDC the seniority of both contracts is added together. Finding that the doctors kept working without any break, the panel held the relationship was never severed, so no 5% payment was due — but the employer must aggregate seniority across both contracts. [Para 124][Para 126][Para 131][Para 133][Para 134][Para 135]

On issue 2 the panel found the only case raised, that of Dr Sem Visoth, unsupported: he signed a letter of 12 June 2018 with his thumbprint stating he had not complained and was content with his contract, and the workers' objection of 22 June 2018 alleging pressure was not evidenced; no doctor was shown to have been moved from a UDC to an FDC. [Para 139][Para 142][Para 149][Para 151][Para 152] On issue 3 the panel found the three-year term was a separate training-repayment condition in trainee doctors' contracts and not a contract duration, that Dr Lav Havan's contract had no clear end date, and that the workers produced no contract showing an FDC over two years. [Para 163][Para 164][Para 165][Para 166][Para 167]

On issue 4 the panel applied Article 161(1) and Article 164 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 10 SKBY of 4 February 1999: all workers are entitled to paid public holidays, and where an enterprise cannot stop work the employer may ask workers to work on a holiday only with their consent and on payment of a day's wage as compensation. Finding that the employer scheduled doctors onto holidays without asking them, the panel held that practice unlawful and ordered voluntary-only holiday work. [Para 169][Para 173][Para 176][Para 180][Para 181][Para 182]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a fixed duration contract expires and the employer continues the same worker on an undetermined duration contract with no break in the employment relationship, the relationship is not severed and no 5% termination compensation under Article 73(6) falls due; the employer must nevertheless aggregate the seniority of both contracts under Article 73(7). Separately, an employer that cannot stop operations may schedule work on a paid public holiday only with the individual worker's voluntary consent, and consent cannot be inferred from a contract clause or from a schedule drawn up unilaterally. [Para 126][Para 133][Para 134][Para 177][Para 180]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's survey of the representation rules — that the absence of a most-representative union may obstruct collective dispute resolution and that workers may then mandate a representative — was reasoning on standing rather than on any of the four demands. [Para 112][Para 113][Para 119]
