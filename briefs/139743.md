# Award No. 292/14 — Vinning Dragon Leather (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 24 October 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ieng Sothy, employer-appointed; An Nan, worker-appointed; Tang Tri, presiding) · Doc ID 139743

## 1. Facts
Vinning Dragon Leather (Cambodia), a factory at Don Keo village, Sen Dei commune, Samrong Tong district, Kampong Speu, faced a collective dispute raised by workers organised through two union federations. Conciliation at the provincial labour department failed on 14 October 2014 and six demands remained unresolved; the workers struck, and were still striking when the panel took up the file. The panel issued injunction No. 054/14 on 17 October 2014 ordering an immediate suspension of the strike and a return to work from the morning of 18 October 2014, which the workers did not obey. [Para 1][Para 10][Para 18][Para 19]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company should remove both of its administrative heads from the company? [Para 2]
2. Whether workers should be allowed to use telephones at the workplace? [Para 3]
3. Whether the company should grant sick leave, on a valid medical certificate, while preserving wages and other benefits? [Para 4]
4. Whether the company should deduct the attendance premium (ប្រាក់ព្រីម) when workers take leave for personal business? [Para 5]
5. Whether the company should refrain from anti-union discrimination? [Para 6]
6. Whether wages and other benefits should be deducted during the strike period? [Para 7]

The panel reached only one question: whether the arbitration should continue at all. [Para 35]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the strike would continue until the employer removed the administration; most workers were not working; the employer had not called the workers to negotiate. [Para 23][Para 24]
**Employer (respondent):** on 16 October 2014 the company asked the Council to issue an order suspending the strike; its administration head, Mr S, said only 200 of those who formerly came in had entered while about 1,000 workers remained outside the factory, that the employer would not negotiate with the workers, and that it would proceed "according to legal procedure". [Para 17][Para 25][Para 26]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** proceedings terminated — the panel closed the file without ruling on any of the six demands, declaring the arbitration terminated in case No. 292/14. [Para 45][Para 46]

No documentary evidence was filed by either party; the panel worked from the labour department's non-conciliation report and minutes of 14 October 2014, its own injunction 054/14, and the pre-hearing meeting of 20 October 2014. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 29][Para 31]

On the procedural question, the panel applied Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council (21 April 2004), which requires disputing parties to suspend all dispute measures — strikes or lockouts, per Article 318 of the Labour Law — for the duration of the arbitration, and to attend every meeting to which the panel summons them. [Para 35][Para 36] The panel read that provision, as it had in earlier awards (Nos 59/06, 39/08, 137/09 and 138/09, and 04/04), to mean it will not continue an arbitration while workers are striking or an employer has locked out, and that it may take the dispute up again only once all the workers' unions stop striking. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39]

The panel found the strike had continued after injunction 054/14, confirmed by both parties at the pre-hearing meeting and by telephone with the two federation presidents and the employer's administration head. It concluded that the workers had no intention of having the dispute resolved by the Arbitration Council, and terminated the proceedings under Article 20. [Para 22][Para 43][Para 44][Para 45]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A party that keeps a strike (or lockout) running after the panel has ordered its suspension under Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 removes the basis for arbitration: the panel will decline to hear the merits and terminate the proceedings, leaving the dispute to be re-filed once the strike has actually ended. [Para 39][Para 42]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that the Council had in several earlier cases (28/06, 34/06, 110/11, 17/13) closed or terminated files for the same reason, and that both sides had confirmed the strike's continuation by telephone on 21 October 2014. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 23]
