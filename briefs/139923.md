# Award No. 276/13 — Sang Vo (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 28 January 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Tuon Si Phan, Pen Bunhea presiding) · Doc ID 139923

## 1. Facts
Sang Vo (Cambodia) Co., Ltd has cut and sewn garments since 27 November 2006 and employs about 4,832 workers [Para 44]. On 29 November 2013 the Free Workers' Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia filed an eight-point demand with the Kampong Speu labour office; conciliation failed on all eight [Para 16][Para 20][Para 21]. At the hearing three points (3, 5 and 8) were settled, the workers withdrew three more (4, 6 and 7), leaving only the minimum-wage claim and the travel-allowance claim [Para 24][Para 25][Para 26]. The factory union had been registered on 30 March 2011 with Mr S as president, Mr C as deputy and Mr T as secretary, but no fresh ministry recognition of that leadership was produced [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise the basic wage to USD 150 a month, i.e. USD 67 above the USD 83 now paid? [Para 52][Para 54][Para 98]
2. Whether the employer must raise the travel and accommodation allowance to USD 20 a month, i.e. USD 10 above the USD 10 now paid? [Para 63][Para 64][Para 119]

Points 3, 5 and 8 were settled at the hearing and points 4, 6 and 7 were withdrawn, so only these two remained for decision [Para 24][Para 25][Para 26].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The statutory minimum wage is not enough to live on: rent, water, electricity and transport have all risen, USD 150 would improve their diet and let them buy milk and meet other family costs, workmates abroad earn about that much, and other factories such as Khmer Brewery and QMI have promised rises; their own research puts a living wage at USD 157 to USD 170 a month [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57][Para 59][Para 60][Para 61]. On travel, shared-room rent has risen to about USD 30 to USD 35 and the bus fare from USD 11 to USD 12 up to USD 13 to USD 14 a month, so USD 20 is needed [Para 66][Para 67].
**Employer (respondent):** The employer will apply Prakas No. 317 of 31 December 2013 on the amount and timing of any minimum-wage increase and cannot pay more at present [Para 62]. The bus fare is currently USD 9 and will rise to USD 10 when the minimum wage rises, and the USD 10 travel and accommodation allowance already exceeds what the law requires [Para 68][Para 69][Para 70].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel refused to consider both remaining claims. No order was made on the merits; the refusal, being an interest-dispute disposition, became final eight days after notification if no written objection reached the Minister [Para 126][Para 127][Para 128].

The panel dealt first with who was properly before it. Prakas No. 099 Article 19 allows representation only under a written authorisation, and the federation official who attended held none, so he could not speak for the factory's workers [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74]. Article 268 of the Labour Law makes a union's rights conditional on registration and on the ministry recognising the list of responsible leaders, and Prakas No. 305 Article 3 caps a leadership mandate at two years [Para 75][Para 77][Para 82]. The three leaders' mandate began on 30 March 2011, more than two years before the hearing of 9 January 2014, and no letter from the Labour Disputes Department confirmed their re-recognition [Para 83][Para 84][Para 85][Para 86]. They were therefore not lawful current leaders and could not represent members; only the three workers who attended in person were claimants [Para 88][Para 89][Para 90].

On the substance the panel classified both claims as interest disputes. Notification No. 103 of 9 April 2013 set the minimum at USD 80 a month for a full-rights worker (USD 61 plus USD 14 plus USD 5 health allowance), the employer pays USD 83, and no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice obliges it to pay USD 150 [Para 98][Para 101][Para 102][Para 103][Para 104]. Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 requires USD 7 of travel and accommodation allowance, the employer pays USD 10, and nothing obliges it to pay USD 20 [Para 119][Para 120][Para 121][Para 122]. Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 turns an interest award into a collective agreement binding on the whole workforce, so only the most representative union, or unions together with more than half the workers, may bring such a claim; three individual workers are not the most representative body [Para 109][Para 111][Para 112][Para 116][Para 117]. Both claims were refused consideration [Para 118][Para 125].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union cannot represent its members before the panel where the two-year mandate of its leadership under Prakas No. 305 Article 3 has expired and no fresh ministry recognition of that leadership has been produced, so only the workers who appear in person are parties; and since an interest award binds the whole workforce, the panel will refuse to consider an interest claim unless it is brought by the most representative union or by unions together representing more than half the workers [Para 84][Para 90][Para 112][Para 117].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that the USD 83 basic wage and the USD 10 travel and accommodation allowance were both more favourable than Notifications No. 103 and No. 230 required, and that the employer had undertaken to follow Prakas No. 317 when the minimum wage rose [Para 102][Para 120][Para 62].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The chair's name on the signature block at line 129 is garbled beyond recovery ("ថៃ ខុន ណា" against a partly legible stamp), while the header at line 1 gives Pen Bunhea as chair; the brief follows the header. The company name is printed as "សាង វ៉ូ" in the title (line 1) but as "សាង វត្តន៍" and "សាងវ៉ូ" elsewhere (lines 1, 46, 89), and the employer's address is given only at commune level with an eight-digit telephone number. Stray page numbers and glyphs ("2017", "-១១-", "%") are embedded in the reasoning at lines 75, 84, 98, 119 and 126, and line 119 also prints the allowance period as "in one year month". The union's registration certificate of 30 March 2011 appears only as a certificate number (2119) and a ministry letter (No. 094/11) at lines 36 and 37. Individual workers' names appear in the award but are reduced here to Mr S, Mr C and Mr T by policy.
