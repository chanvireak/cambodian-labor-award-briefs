# Award No. 038/18 — រូ ស៊ីង ហ្គាម៉ែន ខូអិលធីឌី (ខេមបូឌា) សាខា ទី១ (Arbitration Council, 15 November 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៊ីង សុទ្ធី, ស៊ីន គីម ស៊ាន, គង់ ផល្ឆៈ); dissenting opinion by អ៊ីង សុទ្ធី · Doc ID 138922

## 1. Facts
The employer is a garment manufacturer registered on 30 July 1997; its Democratic Union of Workers was registered on 19 January 2018 with a four-year mandate for president Mr J, vice-president Mr Y and secretary Mr K. [Para 52][Para 53] The employer had long-renewed three-month fixed-duration contracts, paying 5% end-of-contract compensation at each renewal, then cut staff from June 2018 in three rounds — 221 in June, 150 in August and 220 in September — leaving about 40 workers. [Para 59][Para 61][Para 62] Ninety-four workers claim reinstatement with back wages from dismissal to return. [Para 57][Para 63] The parties chose a non-binding award. [Para 18][Para 254]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take the 94 workers back at the same workplace with back wages from the day of dismissal to the day of return, or in the alternative pay severance for an indefinite-duration contract — seniority pay under Article 89 (new), damages in lieu of notice under Article 91 (new), unused annual leave and final wages? [Para 57][Para 112]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** because their successive fixed-duration contracts ran, without any break between renewals, for a total of more than two years, they were indefinite-duration contracts, so ending them was a dismissal; the dismissals ignored the Article 95 collective-dismissal procedure, and the 21 August 2015 agreement cannot save them because it contradicts the Labour Law. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 66][Para 74]
**Employer (respondent):** it cannot comply because the contracts simply expired and the company has no orders and no work; it gave one week's notice and paid the 5% compensation, final wages and unused leave, notified the labour inspectorate twice, and the contracts remain fixed-duration by the workers' own agreement of 21 August 2015. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement was refused; the panel ordered payment in lieu of notice measured by each worker's actual seniority, unused annual leave and final wages, and refused the Article 89 seniority claim and moral damages. No riel or dollar figures are printed in the operative text. [Para 246][Para 247][Para 248][Para 249]
On the first point, the panel applied Articles 67(2) and 73(5): the 94 workers' fixed-duration contracts, added together, exceeded two years by the last day of work on 31 August 2018, so they had converted to indefinite duration and their ending was a dismissal, not a mere expiry. [Para 166][Para 167][Para 168] The 21 August 2015 minutes — by which workers with over two years' service agreed to keep renewing three-month contracts for 5% compensation — were held void under Article 13, because a works agreement cannot displace Articles 67(2) and 73(5). [Para 78][Para 164][Para 165] The 5% payments did not break the employment relationship, which continued without interruption. [Para 162][Para 163]
On remedy, the panel found the successive reductions to be a collective dismissal for reduced activity under Article 95, and that the employer had not fully followed that procedure, having ended the contracts without agreement on seniority ordering. [Para 186][Para 192] Reinstatement was nevertheless refused because the company has no work to give and the workers did not contest that fact. [Para 118][Para 194] Notice of one week fell short of the one to three months required by actual seniority of over two years, so payment in lieu of notice was owed under Articles 75 and 77. [Para 203][Para 206][Para 207] Moral damages under Article 91 (new) were refused because the dismissal rested on a valid reason — the absence of orders. [Para 231][Para 232] Article 89 (new) seniority pay was refused because Prakas 443 applies it only from 2019 and these dismissals occurred in August 2018. [Para 242][Para 244][Para 245]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive fixed-duration contracts that run, without interruption, beyond two years convert to indefinite duration by operation of Articles 67(2) and 73(5); the payment of 5% end-of-contract compensation at each renewal neither breaks the employment relationship nor prevents conversion, and a works agreement that keeps such workers on fixed-duration contracts is void under Article 13. Ending such a contract is a dismissal, and staff reductions carried out in successive rounds are a collective dismissal under Article 95, requiring the statutory ordering by professional skill, seniority and family burden and notice to the labour inspectorate. Where reinstatement is impossible for lack of work, the remedy is payment in lieu of notice measured by actual seniority, unused annual leave and final wages. [Para 164][Para 167][Para 186][Para 207]

**Obiter dicta:** neither party asserted a most-representative union, so the dispute was carried by worker representatives recognised by the Labour Disputes Department, which the panel held sufficient under Prakas 303 and Prakas 362. [Para 56][Para 94][Para 105] The dissenting arbitrator, appointed by the employer, would have left the contracts fixed-duration, treating the choice of contract type as the employer's prerogative and the 5% payments as satisfying the law. [Para 259][Para 260][Para 276]
