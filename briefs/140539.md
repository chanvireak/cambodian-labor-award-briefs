# Award No. 92/08 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូឌា ហយ ហ្វូ (Cambodia Hoy Fu Co.) (Arbitration Council, 18 August 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (គល់ វឌ្ឍនា, អន ណន, គង់ ផល្លៈ) · Doc ID 140539

## 1. Facts
The employer is ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូឌា ហយ ហ្វូ (Cambodia Hoy Fu Co.), on National Road, Sangkat Trapeang Krasaing, Khan Dangkao, Phnom Penh, employing approximately 400 workers [Para 34]. The claimant is the union of workers at the ហយ ហ្វូ factory, affiliated to the Cambodian workers' union confederation [Para 2][Para 35]. Its complaint of 4 June 2008 asked the company to improve working conditions in nine demands: keeping wages and allowances unchanged during work stoppages, keeping the USD 5 attendance bonus during sick leave, itemised Khmer-language payrolls, 15-minute flexibility for pregnant workers, conversion of workers with more than three months' service into full-rights workers, wage rises for two named staff, meal allowance for overtime, correct pay for the nurse, and severance for six named inspection workers [Para 3][Para 4][Para 5][Para 6][Para 7][Para 8][Para 9][Para 10][Para 11]. Conciliation failed on all nine, and report No. 767 of 16 July 2008 reached the Secretariat on 21 July 2008 [Para 14][Para 18]. At the hearing of 1 August 2008 the panel settled seven of them and left Issue 1 (the attendance bonus limb) and Issue 5 (probationary workers) for adjudication [Para 22][Para 23].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must keep paying the USD 5 regular attendance bonus when workers attend work but the company has no work for them? [Para 38][Para 56]
2. (Issue 5) Whether the company must convert probationary workers who have worked more than three months into full-rights workers? [Para 47][Para 72]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The union demanded that the USD 5 attendance bonus be preserved whenever workers come to work and are idle for about two to three hours, or for one to two days [Para 42]; it asked that probationary workers with more than three months' service be made full-rights workers because they had already worked at the company for several years [Para 47]; and it produced a list of 70 workers by section [Para 54].
**Employer (respondent):** On Issue 1 the company first said it could not answer and needed to consult the owner, then on 8 August 2008 said no consultation had taken place and gave no date [Para 43][Para 45]. On Issue 5 it refused conversion because workers who become full-rights workers like to strike, and it paid probationary workers USD 45 a month [Para 48][Para 51]; it put the number of probationary workers over three months at around under 100 [Para 52]. It raised no objection to the union's list of 70 [Para 55].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to keep the USD 5 attendance bonus unchanged whenever workers attend and no work is available, and to convert all 70 workers named in the list into full-rights workers according to each one's seniority from the end of the first probation period, paying the minimum wage required by law [Para 92][Para 93].
On Issue 1, the panel relied on point 3 of Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000, which guarantees workers who attend the required number of working days in a month an attendance bonus of at least USD 5 a month [Para 56]. It held the bonus to be an incentive for regular attendance, not a reward conditional on work being provided [Para 57]. Since the workers did come and the company sent them home, they were not absent without lawful reason; the company's conduct was not a lawful suspension of the contract under Article 71(1) of the Labour Law, the employer having admitted it never sought permission from the labour ministry for the short stoppages [Para 59][Para 60][Para 62]. An unlawful suspension does not relieve the employer of wages, and earlier awards treat wages as including the bonus [Para 64][Para 66][Para 70].
On Issue 5, the panel applied Article 68 of the Labour Law, under which a probationary contract may not exceed three months, two months for specialised workers and one month for ordinary workers [Para 72][Para 73]. Garment workers count as specialised, so the lawful probation was two months [Para 84]. Once probation ends, a worker who stays on must be reclassified as a regular worker on a fixed- or indefinite-duration contract at the full legal wage rate [Para 86]. Notification No. 745 of 23 October 2006 fixed USD 45 a month for probationary workers and USD 50 a month once probation ends [Para 87][Para 89]. The company had kept 70 workers on USD 45 a month after their probation expired [Para 77][Para 90], the union's list of those 70 was unchallenged [Para 54][Para 55], and its fear of strikes was no lawful reason to extend probation, the labour rules being sufficient to deal with strikes [Para 79][Para 81]. The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent written opposition [Para 94].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer who requires workers to attend but provides no work, without the lawful suspension the Labour Law requires, must still pay the attendance bonus in full: the bonus attaches to regular attendance, not to work performed. And a probationary period is capped by law — two months for specialised, garment work — so a worker kept on beyond it becomes a full-rights worker from the date probation ended and is owed the full minimum wage for that whole period [Para 82][Para 86][Para 92].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remark that the labour regulations already give employers enough means to prevent strikes, so fear of strikes cannot justify long probation [Para 80], and its statement that the purpose of probation is to let the employer assess the worker's ability [Para 81], are general observations rather than steps in the order.

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The document is a Khmer original with heavy OCR damage: words are split, several sentences are duplicated, and stray Latin fragments and digits interrupt the Khmer in the findings and the decision (for example a broken phrase about "struction" in the middle of the Issue 5 order). One wage figure is printed inconsistently — a garbled amount ending in "500" for the full-rights minimum appears next to the clear figure of USD 50 a month, and the probationary rate is given as USD 45 in one place and as a garbled sum in another [Para 88][Para 89][Para 90]. The employer is called both a company and simply "the ហយ ហ្វូ factory", and the union is described as having nearly 400 members yet not being representative [Para 36][Para 37]. Several individual workers are named in the demands and in the 70-name list; those names are not reproduced here. The numbering of the parties' representatives is duplicated (two entries numbered 2), so representative roles cannot be matched to names with confidence.
