# Award No. 320/14 — Cambodian Sunrise Garment (Arbitration Council, 25 December 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Eing Sothy (employer-appointed), Vann Pov (worker-appointed), Tang Try (president) · Doc ID 139768

## 1. Facts
Cambodian Sunrise Garment, a garment factory in the Preah Sihanouk special economic zone employing about 100 workers, faced nine collective demands from the Khmer Workers' Movement Union; conciliation settled two, the hearing settled three more, leaving points 1, 2, 4 and 7. [Para 19][Para 24][Para 25][Para 42] Mr K Chitra, a machine mechanic on an indefinite-duration contract since 16 May 2013 and elected president of the in-factory union on 1 September 2013, was dismissed on 30 October 2014 without prior notice and without severance being paid out. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 55] The union had still not applied to register at the Ministry of Labour, and 71 workers plus Mr K Chitra mandated the federation, 72 claimants in all. [Para 43][Para 47]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must convert indefinite-duration contracts into six-month fixed-duration contracts and pay 5 per cent at each contract's expiry? [Para 48]
2. Whether the company must take back Mr K Chitra, elected union president, into employment? [Para 113]
4. Whether the company must stop deducting wages where workers arrive late? [Para 214]
7. Whether the company must deduct 1,000 riel per member from wages to pay union dues? [Para 226]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Short fixed-duration contracts were wanted so that renewals could not simply be refused, with 5 per cent paid at each expiry and on conversion; lateness deductions were unlawful fines, and workers delayed by a broken-down vehicle should not lose pay. [Para 49][Para 74][Para 75] Mr K Chitra had only intervened for a worker refused four days' leave to visit her ill father, and was then pushed out. [Para 59][Para 60] Dues deduction was voluntary and backed by written requests. [Para 78]
**Employer (respondent):** It could not accept the conversion — all workers still held indefinite contracts and it had not decided on three-month contracts; it had a right to refuse. [Para 51][Para 52] Mr K Chitra shouted, disturbed other workers, was often absent without leave and was warned orally many times, though no dates or records were kept; severance was prepared but he never collected it. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 66] Lateness deductions followed internal rules, and dues could not be deducted because the union was unregistered. [Para 76][Para 80]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the company to reinstate Mr K Chitra, and failing reinstatement to pay 15 days' wages in lieu of notice, 15 days' severance, damages equal to the severance, unused annual leave and final wages for October; it rejected the contract-conversion claim, the 5 per cent claims, the three lateness formulations and the dues claim, while ordering the company to stop deducting wages as a penalty for lateness. [Para 213][Para 240][Para 242][Para 243][Para 245][Para 246]

**Issue 1.** Under Article 65 of the Labour Law and Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code a contract arises only from matching offer and acceptance; no party can impose a form or content the other has not agreed. [Para 81][Para 86][Para 92] Following Case No. 56/06-Borik, both the workers' request and the employer's refusal were lawful exercises of their own rights, so conversion could not be ordered. [Para 94][Para 101][Para 104] The 5 per cent claims were demands about the future: no conversion had occurred, so there was no factual basis to decide them. [Para 106][Para 107] The panel followed its earlier orders holding that it resolves actual disputes, not problems that have not yet arisen. [Para 109][Para 111][Para 112]

**Issue 2.** Article 293 requires the labour inspector's consent to dismiss a union delegate or candidate, and Prakas No. 305 SKBy of 22 November 2001 protects election candidates for 45 days before and after the ballot and founders and members during registration plus 30 days. [Para 117][Para 118][Para 123] Three conditions had to be met: the worker is a protected person, the dismissal falls inside the protection period, and the employer was notified of the protected names. [Para 140] The first was met — he was elected president. [Para 144][Para 146] The second failed: the union never filed for registration over one year and two months without explanation, which was not a reasonable period to keep protection alive, so Article 293 did not apply. [Para 150][Para 151][Para 152] The panel therefore turned to Article 74: dismissal on an indefinite contract needs a valid reason going to aptitude or conduct or the undertaking's needs, and the party alleging misconduct bears the burden of proof. [Para 156][Para 165] The employer produced no evidence of the warnings or absences it asserted, so the dismissal lacked a valid reason and reinstatement was ordered. [Para 170][Para 171][Para 172]

**Issue 4.** Article 28 forbids punishing the same misconduct twice and defines a pecuniary penalty as any measure reducing wages otherwise due; deducting pay for lateness is therefore an unlawful fine, not a lawful disciplinary measure. [Para 216][Para 217][Para 220][Para 221] The panel rejected the workers' three specific formulations but ordered the practice stopped. [Para 224][Para 225]

**Issue 7.** Article 268 gives rights only to a registered professional organisation and Article 129(2) permits deduction only on the member's written authority; the union was unregistered, so the company could not be compelled to deduct dues. [Para 227][Para 230][Para 237][Para 239]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union leader elected before the union registers keeps the dismissal protection of Article 293 and Prakas No. 305 only while registration is pursued within a reasonable time; an unexplained delay of more than a year ends that protection, and the dismissal is then judged on Article 74, where the employer must prove the misconduct it alleges or reinstate the worker. [Para 140][Para 151][Para 170]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that it decides existing disputes and not problems that have not yet arisen, and that once the timing condition failed it did not need to examine whether the employer had been notified of the protected names. [Para 109][Para 153]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The election date is given as 1 September 2013 throughout, but paragraph 150 measures the delay from "30 October 2014" to the same 30 October 2014 dismissal while asserting one year and two months — the arithmetic only works from 1 September 2013. [Para 143][Para 148][Para 150] Paragraph 21 prints the panel's creation date as "20 November 2022 2021 2014" and paragraph 29 prints the extended award deadline as "11 December 2019 2014". [Para 21][Para 29] The employer appears as "Cambodian Sunrise Garment", "Cambodia Sunrise Garment" and "Sen Cambodian Sunrise", and the worker as Kim Chitra, Kim Chit and "Trem Chitra". [Para 30][Para 114][Para 143] Paragraph 222 says the lateness claim has no legal basis while paragraph 225 orders the deduction stopped — the two are reconciled only by reading 222 as directed at the three formulations. [Para 222][Para 225] Paragraph 242 carries stray figures "$20.000%%" and "III" inside the order. [Para 242] The union letter is described as raising 13 points in one document and 8 in another, against 9 in the conciliation record. [Para 19][Para 30][Para 31] The president's name is printed "Tang Try" on the face and "Tang Troh" in the signature block. [Para 1][Para 248]
