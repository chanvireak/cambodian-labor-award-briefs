# Award No. 51/08 — ASD Cambodia Co., Ltd and NIFTUC / local union of NITUC (Arbitration Council, 25 April 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy, An Nan (dissenting), chair Kong Phallack · Doc ID 140507

## 1. Facts
ASD Cambodia Co., Ltd, a factory at Kantok, Angsnuol, Kandal employing about 770 workers, faced a collective dispute brought by the National Industrial Federation Trade Union of Cambodia (NIFTUC) and its local NITUC union, which had 313 members and no most representative status. [Para 2][Para 19][Para 20] Of six demands, three were settled in conciliation and issue 1 was conciliated at the hearing, leaving the meal allowance and the termination of Ms K. [Para 14][Para 17] Ms K. worked from 21 November 2005, first as a probationer then as a regular worker without any written contract, and was dismissed after being absent from 10 to 17 March 2008 when her father fell ill, without prior notice or a written warning. [Para 24][Para 26][Para 36]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay a meal allowance of 1,500 riel for two hours of overtime and 3,000 riel for four hours, above the 1,000 riel it pays? [Para 21][Para 37]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay termination payments under the Labour Law to Ms K. in lieu of reinstatement? [Para 24][Para 44]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (NIFTUC and local NITUC):** 1,000 riel no longer buys a meal now that market prices have risen, so the allowance should rise to 1,500 and 3,000 riel, and the union would accept even 50 riel more; and Ms K. did ask for leave through her cousin and in person, so her dismissal for abandonment was unlawful and termination payments are owed. [Para 21][Para 27][Para 51]
**Employer (ASD Cambodia Co., Ltd):** it cannot raise the allowance and prefers to keep the lawful 1,000 riel, noting it must now also pay a US$6 monthly living allowance from April 2008; and Ms K. was absent without permission from 10 to 17 March 2008, so the company treated her as having abandoned her employment. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 26]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the meal-allowance demand was declined for want of representative status, and the company was ordered to pay Ms K. one month's wages and benefits in lieu of notice, a 30-day dismissal indemnity, payment in lieu of unused annual leave and her final wage; the award is non-binding for eight days. [Para 75][Para 88]

On issue 2 the panel applied point 4 of Notification 017 SKBY of 18 July 2000, which entitles an overtime worker to 1,000 riel per day or one free meal; the demand for more therefore exceeded the legal minimum and was an interests dispute. [Para 37][Para 39] Under Article 277 and Clause 43 of Prakas 099 an interests dispute may be brought only by a union with most representative status, since an award would take the place of a collective agreement for a year and bind workers who never struck, so the panel declined to consider it, following Awards 57/04 Evergreen, 60/04 United Art, 08/07 Siu Quinh, 31/03 Hung Wah and 99/04 AIA. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43]

On issue 3 the absence of a written contract made Ms K.'s contract one of undetermined duration under Article 67 paragraph 7. [Para 47] Article 74 permits termination at will only on prior written notice and for a valid reason going to aptitude or behaviour, and the company gave no such reason, relied on internal rules it had never adopted and which in any event did not classify unauthorised absence as minor, medium or serious misconduct, and ignored that she had sought leave through her cousin and in person. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51] Clause 34 of Prakas 099 allowed full relief, so the panel calculated one month's notice under Articles 75 and 77 on two years and five months' service, a 30-day indemnity under Article 89, unused annual leave under Articles 166 and 167, and the final wage due within 48 hours under Article 116, on an average daily wage taken over the last twelve months including overtime and bonuses. [Para 55][Para 59][Para 64][Para 74] Arbitrator An Nan dissented, arguing that a demand for termination payments under the law necessarily includes damages equal to the dismissal indemnity under Article 91, which the worker never waived. [Para 90][Para 92]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a meal allowance above the 1,000 riel fixed by Notification 017 is an interests demand that only a union with most representative status may bring, so the panel will decline it however modest the requested increase; but where an employer dismisses a worker of undetermined duration without written notice, without adopted internal rules classifying the absence as misconduct, and where the worker did seek leave, the dismissal is without valid reason and notice pay, dismissal indemnity, unused annual leave and final wages are all owed. [Para 39][Para 50][Para 51][Para 62]

**Obiter dicta:** the dissenting arbitrator's view that Article 91 damages should be added because a general demand for "termination payments under the Labour Law" implies them, and the panel's note that it would not consider damages because Ms K. did not claim them. [Para 71][Para 91]
