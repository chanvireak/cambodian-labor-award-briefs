# Case No. 266/14 — Kin Tay Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 14 October 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer, Liv Sovanna for the workers, Run Saray chair) · Doc ID 139734 · includes a dissenting opinion by arbitrator Ing Sothy

## 1. Facts
Kin Tay Garment Co., Ltd (គីន តាយ ហ្គាមេន ខូអិលធីឌី) employs about 600 workers and uses two kinds of employment contract: fixed duration and undetermined duration. [Para 33][Para 34] New hires get a two-month probation contract and then fixed-duration contracts of three or six months, renewed repeatedly even after the worker's total service passes two years; on each expiry the employer pays severance of 5 per cent and the worker carries on without a break. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 39] From 18 July 2014 the employer required one day's rest between the expiry of one fixed-duration contract and the start of the next. [Para 40][Para 41] Only about 20 of the 600 workers hold undetermined-duration contracts. [Para 34] The local union of the Cambodian Union Federation is registered and has roughly 450 members, but the panel found it was not the most representative union in the company. [Para 30][Para 31]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must conclude undetermined-duration contracts with the workers who have worked continuously for a total of more than two years? [Para 33][Para 59]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** a fixed-duration contract exposes workers to dismissal on the slightest fault and so chills their freedom to join a union, and the employer has renewed fixed-duration contracts for many workers beyond two years, including Ms C, who started on 04 November 2011 and is still on fixed-duration contracts after nearly three years. [Para 45][Para 47][Para 82]
**Employer (respondent):** it applies Articles 67 and 73 of the Labour Law, each renewal being of no more than two years, so it cannot comply; the one-day break was introduced because the company shed about 30 per cent of its workforce in economic difficulty. [Para 42][Para 48]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to conclude undetermined-duration contracts with the workers whose fixed-duration contracts total more than two years. [Para 84][Para 85] Because the parties had signed the Memorandum of Understanding of 03 October 2012 on improving professional relations in the garment industry, the panel split its decision: a rights-dispute ruling, which the parties agreed to treat as binding and cannot appeal, and an interest-dispute ruling, which they may appeal and on which they chose a non-binding award. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 18][Para 19][Para 20] The interest-dispute limb of the order is "none". [Para 87]

The panel first classified the claim. Under Article 312(2) of the Labour Law it decides disputes over the interpretation and application of legal provisions or a collective agreement according to law and all other disputes according to equity. [Para 51][Para 52] A rights dispute concerns a right already set out in the law, an agreement, a collective agreement, internal rules or established practice; an interest dispute concerns a claim not so set out, decided on fairness. [Para 56][Para 57] The claim here was for a change in the type of contract the Labour Law itself regulates, so it was a rights dispute. [Para 59]

On the merits the panel read Article 67(2): a fixed-duration contract may not run longer than two years, and may be renewed once or repeatedly provided each renewal does not exceed two years; a practice contrary to that rule turns the contract into a true undetermined-duration contract. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65] Article 73(5) adds that without notice the contract continues for a term equal to the original, or is deemed undetermined in duration where the total of the renewals exceeds the Article 67 limit. [Para 66] Following Award 10/03 (Zacksintic), the panel held the reference to total duration in Article 67(2) means the cumulative maximum, not the length of each renewal. [Para 67][Para 71] It treated ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982, paragraph 16(3) — fixed-term contracts should not be used for work of long duration — as persuasive though not binding, and observed that the Labour Law leans towards undetermined-duration contracts because job security serves both worker and employer. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 72][Para 73][Para 74]

It concluded that the employer may not keep issuing fixed-duration contracts where their cumulative total exceeds two years and must instead conclude undetermined-duration contracts, which it ordered. [Para 77][Para 78][Para 84][Para 85]

Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented. In his view the employer is entitled to choose the contract type, and where each fixed-duration contract ended and 5 per cent severance was paid under Article 73(6) the contract was properly terminated; conversion would contradict the parties' will under Article 65, require a second 5 per cent payment and add a further severance payment, and since the employer had complied with Article 73 no conversion should be ordered. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 91][Para 100][Para 105]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer renews fixed-duration contracts so that their cumulative duration exceeds two years, the contracts become undetermined in duration by operation of Articles 67(2) and 73(5) of the Labour Law, and the two-year limit is measured against the total of all renewals, not against each renewal; the employer must then issue an undetermined-duration contract. [Para 67][Para 71][Para 77]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks on ILO Recommendation No. 166 and on the law's preference for job security were treated by the panel itself as context rather than binding authority. [Para 72][Para 74]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The panel found the local union with about 450 members "not the most representative" in a company of about 600 workers, without recording the membership of any rival union; the reasoning on representativity is confined to that bare finding. [Para 31][Para 34]
- The workforce figure is cut off mid-sentence as "about 6" at [Para 30] and appears as about 600 at [Para 34].
- The panel-creation date reads "19 September 2023 2014" [Para 12]; Prakas No. 099 is dated "201 2004" [Para 53]; the referral date is printed "2 17 September 2014" [Para 11]; the Memorandum of Understanding is cited as 03 October 2012 at [Para 16] and as "5 2012" at [Para 86].
- Article 73 is cited as "Article 73(6)" for the 5 per cent severance in the dissent [Para 90] and as Article 73(5) in the majority reasoning [Para 66]; Article 67 is cited as 67(2) and 67(7)–(8) on the same page [Para 68].
