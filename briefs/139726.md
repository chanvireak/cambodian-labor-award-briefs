# Award No. 105/13 — Vai Foul Garment (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 19 June 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambo Na, employer-chosen; An Virak, worker-chosen; Kong Phalana, chair) · Doc ID 139726

## 1. Facts
Vai Foul Garment (Cambodia) Ltd is a garment factory at Village Pong Tik, Damnak Ampil, Ang Snuol, Kandal, employing about 1,020 workers, all on fixed-duration contracts of three months at USD 94 a month. [Para 44][Para 47][Para 50][Para 53] On 5 May 2013 the workers elected a local union of the Cambodian Union Federation (CATU / សហជីព កម្ពុជា), choosing Ms C as president, Mr T as vice-president and Mr S as secretary; the union filed for registration on 10 May 2013 and was awaiting its certificate at the date of the award. [Para 45][Para 49][Para 52][Para 56][Para 57] The employer terminated Mr T and Mr S on 9 May 2013 and Ms C on 10 May 2013, each before the end of that worker's fixed term, and Ms C was also demoted from section leader to sewer on 9 May 2013. [Para 66][Para 77] Of the five demands in the non-conciliation report, three were withdrawn at the hearing — a midday meal of 4,000 riel a day (the company offering 1,000 riel), retention of the USD 5 health bonus, and a regular attendance bonus of USD 20 a month (the company offering USD 10) — leaving only the demand for reinstatement. [Para 2][Para 4][Para 5][Para 6][Para 8][Para 20][Para 21]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back Ms C as union president, Mr T as vice-president and Mr S as secretary and let them work normally again, the employer having terminated them days after the leadership election? [Para 2][Para 47][Para 70]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the three were terminated after the union notified the employer of its elected leadership, without any prior warning or fault; Ms C had been promoted to section leader on 1 April 2013 on the section head's own assessment and was demoted on 9 May 2013 once the employer learned she was union president, so the terminations were anti-union discrimination. [Para 62][Para 64][Para 66][Para 67]
**Employer (respondent):** it admits terminating all three because their work was poor, acting on the section head's evaluation, and says that for an early termination it owes wages to the contract's end date plus severance, which the three have not collected; it also accepts it sought no authorisation from the labour inspector. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 68][Para 69]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer is ordered to take back Ms C, Mr T and Mr S and allow them to resume work normally. [Para 92][Para 93] The award is non-binding and becomes final 8 days after notification if no party objects. [Para 94]

The panel first held the award could only be non-binding: although CATU and the company signed the Memorandum of Understanding of 3 October 2012, the local union had not yet obtained the registration certificate, so it did not hold the status of a professional organisation and the parties to this case could not be treated as MoU signatories. [Para 22][Para 23]

On the merits, the panel applied Article 3, paragraph 3 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001: every worker who is a candidate in a union leadership election enjoys protection against dismissal in the same way as a staff delegate, for 45 days before the election and for 45 days after it where the candidate is not elected. [Para 70][Para 71] The panel read the provision as fixing a time limit only for unsuccessful candidates: for candidates who are elected, no period is stated at which the protection ends. [Para 72][Para 73] It followed Award 40/10-Meng Yan, which held that protection cannot continue indefinitely but must last at least 45 days after the election, or may continue for a reasonable period such as until the union applies for registration. [Para 74][Para 75]

The panel then found the facts: the election was on 5 May 2013; the employer dismissed Mr T and Mr S on 9 May and Ms C on 10 May 2013, before their fixed-term contracts expired and within days of the vote, so all three were inside the protected period. [Para 77][Para 79] On notification, the panel found that on 8 May 2013 the union delivered letter No. 016 SSK of 6 May 2013 naming its leadership, signed for by security and handed to the head of administration, which satisfied the requirement that the union inform the employer of candidacy by any effective means; the employer therefore knew the composition of the newly elected leadership. [Para 80][Para 81][Para 82]

Finally the panel applied Article 293 of the Labour Law: the dismissal of a staff delegate, or of any candidate, may be carried out only with the agreement of the labour inspector, who must decide within one month of receiving the employer's request, following Awards 149/08-Cambo Han Se, 175/09-Apsara Authority and 12/12-Quick Young (Cambodia). [Para 83][Para 85][Para 86][Para 87] The employer admitted at the hearing that it took disciplinary measures ending the three contracts without seeking the labour inspector's authorisation, notwithstanding its receipt of the 8 May notice. [Para 88][Para 89][Para 90] The panel concluded that the dismissal procedure had not been complied with for workers enjoying protection from dismissal, and ordered reinstatement to normal work. [Para 91][Para 92]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker elected to union leadership is protected against dismissal from 45 days before the election and, unlike an unsuccessful candidate, for no fixed end point; a dismissal effected within that window without the prior agreement of the labour inspector is procedurally unlawful whatever ground the employer gives, and the remedy is reinstatement to normal work. [Para 73][Para 75][Para 85][Para 92]

The union discharges its duty to inform the employer of candidacy by any effective means, including a letter naming the leadership that is signed for at the factory gate and handed to the administration head; the employer's knowledge from that date starts the protection. [Para 80][Para 82]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that an employer that ends a fixed-term contract early owes wages to the contract's end date plus severance, and observed that the three workers had not collected the sums offered; that observation did not affect the order, since payment cannot cure a dismissal made without the inspector's authorisation. [Para 68][Para 85]
