# Award No. 29/10 — Minh Huy Garment (Arbitration Council, 9 April 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (An Nan for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhean chair; the employer-party arbitrator's name survives only as an OCR fragment) · Doc ID 140256

## 1. Facts
The Free Union of Workers at the Minh Huy Garment factory, Phnom Penh, filed complaint No. 110/010 of 8 January 2010 raising 18 demands to improve working conditions; conciliation on 5 March 2010 failed on 6 of the 18, and non-conciliation report No. 262 of 17 March 2010 reached the secretariat on 18 March 2010. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 23] The six unresolved demands were that the company pay for health and blood checks and transport and reimburse workers who had already paid; that holiday and Sunday work by three groups be added to wages plus a supplement; that wages be paid by 16:00 and shortfalls refunded at once; that male workers with six months' service receive contracts; that no wage or bonus be deducted where a valid medical certificate is produced; and that three terminated union leaders be reinstated, severed from the collective dispute. [Para 2][Para 4][Para 7][Para 10][Para 12][Para 14]

At the hearing of 30 March 2010 the employer appeared through administrative representatives holding an authorization of that date, but the local union leaders and the worker claimants did not appear, and only two federation officers attended to ask for a postponement. [Para 20][Para 26][Para 69][Para 74]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the officers of the Free Union of Workers of the Kingdom of Cambodia had a lawful right to represent the local union and its members in this dispute without a written authorization from that party? [Para 76]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the local union's leaders had all been dismissed by the company and had moved away leaving no telephone number, so the two federation officers asked for a postponement; they conceded they held no written delegation from the local union. [Para 62][Para 67]
**Employer (respondent):** it attended through representatives with a valid authorization from the director and objected to the postponement, because the two officers had no written authorization from the enterprise union to represent the workers. [Para 69][Para 71][Para 75]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel closed case No. 29/10-Minh Huy, holding that the claimant union had abandoned its demands. [Para 86][Para 87][Para 88]

The panel applied Prakar 19 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY of 21 April 2004, under which a party may appear in person, through counsel, or through any other person who has received written authorization from that party. [Para 76] Following its own Award No. 20/07, the panel read that Prakar as requiring written authorization in every case, and found that neither the enterprise union nor its members had authorized the two federation officers in writing. [Para 77][Para 78][Para 79] It held therefore that the federation officers had no lawful right to represent the local union or its members. [Para 79]

The panel then turned to Prakar 21, which allows it either to continue or to terminate by award where a duly invited party fails to appear without a valid reason. [Para 80] The secretariat had notified the parties on 23 March 2010 of the 30 March hearing, so the invitation was regular. [Para 82] The claimants were absent without valid reason and had sent no lawful representative. [Para 81] A claimant bears the duty to state reasons and adduce evidence before the panel, and by staying away it forfeits the chance to support its demands; the local union never appeared and gave no clear explanation. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 85] On that basis the panel found the union had abandoned its claims and closed the file, so the six unresolved demands were never examined on their merits. [Para 86][Para 87]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a party has been duly invited and neither appears nor sends a representative holding written authorization from that party, Prakar 19 of Prakas No. 099 bars anyone else — including an officer of the federation to which the local union is affiliated — from representing it; affiliation alone confers no standing. Prakar 21 then permits the panel to treat the claim as abandoned and to close the case by award. [Para 76][Para 79][Para 80][Para 86]

**Obiter dicta:** the general remark that a claimant bears the duty to supply reasons and evidence and that absence costs it the opportunity to support its demands is stated more broadly than the standing holding requires. [Para 83][Para 85]
