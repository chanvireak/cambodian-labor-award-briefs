# Order No. 88/10 — Khing Da (Cambodia) Nit Thing Co., Ltd v. Federation of Khmer Youth Voice Unions (Arbitration Council, 25 August 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Eing Sothy, An Non, Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 140304

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឃីង ឌា (ខេមបូឌា) នីត ធីង — Khing Da (Cambodia) Nit Thing Co., Ltd — is a factory at Daun Mien village, Ta Khmao, Kandal province, facing 26 demands from the Federation of Khmer Youth Voice Unions (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព សម្លេង យុវជន ខ្មែរ) and its local union. [Para 1][Para 2] The demands ranged from the reinstatement of a union leader and double annual leave to wages paid by the 7th of each month, a permanent nurse, clean drinking water, a nursery and breastfeeding room, maternity pay at 50%, triple pay for holiday overtime, and the removal of the administration head; conciliation on 2 August 2010 settled none of them. [Para 3][Para 7][Para 11][Para 14][Para 25][Para 27][Para 33] Neither party attended the hearing of 11 August 2010 and neither explained the absence, so the panel made no finding on any of the 26 demands and instead closed the file. [Para 36][Para 40]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Condition 1) Whether both parties were validly notified of the date, time and place of the arbitration proceedings? [Para 45][Para 47]
2. (Condition 2) Whether the parties failed to appear and failed to send a representative to the hearing? [Para 46][Para 52]
3. (Condition 3) Whether the parties gave any valid reason for their non-appearance, such that the panel could close the proceedings by order? [Para 46][Para 56]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), Federation of Khmer Youth Voice Unions and the Khmer Youth Voice Union at Khing Da:** their 26 demands, filed on 31 July 2010 and taken through conciliation on 2 August 2010, sought improved working conditions and, in the first demand, the return to normal work of the local union president, Mr S. [Para 2][Para 33] The conciliator treated demand 1 as an individual dispute. [Para 2] No representative of the workers appeared at the hearing to press any of it. [Para 36][Para 54]

**Employer (respondent), Khing Da (Cambodia) Nit Thing Co., Ltd:** the file records no representative appointed by the employer and no submission, document or argument filed by it, and no one appeared for the company at the hearing. [Para 1][Para 37][Para 54] The panel therefore had no version of the facts from either side to weigh. [Para 40]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel closed file No. 88/10 without determining any of the 26 demands, because both parties were absent without valid reason. [Para 59][Para 60]

The panel began from Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, which allows the panel, where a party duly invited fails to appear without valid reason, either to continue in that party's absence or to terminate the arbitration by issuing an order. [Para 41] Rule 4.7 of the arbitration procedure attached to the same Prakas repeats that choice and adds the precondition that the parties must have been properly notified of the date, time and place before a decision is taken. [Para 42][Para 43] Reading those provisions together with Awards 30/05 (Morial), 16/07 (Lotus), 27/07 (IM & V 3), 95/08 (Yong Va), 132/08 (GHG), 138/08 (Malin), 151/08 (Wilson), 09/09 (Nimbus Thich), 01/10 (Kinner), and 30/10 (Global Apparel), the panel distilled three cumulative conditions. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46]

On Condition 1, the secretariat issued formal written invitations — No. 445 to the employer and No. 446 to the workers, both dated 5 August 2010 — following its usual practice and also telephoning both sides about the date, time and place. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50] Notification was therefore proper. [Para 51] On Condition 2, Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 permits a party to appear in person, with an accompanying person, or through a Council of Lawyers advocate or another person authorised in writing, and Article 20 requires attendance at every session; neither side came and neither appointed a representative such as a federation official or lawyer. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54] On Condition 3, no valid reason was given to the secretariat for either absence. [Para 57] All three conditions being satisfied, the panel exercised the power to terminate rather than proceed, and ordered the file closed. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where both parties have been properly notified of the hearing in writing and by telephone, neither appears or sends an authorised representative, and neither gives a valid reason, the panel may terminate the arbitration by order and close the file without examining the merits of any demand; the three conditions are cumulative and must each be established on the record. [Para 44][Para 47][Para 58] A case so closed leaves the underlying demands undetermined, so the union remains free to refile once a party is prepared to attend. [Para 59][Para 60]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that it could find no facts at all on the demands, precisely because both sides were absent, and declined to proceed in anyone's absence even though the rules would have allowed it. [Para 40][Para 41]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The Prakas is dated 21 April 2004 throughout the order but once dated 21 December 2004 in the Article 20 passage, an internal contradiction that affects no outcome. [Para 41][Para 53] The order's own name is printed with several variant spellings of the company's Khmer name and of the word for "file", and the closing paragraph repeats the case name in two further forms. [Para 59][Para 60] The conciliation report number appears as 487/10 in one place and with a different office code suffix in another. [Para 30][Para 34] The how-to-appear passage breaks into an untranslatable fragment where Latin-script characters and a strayised quotation interrupt the Khmer text. [Para 56] One arbitrator's name is corrupted in the signature block, and another arbitrator's name appears with a variant diacritic; the composition on the face of the order is legible. [Para 61]
