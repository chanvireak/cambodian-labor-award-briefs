# Award No. 324/15 — E Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 23 December 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chhea presiding; Yu Soun Ty, employer nominee; Liv Sovanna, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139491

## 1. Facts
E Garment Co., Ltd, on National Road 3 at Svay Rolum commune, Sa'ang district, Kandal province, was the respondent in a collective dispute brought by union federations on behalf of its sewing workers. [Para 1] Conciliation of six demands was held on 11 December 2015 by the Kandal provincial Department of Labour and Vocational Training, and the worker side walked out of the negotiating table without a settlement. [Para 13] The unresolved points reached the Council on 14 December 2015 under conciliation report No. 2263/15 K.B/KCh dated the same day, and the panel was constituted on 15 December 2015, hearing the case at 08:30 on 22 December 2015. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 12] The employer attended through its human resources head; three federations came to the hearing but said they were not the complainants, and the withdrawing federations asked by letter of 22 December 2015 that the case be closed. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 30]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must stop calculating the weekly output bonus by adding it to the daily wage whenever a sewing worker has not earned the daily base wage? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company must provide a midday meal, or a midday food allowance per worker of the amount printed only as "(USD 1)"? [Para 3]
3. Whether, where it cannot arrange accommodation, the company must instead pay USD 20 per worker per month for lodging and travel? [Para 4]
4. Whether the company must raise the base minimum wage to USD 177 per month with effect from 1 January 2016? [Para 5]
5. Whether the company must pay severance for past service and continue the employment relationship under a new fixed-duration contract? [Para 6]
6. Whether the company must build a nursery and breastfeeding room to the standard the law sets, or pay USD 50 per worker per month for outside childcare? [Para 7]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the six demands above, carried from the conciliation of 11 December 2015 — an end to the output-bonus offset against the daily wage, a meal or its cash equivalent, lodging and travel money, a USD 177 minimum wage from 1 January 2016, severance plus re-engagement on a fixed-duration contract, and nursery provision or USD 50 a month in lieu. [Para 2][Para 5][Para 7][Para 13]
**Employer (respondent):** no substantive defence on the six merits is recorded; the company appeared at the hearing through its HR head and filed a letter of 16 December 2015 transferring authority to Mr K Lei and Mr S S, together with its commercial registration, articles of incorporation, internal rules approved by the Kandal department in 2006, and union registration certificates. [Para 18][Para 24][Para 25][Para 27]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** withdrawn — the panel terminated the arbitration of Case 324/15 and issued an order closing it, leaving all six demands undecided. [Para 64][Para 65]
The panel framed the only question it would answer as whether it should continue the arbitration at all in the absence of the complainant worker party, and turned to Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 SKBy of 21 April 2004, which allows it either to proceed in the absence of a duly invited party or to end the arbitration by order. [Para 36] Rule 4.7 of the Council's procedural rules, annexed to the same Prakas, was read as imposing the same choice, subject to the condition that the disputing parties were properly notified of the date, time and place of proceedings. [Para 37][Para 38]
Following seven earlier awards (Cases 16/07, 27/07, 138/08, 11/11, 131/12, 108/13 and 332/14), the panel held that Article 21 requires three cumulative conditions before it may decline to continue: the party was properly invited, the party did not appear before the panel, and the party gave no valid reason for not appearing. [Para 39][Para 40]
On the first condition, the panel found that invitations No. 1614 K.B/AK/VK/LKA to the federation president and No. 1613 to the company director were issued on 15 December 2015 with the date, place and contact numbers, that the secretariat also telephoned the employer and the federation at 14:44 that day, and that the complainant federation was therefore properly summoned. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43][Para 45][Para 46]
On the second, it read "appearance before the panel" as requiring both presence at the hearing and participation through all four stages — introduction and conflict-of-interest explanation, explanation of procedure and framing of the issues, optional re-conciliation, and the hearing on the merits. The complainant federation was absent and took part in none of them. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49]
On the third, the secretariat telephoned the federation representative again at 08:30 on the hearing day and could not reach him, and no valid reason for the absence was ever shown. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 53]
With all three conditions met, the panel treated the complainant as having abandoned its claim and held that it need not continue the arbitration. [Para 54][Para 60][Para 61][Para 63]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a complainant federation has been properly summoned and notified, fails to appear or to take part in any stage of the hearing, and offers no valid reason for its absence, the panel does not decide the demands on their merits: it finds the claim abandoned and terminates the arbitration by order under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 SKBy, so the same demands can proceed only through a fresh complaint and conciliation. [Para 39][Para 48][Para 60][Para 65]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that both complainant and respondent bear a duty to appear in person to present their contentions and to supply the documents needed for decision, and that the respondent's duty to answer arises only after the complainant has first discharged its own — an allocation of the burden it did not need, since the complainant never appeared. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57][Para 58]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The provision applied is printed as "Article 212" at the point of quotation but as Article 21 in the quoted text itself and in every later reference, and the Prakas number shifts between "099 SKBy" and "099 SKBKh". [Para 36][Para 37][Para 54] Rule 4.7 carries a corrupted promulgation date, "25 21 April 2004". [Para 37] The midday food allowance lost its riel figure to the OCR, leaving only "[…] riel (USD 1)"; it is reproduced here as printed. [Para 3] The source is internally inconsistent on when the complainant left the case: three federations are said to have withdrawn by letter of 22 December 2015 at the hearing, while the Khmer Workers' Spirit Federation is separately said to have withdrawn before the hearing day. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 30] It is also unclear which federations were the original complainants: the three attending federations disclaimed that role and named two others, one of which the panel could not contact. [Para 19][Para 22][Para 23] The caption block is heavily corrupted by OCR, including a stray date fragment "2016" above the case number, and the invitation prefixes are printed inconsistently as "K.B/AK" and "B.B/AK". [Para 1][Para 33][Para 42] Individual representatives of the federations are given as Mr A P, Mr B V and Mr C O.
