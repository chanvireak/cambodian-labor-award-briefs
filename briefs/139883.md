# Award No. 218/13 — Sae Han International National (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 28 October 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Pen Bunnha, president) · Doc ID 139883

## 1. Facts
Sae Han International (Cambodia) Co., Ltd runs a garment factory at Trapeang Thleung, Chom Chao, Pur Senchey, Phnom Penh, employing about 1,100 workers [Para 1][Para 36][Para 37]. Twenty-nine workers, represented by three federations under a 21 September 2013 authority-of-attorney, filed a collective dispute; five of them appeared at the 16 October 2013 hearing [Para 38][Para 39][Para 43]. Conciliation had failed on four claims: early departure for pregnant workers, the attendance bonus, the transport and housing allowance, and the overtime meal allowance [Para 2][Para 4][Para 6][Para 8]. The panel decided only claims 2, 3 and 4 [Para 181].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise the regular attendance bonus (ប្រាក់រង្វាន់ធ្វើការទៀងទាត់) from 10 to 15 US dollars a month, and how the bonus may be deducted for absences? [Para 44][Para 49]
2. Whether the employer must raise the transport and housing allowance by 8 US dollars, taking the total to 15 US dollars a month? [Para 59][Para 60]
3. Whether the employer must add 1,000 riel to the overtime meal allowance, making 2,000 riel for overtime worked from 18:00 to 20:00? [Para 64][Para 65]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the cost of housing, water, electricity and food has risen while wages stay low, so the bonus and the transport allowance must rise; the company's practice of wiping out the whole monthly bonus for a single absence destroys the incentive and punishes genuine illness [Para 52][Para 61][Para 53][Para 55]. The company provides no transport home for overtime workers, and 1,000 riel will not buy a proper meal [Para 66].
**Employer (respondent):** the company cannot afford the increases and applies Ministry of Labour Notification No. 230 K.B/S.C.N of 25 July 2012, which fixes the bonus at 10 US dollars and the transport and housing allowance at 7 US dollars a month [Para 56][Para 63][Para 165]. It also pays 2,000 riel for overtime from 16:00 to 18:00 and 1,000 riel from 18:00 to 20:00 [Para 64][Para 174].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted in form, dismissed on the substance — the panel refused to consider all three claims as interest disputes, but ordered the employer to stop deducting the whole attendance bonus and to deduct only in proportion to days absent, at 10 US dollars a month, and 5 US dollars for one day's absence on a private-clinic certificate [Para 164][Para 181][Para 182][Para 171][Para 180].

*Issue 2 (attendance bonus).* The claim to raise the bonus was an interest dispute. Under Prakas No. 099 S.K.B.Y of 21 April 2004, an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement and removes the right to strike, so the panel may hear it only where the union bringing it is the most representative in the enterprise, or where several unions together represent more than half the workforce [Para 82][Para 83]. Three federations represented 29 of roughly 1,100 workers, and the applicant therefore lacked the legal character to bring an interest dispute [Para 87][Para 88]. The panel nonetheless held that a scheme forfeiting the entire bonus for a certified sick day contradicts the spirit of Articles 72 and 25 of the Constitution and of the ratified international covenants on health, and discourages workers from resting when ill, damaging productivity [Para 158][Para 159][Para 160]. Deduction in proportion to days absent is the most reasonable balance, so the employer may not cancel the whole bonus, only the amount proportionate to the days actually taken [Para 130][Para 131][Para 132][Para 163].
*Issue 3 (transport and housing allowance).* The employer already pays the 7 US dollars that point 1 of Notification No. 230 requires, and no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice imposes more [Para 165][Para 167][Para 168]. The claim was an interest dispute and was refused [Para 169][Para 171].
*Issue 4 (overtime meal allowance).* Point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 requires 2,000 riel a day or one free meal for voluntary overtime at the employer's request, regardless of hours worked [Para 172][Para 173]. The company already pays 2,000 riel for 16:00–18:00 and 1,000 riel for 18:00–20:00, which is more favourable than the notification [Para 174][Para 175][Para 176]. No rule requires the extra 1,000 riel; the claim was an interest dispute and was refused [Para 177][Para 178][Para 180].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance-bonus scheme that forfeits the entire monthly bonus for a single certified sick day is contrary to the spirit of the Labour Law and the ratified health covenants; the employer may deduct only the portion proportionate to the days of absence, and for a one-day absence supported by a certificate from a private clinic recognised by the Ministry of Health, 5 US dollars only [Para 132][Para 136][Para 163]. An interest dispute may be heard only where the claimant union, or the unions together, represents more than half the workers in the enterprise [Para 83].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel reasoned that forfeiture drives workers to attend while ill, harming their health and the factory's output [Para 160]. Arbitrator Ing Sothy appended a dissenting opinion on the deduction reasoning under Prakas No. 099 [Para 185][Para 187].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The order at para 163 prints the monthly deduction as "1,000 US dollars", which cannot be right given the 10-dollar bonus established at paras 45, 145, 164 and 182; the summary order at para 164 states 10 US dollars and has been followed here. The award is headed 28 October 2013 while the hearing is dated 16 October 2013 [Para 1][Para 38]. Paragraphs 2, 49, 50, 163 and 173 contain OCR debris and dropped words, and the worker names at para 38 survive only in part. Issue 1, the 15-minute early departure for pregnant workers, appears in the conciliation summary but in no decision paragraph [Para 2][Para 3].
