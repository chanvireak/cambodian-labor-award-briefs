# Award No. 162/09 — MSI Garment Cambodia Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អឹម អេស អាយ ហ្គាមិន ខាំបូឌា អិលធីឌី) (Arbitration Council, 11 December 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — In Sothy (employer-appointed), Huon Chun Dy (worker-appointed), Kong Polla (president) · Doc ID 140461

## 1. Facts
MSI Garment Cambodia Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អឹម អេស អាយ ហ្គាមិន ខាំបូឌា អិលធីឌី), at Trapeang Lvea, Kambol, Dangkao, Phnom Penh, employed about 434 workers and hosted four unions. [Para 35] The complainants were the Free Workers' Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព សេរីភាព កម្មករ, 50–60 members) and the Voice of Cambodian Workers Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព សម្លេង កម្មករ កម្ពុជា, 121 members), together 181 workers or 41.70% of the workforce; both were properly registered. [Para 37][Para 41][Para 126] Two of seven demands were settled in conciliation on 6 November 2009 and five came forward. [Para 18][Para 19] In slack periods the company sent workers home for two to five days at a time, paying 50% of the base wage, without notifying the labour ministry. [Para 43][Para 93]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay 100% of wages at times when it has no work to give on a temporary basis, having suspended work without the labour inspector's authorisation? [Para 43][Para 81]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay piece-rate sewers the base wage for each day of no work even where they attended a full eight hours? [Para 98]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must raise the regular attendance bonus to USD 10 a month and pro-rate it for days of absence? [Para 129]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must supply nutritional food — milk and biscuits — for children in the workplace nursery? [Para 161]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the company must reduce Saturday working to seven hours? [Para 168]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — Free Workers' Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព សេរីភាព កម្មករ) and Voice of Cambodian Workers Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព សម្លេង កម្មករ កម្ពុជា):** the company stops work for days at a time and pays only 50% without asking the ministry, though it is the company's fault that there is no work and workers must still travel in; piece-rate sewers earn only USD 0.5–1 in a slack day against USD 3–5 normally and should be topped up to the base wage; the USD 5 attendance bonus has not risen despite inflation and competitors pay USD 6–8; the nursery should keep milk and biscuits for hungry children; and a 48-hour week is exhausting, so Saturdays should be seven hours. [Para 43][Para 46][Para 50][Para 58][Para 67][Para 75]
**Employer (respondent) — MSI Garment Cambodia Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អឹម អេស អាយ ហ្គាមិន ខាំបូឌា អិលធីឌី):** revenue has fallen so only 50% is payable, and short stoppages need no ministry notice; it already tops piece-rate pay up to the USD 50 minimum each month; the attendance bonus follows Notification No. 017 of 2000 at USD 5; the nursery is a place to leave children, not to feed them, and food is the parents' responsibility under its own rules; and it applies the Labour Law's six-day, 48-hour week. [Para 47][Para 55][Para 60][Para 71][Para 76]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issue 1 was granted and the company ordered to pay 100% of wages during temporary no-work periods; Issues 2, 3, 4 and 5 were refused. [Para 180][Para 181][Para 182][Para 183][Para 184]
On Issue 1 the panel applied Articles 71(11) and 72(1): a suspension of activity is lawful only if notified to and supervised by the labour inspector, and where that procedure is not followed the employer owes full wages although no work is available. [Para 82][Para 89][Para 90][Para 91] Here the company filed no request with the ministry and simply applied its own 50% rule, so the suspension was unlawful and full wages were due. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 96][Para 97]
On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 108, which guarantees piece-rate workers at least the minimum wage, and Notification No. 745 of 2006 setting USD 45 for probationers and USD 50 for full-status workers per month; since the minimum is monthly, not daily, and the employer already tops up any shortfall, the daily USD 1.92 claim had no legal basis. [Para 99][Para 103][Para 107][Para 109][Para 113][Para 114]
That left the demand as a claim for more than the law guarantees. On Issue 2, and again on Issues 3 and 4, the panel held that an interest dispute may be brought only by the most representative union; under Article 277 that requires more than half the workforce, and the two unions together held 41.70%, so neither individually nor jointly could bring such a claim. [Para 117][Para 118][Para 120][Para 126][Para 127][Para 137]
On Issue 5 the panel relied on Articles 137 and 138 and its earlier awards that the employer fixes working time lawfully and reasonably; an eight-hour day within 48 hours a week complies, the demand exceeds the law and is therefore an interest dispute, and it was refused. [Para 169][Para 172][Para 174][Para 177][Para 179]
On Issue 3 the panel additionally found the company had discharged Notification No. 017, and on Issue 4 that neither law nor the nursery rules of 20 February 2007 require the employer to feed children. [Para 133][Para 163][Para 164][Para 165]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A temporary stoppage of work is a suspension requiring notification to and supervision by the labour inspector; without it the employer owes 100% of wages for the idle days, and the company's own 50% practice is no answer. [Para 90][Para 91][Para 94][Para 97] A demand for more than the Labour Law or a notification guarantees is an interest dispute, which the panel will not hear unless the union bringing it represents more than half the workforce, either alone or jointly with other unions. [Para 118][Para 120][Para 126]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the minimum wage is calculated monthly, so a piece-rate worker cannot claim a daily floor of USD 1.92; and that the employer may set and vary work schedules so long as it acts lawfully and reasonably. [Para 109][Para 172]
