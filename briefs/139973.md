# Award No. 40/12 — Great On Neour Tech Style Factory Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 16 March 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana for the employer party, Tuon Siphann for the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 139973

## 1. Facts
Great On Neour Tech Style Factory Co., Ltd. (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្គ្រេត អន នើរ ថេក ស្ថាយ ហ្វេក ធើរី លីមីធីត) is a garment factory at Village 04, Svay Rolum, Sa'ang District, Kandal Province, employing 1,950 workers; the claimant is the local union of the Khmer Workers' Pride Union Federation, registered on 3 February 2012 with about 80 members. [Para 1][Para 37][Para 38] Of six demands, three were settled in conciliation on 8 February 2012 and the parties settled or withdrew five of the six at the hearing of 6 March 2012, leaving only issue 2. [Para 19][Para 24][Para 25] The employer has announced the lot price (តម្លៃឡូត៍) for all lot types on the 25th of each month since 1998, and pays wages on the 7th. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 49]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must notify the workers of the lot price for each series within one week after the workers begin trial sewing? [Para 39][Para 68]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** piece-rate workers receive the lot price only on the 25th of each month, which deprives them of any chance to discuss or refuse the price before accepting the work, since the price is sometimes too low; the factory has no model group for trial sewing. [Para 43][Para 44]
**Employer (respondent):** trial sewing by all workers for one week cannot show their true capacity, so it cannot set a fair lot price from it; announcing on the 25th is a long-standing practice and a reasonable period in which to measure capacity. [Para 45][Para 46]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to notify the workers of the lot price for each series after the workers have sewn it for seven days; the award is non-binding. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 70]

The panel framed the question under Article 112 of the Labour Law, which requires the employer to take measures to inform workers clearly and understandably of the wage conditions applicable to them before they take up their post and whenever those conditions change, and of the wages payable for each pay period. [Para 50][Para 51] It read that article as obliging the employer to tell piece-rate workers the wage level in advance, because their pay varies with the quantity produced and the price fixed for each lot, so that they can decide whether to accept the lot at that price and later disputes are avoided. [Para 52][Para 54] Article 112(a) does not itself say how many days' notice is required, and the panel followed award 62/04-Ecsin, which held that the duty to notify exists although the period is not fixed and is measured by the employer's practical needs. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57] Previous awards had found the usual period for setting a lot price to be three to seven days, that is one week, in 05/06-Double U & D (issue 7) and 81/09-Van Reach (issue 11). [Para 63][Para 64] Although the employer asserted that it needed longer, it did not identify any difference between lot types that would justify it, and it applies a single date to every lot at once. [Para 65][Para 66] The panel observed that announcing on the 25th, close to the payday of the 7th, appeared contrary to the purpose of Article 112, and concluded that seven days is the appropriate period in which the employer can measure the workers' capacity and then set the price. [Para 61][Para 67][Para 68]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Article 112 of the Labour Law obliges an employer to notify piece-rate workers of the lot price before they agree to sew a lot, because their wage depends on it; the article does not fix the period of notice, but seven days from the start of sewing is the period the panel will treat as sufficient, and announcing prices at the end of the month, days before payday, defeats the purpose of the article. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 61][Para 67]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's suggestion that the employer may select skilled workers to form a model group for trial sewing before fixing prices, and its general finding that piece-rate wages have exceeded US$ 61 a month with a top-up to that figure, are observations that go beyond the order made. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 62]
