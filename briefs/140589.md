# Award No. 17/07 — Cham Thich Thay Garment (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឆាម ចំច ថា យ ហ្គាមេន) v Federation of Independent and Democratic Unions (សហព័ន្ធសហជីពឯករាជ្យ និង ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ) (Arbitration Council, 9 March 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirem, employer-appointed; Vong Vanna, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 140589

## 1. Facts
Cham Thich Thay Garment (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឆាម ចំច ថា យ ហ្គាមេន) employs more than 800 workers at Chhmuoh, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh; the claimant is the Federation of Independent and Democratic Unions (សហព័ន្ធសហជីពឯករាជ្យ និង ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ), whose local union was elected on 14 January 2007 and whose registration receipt is dated 16 January 2007. [Para 22][Para 31][Para 146]
After a strike over 14 demands, the company suspended the local union president (Mr B) from work on 20 January 2007, saying he had spent working time on union business by collecting photographs near the toilets. [Para 31][Para 34][Para 37]
Of the 14 demands, 10 were settled in conciliation on 23 January 2007 and 4 were referred: reinstatement of the three union leaders, proper annual leave, direct access of the union to management, and one day a month for pregnancy checks. [Para 16][Para 17]
At the hearing the claimant withdrew two of the three leaders from issue 1 — one was still working and the other had abandoned her job — so only Mr B's case remained. [Para 26][Para 28][Para 30]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must take back the union president, dismissed on 20 January 2007, and treat that dismissal as lawful? [Para 57]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must arrange annual leave and restore leave lost since the workers were hired? [Para 106]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the union's delegates may submit demands directly to the company administration? [Para 126]
4. (Issue 4) Whether pregnant workers must be given one day a month for health checks with daily wage and the USD 5 attendance bonus kept? [Para 140]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, Federation of Independent and Democratic Unions / សហព័ន្ធសហជីពឯករាជ្យ និង ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ):** the three leaders were removed because they were union leaders, which is discrimination, and their conduct was no serious misconduct; the company owes four years of annual leave; the letter box it offers is unsigned and slow; and pregnancy checks should be paid. [Para 2][Para 4][Para 51][Para 52]
**Employer (respondent, Cham Thich Thay Garment / ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឆាម ចំច ថា យ ហ្គាមេន):** the suspension was not anti-union but a response to time spent on union activity in working hours; it will grant leave only from 23 January 2007 and cannot reconstruct old records; demands may be filed in its letter box; and it will allow check-up time but not pay for it. [Para 3][Para 44][Para 47][Para 141]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must reinstate Mr B with his salary and benefits from the date the award takes effect, must negotiate annual leave within one month, must accept written demands directly through its administration; the pregnancy claim was not considered. [Para 149][Para 150][Para 151][Para 152]
On the contract, the panel applied Articles 65(3) and 67(7): an unwritten contract, or a fixed-duration contract not put in writing, becomes an indefinite-term contract, so Mr B held an indefinite-term post. [Para 57][Para 61][Para 62]
Under Article 74 an indefinite-term contract may be terminated only for a reason concerning the worker's aptitude or conduct or the needs of the enterprise; the company produced no such reason and never supplied the warning letter the panel ordered by 27 February 2007, while Mr B had four years of service without a single warning. [Para 63][Para 65][Para 36][Para 71][Para 72]
On discrimination, Articles 12, 266 and 279 forbid an employer to use union membership or activity as a ground for any decision on work assignment, discipline or dismissal; the suspension came days after the election, and the panel found the photographs did not justify it. [Para 73][Para 77][Para 82]
Article 293 protects staff delegates and election candidates, and Article 3 of Prakas No. 305 extends that protection for 45 days before and after the ballot; where the three conditions are met, dismissal without the labour inspector's prior authorisation is void and the worker is reinstated. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 95][Para 102]
All three conditions were satisfied here — he was a candidate, the dismissal fell inside the protected window, and the union had notified the company of his candidacy. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 98]
On leave, Article 166 imposes a duty the company had ignored for four years, and Article 170 requires leave to be fixed by agreement, so the parties must negotiate rather than have the panel set dates. [Para 114][Para 116][Para 125]
On access, Articles 2 and 138 give management its powers only where exercised lawfully and reasonably, so a rule confining the union to a letter box was set aside. [Para 130][Para 138][Para 139]
The pregnancy claim exceeded the statutory floor and, as an interest dispute, could be brought only by a union with most-representative status under Article 277, which this union, still in registration, did not have. [Para 143][Para 145][Para 147][Para 148]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union leader on an unwritten contract holds an indefinite-term contract, and his dismissal inside the 45-day window around a union election is void unless the employer first obtained the labour inspector's authorisation; the employer cannot cure the absence of a valid reason by pointing to union activity it calls a misuse of working time. [Para 62][Para 95][Para 72]

**Obiter dicta:** a union without most-representative status cannot arbitrate demands that merely exceed the statutory floor, however reasonable they are. [Para 147]
