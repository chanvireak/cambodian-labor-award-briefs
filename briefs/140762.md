# Award No. 08/05 — Winner Knitting (Employer party) v Union of Winner Knitting Factory (Employee party) (Arbitration Council, 23 March 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Arbitrators: Mr Mar Samborana (employer party), Mr An Nan (worker party), Mr Koy Neam (chair) · Doc ID 140762

## 1. Facts
Winner Knitting Factory Ltd sits on national road 21, village 4, Khum Svay Rolum, Saang district, Kandal province, and employs 4,028 workers [Para 36][Para 37]. On 28 February 2005 the Kandal labour department was notified of a strike and MOSALVY officers went to the factory but could not settle the two issues in the non-conciliation report [Para 38]. The report is dated 1 March 2005 and reached the Secretariat on 2 March 2005 [Para 23][Para 39]. The company asked the Council for an award to stop the strike, and the Council ordered the workers to stop immediately and the parties to appear on 4 March 2005 [Para 40]. At the first hearing the parties agreed on issue 1: the piece rate for skirt types 01, 02 and 04 stays at 0.416, and workers below the minimum wage have their basic wage subsidised to USD 45 [Para 40]. A second hearing on 11 March 2005 dealt with issue 2, the suspension of the union president [Para 40][Para 114]. The company suspended him indefinitely on 26 February 2005 by letter copied to the Kandal labour inspector, without sending any termination letter to the inspector [Para 119]. The company alleged the strike cost it USD 300,000 [Para 72]. The textile section struck non-violently from 10:30 on 25 February 2005 until midday on 2 March 2005 [Para 63][Para 125].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must raise the piece rate from USD 0.38 to USD 0.416 for skirt types 01, 02 and 04 [Para 20]?
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must reinstate the union president after he was suspended awaiting termination [Para 114]?

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Employer (respondent), Winner Knitting:** the union president refused three invitations to set the new month's price on 23 February 2005 and stopped the vice-president from attending, so the company set the price without him [Para 46][Para 59][Para 73]. It characterises his conduct as incitement to strike under Article 83(B)(5) of the Labor Law and its suspension letter as notice of termination for serious misconduct causing a USD 300,000 loss [Para 72][Para 74][Para 71]. Only one employer witness, the Chinese section teacher, appeared; ten others gave written testimony and stayed away for security reasons [Para 41][Para 42].
**Employee (claimant), Union of Winner Knitting Factory:** the union president says he refused because he had taken three days' leave and had not yet consulted the workers, and that he never told the company he would attend later [Para 79][Para 120]. He says he never incited the strike, that all workers stopped voluntarily over the low piece rate, and that he held a hammer only while walking about [Para 69][Para 105][Para 106].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel maintained the agreed piece rate of 0.416 with the USD 45 subsidy and the company's right to give advice, ordered the union president reinstated in his previous position with wages from 26 February 2005, and converted the award into a written warning with a disciplinary probation of no more than three months from 26 February 2005 [Para 166][Para 167][Para 168][Para 169][Para 170][Para 172].
On Issue 1 the panel recorded that both parties had agreed [Para 128].
On Issue 2, the panel found the company gave no specific evidence that the union president incited the strike [Para 118]. It noted Article 83(B)(5) of the Labor Law treats incitement of other workers to commit serious offences as a serious offence, and Article 330 treats violent acts during a strike as serious misconduct warranting suspension or disciplinary layoff [Para 129]. Because only the employer's witness was heard, the panel treated his testimony as adding only to management's argument [Para 129]. It found no evidence of incitement and no violence or damage [Para 129][Para 130]. A non-violent strike is not serious misconduct, and an appeal to strike is not serious misconduct under Article 83(B)(5) without evidence of incitement to violence [Para 130][Para 131][Para 132].
The panel nonetheless found the union president failed his duty as a good partner [Para 133]. Article 2(K) of the internal work rule requires each shop steward to respect the responsibilities of the workers' legal representative [Para 127][Para 133]. Article 284 of the Labor Law makes it the shop steward's mission to present grievances on wages and labour law enforcement to the employer [Para 134]. He refused three times, and where he had a proper reason he should have asked for a postponement to gather the data [Para 135][Para 136]. His unclear answer about future attendance caused the company to misunderstand, and he had other choices besides refusing or blocking the vice-president [Para 138][Para 139][Para 141][Para 142]. The panel found the dispute would have been avoided had he attended or given the vice-president the price [Para 146][Para 147].
On the suspension, the panel held the mistake was not serious misconduct under Article 83(b) or 330 [Para 152]. Article 71 permits suspension without pay during disciplinary action defined in the internal work rule, but this rule contains no suspension procedure [Para 153]. Article 27 requires any sanction to be proportional to the misconduct and empowers the labour inspector to control proportionality [Para 155][Para 156]. An indefinite suspension is not proportional, so the company cannot discipline him that way [Para 159][Para 160]. Under equity and Article 34 of Prakas 99 the panel issued a written warning and probation instead [Para 163][Para 165].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A shop steward's refusal to attend piece-rate negotiations, without more, is a breach of the duty of good partnership under Article 284 of the Labor Law and internal work rule Article 2(K), but it is not serious misconduct under Article 83(B)(5) or Article 330, which require evidence of incitement or violence [Para 129][Para 132][Para 152]. An employer may suspend a contract without pay only during a disciplinary procedure that its internal work rule provides, and Article 27's proportionality requirement bars an indefinite suspension [Para 153][Para 159].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR is badly corrupted, rendering "the employee" as "thempoyee", "the employer" as "thempoyer", "skirt" as "shirk", "at the" as "athe", "sent to" as "sento", "into" as "into" and "and" as "and" attached to the following word. "Khum Svay Rorlum" in the header [Para 1] becomes "Khum Svay Rolum" in the summary [Para 36], and "Srok Saang" becomes "Saang district" [Para 22]. The hearing date is printed "4 March 2005 at 11:00 PM" where morning was meant [Para 27]. The reasons cite "February 23 2004" where 2005 is meant [Para 135], and the probation is "not longer tree months" [Para 165]. The suspension power is cited as "Article 71 (7)" without a corresponding sub-article in the text [Para 153], and Prakas 99 is dated "2001" where 2004 is meant [Para 163]. The internal work rule is cited once as "Article 2 (K)" and once as "Article 2(K)" [Para 127][Para 133].
