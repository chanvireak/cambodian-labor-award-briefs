# Award No. 172/13 — Cambo Yun Foung Garment (Arbitration Council, 12 September 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, chosen by the employer party; An Nan, chosen by the worker party; Pen Bun Chha, chair) · Doc ID 139855

## 1. Facts
Cambo Yun Foung Garment, a garment factory at Sangkat Tuek Thla, Khan Sen Sok, Phnom Penh, employed 424 workers. [Para 1][Para 51] The National Union of Yun Fong Garment Industry, registered on 7 June 2013 with about 70 members, put forward 24 claims to improve working conditions; 8 were not conciliated, 3 of those were settled at the hearing, leaving 5 for the panel. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 28][Para 29][Para 52][Para 53] The disputed acts were the refusal of a free midday meal, piece rates that left most sewers below the base wage, refusal of a daily top-up, payment of 50% wages when the employer sent workers home for lack of goods, and deduction of the whole USD 10 attendance bonus for authorised leave. [Para 54][Para 58][Para 74][Para 84][Para 98]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must provide one free midday meal to all workers? [Para 54]
2. Whether the employer must raise piece rates so that at least half of the workers in the sewing section receive wages calculated by output? [Para 119]
3. Whether the employer must top up a worker's pay to USD 3.2 per day whenever daily output pay falls below that figure, the worker keeping the excess on a better day? [Para 142]
4. Whether the employer must pay 100% wages when workers reach the factory and are sent home because there is no work? [Para 163]
5. Whether the employer may deduct the whole USD 10 monthly attendance bonus, or only in proportion to the days of leave it authorised? [Para 186][Para 200]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the union doubted the hygiene of food bought outside the factory; rates set by the employer let only 2 or 3 of 28 sewers earn by output and were changed four times; being sent home without notice costs petrol and the chance of other work. [Para 55][Para 59][Para 63][Para 89]
**Employer (respondent):** the factory never provided lunch and hygiene outside its premises is not its concern; rates are fixed by a computer system it calls (IE) after one week of observation, with monthly top-up to the USD 85 base wage; when there is no work it seeks other work at 100% pay and pays 50% only where it sends workers home. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 64][Para 66][Para 90][Para 91]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the meal claim and the daily top-up claim were dismissed [Para 226][Para 228]; the employer was ordered to reset piece rates so an average-skill worker in normal hours can earn at least the USD 85 base wage it pays [Para 227], to pay 100% wages where suspension of the contract was not lawfully carried out [Para 230], and to deduct the USD 10 attendance bonus only in proportion to authorised leave [Para 231].

Issue 2: the panel found no statute, collective agreement or other agreement between the parties creating a duty to feed workers, and treated the claim as an interest dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍) rather than a rights dispute. [Para 104][Para 105] Under Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, and because Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 makes such an award stand in place of the collective agreement for a year and removes the right to strike on that subject, the panel held it may arbitrate such a dispute only where the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise, or unions together represent over half the workforce. [Para 106][Para 112][Para 113][Para 114] The Yun Fong union was not yet most representative, so the claim was not considered. [Para 116][Para 117][Para 118]

Issue 5: Article 108 requires piece rates to be fixed so that a worker of average skill working normal hours receives at least the guaranteed minimum wage, and Article 137 caps normal hours at eight a day. [Para 119][Para 120] Following its earlier awards, the panel held that the employer may set and change rates but must first run a trial and then check actual results. [Para 124][Para 126] The employer never stated that it had trialled the rate, and only about 30% of sewers reached the base wage on the rate applied, so the rate was too low and Article 108 was breached. [Para 137][Para 138][Para 140]

Issue 6: Notice No. 103 of 9 April 2013 requires top-up to the minimum wage across a month, not day by day, and both parties accepted that no worker received less than the USD 85 base wage; the claim therefore failed. [Para 155][Para 157][Para 160]

Issue 7: Article 71 (point 11 as printed) allows suspension of the contract for serious economic difficulty only under the supervision of the labour inspector, and the panel read that as requiring notification to and verification by the inspector. [Para 167][Para 169][Para 171] Where suspension is lawful there is no wage obligation; where it is not, the employer owes 100%. [Para 175][Para 176] The employer never showed a suspension request to the inspector and suspended unilaterally, which the panel held invalid. [Para 179][Para 181][Para 183]

Issue 8: the USD 10 attendance bonus in Notice No. 230 of 25 July 2012 is a general rule binding all garment and footwear employers, not a payment the employer may grant or withhold at will, and it is a form of wage. [Para 191][Para 217][Para 218] Absence on annual, special, maternity, public holiday or weekly rest permits no deduction; for other leave authorised by the employer the panel could not interpret "without absence" and would decide case by case. [Para 196][Para 197][Para 199] Authorised leave suspends the contract under Article 71 (point 6), so no wage and no bonus are earned for those days, but the employer may deduct only in proportion to them. [Para 219][Para 222][Para 224][Para 225]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A piece rate breaches Article 108 where the employer fixed it without first trialling it on the workers and only a minority of the section can reach the guaranteed wage on it; the employer's freedom to set rates is bounded by the duty to let an average-skill worker earn at least that wage within normal hours. [Para 124][Para 138][Para 140] An employer that sends workers home for lack of goods without notifying the labour inspector and obtaining the inspector's verification has suspended the contract unlawfully and owes full wages for those days. [Para 175][Para 181] A statutory attendance bonus is wages, so authorised leave may reduce it only in proportion to the days taken, never in full. [Para 224][Para 225] The panel will not arbitrate an interest dispute brought by a union that is not most representative. [Para 114][Para 117]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's statements that it could not interpret the phrase "without absence" in Notice No. 230 and would assess authorised absences case by case go beyond the order, which was simply to deduct pro rata. [Para 199] Arbitrator Ing Sothy recorded a dissent on issue 8 only, reasoning that a bonus is a discretionary reward whose giver sets its conditions, so a worker who does not meet them cannot claim it. [Para 235][Para 242][Para 251]
