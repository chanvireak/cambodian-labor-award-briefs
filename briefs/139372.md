# Award No. 219/16 — U.D.S Garment Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 20 October 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, employer-nominated, dissenting; Tun Si Phan, worker-nominated; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 139372

## 1. Facts
The company runs a garment factory at Bak Khaeng, Prek Liep, Chroy Changvar, Phnom Penh, with about 730 workers and no most-representative union [Para 59][Para 60][Para 64]. Between January and mid-February 2016 it replaced the identity documents of 88 workers — 3 men, 85 women, 4 of them pregnant — because some had borrowed other people's papers, which affected their social-security registration; it paid them 5% contract-termination compensation, unused annual leave and final wages, but restarted their seniority from zero [Para 77][Para 78][Para 80][Para 89]. Two workers, Ms I and Ms L, attended the conciliation and arbitration of an earlier collective dispute (case 113/16) on 29 April, 11 May and 25 May 2016, and the company deducted their wages and benefits for those days [Para 70][Para 143]. Six claims went to conciliation, two were settled there, two more were withdrawn at the hearing, leaving four [Para 21][Para 26][Para 27].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must repay the wages and benefits it deducted from Ms I and Ms L for the days they attended the settlement of collective dispute 113/16? [Para 130]
2. Whether the employer must preserve the seniority of workers who changed their identity documents before 2 May 2016? [Para 150]
3. Whether the employer must pay 90 days of maternity benefits to Ms C, who applied for maternity leave after changing her identity documents? [Para 173]
4. Whether the employer must issue six-month fixed-duration contracts to male workers, as it does to female workers? [Para 189]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The two representatives were direct parties to dispute 113/16, whose outcome affected their own wage claims, so the employer had to keep their wages and benefits while they attended; in past practice it never deducted pay for attendance at dispute settlement [Para 70][Para 71][Para 72]. Seniority is continuous service and cannot be erased by correcting identity papers, and the Ministry's Notification No. 127 of 2 May 2016 requires it to be preserved [Para 82][Para 84]. Ms C was entitled to 90 days' maternity benefits under Articles 182 and 183, and men should get the same six-month contracts as women because six months is longer than three [Para 94][Para 101].

**Employer (respondent):** It had warned both workers in advance that it would not pay wages if they attended without being union leaders, and it pays only the three union leaders who are summoned; they came anyway, so their absence was unauthorised [Para 74][Para 75][Para 143]. The document change was voluntary, the workers signed it, the 5% compensation and leave pay were settled, and anyone unhappy should have objected at the time; a party cannot force another to accept a contract form it does not agree to [Para 85][Para 86][Para 87][Para 103]. Ms C's service was under one year measured from the document change, so no maternity benefit was due [Para 95].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to repay the deducted wages and benefits to Ms I and Ms L, and to preserve the seniority of workers who changed identity documents before 2 May 2016; it dismissed the maternity claim and the equal-contract claim [Para 219][Para 220][Para 221][Para 222].

On standing, the panel applied Article 54 of the Law on Trade Unions and Article 11, which confers rights only on registered unions, and held that a union without most-representative status may act only for workers who have actually entrusted it with their dispute, not for all its members [Para 106][Para 111][Para 124][Para 125]. It found the two claimants had signed a transfer of rights on 19 September 2016, so their claims were properly before it [Para 62].

On dispute 1, Article 306 of the Labour Law requires parties to attend every conciliation meeting, and Prakas No. 099 Articles 20 and 14 require the employer to let workers attend hearings held during working hours without any negative effect on their labour rights [Para 139][Para 137]. The panel held that a representative attending conciliation or arbitration cannot have wages, bonuses or other benefits deducted, following Awards 135/10 and 113/16 [Para 139][Para 140]. Because the two workers were direct parties to dispute 113/16 and the panel there had already ruled on the same deduction, the employer had to repay [Para 145][Para 146][Para 147][Para 149].

On dispute 2, the panel adopted the definition of seniority in Award 68/05 — GOLDIA: continuous service conferring added rights, ending only when the worker stops working for the enterprise [Para 150][Para 151]. Paying 5% termination compensation and leave pay at the expiry of a fixed-term contract does not break that continuity, following Award 67/10 — Hong Va [Para 153][Para 157]. It found the workers had never stopped working for the employer; only their papers were corrected, so their seniority continued unbroken [Para 159][Para 160][Para 161]. Notification No. 127 confirms that workers whose identity data are irregular keep their rights including seniority, and the panel applied that principle to changes made before the notification took effect [Para 163][Para 170][Para 171][Para 172].

On dispute 3, the burden lay on the party asserting the claim; Ms C had resigned on 11 July 2016, before the maternity application of 22 July 2016 she relied on, and no supporting document was filed, so the claim failed [Para 179][Para 183][Para 184][Para 186][Para 188].

On dispute 4, Article 65 of the Labour Law and Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code make the contract a matter of mutual offer and acceptance; neither party can impose a term on the other, so the panel dismissed the claim while noting the request itself was lawful [Para 189][Para 197][Para 204][Para 214][Para 218].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may not deduct wages, bonuses or benefits from a worker who attends conciliation or arbitration of a collective dispute in which that worker is a direct party, even where the worker is not a union leader and was not summoned by name; and correcting irregular identity documents, with payment of 5% termination compensation at the expiry of a fixed-term contract, does not interrupt seniority, which runs continuously until the worker actually leaves the enterprise. [Para 148][Para 161][Para 157]

**Obiter dicta:** The employer-nominated arbitrator filed a dissenting opinion under Article 37 of Prakas No. 099, reasoning that workers who attend without a formal invitation naming them act privately, and that a private decision cannot create an obligation for the company to pay wages for an unauthorised absence; he would also have limited the union's representation to members who expressly transferred their rights [Para 224][Para 235][Para 243][Para 244]. The majority noted in passing that a union's request for a longer contract term was itself lawful, only unenforceable without the employer's consent [Para 214].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR is heavily degraded. The award carries a dissenting opinion as an annex, so its conclusion is majoritarian, not unanimous [Para 224]. The employer's name is printed as U.D.S Garment and as U.D.S Garment Co Ltd, with a commercial-register number given as both MOC 28266061 and Inv. 1233 E/2013 [Para 33][Para 59]. The number of workers whose documents were changed appears as 88 at Para 89 but as 100 and 970 in two later recitals [Para 158][Para 159]. The disputed worker's identity is itself confused: the panel treats "Chin Srey Pich" and "Chek Chanthou" as the same person on a shared employee number, and the old number is given as 02057 and the new as 02917 in one place and 02907 in another [Para 91][Para 173][Para 221]. The conciliation report number appears as 11859 in one recital and 1185 elsewhere [Para 17][Para 22]. Article 306 is rendered at one point as a rule about attendance and penalties, and the panel's recital at Para 139 repeats itself. Individual workers are referred to here by initial only.
