# Award No. 014/20 — Hiray Sin Audar (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 19 March 2020)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — You Soun Thy (employer-appointed), An Virak (worker-appointed), Kong Phalla (president) · Doc ID 139295

## 1. Facts

Hiray Sin Audar (Cambodia) manufactures bags and garments for export at Longvek, Kampong Tralach district, Kampong Chhnang, employing 7,910 workers, 64 of whom the conciliation minutes treat as directly concerned. [Para 51] [Para 52] [Para 58]
The factory has four local unions; the Workers' Rights and Interests Union is the most representative and holds a collective bargaining agreement (CBA) with the company for the third term, valid from 17 February 2020 to 16 February 2023. [Para 53] [Para 55]
The Anachak Yuvachun union, registered on 13 December 2019 with 223 members, brought twelve demands; six were settled at conciliation on 26 February 2020, one was withdrawn at the hearing and one was resolved by agreement, leaving issues 3 to 6 for the panel. [Para 12] [Para 13] [Para 18] [Para 57]

## 2. Issues in dispute

Issues 1 and 2 were withdrawn and agreed respectively; the panel considered the remaining four in its own numbering. [Para 18] [Para 19]

3. Whether the employer must allow a woman who has just returned from maternity leave to resign with immediate effect where she produces documents proving a clear reason? [Para 145]
4. Whether the employer must soften its warnings for failed fingerprint scanning, giving only an oral reminder for the first three occasions and a written warning only on the fourth? [Para 162]
5. Whether the employer must make it easier for workers to apply for leave by allowing applications to be made to the group leader alone? [Para 171]
6. Whether the employer discriminated against the union president, Mr T, by transferring him out of Building 4 to work as a mobile electrician and withholding the bonus and promotion promised to him? [Para 108] [Para 178]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** The Anachak Yuvachun union speaks for the complaining workers, and the CBA provisions the employer relies on do not answer the demands, which concern treatment of members who had returned from maternity leave, penalties for scanning failures, leave procedures and the targeting of their president after the union was registered. [Para 60] [Para 97] [Para 117]

**Employer (respondent):** Every one of these demands is already governed by provisions of the CBA concluded with the most representative union, so the company asks only that the CBA be respected; it denies any anti-union motive, saying the transfer kept Mr T's wage, shift and trade intact and that he was moved because a new electrician was recruited for Building 4. [Para 68] [Para 85] [Para 102] [Para 119] [Para 121]

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** dismissed — issues 3, 4, 5 and 6 were all refused; issue 2 was recorded as agreed by the parties under Articles 8 and 27 of the CBA, and issue 1 had been withdrawn. [Para 204] [Para 205] [Para 206] [Para 207] [Para 208]

The panel first decided whether a minority union could bring the case at all. Under Article 54 (new) of the Trade Union Law only the most representative union holds the exclusive right to conclude a CBA and to resolve collective disputes in the enterprise, a reading it had already adopted in Awards 180/16 and 171/16. [Para 129] [Para 130] [Para 131] [Para 132] Article 59 (new) and Article 14 of Prakas 303 nevertheless allow a minority union to represent its own members in collective disputes that do not arise from the operation of the CBA. [Para 134] [Para 136] [Para 139] On the facts the Anachak Yuvachun union, with 223 members against a most representative union holding a CBA valid to 2023, is a minority union, and the panel recognised it as representative of its members for disputes outside the CBA, including the Arbitration Council stage. [Para 133] [Para 141] [Para 142] [Para 144]

Applying that to issue 3, the panel found the demand matches Article 14(a) and Article 21(c) of the CBA, which set notice periods and the documents required after maternity, so the minority union had no standing to claim it; issue 4 matched Article 12 of the CBA on fingerprint scanning; issue 5 matched Article 17 on leave. [Para 146] [Para 154] [Para 163] [Para 168] [Para 172] [Para 175] Each was therefore refused without examination of its merits. [Para 161] [Para 170] [Para 177]

Issue 6 was different: the discrimination claim is not covered by the CBA, so standing existed and the panel reached the merits. [Para 179] [Para 180] Article 2 of the Labour Law gives the employer the right to organise and manage the enterprise, exercised lawfully and reasonably, which prior awards read as permitting transfers that do not cut wages, do not switch day and night shifts, do not substantially change skills and do not move the worker far away. [Para 181] [Para 183] [Para 184] [Para 185] Mr T's wage, shift and electrician trade were unaffected by the move outside Building 4, so the four conditions were met and the transfer was lawful. [Para 186] [Para 187] [Para 188] The panel then applied Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law and Article 62 of the Trade Union Law, which forbid using union membership or activity as a ground for decisions on assignment, pay, benefits or discipline. [Para 190] [Para 191] [Para 192] [Para 194] The burden of proving anti-union discrimination lies on the workers; beyond the bare assertion of his union office, Mr T offered no specific fact or evidence, so the claim failed. [Para 197] [Para 201] [Para 202] [Para 203]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

A minority union registered under the Trade Union Law may represent only its own members, and only in collective disputes that do not arise from the implementation of the collective bargaining agreement. Where a demand reproduces an entitlement already fixed in a CBA concluded with the most representative union, the minority union has no standing and the panel will not examine the merits, however arguable the demand. [Para 134] [Para 142] [Para 154] For a claim that does fall outside the CBA, the workers bear the burden of proving anti-union discrimination; a transfer that leaves wage, shift, trade and workplace distance intact falls within the employer's Article 2 managerial prerogative and does not become discriminatory merely because the worker holds union office. [Para 184] [Para 197] [Para 203]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that a worker who resigns immediately without prior notice forfeits outstanding annual-leave compensation [Para 66]; and it observed that a union with a small membership may still act for non-members who expressly entrust it with their representation [Para 61].
