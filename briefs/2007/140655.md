# Award No. 116/07 — Grace Sun Cambodia Garment Co, Ltd (Arbitration Council, 27 November 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Liv Sovanna, Kong Phallack as chair) · Doc ID 140655

## 1. Facts
Grace Sun Cambodia Garment Co, Ltd employed 820 workers and had two unions in the factory, FTUWKC and KYTU; KYTU is the claimant and neither union held most-representative status. [Para 43] [Para 44] From 11 July 2007 the company suspended the contracts of 19 workers in the lock-stitching team on 50 percent wages while recruiting new workers to take their jobs. [Para 44] [Para 49] Five demands were referred after conciliation failed on 30 October 2007 and were heard on 12 November 2007. [Para 34] [Para 36]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay full wages to the 19 lock-stitching workers whose work it suspended at 50 percent while recruiting replacements? [Para 25] [Para 44]
2. Whether the company must set the number of garments to be lock stitched consistently with the practice in 2000? [Para 51]
3. Whether the company must transfer the head of the lock-stitching team out of the team for bias and inappropriate words towards workers? [Para 54]
4. Whether the company must allow KYTU leaders at the factory to meet for two hours per week? [Para 82]
5. Whether the company must increase the seniority bonus by US$ 1 for workers who have worked more than five years? [Para 84]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** existing workers were less productive only because the number of garments to be lock stitched had risen, and the company neither applied to the Labour Inspector for suspension nor disciplined them, instead replacing them with new workers afraid of the director; Mr S favoured his clique with overtime and used inappropriate words towards a worker. [Para 47] [Para 48] [Para 56]
**Employer (respondent):** the productivity of existing workers kept falling and new workers were simply faster, the 2000 practice was never explained or documented, the overtime and layoff arrangements were the company's decisions rather than Mr S's, and only worker delegates may meet under the law. [Para 45] [Para 51] [Para 57] [Para 83]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to provide full wages to the 19 workers for the period their contracts were illegally suspended; demands 2 and 3 were rejected, and demands 4 and 5 were declined as interests disputes. [Para 126] [Para 128]

Issue 1: Article 71 of the Labour Law exhaustively lists the grounds for suspension, and suspension for serious economic or material difficulty must be under the control of the Labour Inspector. The company suspended the 19 workers for declining productivity, never applied to the Inspector, and did not show it had disciplined them under its internal rules, so the suspension did not comply with Article 71; following Awards 21/03 Loyal Cambodia, 46/04 M&V, 01/04 New Point II and 60/04 United Art, full wages replaced the 50 percent paid. [Para 86] [Para 88] [Para 90] [Para 92]

Issue 2: Article 2 of the Labour Law, as read in Awards 28/04 Raffles Hotel Grand'Angkor, 18/[06] GHG, 20/06 New Star, 108/06 Trinungal Komara and 17/07 Charm Textile, gives the employer the right to manage and direct the enterprise where that management is reasonable and lawful. The change of target followed the replacement of manual by computerised machines, and the panel found no worker received less than the US$ 50 minimum wage, so the employer did not violate the Labour Law and the demand was rejected. [Para 93] [Para 94] [Para 96] [Para 97]

Issue 3: under Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 22 of Decree 38 on Contracts, only the parties to a labour contract may terminate it, so recruiting and dismissing is the employer's right. Workers may demand a transfer or dismissal only with evidence that the person is dangerous to workplace safety (Awards 04/03 Lida, 14/04 Chu Sing, 17/03 and 18/03 Hua Hing, 15/04 Lucky Zone, 16/04 Yada Printing, 32/04 Ecent); the worker party proved only dissatisfaction with Mr S's management style and personal attitude, and the demand was rejected. [Para 104] [Para 105] [Para 107]

Issue 4: Clause 6 of Prakas 286 dated 5 November 2001 grants two hours per week for union tasks to each worker delegate, not to a union, and the demand exceeds what the law provides, making it an interests demand. The panel generally declines interests disputes brought by a union without most-representative status, which is the standing that permits collective bargaining under Article 96(2B) and access to the Council under Article 277; KYTU lacked that status at Grace Sun, so the panel declined to consider the demand. [Para 112] [Para 113] [Para 116] [Para 118]

Issue 5: Clause 5 of Notification 017 dated 18 July 2000 sets the seniority bonus at US$ 5 per month for workers with more than four years' service and says nothing about service beyond five years, so a further US$ 1 is beyond the Notification and is an interests demand. KYTU again lacked most-representative status, and the panel declined to consider the demand, following Award 79/07. [Para 121] [Para 123] [Para 125]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of the labour contract attributed to falling productivity is unlawful unless it rests on an Article 71 ground and, where economic difficulty is relied on, is placed under the control of the Labour Inspector; workers whose contracts are suspended outside Article 71 are owed full wages for the whole period, not the 50 percent the employer chose to pay. [Para 88] [Para 90] [Para 92]

**Obiter dicta:** That the employer's managerial right under Article 2 is conditioned on the change being reasonable and on every worker remaining at or above the minimum wage; and the panel's weighing of the conflicting witness accounts on the shop-floor language used towards a worker. [Para 94] [Para 96] [Para 105]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Heavy OCR corruption throughout ("themloyer", "neworkers", "wit", "bothe"), and the sentence at Para 81 breaks off at "was a" before the accusation against Mr S is completed. The union appears as both "KYFTU" and "KYTU", and a witness's surname is printed two ways, differing only in its final letter, at Para 14 and Para 67. Machine speed at Para 53 reads "between 1,800 and 11,850 rounds per minute", evidently a lost digit. The decision list numbers Issue 4 as "Issue 43" at Para 128, and the seniority figure is printed "USS 1" at Para 120. The case name in the header is truncated to "116/07 - Grace Sun" while the body names Grace Sun Cambodia Garment Co, Ltd; employer representatives are listed under a bare initial "Ms." at Para 2.
