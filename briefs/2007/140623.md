# Award No. 64/07 — Focus Footwear Company (Arbitration Council, 14 August 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry, Ven Pov, Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 140623

## 1. Facts
Focus Footwear Company has operated since 1997 and employs about 1,150 workers, of whom the Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU) claimed 809 as members [Para 19][Para 20]. The company has engaged every worker on six-month fixed-duration contracts since 1997, renewed repeatedly, some workers having served five to eight years [Para 21][Para 22]. Of nine demands, six were conciliated, one more was conciliated at the hearing, and the panel considered Issues 1 and 3 [Para 12][Para 14].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must convert labour contracts whose total length exceeds two years into contracts of undetermined duration? [Para 5]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the company must deduct 1,000 riels per month in union dues from the wages of members who consent? [Para 8]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU):** members on six-month contracts renewed past two years are entitled to undetermined-duration contracts under Article 76(2) of the Labour Law, and the company should also pay 5% severance each time a contract expires and is renewed [Para 20][Para 21]. The union asked the company to deduct 1,000 riels monthly from consenting members' wages [Para 29].

**Employer (respondent), Focus Footwear Company:** it practises six-month contracts and so "only follows the Labour Law" [Para 6]. On dues it said no free staff were available and its payroll system had been installed at great cost and could not easily be changed [Para 9][Para 36]. It paid 5% severance only when it terminated a contract, not when workers signed a new one [Para 25].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company ordered to sign undetermined-duration contracts in replacement of six-month fixed-duration contracts whose total length exceeds two years; the union held to have no right, while its registration is pending, to demand deduction of 1,000 riels union dues [Para 79].

On Issue 1 the panel read Article 76(2) of the Labour Law with Article 67(2) and Article 73(5), and held that "renewal" in Article 67(2) means the cumulative length of the first contract plus all renewals, so a contract renewed beyond two years becomes one of undetermined duration [Para 39][Para 42][Para 45]. It supported that reading with ILO Guideline No. 166 of 1982 and the French original of the Labour Law, which states the two-year ceiling as a total [Para 46][Para 49][Para 51]. It followed Awards 10/03 Jacqsintex and 36/06 Mondotex [Para 43].

The workers' further demand for 5% severance on each renewal was not considered: Article 312(1) confines the panel to the non-conciliation report or matters arising directly from the dispute, and this demand appeared neither there nor as a direct consequence of Issue 1 [Para 56][Para 57].

On Issue 3 the panel reconciled Article 129(2), which lets a worker authorise deductions for union dues in writing, with Article 281, which forbids employers to deduct and pay dues, holding Article 281 exists to prevent employer interference under Article 280 and does not bar deduction on written request, following Awards 05/03 Top One and 62/04 Ecent [Para 60][Para 61][Para 64]. Under Clause 5(5) of Prakas No. 305 of 2001 the employer is then obliged to deduct on 15 days' written notice [Para 66]. But a union must show representativeness under Article 277 and registration under Article 268, and here the union received its registration receipt only on 19 July 2007, five days before the hearing, so the two-month period had not run and its status was unresolved [Para 68][Para 74][Para 76]. The name list filed also bore no worker signatures [Para 77].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive renewals of a fixed-duration contract are aggregated, so that once the first contract and all renewals together exceed two years the contract is one of undetermined duration and the employer must replace it accordingly [Para 52][Para 53]. An employer may lawfully deduct union dues from wages where the worker has authorised it in writing 15 days in advance, Article 281 being aimed only at employer interference, but the demand fails while the union's registration is still within the two-month window of Article 268 and its member list lacks signatures [Para 64][Para 76][Para 77].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the Labour Law tends towards undetermined-duration contracts because long-term jobs secure workers and encourage commitment [Para 44].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
An unofficial English translation with heavy OCR loss of the leading "t" and "th" throughout ("themployer", "thearing", "withe"), and duplicated phrases where the scanner repeated a line [Para 15][Para 56]. The company's Internal Work Rules are dated 19 November 2007, three months after the award itself [Para 16]. The prior awards are cited with garbled and inconsistent names and numbers, one award appearing as both 99/06 and 99/04 AIA in the same sentence [Para 63][Para 73]. The union president's name is spelled three different ways across the findings and the notification, and the administrative manager's surname appears in two forms [Para 16][Para 31].
