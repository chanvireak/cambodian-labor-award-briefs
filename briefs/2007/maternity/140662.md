# Award No. 130/07 — Win Shingtex (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 21 December 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, An Nan, Sok Mathoeung) · Doc ID 140662

## 1. Facts
C.CAWDU and the Cambodian Apparel Workers Democratic Union (CAWDU) filed a collective dispute against Win Shingtex (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. ("Win Shingtex Company") on 1 October 2007, demanding improved working conditions for a workforce of 1,100, ninety percent of them women. [Para 15][Para 23]
Five demands went to conciliation, two were settled at the Department of Labour Disputes, and of the three remitted to the panel the parties settled issues 1 and 3 at the hearing, leaving only issue 2 on maternity pay. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 19][Para 20]
The disputed act was the employer's practice of paying half of two months' wages before maternity leave and the balance on return, rather than the three months' wages in advance the union demanded. [Para 11][Para 28]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay female workers three months' wages and perquisites in advance of maternity leave, as demanded, rather than under the practice set by point 4 of the Agreement dated 11 September 2006? [Para 10][Para 23]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** C.CAWDU and CAWDU argued that the company had not complied with point 4 of the Agreement dated 11 September 2006, and that women should receive three months' wages before taking maternity leave. [Para 23][Para 28]
**Employer (respondent):** Win Shingtex asked to keep the current practice — 50 percent of two months' wages paid in advance and another 50 percent of one month's wages when the worker returned — and sought one month to consult its director. [Para 10][Para 11][Para 39]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay 90 days' wages and perquisites to female workers before their maternity leave begins, where the worker is entitled to that payment. [Para 43]
Article 183 of the Labour Law entitles a woman with one year of uninterrupted service to half her wage and perquisites during maternity leave, but the panel found it gives no guideline on the time of payment. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 33]
The panel read Article 115(3), which forbids payment on a day off and moves payday one day earlier, as meaning the wage cannot be paid during the leave period and must be paid before it begins. [Para 33][Para 34]
It followed its earlier rulings in Arbitral Award 57/06 (Evergreen, Issue 6) and Arbitral Award 97/06 (New Max, Issue 1), both of which required pre-leave payment. [Para 35][Para 37]
Point 4 of the 11 September 2006 Agreement gave 50 percent of two months' wages before leave — less than half of three months — and paid workers from Phnom Penh and Kandal monthly while provincial workers were paid in advance, so it fell below the statutory floor and separated workers by geography. [Para 38][Para 39]
Because Article 13 makes Labour Law provisions public order and voids contrary agreements, the panel declared that provision null and void and fixed the entitlement at 90 days. [Para 40][Para 42][Para 43]
The panel recorded that the parties agreed three or four workers who took leave in November 2007 were paid 50 percent of wages monthly, and that no dispute over calculation of wages remained. [Para 26]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A collective agreement term that pays maternity benefits below the Article 183 standard, or varies them by the worker's province, is void under Article 13 and cannot be applied in place of the statute. [Para 39][Para 42]
Maternity wages and perquisites must be paid before the leave begins, because Article 115(3) bars payment on a day off and the leave period is itself the worker's entitlement. [Para 34][Para 35]

**Obiter dicta:** Pre-leave payment also relieves the burden on workers who take maternity leave in remote provinces. [Para 36]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The document is an unofficial English translation of the authoritative Khmer original, and the translator repeatedly dropped the "th" digraph ("themployer", "thearing", "withemployer"), which I read through rather than silently corrected. [Para 9]
The employer's registered name is split across the head of the award and a stray "Ltd." line, printed with mangled punctuation ("Co,."), so the company is named here as the award later uses it. [Para 1][Para 2]
Paragraph 38 restates the Agreement text of paragraph 23 with words out of sequence, so the two descriptions of point 4 were reconciled against the cleaner paragraph 23. [Para 23][Para 38]
