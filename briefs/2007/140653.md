# Award No. 114/07 — Union Paper Corporation (Arbitration Council, 27 November 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Liv Sovanna, Ang Eng Tong) · Doc ID 140653

## 1. Facts
Union Paper Corporation operates at Prek Takong Village, Sangkat Chak Angre Leu, Khan Meanchey, Phnom Penh with 120 workers and four unions, none of which holds most representative status; the claimant is the Khmer Youth Trade Union (KYTU) at Union Paper Corporation. [Para 1][Para 3][Para 17]
Since 2003 the company had issued one-year fixed-duration contracts; from September 2007 it offered six-month contracts on renewal, 40 workers had held one-year contracts, 30 of those remained on them, and five had signed six-month contracts, the demand covering 35 workers. [Para 17][Para 18][Para 19][Para 20]
The union complained on 20 October 2007, three of six issues were conciliated, non-conciliation report No. 1152 of 30 October 2007 followed, and one more issue was settled at the hearing of 13 November 2007, leaving Issues 1 and 3. [Para 11][Para 12][Para 13][Para 15][Para 16]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must maintain one-year fixed-duration labour contracts in accordance with its earlier practice? [Para 17]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the company must maintain the US$ 10 monthly bonus in the new labour contracts? [Para 24]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — Khmer Youth Trade Union (KYTU) at Union Paper Corporation:** the shift from one-year to six-month contracts cuts the notice period, affects wages and was used not to renew the factory union president's contract; and the US$ 10 bonus, which casual workers receive only as US$ 5, must be carried into new contracts under Article 390 of the Labour Law. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 25][Para 27]
**Employer (respondent) — Union Paper Corporation:** the change rests on Article 73 of the Labour Law, and on each expiry the company paid the compensation the law requires before signing a new contract (which the union accepted); and the bonus will continue only for workers whose existing contracts grant it, since the internal rules contain no such entitlement. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 27][Para 43]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider the demand that the company maintain one-year fixed-duration contracts and declined to consider the demand that the US$ 10 bonus be carried into new contracts; the award was non-binding and would become final eight days after notification absent written opposition. [Para 41][Para 51]
Issue 1: Article 65 of the Labour Law makes the labour contract subject to common law, Decree No. 38 of 28 October 1988 (Articles 1 and 22) requires the consent of both parties to any amendment, and Award 56/06 – Boric holds that neither party may force contract terms on the other; a one-year or a six-month term is therefore lawful, and the demand could succeed only by agreement. [Para 29][Para 31][Para 32][Para 36][Para 40]
Issue 3: the internal work rules of Union Paper Corporation contain no bonus provision and no agreement or collective agreement provides one; Article 390 sits in the transitional provisions preserving benefits under the 1992 Labour Law, so it does not apply to these contracts. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45] The demand was an interest dispute, and because KYTU held no most representative status under Article 277 of the Labour Law and Clause 6 of Prakas 305 of 22 November 2001, the panel followed Awards 84/07 – Yung Hwa II (Issue 1), 108/07 – 8 Star Sportswear (Issue 3), 81/04 – Evergreen (Issue 4) and 09/05 – Kin Tay (Issue 2) and declined to consider it. [Para 47][Para 49][Para 50][Para 51]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The length of a fixed-duration contract is a matter of agreement: a change from one year to six months breaches no law where the employer pays the statutory compensation at expiry and the worker signs voluntarily, so a panel will not restore the longer term. [Para 40][Para 41]
A demand to write an existing bonus into future contracts is an interest demand, and a union without most representative status — at least 50 per cent of the factory's workers and registration with the Ministry of Labour — has no standing to bring it. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51]
Article 390 of the Labour Law protects only benefits held under contracts governed by the 1992 Law, and cannot be invoked to preserve a benefit absent from the internal rules or any agreement. [Para 44][Para 45]

**Obiter dicta:** none identified; the panel added no observation beyond the two demands. [Para 46]
