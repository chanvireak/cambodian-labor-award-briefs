# Award No. ៥៨/០៧ (58/07) — ៨ ស្តារ ស្ពត វែរ អិលធីឌី (8 Star Sport Wear Ltd) (Arbitration Council, 19 July 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៉ីង សុទ្ធី / Ing Sothy, លីវ សុវណ្ណា / Liv Sovanna, គង់ ផល្លៈ / Kong Phallack) · Doc ID 140622

## 1. Facts
រោងចក្រ ៨ ស្តារ ស្ពត វែរ (8 Star Sport Wear Ltd) operates in Sangkat Mean Chey, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh with about 900 workers [Para 17]. Between December 2006 and May 2007 the factory had no work for its workers on about four occasions, each lasting one to three days, and paid 50% wages on those days without placing any suspension under labour inspector supervision [Para 22][Para 24][Para 25]. The union at the factory, សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ ប្រចាំក្រុមហ៊ុន ៨ ស្តារ ស្ពត វែរ (Khmer Youth Union at 8 Star Sport Wear), filed on behalf of 277 workers [Para 19]. Of two non-conciliated issues, the interpreter issue was settled at the hearing and the panel considered only Issue 1 [Para 15][Para 16].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay 100% wages whenever it has no work for the workers to do? [Para 2]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must provide an interpreter for every work group? [Para 4]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ ប្រចាំក្រុមហ៊ុន ៨ ស្តារ ស្ពត វែរ (Khmer Youth Union at 8 Star Sport Wear):** failure to give work is the employer's own serious fault under Article 83 of the Labour Law, so 100% wages are owed; the union expressly disclaimed any back pay and sought the rate only for the future [Para 26][Para 27].

**Employer (respondent), ៨ ស្តារ ស្ពត វែរ អិលធីឌី (8 Star Sport Wear Ltd):** 50% was paid because the workers did not work; the stoppages came from late delivery of materials and a shortage of buyers' orders, not from any refusal to provide work [Para 28][Para 29]. Neither the internal rules nor the contracts say anything about suspension [Para 30].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand for 100% wages in the event of future work stoppages was rejected and declined for consideration, but the company was ordered immediately to stop any suspension of labour contracts that does not comply with the Labour Law [Para 58][Para 59].

On Issue 1 the panel set out Article 71(11) of the Labour Law, which allows suspension only on serious economic or material difficulty, for no more than two months and under labour inspector supervision [Para 31][Para 32]. Of those two conditions, supervision was not met while the two-month limit was, the stoppages having lasted two to three days [Para 37][Para 38]. It therefore found the past suspensions unlawful, following Awards 62/04 Eisin and 92/04 SHP, which ordered 100% wages where a suspension escaped inspection [Para 41][Para 42].

The panel then distinguished the claim before it: the workers sought the 100% rate only for future stoppages and had waived the past [Para 44]. Treating that as a demand about a situation not yet arisen, it declined to decide it, following Award 10/03 Jacqsintex and Awards 68/04 and 36/06 Mondotich [Para 48][Para 52].

Having found the employer had not followed lawful procedure in previous suspensions, the panel invoked Article 34 of Prakas 099 of 21 April 2004, which empowers it to order an immediate stop to unlawful conduct, and made that order [Para 54][Para 55][Para 59].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of labour contracts is lawful only when it is placed under labour inspector supervision and does not exceed two months, and a suspension that escapes supervision has in past awards attracted an order for 100% wages [Para 35][Para 41][Para 43]. Where the union asks only that 100% wages be paid for stoppages that have not yet occurred, the panel will not decide the question, because no one can foresee when or for whom a future suspension will arise — yet it will still order the employer to stop immediately any suspension not in conformity with the Labour Law [Para 48][Para 50][Para 59].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the 100% wage orders in its earlier cases concerned arrears, and that the union here had abandoned any claim for the past [Para 44].
