# Award No. 03/07 — Dynasty Knitting (ឌី យូណាយធីត នីត ធីង) (Arbitration Council, 13 February 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — employer-appointed កៅ ថា ចង់, worker-appointed អាន វីរៈ, president គង់ ផល្លៈ · Doc ID 140583

## 1. Facts
The Free Workers' Union of Cambodia (សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ នៃ ព្រះរាជាណាចក្រ កម្ពុជា), the only union at the Dynasty knitting factory (ឌី យូណាយធីត នីត ធីង) in Sihanoukville, brought a collective dispute in which three of eight demands remained unsettled [Para 1][Para 16]. The weavers had been working style 12000B since late November 2006 at lot rates of USD 8 for the large size, USD 7.50 for medium and USD 7 for small [Para 2][Para 18][Para 19]. They said an average worker needed eight days plus twelve hours of overtime to finish one 48-panel lot, and the company did not contest that [Para 23][Para 24][Para 25]. Conciliation settled five points; the remaining three came to the Council on 19 January 2007 and were heard on 30 January 2007 [Para 11][Para 12].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must raise the lot rates for style 12000B — large from USD 8 to USD 8.50, medium from USD 7.50 to USD 8, small from USD 7 to USD 7.50? [Para 2][Para 19]
2. (Issue 2) Whether a worker with a proper medical certificate is entitled to sick leave of one to six months according to the illness? [Para 36]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must publish the lot price before weaving on a style begins? [Para 43]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant union):** The lot rates leave an average weaver with about USD 1 a day even before the twelve hours of overtime she must work, so they asked for the three rate increases; they also said the company grants only one week of sick leave even on a doctor's certificate for two weeks, forcing them to use annual leave, and that two skilled workers in the sample room make a sample garment without the company ever fixing a lot price, with prices coming only after ten days of full production [Para 20][Para 22][Para 38][Para 43][Para 45][Para 48].

**Employer (respondent):** The company's representative said the president had told him the rates could not be raised and offered no reasons, and the company's written submission after the hearing likewise gave no method for setting lot rates, proposing only that prices be issued on day 6, 8 or 10 depending on how long a style runs [Para 28][Para 29][Para 49][Para 50][Para 52][Para 53].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to top up base wages to the statutory minimum of USD 1.73 a day for any worker who wove style 12000B between late November and the end of December 2006 and was paid less, to grant sick leave of up to six months on a medical certificate, and to notify lot prices within three to seven days after the trial of each style [Para 96][Para 97][Para 98].

On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 108 of the Labour Law, which requires piece rates to yield at least the guaranteed minimum for a worker of average skill in ordinary hours, and Article 137 capping full working time at eight hours a day and forty-eight a week [Para 56][Para 57]. Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 set the garment minimum at USD 45 a month, or USD 1.73 a day, through the end of 2006 [Para 58]. Following Awards No. 03/05, No. 44/06 and No. 30/06, it held that Article 108 is the basis for testing a lot price [Para 59][Para 60][Para 62]. Because evidence arrived after the 9 February 2007 deadline, the panel refused to consider it and decided on what was before it [Para 55].

On the arithmetic, the panel divided the 26 working days in a month by the 8 days an average worker needs per lot, giving 3.25 lots, and multiplied by USD 8 for a monthly base of USD 26, or USD 1 a day, and it expressly excluded the 12 overtime hours from the calculation [Para 69][Para 70]. That fell below the USD 45 minimum for ordinary hours, so the panel ordered a top-up to USD 1.73 a day rather than fixing a new lot price itself, for workers on style 12000B from late November to the end of December 2006 [Para 71][Para 72][Para 96].

On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 71(3) of the Labour Law, under which a contract is suspended during medically certified absence for up to six months, extendable until a replacement is found, together with Article 5 of the company's internal rules of 29 April 2003 [Para 41][Para 42][Para 73][Para 74]. It held that a worker with a proper certificate may take sick leave of up to six months without losing her job [Para 79][Para 97].

On Issue 3 the panel applied Article 112 of the Labour Law, which requires the employer to inform workers clearly of the wage conditions applicable to them, and followed Award No. 62/04 for the proposition that the article does not fix a number of days but does impose a duty to notify [Para 80][Para 82][Para 84]. Evaluating technical difficulty on a sample cannot take more than three days, and the employer's own representative in a prior case accepted three to seven days [Para 86][Para 88]. It rejected the company's day 6, 8 and 10 schedule as unexplained and set three to seven days after the trial of each style [Para 92][Para 93][Para 98].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a piece rate yields less than the statutory minimum wage for a worker of average skill in ordinary hours, the panel orders the employer to top up base wages to the guaranteed minimum rather than fixing a new lot price itself, and the employer must notify the price per lot within three to seven days after the trial of each new style, a longer unexplained timetable being unreasonable under Article 112 [Para 71][Para 72][Para 90][Para 98].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel recorded that overtime hours are not counted when testing whether a lot price satisfies Article 108, so a rate that only reaches the minimum with twelve hours of overtime is unlawful [Para 68][Para 70].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company name is badly garbled in the header and appears in several different spellings through the award, some of them half-Latin [Para 1][Para 15][Para 31]. The hearing is dated 30 January 2006 in an award issued on 13 February 2007, and every document in the file belongs to 2006–2007 [Para 1][Para 8]. The internal rules' annual sick-leave cap is printed as 30 days in one recital and 32 days in another [Para 42][Para 76]. The minimum wage is written once as USD 1.73 a day and once as a four-digit figure that would be ten times as much [Para 72][Para 96]. One monthly payroll list is dated 2022, evidently an OCR substitution [Para 32]. The signature block is heavily corrupted and the signed names cannot be read [Para 100].
