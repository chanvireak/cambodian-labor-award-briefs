# Award No. 63/07 — Phnom Penh Garment City (Arbitration Council, 25 July 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry, Ven Pov, Kong Phallack) · Doc ID 140624

## 1. Facts
Phnom Penh Garment City, at National Road No. 2, Khan Meanchey, Phnom Penh, employs about 857 workers and is claimed by three unions: the Free Trade Union of Workers (FTUW) at the factory, the Labour Free Union of Lever Khmer (LFULK) and the Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU) [Para 25]. Workers struck for four days, 5, 6, 7 and 9 July 2007, after the employer accused 11 workers of stealing scissors and barred talking during working hours [Para 27]. A new investor took over management in November 2006 and progressively reversed old practices, announcing on 5 July 2007 that wages and attendance bonuses would be deducted for strike days [Para 30][Para 33]. One issue remained non-conciliated [Para 18].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must maintain wages and attendance bonuses for the four strike days of 5, 6, 7 and 9 July 2007, as it had always done before? [Para 15]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimants), the Free Trade Union of Workers (FTUW) at Phnom Penh Garment Factory, the Labour Free Union of Lever Khmer (LFULK) and the Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU):** the company never deducted wages or bonuses on strike days from 1996 until the change of management, so the practice had hardened into an oral term of employment binding the employer [Para 15][Para 43].

**Employer (respondent), Phnom Penh Garment City:** it applies the Labour Law [Para 16]. It had paid on strike days out of generosity, not obligation, and in any event gave notice of the new rule at a meeting on 30 June 2007 and by notification [Para 33][Para 45].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the demand that the company maintain wages and attendance bonus for the strike days of 5, 6, 7 and 9 July 2007 [Para 78].

On wages, Article 332 of the Labour Law suspends the labour contract during a strike so that the salary is not paid, and the panel followed Award 04/03 Lida in rejecting the claim [Para 38][Para 39]. Article 334 makes an employer liable for strikers' salaries only where it hires replacements, and none were hired [Para 41][Para 42].

On the practice argument, the panel accepted that the company had paid on strike days since 1996 but found no written principle or agreement; the payments were an act of generosity, unsupported by concrete evidence of an oral contract, and not clear enough to become a contractual clause for old or new workers [Para 44][Para 47][Para 51]. It also rejected the custom argument, since the practice was not followed in every factory in Cambodia and was not believed to be an act of abiding by the law, so neither the material nor the psychological element of custom was made out [Para 57][Para 62][Para 63].

On the attendance bonus, Clause 3 of Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 grants a minimum bonus of USD 5 to workers who come to work regularly, read as 26 days a month or fewer with authorised days off [Para 66][Para 68]. Because the strike was staged without the prior notice and exhaustion of peaceful means required by Articles 323, 324 and 320(4), the strikers did not come to work regularly and lost the bonus, following Awards 04/03 Lida and 15/04 Lucky Zone [Para 69][Para 74][Para 77].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A strike suspends the labour contract under Article 332, so strikers earn no wage unless the employer hires replacements contrary to Article 334; a long-standing practice of paying on strike days does not become an oral contract without evidence that the parties treated it as binding [Para 38][Para 42][Para 52]. A practice becomes a custom only where every enterprise follows it and it is observed as though it were law, and a factory's own habit meets neither test [Para 57][Para 63]. Strikers who strike without the notice required by Article 324 have not come to work regularly and forfeit the attendance bonus under Notification No. 017 [Para 75][Para 77].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that senior workers who had served since 1996 might expect payment on strike days, but should have known the employer could refuse at any time [Para 48][Para 49].
