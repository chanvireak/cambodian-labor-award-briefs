# Award No. 106/07 — M & V International Manufacturing Limited (Company III) (Arbitration Council, 12 November 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry, An Nan, Kong Phallack) · Doc ID 140647

## 1. Facts
M & V International Manufacturing Limited (Company III) runs a factory on National Road No. 2, Sangkat Chak Angre Krom, Khan Meanchey, Phnom Penh, employing 2,876 workers in four unions, of which the Cambodian Industrial Union Federation (CIUF) and the Cambodia Federation of Independent Workers Trade Union (CFIWTU) are the claimants. [Para 26][Para 27]
On 18 September 2007 the workers complained and demanded improvement of 14 working conditions; conciliation settled seven of them, and the remainder reached the Arbitration Council in non-conciliation report No. 1094 of 4 October 2007. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 26]
At the hearing Issue 5 was conciliated and the worker party withdrew Issue 6, leaving Issues 1, 2, 3, 4 and 7 for decision. [Para 24][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must sign one-year fixed-duration labour contracts with its workers? [Para 28]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the US$ 5 attendance bonus must be deducted in proportion to approved leave by dividing US$ 5 by 26 days? [Para 30]
3. (Issue 3) Whether wages in the lamp-checking section must be converted from a monthly wage to a piece-rate wage? [Para 35]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must fix a date for increasing the wages of mobile workers and of workers in the output, cleaning and checking sections? [Para 41]
5. (Issue 7) Whether the piece-rate target must be maintained at not lower than US$ 2.20 per day? [Para 48]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — CIUF and CFIWTU:** three-month contracts create job insecurity and cost workers severance pay and maternity leave; authorised leave for personal commitments does not consume annual leave, so the bonus should be pro-rated; the lamp-checking section was paid by piece rate from 1999 to 2006; and the US$ 2.20 rate was actually paid in March and April 2007. [Para 29][Para 31][Para 32][Para 36][Para 48]
**Employer (respondent) — M & V International Manufacturing Limited:** it complied with the Labour Law on contract duration, about 20 per cent of workers holding three-month contracts; Notification 017 and Letter 317 of 22 May 2007 require deduction of the entire US$ 5 for approved absence; and it could not afford increases for the mobile, cleaning and checking sections, though group leaders and machine repairmen received US$ 5. [Para 29][Para 33][Para 44][Para 45]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to deduct the US$ 5 attendance bonus in proportion to days of approved leave (US$ 5 divided by 26 days for each day); the demands for one-year contracts, conversion of the lamp-checking wage, a date for wage increases and a US$ 2.20 piece rate were rejected. [Para 108]
Issue 1: on Article 65 of the Labour Law, Article 22 of Decree 38 on Contract and Outside-Contract Responsibility, and Award 56/06 – Boric, the panel held that no party may force contract terms on the other; workers could not compel one-year contracts and the company could not impose three- or six-month ones, and the contracts actually signed bind until expiry. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 61][Para 71][Para 74]
Issue 2: the attendance bonus is part of wage under Article 103, and absence authorised by the employer suspends the contract under Articles 71(6) and 72(1), so no bonus is owed for absent days but it is owed pro rata for days worked; Notification 017 of 18 July 2000 and Point 3 of Notification 745 of 23 October 2006 do not fix the qualifying days, as held in Award 48/05 – Manhattan. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 81][Para 88][Para 89]
Issue 3: under Article 2(2) and Awards 62/06 – Quicksew, 108/06 – Trinunggal Komara and 39/07 – San, the employer may choose between piece rate and minimum wage where the change has no significant negative impact; the workers filed no evidence of the monthly wages or benefits lost. [Para 90][Para 92][Para 94][Para 96]
Issue 4: the June–July 2007 discussions produced no agreement, and no law obliges the employer to set a date for a wage increase. [Para 101]
Issue 7: Article 108 requires piecework to yield at least the minimum wage; the US$ 50 monthly minimum equals US$ 1.92 per day under Notification 745/06, and the evidence showed ordinary workers earn at least that on the new rate, so US$ 2.20 could not be imposed. [Para 102][Para 104][Para 105][Para 107]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A refusal to agree to a particular contract duration breaches no law, and a contract signed without coercion binds both parties for its term, so the panel will not rewrite it at one party's demand. [Para 69][Para 71]
Because the attendance bonus is a component of wage under Article 103, an employer must pay it in proportion to days actually worked instead of forfeiting the whole sum when leave is approved. [Para 85][Para 88]
An employer may change the wage system, including from piece rate to a monthly minimum wage, provided the change has no significant negative impact on wages and benefits for the same work, and the party asserting that impact must prove it. [Para 94][Para 96]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that rejecting the one-year contract demand does not remove the workers' right to raise that demand again. [Para 75]
