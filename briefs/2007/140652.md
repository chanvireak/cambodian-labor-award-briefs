# Award No. 111/07 — Yong Wa II (យ៉ុង វ៉ា ទី២) (Arbitration Council, 12 November 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Tuon Siphann, Kong Phallack) · Doc ID 140652

## 1. Facts
The employer is ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉ុង វាំង វាំ ២ (Yong Wa II) at Thmey Village, Ta Khmao, Kandal, employing about 5,500 workers in a factory with four unions; the claimant is សម្ព័ន្ឋ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា. [Para 1][Para 100][Para 101][Para 102]
The union complained on 30 May 2007; conciliation on 26 September 2007 settled 8 of 13 demands, non-conciliation report No. 204/07 was filed on 17 October 2007, and the hearing was held on 26 October 2007. [Para 13][Para 16][Para 18][Para 15]
At the hearing Issues 2 and 4 were settled, leaving Issues 1, 3 and 5 for decision. [Para 22][Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must reimburse workers 10,100 riels in health-check fees paid between 1998 and 2004? [Para 104]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the company must let union leaders and activists enter every factory building outside working hours to recruit members? [Para 111]
3. (Issue 5) Whether the company must reinstate the four sanitation-section workers dismissed on 25 May 2007 for stealing scrap fabric? [Para 124][Para 158]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — សម្ព័ន្ឋ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា:** the health check was paid by the workers though it is the employer's obligation; entry to all buildings is an exercise of union freedom and would let the union recruit members; and the four women did not steal the scrap, the two workers who resigned having shifted blame onto them. [Para 104][Para 114][Para 161]
**Employer (respondent) — ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉ុង វាំង វាំ ២:** it already complied with Award 54/07 – Yong Wa I by repaying health checks from 11 August 2004; building 4 is a laundry with separate management and tight security, so cross-building access is refused on security and production grounds; and the four were dismissed for serious misconduct under Article 83 after 69 bags of scrap were found. [Para 105][Para 106][Para 115][Para 117][Para 130][Para 159]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to reinstate the four sanitation workers from the date the award takes effect and to pay their wages for the whole suspension period until reinstatement; the health-check reimbursement and the building-access demands were rejected. [Para 225][Para 226][Para 227][Para 228]
Issue 1: Article 247 of the Labour Law lets the Ministry fix which health checks the employer must bear; following Awards 30/06 – Bes Tan (Issue 3), 44/06 – Gold Fame (Issue 4) and 45/07 – Yong Wa, the panel held the employer owes the pre-employment check only, and Article 120 bars wage claims after three years, running from the contract date, so claims for 1998–2004 were time-barred. [Para 171][Para 172][Para 179][Para 182][Para 184] The company's res judicata objection failed because Award 54/07 – Yong Wa I was brought by សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ against Yong Wa I, a different party. [Para 166][Para 167][Para 169]
Issue 3: under Article 2 and Article 24 of the Labour Law, and Awards 82/06 – IM&V 3 (Issue 7) and 108/06 – Trinunggal Komara, the employer may organise access to its premises as a management right exercised reasonably; Circular No. 40 of 1 October 1998 requires only a suitable place for union notices, not entry to every building. [Para 186][Para 189][Para 195][Para 196]
Issue 5: Article 83(k)(1) makes theft serious misconduct, but the employer bears the burden of proving it, per Awards 41/04 – Mikasa (Issue 2) and 59/05 – Tak Fat (Issue 3); the security officer never saw who hid the scrap, the inquiry minutes were not signed by the union observers, no proper expert investigation was made, and the two workers who admitted the act had already resigned, so Article 83(k)(1) could not be applied. [Para 202][Para 205][Para 207][Para 210][Para 223]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer dismisses for theft, it must prove the act with evidence that identifies the worker, not merely with a security report recording that the goods were found; an inquiry record unsigned by the attending union representatives and never put to the worker does not discharge that burden, and reinstatement with back wages follows. [Para 205][Para 207][Para 211]
A claim to health-check fees is a wage-related debt subject to the three-year limitation in Article 120, counted from the date of the labour contract, so earlier years cannot be recovered even after a prior award orders repayment from a later date. [Para 182][Para 184]
The employer's right to organise its premises includes refusing cross-building access to union organisers; the duty under Circular No. 40 is to provide a suitable place for union information, not unrestricted entry. [Para 195][Para 196]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the union claiming most representative status held about 3,000 members but had not yet applied, so the point did not need decision in this case. [Para 103]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Dates are corrupted throughout: the award cites "២០១៩" for 2007 in the Yong Wa citations, inserts "២០១៥" inside the quoted text of Article 120, and prints the 24 May 2007 inquiry minutes as "ឆាំ២០០៧". [Para 175][Para 179][Para 215] Several paragraphs are duplicated almost verbatim (the findings on two of the four workers appear twice, and the security report is recited twice), which makes the reasoning longer than it is. [Para 147][Para 153][Para 207][Para 214] The employer's name is printed in at least three forms (យ៉ុង វាំង វាំ ២, យ៉ុង វ៉ា ទី២, យ៉ុង វាំង រាំ) and page markers such as "-១០-" and "-២០-" sit inside sentences. [Para 1][Para 91][Para 132][Para 230] Individual workers, the security officer and union officers are identified in this brief by role only.
