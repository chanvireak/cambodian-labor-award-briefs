# Award No. 148/07 — Pay Her Company (Arbitration Council, 11 February 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators: Hem H Naryth, Sin Kim Sean, Kong Phallack (Chair) · Doc ID 140670

## 1. Facts
Pay Her Company, at Krol Ko Village, Sangkat Kilometer 6, Khan Russei Keo, Phnom Penh, employed around 500 workers, of whom 100 belonged to the Coalition of Cambodian Apparel W.D.U. (C.CAWDU), registered on 22 August 2007 [Para 25][Para 28][Para 26]. The union notified the company of its establishment by letter No. 056/07 dated 19 June 2007, held its election on 23 June 2007 and notified the result on 25 June 2007 [Para 29][Para 30][Para 31]. The company dismissed eight workers — the president, the vice-president, the secretary and five adviser candidates — on 22 June and 26 June 2007, the day of and three days after the election [Para 39][Para 42]. The union filed twelve demands; it withdrew eleven at the hearing, including a US$50 monthly minimum wage, an 800 riel overtime meal allowance and a day-care centre, leaving only reinstatement of the eight [Para 12][Para 22]. The employer did not attend conciliation or either hearing [Para 16][Para 20].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the eight workers were dismissed on the basis of union discrimination? [Para 60]
2. (Issue 1, second question) Whether the employer complied with the law when dismissing the eight workers? [Para 60]

Only Issue 1 of the twelve non-conciliated issues remained for decision; the other eleven were withdrawn by the worker party [Para 22].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The eight were dismissed because they joined together to establish a union; all were candidates in the election and the elected leaders, the dismissals came days after the company was notified of the election, one worker was reinstated after resigning from the union, and the factory kept operating and hiring [Para 40][Para 41][Para 43].

**Employer (respondent):** The company's representatives were absent from the hearing without valid reason and submitted no evidence at all in answer to the allegations [Para 44].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to reinstate the eight union leaders and union advisers [Para 95]

The panel first held that it could hear the case in the employer's absence: Article 21 of Prakas 099 dated 21 April 2004 allows the panel to proceed, or to terminate by award, where a duly invited party fails to appear without good cause, as had been done in Awards 53/04 — Kung Hong and 63/04 — Shine Well [Para 44][Para 46][Para 49].

On discrimination, the panel applied Article 266(1), Article 12 and Article 279 of the Labour Law, which forbid an employer to take union membership or union activity into account in hiring, discipline or termination, and followed Awards 28/07 — De Fang and E Garment [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63]. Although the burden of proving discrimination rests on the worker party, as in Awards River Rich, 01/07 Evergreen and 112/06 Supreme, the union's documentary evidence and testimony stood unrebutted because the employer neither appeared nor filed evidence; the panel therefore found the dismissals were due to union discrimination, coming after notification of the election and its outcome [Para 63][Para 71].

On the legality of the dismissal, Article 293(1) of the Labour Law requires Labour Inspector authorisation before dismissing a shop steward or a candidate, and Clause 3(3) of Prakas 305 dated 22 November 2001 protects election candidates from 45 days before the election to 45 days after, while Clause 4(1) protects founders and members who join during the registration period until 30 days after registration [Para 72][Para 74][Para 75]. Following Award 07/06 — Grand Diamond City, the panel required three conditions — protected status, dismissal inside the protection period, and notification of candidacy to the employer — and found all three met for the three leaders and the five founders, the dismissals falling on 22 and 26 June 2007 [Para 75][Para 80][Para 84][Para 92]. No evidence showed the employer had obtained Labour Inspector approval, so the dismissals contravened Article 293 [Para 93][Para 94]. Under Article 34 of Prakas 099, the panel ordered reinstatement as the full remedy [Para 95].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker who is a founder of a union, or a candidate at its leadership election, who is dismissed inside the protection window of Clause 3(3) or Clause 4(1) of Prakas 305 dated 22 November 2001 without prior Labour Inspector authorisation under Article 293(1) of the Labour Law has been dismissed on grounds of union discrimination, and reinstatement follows [Para 72][Para 75][Para 93]. An employer who stays away from the hearing and files no evidence leaves the union's testimony and documents unrebutted, and the panel will decide on that record [Para 49][Para 71].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that a worker dismissed alongside the eight was later reinstated after resigning from the union, a fact it treated as part of the pattern of discrimination [Para 43].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer party's name and address are printed, but its representatives are recorded only as "Absent" [Para 1]. The union's full name is truncated by OCR to initials ("Coalition of Cambodian Apparel W. D. U"), so the expanded name cannot be verified from this text [Para 1]. The reasoning cites the same 07/06 award under two different names, "Grand Diamond City" and "Dai Young" [Para 75][Para 84], and repeats case numbers mid-sentence ("53/04 = Kung Hong", "28/07 = De Fang") [Para 46][Para 63]. The dismissal table in the facts is badly scrambled: columns, names and dates are interleaved and one dismissal date appears as 27/04/2007 for a worker listed as still employed [Para 42]. Much of the text loses the leading "t" of "the" ("themloyer", "theight"), and Article 12 and Article 279 are each printed twice in garbled form [Para 61].
