# Award No. 73/07 — Sun Shine Company (Arbitration Council, 22 August 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Liv Sovanna, Pen Bunchhea chairing) · Doc ID 140627

## 1. Facts
Sun Shine Company, a garment employer at Phsar Kombol Village, Kandal, faced a collective dispute brought by the Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU) and Khmer Youth Trade Union (KYTU) at Sun Shine Company [Para 1][Para 3]. Of nine demands, the Kandal labour office conciliated eight, leaving one: the workers demanded that they work seven hours on Saturday but be paid for eight hours as usual, which the employer refused [Para 11][Para 12][Para 15]. The non-conciliation report No. 133/07 was filed on 9 August 2007 and the hearing was set for 15 August 2007 [Para 15]. At that hearing the union objected to no arbitrator on conflict grounds but announced it would boycott the proceedings and, after the panel explained that it decides on law and the parties' evidence, the worker party walked out; the panel resumed without it [Para 18][Para 19][Para 21][Para 22]. The employer asked the panel to decide [Para 25].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether a worker party that attends the opening of the hearing and then leaves without showing good cause has failed to be represented, so that the panel may close the case by award without considering the disputed demand? [Para 30][Para 34]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — KYFTU and KYTU):** that the Arbitration Council had never protected the interests of workers or of their union in earlier cases, that it could decide as it wished in this case, and that they would therefore not take part in the hearing [Para 18][Para 20].
**Employer (respondent — Sun Shine Company):** that the demand for seven hours' Saturday work paid at eight hours would affect the factory's operation, its clients and other factory policy, and asked the panel to rule on it [Para 24][Para 25].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — Case 73/07 — Sun Shine is closed and none of the disputed issues is considered [Para 39].
The panel first established its power to proceed in the claimant's absence. Rule 4.7 of the Annex to Prakas No. 099 dated 21 April 2004 allows the panel to proceed or to terminate by award where a party fails to appear, and Award 115/06 — Archid held that a union leaving the hearing room before the process begins counts as such a failure [Para 30]. Clause 21 of the same Prakas was read, following Awards 16/07 Lotus and 27/07 M&V II, as requiring three cumulative conditions: due invitation, absence of representation, and no good cause [Para 30]. On condition one, the Secretariat's invitations No. 332 and No. 333 of 13 August 2007 proved due invitation [Para 28][Para 32]. On condition two, the panel defined "represented at the hearing" as present and attending until the end, through the four phases of introduction, procedure, optional conciliation and the final phase; the worker party took part only in the first phase, so condition two was not satisfied [Para 33][Para 34]. On condition three, the union gave no cause for the boycott, so that condition was met [Para 34][Para 35]. The panel concluded that it had authority to issue an award despite the boycott, noted that the union had been given a full opportunity to prove the claim and had forfeited its complaint by abandoning it, and closed the case without reaching the Saturday-hours demand [Para 35][Para 36][Para 38][Para 39].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Presence at the opening of a hearing is not representation: a party that is duly invited, appears only for the introductory phase and then withdraws without showing good cause is treated as absent, and the panel may then either proceed or terminate the proceedings by award, the claim being forfeited [Para 30][Para 33][Para 34][Para 38].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that a boycott suggests the union was unwilling to bring its dispute in good faith, and its general statement that a claimant must supply the reasons and evidence for its claim [Para 37][Para 38].
