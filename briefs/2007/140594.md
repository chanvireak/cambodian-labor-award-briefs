# Award No. 23/07 — រោងចក្រ យ៉ុង មិន ខេមបូឌា អេភី អិល (Yong Min Cambodia Apparel) (Arbitration Council, 21 March 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Tuon Siphan for the worker party, Kong Phalluh chair) · Doc ID 140594

## 1. Facts
Yong Min Cambodia Apparel (រោងចក្រ យ៉ុង មិន ខេមបូឌា អេភី អិល), Mole village, Dangkao, Phnom Penh, employs about 1,200 workers; the complaint was brought by the Cambodian Industrial Union Federation (សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព ឧស្សាហកម្ម កម្ពុជា) and its factory 9 union. [Para 1][Para 25][Para 26]
Eleven demands were conciliated on 07 February 2007; six were settled and five were referred to the panel, which heard them on 09 March 2007. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 16] The five concerned a USD 5 monthly rise for 69 team leaders and skilled workers, payment of treatment costs for accidents on the commute, permanent status and annual leave for five sanitation workers, annual periodic and special health checks, and the right to leave at 16:00 without permission when a worker does not want overtime. [Para 2][Para 4][Para 6][Para 8][Para 10]
At the hearing both sides confirmed the company had already raised wages by USD 5 for 65 of the 69, leaving four — three mechanics and one welder earning USD 80 to 140 a month — and that the company does not require periodic health checks unless the labour doctor orders them. [Para 28][Para 30][Para 31][Para 52][Para 56]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must raise the monthly wage by USD 5 for the four team leaders and skilled workers who did not receive the increase, when they already earn between USD 80 and USD 140 against a minimum wage of USD 50? [Para 27][Para 30][Para 83]
2. (Issue 2) Whether accidents suffered while travelling from home to the factory and back are work accidents for which the employer must pay treatment costs? [Para 32][Para 99]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the five sanitation workers must be reclassified as permanent workers and granted paid annual leave like other permanent staff? [Para 43][Para 113]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must send workers for periodic and special health checks once a year at its own cost? [Para 50][Para 125]
5. (Issue 5) Whether requiring three levels of written permission before a worker may leave at 16:00 instead of working overtime unlawfully compels overtime? [Para 60][Para 134]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព ឧស្សាហកម្ម កម្ពុជា at Yong Min:** the four remaining team leaders were entitled to the same USD 5 rise given to 65 others, by reference to Notification No. 745 of 23 October 2006; commuting injuries must be paid for because Article 248 and Prakas No. 09 of 19 January 1994 make them work accidents; the five sanitation workers do the same hours and days as permanent staff and should get annual leave. [Para 29][Para 36][Para 44][Para 49][Para 54]
**Employer (respondent) — រោងចក្រ យ៉ុង មិន ខេមបូឌា អេភី អិល:** the four earn the maximum wage already set for their posts and their skills and output differ; injuries outside the factory are not its responsibility; the sanitation workers are "special contract workers" without skills; health checks are only due when the labour doctor requires them; three signatures are needed because a missing worker stops the production line. [Para 33][Para 47][Para 51][Para 56][Para 70][Para 86][Para 93]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay the treatment costs of the four injured workers against the invoices they produce, to reclassify the five sanitation workers as permanent with annual leave, and to let workers go home without permission when they do not want overtime; it refused to consider the USD 5 wage demand and the annual health-check demand. [Para 139][Para 140][Para 141][Para 143]

On Issue 1 the panel held that the employer has the right to manage the enterprise and set wages, provided this is done lawfully and reasonably, citing Awards 06/06 (M V) and 108/06 (Triple N Gall Co Ma Ra). [Para 86][Para 87] Notification No. 745 sets a USD 50 minimum wage for permanent garment and footwear workers from 01 January 2007, and these four earn far more; their posts, skills and output differ from those already raised, so Article 106 does not require the rise. [Para 78][Para 80][Para 90][Para 93][Para 95][Para 96] Because the demand goes beyond what the law grants, it is an interest dispute and the panel declined to consider it. [Para 97][Para 98]
On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 248(2), which treats an accident on the direct journey between home and workplace, without detour for personal ends, as a work accident, and Article 4 of Prakas No. 243 of 10 September 2002 to the same effect. [Para 100][Para 101] The employer conceded the accidents happened on that journey, so Article 249 liability and Article 254 treatment apply and Articles 7 and 8 of that Prakas require support; the "outside the factory" objection was wrong. [Para 104][Para 105][Para 108][Para 111][Para 112]
On Issue 3 the panel found the five sanitation workers put in 8 hours a day and 26 days a month for more than two years, like permanent staff, so under Article 9 they are permanent workers and Article 166(1) gives them paid annual leave. [Para 114][Para 116][Para 119][Para 121][Para 124]
On Issue 4 the panel held that a demand for the labour doctor to come and set a health-check schedule is not a dispute between the employer and a number of its workers but a matter between the workers and the labour doctor, so it falls outside Article 302 and outside the panel's jurisdiction. [Para 126][Para 128][Para 129][Para 131][Para 132]
On Issue 5 the panel applied Article 4 of Prakas No. 80 of 01 March 1999, which makes overtime voluntary and forbids coercion or discipline against workers who decline it; the three-stage permission system compelled overtime and was contrary to law, so the employer must allow workers to leave freely. [Para 133][Para 137][Para 138]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A journey accident between home and workplace falls within Article 248 of the Labour Law and Article 4 of Prakas No. 243 of 10 September 2002, so the employer owes treatment costs even though the injury occurred off the premises; a wage demand exceeding the statutory minimum is an interest dispute the panel will not decide; and a permission regime that in practice prevents a worker from refusing overtime breaches the voluntariness rule in Article 4 of Prakas No. 80 of 01 March 1999. [Para 101][Para 97][Para 137]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's view that an employer's managerial right to set wages is lawful so long as it is exercised lawfully and reasonably, and its deference to the labour doctor's power to schedule health checks, were general statements rather than operative grounds. [Para 86][Para 129]

- The operative order repeats its first item twice, in slightly different wording, at [Para 139], so the numbered list runs 1, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5.
- The sanitation workers' list at [Para 44] carries OCR artefacts mid-line ("She", "S. N"), and the four wage claimants' list at [Para 31] mixes "one month" with "one month of December".
- The award orders payment for "four injured workers" [Para 110][Para 140] but the narrative describes one worker with a broken leg and three workers who hit dogs on their motorcycles [Para 40][Para 102]; the panel never lists the four or their amounts, and no total is stated.
- Headings and clauses are garbled where OCR merged text: "បញ្ហា ចំណុច ទី ៤" appears twice in a single line [Para 50], and the jurisdiction clause reads "ជំពូក ទី ១២ ផ្នែក ទី ពីរ ខែ)" [Para 12]. The workers' reliance on Article 247 and Prakas No. 09 [Para 54] is never answered, because Issue 4 was declined on jurisdiction.
