# Award No. 24/07 — អាយ លែន ធ្នូរី អ៊ីន ដាស ស្ទ្រៀល ខូ អិលធីឌី (I-Land Glory) (Arbitration Council, 3 April 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach for the employer party, Tuon Siphan for the worker party, Kong Chhala chair) · Doc ID 140593

## 1. Facts
I-Land Glory (អាយ លែន ធ្នូរី អ៊ីន ដាស ស្ទ្រៀល ខូ អិលធីឌី) runs a garment factory on Street Lom, Tuol Sangke, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, with about 1,150 workers; the Cambodian Labour Union (សហជីព ការងារ កម្មករ កម្ពុជា) at the factory has about 500 members. [Para 1][Para 16][Para 17]
Mr H (name redacted here) worked as chief cutter in the cutting section under a two-month probation contract from 20 December 2006 to 20 February 2007, and the company ended his employment on 10 February 2007 without notice. [Para 18][Para 29][Para 30] The union filed a two-point complaint on 15 February 2007; conciliation settled one point and left this one unresolved, the hearing was held on 22 March 2007, and some 200 workers supported the claim. [Para 7][Para 8][Para 12][Para 20][Para 51]
The union says he was dismissed for taking part in the strike of 06 and 07 February 2007; the company says he cut cloth wrongly, ignored internal rules and talked during working hours. [Para 22][Para 25] The panel never reached that question: it held the dispute outside its jurisdiction. [Para 56][Para 58]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the Arbitration Council has jurisdiction over this dispute, i.e. whether a demand to reinstate one worker, supported by a union and some 200 workers, is a collective labour dispute within Article 302 of the Labour Law? [Para 38][Para 42][Para 57]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — សហជីព ការងារ កម្មករ កម្ពុជា at I-Land Glory:** the dismissal was union discrimination, because Mr H was the only union activist dismissed after the 06 and 07 February 2007 strike, and he held the "sky-blue" card that marks a worker with full rights. [Para 22][Para 24][Para 33][Para 35]
**Employer (respondent) — អាយ លែន ធ្នូរី អ៊ីន ដាស ស្ទ្រៀល ខូ អិលធីឌី:** there was no discrimination — had it discriminated it would have dismissed every striker; the probation was not renewed because of wrong cutting, breach of internal rules and chatting, and the union's own signature on the 26 March 2007 letter confirms the card colours. [Para 23][Para 25][Para 37]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel refused to consider the workers' demand that the company take Mr H back into work, leaving the parties to the procedure of Article 301. [Para 58][Para 59]

The panel first noted that classifying a dispute as individual or collective is for the Labour Dispute Department of the Ministry, and that it normally follows that classification unless it has a clear reason to reconsider — citing Award 10/03 (Jacques T), 07/05 (Coca Cola), 41/04 (Mikasa) and 77/06 (PCS). [Para 39][Para 45] It then set out Article 302 of the Labour Law and the three cumulative conditions: (a) a dispute between a number of workers and one or more employers, (b) concerning working conditions, the exercise of rights granted to a professional organisation, recognition of such an organisation, or relations between employer and workers, and (c) capable of disrupting the operation of the enterprise or harming social peace. [Para 43][Para 44]
Conditions (a) and (b) were met, since the case was brought by Mr H with a union representing many members and concerns his dismissal and reinstatement. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48] Condition (c) was not: one worker out of about 1,150 is a very small number, the union's and 200 workers' support did not give them a direct interest, and no evidence showed the factory's operation was disrupted or social peace harmed. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51][Para 52][Para 53] Following Awards 77/06 (PCS) and 83/06 (Roo Sing), where the same condition failed, the panel found the dispute was not a collective one and it had no jurisdiction. [Para 45][Para 54][Para 57]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand to reinstate a single worker is not a collective labour dispute under Article 302 merely because a union and a few hundred of a factory's workers support it: the third condition requires evidence that the dispute can disrupt the operation of the enterprise or harm social peace, and a dispute affecting one worker among more than a thousand does not satisfy it, so the panel must decline jurisdiction and remit the worker to the individual-dispute procedure of Article 301. [Para 44][Para 49][Para 53][Para 58]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that it normally follows the Ministry's individual/collective classification unless a clear reason to reconsider appears is a general statement of practice, not the ground of this decision. [Para 39]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The chair is printed as គង់ ជល្លះ in the header [Para 1] and as គង់ ផល្លៈ in the signature block [Para 61]; the worker-party arbitrator named in the header has no name in the signature block [Para 61].
- The company's name is spelled inconsistently throughout — អាយ លែន ធ្នូរី ... ស្ទ្រៀល at [Para 1], អាយ លែន គ្លូរី at [Para 16] and អាយ លែន ក្លូរី at [Para 47] — which appears to be OCR variance rather than different entities.
- The dismissal letter is dated 10 February 2003 in the employer's evidence list [Para 13] but the dismissal is found to have occurred on 10 February 2007 [Para 18]; the letter date is plainly a typographical slip for 2007 and is reported as printed.
- The sentence citing Article 301 at [Para 58] carries a stray insertion ("ក្រុមប្រឹក្សា អាជ្ញា កណ្តាល ៤៦ កត់សំគាល់ ឃើញ ថា") and a broken bracket, so the procedural direction has to be read around the garble.
- The complaint is described as having two points [Para 7], with one settled [Para 8], yet the conciliation minute is also dated 08 February 2007 [Para 14] and 20 February 2007 [Para 15]; both dates are printed and neither is reconciled.
- Individual worker, union-official and employer-representative names appear in full in the party block and evidence lists; they are reduced to initials and roles in this brief.
