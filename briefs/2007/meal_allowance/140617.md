# Award No. 56/07 — Golden Dinen Crown Cambodia Co., Ltd. (ហ្គោល ដិន ក្រោន ខេមបូឌា ខូអិលធីឌី) (Arbitration Council, 25 July 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៉ីង សុទ្ធី appointed by the employer, អន ណន appointed by the workers, គង់ ផល្លៈ chair) · Doc ID 140617

## 1. Facts
Golden Dinen Crown Cambodia Co., Ltd. (ហ្គោល ដិន ក្រោន ខេមបូឌា ខូអិលធីឌី) runs a garment factory at Choam Chao, Dangkao, Phnom Penh with 800 workers. [Para 1][Para 21] The Democratic Union of Garment Workers at Golden Dinen (សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ នៃ រោងចក្រ ហ្គោល ដិន) was formed in June 2006 and registered on 2 August 2006; it claimed 614 members and had rather more than 200 by the hearing. [Para 23][Para 24] In mid-2006 the company brought labour officers and a doctor to the factory for work books and medical checks and deducted 6 USD from each worker's wage for the two, and it also deducted 20, 10 and 5 USD from workers absent five to seven days before payday. [Para 5][Para 25][Para 50] Of the five demands, two were settled; the union withdrew the union-dues demand because Award No. 110/06 had already decided it, and settled the deduction demand at the hearing, leaving only the health-check fee. [Para 14][Para 18][Para 19]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must repay the fee it deducted from wages for the pre-employment medical check? [Para 4][Para 25][Para 36]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the health check is a duty the Labour Law places on the employer, so the 100 riel taken from each member's pay must come back; the union limited the claim to its own members and to the fee paid before November 2006. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 48]

**Employer (respondent):** point 3 of the internal rules requires every worker to be medically examined before starting work, so the cost is the worker's, and it refused to repay. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 43] It promised a schedule of the fees on 20 July 2007 but never produced it. [Para 27]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to repay the 100 riel health-check fee to union members who joined before 3 May 2007, the date the union filed its complaint; the work-book fee was not considered. [Para 52][Para 53]

The panel read Article 247(a) of the Labour Law, under which the Ministry sets the conditions for pre-recruitment and periodic health checks, and Article 247(c)(4), which puts the cost of those checks on the employer. [Para 36][Para 37] It followed Awards 63/04, 64/04, 78/04, 98/04, 106/04, 107/04, 05/05 and 05/06, which held that Article 247(a) obliges the employer to pay before the worker starts. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40] It rejected the argument from the internal rules: because those rules require every worker to be examined before entering service, the legal duty to pay is the employer's, not the worker's. [Para 43]

On quantum the panel noted an inter-ministerial Prakas of 21 November 2006 fixing the fee at 12,000 riel for a Khmer worker and 15 USD for a foreigner, but held that a Prakas cannot apply retroactively, so checks carried out before November 2006 cost 100 riel. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 47][Para 48] Both sides agreed 6 USD had in fact been deducted for the check and the work book, and the panel found the employer held clear records of what it had taken. [Para 50][Para 51]

The union never filed the list of claimant members it had promised for 20 July 2007, and the panel therefore limited the order to members who had joined before the complaint date rather than awarding to an unidentified group. [Para 32][Para 52]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The cost of the statutory pre-employment medical examination is the employer's, not the worker's: Article 247(a) and (c)(4) of the Labour Law place it on the employer, and an internal rule that merely requires workers to be examined does not transfer it, so a deduction for the fee must be repaid. [Para 37][Para 40][Para 43] A Prakas raising the official fee applies only from its date, so checks performed earlier are valued at the rate then in force. [Para 47][Para 48]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the employer's records would show the deductions was not the basis of the order, which rested on the legal allocation of the cost. [Para 51]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The fee is printed as 100 riel throughout the operative part [Para 52][Para 53], while the Prakas quoted in the same award sets 12,000 riel for the same service from November 2006 and 6 USD is recorded as the amount actually deducted [Para 25][Para 45]. The workbook issue is described as settled at [Para 31] but the claimant withdrew only the work-book part of the claim at [Para 30]. The workers' headcount appears as 800 at [Para 21] against 500 claimants at [Para 22], and union membership as 614 in 2006 and more than 200 at the hearing [Para 24]. The worker-side arbitrator's name is printed differently on the face and in the signature block [Para 1][Para 54], and the heading of the jurisdiction paragraph is corrupted where it should cite Chapter 12 of the Labour Law [Para 8].
