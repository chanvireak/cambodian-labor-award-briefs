# Award No. 54/07 — Yong Va Co., Ltd. (ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉ុង វា ខូលី មីធី ដ) (Arbitration Council, 27 July 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (ឈីវ ភិរម្យ appointed by the employer, លីវ សុវណ្ណា appointed by the workers, ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា chair) · Doc ID 140615

## 1. Facts
Yong Va Co., Ltd. (ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉ុង វា ខូលី មីធី ដ) runs three garment branches in Ta Khmau, Kandal, employing about 8,000 workers, some 6,600 of them at Yong Va 1. [Para 1][Para 31][Para 32] The Khmer Youth Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព-សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ) filed 21 demands, 12 settled in conciliation, leaving nine for the panel. [Para 1][Para 24][Para 25] The disputes ranged from the fee of 100 riel the company has charged each worker for the pre-employment health check since 1998, to the suspension of one union official on 15 July 2006 pending a criminal case and the dismissal of a former union president on 3 March 2007. [Para 40][Para 56][Para 68] About 40 toilets serve 1,866 day-shift and 1,769 night-shift workers, and eight were out of service. [Para 96][Para 97]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay termination compensation before handing a worker dismissed for serious misconduct to the police? [Para 1][Para 104]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must reimburse the health-check fee and the work-book fee it charged workers? [Para 3][Para 52][Para 113]
3. (Issue 4) Whether wages must be paid twice a month instead of once? [Para 7][Para 76][Para 147]
4. (Issue 5) Whether the company may deduct the 5 USD attendance bonus for a half-day unlawful absence? [Para 9][Para 80][Para 155]
5. (Issue 6) Whether absence must reach one week, rather than three days, before a worker is treated as having abandoned the job? [Para 11][Para 84][Para 163]
6. (Issue 7) Whether the company must install lighting on the dark road outside the rear gate used by night-shift workers? [Para 13][Para 90][Para 171]
7. (Issue 8) Whether the company must build more toilets to match the workforce? [Para 15][Para 95][Para 184]
8. (Issue 9) Whether day-shift workers may submit leave requests to the administration instead of through their team leader? [Para 17][Para 99][Para 193]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** a worker dismissed for serious misconduct should be paid termination compensation before the company calls the police; the health-check and work-book costs are the employer's; wages should come twice a month as the law says. [Para 33][Para 52][Para 78] They said the rear exit is dark for about 50 metres and workers have been robbed there, that only about 32 of 40 toilets work, and that team leaders lose leave requests. [Para 91][Para 93][Para 96][Para 101] They accepted that the 5 USD bonus is an incentive paid for a full month without unlawful absence. [Para 155]

**Employer (respondent):** the company pays termination compensation according to law, but cannot pay it instantly at night when no accountant is on duty, and the suspension of Mr H was temporary, pending the court, with reinstatement promised if he is cleared. [Para 39][Para 60] It said the worker had already agreed in the 11 August 2004 agreement to drop the 100 riel claim, and that accounting needs time to collect payroll from each section and to transfer funds from Singapore, so 10th of the month is the earliest. [Para 43][Para 79] It will repair the eight broken toilets and said the dark stretch is private land needing the owner's permission and money. [Para 94][Para 186]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered termination compensation paid within 48 hours of dismissal, reimbursement of 10,100 riel per worker for health checks self-paid since 11 August 2004, and repair of the eight broken toilets; the remaining demands were dismissed or not considered. [Para 200][Para 201][Para 208]

On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 166(4) of the Labour Law, which requires wages and all compensation to be paid within 48 hours of the end of work, and found no exception for a worker referred to the police; the company's referral of the worker does not suspend that deadline. [Para 109][Para 111][Para 112][Para 113]

On Issue 2 it found the two 11 August 2004 agreements, whose signatures the company admitted, both meant the company would pay the health-check cost from that date, and it had not done so. [Para 113][Para 116][Para 117][Para 118] Article 247(a) and (c)(4) places that cost on the employer, as Awards 02/03, 63/04, 78/04 and 05/06 held. [Para 120][Para 121][Para 122][Para 125] It ordered 10,100 riel per worker, the figure in the earlier awards. [Para 127] The work-book claim was withdrawn at the hearing and not considered. [Para 55][Para 128]

Issue 3 was not decided. For Mr H the panel recorded the parties' oral agreement that he would be reinstated if the court cleared him, and declined to go further; for the former union president Ms T, whose case the union had stopped supporting, it held the claim was an individual dispute outside its jurisdiction under Chapter 12 of the Labour Law. [Para 133][Para 141][Para 145][Para 146]

Issue 4 was dismissed: Article 116(1) requires wages at least twice a month, but Notification No. 745 of 23 October 2006 fixes a monthly minimum wage of 50 USD for full workers and 45 USD for probationers in garments, and monthly payment is the sector practice, so Article 116 cannot be applied to these workers. [Para 150][Para 153][Para 154]

Issue 5 was dismissed because the 5 USD attendance bonus under point 3 of Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 is an incentive for a full month without unlawful absence, so a half-day absence forfeits it. [Para 156][Para 157][Para 161][Para 162] Issue 6 was dismissed for want of any evidence or argument supporting a one-week waiting period. [Para 167][Para 169][Para 170]

Issue 7 was dismissed: Article 229 and Prakas No. 484 of 23 December 2003 govern lighting inside the workplace, and a demand for lighting on private land outside the compound exceeds the law and is an interest dispute that only a most-representative union may bring, which this union is not. [Para 172][Para 178][Para 179][Para 183]

Issue 8 was partly granted: Article 229 and Prakas No. 052 of 10 February 2000 require hygienic toilets in proportion to the workforce, and since 32 working toilets suffice for the shift headcounts, the panel ordered the eight broken ones repaired and refused the demand for new construction. [Para 187][Para 189][Para 192][Para 208][Para 209]

Issue 9 was dismissed: Article 2 gives the employer the right to organise work and to set the procedure for granting leave, provided it is lawful and reasonable. [Para 197][Para 198][Para 199]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The 48-hour deadline in Article 166(4) of the Labour Law for paying wages and compensation on termination admits no exception: referring a worker dismissed for serious misconduct to the police does not postpone the payment. [Para 109][Para 112][Para 113] The cost of the pre-employment medical check falls on the employer under Article 247(a) and (c)(4) and cannot be shifted to the worker, including by a worker's promise to abandon an accrued reimbursement claim, which binds only if a valid collective agreement says so. [Para 120][Para 125][Para 126] In the garment sector, where Notification No. 745 fixes a monthly minimum wage, Article 116's twice-monthly rule yields to monthly payment. [Para 150][Para 154]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the company need not pay instantly where no accountant is on the night shift, and its remarks on private-land lighting, were not part of any order. [Para 39][Para 178]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer appears as "យ៉ុង វា ខូលី មីធី ដ" on the face [Para 1], "យុង វ៉ា" in the findings [Para 29], and "Yung Va" and "Yong Va" in the reasons. The health-check fee is printed as 100 riel in the demand and 10,100 riel in the order [Para 40][Para 127][Para 201]. Toilet numbers are given as 40 at [Para 185] and "400" at [Para 190]. The order on Issue 3 is garbled, inserting stray Latin initials and a fragment of a name before the reinstatement sentence [Para 202]. Headcounts are inconsistent: 8,000 at three branches and 6,600 at Yong Va 1 [Para 31][Para 32], against 1,866 plus 1,769 shift workers [Para 97]. Article 116 is quoted with a doubled clause at [Para 154].
