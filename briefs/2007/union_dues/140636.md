# Award No. 86/07 — Target Shoes Industry Inc. v Khmer Youth Trade Union at Target Shoes (Arbitration Council, 19 September 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ly Tayseng (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Koy Neam (chair) · Doc ID 140636

## 1. Facts
Target Shoes Industry Inc. employs 1,200 workers at Damnak Thom village, Stung Meanchey, Phnom Penh, and the Khmer Youth Trade Union (KYTU) at the factory is the only union there [Para 18].
Of those workers, 660 applied to the company to have 1,000 riels a month deducted from their wages as union contribution, and the union said it would file the list of members who had completed deduction applications [Para 18].
Conciliation settled six of eight issues; the two left over were forwarded to the Council on 28 August 2007 and heard on 4 September 2007 [Para 15][Para 16].
Since 25 November 2006 the company has required new hires to pay for their own medical check and submit the invoice, and some workers began work without any check [Para 21][Para 22].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must deduct 1,000 riels a month from the wages of KYTU members who have applied in writing, and pay the sum to the union? [Para 25][Para 37]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must reimburse the medical check fee to workers who paid it, and arrange checks for those who have not had one? [Para 23][Para 38]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), Khmer Youth Trade Union at Target Shoes:** the union asked the company to deduct 1,000 riels a month from the wages of the 660 members who had signed applications, promising a full list of names, and said anyone leaving the union could tell the company to stop [Para 18][Para 19]. On the medical fee the union demanded immediate reimbursement and that the company arrange checks for those still unchecked [Para 23].
**Employer (respondent), Target Shoes Industry Inc.:** the company refused the check-off, citing Articles 129 and 281 of the Labour Law, and argued that deducting wages would upset its policy and management and risk instability because workers would protest [Para 19][Para 20]. It accepted that the medical check was its obligation but proposed to reimburse on 1 June 2008, arranging later checks on request [Para 24].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the company was ordered to deduct a 1,000 riel contribution from each consenting member's wages on each pay day and pay it to the KYTU, and to reimburse the 12,000 riel medical check fee on the October 2007 pay day and arrange checks for workers not yet examined [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39].
On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 129, under which a worker may authorise in writing, and revoke at any time, deduction of wages for dues to the union he or she belongs to [Para 27][Para 28]. Article 5 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 makes deduction obligatory once the worker exercises that right, and Awards 03/03 Tonga Garment, 05/03 Top One and 19/06 Xing Hong were followed [Para 31]. The company's reliance on Article 281 was rejected: that article bars deduction or payment of dues without the workers' consent, and cannot release the employer from a deduction the workers requested [Para 32][Para 33]. The panel also adopted its earlier reading of Article 281 as protecting workers from employer interference with union activity under Article 280, citing Award 62/05 Jing Tai [Para 34]. Because some members had left the union, it directed the union to resubmit the written consents of those who still wanted the deduction [Para 34][Para 35].
On Issue 2 both parties accepted that the medical check is the employer's obligation under Article 247, and Article 2(D) of Joint Prakas No. 1191/06 of 21 November 2006 fixes the employer's payment at 12,000 riels for each Cambodian worker [Para 35]. The panel preferred the approach of Awards 19/06 Xing Hong and 44/06 Gold Fame, ordering reimbursement once the award takes effect rather than on the employer's proposed later date, and set payment for the October 2007 pay day [Para 37][Para 39]. It also ordered the company to arrange checks at an appropriate time at its own expense for workers not yet examined [Para 37][Para 39].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Article 281 of the Labour Law bars an employer from deducting or paying union dues without the workers' consent; it does not release an employer from the duty, under Article 129 and Article 5 of Prakas No. 305, to deduct dues from the wages of members who have applied in writing and to remit them to the union each pay day [Para 31][Para 33].
The duty continues until the worker revokes the authorisation or leaves the union, so a list that no longer matches the membership must be resubmitted before deduction proceeds [Para 34][Para 35].
A pre-employment medical check is the employer's expense under Article 247 and Article 2(D) of Joint Prakas No. 1191/06, reimbursable once the award takes effect rather than at a date the employer chooses [Para 35][Para 37].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded the company's concern that permitting deduction would damage management policy and stability, but decided the point on the statute rather than on that concern [Para 20].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The case number is printed as "86107" on the face of the award, evidently 86/07. The translation drops the letter "t" throughout ("he" for "the", "hat" for "that") and truncates some clauses mid-sentence, for example the Prakas No. 305 quotation where the text jumps from "Labour" to "When workers decides to". The employer's proposed reimbursement date is printed as 1 June 2008 in the summary of its position and 1 March 2008 in the reasoning, and the award does not reconcile them [Para 24][Para 36]. The claim is described as 10,100 riels of medical fees while the order awards 12,000 riels per worker under the Joint Prakas, and the two figures are never explained against each other [Para 11][Para 38]. The arbitrator citations are transposed, Article 281 reasoning being attributed to Awards 05/03 and 74/07 as well as 62/05 [Para 34].
