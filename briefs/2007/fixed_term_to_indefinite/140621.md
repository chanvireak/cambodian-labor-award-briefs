# Award No. 61/07 — M & V (3) Company (Arbitration Council, 2 August 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tayseng, Tuon Siphann, Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 140621

## 1. Facts
M & V (3) Company, also printed as M & V (3) International Company, has operated since 1997 and employed about 2,800 workers, of whom C.CAWDU claimed 510 as members and as claimants in this dispute [Para 26][Para 35][Para 37]. The company suspended labour contracts in two shifts of 1,900 and 1,700 workers between January and April 2007 under a Labour Inspector letter, and had terminated three ironing-section group chiefs on 08 September 2006 [Para 51][Para 65]. Of six demands, one was conciliated and the rest reached the panel [Para 27].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must reimburse the 10,100 riels medical check fee to workers who paid it themselves? [Para 15]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must renew each fixed-duration contract for the same duration as the original? [Para 19]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must reinstate the three group chiefs with wages and bonuses from the date work was suspended? [Para 20]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must pay full wages for the period from February to April 2007 when it had no work? [Para 22]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the company must sign first-time fixed-duration contracts of six months or longer with new workers? [Para 23]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers Democratic Union (C.CAWDU):** the minutes of 26 December 2006 signed away a right the Labour Law gives them, so the 10,100 riels must be repaid to each worker; the company also owes 100% wages under the 03 November 2006 agreement, and the three group chiefs were suspended because they were union activists [Para 41][Para 21][Para 62].

**Employer (respondent), M & V (3) Company:** workers must hold a health certificate before they even apply, so the check is their own cost; contract renewal and contract length follow production orders; and the reinstatement claim is an individual dispute for the court [Para 38][Para 20][Para 22]. The wage claim fails because a suspension approved by the Labour Inspector lawfully removes the duty to pay [Para 64].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company ordered to reimburse 10,100 riels to each worker who paid the fee and entered the contract or began work from 11 May 2004, payment to be settled within 15 days; the renewal, full-wage and six-month contract demands rejected; reinstatement declined as an individual dispute [Para 130][Para 134].

On Issue 1 the panel held Article 247 of the Labour Law obliges the employer to pay medical examination costs, so the 26 December 2006 minutes could not bind workers to surrender that right, since Article 13 makes any derogation from a public-order provision null [Para 73][Para 76]. The three-year limitation in Article 120 then cut off fees paid before 11 May 2004 [Para 80][Para 85].

On Issue 2 the panel held that under Article 65 and Articles 1 and 22 of Decree 38 only the contracting parties may amend, renew or terminate a contract, so it could not order renewal; six of the ten contracts filed carried no end date and were therefore of undetermined duration [Para 89][Para 96].

On Issue 3 the panel found res judicata did not bar the claim because Award 103/06 rested on procedure rather than substance, but with no new facts it reached the same conclusion that reinstating the three group chiefs is an individual dispute the panel declines to hear [Para 103][Para 109].

On Issue 4 the panel held that Article 71 allows suspension of up to two months under Labour Inspector control, and Article 72 removes the wage obligation during a lawful suspension; the short-work agreement covered a day or a week, not a two-month suspension [Para 114][Para 119][Para 128].

On Issue 5, following the reasoning on Issue 2 and Award 53/05 Finegis, the panel held it could not dictate the duration of contracts with new workers [Para 129].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where workers have signed away a benefit the Labour Law grants them, the waiver is unenforceable to that extent under Article 13, but the statutory three-year limitation in Article 120 still bars reimbursement of fees paid more than three years before the complaint [Para 77][Para 85]. A suspension approved by the Labour Inspector under Article 71 extinguishes the wage obligation under Article 72, and a short-work agreement does not fill the gap for a two-month suspension [Para 119][Para 128]. An arbitration panel cannot order the renewal of a fixed-duration contract or its length, that choice belonging to the contracting parties alone [Para 89][Para 91].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the parties read "short period" differently and that no law defines it [Para 124].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The document is an unofficial English translation with pervasive OCR damage, pronoun and article fusion throughout ("thearing", "tworkers", "hemployer") [Para 7][Para 29]. It contradicts itself on the caseload, recording five non-conciliated issues at one point and "the remaining 12 non-conciliated issues" shortly after [Para 28][Para 30]. The non-conciliation report carries number 621/07 in one paragraph and 605 in the next [Para 26][Para 28], and the earlier Award 103/06 is dated 21 November 2007 although it precedes this award of 2 August 2007 [Para 57]. The three group chiefs' names appear in two different spellings across the reasons and the decision [Para 101][Para 132].
