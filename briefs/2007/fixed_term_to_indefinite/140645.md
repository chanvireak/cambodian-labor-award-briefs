# Award No. 98/07 — Sky Sino Holdings (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 October 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Ing Sothy (employer-chosen), Ven Pov (worker-chosen), Kong Phallack (chair) · Doc ID 140645

## 1. Facts
Sky Sino Holdings (Cambodia) Ltd, Por Prork Khang Tbong Village, Sangkat Kakab, Khan Dangkor, Phnom Penh, employed approximately 450 workers and had been renamed from QSP in July 2007; most of its workforce were former QSP workers whose seniority the parties agreed had been maintained [Para 1][Para 19][Para 20][Para 24]. The sole union was the Khmer Youth Trade Union, formally "Khmer Youth Trade Union at QSP Factory", then preparing documents for a name change, with around 160 members [Para 2][Para 21][Para 22][Para 24]. Of ten demands, seven were conciliated and Issue 3 was settled at the hearing of 5 October 2007, leaving Issues 1 and 2 [Para 11][Para 14][Para 15]. Non-conciliation report No. 1013 issued on 18 September 2007 [Para 10]. The remaining demands were five percent severance with unused annual leave for some 50 workers on three-month probationary contracts, and reimbursement of a US$3.88 medical check fee deducted from 160 workers in 2006 [Para 24][Para 32].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay five percent severance pay plus unused annual leave on termination of the three-month probationary fixed duration contracts of 50 workers? [Para 24][Para 36]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must reimburse the US$3.88 medical check fee deducted from 160 workers in 2006, given that the deduction was made while the factory was named QSP? [Para 32]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, Khmer Youth Trade Union at QSP Company (Sky Sino) / KYFTU):** the 50 workers started between 1 February and 1 May 2006 at US$40 a month, the company stripped their three-month seniority when issuing ID cards in May 2006, and severance with unused leave is owed on termination of those contracts [Para 24][Para 26]; the company deducted US$3.88 per worker for a medical check and employment book in 2006 and should return it [Para 32][Para 34][Para 35].
**Employer (respondent, Sky Sino Holdings (Cambodia) Ltd):** both demands belong to QSP and not to Sky Sino, so it refused to discuss them [Para 27][Para 32][Para 36]; seniority counts only from the date a worker becomes regular, excluding probation [Para 28]; it would rather arrange fresh medical checks than repay the 2006 fee [Para 6]; and its administrative manager had been in post only half a month and could not say whether the name change was a buy-out or a bankruptcy [Para 30].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the severance and unused leave demand was rejected (Issue 1), and the company was ordered to reimburse the medical examination fee of 10,100 riels to any worker able to prove membership of the Khmer Youth Trade Union (Issue 2) [Para 80][Para 81].
Succession first: Article 87 (1) keeps all labour contracts binding on the new employer where the employer's legal status changes by succession, sale, merger or transfer of funds, and Article 87 (3) that closure other than by act of God, bankruptcy and judicial liquidation not being acts of God, does not release the employer [Para 40][Para 43][Para 46][Para 47]. No evidence of the change was produced by the company, so responsibility passed to Sky Sino [Para 44][Para 45][Para 75].
On severance: Article 67 (7) requires fixed duration contracts to be in writing or they become undetermined, and Article 68 caps probation at three months [Para 48][Para 51][Para 52]. These 50 workers had oral contracts, therefore undetermined duration contracts, per the reasoning in Award 55/04 You Chheng Issue 2 [Para 53][Para 57]. Article 73 (7) gives five percent severance only on termination of a fixed duration contract, so the demand failed [Para 57][Para 60][Para 62].
On annual leave: Article 166 (1) gives 1.5 days a month of continuous service and Article 166 (2) compensation where the worker has not completed two continuous months [Para 66][Para 67]. Article 167 (1) makes the right to take paid leave arise after one year, and Article 167 (3) voids any agreement paying compensation in place of leave, as held in Awards 45/05 B & N Issue 1 and 94/04 Eternity Issue 2 [Para 69][Para 73][Para 74]. The 50 workers were still working, so no compensation was due [Para 74].
On the medical fee, Article 247 (a) and (b) and the Awards 21/03 Loyal Cambodia Issue 7, 19/04 Kbal Koh II Issue 2, 53/04 Kong Hung Issue 3, 64/04 Mercury Issue 1, 78/04 AIA and 05/05 Issue 1 put the cost on the employer, and the panel ordered the standard 10,100 riels [Para 76][Para 77][Para 78][Para 79].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A change of the employer's name or legal status does not extinguish liability for labour obligations incurred before the change: under Article 87 of the Labour Law the contracts remain binding on the successor, and the successor cannot refuse to answer a dispute merely because it arose under the former name [Para 43][Para 45][Para 47]. An oral probationary engagement is an undetermined duration contract under Article 67 (7), so no five percent severance under Article 73 (7) falls due on its expiry, and because Article 167 (3) voids payment in place of leave, no compensation for unused annual leave may be claimed by workers who remain employed [Para 53][Para 60][Para 74].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that a worker continuing after probation must be treated as regular and is entitled to the minimum wage, bonuses under Notification No. 017 of 2000, and annual leave, even though that entitlement did not assist these claimants [Para 56].
