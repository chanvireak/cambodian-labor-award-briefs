# Award No. 119/07 — Royal Crowntex International Inc (Arbitration Council, 11 December 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun, Vong Vanna, Kong Phallack chairing) · Doc ID 140657

## 1. Facts
Royal Crowntex International Inc, a Sihanoukville factory of 1,908 workers, faced a collective dispute filed by the Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU) and the Khmer Youth Trade Union (KYTU) at Royal Crowntex Factory [Para 30][Para 8]. Of 17 demands, five were withdrawn and nine conciliated at the hearing, leaving Issues 7, 14 and 16 for decision [Para 25][Para 26]. The union had applied for registration but was not yet registered, and supplied a name list of 531 claimants after an order of the panel [Para 30][Para 32].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 7) Whether maternity pay must be 50% of the worker's total wage including overtime and perquisites, not the minimum wage alone? [Para 34]
2. (Issue 14) Whether the company must compensate a worker for a motorbike lost inside the factory during working hours? [Para 37]
3. (Issue 16) Whether night-shift workers are owed 200% of the day rate under Circular No. 184, or only 130% under the amended Article 144(2)? [Para 45]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** maternity pay was being calculated without overtime, which forms part of the wage, and the company should pay the whole cost of the lost motorbike, having collected US$410 towards it; night work had been paid at 200% and that entitlement continued until their undetermined-duration contracts expired [Para 34][Para 40][Para 45][Para 46].
**Employer (respondent):** maternity pay already followed the law on minimum wage plus seniority, skill and language bonuses, the company had no duty to guard workers' vehicles, and the 200% rate was properly reduced to 130% when the amended Labour Law took effect in August 2007 [Para 34][Para 38][Para 48].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must pay 50% of wages and perquisites for 90 days' maternity leave, calculated on the worker's average wage over the 12 months before leave; the motorbike claim was declined and the 200% night-rate demand rejected [Para 65][Para 69][Para 80].

On Issue 7, Article 182 grants 90 days' leave and the company's practice complied with it [Para 55]. Article 183 requires half the wage including perquisites, but for workers with one year's uninterrupted service, and does not say whether the base is the minimum wage or total earnings [Para 56][Para 59][Para 60]. Article 103 brings overtime payments within wage, and the panel had held so in Award 24/06-Fortune and the Gold Fame award [Para 60][Para 63][Para 64]. The panel therefore fixed the base as the average wage of the 12 months before leave, multiplied by three months and halved [Para 65].

On Issue 14, Article 302 defines a collective dispute by reference to working conditions, the exercise of professional organisations' rights and enterprise relations, and the loss of a private vehicle falls outside all three [Para 67][Para 68]. No article of the Labour Law makes an employer the insurer of workers' property, and the worker produced no documents, so the panel declined to consider the claim [Para 69].

On Issue 16, amended Article 144(2) sets night work at 130% of the day rate [Para 70]. Circular 184(1) of 14 August 2007 preserves rates already agreed until the contract expires, but the panel held the Circular vague and subordinate to the amended law [Para 71][Para 73][Para 78]. Applying the amendment was necessary to give it meaning, and no agreement to continue 200% was shown, so the demand failed [Para 79][Para 80].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where Article 183 requires half the wage during maternity leave and the statute does not define the base, "wage" is read through Article 103 to include overtime payments, so maternity pay is computed on the average of the 12 months preceding the leave rather than the minimum wage [Para 60][Para 63][Para 65].

A claim for property lost on the employer's premises is not a collective labour dispute within Article 302, since it concerns neither working conditions nor the rights of a professional organisation, and the panel will decline it rather than decide it [Para 68][Para 69].

A statutory amendment fixing a rate prevails over a ministerial circular that preserves higher agreed rates, unless an actual agreement to continue the higher rate is proved [Para 78][Para 79].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the purpose of the amended Article 144 was to encourage night production and so create jobs, and that the union's unregistered status did not bar it from filing the collective demand [Para 79][Para 30].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The circular is cited in different paragraphs as No. 184(1) and No. 185 dated 14 August 2007, and its point 1 is reproduced twice with the same text [Para 45][Para 48][Para 71]. The reasons heading for Issue 7 and Issue 14 are printed twice, once in the parties' section and once under "Reasons for decision" [Para 34][Para 52]. The hearing listed for 16 November 2007 was postponed at the employer's request and heard on 21 November, while the notification of the award is dated 11 December 2007 [Para 21][Para 1]. OCR has merged "the employer" into one token throughout, and one worker is identified only by a fragmentary surname; he is described here by role.
