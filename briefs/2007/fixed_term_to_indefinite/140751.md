# Award No. 112/06 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន រី វឹរ វិច តិច ស្តាយ អិលធីឌី (Arbitration Council, 5 January 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (employer-appointed មាំរ សំបូរ ណា; worker-appointed អន ណន; chair ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា) · Doc ID 140751

## 1. Facts
The employer, ក្រុមហ៊ុន រី វឹរ វិច តិច ស្តាយ អិលធីឌី, a garment factory on National Road 21 at Prek Ta Pring, Saang district, Kandal province, employed 1,748 workers on three types of contract — two months, one year, and indefinite [Para 1][Para 40]. The union សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា held a founding election at the factory on 29 October 2006 [Para 17][Para 24]. Nineteen workers on two-month fixed-term contracts were terminated on 31 October, 3 November and 21 November 2006 [Para 18][Para 19]. They demanded reinstatement, alleging anti-union discrimination, and separately that workers with more than two months' service be made permanent [Para 3][Para 5]. Five of seven demands were settled in conciliation, leaving these two [Para 11].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the dismissal of the 19 workers was anti-union discrimination entitling them to reinstatement, or a lawful termination requiring severance? [Para 42][Para 60]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the workers who had worked more than two months should be reclassified as permanent employees? [Para 114][Para 117]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា):** The federation argued that the 19 were dismissed because they were activists who had voted in the union election notified on 20 October 2006, and that the terminations were unlawful, without valid reason and without payment of their full benefits [Para 20][Para 24][Para 32].
**Employer (respondent — ក្រុមហ៊ុន រី វឹរ វិច តិច ស្តាយ អិលធីឌី):** The company said the workers were temporary staff on written fixed-term contracts, that it ended the contracts because it had no further work, that some had reached their term, and that it was willing to pay $300 each or the statutory amount [Para 4][Para 27][Para 33][Para 34].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel refused reinstatement of the 19 but ordered the employer to calculate and pay each of them statutory termination benefits [Para 137].

On Issue 1, the panel applied Article 3 of Prakas 305, under which every candidate in a union leadership election is protected against dismissal for 45 days before and 45 days after the election if not elected, provided the union notifies the employer of the candidacy by any reliable means [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45]. It found that one worker, the only candidate on the list notified on 20 October 2006, satisfied all three conditions — he was a candidate, was dismissed on 3 November 2006 within the protected period, and had been notified [Para 48][Para 50][Para 51][Para 52][Para 53]. Because his contract expired on 20 November 2006, the panel held it could not order reinstatement beyond the term and instead ordered statutory severance [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57][Para 58]. For the other 18, Article 293 requires the labour inspector's agreement before dismissing a staff representative or candidate [Para 60], and Article 4 of Prakas 305 protects founders and members for 30 days after registration [Para 66][Para 67]. The panel found the union had never been validly registered, the labour department having refused registration by letters 1693 and 1878, so it could not lawfully represent the workers [Para 77][Para 78][Para 119][Para 120][Para 121]. It also found that only the 19 workers had given written authority, the list of some 300 workers being no lawful transfer of representation [Para 122][Para 123][Para 124][Para 125][Para 126]. Only the one candidate appeared on the notified list and the other 13 accepted the terminations [Para 70][Para 71][Para 72]. The panel concluded the terminations of 117 workers, including the 18, were workforce adjustment for lack of production and not anti-union discrimination [Para 87][Para 91]. Nevertheless, under Articles 73, 166 and 167 the employer owed 5% of wages earned during the contract as severance plus 1.5 days of unused annual leave per month worked, and had to compute these individually [Para 102][Para 103][Para 107][Para 108][Para 110][Para 111][Para 112][Para 113]. On Issue 2, the panel held the employment relationship with the 19 had ended and the demand to convert them to permanent status no longer arose, so it declined to consider it [Para 135][Para 136][Para 138]. The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification [Para 139].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-term worker who is a candidate in a union election is protected from dismissal for 45 days before and after the poll under Article 3 of Prakas 305, but where the contract has expired the panel orders statutory severance rather than reinstatement; and a union whose registration has been refused cannot claim representative status, so dismissals attributable to lack of work are not anti-union discrimination [Para 44][Para 53][Para 57][Para 78][Para 91].

**Obiter dicta:** The worker-appointed arbitrator dissented, finding on Articles 12 and 279 that the terminations were anti-union discrimination because six union leaders received $1,500 each against $300 for ordinary workers, and would have ordered reinstatement with back pay [Para 155][Para 157][Para 160][Para 161].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The number of names in the union's 20 October 2006 notice is given as 4 in one passage and 14 in another [Para 47][Para 70]. The company name appears in several spellings — "រី វឹរ វិច តិច ស្តាយ", "រី វើរិច" and "រី វើរ វិច" [Para 1][Para 31]. The dismissal table is badly garbled, with dates and names interleaved [Para 19]. The closing pages include a dissenting opinion whose date is printed as 2006 although the award issued on 5 January 2007 [Para 162][Para 1].
