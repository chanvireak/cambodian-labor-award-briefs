# Award No. 57/07 — Sira Tich Co., Ltd. (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ីរ៉ា តិច ខូ អិលធីឌី) (Arbitration Council, 19 July 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៉ីង សុទ្ធី appointed by the employer, ទួល ស៊ី ផាន់ appointed by the workers, ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា chair) · Doc ID 140618

## 1. Facts
Sira Tich Co., Ltd. (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ីរ៉ា តិច ខូ អិលធីឌី) is a garment factory at Chamkar Leuk, Dangkao, Phnom Penh employing 545 workers. [Para 1][Para 28] The claimant is the Khmer Federation of Democratic Garment Workers' Unions (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព-សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា, C.CAWDU), which filed eight demands; three were settled in conciliation and three more at the hearing, leaving two. [Para 2][Para 21][Para 25][Para 26] The factory's union president Mr L was tested and taken on 3 and 4 April 2007 as head of administration at 200 USD a month without any written contract, and was dismissed at 8 a.m. on 13 June 2007 without notice, the day after the federation notified the company that he was the ninth candidate in a union leadership election. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 34][Para 37]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the dismissal of the union president was unlawful and he must return to his post with his former wage and benefits? [Para 2][Para 30]
2. (Issue 3) Whether workers granted ordinary personal leave keep their daily wage and their attendance bonus? [Para 7][Para 51][Para 95]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissal is anti-union discrimination: the company knew Mr L headed the new union, he was a candidate in the election, and he was removed the morning after the candidate list was served. [Para 2][Para 34][Para 39] On leave, they argued that a worker the company has lawfully permitted to be absent on ordinary personal business should keep both his daily wage and his regular attendance bonus. [Para 51][Para 52]

**Employer (respondent):** Mr L was a probationer with no capacity for the work who refused to follow management instructions, so he cannot be taken back; it denies discrimination. [Para 3][Para 4][Para 40][Para 41] On leave it accepts granting permission but refuses wage and bonus because the worker did not come to work, and the bonus is an incentive for regular attendance. [Para 53][Para 54]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must reinstate Mr L in his old or a suitable post with back pay from 13 June 2007 within 15 days of the award becoming final, and must pay the 5 USD attendance bonus pro rata to the days actually worked; his claim to keep his daily wage while on permitted leave was refused. [Para 124][Para 125][Para 126]

On Issue 1 the panel asked first whether a probation contract existed. [Para 60] Article 68 of the Labour Law requires a probation contract to fix its starting and ending dates, and a probation period may not exceed three months for staff, two for skilled workers or one for ordinary workers. [Para 60][Para 61] Article 67 provides that a fixed-term contract must be in writing and otherwise becomes an indefinite contract. [Para 66][Para 67] The panel found no written contract at all between the company and Mr L, and held that a note on his application form calling him a probationer for three months was not a formal document capable of proving probation. [Para 46][Para 68] His contract was therefore indefinite. [Para 69]

The panel then applied Article 74: an indefinite contract may be terminated by either side, but never without a valid reason relating to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the needs of the enterprise. [Para 72][Para 75] The company alleged incompetence and disobedience but produced no evidence of either, and its assertions were treated as its own opinion only. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 80] The panel also found Mr L protected: Article 3 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 protects every candidate in a union leadership election, for 45 days before and 45 days after the poll if not elected, and the federation had served the candidate list on 12 June 2007, the day before the dismissal. [Para 86][Para 87][Para 89][Para 90] The dismissal was thus invalid, and under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 the panel ordered reinstatement with wages for the period lost. [Para 91][Para 92][Para 94][Para 124]

On Issue 3 the panel held that permitted absence suspends the employment contract under Article 71(6), and that Article 72(1) removes the employer's duty to pay wages while the contract is suspended. [Para 98][Para 99][Para 100][Para 101] The claim to the daily wage therefore failed and was dismissed. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 125] The attendance bonus was different: Article 103 treats bonuses paid as an incentive as part of wages, so the 5 USD bonus under point 3 of Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 is wage, and the panel ordered it reduced in proportion to the days of permitted leave rather than forfeited entirely, as the fairest reading of a Notification that says nothing on the point. [Para 115][Para 117][Para 123][Para 126]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A probation engagement that is not put in a written contract fixing its start and end dates is not a probation at all: the contract becomes indefinite, so the worker can only be dismissed for a valid reason of aptitude, conduct or the needs of the enterprise under Article 74, and the panel will reinstate with back pay under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 61][Para 68][Para 75][Para 92] A candidate in a union leadership election is protected against dismissal under Article 3 of Prakas No. 305 from 45 days before the poll, on the strength of the union's notification, so a dismissal in that window is invalid. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 90] Permitted personal leave suspends the contract and the wage, but an attendance bonus, being part of wages under Article 103, is only reduced in proportion to the days absent. [Para 99][Para 101][Para 117][Para 123]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that denying a pro-rata bonus would be unfair to the employer as well as the worker explains its choice of proportion but decides nothing beyond it. [Para 121][Para 122]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The case name on the face reads "ស៊ី រាំ តិច" while the company is named "ស៊ីរ៉ា តិច" throughout the reasons [Para 1][Para 28]. Paragraph [Para 70] concludes that the dismissal was contrary to law, yet [Para 72] immediately restates the employer's right to dismiss under Article 74 in garbled text with stray Latin fragments; the two passages cannot both be the ratio and the reinstatement order at [Para 94] was followed. The union election date is given as 9 June 2007 on the list filed with the panel [Para 50] and as 14 June 2007 in the worker's own evidence [Para 81][Para 83], while the dismissal is dated 13 June [Para 37]. The employer's exhibits include a letter to the Ministry refusing the union dated 13 December 2007, a date later than the award itself [Para 26][Para 47].
