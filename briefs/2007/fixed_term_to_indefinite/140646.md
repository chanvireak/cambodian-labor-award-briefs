# Award No. 101/07 — Planet Textile (Cambodia) Company Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 October 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Ing Sothy (employer-chosen), Vong Vanna (worker-chosen), Kong Phallack (chair) · Doc ID 140646

## 1. Facts
Planet Textile (Cambodia) Company Ltd, Khan Toul Kork, Phnom Penh, employed around 97 workers [Para 1][Para 29]. Of seven demands raised by the Federation Union of Khmer Democratic Workers (FUKDW) and the Union of Khmer Democratic Workers (UKDW) at Planet Textile Factory, six were conciliated, leaving one: reinstatement of the worker who became UKDW president, referred to here as Ms V [Para 5][Para 20][Para 21][Para 61]. Non-conciliation report No. 1020 issued on 19 September 2007 and the hearing was held on 3 October 2007 [Para 20]. On 24 August 2007 six workers, including Ms V, were asked to sign letters of resignation over about 100 pairs of damaged jeans; she signed at 9:00am and was elected union president at a gathering of some 49 workers that afternoon [Para 59][Para 61][Para 62]. On 28 August 2007 the company took back five of the six but not her [Para 63][Para 64].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must reinstate Ms V, given the company's position that she was a probationary worker who resigned? [Para 13][Para 16]
2. Whether the failure to reinstate her amounted to discrimination on the ground of union membership? [Para 79][Para 80]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, FUKDW / UKDW at Planet Textile Factory):** the refusal to reinstate her was discrimination against the union, since all six workers made the same mistake and five were taken back while she, elected president that same afternoon, was not [Para 31][Para 70][Para 72][Para 109]. She never had a written contract, was never told she was on probation, worked 13 days then 26 and 26 days, and signed a document she did not read because the company put it in front of her [Para 39][Para 40][Para 42][Para 60].
**Employer (respondent, Planet Textile (Cambodia) Company Ltd):** it hired her on 17 July 2007 and she resigned on 24 August 2007 after she and five others destroyed about 100 pairs of jeans, which it displayed at the hearing [Para 36][Para 37][Para 59]. It reinstated the five because hiring was hard in that neighbourhood, but not her because she did not get along with team members and resigned of her own accord [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the workers' demand that the company reinstate Ms V [Para 114][Para 115].
Resignation: Clause 7 of Article 67 of the Labour Law requires a fixed duration contract to be in writing, failing which it becomes undetermined in duration, so her oral engagement was an undetermined duration contract [Para 83][Para 84][Para 87][Para 88]. Clause 1 of Article 74 allows either party to terminate such a contract at will, subject to written prior notice [Para 89][Para 90][Para 91]. On the facts she signed the resignation letter prepared for her, could read and write, was not under pressure, and no evidence showed unwillingness, so the panel found she had resigned [Para 93][Para 94][Para 95][Para 97].
Discrimination: Article 12 and Article 279 forbid any decision on hiring, assignment, discipline or dismissal to turn on union membership or activity [Para 99][Para 100][Para 101]. The panel weighed the sequence — termination that morning, election that afternoon, reinstatement of the other five on 28 August 2007 — but found that among those taken back were the person elected union secretary, the adviser and an activist, and that the worker party produced no testimony or evidence of discriminatory intent [Para 105][Para 109][Para 110][Para 111]. It also noted that the company failed to supply the wage records and worker list the panel ordered, and the union failed to prove three months' service [Para 75][Para 77].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a worker has no written fixed duration contract, the engagement is of undetermined duration under Article 67 (7) of the Labour Law, and either party may end it at will under Article 74 (1) on written notice; a signed letter of resignation will be treated as a resignation where the worker could read it and was under no pressure [Para 84][Para 91][Para 94][Para 97]. A union's suspicion of anti-union motive is not enough: where the employer reinstates other workers who hold union office, the panel will find no discrimination under Article 12 and Article 279 absent testimony or evidence of intent [Para 101][Para 109][Para 111].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded, without deciding anything on it, that the company had disobeyed its order to produce wage records and that the Internal Work Rules had not yet received the visa [Para 25][Para 75].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Unofficial English translation only; no Khmer original in the file, so party names cannot be checked [Para 1]. The award is a chain of one-clause fragments: the claimant's name is split across nearly every paragraph from 13 to 17 and 30 to 42, so each sentence of the reasoning must be read against its neighbours to be joined up [Para 30][Para 42]. The employment start date is given as 17 July 2007 by the company and 17 June 2007 by the worker [Para 36][Para 38]. The procedural section says the Secretariat received the case on 10 July 2006, which cannot be right for a 2007 dispute [Para 21]. The Internal Work Rules are noted as never having been vised, which affects their force. Paragraph 72 contains a garbled passage attributing remarks to a public figure that cannot be reconstructed from the text. OCR drops initial letters throughout.
