# Award No. 69/07 — M & V International Manufacturing Ltd (M&V 3) (Arbitration Council, 14 July 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, Sin Kim Sean, Kong Phallack chairing) · Doc ID 140629

## 1. Facts
M & V International Manufacturing Ltd, operator of the M&V 3 garment factory on National Road 2, Phnom Penh, employs about 2,876 workers, of whom 200 in the overlocking sections were directly involved in this dispute [Para 1][Para 17]. Four unions are present and the Cambodia Independent Workers Union at MV Garment Factory, the claimant, has about 1,250 members but no certificate of most representative status [Para 18][Para 19]. The workers demanded that the piece rate for all PN models be raised so that a worker in overlocking sections E, C and D earns at least US$2.2 per day of eight hours, relying on a verbal agreement of May 2007; the company said the rate was already high and that it topped up any shortfall to US$2.2 at each half-monthly payment [Para 7][Para 8][Para 27][Para 28]. Conciliation failed and the single non-conciliated issue came to the panel [Para 10][Para 14].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must raise the piece rate for all PN models to US$2.2 per day calculated on the workers of overlocking sections E, C and D alone? [Para 20][Para 28]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — Cambodia Independent Workers Union at MV Garment Factory):** that the company had agreed in May 2007 to a piece rate ensuring an average worker in sections E, C and D earned at least US$2.2 for eight hours, that most workers there fell short of it, and that the top-up should be paid as the shortfall arises rather than averaged over half a month [Para 21][Para 22][Para 27][Para 41][Para 42].
**Employer (respondent — M & V International Manufacturing Ltd):** that the rate was already high, that the agreement covered all overlocking sections including collar overlocking rather than E, C and D alone, that more than half of overlocking workers already averaged US$2.2 over the last eleven days, and that any worker below it was raised to US$2.2 at each half-monthly wage payment [Para 8][Para 24][Para 25][Para 26].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejects the demand that the company raise the piece rate for all PN models to US$2.2 per day for workers in overlocking sections E, C and D [Para 47].
The panel grounded the claim in Article 108 of the Labour Law, requiring piecework to be calculated so that a worker of mediocre ability working normally earns at least the guaranteed minimum wage, in Article 137 capping hours at eight per day and 48 per week, and in Notification No. 745 of 2006 setting the garment minimum at US$45 during probation and US$50 per month thereafter [Para 28]. Following Awards 03/05 Flying Dragon (Issue 6), 41/05 Violet (Issue 8) and 44/06 Goldfame (Issue 1), it held that the employer may set or change the piece rate provided a worker of average ability working normal hours can earn the minimum wage, fixed here at US$50 per month for a regular worker [Para 29][Para 30]. On the evidence, the workers did not assert that they were paid below the minimum wage and produced nothing to show it, so the employer was found to meet that obligation [Para 31][Para 32].
The panel then read the May 2007 verbal agreement as silent on whether the rate is calculated daily or by section; absent evidence for a section-by-section calculation, and since most overlocking workers already received US$2.2, the agreement applied across the whole overlocking section and gave no basis for a floor confined to E, C and D [Para 34][Para 35][Para 36][Para 37]. The company's practice of paying a higher rate than the US$1.92 per day that US$50 per month represents, and of topping up to US$2.2 twice a month, was held to comply [Para 38][Para 43][Para 44][Para 46]. The demand that the rate never be averaged over days was rejected as an interference with the employer's right to manage recognised in Article 2 of the Labour Law and in Award 41/05 Violet, the panel accepting that half-monthly averaging still left workers better off than US$1.92 a day [Para 45][Para 46].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A piece rate is lawful if a worker of average ability working no more than eight hours a day can earn at least the statutory minimum wage; the employer's right to manage lets it choose the averaging period, so a demand for a daily, section-specific floor fails where the employer already tops up to the claimed rate at each half-monthly payment and never falls below the minimum [Para 30][Para 45][Para 46].

**Obiter dicta:** that US$2.2 per day exceeds the US$1.92 per day implied by a US$50 monthly minimum, an observation not needed once the minimum-wage obligation was found satisfied [Para 38][Para 46].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award date is printed as 14 July 2007 in the header, yet the non-conciliation report is dated 23 July 2007 and the hearing took place on 1 August 2007, so the printed date precedes the events it decides [Para 1][Para 10]. The demand is described as US$2.2 "per 8 hours" in the non-conciliation report and as "per day" in the reasons, which the brief follows [Para 7][Para 28]. The claimant union appears as the Cambodia Independent Workers Union in the header but as the Cambodian Independent Union in the reasons, and the reasons state that a different number of overlocking workers exists (232) than the 137 said to be involved [Para 3][Para 19][Para 20]. OCR has dropped the letter "t" throughout and lost the section letter at the end of the repeated issue heading [Para 28].
