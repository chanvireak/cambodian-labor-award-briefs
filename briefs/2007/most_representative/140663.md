# Award No. 131/07 — Focus Footwear Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 12 December 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry, Liv Sovanna, Run Saray) · Doc ID 140663

## 1. Facts
Khmer Youth Trade Union (KYTU) at Focus Footwear Factory complained to the Department of Labour Disputes on 4 September 2007 over three working conditions at Focus Footwear Co., Ltd., a factory of 1,124 workers with KYTU as its only union. [Para 14][Para 25]
One of the three issues was conciliated on 19 October 2007 and the two remaining issues went to a hearing on 27 November 2007, where the parties could not agree. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 19]
KYTU holds 800 members at the factory but the panel found it does not yet have most representative status. [Para 26]
The disputed acts were the company's refusal to pay 5 percent severance to workers whose two-year fixed-duration contracts had been converted to undetermined-duration contracts, and its refusal to add US$ 1 of seniority bonus for each year beyond five. [Para 10][Para 31]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay 5 percent severance pay to workers whose two-year fixed-duration contracts were terminated or converted to undetermined-duration contracts? [Para 26][Para 34]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must provide US$ 1 of seniority bonus each year after a worker has completed five years, or whether that demand is an interests dispute the panel must decline to consider? [Para 31][Para 57]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** KYTU at Focus Footwear argued that its members' fixed-duration contracts had run past two years, that the company had implemented Arbitral Award 64/07 only by converting them, and that the 5 percent severance and the additional US$ 1 seniority bonus were therefore owed. [Para 26][Para 29][Para 31]
**Employer (respondent):** Focus Footwear Co., Ltd. refused the severance because the contracts had become undetermined-duration and so no labour relation had ended, and refused the bonus because it already complies with Notification 017 dated 18 July 2000. [Para 10][Para 32][Para 33]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the demand for 5 percent severance pay and rejected the demand to increase the seniority bonus by US$ 1 per year after five years. [Para 65]
On Issue 1 the panel read Article 73 (6 & 7) of the Labour Law as requiring severance equal to at least 5 percent of wages on expiry of a fixed-duration contract, with seniority counting both contracts where an undetermined contract replaces a fixed one. [Para 35][Para 37][Para 38]
Following Arbitral Award 60/06 (New Max, Issue 6), it held severance accrues only when the contract is finished and the labour relation severed; a renewed contract continues the relation. [Para 39][Para 40]
Under Article 67 (2) a fixed-duration contract cannot exceed two years, and renewal beyond that makes it undetermined-duration from the outset — the rule the panel had applied in Arbitral Awards 57/06 (Evergreen, Issue 3), 10/03 (Jacqsintex, Issue 1) and 36/06 (Mondotex, Issue 2). [Para 43][Para 45][Para 47]
Because those contracts were therefore already undetermined-duration and none of the workers had left, no labour relation had been severed and the employer owed no 5 percent severance. [Para 50]
On Issue 2, the panel found Clause 5 of Notification 017 dated 18 July 2000 sets the seniority bonus up to US$ 5 per month for workers of more than four years and says nothing about year five, and Notification 745 (3) dated 23 October 2006 preserved those benefits unchanged. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53]
The demand for a further US$ 1 per year was outside the Notification, and no contract or collective bargaining agreement provided for it, so the panel treated it as an interests dispute. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57]
It declined to consider that interests dispute because the award would replace a collective bargaining agreement for one year under Clause 43 of the Prakas on the Arbitration Council, and because Article 96(2B) and Clause 9(1&2) of Prakas 305 dated 22 November 2001 reserve such negotiation to the most representative union. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60]
KYTU lacked most representative status under Article 277, so the panel followed Arbitral Awards 57/04 (Evergreen), 60/04 (United Art, Issue 3), 08/07 (Sao Quinh, Issue 3) and 116/07 (Grace Sun, Issues 4 & 5) and declined the demand. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 64][Para 65]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-duration contract renewed beyond the two-year maximum of Article 67 (2) is undetermined-duration from the start, so its later "conversion" severs nothing and no Article 73 severance pay falls due while the worker stays employed. [Para 47][Para 50]
A demand for a benefit beyond what a Ministry notification provides, with no collective agreement behind it, is an interests dispute, and the panel will decline it where the referring union does not hold most representative status. [Para 57][Para 60][Para 64]

**Obiter dicta:** The Notice is silent on the seniority rate for year five, which is why the panel could not simply read the US$ 1 increment into Clause 5. [Para 53][Para 54]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
This is an unofficial English translation of the Khmer original, and the OCR has fused many words ("the company", "fixeduration", "undetermineduration", "severance", "nobligation", "Traing"), which I read through without silently correcting the quoted provisions. [Para 9][Para 35]
The currency is printed inconsistently as "US$", "USS" and "US$ 1" within the same passages, and the panel's reasoning on Issue 1 is broken by three garbled, partly duplicated paragraphs where two drafts of the same sentence were merged. [Para 31][Para 48][Para 49][Para 51]
The citation string in paragraphs 62 to 64 is scrambled, with award names and issue numbers repeated and displaced, so the awards relied on are reported here as reconstructed from the least damaged reading. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 64]
