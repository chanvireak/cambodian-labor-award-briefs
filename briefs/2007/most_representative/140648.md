# Award No. 108/07 — 8 Star Sportswear Ltd (Arbitration Council, 8 November 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Liv Sovanna, Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 140648

## 1. Facts
8 Star Sportswear Ltd operates at Russei Village, Sangkat Stung Meanchey, Khan Meanchey, Phnom Penh and employs 960 workers; the claimants are the Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU) and the Khmer Youth Trade Union (KYTU) at the factory. [Para 1][Para 4][Para 33]
KYFTU complained on 6 August 2007 seeking improved working conditions; conciliation settled two of ten issues on 20 September 2007 and the remaining eight went to the Arbitration Council in non-conciliation report No. 1105 of 8 October 2007. [Para 22][Para 24]
At the hearing seven more issues were conciliated, leaving only Issue 3 for decision; the settled demands had included reinstatement of the KYTU factory president (an individual officer, referred to here as Ms P), payment of 156,000 riels in undeducted union dues, retention of bonuses, and a travel allowance for overtime work until 10:00 p.m. [Para 12][Para 18][Para 25][Para 26]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay an additional US$ 5 per month to each worker already receiving the US$ 50 minimum wage? [Para 33]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — KYFTU and KYTU:** the company had already raised probationary wages from US$ 40 to US$ 45 and regular wages from US$ 45 to US$ 50, so a further US$ 5 should go to all workers to keep the increase equal; Notification 745 of 23 October 2006 does not prevent an employer from granting it. [Para 34][Para 35]
**Employer (respondent) — 8 Star Sportswear Ltd:** the Labour Law does not require it to give an increase to workers already receiving US$ 50, though it would comply if the law so required. [Para 35]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider the demand that the company pay an additional US$ 5 per month to workers whose monthly minimum wage is US$ 50 or more, and the award was binding immediately because both parties chose a binding award on 25 October 2007. [Para 49]
On the legal right, Article 104 of the Labour Law guarantees only a wage at least equal to the guaranteed minimum, and Point 1 of Notification 745 of 23 October 2006 sets US$ 45 for probationary and US$ 50 for regular workers; following Awards 33/07 – Gold Fame (Issue 6) and 102/07 – Teratext (Issue 4), the panel held that the notification ensures the minimum and does not entitle workers already above it to a US$ 5 rise. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 39]
No agreement, collective agreement or past practice of a US$ 5 increase existed, and no other regulation provided one, so the demand had no legal basis and was in substance an interest dispute. [Para 41][Para 43]
Under Clause 43 of Prakas 099 of 21 April 2004, an award settling an interest dispute stands in place of a collective bargaining agreement for one year and binds all workers, removing their right to strike on that interest (Articles 96 and 321); because KYTU held no most representative status under Article 277 of the Labour Law and Article 6 of Prakas 305 of 22 November 2001, the panel followed Awards 84/07 – Yung Wah II (Issue 1), 81/04 – Evergreen (Issue 4), 09/05 – Kinh Tay (Issue 2) and case 70/07 (Issue 2) in declining the demand. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46][Para 49]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that does not hold most representative status — more than 50 per cent of the factory's workers, registered with the Ministry of Labour — cannot bring an interest dispute, because an award on such a dispute would operate as a collective bargaining agreement binding every worker and extinguishing their strike right for a year. [Para 44][Para 46][Para 49]
Notification 745 fixes a floor and not a general increase, so a demand for US$ 5 above the minimum must rest on an agreement, a collective agreement or an established practice, and absent one it fails. [Para 39][Para 41]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that an agreement or practice granting a wage increase above the minimum wage would not be contrary to the Labour Law. [Para 40]
