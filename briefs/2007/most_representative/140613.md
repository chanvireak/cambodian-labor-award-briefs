# Award No. 53/07 — E.Garment Co., Ltd. (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អ៉ី ហ្គាមេន ខូ អិលធីឌី) (Arbitration Council, 13 July 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៉ឹង សុទ្ធី appointed by the employer, ស៊ីន គឹម ស៊ាន appointed by the workers, គង់ ជល្លៈ chair) · Doc ID 140613

## 1. Facts
E.Garment Co., Ltd. (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អ៉ី ហ្គាមេន ខូ អិលធីឌី) runs a garment factory on Street 21 at Svay Rolum, Saang, Kandal, with about 1,200 workers, and began operating in early 2006. [Para 27][Para 28] Twelve technicians were sent home on 3 May 2007, one day after the Democratic Union of Workers at E.Garment Factory (សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ នៃ រោងចក្រ អ៉ីហ្គាមេន) notified the company that it had been formed. [Para 42][Para 32] The 12 had all signed employment contracts on the letterhead of Auto Fin Co., Ltd. (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អូតូ ហ្វីន), which had a one-year contract of 2 May 2006 to supply skilled technicians to E.Garment. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 101] Six of eighteen demands survived conciliation, the union having promised a list of 500 claimants but delivered 244 names, and the panel noted that neither in-house union was most representative. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 30][Para 74]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the 12 technicians were employees of E.Garment or of Auto Fin, and whether E.Garment must take them back into normal work? [Para 40][Para 90]
2. (Issue 2) Whether E.Garment must issue identity cards to the 12 technicians? [Para 52][Para 125]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must raise wages by 30 per cent in every section? [Para 60][Para 130]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must pay 50 per cent of wages to women workers before their maternity leave begins? [Para 63][Para 135]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the company must pay a 2,000 riel meal allowance for four hours of overtime? [Para 70][Para 146]
6. (Issue 6) Whether the company must convert monthly-paid workers to piece-rate and issue the piece prices in advance? [Para 78][Para 160]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the 12 are E.Garment workers because they worked at the factory from the outset, took leave and drew wages through the E.Garment supervisor Mr S, and were dismissed by him on 3 May 2007 after the union was formed. [Para 39][Para 42] They acknowledged wearing Auto Fin shirts and carrying Auto Fin cards but said they did not know they were Auto Fin employees. [Para 55] On maternity pay they argued that women with difficult circumstances and distant homes need the half-wage before they stop work. [Para 65] They said no worker was paid below the 50 USD minimum wage. [Para 81]

**Employer (respondent):** E.Garment refused to discuss issues 1 to 3 at all, because the 12 are Auto Fin workers and those questions are Auto Fin's. [Para 36][Para 37] Auto Fin confirmed that it had recruited the 12, some former E.Garment workers and one new, had given them its shirts, cards and shoes, and paid them. [Para 44][Para 48][Para 54] On maternity pay the company pays one month at a time for fear that women will take the money and not return. [Para 66] It already pays a 1,000 riel meal allowance whatever the overtime hours, and the buyer does not permit piece-rates. [Para 76][Para 87]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issue 4 was granted: the company must pay the 50 per cent maternity wage before the leave begins to the women workers on the 244-name list; Issues 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 were dismissed. [Para 177][Para 179]

On Issue 1 the panel asked which company actually controlled the 12, and read Articles 14, 65, 2 and 3 of the Labour Law defining the employment contract, the employer and the worker. [Para 92][Para 94][Para 96][Para 99] It found written contracts between Auto Fin and all 12 and none between E.Garment and any of them, that Auto Fin recruited them, fixed their wages and received the wage funds it channelled for payment at the factory, and that Auto Fin set their hours and leave. [Para 101][Para 105][Para 106][Para 108][Para 112] Article 45 made Auto Fin a labour contractor supplying workers for a lump price, and Article 47 made such a contractor answerable as an employer. [Para 115][Para 118] The panel therefore held Auto Fin the true employer and dismissed the reinstatement claim against E.Garment, while indicating the workers must pursue Auto Fin. [Para 119][Para 122][Para 124] Issue 2 was dismissed on the same reasoning, the cards and shirts in evidence being Auto Fin's. [Para 126][Para 129] Issue 3 fell with it: the past pay rises had followed E.Garment's evaluation passed to Auto Fin, but the employer of the 12 remained Auto Fin. [Para 62][Para 131][Para 134]

On Issue 4 the panel applied Article 183, under which a woman is entitled to half her wage during maternity leave, read with the proviso that the entitlement covers women who have worked in the enterprise at least one year. [Para 136][Para 139] Article 183 does not fix when the money is payable, but Article 115(3) forbids paying wages on a day the worker is entitled to rest and requires payment the day before if the due date falls on a rest day. [Para 140][Para 141] Following Awards 157/06 and 97/06, the panel held the half-wage must be paid before the leave starts and ordered it paid to the women on the 244-name list who take three months' maternity leave. [Para 143][Para 144][Para 145][Para 177]

Issue 5 was dismissed twice over: Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 point 4 gives 1,000 riel a day or one free meal without dividing overtime by hours, and the company already pays 1,000 riel for two hours. [Para 150][Para 151][Para 153][Para 178] The panel added that the demand concerns collective interests, and that it declines to consider interest disputes where the union is not most representative, which this union was not. [Para 156][Para 158][Para 159]

Issue 6 was dismissed because Article 2 of the Labour Law gives the employer the right to organise production, and monthly payment of wages is an accepted method; no legal complaint was raised about the production targets or the wage level. [Para 164][Para 168][Para 169][Para 172][Para 179]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a contractor supplies technicians to a factory under a service contract, recruits them on its own letterhead, fixes and channels their wages, sets their hours and leave, and holds the disciplinary power, the contractor is the employer under Articles 45 and 47 of the Labour Law and the host factory is not; reinstatement, identity cards and wage demands must be brought against the contractor. [Para 115][Para 118][Para 119] A union without most-representative status cannot obtain an award on a demand that concerns collective interests rather than a right. [Para 156][Para 158]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's invitation to the 12 to take their claims to Auto Fin, and its remarks that the employer may set reasonable production targets and that monthly wages are lawful, were not necessary to the orders made. [Para 123][Para 166][Para 170]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The header dates the order 13 July 2007, but the procedural narrative dates the transmission of the non-conciliation file 22 June 2017 at [Para 21]; the hearing itself is 2 July 2007 [Para 18]. The employer-party arbitrator's name and the chair's name each appear in two different spellings between the face of the award and the signature block, one character apart. The number of claimants shifts: 23 in the original demand, 12 in the hearing, a promised list of 500 and later 4,500, and 244 names actually filed [Para 2][Para 35][Para 67][Para 74]. The operative issue 4 paragraph is garbled, printing "450 per cent" and a doubled "50 per cent" before the ordered figure of 50 per cent [Para 177]. "Piece-rate" is printed inconsistently as "silo" and "piece wage" [Para 78][Para 168], and a sentence at [Para 153] is repeated verbatim at [Para 154].
