# Award No. 133/07 — The United Knitting MFG (Cambodia) Company Ltd (Arbitration Council, 17 December 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Liv Sovanna, Kong Phallack) · Doc ID 140664

## 1. Facts
Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU) and Khmer Youth Trade Union (KYTU) at The United Knitting MFG (Cambodia) Company Ltd complained to the Sihanoukville Department of Labour on 23 October 2007 over 21 working conditions at a factory of 1,200 workers. [Para 12][Para 17]
Conciliation failed because the employer was absent, the 21 issues were remitted on 20 November 2007, and at the hearing of 3 December 2007 the panel conciliated most of them, leaving Issues 4, 5, 7 and 10 for decision. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 15]
The union had not registered and never produced its membership list despite an order to file it by 7 December 2007. [Para 17][Para 19][Para 20]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must increase the seniority bonus by US$ 1 per year, beyond Notification 017, for workers with more than five years' service? [Para 20][Para 29]
2. (Issue 5) Whether the employer must pay 50 percent of wages and perquisites, and not merely 50 percent of basic wage, to women taking 90 days' maternity leave? [Para 22][Para 43]
3. (Issue 7) Whether the employer must build a day-care centre or instead provide three cans of milk powder or US$ 30 per month? [Para 23][Para 47]
4. (Issue 10) Whether the employer must deduct the 1,000-riel union contribution fee from the wages of members who authorised the deduction? [Para 25][Para 57]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** KYFTU/KYTU argued that Notification 017 dated 18 July 2000 stops at four years so a further US$ 1 a year was owed, that maternity pay must cover perquisites and not only basic wage, that the company had no day-care centre and so owed milk or US$ 30 a month, and that the 1,000-riel dues should be deducted for consenting members. [Para 20][Para 22][Para 23][Para 25]
**Employer (respondent):** The United Knitting MFG (Cambodia) Company Ltd said it already pays seniority bonus under Notification 017 and cannot afford an increase, would pay only 50 percent of the basic wage on maternity leave, had no day-care centre and asked the panel to decide, and refused the dues deduction because the union was unregistered. [Para 20][Para 22][Para 24][Para 25]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issue 4 declined, Issue 5 ordered at 50 percent of wages and perquisites by the stated calculation, Issue 7 ordered as a day-care centre or reimbursement of fees on actual receipts, and Issue 10 rejected. [Para 58]
On Issue 4 the panel set out Clause 5 of Notification 017 dated 18 July 2000, which reaches US$ 5 per month at four years' service, and Point 3 of Notification 745 dated 23 October 2006, which preserves those benefits. [Para 26][Para 27]
Because the Notification says nothing beyond five years and no agreement covered the point, the demand was an interests dispute, and the panel declined it for want of most representative status under Article 96(2B), Article 277 and Article 9(1) of Prakas 305, following Arbitral Awards 57/04 (Evergreen), 60/04 (United Art, Issue 3), 08/07 (Sui Qinh, Issue 3) and 33/07 (Gold Fame, Issue 2). [Para 28][Para 31][Para 32][Para 33]
On Issue 5 the panel held that Article 182 requires 90 days' leave, which the company grants, and that Article 183 gives half the wage including perquisites to women with a year of service. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 38]
Reading "wage" through Article 103, it held overtime and other period payments are wage, as it had in Arbitral Awards 49/04 (Hou Hing, Issue 2), 96/06 (Wilson, Issue 1) and 33/07 (Gold Fame, Issue 7), and that Article 183 leaves open which wage was meant, as noted in Arbitral Award 24/06 (Fortune, Issue 2). [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41][Para 42]
It adopted the calculation used in Arbitral Awards 68/04 (City New, Issue 4), 18/06 (GHG, Issue 3) and 119/07 (Royal Crowntex, Issue 7): twelve months' wages divided by twelve, multiplied by three and divided by two, with company maternity allowances added. [Para 44]
On Issue 7 the panel found Article 186 obliges an enterprise with at least one hundred women to set up a nursing room and creche, but does not require milk powder or a cash substitute, as held in Arbitral Awards 63/04 (Shine Well, Issue 2) and 68/04 (City New). [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47]
It also followed Arbitral Awards 83/04 (June Textile, Issue 1) and 24/06 (Fortune, Issue 3) in finding the milk demand contrary to the Government's breastfeeding policy, and ordered a centre for children over 18 months or reimbursement on receipts. [Para 47][Para 48]
On Issue 10 the panel read Article 129(2) and Clause 5(5) of Prakas 305 as requiring a written request fifteen days ahead, and held an employer must then deduct and remit dues, per Arbitral Awards 03/03 (Tonga, Issue 9), 05/03 (Top One Garment, Issue 1) and 16/05 (New Point, Issue 11). [Para 50][Para 51][Para 55]
Because the union was unregistered and produced neither the membership list nor written authorisations, it had no standing to collect dues and the employer owed no deduction. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a benefit beyond the ceiling of Notification 017, with no collective agreement behind it, is an interests dispute that the panel will decline where the union lacks most representative status and registration under Article 277. [Para 29][Para 31][Para 33]
Maternity pay under Article 183 is half of the wage as defined by Article 103 — overtime and period payments included, not basic wage alone — calculated over twelve months and spread across the 90 days. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44]
Article 186 requires a creche or nursing room but not a milk-powder or cash substitute, and a union-contribution deduction is owed only on written authorisation from a registered union. [Para 47][Para 55][Para 57]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the Government encourages breastfeeding over milk powder, which supported but did not decide the Issue 7 result. [Para 47]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
This is an unofficial English translation of the Khmer original and the OCR has fused words throughout ("the company", "percent", "wages", "nobligation", "contribution"), which I read through without silently repairing the quoted provisions. [Para 9][Para 26]
Paragraph 15 states that 17 issues were conciliated but lists only twelve numbers, and the operative part labels the seniority ruling "Issue 43" where the reasons discuss Issue 4; both are reported as printed. [Para 15][Para 58]
The union is styled "Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU)" in the head and "Khmer Youth Trade Union (KYTU)" in the body, and the currency appears as "US$", "USS" and "US$30" without spacing. [Para 3][Para 10][Para 20]
Paragraph 44 and paragraphs 50 to 53 contain duplicated draft text where two readings of the same provision were merged, so the calculation and the Prakas 305 wording are reported from the cleaner second reading. [Para 44][Para 51][Para 53]
Demand 21 identifies an individual worker by name in the source; the name is not reproduced here. [Para 10]
