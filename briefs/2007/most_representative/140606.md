# Award No. 43/07 — P.N.E Cambodia PTE Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ភី អែន អ៊ី ខេមបូឌា ភីធីអី អិលធីឌី) v សហជីព កម្មករ រោងចក្រ បាំក់ ភីអ៊ី (Arbitration Council, 30 May 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phalla, president; Ung Sothy; Liv Sovanna) · Doc ID 140606

## 1. Facts
The respondent, ក្រុមហ៊ុន ភី អែន អ៊ី ខេមបូឌា ភីធីអី អិលធីឌី, runs a garment-printing factory in Sangkat Tuek Thla, Khan Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, with 80 workers [Para 18][Para 19]. The claimant is សហជីព កម្មករ រោងចក្រ បាំក់ ភីអ៊ី, the only union in the factory, with 50 members [Para 20][Para 21]. Four demands went to Ministry conciliation; two stayed unresolved and reached the Council [Para 11][Para 12]. The union said the company paid a bonus (ប្រាក់ប៉ូវ) in place of seniority pay and asked that the two be separated, with seniority paid from May 2007 onwards, plus a US$5 monthly increase for every worker earning from US$50 up [Para 2][Para 4].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must separate the bonus payment from seniority pay (ប្រាក់អតីតភាពការងារ) and pay seniority from May 2007 onwards? [Para 22]
2. (Issue 2) Whether workers already earning the US$50 minimum were entitled to the US$5 monthly increase that Notification 745 gave to those below it? [Para 58]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The monthly sum was a bonus for skill and for the team-leader role, not seniority pay; the company converted it to seniority pay from 2005 without telling them, so they lost the bonus while gaining nothing [Para 33][Para 30]. They had earned US$50 since before Notification 745, yet got no raise, while workers who had been on US$45 received the US$5 increase [Para 62][Para 64].
**Employer (respondent):** The payment has been seniority pay since 2000, when Notification 017 was issued, not a bonus, and the payroll columns say so [Para 35][Para 36][Para 27]. Seniority has been paid at or above what the law requires, and the company applies Notification 806 of 15 December 2006 [Para 44][Para 5][Para 65].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both demands rejected; no sum was ordered in riel or dollars [Para 102][Para 103].
On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 103 of the 1997 Labour Law, which lists bonuses among the components of wage, so a bonus is wage [Para 83], and Article 112, which obliges an employer to inform workers clearly of their wage conditions at the time of engagement and at every change [Para 84]. The payrolls from 2003 to 2007 described the payment as seniority pay [Para 77][Para 78]. Because the employer did not show that the 2000 conversion had been clearly notified, the panel fixed the date the workers knew of it at 2005, following their own account at the hearing [Para 86][Para 81]. Workers had thus already received seniority pay, and the panel found they had lost nothing beyond the bonus itself [Para 88]; the demand was refused [Para 89].
On Issue 2 the panel read Notification 745 of 23 October 2006 as raising the sector minimum from US$45 to US$50 a month from 1 January 2007 [Para 61][Para 91], with Notification 806 of 15 December 2006 implementing it [Para 92]. It held that the notification guarantees only the US$50 floor and carries no meaning of a US$5 general increase [Para 96], so workers already above US$50 acquire no right to it [Para 97]. Following the reasoning in Award No. 33/07 (Gold Fame), Issue 6 [Para 98], the panel found the demand had no legal basis [Para 99] and was in the nature of a benefit claim exceeding what the law grants [Para 100][Para 101].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer pays a monthly sum it has entered on the payroll as seniority pay, and the workers have received that sum, the payment is seniority pay and not a bonus, even if it began life as a bonus; a worker who has already received seniority pay cannot recover the earlier bonus as well, and the employer's failure to notify the conversion clearly affects only the date from which the workers are taken to know of it [Para 78][Para 88][Para 86]. A notification raising the minimum wage sets a floor only: workers already paid at or above the new minimum gain no right to the amount of the increase [Para 96][Para 97].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that the union had 50 members but had not yet obtained most-representative status, a point it did not use in the decision [Para 21]; it also recorded that one of the twelve listed claimants had no employee number on the payroll, leaving eleven [Para 48].
