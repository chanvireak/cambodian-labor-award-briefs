# Award No. 45/07 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន វីល សិន ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) ខូលី មីធិត v សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ នៃ ក្រុមហ៊ុន វីល សិន (Arbitration Council, 25 June 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phalla, president; Chiv Phirom; An Norn) · Doc ID 140608

## 1. Facts
The employer, ក្រុមហ៊ុន វីល សិន ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) ខូលី មីធិត, runs a garment factory at Tra village, Chom Chao, Khan Dangkao, Phnom Penh, with about 1,100 workers [Para 1][Para 26]. The claimant is សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ នៃ ក្រុមហ៊ុន វីល សិន, reporting about 190 members of the 188 workers who signed the complaint [Para 2][Para 28][Para 31]. Three unions exist in the factory and none holds most-representative status [Para 29][Para 30]. The demands were a US$15 monthly payment in lieu of a crèche for children aged 18 months to 18 years, back payment of US$10 a month for 2000 to October 2006, and reinstatement of two dismissed workers [Para 32][Para 59][Para 66]. At the hearing the union reduced the age limit to six years and issue 3 was merged into issue 1 [Para 93][Para 22].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issues 1 and 3) Whether the company must keep paying US$15 a month in lieu of a crèche for children from 18 months until the child turns six years old? [Para 32][Para 93]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must back-pay US$10 a month for the period from 2000 to October 2006, when it paid only US$5? [Para 57][Para 59]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the company must take back the two dismissed workers, Mr S and Mr L, and restore them to normal work? [Para 66]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Article 186 of the Labour Law and the Council's award of 24 November 2006 require the US$15 payment to continue, and the panel should extend it to age 18 [Para 32]. They argued the real cost of child care is 60,000 to 70,000 riel a month, so US$10 should be repaid for the years when only US$5 was paid [Para 59]. On the dismissals they said the two had done nothing wrong and were replaced by new hires [Para 87][Para 131].
**Employer (respondent):** It will pay US$15 but only for children aged 18 to 36 months, because the 2006 award set no end age and sector practice stops at 36 months [Para 3][Para 37][Para 46]. It refused the back pay, saying it had already complied with award 96/06 [Para 5][Para 61], and refused reinstatement because severance was paid lawfully and hiring is the employer's right [Para 68][Para 90].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all four demands were refused, and no sum was ordered [Para 139][Para 140][Para 141].
On Issues 1 and 3 the panel applied Article 186, which requires an enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a nursing room and a crèche, and, where the enterprise cannot, allows the mother to place the child in a crèche at the employer's expense [Para 94][Para 96]. The article fixes no end age for that care [Para 97], and neither did the Council's earlier award 96/06 [Para 37][Para 99]. The panel held that expecting an employer to pay indefinitely was unreasonable [Para 100] and that the purpose of Article 186 is served once a child can use public kindergarten from 36 months [Para 103][Para 104]. A Ministry letter of 17 August 2000 recommending the 18-to-36-month range bound only the company it addressed and not the whole garment sector [Para 48][Para 109]; in the absence of contrary evidence, sector practice was 36 months [Para 110], so the US$15 payment for children of 18 to 36 months was correct [Para 111] and the claim to six years was refused [Para 112].
On Issue 2 the panel applied res judicata: a dispute already decided between the same parties on the same point cannot be reopened [Para 118][Para 119], and the earlier award had settled the childcare subsidy [Para 116][Para 120].
On Issue 4 the panel first addressed its own jurisdiction under Article 302, which defines a collective dispute by three cumulative conditions [Para 123][Para 125]. The employer argued that the Ministry had treated the matter as an individual dispute, but it gave no evidence to displace the panel's assumption, so jurisdiction was upheld [Para 127][Para 128][Para 129]. On the merits, the claimant produced no document or witness at all [Para 135] and the two dismissed workers did not attend the hearing [Para 136], so there was no case for the employer to answer [Para 137] and the demand was refused [Para 138].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Article 186 obliges an enterprise with at least 100 women workers to provide crèche care or to pay for it, but it sets no end age; the obligation is satisfied by care up to 36 months, because from that age public kindergarten is available and an indefinite obligation would be unreasonable [Para 97][Para 103][Para 111]. A Ministry letter of guidance addressed to one named company does not establish sector-wide practice [Para 109]. Where a union brings a collective dispute over a dismissal and produces neither documents nor witnesses, and the dismissed workers do not appear, the employer has no case to answer and the demand fails [Para 135][Para 136][Para 137]. A point already decided between the same parties cannot be relitigated before the panel [Para 118].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that no union in the factory yet held most-representative status, a fact it did not treat as a bar in this case [Para 30][Para 31]; it also observed that about 100 other workers were dismissed over the same months [Para 72][Para 75].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is dated 25 June 2007 and the hearing 12 June 2007, but the conciliation history is internally inconsistent: one paragraph leaves the month out of the 2006 filing date and another gives 26 April 2006 where 2007 is meant [Para 83][Para 86]. The Ministry letter cited as authority is dated 17 August 2000 in the reasons and 17 August 2007 in the exhibit list [Para 23][Para 48]. A paragraph on the company's compliance prints the monthly childcare payment as 25 dollars where every other reference, including the order, says 15 [Para 42][Para 36]. The worker-side arbitrator is named one way on the face of the award and differently on the signature page. OCR losses: the demand numbering skips and repeats (issue 3 appears as "36" in one heading) [Para 49]; a Latin-script fragment is embedded in a worker's name at the point where the panel asked for further submissions, so that name cannot be read [Para 79]; and the figure of 188 complainants is stated twice without reconciling it with the union's 190 members [Para 28][Para 31].
