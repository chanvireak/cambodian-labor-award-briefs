# Award No. 89/07 — Lotus Textile Garment (Cambodia) Company Limited v Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union and Khmer Youth Trade Union (Arbitration Council, 3 October 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Ven Pov (worker-appointed), Kong Phallack (chair) · Doc ID 140635

## 1. Facts
Lotus Textile Garment (Cambodia) Company Limited runs the Lotus II factory on Russian Federation Boulevard, Phnom Penh, employing around 3,826 workers [Para 1][Para 23].
Three unions organise there, the claimant Khmer Youth Trade Union (KYTU), affiliated to the Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU), with 199 members, plus two others of about 140 and 166 members; neither party said any union had most representative status [Para 24].
Conciliation settled three of seven issues and the remaining four were forwarded to the Council on 4 September 2007, where the panel could not settle them either [Para 18][Para 19][Para 21].
The union claimed for all workers in the factory, not only its members, but did not file the list of claimants ordered by 25 September 2007 [Para 24][Para 25].
The four demands were a share of profits equal to one year's main wage, US$1 for two hours of overtime including a meal allowance, payment of wages on the fifth of the month, and a US$5 increase in the attendance bonus [Para 26][Para 36][Para 43][Para 47].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must give each worker a share of profits equal to one year's main wage at the end of each year? [Para 26][Para 84]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay US$1 for two hours of overtime work, including a meal allowance? [Para 36][Para 85]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay wages on the fifth day of each month rather than the tenth? [Para 43][Para 86]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must increase the attendance bonus by US$5 per worker? [Para 47][Para 87]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), KYFTU and KYTU at Lotus:** production was going well and wages were paid regularly, so a profit share matching the main wage was due under Article 103 of the Labour Law; at a main wage of US$50 the benefit would be US$50 a year, and at US$60, US$60 a year [Para 26][Para 27][Para 28]. The union also sought US$1 for two hours of overtime because workers always cooperated when asked, payment on the fifth because workers need money, and an extra US$5 on the attendance bonus because the current US$5 is too little [Para 36][Para 43][Para 47][Para 80].
**Employer (respondent), Lotus Textile Garment (Cambodia) Company Limited:** the profit share is not in the Labour Law, and the company already pays bonuses of US$5 to US$30, US$40 and US$50 on merit, which is more than Article 103 requires [Para 28][Para 29]. Overtime is voluntary and paid at double time plus one on Sundays and holidays, better than the law; it has never paid wages late and needs the fifth to the tenth to compute, print, approve and transfer wages; and it already pays the US$5 attendance bonus required by Notification No. 017 without docking it for lateness of 15 minutes [Para 40][Para 45][Para 48].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider Issues 1, 2 and 4 as interests disputes and rejected Issue 3, so nothing was ordered against the company [Para 84][Para 85][Para 86][Para 87].
On Issue 1 the panel read Article 103 as defining the components of wages, including profit sharing, without obliging an employer to distribute profits, and found no law or agreement requiring a share of that size [Para 51]. The demand exceeded what the law provides and was therefore an interests demand [Para 53]. Under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 an award on an interests dispute replaces the collective agreement for a year and would remove other workers' right to strike [Para 54][Para 55], and only a union with most representative status under Article 277 may bring such a claim, which the KYTU lacked [Para 59][Para 60]. Following Awards 57/04 Evergreen, 60/04 United Art, 08/07 Xiao Kinh, 33/07 United Art, 51/07 Gold Fame and 70/07 L.A, the panel declined to consider the claim [Para 57][Para 59][Para 62].
Issue 2 fell the same way: no provision required US$1 for two hours, the demand exceeded the law, and the union lacked standing, so it was declined [Para 64][Para 65][Para 67]. The union's own arithmetic put the lawful minimum for two hours, at a US$50 wage with 1,000 riels of meal allowance and 4,000 riels to the dollar, at 2,923 riels [Para 38].
On Issue 3 the panel applied Article 116(2), requiring payment at least once a month but fixing no day, together with the employer's management right under Article 2 as explained in Award 81/05 Supreme and Awards 14/07 Supreme, 17/07 Charm Textile, 18/07 Trinungal and 54/07 Yung Hwa [Para 69][Para 71][Para 72]. Payment by the tenth was reasonable and lawful, so the demand for the fifth was rejected [Para 74][Para 76].
On Issue 4, Clause 3 of Notification No. 745 of 23 October 2006 preserved the benefits under Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000, whose Clause 3 sets the US$5 monthly attendance bonus that the company already paid [Para 77][Para 78][Para 79]. An increase to US$10 exceeded the law and, the union having no most representative status, was declined [Para 81][Para 83].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a benefit the Labour Law does not require, such as a profit share equal to a year's main wage or a wage above the notified minimum, is an interests demand, and the panel will decline to consider it unless the claiming union holds most representative status under Article 277 of the Labour Law [Para 53][Para 59][Para 61].
Declining such a demand is not a ruling on its merits; it protects other workers from an award that would replace the collective agreement for a year under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 and bar their future right to strike [Para 54][Para 55].
Article 116(2) fixes only the frequency of wage payment, so the employer's right to manage the enterprise under Article 2 lets it choose any pay day that is reasonable and no later than one month after the work [Para 69][Para 71][Para 74].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that the union had claimed for every worker in the factory rather than its own members, and that the ordered list of claimants was never filed, without resting the outcome on that failure [Para 24][Para 25].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The case number is printed as "89107" on the face of the award, evidently 89/07. As with the other English translations in this series, the letter "t" is dropped throughout ("he" for "the", "hat" for "that"), and some sentences are truncated mid-phrase, for example the statement that the current attendance bonus "is" [Para 47]. The reasoning repeats itself: Issue 1's statement of claim and the interests-dispute rule appear twice almost verbatim [Para 26][Para 50][Para 53][Para 66], and the award of 70/07 L.A is cited as if it were the panel's own earlier decision though it is a separate case [Para 59]. Membership figures for the two non-claimant unions are given only as approximations and the employer's financial report ordered for 25 September 2007 was never filed [Para 24][Para 36].
