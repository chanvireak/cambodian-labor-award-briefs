# Award No. 79/07 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន តេរ៉ា តិច (Tera Tech Co.) (Arbitration Council, 5 September 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៉ឹង សុទ្ធី / Ing Sothy, អន ណន / Orn Non, គង់ ផល្លា / Kong Phallach chairing) · Doc ID 140632

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន តេរ៉ា តិច (Tera Tech Co.) employs about 3,035 workers, of whom 400 were directly involved in this collective dispute brought by the សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា (Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers Democratic Union) [Para 1][Para 43][Para 44]. Of thirteen demands, two were conciliated before the panel and two more at the hearing, so the panel decided Issues 1, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10 and 11 [Para 31][Para 35][Para 36]. The live disputes concerned health-check fees for 444 workers from 1998 to 2003, short fixed-duration contracts, notice of piece rates, Saturday meal money, use of annual leave during slack periods, milk money and breastfeeding time, maternity pay, a childcare allowance, and union access to weekly meetings [Para 61][Para 72][Para 91][Para 101][Para 112][Para 255][Para 270][Para 285][Para 295]. A further issue on overtime meal money was settled by agreement that overtime is voluntary [Para 111].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must reimburse 100 riels of health-check fees to 444 workers for 1998 to 2003? [Para 61][Para 150]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the company must stop using short fixed-duration contracts, and fixed-duration contracts at all? [Para 163][Para 165]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the company must issue piece rates for each garment model within three days of production rather than five? [Para 191][Para 315]
4. (Issue 5) Whether the company must pay the meal allowance every Saturday rather than with the monthly wage? [Para 210][Para 217]
5. (Issue 7) Whether the company may apply workers' annual leave to periods when there is no work? [Para 235][Para 241]
6. (Issue 8) Whether the company must pay US$15 a month in lieu of breastfeeding time? [Para 255][Para 319]
7. (Issue 9) Whether the company must pay maternity wages and benefits for the whole leave at once, before the leave begins? [Para 270][Para 276]
8. (Issue 10) Whether the company must build a nursery or pay US$20 a month for childcare? [Para 285][Para 321]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា):** that health checks are the employer's cost and Article 120 does not bar the claim; that short fixed-duration contracts cost them seniority, union freedom, maternity pay and re-employment; that piece rates should follow within three days; that meal money should be paid weekly; that US$15 milk money and US$20 childcare were needed; and that maternity pay must be advanced before leave [Para 62][Para 152][Para 177][Para 198][Para 240][Para 268][Para 280][Para 137].
**Employer (respondent — ក្រុមហ៊ុន តេរ៉ា តិច):** that it pays health checks only from June 2004 under Prakas No. 1191 and Article 120 bars older claims; that Article 67 leaves the length of fixed-duration contracts to the parties and it converts to indefinite terms after two years; that its five-day piece-rate notice matches the 25 April 2007 agreement; that two other unions object to weekly meal payment, which would also attract withholding tax; and that its 24 May and 27 June 2007 agreements already fix milk money at US$6.25 and childcare at US$5 [Para 65][Para 181][Para 199][Para 220][Para 222][Para 265][Para 289].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must keep notifying piece rates within five days, pay the meal allowance every Saturday, allow one hour a day for breastfeeding, and pay 50 percent of three months' wages as maternity pay before leave begins; the health-check, contract, three-day notice, annual-leave, milk-money and childcare demands are rejected [Para 316][Para 317][Para 320][Para 321].
On Issue 1 the panel held health-check fees to be a debt arising from the labour contract, so the three-year limitation in Article 120 applied, as interpreted in Awards 05/06 Double U N D (Issue 1) and 47/07 Chung Fai (Issue 1); the claim reaching back to 1998 was time-barred even though the employer's duty to pay under Article 247 was common ground [Para 151][Para 156][Para 157][Para 160][Para 162].
On Issue 3 the panel applied Article 67, which caps a fixed-duration contract at two years but does not fix a minimum, and Article 22 of the Civil Code, holding that the parties may agree any shorter term; the workers offered no evidence that short contracts suppressed union rights or maternity pay, so both limbs failed [Para 181][Para 182][Para 184][Para 185][Para 190].
On Issue 4 the panel applied Article 112(a) and Award 62/04 Eisin (Issue 2): the employer must notify piece rates, and three to seven days after trialling a model is reasonable; the five-day practice in clause (a) of the 25 April 2007 agreement sits inside that range and was ordered to continue, while the three-day demand was rejected [Para 193][Para 194][Para 197][Para 202][Para 206][Para 316].
On Issue 5 the panel found that Notice No. 017 of 18 July 2000 requires either a free meal or 1,000 riels for overtime days, and that weekly payment costs the parties no more time than daily payment; the tax argument under Prakas No. 1173 did not defeat a right arising from the notice, so Saturday payment was ordered [Para 210][Para 213][Para 217][Para 232][Para 234].
On Issue 7 the panel read Article 170 as allowing annual leave at Khmer New Year or by agreement, and the 25 April 2006, 26 March 2007 and 27 June 2007 agreements as permitting the company to schedule leave in slack periods; a party may not resile from an agreement it signed, so the demand was rejected [Para 238][Para 245][Para 249][Para 253][Para 254].
On Issue 8 the panel applied Articles 184 and 186: breastfeeding time is a right in kind, and paying US$15 instead contradicts the policy favouring breast milk, so the money claim failed while the one-hour right was confirmed [Para 255][Para 260][Para 264][Para 269][Para 320].
On Issue 9 the panel applied Article 183, which gives half wages during maternity leave to women with a year's service, and Article 13: the 4 June 2007 agreement allowing only an advance of two months' wages made the worker worse off and was void, so 50 percent of three months' wages must be paid before the leave starts, following Awards 57/06 Evergreen (Issue 6) and 97/06 Niu Mek [Para 271][Para 279][Para 283][Para 284][Para 320].
On Issue 10 the panel found Article 186 silent on the amount of childcare cost and the 27 June 2007 agreement fixing US$5 reasonable and not contrary to the law, so the US$20 demand was rejected [Para 288][Para 292][Para 293][Para 321].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim for a sum owed under the labour contract, including health-check fees, is extinguished three years after it falls due under Article 120, and Article 247 does not revive it [Para 156][Para 160]. Conversely, an agreement that pays less than a statutory entitlement — advancing two months of maternity wages instead of half pay for three — is void under Article 13, and the statutory sum must be paid before the leave begins [Para 279][Para 283][Para 284].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that weekly meal payment would save both parties the time of daily collection, and its observation that Article 186 leaves the childcare figure to agreement [Para 219][Para 292].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award survives in Khmer only; the panel names are given above in both scripts, and the signature block at the end is so degraded that the second arbitrator's name is only partly legible [Para 1][Para 324]. The panel declined to consider Issue 11 for want of most representative status, so it is absent from §2 [Para 311][Para 322]. Numbers are printed inconsistently: the non-conciliation report is numbered 828 in one paragraph and the conciliation minutes are dated both 18 July and 25 August 2007, and the claim is put at 444 workers but the evidence at 388 [Para 27][Para 42][Para 69][Para 61]. Two paragraphs are visibly doubled by OCR, and references to Article 67 are garbled as Article 65 in one place [Para 165][Para 226].
