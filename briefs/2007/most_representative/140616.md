# Award No. 51/07 — Golden Fame International Bright City Netherly Ltd. (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្គោល ហ្វេម អ៉ិន ធើ ប្រាយ ស៊ីស នីត ធើរលី មីតធីត) (Arbitration Council, 12 July 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (កៅ ថា ច appointed by the employer, ស៊ីន គឹម ស៊ាន appointed by the workers, គង់ ជល្ហៈ chair) · Doc ID 140616

## 1. Facts
Golden Fame (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្គោល ហ្វេម អ៉ិន ធើ ប្រាយ ស៊ីស នីត ធើរលី មីតធីត) employs about 5,000 workers at Kampong Pring, Saang, Kandal, and has three in-house unions, none of them most representative. [Para 1][Para 52][Para 54][Para 55] The Democratic Union of Garment Workers of Cambodia (សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា, C.CAWDU) at the factory filed 21 demands; four were settled and 17 came to the panel. [Para 2][Para 41][Para 42] On 7 March 2007 the company terminated five workers of the dyeing section, four of them union activists and one a union adviser, paying notice, severance, damages and untaken leave under Articles 75, 89, 91 and 166. [Para 62][Para 63] Nine further demands failed for want of the claimant lists the panel had ordered, or because they were interest claims. [Para 248][Para 433]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the dismissal of the five dyeing-section workers was lawful, and whether they must be reinstated with wages from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 61][Para 195]
2. (Issue 4) Whether the company must pay a 1,000 riel meal allowance for every hour of overtime? [Para 89][Para 258]
3. (Issue 7) Whether the company must issue a monthly wage slip immediately to monthly-paid workers as it does for piece-workers? [Para 101][Para 294]
4. (Issue 8) Whether the company must issue piece-rates for each style to all sections before the work is done? [Para 105][Para 306]
5. (Issue 12) Whether the company must stop assigning workers to work outside their skill? [Para 133][Para 359]
6. (Issue 15) Whether new recruits must start on short contracts and move to fixed-term contracts, and whether workers with over two years become indefinite-contract workers? [Para 161][Para 419]
7. (Issue 16) Whether the company must honour its agreements of 24 November 2006 and 5 April 2007? [Para 171][Para 434]
8. (Issue 17) Whether the company must pay the year-end bonus not yet paid in the first round? [Para 189][Para 445]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the five were activists and an adviser with no misconduct; the union had notified the company and the Ministry of their status, so the terminations were discriminatory and they should return with back pay. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 71] They demanded 1,000 riel per overtime hour rising to 2,000 and 3,000 because wages no longer cover rising prices, piece-rates published in advance, and the 5 USD and 1 USD seniority additions. [Para 260][Para 306][Para 398][Para 402]

**Employer (respondent):** the terminations followed the employer's own will, not misconduct, and all statutory compensation was paid, including damages; it says it cannot take the five back. [Para 65][Para 67][Para 69] It pays 500 riel meal money for one overtime hour, 1,000 for two and 1,500 for three, as Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 requires 1,000 riel a day; it needs five months to build a wage-slip system. [Para 258][Para 266][Para 297][Para 303]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must reinstate the five dyeing-section workers and pay their wages from dismissal to reinstatement, pay 1,000 riel meal money for one overtime hour, and apply the two 2006-2007 agreements with six-month contracts; the other demands were dismissed or not considered. [Para 451][Para 454][Para 468]

On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 74 of the Labour Law: an indefinite contract may be terminated by either side, but never without a valid reason relating to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the needs of the enterprise. [Para 199][Para 201] The company admitted the five had committed no misconduct and terminated by its own will alone, so the terminations were unlawful. [Para 205][Para 207] It also found the five protected: the union had notified the company that they were activists and an adviser, and Prakas No. 305 protects members named in such a notification from the moment it is given, without any Ministry acknowledgement. [Para 221][Para 227][Para 230][Para 231] The panel held the dismissal discriminatory in consequence. [Para 234] Under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 it ordered reinstatement in the old positions with wages from dismissal to return. [Para 208][Para 240][Para 451]

Issue 4 was partly granted: Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 fixes meal money at 1,000 riel a day without dividing overtime by hours, so the company's 500 riel for the first overtime hour breaches it and 1,000 riel was ordered; paying 1,000 and 1,500 for two and three hours already met the standard, and the demand for 2,000 and 3,000 was an interest claim. [Para 262][Para 266][Para 267][Para 268][Para 271]

Issue 7 was dismissed: the panel accepted that building a payroll-slip system takes time and allowed the five months the company had agreed with the other union, so no immediate slip was ordered. [Para 302][Para 303][Para 304][Para 457]

Issue 8 was dismissed on its terms but produced a duty: Article 112(b) requires the employer to inform workers clearly of their wages whenever those wages change, and a new style changes piece earnings, so rates must be notified within seven days of trialling each style. [Para 308][Para 309][Para 318][Para 321][Para 323]

Issue 12 was dismissed: Article 2 gives the employer the right to deploy labour, the transfers were temporary, during the January-to-April lull, with the same pay, factory and shift, and cleaning electrical equipment is part of an electrician's work. [Para 366][Para 374][Para 379][Para 381][Para 462]

Issue 15 was dismissed: choosing short then fixed-term contracts is the employer's prerogative, and the claim that workers over two years become indefinite-contract workers failed because the union never filed the names, contract types and seniority the panel ordered. [Para 428][Para 430][Para 432][Para 433][Para 466]

Issue 16 was granted: the agreements of 24 November 2006 and 5 April 2007 required all workers whose contracts were cut to return on 1 April 2007 on six-month contracts, and the company instead issued three-month contracts and did not take everyone back. [Para 436][Para 442][Para 444][Para 468]

Issue 17 was dismissed for want of the list of the 20 workers said to be owed the year-end bonus. [Para 448][Para 450][Para 469]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Terminating an indefinite-term contract without a valid reason connected to the worker's aptitude, conduct or the needs of the enterprise is unlawful under Article 74, and the panel will order reinstatement with wages for the lost period under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 199][Para 207][Para 208] A union activist named in a notification to the employer is protected from dismissal once that notification is given; no Ministry acknowledgement is needed for the protection to attach, and a dismissal in breach is discriminatory. [Para 228][Para 230][Para 231] Meal money for overtime is fixed at 1,000 riel a day by Notification No. 017 and is not multiplied by the hours worked. [Para 262][Para 267]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that cleaning electrical equipment falls within an electrician's trade, and that five months is a reasonable period to build a payroll system, are explanatory only. [Para 303][Para 378]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's name is printed in at least four Khmer variants across the face, the findings and the reasons, including forms reading "ហ្គេល ហ្វេម" and "ហ្គោល ហ្វេ" [Para 1][Para 49][Para 52]. The order on Issue 1 garbles the list of reinstated workers, dropping one surname and inserting a fragment of another [Para 451]. The demand on Issue 14 is pleaded as five years' seniority at [Para 28] and five years at [Para 402], while the payout table in Notification No. 017 stops at four years [Para 408][Para 409]. Paragraph [Para 45] states that 179 issues remained in conciliation where the award elsewhere records 17, and the headcount of 5,000 workers [Para 52] sits against a union membership of 1,315 [Para 56].
