# Awards Nos. 124/07 and 128/07 — D.A Corporation Ltd (Arbitration Council, 18 December 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phyrum, Ven Pov, Ang Eng Thong chairing) · Doc ID 140661

## 1. Facts
D.A Corporation Ltd, a Phnom Penh factory of 600 workers, faced two collective disputes filed by the Khmer Youth Federation Trade Unions (KYFTU) and the Khmer Youth Trade Union (KYTU) [Para 51][Para 4]. Case 124/07 covered 11 demands, of which seven were conciliated; case 128/07 arose from a strike over nine further demands [Para 38][Para 39]. The panel issued an order on 8 November 2007 directing a return to work, and both parties then agreed to submit both files for settlement [Para 40][Para 42]. During the proceedings the company dismissed 29 workers for conduct said to have occurred on the picket line, and that dismissal became an additional issue [Para 83][Para 157]. The union had held its committee election on 23 October 2007 and applied for registration on 1 November 2007, but produced no receipt [Para 80][Para 143].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must maintain a meal allowance of 1,000 riels for each hour of overtime beyond two hours? [Para 53]
2. (Issue 2) Whether wages must be paid no later than the tenth day of the month? [Para 58]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must reimburse the 10,100 riels medical examination fee to workers who paid it themselves? [Para 61]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must deduct 1,000 riels in union dues from members' wages? [Para 65]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the company must warn the Team 2 and Team 3 leaders for their attitude and language towards workers? [Para 70]
6. (Additional issue) Whether the panel has jurisdiction over, and must order reinstatement of, the 29 workers dismissed during the arbitration? [Para 153]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the meal allowance had been paid for each extra hour and must continue, wages were needed earlier to pay rent, medical fees were the employer's burden, and the 29 dismissals punished workers for a lawful strike without any proof of threats or violence [Para 55][Para 60][Para 64][Para 176].
**Employer (respondent):** it would maintain the meal allowance but owed no hourly rate, paid on the fifteenth under a payroll cycle running from the sixth to the fifth, paid medical fees only for new recruits, had never provided a dues deduction service, and dismissed the 29 for serious misconduct during the strike [Para 57][Para 58][Para 64][Para 68][Para 85].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must reinstate the 29 dismissed workers; the meal allowance, pay date, medical fee, union dues and team-leader warning demands were all rejected [Para 178][Para 177].

On Issue 1, Clause 4 of Notification 017 of 18 July 2000 requires a meal allowance of 1,000 riels a day or one free meal for voluntary overtime, which the company already paid [Para 96][Para 97]. A demand for 1,000 riels for each hour beyond two hours exceeds the notification, so it is an interest demand, and the union lacked most-representative status under Article 96(2B), Article 277 and Article 9(1) of Prakas 305 [Para 99][Para 101][Para 103].

On Issue 2, Article 116(2) requires payment within each month but fixes no day, and Article 2 leaves supervision and direction to the employer, following Award 81/05-Supreme and others [Para 105][Para 106][Para 107]. Paying on the fifteenth for the sixth-to-fifth cycle was therefore lawful [Para 109].

On Issue 3, Article 247(A) and (C-4) makes pre-employment medical examinations the employer's expense, as held in Awards 63/04-Shine Well and 107/04-Jacqsintex [Para 111][Para 113]. Article 120 limits such claims to three years from the date the fee fell due [Para 115][Para 120]. Because the union produced no list of workers or dates of examination, the panel found no basis to quantify the claim and rejected it [Para 122][Para 127].

On Issue 4, Article 129(2) permits deduction on each worker's written authorisation, while Article 281 forbids employers from deducting dues; the panel read Article 281 as protecting unions from employer domination, so written authorisation makes the deduction obligatory [Para 129][Para 131][Para 132]. Article 268 conditions a union's rights on registration or on two months' silence after filing, and Article 5 of Prakas 305 requires 15 days' written notice [Para 135][Para 133]. The union had no proof of filing and no member list, so the demand failed, though the panel called this a transitional period [Para 143][Para 146][Para 147].

On Issue 5, Article 2 and Award 108/06-Trinunggal Komara place the power to warn within management's prerogative, and the workers had no evidence of the alleged statements [Para 148][Para 149][Para 152].

On the additional issue, Article 312 extends jurisdiction to matters arising after the non-conciliation report as a direct consequence of the dispute, and the dismissals of 12 November 2007 fell within that class [Para 157][Para 160][Para 161]. Article 331 protects non-strikers' freedom to work and Article 83(B-4) makes threats or assault serious misconduct [Para 162][Para 165]. The company showed only photographs that did not identify the workers or any threat, and brought no witness despite two hearings, so the serious-misconduct ground was unproven and reinstatement was ordered [Para 172][Para 173][Para 174][Para 175].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for an allowance at a rate higher than the notification provides is an interest demand, and the panel will decline it unless the claimant union holds most-representative status under Article 277 and Prakas 305 [Para 99][Para 101].

Where a notification requires a flat meal allowance rather than an hourly rate, the employer discharges its obligation by paying that allowance, and the panel will not rewrite it [Para 96][Para 97].

Article 116 fixes the interval, not the day, of wage payment, so the employer may set the pay date within the month under its Article 2 management authority [Para 105][Para 109].

Although Article 247 requires the employer to bear pre-employment medical costs, a claim unsupported by a list of workers and dates of examination cannot be quantified and will be rejected [Para 111][Para 122][Para 127].

A union that cannot show it filed for registration, and supplies no member list, has no absolute right to require dues deductions under Article 129(2) and Article 268 [Para 143][Para 146].

A dismissal during a pending arbitration is a direct consequence of the dispute under Article 312 and is within jurisdiction, and where the employer proves neither the identity of those who threatened non-strikers nor the threats themselves, reinstatement follows [Para 161][Para 174][Para 175].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel left open a negotiated termination payment should reinstatement prove impossible, and observed that the union's rights would crystallise on registration [Para 176][Para 144].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The medical fee is printed as 100 riels in the findings and 10,100 riels in the reasoning and order, and the demand is alternatively described as 10,100 riels for all workers [Para 61][Para 110][Para 114]. The worker count shifts between 30 and 29, and the decision bloc numbers the dues issue "Issue 45" [Para 75][Para 78][Para 177]. The procedural history is internally inconsistent: the complaint is said to have been received on 18 November 2007 although the non-conciliation report is dated 13 November, and the strike is dated both 6–16 November and "one week and two days" from 16 October [Para 37][Para 78]. Individual team leaders and union officers are named in full in the original; they appear here by team and office only.
