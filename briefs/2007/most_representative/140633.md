# Award No. 84/07 — Yung Wah Industrial (Cambodia) Company Ltd II (Arbitration Council, 14 September 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuochhun, Tuon Siphann, Pen Bunchhea chairing) · Doc ID 140633

## 1. Facts
Yung Wah Industrial (Cambodia) Company Ltd II runs a factory at Thmey Village, Ta Khmao, Kandal, with about 5,500 workers, and its Sample Unit has operated since 2003 [Para 1][Para 9]. The Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers Democratic Union (C.CAWDU) and the Cambodian Apparel Workers Democratic Union (CAWDU) brought one demand: that the monthly wage of 37 workers in the Sample Unit be raised to US$60 because they had served there more than three years [Para 2][Para 3][Para 13]. The 40 sample workers had been recruited out of ordinary sewing units of 70 to 100 workers by the Chief of Unit, who promised a wage increase without naming an amount; only three had since received one, all of them chiefs or specialists [Para 10][Para 11][Para 14][Para 15]. Their wage is the US$50 statutory minimum, while in the sewing units they had earned piece rates [Para 15][Para 32]. Three hearings were held and conciliation failed [Para 6][Para 7].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the 37 workers have a right under the Labour Law to demand that their wage be raised to US$60? [Para 30][Para 31]
2. Whether the Director, or someone authorised on his behalf, agreed to increase the wages of the Sample Unit workers? [Para 38][Para 39]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — C.CAWDU and CAWDU):** that the Chief of Unit had promised a wage increase when recruiting them, that the work is harder than sewing because they sew whole shirts, that they are outstanding and fast workers, and that if any increase is given it must go to all 37 rather than half chosen by capacity [Para 14][Para 16][Para 19][Para 44].
**Employer (respondent — Yung Wah Industrial (Cambodia) Company Ltd II):** that the Chief of Unit could recruit but had no authority to grant increases, that wages follow capacity under Clause 5 of the Internal Work Rules and are reviewed yearly, and that it had offered US$60 to half of the 37 and needed one month to evaluate the rest [Para 17][Para 18][Para 22][Para 23][Para 39].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declines to consider the demand that the company raise the minimum wage to US$60 for the 37 workers [Para 62].
On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 104 of the Labour Law and Notification No. 745/06, setting the minimum at US$45 in probation and US$50 thereafter; since the 37 were paid US$50, no legal obligation to pay US$60 arose, following Award 23/07 Yung Min (Issue 1) [Para 31][Para 32][Para 33]. Article 106, equal pay for equal work, was held inapplicable because no other worker has the same job role and output — other sample workers lack their seniority and sewing-unit workers sew only part of a shirt [Para 33][Para 34][Para 36][Para 37].
On Issue 2 the panel found the Chief of Unit had authority to recruit and propose but not to approve, so her oral promise bound nothing, and Article 5 of the Internal Work Rules bases wages on capacity [Para 41][Para 42][Para 45]. It added that Article 2 of the Labour Law, read with Awards 17/03 and 18/03 Ho Hing (Issue 1), gives the employer the right to manage and to evaluate who deserves an increase, subject to the law and reasonableness; a worker who proves equal capacity and higher performance than a better-paid colleague would be entitled to an increase, and the company should adopt a performance-evaluation policy so workers can understand their standing [Para 46][Para 50][Para 51][Para 53]. The offer of US$60 to half the workers and a month's evaluation of the rest was found reasonable [Para 54].
The panel then held the demand to exceed anything the law or an agreement provides, making it an interest dispute, and applied Article 277 and Clause 6 of Notification No. 305/01 on most representative status, with Clause 43 of Prakas 099/04 on the effect of an award in an interest dispute [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58][Para 59]. As CAWDU lacked that status, the panel declined to consider the demand, following Awards 81/04 Evergreen (Issue 4), 99/04 Eternity (Issue 9) and 09/05 Kin Tay (Issue 2) [Para 9][Para 61][Para 62].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a wage above the statutory minimum is an interest demand, and a union without most representative status has no standing to bring one, so the panel will decline to consider it even where the employer's own pay policy is criticised [Para 58][Para 60][Para 61]. The employer's right to manage includes deciding who merits an increase, but a worker who shows equal capacity and better performance than a better-paid colleague is entitled to one [Para 51].

**Obiter dicta:** that the company ought to adopt a performance-evaluation policy so that workers understand their capacity and stop demanding increases, and that its half-now, half-after-evaluation offer was reasonable [Para 53][Para 54].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The case number is printed as "84107" in the header but appears as 84/07 in the file name, and the non-conciliation report is numbered 174 on one line and 175/07 on the next for the same report [Para 5][Para 6]. The Internal Work Rules are dated 26 May 2006 in the evidence list and 29 May 2006 in the reasons, and their wage clause is called Clause 5 in one place and Article 5 in another [Para 8][Para 17][Para 42]. The union's statute is dated 23 September 2007, after the award date of 14 September 2007 [Para 8][Para 1]. The reasons repeatedly refer to the Chief of Unit by a personal name that the brief replaces with her role, and OCR has dropped the letter "t" throughout [Para 10][Para 15].
