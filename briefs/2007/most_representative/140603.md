# Award No. 39/07 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន សាន ហ្គា មិន (Arbitration Council, 04 May 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៊ុក រី for the employer party, អន ណន for the worker party, ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា chair) · Doc ID 140603

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន សាន ហ្គា មិន, a factory at Damnak Thom, Stung Mean Chey, Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employing about 1,852 workers since 1997, told its staff in January 2007 that monthly wages would be replaced by payment per piece (ស៊ី បុង) from March 2007. [Para 1][Para 20][Para 32][Para 34] Workers organised in សុហជីព សេរី កម្មករ នៃ ព្រះរាជាណាចក្រ កម្ពុជា and សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ ប្រចាំ រោងច្បាស ស្នេហ៍ សាន raised seven demands; four were settled in conciliation on 28 March 2007 and three came before the panel. [Para 1][Para 12][Para 13][Para 14] After the change the workers struck from 09 to 11 April 2007, and 127 former San I workers demanded that the USD 5 transport allowance paid since 2001 be folded back into their base wage. [Para 36][Para 49] The extra meal allowance claim for overtime from 06:00 to 08:00 pm was not decided, the union not being the most representative in the factory. [Para 58][Para 63][Para 70][Para 119]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer may convert monthly-paid workers to piece-rate pay? [Para 71][Para 120]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the USD 5 transport allowance of the 127 transferred workers must be included in their base wage? [Para 100][Para 121]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សុហជីព សេរី កម្មករ នៃ ព្រះរាជាណាចក្រ កម្ពុជា and សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ ប្រចាំ រោងច្បាស ស្នេហ៍ សាន:** piece rates are set without a standard and at low prices, so long-serving workers fall below their old base wage and fear dismissal after three short months; the company already folded the USD 5 travelling allowance into base pay once and must keep doing so. [Para 4][Para 37][Para 42][Para 49]
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន សាន ហ្គា មិន:** piece work lets workers earn more and the company tops up anyone below the base wage; the USD 5 is a travel subsidy for coming from Chak Angre to the new factory and cannot be treated as wage, especially now that the minimum has risen to USD 50. [Para 4][Para 6][Para 40][Para 48][Para 108]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer keeps the right to convert monthly wages to piece rates provided the change guarantees the wages and benefits the workers previously received, and must fold the USD 5 transport allowance back into the base wage of the 127 transferred workers as agreed on 19 June 2001. [Para 120][Para 121]

On issue 2 the panel read Article 2 of the Labour Law as giving the employer the right to manage the enterprise, including the choice of which sections are paid monthly and which by the piece, so long as that right is exercised lawfully and reasonably. [Para 74][Para 78] Article 108 recognises piece-rate pay but requires the rate to yield at least the guaranteed minimum wage to a worker of average skill, and the panel found the conversion could affect wages and other benefits that had crystallised at USD 60 to 70 a month and had become a condition of the contract. [Para 79][Para 81][Para 83][Para 89][Para 92] It therefore upheld the right to convert only on condition that the earlier wage and benefits are guaranteed, following award 109/06. [Para 86][Para 93][Para 99] On issue 3 it held that notice No. 745 of 23 October 2006 raises the floor to USD 50 and does not void existing contracts, so the 19 June 2001 agreement folding USD 5 of travel money into the base wage of 45 USD survives. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 111][Para 112][Para 115] Arbitrator អ៊ុក រី dissented on issue 2, reasoning that conversion is the employer's right and a worker who loses pay after conversion must complain then, not in advance. [Para 123][Para 125][Para 128]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may move workers from a monthly wage to piece rates under its managerial right in Article 2 of the Labour Law, but the conversion is lawful only if it guarantees the wages and benefits already earned; a settled monthly level of pay becomes a contractual condition that the switch may not reduce. [Para 89][Para 92][Para 93][Para 99]

**Obiter dicta:** a worker whose earnings actually fall after the conversion may bring a fresh complaint at that time, which the panel said is the more appropriate moment to judge the change. [Para 125][Para 128]
