# Award No. 115/07 — Whitext Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 21 November 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun, Ven Pov, Kong Phallack chairing) · Doc ID 140656

## 1. Facts
Whitext Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a Phnom Penh factory of 554 workers, faced a collective dispute brought by the Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU) and the Khmer Youth Trade Union at Whitext Factory (KYTU) [Para 22][Para 3]. Of 13 demands raised on 6 September 2007, 11 were settled in conciliation and two were referred to the panel: the attendance bonus for three days' authorised absence, and prior notice of contract expiry [Para 13][Para 14]. The panel heard the case on 14 November 2007 and could not conciliate either point [Para 16].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must maintain the US$5 attendance bonus when workers are absent for three days with the employer's permission? [Para 24]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must give prior notice of the expiry of employment contracts, and not renew them for less than six months? [Para 26]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** 400 workers, 300 of them union members, sought the full bonus for authorised absence and advance notice of expiry so that they would know when their contracts ended and were renewed [Para 26][Para 45].
**Employer (respondent):** the bonus is kept for annual leave, special leave and sick leave only, under Notification 017, and prior notice is owed only for contracts longer than six months under Article 73 [Para 25][Para 46].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both demands were rejected; the company need not maintain the US$5 attendance bonus for three days' authorised absence, though it may only deduct in proportion to the days absent, and need not give notice of expiry where contracts run less than six months [Para 50][Para 51].

On Issue 1, Point 3 of Notification 745 of 23 October 2006 preserves the benefits in Points 3, 5 and 6 of Notification 017 of 18 July 2000, whose Point 3 grants at least US$5 a month to workers who attend regularly [Para 32]. The panel repeated its earlier reading that the notification never states how many days count as regular attendance, citing Awards 62/04-Ecent, 63/04-Shine Well, 15/05-Ving Tay II, 48/05-Manhattan and 57/07-Siratext [Para 32]. Article 103 brings bonuses within wage, and Article 71(6) suspends the contract during absence authorised by the employer [Para 33][Para 34]. Under Article 72(1) suspension releases the employer from paying for days not worked, unless a contrary provision applies [Para 35][Para 36]. The bonus is therefore wage that need not be paid for an authorised absent day, and may be deducted in proportion to days absent, as ordered in Awards 57/07-Siratext and 106/07-M&V III [Para 38][Para 40][Para 42]. Because the worker did not work those days, the demand for the full US$5 failed [Para 43][Para 44].

On Issue 2, Article 73(5) requires ten days' notice of expiry or non-renewal only where the fixed-duration contract exceeds six months [Para 47][Para 48]. The panel followed Award 107/04-Jacqsintex and held that shorter contracts carry no such duty [Para 49]. Most contracts at the factory ran less than six months and were renewed for six months, so the demand had no legal basis [Para 49][Para 50].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus is part of the wage under Article 103, but where a worker is absent with the employer's authorisation the contract is suspended under Article 71(6) and Article 72(1) excuses payment for those days; the employer must therefore pay the bonus for days worked and may deduct only in proportion to days absent, never the whole sum [Para 38][Para 40][Para 41].

The ten-day notice duty in Article 73(5) attaches only to fixed-duration contracts longer than six months, so a demand for notice on shorter contracts is not one the law provides [Para 48][Para 49].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the union claimed 300 members but did not hold most-representative status, and that the worker party never produced the name list of the 400 claimants despite an order of 19 November 2007 [Para 23][Para 26].
