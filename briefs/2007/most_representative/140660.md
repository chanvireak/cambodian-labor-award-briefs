# Award No. 118/07 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ថា ឌីង (Cha Ding) (Arbitration Council, 22 November 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ri, Ven Pov, Kong Phallack chairing) · Doc ID 140660

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ថា ឌីង (Cha Ding), a Phnom Penh factory of about 250 workers, faced a collective dispute filed by សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ and សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ (Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union and its factory union) [Para 32][Para 2]. Eight demands went to conciliation and none settled, the employer being absent without reason [Para 17][Para 18]. The employer was absent from both hearings, on 14 and 19 November 2007, so the panel sat in camera and decided on the union's evidence [Para 20][Para 23]. The union claimed about 180 members, and no union in the factory held most-representative status [Para 34][Para 35].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must grant leave when a worker has an urgent personal need? [Para 36]
2. (Issue 2) Whether overtime must be organised on a voluntary basis only? [Para 41]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must provide a medical room with a nurse on duty and adequate medicine? [Para 45]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must build a day-care centre, or instead provide three one-kilogramme tins of milk and USD 20 a month? [Para 47]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the company must pay all accrued annual leave in one lump sum instead of 1.5 days a month? [Para 49]
6. (Issue 6) Whether 60 probationary workers with more than two months' service must be made full-status with back pay? [Para 51]
7. (Issue 7) Whether the company must warn the section A and section C managers for improper conduct and language? [Para 54]
8. (Issue 8) Whether the company must deduct 1,000 riel union dues from members who agreed to it? [Para 55]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company never granted leave for short urgent absences of 10 to 25 minutes, forced overtime from 4pm to 9pm or 10pm without notice and blocked workers from leaving, had no nurse or medicine for workers who fell ill, and withheld the day-care benefit and statutory conversion of probationary staff [Para 38][Para 41][Para 46][Para 51].
**Employer (respondent):** the company did not appear at either hearing and filed no defence or documents, so its position appears only through the conciliation record, where it refused the dues deduction and the warnings [Para 20][Para 23][Para 55].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must allow leave where a worker's health requires it, apply the voluntary principle to overtime, engage one occupational-health nurse or medic with primary-treatment medicine, build a day-care centre or reimburse actual outside childcare invoices, and convert probationary workers with over two months' service to full status with back pay; the lump-sum annual leave, milk allowance and dues demands were rejected, as were the warnings [Para 141][Para 147][Para 148][Para 149].

On Issue 1, Article 2B(2) gives the employer management authority, but the panel held that refusing an exit when a worker is unwell is neither reasonable nor lawful under Article 229 on hygiene and worker health, while refusing time off to receive visiting relatives is a lawful management choice [Para 64][Para 65][Para 68].

On Issue 2, Prakas No. 80 of 1 March 1999, Article 4, forbids compulsion or discipline against workers who decline overtime [Para 70]. Because the company did not let workers go home even when they refused, the panel ordered overtime to rest on consent [Para 72][Para 74].

On Issue 3, Article 238 requires primary treatment for workers, Article 239 requires an occupational-health service led by a doctor, and Prakas No. 330 of 6 December 2000 requires a permanent infirmary from 50 workers and sets staffing by headcount [Para 76][Para 78][Para 79][Para 81]. The company had none, so a nurse and primary medicine were ordered [Para 84][Para 86].

On Issue 4, Article 186 requires a nursing room and day-care centre where at least 100 women are employed, and Article 187 leaves conditions to a ministerial praksas [Para 87][Para 89]. The Labour Law does not authorise substituting milk or cash, so that part was rejected, but where a centre cannot take children over 18 months the employer must pay actual invoices [Para 91][Para 94][Para 145].

On Issue 5, Article 167 grants annual leave only after a year and Article 166 governs compensation on termination; a collective agreement or waiver providing cash in lieu is void, and leave may not be deferred beyond three years [Para 98][Para 99][Para 100][Para 102]. Both the monthly buy-out and the demand for immediate payment were therefore unlawful [Para 104][Para 107].

On Issue 6, Article 166(3) treats a worker averaging 21 days a month as permanently employed, and Article 68 caps probation, so workers serving more than two months become full-status [Para 109][Para 111]. The order was conversion plus back pay [Para 115].

On Issue 7, Article 26 bars discipline once the employer has known of the fault for more than 15 days, and the incidents dated from July 2007, four months before the hearing, so the right was lost [Para 116][Para 123]. Article 27 leaves the decision to discipline within management's discretion [Para 124][Para 126].

On Issue 8, Article 129(2) and Article 5 of Prakas No. 305 allow deduction of dues only on each member's written authorisation [Para 127][Para 129]. The union supplied a name list but no individual written requests, so the employer owed no deduction [Para 137][Para 138].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer's management authority does not extend to refusing a worker a short exit when illness requires it, because Article 229 obliges every workplace to maintain conditions necessary for workers' health [Para 64][Para 68].

Overtime is voluntary under Article 4 of Prakas No. 80, and preventing a worker from leaving when overtime is refused breaches that principle and cannot stand [Para 70][Para 73].

Leave under Article 167 may not be converted into cash by agreement, and neither a monthly buy-out nor a demand for immediate payment of accrued leave is lawful [Para 100][Para 104].

Probation longer than two months makes the worker permanent under Article 68, entitling them to full status and back pay [Para 111][Para 115].

The right to discipline is lost 15 days after the employer learns of the fault under Article 26 [Para 116][Para 123].

Union dues may be deducted only against each member's written authorisation under Article 129(2) and Prakas No. 305 [Para 127][Para 138].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the employer's absence left its defences untested, and that no union at the factory had most-representative status [Para 23][Para 35].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer is named ក្រុមហ៊ុន ថា ឌីង in the party block but ចា ឌីង in the case name and evidence lists, and the union is printed in two forms that differ by one word [Para 1][Para 28]. The award date is 22 November 2007 while the second hearing was held on 19 November, and one procedural paragraph inserts a stray "2016" into the union's name [Para 1][Para 16]. Several findings are duplicated by OCR, the decision ordering Issue 3 is broken mid-sentence, and the two section managers are identified by transliterated nicknames only [Para 34][Para 143][Para 121]. Individual representatives and members are not reproduced here.
