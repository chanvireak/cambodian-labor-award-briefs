# Award No. 05/07 — Jim Li (Cambodia) PTY Co (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ជី ប លី (ខេមបូឌា) ភីធី អ៊ី) (Arbitration Council, 14 February 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — employer-appointed អ៊ុក រី, worker-appointed ស៊ីន គីម ស៊ាន, president តាំង ទ្រី · Doc ID 140584

## 1. Facts
The Khmer Youth Union at the Jim Li factory (សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ នៃ រោងចក្រ ជីម លី) brought nine demands against Jim Li (Cambodia) PTY Co (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ជី ប លី (ខេមបូឌា) ភីធី អ៊ី), a garment employer in Ang Snuol district, Kandal province [Para 1][Para 14]. Conciliation on 4 January 2007 settled six points and left three unsettled [Para 15]. At the hearing the union withdrew the third, a demand that the sanitation section work ordinary hours, after eleven cleaners thumbprinted a refusal saying they had never made it [Para 39][Para 41][Para 103]. The union had applied on 12 January 2007 to register with the Ministry but had not yet received its certificate [Para 37][Para 38].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether workers may take two consecutive days of leave in a month on necessary personal business while keeping the USD 5 regular-attendance bonus? [Para 2][Para 45]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay wages on the 5th of the following month every month? [Para 5][Para 82]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant union):** Workers need two consecutive days a month to travel to distant provinces to see sick parents or attend family ceremonies, and the attendance bonus must not be cut for that absence [Para 21][Para 45][Para 46]. They also asked for wages on the 5th because they need money for daily living and travel, the company having usually paid only on the 10th or occasionally the 11th [Para 32][Para 85].

**Employer (respondent):** Two consecutive days off would block the production line, especially where a worker invites friends from her team to a ceremony, and the company already agreed with C.CAWDU on one day in each half of the month, with separate-day leave built into its computer system from April 2007 [Para 22][Para 23][Para 25][Para 26]. It said it cannot pay before the 10th because the parent company transfers funds only on the 8th and cash must be withdrawn and placed in pay envelopes for security [Para 34][Para 35].

## 4. Award and reasoning
On Issue 1 the panel noted first that the words "request leave" in the demand reach only lawful leave, a reading both sides accepted [Para 49][Para 50]. It found that point 3 of Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 gives the USD 5 attendance bonus, and that the company's internal rules, Article 5.8 registered on 20 June 2006 with the Kandal labour department, preserve the bonus for annual and public-holiday leave but pro-rate it by days worked for sick leave [Para 52][Para 59][Para 60][Para 61][Para 62]. It held that absence for necessary personal reasons is like absence through illness, and that forfeiting the whole USD 5 for lawful leave would be unjust because the absence is not the worker's fault [Para 63][Para 69][Para 72], following Awards No. 45/05 and No. 48/06 which require the bonus to be pro-rated to days worked [Para 70][Para 75].

It nevertheless refused to decide the claim. A demand going beyond the benefit the law gives is an interest dispute, and only the most representative union may bring an interest dispute to the Council, as held in Awards No. 09/05 and No. 07/06 [Para 77][Para 78][Para 79][Para 80]. The Khmer Youth Union at Jim Li was not the most representative union and its registration was still pending, so the panel declined to consider the demand [Para 80][Para 81].

On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 115 of the Labour Law, which forbids paying wages on a day the worker is entitled to rest, and Article 116, which requires at least two payments a month at intervals of no more than sixteen days [Para 90][Para 91]. It found that the Labour Law fixes no specific payday and that leaving the date to the employer preserves flexibility according to the size of the operation [Para 94][Para 95]. The company's practice of paying by the 10th, earlier where the 10th falls on a holiday or Sunday, matches Article 5.1 of its registered internal rules, and the panel saw no serious problem in it [Para 97][Para 98]. Although paying once a month does not comply with Article 116, the workers had not pleaded that point, so it was not an issue before the panel [Para 92][Para 93].

**Outcome:** dismissed — both remaining demands were rejected, and the award was issued as a binding order taking effect on notification [Para 105][Para 106][Para 107].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that exceeds the benefit the Labour Law grants — such as two consecutive days of leave a month with the attendance bonus preserved — is an interest dispute that only the most representative union may bring, so a union whose registration certificate is still pending cannot have it decided; and because the law fixes no payday, an employer that pays by the 10th under a registered internal rule complies, Article 116's twice-monthly requirement being inapplicable unless pleaded [Para 77][Para 80][Para 94][Para 98].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel stated that the attendance bonus under Notification No. 017 should be pro-rated rather than forfeited where absence is lawful and outside the worker's control, though it decided nothing on that ground for want of jurisdiction [Para 72][Para 75].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The hearing is dated 05 February 2006 in an award issued on 14 February 2007, and a demand refers to a 41-day travel period that no fact supports [Para 1][Para 14][Para 45]. The employer's name is printed in at least four spellings, including one in which the middle syllable changes [Para 28][Para 59][Para 75]. The operative recital of Article 5.8 of the internal rules is corrupted into a phrase about national economics and government that cannot be the rule's text [Para 62]. Notification No. 745 is dated 23 October 2007 although it is a 2006 instrument [Para 52]. The non-conciliation report is dated 27 October 2006 yet recorded as reaching the Council on 22 April 2007, three months after the file itself arrived [Para 12][Para 16]. Article 116 is once printed as a four-digit article number [Para 93]. The signature block is partly illegible [Para 108].
