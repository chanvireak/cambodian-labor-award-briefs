# Award No. 41/07 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន អ៊ឹម និង វ៉េ ម៉ាញ៉ូ ហ្វាក់ ឈើ រឹង អែល ធីឌី (សាខា ទី៣) (Arbitration Council, 28 May 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (មាំរ សំបូរ ណា for the employer party, ស៊ីន គីម ស៊ាន for the worker party, តាំង ទ្រី chair) · Doc ID 140601

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន អ៊ឹម និង វ៉េ ម៉ាញ៉ូ ហ្វាក់ ឈើ រឹង អែល ធីឌី (សាខា ទី៣), a furniture factory on National Road 2, Chak Angre Krom, Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, with 2,817 workers and three unions, put its ironing section to work on item PN0623008 from 23 April 2007 at USD 0.35 per lot. [Para 1][Para 32][Para 34] The ironing workers, more than 40 of them, demanded USD 0.60 per lot and struck from the afternoon of 24 April 2007; they later reduced the demand to USD 0.45, and the company had meanwhile moved from 0.35 to 0.37, then 0.38, and finally to 0.40 per lot, which it applies in payroll. [Para 3][Para 33][Para 36][Para 37][Para 39][Para 40] The workers are represented by សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ឧស្សាហកម្ម កម្ពុជា, which states it is not yet the most representative union at the factory and has about 700 members. [Para 1][Para 43] Conciliation failed on the single demand. [Para 12][Para 15]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must raise the price of lot PN0623008 from USD 0.35 to USD 0.45 per lot, and guarantee a daily minimum wage of USD 1.92 where a worker cannot earn it? [Para 53][Para 55][Para 56]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ឧស្សាហកម្ម កម្ពុជា and the ironing workers:** the price per lot must be fixed at USD 0.45 and, where a worker cannot earn the minimum, the company must guarantee a daily wage of USD 1.92; the demand began at 0.60 and was already a concession. [Para 42][Para 54][Para 55][Para 56]
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន អ៊ឹម និង វ៉េ ម៉ាញ៉ូ ហ្វាក់ ឈើ រឹង អែល ធីឌី (សាខា ទី៣):** the highest price it can pay for PN0623008 is USD 0.40 per lot, which it already applies, and where a worker falls short of the minimum wage the company tops up the wage automatically at each pay day. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 57]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the demand that the price of lot PN0623008 be raised from USD 0.35 to USD 0.45 per lot. [Para 71][Para 72]

The panel first looked for a rule fixing piece rates and found no article of the Labour Law that sets the price of a lot outside the calculation of wages; what Article 108 does require is that a worker of average skill working normal hours receive at least the guaranteed minimum wage. [Para 58][Para 59] It accepted the company's table showing 26 floating ironing workers over nine working days from 25 April to 04 May 2007, and its own count from that table was that roughly 73% earned more than USD 1.92 a day, some up to USD 7, 8 or 10. [Para 45][Para 46] But the table nowhere recorded the hours each worker actually worked, and hours worked are an essential element of the Article 108 comparison, so the panel could not measure the skill of the floating workers against the ironing-section workers. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 51] It also discounted the data for 24 April 2007, when the workers stopped in the afternoon to join the strike, and held the single-day figures the workers produced insufficient to show the rate breached Article 108. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 66][Para 69][Para 70] Article 116(1) requires wages to be paid at least twice a month at intervals of no more than sixteen days, and the panel found the company paid on the 5th and 20th, so that requirement was met. [Para 67][Para 68] On that evidence it declined to fix a new price and dismissed the demand. [Para 71][Para 72]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An arbitration panel will not itself set a piece rate: where workers claim that a rate yields less than the minimum wage guaranteed by Article 108 of the Labour Law, they must produce records of the hours each worker actually worked, because without hours worked the output figures cannot show what a worker of average skill working normal hours would earn, and the claim fails for want of proof. [Para 49][Para 51][Para 58][Para 70]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that no article regulates the price of a lot apart from wage calculation, and its finding that the company already pays wages twice monthly under Article 116(1), are general remarks that do not by themselves decide the demand. [Para 59][Para 67][Para 68]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The demand is stated as USD 0.60 per lot in the issues section and as USD 0.45 in the reasons and the operative order; the employer's offers appear as 0.37, 0.38, 0.40 and, in the issues section, 0.40 — all reported as printed. [Para 3][Para 37][Para 5][Para 72]
- The productivity table contradicts the surrounding text: it records 0 of 26 workers below USD 1.92 on most days, while the reasons speak of only 27% reaching that level and elsewhere of 73% above it. [Para 45][Para 46]
- Dates in the reasoning are corrupted, one day appearing as "ថ្ងៃទី ២៣១ ខែមេសា" and another as "៨ ខែមេសា" where the table covers 23 and 24 April 2007. [Para 61][Para 62]
- The employer's name is printed three ways: អ៊ឹម និង វ៉េ ម៉ាញ៉ូ ហ្វាក់ ឈើ រឹង អែល ធីឌី in the header, "អ៊ឹម & វ៉េ" in the body, and "អ៊ីម និង វ៉េ" in one document entry. [Para 1][Para 17][Para 31]
- The Prakas reference for the Arbitration Council is printed as No. 1998 in one place where other awards of the period cite No. 099, and the chair's name is repeated with stray characters at the signature block. [Para 7][Para 74]
