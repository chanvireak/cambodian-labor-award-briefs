# Award No. 62/07 — Hong Mei Garment (Arbitration Council, 16 August 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Ann Vireak, Kong Phallack) · Doc ID 140620

## 1. Facts
Hong Mei Garment, printed also as Hong Mei Company, employed about 530 workers at Canadia Park, Sangkat Chorm Chao, Khan Dangkor, Phnom Penh [Para 26]. The Cambodian Workers Labor Federation Trade Union (CWLFU) lodged a complaint of 09 April 2007 listing 22 demands; conciliation failed and the 22 non-conciliated issues reached the panel [Para 11][Para 13]. The core of the dispute is two group leaders, Mr E and Mr N, transferred out of the ironing and cutting sections on 24 February 2007 and barred from the factory gates from 27 February 2007 [Para 85][Para 102]. Their union was registered on 15 March 2007, one day after the company treated both men as having resigned on 14 March 2007 [Para 151].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether Mr E and Mr N must be reinstated to their group-leader posts with wages and benefits retained from 24 February 2007? [Para 35]
2. (Issue 9) Whether the company must reimburse workers the 10,100 riels medical check fee it deducted from their wages? [Para 131]
3. (Issue 11) Whether the company must retain the full US$ 5.00 attendance bonus for workers absent on authorised sick leave? [Para 137]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), Cambodian Workers Labor Federation Trade Union (CWLFU):** the transfer and the factory ban were union discrimination against the men the workers had elected president and vice-president, and both should return to their sections with the US$ 25.00 position allowance [Para 118][Para 122]. The medical check is the employer's statutory expense, and a worker ill with a certificate should not lose the attendance bonus [Para 133][Para 139].

**Employer (respondent), Hong Mei Garment:** both men resigned of their own accord on 14 March 2007 after unauthorised leave, so they are no longer workers and cannot bring this dispute [Para 54][Para 68]. The company never received the union notification or a list of leaders, and it relied on its Internal Work Rules for the medical fee [Para 77][Para 135].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement refused on Issue 1; the company ordered to stop charging the 10,100 riels medical check fee but not to reimburse it; ordered to stop deducting the entire US$ 5.00 attendance bonus and to withhold only in proportion to sick days taken [Para 200][Para 207][Para 209].

On reinstatement the panel held that union leadership survives dismissal under clause 3 of Prakas 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001, because the union statute did not end the term on loss of employment [Para 147][Para 154]. It then found the dismissal was not discriminatory: the worker party never produced the election minute or a name list, so the men were dismissed in their capacity as workers rather than as union leaders, and no breach of Article 12 of the Labour Law was established [Para 163][Para 175][Para 183]. The Internal Work Rules tendered to justify the resignations were disregarded as unsigned by the worker delegate and unregistered [Para 73].

On the medical check fee the panel applied Article 247(c), following Awards 63/04 Shine Well and 05/06 W & D, and held the employer must bear pre-employment examination costs [Para 184]. Because no name list of the workers who paid was filed, the reimbursement demand was rejected while the charging practice itself was ordered stopped [Para 184][Para 186].

On the attendance bonus the panel relied on Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 and Awards 15/05 Wing Tai and 48/05 Manhattan to hold that the bonus accrues in proportion to days worked, so total forfeiture for authorised sick leave was wrong [Para 187][Para 191][Para 192]. The further demand for the whole US$ 5.00 was an interest dispute, and the union lacked most representative status under Article 277, so that part was declined [Para 195][Para 197][Para 199].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union leader dismissed before the union is registered retains representative capacity where the union statute does not end the term on loss of employment, but anti-union dismissal is proved only by evidence of the election and of notice to the employer [Para 154][Para 175]. An employer bears pre-employment medical examination costs under Article 247(c) of the Labour Law, yet an individual reimbursement claim fails without a name list identifying the workers who paid [Para 184]. An attendance bonus may be reduced only in proportion to authorised sick days, and a demand for the full bonus is an interest dispute that a union without most representative status cannot bring [Para 192][Para 195].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the employer's refusal to conciliate did not stop it hearing the dispute, and noted that both parties had forgotten the actual cost of the medical check [Para 15][Para 131].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The document is an unofficial English translation with heavy OCR damage: articles and pronouns are fused into the following word ("thearing" for "the hearing", "tworkers", "hemployer"), and party names are split across line breaks [Para 7][Para 14]. The employer is named as "Hong Mei Company" in the header but "Hong Mei Garment" throughout the facts [Para 1][Para 26]. The security guard said to have signed the union notification is printed in two different forms in consecutive paragraphs, and one worker's name appears with and without a doubled vowel [Para 76][Para 44]. Issue numbering in the non-conciliation report skips and repeats, and most demands (Issues 2-8 and 12-22) were disposed of by the employer's undertakings in the hearing rather than by reasoned findings [Para 26][Para 202].
