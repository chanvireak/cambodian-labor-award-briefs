# Award No. 93/07 — Global Footwear International Corporation (Arbitration Council, 2 October 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · ING SOTHY (employer-chosen), LIV SOVANNA (worker-chosen), KONG PHALLACK (chair) · Doc ID 140642

## 1. Facts
Global Footwear International Corporation, Poeuk Village, Angk Snuol District, Kandal Province, employed about 1,600 workers on the settlement report and about 2,000 by the parties' own count; the claimant Trade Union for Development of Workers' Rights (TUDWR), with some 1,000 members, did not hold most representative status [Para 19][Para 20]. Its parent body was the Federation of Trade Union for Development of Workers' Rights (FTUDWR) [Para 2]. Of six demands in the 28 August 2007 complaint, four were conciliated before the hearing and Issue 1, the demand to dismiss a unit chief, was settled at the hearing of 17 September 2007, leaving only the medical check fee [Para 12][Para 15][Para 16]. Non-conciliation report No. 185 reached the Secretariat on 10 September 2007 [Para 11]. The workers sought reimbursement of a 10,100 riel medical check fee paid between 1999 and the end of 2006 [Para 21].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the company must reimburse the 10,100 riel medical check fee to all workers who paid it themselves between 1999 and the end of 2006? [Para 21]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, TUDWR / FTUDWR):** reimbursement of the 10,100 riel check fee is the company's responsibility under the Labour Law, because workers must undergo a medical check after passing the interview in order to obtain an employment book, and the factory has had the same owner since it operated as Sky Star in 1999 despite the 2004 change of name [Para 21][Para 27][Para 30].
**Employer (respondent, Global Footwear International Corporation):** Article 247 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 09 of 19 January 1994 fix no amount, Prakas No. 1191 of 21 November 2006 does not require past reimbursement, and any claim is time-barred after three years under Article 120; the company only began operating in 2004 after buying the factory from its predecessor [Para 21][Para 22][Para 23][Para 29].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to reimburse the 10,100 riel medical check fee to members of the Trade Union for Development of Workers' Rights who had paid the fee themselves, for checks from 28 August 2004 to the end of 2006, the period from January 2007 having already been agreed between the parties [Para 59][Para 60].
Standing first: under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 a representative needs written authority, which the union lacked for non-members, so the panel confined the claim to its own members [Para 35][Para 36][Para 37][Para 38].
On the merits, Article 247 (a) and (c)(4) give the Ministry power to set pre-employment and periodic examinations, and Clause 7 of Prakas No. 09 puts the cost on the enterprise owner; following Award 05/06 W&D Issue 1, and Awards 02/03 Chu Xing, 21/03 Loyal Cambodia, 19/04 Kbal Koh II and 53/04 Kong Hung, the panel held Article 247 of the Labour Law prevails over the 1994 Prakas and the employer must pay the 10,100 riel fee [Para 39][Para 41][Para 42].
Limitation: Article 120 sets three years from the date the wage was due, covering all claims arising from the labour contract [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48]. Following Award 61/07 M&V 3 and Award 05/06 W&D, time runs from signature of the contract or the first day of work where the check preceded employment, and from the date the worker paid where the check came afterwards [Para 51][Para 52][Para 55][Para 56]. The panel found these checks were done after work began and paid by the workers themselves, so the complaint of 28 August 2007 preserved only claims from 28 August 2004 onward [Para 57][Para 58]. It declined to decide whether the factory began in 1999 or 2004, the parties not having asked for that [Para 44].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer must bear the cost of the pre-employment medical examination, Article 247 of the Labour Law prevailing over Clause 7 of Prakas No. 09 of 19 January 1994 [Para 41][Para 42]. A claim to recover that fee is subject to the three-year limitation of Article 120, running from the date the worker paid the fee where the examination took place after employment began, so only fees paid within three years before the complaint was filed may be recovered [Para 55][Para 58]. A union may claim such reimbursement only for its own members unless non-members have authorised it in writing [Para 37][Para 38].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that it would not decide whether the factory's operation began in 1999 or 2004, because neither party asked it to [Para 44].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Unofficial English translation only; no Khmer text survives, so party names cannot be checked against an original [Para 1]. The operative order prints the period as "from 28 August 2007 to the end of 2006" while the reasoning fixes it as 28 August 2004 to the end of 2006 — an impossible range that only the reasoning resolves [Para 58][Para 60]. The reasoning also calls the sum "100 riels" where the rest of the award says 10,100 riel [Para 48]. Worker numbers conflict between the settlement report (1,600) and the parties (2,000) [Para 19][Para 20]. OCR drops initial letters throughout and duplicates sentences at Paras 12, 32 and 40; the panel's arbitrator list loses its formatting between the employer-chosen and worker-chosen names [Para 1][Para 61].
