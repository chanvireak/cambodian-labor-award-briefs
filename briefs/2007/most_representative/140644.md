# Award No. 100/07 — Hoyear (Cambodia) Garment Limited (Arbitration Council, 24 October 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Seng Vuochhun (employer-chosen), Ven Pov (worker-chosen), Kong Phallack (chair) · Doc ID 140644

## 1. Facts
Hoyear (Cambodia) Garment Limited, National Road 2, Sangkat Chak Angre Leu, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employed approximately 914 workers; the complainant Khmer Youth Trade Union (KYTU), with the Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU), said about 22 workers were involved but did not know how many were members, and neither of the two factory unions held most representative status [Para 1][Para 3][Para 23][Para 24]. Of sixteen demands in complaint No. 724 SSYK of 4 August 2007, ten were conciliated, and Issues 3, 5 and 6 were settled at the hearing of 2 October 2007, leaving Issues 1, 2 and 4 [Para 11][Para 12][Para 16]. Non-conciliation report No. 1024 K.B/AK/VK issued on 19 September 2007 [Para 11]. The remaining demands were reimbursement of medical check fees, reinstatement of two KYTU leaders, and full wages for periods when the company had no work [Para 16][Para 24][Para 58].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must reimburse medical check fees to all workers, the same demand already decided in an earlier award? [Para 24][Para 25]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must reinstate the two KYTU leaders whose fixed duration contracts expired in August 2007? [Para 28][Para 66]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the company must pay full wages for short periods in July 2007 when it had no work and told workers to go home, and must stop charging those absences against annual leave? [Para 58][Para 92]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, KYTU / KYFTU):** the medical fee demand repeats Issue 1 of Award 91/06 Hoyear of 8 November 2006 because the company never implemented that award and a court complaint produced no action [Para 25][Para 26]; the two leaders held two-year union mandates and the company renewed other union leaders' contracts but not theirs, which is discrimination [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41]; and workers should receive full pay when sent home for lack of work, keeping their annual leave for other times [Para 60][Para 61].
**Employer (respondent, Hoyear (Cambodia) Garment Limited):** it would pay medical fees only for workers who started after 1 June 2007 and completed probation [Para 27]; the leaders' one-year contracts simply expired, notice was given on 18 July 2007, five percent severance was paid, the Labour Department was notified, and both may continue union work, so there is no discrimination [Para 32][Para 35][Para 37][Para 38]; and it pays 50 percent during stoppages, sometimes uses annual leave, and does not inform the Labour Inspector of suspensions [Para 62][Para 63][Para 64].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel declined to consider Issue 1, rejected the reinstatement demand in Issue 2, and on Issue 4 ordered the company to pay 50 percent of wages for the July 2007 stoppage period and to stop deducting annual leave without the workers' agreement [Para 108][Para 111][Para 112].
Issue 1: because the union could not identify which of the 22 claimants were covered by Award 91/06 Hoyear and which were new, the panel applied the res judicata principle stated in Awards 10/06 North Gaiety Issue 1, 24/06 Issue 4 and 106/06 Quick Sew Issue 5, and declined the demand [Para 64][Para 65].
Issue 2: Article 73 paragraph 1 makes a fixed duration contract expire automatically at its end date, so neither party can force continuation without agreement [Para 65][Para 66]. Article 73 paragraph 5 requires 10 days' notice for contracts over six months and 15 days for those over a year, and notice was given on 18 July 2007 for contracts ending 6 and 8 August 2007, satisfying it [Para 71][Para 73][Para 76]. Article 73 paragraph 7 requires severance of at least five percent of wages, which was paid — US$111.468 and US$98.575 [Para 77][Para 80][Para 33][Para 49]. Article 12 forbids termination on grounds of union membership, but no evidence showed discrimination and both leaders were allowed to continue union work [Para 84][Para 85][Para 88][Para 92].
Issue 4: Article 71 paragraph 11 permits suspension only for serious economic or material difficulty, for no more than two months and under the Labour Inspector's control; the company met neither condition and admitted it never notified the Inspector [Para 93][Para 94][Para 95]. Following Awards 21/03 Loyal Cambodia Issue 8, 46/04 M&A Cambodia Issue 1, 01/04 New Point 2 Issue 1 and 60/04 United Art Issue 1, an unlawful suspension means full wages are owed, so the remaining 50 percent was ordered [Para 97][Para 98][Para 99]. Under Article 170 annual leave is for Khmer New Year or by agreement reported to the Labour Inspector, and no such agreement existed [Para 99][Para 101][Para 103][Para 104][Para 107].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer lays workers off for a short period without a lawful suspension under Article 71 paragraph 11 of the Labour Law — that is, without serious economic difficulty and without the Labour Inspector's control — it must pay full wages for the period, and any half payment already made must be topped up [Para 95][Para 97][Para 99]. An employer may not charge such stoppages against workers' annual leave absent the individual worker's prior agreement and a report to the Labour Inspector under Article 170 [Para 104][Para 106][Para 107]. A demand already decided by the panel will not be re-heard, even where the earlier award went unimplemented [Para 65].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that a fixed duration contract expiring without prior notice would extend for an equal period, or become unspecified in duration if the total exceeded the Article 67 limit — a rule it found unnecessary to apply here [Para 73].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Unofficial English translation only; no Khmer original in the file, so party names cannot be verified [Para 1]. The union leaders' names appear in three spellings across the document, and the second leader's wage figure is attributed to the first by a copying error in the reasons [Para 47]. The orders carry the numbering "Issue 43" where the reasoning has Issue 4 [Para 110]. The medical fee is printed as "100 riel" in the reasons against "medical check fees" elsewhere, and the panel's stated claimant count of 22 conflicts with the 222 workers mentioned in the reasons [Para 64]. A sentence in the employer's account at Para 52 breaks off mid-clause, and OCR drops initial letters throughout.
