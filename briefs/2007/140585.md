# Award No. 06/07 — Atac Garment Co., Ltd. (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អាតាក់ ហ្គាមេន) v Khmer Union of Atac Garment Factory (សហជីព ខ្មែរ នៃ រោងចក្រ អាតាក់) (Arbitration Council, 16 February 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Hem Hou Narith, employer-appointed; Tuon Si Chan, worker-appointed; Kong Phalla, president) · Doc ID 140585

## 1. Facts
Atac Garment Co., Ltd. (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អាតាក់ ហ្គាមេន) runs a factory in Canadia Industrial Park, Phnom Penh, employing 213 workers; the only union on site is the Khmer Union of Atac Garment Factory (សហជីព ខ្មែរ នៃ រោងចក្រ អាតាក់), which claims more than 200 members but has not yet obtained registration as a representative status. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 16]
The union filed a ten-point collective demand to improve working conditions; conciliation on 19 December 2006 settled nine points and referred the single remaining point — deduction of union dues from members' wages and their remittance to the union — to the Arbitration Council. [Para 7][Para 8][Para 9]
By the hearing the union's president, vice-president and secretary had all resigned from the company, and the federation said it did not know of those resignations and had no written delegation to appear in their place. [Para 17][Para 19][Para 20]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company is obliged to deduct union dues from the wages of union members and pay that money over to the Khmer Union of Atac Garment Factory (សហជីព ខ្មែរ នៃ រោងចក្រ អាតាក់)? [Para 1]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, Khmer Union of Atac Garment Factory / សហជីព ខ្មែរ នៃ រោងចក្រ អាតាក់):** the company should deduct dues from members' wages and hand them to the union; members had signed letters with thumbprints authorising the deduction, but no written request was ever put to the company because it had refused when asked orally, and the union finds collection difficult. [Para 25][Para 27]
**Employer (respondent, Atac Garment Co., Ltd. / ក្រុមហ៊ុន អាតាក់ ហ្គាមេន):** it will not make the deduction because it already allows the local union to collect dues itself inside the factory, and most workers do not agree to a payroll deduction. [Para 2][Para 28]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the demand of the Khmer Union of Atac Garment Factory that the company deduct and remit union dues. [Para 40]
Article 129 of the Labour Law lets a worker authorise deduction from wages to pay dues to a union of which he or she is a member, but that authorisation must be in writing and may be withdrawn at any time. [Para 31][Para 32]
Article 5 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 obliges the employer to make the deduction once the worker exercises that right, and the panel read the two provisions together as requiring a written request identifying the members who consent. [Para 33][Para 34]
The panel followed its earlier interpretation in Award No. 03/03 (Tongga/Gamen), Award No. 05/03 (Thob Van), Award No. 16/05 (Nhou Phan) and Award No. 70/05 (Gold Fame), all holding that the employer's duty is triggered by the members' written consent transmitted through the union. [Para 34]
On the evidence, neither the union nor any member had made a written request to the company; the thumbprint cards produced after the hearing were collection cards, not requests that the company deduct, and the officer who held them had left the company without transferring authority to the federation. [Para 30][Para 37][Para 38]
The panel therefore held that the statutory condition for the employer's obligation had not been satisfied. [Para 39]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer is obliged to deduct union dues from a member's wages only where the union transmits, or the member personally makes, a written and revocable request for that deduction; absent such a writing on the record, a demand that the company deduct and remit dues fails even if members had earlier signed thumbprint cards kept by a departed union officer. [Para 32][Para 39]

**Obiter dicta:** none identified.
