# Award No. 14/07 — Sou Pi Meat (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (ស៊ូ ព្រី មៀរ (ខេមបូឌៀ) ខូ អិលធីឌី) v Free Workers' Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ) (Arbitration Council, 28 February 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirem, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Ang Eng Thong, president) · Doc ID 140586

## 1. Facts
Sou Pi Meat (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (ស៊ូ ព្រី មៀរ (ខេមបូឌៀ) ខូ អិលធីឌី), a shoe factory on Toul Sangke road, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, employed 1,182 workers; the Free Workers' Union at the factory (សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ ប្រចាំ រោងចក្រ ស៊ូ ព្រី មៀរ) was founded on 19 November 2006, registered on 22 January 2007, and had 110 members. [Para 16][Para 42]
On 30 December 2006 the head of the electrical section told five of the thirteen electricians that they were being moved to shoe-sole stitching; they refused that afternoon, reported to the new section the next day, and later two resigned, leaving three — the union president (Mr H) and two deputy secretaries (Mr S and Mr C) — who demanded their original posts back. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20]
The company docked half a day's wages and the regular USD 5 attendance bonus for December from the three, who also claimed that money at the hearing even though it appears in no non-conciliation report; the panel declined to decide that claim for want of jurisdiction. [Para 27][Para 118][Para 124]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the transfer of the three union leaders to sole-stitching work was discrimination against the union? [Para 47]
2. (Issue 2) Whether that transfer observed the procedure required by Article 293 of the Labour Law? [Para 58]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the change of work was consistent with the law and the employment contracts in force? [Para 68]
4. (Issue 4) What compensation the workers are entitled to where the employer breached the employment contract? [Para 95]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, Free Workers' Union at the factory / សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ ប្រចាំ រោងចក្រ ស៊ូ ព្រី មៀរ):** all five moved were union leaders and activists, so the transfer was anti-union; their contracts named them electricians, they wanted to keep developing that skill, and sole stitching is unskilled work anyone can do. [Para 38][Para 43]
**Employer (respondent, Sou Pi Meat (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. / ស៊ូ ព្រី មៀរ (ខេមបូឌៀ) ខូ អិលធីឌី):** the move rested on skill and on the need to cut electricians because that section had no work, the new job was comparable to the old, and assigning workers where it needs them is the company's right under its internal rules. [Para 44][Para 72]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to let Mr H, Mr S and Mr C work again in their original places as electricians, and the claim to restore the half-day wage and the December attendance bonus was refused. [Para 125][Para 126]
On discrimination the panel held that the worker alleging it carries the burden of proof, and that the claimants themselves conceded their wages, benefits, hours and working conditions were unchanged and the new section no harder. [Para 49][Para 52] It followed Award No. 79/05 (Evergreen), Award No. 99/06 (Sauk Bai) and Award No. 01/07 (Souk Phrom) in finding no evidence that the union was targeted. [Para 51][Para 57]
On Article 293 the panel read the article, with Article 282, as requiring the labour inspector's consent only where a transfer costs a staff delegate the office held under his mandate; here the three kept their union posts, so no consent was needed. [Para 58][Para 61][Para 65][Para 66]
On the contract, the panel accepted that under Article 2 and Award No. 17/03 and 18/03 (Ho Hing) an employer may move staff where pay, hours and place are unaffected; but the clause "obey the company's assignment" was unreasonable in this case because the contracts named the men as electricians and stitching destroys a skill built up since hiring. [Para 77][Para 82][Para 88][Para 93]
On remedy it applied Articles 67 and 73 to the one-year fixed-duration contracts and held that absence of work is not a lawful ground for unilateral termination, ordering the men back to their old posts. [Para 104][Para 115][Para 108]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A transfer to wholly different work breaches the employment contract, and the remedy is return to the original post, where it strips the worker of the trade the contract was made for, even if pay and hours are untouched; conversely a transfer that keeps pay, hours and the ability to act as a union delegate is not discrimination and needs no labour inspector's consent. [Para 66][Para 88][Para 115]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that Article 293 consent would be required only for a transfer that actually causes loss of union office. [Para 64]
