# Award No. 35/07 — Flying Dragon 3 (Arbitration Council, 8 May 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (You Suonty for the employer party, Tuon Siphann for the worker party, Sok Mathoeung chair) · Doc ID 140598

## 1. Facts
Flying Dragon 3, a garment factory on Chom Chao Street, Damnak Thom, Stoeung Mean Chey, Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employed 1,550 workers and had two unions, FTUWKC and the local C.CAWDU, which was registered with the Ministry of Labour and whose second-mandate leaders were recognised on 2 December 2005; by the hearing it claimed 814 members. [Para 1][Para 18][Para 19]
Under a settlement of case 98/06 recorded on 26 October 2006 the union was to submit documents supporting a check-off of union dues and the employer was to answer by the first week of December 2006; the union sent a request with a name list and signatures or thumbprints, and the employer refused, saying some signatures were not genuine and that workers had denied them. [Para 20][Para 22][Para 24][Para 59][Para 60]
Three demands were unresolved: the dues check-off, payment of US$ 145 (claimed) for the medical treatment of Ms N, the local union president, and the December 2006 wages, ten days of January 2007 wages and 2006 annual-leave indemnity of Ms P, terminated on 22 January 2007 after eight days' absence. [Para 6][Para 7][Para 9][Para 12][Para 73][Para 86]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must deduct union contribution fees from the wages of local C.CAWDU members? [Para 5][Para 55]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay for the medical treatment of Ms N, the local union president, on the basis that her lung condition was a work-related accident or occupational illness? [Para 7][Para 73][Para 78]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay Ms P her December 2006 wages, ten days of January 2007 wages and 2006 annual-leave indemnity despite her absence without notice? [Para 9][Para 86][Para 90]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — C.CAWDU and local C.CAWDU at Flying Dragon 3 Factory:** the Labour Law and Prakas 305 SKBY permit the check-off and the union accepts that some signatures and thumbprints in its list were irregular, asking only that dues be deducted from members the employer considers legitimate; Ms N's illness arose while she was working, so the employer must pay the treatment; Ms P was absent to visit her sick mother and is owed her wages and leave indemnity. [Para 5][Para 26][Para 40][Para 47][Para 25]
**Employer (respondent) — Flying Dragon 3:** it will not deduct dues because the submitted signatures were duplicated and some workers said the marks were not theirs; Ms N had a normal illness that she had suffered before, not a work-related accident, though the company always pays for genuine work accidents; Ms P was absent more than six days without informing anyone and, under point 8 of the Internal Work Rules, forfeits wages and benefits on abandoning her job. [Para 8][Para 22][Para 23][Para 41][Para 42][Para 51][Para 52]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to deduct union contribution fees from members who properly signed or thumbprinted the 41-page list annexed to the award, rejected the demand for Ms N's medical treatment, and ordered the employer to pay Ms P's December 2006 wages, ten days of January 2007 wages and 2006 annual-leave indemnity within two weeks. [Para 98][Para 99][Para 100]

On Issue 1 the panel relied on Clause 5 of Prakas 305 SKBS of 22 November 2001, which lets members request a deduction in writing at least 15 days in advance, and on Article 129(2) of the Labour Law, which allows a worker to authorise deductions for union dues by a writing revocable at any time. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58] Following Awards 62/04 (Y Sin) and 60/05 (Evergreen), it held that the union must supply a proper authorisation list and the employer must deduct only on that basis, though an employer with real doubts may require the union to prove each authorisation. [Para 60][Para 65][Para 66] Of the three kinds of document submitted, only 41 pages reflected the workers' genuine will; members who did not sign or thumbprint themselves are not covered. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70]
On Issue 2 the panel found no accident: the parties never described an event injuring her, and no evidence showed one. [Para 74][Para 75][Para 76][Para 77] Article 248(3) treats defined occupational illnesses like accidents, but no Cambodian Prakas defines occupational illness, and the broad definition in the 2002 Protocol to the Occupational Safety and Health Convention 1981 could not be applied because Cambodia has not ratified that Convention. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 84] With no legal definition and no evidence linking the lung disease to workplace exposure, it refused the claim. [Para 85]
On Issue 3 the panel held that Article 102 and Article 103 define wages as remuneration for work already done, that Article 167(2) gives an indemnity for unused leave under Article 166, and that Article 13(1) makes labour-law provisions public order so that an internal rule contrary to them is null. [Para 91][Para 92] Her absence for six consecutive days from 11 to 18 January 2007 was established, and point 8 of the Internal Work Rules treats abandonment as serious misconduct, but that rule cannot extinguish wages and leave indemnity already earned. [Para 89][Para 94][Para 95][Para 96]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer is bound to deduct union dues only for members whose written authorisation is genuine, so where the union's list contains signatures or thumbprints that are not the workers' own the employer may refuse those entries and require a proper list, while remaining obliged to deduct for the pages that do reflect real consent. Separately, an internal work rule cannot deprive a worker of wages for work already performed or of accrued annual-leave indemnity, even where the worker has committed serious misconduct by abandoning the job. [Para 66][Para 70][Para 91][Para 96]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's discussion of the 2002 Protocol to the Occupational Safety and Health Convention 1981, and its remark that the Convention is not binding because Cambodia has not ratified it, are unnecessary to the result, which rests on the absence of any Cambodian definition of occupational illness. [Para 83][Para 84]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The terminated worker's name is printed "Penh Srey Mom" at the issues stage and "Penh Srey Tom" everywhere else, so the award refers to one person under two surnames. [Para 9][Para 43]
- The union's registration is dated 22 April 2003 in the evidence list [Para 17] but 22 December 2003 in the findings [Para 18].
- The treatment cost is stated as US$ 105 actually spent [Para 37] and as US$ 145 claimed and agreed [Para 73][Para 82]; the decision orders no payment, so the discrepancy is left open.
- The settlement is dated 26 October 2006 in the evidence list [Para 17] and 22 October 2006 in the reasons [Para 59]; the union's request is dated 1 November 2006 [Para 20] and 11 November 2006 [Para 60].
- The illness is dated 15 January 2005 in part of the findings [Para 80] and 15 January 2007 elsewhere [Para 32], and the absence from 11 to 18 January 2007 is described as six consecutive days [Para 89] although the dates span eight.
- This is an unofficial English translation of a Khmer original, and the OCR consistently drops the "t" in "the employer" and similar words, so quoted statutory text is unreliable in detail.
- Individual names of the two workers and the union officers are reduced to initials here, as is the employer's head of administration. [Para 1][Para 2]
