# Award No. 75/07 — GDM Cambodia Branch (Arbitration Council, 4 September 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuochhun, Liv Sovanna, Pen Bunchhea chairing) · Doc ID 140631

## 1. Facts
GDM Cambodia Branch began operating in August 2006 and employs about 420 workers on National Road No. 5, Phnom Penh [Para 15][Para 1]. Of eleven demands, ten were conciliated, leaving one: the Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU) and Khmer Youth Trade Union (KYTU) at GDM Cambodia Branch demanded the reinstatement of the union president at the branch, dismissed at 8:30am on 3 July 2007 while serving a two-month probation from 19 June to 19 August 2007 on a wage of US$45 per month [Para 2][Para 3][Para 16][Para 17][Para 19]. The union had elected its committee at 4:20pm on 2 July 2007 with 64 members voting, and says it notified the company of its candidates on 1 July and again on 3 July [Para 23][Para 24]. The company said the dismissal was for poor performance and chatting at work, not union membership [Para 4][Para 30][Para 31].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the dismissal of the branch union president was an act of discrimination against the union? [Para 43][Para 59]
2. Whether that dismissal complied with the Labour Law? [Para 45][Para 73]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — KYFTU and KYTU at GDM Cambodia Branch):** that the dismissal was discrimination because the union had notified the candidates for its leadership election on 1 July 2007, that the second notification on 3 July was blocked by security guards, and that their registration application was with the Ministry of Labour awaiting corrections [Para 21][Para 24][Para 25][Para 28].
**Employer (respondent — GDM Cambodia Branch):** that the worker was still within a two-month probationary period, that his performance was poor and he chatted during working hours over several days, that the assessment and the dismissal were made by his supervisor, and that there was no discrimination against union leaders [Para 4][Para 5][Para 30][Para 32][Para 36].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement is refused, but the employer must pay damages equal to the whole two-month contract, severance of five percent of two months' wages (multiplied by 5 and divided by 100, as printed), and paid annual leave of one and a half days per month [Para 90][Para 92].
On discrimination the panel applied Article 12 and Article 279 of the Labour Law, which forbid an employer to take union membership or activity into account in dismissal, and followed Award 50/05 Fortune in placing the burden of proof on the worker and requiring evidence that the employer knew of the membership [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49]. The company's poor-performance case collapsed on its own machine records: the same PO and EDP numbers, start and finish times showed the worker with ID T00795 washing six loads to five for the worker with ID T00789 [Para 51][Para 52][Para 54]. The panel nonetheless declined to consider the discrimination claim because the union's notification of 1 July fell on a Sunday, the letter bore no acknowledgement of receipt, and the second notification was dated 4 July rather than the 3 July claimed, so the evidence that the company knew of the election was not clear enough to decide on [Para 22][Para 23][Para 27][Para 57][Para 58][Para 59].
On the special protection of union candidates under Clause 3 of Prakas No. 305 dated 22 November 2001, protecting candidates for 45 days before and, if not elected, 45 days after the election, the panel asked whether the worker was a candidate, whether the dismissal fell in the protected period, and whether the union notified the employer [Para 61][Para 62][Para 64]. The first two were satisfied — he was elected president and dismissed within the protected period — but the third failed for the same notification defects, so he was held not to be a worker entitled to that protection [Para 66][Para 68][Para 71][Para 72].
On the lawfulness of the dismissal the panel applied Article 68, capping probation at three months for regular, two for specialised and one for non-specialised workers, and its own jurisprudence in Awards 69/04 Way and 53/06 Hong Mei that garment workers are specialised, holding that operating the laundry's washing machines was specialised work, so the two-month probation was lawful [Para 74][Para 75][Para 76][Para 78][Para 80]. The contract was for a fixed duration under Article 67, so early termination under Article 73 required damages of at least the remuneration to the contract's end, severance of five percent, and annual leave under Articles 166 and 167 [Para 81][Para 85][Para 86]. Clause 10 of the probation contract, excluding all compensation, was void under Article 13 as less favourable than the law [Para 88][Para 89].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Early termination of a fixed-duration probationary contract is lawful in form but still carries the Article 73 consequences — damages equal to the remaining remuneration, five percent severance and accrued annual leave — and a contractual clause waiving them is void under Article 13 [Para 85][Para 89][Para 90]. Union-candidate protection under Clause 3 of Prakas No. 305 depends on proof that the employer was notified of the candidacy; without it the protection is not engaged, however suspicious the dismissal [Para 62][Para 70][Para 72].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the dismissal "seemed to be for other reasons related to the union", which it could not act on for want of proof [Para 54][Para 55].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The dismissed worker's name and the name of the messenger who delivered the union letters are printed in full throughout the original; both are replaced here by role and initial [Para 3][Para 26]. The non-conciliation report is numbered 802 on one line and 801 on the next for the same report, and the conciliation minutes are dated 25 August 2007 though the conciliation took place on 25 July [Para 7][Para 9][Para 14]. The company's Internal Work Rules are dated 26 October 2007, after the award date of 4 September 2007 [Para 12][Para 1]. The panel mislabels the dismissal day as falling "45 days after the election" when the election was the day before, and OCR has dropped the letter "t" throughout [Para 68].
