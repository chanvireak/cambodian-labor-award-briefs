# Award No. 119/06 — Q.S.P APPAREL LTD. (Arbitration Council, 31 January 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Chiv Phirum, Liv Sovanna, Ang Eng Thong (presiding) · Doc ID 140758

## 1. Facts
Q.S.P APPAREL LTD. operates a garment factory in Phnom Penh with 370 workers, of whom about 160 belong to the Khmer Youth Union at the factory [Para 26]. The union filed a complaint with the Labour Disputes Department on 25 October 2006 seeking improvement of 16 working-condition points [Para 15]. Conciliation on 15 December 2006 settled 9 points, leaving 7 unresolved, and the non-conciliation report of 18 December 2006 was sent to the Arbitration Council secretariat on 21 December 2006 [Para 13]. At the hearing, the parties settled 4 further points and the union withdrew 1, leaving only demands 3 and 6 for decision [Para 21]. Demand 3 concerned wages during periods without work; demand 6 concerned reinstatement of Ms T, a union member working as a goods coordinator since 2 February 2001 on USD 400 per month, with no written employment contract [Para 36]. Ms T was sent home on 15 August 2006 and, by an English-language agreement of 21 August 2006, was to stay off work with her wages preserved pending resolution, but the company never paid her [Para 38]. The employer gave no reason for the stoppage, saying only that it was a new management team [Para 43].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay full wages when it has no work for the workers, and must pay the missing 70% of wages for the no-work periods from September to December 2006 [Para 32]?
2. (Issue 6) Whether the claim to reinstate Ms T is a collective labour dispute within the panel's jurisdiction under Article 302 of the Labour Law, or an individual dispute outside it [Para 72]?

## 3. Positions of the parties
The union demanded full wages whenever the company had no work and payment of the 70% shortfall for September to December 2006, and it submitted a list of workers suspended in that period, though some entries gave names without the days claimed [Para 32]. It also sought reinstatement of Ms T [Para 8]. The employer acknowledged that workers were often without work and that it paid 30% wages during suspensions, by custom and without any written agreement [Para 29]. In November 2006 it suspended all workers' contracts for three weeks without notifying the labour inspector, paying 30% with the workers' agreement, and it promised to produce that agreement to the panel by 19 March 2007 [Para 31]. It did not do so, and it produced no payroll or attendance records by the deadline; only the worker side complied [Para 61]. On Ms T, the employer argued the matter was an individual dispute that the Arbitration Council had no jurisdiction to decide [Para 44]. Ms T said no reason was explained to her and suspected the stoppage stemmed from her ties to a former owner who had conflicted with the current one [Para 42].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Partly granted: the panel ordered the company to stop suspending employment contracts in disregard of Article 71 of the Labour Law and to pay the missing 70% of wages according to the number of days in the workers' annexed list; the reinstatement claim was refused for want of jurisdiction [Para 92].

On Issue 3, the panel held that Article 71(11) of the Labour Law permits suspension of contracts only where the enterprise faces serious economic or material difficulty stopping its activity, for no more than two months, and under labour-inspector supervision [Para 47]. The employer admitted it never notified the labour inspector [Para 51]. A procedurally lawful suspension relieves the employer of wage obligation, but a suspension not following Article 71(11) obliges the employer to pay 100% wages, as in prior awards including No. 21/03-Loyal, No. 01/04-Authority, No. 46/04-M.A.E., No. 60/04-United Art and No. 45/05-B.A.N [Para 53]. The claim was not time-barred: Article 120 gives wage claims a three-year limitation [Para 55]. The panel therefore found the company still owed the 70% back wages for actual suspension days from September to December 2006 [Para 58]. The claimant must ordinarily prove its allegation, but under Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, clauses 24 and 25, the panel may compel production of documents and assess evidence freely [Para 64]. Because the employer admitted the suspensions but could not identify which workers or how many days, and produced no contrary evidence, the panel decided on the evidence available, namely the union's list [Para 69].

On Issue 6, the panel examined the three Article 302 conditions for a collective labour dispute [Para 77]. The first was met because the union, representing a group of workers under Article 266, filed the claim [Para 82]. The second was met because the dispute concerned employer–worker relations [Para 83]. The third was not met: the dispute could not disrupt the enterprise or harm social peace, since it concerned one person's interest [Para 84]. A stoppage of an individual is an individual dispute unless the person is a union leader or has demonstrated worker support; Ms T was neither [Para 86]. The union's complaint of October 2006 covered 16 points, not hers alone, yet no worker action followed her August 2006 stoppage [Para 89]. The matter was therefore individual, to be resolved under Articles 300 and 301, and the panel had no jurisdiction over it [Para 91].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Under Article 71(11) of the Labour Law, contract suspension is lawful only for serious economic or material difficulty halting the enterprise, for at most two months, and under labour-inspector supervision; without that supervision the employer owes 100% wages [Para 47]. Wage claims survive for three years under Article 120 [Para 55]. Where the employer admits suspensions but holds the payroll and attendance records and fails to produce them when ordered, the panel may decide arrears on the workers' evidence under Prakas No. 099, clauses 24 and 25 [Para 64]. A dispute over one worker's stoppage is individual — and outside the Council's jurisdiction — unless that worker is a union leader or enjoys demonstrated worker support; union filing alone does not make it collective under Article 302 [Para 86].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel will normally respect the classification by the Labour Disputes Department and the Minister of Labour of a dispute as individual or collective, absent clear contrary reasons [Para 76].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The operative order contains a corrupted figure "៧ ១១០%" where the reasoning and order state 70% [Para 93]. The annexed worker lists are heavily garbled OCR, mixing scripts and unreadable names and figures, so the worker-day totals cannot be verified [Para 122]. Party and headings alternate between "QSP", "QBP" and "ASP" across the annex pages, and "ARBITRATION" is repeatedly misspelled [Para 10].
