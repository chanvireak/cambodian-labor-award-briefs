# Award No. 31/07 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ផាក វីយូ (Park Yoo Cambodia Garment Factory) (Arbitration Council, 12 April 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (កៅ ថា ច for the employer party, លីវ សុវណ្ណា for the worker party, ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា chair) · Doc ID 140599

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ផាក វីយូ, a garment factory at Troa village, Chom Chao, Dangkao, Phnom Penh, employing about 660 workers, announced on 07 March 2007 that it was closing the factory temporarily for two months. [Para 1][Para 22][Para 25] Workers organised in three unions — សហជីពឯករាជ្យ និង ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ, សហជីពយុវជនខ្មែរ and សហជីពជាតិឧស្សាហកម្មវាយនភ័ណ្ឌកាត់ដេរកម្ពុជា — demanded full wages during the suspension if it was not carried out in accordance with the law, and 50% of one month's wage if it was, without any duty to come and thumbprint. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 3] The parties agreed at the hearing that the suspension actually ran from 09 March to 09 May 2007. [Para 26] Conciliation before the Department of Labour Disputes failed on that single point, and the case reached the panel on 27 March 2007. [Para 8][Para 11][Para 12]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company suspended the workers' employment contracts in accordance with the procedure of the Labour Law? [Para 34][Para 47]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), the three unions cited above:** the company must pay 100% of wages while it suspends the contracts irregularly, and 50% of one month's wage if the suspension is regular, in which case the workers should not be required to thumbprint at the factory. [Para 32][Para 2][Para 3]
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន ផាក វីយូ:** the suspension continues, the company can only pay a USD 5 allowance per month, and workers must come to thumbprint twice a week, on Tuesdays and Fridays, to show they are not working for another factory; the claim cannot be met. [Para 4][Para 5][Para 30][Para 33]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay 100% of wages to the workers throughout the period of suspension not in conformity with the Labour Law, from 09 March 2007 until the day the company allows the workers to return to work. [Para 63]

The panel took jurisdiction under Chapter 12, Section 4, subsection 2(b) (Articles 309 to 317) of the 1997 Labour Law and Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004. [Para 6] It read Article 71(11) as permitting an employer facing serious economic or material difficulty, or a special hardship, to suspend the enterprise's activity for no more than two months, but only where that suspension is placed under the supervision of the labour inspector. [Para 34][Para 36] The company's announcement of 07 March 2007 fixed no start or end date for the suspension, and the parties later agreed the period was 09 March to 09 May 2007. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 44] The employer asserted it had written to the labour inspector around 07, 08 or 09 March, but the panel had ordered production of that letter by 06 April 2007 and no document of any kind arrived. [Para 28][Para 29][Para 45] Absent evidence, the panel held that the employer never notified the labour inspector, so the suspension was not carried out in accordance with the Labour Law. [Para 46][Para 47] Article 72(1) suspends the essential obligations — the worker's duty to work and the employer's duty to pay — only where the suspension is lawful; the panel followed its earlier awards in case 21/03, case 01/04 and case 60/04 to hold that an unlawful suspension leaves the employer with the legal duty to pay 100% of wages. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50]

On the demands it regarded as unnecessary to decide the panel nonetheless discussed them: where a suspension is lawful the employer owes no wage, so the 50% claim does not arise. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 54] It added that requiring workers to thumbprint during a suspension means their duty to work continues, which contradicts the very idea of suspension, and that an employer who imposes it must pay full wages. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 59][Para 61][Para 62]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of employment contracts under Article 71(11) of the Labour Law is lawful only if the employer places it under the supervision of the labour inspector; where the employer cannot prove notification, the suspension is irregular and Article 72(1) does not release it from the duty to pay, so the full 100% of wages remains owed for the whole period of the irregular suspension until the workers are taken back. [Para 36][Para 47][Para 50]

**Obiter dicta:** where a suspension is lawful the employer pays nothing and the workers have no duty to come and thumbprint, and an employer who nevertheless requires thumbprinting during a suspension must pay full wages; the panel reached these views although it had held the questions unnecessary to its decision. [Para 51][Para 54][Para 62]
