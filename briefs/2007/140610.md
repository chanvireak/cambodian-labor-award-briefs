# Award No. 49/07 — Yen Yu In Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉េន យូ អ៉ិន ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) ខូ អិលធីឌី) v Khmer Youth Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ) (Arbitration Council, 3 July 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Kau Thach (employer-appointed), Tuon Si Chan (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhean (president) · Doc ID 140610

## 1. Facts
Yen Yu In Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉េន យូ អ៉ិន ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) ខូ អិលធីឌី) runs a garment factory at Kol village, Kantaok commune, Ang Snuol district, Kandal, with about 1,000 workers [Para 1][Para 33].
The Khmer Youth Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ) brought the collective dispute for a factory branch claiming about 700 members [Para 1][Para 33].
Conciliation at the provincial labour office failed on two points and the file reached the Council on 8 June 2007 [Para 7][Para 10].
The branch had lodged its registration file and held only a receipt dated 5 June 2007 when the panel sat on 21 June 2007 [Para 42][Para 48][Para 49].
Two demands were referred: that the attendance bonus not be cut when a worker takes authorised leave of three days or more, and that the company deduct 1,000 riel a month per consenting member as union dues [Para 2][Para 32].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must deduct 1,000 riel a month from the wages of union members who have authorised the deduction, to pay union dues, when the union held only a registration receipt and was not yet registered? [Para 61][Para 72]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), Khmer Youth Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ):** the union asked in writing on 27 April 2007 for a 1,000 riel monthly deduction per consenting member, attaching a list carrying each member's name, sex, identity number, start date and thumbprint [Para 32][Para 37]. It said workers took only one or two days of authorised leave in a month and still lost the whole attendance bonus [Para 31].
**Employer (respondent), Yen Yu In Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉េន យូ អ៉ិន ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) ខូ អិលធីឌី):** its representative said a welfare fund already existed through the worker representatives, and the company deducted 1,000 riel from any worker who volunteered, for that fund [Para 38]. It said it could deduct 1,000 riel for the union but required the worker representatives to collect the money into the welfare fund instead [Para 41].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the claim that the company deduct 1,000 riel per member per month as union dues, and ordered that the attendance-bonus claim on Issue 1 not be considered at all [Para 87][Para 88].
Issue 1 was never reached on the merits: the panel had required the union to file evidence identifying the workers claiming the bonus by 25 June 2007, received nothing, and so declined to consider the demand, leaving it open were such evidence later produced [Para 19][Para 20][Para 59][Para 60].
On Issue 2 the panel weighed Article 129 of the Labour Law, which lets workers authorise deduction of wages to pay union dues, against Article 281, which forbids employers from deducting wages to pay union dues [Para 61][Para 63]. Reading the two together with its earlier orders 05/03, 62/04, 94/04 and 99/06, it held Article 281 aimed at employer interference with union independence rather than at a deduction the worker authorises in writing, and Prakas 305 Article 5 allowed a written request 15 days in advance [Para 67][Para 70]. On that basis the employer was under a duty to deduct once a written request was made [Para 68][Para 71].
The right was nonetheless not yet exercisable. Article 268 makes a union's entitlements run from registration, or from two months without a ministry response, and the union had held its receipt for only 16 days at the hearing [Para 45][Para 46][Para 80][Para 81]. Unregistered and inside that waiting period it had no full legal rights [Para 50][Para 82], and Prakas 099 Article 19 let a body that was not a party act only with written authority from the disputing party [Para 54][Para 55].
The panel added that without the promised membership list, due on 25 June 2007, it had no basis to order a deduction for identified workers [Para 85]. The employer-appointed arbitrator agreed with the refusal but dissented from the wording of the operative paragraph, which he thought should not carry the reference to the transitional period [Para 94][Para 96][Para 101]. The order is not binding and became final eight days after notification unless a party objected in writing to the Minister [Para 89].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that has filed a registration application but is still inside the two-month waiting period of Article 268(1) of the Labour Law holds no full legal rights, and cannot compel an employer to deduct 1,000 riel union dues from members' wages even where those members have authorised the deduction in writing [Para 81][Para 84].
Deduction of dues at a worker's written request is lawful under Article 129, and the Article 281 prohibition is directed at employer interference with union independence, not at authorised deduction [Para 68].
A claim whose supporting membership list was promised but never filed by the panel's deadline is not decided on the merits, and may be revived if the evidence is later produced [Para 59][Para 60].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded the employer's fear that 50 to 60 workers a day asking for authorised leave, plus about 10 absent without leave, would grow if the concession were made, without relying on it [Para 27][Para 29], and noted the bonus was raised from 5 to 6 US dollars a month [Para 26].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The worker-appointed arbitrator's name is printed with one final syllable in the header and another in the signature block, and the president's name likewise differs between the face of the award and the signature page; both are given above in the header form [Para 90]. The factory union president's name is garbled into unrelated characters in the election-result passage and printed with a variant spelling in the reasoning, so no name is reproduced for union officers here. The reasoning for Issue 1 carries a stray date ("2015 July") belonging to no event in the file [Para 43], the hearing date and hours are printed twice in overlapping fragments [Para 9], and several passages repeat themselves verbatim, notably the Issue 2 decision and a duplicated line closing the employer's attendance figures [Para 28][Para 51][Para 88]. The signature block survives only as fragments of Latin script [Para 102].
