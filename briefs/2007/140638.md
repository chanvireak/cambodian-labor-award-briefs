# Award No. 91/07-JK — JK Forever Co., Ltd v C.CAWDU (Arbitration Council, 26 October 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Nan (worker-appointed), Kong Phallack (chair) · Doc ID 140638

## 1. Facts
JK Forever Co., Ltd runs a factory at Plov Lom, Toul Sangke, Phnom Penh, with about 800 workers; the local union of C.CAWDU there is the claimant, registered on 10 September 2007 [Para 34][Para 36].
None of the four demands was conciliated, and the file reached the Council on 5 September 2007 [Para 15][Para 16].
The first hearing was postponed at the union's request, and the second was halted when the worker-side arbitrator withdrew because his child was in hospital; An Nan replaced him and a third hearing was held on 8 October 2007 [Para 17][Para 19][Para 21].
The demands were a 1,000 riel union dues check-off, the reinstatement of Mr M, a union advisor and mechanic, with his treatment costs, advance payment of 50% of three months' wages to women on maternity leave, and reimbursement of a US$3.5 medical check fee to 149 workers [Para 9][Para 10][Para 11][Para 12].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must deduct 1,000 riel a month from the wages of union members for contribution fees? [Para 9][Para 123]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must reinstate Mr M, keeping his function, position, wage and bonus, and reimburse his treatment fees? [Para 10][Para 124]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay 50% of three months' wages, including perquisites, in advance to women workers going on maternity leave? [Para 11][Para 123]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must reimburse the US$3.5 medical check fee to each worker from whose wage it was deducted? [Para 12][Para 124]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), C.CAWDU:** the company should deduct 1,000 riel a month under Article 129 and Prakas 305, and the union offered to file the membership list and thumbprints once, then only report joiners and leavers each month [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38]. Mr M said an administration officer forced him to resign after absences and that his bronchitis and inguinal hernia came from his work as a mechanic, so his treatment fees were due as a work accident [Para 41][Para 49][Para 66]. The workers also sought the maternity payment in advance and reimbursement of the medical check fee deducted from wages [Para 74][Para 81].
**Employer (respondent), JK Forever Co., Ltd:** it accepted the check-off but required ministry registration, a monthly name list, and an individual written request filed 15 days before each pay day, to protect workers from bad unions and wrong deductions [Para 36][Para 39][Para 40]. It said Mr M resigned voluntarily and was paid his end-of-contract dues, and that his illness was not shown to be work-related [Para 42][Para 43][Para 62][Para 64]. It pays maternity benefit monthly under Article 116 and denies deducting any medical check fee [Para 75][Para 82].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to deduct union contribution fees where a member requests it in writing and to pay 50% of three months' wages with perquisites before maternity leave begins; the reinstatement and medical-fee claims were rejected [Para 123][Para 124].
On Issue 1 the panel read Article 129 paragraph 2 as a right of the worker, not of the union, so a written authorisation given once suffices and can be revoked at any time [Para 87][Para 90][Para 92]. Requiring a fresh request every month, or a new list with thumbprints each month, was not the intention of the law, and Prakas 305 clause 5 only requires the first request 15 days ahead [Para 95][Para 96]. It followed Awards 57/06 Evergreen, 60/06 Quick Sew and 99/06 South Bay [Para 94].
On Issue 2 the panel found that after an initial six-month written contract Mr M worked on without any further contract, so he held an undetermined-duration contract terminable at will under Article 74 [Para 98][Para 99]. He had not shown that his resignation was forced and had received his end-of-contract payment, leaving no further obligation between them [Para 101][Para 104][Para 106]. His treatment claim failed for want of evidence that the bronchitis and hernia arose from his work, and Awards 63/04 Shine Well, 99/06 South Bay, 74/07 Global Apparel and 94/07 Fortune Garment were followed [Para 107][Para 108].
On Issue 3 the panel applied Article 183, which entitles a woman with a year's service to half her wage and perquisites during maternity leave, together with Article 115(3) on payment days [Para 109][Para 111]. Reading them with Award 57/06 Evergreen Issue 6 and Award 97/06 New Max, it held the payment must be made before the leave begins, because the normal pay day falls while the woman is already on leave; Article 116 governs ordinary monthly wages, not maternity leave [Para 112][Para 115][Para 116].
On Issue 4 the panel accepted that under Article 247 the employer bears the medical check fee, as held in Awards 02/03 Chu Hsing, 21/03 Loyal Cambodia, 19/04 Kbal Koh 2 and 53/04 Kong Hong, usually 10,100 riel before 2007 [Para 119][Para 120]. But the union's letters were inconsistent, some claiming US$3.5 and others US$3.7, and did not prove any deduction by the company, so the claim was rejected [Para 121][Para 122].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker's written authorisation under Article 129 paragraph 2 of the Labour Law is given once and stands until revoked, so an employer may not condition the union dues check-off on a fresh request or a renewed list of members with thumbprints every month [Para 92][Para 95][Para 96].
Where a worker continues working after a fixed-term contract expires without a new contract, the relationship becomes one of undetermined duration, terminable at will under Article 74 on lawful payment; a claim of forced resignation must be proved [Para 99][Para 101].
The maternity benefit of half pay and perquisites under Article 183 is payable before the leave begins, because the ordinary pay day falls during the leave and Article 116 governs only normal monthly wages [Para 115][Para 116].
A reimbursement claim resting on letters that disagree on the sum allegedly deducted and do not show any deduction fails for want of proof, even where the underlying obligation is the employer's [Para 121][Para 122].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that the union's summary statement listed 317 members on its first page and 417 on its third, settling nothing and accepting the union's oral figure of 400 [Para 35][Para 36].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The hearing chronology is impossible as printed: the second hearing is dated 27 October 2007 and the third 08 October 2007, while the award itself is dated 26 October 2007, so the second date is presumably a misprint for September [Para 14][Para 17][Para 22]. The claimant's advisor is spelled two different ways within the same document, and the administration officer named at Para 49 and Para 53 is likewise given in two variant spellings; neither variant is reproduced here. The translation drops the letter "t" throughout ("he" for "the", "hat" for "that") and duplicates clauses verbatim, for example the Article 74 quotation and the Article 129(2) sentence [Para 85][Para 99]. At Para 95 the condition imposed by the company is described as filing the request "every day", though the reasoning elsewhere shows it means every month [Para 95]. The medical check sum claimed varies between US$3.5 and US$3.7 and the panel's reference to 10,100 riel is left unreconciled with either figure [Para 120][Para 121]. Paragraph 12 opens with a garbled fragment on advance payment to women on maternity leave, and the exhibit list is truncated mid-item, leaving a hospital receipt with no date [Para 12][Para 30].
