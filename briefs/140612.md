# Award No. 50/07 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន អ៊ឹម និង វ៉េ អាំង ទែរ ណា ស្យូណាល់ ម៉ាញ៉ូ ហ្វាក់ ឈើ រឹង ទី៣ v សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ឯករាជ្យ កម្ពុជា (Arbitration Council, 4 July 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chhea, president; Kau Thach; An Norn) · Doc ID 140612

## 1. Facts
The employer, ក្រុមហ៊ុន អ៊ឹម និង វ៉េ អាំង ទែរ ណា ស្យូណាល់ ម៉ាញ៉ូ ហ្វាក់ ឈើ រឹង ទី៣, runs a garment factory at Building 1623, Sangkat Chake Angre Krom, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, with 2,872 workers [Para 18]. The claimant is សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ឯករាជ្យ កម្ពុជា, which has 1,050 members in the factory but does not yet hold most-representative status [Para 18][Para 19]. Forty-nine workers are directly involved in the dispute [Para 20]. They demanded the replacement of a section head named Svaeng and a team leader named Moeng Mala, alleging poor relations with the workers under their supervision [Para 9]. The section head resigned before the hearing, and the parties agreed to resolve only the case of team leader Moeng Mala [Para 14]. The company refused to transfer her, asserting its exclusive right to appoint and assign staff [Para 3].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must transfer team leader Moeng Mala to another location and replace her? [Para 52]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** They alleged that Moeng Mala allocated overtime unequally, favouring some team members and excluding others [Para 27][Para 28][Para 29], that she coerced workers into overtime by threatening to withhold future overtime [Para 30], that she failed to teach new production models properly [Para 30], that she glared at workers [Para 30][Para 31], and that she used inappropriate language when workers made mistakes [Para 32]. They provided a letter with nine workers' names and thumbprints asserting they were company employees [Para 77].
**Employer (respondent):** The company maintained that appointment and transfer are its exclusive management rights to ensure production runs smoothly [Para 3]. Moeng Mala denied all allegations [Para 33][Para 45], and the company argued the workers had failed to provide clear evidence or witnesses [Para 46]. The company also stated that if any worker has a problem, they can complain to the administration for resolution [Para 48][Para 49].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the demand for Moeng Mala's transfer was rejected [Para 75][Para 76].
The panel applied Article 65 of the Labour Law and Articles 1 and 22 of Decree No. 38 on Contracts [Para 52][Para 53][Para 56]. It held that only the parties to a contract can modify, terminate, or transfer it [Para 60][Para 61]. A third party, including a union or a group of workers, cannot compel a party to a contract to modify it [Para 61]. Transferring an employee to another location is a management right and an internal arrangement for the company's efficient operation [Para 64][Para 66]. The panel noted it consistently held in prior awards that it cannot order an employer to transfer or dismiss a worker or team leader at the request of the workers' party [Para 65]. It added that it can order a transfer or dismissal only where an employee has committed violence or caused harm to health, safety, or the safety of other workers [Para 68]. In this case, the panel found the workers had not provided specific evidence to support their allegations [Para 70][Para 71]. It also found no evidence that Moeng Mala had used violence or caused harm [Para 72]. Therefore, the demand was dismissed as lacking a proper legal basis [Para 73][Para 75].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union or group of workers cannot compel an employer to transfer a team leader, because only the parties to the employment contract may modify it, and the employer's right to manage transfers prevails unless there is proven violence or a threat to health and safety [Para 60][Para 61][Para 64][Para 68]. An allegation of unfair treatment in overtime allocation or poor teaching performance does not meet that threshold and fails without specific evidence [Para 70][Para 71][Para 72].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that the section head had resigned, rendering that part of the demand moot [Para 14]; it also observed that the union was not most representative, though this did not bar the claim [Para 19].
