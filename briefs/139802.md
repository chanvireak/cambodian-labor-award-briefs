# Award No. 109/13 — Bunna Institute Cambodia (Arbitration Council, 24 June 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Tang Try presiding; Seng Vouchhun, employer nominee; An Non, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139802

## 1. Facts
Bunna Institute Cambodia, an educational institution in Sangkat Phsar Thmei 3, Khan Daun Penh, Phnom Penh, formerly New World Institute, employed 102 staff. [Para 1][Para 27][Para 34] The Khmer Democratic Workers' Union at the institute, registered on 18 March 2013 with about 24 members, filed a nine-point demand on working conditions; conciliation on 17 May 2013 settled none of the points. [Para 18][Para 36][Para 37] Four months earlier the employer had dismissed four staff — three union leaders and one member — after they alone refused to thumbprint a pledge to stop union activity. [Para 58][Para 63][Para 143] The employer neither appeared at the hearing of 14 June 2013 nor gave a reason. [Para 22][Para 70]

## 2. Issues in dispute
The panel kept its original numbering after points 4, 7 and 9 were withdrawn. [Para 23]
1. Whether the employer must organise an election of worker delegates as the Labour Law requires? [Para 72][Para 73]
2. Whether the panel has jurisdiction over the demand that the employer register its internal rules with the ministry? [Para 79][Para 81]
3. Whether the employer must grant 90 days' maternity leave and half wages to pregnant staff? [Para 86]
5. Whether the panel has jurisdiction over the demand that the employer register staff with the National Social Security Fund? [Para 100][Para 101]
6. Whether the employer must grant 18 days' annual leave and pay for unused days when a contract ends? [Para 106]
8. Whether the employer discriminated against the union by collecting pledges to abandon union activity and dismissing the four who refused? [Para 124][Para 125][Para 128]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the institute had never held a delegate election; it had no registered internal rules, only a stamped draft circulated as an order; it gave no maternity leave or pay, the example being Ms Y, an assistant head of the studies office, who took leave at the end of 2012, received neither 90 days nor 50 percent, and lost her seniority; it had not registered staff with the Fund; it gave no annual leave, and the two members dismissed on 24 January 2013 were paid nothing for unused leave; and the four dismissals, after the pledge campaign and the statement that the institute did not need a union, were anti-union discrimination. At the hearing the workers reduced the maternity demand from three months at full pay to 90 days at 50 percent. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 41][Para 43][Para 45][Para 48][Para 52][Para 55][Para 57][Para 58]
**Employer (respondent):** advanced no position — no representative, no documents, no appearance on due notice — leaving only the workers' evidence and the conciliation file. [Para 26][Para 65][Para 70]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to hold a worker-delegate election, to grant 90 days' maternity leave with half wages to staff with one year's service, to grant 18 days' annual leave with payment for unused days, and to stop its anti-union practices; the demands on internal rules and on Fund registration were refused for want of jurisdiction. The order is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Minister. [Para 148][Para 150][Para 152][Para 153][Para 154]

The panel first held that it could proceed in the employer's absence, both parties having been duly invited and no valid excuse offered, under Article 21 of Prakas 099. [Para 66][Para 71]

On issue 1 it applied Article 283 of the Labour Law, which requires every enterprise with at least eight workers to elect worker delegates as the sole representatives of the workforce, and point 10 of Article 2 of Prakas 286, which puts the cost of the election on the employer; with 102 staff the threshold was met and no election had ever been held. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 77][Para 78]

On issues 2 and 5 it applied Article 302, which confines a collective labour dispute to working conditions, the exercise of rights granted to a recognised professional organisation in the enterprise, and like matters. Registering internal rules under Article 24, and registering staff with the Fund, were statutory duties owed to public bodies rather than terms of the employment relationship, so the panel declined jurisdiction over both. [Para 81][Para 82][Para 83][Para 84][Para 101][Para 103][Para 104]

On issue 3 it applied Articles 182 and 183: 90 days' leave to give birth, with no seniority condition on the leave itself, and half wages including allowances, that payment being confined to women with at least one year's service. [Para 86][Para 88][Para 89][Para 90][Para 92] The employer's practice breached both, and the order followed the statute rather than the workers' original demand for three months at full pay. [Para 98][Para 99]

On issue 6 it applied Article 166 (one and a half working days per month of service, that is 18 days a year) and Article 167 (leave usable only after a year, and payment for unused days when the contract ends first), following its earlier awards. [Para 106][Para 111][Para 113][Para 123]

On issue 8 it applied Article 271 (freedom to join a union), Article 12 (no employer may use union membership or activity as grounds for discipline or dismissal) and Article 279 (no employer may base hiring, promotion, pay, benefits, discipline or dismissal on union membership or activity). [Para 134][Para 135][Para 137][Para 142] It found that calling the three leaders and other members one by one to thumbprint a renunciation of union activity, posting vacancies for their four posts, then dismissing the four who refused without stated reason and after telling the union it was not needed, was discrimination against the union and a breach of all three articles. [Para 140][Para 143][Para 145][Para 147]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that the employer perform a statutory duty owed to a public authority — registering internal rules with the ministry, or registering staff with the National Social Security Fund — is not a collective labour dispute over working conditions or over the exercise of rights granted to a recognised union in the enterprise, and the panel has no jurisdiction to decide it; the same demand framed as a term of the employment relationship would be within jurisdiction. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 84][Para 103][Para 104]

Collecting individual thumbprints by which workers renounce union activity, and dismissing those who refuse without stating a reason, is anti-union discrimination: the employer's motive is established by the sequence of the pledge campaign, the immediate recruitment for the same posts, and the dismissal of exactly those who declined. [Para 140][Para 143][Para 145]

Maternity: the 90 days are owed regardless of seniority, the half wage only after one year's service. [Para 88][Para 92]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel asked for the list of union members and recorded that none was filed by the deadline, yet decided the discrimination issue without it, so the size of the affected membership played no part in the outcome. [Para 50][Para 51] It also observed that Ms Y lost her seniority on returning from maternity leave, a loss not claimed. [Para 48][Para 49]
