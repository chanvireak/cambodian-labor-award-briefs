# Award No. 021/16 — Star Light Apparel Manufacturing (Arbitration Council of Cambodia, 29 January 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chhea presiding; Ing Sothy appointed by the employer, Toun Si Phan appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 138975

## 1. Facts
Star Light Apparel Manufacturing (ស្តារ ឡៃ ថ៍ អី ភែរីល មេនូហ្វេ ក ឆឺរីង) is a factory at Seda Village, Vihear Suor Commune, Khsach Kandal District, Kandal Province. [Para 1]
Eleven demands were conciliated by the Kandal provincial Department of Labour on 25 January 2016 without agreement and referred by report No. 202/16 of the same date. [Para 18][Para 19]
The workers were on strike; the Kandal Provincial Court had issued injunction No. 008 of 22 January 2016 ordering the strike stopped and a return to work by shift, and about 300 workers continued striking and marched to the Ministry after the referral. [Para 20][Para 23] No hearing was ever held. [Para 31]

## 2. Issues in dispute
The panel recorded the eleven conciliation issues and reached none of them. [Para 2][Para 12][Para 31]
1. Whether the employer must take back Ms T (employee No. 1132538, QC section), whose contract it ended on 15 January 2016, without deducting wages or benefits, and also take on Ms K (employee No. 1132554, FQC)? [Para 2]
2. Whether the employer must stop interfering with the collection of union members and supporters outside working hours, even inside the factory compound? [Para 3]
3. Whether the employer must not discriminate against the union and must let it take part in resolving every worker's problem? [Para 4]
4. Whether the employer must pay pregnant workers their full wage including the "koun bi" (កូន ប៊ី) amount on the day they request maternity leave? [Para 5]
5. Whether the employer must grant sick leave and personal leave without creating difficulties of any kind? [Para 6]
6. Whether the employer must not use workers beyond their strength? [Para 7]
7. Whether the employer must pay workers 46,000 riel for Sunday overtime work? [Para 8]
8. Whether the employer must not end a contract at its expiry without reason? [Para 9]
9. Whether the employer must provide hot and cold drinking water that is hygienic? [Para 10]
10. Whether the employer must provide sufficient toilets and sufficient supplies? [Para 11]
11. Whether the employer must not impose excessive overtime or pressure workers' freedoms? [Para 12]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** they pursued the eleven demands above, led by reinstatement of two dismissed workers and an end to union interference; they did not attend the pre-hearing meeting of 27 January 2016, telling the Secretariat that they were still on strike and marching to the Ministry. [Para 2][Para 23][Para 38]
**Employer (respondent):** it attended the pre-hearing meeting and relied on the Provincial Court's injunction No. 008 of 22 January 2016 and on its own report of 28 January 2016 showing the strike continuing with no fixed end. [Para 22][Para 24][Para 25]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel terminated the arbitration proceedings in case No. 021/16 without deciding any of the eleven issues and ordered nothing. [Para 41][Para 42]
Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 requires the parties, throughout the panel's proceedings, to suspend every dispute measure such as a strike or lockout under Article 318 of the Labour Law, or any other measure that could aggravate the dispute, and to attend every meeting the panel calls. [Para 32][Para 33]
The panel read that provision, as it had in earlier awards (including Nos. 59/06, 31/08, 137/09, 138/09, 110/11 and 040/15), to mean that it will not continue arbitration while the workers are striking or the employer has locked out. [Para 34][Para 35]
On the facts it found the strike had not stopped after the court injunction of 22 January 2016, that the employer attended the pre-hearing meeting of 27 January 2016 while the workers did not, and that the employer's evidence showed the strike still continuing. [Para 36][Para 38][Para 39]
Because the strike continued during the arbitration process, the panel held it could not hold a hearing or resolve the collective dispute, and ended the proceedings under Article 20. [Para 40][Para 41]
No findings of fact on the eleven issues were made, precisely because no hearing took place. [Para 31]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The panel will not hear or decide a collective labour dispute while the workers are on strike or the employer has locked out: Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 obliges the parties to suspend all dispute measures for the duration of the arbitration, and a strike that continues after referral and after a court injunction requires the proceedings to be terminated rather than decided. [Para 32][Para 40][Para 41]
Termination on that ground is procedural, not a ruling on the merits; a referral made while the strike persists cannot produce an award on the demands. [Para 31][Para 42]

**Obiter dicta:** none identified.

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award prints the company name only in Khmer and in four different spellings (ស្តារ ឡៃ ថ៍ អី ភែរីល មេនូហ្វេ ក ឆឺរីង, អី ភែវីល, អី កែរីល, អ៊ីកែរីល), with no English rendering anywhere, so the English name given in the heading is a transliteration and not a printed form; the same variability affects the conciliation report number, printed as "២០២/១៦ កប/ឥណ", "ក.ប/កណ" and "កប/ភណ". [Para 1][Para 15][Para 27][Para 28][Para 42]
The panel expressly recorded that it made no findings of fact on any disputed issue because no hearing was held, so this brief can report only the parties' contentions, not tested facts. [Para 31]
The eleven demands survive only as a bare list with no reasons, dates or amounts beyond the 46,000 riel Sunday-overtime figure, because the case never reached the reasoning stage; some are truncated mid-phrase ("ស្នើសុំ ឱ្យ មិន រើសអើង សហជីព ដោយ អនុញ្ញាតិ ឱ្យ សហជីព ចូលរួម ដោយ ស្រាយ រាល់ កម្មករ មាន បញ្ហា"). [Para 4][Para 8]
Stray year tokens corrupt paras 16 ("ឆ្នាំ ២០២៧" and "2017"), 35 and 43, and the signature block for the worker-appointed arbitrator and the chair has collapsed into OCR noise, leaving only Ing Sothy's name legibly printed on the face; the composition at para 1 is relied on instead. [Para 1][Para 16][Para 43]
The two individual workers in demand 1 are reduced to initials with employee numbers, as required; their Khmer names as printed are not reproduced.
