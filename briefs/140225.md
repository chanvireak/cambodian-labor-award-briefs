# Order No. 237/12 — Master Piece Garment & Textile Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 10 January 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mr Ing Sothy for the employer party, Mr Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Mr Pen Bun Chhea chair; Mr Ing Sothy added a dissenting opinion) · Doc ID 140225

## 1. Facts
Master Piece Garment & Textile Co Ltd, a garment factory at Chom Chao, Phnom Penh with about 236 workers, faced eighteen demands from the Khmer Workers' Consciousness Union, a union that had still not obtained its registration certificate; the non-conciliation report No. 1433 of 11 December 2012 followed a strike from 11 December 2012 and left eighteen points open. [Para 22][Para 42][Para 66][Para 67] At the hearing of 20 December 2012 nine points were conciliated, five were withdrawn by the workers, and four remained for decision: attendance-bonus proration, a midday meal, a motorbike shed and a canteen. [Para 24][Para 31][Para 32] The union claimed 118 workers as claimants, but only three attended and none of them held a written authority to represent the others, so the panel decided the four points for those three alone, having relied on clause 19 of Prakas No. 099 and Awards 161/09 and 43/10. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 94][Para 97][Para 98][Para 99] The employer pays a USD 20 monthly attendance bonus, keeps it whole where permitted unpaid leave does not exceed one and a half days a month, and cuts all of it beyond that. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 75]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must prorate the attendance bonus to the number of days a worker is absent with the employer's permission? [Para 71][Para 100]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must provide one midday meal or a 5,000 riel daily meal allowance? [Para 77][Para 142]
3. (Issue 15) Whether the employer must build a proper motorbike shed? [Para 85][Para 153]
4. (Issue 16) Whether the employer must build a canteen with seating? [Para 88][Para 161]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** cutting the whole attendance bonus for a day of permitted leave costs them too much income, and the permitted absences were ordinary ones such as a short illness, a sibling's wedding or a religious festival; during the strike the employer had given non-strikers a meal and USD 3 a day, so the same should continue; there is no shed and no place to sit and eat. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 77][Para 85][Para 88]
**Employer (respondent):** it keeps its standing practice and relies on point 2 of Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012, which requires at least USD 10 a month for workers who attend every working day without absence; it cannot afford a meal allowance because orders and work have fallen and some workers are suspended, and there is no space on site for a shed or canteen, though it is negotiating to rent neighbouring land. [Para 76][Para 83][Para 84][Para 87][Para 91]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to prorate the attendance bonus to the days a worker is absent with permission, and the meal allowance, motorbike shed and canteen claims were declined; the order is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Minister. [Para 168][Para 169][Para 170][Para 171][Para 172]

On Issue 2 the panel found that the attendance bonus is a wage, since Article 103 includes sums paid as bonuses, so the rules on suspension of the contract apply. [Para 132][Para 133][Para 134] Under Article 71, sixth paragraph, and Article 72, first paragraph, an absence the employer has permitted under the law or an agreement suspends the main obligations of the contract, so no wage is owed for those days; the employer therefore may reduce the bonus but may not forfeit the whole month's entitlement. [Para 135][Para 136][Para 137][Para 140] Notification No. 041 of 7 March 2011, applied in Awards 112/11-Young Va, 136/11-Cambo Han Sa and 154/11-B&N, had been repealed and replaced by Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012, whose point 2 raises the floor to USD 10 a month for workers with no absence; paying USD 20 is lawful, and the word "absence" in that notification is not defined, so the panel declined to read it as covering either kind of absence and decided each case on its facts. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 105][Para 107][Para 110][Para 111][Para 115] Because the complainant workers had themselves asked to be allowed to take unpaid leave, the leave was a suspension by agreement and could not be treated as an absence at all. [Para 121][Para 122][Para 123]

Issues 3, 15 and 16 were held to be interests disputes: no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement or settlement provided for a meal allowance, a shed or a canteen. [Para 142][Para 143][Para 155][Para 158][Para 163][Para 165] Since an interests award becomes a collective agreement binding all workers under clause 43 of Prakas No. 099, the panel can decide such a claim only where the union holds most-representative status or a group of unions covers over half the workforce, following Awards 81/04-Ever Green, 98/04-Great Union, 169/11-Fortune Teo, 02/11-Pou Yuen and 66/11-In Han Sung; this union had no certificate, and the three claimants could not stand for the rest, so all three claims were declined. [Para 145][Para 146][Para 147][Para 148][Para 151][Para 152]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus is a wage, so where a worker takes unpaid leave with the employer's permission the contract is suspended for those days under Articles 71 and 72 and the employer may reduce the bonus only in proportion to the days absent, not forfeit it for the whole month; and a demand for a benefit provided by no law, collective agreement or settlement is an interests dispute that the panel will decline unless the claimant union holds most-representative status or represents over half the workforce, because such an award would bind every worker at the enterprise. [Para 134][Para 140][Para 146][Para 147]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the undefined word "absence" in Notification No. 230 cannot be construed generally, and that it would decide each case on its facts until a competent body interprets it, are guidance for future cases rather than part of the order. [Para 110][Para 115]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The arbitrator appointed by the employer filed a dissent on Issue 2, arguing from dictionary definitions of "bonus" that the giver of a bonus alone sets its conditions, and the order records his signature alongside the majority; both positions are printed. [Para 173][Para 175][Para 183][Para 186][Para 195]
- The employer's corporate name appears in at least four different Khmer spellings across the order and is never given in Latin script, so the English form used here follows the library catalogue and cannot be verified from the award text. [Para 1][Para 34][Para 51][Para 65]
- The claimant count is unstable: 118 claimed, three present, and one operative paragraph prints the number as a corrupted double figure; only the three who attended are decided for. [Para 68][Para 99][Para 150]
- The signature block and the date line of the dissent carry OCR debris, and the postponement date is printed as "ថ្ងៃទី ២៥ ១០ ខែមករា". [Para 33][Para 173][Para 195]
- Stray Latin and French fragments interrupt the reasoning and the dissent, and several reasoning paragraphs are duplicated verbatim. [Para 124][Para 135][Para 180]
- The union president is named repeatedly in the evidence list; he is recorded here by role, as are the three claimants. [Para 40][Para 53][Para 69]
