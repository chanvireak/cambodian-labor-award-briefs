# Award No. 035/20 — Cambodian Cultural Village (ភូមិ វប្បធម៌ កម្ពុជា) and the Tourism Sector Workers' Union at Cambodian Cultural Village (Arbitration Council, 17 July 2020)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, Tuon Si Phan, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 140566

## 1. Facts
The employer runs a cultural village in Siem Reap, registered on 3 June 2003, selling entry tickets and employing 325 workers [Para 145]. From 19 March 2020 it closed to visitors on the Ministry of Health's COVID-19 instructions but kept workers attending to rehearse, then cut working time by 1 hour 30 minutes a day, cut wages by 20 per cent for workers above USD 240 and capped others at USD 200, and suspended the USD 25 monthly food allowance and the two-meal daily benefit for restaurant staff from April to June 2020 [Para 149][Para 150][Para 175]. The union filed three demands; the workers later merged the first two, leaving the panel two points to decide [Para 19][Para 20].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issues 1 and 2, wages) Whether the workers agreed to the 20 per cent wage cut and to wages being reduced to USD 200 from April to June 2020? [Para 237]
2. (Issues 1 and 2, food allowance) Whether the employer could unilaterally suspend the USD 25 monthly food allowance and the two-meal benefit for restaurant staff? [Para 281]
3. (Issues 1 and 2, the USD 5) Whether the USD 5 stopped from workers paid under USD 200 was wage or a living allowance? [Para 296]
4. (Issue 3) Whether abolishing the arts office and reassigning its staff was lawful and reasonable rather than anti-union discrimination? [Para 309]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), Tourism Sector Workers' Union at Cambodian Cultural Village (សហជីព និយោជិត វិស័យ ទេសចរណ៍ នៃ ក្រុមហ៊ុន ភូមិ វប្បធម៌ កម្ពុជា):** the company was not within the scope of the Ministry of Health instruction of 17 March 2020, it never met the most representative union before deciding, and it cut hours, wages and allowances on its own without the labour inspector's authorisation, contrary to Article 71 of the Labour Law [Para 155][Para 159]. Workers did not protest individually because they were waiting for the union to protest for them [Para 162].

**Employer (respondent), Cambodian Cultural Village, registered as ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខល ឆឺរល វីលេច រី ហ្សូត:** the closure left it without income, so it met all department heads on 18 and 30 March 2020, conceded their request to cut only 20 per cent and 1 hour 30 minutes, and 218 workers thumbprinted their agreement, with 210 accepting happily [Para 163][Para 168][Para 251]. The USD 5 is a living allowance, not wage, and no law obliges it to maintain the food allowance [Para 170][Para 295].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to repay the 20 per cent cut taken from workers earning above USD 240 and the amount by which wages of workers on USD 200 to 240 were capped at USD 200, for April to June 2020, to the 29 workers who had not agreed, and to restore their former working hours; it rejected the food-allowance, USD 5 and arts-office demands [Para 328][Para 329][Para 330][Para 331].

On the wage claim the panel held the dispute was properly brought by the most representative union, but that a claim for individual wages and benefits requires each worker's specific written authorisation, citing Article 54 of the 2016 Trade Union Law and Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 [Para 216][Para 221][Para 226]. It accordingly refused to treat the four unauthenticated name lists as claimants but accepted the 28 workers who had thumbprinted a written demand and the 6 union leaders who attended both hearings [Para 227][Para 229][Para 230]. Applying Articles 311, 336, 337, 345 and 350 of the Civil Code, it held that contract terms can be changed only by consent and that a forced modification is void; since only 5 of the claimants had agreed, the old terms survived for the other 29 [Para 237][Para 243][Para 258][Para 272][Para 279].

On the food allowance the panel found that Decision HR 213/15 of 30 June 2015 granting USD 25 a month was the employer's own unilateral act, not an agreement, and that no law or regulation required it; under Article 2 of the Labour Law the management right, exercised lawfully and reasonably, allowed suspension [Para 287][Para 288][Para 291][Para 292]. On the USD 5 it applied the wage definition in Article 103 and Decision HR 146/19 of 6 April 2019, held the sum to be a living allowance, and found on the payroll and tax tables that wages under USD 200 had not in fact been reduced [Para 297][Para 298][Para 301]. On the arts office it again relied on Article 2: the restructuring kept hours, wages and benefits unchanged, and the sole evidence of anti-union motive was a note by one worker who did not appear as a witness, so the discrimination claim failed for want of proof [Para 316][Para 319][Para 321][Para 324].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may not impose a wage reduction or shorter hours by unilateral decision: altering the terms of a labour contract requires each worker's consent, and where consent is missing the panel orders repayment of the sums deducted and restoration of the earlier terms [Para 243][Para 279][Para 328]. An allowance that the employer granted by its own unilateral decision, and that no law, regulation or agreement requires, may be suspended under the management right in Article 2 of the Labour Law [Para 291][Para 292]. A most representative union may bring a collective dispute, but a demand for individual wages and benefits must be supported by each worker's specific written authorisation [Para 226].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel added that the employer keeps the right to arrange the former working hours of the 29 workers as before, and recorded that a majority of the workforce had in fact accepted the new terms [Para 251][Para 328].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The recitals print every year as "ឆ្នាំ ២០" with the last two digits lost, so 2019 and 2020 cannot be told apart from the text alone [Para 14][Para 15]. The operative paragraph states the rejected food allowance as "៤២៥ ដុល្លារ" while the reasons throughout use USD 25 [Para 293][Para 329]. Claimant counts are printed inconsistently as 28, 34, 29, 340 and 297 in different passages, and the panel's own figure of 34 is reached by adding 28 to 6 [Para 231][Para 252][Para 276]. The conciliation report is dated 11 June 2020 in one recital and 10 June 2020 in another [Para 9][Para 15]. The signature block and the appended authorisation forms are destroyed OCR, and individual workers, union officers and the employer's representatives are reduced here to roles or initials.
