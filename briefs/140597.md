# Award No. 28/07 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ដេហ្វាង ហ្គាម៉ន ខូអិលធីឌី (De Fang Garment Co., Ltd) (Arbitration Council, 10 April 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Kong Phalkun chair) · Doc ID 140597

## 1. Facts
De Fang Garment Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ដេហ្វាង ហ្គាម៉ន ខូអិលធីឌី) occupies buildings 28–29 at Canadia Garden, Chom Chao, Dangkao, Phnom Penh, and employs 550 workers; the complaint was filed by the Cambodian Workers' Union Federation (សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា) and its factory union, claiming 64 members and concerning 50 workers. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 23][Para 24][Para 49]
Twenty-two demands went to conciliation on 06 March 2007, which settled nineteen; the three remaining were referred on 19 March 2007, and at the hearing of 29 March 2007 the parties settled points 1 and 2 too — staggered fixed-duration contracts of 3, 6 and then 9 months, and wages and the USD 5 bonus preserved for 1 to 3 days' excused leave. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 4][Para 19][Para 25][Para 26]
Only point 3 remained: Mr R (name redacted here), a chief cutter paid USD 140 a month and elected president of the factory union, was refused entry to the factory from 26 February 2007, when the company paid him to 26 February and barred him thereafter; he had notified the company of the union's formation on 19 February 2007, and about 30 workers struck in support of him on 26 and 27 February 2007. [Para 30][Para 32][Para 39][Para 40][Para 42][Para 43] His last contract ran two months, from 23 January 2007, and the union had not filed its registration papers with the Ministry. [Para 29][Para 32][Para 35]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 3, first question) Whether Mr R, an elected union president, was a worker entitled to the special protection against dismissal? [Para 62][Para 63]
2. (Issue 3, second question) Whether the company's termination of his fixed-duration contract was lawful under the Labour Law? [Para 62][Para 86]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា and its De Fang union:** the dismissal was union discrimination, because he founded the union, was elected its president and joined the strike of 27 February 2007; the company also took about ten workers to sign resignations from the union in front of the federation's office; he asked to return on 27 March 2007 and was refused. [Para 6][Para 45][Para 47]
**Employer (respondent) — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ដេហ្វាង ហ្គាម៉ន ខូអិលធីឌី:** he was absent without leave from 27 February to 06 March 2007, so he abandoned his job; he led an unlawful strike, tried to set up a union and lured workers into it with USD 30 a month; the company accepts he was elected union president and did not contest that it notified him of nothing in advance. [Para 7][Para 8][Para 34][Para 41][Para 44][Para 51][Para 52][Para 53]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the company to take Mr R back into work, finding the termination contrary to Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law even though he held no protected status and his contract had run out. [Para 113][Para 114]

On the first question the panel set out Article 293(1) of the Labour Law, requiring the labour inspector's agreement before a staff delegate or candidate is dismissed, Article 7 of Prakas No. 313 of 27 November 2000, which extends that protection to the president, first vice-president and first secretary of a union registered with the Ministry, and Article 4(1)–(2) of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, which protects founders and members from the date of the registration application until 30 days after registration. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 66][Para 67][Para 68] It drew four conditions from that Prakas: the worker must belong to a protected category, the union must notify the employer of who is covered, a copy of that notice must go to the Ministry, and a registration application must be filed with the Labour Dispute Department. [Para 77][Para 78][Para 79][Para 80]
Conditions (a) and (b) were met: he was elected president, and the union told the employer who he was. [Para 81][Para 82] Conditions (c) and (d) were not, because no copy went to the Ministry and no registration application was filed, so he enjoyed no special protection and the company needed no prior approval to dismiss him. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 85][Para 90]
On the second question the panel turned to Article 12, which forbids any disciplinary or contract-termination decision taken on the ground of union membership or activity, Article 279, which bars discrimination in hiring, assignment, wages, discipline and dismissal, and the penalties in Articles 369 and 373. [Para 91][Para 92][Para 93][Para 94] Following Awards 10/03 (Jacques Titch), 41/04 (Mikasa), 17/07 (Cham Tich Thai), 02/04 (Cambodiana Hotel) and 17/04 (Chhievy You), it found the company ended the employment because he tried to form a union and joined the strike. [Para 87][Para 96][Para 98][Para 99][Para 100]
Article 73 lets a fixed-duration contract expire at its term, and his ran out on 23 March 2007, before the hearing. [Para 103][Para 106][Para 102] The panel nonetheless held that Articles 12 and 279 also forbid a refusal to renew a fixed-duration contract for anti-union reasons, so the non-renewal here was unlawful and reinstatement was ordered. [Para 107][Para 109][Para 111][Para 112][Para 113]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union leader who is not registered, whose union has not sent the Ministry a copy of the notice naming him and has not applied for registration, falls outside the protected categories of Prakas No. 313 and Prakas No. 305, so the employer needs no labour inspector's approval to dismiss him; but Articles 12 and 279 still forbid ending his employment — including by refusing to renew a fixed-duration contract that has run its term — for union membership or activity, and such a termination is unlawful and calls for reinstatement. [Para 83][Para 90][Para 107][Para 111]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's account of the strike, of the resignations the company collected in front of the federation office, and of its observation that remaining members had also resigned, are narrative findings that were not needed once discrimination was established. [Para 47][Para 48]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The last contract is printed as running "from 23 January 2007 to 23 March 2003" [Para 29], while the panel later treats it as expiring on 23 March 2007 [Para 102]; the 2003 date is plainly a slip but is left as printed rather than corrected.
- The chair is គង់ ផល្គុន in the header [Para 1] and គង់ ផល្លៈ in the signature block [Para 116], where the employer-party arbitrator's name is replaced by OCR fragments.
- The number of unresolved points shifts between three and four in one sentence ("០៣ ៤ ចំណុច") [Para 19], and the evidence list mixes section labels (8, 7, 1) out of order [Para 20].
- The deadline for further documents is printed as 30 March 2006 [Para 36] and as 30 March 2007 [Para 56], a year apart for the same request.
- The union claims 64 members but never gave a membership list to the company or the panel [Para 49], and the panel could verify only eight resignation letters dated 28 February 2007 [Para 50]; the company's claim that all remaining members resigned is recorded but unsupported [Para 48].
- Individual names of the worker, the union officers and the employer representatives appear in full in the source and are reduced to initials and roles here. [Para 2][Para 6]
