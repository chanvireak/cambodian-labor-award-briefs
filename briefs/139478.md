# Award No. 309/15 — Vana Mi (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 18 December 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, An Non, Pen Bun Chhea — Chair) · Doc ID 139478

## 1. Facts
Vana Mi (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd. cuts and sews clothing at Samrong village, Sour Phi, Bati, Takeo, employing about 720 workers; the Khmer Youth Union at the factory was registered by the Ministry on 20 July 2015 under certificate no. 3318, with a president, vice-president and secretary confirmed by Ministry letter no. 095/15. [Para 31][Para 32][Para 33][Para 34] On 21 September 2015 the Khmer Youth Union Federation complained seeking improved working conditions; conciliation on 16 November 2015 settled three of eleven demands, two were withdrawn and the remaining six went to the Secretariat on 20 November 2015. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20] At the hearing of 07 December 2015 the parties settled issues 2, 4, 5 and 6 and the workers withdrew issue 1, leaving only issue 3. [Para 17][Para 23][Para 24]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay the full 50% maternity wage, including allowances, for the 90-day maternity leave period before the worker begins her leave? [Para 37][Para 41]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The 50% maternity wage is needed at the time of giving birth and for daily living, and paying it in two instalments causes hardship to pregnant workers who must travel to collect the second half. [Para 38][Para 39]
**Employer (respondent):** It already pays 50% of wages and allowances for 90 days, half before the leave and half on return to normal work; it cannot pay the whole sum in advance because it fears the worker will decide not to come back to the factory. [Para 37][Para 40]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to pay 50% of wages and allowances for the 90-day maternity period at least one day before the worker begins her leave. [Para 54]

Article 182(1) requires every enterprise covered by the Labour Law to grant maternity leave, and Article 183(1) entitles a woman to half her wage during that leave, calculated to include allowances, paid by the employer. Neither article states when the payment must be made. [Para 41][Para 42] The panel therefore turned to Article 115(3), which forbids payment of wages on a rest day and requires payment a day earlier where the due date falls on one, and asked whether the maternity wage is a wage for this purpose; Article 103 defines wages to include the amounts the employer must pay during maternity leave. [Para 43][Para 45][Para 46] Following Award 154/11-B&N, which applied Article 115 to the payment of the 50% maternity wage, and Awards 97/06, 91/07, 58/11, 81/11, 180/14 and 277/15, the panel held that Article 115 governs this payment too. [Para 47][Para 49][Para 50] The employer's practice of withholding the second half until the worker returns therefore does not comply with Article 115, and to comply the employer must pay the whole 50% at least one day before the maternity leave begins. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The half-wage payable during maternity leave is a wage within Article 103, so the timing rule in Article 115(3) applies to it: an employer may not defer any part of it until the worker returns, and must pay the entire 50% for the 90-day period at least one day before the leave begins. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 52]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded the employer's stated fear that advance payment would deter return to work but did not treat it as a legal obstacle, deciding the point purely on Article 115. [Para 40][Para 51]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award states that no documents at all were received from the employer, although both parties are recorded as present at the hearing and the employer's practice is described in detail. [Para 22][Para 26][Para 37] The conciliation record is dated 20 October 2015 while the conciliation itself is said to have taken place on 16 November 2015 and the report is dated 18 November 2015. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 28] The dispositive paragraph prints the rate as "4 50 per cent" where the reasoning throughout says 50 per cent. [Para 52][Para 53] Article numbers are corrupted by OCR — Article 182 is rendered as "មាត្រា១ ២០១៥" and a block of English noise is inserted inside the quotation of Article 182. [Para 41] The company name appears as "Vana Mi", "Vona Mee" and "Vana Mee" across the text, and the employer's representatives are the only individuals given in Latin script rather than Khmer. [Para 1][Para 31][Para 33] Both the arbitrators' signature lines and the union leaders' names are largely illegible or reduced to initials, so the panel composition is recoverable only from the header. [Para 1][Para 56]
