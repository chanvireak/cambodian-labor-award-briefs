# Award No. 237/14 — Yok Mas (Cambodia) Garment IMF Ltd (Arbitration Council, 17 September 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom, appointed by the employer; Tun Si Phan, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chea, president) · Doc ID 139703

## 1. Facts
Yok Mas (Cambodia) Garment IMF Ltd cuts and sews garments and employs about 950 workers; it and the Cambodian Labour Solidarity Trade Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព សាមគ្គីភាព ការងារ កម្ពុជា) both signed the 3 October 2012 Memorandum of Understanding on professional relations in the garment industry. Of 20 claims raised in conciliation, four remained unsettled; one of those was settled at the hearing, leaving three. The union elected a local union at the company on 20 July 2014 but had not yet obtained its registration. [Para 15][Para 26]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to pro-rate the USD 5 "punctuality money" by the number of days of authorised leave taken, in the same way as the USD 10 attendance bonus? [Para 58]
2. Whether the employer must grant male workers whose wives give birth at least 7 days' leave on full wages and benefits without deducting those days from annual leave? [Para 126]
3. Whether the employer must pay an additional 1,000 riel per hour of meal allowance for overtime worked between 16:00 and 18:00, making 2,000 riel per hour? [Para 158]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The USD 5 should be pro-rated like the attendance bonus because it is paid for regular attendance; a father should get at least 7 days on full pay when his wife gives birth, without charging it against annual leave; and the meal allowance of 1,000 riel per overtime hour does not buy adequate food, other companies paying 2,000 riel. [Para 69][Para 92]
**Employer (respondent):** The USD 5 is a special bonus with a condition attached — forfeited entirely on any day's absence, lawful or not — so pro-rating would destroy its special character; the company already applies the Labour Law on special leave; and the law requires only 2,000 riel of meal allowance per day, which is met at 1,000 riel per hour over two hours. [Para 76][Para 94]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three claims were refused and no sum was ordered. [Para 172]
On issue 1 the panel found that the employer pays a USD 10 attendance bonus under Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 and, separately, a USD 5 "punctuality money" from the day the company opened, on 19 June 2012. There is no written agreement on the USD 5, but the employer announced orally to all workers that it would be cancelled in full for any absence from one day upwards, and the payroll shows it under that name. The panel held that Article 2 of the Labour Law leaves the employer free to arrange bonus schemes beyond those the law requires, and that such a scheme is lawful where it does not infringe workers' rights and gives more than the law requires; the USD 5 is therefore a discretionary incentive, not subject to the pro-rating claimed. [Para 63][Para 114][Para 119]
On issue 2 the panel set out Article 171 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 267 of 11 October 2001: special leave of up to 7 days a year may be taken for personal or family events, including a wife giving birth, without loss of wages, but the employer may charge those days against unused annual leave, or require the worker to make up the hours once annual leave is exhausted. The company's practice follows that rule, and granting 7 further days free of any deduction would be a benefit beyond the law. The panel therefore treated the claim as an interest dispute and, the claimant union not being the most representative union in the enterprise, refused to consider it. [Para 130][Para 141]
On issue 3 the panel applied point 2 of Ministry Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011, which entitles a worker who volunteers for overtime at the employer's request to 2,000 riel of meal allowance per day, or a free meal, without specifying hours. The employer pays 1,000 riel per overtime hour, so two hours yields 2,000 riel and the notification is satisfied; the demand for a further 1,000 riel is a benefit above the legal minimum, hence an interest dispute, refused on the same representativeness ground. [Para 159][Para 165][Para 171]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer has voluntarily set a bonus above what the Labour Law or a Ministry notification requires, and has announced its conditions to the workforce, the panel will treat the scheme as an exercise of managerial discretion under Article 2 of the Labour Law and will not rewrite those conditions — such as a forfeiture rule — at the workers' request; a demand to improve on the scheme is an interest dispute that only a most representative union may bring. [Para 114][Para 121]
Compliance with a notification fixing a daily meal allowance is measured against the daily amount, not the hourly rate, so paying 1,000 riel per hour for two hours of overtime discharges the 2,000 riel daily obligation. [Para 159][Para 165]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that borrowing annual leave from the following year to cover special leave, as the company allowed, does not accord with Article 171 and Prakas No. 267. [Para 137]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is dated 17 September 2014 in the header, but the reasons state it would issue on 16 September 2013 and elsewhere give 16 September 2014; the panel's constitution carries "27 August 2019 2014". [Para 78][Para 86] The claim on the USD 5 is described in the conciliation summary as "10 dollars plus 5 dollars divided by 26 days", which does not match the scheme analysed in the reasons. [Para 2] The lunch-allowance claim appears as USD 15.20 per month in the claim summary and USD 15 per month in the issue heading. [Para 77] Issue 4 is printed as "ចំណុច វិវាទ ប៊ី៤", and the reasons carry stray Latin fragments ("John", "Naval", "Joh"), indicating OCR loss in the statutory quotations. [Para 140] The employer's name appears with several Khmer transliterations of "IMF" ("អិម អេហូជី", "អិម អេហ្វ ជី"). [Para 50]
