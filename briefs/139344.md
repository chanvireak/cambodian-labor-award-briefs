# Award No. 76/04 — MV Garment International Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 September 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach, An Nan, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 139344

## 1. Facts
MV Garment International Co., Ltd, on National Road No. 5 in Kampong Chhnang, employed 4,000 workers. On 23 August 2004 some 3,500 workers struck over 17 demands; conciliation on 27 August 2004 settled 14 of them and three went to the Arbitration Council. On 21 August 2004 the employer had already suspended the labour contracts of five union leaders and asked the labour inspectors to dismiss them for serious misconduct, so the live question was whether those suspensions and the dismissal request could stand. [Para 33][Para 37][Para 41]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company should increase the wages of workers holding the position of group chief and above? [Para 19]
2. Whether pregnant workers in the heavy Knitting section should be moved to a light section? [Para 19][Para 20]
3. Whether the five union leaders whose contracts were suspended should return to work? [Para 23]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The company had given increases to some workers in January and July 2004 but not to group chiefs; pregnant knitters should be moved to light work; and the five leaders had done nothing warranting suspension, so they must be reinstated. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 23]

**Employer (respondent):** It had already raised wages by skill in January and July 2004, would provide chairs and unpaid suspension instead of moving pregnant workers, and had filed a dismissal request with the labour inspectors because the five had committed serious misconduct under Article 83 of the 1997 Labour Law. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 24][Para 41]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the company must pay full wages to the five suspended workers from 21 August 2004 until the Labour Inspector informs them, and must reinstate them if the Inspector declines or fails to decide the dismissal within one month; if the dismissal is upheld, wages run from the date of the award. [Para 52][Para 51]

Issues 1 and 2 were disposed of by agreement at the hearing of 10 September 2004, so the panel reasoned only on the third. [Para 39][Para 40]

On issue 3 the employer relied on Article 295, which lets a manager suspend a contract instantly pending the Labour Inspector's decision where there is serious misconduct, and on Article 83(B), which lists incitement of other workers to commit serious offences, threats and abusive language, and serious breaches of discipline. The panel read Article 295 as available only where the conduct actually falls inside Article 83(B). [Para 41][Para 42]

The panel held that an unlawful strike is not itself serious misconduct, and that inciting an illegal strike is therefore not serious misconduct either — the strike from 23 to 25 August 2004 had been called without prior notice, but that illegality did not convert the leaders' conduct into an Article 83(B) offence. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 42]

On the threat allegation, witnesses said only that they had heard a rumour that the union would hire gangsters against workers doing overtime; none had heard it from the five leaders themselves, and the five denied it. The panel treated a rumour heard at second hand as incapable of proving the words were said. Witnesses did say they had directly heard threatening or abusive language, but declined to give further evidence, so that strand was not made out either. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49]

Riding bicycles and motorcycles inside the factory breached the internal rules, but the panel classed it as a disciplinary violation for which a warning was the appropriate response, not a ground for terminating or suspending a contract. [Para 49][Para 50]

Having found no Article 83(B) misconduct, the panel held the Article 295 suspensions incorrect and ordered wages for the suspension period, noting that Article 293 presumes a dismissal request refused if the inspectors do not decide within one month. [Para 50][Para 51]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension under Article 295 is lawful only if the conduct alleged actually constitutes serious misconduct within Article 83(B); calling or inciting an unlawful strike, and breaches of internal rules that merit no more than a warning, do not qualify, so the employer that suspends on that basis must pay the worker's wages for the suspension period. [Para 41][Para 43][Para 49][Para 51]

**Obiter dicta:** That a rumour heard from others cannot establish that threatening words were spoken, and that the employer may warn workers who ride motorbikes on factory premises. [Para 48][Para 49]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer is printed three ways: "MV Garment International Co., Ltd" on the face, "M&V Company international" in the case summary, and the union as "Union of workers at MS Garment International", which also mismatches the NIFTUC affiliation. The order in paragraph 52 runs wages from 21 August 2004, while the reasoning in paragraph 51 says that if the dismissal is upheld wages are calculated from the date the award issued; both are reported as printed. Issue 3 speaks of four leaders while paragraph 41 and the order speak of five. The hearing is fixed at 9:30 a.m. in the hearing record and at 8:00 a.m. in the case summary. OCR losses are heavy throughout ("theemployer", "thearing", "Inon-conciliation", "NIA" for a telephone number, "Pat Viratha" for Phat Virakratha), and individual workers are identified here only by role. [Para 1][Para 23][Para 33][Para 38][Para 30][Para 51][Para 52]
