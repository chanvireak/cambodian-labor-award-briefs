# Award No. 33/12 — Dai Yang (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 16 March 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Tuon Si Phan, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 139958

## 1. Facts
Dai Yang (Cambodia) is a garment factory employing 1,050 workers, and the Cambodian Workers' Trade Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ការងារ កម្មករ កម្ពុជា) is the claimant with 562 members and the status of most representative union, certified until 16 June 2012. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 66][Para 67] On 26 August 2011 the company issued a unilateral notice promising, among other things, an extra 3 USD milk allowance (making 7 USD a month), a 1,000 riel daily travel allowance for workers working 7:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m., and a 1,000 riel holiday meal payment, all effective September 2011; it paid none of it for September and began only in October 2011. [Para 70][Para 74][Para 75] The union's demand that the notice be honoured produced 29 non-conciliated points, of which the workers withdrew 18 and the parties settled 9, leaving the claim on the notice's points 1 and 2. [Para 41][Para 87]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must comply with its notice of 26 August 2011 at points 1 and 2, by paying back the 3 USD milk allowance and the 1,000 riel daily travel allowance for September 2011? [Para 87]
2. Whether that demand is a dispute over rights (វិវាទ អំពី សិទ្ធិ) or over interests, and therefore whether the award binds the parties at once or may be opposed? [Para 81][Para 84]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The strike was the workers' own decision, but the company must still honour the notice it issued; 35 union members with small children claim the 3 USD milk allowance and 562 members claim the 1,000 riel travel allowance. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 85]
**Employer (respondent):** It could not apply the notice from September 2011 because the nationwide strike of 16 to 21 August 2011 cost it about 25,000 USD — missed shipments, air freight instead of sea freight and penalties — so it began paying from October 2011. [Para 78][Para 100]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the company is ordered to pay 3 USD per worker to 35 female workers as milk allowance and 1,000 riel per worker per day to 562 workers working 7:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m., both for September 2011; the rights-dispute order binds immediately and no order is made on the interest dispute. [Para 105][Para 106][Para 107][Para 108]
The panel first classified the demand. Following its reasoning in Awards 05/11-Im Vey (branch 1) and 13/11-Gol Kamvimech, a rights dispute is one concerning an entitlement set by law, a collective agreement, a convention or another instrument; because the entitlement here is written in the employer's own notice, the demand is a rights dispute. [Para 81][Para 82][Para 84]
On the merits, Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 664 of the 2007 Civil Code define the employment contract, and Article 309 of the Civil Code makes a unilateral act a source of obligation, so one party may bind itself alone toward the other. [Para 87][Para 89][Para 91][Para 93] The company did exactly that by issuing the notice, which fixed no condition on entitlement and set a precise effective date; Article 329 of the Civil Code lets the author of a unilateral act determine when it takes effect. [Para 94][Para 97][Para 102] The notice therefore took effect in September 2011 and the September payments were owed. [Para 98][Para 99] The strike losses were a separate matter from what the notice provided and could not excuse non-performance of an unconditional unilateral obligation. [Para 103][Para 104]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A benefit an employer grants by unilateral notice, with no condition attached and a stated effective date, creates an enforceable obligation from that date; the employer cannot withhold it for the first month on the ground that a strike in the preceding month caused it loss, because industrial action is extraneous to the terms of the notice. A claim to such a benefit is a rights dispute, so the panel's order on it binds the parties immediately and cannot be opposed. [Para 84][Para 102][Para 104][Para 107]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observation that a party may create an obligation for itself toward the other by unilateral act, and its two-award survey of the rights-dispute definition. [Para 82][Para 93]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The hearing date is printed as 02 March 2011 in the procedural summary but 02 March 2012 where the summons and hearing are described, and the complaint was received on 17 January 2012, so the 2011 date cannot be right; both are reported as printed. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 39] The record is inconsistent about which points survived conciliation: 29 points are said to leave points 2 and 28 unsettled and merged, while the panel later treats points 1 and 28 as the single merged issue. [Para 42][Para 68] Point 3 of the notice (holiday meal) is included in the demand as filed but the workers dropped it at the hearing, and the panel says it will consider only points 1 and 2. [Para 68][Para 87] OCR has corrupted several Khmer passages, including the chair arbitrator's printed title, and the employer's loss figure is printed as 25,000 USD in digits but as 250,000 in the Khmer words that follow (ម្ភៃ ប្រាំ ម៉ឺន). [Para 78][Para 108]
