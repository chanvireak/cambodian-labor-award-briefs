# Award No. 129/16 — Global A Paraelimited (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 15 June 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Toun Si Phan for the worker party, Vun Saray chair) · Doc ID 139039

## 1. Facts
Global A Paraelimited, a garment factory on National Road 4, Kambol commune, Pur Senchey district, Phnom Penh, announced it would close permanently on 31 October 2016 and began closing in stages. [Para 1][Para 8][Para 16] The workers struck, demanding notice pay (Art. 75), dismissal damages (Art. 89), moral damages (Art. 91), the last month's wage (Art. 116) and annual-leave compensation (Art. 166) according to whether their contracts were undetermined-duration or fixed-duration exceeding two years, plus 5% damages for fixed-duration contracts of under two years. [Para 3] Conciliation on 2 June 2016 failed on all six points and the report was sent to the Secretariat on 3 June 2016. [Para 19][Para 23][Para 24] The panel issued Order No. 009/16 on 6 June 2016 requiring the strike to stop and all workers to return from the morning of 7 June 2016; the workers did not return and continued striking. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 33]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay notice pay (Art. 75), dismissal damages (Art. 89), moral damages (Art. 91), the last month's wage (Art. 116) and annual-leave compensation (Art. 166) to workers on undetermined-duration contracts and on fixed-duration contracts exceeding two years? [Para 3]
2. Whether the workers may demand that the company close its doors and pay all compensation at once during June? [Para 7]
3. Whether the company must provide maternity allowance to pregnant workers in the event of closure? [Para 9]
4. Whether the company must pay other benefits correctly in accordance with the law? [Para 11]
5. Whether the company must repay in full the January 2016 wages deducted from the nine worker representatives? [Para 13]
6. Whether the company must retain all workers whose contracts end this month, paying wages as usual, pending legal resolution? [Para 15]
7. (The procedural question the panel in fact decided) Whether the arbitration proceedings should continue at all? [Para 42]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company owes the full set of statutory termination entitlements by contract type, must close and settle everything in June, must "consider" maternity allowance for pregnant workers if it closes, must repay the deducted January 2016 wages to the nine representatives, and must keep all workers on payroll until the dispute is resolved. [Para 3][Para 7][Para 9][Para 13][Para 15]
**Employer (respondent):** it accepts that contracts exceeding two years remain fixed-duration contracts, has already paid the 5% damages at each contract's end, will pay the rest according to the Labour Law, will close the factory in stages until 31 October 2016 and then permanently, and cannot repay the January wages because the workers did not work. [Para 4][Para 5][Para 6][Para 8][Para 12][Para 14][Para 16]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel ordered termination of the arbitration proceedings in case No. 129/16, deciding none of the six compensation claims. [Para 59][Para 60]

The panel noted it had received no witness or expert evidence beyond the parties' documents, and that it made no factual findings on the disputed issues because it never held a hearing. [Para 34][Para 41] Its reasoning turned on Article (Prakas) 20 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY of 21 April 2004: during the panel's proceedings the disputing parties must suspend all dispute measures such as strike or lockout, and must attend every meeting to which the panel summons them. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44] The panel read that provision, as it had in earlier awards, to mean it will not conduct arbitration while workers are striking or an employer is locking out. [Para 45] It relied on a line of its own orders — Nos. 5906, 3908, 13709 & 13809, 110/11 Simano, 104/13 Sabrina (Cambodia), 11913 Pov Yea, 019/14 Dong Do Tech Style (Cambodia) and 322/14 Jimly (Cambodia) — and on case 04/04 IM SI, where it notified that a party striking during proceedings had no right to have the dispute resolved. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48] It also cited case 18/04 (the hotels), whose order of 22 April 2004 required the strike to stop from 23 April 2004 and reserved the right to terminate if a party breached the order, case 77/05 Franco Nith Ting, where continued strike meant no resolution, and cases 110/11 Simano, 17/13 Internal Fashion, and 066/14 Callisto Apparel (Cambodia), all terminated for continued strike. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 54] Applying that to the facts, the panel found Order No. 009/16 had been disobeyed, the parties confirmed at the pre-hearing meeting of 9 June 2016 that the strike continued, a grace period to 14 June 2016 was granted, and the strike still continued on 14 June 2016. [Para 55][Para 56] It concluded the workers had no intention of having the collective dispute resolved by the Council because they continued to strike without respecting its order, and that it could not continue the proceedings or hold a hearing in that situation. [Para 57][Para 58]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the panel has ordered a strike suspended and the workers ordered back to work, and the workers continue striking through the grace period the panel allowed, the panel will terminate the arbitration proceedings without deciding the merits: Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, Article 20, obliges parties to suspend strike or lockout measures during arbitration, and continued strike is treated as an absence of intention to have the dispute arbitrated. [Para 42][Para 44][Para 57][Para 58]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's survey of its earlier orders, including the reservation of a right to terminate on breach in case 18/04 and the "no right to have the dispute resolved" notification in case 04/04, is illustrative of practice rather than necessary to the order; the panel added that it may resolve the collective dispute afresh once the workers stop striking. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 50][Para 54]
