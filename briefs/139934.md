# Award No. 02/12-Tae Young — Tae Young (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 9 February 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun, Tuon Siphann, chair Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 139934

## 1. Facts
Tae Young (Cambodia) Co., Ltd began operating on 13 June 2011 and employs 647 workers; three unions exist at the factory and the claimant Local Union of CLUF, registered 22 September 2011, claims to represent 472 of them. [Para 31] [Para 32] [Para 33] The employer had paid sewing workers a daily 1,000 riel incentive bonus on reaching the production target, then changed the practice to pay it only for a full week's target; the workers did not agree, and after a strike the employer suspended the three CLUF leaders on 6 December 2011 and on 17 January 2012 notified their dismissal effective 17 February 2012. [Para 34] [Para 35] [Para 36] [Para 39] It sought the Labour Inspector's authorisation to dismiss on 20 December 2012 [2011], and no response had arrived by the 24 January 2012 evidence deadline. [Para 39] [Para 40] Two of the five demands were settled in conciliation and issues 2 and 3 at the hearing, leaving reinstatement alone. [Para 12] [Para 15]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate the three suspended union leaders to their former positions and back pay their wages from the date of dismissal to the date of reinstatement? [Para 33]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that the three leaders were suspended and then dismissed for leading a strike against a unilateral change to the incentive bonus, and that dismissal of union leaders is lawful only with the Labour Inspector's authorisation, which the employer never obtained. [Para 37] [Para 38] [Para 43]
**Employer (respondent):** that it had not terminated the contracts and had maintained wages and benefits, that its request for work suspension was under review by the Department of Labour Disputes of Kandal province, and that the three had committed serious misconduct under Article 83(B)(5) by inciting other workers to strike. [Para 4] [Para 5] [Para 38]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer must reinstate the three union leaders and back pay their wages from the date of dismissal to the date of reinstatement; a binding award, final and enforceable under the MoU of 28 September 2010. [Para 57] [Para 59]

The panel treated the demand as a rights dispute because reinstatement and back pay rest on the Labour Law, applying the definition in Awards 05/11-M & V (Branch 1), 13/11-Gold Kamvimex and 14/11-GHG. [Para 42] [Para 43]
Article 293 requires the Labour Inspector's authorisation before a shop steward or union leader may be dismissed, and Clause 4 of Prakas No. 305 dated 22 November 2001 extends that protection to union leaders under Articles 282 and 293. [Para 43] [Para 49] The employer did make the request, on 20 December 2012 [2011], but over a month had passed by the evidence deadline with no response and no evidence of an appeal; the panel held that silence for the statutory month means the request is rejected. [Para 50] [Para 51] [Para 52]
Under Article 295, a serious-misconduct suspension is only provisional pending the Inspector's decision; once the dismissal is refused the suspension is annulled and its effects — the workers' inability to work and loss of wage — are cancelled by law. [Para 53] [Para 55] [Para 56] Clause 34 of Prakas No. 099 dated 21 April 2004 empowers the panel to order reinstatement and immediate back pay, which it did. [Para 57]
Evidence the employer filed on 27 January 2012, after the deadline, was not considered. [Para 40]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer suspends union leaders for serious misconduct and seeks the Labour Inspector's authorisation to dismiss under Article 293, the statutory one-month silence of the Inspector is a refusal, and the provisional suspension then falls away by force of Article 295, so the workers must be reinstated with back pay from the date of dismissal. The fact that the employer characterises its act as a suspension rather than a termination does not preserve it once authorisation has been refused. [Para 52] [Para 56] [Para 57]

**Obiter dicta:** none identified; the panel decided the case solely on the authorisation and suspension provisions and declined to weigh the misconduct allegation itself. [Para 54]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The request for authorisation is dated 20 December 2012 in two places where the context requires 2011, the year of the dispute. [Para 39] [Para 50] The sole remaining issue is numbered 1 in the issues and reasons but the order calls it "Issue 2". [Para 33] [Para 59] The minutes record two hearings (17 January at 2:00 p.m. and 19 January 2012 at 8:30 a.m.) while the summary lists the first hearing as 13 January 2012. [Para 12] [Para 13] The non-conciliation report appears as "No. 034 KB/KN" and "No 034/11 KB/KN". [Para 12] [Para 27] The OCR drops "th" throughout ("themployer", "undetermineduration", "remaing") and page footers cut into sentences. [Para 34] [Para 16] The three dismissed leaders are reduced to initials per convention.
