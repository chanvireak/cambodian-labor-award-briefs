# Award No. 20/06 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ញូវ ស្តារ ស៊ូ ខូ អិលធីឌី (New Star Shoes Co., Ltd) (Arbitration Council, 31 March 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៊ុក រី / Ouk Ry, អន ណន / An Non, ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា / Pen Bunchhea as chair) · Doc ID 140693

## 1. Facts
The employer is ក្រុមហ៊ុន ញូវ ស្តារ ស៊ូ ខូ អិលធីឌី (New Star Shoes Co., Ltd), a shoe factory on National Road 4, Mondol 1, Khan Mittapheap, Sihanoukville, employing 2,017 workers. [Para 1][Para 14]
The worker party is សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា (Cambodia Workers' Trade Union Federation) together with សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត រោងចក្រ ញូវ ស្តារ, the New Star factory workers' union. [Para 1]
Of fourteen demands lodged on 5 January 2006, eight were conciliated on 17 January 2006 and six were referred to the Council on 10 March 2006; four more (Issues 2, 3, 4 and 6) were settled at the hearing of 21 March 2006, leaving Issues 1 and 5. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 19][Para 20]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must apply its internal rules to sick workers holding a valid medical certificate, so that their wages are not deducted? [Para 27]
2. (Issue 5) Whether the company must pay three months' wages in advance to women workers taking maternity leave? [Para 38]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, ភាគី កម្មករ និយោជិត):** Sick workers with a proper medical certificate should keep their wages, as the internal rules of other factories in Phnom Penh provide — full wages for the first month and 60 percent for the following two — and the company's rules give them less. [Para 33][Para 69][Para 70] On maternity, women need three months' wages in advance because they travel far to their home province and lack money for medicine and health checks; if three months is impossible they asked for half before and half after. [Para 38][Para 40]
**Employer (respondent, ភាគី និយោជក):** The company already applies Article 5 points 6-2 and 6-3 of its internal rules, which protect sick workers more than the Labour Law does; internal rules differ from factory to factory, so comparison with other factories is irrelevant. [Para 28][Para 36][Para 72] It cannot advance three months' or even six weeks' wages because it pays on the 17th of each month, and past advances were lost when women did not return from maternity leave. [Para 41][Para 43]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider both the demand that wages not be deducted from sick workers holding a valid medical certificate and the demand for three months' wages in advance for maternity leave. [Para 94][Para 95]

On Issue 1 the panel asked whether the Labour Law requires paid sick leave. Article 71(3) suspends the employment contract during absence certified by an official doctor, for up to six months, but Article 72(1) provides that a suspended contract carries no obligation to pay wages; no Prakas has set a paid sick-leave rule, the Ministry having only placed sick leave in its model internal rules by Notification No. 14 of 16 August 2002. [Para 45][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49] The law therefore does not entitle a worker to full wages while on sick leave, and the panel had said so in 26/03-Spotwear, 30/04-Haniver and 55/04-Youcheng. [Para 52][Para 53]
The panel nevertheless noted that practice and internal rules at many enterprises give partial pay during sick leave, which is more protective than the law, and that New Star's Article 5 points 6-2 and 6-3 also exceed the statutory floor. [Para 54][Para 72] Because Article 13 of the Labour Law allows an employer to grant benefits better than the law but does not require it to match what other employers grant, the company's practice does not breach Article 13. [Para 71][Para 75]
The demand being one for benefits above the statutory minimum, the panel classified it as an interest dispute, in which it always examines whether the referring union is the most representative; it found that the New Star union had not yet been recognised as most representative under Article 277 and so lacked standing to bring an interest dispute. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 79][Para 80][Para 81]
On Issue 5 the panel held that Article 116 requires wages to be paid at least once a month but fixes no date, so the payment date falls within the employer's management prerogative under Article 2, following 28/04-Raffle Garden, 49/04-Fai Ying Dragon and 15/06-Sing Thai. [Para 85][Para 88][Para 89] No article of the Labour Law requires wages in advance for maternity leave, so that demand too lay beyond the law and was declined, as in 94/04-Ithinity. [Para 91][Para 92][Para 93]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The Labour Law does not oblige an employer to pay wages during sick leave certified by a doctor: Article 71(3) suspends the contract and Article 72(1) removes the wage obligation, so a demand for paid sick leave is a claim above the statutory floor, to be treated as an interest dispute. [Para 47][Para 48]
Only a union recognised as most representative under Article 277 may bring an interest dispute to the panel; without that recognition the panel declines to consider the demand. [Para 79][Para 81]
Where the law fixes a minimum but not a date or an advance, the employer's managerial prerogative governs, and a claim for advance wages that no article requires is declined. [Para 88][Para 91]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's comparison of New Star's internal rules with those of two Phnom Penh garment factories, and its remark that the company's rules, while weaker than theirs, are stronger than the law, were not necessary once the standing point disposed of Issue 1. [Para 69][Para 73]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The operative section numbers the sick-leave decision "Issue 2" although the reasoning and the demand list treat it as Issue 1, and Issue 2 had already been conciliated at the hearing; the brief follows the demand list and reasoning. [Para 19][Para 27][Para 94] The employer is called a shoe factory in the facts but the Ministry's report reference calls it a hotel (សណ្ឋាគារ), and the company name appears in two forms, with and without the "Shoes Co., Ltd" suffix. [Para 1][Para 14][Para 25] The deduction formula is printed inconsistently — "1/52 wage and 199 riel" in the findings, then 1/26 and 1/52 in the internal-rules analysis — and one worked example is garbled beyond recovery. [Para 30][Para 61][Para 63] The document also repeats whole clauses twice, and Khmer text is broken mid-word at several points. [Para 41][Para 85]
