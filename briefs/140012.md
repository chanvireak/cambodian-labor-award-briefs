# Award No. 35/11 — Gold Gear Garment Ltd. v Khmer Youth Trade Union Federation Local Union (Arbitration Council, 11 April 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Vong Vanna, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 140012

## 1. Facts
Gold Gear Garment Ltd. employs 160 workers, 120 of them women, and recognised the Local Union of KYFTU, registered 29 September 2010. A fifteen-point demand went to conciliation on 11 March 2011 without any issue being resolved; at the hearing on 25 March 2011 eleven issues were settled, leaving issues 3, 4, 9 and 13. The employer dismissed three union figures on 17 February 2011, before their final three-month contracts expired and without Labour Inspector authorisation. [Para 1][Para 6][Para 18][Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the dismissal of Chheang Bunsok, Sorn Vuthy and Pon Rathtiny was lawful and whether they should be reinstated? [Para 19]
2. Whether the employer must set up a day-care centre and breastfeeding room? [Para 36]
3. Whether the employer must set up an infirmary and provide a physician and sufficient medicine? [Para 37]
4. Whether the employer must offer six-month rather than three-month fixed-duration contracts after probation? [Para 40]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The three dismissals were union discrimination — all three were dismissed on the same date shortly after the union election, the employer took their membership cards and ignored union letters. The day-care, infirmary and contract demands rested on the Labour Law and on health needs. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 38][Para 39]
**Employer (respondent):** The dismissals were not union discrimination; the workers were inattentive, left for the infirmary without permission and made phone calls, and Pon Rathtiny argued with the director. It acknowledged the legal requirements for a day-care centre and infirmary but refused six-month contracts on production grounds. [Para 24][Para 36][Para 41]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered reinstatement of all three workers, a breastfeeding room and day-care centre (or payment for external care for children over 18 months against receipts), and a permanent infirmary with a nurse on standby and a physician present two hours in every eight-hour shift plus medicine; it rejected the six-month contract demand. [Para 105][Para 106]

On Issue 3, the panel held reinstatement to be a rights dispute because it has a basis in the Labour Law. Chheang Bunsok and Sorn Vuthy, elected president and vice-president on 8 February 2011 and notified to the employer on 9 February, fell within the 45-day post-election protection under Clause 3 of Prakas No. 305; dismissal on 17 February without Labour Inspector authorisation breached Article 293. [Para 42][Para 49][Para 58][Para 59][Para 61] Pon Rathtiny, an advisor, was dismissed on the same date without a warning letter; the employer failed to prove serious misconduct, and the timing after the election shifted the burden to the employer, which did not discharge it. The panel found the dismissal resulted from union membership and activism under Articles 12 and 279. [Para 65][Para 67][Para 71][Para 73]

On Issue 4, Article 186 requires enterprises with at least 100 women to provide a nursing room and day-care centre, with payment in lieu permitted for children over 18 months. The employer conceded the requirement and the panel ordered compliance. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 79]

On Issue 9, Article 238 and Clause 1 of Prakas No. 330 require an infirmary for enterprises with 50 or more employees; Clause 3 sets staffing by headcount. With 160 workers the employer had arranged no first aid, so the panel ordered a permanent infirmary, a nurse on standby and a physician for two hours per eight-hour shift. [Para 81][Para 84][Para 86]

On Issue 13, the panel treated the demand as a rights dispute but held that a contract is an agreement under Article 65 and Decree 38; neither party can force the other to accept terms. A three-month contract does not violate the Labour Law, so the demand was rejected, without prejudice to a future agreement. [Para 89][Para 94][Para 99][Para 103]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Elected union leaders and candidates within 45 days of an election may be dismissed only with Labour Inspector authorisation, and dismissal without it requires reinstatement regardless of the alleged grounds; similarly, dismissal of a union activist shortly after an election without warnings or proven serious misconduct shifts the burden to the employer and constitutes discrimination. [Para 49][Para 61][Para 71]

**Obiter dicta:** A three-month fixed-duration contract is lawful, and rejecting a demand for six-month contracts does not prevent the workers from renewing it if the employer agrees. [Para 99][Para 104]
