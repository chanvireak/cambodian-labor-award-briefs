# Award No. 263/13 — Yong De Wei Garment PTE LTD (Arbitration Council, 14 January 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Ing Sothi (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Pen Bunhea (chair) · Doc ID 139914

## 1. Facts
Yong De Wei Garment PTE LTD, a registered garment factory employing about 476 workers, ended Mr D's employment on 30 November 2013, two days before his three-month fixed-term contract was due to expire on 2 December 2013. He had worked there since 2 January 2013, passed a two-month probation, supervised a cutting team of 27 and was a staff representative and an elected candidate for the local union of the Khmer Workers' Spirit Federation, whose registration application had produced only a receipt dated 9 December 2013. The Federation filed 14 demands on behalf of 60 workers. [Para 1][Para 43][Para 44][Para 45][Para 46][Para 51][Para 52][Para 56][Para 57]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether ending Mr D's employment on 30 November 2013, before his fixed-term contract expired, was a dismissal of a protected staff representative requiring the labour inspector's prior authorisation, and whether he must be reinstated? [Para 50][Para 112]
2. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of 4,000 riel a day to each worker? [Para 68][Para 132]
3. Whether the employer must reduce the regular attendance bonus in proportion to the days of lawful special leave? [Para 76][Para 147]
4. Whether the employer must conclude fixed-term contracts of at least six months instead of three months, and renew them indefinitely? [Para 83][Para 188]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr D had passed probation, supervised 27 workers and was a staff representative and elected union leader; dismissing him two days before expiry without the labour inspector's authorisation was unlawful, and he should return to his post with his role and benefits intact. The union also sought a higher lunch allowance, proportional attendance-bonus deductions and longer contracts. [Para 50][Para 63][Para 64][Para 65][Para 66]

**Employer (respondent):** the contract simply expired; it was not a dismissal. The company processed the paperwork and paid the terminal benefits on 30 November because 1 December was a Sunday and it feared administrative delay. It also said Mr D lacked ability, which is why his contract had been cut from six months to three. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60][Para 62]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to take Mr D back and to pay his wages from the dismissal date until reinstatement; it refused the lunch-allowance claim, ordered the attendance bonus to be deducted only in proportion to lawful leave days, and refused the fixed-term contract claim. The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification if no objection is filed. [Para 131][Para 212][Para 146][Para 213][Para 187][Para 214][Para 210][Para 215][Para 216]

On representation, the panel found the local union held only a receipt and was not yet registered, so Mr D and two other elected candidates were not lawful union leaders; because they attended the hearing in person, however, they could claim for themselves. [Para 105][Para 106][Para 107][Para 110]

On issue 1, the panel reasoned that 2 December 2013 was a Monday and a working day, so the employer's fear of administrative delay was no valid reason to end the contract before its expiry; the ending was therefore a dismissal, not expiry. As a staff representative Mr D was protected by Article 293, and no labour inspector authorisation had been obtained. Reinstatement was ordered. [Para 117][Para 118][Para 119][Para 127][Para 128][Para 129][Para 130]

On issue 2, no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice required a 4,000 riel daily lunch allowance; the panel treated the demand as an interest dispute and refused it, also because the applicant was not representative. [Para 132][Para 134][Para 146]

On issue 3, Notice No. 230 requires USD 10 a month for regular attendance without absence but does not define absence, so the panel applied it case by case: authorised leave cannot be held against the worker, but the employer may deduct the bonus in proportion to the days of leave. [Para 147][Para 156][Para 170][Para 186]

On issue 4, the duration of a contract is a matter of mutual agreement; neither party can force terms on the other, so the demand for six-month terms and indefinite renewal was refused. [Para 199][Para 209][Para 210]

Employer arbitrator Ing Sothi dissented on issues 1 and 3, arguing that non-renewal is not dismissal and that ordering reinstatement contradicts Article 67 and the principles of contract validity, and that the bonus is discretionary. [Para 220][Para 222][Para 227]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Ending a fixed-term contract before its stated expiry is a dismissal, not expiry, where the final day is a working day and the employer's administrative convenience is no justification; where the worker is a protected staff representative, that dismissal without the labour inspector's prior authorisation breaches Article 293 and requires reinstatement with wages from the dismissal date. [Para 118][Para 119][Para 129][Para 131]

**Obiter dicta:** where the last day of a fixed-term contract falls on a weekly rest day, the employer may complete the closing administrative steps on the preceding working day. [Para 115][Para 116]
