# Award No. 117/14 — Dai Men Thour Intra Buy Ltd (Arbitration Council, 27 June 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom for the employer party, An Virak for the worker party, Nhan Somunninth chair) · Doc ID 139573

## 1. Facts
Dai Men Thour Intra Buy Ltd, a garment factory at Trapeang Thleung, Chom Chao, Porsenchey, Phnom Penh with about 516 workers, three of whom were paid above the statutory floor. [Para 45][Para 46] Prakas No. 317 of 31 December 2013 raised the garment minimum wage from 80 to 100 USD a month plus 20 USD on top of wages currently received, effective 01 February 2014. [Para 101][Para 102][Para 103] The employer had already given two of the three a 10 USD rise and the third 20 USD in October 2013, before the Prakas took effect, and argued that this satisfied the new obligation. [Para 52][Para 59][Para 3] The union also claimed maternity pay for 90 days computed on a twelve-month average. [Para 73][Para 147]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must add 20 USD to the wages the three named workers were receiving, applying Prakas No. 317 and Instruction No. 001/14 of 02 January 2014? [Para 95]
2. Whether the employer must pay 50 per cent of wages and allowances for the 90 days of maternity leave, calculated on the average of the previous twelve months? [Para 133][Para 147]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Prakas No. 317 requires the 20 USD to be added to the existing wage of every worker, and the rises granted in October 2013 were a different matter — a skills allowance, not the statutory minimum-wage increase. [Para 65][Para 68][Para 70]
**Employer (respondent):** it raised the three workers' wages in October 2013 precisely in response to their August request, and the 20 USD for 2014 was a one-off that need not be revisited until 2015; maternity pay has always been made monthly at 50 per cent, through relatives where the worker cannot collect it. [Para 66][Para 76][Para 83][Para 86]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted on both issues — the employer must add 20 USD to the wages being received by Ms N and Mr C and 15 USD to that of Ms S, and must pay 50 per cent of wages and allowances for 90 days of maternity leave, computed on the average of the last twelve months and paid before the leave begins; the rights-dispute order became final at once and bound the parties only under the Memorandum of Understanding of 03 October 2012. [Para 132][Para 147][Para 150]

On issue 1 the panel classified the claim as a rights dispute because it turned on the interpretation of a legal norm. [Para 96] Article 107 of the Labour Law reserves the minimum wage to a Prakas issued after consulting the Labour Advisory Committee, and Prakas No. 317 fixes 100 USD for 2014 "in addition to other existing benefits". [Para 100][Para 101] Instruction No. 001/14, Part 1, point 3 confirms that the 20 USD is to be added to the wage each worker is currently receiving. [Para 105][Para 106] The panel held the Prakas to be a legal norm of general and mandatory application, so an agreement between the parties cannot derogate from it, and a rise granted before it entered into force binds only those parties and has no effect against it. [Para 123][Para 125] "Wages currently received" therefore means the wage paid immediately before 01 February 2014: 134 USD for Ms N, 161 USD for Ms S and 225 USD for Mr C. [Para 126][Para 127] After that date only Ms S had received any further increase, of 5 USD, so the employer had discharged its obligation only in part. [Para 130][Para 131]

On issue 2 the panel likewise found a rights dispute, since Article 182 gives every woman 90 days' maternity leave and Article 183 half pay including allowances, but only to a woman who has worked in the enterprise for at least one year. [Para 133][Para 134][Para 135] It read Article 115(3) — wages may not be paid on a rest day, and if payday falls on one it must be paid the day before — together with Article 103, which lists maternity pay as a component of wages, and held that maternity pay for the full 90 days must be paid before the leave starts; the employer's monthly payment at 50 per cent did not comply. [Para 138][Para 140][Para 141][Para 142] It also required the employer to keep facilitating payment by transfer to relatives where the worker cannot attend. [Para 146]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A Prakas fixing the minimum wage is a legal norm of general and mandatory effect, so a wage increase granted before it entered into force cannot be set off against the increase it requires, and "wages currently received" means the wage paid on the day before the Prakas took effect; the employer must add the prescribed amount on top of that figure. Maternity pay of 50 per cent of wages and allowances, computed on the average of the previous twelve months, must be paid for the whole 90 days before the leave begins, not month by month during it. [Para 125][Para 126][Para 142]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that no provision forbids the Ministry of Labour from issuing public policy to respond to economic conditions, and that a conciliation agreement binds only its own parties, were general statements that did not change either order. [Para 111][Para 124]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The operative order on issue 3 as printed at [Para 149] omits the "50 per cent" qualified in the reasons [Para 135][Para 142]; the Council issued an **erratum** dated 03 July 2014 correcting pages 15–16 to read "50 per cent" [Para 153]. This brief follows the corrected text.
- The award carries a correction page with its own date (03 July 2014) inside an award dated 27 June 2014 [Para 152][Para 156].
- The three workers' wages are printed inconsistently: Ms S is 151 USD at [Para 53] and 161 USD at [Para 127], the latter being the figure the panel adopts.
- The claimant's name appears as "Chea Lim" and "Ja Lim Heng" for the same worker [Para 2][Para 59][Para 132]; individuals appear here as Ms N, Mr C and Ms S per convention, and the employer's factory president as Mr T.
- The non-conciliation report is numbered 484 in one place and 284 in another [Para 14][Para 19].
- Stray years and folio fragments intrude: "2019 2004" after Prakas No. 099 [Para 90], "2015" mid-sentence [Para 25], and "2023"/"20" fragments at [Para 120][Para 5].
- The award date is 27 June 2014 on the face [Para 1] and in the erratum [Para 152], but the extension of time runs to 27 June 2014 from 04 June [Para 31].
