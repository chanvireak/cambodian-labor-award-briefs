# Award No. 328/15 — Great On Nutec Style Factory Ltd (Arbitration Council, 21 January 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ang Eng Thong presiding; Ing Sothy, employer nominee; Liv Sovanna, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139492

## 1. Facts
Great On Nutec Style Factory Ltd, at Village 4, Svay Rolum commune, Sa'ang district, Kandal province, sews garments and employed about 1,250 workers at the time of the dispute; it is the renamed Winner Knitting Factory Ltd, the change having been made in 2011 without any severance payment. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 39][Para 40] Conciliation by the Kandal provincial Department of Labour and Vocational Training on 8 December 2015 left four of the demands unsettled, and the file reached the Council on 21 December 2015 under report No. 2271/15 K.B/KCh. [Para 12][Para 8][Para 13] At the hearing of 8 January 2016 both parties appeared; the panel re-conciliated, the parties agreed points 2 and 3, and the workers withdrew point 4, leaving only point 1 for decision, in respect of the 199 workers who had thumbprinted an authority of 7 November 2015 and the six who attended. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 18][Para 19][Para 36][Para 38]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether, where the company changed its name from Winner Knitting Factory Ltd to Great On Nutec Style Factory Ltd, it must pay the workers one round of past-service entitlements — notice compensation under Articles 75 and 77, dismissal damages under Article 89, the Article 91 illness payment, the Article 166-167 annual leave payment and the Article 116 final month's wage? [Para 2][Para 39]
2. Whether, during the suspension of work from 10 November 2015 to 10 January 2016, the company must be barred from removing materials and equipment from the factory? [Para 3]
3. Whether, where no owner appears at the deadline for resuming work, the workers should be allowed to sell the company's assets to pay their wages and other benefits? [Para 4]
4. Whether the company must preserve wages and other benefits during the period the workers voluntarily took strike action? [Para 5]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the renaming left their long service unprotected, and they feared the employer would not take responsibility as some other companies had done when they closed without paying severance; they therefore asked for the package in Article 2 above to be paid once, at the moment of the name change. [Para 42][Para 39]
**Employer (respondent):** the company is still operating normally and has kept the workers' seniority intact through the renaming; no law requires severance to be paid when a company merely changes its name; and the same demand was already decided and ordered in Cases 088/15 and 255/15, so the panel should decline to consider it again. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 50]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the claim for notice compensation, dismissal damages, the illness payment, the outstanding annual leave payment and the final month's wage arising from the change of name from Winner to Great On Nutec. [Para 85][Para 86]
The panel first asked whether it could decide the point at all, since the employer invoked res judicata ("អាជ្ញា អស់ ជំនុំ"). [Para 51] It held that the principle bars re-hearing only where the same dispute was brought by the same parties and fully decided, and noted that no provision of the arbitration procedure contradicts that. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 54] Applying the three conditions it had set in earlier awards — the parties previously litigated the point, the point was brought to the Council, and the Council decided it fully — it found the point had indeed been brought before in Cases 088/15 of 7 May 2015 and 255/15 of 28 October 2015. [Para 54][Para 57] But the parties there were the Cambodia Workers' Union Federation against Great On Nutec, whereas here the claimants are the Khmer Workers' Spirit Union and the Khmer Heart Union of the factory; the parties were therefore not the same and condition one failed, so the panel was free to decide the point afresh. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60][Para 61][Para 63]
On the merits, the panel read Article 87 of the Labour Law as providing that where the employer's legal status changes — by succession, sale, merger, or incorporation — the contracts in force continue between the new employer and the workers of the old enterprise. [Para 65] It found that the renaming did not affect wages, shifts, the factory's location or the workers' seniority, and that the employer had kept all conditions of work, benefits and seniority unchanged. [Para 71][Para 73] Article 311 of the Civil Code and Article 65 of the Labour Law were read as making the creation, modification and extinguishment of contractual obligations rest on the will of both contracting parties, so one party cannot force the other to terminate. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 78][Para 79] Since the workers' contracts were never cut and they lost no seniority or benefit, the employer had already complied with Article 87 and owed nothing on the renaming. [Para 74][Para 83][Para 84]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A change of the employer's name alone, where the employer continues to recognise the workers' seniority, conditions of work and benefits and the contracts are not terminated, is a change of legal status already governed by Article 87 of the Labour Law and does not entitle the workers to demand a severance payment against the employer's will. Separately, the panel will decline to re-hear a decided point only where the parties are the same; a demand already decided between a different union federation and the same employer may be heard on its merits again. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 74][Para 83]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that res judicata exists to avoid contradictory outcomes on the same dispute and to ensure that a dispute ends in a single final decision, and that neither union at this factory had yet obtained its registration certificate from the Ministry, though both had filed and held a receipt dated 21 November 2015. [Para 55][Para 35]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is dated 21 January 2016 in the caption and in the parties' agreed extension of the issuing date, while the panel was constituted on 21 December 2015 and the hearing held on 8 January 2016. [Para 1][Para 14][Para 22] The reasons contain a stray "in 2019" that cannot be squared with the 2015-2016 chronology. [Para 17] The company name is printed several ways — Great On Nutec Style Factory Ltd, "Great On Nutec" alone, "Fret On Nutec", and once as "Bot On Nutec" in the reference to Case 255/15 — and the former name appears both as "Winner Knitting Factory LTD" and as "Winner Knitting Factory". [Para 2][Para 34][Para 45][Para 47][Para 59] The dismissal amounts carry no figures at all: the five heads of claim are named by article only, with no riel or USD sum printed anywhere in the award. [Para 2][Para 85] Case 088/15 is referred to as having decided "point 3" and Case 255/15 as having decided "point 7", while the panel elsewhere describes them as decided points without numbering. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 57] The 199-worker claimant group is identified in the source only by the six workers who attended the hearing, whose names are printed there; those names are not reproduced in this brief.
