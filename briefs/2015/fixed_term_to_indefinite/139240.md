# Award No. 171/15 — Cambodian Cement Chakrey Ting Factory Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 4 August 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Seng Vuoch Hun (employer-appointed, dissenting), An Nan (worker-appointed), Sok Mathoeung (chair) · Doc ID 139240 · unofficial English translation of the Khmer original

## 1. Facts
The respondent employs about 400 workers at a cement factory in Toek Chhou district, Kampot. [Para 18] Mr C was engaged as a driver on an oral contract from 07 August 2014 at the Kampot workplace and was elected secretary of the BWTUC local union on 25 February 2015. [Para 24] On 31 May 2015 the employer directed him to work in Phnom Penh from 2 June 2015, keeping his wages, benefits, hours and position and paying for his accommodation. [Para 25] He refused and continued working at Kampot; he was dismissed on 08 June 2015, his last month's salary having been paid. [Para 27]

## 2. Issues in dispute
The three non-conciliated claims were merged into a single issue at the workers' suggestion: [Para 17]
1. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr C to his previous position and pay him back-pay from the date of termination to the date of reinstatement, and refrain from discrimination against the union and harassment of local leaders and members? [Para 21]
2. Whether the termination of Mr C related to union discrimination? [Para 43]
3. Whether Mr C was entitled to special protection from dismissal as a local union leader, so that the employer required the Labour Inspector's authorisation? [Para 58]
4. Whether the employer had an appropriate reason to terminate Mr C? [Para 94]
5. Whether Mr C is entitled to termination compensation, or to reinstatement with back-pay? [Para 122]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the transfer was an excuse to dismiss him for his union role, the employer having harassed the local union since the election; the dismissal was unlawful because the Labour Inspector's authorisation was never sought; and an oral contract fixing Kampot as the workplace barred relocation without his consent. [Para 28]
**Employer (respondent):** there was no discrimination, since the union president and vice-president still work normally; the transfer offered a chance to improve after a mistake for which he had been warned; and he was dismissed for failing to attend the Phnom Penh worksite between 2 and 8 June 2015. [Para 32]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to reinstate Mr C and pay him back-pay from the date of termination to the date of reinstatement; the allegation of union discrimination was rejected. [Para 127] The award is non-binding. [Para 128]

On discrimination, the panel held that the party alleging facts bears the burden of proof, following Awards No. 148/07 and No. 34/11. [Para 47] The workers brought no evidence or witnesses of employer conduct showing discrimination, gave no other examples, and the local union's president and vice-president remained employed, so the allegation was unproven. [Para 54]

On special protection, Article 293 paragraph 1 requires the Labour Inspector's authorisation to dismiss a workers' delegate, and clause 3 of Prakas No. 305 extends that protection to election candidates from 45 days before the election to 45 days after it, or for a determined period such as until registration is applied for (Awards No. 40/10, 103/12, 191/12). [Para 59] Three conditions were tested: he was elected secretary, which was met; the dismissal came 103 days after the election, outside the period, because two months from receipt of the criminal records on 01 April 2015 was a reasonable duration and registration was requested only on 22 June, fourteen days after the dismissal; and notice of the candidacy reached the employer on 19 June 2015, eleven days after the dismissal. [Para 72] Two conditions therefore failed and no authorisation was required. [Para 84]

On the reason for dismissal, Article 2 gives the employer the right to manage and transfer workers subject to four conditions — no wage deduction, no transfer to a distant workplace, no shift reversal and no work far from the worker's skills. [Para 100] Wages, role, shift and benefits were preserved and an accommodation allowance offered, but the panel found the Kampot to Phnom Penh move "very extreme" and contrary to the spirit of a contract requiring work near his residence, so the transfer was inappropriate. [Para 107] He continued to work at Kampot and the employer did not object, so he could not be treated as absent; that ground failed and no other reason was disclosed. [Para 116]

On remedy, Article 74 forbids termination without a valid reason and Article 91 entitles the other party to damages; only the worker may choose between compensation and reinstatement, and he chose reinstatement with back-pay. [Para 126]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A transfer that preserves wages, role, shift and benefits still falls outside the employer's managerial prerogative where the new worksite is so distant that it defeats the spirit of a contract to work near the worker's residence; and a worker who keeps working at the original site without objection cannot be treated as absent so as to supply a valid reason for dismissal. [Para 105]

**Obiter dicta:** union leaders' protection is not indefinite — it runs 45 days past the election and may extend for a determined period, which the panel measured as two months from receipt of the criminal records needed for registration. [Para 75] The dissenting arbitrator, agreeing that the dismissal was wrongful but noting the employer's refusal to reinstate, would have awarded dismissal indemnity, damages, notice pay and accrued leave instead. [Para 198]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The evidence schedule is expressly omitted from this English version, so the panel's documentary findings cannot be checked. [Para 17] The hearing is recorded at 08:30 A.M. at line 9 and 08:30 P.M. at line 13. Registration is said to have been requested on "22 June 2014" although the election and the registration receipt are dated 2015. [Para 77] The claimant's surname and given name each appear in two spellings, and the company is styled "Factory Co., Ltd" in the title but "Co., Ltd" in the annex. The dissent's own figures conflict: dismissal indemnity of "wages and perquisites for six months" at line 174 against "seven days" at line 199, and notice pay of fifteen days at line 189 against seven days at line 199.
