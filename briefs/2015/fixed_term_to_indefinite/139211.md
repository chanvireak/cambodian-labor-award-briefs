# Award No. 133/15 — JD & Toyoshima Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 17 July 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Suthy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed, dissenting), Tang Try (president) · Doc ID 139211

## 1. Facts

JD & Toyoshima Co., Ltd. (ជេឌី & តូយូស៊ីម៉ា ខូ., អិលធីឌី) cuts and sews garments in Bati district, Takeo, and employed about 600 workers. [Para 58][Para 59] Ms S, president of the enterprise's union and, she said, an elected worker delegate, was dismissed on 2 May 2015 without prior notice and without the Labour Inspector's approval. [Para 70][Para 73] On the afternoon of 5 May 2015, 206 workers stopped work and left the factory to demand her reinstatement; the company deducted half a day's wages and the US$5 regular attendance bonus from them. [Para 95][Para 96] Of the eight original demands, conciliation settled four before the file reached the Council and two more at the second hearing, leaving issues 6 and 8 for decision. [Para 13][Para 20][Para 21]

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. (Issue 6 of the panel's list) Whether the company must take Ms S back as union president and worker delegate and pay her back wages from the day of dismissal to the day of reinstatement? [Para 69]
2. (Issue 8) Whether the company must preserve half a day's wages and the US$5 regular attendance bonus for the 206 workers who stopped work on the afternoon of 5 May 2015? [Para 95]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** The dismissal had no valid reason, gave no notice and bypassed the Labour Inspector, and was aimed at her union office; the stoppage was a protest, not a strike, so no advance notice was required. [Para 73][Para 98][Para 99]

**Employer (respondent):** She was never a worker delegate — the election record names other delegates; she was dismissed for serious misconduct (collecting lottery money and drinking on the job, shouting at managers) and demanded US$3,000 to resign; compensation was offered and she refused it. [Para 77][Para 172][Para 208]

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim for reinstatement and back wages was refused, and the employer was ordered to pay five heads of termination compensation: pay in lieu of notice, severance pay, damages (សោហ៊ុយជម្ងឺចិត្ត), outstanding annual leave and final wages; issue 8 was dismissed in full. [Para 242][Para 243][Para 244]

On issue 6 the panel first asked whether Ms S was a person protected by article 293 of the Labour Law, applying the three conditions it had drawn from Prakas No. 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001: that the person is protected, that the dismissal fell inside the protected period, and that the employer was notified of the protected names. [Para 138] The first was satisfied — she was elected union president on 25 March 2014. [Para 141][Para 144] The second was not: her union never applied for registration at all, and a year and one month passed between the election and the dismissal, which the panel held was not a "reasonable period" for the protection to continue under article 3 of the Prakas. [Para 148][Para 150][Para 151][Para 152] It therefore stopped there and did not examine the third condition. [Para 153]

Turning to her claim to be a worker delegate, the panel took the employer's record of the 11 April 2014 election, which lists two full delegates and two alternates by name, and found that her name appears only among the eight candidates, with nothing in the record saying whether she was a full or an alternate delegate. [Para 171][Para 172] Since the party asserting a fact bears the burden of proving it, and the workers filed no second election record or result, delegate status was not established. [Para 159][Para 160][Para 174]

On the lawfulness of the dismissal the panel applied article 83: serious misconduct under that article means theft, fraud, threats or violence, and it found that asking for US$3,000 in exchange for resigning was a proposal, not coercion — improper for a union leader, but not serious misconduct. [Para 190][Para 191] In any event, article 26 gives an employer seven days from learning of serious misconduct to dismiss, after which the right is forfeited; the company knew of the conduct in March 2015 and acted only on 2 May 2015. [Para 192][Para 195] The dismissal was therefore without valid reason, and article 91 entitled Ms S to damages while leaving the termination itself standing. [Para 206][Para 209]

On issue 8 the panel held the stoppage met all three elements of a strike under article 318 — a group of workers, a stoppage of work, and an aim of obtaining a settlement of their demand — and that article 332(1) suspends the employment contract during a strike, so no wages or attendance bonus were owed for that half day. [Para 220][Para 223][Para 240] Arbitrator An Non dissented on issue 6, reading the evidence as showing Ms S was an elected delegate and that the dismissal, made without the Labour Inspector's approval, required reinstatement with back pay. [Para 263][Para 264]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

Where a union leader is elected but the union never applies for registration, the protection against dismissal in article 293 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 305 lasts only a reasonable time; a delay of more than a year between election and dismissal removes it, so the employer needs no Labour Inspector approval. [Para 151][Para 152] Separately, an employer that learns of alleged serious misconduct must dismiss within seven days or lose the right, leaving only compensation under article 91. [Para 192][Para 195][Para 206]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remark that demanding money in exchange for resigning is not conduct befitting a union leader, although it does not amount to serious misconduct, was not necessary to the result. [Para 190]

## 6. Source defects disclosed

Paragraph 15 dates the panel's constitution to "08 June 2023 2015", two years in one token. [Para 15] The award date is printed as 17 July 2015 in the header and at the foot, and paragraph 24 records the parties' agreement to move it from 29 June 2015 to 17 July 2015. [Para 1][Para 24][Para 265] The company's name shifts between ជេឌី & តូយូស៊ីម៉ា, ជេ & ឌី and ជេវី & across the award, and the commercial register number appears as 1325/2013 and Inv. 1323 E/2013. [Para 14][Para 58][Para 26] The worker's demand is printed as US$3,000 in one passage and as "US$2,500 to US$3,000" in another. [Para 74][Para 78] Paragraph 251 speaks of "10 elections" where a single election is meant, and several prior award numbers in the panel's lists are OCR-garbled beyond reliable reading. [Para 251][Para 158] Long stretches of paragraphs 140, 228 and 234 repeat themselves, and one signature line is lost to noise. [Para 140][Para 234][Para 246]
