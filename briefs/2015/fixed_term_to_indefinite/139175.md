# Award No. 079/15 — Sonando Co., Ltd (Beehive Radio) (Arbitration Council, 5 May 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Kong Phal, president) · Doc ID 139175

## 1. Facts
Sonando Co., Ltd, registered in 1996, operates the FM 105 commercial radio station "Beehive Radio" (វិទ្យុ សំបុក ឃ្មុំ) with about 20 staff. [Para 77][Para 78] Ten staff referred 16 demands after conciliation resolved 9 of them; at the hearing issues 2 and 3 settled, and issues 4 and 7 were merged by the workers into a single issue 7(1), leaving issues 1, 5, 6 and 7(1) for decision. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 20][Para 21][Para 22] All ten complainants had already been dismissed and none sought reinstatement. [Para 89][Para 90]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise wages by 10 per cent each year on top of the monthly wage? [Para 2][Para 204]
5. Whether the employer must pay the 200 per cent (double) rate for work performed on public holidays from 2012 to 2014, equal to 39 days' wages per worker? [Para 6][Para 213]
6. Whether the employer must pay a meal allowance of USD 2 per day and an accommodation allowance of USD 20 per month? [Para 7][Para 236]
7(1). Whether the employer must pay termination compensation to the ten workers, the claim covering both seniority payments on any new contract or mass dismissal and compensation for dismissal? [Para 8][Para 115][Para 245]

Issues 2 (USD 10 monthly health allowance) and 3 (13th-month salary) were settled at the hearing; issue 4 (annual wage arrears for uninterrupted service) was merged into 7(1). [Para 3][Para 4][Para 5][Para 20][Para 21]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Market prices have risen out of proportion to wages and monthly income no longer covers expenses; the employer agreed on 15 September 2014 to a 10 per cent increase but never recorded it in writing. [Para 92][Para 93]
**Employer (respondent):** It cannot meet the demands for lack of capacity, and will comply if the law so requires. [Para 95][Para 96]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the 10 per cent increase and the meal and accommodation allowances were dismissed and the claim for wages during the strike was dismissed, but the employer was ordered to pay holiday pay at 200 per cent for 2012–2014 (39 days' wages per worker) and termination compensation to 8 workers comprising pay in lieu of notice, dismissal indemnity, moral damages and unused annual leave. [Para 237][Para 244][Para 387][Para 392][Para 393][Para 394][Para 396]

**Jurisdiction and representation.** Two of the ten complainants, Mr N and Ms C, worked as driver/house-watchman and as cleaner/housekeeper for the employer's family, living in the employer's household and recorded in the family residence book as nephews of the head of household; the panel held them domestic servants outside the Labour Law under Article 1(g) and declined jurisdiction over them, leaving 8 complainants. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 88][Para 192][Para 196][Para 200][Para 201][Para 202][Para 203] Because the local union was not registered with the Ministry of Labour, the panel applied Article 268 of the Labour Law and its own prior awards to hold that a union may only represent members once registered; only the 8 workers who signed the transfer of rights were parties. [Para 79][Para 82][Para 179][Para 183][Para 186][Para 208][Para 209]

**Issue 1.** The claim was dismissed: the employer owes no duty to grant a 10 per cent annual increase for 2015, and a mere verbal understanding not reduced to writing created no obligation. [Para 93][Para 211][Para 212]

**Issue 5.** Article 164 of the Labour Law and article 4 of Prakas No. 10 SKBY of 4 February 1999 entitle a worker who works on a public holiday to an additional payment equal to one day's ordinary wage, i.e. 200 per cent in total; the panel followed its awards in Sun Jin and Meng Da. [Para 213][Para 214][Para 215][Para 216][Para 218] The employer's argument that a 7-hour day excused the double rate was rejected: article 137 permits an employer to set hours at or below 8, the 7-hour practice was lawful, but it created no debt of one hour per day that could be set off, so the employer could not offset holiday pay against it. [Para 227][Para 228][Para 230][Para 231][Para 232][Para 233] The employer's failure to pay the doubled rate breached article 4 of Prakas No. 10. [Para 233] The panel ordered 39 days' wages per worker for 2012–2014. [Para 235]

**Issue 6.** Dismissed for the same representation reason: the claim concerned only serving staff and no worker beyond the 8 had transferred rights, so the employer owed no meal or accommodation allowance. [Para 239][Para 240][Para 241][Para 243][Para 244]

**Issue 7(1).** Since none of the 8 had a written contract fixing an end date, article 67(1) of the Labour Law made their contracts indefinite. [Para 246][Para 248][Para 249] Four were dismissed for refusing to work 8 hours after three warnings, but the employer had agreed to cancel those warnings, so they could not ground a dismissal, which was in any event disproportionate under article 27. [Para 286][Para 287][Para 290][Para 291][Para 294][Para 296][Para 298] The other four were dismissed for striking from 6 to 11 March 2015; articles 332 and 333 of the Labour Law and article 38 of the Constitution protect the right to strike and bar punishment for participating in one, so those dismissals lacked a valid reason. [Para 305][Para 307][Para 309][Para 312][Para 313] The panel awarded pay in lieu of notice (article 75), dismissal indemnity (article 89), moral damages (article 91) and unused annual leave (articles 166 and 167), computing the daily wage as the average of the last 12 months divided by 26. [Para 88][Para 327][Para 338][Para 350][Para 351][Para 367][Para 389][Para 390][Para 391]

The claim for wages from 6 to 11 March 2015 was dismissed: article 332(1) suspends the contract during a strike so no wages are owed, and no replacement workers were hired to trigger article 334. [Para 376][Para 380][Para 385][Para 386]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker's entitlement to the doubled public-holiday rate under article 164 and Prakas No. 10 is not reduced by an employer's lawful practice of a shorter working day, because hours not worked create no debt that the employer may set off against holiday pay; and a dismissal based on warnings the employer has cancelled, or on participation in a lawful strike, is without valid reason and attracts the payments under articles 75, 89, 91, 166 and 167 [Para 232][Para 298][Para 312][Para 313].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's account of the "past practice" doctrine — that a practice arises from regular, consistent conduct rather than from writing, and may lawfully end where both parties agree, one party denounces it, or the circumstances founding it change — was stated at length although the holiday-pay outcome did not turn on it. [Para 257][Para 260][Para 270][Para 272][Para 273]
