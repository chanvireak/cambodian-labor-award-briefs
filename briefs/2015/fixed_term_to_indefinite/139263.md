# Award No. 195/15 — Sin Tai Garment (Cambodia) Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 12 August 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Tuon Si Phan for the worker party, Vun Saray chair) · Doc ID 139263

## 1. Facts
Sin Tai Garment (Cambodia) Co Ltd, a trouser-cutting factory at Damnak Thom village, Sangkat Phnom Penh Thmei, Sen Sok, Phnom Penh, employing about 2,090 workers, had reduced its production lines from 22 to 20 and its workforce from 2,500 to 2,090. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 32] Every worker was on an undetermined-duration contract concluded after a two-month probation. [Para 30] Fearing that the factory would close without paying them — as they said other factories had done — the workers demanded that the employer terminate all those contracts, pay one instalment of severance and re-engage them on fixed-duration contracts. [Para 2][Para 33] The union, the Khmer Youth Union at Sin Tai, was registered with the Ministry of Labour and its third-term leadership was recognised in 2013. [Para 29]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to terminate the undetermined-duration employment contracts of all the workers, pay one instalment of severance for that termination, and convert those contracts into fixed-duration contracts? [Para 30][Para 43]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** some had worked more than ten years yet remained poor and in debt; the shrinking lines and headcount made them fear a closure in which nothing would be paid, so a severance payment now would let them clear debts and support their families. [Para 31][Para 32][Para 34][Para 35]
**Employer (respondent):** it has no wish to terminate anyone, has registered the company properly and lodged the statutory deposit with a bank, is still receiving plenty of orders, and has not reduced or suspended work; the two lines were merged, not cut. [Para 3][Para 36][Para 37][Para 38][Para 39]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the claim for termination of all undetermined-duration contracts, one instalment of severance and conversion to fixed-duration contracts. [Para 57][Para 58]

The panel framed the question as one of contract law rather than of labour protection. Article 65(1) of the Labour Law defines the employment contract as the instrument creating the working relationship between worker and employer, and Article 311 of the Civil Code provides that a contract is the act by which two or more persons, of concordant will, create, modify or extinguish obligations. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46] Read together, the panel held, those provisions mean that the creation, modification and extinguishment of the obligations of an employment contract must issue from the will of the parties to it, and only the employer and the worker hold the power to extinguish them. [Para 47][Para 48]

From that the panel drew the operative rule: one party to a contract cannot compel the other to extinguish its obligations against that other party's will, following Awards 34/13 (Papilion Tek Stai), 138/13 (Soun Eu Sahakam Nean Hat Phen), 039/14 (E Garmen Co Ltd) and 308/14 (Huy Chhean (Cambodia)). [Para 49][Para 52] On the facts the employer had neither terminated nor formed any intention to terminate, and had expressed no wish to convert the contracts to fixed duration. [Para 51] The panel recorded that the workers' motive was real — long service with continued poverty and debt, and the hope that a payment would ease it — but found the claim had no legal foundation, noting that the workers themselves acknowledged at the hearing that no article of law supported it. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 55] It therefore held that an employer still operating normally and paying its workers owes no severance and cannot be forced into a termination at the workers' request. [Para 56]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An undetermined-duration employment contract can be extinguished only by the concordant will of its two parties under Article 311 of the Civil Code and Article 65(1) of the Labour Law; a worker or union cannot compel an employer that is still operating and has no intention to terminate to dismiss the workforce and pay severance, however real the workers' anxiety about a future closure. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 56]

The panel has no power to rewrite the parties' chosen contract type against one party's will, so a demand to convert undetermined-duration contracts into fixed-duration contracts fails for the same reason. [Para 51][Para 52]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's recital of the workers' poverty and indebtedness, and its note that the employer keeps a statutory bank deposit and is still receiving orders, explain the parties' motives but do not bear on the holding, which rests on the absence of any legal basis for a forced termination. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 53]

- The panel is constituted on "23 July 2019 2015" [Para 12], a double year left uncorrected; the conciliation report is filed 22 July 2015 [Para 6] though the non-conciliation minutes are dated 13 July 2015 [Para 24].

- The demand is recorded inconsistently: "one instalment" of severance [Para 2][Para 33] without any statement of what a "វគ្គ" (instalment) comprises, and no sum is given anywhere.
- OCR corruption runs through the pleadings, e.g. "សហហហព័ន្ធ" at [Para 9] and stray Latin strings at [Para 6] and [Para 60], and the reasoning at [Para 43] is one unpunctuated block that merges the two sub-questions.
