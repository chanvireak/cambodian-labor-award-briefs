# Award No. 300/15 — Vitoria Angkor Khom Pheny Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 30 December 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Yu Soun Ty, An Virak, Kong Phallha — Chair) · Doc ID 139475

## 1. Facts
Vitoria Angkor Khom Pheny Ltd. runs a hotel and restaurant in Siem Reap with approximately 217 employees; the local union is the Vitoria Angkor Employees' Union, registered since 2005 and affiliated to the Tourism and Services Workers Federation of Cambodia. [Para 55][Para 57][Para 58] On 21 October 2015 the Federation complained that the hotel had dismissed fifteen union leaders and activists without valid reason and in breach of the Labour Law; conciliation on 10 November 2015 failed and report no. 090 of 12 November 2015 reached the Secretariat on 17 November 2015. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 15][Para 30] The fifteen were dismissed at a meeting on 16 October 2015, twenty-one days after the union's leadership election of 25 September; nine had stood as candidates and six were activists. [Para 108][Para 128][Para 179]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the fifteen dismissed union leaders and activists must be reinstated in their original posts with wages and benefits from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 61]
2. Whether the nine who stood as candidates in the union leadership election enjoyed protection from dismissal? [Para 159]
3. Whether the six who were activists but not candidates enjoyed the same protection? [Para 196]
4. Whether the dismissals were substantively lawful, and if not, what remedy follows? [Para 211]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer dismissed all fifteen because of anti-union discrimination and without valid reason; none had ever been warned, no written notice of termination was issued, and each was called in individually on 16 October 2015 and dismissed the same day. [Para 62][Para 107][Para 208]
**Employer (respondent):** Some workers had committed faults damaging the company's reputation — posting the complaint on Facebook — and others were simply not renewed at contract end; the employer has lost trust in them and will not take them back. [Para 63][Para 100][Para 102][Para 227]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to reinstate the nine candidates with wages and benefits from dismissal to reinstatement; reinstatement of the six activists was refused but the employer must pay each of them notice compensation, dismissal indemnity, moral damages, unused annual leave and final wages. [Para 268][Para 269][Para 270]

**Contract status.** All fifteen held two-year fixed-duration contracts and had each worked more than two years. Under Article 67(2) a fixed-duration contract may not exceed two years, and Article 73(5) converts it into an undetermined-duration contract where there is no notice of termination and the total duration exceeds that limit; following Award 10/03-Jacqsintex the panel held the reference to be to total duration, not to each renewal, and found all fifteen held undetermined-duration contracts. [Para 142][Para 145][Para 155][Para 156]

**The nine candidates.** Article 293 forbids dismissing a staff delegate, and Prakas no. 305 of 22 November 2001 extends that protection to candidates in a union leadership election for 45 days before the poll and 45 days after it where they are not elected, provided the union notifies the employer of the candidacies by any reliable means. The union's notification of 12 September 2015 listed all nine and was signed for the hotel on 14 September; the election was held on 25 September and the dismissals came on 16 October, twenty-one days later and inside the protected window. [Para 159][Para 161][Para 162][Para 185] The employer produced no evidence of the prior authorisation of the Labour Inspector that Article 293 requires, so the dismissals were unlawful and reinstatement followed, as in Awards 71/09, 191/12 and 273/15. [Para 189][Para 190][Para 191][Para 193][Para 195]

**The six activists.** Activist status alone does not attract the protection, which is confined to delegates, candidates and founders or members during registration; the six fell outside it. [Para 197][Para 198][Para 200] On discrimination, the panel applied Article 12 and Article 279, which forbid using union membership or activity as a ground for dismissal, but held that the party alleging discrimination bears the burden of proof; beyond argument, the workers produced nothing showing the dismissals were motivated by union activity. [Para 202][Para 203][Para 205][Para 210]

**Substantive lawfulness and remedy.** Article 74 permits termination of an undetermined-duration contract only with written notice and for a reason concerning the worker's aptitude or conduct or the needs of the enterprise. The employer alleged defamation on Facebook but filed no evidence, so it proved neither misconduct nor a valid reason; the dismissals were therefore unlawful. [Para 211][Para 213][Para 219][Para 221] Because the relationship had broken down and the employer refused to take them back, the panel held compensation preferable to reinstatement, and applied Article 75 (notice), Article 89 (dismissal indemnity of 15 days per year, capped at six months), Article 91 (moral damages equal to the dismissal indemnity), Article 166-167 (unused annual leave) and Article 116 (final wages within 48 hours). [Para 228][Para 229][Para 230][Para 244][Para 254][Para 265][Para 266]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker elected candidate in a union leadership election is protected against dismissal for 45 days before and 45 days after the poll, and a dismissal within that window without the Labour Inspector's prior authorisation is unlawful and calls for reinstatement — whereas union activism without candidate or delegate status attracts no such protection, and a dismissal of such a worker is unlawful only for want of a valid reason, remedied by compensation where the employment relationship has broken down. [Para 162][Para 189][Para 191][Para 198][Para 229]

**Obiter dicta:** that successive fixed-duration renewals are a device the Labour Law discourages in favour of undetermined-duration contracts because job security benefits workers and long service benefits employers, and that the ILO Termination of Employment Recommendation No. 166 of 1982, though not binding, supports that reading. [Para 147][Para 150][Para 152]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The dismissal date is given as 16 October 2015 in the narrative and reasoning but as 06 October 2015 in the summary of facts. [Para 108][Para 138] The panel records 111 powers of attorney plus three workers present but unnotified, totalling 114 claimants, yet only fifteen were dismissed. [Para 56][Para 59][Para 60] The employer appears as "Vitoria" in the case name and "Victoria" in the union's title, and the chair's name is printed variously as "Kong Phalha", "Kong Phallha" and once as "Kong Phalha" with a broken diacritic. [Para 1][Para 272][Para 273] Stray years "2016" and "2017" are injected mid-sentence by OCR where 2015 is meant, and the wage tables of the dismissal indemnity are lost entirely, leaving no amounts printed. [Para 194][Para 232][Para 270] The signature block degenerates into noise after the first name. [Para 272]
