# Award No. 176/15 — A & J (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 20 August 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Chhiv Phyrum (employer-appointed), Huon Chundy (worker-appointed), Kong Phallack (chair) · Doc ID 139242 · unofficial English translation of the Khmer original

## 1. Facts
The respondent assembles bicycles in the Tay Seng Bavit Special Economic Zone, Svay Rieng, and employed 1,982 workers at the time of conciliation. [Para 25] The CLC ran an election to form a "Democratic Local Union" on 09 May 2015, but the union had not obtained registration from the Ministry when the case was heard. [Para 26] Three workers held successive fixed duration contracts — one of twelve and two of six months, the last running from 01 December 2014 to 31 May 2015 — and were told orally on 18 and 21 May 2015 not to return to work, while being paid to the contractual end date. [Para 42]

## 2. Issues in dispute
The panel merged issues 1 and 3, leaving two claims, and added two questions of its own: [Para 22]
1. Whether the employer must refrain from discriminating against the local union and must reinstate the dismissed workers with back-pay from termination to reinstatement? [Para 13]
2. Whether the employer must convert fixed duration contracts to undetermined duration contracts for workers with more than two years of service? [Para 61]
3. Whether this case concerns termination of the contracts or their non-renewal? [Para 78]
4. Whether Mr K, as the elected local union president, was entitled to special protection from dismissal? [Para 87]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the three were let go without valid reason, having committed no serious misconduct and received no warnings; the employer retaliated because they struck and discriminated against the local union. [Para 30] They later accepted that the demand covered three workers only, not the 24 named in the conciliation report, and did not claim termination compensation. [Para 30]
**Employer (respondent):** there was no termination, only non-renewal of contracts that had expired, and the three would not be reinstated; non-renewal followed section head requests and recorded performance problems, and only 35 contracts were not renewed, so no targeting occurred; the union's letters were never received. [Para 32][Para 48]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the demand for reinstatement and back-pay was rejected; the demand to convert fixed duration contracts was declined for want of a claimant. No damages were awarded. [Para 113]

On standing, Article 268 confers the rights of a professional organisation only once it is registered with the Ministry, as interpreted in Awards No. 023/14, 032/14 and 079/15. [Para 66] The Democratic Local Union was unregistered, so Mr K could not represent its members; under clause 19 of Prakas No. 099/04 the claimants were the three workers present in person, and a fourth worker who attended was held not to be a claimant. [Para 74]

On characterisation, Article 73(1) provides that a fixed duration contract terminates on its stated date, and Article 73(3) entitles the worker to damages of at least the remuneration due to the contract's end where the employer ends it early. [Para 78] Because the employer told the three not to return while continuing to pay them to 31 May 2015, the panel held this was not non-renewal but termination by the employer alone before natural expiry, so the Article 73(3) measure applied. [Para 81]

On discrimination, Articles 12 and 279 forbid weighing union membership or activity in dismissal decisions, but the burden of proof lies on the party alleging it. [Para 83] The workers produced no sufficient evidence; the union's president and the section heads' written requests were before the panel, and the non-renewal rate was 2% against an internal ceiling of 5%, so retaliation was not established. [Para 85]

On special protection, Article 293 paragraph 1 and clause 3 of Prakas No. 305 protect candidates from 45 days before the election to 45 days after, or until registration is applied for; three conditions must be met. [Para 94] The first was satisfied — Mr K was an elected local union leader — and the second too, since he was let go while the union was preparing its registration file. [Para 100] The third failed: the letters said to have been handed to a security guard were never tied to an identified guard, the postal copy went out on 08 June 2015 after his contract had ended, and the employer denied receipt. [Para 106] Following Award No. 171/15, no Labour Inspector authorisation was therefore required. [Para 107]

On the conversion claim, the panel declined to consider it: the three remaining claimants no longer had an employment relationship, and the other 72 workers who had raised it had accepted payment. [Para 112]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer tells a fixed duration worker not to return to work after giving notice, while paying wages to the contractual end date, the panel treats the act as a termination by the employer before natural expiry rather than a non-renewal, and the Article 73(3) measure of damages applies. An unregistered local union cannot represent its members before the panel, so its officers appear only as individual claimants. [Para 81][Para 69]

**Obiter dicta:** the internal practice capping non-renewals at 5% of the workforce, against an actual 2%, was treated as context tending to rebut targeting. The panel also recorded that the workers claimed only reinstatement and back-pay, never termination compensation, which is why no damages were ordered despite the finding of premature termination. [Para 60][Para 108]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The evidence schedule is omitted from this English version, so the documentary findings cannot be checked. [Para 24] The conciliation report is numbered 081/15 at line 16 and 181/15 at line 19. The claim is pleaded for 24 dismissed workers at line 15 but narrowed to three at line 28, and the three were notified on 18 and 21 May 2015 yet described as notified "ten days" before a 31 May expiry. [Para 42] The claimant's name is printed as Meas Somnal, Sonal and Somanl in different places, and the company is styled "A & J (Cambodia)" without a corporate suffix.
