# Award No. 121/15 — Royal Crown Thik International Nal In (Arbitration Council, 25 June 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mr Ly Tai Seng for the employer party, Mr An Vireak for the worker party, Mr Sok Matheung chair) · Doc ID 139202

## 1. Facts
The employer is a garment-cutting and sewing company in Sihanoukville with about 800 workers, of them some 600 women. [Para 50][Para 51] The Democratic Garment Workers' Union Federation (សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា) brought three claims on 05 May 2015: arrears of the meal (noodle) allowance owed since September 2014, milk money for the first month after a worker returns from maternity leave, and the reinstatement of Mr S, dismissed from the export section. [Para 2][Para 4][Para 6][Para 14] At the hearing issue 1 was settled and issue 2 withdrawn in the union's written submission, leaving only the dismissal. [Para 20][Para 21] Mr S had worked there since 03 October 2003 on an indefinite contract at a basic wage of USD 448 a month, with 21 days' paid annual leave, a 13th-month payment and free lunch; 185 workers had transferred their rights to the federation. [Para 53][Para 56][Para 57]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the meal allowance arrears owed from September 2014 to the present? [Para 2]
2. Whether the employer must pay milk money for the first month of a child's life when the worker returns from maternity leave? [Para 4]
3. Whether the employer must take Mr S (employee No. 150, export section) back with back wages from the date of dismissal, or pay compensation if reinstatement is impossible? [Para 6][Para 56]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company owed the meal allowance from September 2014; it should pay the USD 15 childcare allowance from the first month rather than only after the child reaches 18 months; and Mr S was dismissed with no legal basis and no prior notice, contrary to Article 83 of the Labour Law and the internal rules, and wants to return because work is now hard to find. [Para 2][Para 5][Para 62][Para 63][Para 65]
**Employer (respondent):** it could not pay all the arrears at once but offered one month plus one month; it pays the USD 15 childcare allowance only once the child passes 18 months; and it dismissed Mr S for three serious and numerous minor faults, the last being watching videos during working hours and installing a video or music player without IT authorisation on a computer whose internet access had been blocked. [Para 3][Para 5][Para 66][Para 67][Para 94]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement and back wages refused, the dismissal upheld, but the employer ordered to pay Mr S (1) indemnity in lieu of notice equal to 3 months' wages and all benefits, (2) severance of 15 days' wages and allowances per year of service, calculated up to a maximum of 6 months, and (3) payment for unused annual leave. [Para 177][Para 178][Para 179][Para 180][Para 182][Para 183][Para 184]

On Article 83, the panel compared the employer's account with the internal rules approved by the Sihanoukville labour department, whose Article 10 ("ការដាក់ទោស វិន័យ") ranks faults as minor, medium and serious and prescribes education, written warning, suspension without pay of no more than 7 days and dismissal only for a serious fault as defined by the Labour Law. [Para 98][Para 100][Para 103][Para 104][Para 114][Para 120] The rules contain no provision allowing dismissal after three serious faults, and none treating the conduct alleged — watching videos, installing a player, entering blocked sites — as serious misconduct; so the dismissal was not justified on that footing. [Para 144][Para 145]

The panel then turned to Article 74, which allows either party to terminate an indefinite contract with written notice for a reason relating to the worker's aptitude or conduct or the needs of the enterprise, and Article 27, which requires the penalty to match the weight of the fault. [Para 112][Para 146][Para 147][Para 148] It found that Mr S repeatedly watched videos during working hours after two or three warnings from his section head, and had entered internet sites his head had prohibited, and that this failure to respect discipline and instructions was a valid reason within Article 74. [Para 140][Para 141][Para 153][Para 154] The dismissal was therefore lawful and reinstatement, back pay and moral damages were refused. [Para 167][Para 168][Para 177]

Because the dismissal was lawful but effected without notice, Article 75 and Article 77 entitled Mr S, with over ten years' service, to indemnity equal to 3 months' wages and benefits in lieu of notice. [Para 155][Para 156][Para 157][Para 158] Article 89 entitled him to severance of 15 days' wages and allowances per year of service, capped at 6 months. [Para 159][Para 160][Para 163] Since he had already received his final wages, no further order was made on that head, and Article 166 required payment for annual leave not taken. [Para 172][Para 173][Para 175][Para 176]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A dismissal may be lawful under Article 74 of the Labour Law as resting on a reason relating to the worker's conduct, even where Article 83 serious misconduct is not made out and the internal rules do not authorise dismissal: persistent breach of a supervisor's instructions after repeated warnings is such a reason. But where the employer dispenses with the notice period and the contract is indefinite with long service, the worker still recovers the notice indemnity under Articles 75 and 77, severance under Article 89 and unused annual leave under Article 166. [Para 141][Para 145][Para 154][Para 158][Para 163][Para 172]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks on the internal rules' disciplinary scale, and on the absence of any rule permitting dismissal after three serious faults, are not strictly necessary once the dismissal is upheld under Article 74 by another route. [Para 120][Para 148]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The date of dismissal is given three ways: 24 May 2015 [Para 7], 24 April 2015 [Para 58][Para 68][Para 107][Para 162], and 24 April 2014 [Para 128][Para 131]. All are reported as printed; the panel's operative findings use 24 April 2015.
- The employer's name varies between រ៉ូយ៉ាល់ ក្រោន ថិក អ៊ិនធើណេសិន ណល អ៊ីន and រ៉ូយ៉ាល់ ក្រោន ថក at [Para 1], and is shortened to "ក្រុមហ៊ុន រ៉ូយ៉ាល់ ក្រោន ថិក" at [Para 50].
- The evidence list dates the internal rules to 29 October 2003 [Para 29], while the reasoning approves the same rules as registered on 29 October 2013 [Para 98]; the business certificate is given as No. 750 E/2001 of 20 September 2001 [Para 50] but the commercial register certificate as No. 2976 of 21 May 2013 [Para 27].
- The panel's constitution is dated "20 May 2022/2015" [Para 17], and the order extends the award date from "10 June 2019" to 25 June 2015 [Para 24].
- Two hearings are listed, 27 May 2015 at 14:00 and 28 May 2015 at 11:30 [Para 12][Para 13], but the second is dated 28 May 2014 in the procedural summary [Para 18].
- The severance cap is printed as "៤ ០៦ ខែ" (a stray "4" before 06 months) [Para 179][Para 183], and the claim for the meal allowance is described only as "ប្រាក់មី" (noodle money), with no statutory basis stated [Para 2].
- Several sentences are unusable: "បុគ្គលិកផ្នែកនាំចេញ/មិនត្រូវបានអនុញ្ញាតឱ្យ VOA" [Para 125] and repeated duplicated clauses at [Para 151][Para 167].
