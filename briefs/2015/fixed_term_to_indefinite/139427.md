# Award No. 238/15 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន អង្គរ ហេរ៉ូ អ៊ីន ដាស ស្ទ្រី (Arbitration Council, 10 September 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៊ីង សុទ្ធី, appointed by the employer party; អាន វីរៈ, appointed by the worker party; គង់ ផល្ហៈ, chair) · Doc ID 139427

## 1. Facts
Angkor Hero Industry, a Phnom Penh factory at Sre Doun Toch, Ponhea Pon, Prek Pnov, faced a collective dispute filed by two federations to which the company's workers had transferred their right to be represented. [Para 1][Para 22] Conciliation on 26 August 2015 settled seven of fifteen demands and left eight unresolved, which reached the Council on 31 August 2015; the panel was constituted on 1 September and set the hearing for 9 September 2015 at 14:00. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 25][Para 26] The employer attended; the worker party did not, and its two federations told the secretariat by telephone that all the workers had resigned and none would come. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 29]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay women on maternity leave 50 per cent of wages and allowances in advance? [Para 1]
2. Whether the employer must deduct the regular attendance bonus according to a proportional formula? [Para 3]
3. Whether the employer must pay 2,000 riel a day as a lunch allowance? [Para 5]
4. Whether the employer must pay a USD 10 travel allowance? [Para 7]
5. Whether the employer must pay a USD 15 regular attendance bonus? [Para 9]
6. Whether the employer must let pregnant women leave work 15 minutes early? [Para 11]
7. Whether the employer must let pregnant women attend two health checks a month on full pay and benefits? [Para 13]
8. Whether the employer must maintain wages and other benefits during a strike? [Para 15]

None of the eight was decided. The panel put and answered a single preliminary question instead: whether the arbitration should continue where the claimant party is absent. [Para 36]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the eight demands were pressed through the two federations; on the day, neither federation appeared, and both stated by telephone that the workers had resigned and none remained to attend. [Para 22][Para 29]
**Employer (respondent):** attended through its authorised representative and answered the demands on the merits at conciliation — that it pays maternity wages as the Labour Law requires, applies its existing attendance-bonus scale, and cannot yet afford the higher lunch, travel and bonus rates. [Para 2][Para 4][Para 6][Para 8][Para 10] It filed no evidence beyond the delegation letter. [Para 30][Para 31]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** withdrawn — the panel terminated the arbitration of case 238/15 without deciding any of the eight demands, treating the claimant's claim as abandoned. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 65][Para 66]

The panel applied Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY of 21 April 2004 and Rule 4.7 of the arbitration procedure annexed to it: where a party was duly summoned and fails to appear without valid reason, the panel may either continue in that party's absence or close the proceedings by issuing an award. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38] Following its own earlier awards, it identified three cumulative conditions for closing the file — proper summons, non-appearance, and absence of valid reason. [Para 39][Para 40]

On the first condition the panel found the secretariat had issued invitation No. 1192 dated 02 September 2015 to both federations, with the date, time, place and contact numbers, requiring attendance on 9 September 2015 at 14:00. [Para 34][Para 41][Para 42][Para 43][Para 44] On the second it read "appearance before the panel" in Article 21 as requiring both presence at the hearing and participation through all four stages of the arbitration — introduction, explanation of procedure and issues, attempted conciliation, and hearing to judgment — and found the claimant had done neither. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49] On the third it held the federations had given no valid reason, the statement that the workers had resigned being no excuse for the representative body's own default. [Para 50][Para 52][Para 54]

Because the claimant never appeared to state its case, prove its allegations or produce documents, the panel held it could not establish any fact on the eight demands, and that a claimant which does not discharge its burden of argument and evidence abandons its claim. [Para 35][Para 56][Para 57][Para 59][Para 60] The file was therefore closed under Article 21 rather than decided on the merits. [Para 61][Para 64]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claimant party that is duly summoned, does not appear, does not take part in the successive stages of the hearing, and offers no valid reason is taken to have abandoned its claim; the panel will close the proceedings under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 rather than adjudicate demands on which no evidence has been led — and a union federation's report that the workers have resigned does not excuse its own non-appearance. [Para 39][Para 48][Para 52][Para 61]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's account of the parties' reciprocal burdens — the claimant proving its claim first, the respondent then answering it — and its four-stage description of an arbitration hearing were general procedure, not findings on the eight demands. [Para 46][Para 57][Para 58]
