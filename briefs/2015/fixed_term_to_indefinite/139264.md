# Award No. 199/15 — S.H International Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 18 August 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Hun Chundy for the worker party, Taing Try chair) · Doc ID 139264

## 1. Facts

S.H International Co., Ltd is a garment and clothing factory in Ang Keo village, Khan Po Sen Chey, Phnom Penh, which the conciliation report records as employing 160 workers after it released some 400 workers in May 2015. [Para 28][Para 29][Para 35] The plant union, the Free Workers' Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia, filed a complaint on 29 May 2015; one round of conciliation on 20 July 2015 failed and the non-conciliation report was sent to the Secretariat on 27 July 2015. [Para 9][Para 10][Para 22] The union demanded that the employer pay "various compensation in accordance with the Labour Law" to workers on unspecified-duration contracts (កិច្ចសន្យា មាន ថិរ វេលា មិន កំណត់), counted from each worker's date of entry until the present, relying both on what had happened to the 400 released workers and on what it expected to happen to the remaining 160. [Para 34][Para 36][Para 40] The employer filed no documents, called no evidence and did not appear. [Para 15][Para 19][Para 20]

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. Whether the employer is under a duty to pay various compensation in accordance with the Labour Law to the workers who hold unspecified-duration employment contracts? [Para 45][Para 34]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** the 400 workers released in May 2015 received only their last month's wage, the annual-leave indemnity and a 5 per cent contract-termination indemnity, although they had worked in the company since 1997, and payments to those former workers were not in accordance with the Labour Law; the remaining 160 have had no work in July 2015, a machine was trucked out of the factory, July wages had not been paid, and the workers fear further releases. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38][Para 41]

**Employer (respondent):** absent from the hearing without valid reason and unreachable by telephone on the hearing day; its only recorded position, taken from the conciliation report, is that it cannot meet the union's demand because it has not released any workers. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 44]

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider (បដិសេធ ពិចារណា) the workers' claim that the employer pay various compensation under the Labour Law to workers on unspecified-duration contracts, and issued a non-binding award. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 62]

The reasoning rests on jurisdiction, not on the merits. Article 312(1) of the Labour Law provides that the Arbitration Council has no duty to examine any issue other than those stated in the non-conciliation minute, or an issue arising after that minute as a direct consequence of the dispute. [Para 48] The panel adopted its own earlier interpretation in case 10/03-Zacsintec, that the Council was created to resolve labour disputes and not to resolve problems that have not yet arisen, and noted that it had refused to consider claims whose subject matter was a future dispute in awards 68/04, 36/06, 58/07 and 155/13. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51]

Applying that rule, the panel found that the workers themselves accepted that the employer had not yet released the 160 workers, so that both the release and the allegedly unlawful compensation were only their concern about a future date. [Para 52] On the unpaid wages the panel took the pay cycle as decisive: July 2015 wages fell due on 5 August 2015, the workers came to the Council on 4 August 2015, and the pay-day had therefore not arrived. [Para 54][Para 55] No wage or benefit had yet been lost. [Para 57] The panel added that it cannot predict the future, and could not say whether the workers' problems and concerns would occur. [Para 58] It accordingly held that it had no competence to resolve a dispute that has not arisen or has not yet arisen in this case, and dismissed the claim. [Para 59][Para 60]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

Where the event said to trigger an entitlement has not occurred by the date of hearing, the claim is a future dispute and the panel has no jurisdiction under Article 312(1), however strong the underlying grievance: an anticipated dismissal that has not taken place, and wages whose contractual pay-day falls after the hearing, cannot support an award of termination indemnities or unpaid wages. [Para 48][Para 52][Para 55][Para 59]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's statements that it cannot predict the future and that a worker who has lost neither wage nor benefit has nothing to be compensated for are explanatory of the jurisdictional holding rather than independent grounds; and the workers' assertion that the payments made to the 400 former workers were unlawful was recorded but never adjudicated, because the claim as framed concerned the 160 still employed. [Para 41][Para 57][Para 58]
