# Award No. 336/14 — Cambodia Beer Cambrew (Arbitration Council, 30 January 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Tan Try chair) · Doc ID 139779

## 1. Facts
Cambodia Beer Cambrew manufactures beverages and employs approximately 300 female beer promoters who sell and serve its beer in restaurants. [Para 31] The Cambodia Food and Service Workers Federation (CFSWF) and the Democratic Workers Union (DWU) in Angkor Beer Manufacturing Company referred four demands after conciliation failed; non-conciliation report No. 1361 dated 02 December 2014 reached the Secretariat on 03 December 2014. [Para 25][Para 27] Of 49 promoters who thumbprinted an authorisation for CFSWF, 30 later withdrew, leaving 19, plus one promoter who appeared at the hearing without having authorised the federation: 20 claimants. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 39][Para 40] The employer argued that every issue was an individual dispute outside the panel's jurisdiction. [Para 40]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must construct a daycare centre for the infants of female beer promoters aged 0 to 36 months, or alternatively pay USD 30 per month in lieu of a daycare centre? [Para 13][Para 41]
2. Whether the employer must reinstate Ms Ch Pisey and pay her back wages from the termination date of 26 June 2014 to the date of reinstatement? [Para 16][Para 49]
3. Whether the employer must offer a one-year fixed duration contract (FDC) to newly recruited workers and convert FDCs to undetermined duration contracts (UDCs) for workers whose cumulative service exceeds two years? [Para 21][Para 119]
4. Whether the employer must establish temporary measures to lighten the workload of pregnant workers and prevent harm to the fetus? [Para 23][Para 126]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Article 186 of the Labour Law requires a daycare centre, and promoters who take only one month of maternity leave spend USD 30 to USD 40 a month on babysitting. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45] Ms Ch Pisey did serve ice at Angkor Meas restaurant on 23 June 2014 and produced letters from the owner's brother, a waitress and the order clerk; the warnings she accepted were provoked by customers pressing her to drink. [Para 78][Para 80][Para 85] Article 67(2) requires conversion of FDCs to UDCs after two years, and pregnant promoters suspended for lack of a restaurant lose all income. [Para 120][Para 127]
**Employer (respondent):** the current worksite is too narrow to build a daycare centre, which will be built when the enterprise moves; it already limits promoters with infants to five hours a day under Notification No. 04/07 dated 01 February 2007. [Para 45][Para 47][Para 48] Ms Ch Pisey was absent without leave on 23 June 2014 after three warning letters, which is serious misconduct under Article 83 and clauses 14 and 15 of her contract. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58] Contract duration is a managerial prerogative under Article 2 and Awards No. 84/07 and No. 18/07, and each FDC is under two years. [Para 123][Para 125][Para 126]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer is ordered to construct a daycare centre (failing which it pays outside daycare fees for children over 18 months against receipts), to reinstate Ms Ch Pisey with back pay from the date of termination, and to convert FDCs to UDCs where cumulative service exceeds two years; the demands for a flat USD 30, for one-year contracts for new hires, and for lighter duties for pregnant workers are rejected. [Para 251][Para 252][Para 253]

Article 302 requires a dispute between an employer and a certain number of workers over working conditions or union rights, capable of jeopardising the enterprise or social peace. [Para 134][Para 136] The panel held that issues listed in a non-conciliation report are presumed collective and that the objecting employer bears the burden of proof; with 20 claimants, conditions about daycare, reinstatement and contract type, and a real prospect of strike action, all three conditions were met. [Para 135][Para 136][Para 139][Para 140]

On Issue 1, Article 186 obliges an enterprise employing at least one hundred women to install a breastfeeding room and daycare centre, and the panel followed Award No. 81/09-Wonrex Garment: where construction is impossible for children over 18 months, the employer pays fees at any outside centre on production of receipts. [Para 141][Para 143][Para 144] Because receipts vary, a flat USD 30 had no legal basis, so only receipted fees were ordered. [Para 148][Para 149][Para 150]

On Issue 2, the panel first characterised the contract: Ms Ch Pisey began on 24 March 2011 and worked successive FDCs to 31 December 2014, exceeding two years in total, so under Articles 67(2) and 73(5) and Award No. 10/03-Jaxsintex she was a UDC worker. [Para 167][Para 168][Para 169][Para 160] Her claim and the restaurant owner's letter of 25 June 2014 contradicted each other, so a second hearing was held; the worker witness could not recall the date, while the driver, the coordinator and the zone manager's report corroborated the absence, and the panel found the employer discharged its burden. [Para 185][Para 191][Para 192][Para 203][Para 204] But one day's absence did not meet clause 15, which permits termination for three warning letters or two consecutive days of unexcused absence, and under Article 27 and Award No. 109/07-Kingsland the sanction was disproportionate to the misconduct; with no valid reason under Article 74, reinstatement and back pay followed. [Para 211][Para 213][Para 176][Para 218][Para 219]

On Issue 3, Article 65 and Articles 311 and 337 of the Civil Code make contract type a matter of matching offer and acceptance, so neither side can impose a one-year term on the other; that limb was rejected. [Para 221][Para 222][Para 231][Para 234] The conversion limb succeeded: Articles 67(2) and 73(5), read with Award No. 10/03 and ILO Recommendation No. 166, measure the two-year cap against cumulative service, not each renewal, and three named promoters with more than two years' service were still on FDCs without objection from the employer. [Para 238][Para 242][Para 246][Para 247][Para 248]

On Issue 4, the only pregnant promoter identified, Ms C Lysa, had withdrawn her thumbprint from the authorisation, so no claimant remained on that demand and it was rejected. [Para 249][Para 250][Para 251]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The two-year ceiling in Article 67(2) is measured against the worker's cumulative service, not the length of each renewal: once successive FDCs carry a worker past two years, the relationship is a UDC by operation of Article 73(5), and the employer must issue UDCs to any promoter whose service has passed that threshold. [Para 238][Para 246][Para 247] Separately, a disciplinary dismissal must be proportionate under Article 27 and rest on a valid reason under Article 74; a contractual ground for termination is not satisfied by conduct falling short of the threshold the clause itself sets, so a single day's absence cannot support dismissal where the clause requires two consecutive days. [Para 211][Para 213][Para 218]

**Obiter dicta:** ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982 is not mandatory but assists the interpretation of Article 67, and the Labour Law favours UDCs under Article 67 paragraphs 7 and 8 because they secure work for both sides. [Para 161][Para 244][Para 245] The panel also observed that Article 186 leaves the employer an option between building on site and paying outside fees. [Para 147]

## 6. Source defects disclosed- The English text is an unofficial translation and its evidence list was omitted, so the record was reconstructed from the reasons. [Para 30]
- The panel found clause 15 "applies to Chom Pisey's case" yet also found she had three warning letters, which is the clause's other termination trigger; the reasons rest only on the one-day absence, and that inconsistency is unreconciled. [Para 212][Para 213]
- A clarification letter is dated 20 January 2015 in the findings and 20 January 2014 in the reasons; the restaurant's replacement request is variously dated 25 June 2014 and cited for absence on "Monday, 23 June 2014". [Para 85][Para 183][Para 202]
- Coordinator Shit Mosalin is described as female and as "he"/"Mr" across consecutive lines, and the worker is spelled Tit Saren, Tit Sarorn and Tit Saron. [Para 34][Para 101][Para 103][Para 247]
