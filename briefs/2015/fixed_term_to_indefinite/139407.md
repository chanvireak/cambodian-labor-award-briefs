# Award No. 109/15 — Hong Sun Garment (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 22 June 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tai Seng, employer-appointed; Toun Si Phan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chea, president) · Doc ID 139407

## 1. Facts
The employer, Hong Sun Garment (Cambodia), sews clothing with about 130 workers. [Para 39][Para 40] A union was formed at the factory on 12 February 2015 and three of its leaders were taken to the administration office on 14 February 2015, where they signed letters of resignation giving "hate the factory" (ស្អប់ រោងចក្រ) as the reason, and were paid their final wages. [Para 41][Para 49][Para 65][Para 168] They claim they were dismissed for their union roles and seek reinstatement with back wages. [Para 136] The panel also had to decide three remaining demands on bonuses, lunch money and travel allowance. [Para 18]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the three workers' contracts were fixed-duration or undetermined-duration contracts? [Para 138]
2. Whether the ending of their employment was a resignation or a dismissal by the employer? [Para 156]
3. Whether they enjoyed protection against dismissal as elected union leadership candidates, so that prior labour inspector authorisation was required? [Para 178]
4. Whether the dismissal was discrimination against union membership or activity? [Para 215]
5. Whether the employer terminated the contracts lawfully under the Labour Law? [Para 227]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer summoned them, locked the office, refused them their phones and forced them to sign resignation letters — two of them illiterate, so one wrote for all; they had no fault and no prior warning, and were dismissed because they were union leaders. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 52][Para 53][Para 55]
**Employer (respondent):** The three resigned of their own accord that morning and were paid final wages plus seniority and leave compensation that afternoon; the factory was in disorder and near closing, but no dismissal occurred, and the employer never received the union's notice of leadership. [Para 47][Para 50][Para 56][Para 57][Para 204]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — reinstatement and back wages refused for all three, and the bonus, lunch money (4000 riel a day) and USD 5 travel allowance claims refused; no sum is awarded [Para 248][Para 254][Para 255][Para 256]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 67(1), (2) and (7): a fixed-duration contract must be in writing, state its start and end dates, and cannot exceed two years. [Para 138][Para 139][Para 141] The employer produced three-month contracts running 26 November 2014 to 26 February 2015 for two of the workers, and the workers filed no objection to them, so the panel found fixed-duration contracts; the third held a two-month probation contract, which Article 68 permits. [Para 146][Para 147][Para 148][Para 150][Para 155]

On issue 2, applying the burden of proof rule from Awards 79/05 (Evergreen), 101/08 (GVM), 168/09 (Tek Thla Plaza) and 115/10 (GF Mos), the panel weighed the resignation letters and found them abnormal: all three gave the identical reason "hate the factory", which would damage the company's reputation; one letter bore the identity number of a different worker; the employer did not apply its own seven-day notice deduction although it said it always did; and the employer offered no comment on the reason given. [Para 158][Para 161][Para 166][Para 168][Para 170][Para 171] The panel therefore held the resignations did not express the workers' own will and that this was a dismissal. [Para 175][Para 177]

On issue 3 the panel applied Article 293 and Article 3 of Prakas No. 305, requiring three cumulative conditions. [Para 178][Para 181][Para 182][Para 191] Condition 1 was met — all three were elected leaders on 12 February 2015. [Para 193][Para 195] Condition 2 was met — dismissal came two days after the ballot. [Para 197] Condition 3 failed: the notice dated 13 February 2015 carried no signature of receipt by any administrative officer or employer representative, the employer denied receiving it, and the claimants showed no other means used to notify. [Para 205][Para 209][Para 210][Para 212] Without notification the workers were not protected, so no inspector authorisation was required. [Para 213]

On issue 4 the panel applied Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law, which forbid using union membership or activity as a ground for discipline or termination. [Para 215][Para 216] Since the employer had no knowledge of the leadership, no evidence supported the discrimination allegation. [Para 224][Para 225][Para 226]

On issue 5, Article 73(1)–(3) allows an employer to end a fixed-duration contract on its own will but obliges it to pay damages at least equal to the wages due to the contract's end; Article 82 excuses notice and reasons during probation. [Para 227][Para 229][Para 231][Para 241] The three claimed only reinstatement and back wages, never damages, so reinstatement was refused; for the probationer no reason need be given. [Para 236][Para 237][Para 244][Para 246] The remaining claims failed because no employment relationship continued and no claimant remained. [Para 249][Para 253]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where resignation letters bear hallmarks of employer manufacture — identical implausible reasons, mismatched identity numbers, departure from the employer's own notice policy and silence on the stated reason — the panel will find a dismissal, not a resignation, and the employer bears the consequence. [Para 166][Para 175][Para 177] But the protection in Article 293 is not automatic: it requires effective notice of the candidacy reaching the employer, and absent a signed receipt or other proven means the panel will not find the worker protected. [Para 209][Para 212][Para 213]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that the two fixed-term workers could have claimed damages equal to wages to the contract's end under Article 73(3) but had not asked for them, so nothing could be awarded. [Para 233][Para 237]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The union leadership is described inconsistently: one passage gives the president, vice-president and secretary as the three claimants, while another names a different worker as vice-president with the third claimant as secretary, and the notice of 13 February follows the first version. [Para 41][Para 80][Para 88] Worker names change spelling throughout (the president appears as Yim, Yem and Ym Sarong; the secretary as Sam Theary and Sam Theavy). [Para 43][Para 65][Para 238] Stray years have been OCR'd into figures and text — "2017" in the body and "21 April 2019 2004" for Prakas No. 099, and the word "four" appears inside the Article 73 passages. [Para 62][Para 133][Para 229] Paragraphs 250 to 252 are a corrupted repeat ("កណ្តាល នោះ ដែរ") with no content, the signature block is fragmentary, and the employer's own count of 130 workers is given only by the employer. [Para 40][Para 250][Para 252][Para 258] The three claimants are designated Mr Y, Ms T and Ms S here.
