# Award No. 276/14 — Mithapheap Garment (Arbitration Council, 20 November 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Hem Hor Narith appointed by the employer, An Non appointed by the workers, Run Saray chair appointed by both) · Doc ID 139454

## 1. Facts
Mithapheap Garment (ក្រុមហ៊ុន មិត្តភាព ហ្គាមិន), a garment factory employing about 145, dismissed Ms J, head of its sewing section since 12 March 2013, on 9 October 2015 without notice and without any written employment contract. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 37] Ninety-two workers were claimants: ninety-one who transferred their rights to the Independent Labour Union Federation and Ms J herself, who attended. [Para 31][Para 34] Of seven demands, five were settled at conciliation and the two referred were the reinstatement claim and the attendance-bonus claim, the latter abandoned at the hearing. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 52][Para 53]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (ក) Whether Ms J held a fixed-term or an indefinite-term employment contract? [Para 56]
2. (ខ) Whether the employer dismissed her for a valid reason under Article 74 of the Labour Law? [Para 63]
3. (គ) Whether the company-wide agreement by which workers acknowledged that the factory owes no severance or other compensation beyond the annual bonus and monthly wage is valid and enforceable? [Para 85]
4. (ឃ) What compensation is she entitled to on dismissal? [Para 93]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer dismissed Ms J without valid reason and without notice, and never gave her a written warning; she admitted some garments had been sewn to the wrong pattern but every batch had been approved by the employer in advance after inspection of the sample, so fault cannot be attributed to her alone — she should be reinstated with back pay or, failing that, paid the statutory severance: notice pay, dismissal compensation, moral damages and 7 days of unused annual leave. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 50][Para 51][Para 36]
**Employer (respondent):** the factory suffered losses because workers under her supervision sewed many garments to the wrong pattern and she answered that she did not know why, showing she did not take care in her work, so there was no need to keep her; it never issued a written warning but did reprimand her orally, and it will neither reinstate her nor pay severance, because the company is small and because all workers agreed that the factory provides nothing beyond the annual bonus and the monthly wage on termination. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43][Para 45][Para 46][Para 48]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement with back pay was refused, and the employer was ordered to pay one month's wages plus all benefits as notice pay, dismissal compensation of 45 days' wages and allowances, moral damages of 45 days, and 7.5 days of unused annual leave. [Para 135][Para 136][Para 137][Para 138][Para 139][Para 140]

On the contract, Article 67(1) requires a fixed-term contract to state a precise end date, and Article 67(7) requires it to be in writing; a contract that fails these rules becomes indefinite-term. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58][Para 59] Both parties accepted she had no written contract, so her contract was indefinite-term. [Para 61][Para 62]

On the reason for dismissal, Article 74 requires a valid reason relating to the worker's aptitude or conduct or the necessity of the enterprise, and the party alleging a fact bears the burden of proof. [Para 63][Para 65][Para 76] The panel followed awards 79/05, 101/08, 168/09, 115/10 and 148/11. [Para 77][Para 78] It found the employer had not proved that she committed a fault of aptitude or conduct capable of justifying dismissal, since the employer had approved the sample garments before production. [Para 73][Para 81] It added that an employer should issue a written warning, or another disciplinary measure under its internal rules, before proceeding to dismissal, and no written warning was ever given. [Para 75][Para 82] The dismissal therefore lacked a valid reason. [Para 83][Para 84]

On the waiver, Article 13(1) makes the Labour Law public order and voids any unilateral decision, contract or convention that does not comply with it. [Para 85][Para 86] The panel held the agreement cuts off benefits the law guarantees on termination, contrary to Chapter 4, Section 3 of the Labour Law, and is void. [Para 90][Para 91][Para 92]

On the amounts, it applied Article 75 (one month's notice for service over two and up to five years — she had 2 years, 6 months and 26 days) with wages and all benefits for that month; Article 89 (15 days' wages per year, the fraction over six months counted as a full year, giving 45 days); Article 91 (moral damages equal to the dismissal compensation, 45 days, because she proved no separate loss); and Articles 166 and 167 (7.5 days of unused annual leave). [Para 97][Para 105][Para 106][Para 109][Para 115][Para 122][Para 129][Para 130] Final wages had already been paid, so nothing was ordered on that head. [Para 132][Para 134]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employment contract is not in writing and states no end date, it is indefinite-term, and the employer who dismisses must prove a fault of aptitude or conduct capable of justifying dismissal; approving the sample before production defeats a later complaint about that production, and a dismissal should be preceded by a written warning or a disciplinary measure under the internal rules. A company-wide acknowledgement by workers that no severance or other compensation is owed is void under Article 13(1), so the statutory entitlements under Articles 75, 89 and 91 survive it. [Para 62][Para 81][Para 82][Para 92]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that the employer should have warned in writing "before reaching dismissal" states a general practice rather than a statutory precondition, since Article 74 itself requires only a valid reason; and its observations on how a fraction of service over six months is rounded into a full year are general to Article 89. [Para 82][Para 111]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The workers' claim is recorded both as "ប្រាក់ បំណាច់ ផ្តាច់កិច្ចសន្យា ស្របតាមច្បាប់" and as a list of four heads, and the request for reinstatement alternates between "ឬ" (or) and an unconditional demand, so the panel treats the compensation claim as alternative while the pleading reads as cumulative. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 54][Para 140]
- The dismissed worker's name is printed in at least four forms — "ជិន សាវីន", "ជិន សាវឺន", "ជិន សាវី" and "ជិន សាវីន" with a stray "និង" inserted before it. She is referred to here as Ms J. [Para 31][Para 44][Para 72][Para 120]
- The employer's evidentiary list is described as 106 workers acknowledging that the factory owes "លុយ ៥ ភាំគរយ" (garbled, apparently 5 per cent) and no severance, while the union represents 91 transferees and the factory employs about 145. [Para 23][Para 30][Para 31][Para 49]
- The conciliation report is dated 21 October 2015 in the procedural history but 20 October 2015 where the panel records its transmittal, and the minute of conciliation is dated 19 October; the sentence carrying the date is split across two lines. [Para 8][Para 13][Para 14][Para 26]
- Final wages are attributed to Article 116 of the Labour Law in the heading but Article 166 in the finding, and the award never states why reinstatement was refused while compensation was awarded instead. [Para 131][Para 133][Para 135]
- The case number is 276/14 although the award issues in November 2015, and the panel cites Prakas 321 for a thirteenth term of 2015. [Para 1][Para 6]
