# Award No. 151/15 — Rijen Footwear (Cambodia) Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 6 July 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, chosen by the employer; Liv Sovanna, chosen by the workers; Ang Eng Thong, chair) · Doc ID 139228

## 1. Facts
Rijen Footwear (Cambodia) Co Ltd (រីជេន ហ្វ៊ូត វែរ (ខេមបូឌា) ខូអិលធីឌី), a shoe factory at Ang Snuol, Kandal with 413 workers, was sold by its former owner to a new one. [Para 1][Para 21] The two federations representing the workers demanded that the former owner pay severance for contracts of indefinite duration before the sale; he refused, relying on Chapter 4, Article 87 of the Labour Law, and saying he had no intention of terminating anyone's contract. [Para 2][Para 3] Conciliation on 17 June 2015 failed on that single point. [Para 10][Para 11] Workers struck from 16 June 2015; the Council ordered the strike suspended on 24 June and the workers back to their shifts from the afternoon of 25 June. [Para 15][Para 16] They returned on 26 and 27 June, struck again on 29 June, and 326 of the 413 were still out on the hearing date of 30 June. [Para 21][Para 46]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the former owner must pay severance for contracts of indefinite duration when he sells the factory to a new owner? [Para 2][Para 9]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The sale of the factory ended their employment with the former owner, so he owes each of them the severance due on termination of an indefinite-duration contract; they struck to press the demand. [Para 9][Para 43]
**Employer (respondent):** The former owner refuses because he had no wish to terminate the workers' contracts at all, the Law on Labour at Chapter 4, Article 87 providing for the contracts to continue; the company also asked the Council to order the strike suspended. [Para 3][Para 15] At the hearing the employer appeared and asserted that after the Council's order the workers returned on 26 and 27 June and then walked out again on 29 June. [Para 18][Para 21]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel terminated the arbitration proceedings without deciding the severance claim, and ordered nothing. [Para 48][Para 49]

The panel never reached the merits. Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 requires the parties to suspend every dispute measure — strike, lock-out or anything else that could aggravate the dispute — throughout the arbitration, and to attend every meeting the panel convenes. [Para 32][Para 33] Following its earlier awards, the panel held that it will not continue arbitration while the workers are striking or the employer has locked out, and will not proceed where a party has taken a measure that aggravates the dispute. [Para 35][Para 37]

On the facts it found that the Council had issued order No. 022/15 on 24 June 2015 directing an immediate suspension of the strike and a return to work from the afternoon of 25 June 2015 pending completion of the arbitration. [Para 16][Para 28] The strike nonetheless continued, and although the Secretariat was told by telephone on 26 June that the workers had returned, the panel found that they struck again on 29 June and were still on strike at the hearing. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46] It held that a continuing strike aggravated the dispute and made it impossible to continue the proceedings or hold a hearing. [Para 47] It therefore terminated the arbitration under Article 20. [Para 48][Para 49]

One further element shaped the outcome: only one federation appeared. The Khmer Union Federation attended and withdrew its complaint, while the Cambodia Workers' Union Federation was absent and had told the Secretariat by telephone that the workers would not attend because they were striking to obtain a solution from the Minister. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 22] No findings of fact were made on the disputed point, precisely because no hearing was held. [Para 31]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A panel will not arbitrate a dispute while a party is defying the Council's own order: where workers continue or resume a strike after the Council has ordered it suspended under Article 20 of Prakas No. 099, or an employer maintains a lock-out, the panel terminates the proceedings instead of hearing the merits, so the claim is left undetermined and must be brought again once the dispute measures have ceased. [Para 35][Para 37][Para 47][Para 48]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's recital of the former owner's position — that Article 87 of the Labour Law carries the contracts over to the buyer so that no termination and no severance arise — was not adopted or rejected, and played no part in the termination. [Para 3][Para 31]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award contradicts itself on whether a hearing occurred: paragraphs 18 to 22 describe a hearing on 30 June 2015 attended by the employer, and paragraph 31 states that the panel made no findings because it did not conduct a hearing. [Para 18][Para 21][Para 31] The order suspending the strike is dated 24 June 2015 in one place and "24 ខេមថុនា" in another, and the panel's constitution is dated "24 June 2023 2015". [Para 12][Para 16][Para 28] The company name is printed as រីជេន, រី ជេន, រេជេន and វី ជេន across the face of the award, and its Latin form here is a transliteration. [Para 1][Para 11][Para 49] The paragraph that should record the employer's pre-hearing attendance opens with the stray words "អ្នកស្រី", and the panel's operative paragraph is preceded by a garbled duplicate of the reservation clause. [Para 18][Para 41] The worker headcount is 413 in the employer's account but the conciliation report and the panel use 326 strikers without reconciling them. [Para 21][Para 43]
