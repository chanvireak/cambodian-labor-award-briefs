# Award No. 149/15 — Dayang Cambodia Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 17 July 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Vireak, appointed by the workers; Kong Pollak, president appointed by both) · Doc ID 139229 · non-binding award, with a dissenting opinion by arbitrator Ing Sothy annexed

## 1. Facts
Dayang Cambodia Co., Ltd is a garment cutting and sewing enterprise employing about 600 workers, down from more than 1,000. Its workers' union was registered with the Ministry of Labour on 09 March 2015 under No. 3244. Four workers — Ms R, Ms S, Ms O and Mr K — each worked under three successive one-year fixed-duration contracts running from 2012, were given written notice of termination dated 07 April 2015 (Mr K on 07 May 2015), and were dismissed on 23 May 2015, 07 May 2015, 23 May 2015 and 11 June 2015 respectively. [Para 1][Para 53][Para 54][Para 55][Para 123]
A collective dispute was first raised for 16 union members; 12 settled with the employer and only four remained in the claim, seeking reinstatement in their former positions and back wages from dismissal to reinstatement, which the company refused. [Para 2][Para 3][Para 57]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take the four workers back in their former positions and pay back wages from the date of dismissal to the date of reinstatement? [Para 2]
2. Whether the four workers' successive one-year fixed-duration contracts had become contracts of indefinite duration? [Para 107]
3. Whether the four workers, as union members, were entitled to protection against dismissal? [Para 128]
4. Whether the employer terminated the four workers' contracts for a valid reason under the Labour Law, and if not, what compensation is owed? [Para 138]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The contracts were renewed three times with no notice of expiry before each renewal, so under Article 67(2) and Article 73(5) of the Labour Law they became indefinite-duration; the dismissals rested on no valid reason, no serious misconduct and no prior warning, and the employer recruited four replacements while work continued. As union members the four were protected against dismissal under Article 4(1) of Prakas No. 305. [Para 88][Para 90][Para 93][Para 95][Para 96]
**Employer (respondent):** The four held one-year fixed-duration contracts and had already received the 5% end-of-contract payment for 2013 and 2014, so nothing further was owed. The company takes no direct orders and only processes goods for other firms, which forced a workforce reduction; it would re-employ the four only if they accepted two-month contracts. It did not contest that they were union members. [Para 99][Para 101][Para 102][Para 129]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand for reinstatement and back wages was dismissed; the employer was ordered to pay severance under Article 89 calculated at 15 days' wages and allowances per year of service on 3 years 2 months' seniority (capped at six months), moral damages under Article 91 equal to that severance, and payment for unused annual leave under Article 166, with the right to deduct the 5% end-of-contract sums already paid in 2013 and 2014. [Para 202][Para 203][Para 204][Para 205][Para 206]

On contract type the panel held that a fixed-duration contract may not exceed two years and that successive renewals only remain fixed-duration while each renewal stays within that ceiling; absence of written notice of expiry makes the contract renew silently for an equal term, and once the aggregate exceeds two years it converts to indefinite duration. Total service here was three years, so the contracts were indefinite. The panel followed the interpretation of Article 67(2) in Award No. 10/03 and treated ILO Recommendation No. 166 as useful context. [Para 107][Para 109][Para 113][Para 121][Para 124][Para 126]

On union protection the panel read Article 4(1) of Prakas No. 305 as protecting founders and members only for 30 days after registration. Registration was 09 March 2015 and the dismissals fell on 07 May and 11 June 2015, outside that window, so no delegate-style protection applied even though the employer conceded membership. [Para 130][Para 131][Para 134][Para 135]

On the validity of the termination the panel found no serious misconduct under Article 83 and no warning, which the employer did not contest. Article 74 allows termination of an indefinite contract with written notice only for a reason relating to the worker's capacity or conduct or to the operational needs of the enterprise. The absence of direct orders was accepted as a lawful ground for reducing activity under Article 95, but Article 95 requires a selection order and notification to worker representatives; the employer merely gave notice of expiry and non-renewal, so the dismissals were without valid reason. [Para 139][Para 143][Para 160][Para 161][Para 162]

Notice pay was refused: one month's written notice was given, satisfying Article 75(1) read with Article 77, so no payment in lieu was owed. [Para 166][Para 167][Para 168]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer renews a fixed-duration contract successively without written notice of expiry, and the aggregate term exceeds the two-year ceiling in Article 67(2), the contract is an indefinite-duration contract; terminating it without a reason tied to the worker's capacity or conduct or to the enterprise's operational needs, and without the Article 95 procedure where the ground is reduced activity, is a termination without valid reason, entitling the worker to severance under Article 89 and moral damages under Article 91 but not to reinstatement. The 30-day protection in Article 4(1) of Prakas No. 305 runs from the date of union registration and does not revive for later dismissals. [Para 126][Para 134][Para 162][Para 174][Para 186]

**Obiter dicta:** ILO Recommendation No. 166 is not binding but is a helpful context for interpreting Article 67 [Para 119]; the Labour Law favours indefinite-duration contracts because job security serves workers and long service serves employers [Para 115]; the Law does not remove the employer's right to terminate, but requires compensation [Para 137]. The annexed dissent of arbitrator Ing Sothy, preferring reinstatement and denying severance, is a minority opinion and forms no part of the award. [Para 209][Para 229]

One worker's name appears as "Rin Phanny", "Vin Phanny" and "Oun Saveth" across the document [Para 56][Para 58][Para 133]. OCR losses include a garbled digit string in the case header [Para 1], stray Latin fragments inside the reasons [Para 104][Para 170], and several truncated figures where amounts or article numbers were destroyed ("ប្រាក់ឈ្នួល ៤ហើយ", "១៣") [Para 165][Para 186]. The award prints no riel or USD figure for any head of compensation, so the sums are expressed only as multiples of wages.
