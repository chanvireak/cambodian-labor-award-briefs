# Award No. 078/15 — Soun Usahakmen Hat Ten (Arbitration Council, 24 April 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Koy Neam chair) · Doc ID 139170

## 1. Facts
Soun Usahakmen Hat Ten, a garment-cutting factory in Kampong Siem district, Kampong Cham, employing 3,500 to 4,000 workers, did not renew the six-month fixed-term contracts of 68 workers when they expired on 28 February 2015. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 75] Seven women workers brought the claim, alleging the non-renewal punished them for union membership and for taking part in an earlier strike; the factory union had been registered with the Ministry of Labour since 18 September 2012. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 63] The original first issue, a demand that the employer comply with Award No. 014/14 by paying 100 per cent wages, was withdrawn in writing on 6 April 2015, leaving one issue. [Para 2][Para 15][Para 16]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate the three workers whose successive fixed-term contracts together exceeded two years? [Para 81][Para 93]
2. Whether the employer must reinstate the four workers whose successive fixed-term contracts together did not exceed two years? [Para 108][Para 110]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the seven were singled out because they were union members and had been active in the earlier strike; some of those not renewed were later re-hired as new workers, which the workers characterise as a penalty for striking; an employer with a complaint against a worker should issue a warning rather than let the contract lapse. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 72][Para 78][Para 79]
**Employer (respondent):** renewal depends on the section head's assessment of quality, output, diligence and discipline, and non-renewal of a fixed-term contract is the employer's right; some 3,000 workers struck, so discrimination against seven would be implausible; the three warnings issued to one worker for leaving a machine running, uncut thread and a dirty workplace show cause. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 68][Para 71][Para 77]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to take back the three workers whose total service exceeded two years and let them work normally; the claim for the other four was dismissed. [Para 139][Para 140]

On the first group the panel applied Article 67(2) of the Labour Law: a fixed-duration contract may not run longer than two years, and successive renewals that push the total beyond two years convert it into an indefinite-duration contract, a reading it had already adopted in Award No. 10/03 and confirmed by Article 73(5). [Para 81][Para 82][Para 84][Para 87] It noted that the Labour Law leans towards indefinite-duration contracts because job security benefits both sides, and treated ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982 as persuasive context only, since it is not binding. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 90] On the facts, two of the three had joined in February 2013 and the third in June 2012, so each had more than two years' total service and each held an indefinite-duration contract; their contracts had therefore not simply expired but were terminated on 28 February 2015, and Article 74 permits termination only on valid grounds connected with aptitude, conduct or the needs of the enterprise. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 95][Para 96][Para 98] The employer produced no document at all by the evidence deadline to justify those three dismissals, so the panel found no valid ground and held the dismissals unlawful; under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 it ordered reinstatement. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 104][Para 105][Para 106][Para 107]

On the second group the panel found that all four had joined in 2014 and that their total service, even across renewals, fell short of two years, so they remained on six-month fixed-term contracts. [Para 108][Para 109][Para 110][Para 111] Their claim was one of anti-union discrimination, and the panel held the claimant bears the burden of proving it. [Para 113][Para 114][Para 120][Para 125] It found no specific evidence: the workers could not name which of the 68 non-renewed workers had been re-hired or how many, one worker could not date the section head's remark, and the employer had declined to renew 68 workers in total, which undercut any inference of targeting. [Para 126][Para 130][Para 132][Para 135] The three warnings issued to one worker were capable of explaining her non-renewal. [Para 136] The claim was therefore dismissed. [Para 137][Para 138]

Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented on the first group, reasoning that the employer had paid the 5 per cent severance at the end of every fixed-term contract as Article 73(6) requires, so it had discharged its obligations and should not also be ordered to reinstate. [Para 148][Para 158][Para 164]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where successive fixed-term contracts run beyond two years in total, the contract becomes one of indefinite duration under Article 67(2), so allowing it to lapse is a dismissal that must rest on valid grounds of aptitude, conduct or the needs of the enterprise under Article 74; if the employer files no evidence of such grounds, the dismissal is unlawful and reinstatement follows. [Para 84][Para 98][Para 104] Where total service stays under two years, non-renewal at expiry is lawful, and a claim that it was anti-union discrimination fails unless the claimant identifies the comparators and dates on which the inference depends. [Para 111][Para 130][Para 134]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that the Labour Law favours indefinite-duration contracts for the job security they give, that ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982 is non-binding but useful context, and its arithmetic remark that an employer choosing fixed-term contracts over long service ultimately bears the 5 per cent severance repeatedly, are general reasoning not needed for either order. [Para 86][Para 90][Para 160]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The last contracts of the four-worker group are dated 28 February 2014 at [Para 109] but 28 February 2015 at [Para 73] and [Para 92]; 2015 is consistent with the hearing and the non-renewal list, and is used in the brief.
- The award describes the two groups in the reverse order of the demand as originally pleaded (the three-worker group first at [Para 81], the four-worker group second at [Para 108]); the numbering followed here is the panel's own.
- The union's name appears as "Sampean Sapheap Kambuja" in Khmer only, and the employer's name is OCR'd variously ("Soun Usahakmen Hat Ten", "Men Hat Ten", "Sbuon"); the form on the face of the award is used.
- The list of non-renewed workers at [Para 75] gives 68 in total but the component figures (24, 23, 18) sum to 65, leaving three unexplained.
- Individual workers appear by Khmer name only and are redacted here; the award gives no romanised spellings. Arbitrator names are transliterated from Khmer. [Para 142]
- Cited prior awards are reduced to fragments ("10/03-Zaksintik", "51/08-SD", "148/07-Baiheu"), so their identities cannot be checked from this text. [Para 84][Para 100][Para 121]
