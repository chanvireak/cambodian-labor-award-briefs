# Award No. 257/15 — Cambo Yon Sing Garment (Arbitration Council, 2 November 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chhea presiding; Li Tai Seng, employer nominee; Liv Sovanna, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139438

## 1. Facts
Cambo Yon Sing Garment, at Teuk Thla, Sen Sok, Phnom Penh, cuts and sews clothing with about 430 workers; it was previously called Yong Fong and before that Bes Van, the change to its present name dating from June 2014 and passing without any severance payment. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 63][Para 64][Para 65] The Cambodia Workers' Union Federation complained on 25 August 2015 over thirteen points of working conditions, conciliation on 11 September 2015 left three unsettled, and these reached the Secretariat on 17 September 2015. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 15] The authorisation letter of 14 August 2015 carried 189 workers, of whom three representatives attended the hearing. [Para 41][Para 42] The workers withdrew the first issue, on renewal of one worker's contract, at the hearing. [Para 20][Para 21]

## 2. Issues in dispute
2. Whether the employer must remove the Chinese chief supervisor who manages the sewing team leaders from the company? [Para 44][Para 75]
3. Whether the employer must recognise the contracts of workers whose total fixed-duration service exceeds two years as contracts of unlimited duration? [Para 63][Para 105]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the chief supervisor, in about three months, has spoken abusively and behaved improperly — rebuking the nurse for letting a dizzy worker take medicated oil without written request — has raised the hourly quota from 50–60 to 110 garments, and has let contracts lapse out of spite against workers who used their legal leave. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 50][Para 52]
**Employer (respondent):** it cannot remove her, because the company loses money every year and her presence has lifted output and given the workers a chance of continued employment, the higher output also earning them bonuses; the non-renewals were a planned reduction of the eight-person assistant section, not retaliation; and it cannot convert contracts because the company's economic future is uncertain. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 56][Para 71]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel refused the demand to remove the supervisor and ordered the employer to convert to contracts of unlimited duration those workers whose cumulative fixed-duration service exceeds two years. [Para 127][Para 128]
On issue 2 the panel held that under Article 65(1) of the Labour Law and Article 311 of the 2007 Civil Code only the parties to a contract may create, vary or extinguish the obligations it creates, so a third party — including a supervisor who is not a party to the workers' contracts — cannot be removed at the demand of other workers. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 78][Para 80] Article 2 reserves to the employer the management and direction of the enterprise, exercisable lawfully and reasonably, and the panel may order a transfer or dismissal only where the person concerned is shown to endanger the health or safety of other workers. [Para 82][Para 84][Para 91] Here the workers described the conduct only in general terms and produced nothing on what was said to the nurse or how, so the panel could not find the supervisor to be such a danger; it followed Awards 116/10 Vitech and 83/12. [Para 95][Para 96][Para 101][Para 102] It accepted that pressing for higher output falls within managerial prerogative so long as it is lawful and reasonable, and no evidence showed the quota increase to be otherwise. [Para 99][Para 101]
On issue 3 the panel applied Article 67(2), which caps a genuine fixed-duration contract at two years, and Article 73(5), which converts a renewed contract whose cumulative duration passes that cap into one of unlimited duration, reading the reference as going to total duration rather than to each renewal. [Para 110][Para 112][Para 113][Para 118] It followed Award 10/03 Zacksintek and treated ILO Recommendation 166 as useful interpretive context though not binding. [Para 114][Para 119][Para 121] The authorisation letter showed 111 workers with more than two years' service, the employer did not dispute that list, and the 5 per cent end-of-contract payments did not prevent conversion, so the employer was ordered to issue unlimited-duration contracts. [Para 72][Para 108][Para 109][Para 124][Para 125][Para 126]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that the employer remove a supervisor or any other third party from the enterprise cannot succeed: only the parties to a contract of employment may alter it, and the panel will order a transfer or dismissal only on proof that the individual endangers the health or safety of other workers — general complaints about manner and production pressure do not meet that threshold. [Para 78][Para 80][Para 91] A chain of fixed-duration contracts whose cumulative duration passes two years converts by operation of Articles 67(2) and 73(5) into a contract of unlimited duration whether or not the employer paid the 5 per cent end-of-contract sum at each expiry. [Para 113][Para 118][Para 124]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the change of company name in June 2014 was carried out without any severance payment and the workers simply continued, a fact it recorded but did not need, since the severance question was not before it on these issues. [Para 65] It also observed that differences of culture and temperament may explain friction between Chinese supervisors and Khmer workers, without treating that as a defence. [Para 61]
