# Award No. 075/15 — Vai Tech Garment (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 6 April 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ingh Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Tang Tri (president) · Doc ID 139167

## 1. Facts
The Federation of Trade Unions of Workers (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត) filed twelve demands for improved conditions at Vai Tech Garment (Cambodia); three were settled in conciliation on 11 March 2015 and nine went unresolved to the Council through report No. 284 Kb/Ak/Vk of 18 March 2015 [Para 26][Para 27]. The worker-side arbitrator was chosen from the employer list [Para 1]. At the identification stage of the hearing on 31 March 2015 the union asked that arbitrator to withdraw for bias; he refused, and the union then declined to take any further part in the hearing [Para 31][Para 34][Para 35][Para 65]. The award issued 6 April 2015 [Para 1].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay the overtime meal allowance weekly, every Saturday, rather than twice a month? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company must pay a USD 20 monthly attendance premium to 20 workers, above the USD 10 paid? [Para 4]
3. Whether the company must pay USD 150 a month for transport and accommodation? [Para 6]
4. Whether the company must provide a lunch, or 3,000 riel a day in lieu? [Para 8]
5. Whether the company must give each worker a water bottle, replaced every 6 months? [Para 10]
6. Whether overtime pay must include seniority pay, incentive pay and position allowance? [Para 12]
7. Whether the company must pay seniority indemnity to workers in a single instalment? [Para 14]
8. Whether the company must provide three sets of uniforms a year? [Para 16]
9. Whether the company must accept sick leave of three days or less without an outside medical certificate, preserving wages and benefits? [Para 18]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** On the merits, the nine demands as filed [Para 2][Para 4][Para 6][Para 8][Para 10][Para 12][Para 14][Para 16][Para 18]. Procedurally, that the employer-list arbitrator should withdraw because he had decided prior cases with bias and was never neutral, always dissenting against workers' interests, and because he had used improper conduct and language toward the union and workers' representatives [Para 31][Para 32][Para 72].
**Employer (respondent):** That it could not grant the demands and would keep its existing practice: overtime meal money twice a month, a USD 10 premium, USD 10 transport and accommodation, no meal, annual bottle replacement, existing overtime calculation, no lump-sum seniority payment, no uniforms, and its current sick-leave rule [Para 3][Para 5][Para 7][Para 9][Para 11][Para 13][Para 15][Para 17][Para 19]. On the recusal request it made no separate submission recorded in the award.

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** withdrawn — the panel treated the claim as abandoned and terminated the arbitration proceedings, without deciding any of the nine demands [Para 89][Para 91][Para 93][Para 94].
The threshold question the panel set itself was whether to continue once the claimant had stopped taking part [Para 52]. It applied Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY of 21 April 2004, which allows the panel to proceed or to close the case where a properly invited party fails to appear without valid reason, and Rule 4.7 of the Council's procedural rules, which requires that the party have been properly notified of date, time and place [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54]. Reading those provisions as in Cases No. 16/07, 27/07, 138/08, 11/11, 131/12, 108/13 and 332/14, the panel distilled three cumulative conditions: proper invitation, non-appearance, and no valid reason [Para 55].
On condition 1 it found invitation letters No. 384 and No. 385 of 23 March 2015, received and signed for by a federation official on 25 March 2015 at 15:35, giving clear notice of date, place and contact details [Para 49][Para 50][Para 57][Para 58][Para 59][Para 61][Para 62]. On condition 2 it held that "appearing before the panel" in Article 21 means both attending and taking part through to the end of the four stages — identification and conflict explanation, procedure and issues, conciliation if agreed, and hearing — and the union attended only the first and then withdrew [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65][Para 66][Para 67][Para 68].
On condition 3, the union's stated reason was the employer arbitrator's refusal to step down [Para 69][Para 81]. Applying Article 15 of Prakas No. 099, which requires recusal where circumstances raise reasonable doubt about bias or independence, the panel found the first ground — that the arbitrator had dissented in past cases — without legal foundation, since Article 37 makes a dissenting opinion the right of each arbitrator and it cannot establish bias [Para 70][Para 74][Para 75][Para 77][Para 78]. The second ground — improper conduct and language — was not specified and was unsupported by evidence, and the union itself conceded it had no evidence [Para 73][Para 76][Para 79][Para 80]. Both grounds were held unrelated to the conflicts of interest Article 15 addresses, so condition 3 was met [Para 80][Para 82][Para 83].
The panel added that a claimant bears the duty to appear and argue its case with reasons and evidence, and having chosen not to, it abandoned its claim; the panel therefore need not continue and may close the case by order [Para 84][Para 85][Para 87][Para 88][Para 90][Para 92].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claimant union that attends only part of the hearing and then withdraws, for reasons that do not meet Article 15 of Prakas No. 099, has failed to appear without valid reason: the Council will treat the claim as abandoned and close the proceedings under Article 21 rather than rule on the demands [Para 63][Para 67][Para 89]. An arbitrator's dissent in earlier cases is an exercise of a statutory right and is not, by itself, a ground of bias justifying recusal [Para 78].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's statement that the respondent's duty to answer generally arises only after the claimant has discharged its own duty of proof [Para 86].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The hearing date at paragraph 24 reads "៣១ ខែ មីនា ឆ្នាំ ២០១៤" (31 March 2014) where every other reference, including the invitation letters and paragraph 29, gives 31 March 2015 [Para 24][Para 29][Para 57]; the 2015 date is used above. Paragraph 28 reads "១៩ ខែមីនា ឆ្នាំ ២០១៩ ២០១៥", printing two years. Demand 4 prints its figure as "៤ ៣០០០ រៀល", an unresolved digit and amount [Para 8]. Paragraph 40 gives the conciliation minutes two dates in one line ("២៥ ខែកំប្លែង ឆ្នាំ ២០១៧ ... ថ្ងៃទី២៥ ខែ កក្កដា ឆ្នាំ ២០១៥") and corrupts the company name to "វាយ វិច ហ្គាមេន". Paragraph 64 prints the hearing time as "០៨ និង ៣០០០ នាទី". Paragraphs 51, 61 and 71 each repeat their own opening clause, and a stray "ភាគី ១៩" appears at paragraph 67. Paragraph 18 carries an unexplained Latin fragment "Jung Champ". The award refers to Prakas No. 099 as being of the "អាជ្ញាធរ ជាតិ កណ្តាល" (National Arbitration Council) at paragraphs 70, 74 and 87, where elsewhere it is the Arbitration Council. The company name varies between "វ៉ាយ ថិច ហ្គាមេន", "វ៉ាយ ថិច ហ្គាមិន" and "វ៉ាយ ថិច ហ្គា មិន" [Para 1][Para 27][Para 93]. Paragraph 75 attributes to "មូលហេតុ ទី ០១" a statement about "the second ground", inverting the reference.
