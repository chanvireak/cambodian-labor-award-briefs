# Award No. 179/15 — Aekhin Tech Piti E Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 22 July 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Tuon Si Phan for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139252

## 1. Facts
Aekhin Tech Piti E Co., Ltd. occupies Building G of the Vattanac Industrial Park, Steung Meanchey, Phnom Penh, and was represented before the Council by a GMAC officer. [Para 1] On 10 July 2015 the Labour Disputes Department received twelve demands from the workers through the Movement of Workers' Union Federation, covering three months' maternity pay, conversion of indefinite contracts into fixed-duration contracts, freedom from reprimand and from card-scanning when using the toilet, voluntary overtime, no deduction of wages during certified sick leave, appropriate language from supervisors, no wage deduction during a strike, a USD 7 travel allowance increase, 2,000 riel an hour for overtime meals, no company-sponsored union, and no discrimination for union membership. [Para 2][Para 3][Para 4][Para 5][Para 6][Para 7][Para 8][Para 9][Para 10][Para 11][Para 12][Para 13][Para 16]
Conciliation on 10 July 2015 settled nothing and all twelve points were referred on 13 July 2015. [Para 17][Para 18] The workers had struck since 9 July 2015, and on 15 July 2015 the Council issued Order No. 028/15 suspending the strike and requiring a return to work from the morning of 16 July 2015. [Para 20][Para 21] The panel was told on 20 July 2015 that the strike was still on. [Para 22] No hearing took place, so no finding of fact was made on any demand. [Para 30]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the arbitration must continue, or be terminated, when the workers keep striking after the Council has ordered the strike suspended? [Para 31]

The twelve substantive demands in the non-conciliation report were never examined and no order was made on them. [Para 30][Para 2]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the federation sought twelve improvements to working conditions, including the maternity payment and the removal of toilet-related penalties and scanning, but filed no document and called no witness before the panel. [Para 2][Para 4][Para 7][Para 25]

**Employer (respondent):** the company wrote on 13 July 2015 asking for an order suspending the strike that had begun on 9 July 2015, and transferred authority to its representative to appear. [Para 20][Para 24]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel terminated the arbitration in case No. 179/15 without a hearing, leaving all twelve demands undecided and making no award on the merits. [Para 45][Para 46]

The panel put the only question as whether to continue, and took as its starting point Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, under which the parties must suspend every dispute measure during the panel's work — strike or lockout under Article 318 of the Labour Law, or any other step capable of aggravating the dispute — and must attend every meeting convened. [Para 31][Para 32] It read that as an operative duty and recorded that its own earlier awards had refused to proceed whenever workers were striking or an employer had locked out. [Para 33]

It then reviewed the practice it was following: the notice of 29 January 2004 in Case No. 04/04 (IMS), stating that a party striking during proceedings has no right to do so, that the Council cannot resolve the dispute or issue an award while the strike lasts, and that it may take the case up again once all the unions stop striking; the second order of 22 April 2004 in Case No. 18/04 (hotels), requiring the strike and any disruption on hotel premises to stop from 23 April 2004 and reserving the right to close proceedings on breach; the second order of 24 December 2005 in Case No. 77/05 (Franko Nid Thing), which said the Council would not process a dispute while the strike continued; and Case No. 110/11 (Simano), where proceedings were terminated for the same reason. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 37][Para 38][Para 39][Para 40]

On the facts, the panel had itself ordered a return to work from 16 July 2015 precisely so that arbitration could continue, and the parties' information showed the strike still running on 20 July 2015. [Para 42][Para 43] It held that it could not sit to resolve the dispute while that order was disobeyed, and terminated the proceedings under Article 20. [Para 44][Para 45]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A party that keeps a strike, or a lockout, running after the Council has ordered it suspended under Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 deprives the Council of the conditions in which it can sit, so the Council will terminate the arbitration without ruling on the demands, and will only take the dispute up again once the strike has ended. [Para 32][Para 36][Para 44][Para 45]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that it may reopen the file once the strike stops, and its citation of Cases No. 17/13 and No. 066/14 as consistent authority, sit outside the ground of termination. [Para 36][Para 41]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The company name is printed as អេឃីន ថិច ភីធី អ៉ី អិលធីឌី on the face but អេឃីន ថិច ភីធី អ៊ី អិលធីឌី everywhere else [Para 1][Para 16][Para 21]; no Latin-script form is given, so the heading transliterates the face of the award.
- The panel cites its own suspension order as No. 028/15 of 15 July 2015 in the narrative [Para 21], but the evidence list carries "Order No. 027/15 of 13 July 2015" [Para 29] — that is the order number used in a different company's case, and the two entries cannot both be right.
- The award records that no hearing was held and no facts were found [Para 30], so the twelve demands appear in full but unexamined; the report is described as covering twelve points in one place and the petition as covering twelve demands, with no reconciliation of how many workers were behind them [Para 16][Para 17][Para 18].
- The operative paragraph cites "ប្រកាស ២០ នៃ ប្រកាស លេខ ០៩៩" — a Prakas within a Prakas — where Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 is meant [Para 45].
