# Award No. 026/15 — Siang Zhan Printing Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 27 February 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samphor Na, chosen by the employer; Sin Kim Sean, chosen by the workers; Ang Eng Thong, chair) · Doc ID 139139

## 1. Facts
Siang Zhan Printing Co Ltd (ស៊ាង ហ្សាន ព្រីន ធីង ខូអិលធីឌី), a printing plant on National Road 4 at Samrong Tong, Kampong Speu employing about 38 workers, occupied premises leased from the Sapeuna factory; when that lessor stopped letting the site the employer moved the plant to Phnom Penh on 20 September 2014, keeping each worker's contract, position, wage, allowances and hours unchanged and supplying a company vehicle whose travel time counted as working time. [Para 1][Para 49][Para 53][Para 54][Para 55] The claimants refused to work at the new site, and on 20 September 2014 the employer terminated their contracts and paid termination benefits. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 113] The union complained that the statutory severance for fixed-duration contracts had not been paid and that the terminations were unlawful; conciliation failed and one non-conciliated issue reached the Secretariat on 26 January 2015. [Para 8][Para 12][Para 59]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay severance of 5% of wages earned under the final fixed-duration contract to the 20 claimants, and moral damages (សោហ៊ុយជម្ងឺចិត្ត) to those whose contracts were of undetermined duration? [Para 53][Para 79]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** They signed to work for a company situated in Kampong Speu, not Phnom Penh, so they could not lawfully be required to move; the employer dissolved the contracts contrary to legal procedure and without valid reason, which entitles them under Articles 120, 91, 73 and 77 of the Labour Law to the 5% severance and to moral damages. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 66]
**Employer (respondent):** It paid the 5% severance every six months and had already settled the severance for the last contract on 10 September 2014, and undertook to file the supporting documents; the move was forced by the lessor ending the lease, no working condition was worsened, and transport to and from the new site was provided and paid as working time. [Para 67][Para 69][Para 75][Para 101][Para 107]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer is ordered to pay termination severance of at least 5% of the wages earned during each claimant's last fixed-duration contract; the claim for moral damages for the undetermined-duration workers is dismissed. [Para 119][Para 120]
On the severance, the panel applied Article 73(6) of the Labour Law, which requires severance equal to wage and contract duration and, where the collective agreement is silent, at least 5% of the wages earned during the contract. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 63][Para 64] Article 118 places the burden of proving that wages were paid on the employer, and the panel followed the interpretation in an earlier award (No. 361/15), where an employer that produced no evidence of a USD 100 payment was ordered to repay it, and held itself bound by the same approach. [Para 84][Para 85][Para 86][Para 87] The employer promised documents but filed none by the deadline, so the panel found no proof of the 5% severance for the final fixed-duration contracts and ordered payment of the statutory floor. [Para 88][Para 89][Para 90][Para 91][Para 92] It also held that four claimants whose contracts were not attached to the file did not thereby lose their rights. [Para 78]
On moral damages, Article 91 allows such a claim only where one party terminates without a valid reason. [Para 97][Para 115] Article 74 permits termination of an undetermined-duration contract on written notice, and Article 2 reserves to the employer the management and direction of the enterprise. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 103][Para 105] Following earlier awards, the panel held that the right to transfer workers is lawful where four conditions hold: no wage cut, no move to a distant place, no change of shift, and no change away from the worker's skill. [Para 106] The new site was far from the old, but the employer supplied transport and counted travel time as working time, so none of the four conditions was breached. [Para 108][Para 109][Para 110] The workers' refusal was therefore their own, the employer had a valid reason to terminate, and no moral damages were owed. [Para 111][Para 116][Para 117][Para 118]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that relocates its plant for a genuine operational reason, preserving contract, wage, position, hours and skill and providing transport counted as working time, exercises its lawful managerial authority; workers who then refuse to attend the new site break the relationship themselves, so the termination is for a valid reason and no moral damages arise under Article 91. Where the employer asserts it paid severance, Article 118 puts the burden of proof on it, and an unproved assertion leaves the statutory 5% floor payable. [Para 92][Para 109][Para 116][Para 118]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remarks that four claimants lacking attached contracts forfeit nothing, and that moral damages are distinct from notice compensation and from dismissal damages, were not needed to decide either limb. [Para 78][Para 98]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The size of the workforce is printed two ways: about 38 workers in the recital of the non-conciliation report, but 20 claimants in the case itself. [Para 2][Para 49][Para 52] Several dates are corrupted or contradictory: the panel is said to have been constituted on "28 January 2023 2015" [Para 13]; the hearing invitation is dated 05 February 2014 [Para 14] although the hearing was 05 February 2015 [Para 7]; and the non-conciliation report is dated 22 January 2015 in the recitals but 22 February 2015 in the exhibit list [Para 5][Para 12][Para 44]. The company name appears as ស៊ាង ហ្សាន, ស៊ាន ហ្សាន and even "ស៊ាង John ហ្សាន"; the Latin form used here is a transliteration. [Para 48][Para 49] The itemised termination payments are printed with "0]" placeholders where figures stood, so no amount survives. [Para 57][Para 58] The cited precedent's party name is OCR-damaged ("ខេន ស្ព័ត ស៊ូ"), so only its number is given. [Para 85] The signature block is garbled and the final page is OCR noise rather than text. [Para 121]
