# Award No. 177/15 — Fes Sen Cafe Yuri (Arbitration Council, 18 August 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Tuon Si Phan for the worker party, Run Saray chair) · Doc ID 139250

## 1. Facts
Fes Sen Cafe Yuri, a massage-service company in Siem Reap employing about 34 staff, notified all workers on 30 May 2015 that it would close on 14 June 2015, having taken a shareholding with Chou Massage to open a new company, Yuri Chou Massage, on the same premises. [Para 40][Para 47][Para 48] The employer asked the old staff to move to the new company on a wage reduced first by USD 10 and then by USD 2 a month; the workers refused, stopped work on 30 May 2015, and the employer terminated all of them that day. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51][Para 52] Thirty-two workers transferred their rights to the Tourism and Service Workers' Federation of Cambodia, which brought the claim; four points went to conciliation, failed, and reached the Council on 10 July 2015. [Para 41][Para 16][Para 17] At the hearing point 4 was settled and points 2 and 3 were withdrawn, leaving point 1. [Para 21][Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the 32 workers and pay them back wages and other benefits from the date of termination to the date of reinstatement? [Para 66]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the termination was improper because the employer's real purpose was to cut the wages of old staff down to the level of new staff, and any worker who did not accept would have to leave; a change of shareholder does not entitle the employer to reduce wages, which must stay as before. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 68]

**Employer (respondent):** there was no intention to dismiss anyone; workers were given 15 days' notice of the change of employer, told they would have more rest days and that the Labour Law would be fully applied, and asked only for a USD 2 a month reduction; those who refused were paid severance calculated to 14 June 2015 and may reapply to the new company afterwards. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim for reinstatement with back wages was dismissed, and the employer was ordered to pay five heads of termination benefit to the claimant workers: indemnity for dismissal, damages (សោហ៊ុយជម្ងឺចិត្ត), payment in lieu of notice, unused annual leave, and final wages where unpaid. [Para 126][Para 127][Para 132]

The panel first fixed the claimant group: 32 workers had signed a transfer of rights to the federation, the employer promised to name the 9 who had already started at the new company but never did, and no withdrawal letters were produced, so the 32 were treated as the claimants. [Para 60][Para 63][Para 64][Para 65] It then held that the shareholding with Chou Massage, including use of the old company's building to create Yuri Chou Massage, was a "transfer of capital" and so a change in the legal status of the employer within Article 87 of the Labour Law, which keeps existing employment contracts alive between the new employer and the old enterprise's workers. [Para 74][Para 75] The new employer therefore took over both the rights and the obligations of the old one and had to maintain the existing conditions; it could not vary them without the workers' consent. [Para 76][Para 77] Conditioning the move on a USD 2 reduction was thus improper, because it failed to preserve the old contracts and cut into the workers' entitlements. [Para 78]

On the termination, the panel applied Article 74: an indefinite-duration contract may be ended by either side, but no dismissal may occur without a valid reason relating to the worker's aptitude or conduct, or to the operational needs of the enterprise. [Para 83][Para 84] The employer dismissed the 32 for refusing the new wage condition, which was itself contrary to Article 87, so there was no valid reason and the dismissal was unlawful. [Para 87][Para 88] The panel nevertheless read Article 87 as leaving the employer a choice — take the old staff into the new company on unchanged conditions, or terminate with compensation under the Labour Law — and, the relationship having already been dissolved and severance arranged to 14 June 2015, it declined to order reinstatement and confined the remedy to money. [Para 79][Para 80]

It then quantified the five heads: dismissal indemnity under Article 89, at 7 days' wages and allowances for 6 to 12 months' service and 15 days per year beyond that, capped at six months, with a fraction of six months or more counted as a full year, and nothing payable below six months' service; damages under Article 91, equal to the dismissal indemnity for workers with six months or more, and requiring proof of loss below that; payment in lieu of notice under Articles 75 and 77 according to each worker's length of service, from 7 days to 3 months; unused annual leave under Article 166 at 1.5 working days per month; and final wages within 48 hours under Article 116. [Para 91][Para 92][Para 95][Para 108][Para 110][Para 111][Para 121][Para 123][Para 125] Daily average wage was to be computed on the average of the last 12 months, following Award No. 51/04 (Samhan). [Para 97][Para 98][Para 103]

The award was made binding by the parties' agreement of 23 July 2015, not by operation of the ordinary non-binding default. [Para 23][Para 139]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A shareholding, capital injection or use of the old premises to create a new company is a change in the legal status of the employer under Article 87, so the new employer inherits the existing contracts and may not condition employment on a wage reduction the workers did not accept; a dismissal resting on that refusal lacks the valid reason Article 74 requires. Where the relationship has already been dissolved and statutory severance provided, the panel will refuse reinstatement and award the Article 89, 91, 75/77, 166 and 116 entitlements instead. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 78][Para 88][Para 80]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the workers may reapply to the new company after receiving severance, and its calculation example for a worker with 13 months' service, are illustrative and were not necessary to the orders. [Para 81][Para 98]
