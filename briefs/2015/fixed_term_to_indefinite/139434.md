# Award No. 246/15 — Noble Apparel Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 09 October 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Liv Sovanna, chair Pen Bun Chhia) · Doc ID 139434

## 1. Facts
Noble Apparel (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ណូបែល អឹភែរឹល), a garment factory at Damnak Thom 2, Stueng Mean Chey, Mean Chey district, Phnom Penh, employed 109 workers. [Para 51] On 20 August 2015 it notified the labour inspectorate that it would suspend all contracts for two months, 01 September to 31 October 2015, for want of orders after a strike on 31 July 2015; no authorisation was ever granted. [Para 72] [Para 114] On 31 August 2015 the worker representatives agreed in writing to the two months at 50 per cent of base wage. [Para 77] Separately, 16 workers had converted their indefinite-term contracts into six-month fixed-term contracts on 01 March 2014 against a lump sum of US$60 per year of service and a resignation letter, and their contracts expired on 31 August 2015 without renewal. [Para 125] [Para 167]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (The panel's issues 1 and 2, consolidated) Whether the employer must pay 100 per cent of wages during the two-month suspension of 01 September to 31 October 2015, or 50 per cent because the workers agreed to it? [Para 103]
2. (The panel's issue 3) Whether the employer must pay dismissal compensation, moral damages and pay in lieu of notice to the 16 workers whose fixed-term contracts it did not renew? [Para 125]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that the suspension had no valid authorisation and so was unlawful; that if the company holds a ministry letter permitting it, 50 per cent is acceptable, but otherwise full wages are owed; and that the 16 had worked more than two years, that the conversion of their contracts was not a genuine free choice, and that the payout fell short of what the Labour Law requires. [Para 69] [Para 70] [Para 86] [Para 127]
**Employer (respondent):** that it suspended because workers were slow, orders were not met and the last buyer stopped placing orders after seeing the strike of 31 July 2015; that the workers agreed to the suspension and to 50 per cent wages on 31 August 2015 and it asks to apply that agreement; and that the 16 held six-month fixed-term contracts which expired normally, with final wages, 5 per cent end-of-contract indemnity and unused annual leave already paid. [Para 74] [Para 87] [Para 119]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay 100 per cent of wages for 01 September to 31 October 2015, and its other defences on that issue failed; the claims for dismissal compensation, moral damages and notice pay were refused, but the employer was ordered to pay the 5 per cent end-of-contract indemnity, unused annual leave and final wages if still outstanding. [Para 199] [Para 200] [Para 201]

On issue 1, Article 72(1) removes the employer's duty to pay wages during a suspension, but only where the suspension is itself lawful. Article 71(11) permits suspension for serious material or economic difficulty for no more than two months, under the supervision of the labour inspector; following Cases 58/07 and 08/07, that supervision means prior authorisation. The company notified the inspectorate on 20 August 2015 but had received no authorisation by the hearing of 22 September 2015, so the suspension was unlawful and Article 72(1) did not apply. [Para 104] [Para 107] [Para 109] [Para 110] [Para 117] [Para 118]

The 31 August agreement to accept 50 per cent could not save it: Article 13 makes the Labour Law's provisions public order, and any rule created by unilateral decision, contract or agreement that does not respect them is void by operation of law. The workers' consent to a rate below the statutory entitlement had no legal effect. [Para 120] [Para 121] [Para 123] [Para 124]

On issue 2, the panel upheld the conversion. Under Articles 65 and 664 of the Civil Code, and Articles 311, 336 and 337, a contract is formed by matching offer and acceptance, and the resignation letters did not prove a real intention to leave because the workers carried on in the same jobs and simply signed new contracts on 01 March 2014; the change of contract type and the lump sum were the free agreement of both sides. [Para 128] [Para 140] [Para 148] [Para 151] [Para 159]

The 16 therefore held six-month fixed-term contracts, which expire on their term under Article 73(1); the last term ended on 31 August 2015 and non-renewal for lack of work was lawful, with no notice owed because the term did not exceed six months. Articles 75, 77, 89 and 91 govern indefinite-term contracts ended without valid reason, so dismissal compensation, moral damages and notice pay did not arise. What remained were the fixed-term entitlements: the 5 per cent end-of-contract indemnity under Article 73(6), unused annual leave under Article 166, and final wages within 48 hours under Article 116. [Para 161] [Para 169] [Para 170] [Para 189] [Para 190]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of contracts under Article 71(11) is lawful only if the labour inspector has authorised it beforehand; absent that authorisation the employer's duty to pay wages is not displaced by Article 72(1), and the employer owes full wages for the whole period. An agreement by which workers accept a lower rate during such a suspension is void under Article 13, because the Labour Law's wage guarantees are public order and cannot be bargained away. By contrast, a freely agreed conversion from an indefinite-term to a fixed-term contract is effective, and when that term expires the employer owes only the fixed-term entitlements — the 5 per cent indemnity, unused annual leave and final wages — not dismissal compensation. [Para 118] [Para 123] [Para 159] [Para 189]

**Obiter dicta:** that a resignation letter signed as part of a contract conversion does not establish an intention to resign where the worker continues in the same employment, and that the panel may reopen the evidence timetable to let a party answer documents filed on the last day, but is not obliged to where the party declines the extension offered. [Para 148] [Para 101]
