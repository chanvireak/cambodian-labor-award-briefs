# Award No. 213/15 — Ji International Five Star (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 11 September 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Kong Phalda chair) · Doc ID 139415

## 1. Facts
Five Star International Fertilizer (Cambodia) Co., Ltd produces fertilizer at Prek Treng, Kien Svay, Kandal, and employed about 245 workers. [Para 68][Para 69] On 2 June 2015 the employer notified 14 workers of the soil-drying (ហាល ដី) section, among them the three local union leaders, that their contracts would end on 1 July 2015 without renewal. [Para 92] The three had started on 1 November 2012 on probation, then signed two successive 12-month fixed-term contracts to 30 June 2015. [Para 88][Para 90] Conciliation on 16 demands failed; demand 10 was withdrawn and demands 1, 2 and 14 merged, leaving 13 issues. [Para 31][Para 32]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the three union leaders and pay back wages? [Para 82]
2. Whether the employer must pay holiday pay, Sunday overtime and the 5 per cent termination allowance? [Para 108]
3. Whether the employer must pay USD 15 a month for travel and accommodation? [Para 112]
4. Whether the employer must station a permanent nurse at the factory? [Para 115]
5. Whether the employer must provide hygienic toilets? [Para 118]
6. Whether the employer must organise an election of worker delegates? [Para 120]
7. Whether the employer must respect the voluntary principle in overtime? [Para 122]
8. Whether the employer must raise wages to USD 128 a month? [Para 124]
9. Whether the employer must provide a canteen and orderly motorbike parking? [Para 127]
10. Whether the employer must stop using workers to lift overweight loads? [Para 130]
11. Whether the employer must supply sufficient safety and health equipment? [Para 132]
12. Whether the employer must grant special leave to workers in hardship? [Para 134]
13. Whether the employer must stop 12-hour night shifts and pay overtime for them? [Para 136]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the three held open-ended contracts because their fixed-term contracts were renewed twice, the employer ended them irregularly, and as elected union leaders they were protected under Prakas No. 305 and were singled out for union activity. [Para 83][Para 85]
**Employer (respondent):** it cannot take the three back; their fixed-term contracts had run out so this was non-renewal, not dismissal, they had been warned orally and in writing for insubordination, and the soil-drying section has no work in the rainy season. [Para 86][Para 95][Para 96]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement and back wages were refused, as were the 5 per cent allowance, the USD 15 payment and the USD 128 wage; the employer must pay compensation in lieu of notice (Articles 75 and 77), dismissal indemnity (Article 89), unused annual leave (Articles 166 and 167) and final unpaid wages. [Para 307][Para 308][Para 312]

Article 268 gives a professional organisation the rights the Labour Law recognises only once its statutes and the list of its officers are deposited with the Ministry; the local union had not registered, so its leaders had no standing to represent members, only the three who appeared continued the claim, and nine of the 14 authorisation letters were contradicted by signatories denying their thumbprints. [Para 139][Para 159][Para 163]

Article 67(2) caps a fixed-term contract and its renewals at two years in total and Article 73(5) converts a contract exceeding that limit into an open-ended one; probation plus two 12-month renewals passed two years, so the three were open-ended employees, following Award No. 10/03 (Zack Synthesis). [Para 167][Para 180][Para 181]

Article 293 and Article 3 of Prakas No. 305 protect candidates for 45 days around an election and elected leaders for at least 45 days after it, extended for a reasonable period such as until registration is sought; the union never applied and had no receipt to show for it, so protection had lapsed. [Para 183][Para 190][Para 203]

Articles 12 and 279 forbid using union membership or activity as a ground for discipline or termination, but the accusing party bears the burden of proof and the workers offered none. [Para 219][Para 223][Para 229]

Under Article 74 an open-ended contract may be terminated only for a reason concerning the worker's aptitude or conduct or the necessity of the enterprise's operation; no written warnings were produced, so fault was unproven, but the seasonal absence of soil-drying work was a necessity the panel accepted. [Para 237][Para 241] The termination was lawful in substance yet wrong for the contract type, which carries notice pay, the Article 89 indemnity, accrued leave and final wages, but no moral damages under Article 91. [Para 243][Para 261][Para 277]

Issues 5 to 7 and 9 to 13 were not decided because the employer conceded the demand or showed it was already met. [Para 119][Para 137] The nurse claim failed for want of any claimant still employed, though Article 242 and Article 3 of Prakas No. 330 require an enterprise of 50 or more workers to keep a permanent infirmary and health staff. [Para 300][Para 303]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive renewals carrying a fixed-term contract past two years convert it into an open-ended contract under Articles 67(2) and 73(5); ending it at the expiry of the last fixed term is a unilateral termination of an open-ended contract, requiring valid notice and attracting the Article 89 indemnity, accrued leave and final wages even where operational necessity is genuine. [Para 173][Para 253][Para 261]

Elected union leaders keep Prakas No. 305 protection for at least 45 days after the election and only while the union is genuinely moving towards registration; a union that files nothing for close to a year cannot complain that its leaders were dismissed outside the protected window. [Para 201][Para 203]

**Obiter dicta:** the reading of Article 242 and Prakas No. 330 on permanent infirmaries, and the use of ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982 as interpretive context for Article 67 although it is not binding, were not needed for the orders made. [Para 175][Para 302]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The employer carries two names: the face reads ជី អន្តរជាតិ ផ្កាយ ប្រាំ កម្ពុជា [Para 1], while the findings define it as ហ្វាយ ស្តារ អ៊ិនធើណេសិនណល ហ្វើ ថី ឡាយ ហ្សឹ (ខេមបូឌា), rendered here as Five Star International Fertilizer (Cambodia) Co., Ltd [Para 68].
- The union election is dated 27 July 2015 at [Para 70] but 27 July 2014 at [Para 200]. A 1 July 2015 dismissal preceded a 2015 election, so the finding that it came "nearly one year after the election" [Para 203] only holds on 2014.
- The Article 74 finding [Para 241] holds there was no valid reason as to fault but a valid reason of necessity, yet [Para 260] records the dismissal as "without valid reason"; the operative order follows the first reading.
- The operative order prints the rejected allowance as 55 per cent, "៥៥ ភាគរយ" [Para 310], against 5 per cent in the demand and reasons [Para 108].
- OCR losses and stray digits: "មាត្រា ២៩៣ ក ថា ខណ្ឌ ទី១" [Para 183], a bare "៤០" mid-sentence [Para 162], an evidence list whose numbering jumps from 7 to 28 [Para 43], and a panel citation left as Latin gibberish [Para 196].
- The chair appears as គង់ ផល្ទា [Para 1] and គង់ ផល្លៈ [Para 314]; neither party arbitrator signed the copy.
