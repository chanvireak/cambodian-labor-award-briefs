# Award No. 080/15 — D N L Apparel Met (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 10 April 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Kong Phalla chair) · Doc ID 139172

## 1. Facts
D N L Apparel Met (Cambodia) Co., Ltd., a garment factory in Dangkao district, Phnom Penh, employs about 400 workers; the Khmer Union at the factory was registered on 14 March 2013 and Mr K, its president and head of the cleaning section, was the sole claimant at the hearing. [Para 31][Para 32][Para 33][Para 34] From January 2013 his payslips show a skill allowance and a position allowance that rose, disappeared and reappeared repeatedly — USD 50 and USD 50 in January 2013, nothing in October 2013, USD 15.38 of skill allowance in November 2013, both again at USD 50 in December 2013 and January 2014, neither position nor a reduced skill figure in February to May 2014, and USD 20 of skill allowance with no position allowance in January and February 2015. [Para 45][Para 47][Para 48][Para 50][Para 51][Para 52] The employer filed no documents, sent no representative and did not appear. [Para 21][Para 18][Para 95]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must cease discriminating against the Khmer Union? [Para 35]
2. Whether the employer must notify workers before deducting skill and position allowances, reimburse what it deducted in January and February 2015, and restore those allowances to their 2014 levels? [Para 37][Para 99]
3. Whether the employer must apply overtime on a voluntary basis? [Para 53]
4. Whether the employer must inform or consult union leaders when it holds an election of worker representatives? [Para 55][Para 135]
5. Whether the employer must explain the levying of "phasi" from vendors outside the factory fence? [Para 57][Para 142]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the skill and position allowances were paid from the start of employment and were cut without reason or notice; administration was asked about the differences between the 2014 and 2015 figures and gave no explanation; the union was never told of any worker-representative election; and the claim about the levy was raised only to establish that Mr K did not collect it. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 41][Para 43][Para 44][Para 56][Para 58]
**Employer (respondent):** the employer entered no appearance and filed no evidence, so it put nothing before the panel in answer; it told the secretariat by telephone that it was busy with an export shipment and asked the panel to proceed. [Para 21][Para 95]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to put in place a procedure to notify the worker clearly and in an understandable way whenever the conditions of his skill and position allowances change; the remaining claims were not considered or were refused. [Para 147][Para 148][Para 149][Para 150]

Two preliminary rulings framed the case. First, under Article 3 of Prakas No. 305/01 a union leader's mandate may not exceed two years, and Mr K's had expired by the hearing date, so he could not represent the union's members; the panel therefore had jurisdiction only over his own claim as an individual worker. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70][Para 72] Second, the employer had been properly invited, had signed for the invitation at its premises and had confirmed by telephone that it would not attend, so the panel proceeded in its absence under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099, on the claimant's evidence alone. [Para 89][Para 92][Para 93][Para 96][Para 97][Para 98] The panel also noted that all claims in a Ministry non-conciliation report are presumed to be collective disputes, but that the employer may rebut the presumption, and here it had not. [Para 78][Para 80][Para 84][Para 85]

On issue 2 the panel held that the skill and position allowances form part of wages, following its earlier awards that such monthly allowances are expected and paid as part of the wage package rather than earned month by month. [Para 102][Para 104][Para 105][Para 108] Article 112(a) requires the employer to inform workers clearly, precisely and intelligibly of wage conditions before engagement and at every change, and the panel found the employer had not done so. [Para 99][Para 115][Para 116][Para 117] It ordered the notification procedure. [Para 123] It refused, however, to order reimbursement or restoration: Article 312 confines the panel to the issues in the non-conciliation minute or to matters arising after it as a direct consequence of the dispute, and the allowance changes had occurred before the dispute arose. [Para 126][Para 128][Para 131][Para 132][Para 133][Para 134] It added that Article 28 forbids using wage deductions as a penalty. [Para 119][Para 121][Para 122]

Issue 1 and issue 3 were not considered because both parties agreed at the hearing to apply the settlement reached between the employer and the Khmer Union Federation in case No. 271/13 of 25 December 2013. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 53][Para 54] On issue 4 the panel found no election had yet been held or announced, so the dispute had not arisen and was a dispute about the future, which the panel does not decide. [Para 135][Para 136][Para 137][Para 138][Para 141] On issue 5 it held the levying of money from outside vendors is not a labour dispute within Article 309 or any labour instrument, and declined jurisdiction. [Para 143][Para 144][Para 145][Para 146]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A change to a component of wages — including a skill or position allowance, which is part of wages under Article 103 — requires the employer to inform the worker clearly and intelligibly at the time of the change under Article 112(a), and the panel will order a notification procedure where none exists. [Para 108][Para 116][Para 123] The panel's jurisdiction under Article 312 reaches only the grievances recorded in the non-conciliation minute, or matters arising afterwards as a direct consequence of the dispute, so a wage reduction that began before the dispute cannot be recovered through the panel even where the employer has failed to justify it. [Para 126][Para 133] A union president whose two-year mandate under Prakas No. 305/01 has expired cannot represent the membership. [Para 68][Para 70]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that all items in a Ministry non-conciliation report are presumed to be collective disputes unless the employer rebuts the presumption, and that the Council exists to resolve disputes that have arisen rather than hypothetical ones, are general statements not necessary to the single order made. [Para 78][Para 138]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The employer's representative is recorded simply as "absent" and the list of documents received from the employer as "none", so the respondent's case is reconstructed only from its telephone explanation. [Para 1][Para 21][Para 95]
- The number of conciliated issues is printed as "05 6" at [Para 14] and then consistently as five, which is used here.
- No order is issued on issue 3 (voluntary overtime) even though the panel declined to consider it; the dispositive section runs from issue 2 to issues 4 and 5 only. [Para 148][Para 149][Para 150]
- "Phasi" (ភាស៊ី) is never defined in the award, and the claim about collecting it from outside vendors is left unexplained beyond the denial that the claimant collected it. [Para 57][Para 142]
- The claimant's name and the names of all workers are redacted here and appear in Khmer only; the company's Latin name is not printed anywhere in the award, and the rendering above is a transliteration of "ឌី អែន អិល អាល់ធី មេត (ខេមបូឌា) ខូ អិលធីឌី". [Para 1]
- The chair's signature line is corrupted ("20127") though Kong Phalla is named as chair on the face of the award. [Para 152][Para 1]
- Cited prior awards survive only as fragments ("023/14 Vou Khmer", "032/14 Wai Sing Plastic"), so they cannot be identified from this text. [Para 62]
