# Award No. 036/15 — Yakjin (Cambodia) Inc. (Arbitration Council, 26 February 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun, chosen by the employer; Tuon Siphann, chosen by the workers; Pen Bunchhea, chair) · Doc ID 139140

## 1. Facts
Yakjin (Cambodia) Inc. is a textile manufacturer in Por Sen Chey District, Phnom Penh, employing approximately 2,880 workers at the time of the award. [Para 58][Para 57] Three local unions of the Cambodian Labour Union Federation (CLUF) and one local union of the Voice of Khmer Youth Trade Union Federation (VKYUF) held registration from the Ministry of Labour and were the claimants; eight union leaders and members attended. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60][Para 61] Both parties accepted that the enterprise customarily offered two kinds of employment contract — fixed-duration (FDC) and undetermined-duration (UDC). [Para 62] The disputed act is the employer's refusal of a demand, framed by 1,500 workers, that it terminate their contracts, pay termination compensation and then re-offer fixed-duration contracts. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 31]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to terminate the workers' undetermined-duration contracts, pay termination compensation under Articles 75, 77, 91, 116, 166 and 167 of the Labour Law, and then offer them fixed-duration contracts? [Para 29][Para 68]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Those on UDCs have many years of seniority, so termination would produce substantial compensation, whereas each expiring FDC yields only 5% of wages as severance; a nearby enterprise, Marklin, had already terminated its UDCs and rehired on FDCs, and the employer's own periodic surveys of desired benefits showed it was contemplating the same step. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65]
**Employer (respondent):** It will not terminate the contracts because the enterprise's productivity needs the workers, and it cannot meet the demand for five packages of termination compensation; such a termination would confuse the ILO and buyers into thinking the employer had dismissed UDC workers, though it would consider the request on a sympathy basis. [Para 31][Para 32][Para 65][Para 66]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the demand that the employer terminate the UDCs, pay termination compensation and then offer FDCs. [Para 76]
The panel decided the case on a single ground of contract law rather than on the compensation articles invoked. [Para 69] Article 311 of the Cambodian Civil Code provides that a contract is the matching of intentions of two or more parties to create, change or extinguish an obligation, and Article 65 paragraph 1 of the Labour Law provides that an employment contract establishes the working relationship and is subject to the common law, drawn up to suit the parties' needs. [Para 69] From those two provisions the panel derived that the creation, change or extinction of a contract must come from the willingness of the contractual parties, that only the parties may revoke the obligations arising from the employment contract, and that one party cannot force the other to revoke them. [Para 70][Para 71] It followed its earlier awards — 34/13 Papillion Textile (Cambodia) Co., Ltd., 138/13 Manhattan Textile and Garment Corp Ltd., 039/14 E Garment (Issue 1) and 308/14 Huey Chuen (Issue 3) — on the same point. [Para 72] On the facts, the employer had neither revoked the contracts nor shown any willingness to terminate them, so the workers could not compel it to do so; the panel held the demand lacked logical and legal reasoning and that the employer was under no obligation to grant it. [Para 74][Para 75][Para 76]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that an employer terminate existing undetermined-duration contracts, pay termination compensation and rehire the same workers on fixed-duration contracts cannot be granted, because the extinction of a contract requires the matching intention of both parties and one party cannot compel the other to revoke the obligations it has assumed. [Para 69][Para 71][Para 75]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observations that the employer had conducted surveys of workers' preferred benefits, and that a comparable employer had rehired its workforce on FDCs, explain the workers' motive but were not treated as creating any obligation. [Para 65]
