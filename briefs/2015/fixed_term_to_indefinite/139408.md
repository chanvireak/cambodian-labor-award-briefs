# Award No. 209/15 — CMCW (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 21 September 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed and dissenting; An Nan, worker-appointed; Kong Phallack, chair) · Doc ID 139408 · unofficial English translation of the Khmer original

## 1. Facts
CMCW (Cambodia) Co., Ltd manufactures garments with about 358 workers. [Para 33][Para 36] The FUKDW was authorised in writing by 189 workers to act for them, out of a workforce that had fallen from 1,000 in four buildings to 300 in one. [Para 24][Para 38][Para 39] Since July 2014 work has been short: the employer still offers one-year fixed-duration contracts, renewed repeatedly, and pays 5% severance at each natural end while the workers keep working. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 98] 172 workers have more than two years' continuous service. [Para 41][Para 100]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must convert into undetermined-duration contracts the fixed-duration contracts of 172 workers who have worked continuously for more than two years? [Para 97][Para 121]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the employer committed incitements under Article 90 of the Labour Law — by giving insufficient work and cutting income, by pushing workers to resign, and by making workers redundant and relocating them to different skills — so that the 172 serving and 7 resigned workers are owed wages and compensation under Articles 75, 77, 89, 91, 116, 166 and 167, calculated per Article 110? [Para 60][Para 123]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Repeated one-year renewals past two years must be redefined as undetermined-duration contracts, as Awards 10/03 (Jacqsintex) and 29/15 (British American Tobacco) hold; the 2010 confirmation letter is undated, signed by people whose delegate mandate had expired, and void as against public order under Article 13. [Para 42][Para 51][Para 52]
**Employer (respondent):** Article 67(2) and 73(5) permit repeated fixed-duration contracts so long as no single term exceeds two years; a 2010 agreement with workers' representatives, advised by the ILO, accepted that practice in exchange for 5% severance at each contract end, which it has paid; its "if anyone wishes to resign" statement was informational, not pressure. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 80]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer is ordered to offer undetermined-duration contracts to workers who have continuously worked over two years; the wage and compensation claims of the 172 serving and 7 resigned workers are rejected; no sum in riel or USD is awarded [Para 182]

On issue 2 the panel applied Article 67(2), which caps a fixed-duration contract and its renewals at two years and makes any violation an undetermined-duration contract, and Article 73(5), which redefines a contract as undetermined where renewal pushes the total past that limit and no prior notice was given. [Para 114][Para 115][Para 118] Following Award 10/03 (Jacqsintex) and ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982, clause 3, the panel read the two-year limit as covering the combined duration, not each renewal. [Para 115][Para 119][Para 120] The employer did not contest the 172 identity cards showing over two years' service. [Para 100][Para 102] Its 2010 confirmation letter was rejected: it bears no date, so the panel could not fix when it was made, and its nine signatories were elected only in the third-mandate delegates election of 19 March 2011 — a year after the letter's claimed date — with no document proving any 2010 mandate. [Para 107][Para 108][Para 111][Para 113]

On issue 3 the panel applied Article 90, under which indemnity is owed where the employer's incitements pushed the worker to end the contract himself, following Award 84/11 (Supertex). [Para 124] On insufficient work it found the employer still paid full wages, attendance bonus and transport and accommodation allowance to workers punching in twice daily, and 50% to others; under Article 664 of the Civil Code there is no obligation to work, and so no wage obligation, where the employer has no work, but here the employer had performed. [Para 129][Para 131][Para 134][Para 135][Para 136] On pressure to resign it found the statement conditional ("if anyone wishes to resign") and unsupported by any coercive condition. [Para 142][Para 143][Para 145][Para 146] On redundancy and relocation it applied Article 2's management right, as read in Awards 08/09 (Global Apparel), 02/10 (Siem Reap Airways) and 218/12 (New Archid): moving workers between teams inside the same section, here team 7 to team 12, both sewing, is lawful; moving them to a different section would not be. [Para 167][Para 169][Para 170][Para 172][Para 173] It distinguished Supertex, where the employer had suspended contracts without authorisation; here the employer paid in full and held orders to 25 May 2016. [Para 179][Para 180]

Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented on issue 2, holding that where 5% severance is paid at every renewal under Article 73(6), Article 73(5) is not triggered and the contracts remain fixed-duration even past two years. [Para 185][Para 186]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive fixed-duration renewals are measured cumulatively: once the combined duration of an initial contract and its renewals passes two years, the contract is redefined by operation of law as one of undetermined duration, and the panel will order the employer to offer such a contract. [Para 118][Para 120][Para 182] An agreement said to authorise that practice must be proved to have been made by representatives holding a live mandate at its date; an undated letter whose signatories were elected later cannot qualify. [Para 108][Para 113]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the Labour Law favours undetermined-duration contracts because they secure work safety for both sides and long-term work drives commitment — reasoning broader than the conversion order needed. [Para 116][Para 117]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
This is an unofficial English translation and the evidence and exhibits were omitted from it, so no document can be checked against the original. [Para 32][Para 33] A systematic OCR fault drops letters throughout ("themployer", "thearing", "FDCsurpasses"), which sometimes reverses meaning. [Para 46][Para 78] The workforce is given as 358 in the findings but the demands speak of 1,000 workers in four buildings reduced to 300 in one, and the union's authorisation is 182 plus 7, i.e. 189. [Para 24][Para 36][Para 38] The claimed articles are printed as "1166" in the demands and "116" in the decision. [Para 24][Para 60] The seven resigned workers are described in one passage as "dismissed". [Para 121] Several passages are duplicated or garbled, including the whole of the panel's conclusion and the list of nine signatories, which is numbered "( 18)". [Para 56][Para 181] The employer's address and telephone numbers are truncated, and the arbitrators' names appear as both "Ing Sithy" and "Ing Sothy". [Para 1][Para 182]
