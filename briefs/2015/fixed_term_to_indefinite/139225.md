# Award No. 146/15 — Ethon In Das Steel Enterprise (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 23 July 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, chosen by the employer; Sin Kim Sean, chosen by the workers; Koy Neam, chair) · Doc ID 139225

## 1. Facts
Ethon In Das Steel Enterprise, a trouser factory in the Manhattan Special Economic Zone at Bavet, Svay Rieng, employing about 1,470 workers, ended the employment of Mr K and Ms L in January 2015. [Para 49][Para 57][Para 61] Mr K had worked from 19 April 2011 on successive probationary, six-month and three-month contracts; Ms L from 5 February 2013 on the same pattern, each receiving a 5 per cent end-of-contract indemnity at every expiry. [Para 55][Para 58][Para 60][Para 62] Eight workers were initially claimed; six were taken back at conciliation on 9 June 2015 and only these two remained. [Para 9][Para 10][Para 11] The parties agreed on 2 July 2015 to accept a binding award. [Para 18][Para 242]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the contracts of Mr K and Ms L were fixed-duration or indefinite-duration? [Para 114]
2. Whether their dismissals were connected with anti-union discrimination? [Para 132]
3. Whether Mr K and Ms L enjoyed protected status against dismissal? [Para 148]
4. Whether the employer had a valid reason to dismiss them? [Para 167]
5. Whether the employer must take them back and pay wages from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 195]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Neither worker committed any fault and neither was ever warned in writing, so the dismissals lacked valid reason and were anti-union in motive; both held office in the Movement of Workers Union at the factory and should be reinstated with back wages. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 142]
**Employer (respondent):** Mr K was not renewed for persistent misconduct — lateness, eating and talking during working hours, ignoring his team leader, wandering between workstations and using his phone — after oral counselling produced no change; Ms L left her post on 20 January 2015 without permission and then absented herself on 21 and 22 January without notice, so she was dismissed. [Para 70][Para 72][Para 73][Para 174]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement refused for both; the employer ordered to pay Mr K severance, damages and one month's pay in lieu of notice, and Mr K ordered to repay the 5 per cent indemnities he had received; all claims by Ms L refused. [Para 232][Para 233][Para 234][Para 238]

On jurisdiction the panel first held that the dispute was collective: Article 302 requires several workers, a subject-matter of working conditions, and a likelihood of disrupting the enterprise or social peace, and the strike staged after the dismissals satisfied the third limb. [Para 100][Para 103][Para 111][Para 112] The union had standing as representative of the two workers under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099, having taken transfers of rights dated 28 June 2015. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 91]

On contract type, Article 67(2) caps a fixed-duration contract, and the total of its renewals, at two years; anything longer becomes indefinite. [Para 114][Para 116] Mr K's service of about three years and nine months therefore made his contract indefinite, while Ms L's one year and eleven months left hers fixed-duration. [Para 128][Para 129][Para 130][Para 131]

The anti-union claim failed: Articles 12 and 279 prohibit using union membership as a ground of dismissal, but the party alleging it carries the burden, and the workers offered nothing beyond oral assertion — no document, no witness — while the employer had posted union rights on its notice board. [Para 132][Para 133][Para 137][Para 145][Para 147]

Protected status failed too. Article 293 and Article 3(3) of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 protect union leadership candidates for 45 days before and after the election, and the union did hold a leadership election on 23 December 2014. [Para 148][Para 150][Para 151][Para 161] Mr K sat on the union's executive committee and Ms L was deputy secretary-general, but neither was elected president or secretary-general, and no evidence showed either was a candidate. [Para 162][Para 163][Para 164][Para 165]

On valid reason the panel split the two workers. Internal rules Article 10 require oral education, then a written warning placed on file, then dismissal; the employer had counselled Mr K orally but never issued a written warning, so his dismissal was unjustified. [Para 170][Para 176][Para 177][Para 181] Internal rules Article 06 requires the same ladder for unlawful absence, with three consecutive days being a first offence when there is no prior record; Ms L's absence was a first offence, the rules set no threshold of days, and she was dismissed with no warning at all, so her dismissal breached procedure. [Para 185][Para 191][Para 192][Para 193][Para 194]

Reinstatement was nevertheless refused: with no discrimination and no protected status, the employer is free to decide whether to rehire. [Para 195][Para 196] Because Mr K's contract was indefinite, Article 89 gave him severance at 15 days' wages and allowances per year, capped at six months, with his 3 years 9 months rounded to four years; Article 91 gave damages equal to that severance; and Articles 75 and 77 gave one month's pay in lieu of notice for service over two but under five years. [Para 208][Para 211][Para 212][Para 227][Para 231] He must return the 5 per cent indemnities already paid. [Para 234] Ms L had already received her 5 per cent indemnity, final wages, damages equal to the wages of the unexpired term, and annual leave pay, so nothing was owed. [Para 236][Para 237]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Renewals of a fixed-duration contract are aggregated: once total service passes two years the contract becomes indefinite regardless of what the parties signed, and dismissal from it attracts severance, damages and notice pay while the worker repays the 5 per cent indemnities already taken. [Para 116][Para 129][Para 221][Para 234] Separately, a dismissal for misconduct or absence is unlawful where the employer skipped a step in its own internal disciplinary ladder, even if the underlying conduct occurred. [Para 181][Para 194]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remarks that fixed-duration contracts are disfavoured because indefinite ones give workers security and employers commitment, and its reliance on ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982 as interpretive context though not binding, were not necessary to the outcome. [Para 119][Para 122][Para 124]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award carries a dissent by the employer-chosen arbitrator, Ing Sothy, against the reasoning on the first three questions; he would have held the renewals remained fixed-duration and no severance, damages or notice pay were owed. [Para 246][Para 250][Para 253] The panel is said to have been constituted on 23 June "2023 2015". [Para 13] The hearing is dated 2 July 2015 in the narrative but 2 July 2014 in the invitation paragraph. [Para 8][Para 15] Ms L's dismissal date is 20 January 2015 on the workers' account and 24 January 2015 on the employer's, and the panel used the latter without resolving the conflict. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 130] One payment is printed as "405 per cent" where 5 per cent is meant. [Para 59] The company name appears in at least six spellings (អ៊ិស ធើន, អ៊ីស ធើន, អ៉ិស ធើន; ស្ទ្រៀល, ស្វ្រៀល, ស្ទៀល) and the Latin form here is a transliteration. [Para 1][Para 12][Para 31] The number of workers initially reinstated is given as six at conciliation but eight in the complaint. [Para 9][Para 10]
