# Award No. 242/15 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ប្ល៊ូម ហ្វៀល (ខេមបូឌា) នីត ធើរ (Arbitration Council, 23 September 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៊ីង សុទ្ធី, appointed by the employer party; ទូន ស៊ី ផាន់, appointed by the worker party; អាំង អេង ថុង, chair) · Doc ID 139429

## 1. Facts
Bloomfield (Cambodia) Nitwear is a cutting and sewing factory at Sour Vong, Ta Che, Kampong Tralach, Kampong Chhnang, employing about 940 workers. [Para 1][Para 41][Para 42] A single demand failed conciliation on 21 August 2015 and reached the Council, which heard it on 10 September 2015. [Para 9][Para 10][Para 7] The workers struck from 19 to 25 August 2015 without giving the employer notice, and all returned to normal work on 26 August 2015. [Para 49][Para 50] The Free Workers' Union of Cambodia held written mandates from 468 workers, and two further workers attended the hearing without being on that list. [Para 45][Para 78][Para 79]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must maintain the wages and other benefits of all workers for the days of the strike from 19 to 25 August 2015? [Para 2][Para 49][Para 80]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the strike was called to press for improvement of working conditions — a higher piece rate in every section, one-year instead of short fixed duration contracts so that the employer could not threaten non-renewal, and an end to discrimination by nationality and age against applicants over 25 — and Article 104 of the Labour Law guarantees a decent wage to all workers, so pay should not be withheld for the strike days. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 54][Para 55]
**Employer (respondent):** it has no means to pay; it will deduct the daily wage of every worker who struck and will cancel the regular attendance bonus outright for that month, while confirming that it hired no replacements during the strike. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the demand that the employer maintain wages and the regular attendance bonus for all workers during the strike of 19 to 25 August 2015. [Para 106][Para 107]

On representation the panel applied Article 268 of the Labour Law: a professional organisation acquires the rights the law recognises only once its statutes and the list of its leaders are deposited and registered with the Ministry. The local Free Workers' Union was not yet registered and had no certificate, so its three officers had no legal capacity to represent the company's workers as union officers. [Para 64][Para 67][Para 72][Para 73] The panel nonetheless found, under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099, that the same union had been given written authorisation by 468 workers through mandates of 20 August 2015 — 138 with name, employee number and thumbprint, 219 with name and thumbprint only, and 111 with employee number and thumbprint only — which the employer never contested, and it therefore heard the case for those 468 plus the two unlisted workers who appeared, 470 in all. [Para 74][Para 75][Para 76][Para 77][Para 79]

On the wages the panel applied Article 332(1) of the Labour Law, under which the employment contract is suspended during a strike, no work is given and no wage is paid, and Article 72(1), under which suspension extinguishes the essential reciprocal duties. Reading those provisions as its earlier awards had, it held that the employer owes no wage for the days on which workers strike, whether the strike is procedurally lawful or not. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 84][Para 85][Para 86][Para 88]

On the regular attendance bonus the panel cited point 2 of Notification No. 230 K.B/S.CH.N of 24 July 2012, which entitles a worker who attends on all working days of a month without absence to a bonus of at least USD 10 a month, and its consistent earlier holding that a worker on strike is not in regular attendance. [Para 89][Para 91][Para 93] It then tested the strike against Article 320(4), which allows a strike only once peaceful means of settlement have been exhausted, and Article 324, which requires prior notice of at least seven working days: the workers conceded they gave no notice, and the panel found they had not exhausted conciliation, so the strike was irregular. [Para 94][Para 97][Para 98][Para 101][Para 104] The bonus was therefore not owed either, and the demand failed in both limbs. [Para 105][Para 106]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer owes no wage for the days on which its workers strike, because Article 332(1) suspends the contract and Article 72(1) suspends the reciprocal duties of work and pay; the same suspension means the striker is not in regular attendance and so forfeits the monthly attendance bonus, and neither entitlement revives merely because the strike was called for a lawful purpose. [Para 84][Para 86][Para 91][Para 105]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's holding that an unregistered local union's officers cannot represent workers as union officers, while the same union may still act on written mandates from individual workers, was reasoning on standing and did not affect the outcome, which turned on the strike's effect on pay. [Para 67][Para 73][Para 77]
