# Award No. 306/15 — Zhong Yin (Cambodia) B Textile Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 December 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Seng Vouch Hun (employer-appointed), Sin Kim Sean (worker-appointed), Kong Phalla (president) · Doc ID 139480

## 1. Facts
The Democratic Union of Cambodian Garment Workers at Zhong Yin (Cambodia) B Textile Co., Ltd, a weaving and textile plant of about 1,200 workers, filed eight demands after a strike over piece rates for lot PN G0547 and the dismissal of a worker. [Para 19][Para 215][Para 253] Conciliation settled two points, three were withdrawn, and three remained: the non-renewal of a panel-checking worker's contract, the dismissal of the three union leaders, and the dismissal of 47 workers said to have joined the strike. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 28] The employer dismissed the union leaders and the 47 workers on 23 September 2015, treating participation in the protest as serious misconduct under Article 83. [Para 257][Para 354][Para 366]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take Ms M back into work and pay her wages from the date of dismissal until her return? [Para 232]
2. Whether the employer must reinstate the three union leaders — its president, vice-president and secretary — and pay their wages from the date of dismissal? [Para 247]
3. Whether the employer must reinstate the 47 workers dismissed for joining the protest and pay their back wages? [Para 352]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The dismissals were union discrimination against activists who had committed no fault and had been replaced by other workers; the 47 had only exercised the right to strike, and dismissing them breaches Article 333. [Para 237][Para 240][Para 355]**Employer (respondent):** Ms M's contract simply expired and was not renewed in a downturn, and the employer did not know she was an activist; the three leaders were ordinary workers, because the union's registration names a different company, so they enjoyed no protection, and all of them incited serious misconduct in the ironing section. [Para 242][Para 244][Para 265][Para 267]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 1 dismissed; the employer ordered to reinstate the three union leaders with wages from the date of dismissal, and to reinstate the 47 workers with back wages from the date of dismissal. No sums were quantified. [Para 381][Para 382][Para 383]

Before the merits the panel addressed the employer's objection that the union was not the plant's local union because its registration names "Zhong Yin (Cambodia) Textile Co., Ltd" while the employer is "Zhong Yin (Cambodia) B Textile Co., Ltd". [Para 284][Para 286] It found the two are distinct companies with similar names at different sites, capable of causing confusion, but that the union had been voted into existence on 9 July 2015 at this factory's own address by 21 workers, that the employer had signed a 15 July 2015 agreement with the same three men as worker representatives, and that no evidence showed the union was not the local union. [Para 288][Para 290][Para 297][Para 299] Article 268 therefore gave the leaders standing to represent members. [Para 301]

On issue 1 the panel held that Ms M's last fixed-term contract expired on 31 May 2015 after notice on 21 May, and that this was a non-renewal, not a dismissal. [Para 304][Para 306] It followed earlier awards holding that the protection against dismissal in Prakas No. 305 cannot be invoked where a contract has simply run out. [Para 307][Para 309] Her claim of union discrimination also failed: she bore the burden of proof, and nothing showed the employer knew of her activism. [Para 321][Para 323] The claim was dismissed. [Para 326]

On issue 2 the panel applied Article 67(7): a fixed-term contract must be in writing, failing which it becomes a contract of indefinite duration. [Para 327][Para 330] The employer admitted the three had never signed or thumbprinted their fixed-term contracts yet were allowed to keep working, so their contracts were indefinite and their termination was a dismissal. [Para 332][Para 333][Para 335] Articles 293 and 295 require the labour inspector's consent before dismissing a staff delegate, and paragraph 4 of Article 2 of Prakas No. 305 extends that protection to union leaders. [Para 336][Para 339][Para 340] The employer sought no authorisation, so the dismissals were procedurally unlawful and reinstatement followed. [Para 346][Para 350]

On issue 3 the panel applied Article 318: a strike is a concerted work stoppage to obtain a fair settlement of demands. [Para 356][Para 357] All three elements were present — a group of workers, a stoppage on 5 and 23 September 2015, and demands for a higher lot rate and the reinstatement of a dismissed worker — so the protest was a lawful strike. [Para 359][Para 360][Para 361] Article 333 forbids any sanction on a worker for taking part in a strike and makes such a sanction void. [Para 362][Para 364] The employer produced no evidence of serious misconduct under Article 83, and its own case was that the dismissals rested on the strike itself. [Para 373][Para 378][Para 379]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that lets workers keep working without a written fixed-term contract cannot later rely on expiry: Article 67(7) converts the relationship into one of indefinite duration, so ending it is a dismissal, and where the worker is a union leader it is unlawful unless the labour inspector has first consented. [Para 330][Para 333][Para 340]

**Obiter dicta:** The reinstatement protection in Prakas No. 305 does not reach the expiry of a fixed-term contract, and a worker claiming union discrimination must prove the employer knew of the activism. [Para 307][Para 323]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel's own numbering is internally inconsistent: the procedural section records issues 3, 4 and 5 as withdrawn and issues 2 and 7 as settled, yet lists issues 1, 5 and 8 as remaining. [Para 27][Para 28] Ms M's start date appears as 18 September 2013 and, in the reasoning, as "18 September 2019 / 2013"; the date of the leaders' dismissal is likewise printed "23 September 2019 / 2015". [Para 303][Para 343] Her name is printed variously as Mat Phally and "Mat Jally". [Para 232][Para 303] The company appears as Zhong Yin (Cambodia) B Textile, "Zhong Yin (Cambodia) Textile", "Chong Lying" and "Zhong Li Yir". [Para 217][Para 342] Issue 8 is pleaded for 51 workers but reasoned and decided for 47. [Para 270][Para 352] The employer-appointed arbitrator's name survives in the header but his signature line is blank. [Para 385]
