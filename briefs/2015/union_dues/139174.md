# Award No. 082/15 — San Dong Horry S Wood (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 4 May 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Hem Hona Rit, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Kuy Neam, president) · Doc ID 139174

## 1. Facts
San Dong Horry S Wood (Cambodia), a plywood and panel manufacturer in the Sihanoukville Special Economic Zone employing about 66 workers, faced a collective dispute filed by the Khmer Workers' Spirit Trade Union Federation over 14 demands. [Para 34][Para 35] Conciliation at the Ministry of Labour resolved 6 of 14 points; the remaining 8 were referred to the Arbitration Council on 24 March 2015. [Para 12][Para 16][Para 17] At the hearing of 9 April 2015 the parties settled 7 of those 8 points, leaving only demand 4, the travel and accommodation allowance. [Para 14][Para 21][Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pro-rate the punctuality bonus (ប្រាក់រង្វាន់ធ្វើការទៀងទាត់, "prime") to hours of monthly lateness, forfeiting it entirely only beyond 3 hours? [Para 2]
2. Whether the employer must stop assigning workers to tasks outside their skill (e.g. from sawing boards to chemical spraying) without a written warning? [Para 3]
3. Whether the employer must stop discriminating against union representatives and members by giving them fewer boards, reassigning their work, or docking bonus and meal money when leaders attend meetings? [Para 4]
4. Whether the employer must raise the monthly travel and accommodation allowance by USD 8, to a total of USD 15? [Para 5][Para 41]
5. Whether the employer must pay workers USD 0.10 per square metre worked? [Para 6]
6. Whether the employer must ease the taking of four categories of leave, deducting the bonus pro rata to days taken? [Para 7]
7. Whether the employer must preserve full wages and benefits for workers on sick leave supported by a recognised medical certificate? [Para 8]
8. Whether the employer must deduct union dues of KHR 2,000 per member per month from consenting workers? [Para 9]

Only issue 4 was left for decision; issues 1–3 and 5–8 were settled at the hearing. [Para 21][Para 22]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer paid USD 15 per month in 2014, bus fares have risen, and the allowance should return to USD 15 (USD 7 paid plus USD 8 claimed). [Para 42][Para 43]
**Employer (respondent):** It never paid USD 15, it is applying the law by paying USD 7, and it has no capacity to meet the demand. [Para 44][Para 45]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim for USD 15 was dismissed, but the employer was ordered to apply its internal regulations by paying a travel and accommodation allowance of USD 20 per month to all workers. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 64][Para 65]

On the sectoral scope of the statutory figure, the panel held that Notification No. 230 K.B/S.J.N. of 25 July 2012, which fixes USD 7 per month, applies only to workers in the textile, garment and footwear sectors, and that employers paying more must keep doing so. [Para 48][Para 50][Para 51] Because this company manufactures plywood and panels, it falls outside the scope of Notification No. 230. [Para 52][Para 53]

On the employer's own rules, article 5, point 1.4 of the internal regulations dated 30 October 2014 provides USD 20 per month for all workers, excluding only those for whom the company supplies transport or lodging. [Para 46][Para 54][Para 55] The parties agreed at the hearing that the actual payment was USD 7, and the panel found that this practice did not comply with the internal regulations, so paying USD 7 could not discharge the employer's obligation under them. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58] Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY of 21 April 2004 was cited as the source of the panel's power to order payment of sums due. [Para 59][Para 60] The panel therefore rejected the USD 15 claim while ordering compliance with the USD 20 internal standard. [Para 62][Para 63]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer's internal regulations promise a higher benefit than the statutory minimum, the panel enforces the internal regulation against the employer's actual practice, and a ministerial notification fixing an allowance for garment, textile and footwear workers does not set the entitlement of an employer operating in another sector.

**Obiter dicta:** The union was not yet registered with the Ministry of Labour, no letter transferring workers' rights was filed by the federation, and the panel treated only the 3 workers present at the hearing as the complainants. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40]
