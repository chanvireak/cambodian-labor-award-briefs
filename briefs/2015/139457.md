# Award No. 279/15 — Chin Vinh In Das Stri (Cambodia) ("Chin Vinh 2") (Arbitration Council, 15 December 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Run Saray chair) · Doc ID 139457

## 1. Facts
Chin Vinh 2 is a garment-cutting and sewing factory at Vihear Suor, Ksach Kandal, Kandal province, with about 800 workers. [Para 56][Para 57] A local union held its leadership election on 12 October 2014 and told the employer of the result on 16 October 2014; the employer admits receiving that notice. [Para 94][Para 95][Para 96] On 16 December 2014, after a strike from 11 to 24 December, the company issued suspension letters to three of the five elected leaders and dismissal letters to the other two, alleging serious misconduct; it applied to the labour inspectorate for permission on 19 December 2014 but no permission ever issued. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 68][Para 69][Para 73][Para 74]

## 2. Issues in dispute
Of five claims, points 3 and 5 were settled at the hearing and the workers asked that points 1 and 2 be treated as one, leaving two matters for decision. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 22]
1 and 2. Whether the employer must take back the five union leaders and pay them the wages withheld during the period it suspended and dismissed them? [Para 60][Para 110]
4. Whether the employer must dismiss the Chinese section chief of the boiler room, who is said to have insulted workers and once pointed a gun at a worker? [Para 8][Para 98][Para 168]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the suspension and dismissal of the five were unlawful because the company neither sought nor obtained the labour inspectorate's permission, and were in truth a reprisal for union activity — the five were the elected leadership. [Para 66][Para 67] The boiler-room chief had pointed a fire policeman's pistol at a worker, forced boiler staff to work overtime on threat of dismissal and snatched a worker's card; he should go. [Para 99][Para 100][Para 101]
**Employer (respondent):** all five committed serious misconduct — inciting the strike, shutting the gates against company vehicles and other workers, and insulting colleagues; the company did apply to the inspectorate, will keep the three suspended until the court rules, and will not take the other two back. [Para 68][Para 70][Para 112][Para 113] It cannot dismiss the section chief for want of clear evidence: the gun incident was a joke acknowledged as such by the worker concerned and the policeman, the worker has dropped the complaint, and the chief is managed like any Khmer worker, by warning. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 107][Para 108][Para 109]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted in part — the employer was ordered to lift the suspension and take back all five with wages from the date of suspension or dismissal, and the claim to dismiss the section chief was refused; the parties had chosen a binding award. [Para 192][Para 193][Para 194][Para 23]

The panel first read the Kandal court's protective order No. 675/14 of 17 December 2014, which sent strikers back to work "except" the five, and held that the court had only exempted them from the duty to return, not forbidden the employer to re-employ them. [Para 72][Para 114][Para 117] Article 293 requires the inspectorate's consent to dismiss a staff representative, and art 3(3) of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 extends protection to election candidates for 45 days before the ballot and 45 days after it if they lose, while for those elected the Prakas fixes no end date; following Awards 40/10, 35/11 and 154/13, the panel held the protection of elected leaders lasts at least 45 days after the election or a reasonable period, such as until the union files for registration. [Para 118][Para 119][Para 125][Para 126] Protection requires three things: a protected category, an act within the protected period, and notice of the candidacy to the employer. [Para 127]

The three suspended leaders were elected, were acted against 64 days after the ballot, and the union filed for registration on 18 December 2014, so all three conditions were met. [Para 128][Para 134][Para 136][Para 137] Article 295 does allow suspension pending the inspectorate's ruling on serious misconduct, but the suspension here had no end date, the company relied only on awaiting a court decision, and under art 293(2) and (4) silence for one month is a deemed refusal — the request had gone unanswered for nearly a year — so the suspension was void and its effects erased. [Para 147][Para 150][Para 152][Para 155][Para 158] The other two were not elected and so enjoyed only the 45-day window, which had closed; they were nonetheless dismissed for taking part in the strike, and arts 332(2) and 333 require reinstatement after a strike and void any sanction imposed for striking, so their dismissal was unlawful too. [Para 138][Para 139][Para 160][Para 161][Para 162][Para 166]

On issue 4 the panel applied arts 65 and 311 of the Civil Code: only the parties to a contract may dissolve it, so a third party cannot demand an employee's removal, and workers may seek removal only on proof that the person is dangerous and that keeping him endangers others' safety. [Para 168][Para 171][Para 172][Para 175] It accepted that the gun was pointed as a joke, without intent to harm, and that the remaining allegations rested on oral assertion with no evidence or witness. [Para 185][Para 189][Para 190] The claim was therefore refused, though the panel recorded that pointing a gun in jest is improper and can cause accidental death. [Para 186][Para 187][Para 191]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An elected union leader's protection against dismissal under art 293 and art 3(3) of Prakas No. 305 does not expire 45 days after the ballot; it continues for at least that period and, in a reasonable case, until the union lodges its registration, so a dismissal inside that window without the labour inspectorate's prior consent is void and the leader must be reinstated with back wages. [Para 125][Para 126][Para 136][Para 158]
A suspension under art 295 must be time-limited and pursued before the inspectorate: where the employer simply leaves the worker suspended and the inspectorate does not answer within one month, the request is deemed refused under art 293(2) and (4), the suspension is void, and wages run from the date of suspension. [Para 150][Para 152][Para 155][Para 158]
A worker who is not a protected leader but was dismissed for joining a lawful strike must still be reinstated under arts 332(2) and 333, since any sanction for strike participation is void. [Para 160][Para 162][Para 166]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that pointing a firearm in jest is improper because it may cause unintended death, and that the employer would warn the chief as it would any worker, were observations on conduct that did not affect the refusal. [Para 186][Para 187][Para 188]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The Chinese section chief's name is printed in at least four forms across the file — "Chhay Si", "Chhay Si Fu", "Chhay Siv" and "Chhay Sifu" [Para 8][Para 98][Para 99][Para 174]; no single spelling is reliable.
- The dismissal is dated 16 December 2014 in the letters and findings [Para 26][Para 27][Para 139], but 18 December 2015 in the summary of the workers' account [Para 61], a year and two days out.
- The union's registration receipt is dated 18 December 2015 at [Para 97] but 18 December 2014 at [Para 136], and the file also speaks of the union applying on 18 December 2014 [Para 46].
- The suspension is said to run from 18 December 2014 [Para 60] although the letters are dated 16 December 2014 and notice was given on 18 December [Para 75][Para 131].
- The panel is said to have been constituted on "05 November 2021 2015" [Para 17], and the Prakas is dated "22 November 2023 2001" at [Para 123].
- The award records that "the parties chose the award as binding" [Para 23], which is unusual for a rights dispute and is not explained anywhere in the reasons.
