# Award No. 330/15 — Kin Tay Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 15 January 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phalla presiding; Kol Vathana, employer nominee; Liv Sovanna, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139496

## 1. Facts
Kin Tay Garment Co., Ltd, at Tuol Roka village, Chak Angre Krom commune, Mean Chey district, Phnom Penh, cuts garments and employed about 400 workers. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 37] The Cambodia Union at the Kin Tay Garment factory was registered under certificate No. 3154 K.B/VK of 19 May 2014, with Ms C Thida as president, and had over 400 members. [Para 26][Para 36][Para 77] Following conciliation of four demands that failed on 16 December 2015, the file reached the Council on 22 December 2015 under report No. 1613, and 264 workers transferred their rights to the Cambodia Union Confederation by authority of 30 October 2015. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 39][Para 80] The employer did not attend the hearing of 30 December 2015 although the secretariat delivered invitation No. 1686 at the factory on 28 December 2015, where two security guards accepted it but refused to sign; the panel proceeded in the employer's absence under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 11][Para 17][Para 65][Para 69]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay the remaining 50 percent of wages so that workers receive 100 percent for the five months of work suspension — 20 January to 20 February 2015 and 15 July to 16 November 2015 — which the workers say was never authorised by the labour inspectorate? [Para 3][Para 40][Para 42]
2. Whether, on termination of the contracts, the company must pay the statutory severance in full and may not claw back the 5 percent end-of-contract indemnity it had previously paid? [Para 5][Para 45][Para 48]
3. Whether the company must pay a seniority bonus of USD 2 for each year worked, from entry up to the present? [Para 6][Para 52][Para 56]
4. Whether the company must pay 100 percent wages for November and December 2015 to the three union representatives, whose names do not appear on the termination payment list? [Para 7][Para 59][Para 60]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the suspension ran five months without any authorisation from the labour inspectorate and exceeded the statutory two months, and no agreement with the workers' representatives covered the 50 percent wage, so the company must top it up to 100 percent; severance should be paid correctly on termination without the company deducting back the 5 percent it had paid; the seniority bonus of USD 2 a year had never been paid in full; and because the three union leaders were never named on the termination list, their contracts continued and their November and December wages are still owed. [Para 42][Para 48][Para 54][Para 60]
**Employer (respondent):** no position is recorded — the company did not appear at the hearing and filed no documents, so the panel noted it had answered none of the workers' contentions. [Para 43][Para 83][Para 121]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay the additional 50 percent, bringing the five months of suspension to 100 percent of wages, and dismissed the other three claims. [Para 128][Para 129][Para 130][Para 131]
On the suspension, Article 71(11) of the Labour Law allows an enterprise facing serious economic or material difficulty to suspend activity for no more than two months, and only under the supervision of the labour inspectorate. [Para 84][Para 85] Reading that provision with earlier awards (Case 58/07 and Case 08/07), the panel held that supervision means notifying the inspectorate and obtaining its prior authorisation, and that the two-month ceiling is absolute. [Para 86][Para 87][Para 88][Para 89] It found the company had suspended for five months across two periods without any authorisation, and ordered the withheld half of the wages paid. [Para 42][Para 90][Para 128]
On the 5 percent indemnity, Article 73(6) requires the employer to pay an end-of-contract indemnity of at least 5 percent of the wages earned over the contract term, and earlier awards had fixed the payment at the moment a fixed-duration contract ends and the relationship is severed. [Para 94][Para 95][Para 96][Para 97] The panel held that where a fixed-duration contract converts into an indefinite one, no 5 percent indemnity is owed, so the company was entitled to take back what it had paid, and the workers' claim was refused. [Para 97][Para 98][Para 100][Para 129]
On the seniority bonus, the panel applied res judicata: the same point between the same parties had already been decided in Case 102/14, which ordered the company to pay the bonus for the three years up to March 2014, and all three conditions — same parties, same dispute, full decision — were met. [Para 103][Para 106][Para 109][Para 112][Para 114][Para 117] It therefore declined to decide the point again, which also means the workers' complaint that the earlier order was never complied with was left without a remedy in this award. [Para 111][Para 118][Para 130]
On the union representatives, the panel applied the rule that the party asserting a fact bears the burden of proving it, and found no document showing an employment relationship with the three leaders, no evidence that their contracts continued, and nothing to contradict the termination payment list. [Para 122][Para 124][Para 126][Para 127][Para 131]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may suspend activity only for serious economic or material difficulty, only with the labour inspectorate's prior authorisation, and never for more than two months under Article 71(11); a suspension that breaches either condition is unlawful, and the employer must pay the wages it withheld so that the suspended months are paid at 100 percent. Separately, the 5 percent end-of-contract indemnity under Article 73(6) is owed only where a fixed-duration contract ends; once the contract has become indefinite no indemnity is due, and a payment made in error may be recovered. [Para 87][Para 89][Para 90][Para 97][Para 100]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that the three union leaders' names were absent from the company's termination payment list, and observed that the party making an assertion carries the burden of proving it — a point it resolved against the workers for want of any document, without deciding what would follow if a contract had in fact continued. [Para 122][Para 126]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The two suspension periods are printed inconsistently: the demands give 20 January to 20 February 2015 and 15 January to 20 February 2015, while the reasons give 20 January to 20 February 2015 and 15 July to 16 November 2015. [Para 3][Para 4][Para 40] The union's registration certificate is dated 19 May 2014 in the evidence list and 19 May 2015 in the reasons. [Para 26][Para 76] The claimant group is put at 206 identity cards in the evidence and 264 transferred rights in the findings. [Para 20][Para 39] Case 102/14 is said to have been brought against "Jin Yi Garment Co., Ltd" while the panel treats the parties as identical to this case against Kin Tay Garment. [Para 109] The reasons contain a corrupted termination year ("2097") and several broken transitional sentences where OCR has dropped words, including the paragraph introducing the burden of proof. [Para 100][Para 122] The company is printed as "Kin Tay Garment Co., Ltd" on the face of the award and as "Kin Tay Garmen" and "Kin Tay" elsewhere. [Para 1][Para 34][Para 36]
