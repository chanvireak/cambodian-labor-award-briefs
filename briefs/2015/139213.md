# Award No. 140/15 — Smart Axiata Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 17 July 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Chhiv Phyrum (employer-appointed), Tuon Siphann (worker-appointed), Pen Bunchhea (chair) · Doc ID 139213

## 1. Facts

Smart Axiata Co., Ltd, a telecommunications operator on Monivong Boulevard, Phnom Penh, employed about 875 workers; the claimant was the local union of UNICAM, registered on 19 July 2012, whose second-mandate leaders were recognised on 31 December 2014. [Para 27][Para 28] The company's internal work rules had not been signed by the workers' delegates and were not authorised as the law requires. [Para 32] The union brought five demands on behalf of call-centre and customer-service staff; conciliation on 3 June 2015 failed and the file reached the Secretariat on 11 June 2015, with the hearing held on 26 June 2015. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 22] This is an unofficial English translation of the Khmer original, and the documentary evidence was omitted from it. [Para 6][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. Whether the company must convert 118 interns on fixed duration contracts to agents on undetermined duration contracts after six months? [Para 33][Para 35]
2. Whether the company must allow call-centre and customer-service workers a full hour for meals within the eight-hour shift, instead of the present thirty minutes? [Para 46][Para 50]
3. Whether the company must pay a monthly US$15 transport and accommodation allowance, except to those using company transport? [Para 65]
4. Whether the company must raise the minimum wage by US$28 to at least match the garment-sector increase? [Para 68][Para 69]
5. Whether the company must pay double wages for work on public holidays, as it does for work on Sundays? [Para 74][Para 76]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** The 118 interns hold degrees, have passed months of probation and carry the same duties as agents, yet earn less, so their contracts should be converted; Hello Axiata gave an hour for meals and double pay on holidays, and the merger preserved those terms; the company's transport, meal and wage terms lag the garment sector. [Para 35][Para 51][Para 66][Para 77]

**Employer (respondent):** Interns are recruits without required experience who may renew for up to two years under article 67 and may be promoted through internal recruitment, so no conversion is owed; the merger entitled it to apply its own hours and policies, and Notice No. 230 and Prakas No. 283 govern garment, textile and footwear workers, not telecoms. [Para 38][Para 41][Para 56][Para 129][Para 145]

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** dismissed — the demand for conversion was rejected, the mealtime demand was rejected, and the remaining three demands were declined for want of jurisdiction; nothing was ordered to be paid. [Para 173]

On issue 1 the panel reasoned from contract rather than labour law alone: article 65 of the Labour Law and articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code make a contract the matching of offer and acceptance, and no party may force another to accept a form or content it does not agree to. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 87] It followed that the workers may offer to convert, the company may refuse, and the panel — a third party — has no legitimate right to create, change or extinguish the parties' contracts on their behalf; the demand was therefore rejected. [Para 99][Para 102][Para 103]

On issue 2 the panel applied article 87: on a merger all employment contracts in effect remain binding on the new employer, and the novation letter of 31 January 2013 promised to honour Hello Axiata's terms. [Para 108][Para 117][Para 118] But the workers produced no evidence that an hour's lunch was itself a contractual condition, and article 138 gives the employer the exclusive right to set the work schedule, including the lunch break, provided it acts lawfully and reasonably. [Para 112][Para 113][Para 120] Article 229 nonetheless obliges the employer to secure conditions necessary for workers' health, so it must allow adequate time to eat and rest — which thirty minutes within an eight-hour shift, leaving seven and a half hours of work, was held to satisfy; the demand was rejected. [Para 124][Para 114]

On issue 3 the panel found Notice No. 230 of 25 July 2012 confined to garment, textile and footwear workers, so the US$7 allowance did not reach a telecoms employer, and no law, collective agreement, internal rule or practice fixed a US$15 payment. [Para 127][Para 129][Para 130] That made the claim a dispute of interest, which only a union holding most representative status may refer; the local union of UNICAM did not hold it, so the panel declined to consider the demand. [Para 135][Para 138][Para 141]

Issue 4 followed the same path: Prakas No. 283 of 12 November 2014 sets the 2015 garment minimum at US$123 rising to US$128 and does not govern a telecoms operator, so the US$28 increase was an interest demand, declined for the same want of representative status. [Para 142][Para 146][Para 148][Para 150]

On issue 5 the panel held that article 139 and article 164, read with clause 4 of Prakas No. 10, entitle a worker who works a public holiday to 100 per cent of the normal daily wage, while work on the weekly day off attracts 200 per cent; the company was paying exactly that. [Para 153][Para 157][Para 163] Since parity with Sunday pay was not a contractual term carried over from Hello Axiata, the demand was an interest claim and was declined. [Para 167][Para 169][Para 172]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

The panel will not order an employer to convert fixed duration contracts into undetermined duration contracts: contract formation turns on matching offer and acceptance, and the Council, as a third party, has no power to create, vary or extinguish the parties' employment contracts. [Para 87][Para 102][Para 103] A demand for benefits beyond the Labour Law, a collective agreement, the internal rules or established practice is a dispute of interest, which a union lacking most representative status has no standing to bring. [Para 138][Para 141]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observation that the employer could grant an hour's lunch by extending the working day by thirty minutes, and its remark that article 229 obliges an employer to allow adequate mealtime and relaxation, were not part of the order. [Para 60][Para 124]
