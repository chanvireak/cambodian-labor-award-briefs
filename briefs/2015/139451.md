# Award No. 274/15 — Cambo Kotop Ltd (Arbitration Council, 1 December 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ri appointed by the employer, An Non appointed by the workers, Kuy Neam chair appointed by both) · Doc ID 139451

## 1. Facts
Cambo Kotop (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូ ខូថ ប), a garment factory employing about 1,500, dismissed Mr M, staff no. 4407, a machine mechanic hired on 27 September 2012 under an indefinite-term contract, by notice of 18 August 2015 effective 26 August 2015. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 59][Para 60] He had been elected secretary of the Cambodian Workers' Voice Union at a founding election on 22 August 2015, and the federation notified the employer of the elected leaders only on 31 August 2015. [Para 110][Para 118] Of eight original demands, five remained unresolved after conciliation, three of those were settled at the hearing, leaving only the reinstatement claim. [Para 17][Para 22][Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (ក) Whether Mr M enjoyed protection from dismissal as an elected union leader under Article 293 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 305, so that his dismissal required the labour inspector's prior consent? [Para 85][Para 86]
2. (ខ) Whether his dismissal was connected to union discrimination contrary to Articles 12 and 279? [Para 122][Para 123]
3. (គ) Whether the employer dismissed him lawfully under Article 74 of the Labour Law? [Para 135]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr M was a union activist from the day he was hired, left the Khmer union when it stopped serving workers' interests, joined the Free Workers' Union from 8 June 2015, was harassed and pressured by his team leader (the Khmer union president) and by a Chinese section chief, and was the only one of fifteen Voice Union activists dismissed — so the dismissal was anti-union discrimination and he should be reinstated with back pay from the date of dismissal. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 66][Para 75][Para 58]
**Employer (respondent):** the termination rested on the necessity of the company's operations — orders had fallen sharply, one of two production buildings had been closed since April 2015, the workforce had dropped from about 2,300 to just over 1,500 and there was no overtime left; the employer never knew his union history, about ten other workers were terminated the same month, his evaluation rated him D and C, and severance, notice pay, dismissal compensation, sick leave pay, unused annual leave and final wages had already been paid. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 70][Para 73][Para 74][Para 81]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the workers' claim that the employer take Mr M back and pay his wages from the date of dismissal to the date of reinstatement; the award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent written objection to the Minister of Labour. [Para 153][Para 154][Para 155]

On protection, the panel applied Prakas No. 305 and its own earlier awards (40/10 Meng Yang, 154/13 Bouv Kher Garment Factory, 103/12, 191/12), holding protection runs from the day of election until the union obtains registration. [Para 97][Para 100][Para 106] It used the Council's three cumulative conditions. [Para 107] Condition one was met: the federation notified the employer of the 22 August election and Mr M was elected secretary. [Para 110][Para 112] Condition two was met: the dismissal of 26 August fell inside the protection period that began on election day. [Para 114][Para 116][Para 117] Condition three failed: the employer gave notice on 18 August and dismissed him on 26 August, both before it received the 31 August notification naming him. [Para 118][Para 119][Para 120] He therefore did not satisfy all three conditions and did not enjoy protection. [Para 121]

On discrimination, the panel restated that the party making the allegation bears the burden of proof, and found the workers showed no fact explaining how the team leader or section chief pressured him, and produced no evidence at all. [Para 126][Para 131][Para 132] It rejected the allegation. [Para 134]

On lawfulness, Article 74 allows an employer to terminate an indefinite-term contract with written notice for a valid reason relating to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the necessity of the enterprise. [Para 137][Para 138] Both parties accepted the company's real difficulties, the closed building and the collapse in overtime; his evaluation showed D for skill and conduct and C for teamwork and attendance, and he had been warned. [Para 148][Para 149][Para 150] The panel held the termination was for a valid reason within Article 74. [Para 151]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Protection of an elected union leader under Prakas No. 305 requires that the union have notified the employer of the leader's name before the dismissal decision: where the employer issues notice and terminates before receiving that notification, the leader does not satisfy the third condition, the dismissal needs no labour inspector's consent, and it is judged under Article 74 — valid only for a reason relating to aptitude, conduct, or the necessity of the enterprise — with the burden of proving anti-union discrimination resting on the worker. [Para 107][Para 119][Para 120][Para 137][Para 151]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's view that elected leaders would face a high risk of discrimination if protection began only at registration, and that the absence of such protection would contradict Articles 266, 12 and 279, are general observations; so is its reading that Prakas No. 305 does not expressly address dismissal of elected leaders before registration. [Para 96][Para 103][Para 104]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The hire date is printed twice over: "ថ្ងៃទី ២៧ ខែកញ្ញា ឆ្នាំ ២០១៩ ឆ្នាំ ២០១២" (27 September 2019, 2012). The 2012 date is used throughout and the 2019 figure is OCR carry-over. [Para 82]
- The warning history is counted inconsistently: §6 of the facts lists three warnings (oral 23 March 2013, written 17 February 2015, oral 9 April 2015), while the reasoning says he received one written and one oral warning in 2015, garbled as "៧ខត ព្រមាន". [Para 80][Para 150]
- Two different unions appear under near-identical names — the Free Workers' Union of the company, which holds registration certificate No. 3344 of 24 August 2015, and the Cambodian Workers' Voice Union, which the panel records as not yet registered. Only the second is the union of the dismissed worker; the award does not keep the distinction clear. [Para 51][Para 53][Para 64]
- The decision paragraph reads "ចំណុច វិវាទ ទី ១ អ្នកស្រី បដិសេធ", inserting "Mrs" before the operative verb. [Para 154]
- The signature block is badly corrupted, with stray dates including "2017", and names only Ouk Ri and Kuy Neam; the worker-appointed arbitrator An Non, named on the face of the award, is absent from it. [Para 1][Para 156]
- The employer's workforce figure of about 1,500 is stated as a present headcount and elsewhere as a fall from 2,300; the two are consistent only if read as before-and-after. [Para 50][Para 70]
