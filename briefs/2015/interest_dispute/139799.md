# Award No. 360/14 — San Fong International Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 23 January 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, chosen by the employer party; An Non, chosen by the worker party; Kong Phalha, chair chosen by the two) · Doc ID 139799

## 1. Facts
San Fong International Co., Ltd is a garment factory at Phnom Penh Thmey, Khan Sen Sok, employing about 595 workers, and is a member of the Garment Manufacturers Association in Cambodia, a signatory of the memorandum of understanding of 3 October 2012 and of its continuation of 7 October 2014, valid to 2 January 2015. [Para 22][Para 48][Para 49] Between August and December 2014 the company suspended the employment contracts of workers section by section, for periods of one day to one week, without notifying or obtaining the authorisation of the labour inspector, paying 50 per cent of wages during the suspensions. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 59] Of six demands, conciliation on 25 December 2014 left three unresolved; one was settled at the hearing and the workers withdrew issue 1, leaving only issue 2. [Para 14][Para 19][Para 20][Para 21]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay back the remaining 50 per cent of wages for the suspensions it imposed between August and December 2014 without the labour inspector's authorisation? [Para 56][Para 91]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** because the suspensions were imposed without any request to the labour inspector, the 50 per cent already paid is not enough to meet a worker's monthly costs, and the employer must make the wage whole at 100 per cent for the period August to December 2014. [Para 60][Para 61]

**Employer (respondent):** it cannot pay more, because the company's past practice has always been to pay only 50 per cent of wages when it has no work to give, and it kept the travel and accommodation allowance in place during the suspensions. [Para 59][Para 62]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay the additional 50 per cent, bringing the wage to 100 per cent, for the suspensions imposed between August and December 2014 without the labour inspector's authorisation; being a rights dispute, the order is final at once under the parties' agreement of 8 January 2015. [Para 110][Para 111]

Before the merits the panel settled who was properly before it. On Article 268 of the Labour Law, a trade organisation acquires the rights the law recognises only once its statutes and the list of its officers have been deposited and registered with the Ministry; the panel found that the president of the Federation of Trade Unions for Labour Rights had been registered, and that the employer did not contest her claim to represent the union's members. [Para 63][Para 66][Para 70][Para 72][Para 73] Under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 a party may appear in person or through a representative, so the eight workers present at the hearing were parties, as were the eight who had mandated a federation whose representative attended; the six workers of the Workers' Interest Federation whose federation sent no representative, and who did not attend, were not parties. [Para 74][Para 76][Para 78][Para 79][Para 82]

On the character of the claim the panel applied Article 312(2) of the Labour Law and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099: a dispute about a right already conferred by law, agreement, collective convention, internal rules or past practice is a rights dispute decided according to law, while a claim to a benefit not so conferred is an interests dispute decided in equity. [Para 84][Para 86][Para 89][Para 90] Because the demand turned on the suspension provisions of the Labour Law, it was a rights dispute. [Para 92][Para 93]

On the merits the panel set Article 72(1) against Article 71(11). The first provides that a suspension affects only the principal obligations of the contract, so that wages need not be paid; the second allows suspension only where the enterprise faces serious economic or material difficulty, for no more than two months, and requires the suspension to be placed under the supervision of the labour inspector. [Para 94][Para 97][Para 98] Following its earlier awards, the panel read that supervision as requiring prior notification to, and authorisation from, the inspector before any lawful suspension; a suspension outside that procedure does not attract Article 72(1) at all. [Para 99][Para 100][Para 106][Para 107] The employer admitted it had neither notified nor sought authorisation for any of the suspensions, and the panel therefore held that the 50 per cent it had paid, on the strength of company practice and an oral understanding, did not discharge its obligation to pay the full wage. [Para 104][Para 105][Para 108][Para 109]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that suspends employment contracts without first notifying the labour inspector and obtaining authorisation under Article 71(11) of the Labour Law cannot rely on Article 72(1) to withhold wages: the suspension is unlawful, the obligation to pay the full wage survives, and any partial payment made during it does not discharge the balance. A claim for the unpaid balance is a rights dispute to be decided according to law, not an interests dispute. [Para 93][Para 100][Para 107][Para 108]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that the eight workers present in person were parties while six whose federation sent no representative were not, and its account of the registered status of the claimant federation, are procedural findings that did not affect the order. [Para 76][Para 78][Para 73]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- No figure is given anywhere for the wages withheld: the award speaks only of 50 per cent of wages for August to December 2014, and the panel never quantifies the sum ordered. [Para 59][Para 110]
- The panel's constitution date is printed as "02 January 2019 2015" [Para 16], and Prakas No. 099 is dated "21 April 2019 2004" at [Para 86]; the correct years appear elsewhere [Para 1][Para 74].
- The list of seven workers who mandated the Workers' Interest Federation is numbered "1, 2, 3, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7", so seven names carry eight numbers, and one of them is spelled variously "ម៉ៅ ស្រី មុំ" and elsewhere with a different initial [Para 50][Para 77].
- The claimant union president appears as "សំ គីម ហ៊ង", "សំ គឹម ហ៊ង", "សំ ភី មហ៊ង" and "សំ គី មហ៊ង" [Para 54][Para 70][Para 76]; she is referred to here by role only.
- The employer's answer to the third demand is garbled and reads "Man ពិនិត្យ ទៅ លើ ភាព ជាក់ស្តែង ទាក់ទង នឹង អ្នក បញ្ជាទិញ" [Para 7], and the signature block is destroyed by OCR ("monuc", "Trite MUSI", "បន្ទាប់អារម្មណ៍20162"), leaving only the three arbitrator names legible [Para 112].
- The operative part divides the order into a rights-dispute section and an interests-dispute section, but the interests section is blank, so the numbering of the two headings does not correspond to any content [Para 110][Para 112].
