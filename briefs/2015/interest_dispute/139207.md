# Award No. 126/15 — Khov Phalla Buth In Das Stri (KPI) (Arbitration Council, 26 June 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun, employer-appointed; An Nan, worker-appointed; Tang Tri, presiding) · Doc ID 139207

## 1. Facts
Khov Phalla Buth In Das Stri (KPI) provides ready-mixed concrete services in Phnom Penh with about 246 employees, 49 of whom transferred their claims to the Cambodian Labour Union Federation. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 66] Mr C, head of the mechanical section since 27 January 2009 on an indefinite contract, was dismissed on 9 May 2015 without notice for alleged serious misconduct — swapping a used relay between trucks K40 and K39 while claiming it as a new purchase, selling scrap batteries, and unauthorised absence. [Para 70][Para 73][Para 75][Para 76] Seven demands survived conciliation, and the union merged the reinstatement and severance claims into one. [Para 29][Para 30]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr C, and if not, what severance he is owed? [Para 68][Para 145]
2. Whether the employer must dismiss Mr H (laboratory) and Mr R (RMC) at the workers' demand? [Para 207][Para 213]
3. Whether the employer must apply the wage increments provided by the employment contracts? [Para 229][Para 234]
4. Whether the employer must bear 100% of the loss when a company driver causes a traffic accident? [Para 240][Para 247]
5. Whether the employer must pay a regular attendance bonus of USD 10 per month? [Para 248]
6. Whether the employer must pay a travel allowance of USD 07 per month? [Para 264]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr C committed no serious fault: the relay swap was done openly with Mr R and the paint and warehouse chiefs present and was a repair to keep K39 running, and the batteries were scrap sold by his relatives for USD 80 to buy a shared meal before Khmer New Year; he received no written warning on any of the three grounds. [Para 82][Para 89][Para 94] The USD 10 bonus and USD 7 travel allowance are standard in other companies, and the employer should absorb accident losses entirely. [Para 140][Para 143]
**Employer (respondent):** Mr C embezzled company property and stole batteries, and the other two managers only exercised their proper operational authority, so no dismissal is warranted; it deducts half of the insurance excess from drivers to make them careful, and the bonus and travel allowance apply only in the garment sector. [Para 75][Para 105][Para 137][Para 141][Para 144]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must pay Mr C notice pay under Article 75, dismissal compensation under Article 89, unused annual leave under Articles 166–167, moral damages under Article 91 and final wages under Article 116; the demands for reinstatement, for dismissing Mr H and Mr R, for contractual wage increments, for 100% accident liability, and for the USD 10 bonus and USD 07 travel allowance are dismissed. [Para 270][Para 271][Para 272][Para 273][Para 274][Para 275]

On issues 1 and 2 the panel held the party asserting a serious fault bears the burden of proof, and found no sufficient evidence for the relay swap, the battery sale or the absences; the employer's own dismissal decision came on 9 May 2015, more than seven days after it learned of the relay and battery matters, so under Article 26(2) it had forfeited the right to dismiss. [Para 166][Para 167][Para 168] Participation in the strike that began on 12 May 2015 could not be a ground, since the dismissal preceded it, and Article 83's list of serious faults was not met; the employer also admitted issuing no written warning. [Para 172][Para 173][Para 174] Moral damages under Article 91 follow because the termination lacked valid reason, Article 91 being distinct from notice pay and dismissal compensation. [Para 198][Para 202]

On issue 2 the panel applied Articles 65 and 311 of the Civil Code: only the parties to a labour contract may alter or extinguish it, so third parties — including other workers — cannot compel the dismissal of a colleague; a panel may only order dismissal where the worker is shown to endanger others' safety, which was not alleged here. [Para 210][Para 221][Para 226]

On issue 3 no contract stipulated any increment figure, so the demand failed for want of evidence of the asserted obligation. [Para 234][Para 238] On issue 4 the panel held Article 747 of the Civil Code lets an employer claim contribution from an employee according to fault, so the half-share practice is not unlawful, but the employer may not deduct it from wages directly; it must pay wages in full and recover the sum separately. [Para 242][Para 245][Para 246] On issues 5 and 6 the demands were interest disputes, and because the 49 claimants are not a union with majority representativeness in the enterprise the panel declined jurisdiction. [Para 259][Para 261][Para 268]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that knows of an alleged serious fault and does not dismiss within seven days forfeits the right to dismiss on that ground under Article 26(2), and a dismissal that both misses that window and rests on unproven allegations is without valid reason, attracting Articles 89 and 91 compensation. [Para 168][Para 202] Only the parties to a labour contract may terminate it: co-workers cannot obtain an order dismissing a manager, and a panel will order dismissal only on evidence that the worker endangers others' safety. [Para 210][Para 226] An employer may recover accident losses from a driver according to fault under Article 747 of the Civil Code but may not set them off against wages. [Para 246]

**Obiter dicta:** That a bonus or allowance claim framed without any legal or contractual basis is an interest dispute which only a majority-representative union may bring. [Para 250][Para 259]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel's constitution date is printed "25 May 2023 2015" (line 26). The workforce is given as about 246, while 49 workers transferred rights and only 8 attended the hearing, so the representative base is unclear. [Para 26][Para 65][Para 66] Issue 8 is disposed of by dismissing the workers' claim, yet the reasoning records that the employer may not deduct the accident contribution from wages at all — a tension the award does not reconcile. [Para 246][Para 273] The disposition renumbers the surviving issues (1–4, 8, 11, 12) rather than following the original 16-point list, and paragraph 272 repeats its own operative words twice. [Para 272][Para 274] No monetary amounts are stated for any of the five heads of severance. [Para 270]
