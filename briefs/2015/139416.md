# Award No. 220/15 — Kokyo S (Cambodia) International In (Arbitration Council, 11 September 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kol Vathana for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Kong Phalha chair) · Doc ID 139416

## 1. Facts
Kokyo S (Cambodia) International In sews medical garments at Roung village, Lum Chang commune, Samrong, Takeo, with about 530 workers. [Para 39][Para 40] The local union was not registered with the Ministry of Labour, and 32 workers authorised the union federation to bring their claims by letter of 1 August 2015. [Para 41][Para 42] Of ten demands, four were settled in conciliation on 11 August and six went to the panel: a 4,000 riel noon meal allowance, USD 15 travel and accommodation, a USD 15 attendance bonus, a 20 per cent share of profits, 4,000 riel for overtime worked between 4pm and 6pm, and repayment of health-check fees. [Para 18][Para 44]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a 4,000 riel daily noon meal allowance? [Para 79]
2. Whether the employer must pay USD 15 a month for travel and accommodation? [Para 95]
3. Whether the employer must pay a USD 15 monthly attendance bonus? [Para 105]
4. Whether the employer must pay 20 per cent of its profits to the workers? [Para 114]
5. Whether the employer must pay 4,000 riel for overtime worked from 4pm to 6pm? [Para 124]
6. Whether the employer must organise health checks at its own cost and refund fees workers paid themselves? [Para 133]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** market food prices have risen, other factories pay 12 to 15 dollars of attendance bonus and 3,000 to 4,000 riel for an overtime meal, company profits are the fruit of their labour, and 32 workers each paid 30,000 riel at a private hospital for the health certificate the employer demanded. [Para 45][Para 54][Para 61][Para 68][Para 70]
**Employer (respondent):** it has no capacity to pay; its practice is 1,000 riel for the noon meal, 7 dollars travel and accommodation, a 10 dollar attendance bonus and 2,000 riel for an overtime meal, all matching the law and Ministry notifications Nos. 230 and 041/11, and the health certificate is a hiring condition under Article 1.3 of its internal rules. [Para 47][Para 49][Para 52][Para 58][Para 69][Para 71][Para 76]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel refused to consider the meal, travel, attendance-bonus, profit-share and overtime-meal claims for want of standing, and ordered the employer to refund the health-check fees of every worker who paid them personally. [Para 148][Para 149][Para 150][Para 151][Para 152][Para 153][Para 154]

The five refused claims were treated as interest disputes: each asked for a benefit above what the Labour Law, a collective agreement, the internal rules or past practice secured, and the panel found no provision imposing a 4,000 riel meal, USD 15 travel, a USD 15 bonus, a share of profits or a 4,000 riel overtime meal. [Para 80][Para 99][Para 108][Para 119][Para 126] Article 103 lists a share of profits among the components of wages but does not oblige an employer to distribute profits. [Para 116][Para 118]

Article 96 and Article 9(1) of Prakas No. 305 and Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099 allow the panel to decide an interest dispute only where the union bringing it is the most representative in the enterprise, and it read that test as more than half the workers as members. [Para 83][Para 86][Para 91] Here 32 of some 530 workers were claimants, the local union was unregistered, and the panel therefore held the claimants lacked the legal character to bring an interest dispute, refusing to consider those five claims. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 95]

On health checks the panel took the opposite route, deciding the claim as a rights dispute. Article 247 requires the Ministry to fix the conditions of the pre-hiring examination and the medical staff an enterprise must keep, Prakas No. 09 of 19 January 1994 (Articles 1, 3, 5 and 7) makes the examination compulsory and puts its cost on the enterprise owner, and the annex to Prakas No. 1009 of 28 December 2012 prices it at 20,000 riel for a Khmer worker. [Para 133][Para 136][Para 139][Para 140]

The panel found the employer did pay for the statutory examination on entry, but that requiring a health certificate before an interview, at the worker's own expense, is not proper under the Labour Law, since the employer remains bound to provide and pay for the examination. [Para 145][Para 146][Para 147] It accordingly ordered reimbursement of fees the workers had paid themselves and refused the further claim that the employer be ordered to organise the checks, because it already complies. [Para 148][Para 155]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The panel may determine an interest dispute — a claim for benefits beyond those fixed by the Labour Law, a collective agreement, the internal rules or established practice — only where the union bringing it holds more than half the workers of the enterprise as members; a union that is unregistered, or that speaks for a small minority, has no standing and its claim is not considered, however reasonable the demand. [Para 86][Para 91][Para 94]

The cost of the compulsory pre-employment medical examination rests on the employer under Article 247 and Prakas No. 09 of 19 January 1994, so an employer may not make a worker buy a health certificate at her own expense as a condition of being interviewed or hired; fees so paid must be refunded. [Para 139][Para 146][Para 148]

**Obiter dicta:** the observations that a share of profits is a component of wages under Article 103 without being a duty to distribute profits, and that Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099 makes an interest award stand in place of a collective agreement for one year, were not necessary to the refusals. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 116]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The employer's name is printed several ways: ខូក្យូ ស (ខេមបូឌា) អ៊ិនធើណេសិន ណល អ៊ិន on the face [Para 1], "ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខូ ភ្យូស" in the findings [Para 39], "ខូច ភ្យូស" [Para 42] and "ខូភ្យូ ស" [Para 40]; the Council's own heading is garbled as "ស្យូមប្រឹក្សា" [Para 1].
- The numbering of the unresolved issues is broken: the report lists ten demands with four settled, yet the six remaining are printed 01, 02, 05, 07, 08 and 09, and issue 08 appears twice. The panel itself records that the duplicate 08 is claim 09 of the complaint and renumbers it [Para 25][Para 26].
- The evidence deadlines are impossible as printed: filing by 02 September 2015 but objections by 04 August 2015, a month before the 28 August hearing [Para 28]; the extension runs from 08 to 11 September 2015 [Para 29].
- Stray years are embedded in running text — "ឆ្នាំ ២០១៩ ២០១៥" [Para 20], "២០១៦" [Para 92][Para 106], "២០១៧" [Para 140][Para 149] — and the chair is spelled គង់ ផល្ហៈ [Para 1] and គង់ ផល្លៈ [Para 157].
- The 32 claimants are never named, and no election date is given for the local union; the federation's authorisation letter is expressly undated [Para 42].
- The panel finds the employer complied with the law on health checks [Para 146] and in the same breath orders a refund of fees, which reads as a partial contradiction and is resolved here as the two distinct points it drew.
