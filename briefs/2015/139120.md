# Award No. 006/15 — S L 2 Garment Processing Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 29 January 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Tuon Siphann for the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 139120 · unofficial English translation of the authoritative Khmer original

## 1. Facts
S L 2 Garment Processing runs textile manufacturing on Street 371, Russey Village, Steung Meanchey, Phnom Penh, with approximately 1,035 workers; the Local Union of C.CAWDU, holding most-representative status, is the claimant. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 22] Two demands survived conciliation: conversion of new workers' three-month Fixed Duration Contracts to Undetermined Duration Contracts, and reinstatement with back pay of a cutting-section worker, Mr C, dismissed on 13 November 2014 after twice delivering the wrong textile code to the cutting section. [Para 11][Para 15][Para 36][Para 68] The parties agreed to a non-binding award for interest disputes and a binding award for rights disputes. [Para 18]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must convert the employment contracts of workers from FDCs to UDCs? [Para 11][Para 24]
2. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr C, cutting section worker holding ID 13520, and provide him with back pay from the date of termination to reinstatement? [Para 13][Para 35]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** UDCs carry benefits FDCs do not — seniority bonus, annual bonus, payment in lieu of annual leave, incentives for pregnant workers — and before the change new workers moved to UDCs after probation; Mr C should have had a written warning like the five other workers involved, since he acted without fault and was dismissed without prior notice. [Para 27][Para 30][Para 59][Para 66]
**Employer (respondent):** it offers three-month FDCs because productivity has fallen and there is not enough work while it seeks buyers, but keeps all other benefits, including those of pregnant workers, intact; Mr C destroyed property worth about USD 9000, for the second time, which is serious misconduct under Clause 5 of his contract and Clause 10 of the internal work rules. [Para 12][Para 32][Para 67][Para 68][Para 69]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — both workers' demands were rejected, but the panel ordered the employer to pay Mr C termination compensation: pay in lieu of prior notice equal to 15 days' wages and benefits, indemnity for dismissal equal to 7 days' wages and perquisites, payment in lieu of remaining annual leave, and the last month's salary not yet granted. [Para 135][Para 138][Para 139][Para 140][Para 141]

On issue 1 the panel first classified the demand as a rights dispute, because conversion of FDC to UDC is dealt with in the Labour Law, following Awards No. 05/11 M & V1, 13/11 Gold Kamvimex, 14/11 GHG Cambodia and 325/14 Full Fortune Knitting. [Para 73][Para 74] Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 664 of the Civil Code define the employment contract, and Article 311 of the Civil Code plus Article 337 of the Labour Law require a matching offer and acceptance. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 77][Para 78] From that the panel drew that neither party may force the other to accept a contract form or content, and that contracts made under duress are revocable, citing Awards No. 56/06 Boric Garment, 96/13 FWKK, 154/13 Bowker, 164/13 Honda N C X, 188/13 Dewhirst and 204/13 Bowker. [Para 79][Para 82][Para 84] The workers were entitled to make the offer, but the employer was entitled to refuse it; the demand was rejected. [Para 86][Para 87][Para 89]

On issue 2 the panel held Article 83 of the Labour Law and Clause 10 of the internal work rules do not treat unintentional damage to property as serious misconduct, and Clause 5 of the contract was read narrowly. [Para 96][Para 97] Weighing the evidence, it found Mr C did not intend the damage, that the loss was caused jointly by the section head, the team leader and the employer's own failure to have anyone verify textile codes after the QA team had been removed, and that the consequences should not fall on him alone. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 106][Para 107] Nevertheless he had repeated the error, causing a large loss, so dismissal was available under Article 74 — which permits termination of a UDC with prior written notice and a valid reason — and Article 27 proportionality was satisfied; but because there was no serious misconduct, compensation followed: Article 77 (15 days' notice pay), Article 89 (7 days' indemnity for 6–12 months' seniority), Articles 166–167 (unused annual leave) and Article 116 (last month's salary within 48 hours). [Para 108][Para 110][Para 112][Para 113][Para 114][Para 117][Para 119][Para 123][Para 125][Para 132][Para 133] Article 91 damages were refused because the termination rested on appropriate reasons. [Para 131]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer cannot be ordered to convert fixed duration contracts into undetermined duration contracts: the form of an employment contract is a matter of ordinary law requiring matching offer and acceptance under Article 65 of the Labour Law, Article 664 and Article 311 of the Civil Code and Article 337 of the Labour Law, so a demand the employer refuses cannot be imposed. Where a worker's repeated negligent error causes substantial loss, the employer may terminate under Article 74 without establishing serious misconduct within Article 83, but it must then pay notice, dismissal indemnity, unused annual leave and outstanding wages, and it cannot rely on Article 91 to escape those payments. [Para 74][Para 79][Para 96][Para 107][Para 112][Para 131]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that no party shall force another to sign a contract, and its references to duress and revocability, were general contract principles not needed to decide either issue. [Para 82][Para 83]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- Mr C's seniority is put at "approximately one-year" from 19 December 2012 to 13 November 2014 [Para 118], which is nearly two years and inconsistent with the arithmetic; the 6–12 month band of Article 89 giving 7 days' indemnity was nonetheless applied [Para 119][Para 120][Para 123].
- The proportionality finding is garbled: "even though his mistake was not serious, but it was equally proportional to the dismissal" [Para 114], while the panel elsewhere holds there was no serious misconduct at all [Para 107][Para 122].
- The loss is described as 600 metres / 500 pairs of trousers at about USD 9000 [Para 68], and elsewhere as 292 square metres in 35 piles on 24 October 2014 [Para 53]; the earlier warning is dated "the previous fortnight" [Para 56] against 24 October 2014 [Para 53].
- The final conciliation session is dated 02 January 2014 [Para 15] when the complaint is dated 03 December 2014, so the year is wrong.
- Pervasive OCR loss of "th" ("theemployer", "hemployer", "remaing", "Bargaing"), and repeated duplicated phrases in quoted articles ("Article 167 of the Labour Law stipulates Article 167 of the Labour Law stipulates") [Para 124][Para 127]. Evidence lists are omitted from the English version by design [Para 19][Para 20].
