# Award No. 233/15 — Jim Li (Cambodia) PTY Ltd (Arbitration Council, 17 September 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chiv Phirom for the employer party, Sin Kim Sean for the worker party, Kong Phalapha chair) · Doc ID 139423

## 1. Facts
Jim Li (Cambodia) PTY Ltd, a garment factory on National Road 4 in Phnom Penh with 2,200 workers, paid a base wage of USD 105 a month before the statutory minimum rose, and thereafter paid USD 130. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 41][Para 40] The Democratic Workers Union of Garment Workers of Cambodia at Jim Li, registered in 2006 and holding about 130 members, filed six claims; conciliation on 21 August 2015 settled four and left two, which reached the Secretariat on 26 August 2015 and were heard on 8 September 2015. [Para 9][Para 13][Para 38][Para 39][Para 11] Both parties attended. [Para 17]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must add USD 28 to the existing base wage of USD 105, making a total of USD 133 a month? [Para 40][Para 49]
2. Whether the employer must pay wages on the 5th of each month, with payment completed by 14:00? [Para 44][Para 76]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** when the statutory minimum rose to USD 128 the employer did not add the USD 28 difference onto the pre-existing base of USD 105, so the increase was absorbed rather than added; and wages should be paid on the 5th, because workers share a chartered bus with employees of another company that is paid on the 7th and leaves before the Jim Li overtime ends, so the fare falls due on the 7th. [Para 41][Para 45][Para 46][Para 47]
**Employer (respondent):** it cannot afford USD 133 a month; it already pays a base of USD 130 under an agreement with the movement union, which is more favourable than Prakas No. 283; and it will keep paying on the 10th within working hours until it negotiates with another representative union on 18 September 2015. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44][Para 48]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused both claims, declining to order the USD 28 addition to the base wage and declining to order payment on the 5th. The award is non-binding and becomes final 8 days after notification absent a written objection to the Minister. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 98]

On issue 1 the panel read Articles 1, 2 and 4 of Prakas No. 283 of 12 November 2014: from 1 January 2015 the minimum is USD 128 a month for workers with full rights and USD 123 for probationary workers, and the Prakas guarantees that floor rather than mandating a wage increase for every worker. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 56][Para 57] The practice agreed by both parties was a base of USD 130, above the floor, so the employer was already applying something more favourable than the Prakas; the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, agreement between the parties, past practice or internal rules requiring the addition. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60] It characterised the claim as an interest dispute, and applying Article 96 of the Labour Law, Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 — under which an interest award replaces the collective agreement and binds all workers for a year — held that only a most-representative union, or unions together with more than half the workforce, may bring such a claim. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 66][Para 67][Para 68] The union had about 130 members against 2,200 workers, well under half, so it lacked standing and the claim was refused. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74][Para 75]

On issue 2 the panel noted the parties agreed the employer pays on the 10th, inside working hours, so frequency of payment was no longer disputed; the question was the date. [Para 76][Para 77] Article 116 of the Labour Law fixes how often wages must be paid — at least twice a month with no more than sixteen days between payments — but the panel found no law, Prakas, notification, circular or jurisprudence specifying the day, and read Article 2 of the Labour Law as giving the employer the right to manage the enterprise, including setting the pay day, so long as that is lawful and reasonable. [Para 78][Para 81][Para 82][Para 85][Para 87] It weighed the workers' two reasons — the shared bus and the other company's pay date — and found neither a legal ground, adding that the employer was in fact complying by paying monthly within working hours. [Para 91][Para 92][Para 93][Para 94]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A minimum-wage Prakas sets a guaranteed floor and does not require an employer to add the amount of each statutory increase on top of wages already paid; where the employer pays above the floor, a demand for a further increase is an interest dispute that only a most-representative union, or unions representing more than half the workforce, may bring before the panel. As to timing, Article 116 of the Labour Law regulates the frequency of payment, not the day, and Article 2 leaves the employer free to fix the pay day provided it is lawful and reasonable. [Para 57][Para 60][Para 69][Para 82][Para 87]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the employer intended to negotiate with another representative union on 18 September 2015, and that workers' personal transport arrangements are not a legal ground, were context rather than holdings. [Para 48][Para 92]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- Article 116 is quoted at [Para 78]–[Para 79] in a form that first requires payment at least twice a month and then at least once a month; the panel's own finding at [Para 93] treats monthly payment as lawful, so the two passages cannot both be reconciled.
- The reasons for issue 1 open with the heading "issue 09" at [Para 49], apparently a mistyping of issue 01.
- Construction dates are garbled: the panel is said to have been constituted on "28 August 2022 2019 2015" [Para 15], and stray years "2016", "2017" and "2019 2014" appear at [Para 12], [Para 44], [Para 56] and [Para 99].
- The Prakas No. 283 figures are internally confused, the panel recording both USD 123 and USD 128 for full-rights workers in successive paragraphs before settling on 128 [Para 50][Para 52][Para 56].
- The union's membership is stated as "about 130" at [Para 39] and repeated at [Para 72]; the award never states the total membership figure on which the half-workforce test was applied, only the 2,200 headcount [Para 37].
- The claim as recorded in the conciliation report carries a stray digit ("USD 4 base 105") [Para 3], and the chair's name appears as "Kong Phallak" in the signature block [Para 99] against "Kong Phalapha" in the header.
