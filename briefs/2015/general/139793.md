# Award No. 354/14 — Or Sampath Trading (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 14 January 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3 — Ouk Ry (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Sok Matheung (chair) · Doc ID 139793

## 1. Facts
The company operates a garment factory on National Road 11 at Svay Antor, Prey Veng, and says it employs 1,800 workers. [Para 29][Para 30] The workers filed two claims with the provincial labour department on 11 November 2014; conciliation failed on both, and one — separating the hourly wage from piece-rate pay — settled at the hearing, leaving only the incentive-bonus claim. [Para 8][Para 14][Para 15] That claim concerns team leaders in building C, who receive less "loy pov" than team leaders in buildings A, B and E. [Para 33][Para 36]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must state the hourly wage separately from piece-rate pay? [Para 2]
2. Whether the employer must pay team leaders of building C the same incentive bonus (លុយប៉ូវ) as team leaders in the other buildings, without discrimination? [Para 33][Para 43]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Team leaders in building C perform the same duties and carry the same responsibility as team leaders elsewhere, yet receive USD 40–60 a month against USD 70–90, although the employer previously paid all team leaders alike at USD 40–90 according to the actual work. [Para 35][Para 36] The union at the factory claims 1,520 members. [Para 31]
**Employer (respondent):** The factory has four buildings, A, B, C and E, each with different sections — C handles sewing, thread reeling and mock — and bonus levels differ with workload, volume, quality and the company's profit. [Para 34][Para 38][Para 39] The bonus is discretionary, no law requires it, and the employer denies discrimination. [Para 37][Para 40] It puts union membership at just over 1,000. [Para 32]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the claim that building C team leaders be paid the same incentive bonus as those in other buildings. [Para 61]

The panel began from Article 2 of the Labour Law, which it read as conferring on the employer the right to organise and manage the enterprise so long as that right is exercised lawfully and reasonably. [Para 46][Para 49] It followed earlier awards holding that an employer may unilaterally design bonus schemes beyond those the Labour Law requires, in order to encourage productivity. [Para 50][Para 51] On the facts, the employer paid the incentive bonus to team leaders across buildings by reference to workload, volume, quality of work and the company's profitability. [Para 52] The panel held that differentiating a discretionary bonus by building, on those criteria, is reasonable and lawful, so the mere difference in amount does not establish discrimination. [Para 53]

The workers alleged discrimination, and the panel had directed them to file supporting evidence by 5 January 2015. [Para 41] Nothing was filed. [Para 42][Para 59] Applying the rule that the party making an assertion bears the burden of proof, the panel dismissed the claim. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 60]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may set incentive bonuses above the statutory minimum on terms that differ between units of the enterprise, provided the differentiation rests on workload, volume, quality or profitability; a union asserting that such a scheme is discriminatory must file evidence of the comparison, and if it files none the claim fails on the burden of proof rather than on the employer's justification. [Para 53][Para 55][Para 59]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's reference to the parties' competing membership figures — 1,520 claimed by the union, just over 1,000 by the employer — played no part in the outcome, because the claim was decided on the merits as a rights dispute. [Para 31][Para 32]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Only issue 2 was adjudicated; issue 1 on separating the hourly wage from piece-rate pay settled during the hearing, so the award is silent on it. [Para 14][Para 15] The company's registration numbers are printed inconsistently — certificate 2456/09 of 4 December 2009 in the documents list and Co. 14327/09P of the same date in the findings. [Para 20][Para 29] Paragraphs 30 and 47–48 carry OCR debris that has destroyed part of the quotation of Article 2. [Para 30][Para 47] The workers' bonus figures are given as USD 40–90 in one place and USD 40–60 against 70–90 in another. [Para 35][Para 36]
