# Award No. 137/15 — Cambodian Cement Chakrey Ting Factory Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 10 July 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun, employer-appointed; An Nan, worker-appointed; Koy Neam, chair) · Doc ID 139409 · unofficial English translation of the Khmer original

## 1. Facts
The employer, Cambodian Cement Chakrey Ting Factory Co., Ltd, manufactures cement at Prey Tnorng, Toek Chhou, Kampot with about 315 workers. [Para 1][Para 15][Para 16] The BWTUC local union filed five claims; three were settled at conciliation on 29 May 2015, leaving two. [Para 10][Para 11] On 31 December 2014 the employer had signed a conciliation minute agreeing, among other things, to keep issuing insurance as in 2014 and to have medical physicians on site by the end of January 2015. [Para 20][Para 31][Para 47] The union held only a receipt for its registration application, dated 22 June 2015, and 27 workers authorised the federation to act for them. [Para 17][Para 37]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to purchase insurance for the workers in accordance with the Agreement dated 31 December 2014? [Para 43]
2. Whether the employer must ensure there are medical physicians on site? [Para 60]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer insured workers before 2015 and promised in the signed minute to continue "like 2014"; since September 2014 it has borne no medical fees, and the NSSF covers only work-related accidents, not illness or life, so workers pay out of pocket. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24][Para 29]
**Employer (respondent):** It insured only the roughly 90 workers trained in China in 2014, not all workers, and now pays NSSF contributions; it needs time to consider whether to issue insurance at all. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 28][Para 52]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer is ordered to purchase insurance for the 29 workers who are the claimants in accordance with the Agreement dated 31 December 2014, and to ensure there are medical physicians on site in accordance with Prakas No. 330 on "The Establishment of the Enterprise Infirmary" dated 06 December 2000; no sum in riel or USD is awarded [Para 63][Para 64]

The panel first fixed the parties. Under Article 268 of the Labour Law a professional organisation enjoys the rights the law recognises only once its statutes and the list of its responsible officers are registered, and it noted, following Awards 023/14 (Volume Cocomo Apparel) and 032/14 (Wai Shing Plastic Product), that those rights include representing members before the panel. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 36] The local union had held only a receipt since 22 June 2015, four days before the hearing, far short of the two-month period after which registration is deemed granted, so it was not yet a lawful organisation able to represent its members. [Para 37][Para 38] Clause 19 of Prakas No. 099 nevertheless lets a party be represented by anyone expressly authorised in writing; the federation's acting president held 27 written authorisations, and two further workers appeared without being named in the letter but were treated as parties by their appearance. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41] The panel therefore decided for 29 claimants only, not for the workforce. [Para 42][Para 59]

On issue 1 the panel applied Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code: a contract is the matching of declared intentions, formed when an offer and acceptance conform, and it binds the parties to perform. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45][Para 46] It followed Award 271/14 (Kron 2010) on enforcing a wage agreement. [Para 46] The minute of 31 December 2014, signed by employer representatives, worker representatives, a provincial police officer and the conciliators, recorded that the employer would continue to issue insurance as in 2014, invite the insurance company to brief all workers, register work accidents with the NSSF and provide physicians by the end of January 2015. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 31][Para 47] The panel held that "to purchase insurance like 2014" created a contractual term, and that the reference to briefing "all workers" showed the agreement was not confined to a class. [Para 48][Para 57][Para 58] The employer bore the burden of proving its narrower reading of the 2014 practice, following Awards 79/05 (Evergreen Apparel), 101/08 (DGM Enterprise), 168/09 (Teoktla Plaza), 115/10 (G-Foremost) and 148/11 (Dai Young), and produced no evidence that 2014 cover was limited to particular workers. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 55][Para 56] The obligation was therefore owed to the 29 claimants, the only workers properly before the panel. [Para 59][Para 60]

On issue 2 both parties affirmed at the hearing that the employer must provide physicians on site under Prakas No. 330, so their positions coincided; the panel applied Article 5 of the Civil Code, requiring rights to be exercised and duties performed in good faith, and held the oral agreement binding even though the workers declined to reduce it to writing. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 62][Para 63]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A signed conciliation minute is a contract, not a statement of intent: where it records that the employer will continue a benefit "as in 2014", the employer must perform it, and it carries the burden of proving that the earlier practice was narrower than the workforce if it wishes to limit the class of beneficiaries. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 55] A union that holds only a registration receipt, and has not yet passed the two-month deemed-registration period, cannot represent its members, so the panel decides only for the individuals who authorised representation in writing or appeared. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 41]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the NSSF covers only work-related accidents and not general illness or life, which explains why the insurance promise retained content — a point not needed once the minute was found to bind. [Para 29]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
This is an unofficial English translation with all documents and exhibits omitted, so no minute can be checked against the original. [Para 14][Para 15] A systematic OCR fault drops letters throughout ("themployer", "thearing", "withemployer"), sometimes reversing the sense of a sentence. [Para 20][Para 61] The number of non-conciliated issues is printed as "( 102 claims)" where five claims minus three resolved leaves two. [Para 10][Para 11][Para 12] The workforce is 315 in the findings but 316 in the reasons. [Para 16][Para 28] The authorisation letter is dated "31 December 2015" although the complaint was filed on 30 December 2014 and the minute is dated 31 December 2014. [Para 9][Para 20] The panel cites "Article 19" of Prakas No. 099 where Clause 19 is meant, and the operative order repeats itself ("dated 06 dated 06 the Prakas No. 330 on 330 on"). [Para 41][Para 64] Two worker representatives are named inconsistently as Peng Pha and Peng Phand, and Article 268 is quoted with "foregistration" for "for registration". [Para 6][Para 34][Para 41]
