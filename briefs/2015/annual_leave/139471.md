# Award No. 296/15 — MPA International Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 29 December 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Run Saray presiding; Ing Sothy appointed by the employer, Vong Vanna appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 139471

## 1. Facts
MPA International Ltd. supplies private security services from Street 390, Boeung Keng Kang, Chamkar Mon, Phnom Penh, and employs about 1,000 staff. [Para 43][Para 44] Thirteen guards posted at the World Food Programme transferred their rights to the Cambodia Service Workers' Trade Union Federation on 10 November 2015 and claimed September 2015 wages and unused annual leave; conciliation on 12 November 2015 failed. [Para 44][Para 9][Para 45] MPA lost the WFP security contract, which passed to GFS from October 2015; nine of the thirteen moved to GFS and four stayed with MPA without a posting or pay. [Para 56][Para 63][Para 64]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the panel has jurisdiction, the employer arguing that a claim affecting only thirteen workers is an individual dispute? [Para 50][Para 74]
2. Whether the employer must pay September 2015 wages to the thirteen? [Para 47][Para 102]
3. Whether the employer must pay compensation for unused annual leave on termination? [Para 134]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** they worked the whole of September 2015 at the WFP post and were paid nothing; they also left MPA and are owed the annual leave they never took [Para 48][Para 49]. They denied the employer's account that they switched to GFS uniforms from 25 September, saying the WFP contract was still alive until 30 September and the site would not admit them in another company's uniform [Para 69][Para 70].
**Employer (respondent):** the claim is individual, not collective, so the panel has no jurisdiction [Para 50]. On the merits, the thirteen stopped performing their contract on 25 September by working for a competitor in GFS uniform, and did not give the one month's notice clause 15 requires, so no wages or leave are owed [Para 51][Para 55][Para 66].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer must pay September 2015 wages and compensation for unused annual leave to the thirteen workers. [Para 146][Para 147]
On jurisdiction, Article 32 of Prakas No. 099 confines the panel to collective labour disputes, and Article 302 of the Labour Law defines one as a dispute between an employer and a number of its workers over working conditions, the exercise of union rights or recognition of a union, capable of disrupting the enterprise or social peace. [Para 74][Para 86] The panel read Article 302 as three cumulative conditions and found all met: thirteen workers against one employer; a claim about wages and leave, that is about rights and obligations of a group claiming the same thing; and capacity to disrupt, since collective action by such a group would at least partly interrupt operations. [Para 88][Para 90][Para 91][Para 100][Para 101] It followed awards 94/04, 81/06, 45/07 and 029/15. [Para 77][Para 80][Para 95]
On the wages, Article 102 defines wage as the remuneration owed under the contract for work done or to be done, and Article 664 of the Civil Code makes the wage the counter-performance of labour. [Para 102][Para 107][Para 128] The employer relied on a group leader's memorandum of 25 September 2015 to show the thirteen had changed into GFS uniforms; the panel read the document and found no such statement, and no other evidence that they stopped working for MPA between 25 and 30 September. [Para 122][Para 123][Para 124] The party alleging a breach bears the burden, and the employer did not discharge it, so the workers were taken to have worked the full month. [Para 120][Para 125][Para 126][Para 127]
On annual leave, Articles 166 and 167 require paid annual leave of one and a half working days a month and payment for leave unused when the contract ends. [Para 134][Para 137][Para 139] The panel found the contracts terminated — nine workers left for GFS in October, four left MPA in November — and ordered payment according to the days each had left. [Para 140][Para 141][Para 143][Para 145]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim by a defined group of workers, all asserting the same unpaid wages and leave against one employer, is a collective labour dispute within Article 302 even where only thirteen workers are affected, so the panel has jurisdiction; and an employer asserting that those workers stopped performing their contract bears the burden of proving it. [Para 100][Para 101][Para 120][Para 125]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that characterising a dispute as individual or collective is in principle for the labour inspector and the Minister of Labour, and that the panel will generally follow their decision absent a clear reason to depart from it [Para 77][Para 78]; it also observed that the notice and competitor-employment points raised by the employer did not bear on the wage owed for September [Para 131].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Para 11 dates the panel's constitution "18 November 2019 2015", and the contracts in the employer's bundle are dated "3 July 2016 2015". [Para 11][Para 28] The transfer of authority is dated "3 December November 2015", printing two months. [Para 19] The claimant count is unstable: thirteen in the transfer letter, eleven said to be present at the hearing, nine who left for GFS and four who stayed, and para 73 speaks of "10". [Para 44][Para 45][Para 63][Para 64][Para 73] The conciliation report is attributed to "MPA security company" at one point and to a differently transliterated company at another, and the company name is rendered at least five ways ("MPA", "Im P A", "Em P A", "Ei M P A", "M P A International"); the face of the award is followed above. [Para 5][Para 10][Para 43] Paragraphs 5, 66 and 139 contain garbled OCR fragments that cannot be reconstructed. [Para 5][Para 66][Para 139] Individual guards, union officials and the employer's representatives appear only under partial OCR names and are redacted. [Para 1][Para 2]
