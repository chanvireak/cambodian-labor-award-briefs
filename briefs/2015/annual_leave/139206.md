# Award No. 131/15 — Sentor World Garment (Arbitration Council, 24 June 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, presiding) · Doc ID 139206

## 1. Facts
Sentor World Garment (សេនធ័រ វើលដ៍ ហ្គាម៉ិន) is a Phnom Penh garment manufacturer with about 258 workers, of whom 189 thumbprinted authority in favour of Ms Y and Mr R to bring this case. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 42] The company changed its name in May 2014 and was bought by new shareholders who took over management on 10 November 2014, after which the new owners signed fresh employment contracts in December 2014. [Para 50][Para 70] Of four original demands, two were settled at the hearing and one withdrawn, leaving the claim that the employer owes annual-leave compensation and 5% end-of-contract compensation for May–November 2014. [Para 17][Para 18][Para 19]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the new shareholders must answer for annual-leave compensation and 5% end-of-contract compensation for May to November 2014, or only for contracts signed after December 2014? [Para 46][Para 64]
2. Whether the employer must pay cash in lieu of annual leave for May–November 2014 to workers still employed? [Para 75][Para 76]
3. Whether the employer must pay 5% end-of-contract compensation for May–November 2014? [Para 97]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The company always paid annual-leave compensation and the 5% end-of-contract allowance whenever a contract ended and total service reached one year, but paid nothing for May–November 2014; under Article 87 a change in the employer's legal status does not end the employment relationship, so the buyer inherits the obligation. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 56]
**Employer (respondent):** The new shareholders only bought the shares and took over on 10 November 2014, and the contracts with the new owner were signed in December 2014, so the new owner is bound only by those new contracts; it accepts the Article 87 succession principle but refuses the May–November 2014 sums because the workers can produce no written agreement with the old shareholders. [Para 50][Para 52][Para 55]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the cash claim for annual leave for May–November 2014 is dismissed, but the employer must give the workers actual annual-leave days for that period according to each worker's seniority, and must pay 5% end-of-contract compensation for May–November 2014 when the fixed-duration contract ends or the employment relationship is severed. [Para 114][Para 115][Para 116]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 87, under which contracts in force at the date of a change in the employer's legal status — succession, sale, merger or conversion into a company — continue between the new employer and the workers. It followed Award 143/08 (Chamteak Thai) for the proposition that a change in the employer's legal status does not affect the employment contract, and found that what happened here was a change of shareholding and management only, not a change of employer, so Sentor World Garment itself remains liable. [Para 64][Para 66][Para 72][Para 74]

On issue 2 the panel read Article 167(2) as permitting cash in lieu of unused annual leave only where the contract is terminated, and Article 167(3) as voiding any agreement to forgo or waive annual leave; Article 13 makes the Labour Law's provisions mandatory. Paying annual leave in cash while the worker keeps working therefore breaches those articles, and the panel declined to order payment for May–November 2014 even though the employer had paid that way in the past; instead it ordered that the accrued days be granted. [Para 77][Para 80][Para 87][Para 96]

On issue 3 the panel held under Article 73 that 5% end-of-contract compensation is owed when a fixed-duration contract ends and the employment relationship is thereby cut, following Award 60/06 (Numeck Garment); where a contract is renewed the relationship is not severed and the benefit carries into the new contract, so the obligation is triggered by the eventual termination rather than by the renewal. [Para 99][Para 105][Para 106][Para 110]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A change of shareholders and management is not a change of employer: the company remains liable for entitlements accruing before the takeover, and a buyer cannot limit its exposure to contracts signed after it took control. [Para 72][Para 74] Annual leave may not be converted into cash while the employment relationship continues, and any agreement to forgo it is void; the remedy is to grant the days, not to pay for them. [Para 80][Para 87] The 5% end-of-contract compensation under Article 73 falls due only when a fixed-duration contract ends and the relationship is severed, not on each renewal. [Para 105]

**Obiter dicta:** That the employer's long-standing practice of paying annual leave in cash at each contract's end was itself unlawful, though it did not create an entitlement to repeat it. [Para 86][Para 91]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The workforce is given as approximately 258 in the referral report but 189 workers thumbprinted the authority, and the award reasons only for the 189 plus four worker delegates, so the basis for the difference is not explained. [Para 40][Para 45] The disposition is printed twice — once as a numbered summary at paragraphs 114–116 and again under "សម្រេច និងបង្គាប់" at paragraphs 117–119 — and the second version carries OCR damage ("ខែឧសភា ឆ្នាំ ដែល ខែវិច្ឆិកា"), while paragraph 118 misprints the year as 2019. [Para 114][Para 117][Para 118] The company's own name is printed variously (សេនធ័រ វើលដ៏, សេនធ័រ វើលដ៍, សេននធ័រ វើល). [Para 24][Para 39] No monetary figure is stated for either head of relief, so the amounts cannot be verified against the contracts. [Para 116]
