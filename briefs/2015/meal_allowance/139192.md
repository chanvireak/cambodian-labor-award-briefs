# Award No. 102/15 — Or Sambath Trading (Arbitration Council, 5 June 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bunchhea chair; Ly Tayseng, appointed by the employer; Tuon Siphann, appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 139192 · unofficial English translation of the Khmer original

## 1. Facts
Or Sambath Trading (Cambodia) is a weaving business on National Road 11, Por Chenda Village, Svay Ontor, Prey Veng, registered on 4 December 2009 and operating since 2012, employing approximately 1,200 workers. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24][Para 30] The Cambodian Alliance of Trade Unions local union applied for registration on 13 March 2015 and, receiving no reply from the Ministry, became a registered professional organisation two months later under Article 268. [Para 24][Para 39][Para 41] The union filed thirteen demands on 11 March 2015; conciliation resolved one, ten more were settled at the hearing, leaving two for decision — wearing shoes at work and the timing of the overtime meal allowance. [Para 13][Para 15][Para 18][Para 19] The employer requires workers to leave their shoes on shelves outside the production buildings, and pays wages and overtime together between the 8th and 10th of each month. [Para 30][Para 36] The award is non-binding and becomes binding eight days after notification. [Para 81]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must permit workers to wear shoes at the workplace? [Para 29][Para 45][Para 46]
2. Whether the employer must pay the overtime allowance once a week, on Saturday, rather than together with monthly wages? [Para 36][Para 69]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** they must work barefoot, and putting a sweaty or wet foot on the sewing machine pedal after leaving the restroom exposes them to electric shock; leaving shoes outside also causes loss of shoes and confusion, particularly with new shoes, and other enterprises allow shoes inside. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 35] On the allowance, paying it with monthly wages raises their tax deduction, and they need the money weekly to buy meals during overtime. [Para 37][Para 38]

**Employer (respondent):** shoes dirty the fabric, so buyers complain and reduce orders; the electricity system is properly installed, a technical team monitors it, shoes are provided for the restrooms and doormats are placed there, shoe shelves have been arranged outside, and no worker has been electrocuted in three years. [Para 35][Para 52][Para 55][Para 58] It has only three accountants, already burdened with National Social Security Fund and salary tax payments, so weekly payment is impractical. [Para 39][Para 77]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand to wear shoes at work is rejected, but the employer is ordered to implement a measure to prevent loss of the workers' shoes and to be responsible for shoes lost at the enterprise while workers comply with its directions; the employer is ordered to pay the overtime allowance once a week on Saturday. [Para 68][Para 80][Para 81]

On standing, the panel applied Article 268 paragraph 2 and its earlier awards 62/06, 30/08, 31/08 and 94/09, holding that a local union registered with the Ministry may represent its members before the Council, and that the claimants were the CATU members working at Or Sambath Trading. [Para 39][Para 42][Para 43][Para 45]
On issue 3 the panel applied Article 2 (the employer's right to supervise and manage, exercised lawfully and reasonably) and Article 229 (workplaces must be kept clean and maintain hygiene and safety, including individual protective equipment and work clothes). [Para 47][Para 51][Para 52] It found the employer had proper wiring, an on-site electrical team, restroom shoes and mats, and no electrocution in three years, and that the workers had not identified who was shocked or in which section, so excluding shoes from the production floor was a lawful and reasonable exercise of management for cleanliness. [Para 52][Para 57][Para 58][Para 59] Because the loss of shoes flowed directly from that direction, however, the employer could not shift the risk to the workers and had to guarantee the shoes. [Para 61][Para 66][Para 67]
On issue 5 the panel applied point 2 of Notification No. 41/11 of 7 March 2011, which requires a 2,000 riel meal allowance a day, or a free meal, for workers who volunteer for overtime at the employer's request, and read it as requiring payment within a period that lets workers buy food when they work overtime. [Para 70][Para 71][Para 72] Following Awards 76/11, 244/12 and 145/14, it held daily payment reasonable in principle, and since the workers' need was to buy meals, weekly payment on Saturday was justified; the employer's staffing difficulty did not defeat the obligation. [Para 73][Para 77][Para 78][Para 79]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may lawfully require workers to remove their shoes before entering a production floor where it gives a cleanliness reason connected to product quality, but because the risk of loss is created by that direction the employer must provide a secure storage measure and bears responsibility for shoes lost on the premises, and cannot transfer that risk to the workers. The overtime meal allowance under Notification No. 41/11 must be paid within a period that actually lets workers buy food for the overtime they perform, so a demand for weekly payment is grantable even where monthly payment with wages is the established practice. [Para 59][Para 66][Para 67][Para 72][Para 78]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the enterprise's former practice of letting workers keep shoes in plastic bags under the sewing machines was a better way to avoid loss, but that the employer was entitled to depart from it for a proper reason. [Para 62][Para 63]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The documents and exhibits considered are omitted from this English version, which states that the evidence is only in the Khmer original, so the fact-finding cannot be checked against them. [Para 21][Para 22] The procedural dates conflict: the complaint was received on 11 March 2015, the non-conciliation report no. 116/15 is dated 21 April 2015, it is said to have reached the Secretariat on 24 April 2015 at para 13 but to have been referred on 24 March 2015 at para 16. [Para 13][Para 16] The complaint is described as covering thirteen working conditions but twelve issues are listed, with two items numbered "10". [Para 10][Para 11][Para 13] The English text systematically drops the letter "t" from articles and pronouns ("themployer", "hearing"), and the employer's representative is named only as Mr P Sophat, Head of Administration; individual workers are named only as union officers and are not reproduced here. [Para 2][Para 4][Para 81]
