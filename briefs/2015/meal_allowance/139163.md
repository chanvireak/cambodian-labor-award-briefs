# Award No. 068/15 — S L Process Sing (Cambodia) Limited (Arbitration Council, 23 March 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mr Ly Tai Seng for the employer party, Mr An Non for the worker party, Mr Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139163

## 1. Facts
S L Process Sing (Cambodia) Limited carries on business at Village Prey Srey, Sangkat Stung Mean Chey, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh. [Para 1] The Free Workers' Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia, representing the claimants, filed four demands; two were settled at conciliation on 3 March 2015 and two remained unresolved. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 14] The two surviving demands were that the company add USD 28 to the base wage of every worker, including those already earning more than USD 128, and that it pay a meal allowance of 2,000 riel per hour of overtime. [Para 1][Para 4] The panel was constituted on 12 March 2015 and set the hearing for 18 March 2015. [Para 15][Para 16] The employer attended; neither the union nor any worker attended, and the union told the secretariat by telephone that its officers were "busy" without further particulars. [Para 17][Para 18][Para 41][Para 42]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must add USD 28 to the base wage of every worker, including workers already paid more than USD 128? [Para 1]
2. Whether the employer must pay a meal allowance of 2,000 riel for each hour of overtime worked? [Para 4]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the demand was framed as a claim that USD 28 be added on top of the existing base wage for all workers, whatever their current wage above USD 128, and that overtime hours carry a 2,000 riel meal allowance. [Para 1][Para 4] No representative appeared to develop or evidence either claim, and the union gave no reason beyond being busy. [Para 18][Para 41][Para 42]
**Employer (respondent):** the company applies its own wage-policy notification of 26 January 2015, and applies the ministry's notification on the meal allowance. [Para 3][Para 5]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** withdrawn — the panel held that the claimants had abandoned their claim and terminated the arbitration, issuing no order on the merits of either demand. [Para 51][Para 53][Para 55][Para 56]

The panel first asked whether it could proceed in the absence of the claimant union. [Para 24] Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, and Rule 4.7 of the Arbitration Council's procedural rules annexed to it, allow the panel either to continue in the party's absence or to close the case by issuing an award, provided the party was properly notified of the date, time and place. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 26] Following Awards No. 16/07, 27/07, 138/08, 11/11, 131/12, 108/13, 332/14 and 053/15, the panel treated Article 21 as imposing three cumulative conditions: the party was properly summoned, the party did not appear, and the party gave no valid reason for its non-appearance. [Para 27][Para 28]

On condition 1, the secretariat issued invitations No. 341 and No. 340 on 12 March 2015 for the hearing of 18 March 2015 at 2 p.m., with the date, place and contact details attached, and the union's official signed for the letter addressed to its president; service was therefore proved. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 31][Para 32][Para 33]

On condition 2, the panel read "appearing before the panel" as requiring both presence at the hearing and participation through all four stages of the proceedings — introduction and conflict-of-interest explanations, explanation of procedure and identification of the issues, conciliation if the parties agree, and the hearing through to the award. [Para 34][Para 35] The workers were absent and took part in none of the four stages, so the condition was met. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38][Para 39]

On condition 3, the only explanation offered was that the union officers were busy, with no particulars of what prevented attendance, which the panel held was not a valid reason. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 42][Para 43][Para 44]

All three conditions being satisfied, the panel observed that a claimant bears the duty to appear and to present its arguments, reasons and evidence in person, and that it had done none of this; it accordingly treated the claim as abandoned and closed the case without examining the wage or meal-allowance demands. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 49][Para 50][Para 51][Para 52]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a claimant union, properly summoned with the date, time and place of the hearing, neither attends nor participates in any stage of the proceedings and offers only an unparticularised excuse such as being busy, the panel treats the claim as abandoned and terminates the arbitration under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099, without reaching the merits; the three conditions in Article 21 are cumulative, and a telephone explanation of inconvenience is not a valid reason. [Para 27][Para 38][Para 44][Para 51][Para 53]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's textbook description of the four stages of an arbitration hearing, and its general remarks that the respondent's duty to answer arises only after the claimant has first discharged its own burden, were not necessary to the termination. [Para 35][Para 48]
