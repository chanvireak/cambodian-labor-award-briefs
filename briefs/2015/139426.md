# Award No. 235/15 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន គីន តាយ ហ្គាមេន (Arbitration Council, 22 September 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៊ីង សុទ្ធី, appointed by the employer party; លីវ សុវណ្ណា, appointed by the worker party; ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា, chair) · Doc ID 139426

## 1. Facts
Kin Tay Garment is a Phnom Penh cutting and sewing factory on National Road 2, Chak Angre Krom, Mean Chey, employing about 500 workers, some 90 per cent of them women. [Para 32][Para 54] The registered local union, holding a registration certificate of 19 May 2014 and about 200 members, filed three demands which failed conciliation on 13 July 2015 and reached the Council, which heard them on 8 September 2015. [Para 24][Para 33][Para 34][Para 12][Para 9] The employer already pays a USD 5 monthly childcare allowance to the roughly ten women with children aged 18 months to 3 years, and has no nursery (ទារកដ្ឋាន). [Para 38][Para 40][Para 55]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back a worker it had stopped, so that she may serve again? [Para 2] (withdrawn by the workers at the hearing [Para 17])
2. Whether the employer must set up a nursery, or raise the childcare allowance by USD 15 to a total of USD 20 a month, or reimburse childcare against actual invoices? [Para 38][Para 47]
3. Whether the employer must stop deducting the regular attendance bonus as a proportion? [Para 4] (settled by agreement at the hearing [Para 17])

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the Labour Law obliges the employer to provide a nursery for women workers; where the employer cannot build one it must pay childcare against actual invoices; USD 5 a month is not enough, one representative saying she pays about USD 300 a month to leave her child with a neighbour. [Para 41][Para 43][Para 44][Para 45]
**Employer (respondent):** the USD 5 childcare allowance has been paid for six or seven years in place of a nursery; the company faces serious economic difficulty and cannot raise it to USD 20 in total or build a nursery. [Para 39][Para 46]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to set up a nursery on the premises for children aged 18 months and over; where it cannot, women may place their children in an outside nursery with the employer paying the childcare costs against actual invoices; the demand for a flat USD 20 a month was refused. [Para 58][Para 61][Para 62][Para 63][Para 64]

On issue 2 the panel applied Article 186 of the Labour Law: every enterprise employing at least 100 women, married or not, must provide a nursing room on or near the premises and a nursery. It read the article, consistently with earlier awards it cited, as giving the employer a choice only between providing a nursery itself and meeting the real cost of an outside one. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 51][Para 53]

The panel then weighed the facts: the employer employs about 500 workers of whom 90 per cent are women, so the threshold is passed many times over, and the USD 5 allowance it pays does not release it from the duty to provide a nursery within the company. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 56][Para 57]

On the money claim the panel held that Article 186 nowhere fixes USD 20 a month, and that the workers had not shown that USD 20 corresponds to childcare charged on actual invoices. The demand for a flat sum therefore had no legal basis and was refused, the employer being left to pay whatever genuine invoices an outside nursery issues. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60]

Issues 1 and 3 were disposed of by the parties themselves, the workers withdrawing the reinstatement claim and both sides agreeing on the attendance-bonus deduction, so the panel considered issue 2 alone. [Para 17][Para 18]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An enterprise employing 100 or more women cannot discharge its Article 186 duty by paying a fixed monthly childcare allowance: it must provide a nursery for children aged 18 months and over, and only where it genuinely cannot do so may it instead bear the actual invoiced cost of an outside nursery. A demand for a flat higher allowance fails unless the sum is shown to match real invoiced cost. [Para 47][Para 56][Para 58]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's findings that the local union held a valid registration certificate and about 200 members, and that the employer accepted this, were not contested and played no part in the outcome. [Para 34][Para 35]
