# Award No. 196/15 — Long Lead (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 17 August 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Kong Phalna chair) · Doc ID 139261

## 1. Facts
Long Lead (Cambodia), a shoe manufacturer at Chamkar Svay village, Sethei commune, Samaki Mean Chey district, Kampong Chhnang, employing about 1,500 workers, had since 27 September 2012 kept paying wages and benefits to workers on certified sick leave under point 09 of a collective agreement with the workers. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 37][Para 38] From May 2015 it stopped paying the wage, though it kept the other benefits. [Para 39][Para 63] The Khmer Youth Union Federation, registered with the Ministry of Labour, took up eleven demands; nine were settled in conciliation on 20 July 2015 and two — the sick-leave wage and repayment of the sums already deducted — went to the panel. [Para 9][Para 10][Para 11][Para 24] The employer's stated reason was that the company was not profitable and the minimum wage had risen sharply. [Para 40]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is bound to maintain wages and other benefits for workers who take sick leave with a valid medical certificate? [Para 37][Para 50]
2. Whether the employer must reimburse the wages it deducted from workers on certified sick leave from May 2015 onwards? [Para 45][Para 70]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the 27 September 2012 agreement, the employer's own practice over nearly three years, and Article 169 of the Labour Law all oblige the employer to keep the wage; it cannot withdraw the benefit on its own. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 46]
**Employer (respondent):** paying a worker who stayed home the same as one who came to work was unreasonable and unfair to those who attended, and it wished to encourage regular attendance; it accepts no obligation to repay absent a law ordering it. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 48]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to maintain wages for workers on sick leave supported by a valid medical certificate, and to reimburse the wages it deducted from such workers from May 2015 onwards. No sum is quantified in the award. [Para 76][Para 77]

On issue 01 the panel treated the dispute as one of contract, not of statutory sick pay. Both parties agreed that a collective agreement of 27 September 2012, at point 09, provided that no wage would be deducted where a worker was absent through illness with a proper medical certificate; the panel recorded that the employer had in fact applied that clause from 27 September 2012 until April 2015 and stopped only from May 2015. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39][Para 62][Para 63] It then applied the Civil Code: Article 311 defines a contract as the mutual intention of two or more persons to create, modify or extinguish obligations, and Article 336 gives a contract effect through offer and acceptance, Article 337(1) and (3) binding the party who makes an offer once the other accepts. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 55][Para 58] Following Award 271/14, which held that an employer must apply a wage-increase agreement it made with its workers, the panel found the employer obliged to honour point 09. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61]

The employer's fairness argument did not displace that obligation. The panel accepted that a contract may be modified or extinguished, but only by the mutual intention of both parties under Article 311; here the workers never agreed to the withdrawal and the employer stopped applying the clause unilaterally, so the duty to maintain wages survived. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67][Para 68]

On issue 02 the panel reasoned by reference to the same finding: because the obligation to maintain the wage continued, the deductions made from May 2015 were themselves a breach of the agreement, and the withheld sums had to be repaid. [Para 70][Para 71][Para 72][Para 75] It grounded the power to order payment in Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council of 21 April 2004, which lets the panel award civil compensation or damages that are fair and just, including an order to pay immediately the sums due. [Para 73]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a collective agreement provides that no wage will be deducted from a worker who is absent through illness with a valid medical certificate, the employer is bound by it under Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code and cannot withdraw the benefit unilaterally, however reasonable it considers the resulting cost; the clause may be modified or extinguished only with the workers' consent, and wages deducted in breach must be reimbursed. [Para 61][Para 66][Para 68][Para 72]

A long course of performance of such a clause is evidence of the parties' intention, and its unilateral discontinuance is itself the breach rather than a lawful variation. [Para 62][Para 64]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the employer wished to reward attendance and that the minimum wage had risen are recorded as the employer's motives and play no part in the order. [Para 40][Para 44]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The governing agreement is dated 27 September 2012 throughout the reasoning [Para 37][Para 50][Para 61], but 27 September 2015 in three places [Para 42][Para 46][Para 67]; the earlier date is the one consistent with the performance findings at [Para 38].
- The union's registration certificate is dated 12 July 2010 in the evidence list [Para 24] and 12 July 2015 in the findings [Para 36].
- The panel is constituted on "28 July 2019 2015" [Para 12], an uncorrected double date.
- Commercial registration numbers conflict: No. 7109 of 04 December 2012 [Para 19], statutes No. 1878/2009 of 14 September 2009 [Para 21], and certificate Inv. 1499 E/2009 of 14 September 2009 [Para 33].
- No number of affected workers is stated anywhere; the only evidence on quantum is an untitled "list of workers from whom the company deducted money" [Para 28].
- Article 169 of the Labour Law is invoked by the workers [Para 42] but never analysed by the panel, which reasons entirely from the Civil Code and the agreement.
- Section headings and numbering are badly eroded: [Para 45] repeats issue 02 as a cross-reference to issue 01, [Para 74] is reduced to a quotation mark, and the signatures block at [Para 78] is fragmentary.
