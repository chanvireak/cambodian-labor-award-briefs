# Award No. 2/15 — Nex-T (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 7 September 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Kong Phalha chair) · Doc ID 139418

## 1. Facts
Nex-T (Cambodia) Co., Ltd cuts and sews garments at Street 99, Mor village, Dangkao, Phnom Penh, with about 880 workers. [Para 48][Para 50] Ms C began work in the sewing section on 20 January 2014, was dismissed on 29 November 2014, and was reinstated on 4 April 2015 by Award No. 012/15 of 6 February 2015 without any new written contract. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 56] She had been elected president of the local union on 30 November 2014, a union still unregistered when the hearing took place. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 139] The employer gave notice on 10 June 2015 and dismissed her on 19 June 2015, alleging that she lied about being a union leader and that the company's notification of the election never reached it; after reinstatement it had moved her to the sample room. [Para 57][Para 67][Para 68][Para 70]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether Ms C enjoyed protection from dismissal as an elected union leader? [Para 110]
2. Whether her dismissal was related to union discrimination? [Para 147]
3. Whether she committed serious misconduct? [Para 160]
4. Whether the employer dismissed her lawfully under the Labour Law? [Para 186]
5. Whether the employer must return her to her original workplace as well as reinstate her with back pay? [Para 196]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissal was union discrimination under Article 12; she committed no serious misconduct and never received even a written warning; being moved to the sample room cut her off from the other workers and men were posted to watch her, so she must be restored to her old place. [Para 60][Para 63][Para 64][Para 77][Para 79]
**Employer (respondent):** it did not discriminate, the factory has four or five other unions and encourages lawful organising; she was never a union leader because no election notice ever reached the company, so no inspector's authorisation was needed; she lied to the employer, the workers and the arbitrators, which is serious misconduct under Article 83; and the transfer to the sample room rewarded good work with a chance of higher pay. [Para 6][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70][Para 76][Para 153][Para 154]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel found the dismissal unlawful, ordered the employer to take Ms C back to the sample room where she had been working and to pay her wages from the date of dismissal until she returns, and refused the demand that she be put back in her original workplace. [Para 207][Para 208]

On jurisdiction, the employer objected only after the hearing, by written submission, that this was an individual dispute outside the panel's competence under Prakas No. 318 and Articles 300 and 301; but it had twice told the hearing it did not contest jurisdiction, the file came to the panel with no note from the labour inspector treating the dispute as individual, and the employer gave no valid reason for its objection. [Para 96][Para 99][Para 103][Para 108]

The panel held the dispute within its competence. [Para 110]

On protection, both sides accepted the contract was open-ended, so Article 293 and Article 3 of Prakas No. 305 governed. [Para 110][Para 112] Ms C satisfied the first condition, having been elected president on 30 November 2014, but not the second: the dismissal on 20 June 2015 came some months after the election, and although an elected leader keeps protection for a reasonable period such as until registration is applied for, about eight months had passed and the union had still filed nothing by the date of the hearing, so the protection had run out. [Para 132][Para 141][Para 142][Para 144]

On discrimination, Articles 12 and 279 forbid an employer to use union membership or activity as a ground for hiring, discipline or termination, but the worker carries the burden of proof and Ms C offered only her own oral assertion; the move to the sample room, where pay reached USD 400 to 500 a month, was explained as a reward for good work, so no discriminatory intent was shown. [Para 147][Para 148][Para 157][Para 159]

On misconduct, the internal rules of 13 September 2011 grade faults as light, medium and serious and require spoken then written warnings before any dismissal; Article 83(b) of the Labour Law lists the serious faults, and neither it nor the rules treat a claim to be a union leader as one. [Para 176][Para 182][Para 183] The employer produced no warning at all and conceded she had good skills, so no serious misconduct was established. [Para 184][Para 185]

Under Article 74 an open-ended contract may be terminated at will only for a valid reason concerning the worker's aptitude or conduct or the necessity of the enterprise, and the employer showed none, so the dismissal was unlawful. [Para 187][Para 189][Para 194][Para 195]

Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 allows the panel to order reinstatement and immediate payment, which it did. [Para 192] On the workplace, Article 2 gives the employer the right to manage and assign staff, including transfers, subject to four limits — no wage cut, no distant relocation, no day-for-night shift change and no transfer far from the worker's skill; none was breached, and the sample room was also where she worked before. [Para 201][Para 205][Para 206]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An elected union leader's protection under Article 293 and Article 3 of Prakas No. 305 lasts 45 days after the election and may be extended only for a reasonable period while the union is actually seeking registration; where the union has filed nothing months after the election, a dismissal falls outside the protected period and needs no inspector's authorisation. [Para 135][Para 142][Para 144]

A dismissal for serious misconduct must point to a fault listed in Article 83 of the Labour Law or in the enterprise's internal rules and must follow the graduated warnings those rules prescribe; where the employer proves neither the listed fault nor any warning, the dismissal of an open-ended employee is unlawful under Article 74 and the panel will order reinstatement with back pay. [Para 183][Para 185][Para 195][Para 208]

**Obiter dicta:** the observations on the employer's managerial right to transfer staff subject to four limits, and on the panel's practice of treating every demand in a non-conciliated report as a collective dispute unless the hearing shows otherwise, were not necessary to the orders. [Para 98][Para 201][Para 205]

- Two dismissal dates are given: 19 June 2015 in the findings [Para 57] and 20 June 2015 in the reasons [Para 138][Para 141]; the panel counts the protected period from 20 June.

- Procedural dates conflict: conciliation on 12 August 2015 [Para 17] but the report dated 17 August [Para 13]; the panel is said to have been constituted on "18 August 2015" with the year corrupted to 2022 [Para 19].
