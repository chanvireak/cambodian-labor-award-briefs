# Award No. 297/15 — Hong Sen Tech Style (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 30 December 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, An Virak — dissenting, Kong Phallha — Chair) · Doc ID 139474

## 1. Facts
Hong Sen Tech Style (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. operates a garment and footwear plant at Ta Du village, Roka Krao commune, Don Keo, Takeo, employing more than 500 workers. [Para 66][Para 67] On 31 October 2015 the Ministry received a complaint from two federations pressing thirteen demands on working conditions; conciliation on 12 November 2015 failed on all thirteen and report no. 861 of 13 November 2015 reached the Secretariat on 17 November 2015. [Para 19][Para 22][Para 23] The parties settled six demands (4, 7, 8, 9, 11, 13) and the workers withdrew demand 10, leaving six: 1, 2, 3, 5, 6 and 12. [Para 28][Para 29][Para 30] Fourteen workers attended the hearing of 02 December 2015, and the panel treated the claim as brought for 875 workers holding powers of attorney. [Para 21][Para 73][Para 74]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay a US$12 monthly transport and accommodation allowance? [Para 75]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay 2,000 riel per day as a midday meal allowance to all workers? [Para 81]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must deduct the attendance bonus only in proportion to the days of authorised personal leave taken? [Para 88]
4. (Issue 5) Whether the employer must pay a 2,000 riel meal allowance for work on public holidays and US$1 transport for each Sunday or holiday worked? [Para 193]
5. (Issue 6) Whether the employer must allow pregnant workers one paid day per month for antenatal checks? [Para 220]
6. (Issue 12) Whether the employer must maintain wages, attendance bonus, transport allowance and seniority bonus during the strike of 31 October to 20 November 2015 and refrain from dismissing strikers? [Para 227]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** US$7 a month does not cover commuting that has risen in price and is paid over fourteen days; lunch costs them 2,500 to 3,000 riel a day and other factories pay 2,000; the attendance bonus is withheld in full even for a single authorised absence, which is unfair; a half day a month is not enough for a pregnant worker living far away to reach a clinic and return. [Para 78][Para 84][Para 93][Para 106]
**Employer (respondent):** It has no capacity to meet the demands; it already pays US$7 transport and lodging, will raise this to US$8 from November 2015, will pay 1,000 riel towards lunch from May 2016, and applies a graduated deduction of half the bonus for a first leave and all of it for a second; the strike was unlawful and workers absent from 04 November 2015 were dismissed for serious misconduct. [Para 76][Para 82][Para 89][Para 126][Para 127]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — every remaining demand was declined or rejected and nothing was ordered payable; Issue 3 was rejected by majority over Arbitrator An Virak's dissent. [Para 272][Para 276][Para 283]

**Standing to bring an interest dispute.** Issues 1, 2, 5 and 6 were demands for benefits exceeding the law, hence interest disputes. Under Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 43 of Prakas no. 099 (2004) an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement and binds all workers for one year, extinguishing their right to strike over the same subject; the panel therefore follows Awards 81/04-Evergreen, 202/14 and 140/15 in requiring the claimant union to be the most representative in the enterprise, or unions together representing over half the workforce. [Para 146][Para 151][Para 154] The two claimant federations had built local unions that were not registered at the Ministry, while the registered union, Cambodia Labour for Cambodia, was applying for most-representative status; the complainant was thus not most representative and the panel declined to consider the interest disputes. [Para 70][Para 71][Para 156][Para 158]

**Issue 1.** Notification no. 230 of 25 July 2012 requires at least US$7 monthly transport and lodging, which the employer pays; no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice obliges US$12. Declined. [Para 141][Para 143][Para 144][Para 159]

**Issue 2.** No instrument requires a 2,000 riel meal allowance, and the employer's undertaking of 1,000 riel from May 2016 was less than the demand. Declined. [Para 161][Para 162][Para 166]

**Issue 3.** Notification 230 replaced Notification 041/11 and added the words "without absence", making the US$10 attendance bonus conditional on regular attendance; Award 181/12-Niu Orean held that a worker absent on personal business unrelated to the contract or the law does not satisfy the condition, and that granting leave does not thereby concede entitlement. The company's practice of deducting half for a first leave of up to two days and all for a second is a reasonable application benefiting workers, so the demand for strict pro-rata deduction was rejected. [Para 172][Para 176][Para 183][Para 191][Para 192]

**Issue 5.** Work on a public holiday is not overtime; Article 164 and Prakas no. 10 (1999) give a compensatory payment equal to one day's ordinary wage, calculated as an extra day on top of the monthly wage. No provision grants a 2,000 riel meal allowance or US$1 transport for holiday work, so both limbs were declined as interest demands. [Para 199][Para 200][Para 206][Para 210]

**Issue 6.** The employer allows half a day a month; no law or agreement requires a full paid day, so the demand exceeded the law and was declined for the same standing reason. [Para 220][Para 221][Para 226]

**Issue 12.** For 04 to 20 November 2015 the dismissed strikers had no employment relationship with the employer, so no claim for that period was considered. [Para 229][Para 230][Para 272] For 31 October to 03 November, Article 332(1) suspends the contract during a strike and no wages are owed; Award 27/10-Hat Inthai holds this applies whether or not the strike was lawful. Transport and lodging allowance and the seniority bonus are themselves wage components under the same article, so none had to be maintained. The attendance bonus was also lost because the strike breached Article 320(4) and the seven-day notice in Article 324 — it began on 27 October with no prior notice. [Para 234][Para 235][Para 246][Para 248][Para 264][Para 269][Para 283]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A panel will decline to decide an interest dispute — a demand for benefits beyond what law, agreement or practice already grants — unless the union bringing it is the most representative in the enterprise or the claimant unions together represent more than half the workers, because an award on such a dispute displaces the collective agreement for a year and removes the right to strike over the same subject. [Para 153][Para 154][Para 158]

**Obiter dicta:** that consenting to unpaid personal leave is a suspension of the contract implying no wage for those days, and that the employer's graduated attendance-bonus deduction is a policy benefiting workers rather than a penalty. [Para 191][Para 307][Para 323]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Arbitrator An Virak filed a dissenting opinion on Issue 3, reproduced as an annex; he would have ordered deduction in proportion to the days of authorised leave, reasoning that the bonus is a wage component under Article 103 and that consenting to leave suspends only the obligation to pay for those days. [Para 285][Para 287][Para 319][Para 325] The award states six issues remained but lists five, omitting Issue 5, which the reasons and the orders nevertheless decide. [Para 30][Para 193][Para 277] The award date was extended by agreement from 15 to 31 December 2015 yet issued on 30 December. [Para 33][Para 327] The chair is printed as "Kong Cholha" in the header and "Kong Phallha" at the signature. [Para 1][Para 285] The claimant count appears as 49 and 812 powers of attorney yet 875 workers, with 14 present. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 74] The employer's name varies between "Tech Style", "Tech Sthan" and "Tech Stay" across the text. [Para 1][Para 134] The agreement is dated 31 November 2015, a date that does not exist, and stray years "2016", "2017" and "2019" are injected into sentences by OCR. [Para 76][Para 192][Para 292]
