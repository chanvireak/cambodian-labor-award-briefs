# Award No. 203/15 — Mee Ree Ei Pheril (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 7 August 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Kong Phalla chair) · Doc ID 139266

## 1. Facts

Mee Ree Ei Pheril (Cambodia) is a garment and clothing factory on Street 371, Sangkat Stung Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employing about 720 workers, none of whose unions has most-representative status. [Para 1][Para 28][Para 29][Para 30] In July 2015 workers lodged three demands to improve working conditions; conciliation on the day the complaint was received settled two and left one — a demand for a regular attendance bonus of USD 15 per month — unresolved. [Para 10][Para 11][Para 12] The company already pays a regular attendance bonus, which both parties agree is USD 10 per month, and no agreement on the matter has ever been signed. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 38] The union behind the claim, the Khmer Workers' Spirit union at the company, has 172 members. [Para 31]

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. Whether the employer is under a duty to provide a regular attendance bonus of USD 15 per month to the workers? [Para 37][Para 32]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** every worker should receive USD 15 a month because house rent has gone up, goods have gone up in price, and they need more money for food, for tonic, and to pay water and electricity bills. [Para 34][Para 35]

**Employer (respondent):** the company has no present capacity to grant the increase and is waiting to see the adjustment of the minimum wage before deciding anything. [Para 4][Para 36]

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider (បដិសេធ ពិចារណា) the claim that the employer provide a regular attendance bonus of USD 15 per month, on two independent grounds: that the company already complies with the applicable notification, and that the claimant union lacks standing to bring an interest dispute. [Para 56][Para 57]

On the amount, the panel applied Notification No. 230 K.B/S.J.N of 25 July 2012 of the Ministry of Labour, point 2 of which provides that workers who attend on all the working days of a month without absence are to receive an attendance bonus of at least USD 10 per month. [Para 37] It found that the company's practice of paying that bonus satisfied the notification, so no entitlement to a higher sum arose from it. [Para 38][Para 39] It then searched for any other source of a USD 15 obligation — a provision of the Labour Law, a collective agreement, an agreement between the parties, an internal rule, or past practice — and found none. [Para 40] The claim was therefore characterised as an interest dispute (វិវាទ អំពី ផលប្រយោជន៍) rather than a rights dispute. [Para 41]

That characterisation brought Article 96 of the Labour Law into play, which defines a collective agreement and requires it to be made between employers or employers' organisations on one side and one or more workers' organisations on the other. [Para 42][Para 43] Article 9(1) of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 gives the right to request negotiation of a collective agreement applying to all the workers represented to the union with most-representative status at the enterprise. [Para 44][Para 45] The panel reasoned that most-representative status is what confers the legal right both to negotiate a collective agreement and to bring an interest dispute to the Council for resolution. [Para 46] It recorded that it had refused to hear interest disputes brought by unions without most-representative status in awards 02/11, 66/11 and 206/12, and that its own earlier awards 81/04, 98/04 and 202/14 were to the same effect. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 52][Para 53]

On the facts, the claimant union at this company is not the most representative union. [Para 54] The panel concluded that the claimant therefore lacked the legal character required to bring an interest dispute to the Council, and dismissed the claim on that basis as well as on the merits of the amount. [Para 55][Para 56]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

Two rules decide this case. First, the statutory floor for a regular attendance bonus is the USD 10 per month fixed by point 2 of Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012, and an employer that pays that sum discharges the obligation; a demand for more must point to a law, collective agreement, internal rule or established practice that fixes the higher figure. Second, such a demand is an interest dispute, which may be brought only by the union holding most-representative status at the enterprise under Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9(1) of Prakas No. 305; a minority union's interest claim is inadmissible however reasonable the underlying need. [Para 37][Para 39][Para 40][Para 46][Para 55]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's statement that an award resolving an interest dispute takes the place of a collective agreement and has effect for one year, and that it thereby removes the workers' right to strike over that interest claim, is a general observation on the effect of interest awards (Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099) rather than a ground of the dismissal, which rested on the absence of any USD 15 entitlement and on standing. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49]
