# Award No. 104/15 — Anco Brother Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 12 June 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bunchea presiding; Li Tai Seng, employer nominee; Sin Kim Sean, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139196

## 1. Facts
Anco Brother Co., Ltd, a general trading company at Street 217, Voat Phnum, Phnom Penh, employs about 800 workers and ran a promotion and sales section for Budweiser beer staffed by twelve women. [Para 1][Para 53][Para 54][Para 56] Its Singapore supplier ended the promotion contract, and the company closed the section on 31 January 2015. [Para 71][Para 107][Para 180] On 27 January 2015 the eight women who bring this case were called to collect their final wages and signed resignation letters the administration had prepared and dated 2 January 2015, without being told what the paper was; the section head had told them on 23 January 2015 that the section would be dissolved. [Para 58][Para 86][Para 88][Para 105][Para 160] They had worked without written contracts since dates between 1998 and 2012, and the union federation held their authorisations to act. [Para 54][Para 97][Para 101]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the eight women were dismissed by the employer when it closed the Budweiser promotion and sales section on 31 January 2015, rather than resigning of their own will? [Para 56][Para 158][Para 167]
2. Whether, on that dismissal, the employer owes severance damages under Article 89, payment in lieu of notice under Articles 75 and 77, and annual leave compensation under Articles 166 and 168? [Para 60][Para 189][Para 202][Para 212]
3. Whether the employer must also pay moral damages under Article 91? [Para 67][Para 235]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the closure of the whole section on one day was a collective dismissal under Article 95, carried out without the statutory order of selection or written notice; the resignation letters were void under Article 349 of the Civil Code because the administration made them sign before releasing their wages, without showing them the text, and some cannot read; they never intended to resign; and they claim severance, notice, annual leave and moral damages. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 62][Para 80][Para 81][Para 84]

**Employer (respondent):** the women resigned of their own accord, were paid their final wages plus one month's support, and have no further claim; they were floating or occasional workers of the promotion section, not permanent employees of Anco, hired for a Singapore company which stopped supplying the section, and were never registered for tax; the section head was told orally two or three months in advance. [Para 3][Para 58][Para 72][Para 74][Para 108][Para 110]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must pay the eight women (1) severance damages under Article 89, (2) payment in lieu of notice under Article 75, and (3) annual leave compensation for the last three years, from January 2012 to January 2015; the claim for moral damages under Article 91 was refused. [Para 248][Para 249][Para 250][Para 251]

On the employment relationship the panel applied Articles 1, 2 and 3 of the Labour Law and found that Anco itself recruited the eight women on its own forms, set their wages, hours and leave, disciplined them, and paid them, so it was their employer even though a Singapore company contracted for the promotion. [Para 121][Para 123][Para 141][Para 144][Para 151] The relationship therefore existed notwithstanding the absence of a written contract. [Para 152]

On the contract the panel applied Article 67(1) and Article 76(7): a fixed-term contract must be in writing and state its end date, and an unwritten one becomes a contract of indefinite duration, which is what these eight had. [Para 154][Para 155][Para 157]

On characterisation the panel weighed the two facts both sides accepted — that the section was dissolved and that the women learned of it on 23 January 2015 — and found the resignation letters were a form the company itself prepared, that the employer had admitted it would dissolve the section, and that the women did not intend to resign; the termination was therefore the employer's act, not a resignation. [Para 162][Para 164][Para 166][Para 167]

On collective dismissal the panel accepted that Article 95 requires a selection order by professional merit, seniority and family burden, notice to worker representatives and priority for rehiring, and that none was followed. [Para 169][Para 170][Para 184] It held, however, that where an entire section is closed at once with no workers retained, there is no selection to order, following its reasoning in Case No. 32/15 (HSAN); the failure was therefore not itself a breach. [Para 185][Para 187][Para 188]

On severance the panel found all eight had more than two years' service and applied Article 89: fifteen days' wages and fringe benefits per year of service, capped at six months' wages and benefits. [Para 192][Para 193][Para 194] The daily rate is the average of the last twelve months' total wages, including overtime and bonuses, divided by twelve and then by 26. [Para 196][Para 197][Para 201]

On notice, the employer gave no written notice scaled to seniority as Articles 74 and 75 require, so payment in lieu of notice is owed for the notice period corresponding to each woman's service. [Para 203][Para 204][Para 206][Para 207]

On annual leave the panel applied Article 166 (18 days a year, one more day for each three years of service) and Article 168 (payment on termination), but limited recovery by the three-year prescription in Article 120 to January 2012–January 2015, at 18 days for one to three years' service rising to 23 days for sixteen to eighteen years. [Para 213][Para 219][Para 221][Para 227][Para 228]

On moral damages the panel held that Article 91 requires a dismissal without valid reason, and that closing the section because the Singapore supplier stopped supplying it was a valid reason related to the needs of the enterprise under Article 74; moral damages were refused. [Para 235][Para 245][Para 246][Para 247]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer prepares the resignation letters, calls the workers to sign them as the condition of collecting their wages, and has already decided to close their section, the termination is a dismissal by the employer, not a resignation, whatever the paper says; and an employer that closes a whole section owes Article 89 severance, notice pay and three years' annual leave, but no moral damages, because the closure answers a need of the enterprise. [Para 164][Para 166][Para 188][Para 246]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that where a whole section is closed and no worker is kept on, Article 95's order of selection by merit, seniority and family burden has nothing to operate on, an observation it did not need because the dismissal was lawful either way. [Para 185][Para 188]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel's own account of the resignation letters gives two dates: the letters are dated 2 January 2015 but were signed on 27 January 2015. [Para 24][Para 86][Para 95] The award date was moved by agreement from 28 May 2015 to 12 June 2015. [Para 19] The section is said to have had twelve women while eight complain, and the eight are named only in the source. [Para 56][Para 59] The company is written អាន កូ, អាន កូន and អាន ក្ខ, and the Ministry letter is dated "08 July 205". [Para 1][Para 91][Para 23] A letter from the Ministry of Commerce is described as coming from the "Minister of Commerce" but addressed to the company's governor. [Para 23] The statement of claim cites "Article 1666" for annual leave. [Para 116] The operative order is set out twice in identical terms, and in both versions the annual leave order and the refusal of moral damages run together in a single unpunctuated sentence. [Para 248][Para 250][Para 251][Para 253]
