# Award No. 240/15 — Vonammy (Cambodia) Garment (Arbitration Council, 23 September 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambor Na, Tun Si Phan, chair Pen Bun Chheang) · Doc ID 139430

## 1. Facts
Vonammy (Cambodia) Garment, a garment factory at Bati district, Takeo province employing about 700 workers, told six workers of its metal section (ជាងដែក) to take their final wages and rest, promising to call them back when work resumed; they were never recalled. [Para 29] [Para 38] Twelve workers assigned their claims to the union federation by letter of 15 August 2015, and six of them — with tenures the panel puts at six to eleven months — pursued the demand that the employer recognise them as full-status workers and take them back with back wages. [Para 33] [Para 174] The employer did not appear at the hearing of 09 September 2015. [Para 14]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the six metal-section workers were casual workers (កម្មករ បណ្តែត) or permanent workers (កម្មករ ជាប់លាប់)? [Para 72]
2. Whether their employment contracts were fixed-term or indefinite-term? [Para 90]
3. Whether their dismissal was connected to union discrimination? [Para 112]
4. Whether they were persons protected against dismissal? [Para 127]
5. Whether the employer had valid grounds to dismiss them? [Para 161]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that they had worked more than ten months continuously, eight hours a day, with no contract beyond a three-month probation, so they were permanent employees, not casual labour; that the employer stopped them without valid reason, telling them to collect final wages and wait, and that the stoppage followed the election of one of them as union secretary. [Para 2] [Para 37] [Para 40] [Para 53]
**Employer (respondent):** absent from the hearing and from the conciliation, but on the record of the conciliation it maintained that it hired these workers as "floating" labour to fill in on the ironwork when work ran out, letting them rest until new orders arrived, when it would invite them back. [Para 3] Its written final-wage schedules treated all six as casual. [Para 70]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to take all six workers back and pay wages from the date of dismissal to the date of return; if it cannot take them back it must pay fifteen days' wages in lieu of notice, dismissal compensation of seven days' wages and fringe benefits, unused annual leave, moral damages equal to seven days' wages, and any final wages still unpaid, all computed on actual seniority. [Para 171] [Para 193] [Para 196]

On issue 1, Article 9 of the Labour Law distinguishes the permanent worker, who does regular work of a permanent character, from the casual worker, whose work is unstable, of short duration, intermittent or seasonal. The panel found the six worked eight hours a day, four to five days a week and sometimes seven, with no seasonal stoppage, on ironwork that is a standing function of the factory; following its reasoning in Case 58/13 (TV5) it held them permanent, not casual. [Para 73] [Para 79] [Para 81] [Para 86] [Para 89]

On issue 2, Articles 65 of the Labour Law and 664 of the Civil Code define the contract; Article 67(1) and (7) requires a fixed-term contract to be in writing with a clear end date, failing which it becomes indefinite. The employer produced no contracts, and the workers' evidence was that they signed only a three-month probation contract and later received a 5 per cent payment every three months, which the panel held could be a separate arrangement and did not prove a fixed term; the employer's absence left the assertion unrebutted, so the contracts were presumed indefinite. [Para 97] [Para 98] [Para 105] [Para 106] [Para 110]

On issue 3, Articles 12 and 279 forbid an employer to use union membership or activity as a ground for hiring, discipline or dismissal, but the party alleging discrimination carries the burden of proof. The workers asserted it without witnesses or specific evidence, and the union president and vice-president were still working normally in the factory, so the claim failed. [Para 112] [Para 113] [Para 117] [Para 124] [Para 125] [Para 126]

On issue 4, Article 293 and Prakas 305 (arts 3–4) protect candidates and union founders during registration; Prakas 313 art 7 protects the president, first vice-president and first secretary registered with the Ministry. Only the secretary held that status, and his dismissal on 15 August 2015 came some six months after the 22 February 2015 election — beyond the 45 days the Prakas allows — while the union produced no registration receipt for the other five, so the first condition failed and the panel did not reach the third. [Para 143] [Para 144] [Para 151] [Para 153] [Para 157] [Para 159]

On issue 5, Article 74 allows an indefinite-term contract to be terminated at either party's will but never without valid grounds going to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the operational needs of the enterprise. With the employer absent, the panel presumed the dismissal was for lack of work; lack of work cannot justify dismissing permanent staff, so there were no valid grounds, and Prakas 099 art 34 empowered the order of reinstatement or compensation. [Para 162] [Para 167] [Para 168] [Para 169] [Para 175]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker who performs the same work at full hours, week after week and without seasonal interruption, for months on end is a permanent employee on an indefinite-term contract whatever label the employer attaches to the post or the payroll; a fixed term exists only in a written contract stating its end date, and in the absence of one the employer's "floating labour" characterisation does not survive. Dismissing such a worker for want of work is a termination without valid grounds under Article 74 and draws reinstatement with back pay, or the full statutory package if reinstatement is impossible. [Para 89] [Para 98] [Para 110] [Para 168]

**Obiter dicta:** that protected status under Prakas 313 art 7 attaches to the three senior office-holders only, and that the panel expressly declined to consider the third protection condition once the second had failed. [Para 143] [Para 159]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The header dates the award 23 September 2015, while the paragraph recording the panel's constitution carries a stray year — "03 September 2019 -2- 2015" — that cannot be reconciled with the hearing of 09 September 2015. [Para 1] [Para 12] The reasons state that the workers' minimum seniority was four years, whereas the findings and the compensation calculation give six to eleven months; the four-year passage sits inside the quotation of Case 58/13 and appears to describe that case's workers, but the award never says so. [Para 80] [Para 174] [Para 182] The final-wage table is scrambled by OCR, with row numbers and contract dates interleaved and the sixth worker's name reduced to a stray character, and the same workers appear variously as សួន, សូន and សុន ភក្តី and as ឡុង ឡូន and ឡុង ឡ. [Para 54] [Para 174] The 5 per cent payments are dated "to June 2015", "to July 2015" and "to February 2015" with a lost year digit, and the panel cites Article 73 for the fifteen-day notice entitlement while quoting Article 75 as its source. [Para 46] [Para 48] [Para 172] [Para 175] One passage of the reasoning is unrecoverable ("ពួក an Annymicrinance"). [Para 89] Individual names are reduced to role or initial per the briefing convention.
