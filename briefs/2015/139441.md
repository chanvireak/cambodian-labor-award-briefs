# Award No. 259/15 — Khem F Ever (Arbitration Council, 20 October 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Uk Ri for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 139441

## 1. Facts
Khem F Ever (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេម ហ្វ អេវើ), a garment-cutting enterprise in Steung Meanchey, Phnom Penh employing about 1,800 workers, dismissed two workers — Mr C, deputy president of the Khmer union at the factory, and Ms S, its secretary — after an election of union leaders held on 26 May 2015. [Para 1][Para 57][Para 196] The workers say they were dismissed on 30 July 2015 without valid reason and because of their union role; the employer says they abandoned their jobs by six consecutive days of unauthorised absence from 31 July to 5 August 2015 under its internal rules. [Para 83][Para 140][Para 141] Seven demands were referred from the non-conciliation report of 21 September 2015; at the hearing the worker party withdrew demands 2 to 7, leaving only the reinstatement claim. [Para 12][Para 21][Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr C and Ms S to their former positions and pay them back wages from the date of dismissal until the date they return to work? [Para 2][Para 72]

Issues 2 to 7 of the non-conciliation report — uniforms or their cash equivalent, a nursery and breastfeeding room, voluntary overtime, finger-printing of overtime consent, a doctor on duty, and a 90-day wage advance for pregnant workers — were withdrawn and not decided. [Para 3][Para 4][Para 5][Para 6][Para 8][Para 9][Para 21]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the two were dismissed on 30 July 2015 with no valid reason and out of union discrimination, both being leaders of the Khmer union at the factory; on 28 July 2015 administration called them to the canteen and urged them to resign from the union, and on 30 July the head of administration paid them severance without giving any reason. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 85][Para 140]
**Employer (respondent):** the two abandoned their employment by six consecutive days of unlawful absence, 31 July to 5 August 2015, which the internal rules of 27 June 2012 treat as abandonment; the head of administration did not meet them on 28 July, and the company never received notice of the union's founding or of the elected leaders. [Para 95][Para 102][Para 141]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer is ordered to take Mr C and Ms S back into work and to pay them back wages from the date of dismissal until the date of their return. [Para 222][Para 223]

*Jurisdiction.* The panel recalled that the Labour Inspector and Minister decide whether a dispute is individual or collective, that it normally follows that decision absent clear reason to differ, and that it presumes a referred dispute collective unless a party objects, in which case that party bears the burden of proof. [Para 114][Para 119][Para 120] The employer objected to collective character only in its note of 29 September 2015, put no argument or evidence at the hearing, and at the hearing answered that it had itself resolved the two workers' claims, contradicting its note; the panel therefore held the dispute collective and itself competent. [Para 64][Para 121][Para 122][Para 123][Para 126]

*Union representation.* Article 268 of the Labour Law gives a professional organisation the rights the law recognises only once its statute and the list of its leaders are deposited and registered with the Ministry. [Para 128][Para 129] The Khmer union at the company held only a receipt of application dated 9 September 2015 and no registration certificate, so it could not represent its members on this demand. [Para 58][Para 134][Para 135] That did not end the case: clause 19 of Prakas no. 099 allows a disputing party to appear in person or through an authorised person, and both workers appeared in person. [Para 137][Para 138][Para 139]

*Type of contract.* Article 67(2) caps a fixed-duration contract at two years and allows renewal only while total duration stays within two years; Article 73(5) converts a fixed-duration contract into an indefinite one where the total exceeds that limit, as the panel had explained in Award 10/03 Zexintex. [Para 142][Para 146][Para 147][Para 148] Mr C began on 7 August 2012 and Ms S on 2 January 2013, each on a two-month probation then successive six-month contracts, so each renewal series exceeded two years and both held indefinite-duration contracts. [Para 157][Para 158][Para 159][Para 160]

*Dismissal or abandonment.* The internal rules treat six or more days of unlawful absence in a month as abandonment. [Para 163] The employer counted 31 July to 5 August 2015, but itself accepted that both workers came to work on 1 August, and 2 August was a Sunday weekly rest day, so six days of absence were not made out; the workers also filed a reinstatement claim on 6 August 2015. [Para 167][Para 168][Para 169][Para 170] The panel held the separation a dismissal by the employer, not abandonment. [Para 171]

*Protected status.* Article 293 forbids dismissal of a staff delegate or candidate without the labour inspector's prior agreement, and Prakas no. 305 of 22 November 2001 extends that protection to union leaders. [Para 172][Para 173][Para 179] Following Award 40/10 Meng Yan, it applied three conditions: a protected person, a dismissal within the protection period, and prior notice of the protected names to the employer. [Para 186][Para 193] Both were elected leaders on 26 May 2015, so condition one was met. [Para 196][Para 200] They were dismissed 2 months 11 days after the election — beyond the 45 days Prakas 305 fixes for unsuccessful candidates but a reasonable period for continuing the protection of elected leaders under Award 40/10 — so condition two was met. [Para 202][Para 203][Para 204] The employer knew their names from the hearing of case 175/15 on 21 July 2015, which satisfied condition three. [Para 206][Para 209][Para 210][Para 211] No request for the labour inspector's authorisation was made, so the dismissal breached Article 293; under clause 34 of Prakas 099 the panel ordered reinstatement. [Para 214][Para 215][Para 217][Para 220][Para 221]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker elected as a union leader who is dismissed without the labour inspector's prior authorisation under Article 293 is entitled to reinstatement with back wages, and the 45-day post-election limit of Prakas no. 305 does not cut off an elected leader's protection where the dismissal follows within a reasonable period of the election and the employer already knew the names of the elected leaders. Failure to register the union does not bar the affected workers, who may bring the claim in person under clause 19 of Prakas no. 099. [Para 135][Para 139][Para 203][Para 215]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that fixed-duration contracts are disfavoured because indefinite contracts promote job security, and that ILO Recommendation 166 is persuasive but not binding, are context not needed once the two-year total was exceeded. [Para 149][Para 150][Para 155]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The dismissal date is printed two ways: the workers' narrative and the issue statement say 30 July 2015 [Para 83][Para 140], while the panel's findings say the employer dismissed them on 5 August 2015 [Para 201] and elsewhere that no dismissal occurred before the absence [Para 171]. Both dates are reported as printed.
- The union election date appears as 26 May 2015 [Para 196][Para 202], 6 May 2015 [Para 57], and garbled as "26 March 2016 May 2015" [Para 67].
- The panel states that "2 of the three conditions" were met [Para 212] after finding conditions one, two and three each satisfied [Para 200][Para 204][Para 211]; read as a typographical "3".
- A contract expiry date is printed as "7 September 2019 2015" [Para 79]; the reasons use 7 September 2015 [Para 158].
- Prakas 099 is dated 21 April 2004, but the reasoning once prints "21 April 2019 2004" [Para 138], and the internal rules are dated 27 June 2012 at [Para 161] but 27 June 2012 with article 6 at [Para 110].
- The two dismissed workers' names are on the face of the award and throughout the reasons; they are reduced to Mr C and Ms S here, and union officials and representatives to their offices, under the naming convention.
