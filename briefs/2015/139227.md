# Award No. 148/15 — Im and V Cutting International (Branch 3) (Arbitration Council, 16 July 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, chosen by the employer; Tun Si Phan, chosen by the workers; Von Saray, chair) · Doc ID 139227

## 1. Facts
Im and V Cutting International (Branch 3) (អ៊ឹម និង វ៉េ កាត់ដេរ អន្តរជាតិ), a garment factory at Chak Angre Krom, Phnom Penh with about 2,728 workers, faced thirteen demands for improved conditions filed on 23 May 2015. [Para 20][Para 42] The Federation of Democratic Workers' Unions took transfers of rights from 348 workers, and eight appeared at the hearing. [Para 43][Para 46][Para 47] Conciliation on 12 June 2015 failed on all thirteen points; at the hearing three were agreed, six withdrawn and two merged, leaving the overtime meal allowance, maternity pay, and the holiday incentive for the automatic building. [Para 21][Para 26][Para 27][Para 28] The parties chose a binding award for rights disputes and a non-binding one for interest disputes. [Para 29]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay an overtime meal allowance of 5,000 riel for 16:30 to 18:30 and 3,000 riel for 18:30 to 20:00, per day? [Para 48]
2. Whether the employer must pay 100 per cent of wages for four months' maternity leave? [Para 91]
3. Whether the employer must pay the 2.5 US dollar holiday incentive to automatic-building workers who work only eight hours, as it does for workers in other buildings, and raise that incentive where ten hours are required? [Para 96][Para 119]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The cost of eating before overtime exceeds the meal allowance the employer pays, so it must rise to 5,000 riel and 3,000 riel; 50 per cent of wages over three months is not enough to meet the costs of childbirth, and most women are not strong enough afterwards and take another month's unpaid leave; Article 106 requires equal pay for work of equal conditions and skill, so automatic-building workers doing eight hours on a holiday should receive the same 2.5 dollars as other buildings. [Para 50][Para 55][Para 56][Para 65][Para 67]
**Employer (respondent):** It cannot meet the meal claim because it only raised the allowance in early June 2015; it always grants the extra month of unpaid leave but pays no wages for it; and the automatic-building workers agreed orally to work ten hours on holidays to earn the 2.5 dollars, the machinery they inspect having to run continuously, so eight hours cannot be allowed. [Para 51][Para 57][Para 59][Para 68][Para 71]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — every remaining claim was refused, the rights claim on the evidence and the interest claims for want of a most-representative claimant; no sum was ordered. [Para 124][Para 126][Para 127]

Three preliminary findings framed the case. The applicant was the workers themselves and the 348 who transferred their rights, not the factory union. [Para 47] On the character of the dispute, the panel followed its own awards in holding that a rights dispute concerns an entitlement found in the Labour Law, an agreement, the internal rules, past practice or a collective agreement, and anything else is an interest dispute. [Para 99][Para 101] On standing, Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 makes an award on an interest dispute a substitute collective agreement binding all workers for a year, so only a most-representative union, or unions together holding over half the workforce, may bring one. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 85]

The overtime meal claim failed on both limbs. No provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, past practice or agreement fixed 5,000 and 3,000 riel, so the claim was an interest dispute, and the applicant was not most-representative. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 88][Para 89][Para 90] The panel noted in passing that the employer's current practice of 3,000 riel and 5,500 riel is more favourable than the law requires. [Para 74][Para 75]

The maternity claim failed the same way: no instrument required 100 per cent of wages for four months, so it was an interest dispute and outside the panel's reach for want of a most-representative claimant, 50 per cent over three months being the current practice. [Para 52][Para 91][Para 92][Para 94][Para 95]

The holiday incentive was the only rights dispute. Article 106 requires equal wages for work of equal conditions, professional skill and output, and the party asserting a breach carries the burden. [Para 98][Para 104] The automatic-building workers inspect machinery that must run continuously and therefore work ten hours, while other buildings' workers do eight; the workers offered no evidence comparing conditions, skill or output. [Para 105][Para 106][Para 110][Para 111] The employer's account of an oral agreement to work ten hours for 2.5 dollars went uncontradicted, and the panel found the employer had genuinely agreed those terms and, generously, would keep the incentive where a worker with good reason took two hours off. [Para 112][Para 113][Para 115][Para 116] The claim was therefore refused. [Para 117][Para 118] The associated demand to raise the incentive for a ten-hour holiday shift was an interest dispute and refused for want of standing. [Para 120][Para 122][Para 123]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a rate or benefit that no law, agreement, internal rule, past practice or collective agreement fixes is an interest dispute, and the panel will not decide it unless the claimant is the most-representative union or a group of unions representing over half the workforce — an award on it would displace the collective agreement for the whole plant. [Para 85][Para 99][Para 101] A claim of unequal pay under Article 106, by contrast, is a rights dispute the panel will hear, but it fails unless the claimant proves that the compared groups' conditions, professional skill and output are equal. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 111]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observations that the employer's existing meal allowance and its willingness to keep the holiday incentive for workers who take two hours' justified leave are more generous than the law requires did not affect any order. [Para 75][Para 116]
