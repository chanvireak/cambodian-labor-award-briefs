# Award No. 143/15 — Cambodia Beer Khmer Brew Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 28 July 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (You Soun Thy, employer-appointed; Sin Kim Sea, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · Doc ID 139222

## 1. Facts
The respondent brews and sells beer, employs about 2,200 staff and is registered under Inv. 050/95E of 2 August 1995. [Para 58][Para 59] On 18 May 2015 the Federation of Food and Service Workers' Unions of Cambodia filed eight demands; conciliation on 8 June 2015 failed on all eight and report No. 592 reached the Council on 15 June 2015. [Para 23][Para 25] Two hearings were held, on 29 June and 6 July 2015. [Para 22] Of 426 workers who thumbprinted the claim, 35 withdrew, leaving 391 claimants out of about 2,200 — under a fifth of the workforce. [Para 62][Para 64][Para 66]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must increase wages according to the worker's diploma — 10 USD for an associate degree and 20 USD for a bachelor's degree? [Para 2][Para 67]
2. Whether the employer must buy health insurance for all workers? [Para 4]
3. Whether the employer must grant two weeks' paternity leave to male workers to care for a wife after childbirth, without deducting from annual leave or wages? [Para 6]
4. Whether the employer must pay a 10 USD monthly travel allowance to workers at the Preah Sihanouk plant? [Para 8][Para 98]
5. Whether the employer must provide one meal per shift to workers at the Preah Sihanouk plant? [Para 10]
6. Whether the employer must stop deducting from sales staff's wages when a customer buys beer and does not pay the company? [Para 12][Para 229]
7. Whether the employer must stop discriminating, reinstate one dismissed worker (Mr C) and pay him back wages from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 14]
8. Whether the employer must raise all workers' wages by 30% above current levels? [Para 16]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** workers study at their own expense and time to gain diplomas, which raise their output for the company, so pay should follow the diploma; the production section is exposed to chemicals and the service section is required to drink beer while serving, so insurance is needed; the company already gives managers a fuel allowance of 60 litres (car) or 30 litres (motorbike) a month; and recouping unpaid customer debts from commission shifts the whole risk onto sales staff. [Para 68][Para 79][Para 99][Para 127]
**Employer (respondent):** pay rises follow an evaluation of effort, discipline and results, and some diplomas bear no relation to the job; insurance is not a legal duty and will be bought through the National Social Security Fund when available; the internal rules already give seven special leave days for a birth; the travel allowance and shift meal are not legal duties; the sales deduction is a condition accepted on recruitment and deters fraud; and it will not reinstate Mr C, though it will pay the five statutory heads of compensation. [Para 72][Para 74][Para 87][Para 115][Para 119][Para 167]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to stop deducting sales staff's wages where a customer fails to pay; it refused or rejected the other seven claims, ordering no money on them. [Para 250][Para 291][Para 296][Para 297]

Before the merits the panel examined standing. Under Article 268 of the Labour Law a union acquires legal rights only when its statutes and officers' list are registered; the factory union's registered leadership had completed its second term and no new leadership was recognised, so the old officers could not represent members. [Para 172][Para 174][Para 179] The 391 workers had, however, thumbprinted transfers of rights to the federation, and under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 a party may be represented by any person authorised in writing; the federation could therefore represent those 391 alone. [Para 180][Para 184][Para 185]

On issue 1 the panel found no provision of law, collective agreement, internal rules or practice requiring pay to follow diplomas, held the demand to exceed what the law secures, and called it an interest dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍). [Para 186][Para 187][Para 188] Applying Article 96 of the Labour Law, Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 — the last making an interest award a substitute collective agreement for a year and removing the right to strike — it held that only a most representative union, or unions with more than half the workforce, may bring such a claim, following Awards No. 81/04 Evergreen, No. 98/04 Great Union and No. 101/15. [Para 194][Para 195][Para 197][Para 198] The claimants being 391 of 2,200, they were not the most representative, so issues 1 to 5 and 8 were refused on that ground. [Para 200][Para 208][Para 290]

On issue 6 no such bar applied, because it concerned an existing right. Article 28 forbids pecuniary penalties, defined as any measure reducing the wage normally earned, and Article 103 lists commission among the components of wage; an agreement to the contrary is void under Article 13. [Para 229][Para 232][Para 239][Para 244] The panel rejected reliance on Articles 128 and 130, which concern advances of money or materials rather than liability for a customer's debt, found the deduction unlawful and ordered it stopped, noting that lawful checks before selling on credit remain open. [Para 245][Para 249][Para 250]

On issue 7 the panel held the claim a collective dispute under Article 302 — supported by a large part of the workforce and capable of disturbing the enterprise and social peace, the union having warned of a strike. [Para 253][Para 256][Para 259] On the merits it found no evidence of union discrimination, the workers asserting it without particulars, and applied Articles 12 and 279, which bar using union membership to decide hiring or dismissal. [Para 261][Para 266][Para 267] Under Article 74 a dismissal without a valid reason relating to aptitude or conduct is not absolutely prohibited: it may proceed on payment of compensation, unless a specific prohibition such as anti-union discrimination applies. [Para 270][Para 283] The employer having offered the five heads, the panel refused reinstatement and back pay. [Para 284][Para 297]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Commission is part of wages under Article 103, and a deduction imposed on sales staff to recover a customer's unpaid debt is a pecuniary penalty prohibited by Article 28, void even where the worker accepted it on recruitment; the employer's remedy is lawful supervision, not deduction. A dismissal without a valid reason is not forbidden outright where no specific prohibition such as anti-union discrimination applies, provided the employer pays the statutory compensation; but a demand for a benefit above the law is an interest dispute which only the most representative union, or unions representing more than half the workforce, may bring. [Para 232][Para 237][Para 244][Para 283][Para 197]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that Article 268 registration is what confers a union's legal rights, so leadership whose term has expired cannot represent members; and its suggestion that the employer tighten checks before selling on credit as an alternative to deduction. [Para 174][Para 179][Para 249]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The claimant headcount is printed three ways: 398 thumbprints at paragraph 62, 426 minus 35 withdrawals giving 391 at paragraphs 63 to 66, and 425 supporters at paragraph 165; the operative figure of 391 is used here. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 165] The travel allowance is 10 USD per month in the demand and reasoning but 100 USD in the operative order. [Para 216][Para 294] Issue 7 originally listed two workers; the second (Mr S) is withdrawn at paragraph 131 though the recitals still name him. [Para 14][Para 131] The hearings are dated 29 June and 6 July 2015, but the panel is said to have been constituted on 17 June while the report reached the secretariat on 15 June. [Para 21][Para 26] The chair appears as Pen Bun Chhea in the header and as "ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈឿន ឆាយ" in the signature block; the employer's counsel and the dismissed workers are referred to by initial. [Para 1][Para 299] OCR debris interrupts the reasoning at paragraphs 189, 213 and 288 ("ភ្នំ គូ", stray numerals inside the phrase "not the most representative union").
