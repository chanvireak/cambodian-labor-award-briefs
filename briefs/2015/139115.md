# Award No. 058/15 — Jiun Ye Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 23 March 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Tuon Siphann, worker-appointed; Pen Bunchhea, chair) · non-binding award · unofficial English translation of the Khmer original · Doc ID 139115

## 1. Facts
Jiun Ye Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd ("Jiun Ye") manufactures textiles at Spean Por village, Samaki Meanchey district, Kampong Chhnang, under commercial registration No. Inv. 0229 E/2012 of 24 January 2012, and employs approximately 3,870 workers. [Para 26][Para 27] The local unions of VKYUF, CUFWS, WUF and CLC had not obtained registration in the enterprise and were not authorised by its workers by any letter. [Para 27] Seven demands were conciliated without result; at the hearing the workers withdrew issue 4, and the employer's agreement of 23 February 2015 on maternity wages settled issue 6, leaving issues 1, 2, 3, 5 and 7. [Para 20][Para 22][Para 23] The panel heard the dispute only for the five workers present, who had been the claimants in case No. 353/14 against the same employer. [Para 33][Para 34]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate the 13 dismissed workers and maintain their other benefits? [Para 34]
2. Whether the employer must provide a realistic transport fee to drivers collecting workers for work on public holidays or Sundays? [Para 35]
3. Whether the employer must remove the head of the security department, Mr S, from his post? [Para 42][Para 91]
5. Whether the employer must pay a skills bonus of USD 15, USD 10 or USD 5 according to whether the worker operates a triple, double or single sewing machine? [Para 54]
7. Whether the employer must deduct the attendance bonus in proportion to days of leave taken for personal commitments? [Para 62]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The 13 dismissed workers committed no serious misconduct, the terminations by telephone were unlawful and harmed their benefits, and the dissenting opinion in case No. 353/14 supports them; the employer forces overtime on holidays and Sundays, so it should share the drivers' fee, which workers pay themselves at 2,000–4,000 riel a day; the security head abused, threatened and bullied workers at their homes, discriminated against the union, blocked dismissed workers and destroyed their ID cards and phones, conduct amounting to serious misconduct; workers who can run multi-needle machines work harder and should earn more; and an attendance bonus is wages, so it may only be deducted proportionally. [Para 35][Para 39][Para 40][Para 42][Para 43][Para 44][Para 45][Para 46][Para 47][Para 56][Para 57][Para 64][Para 65]
**Employer (respondent):** It complied with Award No. 353/14 and did not reinstate the 13; it does not force overtime, which is voluntary, and the USD 10.41 monthly transport and accommodation allowance exceeds the law; it terminated the 13 for unrest and serious misconduct, denies seizing or destroying phones, and admits visiting parents to inform them of their children's activities but denies threats; it has never paid a skills bonus and will instead promote capable multi-machine workers to section or team head; and its practice of deducting USD 2, 4 or 6 for one, two or three days' leave and the full bonus for four days or more is lawful. [Para 35][Para 41][Para 42][Para 47][Para 50][Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 57][Para 58][Para 65][Para 66][Para 67][Para 68]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted in form, dismissed in substance — the panel declined to consider issues 1, 2 and 5, rejected the demand to remove the manager under issue 3 but ordered the employer to refrain from conduct affecting workers' rights, dignity and reputation, and rejected issue 7; the award is non-binding and becomes binding eight days after notification. [Para 120][Para 121][Para 122][Para 123][Para 124]

On issues 1 and 7 the panel applied res judicata. Article 313 and the procedural rules are silent, so it held it has discretion not to reconsider an issue already decided, and set three conditions: the same parties argued the same issue, it was previously referred to the Council, and the Council decided its reasoning. All three were met because the five claimants and the enterprise were the parties in case No. 353/14 and that award had decided both issues, so it declined issue 1 and rejected issue 7. [Para 70][Para 72][Para 73][Para 75][Para 78][Para 79][Para 113][Para 119]

On issue 2 the panel applied Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012, which sets transport and accommodation allowance at USD 7 a month, against the USD 10.41 actually paid, and found no provision, regulation, agreement, collective agreement, past practice or internal rule requiring a separate drivers' fee. The claim was therefore an interest dispute; under Article 96(2) of the Labour Law, Clause 9 of Prakas No. 305 and Clause 43 of Prakas No. 099 an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement for a year and removes the right to strike, so it may only be made for a union with most-representative status. The five claimants held no such status and could not refer an interest dispute. [Para 81][Para 82][Para 83][Para 84][Para 88][Para 89][Para 90]

On issue 3 the panel applied Article 65(1) of the Labour Law and Article 311 of the Civil Code: only the parties to a contract may revoke the obligations it creates, so a third party cannot compel the employer to dismiss a colleague, and the workers could succeed only by showing the manager was dangerous and that retaining him endangered others' safety. They did not: the blocking of dismissed workers and the visits to parents were measures directed at those workers, not daily conduct endangering the workforce. But Clause 34 of Prakas No. 099 empowers the panel to order an immediate end to illegal conduct including retaliation, so it ordered the employer to stop conduct affecting workers' and parents' rights, dignity and reputation. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 95][Para 101][Para 102][Para 104][Para 105][Para 107][Para 108]

On issue 5 the panel found no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice creating a skills bonus — the practice had been never to pay one — so the claim was again an interest dispute and was declined for want of most-representative status. [Para 108][Para 109][Para 110][Para 111]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the same parties bring an issue the panel has already decided on its reasoning, the panel may decline to reconsider it, and it will do so where the parties, the referral and the earlier reasoned decision all coincide; and a claim for a benefit that no law, agreement, internal rule or practice grants is an interest dispute that the panel cannot entertain unless the referring union holds most-representative status. [Para 73][Para 84][Para 89][Para 110]

**Obiter dicta:** That visiting workers' parents at home with reinforcement personnel, though lawful in itself, is inappropriate and outside the manager's supervisory authority, which extends only to the premises and working hours; and that the enterprise should meet a transport and accommodation allowance of USD 10.41 where the law requires USD 7. [Para 81][Para 105][Para 106][Para 107]
