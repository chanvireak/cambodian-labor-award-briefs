# Award No. 175/15 — Camforever Co Ltd (Khem Hou Ever) (Arbitration Council, 11 August 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3 — Seng Vouchhun (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Sok Mathoeung (president, chosen by the two) · Doc ID 139244

## 1. Facts
Camforever Co Ltd, a garment factory at Stung Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employed about 1,900 workers. [Para 52] Mr M was a warehouse assistant and a helper delegate (ប្រតិភូជំនួយ) from 25 October 2011; Mr N was a warehouse worker from October 2012 and a founder of the Khmer Union at the plant; both worked on successive six-month fixed-term contracts after a two-month probation. [Para 57] A union leadership election was held on 26 May 2015, at which Mr M was elected president and Mr N stood but was not elected. [Para 66] On 22 May 2015 the director saw Mr N punching Mr M's attendance card, and both workers admitted the practice had gone on for about a year. [Para 69][Para 70] The company suspended both from 22 to 27 May 2015 on 50% wage, then dismissed them on 28 May 2015 when they returned. [Para 58][Para 59]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the two workers' contracts were fixed-term or indefinite-term? [Para 122]
2. Whether the dismissals resulted from union discrimination? [Para 143]
3. Whether the dismissal of Mr M, a helper delegate, was lawful? [Para 158]
4. Whether Mr N was a protected worker, whether he committed grave misconduct, and whether his dismissal was lawful? [Para 177][Para 201][Para 224]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissals were procedurally unlawful and motivated by union discrimination; Mr M was a delegate and Mr N a union founder, both were barred at the gate on 28 May 2015, and both should be reinstated with back pay from the date of dismissal. [Para 110][Para 113][Para 114]
**Employer (respondent):** the two workers cheated the company by punching each other's cards for roughly a year, which it treats as grave misconduct under Article 83 of the Labour Law; it notified the Ministry and sought authorisation to dismiss, denies any anti-union motive, and says it never received notice of the election result. [Para 115][Para 157]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer is ordered to take both Mr M and Mr N back from the date the award takes effect, and to pay back wages from the date of dismissal until the date of return (no sum is quantified in the award). [Para 234][Para 235]

On duration, the panel applied Article 67(2) and Article 73(5) of the Labour Law: a fixed-term contract may not exceed two years, and successive renewals beyond that limit convert the relationship into an indefinite-term contract. [Para 122][Para 127] It followed its own Award No. 10/03 (Zaksintek), which read the two-year cap as applying to the aggregate of all renewals rather than to each renewal. [Para 128] Both parties agreed Mr M had served 3 years 7 months 3 days and Mr N about 2 years 7 months, and the employer had shown no written notice of expiry, so the panel found both contracts indefinite. [Para 138][Para 142]

On discrimination, the panel restated that the party alleging discrimination carries the burden of proof. [Para 149] No document showed the employer knew the composition of the elected leadership before 28 May 2015, and the workers produced no evidence linking the dismissals to union activity, so this ground failed. [Para 152][Para 153]

On Mr M, the panel found he was a helper delegate and that Article 293(1) requires the labour inspector's consent before a protected delegate can be dismissed. [Para 159] The employer lodged its dismissal request on 25 May 2015 but dismissed him on 28 May 2015 without any authorisation, which had still not arrived by the hearing. [Para 172][Para 173] Under Article 4(4) of Prakas No. 305 the measure was therefore void, and the panel ordered reinstatement with back pay. [Para 169][Para 175][Para 176]

On Mr N, the panel applied Article 3(3) of Prakas No. 305, which protects election candidates for 45 days before and 45 days after the poll if they are not elected, subject to three cumulative conditions including that the union notify the employer of the candidacies by any workable means. [Para 177][Para 178][Para 184] He met the first two conditions but not the third: the union's notice travelled only through GMAC, which replied that it had no arrangement to pass such notices on, so the employer learned of the election only at the Ministry conciliation on 3 July 2015. [Para 105][Para 196][Para 198] He thus held no protected status. [Para 199] The panel nevertheless held the dismissal unlawful: the internal rules and card-control notice prescribe three graded steps — oral warning, written warning, then dismissal — and the employer skipped them all. [Para 216] Card-punching was not listed as fraud or as grave misconduct enabling immediate dismissal, and the employer adduced no evidence of grave misconduct. [Para 220][Para 222] Since Article 74 allows termination of an indefinite contract only for a reason related to the worker's aptitude or conduct or the needs of the enterprise, the dismissal was unlawful and reinstatement followed. [Para 225][Para 231]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a fixed-term contract is renewed successively so that the aggregate exceeds two years, it becomes an indefinite-term contract, and the two-year cap in Article 67(2) is measured against the total engagement, not each renewal [Para 122][Para 127][Para 128]. A worker who is not shown to hold protected status may still only be dismissed for a lawful reason under Article 74, and an employer whose own internal rules grade penalties in stages must exhaust those stages; conduct the rules do not list as grave misconduct cannot be converted into a ground for immediate dismissal [Para 216][Para 221][Para 220][Para 222][Para 232]. Candidate protection under Prakas No. 305 depends on the union actually conveying the candidacies to the employer by some workable means [Para 184][Para 197][Para 198].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982 is not binding but is useful context for reading Article 67, and observed that Cambodian labour law leans toward indefinite-term contracts because job security serves both worker and employer. [Para 132][Para 134][Para 129]

The employer's request to the Ministry is dated 23 May 2015 at [Para 5] and 25 May 2015 at [Para 172]. The union's officers are named as Chhay Chantha and Chan Sochin at [Para 53] but as Si Tha and Sok Chin at [Para 66]. The employer-appointed arbitrator's signature block lost his name at [Para 237], though it appears in the header. One stray reference to "ក្រុមហ៊ុន L" survives at [Para 5]. Individual workers are rendered inconsistently across the text (Mouv Leanghao / Mory Leanghao); they are given as Mr M and Mr N here.
