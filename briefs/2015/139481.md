# Award No. 312/15 — Kamchay Mar Trading (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 29 December 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Uk Rithy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Kong Phalla (president) · Doc ID 139481

## 1. Facts
The National Union of the Kamchay Mar Garment Industry, at a weaving and garment plant of about 2,500 workers in Prey Veng, lodged ten demands and six went unresolved: holiday pay, a day-care centre, union interference, contract length, conversion of long contracts, and the transfer of one worker. [Para 13][Para 52][Para 20] At the hearing the parties agreed the employer had not interfered with the union, so that item fell away, leaving five. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 20] Before the merits the panel ruled on who was properly before it, holding that the union's second-term leadership was not yet recognised by the Ministry of Labour and that no written authority from the workers had been given to the federation, leaving only the five workers present as claimants. [Para 55][Para 57][Para 113]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay wages for work on paid public holidays amounting to three times the ordinary day's wage (ordinary wage × 2 + 1)? [Para 115]
2. Whether the employer must provide childminders in the workplace day-care centre by 15 December 2015? [Para 140]
3. (Issue 3, union interference, was settled at the hearing and not considered.) [Para 68]
4. Whether the employer must issue six-month fixed-term contracts instead of three-month contracts? [Para 153]
5. Whether the employer must convert the fixed-term contracts of workers whose total service exceeds two years into contracts of indefinite duration? [Para 180]
6. Whether the employer must transfer Mr C back to Building A in his original post as lot recorder? [Para 202]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Most factories in Phnom Penh pay double the ordinary wage plus one for holiday work, and the company already pays only one extra day; the day-care centre exists but has had no childminder for a long time; three-month contracts are too short and leave workers afraid and the union losing members; about 70 per cent of members have worked over two years on fixed terms. [Para 61][Para 66][Para 73][Para 77]**Employer (respondent):** It pays holiday work in accordance with Prakas No. 10 and cannot afford more; it needs time to advertise and recruit childminders, promising the first week of January 2016; contract length depends on orders received; no worker has a fixed-term contract exceeding two years; and Mr C knowingly accepted the warehouse post at the same wage. [Para 63][Para 67][Para 74][Para 76][Para 95]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand for triple holiday pay was refused; the employer was ordered to install childminders by the first week of January 2016; the six-month contract demand was refused; the employer was ordered to convert to indefinite-duration contracts the workers whose aggregate fixed-term service exceeds two years; and Mr C's transfer demand was refused. [Para 211][Para 212][Para 214][Para 215]

On the holiday-pay claim the panel read Article 164 with Article 4 of Prakas No. 10 of 4 February 1999: a worker who works on a paid public holiday receives, besides the holiday pay already earned, compensation equal to one ordinary day's wage. [Para 115][Para 117][Para 118] That practice is what the company already follows and is lawful, and no statute, collective agreement or contract obliges an employer to pay three times. [Para 123][Para 124] The claim exceeded what the law provides and was a dispute over interests rather than rights. [Para 125][Para 126] Because the claimant union was not the most representative union in the enterprise, the panel declined to consider that interest dispute at all, citing Article 96 and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 132][Para 137][Para 138]

On the day-care centre the panel applied Article 186, which requires an enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a nursing room and a day-care centre, and Article 187, under which a Prakas sets the standards. [Para 140][Para 144] No such Prakas has yet been issued, but the panel held a centre should at minimum have a childminder and a clean environment. [Para 145][Para 146] Since the centre exists but is unstaffed, and recruitment genuinely takes time, the panel refused the 15 December deadline and ordered compliance by the first week of January 2016 — the employer's own undertaking. [Para 149][Para 150][Para 151]

On the six-month contract demand the panel applied Articles 65 and 66 and Civil Code Articles 311, 336 and 337: a contract arises from matching offer and acceptance, and neither party may impose a form or term the other does not accept. [Para 153][Para 158][Para 160][Para 162] The workers' proposal was lawful and so was the employer's refusal; neither could compel the other, so the claim was refused. [Para 174][Para 175][Para 177][Para 179]

On conversion the panel applied Article 67(2) and Article 73(5), again measuring the two-year ceiling by aggregate duration rather than by each renewal, and cited ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982 as context. [Para 186][Para 189][Para 193][Para 195] It found from the contracts and the employer's own staff list — which the employer did not contest — that the claimants' total fixed-term service already exceeded two years while they remained on fixed terms. [Para 185][Para 198] The employer was therefore barred from issuing further fixed-term contracts to them and was ordered to convert. [Para 199][Para 201]

On the transfer the panel applied the same contract principles: Mr C signed successive warehouse contracts from 24 January 2014 to 28 August 2015 knowing the work, with no change of shift or wage and only about five metres between the buildings, so the original lot-recorder contract had come to an end and been replaced by agreement. [Para 206][Para 207][Para 208][Para 209] The claim was refused. [Para 210]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may not keep issuing fixed-term contracts to a worker whose successive contracts already total more than two years; at that point Article 67(2) requires the relationship to be converted to one of indefinite duration, and the five per cent indemnity paid at each expiry does not prevent the conversion. [Para 189][Para 199][Para 200]

**Obiter dicta:** Neither party to an employment contract can force the other to adopt a contract form or term it has not accepted, so a demand for longer fixed-term contracts fails even though it is lawful to make. [Para 175][Para 177] A day-care centre is defective in substance if it has no childminder, even before the Article 187 Prakas is issued. [Para 146]
