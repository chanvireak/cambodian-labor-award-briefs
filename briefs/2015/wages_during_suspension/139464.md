# Award No. 290/15 — Great On Neur Thek Sthay Fek Theri Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 8 December 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Aing Eng Thong (president), Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed) · Doc ID 139464

## 1. Facts
The company is a garment cutting and weaving enterprise registered in 2011, employing about 1,250 workers [Para 48][Para 49]. On 2 November 2015 it suspended all its workers' contracts for two months, from 10 November 2015 to 10 January 2016, and the labour inspector authorised the suspension [Para 23][Para 60][Para 88]. During the suspension the company paid each worker USD 30 a month as accommodation costs plus a seniority bonus, although it had never provided housing before [Para 59][Para 98][Para 108]. Two federations — the Khmer Youth Union Federation and the Cambodian Workers' Union Federation, the latter holding transfers of rights from 255 workers — brought the claim, and 20 workers attended the hearing [Para 51][Para 55][Para 56].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay wages at 50 percent for the two months in which the contracts were suspended, from 10 November 2015 to 10 January 2016? [Para 57][Para 61]
2. Whether the employer must refrain from removing goods from the factory and from changing the company's name, and pay five heads of severance if the name changes? [Para 57][Para 58]
3. Whether the employer must provide accommodation during the suspension, or pay 50 percent wages in its place? [Para 91][Para 92]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** during a suspension the employer still owes wages and lodging; the company in fact had work, gave overtime, hired casual labour and sent goods to other companies, so the suspension was not genuine; USD 30 does not cover rent of USD 25 to 50 a month, and the law requires at least USD 7 [Para 63][Para 64][Para 91].

**Employer (respondent):** the suspension was authorised by the labour inspector, so no wages are owed for it; the employer accepted that it formerly had work and hired casual labour, but only before the suspension; the law requires only USD 7 for travel and lodging, and it chose to pay USD 30 plus a seniority bonus out of concern for the workers; no goods have been removed and there is no intention to change the company name [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67][Para 68][Para 69].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the claim for 50 percent wages over the suspension was refused, and the claim about removing goods and changing the company name was not decided because the parties had taken the same position on it; no sum was awarded [Para 73][Para 74][Para 113][Para 114]

The panel began with Article 71(11) of the Labour Law, which allows suspension of an enterprise's activity for serious economic or material difficulty and requires the suspension to be placed under the labour inspector's supervision, for no more than two months [Para 75][Para 76]. Following earlier awards, it read supervision as requiring notice to the labour inspector and an on-site check and confirmation that the difficulty is real [Para 78][Para 79][Para 80]. Article 72(1) then provides that suspension affects only the essential obligations of the contract — the worker's duty to work and the employer's duty to pay — while other obligations continue [Para 81][Para 82]. Earlier awards had drawn the consequence both ways: where the suspension procedure is properly followed the employer owes no wages, and where it is not the employer owes 100 percent [Para 82][Para 83]. In this case both parties accepted, and the panel found, that the suspension from 10 November 2015 to 10 January 2016 was authorised by the labour inspector, so the procedure was lawful and no wages were owed for those two months [Para 88][Para 89][Para 111][Para 112]. The claim for 50 percent was accordingly refused [Para 113].

On accommodation the panel applied Article 72 again and Award No. 01/07 — Sokh Prim: the employer's duty to provide lodging continues during a suspension only where such a duty already existed — by law, collective agreement, internal rules or past practice — while the worker was working [Para 92][Para 93][Para 95]. The panel noted that the duty arises particularly in agriculture under Article 204, where workers engaged permanently at one place are entitled to free housing [Para 96]. Here the company had never provided housing before the suspension, and no law, collective agreement, internal rule or practice imposed the duty [Para 98][Para 99][Para 100]. The statutory floor under Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 is USD 7 a month for travel and lodging for garment, textile and footwear workers, and workers given transport or lodging do not receive it [Para 101][Para 102][Para 105]. Since the employer had paid USD 30 a month, more than the statutory minimum, and had done so voluntarily and throughout the two months, the panel found the payment more favourable than the law required and no further sum due [Para 108][Para 109][Para 110].

The second claim was not adjudicated: both sides said no goods had been removed and no name change was intended, so the panel treated the point as settled and declined to consider it [Para 73][Para 74].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a suspension of contracts is authorised by the labour inspector under Article 71(11) and kept within two months, it is lawful and the employer owes no wages for the suspended period; if the procedure is not followed, full wages are owed [Para 82][Para 83]. The employer's duty to house workers continues through a suspension only where it already existed by law, collective agreement, internal rule or practice, and a voluntary payment above the statutory USD 7 travel-and-lodging allowance discharges it [Para 93][Para 95][Para 105].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the housing duty under Article 72 attaches particularly to permanent agricultural work under Article 204 [Para 96], and that a party which states the same position as its opponent leaves nothing for the panel to decide [Para 73].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award date appears as 8 December 2015 on its face [Para 1], but the suspension period ends "10 January 2015" in one place [Para 58] and "10 January 2019 2016" in another [Para 70]; the panel's constitution is dated "13 November 2023 2015" [Para 15], and the hearing date is given as 11 November 2015 in the procedural section but 20 November 2014 in the invitation paragraph [Para 11][Para 16]. The company name is printed in at least three forms — "Great On Neur Thek Sthay", "…Thek Sthay Fek Theri" and "…Thek Sthay Fe - Theri" — and its registration number appears as both Inv.1195 E/2011 and 8649 [Para 1][Para 22][Para 27][Para 48]. The worker-side union is described once as registered to a different company ("Vinner") [Para 33]. Numbers of claimants are inconsistent: 255 transfers of rights, 20 at the hearing [Para 51][Para 55], and the phrase "50 percent" is used both for wages and for a five-head severance claim [Para 57]. Stray digits and English fragments ("1939", "typhal", "Montarti") remain in the text [Para 91][Para 116].
