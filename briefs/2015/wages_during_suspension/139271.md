# Award No. 207/15 — Center World Garment (Arbitration Council, 27 August 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Ang Eng Thong, chair chosen by both) · non-binding award · Doc ID 139271

## 1. Facts
The company sews garments on National Road 2 in Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, and employed about 210 workers, all on fixed-duration contracts of two, three or four months. From 18 May to 30 June 2015 it had no work and told the workers to stay home, paying 50 per cent of wages; it says it had no orders, was closing down, and applied to the labour inspectorate to suspend but received no decision. 189 workers transferred their representation to the Cambodia Confederation of Trade Unions by letter of 5 June 2015, and the union pressed one remaining demand for the missing half of the wages. [Para 44][Para 47][Para 48][Para 53][Para 54][Para 44]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay 100 per cent of wages for the period from 18 May to 30 June 2015 when it had no work to give? [Para 58]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer had no work for them and no written authorisation from the labour inspector to suspend the contracts, so the suspension was unlawful and the full wage is owed for the whole period, not the 50 per cent paid. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 52]
**Employer (respondent):** The company faced genuine economic difficulty, with no work and no buyers, and was in the process of closing; it applied to the labour inspectorate for a suspension but the inspector neither decided nor came to inspect, and it can afford only 50 per cent plus the other benefits it has maintained. [Para 3][Para 53][Para 54]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to pay a further 50 per cent of wages so that the workers receive 100 per cent for the unlawful suspension: to 170 workers from 18 May 2015 to 30 June 2015, and to 19 workers from 18 May 2015 to 17 June 2015, the day their contracts were ended. [Para 81][Para 82]

The panel began from Article 71 (11) of the Labour Law, under which an enterprise in serious economic or material difficulty may suspend activity for no more than two months, and that suspension must be placed under the supervision of the labour inspector. Following its earlier awards, it read supervision as requiring notification to the inspector, an inspection and written confirmation that the difficulty is real, and a written authorisation of the suspension; where suspension is lawful the employer owes no wages, and where it is not, full wages are owed although no work was provided. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 61][Para 62][Para 64][Para 65][Para 66][Para 67]

On the facts the panel found that the employer had filed a request covering 18 May to 30 June 2015 but that nothing in the file showed any authorisation from the labour inspector, so the suspension was not in accordance with Article 71 and the employer owed 100 per cent. Since 50 per cent had been paid, it ordered the balance for the suspension period. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 71][Para 72][Para 73]

On the arithmetic, the panel noted that of 189 workers who had transferred representation, 19 were among the 22 whose fixed-duration contracts were not renewed on 17 June 2015, so their period ends on that date while the remaining 170 are covered to 30 June 2015. It expressly declined to decide whether the non-renewal of those 22 contracts was lawful, because that question was not among the issues before it. [Para 57][Para 76][Para 77][Para 78]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of employment contracts for economic difficulty is lawful only where the employer has notified the labour inspector, the inspector has inspected the enterprise and confirmed the difficulty in writing, and the suspension has been authorised in writing; absent that authorisation the employer must pay full wages for the whole period, although no work was given, and any 50 per cent already paid is deducted. [Para 62][Para 65][Para 67][Para 70]

**Obiter dicta:** That all contracts in the enterprise were fixed-duration, none exceeding six months, and that 5 per cent was paid on each termination; and that the lawfulness of not renewing 22 fixed-duration contracts fell outside the issue and was not decided. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 77]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel's constitution is dated "07 July 2019 2015". The conciliation report No. 882 is dated 6 August 2015 although the final conciliation session is dated 27 July 2015, and the employer's request to suspend is dated 10 May 2015 for a period starting 18 May 2015 while the complainant union's letter is dated 5 June 2015. The award order writes the end of the period as "30 December June 2015". The company's registration appears under two different instruments, a Ministry of Commerce letter of 10 April 2014 and a certificate Inv. 1048 KH/2014 of 28 March 2014, and its name is transliterated at least five ways. The workforce is put at about 210 but 189 transfers are counted. Worker and union representatives are redacted. [Para 14][Para 8][Para 12][Para 27][Para 79][Para 21][Para 43][Para 44]
