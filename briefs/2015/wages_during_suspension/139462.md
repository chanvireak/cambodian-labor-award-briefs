# Award No. 285/15 — Teng Sun Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 December 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Aing Eng Thong (president), Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed) · Doc ID 139462

## 1. Facts
Teng Sun Ltd makes bags. Its workers were engaged on fixed-duration contracts of three months each, and in July and August 2015 the company suspended the contracts of part of its workforce, agreeing with the unions on 10 August 2015 to pay 50% of wages for the unexpired term together with a 5% premium of USD 20 [Para 38][Para 51][Para 54]. The workers then elected to end their contracts and receive the 50% wage payment, but say the 5% end-of-contract indemnity was never paid [Para 44][Para 47][Para 48]. The union federation took up 114 workers' claims; six workers attended the hearing [Para 34][Para 37].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay wages at 100% instead of 50% for the unexpired term of the fixed-duration contracts, and pay the 5% end-of-contract indemnity? [Para 38][Para 61]
2. Whether the employer must pay 100% wages, rather than the 50% paid, for the period in which the workers' contracts were suspended in July and August 2015, no notice having been given to the labour inspector? [Para 53]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer had no valid reason to end the contracts; they claim a top-up from 50% to 100% of daily wages for the unexpired term, plus the 5% indemnity for the last fixed-duration contract of each of the 114 claimants, and full wages for the suspension months because the employer never obtained the labour inspector's authorisation [Para 39][Para 40][Para 56][Para 58].

**Employer (respondent):** it did not terminate the contracts but only gave the workers a rest, offering two options — 50% wages during the rest with no 5% indemnity, or termination with 50% wages for the remaining term plus the 5% indemnity — and those who chose termination were paid both parts; strikes had broken out three or four times a month and buyers withdrew orders [Para 41][Para 43][Para 45][Para 49][Para 50].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim for wages at 100% for the unexpired term was dismissed; the employer was ordered to pay the 5% end-of-contract indemnity on the last fixed-duration contract of each worker; the claim for 100% wages during the suspension in July and August 2015 was dismissed. No global sum was fixed [Para 95][Para 111][Para 112][Para 113]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 73(1) and (2) of the Labour Law: a fixed-duration contract ends at its term, may be dissolved early by mutual agreement made in writing before the labour inspector and signed by both parties, and may otherwise be dissolved early only for serious misconduct or force majeure [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65][Para 66]. The contracts here were indeed ended before their term [Para 67], but the minutes of the 10 August 2015 meeting and the report of 14 September 2015 showed that all the workers agreed to take 50% of the remaining daily wages, with the labour inspector present, so the ending was lawful under Article 73(1) and not a unilateral act of the employer [Para 68][Para 69][Para 76][Para 77]. The top-up to 100% was therefore refused [Para 79]. The 5% indemnity stood differently: Article 73(6) requires the employer on termination to pay an indemnity at least equal to 5% of the wages paid during the contract [Para 80][Para 81]. Under Article 118 of the Labour Law the employer bears the burden of proving payment [Para 84][Para 85], following Award No. 361/14 — Kent Sport Shoes [Para 86]; the employer produced nothing by the 02 December 2015 deadline [Para 90][Para 91][Para 92], so the panel ordered the 5% indemnity to be paid [Para 94][Para 95].

On issue 2 the panel applied Article 71(11): where an enterprise faces serious economic or material difficulty, suspension of activity must be placed under the labour inspector's supervision and may not exceed two months [Para 97][Para 98]. Following Awards No. 58/07 and 08/07, supervision means notifying and obtaining the labour inspector's prior authorisation [Para 99][Para 100][Para 101]. The panel found the employer suspended the contracts without that prior authorisation, so the suspension was not lawful and full wages were owed for July and August 2015 [Para 107][Para 108]. The claim was nonetheless refused, because the workers never identified which or how many of them had been suspended and were claiming on this head [Para 55][Para 109][Para 110].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-duration contract ended by written agreement made before the labour inspector and signed by both parties is lawfully dissolved under Article 73(1), so no wages are owed for the unexpired term [Para 63][Para 76]; the 5% end-of-contract indemnity under Article 73(6) nevertheless remains due on that contract, and the employer must prove payment [Para 81][Para 85]. A suspension of contracts without the labour inspector's prior authorisation breaches Article 71(11) and obliges the employer to pay full wages for the suspended period [Para 98][Para 101], but only to workers the claim identifies [Para 109][Para 110].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel restated the general rule that the party asserting or accusing bears the burden of proof [Para 102], and recorded without deciding anything that neither side disputed that 60 to 70 percent of contracts were suspended in July and August 2015 [Para 54].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The workforce figure is printed two ways: 128 workers signed the transfer of rights [Para 33] but the federations claimed for 114 [Para 34]. The panel's constitution is dated "10 November year year 2015" [Para 11] and the extension of the award date reads "03 December 2021 2015" [Para 18]. Contract length appears as "15 3 months" [Para 67]. Notices and minutes are attributed to different federations under inconsistent names — "Khmer Workers' Union Federation", "Labour Movement Union Federation" and "Union Federation for Building Workers' Rights" — for what appears to be the same body [Para 32][Para 51]. Stray English fragments ("Ling Gilliam", "John") and a duplicated final order block [Para 69][Para 99][Para 113] indicate OCR contamination, and [Para 87] orders payment of USD 100 with a cross-reference to Award No. 026/15 that reads as text carried over from another award.
