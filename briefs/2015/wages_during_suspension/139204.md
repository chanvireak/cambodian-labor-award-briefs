# Award No. 116/15 — Siem Reap International Airport (CAMS) (Arbitration Council, 23 June 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, employer-appointed; An Nan, worker-appointed; Kong Pollapha, presiding) · Doc ID 139204

## 1. Facts
Cambodia Airports Management Services (CAMS) operates Siem Reap International Airport under a public-service mandate with 479 employees, of whom roughly 300 belong to the claimant union. [Para 82][Para 85][Para 87] After a December 2014 strike and a 4 December 2014 settlement agreement, the union filed seven collective demands, conciliation failed on all seven, and the Ministry of Labour referred them to the panel on 18 May 2015. [Para 22][Para 56][Para 213] The disputed acts are: use of unregistered internal regulations, a USD 200 payment to non-strikers, hiring of contractor staff, two suspensions, a dismissal of a union activist, withheld allowances and multi-skilling. [Para 2][Para 105][Para 130][Para 147]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to register its internal regulations (បទបញ្ជាផ្ទៃក្នុង) in accordance with the law? [Para 94][Para 227]
2. Whether the employer discriminated against the union by using its right to hire outside staff, and whether it must pay USD 200 to every worker who joined the strike? [Para 105][Para 248]
3. Whether the employer must take back Mr T and pay his wages for the period of suspension? [Para 130][Para 325]
4. Whether the employer must pay language and skill allowances to all Weight & Balance staff? [Para 147][Para 383]
5. Whether the panel has jurisdiction over the demand to reinstate union activist Mr P, and if so what severance he is owed? [Para 443][Para 462]
6. Whether the employer must repay USD 228,20 deducted from Mr S's wages during his suspension? [Para 201][Para 519]
7. Whether the employer must suspend its multi-skilling programme pending resolution? [Para 213][Para 526]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Article 24 of the Labour Law makes labour-inspector visa mandatory before internal regulations may be applied; the USD 200 paid only to non-strikers was indirect punishment contrary to Article 333 and chilled union membership; the suspensions and dismissal were anti-union reprisals and the allowances were contractual entitlements under the internal regulations. [Para 95][Para 111][Para 149][Para 222]
**Employer (respondent):** The old regulations were visaed but lost, and the ministry has not yet answered; hiring contractor staff is an operational right under Article 2 and clause 5 of the collective agreement, as Award 57/12 held; the USD 200 was a thank-you gift paid after the strike ended, including to 20 strikers; the allowances rest on managerial discretion. [Para 98][Para 116][Para 122][Para 425]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must obtain a labour-inspector visa for its internal regulations before applying them, and must pay 100% of wages and back pay to Mr T for the suspension period should the court find him without fault; the employer must pay Mr P notice pay under Article 75, dismissal compensation under Article 89, unused annual leave under Articles 166–167 and final wages under Article 116; all other demands dismissed. [Para 540][Para 542][Para 545][Para 547]

On issue 1 the panel read Article 24 as imposing a duty to consult worker representatives and then obtain the labour inspector's visa, and held that a party asserting facts bears the burden of proof; the employer produced no document showing a visaed regulation ever existed, so the panel refused to presume one. [Para 227][Para 243][Para 246]

On issue 2 the panel applied res judicata (អាជ្ញាអស់ជំនុំ) and found the two prior-award conditions met for the contractor-hiring complaint, following Award 57/12; but because the discrimination claim itself was new, it examined it and found no evidence, since 184 union members and 20 strikers were among the 200 recipients. [Para 249][Para 252][Para 264][Para 321] On the USD 200, Article 332 suspends wages during a strike, yet the employer had maintained full wages and benefits; the payment was thus a discretionary gratuity, not a penalty under Article 333. [Para 295][Para 301][Para 322]

On issue 3 the panel found suspension pending investigation is not among the eleven grounds in Article 71 or in Article 332, but is not prohibited either; it is lawful if wages continue. Because Article 38 of the Constitution presumes innocence until final conviction, the employer owed full wages, and the reinstatement demand was refused as the contract remains merely suspended, not terminated. [Para 346][Para 354][Para 374][Para 381]

On issue 4 the panel held the internal regulation's allowance clause is expressly conditioned on managerial decision by role; the employer substituted foreign-language training for the language allowance, which the union conceded, and the 17-name claim list bore no signatures or thumbprints, so no clear basis for adjudication existed. [Para 401][Para 405][Para 407]

On issue 5 the panel first satisfied itself of jurisdiction: a union-backed demand affecting 300+ members meets all three Article 302 conditions even where only one worker is affected, since a strike remains possible. [Para 451][Para 454][Para 457] On the merits, the USD 150 taken from a passenger without an invoice was a valid ground for termination but was not a serious fault under Article 83 or Article IV of the regulations, so Articles 75, 89, 166 and 116 applied; no mental-damages award under Article 91 because the employer had proper grounds. [Para 483][Para 490][Para 512]

On issues 6 and 7 the panel held the suspension fell under Article 71(7), and Mr S had signed the reinstatement letter accepting no wages; on multi-skilling it found the 4 December 2014 agreement's clause 5 did not record any suspension of the programme. [Para 521][Para 524][Para 537]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that applies internal regulations without the labour inspector's visa breaches Article 24, and the party asserting that a visaed version once existed bears the burden of proving it. [Para 246][Para 243] A suspension pending investigation or criminal proceedings is lawful outside Article 71 but only if full wages continue, because Article 38 of the Constitution presumes the worker innocent until final conviction. [Para 354][Para 374] A gratuity paid after a lawful strike to a group that includes strikers and union members is a discretionary management act, not indirect punishment under Article 333. [Para 322]

**Obiter dicta:** That a single-worker demand backed by a 300-member union can constitute a collective dispute, and that "may cause" disruption suffices under Article 302 without actual disruption. [Para 457][Para 458]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel's creation date is printed "18 May 2021 2015" (line 23) and the award date appears only in the header. The rehire letter for Mr S is dated 17 March 2015 in the document list (line 69) but "17 September March 2015" in the reasoning (line 522). Line 289 states the USD 200 went to "2 workers" while line 125 and line 314 state 184 recipients including 20 strikers. Mr P's name is OCR-rendered inconsistently (ពេជ្រ សោភ័ណ្ឌ / ពេជ្រ សុភ័ណ្ឌ), and the amount "USD 228,20" retains the printed comma decimal. Article IV is noted in the award itself as mis-numbered "Article V" in the regulations. [Para 69][Para 125][Para 289][Para 469][Para 522]
