# Award No. 271/15 — FCF Inspection (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 27 November 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tai Seng appointed by the employer, Sin Kim Sean appointed by the workers, Pen Bun Chhea chair appointed by both) · Doc ID 139453

## 1. Facts
FCF Inspection (Cambodia) Co., Ltd., a garment-inspection company on Street 217, Dangkao, Phnom Penh, employing about 300, suspended work intermittently in April, June, July and September 2015 and paid only 50 per cent of wages during those periods. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 52] The union — the Democratic Union of Garment Workers of the company, with about 248 members and 162 workers who transferred their rights to the federation — demanded full wages and benefits for the suspended days and pro rata deduction of the USD 10 attendance bonus. [Para 42][Para 45][Para 46] The employer was represented at the hearing by its general manager, Ms O Yoshiko, whose authority the workers challenged because she was not named in the letter of delegation. [Para 47][Para 79]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (ក) Whether the suspensions of work in April, June, July and September 2015 were carried out in accordance with legal procedure? [Para 96]
2. (ខ) Whether the agreement under which workers accepted 50 per cent of wages during a suspension is valid? [Para 108]
3. (គ) Whether the employer must top up that 50 per cent to 100 per cent, and pay 100 per cent transport and accommodation allowance, a 6,000 riel daily meal allowance and USD 1 daily fuel allowance, for those four months? [Para 117]
4. Whether the employer must deduct the regular attendance bonus pro rata, according to the days a worker was absent with the employer's lawful permission? [Para 134]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer never gave proper advance notice before there was no work and sent workers home on arrival, sometimes notifying them only one day ahead; it never obtained the labour inspector's authorisation for any suspension; the 50 per cent wage paid was not enough to live on; and the attendance bonus, which is part of wages under Article 103, must be cut only in proportion to the days of authorised absence, not forfeited entirely. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 68][Para 76][Para 77]
**Employer (respondent):** the company inspects garments for other firms rather than sewing, so suspensions are irregular and it is impossible to seek authorisation every time; it is not a continuous suspension; a contract clause accepted by the workers provides 50 per cent wages when there is no work, has been applied for six months and was notified through the section team leaders; and it wishes to keep its old practice of deducting the whole attendance bonus for any absence, since otherwise workers would take more leave. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65][Para 66][Para 71][Para 72]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted on both issues — the panel ordered the employer to pay 50 per cent daily wages on top of the 50 per cent already paid (100 per cent in total), 100 per cent transport and accommodation allowance, 6,000 riel daily meal allowance and USD 1 daily fuel allowance for the four months, to union members and workers who transferred their rights but excluding the 319 workers on the employer's list who did not claim; and ordered the attendance bonus deducted pro rata to days of authorised absence. [Para 174][Para 132]

As a threshold, the panel rejected the objection to Ms O Yoshiko's standing. Article 138 of the Commercial Enterprise Law lets the articles of association or internal rules define managers' roles, and Article 2 of the company's internal rules of 17 December 2012 makes the director and general manager the core of the board with full responsibility. [Para 85][Para 87][Para 89] Following award 71/08, a general manager may represent the company, so she was properly before the panel alongside the three delegated staff. [Para 91][Para 93]

On the suspension issue, Article 71(11) permits suspension only for serious economic or material difficulty and requires the suspension to be under labour inspector supervision, for no more than two months. [Para 96][Para 98] The panel read that supervision as requiring prior notification and authorisation, following awards 58/07, 08/07, 22/05, 198/13 and 160/14. [Para 99][Para 100] The parties agreed the suspensions occurred and that no authorisation was obtained. [Para 102] The panel rejected the argument that an inspection company falls outside the rule: Article 71(11) contains no exception based on the nature of the business, and intermittent suspension still requires prior authorisation. [Para 106][Para 107]

On the 50 per cent clause, Article 13(1) makes the Labour Law public order and voids any unilateral decision, contract or convention less favourable than it. [Para 108][Para 109] The panel held the clause void. [Para 115][Para 116]

On the remedy, Article 72(1) removes the wage obligation during a suspension, but only where the suspension itself was lawful. [Para 117][Para 118][Para 119] Where the employer suspends unlawfully it owes 100 per cent wages and other benefits even though no work was given, as held in awards 21/03, 09/10 and others. [Para 121][Para 123][Para 124] The panel applied the order only to union members and workers who transferred rights, excluding the 319 named on the employer's list of workers who made no claim. [Para 130][Para 131][Para 132]

On the attendance bonus, the panel found Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 replaced Notification No. 041 of 2011 and raised the minimum to USD 10 per month for workers with no absence. [Para 134][Para 136][Para 139] It accepted that the phrase "without absence" is ambiguous, that neither the Labour Advisory Committee nor any competent body has interpreted it, and that it cannot be read to cover both authorised and unauthorised absence without regard to legality and reasonableness. [Para 142][Para 143] The bonus is a wage under Article 103, and authorised absence suspends the contract under Article 71(6) and 72(1), so neither wages nor the bonus are owed for those days — but only for those days. [Para 163][Para 165][Para 169][Para 170] The employer must therefore deduct pro rata and may not forfeit the whole bonus. [Para 171][Para 172]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that suspends work for economic or material difficulty without the labour inspector's prior authorisation suspends unlawfully, whatever its line of business and however intermittent the stoppage; Article 72(1) then does not relieve it of wages, and any clause limiting workers to 50 per cent pay is void under Article 13(1), so full wages and benefits are owed. The attendance bonus is a wage, and absence authorised by the employer suspends the contract, so the bonus may be reduced only in proportion to the days absent, never forfeited in full. [Para 106][Para 116][Para 121][Para 165][Para 171]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the attendance-bonus notification is a general rule binding all textile, garment and footwear employers rather than a discretionary management tool, and its survey of what counts as a working day in a month, are general observations; so is its refusal to interpret "without absence" in the absence of guidance from the Labour Advisory Committee. [Para 139][Para 142][Para 157]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The workers' claim is printed as "ប្រាក់ ឈ្នួល ១០០ %" in the conciliation report but as a 50 per cent top-up in the reasons and the order, and the reason for the shift (that 50 per cent had already been paid) appears only in the parties' positions. [Para 3][Para 50][Para 94]
- The claimant lists are inconsistent: the union claims 259, 196, 267 and 128 workers for April, June, July and September, while the employer's non-claimant list has 319 names, and the total headcount is given as about 300. [Para 40][Para 54][Para 57][Para 58]
- Company names appear in at least five variants for one employer: "អេហ្វស៊ី អេហ្វ អ៊ិន ស្តេក សិន", "FCF Inspection", "FCF LECF Inspection", "Life Inspection" and "ហ្វេស៊ិ ហ្វេស អ៊ិន ស្តេក សិន". [Para 29][Para 30][Para 39][Para 43]
- The panel is described as appointed under Prakas 130 of 16 June 2014 for a twelfth term, unlike the thirteenth term cited in awards of the same period; the case number also appears once as "២៧១(១៥". [Para 1][Para 6][Para 43]
