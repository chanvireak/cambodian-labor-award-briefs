# Award No. 194/15 — Hsu Garment E-Brill (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 14 August 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Houn Chundy for the worker party, Tang Tri chair) · Doc ID 139260

## 1. Facts
Hsu Garment E-Brill (Cambodia), a garment-cutting enterprise on National Road 2, Sangkat Chak Angre Krom, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employing about 430 workers, calculated the daily wage by dividing the base wage by the actual number of working days in each month after subtracting that month's Sundays, and accepted only medical certificates issued by state hospitals when workers took sick leave. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 56][Para 64] On 26 June 2015 the Democratic Confederation of Labour Unions submitted nine demands on working conditions; three were settled in conciliation and the workers withdrew four of the six that remained, leaving issues 01 and 04 for the panel. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 25][Para 26] Forty-five workers mandated the confederation in writing and two more appeared in person, so the panel decided the case for forty-seven workers only. [Para 48][Para 51]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must calculate the normal daily wage by dividing the base wage by 26 days, as the fixed basis for computing overtime pay? [Para 52][Para 65]
4. Whether the employer must accept medical certificates from private hospitals and licensed private clinics, and refrain from deducting annual leave when a worker takes certified sick leave? [Para 59][Para 92]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the Labour Law allows only 26 working days a month, so 26 must be the fixed divisor; a divisor that moves with the calendar each month silently reduces the daily rate and so reduces overtime pay, holiday compensation and travel allowance. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 81]
**Employer (respondent):** the divisor must follow the actual days of each month because the payroll formula is already built into its computer system, it shows the true daily rate, and an absent day correctly earns nothing; it accepts only state-hospital certificates because those are the ones it recognises. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 64]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to compute the normal daily wage by dividing the base wage by 26 days as the fixed divisor for overtime, and to accept medical certificates issued by state hospitals, private hospitals and private clinics recognised and licensed by the Ministry of Health. No sum of money is printed. [Para 88][Para 101][Para 102][Para 103]

On issue 01 the panel began with the pay rules: Article 139 (new) and Article 144 of the Labour Law and Article 5 of Prakas No. 80 of 01 March 1999 fix overtime at 150 per cent of the normal wage, but neither states how the normal daily wage itself is computed. [Para 66][Para 68][Para 69] It then looked for a fixed figure and found one in two instruments: point 4 of Ministry Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2013, which takes no more than 26 working days in a month for the travel and accommodation allowance of newly hired workers, and Article 4 of Prakas No. 10 of 04 February 1999, which pays one day's normal wage for work on a public holiday. [Para 70][Para 72][Para 75] Reading both together, the panel concluded that the ordinary working days in a month are fixed at 26 for these calculations. [Para 76]

It then tested the employer's method against the 2015 calendar, month by month, and averaged the result: [(27 x 4) + (26 x 6) + 24 + 25] / 12 = 26.08 days. [Para 78][Para 79] Because 26.08 exceeds 26, a divisor that moves with the calendar is larger than the legal divisor and therefore yields a smaller daily rate, so the employer's method costs the workers money on every overtime hour, holiday payment and travel allowance computed from it. [Para 81][Para 82] The panel accepted that the employer may choose its own calculation method, but held that the workers may equally demand a method that is certain, intelligible and transparent; a fixed 26 both protects the entitlements set by Notification 230 and Prakas 10 and removes the monthly fluctuation that made workers fear losses. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 85][Para 86][Para 87]

On issue 04 the panel applied Article 71(3), which suspends the contract for a worker's absence through illness certified by an official doctor (គ្រូពេទ្យផ្លូវការ). [Para 92][Para 93] Following Awards 102/07, 72/08 and 39/09, it read "official doctor" as a doctor trained at a school recognised by the Ministry of Health, holding a valid diploma and a licence to practise issued by that Ministry — not as a doctor employed by a state hospital. [Para 94][Para 95][Para 97] The employer's rule accepting only state-hospital certificates was therefore wrong, and it was ordered to accept certificates from recognised state or private hospitals and licensed private clinics. [Para 99][Para 100][Para 101]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
For every calculation built on the normal daily wage — overtime under Article 139 (new) and Prakas No. 80, holiday compensation under Article 4 of Prakas No. 10, and the allowances governed by Notification No. 230 — the divisor is a fixed 26 days a month, not the actual number of working days in the calendar month; a fluctuating divisor that averages above 26 (26.08 days for 2015) understates the daily rate and must be replaced. [Para 76][Para 81][Para 87]

A medical certificate from any doctor trained and licensed by the Ministry of Health satisfies Article 71(3), whether the doctor practises in a state hospital, a private hospital or a licensed private clinic; an employer may not confine sick-leave cover to state-hospital certificates. [Para 94][Para 100]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that an employer retains the right to choose its own payroll method, and that a fixed divisor is also simpler and less confusing to administer, are reasons of convenience rather than conditions of the order. [Para 84][Para 86]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award is dated 14 August 2015 on its face [Para 1] but the parties' agreed extension runs to 15 August 2015 [Para 29]; the hearing was 29 July 2015 [Para 18].
- The employer is printed under at least four spellings — Hsu, Hvse, Fse and Fue Garment E-Brill — across [Para 1], [Para 30] and [Para 44]; the transliteration used above follows the face of the award.
- Registration data conflict: commercial registration No. 7094 of 27 August 2014 [Para 30], statutes deposited as No. 0114/2011 of 12 January 2011 [Para 32], and certificate Inv. 01 E/2011 of 12 January 2011 [Para 44].
- Worker counts conflict: 45 mandating workers (44 with names and thumbprints, one with thumbprint and ID number) [Para 48], then 47 parties [Para 51], against a claim about 260 workers and an employer answer about 26 who signed [Para 10][Para 11].
- The arithmetic in the monthly table does not reconcile: 31 days less 4 Sundays is stated as 27 days at [Para 78], and February 2015 is given as 24 days, yet the average is printed as 26.08 [Para 79].
- The conciliation figures shift between paragraphs: nine points with three settled and six remaining [Para 20][Para 21], then six remaining of which four were withdrawn [Para 25].
- Award numbers cited at [Para 94] are partly garbled ("102/07-Terapi", "39/09-Khlin Thom Mejeur"), and the panel's own signatures at [Para 105] are reduced to OCR noise ("Hun 5733 WHTP", "2016").
