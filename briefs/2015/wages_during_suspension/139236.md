# Award No. 166/15 — Sin Lan (Cambodia) Khom Pheni Ltd (Arbitration Council, 16 July 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Nan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chia, chair) · Doc ID 139236

## 1. Facts
Workers at the Sin Lan (Cambodia) Khom Pheni garment factory went on strike from 1 July 2015 over seven unresolved demands recorded in the non-conciliation report. The company asked the Council on 6 July 2015 for an order halting the strike, and on 7 July 2015 the Council issued order No. 025/15 K.B.A. requiring the strike to stop immediately and all strikers to return to their shifts from the morning of 8 July 2015. Both parties attended the hearing of 14 July 2015 and both acknowledged that the strike was still running, so the panel decided only whether it could proceed at all. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 25][Para 46]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay seniority indemnity (ប្រាក់បំណាច់អតីតភាពការងារ) to the workers correctly? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company must pay the seniority indemnity it has never paid since the outset? [Para 4]
3. Whether the company must stop threatening the workers? [Para 6]
4. Whether the company must stop its deception of the workers? [Para 8]
5. Whether the company must give clear reasons whenever it suspends a worker from work? [Para 10]
6. Whether the company must issue employment contracts of six months each? [Para 11]
7. Whether the company must stop discriminating against male workers? [Para 12]
8. Whether the panel may continue the arbitration proceedings at all, the strike having continued while the case was before it? [Para 34]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the workers maintained the strike until the employer resolved their demands, and told the panel on the hearing date that their position was unchanged; their demands concern unpaid seniority indemnity, threats and deception by the company, unexplained suspensions, six-month contracts and discrimination against male workers. [Para 3][Para 5][Para 26][Para 47]

**Employer (respondent):** the company rejected each of the seven demands in conciliation and, after the strike began, applied to the Council for an order suspending it; its reports of 9 and 13 July 2015 record that workers came into the factory on 10 and 11 July but sat without working and went home at midday on 11 July. [Para 3][Para 21][Para 24][Para 27]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel discontinued the arbitration proceedings in case 166/15 without ruling on any of the seven demands and ordered nothing in riel or USD. [Para 49][Para 50]

The panel framed the only question it would answer as whether the arbitration should continue, and turned to clause 20 of Prakas No. 099 SChBy of 21 April 2004. That clause requires the parties, throughout the panel's proceedings, to suspend every disputive measure — strike or lockout under article 318 of the Labour Law, or any other measure capable of aggravating the dispute — and to attend every meeting to which the panel summons them. [Para 34][Para 35]

The panel read that clause through its own earlier practice, citing a run of previous awards (among them 110/11 Simano, 104/13 Sabrina (Cambodia), 119/13 Pou Yie and 019/14 Dongduk Tech Style (Cambodia)), the notice of 29 January 2004 in case 04/04 and the second orders in cases 18/04 and 77/05, for the rule that the Council will not run an arbitration while workers are striking or an employer is locking out, and will suspend or terminate proceedings where a party breaches its orders. Applying that line, and finding that the workers were still striking on 14 July 2015 and that this was aggravating the dispute and preventing the panel from continuing, it terminated the proceedings under clause 20. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38][Para 43][Para 44][Para 45][Para 48]

The panel recorded expressly that it made no findings of fact on the disputed issues because it did not conduct a hearing on them, and its dispositive order is confined to terminating the arbitration in this case. [Para 33][Para 49][Para 50]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a strike or lockout is still in progress when a collective dispute is before it, a panel will not arbitrate: clause 20 of Prakas No. 099 obliges the parties to suspend every disputive measure for the duration of the proceedings, and breach of that obligation — or of an order made under it — leads the panel to suspend or terminate the arbitration rather than decide the merits. [Para 34][Para 36][Para 44][Para 48][Para 49]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's account of the workers' conduct on 10 and 11 July 2015, sitting in the factory without working and leaving at midday, was recited from the employer's reports and played no part in the termination, which rested on the continuation of the strike as acknowledged by both parties. [Para 24][Para 46]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Paragraph 23 states that after the Council's order the strikers did suspend the strike and comply, while paragraphs 24 to 26 and 46 to 48 state repeatedly that the strike continued to 14 July 2015; the latter is the finding on which the award turns. Paragraph 33 says the Council made no factual findings because it "did not conduct a hearing", yet paragraphs 17, 25 and 46 record a hearing held on 14 July 2015 at 14:00 and both parties present and heard. Paragraph 18 records that ten complaints were received while paragraph 19 speaks of nine demands of which seven were unresolved, and the report itself lists seven. The employer's name is printed in at least four different forms across the document ("Khom Pheni", "Gamen Upakeni", "Um Pheni" and a truncated "Khom Pheni Limited"), and the arbitrator An Nan appears in the signature block as An Lan, with the chair's name printed both as "Thai Bun Na" and as Pen Bun Chia. A page marker and an unresolved "<unk>" token survive in paragraph 51, where the signature block is otherwise destroyed, and paragraph 40 repeats paragraph 39 with a stray "Challein" fragment.
