# Award No. 063/15 — Men U Garment Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 March 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mr M Sombor Na for the employer party, Mr An Non for the worker party, Mr Pe Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139161

## 1. Facts
Men U Garment Co Ltd, registered in 2012 and cutting garments at Village Ou Trav, Anlong Thmey, Prey Nob, Preah Sihanouk, employed about 1,033 workers. [Para 1][Para 51] Of nine demands filed, two were settled at conciliation and five more at the hearing, leaving issues 1 and 5. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 27][Para 28] Both concern money the employer deducts when a worker is absent: the regular-attendance bonus of USD 10 a month, cut by USD 3.84 for one day, USD 7.68 for two days and entirely from three days, under an agreement of 26 August 2014; and the USD 10 monthly travel and accommodation allowance, cut pro rata for any absence. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 67] 103 workers transferred their rights to the union. [Para 53][Para 56]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to deduct the regular-attendance bonus only pro rata, by the number of days of leave the worker takes with proper authorisation from the employer? [Para 74][Para 118]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to retain the travel and accommodation allowance at 100% while a worker is on authorised or unauthorised leave, and to repay the amounts already deducted? [Para 119]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** they applied for leave properly and the employer granted it, yet the bonus was still cut; the panel has repeatedly ordered pro-rata deduction where leave is authorised, and neighbouring factories such as Kip Tha Sport Thing Good and Royal Crown Thich do the same. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61] On the allowance, Article 103 of the Labour Law means travel and accommodation money is not wages, so it must be paid in full whatever the reason for absence. [Para 70]
**Employer (respondent):** the USD 10 attendance bonus is only for workers who come regularly, so any absence forfeits it, and one day's absence already disrupts the production line and forces it to find a replacement; it cannot copy other companies but will keep applying the 26 August 2014 agreement. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 64][Para 65][Para 66] On the allowance, it owes nothing when the worker does not come to work, and the USD 10 it pays for a full month already exceeds the law. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 73]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to deduct the regular-attendance bonus pro rata to the number of days of leave properly authorised by the employer, and to retain the travel and accommodation allowance at 100% during leave, authorised or not, and to repay the amounts deducted. [Para 134][Para 135][Para 136]

On issue 1, the panel traced the USD 10 bonus to point 2 of Notification No. 230 K.B/S.J.N of 25 July 2012, which replaced Notification No. 041 of 7 March 2011 (then USD 7). [Para 74][Para 75][Para 76] It held that this notification is not a discretionary incentive that each employer may shape, but a general legal rule binding every employer in the textile, garment and footwear sector, so the USD 10 is an obligation owed to workers who attend the days required in the month. [Para 79][Para 81] Because the notification's phrase "without absence" was never clarified by the competent body, the panel refused to read it as covering both authorised and unauthorised absence without regard to legality and reasonableness; absence expressly recognised by law — annual leave, special leave, public holidays — cannot justify any deduction. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 85][Para 150] Leave for personal business authorised by the employer is a suspension of the contract under Article 71(6), so no wage is owed for those days, but the bonus is a form of wage under Article 103 and the employer may deduct only what corresponds to the days actually absent. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 104][Para 107][Para 109][Para 111] The 26 August 2014 agreement, which forfeits the whole bonus from three days, is less favourable than the law and void under Article 13(1). [Para 113][Para 114][Para 117]

On issue 5, Notification No. 230 requires USD 7 a month for travel and accommodation, and the employer's USD 10 is more favourable and must be preserved. [Para 119][Para 121][Para 122][Para 124][Para 127] Nothing in the notification conditions that payment on a full month's attendance, and the panel found no other condition to that effect; deducting it pro rata for absence therefore defeats the notification's purpose, and under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 the panel ordered repayment. [Para 124][Para 128][Para 129][Para 130][Para 131][Para 133]

The employer-party arbitrator, Mr M Sombor Na, dissented on issue 1 in an annex: pro-rata deduction for authorised personal leave would strip the "regular attendance" bonus of meaning and value, and he would have dismissed the claim and required the parties to apply the 26 August 2014 agreement. [Para 138][Para 140][Para 155][Para 156]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The regular-attendance bonus fixed by Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 is a statutory minimum wage component for the garment, textile and footwear sector, not a gratuitous incentive: an employer may reduce it only in proportion to days of absence, and may not forfeit it in full for authorised leave, any agreement to the contrary being void under Article 13(1). A travel and accommodation allowance paid above the notification's USD 7 floor is a more favourable benefit that must be kept at 100% and cannot be made conditional on full monthly attendance. [Para 79][Para 109][Para 117][Para 127][Para 130]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the ministry's phrase "without absence" remains unclarified, and that it would decide each claim on its own facts rather than construe the phrase generally, are observations that did not drive the orders. [Para 82][Para 86]
