# Award No. 269/15 — Im Vey Garment International (Kampong Chhnang branch) (Arbitration Council, 12 November 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom chosen by the employer party, Liv Sovanna chosen by the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139447

## 1. Facts
The Kampong Chhnang branch of a garment manufacturer employing about 3,664 workers did not renew the fixed-duration contracts of 180 workers in the pressing, ironing and washing sections when they expired on 30 September 2015, giving seven days' notice and paying the 5 per cent termination indemnity, final wages and unused annual leave. [Para 49][Para 52][Para 53][Para 55][Para 56] Most of the 180 held successive three-month contracts whose total service exceeded one year but was under two. [Para 52] They claimed damages equal to the wages of the last contract plus payment in lieu of notice, arguing the notice should have been measured by their accumulated seniority. [Para 51][Para 58]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay damages equal to the wages of the last contract and payment in lieu of notice to the 180 workers whose contracts it did not renew? [Para 51][Para 64]

The first claim, for reinstatement of the 180, was withdrawn at the hearing once the employer refused, leaving this issue alone for decision. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 18]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer ended the contracts without lawful notice; notice must be calculated by each worker's seniority counted from the probationary period, and where lawful notice is not given the termination is void and the contract continues for the length of the last term. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 59]
**Employer (respondent):** orders fell away in recent months, forcing cost cuts and non-renewal of both workers and some office staff; the contracts reached their term, and the Labour Law fixes no notice period for a three-month fixed-duration contract, though the company chose to give seven days to everyone. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 62][Para 63]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both the damages and the payment in lieu of notice were refused for all 180 workers. [Para 84][Para 85]

The panel began with Article 73(3) of the Labour Law, which gives damages of at least the wages due to the contractual end date only where the employer dissolves the contract early of its own will, outside the grounds in paragraphs 1 and 2. [Para 64][Para 65] It found that Article 73(5) does require prior notice where a fixed-duration contract exceeds six months (ten days) or one year (fifteen days). [Para 70][Para 71] But it read the trigger for that duty as the duration written into the contract, not the worker's cumulative service: the words used are "a fixed-duration contract exceeding six months" and "a contract of a duration exceeding one year", so the notice is fixed by reference to the term of the contract itself. [Para 73][Para 74] The workers' contention that seniority from probation should govern therefore had no legal basis. [Para 75]

Following Award No. 122/10, the panel held that a fixed-duration contract of less than six months requires no prior notice at all. [Para 76] The last contracts here were each of three months only, so no notice was owed. [Para 78] The employer had in fact given seven days' notice before expiry and had let the contracts run to their term rather than cutting them short. [Para 79][Para 82] Giving seven days was a practice more favourable than the law requires, but it created no obligation to give more. [Para 81] Because the contracts expired at their stated term and were not renewed, Article 73(3) damages did not arise, and the claim for moral damages equal to a full contract period failed with it. [Para 65][Para 83]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where fixed-duration contracts run their full term and are simply not renewed, the notice obligation in Article 73(5) is measured by the duration of the contract itself, not by the worker's accumulated service across successive renewals: a three-month contract requires no prior notice, and giving some notice as a matter of practice does not convert that practice into a legal entitlement to more. Damages under Article 73(3) arise only on early dissolution by the employer, not on expiry at term. [Para 74][Para 76][Para 79][Para 82][Para 83]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that seven days' notice exceeded what the law required, and its acceptance of falling orders as the reason for non-renewal, were observations on the employer's conduct rather than grounds of the order. [Para 61][Para 81]
