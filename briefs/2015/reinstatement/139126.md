# Award No. 013/15 — Suon Osophea Mean Hat Then (Arbitration Council, 6 February 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (chair, appointed by the two members) · Doc ID 139126

## 1. Facts
The employer operates the Mean Hat Then worksite in Kampong Siem district, Kampong Cham. [Para 1]
Four demands were filed with the provincial labour department on 08 January 2015; three were settled in conciliation and one remained: that the company continue Mr Y's employment contract until the sports-section agreement was implemented in March 2015. [Para 2][Para 9]
The panel was constituted on 16 January 2015 and fixed a hearing for 23 January 2015 at 14:00. The employer attended through its administrative adviser; the worker party did not attend and sent no representative. [Para 7]
Neither party elected to be bound, and no evidence was taken beyond the employer's written authority and two conciliation records. [Para 14]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must extend Mr Y's employment contract until March 2015 so that the sports-section agreement can be carried out? [Para 2]
2. Whether the panel may nonetheless issue an award when the claimant party fails to appear at the hearing without a valid reason? [Para 26]
3. Whether the claimant's absence amounts to abandonment of its claim, so that the arbitration must be terminated? [Para 56]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The claim, as recorded in the non-settled conciliation report, was that the contract should be continued until March 2015 to allow the sports agreement to be performed; no submissions or evidence were presented at hearing because the party was absent. [Para 2][Para 25]
**Employer (respondent):** The employer attended through Mr S, administrative adviser, and produced a written delegation of authority plus conciliation records; it did not have to answer the claim because the claimant never appeared. [Para 1][Para 14]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** withdrawn — the panel terminated the arbitration of case 013/15, treating the claim as abandoned by the claimant party. No compensation, reinstatement or payment was ordered. [Para 56][Para 58][Para 59]
The panel first asked what procedure applies where a party is absent. Reading Prakas No. 099 Article 18 (parties must be invited to state their claims in person), Article 20 (parties must attend every session) and Article 21 (where a duly invited party fails to appear without valid reason, the panel may continue in absentia or close the case by award), together with Rule 4.7 of the annexed procedural rules, it held that the panel may choose either course but only if three conditions are met: valid notification, non-appearance, and no valid excuse. [Para 26][Para 34]
It then tested each condition. Notification was valid: invitation No. 068 of 19 January 2015 was signed for by a union official on 20 January 2015 and stated the date, time, place and contact details. [Para 39]
Non-appearance was established: the worker party was absent and had appointed no authorised representative, which the panel read as failing both limbs of "appearing" — presence at the hearing and participation throughout its four stages. [Para 44]
No valid excuse was given to the secretariat, so the third condition was also satisfied. [Para 47]
Because the claimant is the party bearing the burden of presenting argument and evidence, and it neither appeared nor explained its absence, the panel treated the claim as abandoned rather than proceeding in absentia. It noted that a respondent's duty to rebut only arises once the claimant has discharged its own duty, which never happened here. It therefore closed the proceedings by award. [Para 52][Para 57]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a claimant party has been validly notified, fails to appear in person or through an authorised representative, and offers no valid reason, the panel may treat the claim as abandoned and close the arbitration by award without examining the merits, because the burden of argument and evidence rests on the claimant and a respondent cannot be required to answer a case that has not been presented. [Para 34][Para 55][Para 57]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the alternative course — continuing the hearing in the party's absence — remains open on the same three conditions, so termination is a discretionary choice rather than an automatic consequence of absence. [Para 33]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The scanned original is badly damaged. The employer's name is printed variously as "Suon Osophea Mean Hat Then", "Men Hat Then" and "Metak Cambodia Limited", and separate letters refer to a "Mean Hat Phnom Penh" and a "Men Hat Than Phnom Penh" factory closure (paras 1, 9, 15, 16). The claimant is named at para 2 as the Free Workers' Union of Cambodia, but para 8 records the complaint as coming from two other bodies, the Cambodian Labour Movement Development Federation and the company union. Paragraph 46 contains untranscribed OCR garbage in place of operative text. The individual worker is identified only by the initial "Mr Y" in the surviving text.
