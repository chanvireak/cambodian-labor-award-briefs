# Award No. 88/15 — Great Honour Textile Factory Limited (Arbitration Council, 7 May 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy chosen by the employer, Tuon Si Phan chosen by the workers, Tang Tri chair) · Doc ID 139180

## 1. Facts
Great Honour Textile Factory Limited knits sweaters at Svay Rolum, Sa'ang, Kandal, with about 1,400 workers. [Para 54][Para 55] The factory was formerly Winner Knitting Factory Ltd; the investment project was bought by Great Honour in 2011 and the name changed, with seniority, conditions and benefits carried over. [Para 35][Para 114] The Cambodian Workers Union Federation filed three claims: a USD 28 monthly increase for the nine weaving-section team leaders, enough work for that section, and severance on the 2011 name change. [Para 3][Para 5][Para 7] The branch union had held elections on 19 March 2015 but was not yet registered with the Ministry, and only federation officials and three workers attended the hearing. [Para 55][Para 60]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the nine weaving-section team leaders an additional USD 28 per month on top of their existing basic wage, as it does for team leaders in other sections? [Para 136]
2. Whether the employer failed to provide sufficient work to the weaving section, contrary to Article 83 of the Labour Law? [Para 180]
3. Whether the employer must terminate the contracts and pay severance when the company changed its name from Winner Knitting Factory to Great Honour Textile Factory Limited in 2011? [Para 216]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** paying the USD 28 supplement to team leaders in every section except weaving is unequal treatment, since weaving team leaders are skilled workers like the others; the weaving section has had almost no work since October 2014, output has collapsed, wages have fallen from USD 150–250 to USD 90–110, and the alternative work assigned is outside their skill. [Para 69][Para 102][Para 103] On the name change, other companies pay severance when a company is renamed, and the workers' contracts were made with Winner, not Great Honour. [Para 109][Para 110]
**Employer (respondent):** Prakas 283 fixes a minimum of USD 128, not a USD 28 top-up, and the employer already raised all nine to USD 128 from January 2015; the supplement is a discretionary reward for team leaders who perform, and the weaving leaders do not — they idle by the bathrooms, mis-measure panels, and ignore their supervisor's instructions. [Para 70][Para 76][Para 166][Para 177] Work is short because orders are short, the workers were reassigned to checking and sorting at 100% of wage and refused, and the name change preserved all seniority and benefits under Article 87. [Para 111][Para 204][Para 230]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three claims rejected; no sum was ordered. [Para 243][Para 244][Para 245]

On standing, the panel held that the branch union's unregistered status meant its officers could not represent the members, but Prakas 099/04 Article 19 lets a party appear through any person holding written authority, and the federation held two authorisation letters — 11 team leaders for issues 1 and 2 and 242 workers for issue 3 — so the federation could act. [Para 119][Para 124][Para 125][Para 127][Para 133] Because two of the 11 leaders had transferred out of weaving, issues 1 and 2 were considered for nine leaders only, and issue 3 for the 242 signatories. [Para 130][Para 134][Para 135]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 106, which requires equal conditions of work, equal professional skill, equal output and no discrimination based on origin, sex or age; following Award 68/04 it held the claimant must prove all four, and the workers proved none — they did not show that weaving leaders' skill or output matched the other sections'. [Para 147][Para 148][Para 153][Para 154] Under Article 2 the employer's right to manage the enterprise permits it to set incentive rules for staff who perform well, and the panel accepted the photographic and output evidence that the weaving leaders were inattentive and that 2014 output fell below 2013. [Para 158][Para 166][Para 175][Para 178]

On issue 2 the panel found Article 83(k)(5) — employer fault for failing to supply sufficient work to workers paid by output — inapplicable, because the claimants are monthly-paid team leaders, not piece-rate workers, and they lost no wages. [Para 190][Para 191][Para 193] Reassignment to checking and sorting while knitting orders are short was a lawful and reasonable exercise of the management right under Article 2, preserving 100% of wages and offering training, so the workers were obliged to comply. [Para 204][Para 207][Para 211]

On issue 3 the panel applied Article 87: where the employer's legal position changes by sale or transfer, contracts in force continue between the new employer and the workers. [Para 217] The acquisition agreement and the 2011 notice both preserved conditions, benefits and seniority, and the pay sheets still recorded each worker's original start date. [Para 114][Para 229][Para 230] Since the employer never terminated and nothing was lost, no severance was owed, and a party cannot be compelled to terminate against its will. [Para 236][Para 239][Para 241]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A change in the employer's legal identity — the sale of an investment project and the renaming of the company — does not terminate the employment contracts, which pass to the new employer under Article 87; severance is owed only if the contracts are actually terminated, and a worker cannot force an employer to terminate and pay where seniority, conditions and benefits have been preserved. [Para 217][Para 230][Para 241] A claim for equal pay under Article 106 fails unless the claimant proves equal working conditions, equal professional skill, equal output and the absence of discrimination; an allowance paid to one section is not by itself evidence of unequal treatment, and setting performance-based incentives remains within the employer's Article 2 management right. [Para 147][Para 148][Para 158][Para 175]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that reassignment during slack periods also lets workers acquire additional skills and smooths the production chain, and its observation that team leaders should set a good example for their teams, are general observations on good practice and were not necessary to the dismissal. [Para 173][Para 207]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The conciliation report is numbered 551/15 at [Para 11] and [Para 50] but 55/15 at [Para 15]; the panel itself is described as constituted on "30 March 2015" printed as "២០ ២០១៥" [Para 16].
- The union registration is said to be missing "from the Ministry of Health, Labour and Vocational Training" at [Para 124], an evident corruption of the Ministry of Labour alone.
- The claimant's authorisation letter is numbered 4374/15 but dated 21 March 2014 at [Para 47], a year before the file was opened.
- Numbers are unstable: the nine leaders are "128 dollars 20 america" at [Para 180], the minimum wage is printed both as "128 (one hundred twenty-eight)" and as "123" in the same Prakas quotation at [Para 137][Para 141], and the alternative-work wage is "១០០០ ភាគរយ" (1,000%) at [Para 105] where 100% is meant.
- Worker and union names survive in the source and are abbreviated here as Mr C and Mr V (the two leaders who left weaving) and Mr N, Mr M and Mr P (the three who attended); the award prints them inconsistently, e.g. "ណយ រដ្ឋា" and "នីម ស្រី ពៅ".
