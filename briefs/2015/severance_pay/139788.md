# Award No. 341/14 — Royal Rattana Hospital (Royal Phnom Penh) (Arbitration Council, 12 January 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom for the employer, Liv Sovanna for the workers, Ang Eng Thong chair; Liv Sovanna dissenting) · Doc ID 139788

## 1. Facts
Royal Rattana Medical Services Co Ltd ran Royal Rattana Hospital until 30 September 2014, then closed that site, opened Phnom Penh Medical Services Co Ltd ("Royal Phnom Penh Hospital") in Teuk Thla offering the same health services, and moved the equipment and staff across. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 46] Ten workers refused to move; nine mandated a lawyer and appeared. They had already received final wages, severance, pay in lieu of notice and annual-leave pay, and claimed instead moral damages (សោហ៊ុយជម្ងឺចិត្ត) under Article 91 and an annual bonus of one month's wage. [Para 42][Para 45][Para 50]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the workers may claim moral damages under Article 91 of the Labour Law when the employer closed the old hospital and opened a new company at a new site and they declined to transfer? [Para 80][Para 104]
2. Whether the employer owes an annual bonus equal to one month's wage to workers whose contracts ended on 01 October 2014, before the year-end evaluation? [Para 112][Para 121]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the old hospital was officially closed and a new hospital opened under new management, so the employer terminated them and owes moral damages; the bonus had always been at least one month's wage after evaluation, and they had already been evaluated once. [Para 51][Para 75][Para 61] They gave three reasons for refusing to transfer: no answer to their wage-rise request, distrust of an unwritten promise to preserve pay and benefits, and some preferred to rest or had their own business. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60]
**Employer (respondent):** both hospitals sit under one parent employer with unchanged management, the move was to expand the business, notice was given orally at meetings, and each worker was offered either transfer on unchanged pay and benefits or a lawful settlement; the workers ended the contracts themselves by refusing, and the bonus is payable only after the October–November evaluation, with December wages. [Para 49][Para 52][Para 56][Para 70][Para 78]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both claims rejected; no sum ordered. [Para 129][Para 130]

On the first issue the panel asked whether the relocation and renaming was a cessation of the enterprise or a change in the legal status of the employer, and applied Article 87(1): where the employer's legal status changes by transfer of funds, existing contracts continue between the new employer and the workers of the old enterprise. [Para 83][Para 85][Para 86] Because the two hospitals served the same clients under one parent, kept the same management and had all staff and equipment transferred, the panel held this was a change of legal status, so the contracts continued with Royal Phnom Penh Hospital; the workers were entitled to refuse to move, and the employer was equally entitled to terminate those who refused and pay statutory compensation. [Para 88][Para 93] It then examined the three stated reasons and found none sufficient: the wage-rise request was a matter for the employer's evaluation of performance, the fear about preserved benefits was only an apprehension that had not materialised, and wanting to rest or run a private business is no valid reason to refuse a transfer. [Para 97][Para 98][Para 99][Para 100] Although the panel recorded the ending as a dismissal at the employer's initiative because that favoured the workers, it held the change of legal status had not affected their contracts, that they were obliged to work at the new hospital, and that their refusal was voluntary and unconnected to any wrongdoing by the employer; the employer therefore had valid reason to terminate and Article 91 was not engaged. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 105][Para 108][Para 109][Para 110]

On the second issue the panel held that a bonus scheme outside the statutory bonus is a unilateral managerial arrangement the employer may set, following Award No. 24/05-Veary Veal and its own earlier awards. [Para 115][Para 117][Para 120] The scheme paid only after the annual evaluation held in October–November and paid with December wages; the nine contracts ended on 01 October 2014, before any evaluation, so the condition for earning the bonus was never met and withholding it was a reasonable exercise of the right to manage under Article 2. [Para 121][Para 124][Para 127]

Arbitrator Liv Sovanna dissented: the two companies are separate legal persons with no transfer of funds, so the closure was a cessation of the enterprise, the workers had a free choice, and the employer owed both moral damages and a bonus apportioned to the period served. [Para 137][Para 147][Para 179]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Closing an enterprise and reopening it at a new site under a new company name, where the same parent holds both, management is unchanged and staff and equipment are transferred, is a change in the legal status of the employer under Article 87(1) rather than a termination: the contracts continue with the new employer, a worker may decline the transfer, but the employer may then terminate for valid reason and owes no moral damages under Article 91. An annual bonus payable by the employer's own practice only after the year-end evaluation is not owed to workers whose contracts end before that evaluation. [Para 86][Para 88][Para 93][Para 110][Para 124]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's statement that it would treat the ending as an employer-initiated dismissal because that benefits the workers more is a device for testing the claim, not a finding that the employer dismissed them; its survey of earlier awards on managerial prerogative is general reasoning. [Para 103][Para 116]
