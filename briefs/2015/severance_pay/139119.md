# Award No. 005/15 — Can Sport Shoe Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 27 January 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 139119 · unofficial English translation of the authoritative Khmer original

## 1. Facts
Can Sport Shoe Co., Ltd runs a footwear factory at Chomkar Svay Village, Samaki Meanchey, Kampong Chhang, employing approximately 5,403 workers. [Para 27][Para 28] A collective dispute covering nine demands went to the MoLVT conciliator: four were settled there, and five reached the Arbitration Council, where issue #3 was settled in further conciliation and the workers withdrew #1 (reinstatement), the 5% severance part of #2, #4 and #5. [Para 21][Para 23][Para 24] What remained was the demand that the employer keep paying cash in lieu of six unused days of annual leave each January while contracts were still running, a practice the employer announced it would stop from 2015. [Para 34][Para 35]

## 2. Issues in dispute
Following the numbering of the non-conciliation report No. 199:
1. Whether the employer must reinstate two dismissed workers, a warehouse section worker and a quality control section worker? [Para 17]
2. Whether the employer must pay in lieu of annual leave, and 5% severance pay? [Para 17][Para 32]
3. Whether the employer must remove a Chinese worker holding ID 132 from building A? [Para 18]
4. Whether the employer must remove two Cambodian administrators and one Chinese administrator from their posts? [Para 18]
5. Whether the employer must repay USD 100 of miscalculated wages paid on 09 December 2014 to Ms M? [Para 18]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the enterprise's practice was to pay cash for the six days the workers agreed not to take, every January; workers found it hard to get leave from section heads and team leaders, needed the money, and pointed to other enterprises (Jiun Ye and Long Lead) that still pay in lieu of remaining leave annually. [Para 34][Para 35]
**Employer (respondent):** it complies with Article 166 of the Labour Law, and its buyers do not allow payment in lieu of remaining annual leave for the duration of the employment contracts. [Para 35]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the workers' demand that the employer pay in lieu of six days of annual leave annually while their employment contracts are still effective, as in the past practice; the award is non-binding. [Para 42][Para 43]

The panel reframed the question as whether a practice of buying out remaining leave each January, during a live contract, complies with the Labour Law at all. [Para 36] Article 167 paragraph 2 allows compensation calculated on the Article 166 basis only where the contract is breached or expires before the worker has acquired the right to use paid leave, so payment in lieu is owed only on termination and does not apply while contracts remain effective. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38] Article 167 paragraph 3 then voids any collective bargaining agreement providing compensation in place of paid leave, and any agreement by which a worker renounces or waives the right to paid annual leave, which in the panel's reading prohibited the employer from buying out leave while workers were still employed. [Para 39]

The panel followed its earlier awards holding that a demand for payment in lieu of annual leave contradicts the spirit of Article 167 paragraph 3 and is inappropriate and illegal — Award No. 94/04 Eternity Apparel (issue 2), No. 45/05 B & N Cambodia (issue 1), No. 88/12 Magnate Footwear (issue 6) and No. 73/13 Yakjin (issue 8). [Para 39][Para 40] It therefore found the past practice itself in breach of Article 167 paragraph 3, and the employer's 2015 decision to end it not a violation of the Labour Law. [Para 40][Para 41] The demand was held inappropriate, unreasonable and without legal basis. [Para 42]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that an employer pay cash in lieu of annual leave not taken, while the employment contract is still effective, fails: Article 167 paragraphs 2 and 3 of the Labour Law confine compensation for unused paid leave to the case where the contract is breached or expires, and void any agreement waiving the right to paid annual leave. Consequently an employer's withdrawal of a long-standing buy-out practice is lawful, and the practice's earlier existence confers no enforceable entitlement. [Para 37][Para 39][Para 41]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's comparison of this enterprise with Jiun Ye and Long Lead, and its observation that workers had difficulty obtaining leave from section heads, were noted as the workers' reasons but played no part in the legal holding. [Para 35]
