# Award No. 301/15 — Uniform and Socks MFG (Cambodia) Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 15 December 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry, Sin Kim Sean, Kong Phallack — Chair) · Doc ID 139473

## 1. Facts
Uniform and Socks MFG (Cambodia) Ltd. (USM) manufactures socks at Odem Village, Chom Chao, Phnom Penh, employing approximately 210 workers; the CFWU local union had not yet been registered by the Ministry. [Para 19][Para 21][Para 22] On 21 September 2015 the CFWU wrote demanding improved working conditions, listing sixteen demands; conciliation on 06 November 2015 failed and non-conciliation report no. 1432 of 17 November 2015 sent the file to the Secretariat. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 14] The workers withdrew issues 1, 3, 4, 5, 10, 14 and 15, leaving issues 2, 8, 11, 12, 13 and 16. [Para 16][Para 17] The employer did not appear at the 03 December 2015 hearing. [Para 15]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must facilitate workers' requests for personal commitment leave? [Para 25]
2. (Issue 8) Whether the employer must be ordered not to terminate workers without reasons? [Para 28]
3. (Issue 11) Whether the employer must educate Management Staff Member Lon Darith and Supervisor Maila to cease improper language and conduct towards workers? [Para 31]
4. (Issue 12) Whether the employer must cease setting production targets workers cannot achieve? [Para 32]
5. (Issue 13) Whether the employer must complete wage payment for the first shift by 02:00 P.M.? [Para 34]
6. (Issue 16) Whether the employer must cease relocating workers to work different from their expertise? [Para 36]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Leave for personal commitments is significant — sickness of relatives or friends — yet permission is refused, and team leaders have thrown leave forms away; the employer previously set unachievable production targets and made workers redundant before replacing them, so they fear repetition; first-shift workers wait hours for wages after finishing at 02:00 P.M.; mechanics were moved to trimming frays, ironing socks and unloading boxes outside their expertise. [Para 27][Para 36][Para 37][Para 52]
**Employer (respondent):** Absent and unrepresented; the Administration staff member reached by telephone on the hearing date said he did not know who would attend because the Chief of Administration was away, and the Chief did not answer calls. [Para 41][Para 3]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to educate Lon Darith (Manager) and Maila (Supervisor) to cease improper language and conduct, and to complete wage payment for first-shift workers by 02:00 P.M.; it declined to consider issues 2, 8 and 12 and rejected issue 16. [Para 89]

**Absence.** Applying Clause 21 of Prakas no. 99 (2004), the panel held that a party duly invited who fails to appear without good cause may be proceeded against in its absence, following a line of prior awards from 53/04-Kuong Hong Washing to 163/14 XIN CHANG XIN. The employer had received the invitation by e-mail, did not seek an adjournment, and therefore waived its right to respond; the workers' assertions were accepted as unchallenged. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 42][Para 45]

**Issue 2 (leave).** Article 2 of the Labour Law gives the employer the right to manage production and to set lawful, reasonable procedures for granting leave, and Award 171/13-Bowker Garment required the employer to weigh the significance of a requested absence against the production chain. But the workers could not name the team leader who discarded the forms or the affected workers, and the panel followed Award 150/14-Lianfa in holding that facts must be sufficient, precise and clear; with no actual occasion identified, it declined to decide. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 53][Para 51]

**Issue 8 (termination).** The panel accepted that redundancies had occurred before the Khmer New Year, but the workers conceded the employer paid severance and does not presently dismiss without reasons; the complaint was of a future risk. Following 10/03-Jacqsintex and five similar awards, the panel held it settles labour disputes, not disputes that have not yet occurred, and declined to consider the demand. [Para 29][Para 56][Para 57][Para 58]

**Issue 11 (conduct).** Cursing workers as having "no brain" or a brain "like the pigs" is language no manager or supervisor should use, and striking a worker's face is violence amounting to serious misconduct under Article 83. Under Clause 34 of Prakas no. 99 the panel may order a party to cease illegal or prohibited conduct, so it ordered the two named staff educated to stop. [Para 60][Para 63][Para 64]

**Issue 12 (targets).** The workers said the practice had already changed and that they feared its return; the same future-dispute reasoning applied and the demand was declined. [Para 33][Para 68]

**Issue 13 (wages).** Article 115 paragraph 3 (payment not on a day off) as read in Award 143/08, together with Article 137's eight-hour day, means wages must be paid within working hours; since the first shift ends at 02:00 P.M., payment to those workers must be complete by then. [Para 69][Para 72][Para 76][Para 77]

**Issue 16 (relocation).** Article 2 permits supervision that is lawful and reasonable, and Award 11/15-Dignity Knitter allowed reassignment of workers with no work to do. The mechanics kept full wages, suffered no discipline for resulting lateness, were moved only intermittently, and had the skills for the tasks; no other affected worker was identified, so the demand was rejected. [Para 77][Para 84][Para 85][Para 87][Para 89]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer's non-appearance after due invitation waives its right to answer and the panel will accept the workers' factual assertions — but acceptance goes only to whether a fact is contested, not to whether it is proved: where the workers cannot identify the occasion, the person or the affected workers, the panel still has no basis to decide and will decline the demand. [Para 45][Para 54][Para 55]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that good employment relations require the employer to listen to the reasons of both sides, and that some personal commitments are significant enough that permission should be granted while others are not. [Para 48][Para 51]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The English text is an unofficial translation with systematic OCR loss of the letter "h" ("temployer", "hemployer", "wit", "aring"), making some sentences ungrammatical though readable. The hearing date is given as 03 December 2015 in the procedure section but as "04 December 2015, thearing date" in the narrative of the telephone call. [Para 12][Para 40] The sixteen demands are printed with running numerals restarting inside paragraphs 10–11, so the list order had to be reconstructed from the withdrawal list and the decided issues. [Para 10][Para 16] Paragraph 63 duplicates and garbles the quoted insults; paragraph 77 is truncated mid-word ("other tas…"); the signature block is largely illegible apart from the printed names. [Para 63][Para 77][Para 91] The supervisor Maila is described as "Pilipino" (Filipino) and is referred to as "he" in one passage and by a female name elsewhere. [Para 31][Para 61]
