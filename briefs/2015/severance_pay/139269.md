# Award No. 201/15 — Phnom Penh International Airport (Cambodia Airports Services) (Arbitration Council, 26 August 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Kong Phalla, chair chosen by both) · non-binding award · Doc ID 139269

## 1. Facts
The airport services company employed about 830 staff, roughly 200 of them women, and recognised the Independent Union of Phnom Penh International Airport Workers, with some 526 members. Conciliation of six union demands failed on 17 July 2015 and the case reached the Council by report No. 826 Kប/អក/វក dated 27 July 2015; the hearing was held on 4 August 2015. The union sought reinstatement and back pay for three members dismissed in 2014 and 2015 — its senior adviser Mr Y and two members, Mr C and Mr D — plus a workplace nursery (ទារកដ្ឋាន) and canteen, an end to multi-skilling (ពហុជំនាញ), and withdrawal of warning letters issued to six apron drivers. [Para 118][Para 122][Para 32][Para 35][Para 123][Para 178]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr Y, a union adviser, and pay back wages, his dismissal being alleged to be anti-union discrimination? [Para 123]
2. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr C and pay back wages after dismissing him over a passenger's stolen tablet? [Para 160]
3. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr D and pay back wages for repeated lateness and absence? [Para 178]
4. Whether the employer must provide a nursery and a canteen selling food to staff? [Para 201]
5. Whether the employer must stop applying multi-skilling? [Para 217]
6. Whether the employer must withdraw warning letters from six named apron drivers? [Para 218]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The dismissals were retaliation against union activists, the alleged theft was never proved and the tablet photograph could have come from the colleague who lent the device, Mr D's lateness was notified and excused, the Milky Way school does not take children under 30 months, the canteen is an administration-only room with no food for sale, and the six drivers were warned for refusing cargo work that was never their job. [Para 128][Para 170][Para 182][Para 204][Para 213][Para 219]
**Employer (respondent):** The three dismissals were individual disputes outside the Council's jurisdiction and in any event lawful: Mr Y repeatedly refused assigned cargo hauling despite three meetings and three warnings, Mr C's dismissal followed a disciplinary council, and Mr D had a history of unexcused lateness recorded in signed acknowledgements; it had contracted the Milky Way school at its own cost, provided a dining room and a monthly food allowance, and needed multi-skilling, a point already decided in its favour. [Para 121][Para 143][Para 165][Para 189][Para 208][Para 218]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the reinstatement claims of Mr Y and Mr D were dismissed but the company was ordered to pay each termination benefits: to Mr Y, 06 months' wages and benefits as indemnity, 03 months' wages in lieu of notice, unused annual leave and final wages for June 2015; to Mr D, 135 days' wages and benefits, 02 months' wages in lieu of notice, unused annual leave and final wages for August 2014. The company was ordered to reinstate Mr C with back wages from dismissal to return, and to arrange a nursery for women workers, paying outside nursery fees from 18 months of age where it cannot provide one. The canteen, multi-skilling and warning-letter demands were refused, the last two without consideration on the merits. [Para 461][Para 462][Para 468][Para 466][Para 472][Para 473][Para 474][Para 475]

On jurisdiction the panel held the union was the proper claimant under Article 268 of the Labour Law, since a registered union's leaders may represent all members before the Council, and that issues 1 to 3 were collective disputes under Article 302: the parties were the employer and the union, the subject was working conditions and the exercise of professional-organisation rights, and the dispute could disrupt the enterprise or social peace. The employer, having contested that characterisation, bore the burden of proving it. [Para 233][Para 237][Para 238][Para 240][Para 245][Para 247]

On issue 1 the panel found no evidence of anti-union discrimination, the burden lying on the workers, and accepted that the dismissal rested on refusal of assigned work after three explanatory meetings and warnings. Because the internal rules lacked a proper visa and did not clearly list that refusal as heavy misconduct, the conduct fell under Article 74 rather than Article 83, so dismissal was lawful but compensation was owed under Articles 89, 75, 77, 166 and 116, the 15-day-per-year indemnity being capped at six months. [Para 260][Para 266][Para 281][Para 283][Para 291][Para 296][Para 312]

On issue 2 the panel held the employer had not discharged its burden of proving theft: the photograph of Mr C on the tablet was equally consistent with his account of borrowing it from the colleague on duty that flight, no witness was produced, and suspicion alone is not a valid reason to terminate under Article 74. Reinstatement and back pay followed, under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 338][Para 340][Para 341][Para 349][Para 350]

On issue 3 the panel found the lateness and absences documented and admitted, again held the conduct was a valid Article 74 reason but not Article 83 heavy misconduct, and awarded the statutory indemnity, notice pay, leave and final wages. [Para 366][Para 374][Para 379][Para 387][Para 394]

On issue 4 the panel applied Article 186: an enterprise using at least 100 women workers must provide a nursing room and a nursery, and where it cannot, the employer pays actual invoices for outside care from 18 months of age. A school taking children only from 30 months did not comply, so a nursery was ordered; but Articles 42 and 43 define and license canteens without imposing a duty to open one, and no collective agreement term required it, so that demand, being a claim for benefits, was refused. [Para 400][Para 408][Para 409][Para 418][Para 425]

On issue 5 the panel applied res judicata: the same union had raised the same multi-skilling demand against the same employer in case 53/13, decided on 10 April 2013, and all three conditions were met, so it declined jurisdiction. On issue 6 the workers produced no warning letters and the employer had not brought its disciplinary file, so the demand failed for want of evidence. [Para 427][Para 439][Para 441][Para 442][Para 459][Para 460]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A registered union may bring a collective dispute in its own name for its members where the Article 302 conditions are met, and the employer contesting that characterisation carries the burden; a dismissal is unlawful and reinstatement with back pay follows where the employer cannot prove the misconduct alleged, suspicion and ambiguous circumstantial evidence being insufficient, while documented refusal of assigned work or repeated lateness is a valid Article 74 reason supporting dismissal yet not Article 83 heavy misconduct, so severance, notice pay and leave remain payable; and Article 186 obliges an employer with 100 or more women workers to provide a nursery, or to fund outside care from 18 months, which a 30-month school placement does not satisfy. [Para 237][Para 341][Para 283][Para 409]

**Obiter dicta:** That the employer's right to manage under Article 2 lasts only while exercised lawfully and reasonably; that an unregistered or unvisaed internal rule cannot convert conduct into heavy misconduct; and that while a collective agreement is in force a union cannot claim further benefits unless the employer agrees to vary it. [Para 447][Para 281][Para 423]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is heavily OCR-damaged. The date the panel was constituted reads "28 July 2022 2015". The employer's registered name appears in at least four spellings across the file. The canteen demand is written អាគារូបដ្ឋាន (a building) in the decision but អាហារដ្ឋាន (canteen) in the reasons. Mr C's start date is given as 1 June 2005 by the employer and 15 June 2005 by the workers, and his dismissal as 26 August 2014 in the union's submissions but 3 June 2015 in the findings. In the reasons on issue 3 the panel twice names Mr Y where Mr D is meant. Mr D's surname is spelled three ways. One page carries the figure "2017" and another "2016" inside otherwise 2015 text.
