# Award No. 120/15 — Forfik Kros Brai Vet Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 19 June 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mr Hem Huonarit for the employer party, Mr An Non for the worker party, Mr Pen Bun Chheam chair) · Doc ID 139200

## 1. Facts
The employer is a garment-cutting and sewing company on National Road 4 at Bek Chan, Ang Snuol, Kandal, employing about 2,500 workers. [Para 45][Para 46] The Union Federation of Khmer Workers' Spirit (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ស្មារតី កម្មករ ខ្មែរ) filed a 13-point complaint on 24 April 2015; conciliation on 07 May 2015 settled 5 points and left 8, of which the panel's hearing on 28 May 2015 settled 3 more (issues 02, 05 and 07), leaving five for decision. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 29][Para 30] The local union had held an election on 21 April 2015 but had not yet been registered with the Ministry of Labour. [Para 47][Para 88] Rights were transferred by 84 workers, one without a thumbprint, giving 83, plus 3 leaders present in person — 86 claimants in all. [Para 48][Para 93][Para 95]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise the midday meal allowance by 1,500 riel, to 4,000 riel a day, paid every Monday? [Para 51]
2. Whether the employer must pay 2,000 riel an hour as a meal allowance for overtime work? [Para 56]
3. Whether the employer must pay wages and benefits withheld during the strike of 01 to 08 April 2015, without offsetting annual leave? [Para 60]
4. Whether the employer must pay a skills allowance of USD 15, USD 10 and USD 5 for workers able to operate three, two and one machines? [Para 70]
5. Whether the employer must pay the same incentive bonus to the materials-section assistants and the sleeve-assembly section as to sewing workers? [Para 76]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** 2,500 riel is not enough to buy lunch and neighbouring factories pay 3,000 riel; the same shortfall is said of the 3,000 riel overtime meal payment; wages were withheld during the strike although the employer brought in daily-paid outsiders to replace strikers; skills pay would reward skill and let multi-machine workers cover for others; assistants and sleeve-assembly workers do more work than sewers and should share their bonus. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 57][Para 62][Para 72][Para 78][Para 79]
**Employer (respondent):** it already pays 2,500 riel a day, above the legal minimum, and 3,000 riel per overtime session, again above Notification No. 041/11, but the company is newly opened and cannot pay more; wages for the strike days were paid by offsetting annual leave and the workers did not deny it; there is no skills-pay policy, only an EGS output bonus of USD 80 to USD 90 a month; and the bonus differential follows skill and section. [Para 3][Para 55][Para 59][Para 64][Para 74][Para 75][Para 80][Para 82]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all five remaining claims were refused, the panel declining to consider them as interest disputes. [Para 175][Para 176][Para 177][Para 178][Para 179]

On standing, Article 268 of the Labour Law gives a professional organisation the rights the law recognises only once it is registered with the Ministry, and Prakar 19 of Prakas No. 099/04 requires written authorisation for a representative. [Para 84][Para 85][Para 90][Para 91] The local union was unregistered, and one of the 84 transfer letters lacked a thumbprint, so the panel proceeded for the 83 verified workers plus the 3 leaders who appeared. [Para 88][Para 92][Para 93][Para 95]

On the meal allowance, the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, collective bargaining agreement, internal rules, contract or past practice requiring 4,000 riel a day paid on Mondays; the practice was 2,500 riel paid each Wednesday. [Para 96][Para 97] It classified the claim as an interest dispute. [Para 98] Under Article 96 only the most representative union may negotiate a collective agreement, and under Prakar 9 of Prakas No. 305 only such a union may bring an interest dispute; Prakar 43 of Prakas No. 099/04 makes an award on an interest dispute operate as a collective agreement binding all workers in the enterprise for one year and removes their right to strike on that subject. [Para 99][Para 102][Para 103][Para 104][Para 105] The claimant union was not the most representative, so the panel could not decide the claim. [Para 106][Para 108][Para 109][Para 110] The same reasoning disposed of the overtime meal allowance, the skills allowance and the bonus claim — each a benefit above the statutory floor, and each refused for want of representativeness. [Para 115][Para 116][Para 119][Para 153][Para 154][Para 157][Para 173][Para 174]

On strike wages, Article 332(1) suspends the employment contract during a strike and provides that no wages are paid; Article 72 confirms suspension removes the wage obligation; Article 334 forbids hiring replacements and, where replacements are hired, obliges the employer to pay the strikers. [Para 120][Para 121][Para 123][Para 126][Para 128] The workers said outsiders were hired for the cloth warehouse and other sections but could not say how many or where, and offered no evidence; the employer denied it. [Para 129][Para 130][Para 133][Para 135] The employer had in fact paid the strike-period wages by deducting equivalent annual leave, and the workers accepted the money without objecting. [Para 137][Para 138][Para 139][Para 147] The panel therefore refused to award the wages again while leaving annual leave intact, holding that a worker who wants to keep the leave must repay the wages received for April. [Para 148][Para 149][Para 150]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that is not registered under Article 268 of the Labour Law is not the most representative organisation in the enterprise, and the panel will refuse to decide an interest dispute — a claim for a benefit above what the law, a collective agreement, internal rules or established practice already provides — brought by it, because an award on such a dispute would bind every worker as a collective agreement and extinguish their right to strike on the subject. [Para 89][Para 98][Para 105][Para 106][Para 109] Where an employer has paid strike-period wages by deducting an equivalent number of annual leave days and the workers accepted payment, the panel will not order the wages a second time while the leave is restored. [Para 147][Para 149]

**Obiter dicta:** the remarks that Article 334 obliges an employer who hires strike replacements to pay the strikers, and that an EGS output bonus is a unilateral management measure, were not applied — the first for want of evidence, the second because no claim turned on it. [Para 128][Para 163][Para 168]
