# Award No. 100/15 — Chung Fai Knitwear FTY (Arbitration Council, 5 June 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Sok Matherng presiding; Mar Sambor Na, appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna, appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 139191

## 1. Facts
Chung Fai Knitwear FTY ("Chung Fai"), in Prek Ta Long, Chak Angre Krom, Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, sews garments and employed about 450 workers. [Para 37][Para 38] The Free Workers' Union of Chung Fai Knitwear factory, registered with the Ministry of Labour on 18 November 2005, filed a complaint on 31 March 2015 that piece-rate ("ដេរ ស៊ី បុង") workers whose output fell short were not being topped up to the statutory minimum wage; conciliation on 9 April 2015 failed on the single issue, and 28 workers transferred their rights to the union on 18 May 2015. [Para 9][Para 10][Para 22][Para 40] Only one issue survived, and both parties agreed the employer had not topped up some piece-rate workers to the minimum wage. [Para 17][Para 43] The award is non-binding and becomes final 8 days after notification. [Para 89]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must top up the piece-rate wages of workers whose output falls below the guaranteed minimum wage — USD 100 a month for 2014 and USD 128 a month for 2015 — from October 2014 onwards? [Para 43][Para 46][Para 59]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the minimum wage Prakas applies to piece-rate workers as much as to time-rate workers, and the reason output falls short is that the employer did not raise piece rates when the minimum wage rose; they later found that some workers had been underpaid since September 2014 and claimed the shortfall for 26 named workers. [Para 45][Para 47][Para 48][Para 54]

**Employer (respondent):** it does top up workers who try hard and still fall short, but not those who do not try — those who talk on the phone, eat snacks and do not work; only two or three workers are affected and it will not top them up because they refused to accept a warning letter. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer is ordered to top up piece-rate wages to USD 100 a month for full-rights workers in 2014 and USD 128 a month for 2015, for all 26 named workers, according to each worker's actual shortfall. [Para 87][Para 88]

The panel set out the statutory floor: Prakas No. 317 of 31 December 2013 raised the garment, textile and footwear minimum from USD 80 to USD 100 a month for 2014, effective 1 February 2014, and Instruction No. 001/14 of 2 January 2014 provides that a piece-rate worker is paid by actual output but, where output falls below the minimum, the employer must add enough to reach USD 95 for probationary and USD 100 for full-rights workers. [Para 59][Para 61][Para 62][Para 64] Prakas No. 283 of 12 November 2014 then fixed 2015 at USD 128 a month for full-rights and USD 123 for probationary workers, effective 1 January 2015. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 73]

On the employer's justification, the panel held that Article 2 of the Labour Law gives the employer the right to manage and discipline only so long as that right is exercised lawfully and reasonably, and that Article 28 forbids monetary penalties — any measure that reduces wages the worker has normally earned by doing the work — and also forbids punishing the same fault twice. [Para 79][Para 81][Para 83] Withholding the minimum-wage top-up because a worker refused a warning letter or did not work hard was therefore unlawful. [Para 85][Para 86]

On the facts, the panel found the workers' evidence listed 26 underpaid workers, the wage tables established only 22, and four workers had not attached pay slips; because the employer did not contest the workers' evidence by the deadline and both parties agreed the exact shortfall figures, the panel granted the top-up for all 26 on each worker's actual shortfall. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 56][Para 57][Para 58]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A piece-rate worker whose output falls below the statutory minimum wage is entitled to be topped up to that minimum, and the employer's right to manage and discipline under Article 2 does not permit it to withhold the top-up as a sanction, because Article 28 prohibits monetary penalties and the minimum wage is a guaranteed floor rather than a reward for effort; the shortfall is owed worker by worker on the proven figures, and an employer that files no objection to the workers' shortfall schedules cannot later dispute them. [Para 64][Para 79][Para 85][Para 86][Para 58]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the employer may still discipline a worker who breaches the internal rules, but only through lawful disciplinary measures and not by cutting wages. [Para 84][Para 85]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award does not fix the starting month of the top-up: the dispute is described as running from October 2014 at paras 43 and 44, from September 2014 at para 45, and the order simply says "for 2014" and "for 2015" without a month. [Para 43][Para 45][Para 87] The claimant group is 28 workers who transferred rights at para 40 but 26 in the claim and the order at paras 54 and 87. [Para 40][Para 54] Stray years intrude: the panel is constituted on "28 April 2016 2015" at para 12, the Instruction is dated "31 December 2015 2013" at para 62, and "2016" and "case 20" appear at paras 74 and 88. [Para 12][Para 62][Para 74][Para 88] The 2015 probationary figure is printed as "123 (one hundred [Cambodia] dollars)" at para 72, and the four workers without pay slips are not identified. [Para 58][Para 72] Individual workers appear in the award only by Khmer name and are not reproduced here.
