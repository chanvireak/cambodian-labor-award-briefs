# Award No. 289/15 — M & V International Manufacturing Ltd, Kampong Chhnang Branch (Arbitration Council, 22 December 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Tang Tri (president), Chiv Phirom (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed) · Doc ID 139465

## 1. Facts
M & V International Manufacturing Ltd is a garment manufacturer registered in 1995 and employing about 3,276 workers; the claim concerns its Kampong Chhnang branch [Para 69][Para 70]. In 2015 the Cambodian Democratic Garment Workers' Union Federation began organising at the branch: it notified the company of an election on 31 August 2015, held the election on 15 September 2015, and notified the results on 16 September 2015, by post and by e-mail [Para 53][Para 54][Para 57][Para 108][Para 110]. One of the two men, Mr S, was elected vice-president of the local union; the other, Mr S, stood but was not elected; their fixed-duration contracts ran out on 30 September and 31 October 2015 and were not renewed [Para 96][Para 131][Para 132]. The local union had not received its registration certificate from the Ministry when the case was heard [Para 71][Para 203]. Of seven claims, three were withdrawn, leaving issues 1, 4, 5 and 7 [Para 19][Para 20]. The parties agreed on 30 November 2015 that this award would be binding [Para 21][Para 208].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the two election candidates — one elected vice-president, one not elected — and pay their wages again from the date of termination? [Para 75][Para 128]
2. Whether the employer must use fixed-duration contracts of one year rather than three or six months? [Para 113][Para 163]
3. Whether the employer must not deduct wages or benefits when a worker faints or falls ill during working hours and has a proper medical certificate? [Para 117]
4. Whether the employer must deduct union dues from members' wages and pay them directly into the federation's bank account, although the local union is unregistered? [Para 120][Para 183]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** both men were union election candidates and therefore protected under Prakas No. 305; the employer ended their contracts without the labour inspector's authorisation, and renewed other workers while letting these two go, which shows anti-union discrimination [Para 92][Para 93][Para 106].

**Employer (respondent):** orders fell from early August 2015, so the company adopted a policy of not renewing contracts that had run their term and of hiring no new workers, affecting between 200 and 300 people; only pregnant women were renewed, precisely to avoid a discrimination complaint; there was no anti-union motive, and three properly registered unions exist in the company [Para 87][Para 88][Para 90][Para 105].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — reinstatement and back wages were refused, the claim for one-year contracts was refused, and the claim that the employer must deduct dues was refused; the wages claim for fainting or illness was not decided because the employer conceded it; no sum was awarded [Para 205][Para 206][Para 207]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 73(1): a fixed-duration contract expires normally when its term is reached [Para 129][Para 130]. The last contracts of the two men ended on 30 September and 31 October 2015 [Para 131][Para 132]. Article 73(5) requires ten days' advance notice of expiry or non-renewal only for contracts longer than six months, and says nothing about shorter ones [Para 133][Para 134]; the employer's notifications of 23 September and 8 October 2015 were therefore an act the law does not require [Para 135][Para 136]. Turning to the protection in Article 293 and Article 3 of Prakas No. 305, the panel read the 45-day shield as attaching to dismissal alone, since it is the dismissal of a protected worker that requires the labour inspector's consent [Para 137][Para 138][Para 139][Para 142][Para 147]. For elected leaders the Prakas fixes no end point, and the panel repeated that protection should last at least 45 days after the election or for a reasonable period such as until registration is applied for [Para 144][Para 145]. Whatever its length, it does not reach the natural expiry of a fixed-duration contract, and no provision requires the employer to seek authorisation for non-renewal at expiry [Para 148][Para 150][Para 153]. On discrimination the panel applied Articles 12 and 279, which forbid using union membership or activity as a basis for decisions on hiring and allocation of work [Para 154][Para 155][Para 156], but held that the worker who alleges discrimination carries the burden of proving it [Para 159][Para 160]; no specific evidence was produced that these two were singled out for their union role [Para 161], so reinstatement and back wages were refused [Para 162].

On issue 4 the panel held that a contract is the meeting of two wills: under Articles 65 and 311 of the Labour Law and Articles 664, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code, a contract is formed only by offer and acceptance, and neither party — nor the panel as third party — can impose a form or content the other has not accepted [Para 164][Para 170][Para 173][Para 176][Para 178][Para 179][Para 180]. The employer declined one-year contracts; the claim was refused [Para 181][Para 182].

On issue 7 the panel applied Article 129(2), which lets a worker authorise deduction of union dues in writing and revoke that authorisation at any time [Para 185][Para 186], together with Article 5 of Prakas No. 305, which requires at least 15 days' written request before the employer must act [Para 187]. Article 268 then conditions the rights of a workers' organisation on lodging its statutes and the list of its officers with the Ministry for registration [Para 190][Para 191]. Following Award No. 133/07 — Dyna Thitithing, an unregistered union has no legal personality and cannot claim dues through the employer [Para 192][Para 194]. Here the local union had no certificate [Para 203], and the claim sought payment to the federation rather than to the local union; the employer therefore had no duty to deduct [Para 195][Para 201][Para 204].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a fixed-duration contract simply runs out, the employer needs no labour inspector's authorisation and the Article 293 and Prakas No. 305 protection of union candidates does not apply, because that shield attaches to dismissal, not to non-renewal at expiry; a worker alleging anti-union discrimination must prove it [Para 147][Para 148][Para 159]. An employer cannot be ordered to conclude a contract in a form or for a term it has not accepted, and it owes no duty to deduct union dues for a local union that has not been registered with the Ministry [Para 178][Para 194].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that contracts shorter than six months attract no statutory notice of expiry [Para 134], and that the claim about fainting and illness fell away once the employer confirmed it made no such deduction [Para 118][Para 119].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel's name is printed as "An Non" in the composition and as "An Nona" at the signature [Para 1][Para 208], and the president's name appears as "Tang Tri" on the face of the award but "Taṃg Tri" in the signature block. The award date is 22 December 2015 [Para 1], while the extension clause reads only "from 09 6 2015 to 22 December 2015" [Para 23]. The company name is rendered in at least five ways ("M & V", "Mill & V", "M&V", "Im & V", "M & V International Manufacturing"), and its registration number is given as Inv. 037 E/1995 with an incorporation date of both 12 June 2014 and 5 July 1995 [Para 27][Para 48][Para 52][Para 69]. Worker head counts differ: 79 membership applications and 75 deduction requests, against 225 workers said to have fingerprinted the transfer of rights [Para 61][Para 62][Para 74]. The claim on issue 5 is recorded as both live and withdrawn in different places [Para 19][Para 117]. Individual workers' names are printed unredacted throughout and are reduced here to Mr S; several names appear in two or three different spellings, and one worker is given a second, invented-looking job title in a single passage [Para 135].
