# Award No. 192/15 — E Garmen Cambodia Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 21 August 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (You Soun Ty for the employer party, Toun Si Phan for the worker party, Ang Eng Thong chair) · Doc ID 139259

## 1. Facts
E Garmen Cambodia Co Ltd, a garment-cutting enterprise at Village 3, Svay Rolum commune, Sa'ang district, Kandal, employing about 2,970 workers, stopped work in its weaving section on four occasions between March 2014 and June 2015 — 01–31 March 2014, 01–18 April 2014, 20 November 2014 to 21 January 2015, and 25 May to 16 June 2015 — paying 50 per cent of wages during each stoppage. [Para 34][Para 38][Para 67] On 05 June 2015, 137 workers petitioned the Kandal provincial department of labour and vocational training for the missing 100 per cent and for an end to pressure to change their work section. [Para 8][Para 26][Para 35] The employer characterises the stoppages not as suspension but as an agreed rest at 50 per cent while orders were short, saying it offered alternative work and retraining and that 160 workers refused it. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the worker party 100 per cent wages for the four periods in which it suspended the employment contract without complying with the law? [Para 38][Para 67]
2. Whether those four stoppages were suspension of the employment contract under Article 71, and whether the employer's failure to seek the labour inspector's prior authorisation obliges it to pay the withheld 50 per cent? [Para 70][Para 82]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the stoppages were unlawful suspensions because the employer never sought the labour inspector's permission beforehand, so full wages are owed for all four periods; the workers never agreed to accept 50 per cent. [Para 41][Para 46][Para 66]
**Employer (respondent):** the rests were not suspension at all but a mutual arrangement at a time when there was no work, the workers agreeing to 50 per cent, so no authorisation from the labour inspector was required. [Para 42][Para 45][Para 68][Para 69]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted in substance — the panel ordered the employer to pay the 50 per cent of wages still owing for the four periods in which it suspended the employment contract without following procedure, the other 50 per cent having already been paid. No money figure is printed; the order is expressed in percentages only. [Para 89][Para 90]

The panel first had to settle who the claimant was. Article 268 makes the deposit of statutes and the list of responsible officers with the Ministry the condition on which a professional organisation acquires recognised rights, and two workers, Mr C and Mr N, claimed to be president and vice-president of the union at the factory. [Para 47][Para 50][Para 54] The panel found they had never deposited the union's registration with the Council and could not therefore be presumed to hold a representative mandate; Article 268 as interpreted in Awards 023/14 and 032/14 extends to a union's right to represent its members before the Council. [Para 51][Para 55][Para 57] Article 19 of Prakas No. 099/04 allows a party to appear through a representative authorised in writing, but the 137 signatories had transferred no authority to these men. [Para 58][Para 61] The six workers who did appear were, however, elected employee delegates of the enterprise, and Article 283 makes such delegates the sole representatives of the workforce; the panel accordingly treated them as the worker party. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 65]

On the first issue the panel turned to Article 71(1), which suspends the contract where the enterprise meets severe material or economic difficulty bringing its activity to a halt for not more than two months, and requires the suspension to be placed under the supervision of the labour inspector. [Para 70][Para 71] The employer admitted the four stoppages and attributed them to the shift of weaving from hand to computerised production and to insufficient orders — facts the panel held showed severe economic difficulty within Article 71(1). [Para 78][Para 79] The stoppages were therefore suspension of the contract, not the agreed leave the employer asserted. [Para 80]

That did not save the employer, because the second question was procedural. Article 72(1) preserves the employer's duty to pay wages during suspension, and Article 71 requires supervision by the labour inspector — read in Awards 58/07, 08/07 and 72/05 as an obligation to notify and obtain prior authorisation. [Para 82][Para 73][Para 74][Para 77] The employer conceded it never sought that authorisation before any of the four suspensions, and paid only 50 per cent throughout. [Para 85][Para 87] The panel held that where the employer fails to follow the suspension procedure it must pay 100 per cent of wages even though no work was given, following Awards 60/06, 74/07, 28/08, 53/08 and 71/09. [Para 84] Because 50 per cent had already been paid, only the remaining 50 per cent was ordered. [Para 89]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A stoppage caused by severe economic difficulty is a suspension of the employment contract under Article 71(1) of the Labour Law, but it is lawful only if the employer notifies and obtains the labour inspector's prior authorisation; where that procedure is not followed the suspension is unlawful and the employer owes 100 per cent of wages even though no work was provided, credit being given for any part already paid. [Para 79][Para 84][Para 88][Para 89]

Separately, a person claiming to lead a union at the enterprise cannot represent the workers before the panel unless the union's statutes and officers' list have been deposited under Article 268 and the individual holds a written mandate from the complainants; elected employee delegates under Article 283 may represent them instead. [Para 57][Para 61][Para 65]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the rights recognised by Article 268 include a union's right to represent its members in disputes before the Council, and its citation of Volume Co and Yasing Plastic, are adoptions of earlier reasoning rather than holdings on the facts here. [Para 51][Para 53]
