# Award No. 155/15 — Yan Zyi (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 16 July 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chia, chair) · non-binding award · Doc ID 139234

## 1. Facts
Yan Zyi is a garment-sewing enterprise that began operating in January 2015 and employed roughly 100 workers. On 15 June 2015 the Labour Disputes Department received a claim from 52 of those workers, acting through three union federations holding written mandates dated 13 June 2015, seeking improvement of eighteen working conditions. Conciliation settled nine points and the remaining nine came to the Council, where the parties agreed two more and the workers withdrew five, leaving only dispute points 04 and 06 for adjudication. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41][Para 26][Para 31][Para 32][Para 33]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to calculate wages for work performed on public holidays at twice the ordinary daily wage plus one (គុណពីរ បូកមួយ)? [Para 45]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to allow pregnant workers one day per month to attend prenatal examinations while preserving their wages and other benefits? [Para 52]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the practice of other enterprises such as Pou Yuen and Meng Da Huot Wear is to pay twice plus one for holiday work, and a public holiday is a rest day, so the employer owes the same supplement as for weekly rest-day work; a half day is unworkable because travel to a distant clinic and queueing for a number prevent a timely return to the line. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 54][Para 55]

**Employer (respondent):** the factory opened only in January 2015 whereas the comparators have operated for years and can afford the demand; the company applies the law and asks to continue doing so, and a half day suffices because the health centre sits near the factory. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 56]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider both remaining claims, so nothing was ordered in riel or USD. [Para 75][Para 82][Para 83][Para 84]

On point 04 the panel read article 164 of the Labour Law together with article 4 of Prakas No. 10 SChBy of 4 February 1999. Those provisions entitle a worker who works on a public holiday, in addition to the wage for the work done, to a compensatory payment equal to one ordinary day's wage, fixed by ministerial regulation. The panel found that the employer's actual practice — the ordinary daily rate plus one further day's wage — conformed to that regime, and that no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, agreement, internal rule or past practice between these parties required twice plus one. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 59][Para 61][Para 62]

Because no existing norm granted the higher rate, the panel characterised the demand as an interest dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍) rather than a rights dispute. It then applied article 96 of the Labour Law and article 9(1) of Prakas No. 305 SChBy of 22 November 2001: only the most representative union may compel the employer to conclude a collective agreement, and only such a union may bring an interest dispute to the Council. Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099 SChBy of 21 April 2004 supplied the reason for that restriction — an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement for one year and, in the panel's view, thereby removes the workforce's right to strike over that subject. The 52 claimants did not constitute a most representative union in the enterprise. [Para 63][Para 66][Para 67][Para 68][Para 69][Para 73][Para 74]

On point 06 the panel repeated the analysis: no Labour Law provision, collective agreement, agreement, internal rule or past practice obliged the employer to grant a full day, the established practice being a half day with wages and benefits preserved, so the demand was again an interest dispute, and the panel expressly cross-referred to its reasoning on point 04. The same want of standing defeated it, and the panel recorded that the 52 claimants were likewise not a most representative union for this purpose. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 78][Para 79][Para 80][Para 81]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A panel will refuse to consider an interest dispute — a demand for a benefit that no law, collective agreement, agreement, internal rule or established practice already confers — unless the claimant is the most representative union in the enterprise, or several unions together representing more than half of the workforce; the reason is that an award on such a dispute substitutes for the collective agreement for one year and extinguishes the right to strike on that subject. [Para 62][Para 69][Para 70][Para 71][Para 74]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's findings that paying the ordinary daily rate plus one day's compensation satisfies article 164 and Prakas No. 10, and that a half day for prenatal checks is lawful, were not necessary to the outcome, which rested on standing alone. [Para 61][Para 77]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Paragraph 28 prints the panel's constitution date as 26 June of both 2023 and 2015 in the same sentence; the 2015 reading is the only one consistent with the hearing date. Paragraph 35 sets the deadline for objecting to documents at 13 June 2015, a date earlier than the 7 July 2015 hearing and inconsistent with the 9 July 2015 filing deadline beside it, so one of the two months is misprinted. The signature block at paragraph 85 renders the chair's name both as Pen Bun Na and Pen Bun Chia, while the header prints Pen Bun Chia, and the employer-side signature is reduced to the fragment "mony". The claim headings in the report are numbered 2, 3, 4, 5, 9, 11, 12, 15 and 18 from the original eighteen, whereas the panel renumbers the surviving disputes 01 to 09 and then decides points 04 and 06; the two numbering systems do not map onto each other, and paragraph 26 and paragraph 27 each report nine points, one as settled and one as unresolved. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 28][Para 35][Para 85]
