# Award No. 011/15 — DIC Nerty Needle Ther Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 11 March 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Sin Kim Sean (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (chair, appointed by the two members) · Doc ID 139127

## 1. Facts
The company cuts and sews garments and states that it employs about 2,000 workers. [Para 90]
The Cambodian Democratic Garment Workers' Federation filed twenty demands with the Kampong Cham labour department on 05 December 2014; conciliation settled two of twelve points, and three more (02, 03, 09) settled at hearing, leaving six for adjudication, with issues 05 and 06 consolidated. [Para 29][Para 37]
Ninety-two workers transferred their claims to the federation president; the company ended a series of fixed-term contracts between September 2014 and January 2015 — 29 in September, 56 in October, 24 in November, 21 in December and 17 in January. [Para 114]
Both sides elected a non-binding award. [Para 38]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company discriminated against four pregnant workers by letting their fixed-term contracts expire because they were pregnant? [Para 99]
2. Whether the company must rehire the 146 fixed-term workers whose contracts it did not renew? [Para 228]
3. Whether the company must give the weaving section 100% wage arrears for work not provided during allegedly unlawful suspensions? [Para 236]
4. Whether the company must reinstate Ms P, a union activist and worker delegate dismissed under an internal rule? [Para 268]
5. Whether pregnant workers must be allowed one paid day a month to attend antenatal checks? [Para 280]
6. Whether night-shift wages must be paid at 200% plus one extra hour, as practised since 2006? [Para 297]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The four pregnant workers' contracts were allowed to lapse although the company knew of their pregnancies from medical certificates and letters sent to their homes; ordinary workers were let go in the same months, so the pattern was not pregnancy-specific. Weaving workers were left without work and paid only 50%, which the employer blamed on them, and Ms P was dismissed for absence caused by her mother's illness. Night-shift pay had been 200% since 2006 and was now being cut. [Para 152][Para 192]
**Employer (respondent):** Orders fell sharply between August and March, so contracts were not renewed across whole sections, and selection followed workload, performance and discipline, not pregnancy. Weaving workers were offered other work and refused it; Ms P abandoned her job by absenting herself 35 days without leave, and the Minister of Labour authorised her dismissal. Night-shift pay should follow Prakas No. 185 at 130%. [Para 111][Para 196]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed. Every remaining claim was refused — issue 01 on the merits, issues 04, 08 and 10 for want of jurisdiction, and issues 05 & 06 and 07 on their merits. No payment or reinstatement was ordered. [Para 302][Para 307]
On pregnancy, the panel applied Article 46 of the Constitution and Article 182(3) of the Labour Law, which bar dismissal on grounds of pregnancy, and placed the burden of proof on the complainant. It found the company ended contracts section-wide when orders fell, and the four women were caught up in groups of 29, 56, 24, 21 and 17; two of them had documented poor work records, and two were simply in sections with no work. It also noted that 11 women did take maternity leave with 50% pay, which undercut the claim of a discriminatory pattern. [Para 197][Para 226]
The claim for 146 workers was not considered because the union president had no written authority to represent them: of the 133 names submitted, none appeared in the mandate list or at the hearing, so Prakas No. 099 Article 19 was not satisfied. [Para 233][Para 234]
The weaving claim failed because the panel found the employer had no weaving work, offered other work in the same building at the same 100% wage and offered training, and the workers refused; under Article 2 the right to direct the enterprise is lawful where exercised reasonably and without prejudice to wages or benefits. [Para 256][Para 262]
Ms P's claim failed because the employer had followed Article 293: the labour inspectorate refused permission, the company appealed, and the Minister of Labour authorised the dismissal in letter No. 2143 of 12 November 2014, subject to payment of outstanding wages and benefits. [Para 276][Para 277]
The antenatal-day and night-shift claims were both treated as interest disputes, which the panel declined to hear because the complainant was not the most representative union in the enterprise; the panel noted the night-shift issue affected 359 listed workers, none of whom was in the mandate or present. [Para 294][Para 299]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union federation may not bring a collective claim on behalf of workers whose names appear nowhere in a written mandate and who are absent from the hearing; without that authorisation the panel has no basis to consider the claim, however numerous the workers listed. [Para 233][Para 234]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that letting fixed-term contracts expire section-wide when orders fall, and requiring performance-based selection rather than pregnancy status, rebuts an inference of pregnancy discrimination even where several pregnant workers are among those not renewed. [Para 213]
