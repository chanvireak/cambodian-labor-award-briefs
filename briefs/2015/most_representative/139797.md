# Award No. 358/14 — Asli Electronics (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 28 January 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouchhun for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Pen Bunhea chair) · Doc ID 139797

## 1. Facts
Asli Electronics (Cambodia), an electronics-parts manufacturer in the Sihanoukville Special Economic Zone employing over 600 workers, was asked by the Khmer Workers' Spirit Federation to settle six points of working conditions; conciliation on 24 December 2014 failed on five and the file reached the Council on 29 December 2014. [Para 1][Para 40][Para 15] Fifty workers transferred their rights to the federation and two more attended, so the panel decided only for those 52 claimants. [Para 44][Para 46] The disputed acts concern early release for pregnant workers, exit at lunch, the attendance bonus, and check-off of union dues. [Para 48][Para 56][Para 59][Para 67]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to let pregnant workers leave 15 minutes before the end of the working day? [Para 71]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to let workers leave the premises during the midday break without their team leader's permission? [Para 86]
3. Whether the employer is obliged to deduct the regular attendance bonus in proportion to the days a worker took authorised leave for personal business? [Para 109]
4. Whether the employer is obliged to deduct Riel 1,000 from the wages of the 424 workers who volunteered to pay union dues to the Khmer Workers' Spirit Federation? [Para 155]
5. Whether the employer is obliged to raise the regular attendance bonus to USD 15 per month, i.e. USD 5 above the existing USD 10? [Para 167]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** five minutes is not enough for a pregnant worker to put her things away, walk to the fingerprint scanner and get out; the attendance bonus is part of wages, so authorised leave should not cost the month's bonus; the union lodged signed consent forms for the 424 members but the employer refused to deduct. [Para 50][Para 60][Para 65]
**Employer (respondent):** pregnant workers are grouped at the end of the line only 7 to 8 metres from the scanner, so 5 minutes suffices and has been the practice for years; unrestricted exit at lunch led to workers not returning and slipping back through the fence to scan out, so the slip and security check are necessary; the USD 10 bonus is what the law requires and Article 281 forbids deducting wages to pay union dues. [Para 53][Para 57][Para 66][Para 70]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel refused to consider issues 1 and 5, ordered the employer to allow workers to leave the premises during the midday break without the team leader's permission (issue 2), ordered the bonus to be deducted in proportion to days of authorised personal leave (issue 3), and dismissed the union-dues claim (issue 4). [Para 176][Para 177][Para 178][Para 180]

On issue 1 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, no collective agreement and no agreement between the parties requiring 15 minutes, so the claim was an interest dispute. [Para 73][Para 74] Under Article 96 and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, an interest dispute can be decided only for the union most representative in the enterprise, because under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 the award replaces the collective agreement and binds all workers for a year. [Para 75][Para 77][Para 78] The claimant had been ordered to file its receipt of application for registration and did not, so the panel found no evidence the federation held one; with 52 claimants among more than 600 workers it was not the most representative union and lacked standing. [Para 43][Para 83][Para 85]

On issue 2 the panel read Article 137 (eight hours a day, forty-eight a week) with clause 6 of the internal rules of 3 March 2012 and the 40-minute break taken in three sittings at 11:00, 11:20 and 11:40. [Para 86][Para 88][Para 91] Following Award No. 23/14 (Volume Co Mui Eiffel (Cambodia)), it held the midday break is not working time under the employer's control and the worker is free to attend to personal matters without permission. [Para 99][Para 100] The written request, signed slip and security check therefore exceeded the employer's managerial prerogative under Article 2 and were neither lawful nor reasonable; the employer's evidence of abuse justified disciplining the individuals concerned, not a blanket permission regime. [Para 97][Para 105][Para 107]

On issue 3 the panel noted the company is not in the textile, garment or footwear sector, so Notification No. 230 K.B/S.C.N of 25 July 2012 does not apply directly; but the employer had been paying the USD 10 bonus under it and confirmed at the hearing that it accepted the notification, which made compliance obligatory. [Para 109][Para 113] The word "absence" had never been clarified by the Labour Advisory Committee and the panel declined to read it as covering both authorised and unauthorised absence. [Para 123][Para 128] Absences the law itself provides for — annual leave, special leave, public holidays — permit no deduction, so the worker keeps the full USD 10; absence for personal business authorised by the employer is not provided for, and since the contract is suspended under Article 71(6) and Article 72(1) relieves the employer of wages for those days, the bonus is payable only in proportion to days worked. [Para 126][Para 146][Para 149][Para 153]

On issue 4 the panel applied Article 268 (a professional organisation acquires legal rights only on registration with the Ministry) and Article 129(2) (a worker may authorise deduction of wages for dues of a union he belongs to), following Awards No. 023/14 Volume Co Mui Eiffel and No. 032/14 Wei Sing Plastic. [Para 155][Para 158][Para 160] The factory union had not obtained its registration certificate, so it was not a lawful professional organisation with the right to require check-off and the conditions for deduction were unmet. [Para 164][Para 166]

On issue 5 the panel found no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice requiring USD 15, so the claim exceeded what the law provides and was an interest dispute; for the reasons given on issue 1 the claimant was not the most representative union and the claim was not considered. [Para 170][Para 172][Para 175]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An award on an interest dispute binds every worker in the enterprise for a year and displaces the collective agreement, so the panel will decide such a claim only where the claimant is the most representative union in the enterprise; a union that cannot show a registration certificate from the Ministry is not a lawful professional organisation and cannot compel the employer to check off dues from members' wages. Where an employer has in fact been applying a ministerial notification and confirms at the hearing that it does so voluntarily, the notification's minimum becomes enforceable even outside its sector, and the bonus may then be reduced only in proportion to days of absence the notification does not excuse. [Para 77][Para 113][Para 153][Para 164]

**Obiter dicta:** the direction that the employer should discipline under the internal rules those workers shown to have left at lunch and not returned, and the remark that a midday break is time the worker may use for personal affairs, support but do not found the order on issue 2. [Para 99][Para 105]
