# Award No. 152/15 — G4S Security Services (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 13 August 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, appointed by the employer; An Vireak, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, president appointed by both) · Doc ID 139231 · non-binding award

## 1. Facts
G4S Security Services (Cambodia) Ltd provides security services and employs about 733 workers; its Cambodia Labour Service Union is registered with the Ministry of Labour under No. 2512 dated 21 June 2012, with a second-term leadership recognised by letter of 31 October 2014. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65][Para 66]
The union federation filed a complaint on 19 May 2015 seeking improvement of 14 working conditions; conciliation settled two, and the 12 unresolved points were referred to the panel. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 34] At the hearing the security staff withdrew nine points (1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 11), leaving points 2, 10 and 12. [Para 38][Para 39]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a meal allowance of 3,000 riel per day for overtime worked by security staff? [Para 4][Para 97]
2. Whether the employer must raise the base wage by 18 US dollars, from 110 to 128 US dollars per month, for all security staff? [Para 20][Para 118]
3. Whether the employer may pro-rate and deduct the 20 US dollar monthly allowance for days on which security staff take ordinary or annual leave? [Para 24][Para 129]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Food at some work sites is expensive so security staff spend heavily; a meal allowance for overtime would ease that burden, and overtime is otherwise excessive — the Law permits only two hours a day, yet security staff work a full eight-hour shift of overtime. Inflation, expensive rent and electricity, costly food and long commuting make the 110 US dollar base wage inadequate, and both ordinary and annual leave are rights that should not touch wages or the allowance. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74][Para 81][Para 84][Para 92]
**Employer (respondent):** It has never had a policy of providing meals or a meal allowance for overtime, and it pays overtime in accordance with the Labour Law; the company gives rice to security staff at the Pchum Ben festival and has no policy of forcing overtime. It raised monthly wages from 75 to 110 US dollars and the allowance from 10 to 20 US dollars in July 2014 but lost a client in doing so, and any further rise would force it to renegotiate prices with clients. The 20 US dollar allowance is a lump sum covering food, travel, accommodation and health, paid only on a full 26-day month, and deducting it pro rata preserves equality with staff who work the full month. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 79][Para 85][Para 86][Para 93][Para 94]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three demands were refused and nothing was ordered. Points 2 and 10 were declined as interest disputes carried by a union without most-representative status; point 12 was dismissed on its merits. [Para 117][Para 128][Para 144][Para 145][Para 146][Para 147]

On the meal allowance, the parties agreed the company had never provided meals or a meal allowance for overtime. Notification No. 041/11 of 07 March 2011 does grant 2,000 riel a day or one free meal to workers who volunteer for overtime at the employer's request, but its scope is limited to the textile, garment and footwear sectors; G4S provides security services, outside that scope, and no Ministry Prakas yet fixes such a benefit for security services. Nor did any law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice create a 3,000 riel entitlement. The panel therefore classified the demand as an interest dispute. Under Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305, the panel may decide an interest dispute only where the union bringing it is the most representative in the enterprise; this union was not, so the panel declined to consider the claim. [Para 98][Para 100][Para 101][Para 102][Para 103][Para 106][Para 110][Para 115][Para 117]

The wage demand followed the same path. Articles 104 and 107 of the Labour Law leave minimum wages to a Ministry Prakas, and no Prakas sets a minimum wage in the security sector; no agreement, collective agreement or law fixed 128 US dollars either. That too was an interest dispute, and again the union lacked most-representative status, so the demand was not considered. [Para 118][Para 119][Para 121][Para 123][Para 124][Para 128]

The allowance deduction was decided differently, on the merits. The panel found the 20 US dollar allowance is not an obligation imposed by the Labour Law but a reward the employer created to encourage discipline and full attendance. Under Article 2 of the Labour Law the employer has the right to organise and manage the enterprise provided it acts lawfully and reasonably, and the panel had held in earlier awards that an employer may unilaterally set incentive payments. Pro-rating the allowance by days of leave therefore fell within the employer's discretion and was neither unreasonable nor contrary to law, since the condition for earning it — a full 26-day month — was not met. [Para 135][Para 136][Para 138][Para 140][Para 141][Para 142][Para 143]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a claimed benefit is fixed by no law, collective agreement, internal rule or established practice, the demand is an interest dispute, and the panel will decline to decide it unless the union that brought it holds most-representative status in the enterprise, measured by membership exceeding half the workforce. By contrast, a discretionary allowance that the Labour Law does not require falls within the employer's management right under Article 2, and the employer may pro-rate it by days not worked so long as the rule is lawful and reasonable. [Para 110][Para 117][Para 141][Para 143]

**Obiter dicta:** Notification No. 041/11 confers no meal benefit on the security sector, and no Prakas yet sets a minimum wage there [Para 101][Para 121]; an award on an interest dispute stands in place of a collective agreement and binds all workers in the enterprise for one year, which is why the most-representative threshold matters [Para 108][Para 109].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's name is printed in at least four different transliterations across the award — "ជី ៤ អេស៊ីឃ្យួវីធី សឺវីស", "ជី ៤ អេស៊ី ឃ្យួរី ធី សឺវីស", "…សីវីស" and "ក្រុមហ៊ុន សន្តិសុខ ជី ៤ អេស" [Para 1][Para 28][Para 63]. The conciliation report is dated 23 June 2015 in one paragraph and 22 June 2015 in another [Para 28][Para 34]. Two demands on health and safety insurance have their coverage figures corrupted ("២៤ ម៉ោង ២" and "២៤ ម៉ោង ៤") [Para 6][Para 8], and stray Latin and digit fragments break several lines ("២ Rong , 1974", "២០ ៤ ដុល្លារ") [Para 64][Para 142]. The dispositive paragraphs are garbled, point 10 opening with "ឬ បដិសេធ" and point 12 carrying stray figures, though the sense is recoverable [Para 146][Para 147].
