# Award No. 350/14 — Scan Dinevian Tech Style (Cambodia) Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 19 January 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3 — Ouk Ry (employer-appointed), An Vireak (worker-appointed), Aing Eng Tong (chair) · Doc ID 139791

## 1. Facts
The company is a garment manufacturer at Toul Sangke, Phnom Penh, employing about 670 workers, most of them women. [Para 47][Para 48] Of 18 claims raised in November 2014, 14 were settled in conciliation and 4 were referred to the panel; 62 workers transferred their right to be represented to two federations. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 51] The four unsettled claims concerned a nursery and breastfeeding room, a midday meal, the length of fixed-duration contracts for men, and benefits withheld from staff on a basic wage of USD 500 or more. [Para 53][Para 61][Para 71][Para 80]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to provide a nursery (ទារកដ្ឋាន) and a breastfeeding room inside the factory? [Para 53]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to provide a midday meal, or 3,000 riel per day in lieu, to all workers? [Para 61]
3. Whether the employer is obliged to issue six-month fixed-duration contracts to male workers as it does to female workers? [Para 71]
4. Whether the panel has jurisdiction over the claim that workers on a basic wage of USD 500 and above were denied overtime pay, transport and housing allowance, attendance bonus, meal allowance and other benefits? [Para 162][Para 163]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The nursery and breastfeeding room are a statutory requirement under the Labour Law, and a nursery must meet a standard — childminders and enough equipment, not an empty room. [Para 55][Para 56] On meals, workers faint for lack of food, and 40 other factories already pay 1,000–2,000 riel in lieu. [Para 63][Para 65][Para 69] Three-month contracts for men expose them to dismissal and discriminate against them, since women receive six months. [Para 73][Para 74] The contract itself lists the benefits that were not paid. [Para 85][Para 97]
**Employer (respondent):** The nursery is being built to the law and will be finished within one month. [Para 59][Para 60] No law requires a midday meal, no worker has fainted from hunger, and the wage paid already exceeds the legal minimum. [Para 66][Para 67] Contract duration follows skill and the production chain, and is the employer's prerogative; the company issues only indefinite contracts and three-month contracts [Para 77][Para 79] and submitted a three-month contract for Mr N Pech Pisey at USD 500. [Para 96] The high basic wage already absorbs overtime, transport, attendance bonus and meal allowance. [Para 88][Para 100]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to arrange a nursery for female workers with children, and, where it cannot itself receive children over 18 months, to bear the actual invoiced cost of an outside nursery; the claims on meals, contract duration and the USD 500-plus benefits were dismissed. [Para 190][Para 191][Para 192][Para 193][Para 194]

On issue 1 the panel read Article 186 of the Labour Law: an enterprise employing at least 100 women must provide a breastfeeding room on or near the premises and a nursery. [Para 102][Para 104] Both sides accepted that no nursery exists yet. [Para 107] Because the Ministry has still not issued the Prakas contemplated by Article 187 fixing hygiene, childminder and equipment standards, the panel set its own minimum — a hygienic and clean environment for the children's health and to make things easier for mothers at work. [Para 110][Para 112][Para 113] It followed Awards 81/09, 39/13 and 91/13 on the same point. [Para 106]

On issue 2 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules or past practice obliging the employer to supply lunch or 3,000 riel. [Para 117] The claim was therefore an interest dispute. [Para 118] Under Article 96 of the Labour Law and Prakas 305, an interest dispute may only be brought by the most representative union; 62 of roughly 670 workers had transferred rights, so the claimant union was not most representative and the panel declined to consider the claim. [Para 122][Para 127][Para 129][Para 130]

On issue 3 the panel applied Articles 65 of the Labour Law and 664, 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code: a contract arises from matching offer and acceptance, and neither party may impose terms the other does not accept. [Para 133][Para 135][Para 143] The workers' request for six-month contracts was not contrary to the spirit of the law, but the employer's refusal was equally lawful. [Para 156][Para 157]

On issue 4 the panel noted that labour inspectors and the Ministry decide whether a dispute is individual or collective, and it ordinarily follows that decision absent clear reason to depart. [Para 163][Para 164] Article 302 requires three cumulative conditions; the first — a dispute between a number of workers and one or more employers — failed, because only Mr N Pech Pisey pressed the claim. The second claimant, Mr D Sul Kiphy, had not transferred representation rights and did not attend. [Para 175][Para 178][Para 184][Para 185] The panel therefore lacked jurisdiction. [Para 188]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer with at least 100 women workers must provide a nursery and breastfeeding room under Article 186 of the Labour Law even before the Article 187 Prakas is issued, and where it cannot receive children over 18 months itself it must pay the invoiced cost of an outside nursery; but the panel will not create a meal allowance, nor compel either party to accept a contract duration it has not agreed, and will decline an interest claim brought by a union that is not most representative. [Para 104][Para 117][Para 127][Para 157]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remark that a nursery should at minimum be hygienic and clean, and its general statement that parties must honour an agreed contract until its expiry date. [Para 113][Para 160]

The company name is printed three ways across the award — Scan Dinevian Tech Style, Sken Dinevian, and Sten Di Nevean. [Para 11][Para 47] The number of workers who transferred representation rights is given as "20" at para 129 and as "4" at para 176, against 62 earlier. [Para 51][Para 129][Para 176] The award is dated 19 January 2015 on its face, the parties having agreed to extend issuance from 8 January to 19 January. [Para 23]
