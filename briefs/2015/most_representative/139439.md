# Award No. 250/15 — LS Printing (Arbitration Council, 2 October 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chhea presiding; Seng Vouch Hun, employer nominee; Tuon Si Phan, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139439

## 1. Facts
LS Printing, at Chom Chao, Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh, prints designs onto garments and employs about 100 workers. [Para 45] The Cambodia Workers' Union Federation complained on 4 August 2015 on sixteen points; conciliation on 31 August settled none, and the file reached the Secretariat on 9 September 2015. [Para 24][Para 26] The Secretariat served the invitation on the director in person on 16 September 2015 and telephoned on the hearing day without answer; the employer never appeared. [Para 108][Para 109] The local union held only a receipt dated 21 September 2015 acknowledging its registration application, so the two months in Article 268(2) had not run, but one of the three workers present, Mr L, was an elected staff delegate. [Para 118][Para 120][Para 127]

## 2. Issues in dispute
The workers withdrew issues 1, 2, 6 and 8, leaving twelve. [Para 32][Para 33]
3. Whether overtime must be organised on the voluntary principle, with no compulsion or discipline for refusal? [Para 50]
4. Whether the employer must pay the seniority bonus to every worker with a year's service or more? [Para 57]
5. Whether the employer must pay 100 per cent of wages where it has no work, requires workers to punch in, then sends them home? [Para 63]
7. Whether the employer must stop requiring more than ten hours' work a day and limit overtime to two hours? [Para 67]
9. Whether wages and the regular-attendance bonus must be preserved when a worker is sent home sick during working hours? [Para 70]
10. Whether the employer must provide an infirmary, a nurse, a doctor and adequate medicines? [Para 75]
11. Whether the employer must supply hygienic drinking water? [Para 78]
12. Whether the employer must cool the production building as previously agreed? [Para 82]
13. Whether night-shift work must be paid at the statutory night rate? [Para 88]
14. Whether daily-paid workers must receive the same benefits as monthly-paid workers? [Para 91]
15. Whether the employer must keep the toilets clean? [Para 97]
16. Whether special leave must be granted without deducting wages and the attendance bonus? [Para 99]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** overtime is imposed rather than offered, a worker who declines needing written permission from the team leader, section head and management; seniority bonus, night-shift premiums and benefits are withheld, daily-paid workers receiving none; suspension of work is never authorised by the labour inspector; the infirmary amounts to a first-aid box, the drinking water turns red overnight, the building is unbearably hot and the toilets are filthy; and marriage leave was deducted from wages and the attendance bonus. [Para 50][Para 57][Para 62][Para 71][Para 77][Para 78][Para 94][Para 103]
**Employer (respondent):** absent. It filed no evidence, did not answer the Secretariat's telephone call, and was recorded as having abandoned its right of reply. [Para 31][Para 36][Para 109][Para 124]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel proceeded in the employer's absence under Article 21 of Prakas 099 and granted issues 3, 4, 5, 7, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15 and 16, while dismissing issue 9 in both its limbs. [Para 105][Para 111][Para 345][Para 350]
On standing, Article 268(2) deems a union registered only after two months without a response from the Ministry; the receipt was dated the morning of the hearing, so the union was not yet a lawful organisation and could not itself represent the workforce. [Para 118][Para 121][Para 122] The panel instead relied on Article 283, under which an enterprise with at least eight workers elects staff delegates who alone represent all the workers, and accepted Mr L in that capacity. [Para 127][Para 128][Para 129]
Issue 9 was an interest dispute — a demand for a new benefit rather than the application of an existing rule — and under Prakas 099 Article 43 only the most representative union may bring such a claim, which this union was not. [Para 197][Para 201][Para 205][Para 232][Para 235] The panel nonetheless held total forfeiture of the ten-dollar attendance bonus for two sick days disproportionate and a pro-rata deduction for the days lost reasonable. [Para 225][Para 228][Para 236][Para 352]
On issue 3, Article 4 of Prakas 80 of 1 March 1999 requires overtime to rest on the worker's consent and forbids pressure or discipline against a worker who declines; requiring prior written approval from three levels of management inverts that rule, and the panel ordered overtime to be organised voluntarily. [Para 130][Para 133][Para 142][Para 143] On issue 7, Notification 30/15 permits no more than two hours' overtime and a ten-hour day in total, even where the worker consents. [Para 179][Para 182][Para 187]
On issue 4 the panel applied Notification 041/11 of 7 March 2011, which entitles every worker with a year's service to the tabled seniority bonus, and found partial payment to be non-compliant. [Para 144][Para 146][Para 155][Para 157] On issue 5, Articles 71(11) and 72(1) relieve the employer of wages only where suspension is authorised and supervised by the labour inspector; here it was not, so 100 per cent wages were owed for the suspended days. [Para 160][Para 173][Para 175][Para 176]
On issues 10 and 11, Article 242 and Prakas 330 require an enterprise with fifty or more workers to keep an infirmary with a nurse and a doctor present two hours per shift, and Prakas 054 requires adequate hygienic drinking water; the panel ordered both, plus adequate medicines. [Para 237][Para 255][Para 263][Para 267][Para 273] On issue 12, Article 229 and Prakas 147 obliged the employer to take every means to make the workplace acceptably cool. [Para 281][Para 290] On issue 13, Article 144 as amended defines night work as 22:00 to 05:00 and requires 130 per cent of the day rate. [Para 291][Para 299][Para 300] On issue 14, Articles 9 to 11 make the rights of permanent and occasional, and of monthly and daily-paid, workers identical absent contrary agreement. [Para 304][Para 312][Para 316] On issue 15, Prakas 052 requires clean toilets with soap and water. [Para 319][Para 331] On issue 16, Article 171 and Prakas 267 let the employer offset special leave against annual leave or demand repayment, but not deduct wages and the attendance bonus outright. [Para 333][Para 343][Para 344]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union still inside the two-month deemed-registration period cannot bring an interest dispute, but staff delegates elected under Article 283 may represent the whole workforce, so a claim survives on that footing. [Para 121][Para 129] Overtime rests on individual consent, and an employer that makes a refusal conditional on written approval by several levels of management has reversed the statutory rule and must reorganise overtime as voluntary. [Para 133][Para 142] A worker sent home sick on an official medical certificate has a suspended contract, and the employer may deduct the attendance bonus pro rata for the days lost but not forfeit it entirely. [Para 228][Para 236]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the employer, by staying away after personal service of the invitation, abandoned its right to reply, and decided the case on the workers' evidence alone — a point it did not need to develop once absence was established. [Para 109][Para 124]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel is constituted on "11 September 2019 2015", the year duplicated by the OCR. [Para 28] The employer is "LS Printing" on the face of the award and in the conciliation report, but "LS Garment Cambodia" in the passages on the union and the infirmary. [Para 1][Para 118][Para 251] Mr L's start date is printed 13 June 2015 in the claim summary, which cannot be right where the award treats him as having over a year's service, and the same passage elsewhere gives 13 June 2014. [Para 62][Para 149] Sixteen issues were conciliated but twelve considered, the survivors being numbered 3, 4, 5, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15 and 16. [Para 32][Para 33] Issue 9 is dismissed under two headings and re-appears at the order stage, where a pro-rata attendance-bonus deduction is nonetheless ordered. [Para 235][Para 351][Para 352] The employer filed nothing, so the source records no respondent position at all; individual worker, union and delegate names are not reproduced here.
