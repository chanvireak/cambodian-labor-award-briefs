# Award No. 154/15 — Yunisun Garment (Arbitration Council, 22 July 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kol Vathanak, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Kong Pollak, president appointed by both) · Doc ID 139232 · non-binding award, with two annexed dissents (Kol Vathanak on the contract issue; An Non on the attendance bonus)

## 1. Facts
Yunisun Garment is a garment cutting and sewing enterprise employing about 635 workers. [Para 47][Para 48] Three federations — Samaki Workers' Federation (Independent), Samaki Workers' Federation of Cambodia and the Federation of Unions for Cambodian Workers — received a transfer of rights from 365 workers; none of them had a local union inside the company. [Para 49][Para 82]
On 13 June 2015 the Labour Disputes Department received a complaint about a strike led by those federations demanding improvement of 24 working conditions. Conciliation on 16 June settled 16 points, leaving 8; at the hearing point 1 was partly settled and the workers withdrew points 7 and 8, leaving six points for decision. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24][Para 28][Para 29][Para 30]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pro-rate the attendance bonus by the number of days a worker was absent with the employer's permission? [Para 50][Para 85]
2. Whether the employer must raise the attendance bonus from 12 to 15 US dollars a month? [Para 57][Para 114]
3. Whether the employer must raise the travel and accommodation allowance from 7 to 10 US dollars a month? [Para 61][Para 135]
4. Whether the employer must pay a meal allowance of 3,000 riel, instead of 2,000 riel, for overtime worked from 16:00 to 18:00? [Para 64][Para 143]
5. Whether the employer must issue six-month fixed-duration contracts in place of three-month contracts? [Para 67][Para 152]
6. Whether the employer must convert fixed-duration contracts to indefinite-duration contracts where the aggregate duration exceeds two years? [Para 178][Para 198]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Absence on genuine necessity with the employer's permission — a death in the family, for instance — should not cost the bonus, which should instead be cut in proportion to days absent; a 3-month contract exposes workers to repeated job loss and paperwork costs, so 6-month contracts would be better, and successive fixed-duration contracts totalling more than two years must become indefinite. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 69][Para 73]
**Employer (respondent):** It applies the law and its established practice: the attendance bonus is 12 US dollars a month and is cut by 6 dollars for any absence in the first half of the month and 6 dollars for any absence in the second half; contract duration depends on orders received and seasonality. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 56][Para 70]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand for conversion of contracts was granted and the employer ordered to convert to indefinite-duration contracts those workers whose successive fixed-duration contracts total more than two years, found to be 72 in all; points 1 to 5 were refused. [Para 197][Para 198][Para 203]

On the attendance bonus the panel found Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 had repealed and replaced Notification No. 041/11, adding the words "without absence" to the condition for earning at least 10 US dollars a month. The condition is therefore that the worker attend every working day of the month with no absence. Leave granted by the employer for personal reasons unconnected with contractual duty or with any legal entitlement is not performance of that condition, and granting the leave does not imply that the condition was met; a worker absent for such reasons has no pro-rated entitlement. The employer's 6-dollar/6-dollar deduction rule was reasonable and benefited workers by not withdrawing the whole bonus, so the claim failed. [Para 86][Para 89][Para 90][Para 91][Para 97][Para 98][Para 111][Para 199]

Points 2, 3 and 4 were benefits above the statutory floor, and the panel classified each as an interest dispute. The employer was already paying more than Notification No. 230/12 required for the bonus and for the travel allowance, and for the meal allowance it was complying with Article 2 of Notification No. 041/11, which sets 2,000 riel. No law, collective agreement, internal rule, contract or past practice established 15 dollars, 10 dollars or 3,000 riel. Under Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 the panel may decide an interest dispute only where the complainant union is most representative; the three federations, having no local union in the company, were not, so the panel declined to consider these claims. [Para 116][Para 117][Para 119][Para 120][Para 128][Para 132][Para 137][Para 145][Para 146][Para 151]

The six-month contract demand was refused on freedom of contract: under Article 65 of the Labour Law and Articles 311, 336, 337 and 664 of the Civil Code a contract arises from matching offer and acceptance, and neither party may impose a term or contract type the other does not accept. The workers' proposal was lawful, and so was the employer's refusal; the workers could not compel six-month terms. [Para 152][Para 162][Para 172][Para 173][Para 177][Para 202]

On conversion the panel held that Article 67(2) caps a fixed-duration contract, and any renewal, at two years, and that Article 73(5) treats successive renewals without notice of termination as converting the contract to indefinite duration once the aggregate exceeds that ceiling; it followed Award No. 10/03 and read ILO Recommendation No. 166 the same way. It found three workers present at the hearing and 69 more from identity cards to have contracts aggregating more than two years, and ordered conversion for all 72. [Para 183][Para 193][Para 194][Para 195][Para 197][Para 198][Para 203]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus conditioned on attending every working day of the month without absence is not earned, even in part, by a worker who is absent with the employer's permission for a personal reason unconnected with contractual duty or legal entitlement: permission to be absent is not performance of the condition. Separately, where successive fixed-duration contracts aggregate more than two years, the employer must convert them to indefinite-duration contracts, and the panel will decline benefit claims above the statutory floor unless the complainant union is most representative in the enterprise. [Para 98][Para 128][Para 193][Para 198]

**Obiter dicta:** The parties' freedom to choose contract type means the panel will neither compel six-month terms nor forbid three-month terms [Para 172][Para 173]; the annexed dissents of Kol Vathanak, who would have refused conversion because the 5% end-of-contract payment was made each year, and of An Non, who would have ordered pro-rating, are minority opinions forming no part of the award [Para 209][Para 211][Para 220].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The paragraph constituting the panel dates it "២០២៣ ២០១៥" in the same sentence [Para 25], and the extension paragraph repeats "ឆ្នាំ" [Para 33]. Prakas No. 305 is dated "22 November 20019" [Para 123]. The paragraph identifying the headcount by identity cards writes "កម្មករនិយោជិត ចំនួន ០៣ ខែ នាក់" — a stray month unit inside the number [Para 196] — and the overtime hours in the demand appear as "ម៉ោង ១៨០ 00" [Para 8]. Two provisions of Prakas No. 099 are cited inconsistently, once as Article 43 and once as Article 43(1) [Para 108][Para 126]. The award gives two different commercial registration particulars for the company, a certificate numbered 7233 dated 29 November 2013 and an "Inv. 1239 E/2007" dated 07 August 2007, without explaining the relationship [Para 35][Para 47].
