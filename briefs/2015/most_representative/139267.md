# Award No. 200/15 — Cambodia Air Port Management Services (Siem Reap International Airport) (Arbitration Council, 2 September 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambor Na for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Kong Phalla chair) · Doc ID 139267

## 1. Facts

Cambodia Air Port Management Services (CAMS) provides ground and airport services at Siem Reap International Airport and employs about 539 workers, 107 of them women. [Para 1][Para 107][Para 108] The Cambodia Tourism Industry Workers' Union at the airport is recognised by the employer as the most representative union and holds a registered collective agreement (No. 459, 2 April 2014) valid for three years from 1 January 2014. [Para 110][Para 124][Para 125] Eleven demands went to conciliation on 20 July 2015 and none was settled; at the hearing the union withdrew demand 4, agreed demand 10 on bilingual documents, and merged demands 7 and 11, leaving eight issues for decision. [Para 31][Para 32][Para 36][Para 37][Para 38]

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. (Issue 01) Whether the employer must establish a nursery and a canteen at the workplace? [Para 111][Para 226]
2. (Issue 02) Whether the employer must stop the harassment of Mr S, the union's vice-president, and take him back? [Para 126]
3. (Issue 03) Whether the employer's non-renewal of Mr N's fixed-duration contract was lawful, and whether he must be reinstated with wages? [Para 262][Para 263]
4. (Issue 05) Whether the employer must allow four union leaders to bring mobile phones into the airside area? [Para 293]
5. (Issue 06) Whether the employer must take back Mr P, a union activist, and pay him wages from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 312]
6. (Issues 07 & 11) Whether the employer must replace its general manager at the airport and stop discriminating against union leaders and activists? [Para 187][Para 352]
7. (Issue 08) Whether the employer must abolish positions it created without union agreement? [Para 380]
8. (Issue 09) Whether the employer must obtain union agreement on work instructions before applying them? [Para 396]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** Article 186 obliges an employer to provide a nursery; women have had to leave infants at an outside security office containing X-ray equipment, and food sellers are far away within a 45-minute break; Mr N was given no clear reason for non-renewal and signed an English letter he was not told the content of; Mr P's absences were covered by colleagues. [Para 117][Para 119][Para 139][Para 167][Para 186]

**Employer (respondent):** the Florida school arrangement for children aged 2½ to 6 is more favourable than the law; canteen space with tables exists and USD 85 monthly food allowance is paid; airside is a security zone where unauthorised phones are banned; management appointments, position structures and work instructions are its lawful prerogative. [Para 121][Para 123][Para 195][Para 203]

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered a nursery for women workers' children, with employer-funded outside care on actual invoices for children over 18 months if none can be provided in-house; it dismissed the canteen, phone, harassment, general-manager, new-position and work-instruction claims, declined to consider Mr S's dismissal, reinstated Mr N with back pay under an unspecified-duration contract, and refused to reinstate Mr P while awarding him four termination payments. [Para 407][Para 409][Para 411][Para 414]

Article 186 requires any enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a breastfeeding room and a nursery; 107 women are employed and no nursery exists. [Para 213][Para 219][Para 220] The Florida school contract covers only children aged 2½ to 6, so it does not discharge a duty that runs from 18 months. [Para 221][Para 222][Para 224]

The canteen claim failed: Articles 42 and 43 define and license canteens and provide for labour-inspector supervision but impose no duty to create one, and no law, collective agreement, internal rule or practice requires it. [Para 226][Para 230][Para 231] The panel classed it an interest dispute and held that while the 2014 collective agreement runs, the union cannot bring further interest claims unless the employer agrees to amend it. [Para 232][Para 244][Para 246]

On Mr S, Article 293 forbids dismissing a union delegate without the Labour Inspector's authorisation; the Siem Reap provincial department authorised it by letter 061 of 29 June 2015 and the union appealed to the Minister on 8 July 2015, so the matter falls within the Minister's competence. [Para 247][Para 258][Para 259][Para 260]

Article 73(5) requires ten days' notice for a fixed-duration contract over six months; Mr N was notified on 22 May 2015 for a 28 May expiry — six days. [Para 263][Para 273][Para 274] The contract was renewed for an equal term and, his three successive one-year contracts exceeding two years in total, became unspecified-duration under Article 67(2); reinstatement with back pay followed. [Para 277][Para 278][Para 292]

The phone claim failed on safety: Civil Aviation Secretariat letter 031/14 of 12 November 2014 prohibits unauthorised phones in the airside zone, and the employer's rule barring only non-managers was consistent with Article 2 and clause 5 of the collective agreement. [Para 300][Para 307][Para 309]

Mr P's dismissal was lawful but not for serious misconduct: Article 83(b)(3), internal rule 4(11), clause 11.9 and the Weight and Balance rule 5.3 do not make swapping flights serious misconduct, yet repeated unexcused absences were a reasonable ground under Article 74, proportionate under Article 27. [Para 313][Para 318][Para 327] He recovered no moral damages but received the Article 89 dismissal indemnity, notice pay, unused annual leave and final wages. [Para 337][Para 351]

The general-manager claim failed for want of evidence: the parties agreed he used no violence or insults, and only the parties to a contract may terminate it. [Para 366][Para 376][Para 379] The new-position claim failed because both sides agreed no new posts were created, only renamed ones carrying better benefits. [Para 388][Para 394][Para 395] Requiring union consent to work instructions would intrude on management's Article 2 prerogative. [Para 404][Para 405][Para 406]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

Two rules carry the award. First, the Article 186 duty on an enterprise employing at least 100 women is discharged only by a nursery and breastfeeding room at the enterprise: a subsidised arrangement limited to children aged 2½ and over leaves the statutory duty unperformed for younger children, and the employer must then fund outside care on actual invoices. Second, a fixed-duration contract ended on short notice is renewed for an equal term, and once successive renewals exceed two years in total it becomes unspecified-duration under Article 67(2), so reinstatement with back pay follows. A related rule: conduct that is a "reasonable ground" for dismissal under Article 74 need not be serious misconduct under Article 83, and the statutory termination indemnities survive. [Para 223][Para 263][Para 277][Para 278][Para 327]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's suggestion that the four union leaders may apply to the Civil Aviation Secretariat for authorisation to carry phones airside, and its remark that airside restrictions serve safety, are advisory and did not affect the dismissal of that claim. [Para 307][Para 310]
