# Award No. 230/15 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ិច ផ្លាស់ អ៊ិន ដាស ស្ទ្រី ខូអិលធីឌី (Arbitration Council, 23 September 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៊ុក រី, appointed by the employer party; អន ណន, appointed by the worker party; ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា, chair) · Doc ID 139425

## 1. Facts
The employer is a garment manufacturer at Prek Anchanh, Mukh Kampul, Kandal, employing about 2,700 workers. [Para 39][Para 40] Six collective demands went to conciliation, which failed on 18 August 2015, and the Council heard the case on 3 September 2015. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 17] The demands followed the employer's suspension of a union leader (name redacted) on 1 July 2015 and a stay-away from 8 to 23 July 2015, during which the employer stopped between about 200 and 245 workers; only five of them, all local union leaders, pressed claims before the panel. [Para 49][Para 53][Para 54][Para 66][Para 127]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back all the workers it stopped during the strike? [Para 2][Para 128]
2. Whether the employer must take back the five workers who were union representatives? [Para 3][Para 133]
3. Whether the employer must pay USD 5 a month for travel and accommodation on top of the USD 7 already paid? [Para 4][Para 168]
4. Whether the employer must pay 3,000 riel a day for lunch, that is 1,500 riel on top of the 1,500 riel already paid? [Para 5][Para 184]
5. Whether the employer must pay a USD 5 monthly attendance bonus whenever a worker works 18 hours of overtime? [Para 6][Para 190]
6. Whether the employer must maintain wages and other benefits during the strike? [Para 7][Para 209]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer stopped 245 workers to punish a lawful strike called to win back a suspended leader; the five leaders had no fault and no warning; the USD 7 allowance and 1,500 riel lunch money fall far short of real costs of USD 12 to 15; the employer raised the overtime threshold from 18 to 36 hours so that the attendance bonus could never be earned. [Para 49][Para 59][Para 73][Para 81]
**Employer (respondent):** the five were absent without leave from 2 to 7 July, which Article 6(b) of the internal rules treats as abandonment, and were dismissed on 8 July; the employer never knew they were union leaders; it cannot pay above the law; the allowances and the bonus are voluntary and fall within its managerial discretion. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63][Para 65][Para 75][Para 84]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 1 dismissed for want of a claimant; on issue 2 the employer was ordered to take back the five workers named in the mandate of 18 August 2015; issues 3, 4, 5 and 6 dismissed. [Para 132][Para 167][Para 183][Para 189][Para 208][Para 234][Para 235][Para 236]

On standing the panel applied Article 268 of the Labour Law, Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY of 21 April 2004 and Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001: a union may act only for workers who have authorised it in writing. A mandate of 23 July 2015 carried 44 thumbprints and employee numbers but no names, and the workers never supplied the identity documents the panel ordered by 8 September 2015; a second mandate of 18 August 2015 named five workers and was valid. Only those five were proper claimants, which disposed of issue 1, the claim for every stopped worker, without reaching its merits. [Para 105][Para 121][Para 123][Para 124][Para 127][Para 129][Para 130]

On issue 2 the panel held the six-day absence was not abandonment but a strike: all three elements of Article 318 were present, namely a group of workers, a stoppage, and a demand for a settlement — here the reinstatement of the suspended leader. [Para 136][Para 137][Para 141][Para 142] Because Article 38 of the Constitution and Articles 319 and 332 guarantee the right to strike, an employer may not dismiss workers for taking part, and reinstatement was ordered. [Para 143][Para 145][Para 146][Para 152][Para 153] The panel also found the employer had not proved its assertion that it had sued the five in the Kandal court. [Para 159][Para 161]

On issues 3 and 4 the panel found the employer already paid the USD 7 allowance required by Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012, and the 1,500 riel lunch money it had chosen to pay; no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice obliged it to pay more. Both demands were interest disputes that only a most-representative union may bring, and five workers lacked that standing, so both were dismissed. [Para 168][Para 170][Para 172][Para 174][Para 182][Para 185][Para 188]

On issue 5 the panel held, under Article 2 of the Labour Law, that the attendance bonus is a voluntary incentive outside statutory wages, and that setting its threshold and its deductions falls within the employer's right to manage the enterprise; the scheme was neither unreasonable nor contrary to law, so the claim failed. [Para 193][Para 196][Para 205][Para 206][Para 207]

On issue 6 the panel applied Article 332(1) and Article 72(1): the contract is suspended during a strike, no work is given and no wage is owed, whatever the legality of the strike. The regular attendance bonus failed for a further reason — the workers conceded they struck without the notice Article 324 requires, so they were not in regular attendance. [Para 211][Para 212][Para 213][Para 215][Para 229][Para 233]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Absence in a group to press a demand — here reinstatement of a suspended union leader — meets Article 318 and is a strike, not abandonment of employment; a dismissal founded on that absence is therefore unlawful and reinstatement follows, even where the strike itself was irregular. Conversely, a union holding only thumbprints and employee numbers, which does not supply the names the panel orders, represents nobody: the panel will hear only the claims of workers named in a valid written mandate. [Para 124][Para 141][Para 152]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement, binds all workers and extinguishes their future claims on the same subject, and its finding that the employer failed to prove its court complaint, were not needed to decide any of the six demands. [Para 161][Para 176]

- The award is dated 23 September 2015 in the header [Para 1], but the extension in the reasons is printed with two years for one date: "ពី ថ្ងៃទី ១៥ ខែកញ្ញា ឆ្នាំ ២០១៩ ឆ្នាំ ២០១៥ ទៅ ថ្ងៃទី ២៣ ខែកញ្ជូ ឆ្នាំ ២០១៥". [Para 22]
- The six-day absence ends 07 July 2017 at [Para 46] but 07 July 2015 at [Para 61] and [Para 134].
- The Kandal court's protection order is cited as No. 179 "ត" at [Para 55] and No. 179 "គ" at [Para 53], [Para 57] and [Para 69].
- The 18 August 2015 mandate is dated 18 September 2015 at [Para 118] and [Para 126].
- Worker counts and amounts are garbled: "០៥០ នាក់" for five workers [Para 133], "០៥៦ នាក់" and "០៥" elsewhere [Para 182][Para 188], "០៥៦ ដុល្លារ" for USD 5 [Para 190], "១៥០០០ រៀល" for 1,500 riel [Para 184], and "២០១៧ ១៥០០ រៀល" in the dispositive list [Para 238].
- The demand list names seven union representatives [Para 3], but the claim and the award concern five [Para 133][Para 236]; the strike is dated 8–23 July 2015 [Para 54] while the reasons also give 23 July as its start [Para 140].
- Individual workers and union officials appear by name in the source; they are redacted here.
