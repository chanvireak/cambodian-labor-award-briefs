# Award No. 191/15 — Zena Dai Dong (Cambodia) Electronics (Arbitration Council, 18 August 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Kong Phalha chair) · Doc ID 139258

## 1. Facts
The company manufactures cases and employs about 551 workers, more than 100 of them women; it sits at Ang Snuol district, Kandal. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 61] Seventeen demands were conciliated on 10 July 2015 without agreement and referred on 21 July 2015; at the hearing of 31 July 2015 the parties settled thirteen of them and the panel was left with four. [Para 24][Para 29][Para 30] The claimants were six workers who had mandated the Khmer Youth Voice Union Federation, three of whom attended; the factory union was still unregistered. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51] By agreement of 31 July 2015 the parties elected a binding award, so the outcome binds them alone. [Para 31][Para 141]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay USD 15 a month for travel and accommodation? [Para 66]
2. Whether the employer must conclude one-year fixed-term contracts with the workers? [Para 86]
3. Whether the employer must establish a day-care centre for the children of its women workers? [Para 112]
4. Whether the employer must hold an election of worker representatives? [Para 123]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer pays only USD 7 while bus fares alone cost USD 12 to 13 a month and food prices have risen; three- and six-month contracts leave no security and let the employer terminate at will; the day-care centre is a legal duty; and the old worker representatives' term has expired with no election held. [Para 54][Para 57][Para 60][Para 64]
**Employer (respondent):** it cannot afford more than the USD 7 it already pays; short contracts let it reduce staff when orders fall; it cannot build a day-care centre but has paid USD 10 a month to mothers since June 2015; and it will hold the election, but needs about three months because administration lacks staff. [Para 55][Para 58][Para 62][Para 65]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the travel-allowance and one-year-contract demands were dismissed; the employer was ordered to establish a day-care centre, or to reimburse the actual cost of an outside centre, and to hold the worker-representative election; no money award was made. [Para 136][Para 137][Para 138][Para 139][Para 140]

On issue 1 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, contract or past practice requiring USD 15, so the demand was a claim for more than the law gives and therefore an interest dispute. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 69] Applying Article 96 and Article 9(1) of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, and Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099, it held that only the most representative union, or unions together representing more than half the workforce, may bring an interest dispute, because such an award replaces a collective agreement for a year and removes the right to strike over the same demand. [Para 73][Para 76][Para 77][Para 78] The six claimants were not such a union, so it declined to consider the claim at all. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 85]

On issue 2 it applied Article 65 of the Labour Law and Articles 664, 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code: a contract arises only from matching offer and acceptance, and neither party may impose a term the other does not accept, so the workers could not compel a one-year term and the employer could not compel a short one. [Para 86][Para 88][Para 97][Para 101] The demand was dismissed. [Para 109][Para 111]

On issue 3 Article 186 requires any enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a nursing room and a day-care centre; the company has over 100 women workers and no centre, so the panel ordered one, and where that is impossible, ordered the employer to pay the invoice for an outside centre for children from 18 months. [Para 61][Para 112][Para 116][Para 119][Para 121][Para 122] On issue 4 Article 292 makes the election the employer's duty, with public notice within 15 days of a request and the election within 45 days under Prakas No. 286 of 5 November 2001; no election had been held for the new term, so it was ordered after the award. [Para 123][Para 124][Para 134][Para 135][Para 140]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for more than the Labour Law, a collective agreement, the internal rules or established practice provides is an interest dispute, and an interest dispute may be brought to the panel only by the union that is the most representative in the enterprise, or by unions together representing more than half the workers; a minority group of claimants therefore cannot obtain an award on it. Separately, an employer of at least 100 women must provide a day-care centre, and if it cannot, must bear the actual cost of an outside one. [Para 78][Para 84][Para 112][Para 116]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's wider discussion of freedom of contract — that neither side may impose a contract term on the other — was general reasoning supporting the dismissal on issue 2 rather than a finding on the facts, and its remark that an interest award deprives workers of the right to strike over the same demand was explanatory. [Para 77][Para 97][Para 101]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The panel records that thirteen of the seventeen issues were settled at the hearing but lists only twelve numbers (01, 02, 03, 04, 05, 06, 08, 12, 13, 14, 15, 17) [Para 29], which cannot be reconciled with the four issues said to remain [Para 30].
- The worker-party arbitrator is named អន ណន on the face but អន លាន in the signature block [Para 1][Para 142].
- The company name varies between ហ្ស៊ិ ណា-ដាយ ដុង, ហ្សូណា-ដាយ ដូង, ហ្សិណា ដាយ ដុង and ហ្ស៊ិណា ដាយ ដុង, and no Latin-script form appears in the text, so the heading uses the catalogue title, Zena Dai Dong (Cambodia) Electronics [Para 1][Para 21][Para 42][Para 47].
- The company's registration is given three ways: certificate of 5 December 2014, statutes deposited as No. 3784/2012 of 26 November 2012, and commercial registration Inv. 3035 E/2012 of 26 November 2012 [Para 37][Para 38][Para 47].
- The award date was extended from "11 August, year year 2015" to 18 August 2015 [Para 33], and stray years 2017 and 2016 appear inside the operative reasoning and order [Para 77][Para 136].
- The order on issue 4 sets no deadline, although the workers asked for one month and the employer proposed three, so the award does not decide the point argued [Para 64][Para 65][Para 140].
- The employer's USD 10 monthly payment to mothers, said to run from June 2015, is recorded but not addressed in the order on issue 3 [Para 62][Para 138].
