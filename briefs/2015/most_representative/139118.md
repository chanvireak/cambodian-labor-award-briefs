# Award No. 002/15 — Perfect Growth Private Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 16 February 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun, employer-appointed; Tuon Siphann, worker-appointed; Pen Bunchhea, chair) · non-binding award · unofficial English translation of the Khmer original · Doc ID 139118

## 1. Facts
Perfect Growth Private Co., Ltd manufactures textiles at Svay Chrum village, Bek Chan commune, Ang Snuol district, Kandal, under registration No. Inv 1070 E/2013 of 31 May 2013, and employed approximately 2,000 workers. [Para 35] The Local Union of VKYUF, registered by certificate No. 3226 of 8 January 2015 with 400 members, brought 17 demands; eight were withdrawn at the hearing, issues 2 and 8 and issues 9 and 10 were merged, leaving 2&8, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9&10 and 15. [Para 31][Para 35][Para 39][Para 40] The central claim was for reinstatement of Mr P, a sewing worker and local union committee member whose six-month fixed-duration contract ran from 12 May to 12 November 2014 and was not renewed on 11 November 2014. [Para 45][Para 109]

## 2. Issues in dispute
2 & 8. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr P and pay back pay and other benefits from termination to reinstatement, the non-renewal being a dismissal without clear reason, without notice, and on grounds of union discrimination? [Para 40][Para 42][Para 43]
3. Whether the employer must stop withholding tax on workers' salaries, or bear that tax itself? [Para 71][Para 134]
4. Whether the employer must raise the monthly attendance bonus from USD 10 to USD 15? [Para 73]
5. Whether the employer must raise the monthly accommodation allowance from USD 10 to USD 15? [Para 74][Para 75]
6. Whether the employer must pay a monthly skills bonus of USD 20, USD 40 or USD 60 according to single, double or triple sewing machine use? [Para 76]
9 & 10. Whether the employer must pay assistants a USD 20 monthly position bonus and incentives equal to those of sewing workers? [Para 84][Para 169]
15. Whether the employer must raise the monthly payment in lieu of milk formula from USD 5 to USD 20 for workers with children aged 18 months to 3 years? [Para 89]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr P held a two-month probation from 12 March 2014 and then a six-month contract from 12 May to 12 November 2014, earned about USD 180–200 and reached his targets; on 11 November 2014 the administration told him he had missed his production target and ended his contract — a dismissal without clear reason and without the statutory notice, and union discrimination because he sat on the local union committee; his absences on 20 and 22 October 2014 were sick leave requested through his team leader by handing in his ID card and by telephone; and letter No. 226/14 notified the employer of the union's elected officers. [Para 42][Para 45][Para 48][Para 49][Para 56]
**Employer (respondent):** It did not terminate him — his contract expired on 12 November 2014 and it simply did not renew it, because he had been absent without informing his head of section or team leader on several occasions, including 20 and 22 October 2014, and had twice been warned in writing; it paid him 5% severance, pay in lieu of annual leave and his last month's salary, which he refused; salary tax is withheld because the tax law requires it; it already pays what Notification No. 230 requires, plus 11 extra cars; and its team bonus, treated as a skills bonus, rewards collective output, assistants ranking below sewers as their work is lighter. [Para 45][Para 47][Para 73][Para 76][Para 82][Para 87][Para 88]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed on the merits — the panel rejected the reinstatement and equal-incentive demands and declined to consider issues 3, 4, 5, 6, 9 (position bonus) and 15 as interest disputes; the award is non-binding and becomes binding eight days after notification. [Para 186][Para 187][Para 188]

On issue 2&8 the panel asked first whether the event was a dismissal or a non-renewal. Under Article 73(1) of the Labour Law a fixed-duration contract ends at its stated date, and following Awards No. 100/07 (Hoyear) and No. 64/09 (Sinomax II) renewal depends on the parties' agreement, so neither can force an extension. Reading the contract's terms and the parties' intention, it held the term was six months and not "six months and one day", so the contract ended on 11 November 2014 and what occurred was a non-renewal, not a termination; Article 73(5) requires ten days' notice only for contracts exceeding six months, so none was owed. [Para 100][Para 106][Para 114][Para 116]

On union discrimination it applied Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law, which forbid any account to be taken of union membership or activity in hiring or dismissal decisions, and the rule that the party alleging discrimination bears the burden of proof. The notification letter naming Mr P as a committee member was dated 12 November 2014 and the employer never received or signed it, while the non-renewal occurred on 11 November 2014, so the employer could not have known of his committee role when it decided; two warning letters and an attendance list proved the absences, and the workers produced nothing to show permission was sought. The non-renewal was therefore not discriminatory. [Para 117][Para 119][Para 127][Para 128][Para 129][Para 132][Para 133]

Issues 3, 4, 5, 6, 9 and 15 failed one test: no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rule, contract or past practice obliged the employer to bear salary tax, to exceed the USD 10 attendance bonus or the Notification No. 230 allowance, to create a skills or position bonus it had never paid, or to raise the milk-formula payment above USD 5. Each was "beyond the law" and so an interest dispute. [Para 135][Para 146][Para 152][Para 158][Para 164][Para 181]

On those interest disputes the panel applied Article 96(2) of the Labour Law, Clause 9 of Prakas No. 305 and Clause 43 of Prakas No. 099: such an award replaces the collective agreement for a year and removes the right to strike, so it may only be made where the referring union holds most-representative status or the collective unions represent more than half the workforce. VKYUF held no such status and was not competent to refer them. [Para 137][Para 139][Para 140][Para 145][Para 150]

On the second limb of issue 9&10 alone the panel reached the merits: Article 2 gives the employer the right to manage lawfully and reasonably, and it may by unilateral decision create bonuses not required by law to encourage productivity. Paying sewers more than assistants, who facilitate their work and rank second, was lawful differentiation by skill, type and section. [Para 170][Para 171][Para 175][Para 176][Para 177][Para 178]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-duration contract of exactly six months expires at its stated date, so non-renewal is not a dismissal, the ten-day notice of Article 73(5) does not apply, and no severance or reinstatement follows; and an employer's decision not to renew is unlawful only where union membership or activity was actually known to it and formed the basis of the decision, the burden of proving which lies on the worker. [Para 106][Para 116][Para 119][Para 128]

**Obiter dicta:** That an employer may unilaterally create bonuses not required by law, and that differentiating incentives between sewers and assistants by skill, type and section is reasonable, were reasoned although the demand had already failed for want of most-representative status. [Para 176][Para 177]
