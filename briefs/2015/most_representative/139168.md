# Award No. 076/15 — Green Thien International Nal (Cambodia) PLC (Arbitration Council, 21 April 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ingh Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Kong Pollakha (president) · Doc ID 139168

## 1. Facts
404 of the roughly 4,608 workers at the Green Thien garment factory, formerly QMI, transferred their rights to the Federation of Unions Supporting Cambodian Workers (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព គាំទ្រ កម្មករ កម្ពុជា) and demanded that the company keep in force the indefinite-duration contracts they held from 1997 to the end of 2006, together with their seniority from that period, following the change of company name from QMI to Green Thien [Para 2][Para 51][Para 53][Para 54]. Conciliation on 12 March 2015 failed on the single point, and report No. 282 Kb/Ak/Vk of 18 March 2015 reached the Council on 19 March 2015 [Para 7][Para 11][Para 12]. A hearing was held on 1 April 2015 at 2:00 pm with both parties present [Para 9][Para 15]; by agreement the award date moved from 10 April to 21 April 2015 [Para 20], and it issued as a non-binding order [Para 79].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must maintain the indefinite-duration contracts held by 404 workers from 1997 to the end of 2006, and their seniority over that period, notwithstanding the conversion to fixed-duration contracts in 2007? [Para 55]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The 1997–2006 indefinite-duration contracts should stay in force because the workers fear that in future the employer will dismiss them without counting their seniority correctly [Para 57][Para 69].
**Employer (respondent):** In 2007 all workers from the 1997–2006 period received 5% severance and agreed to convert their indefinite-duration contracts into one-year fixed-duration contracts, taking 5% severance each year since; the conversion was agreed with the most representative union, the Green Thien garment workers' development union; and any future dismissal will be compensated as the Labour Law requires [Para 59][Para 62][Para 63][Para 64][Para 72].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider the demand that the company maintain indefinite-duration contracts and seniority from 1997 to the end of 2006 for the 404 workers [Para 77][Para 78].
The panel first recorded what was common ground: both parties agreed that from 1997 to the end of 2006 the employer did in fact apply indefinite-duration contracts, and that since 2007 one-year fixed-duration contracts have applied without dispute between them [Para 55][Para 58][Para 65][Para 67]. On that agreed basis the historical contract type was not in issue, and the panel said so expressly [Para 66][Para 68].
What remained was the workers' forward-looking anxiety about a dismissal that had not happened and about how seniority would be computed if it did [Para 69][Para 71]. The panel held that the demand turned on a state of affairs that might arise in the future and did not yet exist, since no worker among the 404 had been dismissed [Para 70][Para 71][Para 73]. It applied its established position that the Council is created to resolve labour disputes, not to resolve problems that have not yet arisen, following Cases No. 10/03, 14/06, 141/08, 70/09, 123/09 and 42/10 [Para 74][Para 75][Para 76]. It noted the employer's undertaking to pay statutory compensation on any future dismissal, but did not rest its decision on that [Para 72]. Accordingly it declined to adjudicate the demand [Para 77][Para 78].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The Council will not adjudicate a demand that depends on an event which has not occurred: a claim founded on a feared future dismissal, and on how seniority would then be calculated, is not yet a labour dispute, and is refused, leaving the workers to bring it if and when a dismissal occurs [Para 71][Para 73][Para 74].

**Obiter dicta:** The employer's statement that it will pay compensation according to the Labour Law if any worker is dismissed in future [Para 64][Para 72].
