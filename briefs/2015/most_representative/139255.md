# Award No. 180/15 — Five Stars Cotton Garment (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 17 August 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Tuon Si Phan for the worker party, Kuy Neam chair) · Doc ID 139255

## 1. Facts
The company, a garment factory, employed 575 workers; the Free Workers' Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia held mandates from 69 of them but had no registration certificate from the Ministry at the date of the award. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43] Conciliation of 11 demands on 8 July 2015 settled six and left five — piece rates, the attendance bonus, the shop-steward election, a quota bonus and strike benefits — which were referred on 13 July 2015. [Para 17][Para 18][Para 23] The workers struck from 11:30 on 30 June to 8 July 2015, and the company suspended part of its operations from 22 July to 22 August 2015, retaining 140 of 575 workers with the Labour Department's permission. [Para 65][Para 103] Payroll records showed most piece-rate sewers below the minimum wage: 42 of 62 in May 2015 and 33 of 63 in June 2015. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 123]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise piece rates in every section and every group? [Para 106]
2. Whether the employer must pay the full USD 11 attendance bonus notwithstanding days of leave it authorised, or may deduct it in proportion to those days? [Para 130]
3. Whether the employer must re-run the election of worker representatives according to law? [Para 170]
4. Whether the employer must pay a 1,000 riel daily bonus to each worker in a group that meets its quota for a single day? [Para 184]
5. Whether the employer must maintain wages and other benefits during the strike? [Para 203]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the rates were set so low that about 80 per cent of sewers could not reach the minimum wage, some piece rates had been cut when a garment style was dropped, the June election was held without notice, and the strike was caused by the company's own unlawful election. [Para 53][Para 58][Para 79][Para 104]
**Employer (respondent):** it applies the law and tops up to USD 128 for any worker who falls short, the attendance and quota bonuses are conditional incentives within its managerial discretion, and a strike without prior notice suspends the contract, so no wage or benefit is owed for strike days. [Para 71][Para 77][Para 105][Para 187]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand for higher piece rates was rejected but the employer was ordered to set rates so that an average-skill worker in ordinary hours earns at least the guaranteed minimum wage; the attendance bonus may be deducted only pro rata for authorised leave days (USD 11 a month); the shop-steward election must be re-run; the quota-bonus and strike-benefit demands were dismissed; the award is non-binding. [Para 213][Para 214][Para 215][Para 216][Para 217][Para 218][Para 219]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 108 of the Labour Law, which requires piece rates to yield at least the guaranteed minimum wage to a worker of average skill, and Notification No. 283 of 12 November 2014 setting USD 128 a month from 1 January 2015, following awards 03/05, 41/05 and 44/06. [Para 110][Para 115][Para 116] It found more than half of the piece-rate sewers below USD 128 in every month from January 2015 and no explanation from the employer of how its rates were calculated, so the demand for a general increase was refused while the rate-setting itself was corrected. [Para 123][Para 124][Para 128][Para 129]

On issue 2 it held that the USD 10 attendance bonus in Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 is a statutory entitlement, not a discretionary gift, so absence on annual, special or public-holiday leave cannot reduce it; personal leave authorised by the employer, however, suspends the contract under Articles 71(6) and 72(1), so neither wage nor bonus accrues for those days and a pro-rata deduction is lawful. [Para 137][Para 143][Para 166][Para 168]

On issue 3 Article 292 makes holding the election the employer's duty, and Prakas No. 286 of 5 November 2001 requires public notice and an election within the statutory periods after consulting the most representative union; the 29 June 2015 election was held without prior public notice, so a fresh election was ordered. [Para 170][Para 174][Para 182][Para 183]

On issue 4 no law requires a 1,000 riel daily quota bonus, and under Article 2(2) an employer may attach conditions to bonuses of its own creation provided they are reasonable and lawful; the three-day quota condition was upheld and the demand dismissed. [Para 188][Para 196][Para 200][Para 202] On issue 5 Articles 332 and 72(1) suspend the contract during a strike, and following award 27/10 the panel held that neither wage nor bonus is owed for strike days, dismissing the demand. [Para 203][Para 206][Para 211][Para 212] Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented on issue 2, reasoning that a "bonus" (ប្រាក់រង្វាន់) is conditional and not wage, whereas a sum paid as gratification under Article 103 is wage and cannot be apportioned, so the pro-rata order was wrong. [Para 222][Para 227][Para 230]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may fix piece rates unilaterally, but the rates must be such that a worker of average skill working ordinary hours earns at least the statutory minimum wage; where most piece-rate workers fall below it and the employer cannot explain how the rates were set, the panel will refuse a general increase yet order the rates reset. Separately, a statutory attendance bonus cannot be withheld for lawful absence, and leave authorised by the employer suspends the contract so that only a pro-rata deduction is permitted. [Para 126][Para 129][Para 153][Para 168]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that employers may create incentive bonuses beyond those the law requires, and that bonuses come in many conditional forms, were general remarks not needed for the outcome; the dissent's discussion of the definition of bonus is opinion, not decision. [Para 193][Para 197][Para 229]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The reasons on issue 2 order a deduction of "USD 116" (១១៦) while the operative order and the heading say USD 11 (១១); the two figures cannot both be right [Para 169][Para 215][Para 130].
- The union's identity is inconsistent: lines 27–28 refer to a "Cambodian Workers' Union" factory branch registered as No. 3250, while the body finds the Free Workers' Union at the company unregistered [Para 27][Para 28][Para 42].
- The January 2015 payroll finding reads "below USD 1" because the digits of 128 were lost [Para 46]; the award date was extended from "015 2015" to 17 August 2015 [Para 26]; the panel is said to have been constituted in "2 2015" [Para 19]; and the 29 June 2015 election is dated "29 July 2019 2015" in the employer's account [Para 180].
- The company name appears as ហ្គាមេន, ហ្គាមិន and ហ្គាមិ, and the union's name as សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ and សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា; no Latin-script form appears in the award text, so the heading uses the catalogue title, which renders it Five Stars Cotton Garment [Para 1][Para 18][Para 40][Para 181].
- The award states both that the strike was called without notice and that the workers struck from 30 June to 8 July 2015, while conciliation occurred on 8 July 2015 [Para 103][Para 210][Para 37].
- OCR losses include garbled statutory quotations at Articles 108, 137 and 292 [Para 110][Para 111][Para 170], and duplicated sentences in the reasoning [Para 122][Para 209].
