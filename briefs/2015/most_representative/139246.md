# Award No. 173/15 — Sok Fin KC D (Arbitration Council, 30 July 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3 — Chhiv Phirom (employer-appointed), Vong Vanna (worker-appointed), Kong Phalha (president, chosen by the two) · Doc ID 139246

## 1. Facts
Sok Fin KC D runs a rubber plantation at Bu Sra, Pech Chreada district, Mondul Kiri, with about 336 workers split between permanent staff (about 136) and casual labour (about 200). [Para 64][Para 65][Para 70] The Cambodia Labour Union organised an election at the company but had not registered the resulting local union with the Ministry. [Para 66] Mr H, a tractor driver employed since 7 August 2009 on an indefinite-term contract, was dismissed on 6 February 2015 for using a company tractor to haul 12 pieces of wood he had collected on the plantation. [Para 80][Para 81][Para 82] Of 222 rights-transfer letters filed, the employer verified only 135 as its own workers, and the workers did not contest that check. [Para 141][Para 143]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr H and pay him back wages from dismissal to return? [Para 144]
2. Whether the employer must pay 30,000 riel a day to all casual workers? [Para 270]

The award records eleven claims; claims 2, 6, 10 and 11 were withdrawn and claims 3, 4, 5, 7 and 9 were settled by agreement at the hearing, leaving only claims 1 and 8 for decision. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 26]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissal was retaliation barred by Article 333, because Mr H had convened a 25 January 2015 meeting of workers to prepare demands and then led the 9 March 2015 strike. [Para 86][Para 101][Para 104] The wood was scrap the company no longer used, taken to burn for charcoal at his brother's still, not sold. [Para 97][Para 98]
**Employer (respondent):** Mr H used the company tractor and took 12 pieces of wood from the plantation to sell to outsiders, which the internal rules and Article 83 make grave misconduct; the company knew nothing of any union activity until after the dismissal. [Para 90][Para 91][Para 114]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement and back wages are refused and the dismissal is held lawful, but the employer must pay severance equal to 90 days of wage and allowances, two months' wage in lieu of notice, unused annual leave, and the February 2015 wage; the casual-wage claim is dismissed for want of jurisdiction. [Para 266][Para 267][Para 284][Para 290]

On jurisdiction the panel found a collective dispute: 135 workers had transferred rights over all eleven claims including claim 1, and a strike had followed, satisfying Article 302. [Para 157][Para 158][Para 163][Para 166]

On Article 333 the panel held the protection applies only where the employer punishes a worker for joining a strike. [Para 171][Para 173] Mr H was dismissed on 6 February 2015, a month before the 9 March 2015 strike, and the 25 January 2015 meeting took place at a worker's house on a Sunday, which was not a working day under his contract, so no stoppage of work occurred and there was no strike to retaliate against. [Para 181][Para 182][Para 185][Para 187] The claim of anti-union motive therefore failed.

On grave misconduct, the panel found that the internal rules classify criminal misconduct as dismissable only after a court has determined that the conduct is criminal, and the employer never filed a complaint. [Para 195][Para 196][Para 198][Para 200] It also found the employer proved neither sale nor value: the wood was scrap, the tractor was used while being returned after the shift, and no working time was lost, so the conduct did not reach Article 83. [Para 220][Para 221][Para 224][Para 225]

The panel nevertheless upheld the dismissal under Article 74: Mr H had been warned orally about the same tractor use about two months earlier and repeated it, which supplied a reason relating to his conduct. [Para 234][Para 235][Para 236] Because the dismissal was not for grave misconduct, severance and notice pay followed: 5 years 6 months of service counted as 6 years, giving 90 days, and service over five years gave two months' notice. [Para 239][Para 254] Moral damages were refused because the dismissal was justified. [Para 249][Para 250]

On claim 8, the panel found no law, collective agreement or practice fixing a daily rate for casual workers, classified the claim as an interest dispute, and declined it because the claimant union was not the most representative in the enterprise. [Para 270][Para 279][Para 282][Para 283]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that dismisses a worker for repeated unauthorised use of company equipment after an oral warning acts on a valid reason relating to conduct under Article 74, even where the conduct falls short of the grave misconduct listed in Article 83 — but because the dismissal is then not for grave misconduct, severance and payment in lieu of notice remain owed [Para 235][Para 236][Para 239]. Where internal rules make criminal misconduct dismissable only once a court has characterised the conduct as criminal, an employer that never files a complaint cannot rely on that ground [Para 195][Para 198][Para 200]. A claim to set a new daily wage for casual labour is an interest dispute that only the most representative union may bring [Para 280][Para 283].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that a preparatory meeting held on a non-working Sunday, with no cessation of work, does not amount to a strike under Article 318, so Article 333 cannot be engaged by it. [Para 181][Para 185]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Mr H's start date is printed as 7 August 2009 at [Para 80] and [Para 239] but as 7 August 2015 — after his own dismissal — at [Para 228]. The contract in evidence is dated 22 August 2015, six months after the dismissal it concerns, at [Para 31]. The strike date appears as "09 March 2023 2015" at [Para 162]. The final conciliation is dated 8 April 2015 at [Para 19], yet the conciliation report is dated 22 May 2015 at [Para 15] and [Para 20]. The claim value is rendered as "30 riel (thirty thousand riel exactly)" at [Para 9] and as 30,000 riel elsewhere. The operative order is repeated at [Para 266]–[Para 290] with overlapping and partly contradictory wording. Company name forms vary (Sok Fin, Sok Hin, Sokhfin) and the president's name appears as both Kong Phalha and Kong Phalla.
