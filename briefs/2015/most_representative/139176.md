# Award No. 083/15 — Alaska Massage Centre (Arbitration Council, 28 April 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; Hun Chundy, appointed by the workers; Run Saray, president) · Doc ID 139176

## 1. Facts
Alaska Massage Centre (មជ្ឈមណ្ឌល ម៉ាស្សា អាឡាស្កា), a massage service in Siem Reap employing 160 staff of whom 145 are women, faced two interest demands filed by the Cambodian Food and Service Workers' Federation (CFSWF) on behalf of 98 staff. [Para 9][Para 29][Para 32] Conciliation on 17 March 2015 failed on both points and they were referred to the panel on 24 March 2015. [Para 10][Para 11] The local union had been registered since 12 November 2013 with about 100 members, but none of its three registered leaders attended the hearing; the person who appeared claimed to be acting president without any legal document proving that authority. [Para 19][Para 30][Para 31][Para 60][Para 61]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a health allowance of USD 100 per year to all massage staff? [Para 2][Para 34][Para 68]
2. Whether the employer must raise the base wage to USD 177 per month for all massage staff? [Para 3][Para 45][Para 83]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Massage work runs 8 hours of physical strain on hands, fingers, shoulders and knees; it causes stomach illness from irregular meals, numbness of the hands and back pain, and the National Social Security Fund covers only work accidents, not health care; the base wage of USD 45–70 cannot cover basic monthly needs. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 37][Para 45][Para 47]
**Employer (respondent):** The centre has foreign (mainly Korean) clients for only 4 months a year and few for the other 8, and competing massage shops have cut its clientele; it has no capacity, and no law or prakasa fixes a minimum wage for massage; a clinic with a doctor 2 hours a day and on-call medicine is already in place, and tips lift monthly income to at least USD 100. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41][Para 42][Para 49][Para 50]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider both claims, on health allowance and on the USD 177 base wage, without reaching their merits. [Para 82][Para 93][Para 94][Para 95]

**Representation.** Applying Article 268 of the Labour Law and its own earlier awards, the panel held that a union only acquires the rights the law recognises once it and its list of officers are registered with the Ministry of Labour. [Para 54][Para 56][Para 57] The registered leaders of the local union did not appear, and the self-described acting president produced no legal instrument showing authority to represent members, so he could not act for them. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 62] However, rule 19 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY permits a party to be represented by any person holding its written authorisation, and the federation held such authorisation from 98 staff under the petition of 19 December 2014, so the federation — not the local union — was the lawful representative of those 98 complainants. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65][Para 66][Para 67]

**Interest-dispute character.** Both claims were found to be interest disputes, since no provision of the Labour Law, no collective agreement, no internal regulation and no past practice obliged the employer to pay a USD 100 annual health allowance or a USD 177 base wage. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 89][Para 90] The panel reasoned that under article 43 of Prakas No. 099 an award on an interest dispute replaces a collective agreement and binds all workers in the enterprise for one year, extinguishing their right to strike over that interest; only a union with most-representative status — membership above half the workforce — may therefore bring such a claim, or several unions together. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 78] Following its awards in Evergreen, Grit Union and Min Kun Thach, the panel found the 98 complainants were not the most representative body, and that the complainants accordingly lacked the legal standing to bring an interest dispute. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 81][Para 91][Para 92]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim that creates a new benefit rather than enforcing an existing legal, contractual or past-practice entitlement is an interest dispute, and the panel will decline to consider it unless the claimant union holds most-representative status in the enterprise — membership above half the workers — because an interest award binds the whole workforce and removes their right to strike on that subject for a year.

**Obiter dicta:** On the merits the panel observed that article 104 and article 107(2) of the Labour Law require a minimum wage to be fixed by ministerial prakasa after consulting the Labour Advisory Committee, and that no such prakasa yet exists for the massage sector. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 86]
