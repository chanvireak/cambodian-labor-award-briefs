# Award No. 077/15 — C.P. Cambodia Chamkat (Arbitration Council, 29 April 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tai Seng for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139171

## 1. Facts
C.P. Cambodia Chamkat, an agro-industrial company at Km 25 on National Road 4, Bek Chan commune, Ang Snuol district, Kandal, employing about 1,578 workers, faced six demands from a factory union registered on 14 December 2004 with roughly 220 members. [Para 1][Para 47][Para 48][Para 50][Para 51] Conciliation at the Kandal labour department on 13 March 2015 failed on all six, and the panel heard the case on 2 April 2015. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 19] The dispute is dominated by one characterization: the panel found the company's business is agro-industry, not garment, textile or footwear manufacture, which determines whether the Ministry's minimum-wage and benefit Prakas reach it at all. [Para 47][Para 105][Para 106]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must add USD 28 per month to the wages each worker already receives? [Para 52][Para 98]
2. Whether the employer must pay a seniority bonus to workers who do not receive one, as garment factories do? [Para 57][Para 126]
3. Whether the employer must pay an attendance bonus of 50 riel per month to monthly-paid workers and a transport and accommodation allowance of 80 riel per month to workers given neither? [Para 66][Para 138]
4. Whether the employer must grant unused annual leave accrued to the end of 2013, allow it to be taken all at once, or pay it out in cash? [Para 72][Para 150]
5. Whether the employer must pay three times the normal day's wage for work performed on paid public holidays? [Para 85][Para 205]
6. Whether the employer must revert from three work shifts to two? [Para 91][Para 222]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer failed to pass on the government's 2014 wage increase of USD 28; it withholds the seniority and attendance bonuses and transport allowance that other factories pay; workers were never told of their annual leave and some do not know whether sick days consumed it; work on a public holiday should carry triple wages as Sunday work does; and the third shift has cost them overtime earnings. [Para 54][Para 59][Para 62][Para 78][Para 86][Para 94]
**Employer (respondent):** the company is agro-industrial, outside the Prakas on minimum wage and benefits, and in any event pays no less than USD 130 per month, above the statutory USD 128; unused leave may be carried forward but not cashed out while employment continues; holiday work already attracts the normal holiday wage plus one day's wage; and three shifts of eight hours are required because production runs 24 hours a day. [Para 56][Para 69][Para 75][Para 89][Para 95][Para 166]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to carry unused annual leave forward for use in the following year; the other five claims were refused, four of them for want of standing and two on the merits. [Para 240][Para 241][Para 242][Para 243][Para 245][Para 246][Para 247]

On issue 1 the panel set out Prakas No. 283 of 12 November 2014, which fixes USD 128 per month for full-rights workers and USD 123 for probationers in the textile, garment and footwear sectors, and held that its scope covers only those sectors. [Para 98][Para 99][Para 103][Para 104][Para 105] Because the company is agro-industrial, the Prakas does not apply; no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice obliged the employer to add USD 28, so the demand exceeded the law and was an interest dispute, and the union's 220 members out of some 1,578 workers did not make it the most representative union entitled to bring such a dispute. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 108][Para 109][Para 110][Para 119][Para 122][Para 123][Para 124] Issues 2, 3 and 5 were disposed of by the same route: Prakas No. 041/11 on seniority bonuses and Notification No. 230 on attendance bonus and transport allowance are likewise confined to textile, garment and footwear enterprises, the demands exceeded the law, and the union lacked standing. [Para 126][Para 129][Para 131][Para 132][Para 141][Para 142][Para 143][Para 144][Para 147]

On issue 4 the panel split the demand in three. It granted the carry-forward: under Article 167(4) a worker may postpone unused annual leave into the following year, but not beyond three consecutive years, and the leave may not be confiscated for non-use. [Para 170][Para 171][Para 172][Para 179][Para 180] It refused the demand to take all accrued leave at once, because Article 170 makes the timing of leave dependent on agreement between employer and worker, a rule that protects both sides. [Para 181][Para 188][Para 193][Para 194] It refused the cash payout, because Article 167(2) allows compensation only where the contract is cut or ends, and Article 167(3) renders void any arrangement to pay in lieu of leave during employment. [Para 195][Para 197][Para 199][Para 203][Para 204]

On issue 5 the panel held that Article 164 and Article 4 of Prakas No. 10 of 4 February 1999 entitle a worker who works on a paid public holiday to one additional day's wage on top of the holiday pay already received — 200 per cent in total — and that no instrument provides 300 per cent, so the claim exceeded the law and was an interest dispute. [Para 206][Para 207][Para 209][Para 214][Para 215][Para 216] On issue 6 it held that the employer's power to fix hours and shifts under Article 2 and Article 138 permits three eight-hour shifts, which do not breach the eight-hour daily limit in Article 137, and that the loss of overtime earnings is not a reasonable ground to restrain that power. [Para 225][Para 227][Para 233][Para 237][Para 238]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A Prakas or Notification that fixes minimum wages or sector benefits for textile, garment and footwear enterprises does not reach an enterprise whose business is agro-industry, and a demand for a benefit beyond what any applicable text or practice provides is an interest dispute that only the most representative union in the enterprise may bring. [Para 105][Para 109][Para 141] Unused annual leave may be carried into the following year, up to three consecutive years, but cannot be taken in one block without agreement or converted into cash while the contract subsists. [Para 172][Para 179][Para 199] Work on a paid public holiday earns 200 per cent of a normal day's wage, not 300 per cent. [Para 209][Para 215]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that shift scheduling is the employer's prerogative so long as it is lawful and reasonable, and that the workers' loss of overtime pay is not a ground to interfere with it, go beyond what was needed once the union's standing had already failed on four issues. [Para 227][Para 237]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The panel constitution date reads "23 March 2019 2015", two years printed together. [Para 23]
- The company's sector is OCR'd as "ក សិឧស្សាហកម្ម" with the word broken across a space; it is read here as agro-industry (កសិឧស្សាហកម្ម), which is the pivot of the whole award. [Para 47][Para 106]
- The order on issue 3 prints the attendance bonus as "150 riel" where the claim and reasoning use 50 riel. [Para 242][Para 138]
- The order on issue 4(3) refers to leave "of 2019" where the claim concerns leave accrued to the end of 2013; the reasons at [Para 163] are also garbled as "the year 2019". [Para 245][Para 163]
- The USD 28 figure is never derived: the parties agree only that the employer pays USD 130 and that the statutory minimum is USD 128, and the award nowhere reconciles the claimed USD 28 with those figures. [Para 52][Para 99]
- The union is described as having "about 220" members in four places but the numeral is sometimes rendered "10" or "1" before it. [Para 122][Para 134][Para 146][Para 218]
- The signature block is corrupted: after the chair's title the text reads "Thai Mong Na 22017", conflicting with Pen Bun Chhea named as chair on the face of the award. [Para 249][Para 1]
- Cited prior awards survive only as fragments ("81204 Evergreen", "98 04 Grey Union", "6304 San Wel"), so they cannot be identified from this text. [Para 119][Para 158]
