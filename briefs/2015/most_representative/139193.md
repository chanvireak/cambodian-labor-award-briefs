# Award No. 099/15 — Sin Tai Gaman (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 4 June 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bunchhea presiding; Ly Tayseng, appointed by the employer, dissenting; An Non, appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 139193

## 1. Facts
Sin Tai Gaman (Cambodia) Co., Ltd ("Sin Tai"), at Damnak, Phnom Penh Thmei, Sen Sok, Phnom Penh, sews garments and employed about 2,200 workers. [Para 38][Para 39] The Khmer Workers' Spirit local union had not yet obtained its registration certificate from the Ministry of Labour, and 148 workers transferred their rights to the federation on 20 April 2015; they are the claimants. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 42] Conciliation on 20 April 2015 settled eight of fourteen demands and left six, and the panel decided those six. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 25] The award is non-binding and becomes final 8 days after notification. [Para 217]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must terminate the open-ended (unlimited-duration) employment contracts of all the workers, pay termination compensation for the period served, and convert those contracts into fixed-duration contracts? [Para 43][Para 44][Para 90]
2. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of 4,000 riel a day? [Para 49][Para 105]
3. Whether the employer must pay an overtime meal allowance of 2,000 riel an hour? [Para 56][Para 123]
4. Whether the employer must pay an attendance bonus of USD 15 a month and deduct it pro rata to the days a worker was absent with the employer's lawful permission? [Para 61][Para 131]
5. Whether the employer must pay a milk allowance of USD 30 a month to women with young children in place of a nursery? [Para 72][Para 195]
6. Whether the employer must allow pregnant workers one day a month for antenatal checks with wages and benefits maintained? [Para 81][Para 203]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** some had worked up to ten years and received nothing beyond their last wage and unused annual leave, so they wanted a 5 percent termination payment and a switch to fixed-duration contracts; other companies pay 4,000 riel a day for lunch and 2,000 riel an hour for overtime meals, and food and goods have risen in price; the USD 7 milk allowance is not enough because the employer provides no nursery; pregnant workers wait a long time at health centres. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 51][Para 53][Para 58][Para 76][Para 77][Para 83]

**Employer (respondent):** it has not terminated any contract and has no intention of doing so, and follows ILO advice to keep open-ended contracts because they benefit workers; it began paying 1,500 riel a day for lunch in 2015 and productivity has not improved; it already pays 2,000 riel for overtime between 4 and 6 p.m. and 10 USD attendance bonus, more generous than the law; it pays USD 7 milk money up to age three under an agreement with a union since 2010, and allows half a day a month for antenatal checks without cutting pay. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 54][Para 55][Para 60][Para 79][Para 80][Para 84][Para 88]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand to terminate and convert the open-ended contracts is refused; the lunch allowance, the 2,000 riel hourly overtime meal allowance, the USD 15 attendance bonus, the USD 30 milk allowance and the full day for antenatal checks are all refused consideration as interest disputes; but the employer is ordered to deduct the USD 10 attendance bonus pro rata to the days a worker was absent with the employer's lawful permission. [Para 192][Para 193][Para 194][Para 210][Para 211][Para 212][Para 213][Para 215][Para 216]

On the contract claim the panel applied Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 311 of the Civil Code: a contract is created, varied or extinguished by the will of the parties, so one party cannot force the other to terminate or convert an open-ended contract against its will, and a fixed-duration contract is what carries the 5 percent end-of-contract indemnity. [Para 91][Para 93][Para 94][Para 95][Para 96][Para 101][Para 102]

On issues 2, 3, 5 and 6 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, contract or past practice granting the sums claimed, so each was an interest dispute; because the 148 claimants were not a most representative union, it refused to consider them, following Awards 81/04, 98/04, 201/14, 02/11, 66/11 and 206/12. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 108][Para 118][Para 120][Para 121][Para 197][Para 204]

On issue 4 the panel held that point 2 of Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 requires every employer in the textile, garment and footwear sector to pay at least USD 10 a month attendance bonus, so it is a general legal norm and not a bonus the employer may dispose of; that it is a form of wage under Article 103; that absence authorised by the employer is a suspension of the contract under Articles 71 and 72, so no wage and no bonus are earned for those days; and that the employer may therefore deduct only a pro-rata amount, not the whole bonus. [Para 148][Para 173][Para 174][Para 176][Para 178][Para 179][Para 180][Para 181] Where the absence was not authorised, by contrast, the worker has not met the "without absence" condition and forfeits the bonus entirely. [Para 184][Para 185][Para 191]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The USD 10 monthly attendance bonus required by Notification No. 230 is a statutory minimum wage component, not a discretionary reward, so where a worker is absent with the employer's authorisation the contract is suspended and the employer may reduce the bonus only in proportion to the days absent; but where the absence is unauthorised the condition of regular attendance fails and the whole bonus may be withheld. [Para 148][Para 180][Para 181][Para 191]

**Obiter dicta:** the employer-appointed arbitrator, Ly Tayseng, dissented on issue 4, holding that the order went beyond the parties' claim because the workers had asked only for pro-rata deduction and had not addressed the case of unauthorised absence. [Para 220][Para 221][Para 222]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel is split: the presiding award orders pro-rata deduction, while the annexed dissent by the employer's arbitrator says the order exceeds the claim; the majority order stands and the dissent is reported rather than resolved. [Para 194][Para 220][Para 222] The number of pregnant workers is printed as "from 500 to 60" at para 84, evidently 50 to 60. [Para 84] Stray years and digits intrude: the panel is constituted on "27 April 2022 2015" at para 22, and "2016", "2017" and "2020" appear in paras 93, 114, 116, 195 and 218. [Para 22][Para 93][Para 195] The union registration status is recorded as not yet granted at para 40, yet the panel treats the 148 workers, not the union, as claimants at paras 120 and 42. [Para 40][Para 42][Para 120] The text of issue 3 is duplicated and garbled at para 56, and the attendance-bonus paragraph is numbered "point 224" at para 150 against "point 2" elsewhere. [Para 56][Para 150] Individual workers and union officers are not reproduced here.
