# Award No. 197/15 — Kan Tek (Cambodia) Hop Beresin Limited (Arbitration Council, 17 August 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborna for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139262

## 1. Facts
Kan Tek (Cambodia) Hop Beresin Limited, at Choum Pol village, Po Pel commune, Tram Kak district, Takeo, assembling computer and telephone electrical components and employing about 1,500 workers, was newly in operation — the employer said four to five months — when three federations brought nine demands on 13 July 2015. [Para 14][Para 33][Para 34][Para 41] Six demands were settled in conciliation on 21 July 2015 and three went to the panel; at the hearing the parties settled two more, issues 07 (re-running the employee-delegate election) and 01 (health-check fee and travel costs), leaving only issue 06. [Para 15][Para 20][Para 43][Para 44] The local unions of all three federations had not yet been registered with the Ministry of Labour, and 42 workers transferred their rights to 11 union representatives, with six more attending in person, four of them not on the transfer list — 46 complainants in all. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 37][Para 38]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay each worker an incentive bonus of USD 3, paid immediately, whenever it has workers work on Sundays and public holidays? [Para 39][Para 46][Para 63]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Sunday and public holidays are rest days, so a worker who gives them up deserves a special incentive; other factories, such as MF and JSD, pay USD 1 a day for Sunday and holiday work, and the worker must also pay his or her own transport because the company bus runs only on ordinary days. [Para 39][Para 40]
**Employer (respondent):** the company has operated only four or five months and cannot afford the payment, and a demand for USD 3 exceeds what the law requires. [Para 41][Para 42]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider the claim for a USD 3 incentive bonus for Sunday and public-holiday work. [Para 63][Para 64]

The panel first classified the claim. It found no provision of the Labour Law, no collective agreement, no internal rule and no past practice between the parties obliging the employer to pay USD 3 per worker whenever work is performed on a Sunday or public holiday, so the demand went beyond what the law fixes and was a dispute over interests (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍) rather than a dispute over rights. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49]

That classification determined jurisdiction. Article 96(2) of the Labour Law defines a collective agreement as a written agreement between employers and union organisations, and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 gives the most representative union the right to request that the employer conclude a collective agreement applying to all the workers it represents. [Para 50][Para 53][Para 54] Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 provides that an award resolving an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement and has effect for one year from the date it becomes final, unless the parties agree a new collective agreement — with the consequence, which the panel spelled out, that such an award applies to every worker in the enterprise and strips them of the right to strike over those same interests in future. [Para 56][Para 57] Because the price of deciding an interest dispute is that heavy, the panel held it may do so only where the claimant is the most representative union in the enterprise, or several unions together holding more than half the workforce as members. [Para 58][Para 60]

Applying that test, the panel noted the awards it followed — 81/04, 04/98, 202/14 and 101/15, and on the negative side 02/11, 66/11, 206/12 and 143/15 — and found that the 46 workers who were the original complainants were not a most-representative union; the local unions had no registration and the complainants were individuals acting through federations whose local branches were unregistered. [Para 59][Para 61][Para 62] The claim therefore lacked the legal character required to bring an interest dispute before the panel, and it was refused. [Para 62][Para 63]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a benefit that no law, collective agreement, internal rule or established practice requires is an interest dispute, and the panel may decide an interest dispute only where it is brought by the most representative union in the enterprise, or by unions together representing more than half of the workers; an award in such a case stands in place of a collective agreement for a year and removes the right to strike on those interests, so a group of individual workers, or federations whose local unions are unregistered, cannot invoke that jurisdiction. [Para 49][Para 57][Para 58][Para 62]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that other factories pay USD 1 a day for Sunday and holiday work, and that the company had been operating only four or five months, are recorded but play no part in the refusal, which rests on jurisdiction. [Para 40][Para 41]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The employer's name is unstable across the text — Kan Tek, Pon Tek, and the Khmer variants "ខប ភើរេសិន", "ខំម ភេរេ សិន" — at [Para 1], [Para 30] and [Para 35]; the form used above is the one on the face of the award.
- The panel is constituted on "28 July 2019 2015" [Para 17], a double year left uncorrected; the conciliation report is numbered 549 at [Para 11] and 549/14 at [Para 16].
- The hearing is recorded at 08:30 "រសៀល" (afternoon) at [Para 13] and 08:30 "ព្រឹក" (morning) at [Para 18].
- The summary of the hearing states that the remaining unsettled points were "01 57" [Para 20], an OCR corruption of "01"; the letters of request at [Para 27] are likewise garbled ("ធម្ម៥៥៤").
- The complainant count reconciles only by inference: 42 mandating workers [Para 36] plus six attending [Para 37], of whom four were not on the list, giving 46 [Para 38].
- The claim is put at USD 3 per worker [Para 39] but the comparison factories are said to pay USD 1 a day [Para 40], and neither figure appears in the operative order, which is a bare refusal.
- The signatures at [Para 66] are fragmentary and carry an orphan year "2017"; the chair's name appears as Pen Bun Chhea only there and at [Para 1].
