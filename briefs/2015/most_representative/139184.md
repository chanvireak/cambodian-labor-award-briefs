# Award No. 093/15 — Sun Well Shoe Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 18 May 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy appointed by the employer, An Non appointed by the workers, Tang Tri chair) · Doc ID 139184

## 1. Facts
Sun Well Shoe Co., Ltd manufactures footwear and employed roughly 950 workers at the date of the hearing; the Development Workers' Union Federation, the claimant, had close to 200 members in the factory. [Para 34][Para 35] The union filed eight demands on working conditions and benefits, all eight of which failed conciliation before the Ministry of Labour on 30 March 2015 and were referred to the Council on 09 April 2015. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 17] At the hearing the parties settled demands 5 and 7 by agreement and the workers withdrew demand 1, leaving five issues for adjudication. [Para 21][Para 22] The employer elected a binding award and the workers a non-binding one, so the award is non-binding. [Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must remove the head of administration, Ms M, from the company? [Para 39]
2. Whether the employer must pay compensation in lieu of unused annual leave within 2 days or 48 hours after the June and December cut-off, rather than one month later? [Para 46][Para 108]
3. Whether the employer must pay the 5 per cent end-of-contract indemnity within 2 days or 48 hours — or at the latest on the 10th-day pay day — each time a fixed-duration contract expires? [Para 53][Para 125]
4. Whether the employer must replace the microphone used to play music with a bass speaker for the workers to listen to? [Para 67][Para 135]
5. Whether the employer must raise the overtime rate to 4,000 riel per hour, i.e. 200 riel above the existing 3,800 riel? [Para 77][Para 154]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the head of administration used loud and inappropriate language at workers who spent too long in the toilet or the clinic and had docked 2,000 riel for union dues without consent; annual leave and the 5 per cent indemnity should be paid immediately because workers had served long and prices had risen; a bass speaker sounds better than a muffled microphone; and 4,000 riel an hour was needed as goods, electricity and rent had risen. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 47][Para 69][Para 79]
**Employer (respondent):** the head of administration committed no fault — her words were advice, not insults, as both sides agreed; the company already pays annual leave in cash twice a year under its agreement with the union and cannot audit the balances of more than 900 workers at short notice; the 5 per cent indemnity is paid between the 15th and 20th of the month after expiry; microphone and bass speaker sound the same; and its base wage already exceeds the 2015 minimum wage of USD 133 a month, overtime being correctly calculated at 150 per cent. [Para 42][Para 44][Para 50][Para 59][Para 70][Para 80][Para 81]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — every remaining demand was rejected: no removal of the head of administration, no order on the timing of annual-leave or indemnity payments, and the panel declined to consider the bass-speaker and overtime-rate demands. [Para 167][Para 168][Para 169][Para 170][Para 171]

**Issue 2 (removal of the head of administration).** The panel treated the question as whether an employer is obliged to dismiss a managerial employee at a union's demand. Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 311 of the 2007 Civil Code confine the power to extinguish the obligations of an employment contract to the parties to that contract, so a third party cannot dissolve it; only the employer could terminate the head of administration's contract. [Para 82][Para 86][Para 87][Para 89] Article 2 gives the employer the right to organise and manage the enterprise provided it acts lawfully and reasonably, following Awards 116/10, 83/12, 08/13, 112/14 and 215/14. [Para 90][Para 94][Para 96][Para 100] The panel might order a transfer or dismissal only where a worker is shown to endanger the health and safety of others; here both sides agreed no insult was used and nothing showed such danger. [Para 102][Para 105][Para 106][Para 107]

**Issue 3 (annual leave paid in cash).** Article 167(a) paragraph 2 permits payment for unused annual leave only where the contract has been severed before the worker took the leave, and paragraph 3 voids any collective agreement or waiver to the contrary. [Para 109][Para 112][Para 113] Because these workers were still serving, the payment in cash was not lawful, and the panel expressly declined the union's invitation to rule only on timing while ignoring legality. [Para 116][Para 118][Para 122][Para 123] Article 116(a)(5) imposes the 48-hour payment duty only on termination. The demand was therefore contrary to the spirit of Article 167(a)(3) and rejected, consistently with Awards 94/04, 45/05, 88/12 and 73/13. [Para 115][Para 124]

**Issue 4 (5 per cent indemnity).** Article 73 provides that a fixed-duration contract expires naturally at its term, and Article 73(6) requires the end-of-contract indemnity, at least 5 per cent of wages paid, on termination. [Para 125][Para 127][Para 128] The panel held the 5 per cent is owed when the contract is dissolved and the parties' rights and duties are cut off, not each time a six-month contract ends where the employment relationship continues. [Para 129][Para 130][Para 132][Para 133]

**Issues 6 and 8 (bass speaker; overtime rate).** Both were interest disputes, not rights disputes: no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules or past practice required a bass speaker or a 4,000 riel rate, and the employer was already discharging Article 139 (new) and Article 5(a) of Prakas No. 80. [Para 137][Para 160][Para 161][Para 162] Under Article 96 and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 only the most representative union may bring an interest dispute, and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 makes such an award a collective agreement binding all workers and removing their right to strike on the point. [Para 144][Para 146][Para 147][Para 148] The claimant, with some 200 of 950 workers, was not the most representative union, so the panel refused to consider those demands, following Awards 02/11, 66/11 and 206/12. [Para 149][Para 151][Para 152][Para 153][Para 166]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union cannot require the dismissal or transfer of a manager or worker: the power to extinguish an employment contract belongs only to its parties, and an employer's right to manage under Article 2 will be displaced only where the person is shown to endanger the health and safety of other workers. [Para 87][Para 102][Para 106]
An employer may not pay out unused annual leave in cash while the employment relationship subsists, and an award will not regulate the timing of a payment the law forbids. [Para 113][Para 116]
The 5 per cent end-of-contract indemnity falls due only when a fixed-duration contract is dissolved and the employment relationship ends, not on each successive renewal. [Para 129][Para 132]
Where a demand exceeds what any law, agreement or practice provides, it is an interest dispute, and the panel will decline to consider it unless the referring union is the most representative in the enterprise. [Para 140][Para 149][Para 152]

**Obiter dicta:** the observations that a Council award on an interest dispute becomes a collective agreement binding all workers and depriving them of the right to strike on that subject, and that the Council may order removal of a worker who endangers others, were not applied to any demand here. [Para 102][Para 148]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The panel is said to have been constituted on "10 April 2019 2015" [Para 18] — two years are printed; the hearing was 23 April 2015 and the award 18 May 2015. [Para 1][Para 14]
- The reasons state that workers may obtain the "5 per cent" indemnity as "45 per cent" [Para 130], contradicting Article 73(6) and the rest of the award. [Para 128]
- Article numbering is inconsistent: "Article 167(a) paragraph 2" is cited as "ថា ខណ្ឌ ទី២" [Para 109] while "Article 116(a) paragraph 6" is invoked at [Para 122] for a five-paragraph provision.
- The union's membership is stated as "close to 200" against an enterprise of "about 950" [Para 35] and elsewhere as "more than 900 workers" [Para 50]; the two were read together, not reconciled.
