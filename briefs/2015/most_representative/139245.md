# Award No. 172/15 — Cambodia Beer Production Factory (Arbitration Council, 17 August 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3 — Mar Sombona (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Pen Bunchhea (president, chosen by the two) · Doc ID 139245

## 1. Facts
Cambodia Beer Production Factory brews beer at Prek Eng, Chbar Ampov, Phnom Penh, and employs about 350 workers, roughly 115 of them beer promoters. [Para 66][Para 67] The Democratic Union of Workers at the factory was registered in 2009, and its current leadership has not yet applied for re-recognition. [Para 53][Para 69] Forty-seven promoters transferred their rights to the Cambodian Food and Service Workers' Federation (CFSWF) to pursue six claims, including a wage increase and the reinstatement of Ms C, a promoter employed since 6 February 2005 on successive one-year contracts. [Para 51][Para 121] Ms C was warned on 6 April 2015 for four days' unauthorised absence, suspended on 8 April 2015, and dismissed on 18 May 2015 for the same absence. [Para 124][Para 125]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise the promoters' base wage by USD 77, to USD 177 per month? [Para 76]
2. Whether the employer must stop requiring women to cut the rings off beer cans and settle invoices directly with depots and shop owners? (settled by agreement at the hearing) [Para 25]
3. Whether the employer must pay maternity leave at 100% of wage? [Para 189]
4. Whether the employer must pay a monthly seniority bonus on the stated scale? [Para 225]
5. Whether the employer must reimburse childcare invoices for children sent to private nurseries? [Para 229]
6. Whether the employer must reinstate Ms C and pay her back wages? [Para 248]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** USD 100 a month cannot cover rent, utilities, travel and school fees, and Article 104 requires a minimum wage consistent with human dignity; the internal rules promise 100% maternity pay; seniority and childcare costs should be paid. [Para 78][Para 83][Para 88] Dismissal was unlawful: Ms C gave notice of her four-day absence, and the supervisor failed to pass the request to administration. [Para 135]
**Employer (respondent):** wages were raised in early 2014 and cannot be raised again; promoters work only four and a half hours a day, and the internal rules on maternity pay apply only to staff on 44 hours a week. [Para 84][Para 91] Ms C was absent four days without authorisation or valid reason, which the internal rules and her contract make a ground for dismissal. [Para 139][Para 141]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — claims 1, 3, 4 and 5 are dismissed; on claim 6 the employer must reinstate Ms C and pay back wages from dismissal to return, and if it refuses must pay severance equal to 150 days of wage and allowances, three months' wage in lieu of notice, moral damages equal to the severance, and unused annual leave. [Para 345][Para 349]

On claims 1 and 4, the panel held it lacked the power to decide: Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 confine interest disputes to the most representative union, and the 47 claimants did not meet that test, so the panel declined to consider the wage and seniority claims at all. [Para 181][Para 183][Para 187] It reached the same result on the seniority bonus, which it also classified as an interest dispute. [Para 227][Para 229]

On maternity pay, Articles 182 and 183 require 90 days at half wage including allowances, and the panel found the employer had paid exactly that. [Para 189][Para 191][Para 193] The internal rules offer a choice: 90 days at 50% plus one hour a day for nursing, or 90 days at 100% with no nursing hour. [Para 197][Para 199] Because Ms C's contract clause 11 provides 50%, and she signed it, the panel held she had elected the first option and the employer owed nothing more. [Para 208][Para 220] It added, however, that the employer must still allow one hour a day for nursing during the year after birth, which it had not done. [Para 222][Para 223]

On childcare, Article 186 obliges enterprises with at least 100 women to provide a nursery, and to pay for an outside nursery where a child is over 18 months. [Para 235][Para 236] The panel found the factory had no nursery in 2015 and that Ms S had paid USD 50 a month to a neighbour, but her child was under 18 months throughout, so the reimbursement condition was not met. [Para 242][Para 243][Para 245][Para 246]

On claim 6, the panel first upheld its jurisdiction: although the employer argued this was an individual dispute, 47 workers had transferred rights and threatened strike action, satisfying the three conditions of a collective dispute under Article 302. [Para 256][Para 262][Para 267] On the contract, successive one-year renewals beyond two years converted it to an indefinite-term contract, following Award No. 10/03 (Zaksintek). [Para 274][Para 282][Para 285] On the misconduct, the panel found the four-day absence proven, but also found the employer had already disciplined that same absence by the 7 April 2015 warning and then dismissed her for it again. [Para 292][Para 295] Article 28 forbids double punishment for one fault, so the dismissal was unlawful and reinstatement with back pay followed. [Para 296][Para 301][Para 303][Para 311]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A disciplinary warning issued for a specific absence exhausts the employer's power to punish that fault: dismissing the worker later for the same absence is double punishment barred by Article 28 and renders the dismissal unlawful [Para 297][Para 298][Para 301][Para 302]. Separately, an interest dispute about wages or bonuses may only be brought by the most representative union in the enterprise, and a panel will decline jurisdiction over it even where the individual claims are otherwise arguable [Para 183][Para 184][Para 187]. Where a collective agreement fixes maternity pay at 50%, a worker who signed it is taken to have chosen that option over the 100% alternative [Para 220][Para 221].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that employers must still grant the one-hour daily nursing break regardless of which maternity option was chosen, even though it awarded nothing for the breach. [Para 222][Para 223]

Paragraphs [Para 344]–[Para 350] repeat the operative order almost verbatim, so the dispositive terms appear twice with slightly different wording. Union officer names are rendered inconsistently across [Para 53] and [Para 66]. The claimant list shrank from 48 to 47 without explanation beyond one missing thumbprint at [Para 165].
