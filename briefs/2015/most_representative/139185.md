# Award No. 091/15 — Double U & D (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 05 May 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Uk Ri appointed by the employer, An Non appointed by the workers, Kong Phal chair) · Doc ID 139185

## 1. Facts
Double U & D (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a garment manufacturer registered on 19 November 1997, employed about 1,800 workers at the hearing. [Para 49][Para 50] Eighty-two workers, all on indefinite-duration contracts, transferred their rights to the Khmer Workers' Spirit Trade Union Federation, which filed eleven demands on 12 March 2015; eight remained non-conciliated and were referred to the Council on 03 April 2015. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 51][Para 54] Both parties elected a non-binding award. [Para 32]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must terminate the employment contracts once in order to pay the statutory severance allowances and re-issue fixed-duration contracts of 6 months or 1 year? [Para 55][Para 95]
2. Whether the employer must provide a midday meal allowance of 4,000 riel per day? [Para 108][Para 109]
3. Whether the employer must provide an overtime meal allowance of 2,000 riel per hour for the hours from 16:00 to 18:00? [Para 125]
4. Whether the employer must raise the regular attendance bonus from USD 10 to USD 15 per month? [Para 136][Para 137]
5. Whether the employer must raise the travel and accommodation allowance from USD 7 to USD 15 per month? [Para 150]
6. Whether the employer must pay 50 per cent of wages for 03 months to a woman worker before she begins maternity leave? [Para 158]
7. Whether the employer must reimburse half a day's wage and a USD 5 attendance bonus deducted from 04 workers when they attended a Council hearing? [Para 177]
8. Whether the employer must issue the payslip 03 days before the monthly pay day? [Para 196]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** indefinite-duration contracts mean workers receive none of the five statutory termination allowances if they resign, so the contracts should be closed once and replaced with fixed-duration ones carrying a 5 per cent indemnity on expiry; food, transport and rent have risen and neighbouring factories pay meal, bonus and travel allowances of USD 12–15 a month; and maternity pay should be advanced before birth because workers need it for delivery costs and live too far away to collect it afterwards. [Para 56][Para 58][Para 62][Para 71][Para 74][Para 77][Para 78]
**Employer (respondent):** the company lawfully uses indefinite-duration contracts since 2008 and will not terminate them against its will; it has never paid a midday meal allowance and pays only what the law requires; it already pays 4,200 riel daily in combined allowances — 1,000 riel food, 2,000 riel overtime meal money and 1,200 riel incentive — plus the statutory USD 10 bonus and USD 7 travel allowance; it pays the 50 per cent maternity wage after delivery on production of the birth certificate; and the four workers were docked for failing to complete the leave form. [Para 55][Para 64][Para 69][Para 72][Para 76][Para 84]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issues 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 8 were rejected or declined; the panel ordered the employer to pay 50 per cent of wages for 03 months to pregnant women workers before they begin maternity leave, and to reimburse half a day's wage plus the USD 5 attendance bonus to each of the 04 workers. [Para 212][Para 217][Para 218]

**Issue 1 (contract conversion).** Article 311 of the Civil Code and Article 65 of the Labour Law make the creation, modification and extinction of contractual obligations depend on the will of the parties, so one party cannot force the other to dissolve a contract, following Awards 34/13, 138/13, 039/14 and 308/14. [Para 95][Para 98][Para 99][Para 100] The employer had no wish to terminate, so the demand lacked legal basis. [Para 104][Para 106]

**Issues 2 to 5 (meal, overtime meal, bonus and travel allowances).** None of these was required by the Labour Law, a collective agreement, the internal rules or past practice; the employer was already meeting the applicable Prakas — 2,000 riel overtime meal money under point 2 of Notification No. 041/11, USD 10 under Notification No. 230, and USD 7 under the same notification. [Para 109][Para 125][Para 126][Para 128][Para 137][Para 140][Para 150][Para 152] Each demand therefore exceeded the law and was an interest dispute, which under Article 96, Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 only the most representative union may bring, since such an award becomes a collective agreement binding all workers and removes their right to strike. [Para 112][Para 115][Para 118][Para 120] The claimant represented 82 of about 1,800 workers and was not the most representative union, following Awards 169/11, 02/11 and 66/11, so the panel declined to consider them. [Para 121][Para 124][Para 135][Para 158]

**Issue 6 (maternity pay in advance).** Articles 182 and 183 require the employer to pay half wages, including ancillary pay, for the 90-day maternity leave, but fix no payment date; Article 115(3) forbids paying wages on a rest day and Article 103 counts maternity payments as wages. [Para 159][Para 161][Para 162][Para 163][Para 165] Following its earlier awards (13908 SVP, 27/12, 35/13), the panel held maternity pay must be paid at least one day before the leave begins. [Para 167][Para 171] Paying only after delivery on production of a birth certificate and yellow card was inconsistent with Article 115 and imposed hardship on women who need the money for delivery and health costs. [Para 173][Para 176]

**Issue 7 (deductions for attending a hearing).** Article 14 of Prakas No. 099 requires the employer to let a worker attend a Council hearing during working hours without any negative effect on their rights, and Article 34 empowers the panel to order immediate payment of sums due. [Para 177][Para 194] The employer admitted the four workers sought and received permission from the general manager; the panel held they had not breached internal rules 07 and 09, and ordered repayment of the half-day wage and USD 5 bonus to each. [Para 183][Para 192][Para 195]

**Issue 8 (payslip timing).** Article 112 requires the employer to inform workers clearly of their wages whenever those may change, and the employer does issue an itemised payslip with the wage on pay day, allowing objections the next morning. [Para 196][Para 208] The article obliges clarity, not prior delivery, so the demand was rejected, following Award 61/13. [Para 205][Para 211]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Neither party to an employment contract can compel the other to dissolve it in order to convert it into a fixed-duration contract, because extinction of contractual obligations depends on the will of both parties. [Para 99][Para 100][Para 104]
Maternity pay under Articles 182 and 183 must be paid at least one day before the 90-day maternity leave begins, and an employer may not condition it on delivery or on production of a birth certificate or yellow card. [Para 171][Para 173][Para 175]
An employer must release a worker to attend a Council hearing during working hours without deducting wages or attendance bonuses, and the panel will order repayment of such deductions under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 193][Para 195]
A demand for a benefit above what the law, a Prakas or past practice provides is an interest dispute that the panel will decline unless the referring union is the most representative in the enterprise. [Para 120][Para 123]

**Obiter dicta:** the remarks that itemised payslips promote transparency, and that workers should nonetheless complete leave forms properly while employers should remind them of the procedure, were not necessary to the rejections. [Para 189][Para 204]
