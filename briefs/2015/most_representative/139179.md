# Award No. 84/15 — JS D Tech Style (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 24 April 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambor Na chosen by the employer, An Nan chosen by the workers, Kong Phalha chair) · Doc ID 139179

## 1. Facts
JS D Tech Style (Cambodia), a shirt-producing factory at Trapeang Sab, Bati, Takeo employing about 860 workers, was presented on 3 December 2014 with an 18-point list of working conditions by three union federations acting on authorisations thumbprinted by 12 workers. [Para 61][Para 68][Para 70] Conciliation at the provincial Labour Department failed on all 18 points on 19 March 2015 and the file reached the Council on 24 March 2015. [Para 25][Para 26] Seven of the 12 workers later withdrew their claims and authorisations, leaving five claimants, and at the hearing the parties settled 12 of the 18 points, leaving six for the panel. [Para 73][Para 29][Para 30]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must provide one free midday meal, or 4,000 riel per day, to each worker? [Para 145]
2. Whether the employer must pay an overtime meal allowance of 2,000 riel per hour for work from 16:00 to 18:00? [Para 163]
3. Whether the employer must conclude fixed-duration contracts of 6 months to 1 year with the workers? [Para 175]
5. Whether the employer must pay a regular attendance bonus (ប្រាក់ព្រីម) of USD 15 per month? [Para 204]
7. Whether the employer must pay USD 15 per month in travel and accommodation allowance? [Para 214]
18. Whether maternity pay must be calculated on the average wage of the last 12 months rather than the basic wage? [Para 224]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** no law or agreement requires the benefits claimed, but other companies grant midday meals and the current wage does not cover rising food, transport and rent costs; short fixed-duration contracts leave them anxious about job security and afraid of non-renewal. [Para 76][Para 81][Para 85][Para 91][Para 97] Maternity pay should be 50% of total wage including allowances, as received while actually working. [Para 124]
**Employer (respondent):** the company already applies the law and in several respects pays above it — 3,000 riel overtime meal allowance, a USD 10 attendance bonus, USD 8 travel and accommodation, and 90-day maternity leave — and cannot grant more; it uses 1, 2, 3 and 6-month contracts because orders are scarce. [Para 82][Para 123][Para 166][Para 205][Para 214]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issues 01, 02, 03, 05 and 07 were dismissed, and on issue 18 the panel ordered the employer to recompute maternity pay by taking the wage of the last 12 months before leave, dividing by 12, dividing by 2 for the 50% rate, and multiplying by 3 for the 90 days. [Para 233][Para 234][Para 235][Para 236][Para 237][Para 238]

The panel first addressed standing. Article 268 of the Labour Law gives a professional organisation the rights the law recognises only once its statutes and list of leaders are deposited and registered with the Ministry; the panel read this, following Awards 023/14 and 032/14, as also governing a union's right to represent its members before the Council. [Para 126][Para 129][Para 130] The Khmer Youth Voice Union had no registered branch at the company, and only its federation officials, not the elected branch president, appeared; the claimant was therefore not the most representative union in the enterprise. [Para 134][Para 135] Under Article 96 of the Labour Law and Prakas 305/01 on representativeness, and Prakas 099/04 Article 43, an interest award stands in place of a collective agreement and binds all workers for one year, so the panel will decide an interest dispute only where the claimant union holds more than half the workforce. [Para 149][Para 150][Para 152][Para 154] The claimant having failed that test, issues 01, 02, 05 and 07 were dismissed without reaching the merits. [Para 161][Para 162][Para 174][Para 223]

On the merits the panel nonetheless found that no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice obliged the employer to provide a free midday meal or 4,000 riel, and that the overtime meal allowance of 2,000 riel per day under Notification 041/11 was exceeded by the 3,000 riel actually paid for two hours of overtime. [Para 146][Para 147][Para 164][Para 167] Notification 230/12 requires a minimum USD 10 attendance bonus and USD 7 travel and accommodation; the employer paid USD 10 and USD 8. [Para 204][Para 205][Para 214][Para 215]

On issue 3 the panel held that a contract is formed by matching offer and acceptance under Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code, so neither party may impose a duration on the other; the workers' request was lawful but the employer's refusal was equally lawful. [Para 180][Para 186][Para 197][Para 198] On issue 18, Articles 182 and 183 give a woman 90 days' leave on half pay of wage and allowances; the employer's use of basic wage plus travel and accommodation was wrong, and the panel adopted the 12-month averaging method used in Awards 06/08, 52/12 and 186/13. [Para 224][Para 226][Para 227][Para 231]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that has not registered a branch at the enterprise under Article 268 of the Labour Law is not the most representative union there, and the panel will not entertain an interest dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍) it brings, because an interest award displaces the collective agreement and binds the whole workforce for a year — a power reserved to a union representing more than half the employees. [Para 135][Para 154][Para 161] Maternity pay under Articles 182–183 is half of the average monthly wage of the 12 months preceding leave, inclusive of allowances and bonuses, not half of the basic wage. [Para 226][Para 231][Para 232]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that a fixed-duration contract of more than two years converts into an indefinite one, and that a party may seek annulment of a contract procured by coercion, were general statements on consent and were not applied to any order here. [Para 202][Para 187]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The operative order on issue 02 at [Para 234] reads both "dismiss the claim" and "order the employer to pay the overtime meal allowance of 2,000 riel per hour", contradicting the reasoning at [Para 174], which dismisses it outright. The reasoning was treated as controlling.
- The award date is 24 April 2015 in the header [Para 1], the hearing was 6 April 2015 [Para 23], and the extension of the deadline is printed as "20 April 2015 to 24 April 2015" but garbled as "ឆ្នាំ ២០ ២០១៥" [Para 33].
- Prakas 099/04 is dated "21 April 2015 2004" at [Para 136], an evident OCR duplication of the year.
- The attendance bonus finding says the employer paid USD 10 "to 20 workers" [Para 205], a figure that cannot be reconciled with the 860-strong workforce.
- Worker names survive in the source and are abbreviated here as Mr C (the branch president who alone attended), Mr T, Mr M, Mr P and Mr S; the award itself prints "Mr" forms inconsistently and one list entry is corrupted at [Para 139].
