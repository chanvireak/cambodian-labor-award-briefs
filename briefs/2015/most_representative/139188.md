# Award No. 092/15 — Syu An Fang Garment Factory Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 20 May 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy appointed by the employer, Tuon Si Phan appointed by the workers, Run Saray chair; Ing Sothy filed a dissenting opinion on issue 3) · Doc ID 139188

## 1. Facts
Syu An Fang Garment Factory Co., Ltd is a garment manufacturer that began operating in November 2014 and employed about 580 workers. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 59] On 15 February 2015 the Cambodia Labour Workers' Union Federation held an election and elected a factory leadership, which had still not been registered. [Para 50][Para 51] Of 17 demands, 10 were settled in conciliation and 7 referred to the Council. [Para 15][Para 16] On 31 March 2015 the employer dismissed five women workers, three of them elected union leaders, after they attended the conciliation of 27 March 2015; both parties elected a non-binding award. [Para 22][Para 93][Para 277]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must give 100 days' maternity leave and maternity pay of 60 per cent of wages and ancillary benefits? [Para 55][Para 135]
2. Whether the employer must raise the regular attendance bonus to USD 15 a month? [Para 159]
3. Whether the employer must reduce the attendance bonus in proportion to the days a worker is absent, both where leave is authorised and where it is not? [Para 170]
4. Whether the employer must pay a midday meal allowance of 4,000 riel a day? [Para 223]
5. Whether the employer must pay a travel and accommodation allowance of USD 15 a month? [Para 230]
6. Whether the employer must pay a milk allowance of USD 15 a month to mothers of children aged 18 to 36 months? [Para 239]
7. Whether the employer must provide a permanent nurse, a doctor and adequate medicines at the factory? [Para 248]
8. Whether the employer must reinstate the five dismissed workers and pay their wages and benefits from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 269][Para 380]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** 100 days and 60 per cent would protect women at childbirth and the ILO is moving that way; the attendance bonus should match the USD 12 or more paid by most factories; most factories pay 1,500 to 2,000 riel a day for meals; bus fares alone are USD 8 to 9 a month; milk costs four to five cans a month; and the five women were dismissed for being union leaders who attended the conciliation, not for any fault. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 62][Para 72][Para 76][Para 81][Para 95][Para 96]
**Employer (respondent):** the company only started in November 2014 and pays what the law requires — the statutory USD 10 attendance bonus and USD 7 travel allowance, and no meal or milk allowance; it is building a nursery and has a clinic room with three beds but cannot recruit a doctor because the site is remote; and it simply did not wish to continue the contracts of the five. [Para 59][Para 64][Para 73][Para 77][Para 83][Para 90][Para 100]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issues 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6 were dismissed or declined; the attendance bonus may be reduced only in proportion to authorised absence days and must otherwise be paid in full; a permanent nurse, a doctor present at least two hours a day and adequate medicines were ordered; and all five women were reinstated with back pay and benefits from 31 March 2015. [Para 371][Para 373][Para 374][Para 377][Para 378][Para 380]

**Issue 1 (maternity).** Articles 182 and 183 give 90 days and half pay; the demand for 100 days and 60 per cent exceeded the law, no collective agreement or practice supported it, and it was therefore an interest dispute. [Para 135][Para 137][Para 141][Para 143][Para 144] Under Article 96, Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 only the most representative union may bring such a dispute, and the claimants were not such a union, so the panel declined it, following Awards 81/04, 98/04 and 202/14. [Para 147][Para 150][Para 152][Para 156][Para 158] Issues 2, 4, 5 and 6 were disposed of the same way: each demanded more than Notification No. 230 or the law provided. [Para 164][Para 165][Para 168][Para 233][Para 242][Para 247]

**Issue 3 (proportional bonus deduction).** Notification No. 230 point 2 requires at least USD 10 for a worker who attends all working days with no absence. [Para 159][Para 174] With Articles 71(6), 72(1) and 103, the panel held that authorised absence suspends the contract and no wage or bonus is owed for those days, so a proportional reduction is lawful; but where the absence is of a kind the law itself provides for — annual leave, special leave, public holidays — no deduction may be made and the full USD 10 is payable. [Para 182][Para 183][Para 202][Para 207][Para 208] Unauthorised absence forfeits the whole bonus. [Para 212][Para 221] Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented, arguing that the USD 10 is pay given as a reward under Article 103 and must be paid in full and never apportioned. [Para 384][Para 387][Para 395]

**Issue 7 (clinic).** Article 238 requires first aid and Article 242 requires an enterprise with 50 or more workers to keep a permanent infirmary staffed by a nurse and, by reference to Article 3 of Prakas No. 330, a doctor for a minimum number of hours; medicines must be adequate under Article 242(4) and Article 4 of the Prakas. [Para 248][Para 249][Para 263] With 580 workers the factory fell in the 301–600 band, requiring one nurse and a doctor for two hours; it had no permanent nurse and inadequate medicines, the same type being handed out for different complaints. [Para 258][Para 259][Para 267][Para 268]

**Supplementary issue (reinstatement).** Article 312 and Article 33 of Prakas No. 099 let the panel hear the dismissal because it occurred after the conciliation minute and was a direct consequence of the dispute. [Para 271][Para 272][Para 280] The employer produced no written fixed-duration contracts, so Article 67(7) converted them into indefinite-duration contracts and the terminations were dismissals, not non-renewals. [Para 289][Para 292][Para 294] For the three elected leaders, Article 293 and Articles 3(3) and 4 of Prakas No. 305 gave 45 days' protection after the election; the dismissal came 44 days after and the employer had not sought the labour inspector's consent, so reinstatement was ordered. [Para 305][Para 306][Para 342][Para 352][Para 357] The other two were dismissed for no valid reason under Article 74, the employer saying only that it was "not satisfied", and were also reinstated with back pay. [Para 367][Para 369][Para 370]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A regular attendance bonus required by Notification No. 230 must be reduced in proportion to days of authorised absence, since absence suspends the contract, but not at all for annual leave, special leave or public holidays; unauthorised absence forfeits it entirely. [Para 183][Para 207][Para 212]
An enterprise with 50 or more workers must keep a permanent infirmary with a nurse and a doctor for the hours set by Prakas No. 330, and adequate medicines; difficulty in recruiting is no answer. [Para 249][Para 259][Para 267]
Union leaders elected in a factory election are protected from dismissal for 45 days after the election; dismissal within that period without the labour inspector's prior consent is unlawful and requires reinstatement. [Para 306][Para 342][Para 357]
Where an employer produces no written fixed-duration contract, the contract is indefinite-duration under Article 67(7), so ending it is a dismissal requiring a valid reason under Article 74. [Para 292][Para 369]

**Obiter dicta:** the remarks that the two non-leader workers were not shown to be election candidates, and that the postal service returned the union's notice marked "recipient does not accept" without that defeating notification, were not necessary to the order. [Para 336]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The factory is named in at least four transliterations in the source (para 1, 16, 48 and 49), and the union once as the federation and once as its factory branch.
- The attendance bonus is stated as "100 dollars" at [Para 170] where USD 10 is meant, and the orders give "4 15 dollars" [Para 372] and "156 dollars" [Para 377] against USD 15 elsewhere.
- The postal return of the union's notification is dated 20 February 2015 at [Para 113] but 25 February 2015 at [Para 344].
