# Award No. 206/15 — Perfect Growth Private Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 25 August 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Kong Phalla, chair chosen by both) · non-binding award · Doc ID 139270

## 1. Facts
The company sews garments at Ang Snuol, Kandal, with about 2,600 workers in three buildings of 24 sewing lines and 30 toilets each, and the Khmer Youth Voice Union of the company is registered with the Ministry of Labour under No. 3226 Kប/វក of 8 January 2015. Of nine demands filed after conciliation, one was settled by the parties, issue 2 was settled at the hearing, and the union withdrew issues 1, 3, 4 and 7, leaving only the demand for 12 more cleaning staff, a USD 14 monthly wage rise for machine mechanics, and the transfer of a security guard. [Para 41][Para 49][Para 48][Para 28][Para 29][Para 30]

## 2. Issues in dispute
5. Whether the employer must hire 12 more cleaning workers, four per building, for the sewing lines and toilets? [Para 75]
6. Whether the employer must raise machine mechanics' wages by USD 14 per month as promised? [Para 95]
8. Whether the employer must transfer a security guard nicknamed Mab away from his daily post? [Para 117]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Four cleaners per building is not enough: three cover all 24 lines and one all 30 toilets, so the cleaners are exhausted, have no rest, and the workplace and toilets are dirty and smell; the machine-section head promised at recruitment that wages would rise after one year; and the guard has a bad manner and speech, accused workers of stealing company shirts and demanded to search their belongings. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51][Para 54][Para 58][Para 59]
**Employer (respondent):** There are 36 cleaners, 12 per building, an audit report confirms the premises are clean, and the number of cleaners is a management decision; no wage promise was ever made; and a guard cannot be moved at once, since any fault must first be examined, and no worker is transferred on demand. [Para 52][Para 56][Para 60][Para 61]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three remaining demands were refused; no money was ordered and no staffing or transfer direction was made. [Para 132][Para 133][Para 134]

On issue 5 the panel recalled Article 2 of the Labour Law, under which the employer manages and directs the enterprise so long as it acts lawfully and reasonably, and held that the party asserting a claim bears the burden of proof. The workers produced no evidence of the fatigue, dirt or smell alleged, and although the panel had ordered the employer to file the audit report it relied on, that report was never filed by the 19 August 2015 deadline. The panel nonetheless held that the employer must ensure a hygienic and comfortable workplace, but that deciding how many cleaners to employ is the employer's prerogative, so the demand failed. [Para 79][Para 83][Para 87][Para 90][Para 91][Para 92][Para 93][Para 94]

On issue 6 the panel again applied the burden of proof and found no Labour Law provision, collective agreement, internal rule, agreement or past practice obliging the employer to pay the USD 14 increase, characterising the demand as an interests dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍). It then examined representation: under Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, only the most representative union may propose a collective agreement, and under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 an award on an interests dispute stands in place of a collective agreement for one year, so the Council will decide such a dispute only where the claimant union is the most representative one, or several together hold more than half the enterprise's workers. The union here admitted it was not, so the panel declined to consider the claim. [Para 97][Para 102][Para 103][Para 105][Para 106][Para 108][Para 109][Para 115][Para 116]

On issue 8 the panel applied Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 311 of the Civil Code: only the parties to a contract may create, vary or extinguish its obligations, so a third party has no right to dissolve another's employment contract, and only the employer may decide to move a guard from his post. Following earlier awards, a worker cannot demand the removal of another worker unless he proves that person is dangerous and cannot be kept in the enterprise without endangering others; bad manners and speech, and a search of bags that is in fact a guard's duty, fell short of that. [Para 117][Para 119][Para 120][Para 122][Para 123][Para 124][Para 129][Para 130]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The Council will decide an interests dispute only where the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise, or where unions together represent more than half the workforce, since such an award replaces a collective agreement for a year; and a demand that staffing levels, wage increases or another employee's removal be ordered fails where the claimant shows neither a legal or contractual source of the obligation nor evidence that the employer's lawful managerial choice was unreasonable, the assertion that a co-worker is dangerous requiring proof. [Para 109][Para 93][Para 102][Para 124]

**Obiter dicta:** That the employer must in any event guarantee hygienic and comfortable working conditions, whatever the number of cleaners it chooses; and that a security guard's search of workers' bags on suspicion of theft is a normal performance of duty, not misconduct. [Para 92][Para 129]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The conciliation report number 1263/15 is dated 3 August 2015 in the procedural summary and 28 July 2015 in two later passages, and the conciliation itself is dated both 27 July and 3 August. The employer's registered name is transliterated at least six different ways across the file, and the union's local name varies with it. The Prakas No. 099 citation is printed "21 April 2019 2004", and the quoted Article 96 text has the year 2015 embedded in it. The award says nine demands were conciliated but that one was settled and eight sent on, and separately that one of the eight was settled at the hearing. Union officers appear only by role and are redacted here. [Para 19][Para 24][Para 42][Para 17][Para 23][Para 28][Para 48][Para 106][Para 107]
