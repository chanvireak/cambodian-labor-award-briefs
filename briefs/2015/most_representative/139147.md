# Award No. 043/15 — M&V International Manufacturing Ltd (Arbitration Council, 10 March 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambor Na, appointed by the employer; An Vireak, appointed by the workers; Sokh Mathoeung, chair) · Doc ID 139147

## 1. Facts
The company, a weaving and garment manufacturer, employs about 2,880 workers. [Para 36][Para 37] Three unions represent the workforce: the Democratic Union of Garment Workers (Cambodia) with about 1,160 members, the Independent Union of Cambodian Workers with about 400, and the Women's Work Union of Cambodia with about 100 paying members. [Para 38][Para 40][Para 42] The workers demanded a 28% increase in the piece rate (តម្លៃឡូត៍) for old models, matching the 2015 minimum wage rise from USD 100 to USD 128 a month. [Para 48] Conciliation on the single demand failed on 29 January 2015 and the report reached the secretariat on 06 February 2015. [Para 10][Para 11]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise piece rates on old models by 28%, corresponding to the 2015 minimum wage increase? [Para 66]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Each time the Ministry raised the minimum wage, the employer used to raise piece rates too, but in 2015 it refused. [Para 49][Para 112] Old-model lot workers are skilled and fast, yet earn about the same as workers paid by the month (fixed wage), and old models were simply re-labelled with new PN numbers. [Para 53][Para 54] They rely on the agreement of 25 June 2013 with the Democratic Union federation. [Para 85]

**Employer (respondent):** A 28% increase cannot be granted, but the company has built a formula for calculating piece rates — IE time plus section and team head time plus worker time, divided to an average — which holds whatever minimum wage is announced. [Para 3][Para 55][Para 56] From 70% to 80% of piece workers already earn at or above the minimum, and shortfalls are topped up to the minimum. [Para 60][Para 61]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the demand for a 28% increase on old-model piece rates. [Para 126][Para 127]

The panel applied Article 108 of the Labour Law, under which piece-rate pay must be fixed so that a worker of average skill working normally earns at least the guaranteed minimum wage, and Article 137 on normal hours; Prakas No. 283 (12 November 2014) set the 2015 minimum at USD 128 a month for full-rights workers and USD 123 for probationers, with top-up required where output falls below those figures. [Para 66][Para 68][Para 69][Para 74] It followed prior awards holding that the employer may set or change piece rates, but must do so such that an averagely skilled worker can earn the minimum wage. [Para 77][Para 78]

On the facts, both parties accepted that 70%–80% of piece workers receive at least the minimum and that the employer tops up any shortfall, so the employer was applying Prakas No. 283 correctly. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 84] The 2013 agreement relied on by the workers was tied to Prakas No. 103 and said nothing about adjustment when Prakas No. 283 issued, so it could not be used to compel a rise. [Para 85][Para 86] The panel held the demand unsupported by any law, agreement, collective agreement or contract. [Para 87]

Because the claim was an interest dispute, the panel also examined union representativeness under Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 (22 November 2001), following awards that an interest dispute may only be brought by the most representative union or by unions together holding over 51% of the workforce. [Para 88][Para 91][Para 95][Para 98] The three unions together held about 1,660 members out of 2,880, above 51%, so they were entitled to bring the dispute. [Para 44][Para 105][Para 107] Deciding on equity under Article 312(2), the panel found the evidence on actual PN numbers, old and new rates and the justification for 28% insufficient, and declined the claim. [Para 108][Para 111][Para 122][Para 124][Para 125]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand to raise piece rates by the same percentage as a minimum-wage increase has no legal basis where the employer's rate-setting formula keeps an averagely skilled worker at or above the statutory minimum and tops up any shortfall; a prior collective agreement tied to an earlier minimum-wage Prakas does not carry over to a later one. [Para 84][Para 86][Para 87] An interest dispute may be brought only by the most representative union, or by unions whose combined membership exceeds 51% of the workforce. [Para 98][Para 106]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that where no single union is most representative, unions may act jointly if their combined membership is more than half the workforce — an observation on standing that did not determine the merits. [Para 100][Para 101] It also remarked that an award settling an interest dispute becomes a collective agreement binding all workers in the company for one year. [Para 96][Para 97]
