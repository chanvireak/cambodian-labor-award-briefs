# Award No. 044/15 — Ying Hi (Cambodia) Garment (Arbitration Council, 6 March 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun appointed by the employer party, An Non appointed by the worker party, Aing Eng Thong chair) · Doc ID 139402

## 1. Facts
Ying Hi (Cambodia) Garment is a garment-cutting and sewing enterprise at Dangkor, Phnom Penh, with about 200 workers. [Para 47][Para 48] Thirty workers transferred their rights to the Cambodia-ASEAN Trade Union Confederation (សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្ពុជា-អាស៊ាន) by undated letter of 2 January 2015; there was no local union at the factory and the confederation held no most-representative status. [Para 48][Para 51] Ten demands went to conciliation on 2 February 2015 and all ten failed; at the hearing the parties settled five (issues 1, 2, 3, 7 and 8), leaving issues 4, 5, 6, 9 and 10. [Para 20][Para 25][Para 26] Those five concerned a 3,000 riel lunch allowance, a 4,000 riel overtime meal allowance, $20 travel and accommodation money, and two challenges to the employer's practice of forfeiting the whole $15 monthly attendance bonus. [Para 52][Para 59][Para 64][Para 69][Para 76]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of 3,000 riel per day? [Para 52][Para 83]
2. Whether the employer must pay 4,000 riel of overtime meal money per day for overtime worked from 16:00 to 18:00? [Para 59][Para 100]
3. Whether the employer must pay $20 a month in travel and accommodation money to each worker? [Para 64][Para 111]
4. Whether the employer must stop forfeiting the attendance bonus when a worker arrives five minutes late three times in a month? [Para 69][Para 120]
5. Whether the employer must reduce the attendance bonus proportionately, by reference to the number of days a worker was absent with the employer's authorisation, rather than forfeiting it entirely? [Para 76][Para 132]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** food prices have risen and about 90 per cent of neighbouring factories pay a lunch allowance; 2,000 riel of overtime meal money is not enough to buy a meal and other factories pay 3,000; bus fares and rent have risen. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 61][Para 62][Para 66] Lateness is occasional and caused by personal business, and forfeiting the whole bonus for it demoralises workers; other companies reduce the bonus pro rata for authorised leave. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 79][Para 80]
**Employer (respondent):** the minimum wage has only just been raised and the employer has no capacity to pay more; no law requires a lunch allowance. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58] The employer already complies with the law and pays above it, and the attendance bonus is given only to workers who attend regularly; a worker who asks for leave thereby accepts that the bonus will be deducted. [Para 63][Para 68][Para 75][Para 81][Para 82]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issues 4, 5 and 6 were dismissed for want of standing, issue 9 was dismissed on the merits, and on issue 10 the panel ordered the employer to reduce the attendance bonus proportionately to the number of days a worker was absent with the employer's valid authorisation, instead of forfeiting it. [Para 177][Para 178][Para 179][Para 180][Para 181]

On standing, the claimant was thirty individual workers who had delegated their rights to a trade-union confederation, not a local union with most-representative status. [Para 48][Para 51][Para 97] The panel repeated its settled analysis: an arbitral award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement, binds every worker in the enterprise for one year and removes the right to strike over that subject, so under Prakas No. 099, Article 43, the Council will decide an interest dispute only where the claimant is the most-representative union, or several unions together holding more than half the workforce. [Para 92][Para 93][Para 94][Para 95] Prakas No. 305, Article 9, likewise reserves the right to request a collective agreement to the most-representative union. [Para 89] It had refused interest disputes on that ground before, and did so here. [Para 96][Para 98]

That disposed of issues 4, 5 and 6, each of which sought more than the law, a collective agreement, internal rules or practice gave: no instrument requires a lunch allowance at all, [Para 84][Para 85] Notice No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 requires only 2,000 riel of overtime meal money and the employer pays that, [Para 100][Para 101][Para 103] and Notice No. 230 of 25 July 2012 requires $7 of travel and accommodation money while the employer pays $10, which is more favourable. [Para 111][Para 112][Para 113]

Issues 9 and 10 turned on Notice No. 230, so they were rights disputes and were decided. [Para 120][Para 121][Para 122] Point 2 of that Notice gives a minimum $10 attendance bonus to workers who attend on all the working days of the month "without absence"; the panel held that lateness means the worker has not attended regularly, so no entitlement arises — and the employer pays $15, above the minimum — dismissing issue 9. [Para 120][Para 124][Para 129][Para 130] On issue 10 it held that the attendance bonus is a wage, since Article 103 of the Labour Law includes sums paid as a bonus, [Para 165][Para 166][Para 167] and that leave granted by the employer suspends the contract under Article 71(6) and Article 72(1), so no wage is owed for the absent days. [Para 168][Para 169][Para 171] Deduction must therefore be proportionate to the days absent; forfeiting the whole month's bonus for a handful of authorised days takes wages the worker has earned and is not fair to either side. [Para 164][Para 172][Para 173][Para 175]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus is a wage within Article 103 of the Labour Law, and absence authorised by the employer suspends the employment contract under Articles 71(6) and 72(1), so the employer may withhold the bonus only in proportion to the days of authorised absence; it may not forfeit the entire monthly bonus for such an absence. [Para 167][Para 171][Para 173][Para 176] Conversely, a worker who arrives late has not attended regularly within point 2 of Notice No. 230 of 25 July 2012 and acquires no entitlement to the statutory minimum attendance bonus for that month. [Para 122][Para 129]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the phrase "without absence" in Notice No. 230 is ambiguous, that the Labour Advisory Committee has never explained it, and that each claim must therefore be assessed on its own facts, are observations the panel did not need in order to decide issues 9 and 10 as it did. [Para 143][Para 144][Para 148][Para 149]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award date is printed as 6 March 2015, and [Para 28] records that the parties agreed to postpone issuance from 3 March 2015 to 6 March 2015; that paragraph is heavily corrupted ("ឆ្នាំ ២០១៦០?៣០", "២០២៣"), but the two dates are legible.
- Year fragments from other documents are embedded throughout: "ឆ្នាំ ២០២៣" at [Para 3], "ឆ្នាំ ២០២១ ២០១៥" at [Para 22], "ឆ្នាំ ២០២៣" at [Para 101], "២០១៥" inside the order at [Para 176] and "២០១៧" at [Para 143]. Read literally they contradict the 2015 dates; the 2015 dates were used.
- The arbitrator chairing the panel is printed "អាំង អេង ថុង" at [Para 1] and "អាំង អេង ថ្កុង" at [Para 183]; the worker representatives appear only as given names, "សារី សុទុន" and "រិនិមល" [Para 49], and are redacted here.
- The attendance bonus is described as $15 paid by the employer at [Para 69] and [Para 124] but the statutory minimum is $10 at [Para 120] and [Para 146]; the panel's reasoning that a worker receives "one hundred per cent, namely 10 dollars" therefore does not match the sum actually paid.
- [Para 102] prints the overtime meal money as "៤១០០០ រៀល" and [Para 112] adds "ក្នុង ឆ្នាំ មួយ ខែ" (per year a month); [Para 61] contains the broken fragment "៣ចំនួន ៣ ២០០០ រៀលើ", and [Para 178] prints "ចំនូន ១ ៤០០០ រៀល". The operative figures were taken from the uncontested statements of practice at [Para 59] and [Para 64].
- [Para 122] and [Para 135] garble the Notice No. 230 text ("ដោយ គ្មាន អវត្តមាន", "ការ ឡើង ទូត ភ្លាម"), and [Para 28] is the only record of the postponement, so the reason for it cannot be checked.
