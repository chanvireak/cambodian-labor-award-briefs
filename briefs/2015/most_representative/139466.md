# Award No. 287/15 — Kon Thek (Cambodia) Hop Operation Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 21 December 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Kuy Nam (president), Seng Vouch Hun (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed) · Doc ID 139466

## 1. Facts
Kon Thek (Cambodia) Hop Operation Co., Ltd makes electronic components and employed about 3,800 workers [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65]. The Khmer Free Workers' Union at the company was registered with the Ministry on 21 July 2015 but holds no certificate of most-representative status [Para 67][Para 68]. The union filed 23 claims ranging from the pay of team leaders to allowances and to the removal of named managers and of a rival union; conciliation on 4 November 2015 failed [Para 31][Para 32]. At the hearing the parties reached agreement on 12 of the 23 points and the union withdrew point 11, leaving points 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 15, 16, 17, 18 and 23 for decision [Para 36][Para 37]. The parties agreed on 27 November 2015 that the award would be binding [Para 38][Para 260].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must fold the USD 25 role allowance into the base wage of every team leader? [Para 69]
2. Whether the employer must refrain from dismissing workers arbitrarily, and whether contracts must renew automatically where the worker has no serious misconduct and fewer than three warnings? [Para 76][Para 78][Para 142]
3. Whether the employer must pay USD 5 a month for health care, 4,000 riel for meals on overtime worked on rest days and holidays, 2,000 riel for lunch and a USD 15 monthly attendance bonus? [Para 81][Para 87][Para 91][Para 94]
4. Whether the employer must dismiss the administrative manager, Mr I, and the worker Mr Y? [Para 97][Para 103]
5. Whether the employer must remove from the factory the union it is said to have created? [Para 106][Para 238]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** team leaders are skilled workers whose role allowance ought to form part of basic pay, since overtime is calculated on basic pay alone; the employer dismissed workers without cause, denied health cover, meal and attendance allowances paid elsewhere, tolerated a manager who took money from job applicants and abused workers, and set up a compliant union to displace theirs [Para 71][Para 73][Para 77][Para 82][Para 99][Para 106].

**Employer (respondent):** basic pay of USD 128 plus a role allowance of USD 20 to 25 already complies with the law and no agreement provides for consolidation; it cannot add allowances with no legal or contractual basis; it cannot dismiss staff on a worker's demand without evidence, and it has its own code of ethics and will instruct managers on language; it did not create the rival union [Para 74][Para 85][Para 92][Para 96][Para 102][Para 110][Para 239].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — on the ten remaining points the panel refused every claim, and it recorded the 12 points the parties had settled; no sum was awarded [Para 249][Para 250][Para 251][Para 257][Para 258]

On point 1 the panel applied Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 664 of the Civil Code: a labour contract is formed by offer and acceptance, and neither party nor the panel may impose terms the other has not accepted [Para 114][Para 116][Para 119][Para 123][Para 129][Para 130]. Both sides agreed that no agreement consolidated the role allowance, and the contract provides basic pay of USD 128 plus a role allowance of USD 20 to 25, so the claim failed [Para 75][Para 131][Para 132][Para 135][Para 136].

On point 2 the panel held that a claim that no worker be dismissed arbitrarily, with no identified worker, is a claim about a dispute that has not yet arisen, and that the panel exists to resolve existing labour disputes rather than hypothetical ones; it declined to consider the claim [Para 137][Para 138][Para 139][Para 141]. The demand for automatic renewal of fixed-duration contracts failed for the same reason as point 1 — renewal would alter terms the employer had not accepted [Para 160][Para 161].

On the allowances in point 3 and, in the same terms, points 5, 15 and 16, the panel found no law, collective agreement, contract, internal rule or past practice creating the obligation [Para 164][Para 180][Para 187][Para 194], and classified each as an interest dispute [Para 165][Para 181][Para 188][Para 195]. It then applied Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305: an interest dispute leading to a collective agreement, and hence to an award replacing it under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099, may be brought only by a union with most-representative status at the enterprise [Para 166][Para 168][Para 169][Para 170][Para 171][Para 172]. The Khmer Free Workers' Union had no such certificate [Para 174][Para 175], so the panel refused to consider these claims [Para 176][Para 184][Para 191][Para 198].

On points 17 and 18 the panel held that only the parties to a contract may dissolve it, and a third party — including the workers — cannot compel the employer to dismiss the administrative manager or a fellow worker [Para 201][Para 203][Para 204][Para 225][Para 226]. Earlier awards allowed such a demand only where the worker proves the person is dangerous and cannot be kept without endangering others [Para 208][Para 227]; no such evidence was produced about either man [Para 213][Para 218][Para 233][Para 236], and the employer's undertaking to instruct its managers under its code of ethics was a reasonable and acceptable response [Para 214][Para 215].

On point 23 the panel applied Article 266(4), which forbids a union or association combining employers and workers, and Article 280, which forbids employer interference aimed at placing a workers' organisation under the employer's control [Para 240][Para 241][Para 242][Para 243]. The union alleging such a body bears the burden of proof [Para 244]; no specific evidence showed the employer created or controlled the rival union, so the claim was refused [Para 247][Para 248].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The panel cannot rewrite a contract: a term the employer has not accepted — a consolidated base wage, an automatic renewal, a new allowance — will not be ordered, and a claim to restrain a dismissal that has not yet occurred or that concerns no identified worker is a hypothetical dispute the panel will not hear. Claims for new benefits are interest disputes that only a union holding most-representative status at the enterprise may bring, and a workers' demand that the employer dismiss a manager or a fellow worker fails because only the contracting parties may dissolve a contract, unless the claimant proves the person is dangerous to others. An allegation that the employer created or controls a rival union must be proved.

**Obiter dicta:** the panel accepted the employer's commitment to instruct its administrative staff to speak to workers appropriately under its internal code of ethics, treating that as a reasonable response even though the dismissal claim failed [Para 214][Para 215].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award's face gives the company as "Kon Thek (Cambodia) Hop Operation Ltd" while the body of the text runs the name as "Korn Thek", "Kon Thek" and "Koun Thek" interchangeably [Para 1][Para 63][Para 64]. The union is named "Khmer Free Workers' Union" but its registration letter bears a different federation title in one place [Para 48]. The award date is 21 December 2015 [Para 1] and the extension clause reads "from 07 December 2 2015 to 21 December 2015" [Para 40]. The claim list is printed twice with different wording, and issue 11 is recorded as withdrawn although the numbering still runs to 23 [Para 36][Para 37]. Prakas No. 099 is cited as "21 April 2023 2004" [Para 170]. Individual names of the manager and workers are printed unredacted and are cited here as Mr I and Mr Y; several passages of the reasoning are duplicated almost verbatim across points 4, 5, 15 and 16 [Para 164][Para 180][Para 187][Para 194], and the signature block garbles the president's name as "Kmuoy Nam" against "Kuy Nam" on the face of the award [Para 1][Para 261].
