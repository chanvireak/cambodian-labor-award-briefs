# Award No. 016/15 — San Dong Furniture & Wood (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 13 February 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Hem Hour Narith (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Sokh Matheung (chair, appointed by the two members) · Doc ID 139128

## 1. Facts
The company makes boards and plywood and employs about 120 workers at the Sihanoukville special economic zone. [Para 30]
A strike led by the Khmer Workers' Movement Union Federation raised eleven demands; seven were settled in conciliation and four remained, one of which (union dues) the workers withdrew at the hearing, leaving three. [Para 11][Para 19]
Only two workers attended the hearing, together with one federation officer; neither produced a written authority transferring the claimants' rights to the federation. [Para 31][Para 34]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must raise the piece rate for board production by 10 cents, equal to 400 riel, per square metre? [Para 35][Para 37]
2. Whether the company must reduce the regular attendance bonus pro rata to the number of days of personal leave taken? [Para 43][Para 48]
3. Whether the company must maintain full wages and other benefits for workers absent on properly certified sick leave? [Para 54][Para 56]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Board production is heavy, strength-based and technical work, so the rate should rise by 10 cents a square metre; and because attendance is demanding, cutting the whole $20 attendance bonus for a single day's personal leave is excessive and no agreement permits it. [Para 38][Para 49]
**Employer (respondent):** It already added 3 cents, about 120 riel, per square metre so that workers reach the $128 minimum wage, and 80% of workers accepted this; current rates are 30 to 38 cents depending on board type, and with overtime workers earn $300–400 a month. The bonus is a discretionary scheme paying $20 for 26 days' attendance, applied since 2011. [Para 39][Para 52]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted. The company was ordered to maintain full wages for the first month of certified sick leave and 60% for the second and third months; its refusal to preserve the attendance bonus during certified sick leave was upheld, and the piece-rate and bonus-deduction claims were refused consideration. [Para 144][Para 148]
On the piece rate, the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rule, agreement or past practice obliging the company to pay 400 riel a square metre, so the claim was an interest dispute. Because the claimants were two individual workers and not the most representative union, the panel declined to consider it. [Para 65][Para 80]
The attendance-bonus claim was refused on the same basis, and also failed on the merits: Notice No. 230 of 2012, which guarantees a minimum $10 attendance bonus, applies only to textiles, garment and footwear enterprises, and this company makes boards and plywood. The $20 bonus was therefore a benefit above the statutory floor, which an employer may set unilaterally to encourage attendance, and withholding it for more than four hours' absence or more than two days' sick leave was a lawful and reasonable exercise of management right under Article 2. [Para 82][Para 100][Para 104]
On sick leave the panel applied Articles 71(3) and 72(1), under which certified illness suspends the contract and releases both parties from work and wage, unless a contrary rule provides otherwise. The company's internal rule 04(3.5) of 30 October 2014 does provide otherwise — full pay for the first month, 60% for months two and three — and the panel departed from its earlier award 171/13, which had confined that clause to long-term illness, on the ground that the clause is unqualified. Because the workers did not specify the length of their absences, the panel interpreted the clause generally: any certified sick leave within the first month attracts full pay, and beyond one month 60%. [Para 108][Para 127][Para 132]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer's internal rules promise full wages for the first month of certified sick leave and 60% for the following two months without limiting that promise to long-term illness, the rule binds the employer and displaces the default under Articles 71(3) and 72(1) that suspension of the contract relieves the employer of paying wages. [Para 135][Para 136]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that an employer may unilaterally design bonus schemes above the statutory minimum to encourage attendance, and that Notice No. 230's $10 attendance-bonus floor reaches only textile, garment and footwear enterprises. [Para 100]
