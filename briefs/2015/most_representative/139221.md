# Award No. 141/15 — Cambodian Huy Fu Garment & Nithing Factory Kompeny Ltd (Arbitration Council, 2 July 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed, dissenting; An Non, worker-appointed; Kong Phalha, chair) · Doc ID 139221

## 1. Facts
About 280 workers struck at the respondent's garment factory in June 2015 and 19 demands went to conciliation; 8 were settled on 11 June 2015, leaving 11, which reached the Council by report No. 607 of 12 June 2015. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 48] At the hearing of 23 June 2015 the parties narrowed the unresolved points to four — demand points 4, 6, 13 and 16 — and the workers then withdrew point 13, leaving three for decision. [Para 28][Para 36][Para 37][Para 38] The claimant was the Federation of Trade Unions of Workers (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត), which held a transfer of rights from 189 of the workers; the factory-level union had not yet received its registration certificate. [Para 49][Para 51][Para 52]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must calculate overtime pay on a base that includes the seniority bonus and the position allowance, and not on the base wage alone? [Para 4][Para 53]
2. Whether the employer must pay 3,000 riel per day as midday meal money? [Para 8][Para 57]
3. Whether the employer must pay the attendance bonus (ប្រាក់រង្វាន់ធ្វើការទៀងទាត់) at 15 USD per month instead of the 10 USD it currently pays? [Para 16][Para 60]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer takes only the base wage of 128 USD as the overtime base and includes neither the seniority bonus nor a position allowance, although in practice the workers receive no position allowance and only the seniority bonus; that bonus should form the base because it grows with service, experience, effort and loyalty. On the meal money they pointed to neighbouring factories paying 2,000 riel, and on the attendance bonus to the rise in transport costs and the distance of their housing from the factory. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 55][Para 58][Para 61]
**Employer (respondent):** using only the base wage for overtime is what the law requires and is its current practice; it cannot pay the meal money or the higher attendance bonus because it is facing an economic crisis; it already pays the 10 USD attendance bonus that the parties agree is the practice. [Para 56][Para 59][Para 60][Para 62]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — on demand point 4 the panel ordered the employer to include the seniority bonus as a base for computing overtime pay; on points 6 and 16 it refused to consider the claims. [Para 104][Para 105][Para 106]

On point 4 the panel began with Article 139 of the Labour Law, as amended on 20 July 2007, which requires overtime at a 50% premium by day and 100% at night or on the weekly rest day, and Article 5 of Prakas No. 80 of 1 March 1999, which sets 150% and 200% of the "wage during normal working time". [Para 63][Para 64][Para 66][Para 67] Neither text says whether the calculation base is the base (minimum) wage alone, and neither defines "wage during normal working time". [Para 65][Para 68][Para 69] Turning to Article 102, the panel read "wage" as the remuneration for work actually performed, and read Article 103 as listing items of monthly income more broadly, including bonuses and allowances; it therefore held that "wage during normal working time" is the Article 102 wage, that is the remuneration for the work done, excluding benefits. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 73][Para 74][Para 75]

The seniority bonus, however, was held to be different in kind: under point 3 of Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 it is granted to workers with more than one year's service on a rising scale, and the panel found it attached to length of service, accumulated experience, effort and loyalty — following the reasoning of Award No. 255/13 Pou Yuen on issue 1. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 78] It accordingly ordered the bonus to be included in the base for overtime. [Para 79][Para 104] The employer-appointed arbitrator, Ing Sothy, recorded a dissent in the annex: the base wage exists to guarantee the legal minimum and to serve as the base for other payments, whereas the seniority bonus is an incentive for long service, and no legal provision supports adding it to the overtime base; the majority, he said, proceeded on equity, which he would apply symmetrically to deductions. [Para 112][Para 113][Para 114][Para 115][Para 116]

On points 6 and 16 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, agreement or past practice requiring 3,000 riel meal money or a 15 USD attendance bonus, and characterised both as interest disputes (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍). [Para 82][Para 83][Para 98][Para 99] Applying Article 96 of the Labour Law, Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 — under which an interest award replaces the collective agreement for one year and removes the right to strike — it held that only a most representative union, or unions together with more than half the workforce, may bring such a claim, following Awards No. 81/04 Evergreen, No. 98/04 Great Union and No. 169/11 Fortune Tea. [Para 84][Para 87][Para 90][Para 91][Para 92][Para 93] The claimant was not the most representative union, so it declined to consider both claims. [Para 94][Para 95][Para 101][Para 102]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The seniority bonus granted by Notification No. 041/11 is remuneration attached to length of service, experience and loyalty, and so forms part of the "wage during normal working time" on which the overtime premium is computed, even though the general benefits excluded by Article 102 do not. A demand for a meal allowance or a higher attendance bonus that no law, agreement, internal rule or practice secures is an interest dispute, which the panel will not consider unless it is brought by the most representative union or by unions representing more than half the workforce. [Para 75][Para 78][Para 92][Para 95]

**Obiter dicta:** the dissenting arbitrator's view that the majority's inclusion of the seniority bonus rests on equity rather than law, and that equity would equally require the same base when wages are deducted for absence; and the panel's remark that a factory-level union awaiting registration can still litigate through a federation holding transfers of rights from 189 workers. [Para 49][Para 115][Para 116]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The demand numbering is printed two ways: the recitals number the eleven unresolved demands 1 to 11 and label each with the original demand point in brackets (3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 14, 15, 16, 19, 13, 11), while the reasoning and the operative order use the original points 4, 6 and 16; the brief follows the panel's decision numbering. [Para 2][Para 18][Para 38][Para 104] The number of unresolved points does not reconcile: paragraph 30 says 19 points with 8 settled and 11 unresolved, paragraph 36 says only four remained (4, 6, 13, 16), and the withdrawal of point 13 is recorded at paragraph 37. [Para 30][Para 36][Para 37] Paragraph 32 prints the panel's constitution date as "15 January 2021 2015". [Para 32] The reasoning at paragraphs 73 to 75 first excludes benefits from the overtime base and then includes the seniority bonus in it; the two passages are reproduced as printed and the order at paragraph 104 follows the second. [Para 75][Para 79][Para 104] The signature block is heavily garbled ("បច្ចេកទេស", "1945222", "2017202"), and the award date appears only in the closing line, not the header line as usual. [Para 109][Para 110][Para 117] The company name is transliterated inconsistently across the award and its conciliation report. Individual workers and union officials are referred to by initial.
