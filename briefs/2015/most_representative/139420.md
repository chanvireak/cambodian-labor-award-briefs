# Award No. 227/15 — Vanna Momi (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 11 September 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborna for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Ing Eng Thong chair) · Doc ID 139420

## 1. Facts
Vanna Momi (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd, a garment factory in Bati district, Takeo province, employed about 700 workers. [Para 26] Two federations — the Cambodian Economic Workers Union Federation and the Women's Labour Union Confederation of Cambodia — received transfer of rights from 30 workers, and a further 8 workers attended the hearing without transferring their rights. [Para 27][Para 30][Para 32] Conciliation at the Takeo provincial Department of Labour on 14 August 2015 settled 4 of 6 claims; the 2 unresolved claims were sent to the Secretariat on 20 August 2015, and the panel was constituted on 24 August 2015. [Para 6][Para 9][Para 10][Para 32]

## 2. Issues in dispute
The panel kept the numbering of the non-conciliation report, so the two remaining claims are its issues 2 and 3. [Para 33][Para 39]
1. Whether the employer must add 1,000 riel a day to the 1,000 riel lunch allowance already paid, bringing the total to 2,000 riel a day? [Para 33][Para 44]
2. Whether the employer must pay a 2,000 riel meal allowance for overtime to workers who work on public holidays and Sundays? [Para 39][Para 78]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer promised orally in April 2015, and again at the conciliation of 14 August 2015, that it would consider adding 1,000 riel after three months; work performed on a public holiday is overtime and should therefore carry the 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance, which the employer already pays for Sunday work. [Para 34][Para 41][Para 39][Para 45]
**Employer (respondent):** the company has no orders because buyers withdrew them after the August 2015 strike, so it cannot pay; it will consider the 2,000 riel only if it has sufficient orders over six months, and it does not require workers to work overtime on public holidays, for which it already pays twice the wage plus the ordinary wage. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 42]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider both claims: the additional 1,000 riel lunch allowance and the 2,000 riel holiday overtime meal allowance. The award is non-binding and becomes final 8 days after notification if no party objects in writing to the Minister of Labour through the Secretariat. [Para 100][Para 101][Para 102]

On issue 2 the panel applied Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code: a contract is formed by matching offer and acceptance, and no party can impose a contract on another that it has not agreed to. [Para 46][Para 51][Para 53] The record showed the workers only put a proposal — two months — which the employer refused, asking instead to discuss again after three months; the panel therefore found no agreement on the extra 1,000 riel and no obligation to pay it. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58] The claim of an oral promise in April 2015 was supported by nothing beyond argument, so the workers did not discharge the burden of proof, which the panel has consistently placed on the party making the allegation. [Para 60][Para 62][Para 63][Para 64] Independently, no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, employment contract, agreement or past practice requires the addition, and the existing 1,000 riel lunch allowance is already more favourable than the law. [Para 65][Para 66] The panel then classified the claim as an interest dispute and held it inadmissible: under Article 96 of the Labour Law, Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099, an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement and binds all workers for one year, so only a most-representative union — or several unions together with more than half the workforce — may bring such a claim, and these 38 workers were not such a union. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 70][Para 72][Para 75][Para 76]

On issue 3 the panel held that holiday work is not overtime: Article 137 caps normal hours at 8 a day and 48 a week, Article 164 gives workers who work on a public holiday an additional allowance set by ministerial Prakas, and Articles 2 and 4 of Prakas No. 10 of 4 February 1999 fix that allowance at one day's normal wage. [Para 79][Para 82][Para 84][Para 86][Para 87] Following earlier awards, the panel held that holiday work is paid at a different rate from overtime and does not generate overtime entitlement, so no 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance is owed. [Para 88][Para 89][Para 92] No law, collective agreement or practice provides for that allowance, and the claim exceeded the statutory floor, making it an interest dispute inadmissible for the same representative reason as issue 2. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 95][Para 98][Para 99]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer's statement that it will "consider" a benefit at a later date, which the workers' side does not accept as offered, is not an offer accepted so as to form a contract under Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code; absent any legal source or binding agreement, and where the existing benefit already exceeds the statutory minimum, the panel will not order an increase. Work on a public holiday is compensated under Article 164 and Prakas No. 10 by an amount equal to one day's normal wage, not as overtime, and so carries no overtime meal allowance. A claim for a benefit above the statutory floor is an interest dispute that only a most-representative union, or unions together representing more than half the workforce, may bring. [Para 53][Para 58][Para 66][Para 72][Para 86][Para 92]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that it would apply any legal rule obliging employers to raise the lunch allowance (the employer had offered to comply with such a rule), and its refusal to weigh the employer's order book, were not necessary to the outcome. [Para 37][Para 36]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The year is doubled at [Para 11] ("២០ ២០១៥"), and [Para 12] is a bare fragment ("២០១៥ ។") with no content.
- Prakas No. 099 is dated "21 April 2019 2004" at [Para 70]; the header at [Para 4] gives 21 April 2004.
- A Latin string, "Xammassuan", interrupts the reasons at [Para 59] and carries no meaning.
- [Para 74] and [Para 91] repeat clauses ("the Arbitration Council ... agrees with the interpretation of the Arbitration Council") leaving the sentence incoherent.
- Issue numbering begins at 2 because 4 of the 6 claims were settled at conciliation; the award gives no issue 1. [Para 9][Para 33]
- Stray digits appear in amounts at [Para 37] ("4 1000 riel") and [Para 40] ("1 2000 riel"); the operative figures are 1,000 and 2,000 riel.
- Worker numbers are printed as 30 transferring rights and 8 not [Para 27][Para 30], while the claimant group is later called 38 [Para 75]; 30 plus 8 reconciles to 38 only if the eight are added.
