# Award No. 009/15 — Kamchay Mear Trading (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 02 February 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 139122 · Khmer original

## 1. Facts
Kamchay Mear Trading (Cambodia) Co., Ltd weaves and sews garments on National Road 8 at Kamchay Mear district, Prey Veng, employing approximately 2,500 workers. [Para 1][Para 33] Two federations filed: NIFTUC with four demands and the Workers' Union Federation (WUF) with twenty; conciliation on 24 December 2014 left nine unresolved, and further conciliation at the hearing of 20 January 2015 settled six, leaving three WUF demands. [Para 2][Para 17][Para 22][Para 23] WUF appeared at the hearing holding transfer-of-rights letters from 286 workers, identified only by employee number, dated 20 November 2014. [Para 27][Para 34][Para 52] The parties chose a non-binding award. [Para 24]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the overtime meal allowance once a week, every Saturday? [Para 5][Para 55]
3. Whether the employer must provide one midday meal or 2,000 riel per day? [Para 7][Para 39]
4. Whether the employer must calculate overtime wages including the function allowance, incentive bonus, responsibility allowance and seniority bonus? [Para 8][Para 43]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the current practice pays the overtime meal allowance only once a month together with the monthly wage, while workers need cash day to day and have endured that timing for a long time; other companies give a midday meal or 2,000 riel a day; and the incentive bonus is a reward for the team leader's own skill, so a skilled team leader is more productive in overtime and the bonus should form part of the overtime base. [Para 37][Para 41][Para 46]
**Employer (respondent):** it keeps paying the meal allowance monthly because it lacks time to compute separate meal and wage figures; it cannot provide midday meals and no law requires it; and no law requires folding the incentive bonus into the overtime base — the bonus runs from USD 2 to USD 60 a month, varies with quality and the company's performance, and is not a skill allowance. [Para 38][Para 42][Para 47][Para 49]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay the overtime meal allowance once a week, every Saturday, to the workers; it declined to consider the midday meal demand and rejected the demand to fold the incentive bonus into the overtime base. No money sum was fixed. [Para 74][Para 96][Para 122][Para 123][Para 124][Para 125]

On issue 1 the panel applied point 2 of Ministry of Labour Notification No. 041/11 of 07 March 2011: a worker who voluntarily works overtime at the employer's request must receive 2,000 riel a day for meals or one free meal, without any limit on the number of overtime hours. [Para 58][Para 59] The notification does not fix when the money must be paid. [Para 60] The panel reasoned that the meal exists to give the worker strength to work further overtime, food being an unavoidable daily need the employer must provide, and that in default of a meal the 2,000 riel substitutes for it. [Para 61][Para 62] Payment must therefore fall in a period that actually lets the worker buy food for the overtime days, whether daily, weekly or monthly. [Para 63] Noting its earlier awards that a free meal must be given on each day of overtime, it held weekly payment reasonable and necessary, following Awards No. 76/11, No. 146/12 Wing Star Shoe and No. 244/12. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 69][Para 70]

On issue 3 the panel first classified the demand as an interest dispute, since it exceeds what the Labour Law requires. [Para 77][Para 78] Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 give the most representative union the right to request a collective agreement, and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 makes an award on an interest dispute stand in place of a collective agreement for one year. [Para 79][Para 82][Para 85][Para 86] Because such an award binds all workers and removes their right to strike over the same interest demands, the panel will decide an interest dispute only where the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise, or several unions together hold more than half the workforce — Awards No. 81/04, No. 98/04 Great Union, No. 169/11, No. 228/13 and No. 172/14. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 89][Para 92] The claimant represented 286 of about 2,500 workers, so it was not most representative and lacked standing; the panel refused to consider that demand. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 95][Para 96]

On issue 4 the panel read Article 139 (new) and Article 5 of Prakas No. 80 of 01 March 1999 as leaving "wage during normal working time" undefined, then construed it through Articles 102 and 103 of the Labour Law: the narrow wage is pay for work done, while the broad wage covers all monthly income including benefits. [Para 98][Para 101][Para 103][Para 106][Para 107][Para 108][Para 109] It held the base for overtime to be the narrow wage, so the base wage must be combined with the seniority bonus, which the law requires after one year, but not with a skill or responsibility allowance the employer does not grant, nor with the incentive bonus, which varies monthly and rewards the team leader rather than paying for work performed. [Para 111][Para 112][Para 116][Para 118][Para 120][Para 121]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A panel will not decide an interest dispute brought by a union that is not the most representative in the enterprise — or by several unions holding more than half the workers — because an award on interests replaces a collective agreement for a year and strips all workers of the right to strike over those demands, so Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 and the most-representative rules in Article 96 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 305 are jurisdictional. On the merits, "wage during normal working time" under Article 102 is the narrow wage for work done: the overtime base includes the base wage and the statutory seniority bonus, but not a discretionary incentive or responsibility allowance. [Para 87][Para 108][Para 111][Para 118]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that the overtime meal allowance may be paid daily, weekly or monthly so long as the worker can buy food, and that differing companies grant skill allowances on differing policies, were explanatory and not part of the order. [Para 63][Para 115]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The workforce figure is corrupted where it matters: the claimant's 286 workers are measured against "ប្រហែល ១០ ២៥០០ នាក់" [Para 93], which reads as 10,2500 and is elsewhere printed as approximately 2,500 [Para 33].
- The same claimant body is counted as "ចំនួន ២ ២៨៦ នាក់" [Para 53] and as 286 at [Para 34] and [Para 52].
- The company name is printed only in Khmer and is transliterated here; it also appears corruptly as កំចាយ មាវ មក ខឹង at [Para 28].
- Prior-award citations are badly eroded, e.g. "លេខ ៧៦|១១ ហ្យុង ថិច" [Para 69], "លេខ ២៤៤ ១២ខម ក្លីត អន នើរ" [Para 69] and "លេខ ១៦៩/១១" [Para 90], so several case names cannot be recovered reliably.
- Article 139 is cited as "មាត្រា ១៣៩ ថ្មី នៃ ច្បាប់ ស្តីពី វិសោធនកម្ម ... ចុះ ថ្ងៃទី ២០ ខែកក្កដា ឆ្នាំ ២០១៩ ២០០៧" [Para 98], carrying two conflicting years; the quotation also breaks off mid-sentence at [Para 99] and [Para 100].
- Duplicated and broken sentences recur in the reasoning, e.g. "ក្រុមប្រឹក្សា អាជ្ញា កណ្តាល កត់សម្គាល់ ឃើញ ថា" twice at [Para 114] and the stray "រូប -១៣-" at [Para 122].
