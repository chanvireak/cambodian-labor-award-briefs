# Award No. 291/15 — T & K Garmen Industry Co., Ltd. ("T & K 2") (Arbitration Council, 28 December 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bunchea presiding; Ing Sothy appointed by the employer, An Lean appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 139467

## 1. Facts
T & K Garmen Industry Co., Ltd., trading as "T & K 2", cuts and sews garments at Damnak Thom, Stung Meanchey, Phnom Penh, and employs about 700 workers. [Para 54] On 1 October 2015, 75 workers transferred their rights to the Federation of Trade Unions, which filed ten demands; conciliation on 6 November 2015 failed on all ten, and report No. 1411 reached the secretariat on 13 November 2015. [Para 55][Para 20][Para 16] At the hearing of 1 December 2015 the parties settled demands 6, 7 and 10, leaving seven demands for the panel. [Para 18][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise wages by 30% for its 13 team leaders? [Para 60][Para 116]
2. Whether the employer may reduce the USD 18 monthly attendance bonus in proportion to the days of leave it has authorised, instead of forfeiting the whole bonus? [Para 131][Para 69]
3. Whether the daily wage used to pay annual leave must be the average of total wages over the last twelve months? [Para 174]
4. Whether the 5% severance must be paid within 48 hours of termination? [Para 186]
5. Whether wages and benefits must be maintained for sick leave certified by a clinic registered with the Ministry of Health? [Para 201]
8. Whether pregnant workers must be allowed one day a month for antenatal checks without loss of pay? [Para 208]
9. Whether piece-rate ("ឡូត៍") prices must be raised so that even the least productive worker earns the minimum wage? [Para 215]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** team leaders carry more responsibility on fixed pay while production workers can earn more by output, so 30% is due [Para 63]; a single day's absence forfeits the entire bonus [Para 70]; severance should be immediate because workers live far from the factory [Para 80]; piece-rate pay should never fall below the minimum wage [Para 106]; and leave pay should be averaged over twelve months [Para 76].
**Employer (respondent):** it cannot afford 30% with the minimum wage increase due in early 2016 [Para 64]; the bonus cut follows a 30 October 2012 agreement with the solidarity union [Para 72]; leave pay is based on the base wage and severance is paid on the 18th of each month, as since 2011 [Para 75][Para 82]; it already accepts certificates from Ministry-registered private clinics [Para 88][Para 202]; a nursery exists and two hours a month are given for antenatal checks [Para 95][Para 209].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must reduce the attendance bonus only in proportion to authorised leave days, compute annual-leave pay on the twelve-month average of total wages, and pay the 5% severance within 48 hours of termination; the wage-increase, antenatal-day and piece-rate claims are dismissed and the sick-leave claim is not considered because the employer already complies. [Para 224][Para 225][Para 226][Para 207]
Demands 1 and 8 were treated as interest disputes. Reading Article 96 of the Labour Law with Prakas No. 099 (21 April 2004), Article 43, the panel held that an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement for a year and removes the right to strike, so it will hear one only from the most representative union, meaning one with more than half the workers as members; the claimant was 75 individual workers, not a union. [Para 118][Para 124][Para 126][Para 128] On demand 1 it also found no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice creating a 30% duty [Para 116]; on demand 8 it found the existing two hours a month more favourable than the law [Para 211].
On demand 2, Notification No. 230 K.B/S.J.N of 25 July 2012 fixes the attendance bonus at USD 10 a month for workers with no absence, and the parties agreed the company pays USD 18. [Para 137][Para 65] Reading that notification with Article 103 (a bonus is a wage component) and Articles 71(6) and 72 (authorised absence suspends the contract, so no wage is owed for those days), the panel held the employer may cut the bonus only in proportion to the days absent, not forfeit it. [Para 165][Para 168][Para 171][Para 173] It followed awards 114/11, 136/11 and 158/11 [Para 136].
On demand 3, Article 168 requires the average of wages, bonuses, allowances and benefits over the twelve months before leave; using the base wage did not satisfy that, so the panel ordered the twelve-month average, following award 25/08-SD [Para 176][Para 179][Para 183][Para 185].
On demand 4, Article 73(6) requires severance at termination and Article 116 requires all wages and allowances within 48 hours; two terminated workers were paid about 72 hours after leaving, so the practice breached the law [Para 188][Para 190][Para 198].
On demand 9, Article 2 of Prakas No. 283 of 12 November 2014 already requires a top-up to USD 123 for probationary and USD 128 for full-rights workers where piece-rate output falls short, which the employer does; the claim was also too vague to decide [Para 215][Para 217][Para 220][Para 221].
The employer-appointed arbitrator, Ing Sothy, dissented on demand 2, arguing that a conditional bonus is not wage and cannot be apportioned. [Para 231][Para 238]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus fixed by ministerial notification is a wage component: where the worker is absent on leave the employer itself authorised, the employer may reduce the bonus only in proportion to the days absent and may not forfeit it in full. [Para 165][Para 173] A panel will entertain an interest dispute — a demand for a benefit not yet owed — only where the most representative union in the establishment brings it. [Para 126][Para 128]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that no competent body has yet interpreted the words "without absence" in Notification No. 230, so it decides such claims case by case on the facts [Para 143][Para 144]; and it observed that the employer's two hours a month for antenatal checks is more favourable than the law [Para 211].

## 6. Source defects disclosed

[Para 22] The commercial registration certificate is No. 2561 of 10 May 2009 at para 30 but "Inv. 1461 E/2009" of 21 April 2009 at para 53. [Para 30][Para 53] Para 116 states demand 1 as a 300% increase in the question, against 30% elsewhere. [Para 116][Para 60] The panel's own agreement is dated "35 October 2016" at para 72, and "30 October 2012" in the same sentence; 2016 falls after the award. [Para 72] Para 200 prints "2 05 percent" and the order at para 224 inserts a stray "4" into the proportional-deduction order. [Para 200][Para 224] The workers' arbitrator is "An Lean" in the panel list and "Un Non" in the signature block; the employer's arbitrator appears as both "Ing Sothy" and "Oung Sothy". [Para 1][Para 229] The company name varies across the document ("T & K 2", "T & K Garmen Industry", "T & K German In Dasry", "T & K Garmen In Das"); the face of the award is followed above. [Para 30][Para 35][Para 45] Individual workers appear only as partial OCR strings, which are redacted here, and para 101 contains a garbled OCR fragment. [Para 84][Para 101]
