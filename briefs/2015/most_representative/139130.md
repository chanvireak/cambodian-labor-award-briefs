# Award No. 017/15 — Ye Pan Rock S.E.A (Phnom Penh) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 19 February 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Virak (worker-appointed), Tang Tri (president) · Doc ID 139130

## 1. Facts
The respondent is a factory in the Phnom Penh Special Economic Zone, registered on 4 January 2012 and employing about 579 workers. The Federation of Cambodian Workers' Unions lodged 16 demands on 2 December 2014; eight were settled in conciliation, eight remained unresolved, and issue 5 was withdrawn at the hearing, leaving issues 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7 and 8. Only three individual workers signed the letter of authority, and the factory-level union was still unregistered, its application having been filed on 4 December 2014. [Para 22][Para 27][Para 61]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise the attendance bonus from 10 USD to 15 USD per month? [Para 68]
2. Whether the employer must pay an overtime meal allowance of 2,000 riel per hour, that is 4,000 riel per day? [Para 75]
3. Whether the employer must add 10 USD per month to the 15 USD already paid to each team leader? [Para 83]
4. Whether the employer must add 5 USD to the travel and accommodation allowance, bringing it to 15 USD per month? [Para 89]
6. Whether the employer must supply one further set of work clothing? [Para 98]
7. Whether the employer must enlarge the motorbike parking area? [Para 103]
8. Whether the employer must take back Mr L (Lim Kim Hong) into employment? [Para 109]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** other factories, such as Thai G Ving, pay a 20 USD attendance bonus; the 1,500 riel hourly meal allowance is insufficient because workers regularly work at least two hours of overtime; other employers give team leaders 25 USD; travel costs 12 to 13 USD and rent about 35 USD a month; two shirts are not enough in the rainy season. [Para 69][Para 91][Para 100]

**Employer (respondent):** it already pays the 10 USD attendance bonus required by Notification No. 230; 1,500 riel per hour for overtime meals is adequate and Thai G Ving pays only 1,000 riel; other payments to team leaders are the employer's own decision provided they do not fall below the legal minimum; it pays 10 USD plus transport and 5 USD accommodation, exceeding the notification; and the non-renewal of Mr L's fixed-term contract reflected his refusal to work overtime, not discrimination. [Para 70][Para 80]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 6 granted (two sets of work clothing per year ordered); issues 1, 2, 3, 4 and 7 dismissed as interest disputes the claimant was not competent to bring; issue 8 dismissed. [Para 220][Para 224][Para 226]

On issues 1, 3 and 4 the panel held that the employer already met or exceeded the applicable minimum: 10 USD against the 10 USD in point 2 of Notification No. 230, 15 USD against a nil requirement for team leaders, and 10 USD plus transport and accommodation against the 7 USD in point 1, which is not payable at all where the employer provides transport or lodging. Since no law, collective agreement, internal regulation or past practice required more, the claims exceeded the legal minimum and were interest disputes. Applying Article 43 of Prakas No. 099, an award on an interest dispute substitutes for the collective agreement and binds the whole workforce, so only the most representative union — or unions together holding over half the workers — may bring one. Three individual complainants were not such a union. [Para 135][Para 147]

On issue 2 the panel found the employer paid 1,500 riel per hour and that point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 requires 2,000 riel per day or one free meal, so the practice was more favourable than the minimum; the claim for 2,000 riel per hour was again an interest dispute, disposed of on the same jurisdictional ground. [Para 156]

Issue 6 was treated differently because it was a claim of right. Article 6 of Prakas No. 307 requires the employer to provide at least two sets of clothing free of charge each year, the first within 15 days of engagement. Supplying two shirts once, replaceable only when worn out, did not satisfy that annual obligation, and the panel ordered two sets per year. [Para 183][Para 188]

On issue 8 the panel applied Article 73(1) of the Labour Law: a fixed-duration contract terminates automatically on its expiry date. Mr L's last contract ran from 1 June to 30 November 2014; because 30 November was a Sunday the panel held the contract ended on 29 November 2014. Neither party can compel the other to continue a contract absent agreement, as Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the 2007 Civil Code confirm, so the claim for reinstatement failed. [Para 205][Para 218]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for more than a minimum fixed by notification or prakas is an interest dispute that only the most representative union in the enterprise may bring, because the resulting award displaces the collective agreement for the whole workforce; a demand for the minimum itself is a claim of right that individual workers may bring and that the panel will enforce. A fixed-duration contract ends by operation of law on its term, and no reinstatement order can outlast it without fresh agreement. [Para 147][Para 188]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed more broadly, drawing on the Civil Code, that neither party to any contract may be forced into terms to which it did not consent, an observation wider than the Labour Law ground that decided the case. [Para 208]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award states at one point that 1,500 riel per hour "equals 4,000 riel per day" for workers doing at least two hours of overtime, while elsewhere it records that two hours of overtime earns 3,000 riel per day; 1,500 × 2 is 3,000 riel. The face of the award names Tang Tri as president of the panel, but the signature block gives the presiding arbitrator's name as "Avuth", which appears to be an OCR corruption rather than a second person. Stray year stamps ("2017", "2016") appear at the foot of the document and inside a Civil Code quotation; the face and the reasons both date the award 19 February 2015. [Para 155][Para 228]
