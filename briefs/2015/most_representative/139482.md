# Award No. 305/15 — C.W.K.H. Garment (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 24 December 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothi (employer-appointed), An Virak (worker-appointed), Kong Phalla (president) · Doc ID 139482

## 1. Facts
The Federation of Unions for Workers' Rights brought seven unresolved demands for the workers of C.W.K.H. Garment (Cambodia) Ltd, a garment factory of about 448 employees, after a strike: a higher midday meal allowance, employment certificates, a day-care centre, an evening meal allowance, an end to abusive language by supervisors, longer fixed-term contracts, and the reinstatement of two dismissed union leaders. [Para 2][Para 85][Para 8] Conciliation settled three items and the workers withdrew a fourth, leaving the meal allowance, the contract term and the reinstatement claims. [Para 19] At the hearing the panel held the plant-level union was unregistered, that the 191 thumbprints collected were consent to strike rather than an authority to represent, and that only the ten workers present were claimants. [Para 145][Para 151][Para 152][Para 158] The two leaders had been told on 30 April 2015 that their contracts would not be renewed, then re-signed three-month contracts on 4 May 2015, which expired on 31 October 2015. [Para 221][Para 222]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise the midday meal allowance from 1,000 to 2,500 riel per day for all workers? [Para 159]
6. Whether the employer must use fixed-term contracts of six to twelve months instead of three months? [Para 177]
7. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr D and Ms S to their former posts? [Para 205]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** 1,000 riel a day is too little to buy food and no agreement or internal rule fixes it at that figure; three-month contracts leave workers without security and are easy to end; the two leaders were dismissed without cause and because of their union roles. [Para 96][Para 104][Para 123][Para 124]**Employer (respondent):** It cannot afford more because the previous year's strike lost it orders and it has just raised the minimum wage; contract length depends on orders, and good workers are not dismissed; the two were not dismissed for union reasons — the employer never received any union notice — but because they were high-paid staff it could no longer pay in a downturn. [Para 98][Para 100][Para 105][Para 126][Para 128]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted on liability, refused on relief — the meal-allowance and longer-contract claims were refused, and the reinstatement claim was refused; but the panel found the two leaders' dismissals unlawful and set out the compensation owed (notice pay, dismissal indemnity, unused annual leave, moral damages and final wages) without quantifying it in the order. [Para 313][Para 314][Para 315][Para 316]

On the meal allowance the panel found no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice obliging the employer to pay 2,500 riel, so the demand was a dispute over interests. [Para 161][Para 162] Because the claimant was not the most representative union — Article 96 and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 make an award on interests a collective agreement binding all workers for a year — the panel declined to consider it at all. [Para 169][Para 171][Para 174][Para 176]

On the contract term the panel applied Article 65 and Civil Code Articles 311, 336 and 337: contract form rests on matching offer and acceptance, and neither party may impose terms the other rejects. [Para 177][Para 182][Para 185] The workers' request was lawful and so was the employer's refusal, so the claim failed. [Para 198][Para 199][Para 201]

On reinstatement the panel first classified the contracts: under Article 67(2) aggregate fixed-term service beyond two years converts to indefinite duration, and Mr D had about 2 years 7 months and Ms S about 2 years 6 months. [Para 206][Para 218][Para 228][Para 229] The 30 April 2015 non-renewal notices followed by fresh three-month contracts showed no genuine intention to end the relationship, and continuing fixed terms past two years was contrary to the spirit of the law. [Para 225][Para 226][Para 227] Union discrimination was not proved: the accusing party bears the burden and no evidence or witness supported it. [Para 238][Para 242][Para 244] The Prakas No. 305 protection also failed, because its third condition — notifying the employer of the candidates — was unmet: the hand-delivered letter was denied by the administrative officer and the postal copy never arrived, the union having given a wrong address. [Para 253][Para 265][Para 269][Para 273] Under Article 74 a dismissal requires a valid reason going to aptitude, conduct or the needs of the enterprise; the employer's claim of falling orders was unsupported, and only four workers were terminated that October, two of them the leaders, while twenty left voluntarily. [Para 276][Para 280][Para 282][Para 284] The dismissal was therefore unjustified, and the panel set out the heads of compensation under Articles 77, 89, 166, 91 and 116, computing average wages over the last twelve months. [Para 286][Para 292][Para 302][Para 306][Para 310]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer serves a non-renewal notice and then immediately re-engages the same worker on a further fixed-term contract, the notice does not break continuity: the aggregate service is counted, and once it passes two years the contract is of indefinite duration, so a later termination must rest on a valid Article 74 reason or attract the statutory compensation. [Para 225][Para 227][Para 229]

**Obiter dicta:** The Prakas No. 305 protection for union leadership candidates is lost if the union does not actually bring the candidacy to the employer's knowledge, and a mis-addressed postal notice does not cure that failure. [Para 269][Para 272] A demand that exceeds any legal or agreed entitlement is a dispute over interests, which only the most representative union may bring. [Para 162][Para 171]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The dispositive section contradicts the reasoning: paragraphs 285–311 work out five heads of compensation for unlawful dismissal, yet the order simply refuses the reinstatement claim without directing payment of any sum. [Para 284][Para 313] The employer is named "C.W.K.H. Garment" on the face of the award but appears as "CWKH", "Si Dab Bel Yu Ke Ej" and "Si Dab Bel Yu Ke Ed" in the narrative and evidence lists. [Para 11][Para 30][Para 84] Mr D's start date is printed as 2 May 2013, and in the reasoning as a corrupted string reading "2 May 2022 / 2016 / 2019". [Para 113][Para 220] The conciliation report is dated 17 November 2015 in one place and 11 November 2015 in another. [Para 11][Para 15] The panel's constitution is printed "20 November 2021 2015". [Para 16] The union president's name alternates between "Din Hong" and "On Hong", and the employer's own list of 24 October terminations is contradicted by the finding of four. [Para 108][Para 279][Para 282] The president's signature line carries the name "Kong Phalla" but a garbled glyph in place of a signature. [Para 318]
