# Award No. 167/15 — Ten Tek In Das Steel Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 7 August 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — សេង វូច ហ៊ុន (employer-appointed), លីវ សុវណ្ណា (worker-appointed), ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា (president) · Doc ID 139239

## 1. Facts
The respondent is a garment cutting and sewing enterprise in Kong Pisey district, Kampong Speu, registered in 2014 and employing about 287 workers, of whom 37 had transferred their representation rights to two union federations. [Para 49] Conciliation of 19 demands raised by three federations failed on 2 July 2015 and the file reached the Council on 6 July 2015. [Para 26] The workers struck from 1 to 10 July 2015 without prior notice; the employer withheld wages for the strike days and deducted the whole regular attendance bonus. [Para 74]

## 2. Issues in dispute
Of the 11 claims pressed by the two federations that appeared, points 6–9 were settled at the hearing and point 10 was withdrawn, leaving six: [Para 35]
1. Whether the employer must pay USD 10 per month as transport and accommodation allowance instead of the USD 7 it pays? [Para 87]
2. Whether the employer must pay a regular attendance bonus of USD 12 per month instead of USD 10? [Para 108]
3. Whether the employer must pay 4,000 riel per day as a lunch allowance instead of 2,000 riel? [Para 118]
4. Whether the employer must pay 4,000 riel as a meal allowance to workers doing overtime from 16:00 to 18:00 instead of 2,000 riel? [Para 125]
5. Whether the employer must reduce the regular attendance bonus in proportion to the days of absence authorised by the employer, rather than deducting it in full? [Para 133]
11. Whether the employer must maintain wages and the attendance bonus during the strike of 1 to 10 July 2015? [Para 177]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** they are skilled workers whom other employers paid a USD 15 car allowance, so USD 7 is insufficient; other companies pay USD 18–20 attendance bonus and 4,000 riel lunch money; the attendance bonus is equivalent to monthly wage and must therefore be pro-rated like wages; the strike was not wholly their fault because the employer delayed negotiating their proposals. [Para 54]
**Employer (respondent):** the company had been operating only three months and could not afford the increases; it did not evade negotiation but attended conciliation on 2 July 2015 where the workers were absent; the attendance bonus rewards a full month of regular work, so any absence forfeits it entirely and the deduction was lawful. [Para 79]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — on point 5 the panel ordered the employer to reduce the regular attendance bonus in proportion to the number of days the worker was absent with the employer's lawful authorisation; points 1, 2, 3, 4 and 11 were dismissed. No monetary sum was awarded. [Para 200]

The panel first narrowed the case: the Khmer Workers' Spirit Federation did not appear because no workers still relied on it, so only the 11 points of the two attending federations were considered. [Para 31] On points 1 to 4 it held that each claim sought a benefit above the statutory floor and was therefore an interests dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍) under Article 96 of the Labour Law. [Para 91] Because Article 43(1) of Prakas 099 makes such an award a substitute collective agreement binding for one year and removes the right to strike over interests, the panel would only decide the claim if the claimant were the most representative union, or the unions together represented more than half the workforce; the claimant was not, so it lacked standing. [Para 100]

In the alternative it checked the merits. On point 1 the employer already paid USD 7 per month, as point 1 of Prakas 230 of 25 July 2012 requires, and no law, agreement or past practice fixed USD 10. [Para 87] On point 2 the employer paid the USD 10 minimum set by point 2 of the same Prakas, so it had complied. [Para 111] On point 3 no instrument required 4,000 riel lunch money and practice was 2,000 riel. [Para 119] On point 4, point 2 of Prakas 041/11 of 7 March 2011 requires 2,000 riel or one free meal for voluntary overtime, which the employer paid. [Para 126]

Point 5 succeeded. The panel found the USD 10 attendance bonus is fixed by point 2 of Prakas 230, not a bonus the employer may dispose of at will, and Article 103 lists bonuses among wages, so it is a form of wage. [Para 167] Absence authorised by the employer suspends the contract under Articles 71(6) and 72(1), so no wage and no bonus is earned for those days; the employer may therefore deduct, but only pro rata and not the entire bonus. [Para 174]

Point 11 failed. Article 332(1) suspends the contract on the day of a strike and no wages are payable, and Article 72(1) confines suspension to the principal obligations; following Award No. 27/10 (Hat IN The Bry), wages and bonuses need not be paid whether the strike was lawful or not. [Para 180] Here the strike began without the seven days' notice required by Article 324 and without exhausting conciliation under Article 320(4), so the workers were not in regular attendance and the deduction stood. [Para 195]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim for a benefit above the statutory minimum is an interests dispute, and the panel will decline to decide it unless the union bringing it is the most representative union in the enterprise or the unions together represent more than half the workers. Where, however, a statutory attendance bonus has accrued and the worker is absent with the employer's authorisation, the bonus is a form of wage that may be reduced only in proportion to the days absent, not forfeited in full. [Para 175]

**Obiter dicta:** the 10-dollar attendance bonus in Prakas 230 is a mandatory general norm binding every textile, garment and footwear employer rather than a discretionary incentive, although bonus amounts generally remain the employer's to set. The panel also declined to interpret the words "without absence", holding that each claim must be assessed on its own facts. [Para 145]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel constitution date reads "08 July 2019 2015" at line 28. A stray "2016" intrudes into the figure of 11 issues at line 33, and the strike end date at line 188 reads "19 10 July 2015" against 10 July everywhere else. Line 118 prints the lunch figure as "2 4000 riel". At line 202 the president's name appears twice in different forms — "ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ណាស់" and "ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា". The company's Khmer name is transliterated four ways (ស្ទៀល, ស្ទ្រៀល, ស្រៀល, ស្រៀ), and the demanding federation appears as both ស្មារតី កម្មករ ខ្មែរ and សាមគ្គីភាព ការងារ កម្ពុជា. Individual worker names at lines 52 and 85 are legible only in part.
