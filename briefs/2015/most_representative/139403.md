# Award No. 108/15 — Kon Tek (Cambodia) Knob Precision (Arbitration Council, 15 June 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun appointed by the employer party, Tuon Si Phan appointed by the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139403

## 1. Facts
Kon Tek (Cambodia) Knob Precision manufactures telephone components at Tram Kak, Takeo, and employed about 750 workers. [Para 42][Para 43] Seven federations filed a fourteen-point collective dispute in March 2015; thirteen workers delegated their rights to them, but eight then withdrew their thumbprints, leaving five claimants. [Para 19][Para 45][Para 46] Four maintenance workers — three electricians and a plumber, hired between December 2014 and January 2015 at $5 a day paid monthly — were dismissed on 7 March 2015, two days after a union election at which three of them were elected president, vice-president and secretary and the fourth stood and lost. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54][Para 177][Para 178][Para 181] Two issues were settled in conciliation, three more at the hearing, and issues 1 and 2 were merged. [Para 20][Para 25][Para 26]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must stop discriminating against the union, take back the four dismissed union leaders and candidates, and pay their wages and benefits from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 47][Para 118]
2. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of 2,000 riel a day? [Para 199]
3. Whether the employer must pay overtime meal money of 2,000 riel per hour? [Para 215]
4. Whether the employer must pay $10 a month of travel and accommodation money? [Para 229]
5. Whether the employer must stop using the misleading term "daily-wage worker" (កម្មករ ស៊ី ថ្ងៃ)? [Para 236]
6. Whether the employer must pay daily-wage workers the benefits it withholds from them? [Para 245]
7. Whether the employer must abstain from dismissing workers without cause? [Para 248]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the four were permanent workers doing regular electrician and plumbing work for eight hours a day, six days a week, and were never told their work was temporary; the dismissal came two days after the election and was plainly anti-union, with no authorisation from the Labour Inspector. [Para 137][Para 140][Para 181][Para 195] Daily-wage workers do 26 days a month like everyone else, so calling them "daily-wage" cheats them of the benefits permanent workers receive. [Para 92][Para 101][Para 102]
**Employer (respondent):** the four were not probationers but daily-wage workers paid $5 a day; three electricians had been dismissed days earlier because the electrical and water systems were now connected and the work had ended, and a daily-wage worker never becomes permanent however long he serves. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 67][Para 68][Para 96] The company opened only in January 2015 and cannot afford the allowances claimed. [Para 79][Para 82]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to take back the four workers with their wages and other benefits from the date of dismissal to the date of return, and to give daily-wage workers the same benefits as permanent workers; the lunch, overtime-meal, travel-money and "daily-wage" wording claims were dismissed, and issue 7 was recorded as no longer disputed. [Para 251][Para 252][Para 258][Para 259][Para 261]

On status, the panel found the four were permanent workers: Article 9 of the Labour Law distinguishes permanent from casual work by the stability of the job, and their work as electricians and plumber was regular and permanent, not seasonal or intermittent. [Para 119][Para 121][Para 128][Para 142] It followed its reasoning in Case 58/13 (TV5) that regular weekly service over years is permanent employment. [Para 124][Para 126][Para 127] No written contract with a precise end date existed, which the parties admitted; under Article 67(1) and (7) a fixed-duration contract must be in writing and state its end date, failing which it becomes a contract of indefinite duration, so the four held indefinite-duration contracts. [Para 143][Para 150][Para 151][Para 154][Para 155]

On the dismissal, Article 293 and Articles 3 and 4 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 protect union election candidates for 45 days before and 45 days after the poll, and a dismissal without prior authorisation from the Labour Inspector is null. [Para 156][Para 158][Para 159][Para 193][Para 194] The panel found the election of 5 March 2015 occurred, the employer having produced no evidence to disprove it, that notice of the candidates reached the employer through its security guard on 6 March 2015, and that all four were dismissed on 7 March 2015 — inside the protected window; the employer never sought the Inspector's authorisation. [Para 170][Para 176][Para 182][Para 185][Para 189] The dismissals were therefore null, and reinstatement with back pay followed under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 196][Para 197][Para 198]

Issues 2, 3 and 4 sought more than any law, collective agreement, internal rule or practice provided, so they were interest disputes; the claimant was five individual workers, not a most-representative union, and under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 an award on an interest dispute binds all workers in the enterprise, so the panel declined them for want of standing. [Para 200][Para 201][Para 208][Para 209][Para 210][Para 213][Para 214] On issue 5 it held that the label "daily-wage worker" describes only the method of wage payment under Article 11 and is not decisive; the substance is that the workers are permanent, so it dismissed the demand to ban the term but ordered equal treatment. [Para 238][Para 239][Para 242][Para 243]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker hired without a written fixed-duration contract stating a precise end date holds a contract of indefinite duration, whatever label the employer uses, because Article 67(1) and (7) of the Labour Law make writing and a stated end date conditions of a valid fixed-term contract; the worker is permanent under Article 9 and entitled to the same benefits as other permanent workers. [Para 121][Para 151][Para 155][Para 242][Para 258] A union leader or election candidate dismissed within the 45-day protected period of Prakas No. 305 without the Labour Inspector's prior authorisation is dismissed nulliter, and must be reinstated with wages and benefits for the intervening period. [Para 158][Para 182][Para 194][Para 198]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that a labour contract need not be in writing to exist, since Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 664 of the Civil Code agree on that point, and that the term "daily-wage worker" is unimportant in itself, are general remarks not necessary to the orders made. [Para 143][Para 147][Para 149][Para 242]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award date conflicts: the header gives 15 June 2015, but [Para 30] records that the parties agreed to postpone issuance from 4 June 2015 to 16 June 2015. Both are reported; the header date is used in the title.
- The claimant is described as thirteen workers who delegated their rights [Para 45], eight of whom withdrew [Para 44][Para 46], leaving five [Para 46][Para 212]; elsewhere the panel refers to "the five workers" as complainants while the orders concern four. The orders name four.
- The arbitrator appointed by the worker party is printed "ទូន ស៊ី ជាន់" at [Para 1] and "ទួន ស៊ី ផាន់" at [Para 263]; the employer is written "គំន ថេក" at [Para 1] and "គ័ន ថេក" elsewhere. One worker's name appears as "សំខាន់ តាំង អូន" at [Para 195] against "សំ តាំង អូន" throughout; workers are redacted here.
- Several sentences are broken across paragraphs, losing their sense: [Para 137]–[Para 138] ("... និយោជក មិន បាន កំណត់ ... ថា ការងារ ។ / របស់ ពួកគាត់ គឺជា ការងារ រយៈពេល ខ្លី"), [Para 251]–[Para 252] (the order for issues 1 and 2 splits mid-sentence), and [Para 171]–[Para 172].
- English and numeric noise from other documents survives inside the Khmer: "Lin" [Para 160], "Gro" [Para 190], "ZV" [Para 127][Para 233], "2_," [Para 34], "CC" [Para 263].
- The issue numbering skips: issues 1 and 2 were merged, issues 3 and 4 were settled in conciliation and never described, and the panel's order addresses 1–2, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 and 11, so the original claim's points 3 and 4 cannot be identified.
