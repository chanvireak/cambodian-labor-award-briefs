# Award No. 103/15 — Teng Sun Limited (Arbitration Council, 29 May 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bunchea presiding; Hem Hunarith, employer nominee; An Non, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139195

## 1. Facts
Teng Sun Limited sews bags at National Road 41, Samrong Tong district, Kampong Speu, and says it employs about 720 workers. [Para 2][Para 34][Para 35] Its Free Union of Workers, certified on 31 October 2014, claims 514 members but conceded that it was not yet the most representative union in the company. [Para 37][Para 38] On 26 March 2015 the Kampong Speu labour department received the union's complaint over eight demands; conciliation on 2 April 2015 settled none, and report No. 1079/15 of 9 April 2015 reached the Secretariat on 27 April 2015. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 17] The panel heard the case on 7 May 2015 and, at the parties' request, moved the award date from 26 May to 29 May 2015. [Para 19][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise the midday meal allowance to 3,000 riel a day? [Para 3][Para 40]
2. Whether the employer must pay a 2,000 riel meal allowance for each hour of overtime worked from 16:00 to 18:00? [Para 4][Para 100]
3. Whether the employer must reduce the regular attendance bonus in proportion to the days of leave it has authorised, instead of forfeiting it? [Para 5][Para 111]
4. Whether the employer must raise the travel and accommodation allowance to USD 15 a month? [Para 6][Para 154]
5. Whether the employer must pay a 4,000 riel meal allowance for overtime worked on public holidays and Sundays? [Para 7][Para 60]
6. Whether skilled workers — table cutters and operators of two-needle, overlock and bartack machines — must receive a USD 15 skills allowance? [Para 8][Para 203]
7. Whether the basic wage of Khmer team leaders must rise by USD 40 to USD 188 a month? [Para 9][Para 210]
8. Whether the employer must stop deducting wages and the attendance bonus when a worker takes one hour of authorised leave? [Para 10][Para 216]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the midday meal allowance should be 3,000 riel because nearby factories such as Wai Fu Garment already pay 2,000, and the employer had promised an increase if business improved; the overtime meal allowance should be 2,000 riel an hour, doubled on holidays and Sundays, because 2,000 riel cannot buy a meal; travel and accommodation of USD 15 is needed against real rent of USD 20 and transport of USD 15–17; skilled cutters and machine operators are faster and deserve USD 15; team leaders carry 20 subordinates and are blamed for their errors; and one hour of authorised leave should cost neither wage nor bonus. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 57][Para 68][Para 72][Para 79]

**Employer (respondent):** the company is short of work, has dismissed some workers and put 200 to 300 on 50 per cent wages, so it cannot raise the meal allowance; it already pays 2,000 riel a day for overtime meals as Notification 041/11 requires; it pays USD 10 travel allowance against the USD 7 Notification 230 requires; the attendance bonus is a 20-dollar incentive for full attendance, deductible on a sliding scale; and it pays 2,000 riel to those who work holidays and Sundays. [Para 43][Para 49][Para 53][Para 65]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 3 was granted: the employer must deduct the regular attendance bonus in proportion to the number of days of leave it has lawfully authorised; issues 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8 were refused. [Para 243][Para 241][Para 242][Para 244][Para 245][Para 246][Para 247][Para 248][Para 249]

On issue 1 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, contract or past practice requiring 3,000 riel, so the claim sought a benefit above the law — an interest dispute — and under Article 43 of Prakas 099 an interest award replaces the collective agreement and binds all workers, which is why the panel decides one only for the most representative union. [Para 84][Para 86][Para 91][Para 93] The claimant was not that union, so the claim was refused. [Para 97][Para 98]

On issue 2, point 2 of Notification 041/11 gives a worker who volunteers for overtime 2,000 riel a day or one free meal, not 2,000 riel an hour, and the employer pays that sum for overtime from 16:00 to 18:00, so the obligation was met. [Para 101][Para 102][Para 104]

On issue 3 the panel held that the attendance bonus is a sum paid as a bonus and so part of wages under Article 103. [Para 143][Para 145] Leave the employer authorises suspends the contract under Article 71(6) and Article 72, so no wage is owed for days not worked. [Para 146][Para 149] The employer therefore may not forfeit the whole month's bonus but may reduce it only in proportion to the days of authorised absence, and the panel ordered accordingly. [Para 151][Para 153]

On issue 4, point 1 of Notification 230 of 25 July 2012 requires USD 7 for travel and accommodation; the employer pays USD 10, and no instrument obliges it to pay 15. [Para 154][Para 155][Para 161]

On issue 5 the panel separated holiday work from Sunday work. Work on a public holiday is governed by Articles 137 and 164 of the Labour Law and Articles 2 and 4 of Prakas No. 10 of 1999: the worker earns one day's wage as compensation, not an overtime meal allowance. [Para 165][Para 167][Para 169] Sunday work beyond the normal 48 hours is overtime, and Notification 041/11 gives 2,000 riel a day for it, which the employer pays. [Para 192][Para 195] The claim for 4,000 or 6,000 riel therefore failed on both branches. [Para 202]

Issues 6 and 7 were refused on the ground used for issue 1: no law, agreement, internal rule or practice created a skills allowance or the USD 40 increase for team leaders, both were interest disputes, and the claimant was not the most representative union. [Para 204][Para 209][Para 210][Para 215]

On issue 8 the panel again split the claim. Deducting one hour's wage for one hour of authorised leave is lawful: Article 664 of the Civil Code makes wages the price of work done and Article 102 defines wages as paid for work performed, so nothing is owed for an hour not worked. [Para 217][Para 219][Para 224][Para 231] The claim to bar any deduction from the attendance bonus for that hour had no basis in law or agreement, was an interest dispute, and failed for want of representativeness. [Para 234][Para 236][Para 240]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A regular attendance bonus is part of wages, and where a worker takes leave the employer has authorised the contract is suspended for those days, so the employer may reduce the bonus only in proportion to the days absent and may not confiscate the whole month's bonus; conversely, no wage is owed for an authorised hour or day not worked. [Para 145][Para 149][Para 231] A demand that no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or established practice supports is an interest dispute, which the panel will not decide where the claimant union is not the most representative. [Para 93][Para 97]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that it had no authoritative interpretation of "without absence" in point 2 of Notification 230 and would assess such claims case by case. [Para 121][Para 127]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel's constitution is printed "29 April 20 2015". [Para 18] The complaint is dated 26 March 2015, yet the conciliation report of 9 April 2015 relates to a conciliation held on 2 April 2015. [Para 15][Para 13] On issue 1 the demand reads "2,000 riel additional" but 3,000 in the reasons; on issue 5 the demand list says 4,000 riel while the reasons and the order say 6,000; on issue 4 the demand reads USD 5 additional and USD 15 in the reasons. [Para 3][Para 40][Para 7][Para 61][Para 6][Para 56] The order on issue 8 records "401 hours" for one hour. [Para 240] The company's name appears as តេង ស៊ុន លីមីធីត, តេង ស៊ូ and "Teng All Sun". [Para 34][Para 96] Membership of 514 against a workforce of about 720 sits oddly with the union's own concession; the panel decided on the concession without counting. [Para 35][Para 38]
