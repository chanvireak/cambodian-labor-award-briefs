# Award No. 292/15 — Jewelry Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 21 December 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bunchea presiding; Seng Vouch Hun appointed by the employer, Tuon Si Phan appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 139469

## 1. Facts
Jewelry Co., Ltd. processes steel at Sangkat 4, Preah Sihanouk, and employs about 77 workers, 64 of them union members. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 63] The construction and forestry workers' union filed eight demands; two settled in conciliation on 10 November 2015, and at the hearing of 1 December 2015 the parties settled the 10% wage-increase demand, leaving five. [Para 19][Para 24][Para 25] The company had suspended production for two months in January and February 2015 without the labour inspector's authorisation, and had agreed with the union on 9 May 2015 to postpone a housing and transport allowance for three months, to August 2015. [Para 80][Para 81][Para 117]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a USD 17 monthly housing and transport allowance under the agreement of 9 May 2015? [Para 107]
3. Whether the employer must pay a compensation package for the two-month suspension of January and February 2015? [Para 127]
4. Whether the employer must pay a USD 10 monthly regional allowance to workers from other provinces? [Para 151]
5. Whether the employer must pay USD 10 for Khmer New Year and USD 10 for Pchum Ben, or hold a New Year party, as past practice? [Para 170][Para 180]
6. Whether the employer must raise the 2016 base wage to USD 140 under the Ministry of Labour Prakas? [Para 199]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the May 2015 agreement promised the housing and transport allowance and the company never paid it, so USD 17 a month is due, or USD 7 as a fallback [Para 66][Para 67][Para 75]; the company promised a package for the suspension period [Para 76]; regional allowance is paid by other companies they had worked for [Para 87]; and the 2016 minimum wage Prakas should apply because they had always followed Ministry Prakas [Para 96][Para 214].
**Employer (respondent):** the May agreement fixed no amount and the October 2015 negotiation of USD 5 was refused, so no figure was ever agreed [Para 72][Para 118][Para 123]; the agreement itself makes the suspension package conditional on financial recovery, and production fell sharply in 2015 against 2014 [Para 84][Para 137][Para 147]; the regional allowance is paid only when the company posts a worker to another province [Para 153]; and the USD 140 Prakas covers only textiles, garments and footwear, not metal processing, while the company already pays USD 128 plus USD 5 [Para 97][Para 99][Para 213].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all five remaining demands were refused, and the award becomes final 8 days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Minister of Labour. [Para 217][Para 218][Para 219][Para 220][Para 221][Para 222]
On demand 1 the panel applied Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code: a contract arises from an offer and an acceptance, and neither a party nor the panel as third party may impose a term on a party that did not accept it. [Para 107][Para 111][Para 122][Para 124] The May agreement postponed the allowance to August 2015 but named no amount, the October 2015 negotiation produced only an employer offer of USD 5 that the workers refused, and so no sum was ever agreed. [Para 117][Para 118][Para 120][Para 123][Para 126]
On demand 3 the panel read clause 5 of the same agreement as making the package payable only when the company's finances recover, and held the claimant bears the burden of proof. [Para 137][Para 140][Para 141] The employer produced 2014 and 2015 production figures showing a significant fall, the union produced nothing to contradict them, and the panel was satisfied the financial crisis was real. [Para 146][Para 147][Para 148][Para 149][Para 150]
On demands 4 and 5 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, contract or past practice creating the obligation, so each was an interest dispute. [Para 154][Para 155][Para 156][Para 173][Para 175] Reading Article 96 of the Labour Law with Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099, it held that an interest award replaces the collective agreement for a year and extinguishes the right to strike over the same interest, so it will act only for the most representative union; this union held 64 members but no most-representative certificate, so the panel refused to consider the claims. [Para 159][Para 160][Para 161][Para 167][Para 168] On the New Year party it additionally held that a past practice must be an established and repeated practice accepted by both sides, that one 2014 party proved none, and that changed economic circumstances can lawfully end a practice. [Para 181][Para 185][Para 187][Para 197][Para 198]
On demand 6, Article 1 of Prakas No. 409/15 of 8 October 2015 sets USD 140 for textile, garment and footwear workers only; the company processes steel, so the Prakas does not reach it, and the May agreement's USD 128 plus USD 5 had been applied. [Para 199][Para 200][Para 201][Para 213][Para 215]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A settlement agreement that defers a benefit without fixing its amount does not by itself create an enforceable sum: where later negotiation fails, the panel will not choose a figure, because a term cannot be imposed on a party that never accepted it. [Para 122][Para 123][Para 126] An interest dispute may be heard only where the most representative union in the establishment brings it. [Para 160][Para 168]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel encouraged the parties to resume bargaining with mutual understanding to reach an acceptable result [Para 125]; and it observed generally that a past practice is not perpetual, since the parties may end it by agreement or because the circumstances that grounded it have changed [Para 187][Para 188][Para 189].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The May 2015 agreement's wage clause is printed as USD 50 extra at para 101 but USD 5 at paras 212 and 213. [Para 101][Para 212] Para 21 dates the panel's constitution "16 November 2023 2015"; para 161 dates Prakas No. 099 "21 April 2019 6004"; and para 9 omits the month from the conciliation report's date. [Para 21][Para 161][Para 9] The demands are numbered in two series (the report's 1 to 8 and the panel's 1(1) to 6(8)), which the award never reconciles, and issue 2(2) disappears after settlement without explanation. [Para 25][Para 19] Stray digits interrupt the text at paras 127, 201 and 90 ("257", "157", "2017"). [Para 127][Para 201][Para 90] The company name appears as "Jewelry", "Jewel Lin" and "Jeuvelin" variants across the file; the romanisation in the title is approximate. [Para 16][Para 48][Para 59] The employer-appointed arbitrator's name is lost to OCR in the signature block, where only a garbled fragment remains. [Para 223] Individual representatives and union officials appear only under partial OCR names and are redacted. [Para 1][Para 2]
