# Award No. 024/15 — Vanny Full Garment (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 25 February 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Vireak for the worker party, Kong Phalja chair) · Doc ID 139135

## 1. Facts
Vanny Full Garment (Cambodia) Ltd, a garment factory at Pong Teuk, Ang Snuol, Kandal, ran its whole workforce on three-month fixed duration contracts (FDCs) renewed repeatedly. [Para 66][Para 232] Two union activists, Mr K (employee no. 5384, ironing) and Ms S (employee no. 5377, inspection), worked from 21 May 2012 on successive three-month contracts until the last expired on 20 October 2014 — 2 years, 4 months and 29 days. [Para 82][Para 189] The employer notified non-renewal on 10 October 2014: for Mr K inattention and long toilet absences, for Ms S repeated failure to detect unlined trousers, for which she had a written warning of 2 October 2014. [Para 93][Para 100][Para 103][Para 105] Of six demands conciliated on 19 January 2015 and referred on 26 January 2015, two more settled at the hearing of 9 February 2015 and points 4 and 5 were withdrawn, leaving points 1, 2 and 6. [Para 19][Para 24][Para 25][Para 26]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the two union activists, Mr K and Ms S, as ordinary workers again? [Para 2][Para 74]
2. Whether the employer must stop using fixed duration employment contracts, now and in future? [Para 4][Para 107]
6. Whether the employer must let the union use a meeting room for two hours during working time every Saturday? [Para 11][Para 115]

Points 3 (drinking water), 4 (4,000 riel daily lunch allowance) and 5 (milk powder for workers with small children) were settled at conciliation or withdrawn and were not decided. [Para 6][Para 8][Para 24][Para 25]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** having worked over two years on rolling three-month contracts, both had contracts of undetermined duration, so terminating them was unlawful and discriminatory against union activists; the employer must also stop using FDCs, which risk losing rights and jobs, the work being permanent not seasonal. [Para 77][Para 78][Para 81][Para 108][Para 110]
**Employer (respondent):** all workers are on FDCs by long practice and want the 5% end-of-contract payment; Mr K was warned orally for inattention and long toilet visits, and Ms S repeatedly missed unlined trousers after a written warning; the meeting room is for staff delegates only. [Para 96][Para 111][Para 112][Para 117][Para 241]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted on point 1, dismissed on points 2 and 6 — reinstatement refused, but the employer was ordered to pay Mr K and Ms S, for the termination of their contracts: 2/3 of one month's wage and allowances in lieu of notice, dismissal compensation for 2 years of service equal to 30 days' wage and allowances, payment for unused annual leave, and final wages outstanding; and to pay Mr K moral damages (សោហ៊ុយជម្ងឺចិត្ត) equal to 30 days' wage and allowances. [Para 259][Para 260][Para 261][Para 262][Para 263][Para 264][Para 265][Para 266]

**Point 1.** The panel applied Article 67(2) of the Labour Law: an FDC may not run longer than two years, and under Article 73(5) a fixed term contract renewed without prior notice beyond that total becomes a contract of undetermined duration — a reading it followed from award No. 10/03-Zaksintek and from ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982, treated as non-binding interpretive context. [Para 124][Para 127][Para 128][Para 133][Para 137] The facts were agreed: entry 21 May 2012, three-month renewals to 20 October 2014, total over two years, so both contracts had become of undetermined duration. [Para 142][Para 143] Termination then fell under Article 74, which forbids dismissal without a valid reason concerning aptitude, conduct or the needs of the enterprise. [Para 154][Para 155] For Mr K the employer produced no evidence and admitted only oral warnings; the panel could not find the alleged misconduct proved, so the dismissal was wrong. [Para 163][Para 166][Para 169][Para 171] Because the employer stood absolutely against taking him back, the panel declined to order reinstatement and ordered statutory compensation instead. [Para 173] For Ms S the repeated inspection error was proved and admitted, with oral and written warnings, so dismissal was within the internal rules — but the fault was light, not serious misconduct under Article 83, so severance remained payable. [Para 179][Para 183][Para 185] Applying Articles 75, 77, 89, 166, 167, 91 and 116, and treating the final 4 months 29 days as no full year, the panel fixed notice at one month (ten days already given, leaving 20 days), dismissal compensation at 30 days for two years, and moral damages for Mr K alone. [Para 186][Para 191][Para 196][Para 197][Para 202][Para 208][Para 211]

**Point 2.** Dismissed. A labour contract arises from the parties' will (Civil Code Articles 311, 336, 337, 664), and no party may force another to accept a contract type or terms it does not agree to; using FDCs is lawful, and both the workers' demand and the employer's refusal are exercises of a right. [Para 212][Para 214][Para 224][Para 226][Para 234][Para 235][Para 237][Para 239]

**Point 6.** Dismissed without consideration of the merits. No law, collective agreement or internal rule obliges the employer to give the union two hours of working time on Saturdays, so this is an interest dispute; under Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 only the most representative union may carry an interest dispute, and Prakas No. 099 Article 43 makes such an award a collective agreement binding all workers. [Para 243][Para 244][Para 246][Para 248][Para 250] The claimant here was 123 workers who had transferred their rights to a union federation, and the Cambodia Union at the factory was not the most representative union; the panel therefore lacked the required standing. [Para 256][Para 257][Para 258]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive fixed duration contracts whose combined duration exceeds two years convert into a contract of undetermined duration under Articles 67(2) and 73(5) of the Labour Law, and the employer then bears the burden of proving a valid reason under Article 74 for ending it; where it produces no evidence of the alleged misconduct, the dismissal is unlawful and statutory notice, dismissal compensation, leave and final wage payments follow, while moral damages under Article 91 are available only where the dismissal was itself unjustified. [Para 124][Para 137][Para 166][Para 173][Para 197][Para 208]

**Obiter dicta:** the remarks that ILO Recommendation No. 166 is non-binding yet useful context, and that a party seeking reinstatement may receive compensation where the employer refuses absolutely, are observations not required by the order. [Para 135][Para 172][Para 173]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- Arbitrator Ing Sothy filed a dissenting opinion annexed to the award, arguing that because the employer had always paid the 5% end-of-FDC payment under Article 73, no further severance should be ordered in point 1; the majority's order stands and the dissent is recorded here. [Para 269][Para 271][Para 280]
- The reasons are internally inconsistent on Ms S: at [Para 207] the panel says the employer had valid grounds to dismiss her, while at [Para 185] and [Para 195] it finds her fault was only light and not serious misconduct, which is what makes severance payable. Both passages are reported.
- The award number is printed "024/15" in the header [Para 1] but the annex heads the file "សំណុំរឿង លេខ ០២៤/១៥", spelling the company "វ៉ៃ ហ្វ៊ុល" and "ហ្វ៊ូល" in successive lines [Para 269].
- The employer's name appears in at least five transliterations — "វ៉ៃ ហ្វុល ហ្គាមេន", "វ៉ៃ ហ្គាមេន", "វ៉ៃ ហ្វ្លុ មេន", "វ៉ៃ ហ្គ្រា មេន", "វ៉ៃ ហ្វល" [Para 1][Para 20][Para 30][Para 50][Para 66].
- The workforce is given as "ប្រហែល ៩៧២ នាក់" at [Para 67] but "៩៣២ នាក់" by the employer at [Para 111]; the claimant group is 123 workers at [Para 68] but five attend at [Para 69] [Para 70].
- Contract dates conflict: entry 21 May 2012 [Para 82], renewals from 20 July 2012 [Para 142], yet the first three-month contract runs 6 July to 5 October 2012 [Para 43]. Service is computed as 2 years 4 months 29 days [Para 189], though 21 May 2012 to 20 October 2014 is 2 years 4 months 30 days.
- Heavy OCR loss throughout the annex: stray page marks ("-១៥-", "Ama", "1An4<") and Article 664 of the Civil Code rendered "៦៦៤" [Para 214].
