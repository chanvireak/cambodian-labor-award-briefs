# Award No. 264/15 — Capitol Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 07 December 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry for the employer party, Ann Vireak for the worker party, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 139442

## 1. Facts
Capitol Co., Ltd, a road transport company employing about 250 workers, faced a collective dispute brought by the Cambodian Labour Confederation (CLC) for 53 authorising drivers. [Para 47][Para 48] Conciliation failed and six issues were referred on 30 September 2015; at the hearing of 27 October 2015 the parties settled issue 6 by agreement, leaving issues 1 to 5, and extended the award date to 7 December 2015. [Para 38][Para 42][Para 43][Para 44] After a strike from 22 July to 8 August 2015, five drivers terminated on 9 July 2015 and two more who had refused to drive out of Siem Reap on 10 July 2015 sought reinstatement. [Para 54][Para 102][Para 219]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must increase wages by 10% every year in addition to current wages? [Para 26][Para 50]
2. Whether the employer must cease requiring drivers to pay a deposit before commencing employment and return deposits already paid? [Para 26][Para 65]
3. Whether the employer must pay unpaid entitlements from commencement of employment to date — payment in lieu of annual leave, wages on holidays and weekly time-off, and overtime wages? [Para 27][Para 72]
4. Whether the employer must reinstate the seven named drivers and pay back wages from termination to reinstatement? [Para 27][Para 100]
5. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr K, named in provisional relief order no. 462 of 31 July 2015 by the Phnom Penh Court of First Instance, and pay his wages and back pay? [Para 35][Para 116]

Issue 6, on cars and equal shifts, was settled by the parties and not considered. [Para 37][Para 144]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** driver wages average US$ 160 to US$ 240 monthly plus a 50,000 riel provincial return-trip allowance; the 10% claim rests on Articles 104, 105 and 107 of the Labour Law and on need; the deposit is a condition of getting the job and breaches Article 44; drivers work seven days a week without leave, holidays, weekly time-off or overtime pay; the July terminations were union discrimination and the Siem Reap dismissals punished a lawful strike, contrary to Articles 332 and 333. [Para 58][Para 70][Para 73][Para 102][Para 109]
**Employer (respondent):** wages run from US$ 160 to US$ 275 by achievement and seniority under clause 5 of the Internal Work Rule approved on 10 June 2015, and a 10% rise for drivers would force rises for other staff and higher ticket prices; the deposit secures careful driving and is returned at the end of employment; overtime and holiday wages have already been paid; the five were made redundant for falling passengers, and the Siem Reap drivers abandoned their jobs under clause 6. [Para 53][Para 61][Para 67][Para 91][Para 103][Para 111]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 1 declined; deposits removed and refunded; issue 3 rejected; reinstatement with back pay for Mr R, Mr So and Mr K, with compensation in the alternative for Mr K. [Para 336][Para 342]

*Issue 1.* The demand exceeds the Labour Law, so it is an interest dispute. [Para 152] Under Article 96 and clause 9 of Prakas no. 305 only the union with most representative status (MRS) may compel negotiation of a collective agreement, and the panel settles interest disputes only where the claimant union holds the MRS or represents over 51% of the enterprise's workers (81/04 Evergreen, 98/04 Great Union, 169/11 Fortune Teo, 02/11 Pu Yean, 66/11 In Han Sung). [Para 153][Para 156][Para 157] The claimants were 53 workers acting through the CLC, not the MRS union, so the panel declined to consider the claim. [Para 158][Para 159]

*Issue 2.* Article 44 forbids subjecting the making or keeping of an employment contract to a cash guarantee of any form, and Article 127 restricts set-off against wages (21/03 Loyal, 86/06 Kingsland, 04/09 E-Garment). [Para 160][Para 162][Para 163] The deposit was a condition of obtaining the job and so violated Article 44; under clause 34 of Prakas no. 099 the panel ordered the practice stopped and deposits refunded. [Para 167][Para 168]

*Issue 3.* Article 167(2) allows payment in lieu of annual leave only when the contract ends and Article 167(3) voids any agreement paying or waiving it during employment (94/04 Eternity, 71/09 Hytex, 06/10 Yakjin). [Para 178][Para 179][Para 180] Only the five terminated drivers could argue employment ended, and no court decision terminating them was produced, so all 51 relationships continued and the claim failed. [Para 183][Para 186][Para 188] On holidays, weekly time-off and overtime the alleging party bears the burden; the workers' lists were objected to and unsupported, while the employer filed payroll evidence and the workers accepted receiving US$ 30 to US$ 40 extra for holiday work. [Para 196][Para 206][Para 208] The burden was unmet and the claim rejected. [Para 210]

*Issue 4.* The five terminated on 9 July 2015 had their contracts pending before the Court of First Instance on the employer's application, so the panel declined to consider their reinstatement. [Para 217] For Mr R and Mr So, Article 332 reinstates a worker at the end of a strike and Article 333 voids any sanction for striking; a notice requiring return by 11 July 2015 or be deemed resigned was an order only a court may make after declaring a strike unlawful, so clause 6 could not found abandonment, and reinstatement with back pay was ordered. [Para 218][Para 227][Para 231][Para 233]

*Issue 5.* Mr K returned his key and filed a leave form to join the strike on 28 July 2015; the employer rejected the form over a signature mismatch and he was absent 28 July to 6 August 2015. [Para 249][Para 250][Para 259] As drivers attend only on scheduled departure days, some seven or eight a month, and he struck only on 28 and 29 July, he had not taken six days' unauthorised leave, and there was no valid reason for termination under Article 74. [Para 263][Para 268][Para 284] Reinstatement with back pay was ordered; failing that, fifteen days' wages and benefits in lieu of notice (Articles 75, 77), seven days' wages and perquisites as dismissal indemnity (Article 89), annual leave in proportion (Articles 166, 167), damages equal to that indemnity (Article 91) and outstanding wages (Article 116), with holiday and weekly time-off wages refused as covered by issue 3. [Para 310][Para 311][Para 320][Para 328][Para 333][Para 342]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may not make a cash deposit a condition of hiring or continued employment, and must refund deposits taken, because Article 44 admits no cash guarantee at any stage. [Para 165][Para 168]

A return-to-work notice issued during a strike cannot convert strikers into abandoners: only a court may order strikers back after declaring the strike unlawful, so a sanction founded on such a notice is void under Article 333 and the workers are reinstated under Article 332. [Para 227][Para 231]

An interest dispute — a demand for more than the law gives — may be brought only by the union holding most representative status or over 51% of the workforce; otherwise the panel declines it. [Para 156][Para 159]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that an award settling an interest dispute would replace a collective agreement for one year under clause 43 of Prakas no. 099, and so extinguish the demand, did not arise once it declined issue 1. [Para 155]
