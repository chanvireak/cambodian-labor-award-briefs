# Award No. 159/15 — YVP Trading Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 28 July 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom, employer-appointed; An Nan, worker-appointed; Tang Try, chair) · non-binding award · Doc ID 139238

## 1. Facts
YVP Trading sews garments in Canadia industrial park and employed about 700 workers. Workers struck from 16 June to 1 July 2015 without prior notice, and conciliation on 23 June 2015 failed on all 19 demands; of the nineteen, eleven were settled at the hearing, one was withdrawn, and six remained for adjudication. Nine union federations still acted for the claimants, but none had a local union inside the company and only the fourteen workers who attended the hearing retained their mandates. [Para 45][Para 33][Para 34][Para 35][Para 47][Para 48][Para 88]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reduce the attendance bonus pro rata to the days a worker is absent with the employer's permission, rather than cancel it entirely? [Para 53]
2. Whether the employer must pay a midday meal allowance of 2,000 riel a day? [Para 60]
3. Whether the employer must pay a skills allowance of USD 5 a month to workers who can sew on two or three machines? [Para 64]
4. Whether the employer must raise the attendance bonus to USD 15 a month? [Para 68]
5. Whether the employer must pay a meal allowance of 2,000 riel per hour of overtime? [Para 72]
6. Whether the employer must preserve wages and the attendance bonus during the strike of 16 June to 1 July 2015? [Para 189]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** cancelling the whole attendance bonus for any absence leaves them short of money, and other companies reduce it pro rata; other factories, including those in Canadia park, already pay 2,000 riel a day for meals; a skills allowance rewards workers who can run several machines; and the strike was not purely their fault because they had put a negotiating proposal to the employer on 13 June 2015 and the employer refused to settle. [Para 57][Para 61][Para 66][Para 70][Para 80][Para 203]

**Employer (respondent):** the bonus is earned only by turning up regularly, so absence means the condition is not met; there is no law requiring a meal allowance and the company cannot afford it; and the strike was unlawful because no prior notice was given. [Para 58][Para 62][Para 67][Para 82][Para 203]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to reduce the attendance bonus pro rata to days of absence that it had authorised, and the other five demands were refused. [Para 209][Para 210][Para 211][Para 215]

On issue 1 the panel noted that the attendance bonus is fixed by Notification No. 230 K.B/S.Ch.N of 25 July 2012, which guarantees at least USD 10 a month to a worker who attends regularly "without absence", and that neither the Labour Advisory Committee nor any competent body has said whether that phrase covers authorised absence. It declined to presume an answer and held that each case must be decided on its facts. Where the employer has authorised the leave, articles 71(6) and 72(1) of the Labour Law suspend the contract and relieve the employer of wages for those days, so the bonus may be reduced pro rata to the days taken, but not forfeited wholesale. Where the absence was never authorised, the line of prior awards allows the employer to withhold the bonus entirely. [Para 97][Para 100][Para 101][Para 128][Para 130][Para 131][Para 136]

On issue 2 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rule, contract, agreement or past practice requiring 2,000 riel a day, held the demand to be a benefit above the statutory floor and therefore an interest dispute, and refused to consider it because the fourteen claimants were not a most representative union, applying article 96 of the Labour Law, article 9 of Prakas No. 305 SChBy and article 43 of Prakas No. 099 SChBy. [Para 146][Para 147][Para 152][Para 155][Para 158][Para 159][Para 160]

Issue 3 travelled the same path: no instrument obliged the employer to pay USD 5 for multi-machine skills, so the claim was an interest dispute and failed on the same want of standing. [Para 162][Para 164][Para 166][Para 167]

On issue 4 the panel found that the employer already pays the USD 10 that Notification No. 230 requires, that no law or agreement requires USD 15, and that the demand was again an interest dispute beyond the law, refused on standing. [Para 170][Para 171][Para 172][Para 174][Para 178]

On issue 5 the panel construed point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 K.B/S.Ch.N of 7 March 2011, which entitles a worker who volunteers for overtime to 2,000 riel a day or a free meal. The employer pays 2,000 riel on each overtime day, which satisfies the instrument; there being no rule requiring 2,000 riel per hour, the claim was an interest dispute and was refused. [Para 179][Para 180][Para 181][Para 183][Para 185][Para 188]

On issue 6 the panel applied articles 332 and 72(1): the contract is suspended during a strike and no wages are due, following Award No. 27/10. It then held the strike unlawful because no notice was given as article 324 requires and the conciliation and arbitration route under article 320(4) had not been exhausted, so neither wages nor the attendance bonus were owed for 16 June to 1 July 2015. [Para 189][Para 192][Para 193][Para 197][Para 203][Para 204][Para 207][Para 208]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus that is conditional on regular attendance may be reduced in proportion to the days a worker is absent with the employer's authorisation — because the contract is then suspended and wages for those days are not owed — but it may be withheld altogether where the absence was never authorised. [Para 130][Para 131][Para 136]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's statement that it could not interpret "without absence" in Notification No. 230 generally, and that every case must be assessed on its own facts, went beyond what the two categories before it required. [Para 101][Para 105]
