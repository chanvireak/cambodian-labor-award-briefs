# Award No. 101/15 — Chevron (Cambodia) Limited (Arbitration Council, 2 June 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phally presiding; Ouk Ri, employer nominee; Huon Chundy, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139194

## 1. Facts
Chevron (Cambodia) Limited, registered since 3 August 1995, runs 24 Caltex fuel stations with depots and a head office, employing about 534 workers, more than 100 of them women. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 115] Its Democratic Union of Workers, certified on 12 January 2009 with about 380 members, struck over ten demands; conciliation on 9 April 2015 failed on all ten and report No. 416 of 23 April 2015 followed. [Para 25][Para 29][Para 87][Para 88] 359 workers authorised the Food and Service Workers' Federation of Cambodia to represent them, and the panel heard the case on 8 May 2015. [Para 90][Para 27] The workers withdrew issue 6, compensation for the dismissed Mr L, leaving nine issues. [Para 34][Para 35]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether a woman on maternity leave is entitled to 100 per cent of her wage rather than the statutory half? [Para 93][Para 174]
2. Whether a father is entitled to two weeks' paternity leave? [Para 101][Para 196]
3. Whether the company must meet actual private nursery costs, or pay USD 50 for milk, where it has no nursery? [Para 108][Para 208]
4. Whether all workers are entitled to a share of annual profits? [Para 143][Para 260]
5. Whether workers are entitled to USD 100 in addition to their health allowance? [Para 147][Para 267]
7. Whether a worker with three years' service is entitled to a USD 5 seniority bonus? [Para 152][Para 273]
8. Whether all workers are entitled to a USD 60 wage increase? [Para 155][Para 279]
9. Whether drivers on provincial mission are entitled to USD 20 per night? [Para 163][Para 285]
10. Whether drivers are entitled to USD 350 a month? [Para 170][Para 299]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** half wages cannot sustain a woman's health and living standard on maternity leave, and Cambodia must guarantee them even though it has not ratified ILO Convention No. 183; paternity leave is distinct from the seven days' special leave the employer cites; a company with no nursery must meet real childcare invoices or pay USD 50 for milk; the other claims rest on the cost of living and on the workers' skill and risk. [Para 94][Para 96][Para 105][Para 165]

**Employer (respondent):** it applies the Labour Law and ministerial praksas, paying half wages under Article 183 and seven days' paid special leave under Article 171 and Prakas 267; each station, depot and head office is a separate enterprise employing fewer than 100 women, so Article 186 does not apply; it has just raised the health allowance from USD 100 to USD 150 a month and runs bonus programmes and NSSF contributions instead of a profit share; mission allowance is under review because a provincial trip normally lasts a day. [Para 99][Para 104][Para 112][Para 150][Para 166]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must arrange a nursery (ទារកដ្ឋាន) at its stations, depots and head office, or nearby within a reasonable distance, and pay private nursery fees against actual invoices where it cannot take children over 18 months; the USD 50 milk alternative and issues 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9 and 10 were refused. [Para 257][Para 258][Para 259][Para 305][Para 306][Para 310]

On issue 1 the panel read Articles 182 and 183 as giving 90 days' maternity leave and half wages including fringe benefits, but only to a woman with a year's service, and found no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice promising 100 per cent. [Para 174][Para 175][Para 178][Para 181] The claim was thus a demand beyond the law — an interest dispute — and under Article 43 of Prakas 099 an award on an interest dispute displaces the collective agreement and removes the right to strike, so the panel decides one only where the claimant is the most representative union, or unions with more than half the workforce. [Para 183][Para 189][Para 191] The 359 claimants were not such a union, so the panel declined to consider the claim. [Para 193][Para 195]

On issue 2, Article 171 and Prakas 267 give seven days' paid special leave on a wife's confinement, which the employer already grants; two weeks' paternity leave has no basis in law or agreement, and the panel refused it on the same representativeness ground. [Para 196][Para 200][Para 201][Para 204][Para 208]

On issue 3 the panel applied Article 186, requiring any enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a nursing room and nursery, with Article 2(b) paragraphs 2 and 3, under which one enterprise may hold many establishments all subject to it. [Para 208][Para 210][Para 212] It rejected the separate-enterprise argument: nothing showed the stations, depots and head office registered as branches or commercial representative offices under Articles 273, 276, 278 and 281 of the Law on Commercial Enterprises, the enterprise-opening declarations did not make them separate enterprises, the contracts named Chevron as employer, and the statutes list fuel-station operation as its own business object. [Para 235][Para 236][Para 237][Para 242][Para 244] Counting women across the company, the threshold was met and the duty applied. [Para 248] Article 186 does not, however, require a nursery inside each station: the employer may provide one nearby, let neighbouring stations share it, or pay private nursery fees against actual invoices where it cannot take children over 18 months. [Para 249][Para 250][Para 251] The USD 50 milk allowance was refused because Article 186 provides no cash substitute. [Para 255][Para 256]

Issues 4, 5, 7, 8 and 10 were refused on the same two grounds used for issue 1: no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice created the entitlement, each claim was an interest dispute, and the claimants were not the most representative union. [Para 261][Para 267][Para 273][Para 279][Para 299]

On issue 9 the panel held that Article 103 puts benefits given to facilitate a worker's functions outside wages, and that under Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 664 of the Civil Code a worker need not bear the travel costs of a mission for the employer. [Para 285][Para 291][Para 292] No law fixes the amount of a driver's mission allowance, the employer pays USD 15.5 per provincial trip regardless of days, and the workers proved no outlay of USD 20 a night, so the claim failed for want of evidence. [Para 293][Para 294][Para 297][Para 298]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A company's fuel stations, depots and head office count as one enterprise for Article 186 of the Labour Law unless each site is registered at the Ministry of Commerce as a separate company, branch or commercial representative office; the 100-woman threshold is therefore counted across the whole company, and the resulting duty is satisfied by a nursery at or near the sites, shared between neighbouring sites or paid for by invoice, not by a nursery in every station. [Para 235][Para 248][Para 250] A demand that no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or established practice supports is an interest dispute, which the panel will not decide unless the claimant is the most representative union or unions representing more than half the workforce. [Para 191][Para 194]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the employer ought to bear the incidental costs a worker incurs while travelling on mission for it, though the USD 20 claim failed for lack of evidence. [Para 292]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The hearing is dated 8 May 2015 in the procedural summary, but the invitations cited are for 25 May 2015 at 2.00 p.m. [Para 27][Para 31] The panel's establishment date is printed "28 April 2019 2015". [Para 31] On issue 1 the agreed practice appears as 450 per cent of wages where the reasons record 50 per cent. [Para 93][Para 180] Issue 7's qualifying period reads "300 years" in the claim and three years in the reasons. [Para 15][Para 152] The company's name is spelled four ways in Khmer (ឈេហ្វរ៉ុន, ឈហ្វរ៉ុន, ឈេហូរ៉ុន, ឈហូរ៉ុន) and the English form survives only as "CHEVRON (CAMBOD14)LMITED". [Para 1][Para 141] The number of sites is 24 in the establishment list and 26 in the employer's submissions, and the representativeness finding is garbled by stray digits. [Para 115][Para 135][Para 264] The order skips issue 6. [Para 305]
