# Award No. 059/15 — Teng Sun Limited (Arbitration Council, 17 March 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, chosen by the employer; An Non, chosen by the workers; Kong Phallak, chair chosen by the two) · Doc ID 139156

## 1. Facts
Teng Sun Limited (ក្រុមហ៊ុន តេង ស៊ុន លីមីធីត) sews bags on National Road 41 at Veal Vong village, Sen Dei commune, Samrong Tong district, Kampong Speu, and told the panel it employs about 720 workers. [Para 1][Para 38][Para 39] On 02 February 2015 the provincial Department of Labour received a complaint from the Federation of ASEAN Workers' Unions, whose local union at the factory was registered on 24 October 2014 and claimed 71 members. [Para 10][Para 32][Para 42] Conciliation on 06 February 2015 covered 13 demands, settled 10 and left three; the hearing was on 05 March 2015 and settled nothing further. [Para 11][Para 16][Para 9] Two disputed acts underlie the live issues: the employer stopped paying a monthly incentive bonus from October 2014, and it issues three-month fixed-duration contracts where the workers want six. [Para 45][Para 57][Para 132]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the monthly incentive bonus again, as it did before October 2014? [Para 43][Para 72]
2. Whether the employer must pay a midday meal allowance of 2,000 riel a day, where it currently pays 1,000 riel? [Para 52][Para 97]
3. Whether the employer must convert three-month fixed-duration contracts into six-month fixed-duration contracts? [Para 57][Para 112]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The bonus was paid every month from February 2014, so they want it as before; 1,000 riel cannot buy enough food now that prices have risen, and neighbouring factories already pay 2,000 riel; three-month contracts are too short, workers fear non-renewal, and six-month contracts would bring them more of the 5% severance at each expiry. [Para 48][Para 54][Para 59][Para 60]
**Employer (respondent):** The bonus cannot be restored because the company is newly opened and has lost heavily through strikes and a lack of orders, while the 2015 base wage has risen to USD 128 a month; it cannot pay 2,000 riel because it has already lost more than USD 02 million since opening, and workers can earn over USD 50 a month with overtime; and the Labour Law fixes no contract duration, and it has never dismissed or refused to renew a worker without serious misconduct. [Para 49][Para 55][Para 56][Para 61]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three demands refused, the second without any consideration of its merits; nothing was awarded. [Para 139][Para 140][Para 141]

On representation, the panel applied Article 268 of the Labour Law, under which a professional organisation obtains the rights the law recognises once its statutes and the list of its officers are deposited and registered with the Ministry, registration being deemed within two months if the Ministry does not answer. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 65] Following awards 23/14 Volume Co Mo Ei Ke Vil and 32/14 Yai Sing Plastic, it held that those rights include the union's right to represent its members before the panel; the ASEAN Workers' Union was registered, its officers attended, and it was therefore the claimant. [Para 66][Para 69][Para 70][Para 71]

On issue 1, the panel treated "past practice" (ការអនុវត្ត កន្លងមក) as a legal term of art: an actual practice, accepted by both parties, repeated regularly and consistently over time, and need not be written. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 81][Para 82] Adopting award 95/09 Tak Fhat, and noting earlier awards on annual leave, wage payment, bonuses (18/07 Trihung Galaxy) and meal allowances, it found the bonus was paid every month from February to October 2014 and that both parties acknowledged the practice as regular. [Para 84][Para 85][Para 88] It then applied 95/09's holding that a past practice may lawfully end where the parties agree to end it, agree a new practice in its place, or the circumstances founding it change. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 91] Point 2 of the agreement of 24 October 2014 between the employer and three unions provides that the monthly incentive bonus is abolished from October 2014; both parties admitted signing it, so the panel held the practice had lawfully ended and no obligation to continue survived. [Para 46][Para 49][Para 50][Para 93][Para 94][Para 95]

On issue 2, the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, agreement, internal rule or past practice obliging the employer to pay 2,000 riel, classified the demand as one over interests, and applied Article 96 of the Labour Law with Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 SAK BY: an interest award under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 SAK BY replaces the collective agreement for a year and removes the right to strike on that subject, so only the most representative union may bring such a claim. [Para 98][Para 99][Para 100][Para 101][Para 103][Para 104][Para 105] The claimant union was not the most representative in an enterprise of about 720 workers, so the panel declined to consider the demand. [Para 106][Para 108][Para 109][Para 110][Para 111]

On issue 3, the panel read Article 65 of the Labour Law with Articles 311, 336, 337 and 664 of the 2007 Civil Code: a contract arises from the meeting of an offer and an acceptance, and no party may compel another to accept a form, type or content it does not agree to. [Para 112][Para 114][Para 117][Para 122][Para 124][Para 126] It held that the workers' request for six-month contracts was consistent with the spirit of the law, being an exercise of their right to propose, and that the employer's refusal was equally lawful, being an exercise of its right to answer; neither side could impose its term on the other, and a contract made under compulsion may be void. [Para 134][Para 135][Para 136][Para 137][Para 138][Para 139]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A regularly repeated benefit that both parties acknowledge becomes a past practice, but a past practice ends lawfully once the parties sign an agreement abolishing it from a stated month; after that the panel will not order the benefit restored, however long it was paid before. [Para 82][Para 88][Para 90][Para 95]

A demand to raise a meal allowance above the rate actually paid is a dispute over interests, and the panel will not consider it unless the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise, because an interest award replaces the collective agreement and removes the right to strike. [Para 99][Para 104][Para 110]

No party to an employment contract may compel the other to accept a contract duration it does not agree to: both the workers' proposal of six months and the employer's insistence on three are lawful exercises of their respective rights, and the panel will order neither. [Para 124][Para 136][Para 137][Para 139]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's account of the bonus scale by points (one point USD 2 up to seven points USD 24), and its remark that the workers' request for longer contracts was not contrary to the spirit of the law, were not necessary to the outcome. [Para 43][Para 134]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The meal-allowance figure is printed as 2,000 riel throughout the claim and the order [Para 52][Para 97][Para 111] but the workers' reason is printed as "ប្រាក់ចំនួន ១០ រៀល" — 10 riel — at [Para 54], which is plainly an OCR loss of 1,000. The issue question at [Para 97] carries intruded fragments ("២០១៧ ថ្ងៃទី ១៦ ខែកក្កដា") inside the sum claimed. The conversion demand is printed as "២០២ ខែម្តង" at [Para 112] where three months is meant, and as three months at [Para 57], while the parties' agreed position at [Para 57] is two-month contracts for probationary and three-month for full-status workers. Paragraph 105 carries a block of intruded English ("evernments in minitionary of their provicion …"). Prakas No. 099 is dated "21 April 2019 2004" at [Para 103]. The chair is printed គង់ ផល្ហៈ in the header [Para 1] and គង់ ផល្លៈ in the signature block [Para 143], which also carries a stray "022". Paragraph 61 repeats "ភាគី និយោជក បាន បញ្ជាក់ ថា" twice, and [Para 139] reads "និយោជកម្ពុជា" for the employer. The company's own documents describe the employer as employing 720 workers at [Para 39] while the union claims 71 members at [Para 42]; no payroll evidence settles the ratio.
