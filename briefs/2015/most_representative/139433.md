# Award No. 247/15 — In Han Sung Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 September 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, An Non, chair Pen Bun Chhia) · Doc ID 139433

## 1. Facts
In Han Sung Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a garment factory on National Road 4 at Ang Keo village, Chom Chao, Pou Senchey district, Phnom Penh, employed 224 workers. [Para 36] Its factory union, registered by certificate 2350 of 26 December 2011 and with a recognised second-term leadership under letter 619 of 16 June 2015, had 73 members — under a third of the workforce. [Para 37] [Para 38] The federation filed five demands on 27 July 2015; conciliation on 31 August 2015 settled four and left one, the demand for a 2,000 riel midday meal allowance, which both sides agreed the company does not pay at all. [Para 11] [Para 39]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a midday meal allowance of 2,000 riel a day to the workers? [Para 39]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that the midday meal is essential food for the workers' health and the energy that drives the factory's production line; that providing it encourages effort and helps the company attract workers; and that about 20 other factories in the area already pay such an allowance, a list of them being filed. [Para 40] [Para 42] [Para 43]
**Employer (respondent):** that it has no capacity to meet the demand. [Para 44]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider the claim, so no allowance was ordered. [Para 61]

The panel first classified the demand. It found no provision of the Labour Law, no collective agreement, no internal rule (registered as 010 of 17 January 2012) and no past practice obliging the employer to pay a midday meal allowance of 2,000 riel a day, so the claim sought a benefit above the statutory floor and was an interests dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍), not a rights dispute. [Para 46] [Para 47] [Para 48]

That classification decided the case. Under Article 96 of the Labour Law and Prakas 305 art 9(1), the union with the most representative status may require an employer to negotiate a collective agreement applying to all the workers it represents; under Prakas 099 art 43(1), an award resolving an interests dispute replaces the collective agreement, binds every worker in the enterprise for one year and extinguishes the right to strike over that demand. An interests claim may therefore be heard only from the most representative union, or from unions together representing more than half the workforce. Here the claimant union held 73 of 224 memberships and was not the most representative, so it lacked standing and the panel refused to examine the merits. [Para 52] [Para 55] [Para 56] [Para 58] [Para 59]

The evidence that other factories pay the allowance, and the argument that it would improve health, morale and recruitment, were left unweighed once standing failed; the panel did not hold that such a payment would be unlawful, only that this claimant could not ask for it. [Para 43] [Para 60]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for an allowance the Labour Law does not require, and which no collective agreement, internal rule or established practice provides, is an interests dispute; because an award on an interests dispute displaces the collective agreement, binds all workers for a year and removes the right to strike over the demand, it may be brought only by the most representative union in the enterprise or by unions commanding more than half the workforce. A union with a third of the membership is refused a hearing whatever the merits of its demand. [Para 48] [Para 56] [Para 59]

**Obiter dicta:** that approximately 20 neighbouring factories pay a midday meal allowance, which the panel recorded without treating neighbouring practice as a source of legal obligation. [Para 43]

[Para 1] [Para 13] [Para 46] The respondent's name is printed as អ៉ិន ហាន ស៊ុង, អ៊ិន ហាន ស៊ុង and, in the union's own title, អ៊ិន ហាន ស៊ុង ហ្គាមេន … ខូអិលធីឌីជូល. [Para 1] [Para 37] The federation's complaint letter 113/15 is dated 27 July 2015 in one entry and 27 January 2015 in the duplicate, where its author is also misprinted as a bank union federation. [Para 26] The union's address is given in Khan Dangkao while the factory sits in Khan Pou Senchey. [Para 1] The claim amount appears once as "១ ២០០០ រៀល" and the order is set out twice in near-identical wording. [Para 45] [Para 60] [Para 61] Individual names are reduced to role or initial per the briefing convention.
