# Award No. 224/15 — Akhen Tech PTY Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 8 September 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Pen Bun Chea chair) · Doc ID 139419

## 1. Facts
Akhen Tech PTY Co., Ltd cuts and sews garments at Canadia Garden, Chom Chao, Por Sen Chey, Phnom Penh, and runs two branches, branch 1 with about 1,000 workers and branch 2 with about 350. [Para 1][Para 48] Seven workers authorised the union federation to act for them by letter of 10 July 2015, though the letter carried only identity numbers and thumbprints and no names; one of the seven, Ms C, had neither. [Para 49][Para 51] Conciliation on nine demands settled seven and left two: an increase in the travel allowance and a higher overtime meal allowance. [Para 12][Para 13]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay USD 14 a month in travel and accommodation allowance, that is USD 7 on top of the USD 7 already paid? [Para 55][Para 67]
2. Whether the employer must pay 2,000 riel per hour of the overtime meal allowance rather than 2,000 riel per occasion? [Para 87]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** market prices, rent, water, electricity and transport have all risen, and neighbouring factories pay more than USD 7 a month, so the allowance should be doubled to USD 14; the overtime meal allowance should be paid hourly so that it adds to the daily food cost, as other companies pay above the legal rate. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 61]
**Employer (respondent):** it cannot pay these demands and asks to apply the law only; it already pays USD 7 of travel and accommodation under Ministry Notification No. 230, and it pays 2,000 riel for the overtime meal, which is above the 1,000 riel first fixed and matches Notification No. 041/11, so it could not lawfully be asked for more. [Para 6][Para 59][Para 62][Para 63][Para 66]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider both claims because the claimant was not a union with the standing to bring an interest dispute. [Para 86][Para 96][Para 97][Para 98]

The travel claim turned on Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012, whose first point fixes USD 7 a month for travel and accommodation and excludes workers for whom the employer provides transport or lodging. [Para 67] Both sides agreed the company pays exactly USD 7, no law, collective agreement, internal rule, contract or past practice entitles anyone to USD 14, and the panel therefore held the demand to be a benefit above the law, that is an interest dispute. [Para 68][Para 70][Para 71][Para 72]

The overtime meal claim turned on point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011, which gives a worker who volunteers for overtime 2,000 riel a day or one free meal. [Para 88] The company pays 2,000 riel each time overtime is worked, and no instrument entitles the workers to 2,000 riel an hour, so this too was an interest dispute. [Para 89][Para 91][Para 92]

Article 96 of the Labour Law defines a collective agreement and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 reserves the right to request one to the most representative union; Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 provides that an interest award stands in place of a collective agreement for one year and removes the right to strike over the same subject, which is why the panel may decide such a dispute only where the union before it holds more than half the workers of the enterprise. [Para 73][Para 78][Para 79][Para 80]

Here the claimants were seven workers out of a workforce of roughly 1,350 across two branches, and the panel found the union was not the most representative in the company, so the claimants lacked the legal character to bring an interest dispute and both claims were refused. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 85]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a demand asks for more than the Labour Law, a Ministry notification, a collective agreement or established practice already secures, it is an interest dispute, and the panel will decide it only if the union bringing it represents more than half the workers of the enterprise; a minority union's claim is refused without any examination of its merits, however strong the economic case. [Para 72][Para 80][Para 85]

The lawful travel and accommodation allowance is USD 7 a month under point 1 of Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012, and the lawful overtime meal allowance is 2,000 riel a day or one free meal under point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011; an employer that pays those amounts complies with the law and cannot be ordered to pay more at the suit of a union without majority standing. [Para 67][Para 88][Para 90]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that an employer which pays above the statutory rate invites conflict when a new notification or law later sets a lower figure, apparently prompted by the company's own account of raising the meal allowance from 1,000 to 2,000 riel, was not necessary to the refusal. [Para 62][Para 65]

- The employer is named អេឃីន ថិច ភីធី អ៉ី អិលធីឌី on the face [Para 1] and in the report [Para 8], but the summary of dispute calls the factory "រោច ចក្រ អេឃីន" [Para 22] and a union letter is addressed to "L អេឃីន" [Para 40].
- The claimed overtime meal figure is printed as "២០ រៀល ក្នុង មួយ ម៉ោង" at [Para 5] and as "២០ រៀល ក្នុង មួយ ថ្ងៃ" at [Para 89], while the claim as reasoned and decided is 2,000 riel an hour against 2,000 riel a day [Para 60][Para 87][Para 98]; the zeros are lost throughout.
- The travel figure is printed three ways: USD 14 [Para 67][Para 86], "១ ១៤ ដុល្លារ" [Para 67] and "៤ ១៤ ដុល្លារ" [Para 97].
- Notification No. 230 is dated 25 July 2015 in the evidence list [Para 23] but 25 July 2012 in the reasons [Para 67]; the conciliation is dated 14 August 2015 [Para 12] while its report bears 17 August and reached the secretariat on 18 August [Para 8][Para 13].
- The panel's constitution is recorded as 19 August with the year corrupted to 2022 [Para 14]; the hearing is 25 August 2015 at 8.30 a.m. [Para 10][Para 15], after conciliation on 14 August [Para 12] and a report dated 17 August [Para 13], leaving the panel four working days for the case.
- The authorisation letter is said to lack all names and to carry only identity numbers and thumbprints [Para 49], yet six claimants are treated as named in it [Para 54]; one claimant, Ms C, is recorded as having neither number nor thumbprint [Para 51]. The award never explains how the six were identified.
