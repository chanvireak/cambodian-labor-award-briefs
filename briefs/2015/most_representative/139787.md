# Award No. 343/14 — Global Apparel Limited (Arbitration Council, 14 January 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Nan, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, chair appointed by both) · Doc ID 139787

## 1. Facts
Global Apparel Limited, a garment factory at Tuol Pongro, Chom Chao, Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh, employed about 2,354 workers under internal work rules checked by the labour inspectorate on 14 June 2014. [Para 32][Para 39][Para 40] On 3 December 2014 the union filed eight demands; conciliation on 5 December left six unresolved, and one more was settled at the hearing, leaving five. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 25] All five concern monthly allowances and their deduction: travel and accommodation money, the regular attendance bonus, overtime meal money, and the cutting of the attendance and incentive bonuses when a worker takes leave. [Para 45][Para 50][Para 55][Para 59][Para 68]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a travel and accommodation allowance of US$15 per month rather than the US$7 it pays? [Para 45][Para 74]
2. Whether the employer must pay a regular attendance bonus of US$20 per month rather than US$10? [Para 50][Para 93]
4. Whether the employer must pay overtime meal money of 4,000 riel per day rather than 2,000 riel? [Para 55][Para 104]
5. Whether the employer must stop deducting the regular attendance bonus when a worker takes leave, allowing three days' absence without deduction and cutting pro rata only from the fourth? [Para 59][Para 115]
6. Whether the employer must stop deducting the incentive bonus when a worker takes one day of annual leave? [Para 68][Para 129]

Issue 3, lunch money of 4,000 riel per day, was settled at the hearing and not decided. [Para 24]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** US$7 does not cover a rented room and travel, and neighbouring employers such as Van Sen Chuling Cambodia Co., Ltd. already pay more; factories nearby pay a US$20 attendance bonus; market food prices have risen; other factories allow three days' leave before cutting the attendance bonus; and because the annual leave day is already deducted from their balance, cutting the incentive bonus as well takes the same day twice. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 52][Para 57][Para 66][Para 72]
**Employer (respondent):** it already pays benefits above the Ministry's notifications, including a US$10 incentive bonus, and orders are falling; it pays the statutory US$7 travel allowance, the statutory US$10 attendance bonus and the statutory 2,000 riel overtime meal money; it maintains its existing practice on bonus deductions; and the incentive bonus is discretionary, cut by US$5 for a day of annual leave and by the full US$10 for a day of legal leave not charged to annual leave. [Para 48][Para 53][Para 58][Para 67][Para 73]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider all five claims, holding that the claimant union was not the most representative union in the enterprise and so had no standing to bring an interest dispute; nothing was ordered against the employer. [Para 90][Para 92][Para 145]

Each claim was first measured against the applicable notification. On travel, Prakas No. 230 K.B/S.J.N of 25 July 2012 point 1 sets US$7 a month and the employer pays US$7, so the statutory duty is discharged, and no law, collective agreement, agreement, internal rule or past practice requires US$15. [Para 74][Para 76][Para 77] On the attendance bonus, point 2 of the same Prakas sets a minimum of US$10 and the employer pays US$10. [Para 93][Para 96] On overtime meal money, point 2 of Prakas No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 gives 2,000 riel a day or one free meal whatever the hours worked, and the employer pays 2,000 riel for overtime from 3:15 to 5:15 in the afternoon. [Para 104][Para 106][Para 107] On the attendance bonus deduction, the same US$10 floor applies, and the employer's two-stage scheme — one unauthorised day in each half-month free, US$5 for a second, nothing deducted for paid leave — contradicts no rule. [Para 115][Para 116][Para 121] Each demand was accordingly classified as an interest dispute, a claim to improve terms rather than to enforce a right. [Para 78][Para 99][Para 110]

On standing, the panel reasoned from Prakas No. 099 article 43: an award resolving an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement, binds every worker in the enterprise for one year, and extinguishes their right to strike on that demand in future. [Para 85][Para 86] A panel can therefore decide such a claim only where the union before it is the most representative union in the enterprise, or several unions together represent more than half the workforce. [Para 87] Here four employee delegates out of twenty-four filed the complaint, and the panel found the complainant was not the most representative union, following its earlier awards that declined interest claims on that ground. [Para 88][Para 89][Para 90] The same refusal was applied to each of the five.

Issue 6 also received a merits answer. The incentive bonus is not required by the Labour Law; under Article 2 the employer's right to manage and run the enterprise covers the grant of such a bonus and the conditions attached to it, so reducing it where the 26-day attendance condition is not met is a reasonable exercise of that right and does not breach the law. [Para 140][Para 141][Para 142][Para 143]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A panel will not decide an interest dispute — a claim to improve terms beyond what the law, a collective agreement, internal rules or established practice already provide — unless the union bringing it is the most representative union in the enterprise, or a group of unions representing more than half the workers, because the resulting award displaces the collective agreement for a year and extinguishes the right to strike on that demand. [Para 86][Para 87] Where the employer already pays the minimum fixed by the Ministry's notification, a demand for more is an interest claim and not a rights claim, and is refused for want of standing however reasonable the comparison with other factories. [Para 77][Para 96][Para 107] A bonus the Labour Law does not require, conditioned on twenty-six days of full attendance, may be reduced when the condition is not met, that being within the management prerogative preserved by Article 2. [Para 142][Para 143]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the claimant's comparison with neighbouring factories [Para 48][Para 52] and the fall in orders [Para 49] did not affect the statutory minimum, and its observation that the one-year effect of an interest award removes the workers' right to strike on the same demand in future [Para 86], are explanatory rather than necessary to the refusal.
