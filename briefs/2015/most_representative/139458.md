# Award No. 281/15 — Capitol Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 17 December 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Virak for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139458

## 1. Facts
Capitol Co., Ltd carries passengers by coach from Phnom Penh, employing about 200 drivers; 39 drivers transferred their rights to the Labour Union of Cambodia and 41 were complainants, of whom 13 attended the hearing. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 69][Para 72] Six drivers on undetermined-duration contracts picked up passengers along the Phnom Penh–Bavel route on 26, 27 and 28 September 2015 and kept the fares without telling the company; the company terminated all six by notice on 29 September 2015, and no severance has been paid. [Para 77][Para 79][Para 83][Para 84][Para 130][Para 131]

## 2. Issues in dispute
The conciliation left two points unresolved of three claimed. [Para 14][Para 15]
1. Whether the employer must take the six drivers back and pay their wages from the day of termination to the day of return? [Para 75][Para 130]
2. Whether the employer must pay a further USD 10 of trip allowance on top of the 50,000 riel already paid for each trip? [Para 106][Para 162]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissal was unequal treatment and a reprisal: all six had carried roadside passengers before, yet the company had only ever warned drivers, entered a note in the trip book, fined them or made them buy back tickets — and one driver who did the same on 29 August 2015 was merely made to pay 44,000 riel. [Para 86][Para 87][Para 88] Since the July 2015 strike and the protest over the suspended union leaders they had been given almost no trips while non-strikers were kept busy, which shows union discrimination. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 92] The 50,000 riel trip allowance is too small because drivers must buy cleaning materials out of it. [Para 108][Para 109]
**Employer (respondent):** the six knew the practice was forbidden and had been warned repeatedly, yet persisted; the dismissal rests on serious misconduct under art 83, not on discrimination. [Para 94][Para 96][Para 101][Para 133] Falling trip numbers reflect fewer passengers after the July strike, not allocation, and basic wages continued to be paid. [Para 99][Para 100] The 10,000 riel rise in 2008 already covered cleaning materials, and competition leaves no room for more. [Para 110][Para 111][Para 112]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both claims were refused: the six drivers were not reinstated and the USD 10 trip allowance was not ordered. [Para 178][Para 179]

The panel first rejected the employer's objection that this was an individual dispute, applying art 302 to hold that a dispute between one employer and 41 drivers over working conditions, capable of disrupting the enterprise or social peace, meets all three statutory conditions, and that the employer bore the burden of proving otherwise. [Para 118][Para 121][Para 122][Para 123][Para 125][Para 127][Para 128]

On issue 1 it separated discrimination from the lawfulness of the dismissal. Discrimination is proved by the accusing party, and the panel weighed the hearing explanations against the documents. [Para 140][Para 141][Para 143] It found the workers had shown no fact indicating unequal treatment or union reprisal and had produced no supporting evidence. [Para 145][Para 146][Para 147][Para 148] On the merits, art 2 of the internal rules, approved by the labour inspectorate on 10 June 2015, forbids receiving personal passengers in working hours without authorisation. [Para 104][Para 151] The six admitted carrying roadside passengers, keeping the fares and not reporting them; the panel characterised this as taking personal profit from an opportunity, which falls within art 83's serious misconduct of misappropriation. [Para 153][Para 154][Para 155][Para 157][Para 159] The dismissal was therefore not contrary to the Labour Law. [Para 160] The driver fined 44,000 riel was distinguished: his two passengers had telephoned the company to buy its service, so he had committed no dismissible fault. [Para 97][Para 98]

On issue 2 no law, collective agreement, internal rule, contract, agreement or past practice obliges the employer to add USD 10 per trip, so the claim sought a benefit the law does not fix and was an interests dispute. [Para 163][Para 164][Para 165] Under art 96 and art 9 of Prakas No. 305, and art 43 of Prakas No. 099, an award on an interests dispute replaces the collective agreement for a year and binds all workers, so the panel may hear one only from the union with most-representative status; this complainant union was not, and the panel declined to consider the claim. [Para 166][Para 169][Para 170][Para 171][Para 175][Para 176]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A driver who collects passengers along the route and keeps the fares without notifying the employer commits the serious misconduct of misappropriation under art 83, and dismissal is lawful where the internal rules — duly approved by the labour inspectorate — forbid receiving personal passengers in working hours without authorisation; proof that another driver was treated more leniently does not by itself establish that the sanction was discriminatory, because discrimination must be pleaded and proved by the worker, not inferred from the outcome. [Para 148][Para 157][Para 159][Para 160]
A claim for an allowance above what the law, a collective agreement, the internal rules or established practice provides is an interests dispute, which only the most representative union in the enterprise may bring. [Para 164][Para 172][Para 175]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's reading of art 302, holding a multi-worker claim over conditions to be a collective dispute and putting the burden on an employer who denies it, was decided before the merits and did not change either order. [Para 122][Para 128]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award date is 17 December 2015 on its face [Para 1], but the registration receipt for the union in this file is dated 11 November 2015 and the union's own letter of 5 July 2015 refers to a prior Council order, so the procedural chronology is only partly reconstructable [Para 42][Para 52].
- The panel is said to have been constituted on "10 November 2023 2015" [Para 16], and art 43 of Prakas No. 099 is dated "21 April 2019 2004" [Para 170].
- The trip allowance is stated as USD 10 claimed [Para 5][Para 162] but the operative order prints "1,000 US dollars" [Para 179], and paragraph 20 of the reasons is garbled to the point of being unreadable [Para 20].
- The worker count moves between 39, 41 and 13 attendees [Para 69][Para 70][Para 72], and one passage says "40 bus drivers" where 41 is meant [Para 125].
- The company name appears as "Capitol", "Capitol Co., Ltd", "Kapito" and "Kapitol" in transliteration [Para 1][Para 15][Para 35][Para 67].
- Individual drivers' names are printed inconsistently ("Norn Vanna" / "Non Vanna", "Nyan Sovannara" / "Nyanh Sovannara") and are redacted here [Para 75][Para 130].
