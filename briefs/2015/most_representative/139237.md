# Award No. 158/15 — Kon Thek (Cambodia) Khob Pher Sen (Arbitration Council, 21 July 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Run Saray, chair) · non-binding award · Doc ID 139237

## 1. Facts
Kon Thek manufactures electronic components in Takeo province and, on the workers' account, employed about 3,000 people. On 12 June 2015 the provincial labour department received a complaint from six union federations raising 20 demands; 13 were settled in conciliation on 22 June 2015 and the remaining seven reached the Council, which renumbered them 01 to 07 from the original points 04, 05, 08, 09, 13, 19 and 20. Only 13 workers attended the hearing, and only those 13 were treated as claimants. The employer did not appear and the panel proceeded in its absence. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 27][Para 40][Para 45][Para 46][Para 119]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a skills allowance of USD 30 a month to welding and electrical technicians, equal to that paid to machine technicians? [Para 47]
2. Whether the employer must pay a travel and accommodation allowance of USD 15 a month? [Para 57]
3. Whether the employer must pay a midday meal allowance of 2,000 riel a day? [Para 64]
4. Whether the employer must pay a meal allowance of 2,000 riel per hour of overtime? [Para 68]
5. Whether the employer's requirement that workers use a card and observe a time limit to enter the toilet is a lawful exercise of management? [Para 200]
6. Whether the employer must repay an acting allowance of USD 15 a month from February to June 2015 to 15 team leaders at building A1 and honour its promise of USD 25? [Para 222]
7. Whether the employer must preserve wages, the attendance bonus and the travel and accommodation allowance during the strike of 11 June to 2 July 2015? [Para 245]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer pays unequal skills allowances for work of comparable value, since electrical and welding work is done at height and harms eyesight; the travel, meal and overtime meal allowances lag behind rising rents, food and neighbouring factories paying USD 10 to 15; the toilet card rations access to one card per group of 25 to 40 workers and a five-minute limit, causing kidney and bladder illness; and a February 2015 promise of USD 25 a month was honoured for only 15 of 30 team leaders. [Para 49][Para 52][Para 60][Para 77][Para 86][Para 90]

**Employer (respondent):** the employer was absent and filed no submission, so the panel recorded that it had waived its right of reply on each demand and accepted the workers' assertions as uncontradicted. [Para 138][Para 157][Para 178][Para 187]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to stop using cards and time limits for toilet access, and to pay Ms K, a team leader at building A1, USD 15 a month in acting allowance for February to June 2015 and USD 25 a month thereafter; all five other demands were refused. [Para 217][Para 244][Para 285][Para 288][Para 289]

On issue 1 the panel applied article 106 of the Labour Law, which requires equal wages only where the work, the professional skill and the output are equal, and held all three conditions unmet: machine, welding and electrical technicians sit in different departments under different supervisors, with different skills and results. It accepted that skills and acting allowances count as part of wages for overtime purposes, following Award No. 74/08, but that did not make the roles comparable. [Para 121][Para 122][Para 137][Para 140][Para 142][Para 145][Para 148][Para 154]

On issues 2, 3 and 4 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, agreement, internal rule or past practice obliging the employer to pay USD 15, 2,000 riel a day or 2,000 riel an hour. Each demand therefore exceeded the law and was characterised as an interest dispute; under article 96 of the Labour Law, article 9(1) of Prakas No. 305 SChBy and article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099 SChBy, only a most representative union may bring such a dispute, and the 13 claimants were not one, so the panel declined to consider them. [Para 159][Para 160][Para 161][Para 170][Para 174][Para 176][Para 180][Para 185]

On issue 5 the panel balanced article 2 of the Labour Law, which gives the employer the right to organise and manage the enterprise, against the worker's right to use the toilet. Following Award No. 275/13 (Kala Khem Investment), it held that the management right cannot override that right, that a single card per group of 25 to 40 workers plus a five-minute limit is an unreasonable restriction capable of damaging health, and that discipline for abuse is the lawful route instead. Under article 34 of Prakas No. 099 it ordered the practice stopped. [Para 203][Para 210][Para 212][Para 214][Para 216][Para 217]

On issue 6 the panel turned to articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code: a contract arises from offer and acceptance, and the employer's February 2015 promise of USD 25 a month after a one-week probation bound it. Because the workers identified only one of the fifteen affected team leaders, Ms K, and never filed the other fourteen names by the evidence deadline, the panel decided the point for her alone, ordering the USD 15 shortfall for February to June 2015 and the full USD 25 thereafter. [Para 221][Para 223][Para 226][Para 234][Para 239][Para 240][Para 244]

On issue 7 the panel held the strike unlawful: it began on the evening of 11 June 2015 with no prior notice as article 324 requires and without exhausting the conciliation and arbitration procedure under article 320, so articles 332 and 334 did not assist the workers. Wages, the attendance bonus and the allowances were therefore not payable for 11 June to 2 July 2015, and the panel also noted the workers filed no evidence of the deductions alleged. [Para 246][Para 263][Para 264][Para 265][Para 267][Para 279][Para 283]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer's right to manage under article 2 does not extend to rationing toilet access by card and clock, since a restriction that makes workers wait and forgo bodily functions harms health and can be met instead by disciplinary measures; and a unilateral promise of an allowance made to induce a probation, once its stated condition is satisfied, binds the employer as a contract under article 337 of the Civil Code. [Para 210][Para 212][Para 240]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's holding that skills and acting allowances form part of "wage" under articles 102 and 103 so as to enter the overtime calculation, following Awards 78/13 and 107/13, was unnecessary because the article 106 conditions failed on the facts. [Para 131][Para 132][Para 134]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Paragraph 22 gives the panel's appointment as both 2019 and 2015 in the same sentence. The award states 13 workers attended the hearing at paragraph 45 and 12 at paragraph 218. Paragraph 243 orders arrears "from February 2014 to June 2014" where the whole case concerns 2015, and the dispositive order at paragraph 289 repeats the year twice. The employer's name appears variously as Kon Thek, Khom Thek, Khob Pher Sen and Khou Phe Re Sen, and the numeral "20" is scattered through the text as noise, so that the claim reads "USD 20 15" at paragraphs 222, 235 and 244. Paragraph 164 is a bare ellipsis, paragraphs 201 to 202 are fragments of article 2, and paragraph 230 dissolves into meaningless Khmer in the middle of the Civil Code reasoning, so the reliance on Award No. 271/14 is only partly legible. The strike end date is given as 2 July 2015 in the reasoning but the workers' own account says they returned on 3 July. [Para 22][Para 45][Para 218][Para 230][Para 243][Para 289]
