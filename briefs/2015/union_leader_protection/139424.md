# Award No. 229/15 — Ri Jen Footwear (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 28 September 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chiv Phirom for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Tang Tri chair) · binding award by agreement of 31 August 2015 · Doc ID 139424

## 1. Facts
Ri Jen Footwear (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a shoe factory in Ang Snuol district, Kandal province, employed about 325 workers. [Para 59][Para 60] After a strike from 16 to 30 June 2015 demanding termination for 295 workers because of a change of shareholder, 207 workers transferred their rights to the Solidarity Labour Union Federation of Cambodia and conciliation on 13 August 2015 left five claims unresolved. [Para 63][Para 15][Para 16][Para 170][Para 172] The two claimants left in issue 1 were dismissed on 2 July 2015 after they had signed the 1 July 2015 settlement and then changed their minds and declined the four compensation items. [Para 84][Para 123][Para 158][Para 161] The parties agreed the award would be binding. [Para 22][Para 243]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr S and a second worker (name redacted) and pay them all wages from the date of dismissal to the date of return? [Para 66][Para 122][Para 123]
2. Whether the employer must rehire 205 workers under new fixed-duration contracts, or else pay them five items of termination compensation plus dismissal indemnity? [Para 101][Para 178][Para 181]
3. Whether the employer must pay the shortfall in notice compensation, calculated on the average wage of the last 12 months divided by 26 and multiplied by the days of notice owed? [Para 108][Para 217]
4. Whether the employer must modify the roof, which makes the workplace excessively hot? [Para 113][Para 226]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the two were dismissed without valid reason or prior notice although they were union leaders elected on 20 June 2015 and notified to the employer on 22 June 2015; they refused the four-item compensation and asked to keep working; the 205 should be rehired on new contracts or paid five items plus indemnity; notice pay was wrongly computed on the minimum wage; and the building is unbearably hot. [Para 70][Para 104][Para 109][Para 114]
**Employer (respondent):** the two were terminated at their own written request under points 3 and 5 of the 1 July 2015 agreement; it knew nothing of any union leadership and never received the notification letter; the new owner may hire or not hire after termination; no law requires a 12-month average for notice pay; and it will fix the roof in October 2016. [Para 71][Para 95][Para 107][Para 111][Para 235]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement under issues 1 and 2 was refused; the employer must pay the shortfall in notice compensation at the 12-month average and take immediate measures (fans, extractors or air conditioning) to bring workplace heat to an acceptable level; issue 5 was not decided, the parties agreeing it was no longer disputed. The award is binding and final at once. [Para 240][Para 241][Para 242][Para 121]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 293(1) of the Labour Law and Articles 3(3) and 4 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, which protect union leaders and candidates against dismissal but require the union to notify the employer of the protected names by any available means. [Para 126][Para 128][Para 134][Para 135] It found the letter of 22 June 2015 had been handed to a security guard of the GSS security company who signed the receipt; as he was not an employee of Ri Jen and had no administrative or legal function, the receipt was not notice to the employer, and the union tried no other channel such as registered post or the commune authority. [Para 147][Para 149][Para 150][Para 153] The protection therefore failed. [Para 154] The termination was also not unilateral: the two had signed the 1 July 2015 minute, later declined its four items and asked to stay, but the employer acted on their earlier written request, so Article 74 did not apply; the balance of notice pay under point 1 remained due. [Para 156][Para 160][Para 175][Para 177]

On issue 2 the panel held that terminations made at the workers' own request and accepted by the employer are terminations by agreement, so Article 89 requires no dismissal indemnity, which is owed only where the employer acts unilaterally. [Para 181][Para 187][Para 189] A new fixed-duration contract is a fresh contract requiring offer and acceptance under Articles 65 and 664 of the Labour Law and Articles 311 and 336–337 of the Civil Code; no party can force another to contract. [Para 190][Para 192][Para 200][Para 213]

On issue 3 the panel applied Article 77 of the Labour Law and Article 34 of Prakas No. 099, following earlier awards that notice compensation uses the average monthly wage of the previous 12 months including overtime and bonuses, not the minimum wage, which the employer had wrongly used. [Para 217][Para 219][Para 221][Para 224][Para 225][Para 241]

On issue 4 both parties accepted the building was genuinely hot, and the employer had confirmed as much to the ILO and in its own letter. [Para 232][Para 233] Article 229 of the Labour Law and Articles 1 and 3 of Prakas No. 147 of 11 June 2002 require the employer to keep the thermal environment compatible with workers' health and to use every means to cool the workplace; the panel found the duty unmet and ordered immediate measures. [Para 226][Para 227][Para 228][Para 237][Para 238][Para 242]

**Dissent:** arbitrator An Non, appointed by the worker party, dissented on issue 1 only, recording that the employer had no Article 74 ground and acted contrary to point 2 of the 1 July 2015 agreement, since the two workers had withdrawn their requests and refused the compensation; he would have ordered reinstatement and back pay under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 246][Para 252][Para 258][Para 259][Para 260]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Union-leader protection under Article 293(1) of the Labour Law and Articles 3(3) and 4 of Prakas No. 305 depends on the union actually notifying the employer of the protected names by any available means; leaving the letter with a contractor's security guard who has no administrative or legal function does not engage the protection, and the union must then use another channel such as registered post or the commune authority. A termination made at the worker's own written request and accepted by the employer is a termination by agreement, not a unilateral dismissal, so neither Article 74 grounds nor the Article 89 dismissal indemnity arises, and no party can compel the other to enter a new fixed-duration contract. Notice compensation is computed on the average wage of the previous 12 months divided by 26 and multiplied by the days of notice owed. [Para 148][Para 153][Para 188][Para 213][Para 223]

**Obiter dicta:** the employer's stated intention to fix the roof in October 2016 was noted but not made a condition, the order being for immediate measures; and the refusal to sign new contracts was lawful independently of the change of ownership. [Para 235][Para 211]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award carries two dates: the header and the dissent give 28 September 2015 [Para 1][Para 261], while the parties postponed issue from 3 to 7 September 2015 [Para 24].
- Dates are corrupted: constitution reads "24 August 2022 2015" [Para 17], evidence deadlines 03 and 06 January 2014 precede the case [Para 23], and stray years 2013, 2017 and 2023 appear at [Para 138], [Para 256] and [Para 260].
- Worker numbers conflict: 295 signed the termination request [Para 72], the reliance letter carries 244 fingerprints [Para 79], 205 were paid [Para 103], and the claimants are given as 212 [Para 65].
- The notification issue is recorded inconsistently: the panel finds at [Para 148] that a security guard's receipt cannot be valid notice, yet the union's own account at [Para 96] has the same guard signing, and [Para 97] adds a receipt stamp.
- The company name appears in several transliterations ("Ri Jen", "Vi Jen", "Ri Jen Fout Wear"), and the operative paragraph at [Para 240] garbles a claimant's name and inserts stray Latin text.
- The chair's name is reduced to gibberish in the signature block [Para 244]; only the header preserves "Tang Tri".
