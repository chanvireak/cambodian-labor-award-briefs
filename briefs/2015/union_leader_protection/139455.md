# Award No. 278/15 — S L Garment Processing Sing (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 18 December 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun for the employer party, Sin Kim Sean for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139455

## 1. Facts
S L Garment Processing Sing (Cambodia) cuts and sews garments and washes clothes, employing about 3,000 workers. [Para 55][Para 56] On 18 and 19 July 2015 it suspended the contracts of every section except water treatment (Water Treatment) and the boiler house (Power Plant), notifying workers only on 17 July and paying 50% of wages, without asking the labour inspectorate for authorisation. [Para 62][Para 64][Para 147][Para 148] Separately, a union activist and outgoing staff representative, Ms M, stopped attending work from 13 June 2015; the company posted a notice of job abandonment on 23 June 2015. [Para 84][Para 86][Para 162]

## 2. Issues in dispute
The claim listed seven points; points 1 to 4 were withdrawn at the hearing, point 5 was settled by agreement, and only two were decided. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 21]
6. Whether the employer must pay a further 50% of wages for 18 and 19 July 2015, having suspended the contracts without the labour inspectorate's permission? [Para 62][Para 136]
7. Whether the employer must take Ms M back into work and pay her wages and other benefits from the day of termination to the day of return? [Para 72][Para 155]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** suspending all sections but water treatment and power plant was a suspension of the contract itself; the company gave one day's notice and never sought the inspectorate's approval, so the withheld half of the wage is owed. Ms M was removed while serving as staff representative and before the fresh election, and was the only union member among the 50 workers in her section, so the act was tied to her union activity. [Para 62][Para 66][Para 77][Para 78][Para 79]
**Employer (respondent):** paying 50% during idle spells is its standing practice, agreed with workers and the union at meetings on seven days' notice, and the July suspension, though immediate, was agreed by the workers. It never dismissed Ms M; she abandoned her post, which art 6 of the internal rules treats as abandonment once six days are missed. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 70][Para 80][Para 148]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay an additional 50% of the wage for the 18 and 19 July 2015 suspension; the claim for Ms M's reinstatement, back wages and benefits was dismissed. [Para 186][Para 187]

On the preliminary point the panel read art 268 of the Labour Law as conferring a union's rights only once its statutes and list of officers are lodged with the Ministry. [Para 121][Para 124] The three newly elected leaders had no ministry recognition, so they could not represent members. [Para 128][Para 129] The federation itself, however, held written transfers of rights from 713 workers, and six workers appeared at the hearing; those were the original complainants and the panel confined itself to them. [Para 132][Para 134]

Issue 6 turned on art 72(1): a suspension touches the main obligations of the contract, including the duty to pay wages, save where a contrary rule provides otherwise. [Para 136] The panel followed its earlier awards holding that a company which suspends work under the inspectorate's supervision owes nothing, but one that disregards the procedure owes 100%. [Para 139] It read art 71(11) with prior awards to require notice to and prior authorisation from the labour inspectorate before a lawful suspension, distinguishing suspensions covered by an authorisation letter, which are de jure. [Para 143][Para 144][Para 145] On the facts the employer conceded it never applied for authorisation, so the suspension was procedurally invalid. [Para 148][Para 149] Article 72(1) therefore did not apply, and the employer could not escape the duty to pay 100%. [Para 150][Para 151] The workers' consent to 50% could not release it from that statutory duty. [Para 153] The further 50% was ordered. [Para 154]

Issue 7: the panel quoted art 6 of the internal rules — fewer than two days' absence is a light fault, two to six a medium one, and six days in a month or six consecutive days amounts to abandonment. [Para 159][Para 161] The June 2015 attendance and overtime list showed no check-in for Ms M from 13 June 2015. [Para 163][Para 171] Because the workers asserted she was present on 13 and 15 June and impugned the company's records, the burden of proof lay on them, and they produced nothing capable of displacing those records. [Para 168][Para 169][Para 170] Counting from 16 to 23 June gave six days, matching art 6. [Para 175][Para 176] Article 293 requires the inspectorate's consent only for dismissal, not for abandonment. [Para 178][Para 179][Para 181][Para 182] As the contract ended by her own act, the panel did not reach the union-discrimination allegation. [Para 183][Para 184]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of contracts for want of work relieves the employer of wages only where it has notified the labour inspectorate and obtained its prior authorisation; without that authorisation art 72(1) does not apply, the suspension is invalid in procedure, and the employer owes the full 100% wage, the workers' agreement to accept 50% being unable to waive an entitlement fixed by statute. [Para 144][Para 149][Para 151][Para 153]
Where absence without leave reaches the number of days the internal rules define as abandonment, the contract ends by the worker's own act, and art 293's protection of staff representatives — which requires the inspectorate's consent before dismissal — does not apply, so no reinstatement is owed; the worker who asserts she attended work bears the burden of proving it against the employer's attendance records. [Para 168][Para 173][Para 182][Para 184]

**Obiter dicta:** the reading of art 268, that a union's rights begin only on lodging its statutes and officers with the Ministry, and the consequent holding that unrecognised new leaders cannot represent members, were preliminary observations that did not determine either order. [Para 124][Para 129] So too the panel's note that it did not examine the union-discrimination complaint once abandonment was established. [Para 183]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The file number is 278/15 on the face of the award [Para 1] but 287/15 in the employer's evidence list [Para 44], and back to 278/15 in the workers' list [Para 48].
- The panel is said to have been constituted on "03 November 2019 2015" [Para 16]; the hearing was 16 November 2015 and the award originally due 30 November 2015, extended by agreement of the parties to 18 December 2015 [Para 12][Para 24].
- The number of workers covered is 713 in the findings [Para 132] but 719 in the passage defining whom the panel would consider [Para 135].
- The reasoning at [Para 25] is interrupted by untranslated Latin-script fragments ("the struction of the", "the fight"), and several evidence entries are printed twice in one line [Para 35][Para 43][Para 45][Para 49].
- The start of the absence is put at 13 June 2015 by the employer and the attendance list [Para 84][Para 163], but the panel also counted eight working days from 13 to 23 June and then six days from 16 to 23 June, noting the tension itself [Para 172][Para 174][Para 175].
- Article 83 is cited in the workers' argument as making lack of work the employer's fault [Para 67], but the reasons rest on arts 71(11) and 72(1) only; the citation was not carried into the decision.
