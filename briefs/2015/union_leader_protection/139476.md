# Award No. 303/15 — Five Star Khon Thinth Gamean (Cambodia) Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 29 December 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy — dissenting in part, Tuon Sy Phan, Pen Bun Chhea — Chair) · Doc ID 139476

## 1. Facts
Five Star Khon Thinth Gamean (Cambodia) Ltd., a garment factory employing 480 to 500 workers, changed its name on 17 July 2015 to Meridian Fashion (Cambodia) Ltd.; the Free Workers' Union of Cambodia had a local union there that was not yet registered with the Ministry. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57][Para 85] Two sewing workers, both hired on 13 October 2014 on successive three-month contracts running to 13 September 2015, had their contracts suspended from 21 July to 21 August 2015 and were dismissed on 22 August 2015, before expiry. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63][Para 64][Para 74][Para 75][Para 76] They complained on 02 September 2015; conciliation failed on both demands, which the parties agreed to merge, and report no. 2786 of 16 November 2015 reached the Secretariat on 18 November 2015. [Para 31][Para 17][Para 6][Para 12]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issues 1 and 2, merged) Whether the employer must reinstate both dismissed workers in their posts and pay wages and benefits from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 59]
2. Whether the first claimant, as an elected union leader, was a protected worker whose dismissal required the Labour Inspector's prior authorisation? [Para 94]
3. Whether the second claimant, who was not named in any notification of elected leaders, was likewise protected? [Para 134]
4. Whether termination of the second claimant's fixed-term contract before expiry was lawful, and what compensation follows? [Para 139]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** They were dismissed because they refused the employer's proposal to suspend their contracts for two months, and the employer breached clauses 8 and 10 of the agreement of 08 July 2015, by which it undertook not to dismiss without reason and not to discriminate against union leaders. [Para 60][Para 68][Para 69]
**Employer (respondent):** The first claimant ignored internal rules, worked as he pleased, talked on the phone during working hours, argued with the team leader and held himself out as a union leader; the second was let go in a redundancy affecting sixty others, and because both held three-month contracts no notice was required; it would pay the 5% termination indemnity, unused leave and final wages. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67][Para 77][Para 78][Para 79]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to reinstate the first claimant and pay his wages from dismissal to reinstatement; reinstatement of the second claimant was refused, but the employer must pay her moral damages, the 5% termination indemnity, unused annual leave and final wages. [Para 172][Para 173][Para 174]

**Which election governs.** The employer argued there had been two union elections, and two notification letters carried different dates (09 July and 07 August 2015). Reading the union's letter no. 310/15 of 09 December 2015, which admitted a clerical error and confirmed a single election, together with the rejection letter signed by one of the notified leaders, the panel found the election took place once, on 09 July 2015, and that the notification of 07 October 2015 naming the elected leaders was the document capable of grounding protection. [Para 90][Para 91][Para 110][Para 112][Para 113][Para 114]

**The three conditions.** Protection requires that the worker be of the protected category, that the dismissal fall inside the protected period, and that the union have notified the employer of the names by any reliable means; Prakas no. 305 of 22 November 2001 gives candidates protection for 45 days before the poll and 45 days after it, and Article 293 forbids dismissal of a delegate or candidate without the Labour Inspector's prior consent. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 104][Para 107] The panel followed Award 181/14-Syung Yin, which extended the candidate's protection at least 45 days past the poll and until the union applies for registration. [Para 105][Para 106]

**First claimant.** He was named as elected union president in the notification of 07 October 2015, satisfying the first condition; the election was on 09 July and he was dismissed on 22 August 2015, inside the 45-day window, satisfying the second; and the employer admitted receiving the notification by post on 19 July 2015, satisfying the third. [Para 115][Para 118][Para 120] Since the employer sought no authorisation from the Labour Inspector, and filed no evidence of having done so, the dismissal breached Article 293 and reinstatement with back pay followed, the panel invoking Article 34 of Prakas no. 099 and Awards 71/09 and 91/11. [Para 124][Para 126][Para 131][Para 132]

**Second claimant.** Her name does not appear in the notification of elected leaders, so she was not of the protected category; failing one condition is enough to lose protection, and the panel did not reach the other two. Reinstatement was therefore refused. [Para 135][Para 136][Para 137][Para 138]

**Her termination.** Article 73(1)-(2) lets a fixed-term contract end at its term, or be cut short only by written agreement made before the Labour Inspector and signed by both sides, or for serious misconduct or force majeure. She did not agree, and the redundancy invoked by the employer is neither misconduct nor force majeure, so the employer terminated unilaterally before expiry and owed compensation under Article 73(3): moral damages of at least the wages due to contract end, plus the 5% termination indemnity under Article 73(6), unused annual leave under Articles 166-167 and final wages under Article 116. [Para 141][Para 142][Para 143][Para 148][Para 150][Para 151][Para 155][Para 163][Para 165]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a union notifies the employer that a worker has been elected a union leader, the worker is protected from dismissal for 45 days after the poll — and beyond that until the union applies for registration — so a dismissal inside the window without the Labour Inspector's prior consent is unlawful and requires reinstatement with wages from dismissal to reinstatement; but a co-worker not named in that notification is unprotected, and the early ending of a fixed-term contract without her written consent and without misconduct or force majeure entitles her only to compensation measured to the contract's end. [Para 97][Para 105][Para 124][Para 138][Para 146]

**Obiter dicta:** none identified; the panel confined itself to the two claimants and expressed no view on the sixty other redundancies. [Para 77]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented in part: he agreed that the first claimant should be reinstated but argued he should recover wages only to the expiry of his fixed-term contract, not to reinstatement, because he remained free to seek other work during the proceedings and no law makes the claim itself a loss. [Para 178][Para 180][Para 182][Para 183] The award records two union election dates, 09 July and 07 August 2015, and resolves them only through the union's later letter admitting a clerical error. [Para 83][Para 90] It also satisfies the third protection condition with a notification the employer admits receiving by post on 19 July 2015, while relying for the names of the elected leaders on a notification dated 07 October 2015 — the two documents are never reconciled. [Para 114][Para 120] The employer's own name is unstable: the case is styled Five Star Khon Thinth Gamean, but the entity is described as Five Star "or" Meridian Fashion after the 17 July 2015 change, and both names appear in the same sentence throughout the evidence list. [Para 22][Para 28][Para 85] Each claimant's name is printed in several different transliterations across the award, as is the chair's in the signature block. [Para 59][Para 134][Para 176] Stray years "2017" and "2019" are injected into sentences by OCR, and the final paragraph of the dissent degenerates into unreadable noise. [Para 12][Para 176][Para 184]
