# Award No. 211/15 — Five Star Knit Tin Garment (Cambodia) Ltd., Kampong Speu (Arbitration Council, 4 September 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3 — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed, dissenting in part), Tuon Si Phan (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (chair, chosen by the two) · Doc ID 139413

## 1. Facts
The employer is a garment-cutting and sewing company in Trapaing Chhouk village, Kampong Speu, with about 585 workers, and the Free Workers' Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia is the complainant on behalf of 34 workers. [Para 49][Para 51][Para 55] After a strike from 30 June to 8 July 2015 over irregularities in the employer's election of worker delegates, the parties signed an 11-point agreement at the provincial labour department on 8 July 2015. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58][Para 104] A union leadership election was held on 9 July 2015, in which Ms N became secretary and Ms T a committee member; both were dismissed on 10 July 2015. [Para 62][Para 64][Para 83][Para 84] The employer then suspended all contracts from 21 July to 21 August 2015 for want of buyer orders, paying only a 20 US dollar travel and accommodation allowance. [Para 82][Para 89][Para 203]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take Ms N and Ms T back into work and repay their wages and other benefits from the date of dismissal until the date of return? [Para 56][Para 119]
2. Whether the employer must pay 100% of wages, rather than a 20 US dollar monthly allowance, during the suspension of contracts from 21 July to 21 August 2015? [Para 88][Para 191]
3. Whether the employer must implement the agreement of 8 July 2015, in particular point 3 allowing pregnant women one day per month for a health check? [Para 104][Para 216]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the two dismissals were improper and amounted to union discrimination, since both women were elected leaders and the agreement's points 8 and 10 promised no dismissal without cause and no discrimination against union leaders; the suspension was unlawful because workers never saw either the employer's request or the Ministry's authorisation; and the 8 July agreement was breached when a pregnant worker was docked half a day's pay after attending a health check. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 92][Para 115][Para 204]
**Employer (respondent):** the dismissals were justified because the two led an unlawful strike that caused substantial loss and led buyers to suspend orders, so all contracts were suspended and 30 reduced; it had applied to the department on 20 July and the labour inspector came and made a record on 21 July; and the pregnant worker was allowed a day off without any wage deduction. [Para 82][Para 98][Para 99][Para 117][Para 134][Para 205][Para 218]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must take Ms N and Ms T back into work and pay back wages from dismissal until the expiry of their last fixed-duration contracts; pay 100% of wages for the suspension period of 21 July to 21 August 2015; and implement the 8 July 2015 agreement so that a pregnant woman may take one day per month for a health check without any deduction from wages, annual leave or special leave. The award is binding, the parties having elected binding arbitration. [Para 18][Para 238]
On union discrimination the panel set out Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law, which forbid an employer from using union membership or activity as a ground for hiring, discipline or termination, and recalled that it had ordered reinstatement on that basis in earlier cases. [Para 121][Para 122][Para 123] It held that the accusing party bears the burden of proof, and found the workers had offered only assertion, with no evidence capable of convincing the panel that the dismissals were motivated by union discrimination. [Para 128][Para 135][Para 136] It accordingly found no discrimination. [Para 136]

On protection of union leaders, the panel applied Article 293 of the Labour Law and Articles 3 and 4 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, and the three conditions from Award No. 50/05 and successors: protected status, dismissal within the protected period, and notice of the protected names to the employer. [Para 137][Para 139][Para 140][Para 157] Ms T was only a committee member, not one of the three key leaders contemplated by Article 7 of Prakas No. 313, so she enjoyed no protection. [Para 148][Para 149][Para 150] Ms N was a protected candidate, and was dismissed one day after the election, satisfying the second condition, but the union notified the employer only on 15 July 2015, five days after her dismissal, so the third condition failed; she therefore had no protection either. [Para 167][Para 168][Para 169][Para 172][Para 173][Para 174][Para 175]

The dismissals nonetheless failed on another ground. Article 332(2) of the Labour Law requires workers to be taken back when a strike ends, and Article 333 forbids any penalty for participating in a strike, such penalty being void; the panel had consistently held that dismissal based on strike participation is unlawful. [Para 180][Para 181][Para 182][Para 183] Both women were dismissed before their fixed-duration contracts expired, on 18 July 2015 and 11 July 2015 respectively, and solely for joining the strike. [Para 187][Para 188] Under Article 73 the panel ordered their return and payment from dismissal to the contract end date. [Para 189][Para 190]

On the suspension, Article 71(11) requires suspension to be supervised by the labour inspector, and Article 72(1) makes suspension affect the duty to work and to pay wages. [Para 191][Para 197] The panel held that a suspension is lawful only if the employer's economic difficulty is verified and the suspension is placed under inspector supervision, and that the inspector's visit is not authorisation. [Para 193][Para 199][Para 207][Para 208] Here the record was made on the day the suspension began, not before it, and the employer never produced the authorisation letter it had promised, so the suspension was procedurally invalid and 100% wages were owed. [Para 209][Para 210][Para 212][Para 213][Para 215]

On the 8 July agreement, the panel read Article 171 of the Labour Law with Prakas No. 267 of 11 October 2001, which permits up to seven days of paid special leave a year and allows an offset against annual leave only where annual leave has not been used. [Para 219][Para 223][Para 224][Para 225] It found that point 3 of the agreement gave the employer no right to offset a pregnancy health check against special leave, and that a monthly health-check day is not special leave at all; the employer's practice of deducting was contrary to the agreement. [Para 229][Para 231][Para 232][Para 234][Para 235]

**Dissent:** Ing Sothy dissented on issue 2 of complaint 2, reasoning that the inspector did attend and made a record, and that no law or regulation requires a written authorisation letter for a suspension, so the employer breached nothing. [Para 242][Para 248][Para 249][Para 250][Para 251]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may not suspend contracts of its own motion on grounds of lost orders: the suspension is valid only where the labour inspector has verified the economic difficulty in advance and placed the suspension under supervision, and the inspector's later record of a visit is not authorisation, so an unverified suspension obliges the employer to pay full wages. [Para 193][Para 207][Para 212][Para 213]

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that a committee member falls outside the three key leaders protected by Prakas No. 313, and the recital of the seven-day special-leave ceiling, explain the statutory setting but do not by themselves decide the case. [Para 148][Para 225]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The date of the panel's constitution is printed twice on one line as "១១ ខែសីហា ឆ្នាំ ២០២២ ២០១៥", and the hearing listed for 17 August 2015 is dated 2014 in the invitation paragraph. [Para 13][Para 14] The union election is dated 9 July 2014 in the reasoning and 9 July 2015 elsewhere. [Para 161][Para 166] The workforce is given as about 585 and the number suspended as 580, while a witness said 140 remained at work and about 40 were working by the hearing. [Para 49][Para 97][Para 98] The complainant group is variously 34 workers and "32 others". [Para 51][Para 54] The signature block is heavily corrupted, reducing one arbitrator's name to the fragment "ថៃ ប៊ុន ណាស់", so the composition is taken from the face of the award. [Para 240]
