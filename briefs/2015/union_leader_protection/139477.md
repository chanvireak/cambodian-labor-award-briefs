# Award No. 302/15 — Seri (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 15 December 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy — dissenting in part, Tuon Sy Phan, Pen Bun Chhea — Chair) · Doc ID 139477

## 1. Facts
Seri (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd. cuts and sews clothing at Chambok village, Samrong Tong, Kampong Speu, employing about 652 workers. [Para 46][Para 47] The Free Workers' Union of Cambodia held a leadership election at the factory on 25 October 2015 and notified the employer by letter and EMS post on 27 October 2015; the postal receipt shows the company received the notification on 30 October 2015. [Para 71][Para 74][Para 75] On 31 October 2015 the head of administration called in the three elected leaders — president, vice-president and secretary — and dismissed them, withholding their cards and, according to the workers, physically preventing them from leaving until 04:20 P.M. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 72] The employer did not attend the hearing of 04 December 2015; a company representative told the Secretariat that the director was away and that no power of attorney had been given. [Para 20][Para 87] Six demands were filed and demands 3 to 6 were withdrawn, leaving two. [Para 21][Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must reinstate the three dismissed union leaders in their original posts and pay their wages from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 91]
2. Whether all three were protected workers whose dismissal required the Labour Inspector's prior authorisation? [Para 95]
3. Whether the dismissals were carried out in accordance with Article 293 of the Labour Law? [Para 130]
4. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay wages within working hours, so that payment is complete before 04:00 P.M.? [Para 141]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** All three were candidates elected as union leaders and therefore protected; the employer dismissed them with no authorisation from the Labour Inspector and no valid reason, and had never warned them for any fault. [Para 51][Para 55][Para 92] Wages are paid from 03:40 P.M. but finish five to thirty minutes after the 04:00 P.M. end of work, so workers miss the shared shuttle, which leaves at 04:00 P.M. for employees of several factories. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 79][Para 80]
**Employer (respondent):** Absent and unrepresented; it presented only a letter of 09 December 2015 asking the panel to reject certain union documents, and by not appearing waived its right to answer the workers' assertions. [Para 25][Para 93]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to reinstate all three workers with wages from dismissal to reinstatement, and to pay wages within working hours. [Para 152][Para 153]

**Proceeding in the employer's absence.** Clause 21 of Prakas no. 099 (2004) allows the panel to continue where a duly invited party fails to appear without good cause, as held in Awards 53/04, 63/04, 148/07, 99/09, 173/11, 142/12, 109/13 and 163/14. The employer had been properly summoned, gave no valid reason and did not attend, so the panel proceeded and accepted the workers' uncontested assertions. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 88][Para 89][Para 94]

**Protection.** Article 293 forbids dismissing a staff delegate or candidate without the Labour Inspector's consent, and Prakas no. 305 of 22 November 2001 extends that protection to every candidate in a union leadership election for 45 days before the poll and 45 days after it, on condition that the union notifies the employer of the candidacies by any reliable means. [Para 95][Para 97][Para 99] Following Awards 40/10-Meng Yan, 103/12 and 191/12, the panel held the protection of an elected leader continues at least 45 days past the poll and may run until the union applies for registration, and it applied the three-condition test: protected category, dismissal inside the protected period, and notification. [Para 106][Para 111][Para 113]

**Applying the test.** The election on 25 October 2015 returned the three as president, vice-president and secretary, so they were of the protected category; the dismissals on 31 October 2015 fell seven days after the poll, well inside the 45-day window; and the notification of 27 October 2015 naming all three, sent by EMS and received by the company on 30 October 2015, satisfied the third condition. [Para 116][Para 117][Para 120][Para 121][Para 124][Para 126] As no authorisation from the Labour Inspector was sought — the employer having defaulted — the dismissals breached Article 293, and the panel ordered reinstatement with back pay, following Awards 71/09-Hai Tech, 09/10-Chung Hao and 91/11-Quick Stu. [Para 130][Para 131][Para 134][Para 136][Para 139]

**Wages within working hours.** Article 115(3) forbids payment on a rest day and requires it a day earlier where the due day falls on one; as interpreted in Award 143/08-Cham Tech Tha Y, payment must therefore be made on a working day, meaning within working hours. Article 137 caps the working day at eight hours. Since the normal day ends at 04:00 P.M. and payment habitually overruns it by five to thirty minutes, the employer was ordered to complete payment before 04:00 P.M. [Para 141][Para 142][Para 144][Para 146][Para 150][Para 153]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that notifies the employer by any reliable means that named workers have been elected as its leaders secures for them the protection of Article 293, so that a dismissal inside the 45-day period after the poll, made without the Labour Inspector's prior authorisation, is unlawful and requires reinstatement with wages from dismissal to reinstatement. [Para 97][Para 121][Para 124][Para 134]

**Obiter dicta:** that wages must be paid within working hours is derived from the rest-day rule in Article 115(3) rather than from any express provision, so the reasoning depends on the panel's earlier interpretation in Award 143/08 rather than on the text alone. [Para 142][Para 150]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented in part: he agreed that all three should be reinstated but dissented from the order to pay wages up to reinstatement, arguing that because their contracts were fixed-term they were entitled to wages only to the contractual end date, and that during the proceedings they were free to take other work and suffered no loss of that kind. [Para 157][Para 158][Para 160][Para 161] The award states each claimant's last contract end date, but two of the three dates are impossible on the facts as printed — one worker said to have started on 21 April 2015 is recorded with a last contract expiring on 21 April 2015, and another dismissed on 31 October 2015 is recorded with a contract that had already expired on 14 October 2015. [Para 54][Para 60][Para 66] The workers' names appear in two or three transliterations each, and the case name is given as "Seri (Cambodia) Garment" while the evidence list also calls the plant "Seri (Cambodia) Garment and Footwear". [Para 1][Para 26][Para 124] The hearing date and time are printed twice in consecutive paragraphs, and stray years "2017" and "2019" are injected into sentences by OCR, while the final signature line collapses into noise. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 14][Para 164]
