# Award No. 284/15 — Meri Dan Fe Sin (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 17 December 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Pen Bun Chhea (president), Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed) · Doc ID 139461

## 1. Facts
The company began garment sewing in October 2014 and employed about 470 workers. Two women, Ms L and Ms N, were elected president and vice-president of the Sampréan Sahakhiy Srey Kât Dei union at the company on 10 July 2015; the union told the company of the result on 11 July 2015 by letter dated 13 July 2015, which the company's security staff refused to accept, and also sent the result by post on 13 July and by EMS on 14 July 2015 [Para 36][Para 57][Para 100]. On 22 August 2015 the employer notified both women that their fixed-duration contracts — running to 31 August and 20 September 2015 respectively — were at an end, and barred them from returning to work [Para 43][Para 45][Para 46]. The employer never sought the labour inspector's permission [Para 55][Para 108].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer terminated the two women's contracts, or merely declined to renew them? [Para 65]
2. Whether the employer terminated them in accordance with the procedure of the Labour Law? [Para 65]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissals were discrimination against the union; both women were elected leaders protected from dismissal, the employer sought no authorisation from the labour inspector, so the terminations are void and they should be reinstated with wages and benefits running from the date of dismissal [Para 49][Para 63].

**Employer (respondent):** the company had little work and twice suspended its workers' contracts; the two women committed serious misconduct by inciting the strike of 30 June to 8 July 2015; there was no work to give them, so the claim could not be met [Para 50][Para 52][Para 64].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the terminations were declared void (មោឃៈ) and the employer was ordered to take both women back and to pay their wages and other benefits from the date of dismissal until the date they resume work; no sum was fixed in riel or USD [Para 116][Para 117][Para 118]

On issue 1 the panel held that where an employer ends a fixed-duration contract before the expiry date, the act is not a case of not renewing the contract but a unilateral dismissal before term [Para 72][Para 73]. The employer's notice of 22 August 2015 came weeks before the contractual end dates of 31 August and 20 September 2015, so the first issue was answered in favour of termination [Para 43][Para 45].

On issue 2 the panel applied Article 293(1) of the Labour Law, which forbids dismissal of a staff delegate without the labour inspector's agreement, extended to union matters by Article 3 of Prakas No. 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001 [Para 75][Para 76]. Article 3(3) of that Prakas protects workers who stand as candidates for union leadership [Para 77]. The panel noted its own earlier ruling that this protection does not reach elected leaders before the union is registered, but followed Award No. 40/10 — Meng Yan, which read the provision as protecting elected leaders from the day of election for at least 45 days afterwards, or for a reasonable period such as until they apply for registration [Para 82][Para 83][Para 85]. It then applied the three conditions settled in earlier awards: that the worker is of the protected class, that the dismissal fell within the protected period, and that the employer was notified of the protected names by any practicable means [Para 86][Para 87]. All three were satisfied: the two were elected president and vice-president [Para 92][Para 93]; the dismissal of 22 August 2015 fell within 45 days of the 10 July 2015 election [Para 97][Para 98]; and the union notified the company by letter, by post and by EMS, the last of which the employer did not contest [Para 100][Para 103][Para 105]. The panel added that the local union's want of registration at the Ministry did not defeat the protection [Para 81][Para 82]. Because the employer sought no authorisation, it breached the dismissal procedure for protected workers [Para 108][Para 109], and under Prakas No. 099 SKBY Article 34 the panel may order reinstatement and immediate payment [Para 115]. Measures taken in breach of those rules are void [Para 114].

Arbitrator Ing Sothy recorded a dissent: he agreed that both women should be reinstated but objected to paying wages and benefits from dismissal up to the date of return, arguing that their contracts were of fixed duration, that Article 73(3) of the Labour Law entitles them to wages only until the contractual end date, and that nothing prevented them from earning elsewhere in the meantime [Para 122][Para 124][Para 126][Para 128].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Ending a fixed-duration contract before its expiry date is a unilateral dismissal, not a non-renewal; where the worker is an elected union leader the dismissal is void unless the labour inspector's prior authorisation was obtained, and the protection runs from the day of election irrespective of whether the local union has yet been registered.

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that no evidence showed either woman had already been paid wages and allowances through to the end of her contract [Para 48], and recorded that the award is non-binding and becomes final only if no party objects within 08 days [Para 119].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel's constitution is dated "06 November 1992 2015", the year printed twice [Para 14]; Ms L's probation is printed as "42 months" where two months is evidently meant [Para 44]; the dismissal date appears as "the 2nd, 22 August 2015" [Para 96]; Prakas 099 is cited as "21 April 2019 2004" [Para 115] and a stray "2017" sits in the EMS paragraph [Para 102]. The panel's paraphrase at [Para 82] is grammatically broken ("Article 3(3) ... does not ... and states"), read here as holding that the provision does not yet cover elected leaders before registration. Case numbers in the lists of prior awards are partly illegible [Para 84][Para 87][Para 111], and line 3 carries a fragment naming a different company ("Star Khât Thin Gamin (Cambodia) Ltd"), apparently stray text from another document. No money amounts are printed anywhere in the award.
