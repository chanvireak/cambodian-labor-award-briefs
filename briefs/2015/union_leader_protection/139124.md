# Award No. 008/15 — Royal Cambodia Phnom Penh Golf Club (CVIKHA Investment Development Co., Ltd) (Arbitration Council, 27 February 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Seng Vouch Hun (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (chair, appointed by the two members) · Doc ID 139124

## 1. Facts
Some 210 employees worked for the Royal Cambodia Phnom Penh Golf Club, operated by CVIKHA Investment Development Co., Ltd. Seven workers, through two federations, filed a collective complaint on 03 December 2014 raising nineteen demands; conciliation on 31 December 2014 failed on all nineteen. [Para 81]
Five workers were dismissed — one (Mr B) on 05 October 2014 for playing golf without paying, four others on 28 November 2014 over a golf-cart rental arrangement. One of the five, Mr V, was the elected worker delegate (ប្រតិភូ បុគ្គលិក). [Para 89][Para 115]
Both sides elected a non-binding award (សេចក្តីបង្គាប់ មិន ជាប់ កាតព្វកិច្ច). [Para 36]

## 2. Issues in dispute
Nineteen points were filed. Five (07, 09, 15, 16, 17) settled at hearing; seven (01, 02, 06, 08, 13, 18, 19) were consolidated into a single issue; eight remained for adjudication. [Para 35]
1. Whether the employer must take back the worker delegate and the five dismissed workers, or failing that pay their wages and statutory benefits, and must do the same for a further 146 workers still employed? [Para 190]
2. Whether the employer owed overtime pay for work beyond the lawful day (13 hours in one day)? [Para 145]
3. Whether the employer underpaid wages for work on Sundays and public holidays? [Para 152]
4. Whether the employer must compensate two pregnant workers dismissed without payment? [Para 159]
5. Whether annual leave must be increased by one day every three years under Article 166? [Para 168]
6. Whether meal allowance is owed for overtime work? [Para 172]
7. Whether wages and allowances must be paid together in a single payment? [Para 176]
8. Whether the employer must accept medical certificates from private clinics recognised by the Ministry of Health? [Para 182]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The five committed no grave misconduct; the golf game and the cart arrangement were innocent or tolerated, and the dismissals ignored procedure. They sought reinstatement with back wages, or in the alternative severance for all 151 workers, plus overtime, holiday pay, meal allowance, consolidated payment and recognition of private-clinic certificates. [Para 219]
**Employer (respondent):** The five committed grave misconduct under Article 83 and internal rule 10 — playing golf without payment and renting an outside cart to guests for profit — and refused to sign acknowledgements; the delegate's dismissal was referred to the labour inspector. Overtime was paid, rosters covered rest days, and only state medical certificates were accepted. [Para 140]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted. The employer was ordered to pay the five dismissed workers termination compensation — notice in lieu of 02 or 03 months, severance of 06 months' wages and allowances (120 days for Mr S; 45 days for Mr N), outstanding annual leave and last unpaid wages — plus moral damages (សោហ៊ុយ ជម្ងឺចិត្ត) of 06 months for Mr V alone. Annual leave was ordered increased by one day every three years. The claim for the 146 workers, and the overtime, holiday-pay, pregnancy, meal-allowance and consolidated-payment claims, were dismissed. [Para 430]
On the dismissals, the panel found Mr B did play golf without authorisation but that neither the internal rules nor the 17 March 2014 company declaration classed this as grave misconduct, so dismissal was disproportionate; the same reasoning applied to the cart case, where the company had no carts left to rent, so renting an outside cart was not exploitation of company property, though it did breach discipline. [Para 210][Para 224][Para 227]
Mr V's dismissal was separately unlawful: as a worker delegate he enjoyed protection under Article 293, the employer's request to the labour inspector drew no reply within one month and was therefore deemed refused. The panel noted it would ordinarily order reinstatement but, because the workers had asked for compensation in the alternative, awarded damages instead. [Para 230][Para 243][Para 245]
The claim for the 146 still-employed workers failed because Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 311 of the Civil Code reserve contract termination to the parties' will; the workers could not compel their own dismissal to trigger payments. [Para 307]
Overtime and Sunday/holiday claims failed on evidence: the employer produced September and October 2014 overtime schedules which the workers did not contest, and the workers produced no list of who worked unpaid rest days. [Para 322]
The pregnancy claim failed for want of proof, the burden lying on the complainant. Meal allowance failed because no law, collective agreement or practice created it in this enterprise. Consolidated payment failed because Article 116 requires at least two payments a month, not one. [Para 394]
The annual-leave claim succeeded: Article 166 adds one day per three years' seniority, and the employer had given only one extra day regardless of tenure. The medical-certificate claim succeeded: Article 71 recognises certificates from any officially qualified doctor, not only state hospitals. [Para 361][Para 407]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an internal rule and a company declaration list the sanctions available for playing golf or using a cart without permission, unauthorised use is a disciplinary breach but not "grave misconduct" (កំហុស ធ្ងន់) within Article 83 unless the rule itself classifies it as such; dismissal on that ground is unlawful and attracts notice, severance, leave and wage payments, and for a protected worker delegate also moral damages. [Para 280]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that it would normally have ordered reinstatement of an unlawfully dismissed delegate, and departed only because the claimants had claimed compensation in the alternative. It also observed that a dismissal request unanswered by the labour inspector for over a month is deemed refused. [Para 245]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
OCR losses are heavy: stray years ("2017", "2019 2012", "2023") intrude into dates at paras 1, 148 and 221; the internal rule is cited as both 09 August 2001 and 21 July 2001 (paras 44, 195). The two pregnant workers are named twice in different forms (paras 161, 346), and the award date is 27 February 2015 in the header though the parties are recorded as extending it to 23 February 2015 (para 38). The panel's own numbers conflict: para 279 says the employer had valid grounds to dismiss four of the five, while para 227 finds none committed grave misconduct; para 283 reads "to 4 workers ... all 05". The signature block lists An Non as chair, though the header names Pen Bun Chhea as chair.
