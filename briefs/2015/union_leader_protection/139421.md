# Award No. 226/15 — BUT Foul Win Do Enterprise (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 18 September 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chiv Phirom for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Tang Tri chair) · Doc ID 139421

## 1. Facts
BUT Foul Win Do Enterprise (Cambodia) Ltd makes window blinds, doors and windows in the San Thong Sanwel special economic zone, Svay Rieng province, and employed 635 workers. [Para 57][Para 127][Para 143] A strike over working conditions and benefits led to conciliation of 11 claims on 17 August 2015, none of which settled; the case reached the Secretariat on 20 August 2015. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20] At the hearing the parties agreed 6 of the 11 claims and the workers withdrew 2, leaving issues 3, 6 and 10, and the parties agreed to postpone the award from 11 to 18 September 2015. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 28]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must return the 8 workers it moved into the breastfeeding room with no work from 24 to 31 August 2015 to their original workplaces? [Para 59][Para 60][Para 83]
2. Whether the employer must deduct union dues from the wages of workers who became members of the Movement Workers Union? [Para 63][Para 95]
3. Whether the employer must provide an infirmary room, medicines and a doctor on duty during working hours in accordance with the Labour Law? [Para 79][Para 126]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** after the strike of 12 August 2015 and the return to work ordered by the panel on 24 August 2015, the employer parked 8 workers — including the union president, vice-president and secretary — in the breastfeeding room without work; the employer refuses to deduct dues despite written consent signed by members on 14 July 2015; and the single nurse and existing stock do not meet the statutory infirmary standard. [Para 60][Para 63][Para 79]
**Employer (respondent):** it declines to answer the redeployment complaint because those facts arose after the case was sent to the panel; Article 281 forbids deducting wages to pay union dues, and the consent letters are undated copies without the union's signature; it is still looking for a doctor, which is difficult at a remote provincial site, and refers serious cases to a nearby hospital at its own cost. [Para 61][Para 64][Para 66][Para 73][Para 80][Para 81]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the redeployment claim was refused; the employer must deduct union dues from the wages of members of the Movement Workers Union whenever a member makes a written request; and must provide an infirmary room and medicines per the annex to Prakas No. 330, with 2 nurses and a doctor present at least 3 hours in each 8-hour shift. [Para 147][Para 148][Para 149]

On issue 3 the panel held it had no power to decide: Article 312(1) of the Labour Law and Article 33 of Prakas No. 099 confine the panel to the issues recorded in the non-conciliation minute plus matters arising after that minute as a direct consequence of the dispute. [Para 86][Para 87][Para 88] The transfer to the breastfeeding room occurred on 24 August 2015, after the minute of 17 August 2015, and the panel found it was not a direct consequence of any recorded issue, so the claim was refused — consistently with earlier awards refusing claims neither recorded nor consequential. [Para 92][Para 93][Para 94]

On issue 6 the panel read Articles 281 and 129 together: Article 281 prohibits an employer from deducting wages to pay union dues or paying dues on the worker's behalf, but Article 129(2) permits deduction where the worker himself requests it in writing, revocably at any time. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 100] The panel rejected the employer's reading that Article 281 is an absolute bar, holding that it targets deductions made without a proper written request from the worker concerned, while Article 129(2) supplies the lawful route. [Para 112][Para 114][Para 116][Para 118] On the evidence it found 384 workers had signed consent letters for July 2015 and 11 for June 2015, in three different forms, each agreeing to a 4,000 riel monthly deduction. [Para 68][Para 108] It held the will of the workers was clear and Article 129 satisfied, and that where a document is unclear the employer may ask the union to clarify or complete it but cannot use that as a general ground to refuse deduction. [Para 109][Para 110][Para 123][Para 124][Para 125]

On issue 10 the panel applied Articles 238 and 242 of the Labour Law, which require primary care for workers and, for establishments with at least 50 workers, a permanent infirmary with a doctor and one or more nurses on duty day and night, at the employer's cost, and Articles 1, 3 and 4 of Prakas No. 330 of 6 December 2000, whose table sets staffing by headcount. [Para 128][Para 129][Para 131][Para 133][Para 135][Para 136] With 635 workers the employer needed 2 nurses and a doctor for at least 3 hours per 8-hour shift, but fielded one nurse only, so it had not met its occupational safety and first-aid duty. [Para 143][Para 144][Para 146]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The panel decides only the issues recorded in the non-conciliation minute and matters arising after that minute as a direct consequence of the recorded dispute; a new unilateral act occurring after conciliation, even one affecting the claimants, is outside jurisdiction unless it is such a consequence. Article 281 of the Labour Law bars an employer from deducting wages for union dues on its own initiative, but does not override Article 129(2): a deduction is lawful, and must then be made, where each member requests it in writing and may revoke the request at any time. Deficiencies in the form of consent documents entitle the employer to seek clarification, not to refuse deduction wholesale. An establishment with 50 or more workers must maintain an infirmary staffed to the Prakas No. 330 table for its headcount. [Para 93][Para 116][Para 118][Para 122][Para 124][Para 145]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the employer may require the union to complete unclear documents, and that an employer cannot interfere in the internal affairs of a union, were observations on how the parties should behave rather than orders. [Para 113][Para 123]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The constitution date of the panel is garbled as "24 August 2022 2019 2015" [Para 21]; the conciliation minute and hearing dates place it in 2015.
- The evidence deadline for the employer to file documents is given as 04 August 2015, before the 31 August 2015 hearing [Para 27].
- The operative order at [Para 125] contains a stray "2016" inside the sentence, and the date range of the redeployment reads "from 24 to year, 31 August 2015" at [Para 92].
- The signature block at [Para 151] is corrupted: the employer-party arbitrator's name appears as "Chiv Komny (AMud)" against "Chiv Phirom" in the header, and the worker-party arbitrator's signature line is reduced to meaningless characters.
- Worker names survive only as initials or fragments in the union officer list [Para 58][Para 59]; the factory is abbreviated "B.W" at [Para 74] and one consent-letter passage carries a stray "Ling" [Para 68].
- The company name exists only in Khmer transliteration and is rendered here phonetically; the award prints it inconsistently, including "B.U.T." variants and "Khmer L.T.D." forms [Para 15][Para 20].
- Lines 151–166 append the full text of Prakas No. 330 with heavy OCR damage to its staffing table and annex, including duplicated rows and Latin drug names; the figures used (2 nurses, doctor 3 hours) were taken from the readable 601–900 band.
