# Award No. 273/15 — Victoria Angkor Hotel Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 3 December 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Yu Sonthy appointed by the employer, An Virak appointed by the workers, Tang Tri chair appointed by both) · Doc ID 139452

## 1. Facts
Victoria Angkor Hotel Co., Ltd, trading as Victoria Angkor Resort & Spa in Siem Reap, a hotel and restaurant company employing about 217, faced nine demands from its employees' union, of which four were settled at conciliation on 1 October 2015 and three more were withdrawn at the hearing, leaving two. [Para 74][Para 75][Para 18][Para 23] On 24 September 2015 the hotel dismissed Mr N, a deputy union president, and Ms O, a senior union adviser and activist, one day before the union's new-term leadership election. [Para 88][Para 102][Para 162] The workers also demanded that the employer dismiss the general manager (Mr R) and the executive director (Mr B). [Para 78][Para 180]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to dismiss the hotel's two managers, the general manager (Mr R) and the executive director (Mr B)? [Para 119]
2. Whether the dismissal of Mr N and Ms O was lawful, so that the employer must take them back at their original workplace and pay their wages from the date of dismissal? [Para 142][Para 171]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the general manager had no professionalism — he drank alcohol and slept on the hotel's brick paving, publicly accused the former manager and the female union leader of a close relationship that won the union excessive benefits under the collective agreement (producing a photograph), insulted a tricycle driver while drunk, and sent a female employee in a short uniform to clean a high place by ladder, while the executive director loudly repeated the relationship rumour; the two union leaders were dismissed without valid reason and must be reinstated with back pay. [Para 79][Para 81][Para 82][Para 83][Para 84][Para 91]
**Employer (respondent):** management appointments and dismissals are decisions of the shareholders, and no conduct of either manager endangered any worker (a point both parties accepted); Mr N was dismissed for serious breach of hotel rules — defaming the hotel on Facebook, accusing it of discriminating against pregnant women, bypassing the collective agreement by complaining straight to the provincial labour office and publicising the complaint, and inciting a strike — while Ms O had a special relationship with the former general manager that created a conflict of interest in the collective bargaining. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 92][Para 93][Para 95][Para 106][Para 107]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim to dismiss the two managers was refused, and the employer was ordered to take Mr N and Ms O back and pay their wages from the date of dismissal to the date of return; the award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent written objection to the Minister of Labour. [Para 180][Para 181][Para 182]

On issue 1, the panel relied on Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 311 of the 2007 Civil Code: only the parties to a contract may create, vary or extinguish its obligations, so a third party has no right to terminate an employment contract between employer and worker. [Para 119][Para 121][Para 122][Para 124] It followed its earlier awards (116/10, 83/12) holding that the Council cannot order an employer to dismiss or transfer a section chief or team leader, because staffing is the company's internal management under Article 2. [Para 130][Para 132][Para 133] The exception is a worker shown to be dangerous, whose retention would endanger others' safety, or one who has used violence. [Para 134][Para 135] Both parties agreed that no act of either manager could endanger other staff, and the panel found no such facts, so the claim failed. [Para 136][Para 137][Para 138]

On issue 9, the panel applied Article 293 and Prakas No. 305 through its three conditions. [Para 142][Para 156] Condition one was met: both were candidates in the new-term election. [Para 158][Para 160] Condition two was met: they were dismissed on 24 September 2015, one day before the 25 September election, inside the 45-day pre-election protection window. [Para 161][Para 162][Para 164] Condition three was met: the union notified the employer of its candidates by letter of 12 September, acknowledged by the human resources officer on 14 September, naming both. [Para 109][Para 110][Para 166][Para 169] Both therefore enjoyed protected status, and Article 293 required the employer to obtain the labour inspector's prior authorisation before dismissing them. [Para 171] The employer produced no evidence that it had sought that authorisation, so the dismissals were unlawful. [Para 172][Para 173][Para 177] The panel ordered reinstatement with back pay, following earlier awards 71/09, 09/10 and 91/11. [Para 174][Para 179]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A panel will not order an employer to dismiss a manager at the demand of the workforce: only the parties to an employment contract may terminate it, and staffing is an internal management prerogative — unless the worker is shown to be dangerous or violent, so that retaining him threatens others' safety. Separately, an elected or candidate union leader dismissed while protected under Article 293 and Prakas No. 305 without the labour inspector's prior authorisation is dismissed unlawfully, and the remedy is reinstatement with wages from the date of dismissal. [Para 122][Para 132][Para 134][Para 171][Para 177]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's statement that the protection of Prakas No. 305 extends to candidates for 45 days before the poll and, for unsuccessful candidates, 45 days after it, and its description of the 30-day post-registration protection for founders and members, go beyond what the two dismissals required. [Para 145][Para 153][Para 154]

They are referred to here by role and initial only. [Para 78][Para 119][Para 136][Para 180]
- The company is named "វីតូរីយ៉ា អង្គរ ខម ភេនី លីមីធីត" in the style, "វិចតូរីយ៉ា អង្គរ" in the conciliation report, and "Victoria Angkor Resort and Spa" in the disciplinary notices. [Para 74][Para 19][Para 99]
- The conciliation report is numbered both 079 and 079 ម.ក.ប, and the case-creation date is printed as "២២ ខែតុលា ឆ្នាំ ២០១៩ ២០១៥". [Para 14][Para 19][Para 20]
- The union's registration is given as certificate 356 of 16 April 2005 in the evidence list and 756 of 6 April 2005 in the findings; the employer asserted there had been no union registration from 2009 to 2015. [Para 50][Para 76][Para 112]
- The award does not resolve the conflict between Mr N's status as president of the union since 2009 and his election as deputy president in the new term without ministerial recognition of that leadership. [Para 90]
- The signature block prints An Virak twice and does not name the employer-appointed arbitrator Yu Sonthy; the header spells his name "យូ សូន ទី". [Para 1][Para 183]
- Dates in the evidence list appear as "២០១៩" and "២០៦" where 2015 and 2016 are meant. [Para 36][Para 47]
