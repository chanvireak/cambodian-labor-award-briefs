# Award No. 267/15 — San Feng (Cambodia) Company Limited (Arbitration Council, 11 November 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Somborana for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 139444

## 1. Facts
San Feng (Cambodia) Company Limited, a curtain manufacturer in the Manhattan Special Economic Zone in Svay Rieng employing about 1,600 workers, dismissed six workers said to be leaders of the newly elected VKYUF local union on 25 September 2015, after incidents of 12 to 25 September and a strike from 26 to 30 September 2015. [Para 42][Para 63][Para 96] The union, which held an election on 2 September 2015, was not yet registered with the Ministry, and the employer refused to receive the notification of candidates on 8 September 2015 and again on 14 September 2015, when a Ministry representative signed for it. [Para 42][Para 73][Para 78][Para 80] Three issues were referred; the workers withdrew issue 2 (implementation of the employment contract), leaving the reinstatement claim and the claim to wages during the strike. [Para 35][Para 36]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate the VKYUF local union leaders — the vice-president, secretary, advisor, treasurer and two committee members — and pay them back wages from termination to reinstatement? [Para 21][Para 27]
2. Whether the employer must maintain full wages and other benefits for striking workers? [Para 28]

The panel also recorded an additional demand, raised at the hearing, for the reinstatement of seven further workers dismissed between 2 and 19 October 2015. [Para 104][Para 105]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the six were dismissed without proper reason although they had committed no mistake and received no warning; the dismissal breached legal procedure because they were local union leaders and showed discrimination against the local union; the incidents of 12 to 16 September 2015 were already settled on 23 September 2015 and could not be revived, and bringing walkie-talkies is not serious misconduct; they accepted the strike was called without prior notice and had no legal basis for the wage claim. [Para 62][Para 69][Para 97]
**Employer (respondent):** the six committed serious misconduct under clause 9.3.6.15 of the Internal Work Rules and Article 83(2)(3), (4) and (5) of the Labour Law — banging tables and turning off lights on 12 September, threatening workers and blowing powder with air-pipes on 15 September, storming the premises on 16 September, and bringing walkie-talkies and refusing to work on 25 September; it would not maintain wages for an unlawful strike, during which it recruited no replacements. [Para 63][Para 67][Para 99][Para 104]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered reinstatement with back pay for Mr P, Mr O and Mr T, rejected the claim for Ms L, rejected the strike-wage claim, and declined to consider the additional demand. [Para 199]

*Who the claimants are.* Article 268 gives a professional organisation the rights the Labour Law grants only once its charter and the names of its managers are filed with the Ministry, which earlier awards (023/14 Volume Cocomo, 032/14 Wai Shing, 079/15 Sonando, 176/15 A & J) read to include the right to represent members. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 108] The VKYUF local union was unregistered, so those claiming to be its leaders could not represent all its members; but under clause 19 of Prakas no. 99/04 a party may appear in person, and the nine who attended became the claimants. [Para 109][Para 110][Para 115][Para 116] Two of the six neither authorised the union in writing nor appeared, so only four were considered. [Para 112][Para 119][Para 120]

*Special protection.* Article 293 requires the labour inspector's authorisation before dismissing a workers' delegate, and clause 3 of Prakas no. 305 extends that to candidates — 45 days before and after the election if not elected — while 40/10 Meng Yan puts an elected leader's protection at least 45 days out, or until registration is applied for. [Para 127][Para 130] The panel applied its usual three conditions: a worker defined in Prakas no. 305, dismissal within the protection period, and notification by reliable means. [Para 131] Ms L was not shown to be a candidate at all, so she failed the first and the panel did not reach the others; Mr P was the elected secretary and Mr O and Mr T unelected candidates for advisor and treasurer, which brought them within the prakas. [Para 132][Para 135][Para 136] The election was on 2 September 2015 and the dismissals on 25 September 2015, 23 days later, so all three were within the protection period. [Para 141] Notice was given: letter no. 204/15 of 8 September 2015 was signed "Received" by the Ministry officer on 14 September 2015, a proper and reliable means despite the employer's denial of knowledge. [Para 147][Para 149][Para 150] No authorisation was sought, so the dismissals were null and void under clause 4 of Prakas no. 305, and under clause 34 of Prakas no. 099 reinstatement with back pay was ordered. [Para 154][Para 155]

*Ms L.* Being unprotected, she was tested for serious misconduct and union motive. [Para 156][Para 157] The witnesses could describe the incidents but not identify her, and clause 9.3.6.15 lists phones and electronic devices as an "other mistake" attracting a warning, dismissal following only three warnings; bringing a walkie-talkie was therefore not serious misconduct. [Para 160][Para 163][Para 165][Para 166] But on union discrimination the alleging party bears the burden, and the workers produced no evidence that union activity was the ground, so that limb failed and her claim was rejected. [Para 171][Para 172][Para 173]

*Strike wages.* Article 332(1) suspends the employment contract during a strike, so wages and work allowances are not paid, and Article 334 obliges payment only where replacements are recruited, which they were not. [Para 175][Para 177][Para 178] The attendance bonus under Notification no. 230 of 25 July 2012 requires regular attendance; this strike was procedurally incorrect — no seven days' notice under Article 324, no prior peaceful settlement under Article 320(4) — so the bonus was not due. [Para 179][Para 182][Para 183] The transport and accommodation allowance likewise follows from Article 332. [Para 185] No law, agreement or rule creates a food allowance, and the employer's US$ 14 monthly payment, though better than the law requires, was held not payable during a strike on the authority of 50/15 Vantage River Textile. [Para 186][Para 188][Para 189]

*Additional issue.* Article 312(1) and clause 33 of Prakas no. 099 confine the panel to issues in the non-conciliation report and their direct consequences arising later; the seven dismissals of 2 to 19 October 2015 were a separate act, appearing in neither report no. 346/15 nor the conciliation minutes, so the panel declined them. [Para 192][Para 198][Para 199]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union leader or candidate for union office dismissed without the labour inspector's prior authorisation is entitled to reinstatement with back wages, and notification is reliable where a Ministry officer signs "Received" for the letter at a conciliation meeting, even if the employer denies knowledge. [Para 149][Para 150][Para 155]

Bringing a walkie-talkie into an enterprise contrary to internal rules is an "other mistake" requiring a warning, not serious misconduct, but that alone does not win reinstatement: the worker must still prove union activity caused the dismissal. [Para 165][Para 172]

**Obiter dicta:** its account of the unregistered union's inability to represent members was not necessary to the orders, which rested on the claimants' own personal appearance and protection. [Para 109]
