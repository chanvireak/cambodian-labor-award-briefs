# Award No. 054/15 — Echo Base Factory Ltd (Arbitration Council, 31 March 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, chosen by the employer; Sin Kim Sean, chosen by the workers; Kong Phallak, chair chosen by the two) · Doc ID 139157

## 1. Facts
Echo Base Factory Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អ៊ីខូ បេស ហ្វេក ធើរី អិលធីឌី) weaves and sews at Prek Ta Pring village, Setbo commune, Saang district, Kandal, and told the panel it employs about 537 workers. [Para 1][Para 145][Para 146] On 20 January 2015 the Kandal Department of Labour received a complaint of 25 November 2014 from the federation of Democratic Unions of Cambodian Garment Workers; conciliation on 04 February 2015 failed on all seven demands and the hearing settled two, leaving five. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 28][Para 29] The disputed acts are the employer's changes in December 2014 and January 2015: it stopped renewing fixed-duration contracts and cut the night premium from 200% to 130%, and it declines to publish piece rates ahead, to issue payslips before payday, or to hire cleaners for the automatic machines. [Para 223][Para 329][Para 256][Para 301][Para 286]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must issue the piece rate (តម្លៃ ឡូត៍) for each PN series three days before putting a new series into production? [Para 248]
2. Whether the employer must hire cleaners for the machines in the automatic section? [Para 270]
3. Whether the employer must issue the monthly payslip before payday? [Para 287]
4. Whether the employer must reinstate the seven workers it stopped employing at the end of December 2014 and pay back wages? [Para 304][Para 305]
5. Whether the employer must keep the night premium at 200% and pay twelve hours for eleven hours worked at night, as before? [Para 344]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Piece rates fall month to month for the same PN series, differ between branches, and change for series already sewn, so rates must be published three days ahead. [Para 156][Para 157] The employer promised cleaners once operators went from six to eight machines, and cleaning takes 16 to 24 minutes from their own work. [Para 169][Para 173] Payslips must precede payday so errors can be challenged, as Article 112 requires. [Para 178] The seven were union leaders and activists with no prior fault, replaced by new hires — anti-union discrimination under Articles 12 and 279. [Para 197][Para 198][Para 199] The 200% rate and twelve hours' pay for eleven were a practice more favourable than the law and must continue. [Para 344][Para 225]
**Employer (respondent):** It never dismissed the seven; their three-month contracts expired on 31 December 2014 and were not renewed for want of work from August to March, and it declined to renew 148 workers that month, rehiring several union members. [Para 199][Para 200][Para 203][Para 210] Night work now attracts the statutory 130% because the 2015 minimum wage rose to USD 128 and orders have fallen; the 200% rate was voluntary, from when wages were low. [Para 232][Para 233][Para 363][Para 364] Cleaning by operators is justified because the machines need technical handling; slips are given on payday. [Para 170][Para 302]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all five surviving demands refused on the merits; nothing was awarded. [Para 379][Para 380][Para 381][Para 382][Para 383]

The panel first held the union entitled to appear under Article 268 of the Labour Law: it held registration certificate No. 3128 of 21 March 2014 and its president attended. [Para 237][Para 244][Para 247]

On issue 1, Article 112(a) obliges the employer to inform workers clearly of their wage conditions and of any change, but fixes no number of days. [Para 248][Para 250][Para 251] Following awards 62/04 E-Sun and 05/06 Double U&N, the panel held three to seven days after trial sewing to be reasonable, and found the employer in fact announces all rates on the 25th of each month; the workers produced no evidence of a price cut or code change for a series already sewn, so their complaint was a concern, not a proven grievance. [Para 256][Para 257][Para 261][Para 265][Para 266][Para 268]

On issue 2, Article 2 gives the employer the right to organise and manage the enterprise lawfully and reasonably. [Para 270][Para 271] Cleaning is done in paid working hours by monthly-paid operators who lose no wage, the technical requirements were explained before they took the machines, and the alleged promise was oral and unproven. [Para 276][Para 280][Para 283][Para 285]

On issue 3, the panel read Article 112(b) as requiring clear notification of the wages for each pay period, not delivery of the slip before payday, following awards 05/13 and 61/13; the employer issues the slip on the payday itself and so complied. [Para 296][Para 299][Para 301][Para 302]

On issue 4, the parties agreed the last contracts ran from 01 October to 31 December 2014 and that the workers did not object when told they had expired. [Para 305][Para 307][Para 308] The panel held that expiry of a fixed-duration contract is not a dismissal, so Article 293 — requiring the labour inspector's consent only for dismissal — did not apply, citing awards 10/09 New York Cambodia, 155/09 USA and 325/14 Four Four Unit Thing. [Para 309][Para 311][Para 312][Para 313][Para 315] On the discrimination plea under Articles 12 and 279, it found the employer knew the seven were union members, but also that it declined to renew 148 workers that month, that six of the nine named had merely reached expiry, that five union members were rehired, and that two had documented quality faults and warnings; the bare assertion of discrimination was not enough. [Para 317][Para 327][Para 330][Para 335][Para 341][Para 342]

On issue 5, the 200% rate and the twelve-hours-for-eleven practice had run since 2012, about three years, acknowledged by both sides, and were therefore a past practice. [Para 356][Para 357][Para 358] Adopting award 95/09 Tak Fhat, the panel held that a past practice ends lawfully where the parties agree to end it, agree a new practice, or the circumstances founding it change. [Para 359][Para 360][Para 361] It found those circumstances — low wages, then available orders — had changed: the minimum wage rose to USD 128 and orders fell, and the new 130% rate matches Article 144 as amended and Prakas No. 185 of 2007. [Para 363][Para 364][Para 366][Para 370][Para 373] The workers did not dispute the fall in orders, and their point that the company grew from two to five factories proved nothing about timing. [Para 374][Para 376][Para 377]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A past practice more generous than the statutory minimum is not entrenched: where the circumstances founding it — wage levels and order volumes — change, the employer may lawfully reduce it to the statutory standard, and the panel will not order the more generous practice restored. [Para 360][Para 366][Para 370][Para 377]

Expiry of a fixed-duration contract and the employer's refusal to renew it is not a dismissal, so neither reinstatement nor the protection of Article 293 applies; reinstatement for anti-union non-renewal requires evidence of the discriminatory motive, and a bare assertion of it — against mass non-renewal and the rehiring of union members — does not suffice. [Para 309][Para 313][Para 327][Para 341][Para 342]

Article 112 obliges the employer to inform workers clearly of their wage conditions and of any change, but fixes no deadline and does not require the payslip before payday. [Para 251][Para 299]

**Obiter dicta:** The remarks that publishing all rates at once each month avoids strikes, and that the workers' two-to-five-factories point was unproven as to timing, were not necessary to the outcome. [Para 161][Para 376]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The seven claimants are printed as "៧២ នាក់" — 72 persons — in the issue question at [Para 305], and as seven elsewhere [Para 304][Para 343][Para 382]. The finding on the 2014 non-renewals opens "ក្នុង ឆ្នាំ ២០២២" (in the year 2022) at [Para 337], and the order reduces the night rate to "២០ ភាគរយ" — 20% — at [Para 383] where 200% is meant. Paragraph 179 carries intruded English ("<unk> would \" Sungrom\" …"), and paragraphs 227 and 249 print a doubled month ("ខែធ្នូ កុម្ភៈ") and a stray year. The orders at [Para 269] and [Para 303] read "និយោជកម្ពុជា" for the employer. The union's officer record is inconsistent: the election letter at [Para 212] lists both Mr Y and Mr R as vice-president.
