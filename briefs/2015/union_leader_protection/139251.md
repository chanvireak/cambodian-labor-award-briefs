# Award No. 178/15 — Sich Plass In Das Stri Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 20 July 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Kong Phalha chair) · Doc ID 139251

## 1. Facts
Sich Plass In Das Stri Co., Ltd. operates in Mukh Kampul district, Kandal province, and its workers brought eleven demands on 6 July 2015 through three federations, including the reinstatement of a worker dismissed on 1 July 2015, a USD 5 travel and accommodation supplement, 3,000 riel for lunch, voluntary overtime, no deductions when a worker is sick with a proper medical certificate, a USD 5 attendance allowance for overtime from 18 hours, an infirmary with medicines and a doctor on duty, no discrimination in recruitment, withdrawal of the company's complaint against a worker, recognition of the three union leaders, and maintenance of wages and benefits during the strike. [Para 3][Para 4][Para 5][Para 6][Para 7][Para 8][Para 9][Para 10][Para 12][Para 13][Para 14][Para 18]
Conciliation on 9 July 2015 failed and all eleven points were referred to the Council on 10 July 2015. [Para 19][Para 20] Workers struck on 8 and 9 July 2015; the company asked the Council on 9 July for an order returning them to work, and on 13 July 2015 the Council issued Order No. 027/15 suspending the strike and requiring a return to work from the morning of 14 July 2015. [Para 21][Para 22] By 17 July the company reported about 150 workers still striking and the union federation reported 500 to 600. [Para 23] No hearing was ever held, so the panel made no findings of fact on any of the eleven demands. [Para 35]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the arbitration proceedings should continue, or be terminated, where the workers have kept striking after the Council ordered the strike suspended? [Para 36]

The eleven substantive demands listed in the non-conciliation report were not reached and were not decided. [Para 35][Para 3]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the federations sought the eleven improvements to working conditions and put nothing before the panel in support of them, filing no documentary evidence at all. [Para 18][Para 27]

**Employer (respondent):** the company asked the Council to order the workers back to work on the ground that it had received no notice of the strike and that the strike of 8 and 9 July 2015 did not follow the procedure of the Labour Law. [Para 21]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel terminated the arbitration in case No. 178/15 without a hearing or any order on the merits, leaving all eleven demands undecided. [Para 49][Para 50]

The panel framed the only question as whether the arbitration should proceed at all, and started from Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, which requires the parties to suspend every dispute measure — strike or lockout under Article 318 of the Labour Law, or any other step that could aggravate the dispute — while an arbitration panel is functioning, and to attend all meetings the panel convenes. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38] It read that provision as an obligation on the parties, not merely an aspiration, and noted that earlier awards had refused to continue where workers were striking or an employer had locked out. [Para 38][Para 39]

It then traced the Council's practice on non-compliance: in Case No. 04/04 (IMS) the Council issued a notice of 29 January 2004 stating that a party which strikes during proceedings has no right to do so and that the Council cannot resolve or issue an award on the dispute, though it may take the case up again once all the unions stop striking; in Case No. 18/04 (hotels) it ordered the strike and any disruption on hotel premises stopped from 23 April 2004 and reserved the right to close the proceedings if a party breached the order; in Case No. 77/05 (Franko Nid Thing) it said it would not process the dispute if the strike continued; and in Cases No. 110/11 (Simano) and No. 17/13 (Internal Fashion 2 Royal) it declined to continue for the same reason. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 42][Para 43][Para 44][Para 45]

Applying that practice, the panel found on the parties' own information of 17 July 2015 that the workers were still striking and had not complied with Order No. 027/15. [Para 47] It held that it could not hold a hearing to resolve the dispute while its own order was being ignored, and therefore terminated the proceedings under Article 20. [Para 48][Para 49]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the Council has ordered a strike suspended under Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 and the workers keep striking, the Council will terminate the arbitration instead of hearing the demands on their merits, and will take the dispute up again only once the strike has ended; the same applies in reverse to an employer that locks out. [Para 38][Para 41][Para 48][Para 49]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's statement that it retains the right to reopen the case once the strike stops, and its account of the employer's request for a return-to-work order, were not necessary to the termination. [Para 21][Para 41]
