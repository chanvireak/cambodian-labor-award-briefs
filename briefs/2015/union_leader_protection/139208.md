# Award No. 128/15 — I Cheng (Cambodia) Hub Production (Arbitration Council, 30 June 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun, employer-appointed; An Virak, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, presiding) · Doc ID 139208

## 1. Facts
I Cheng (Cambodia) Hub Production (អ៊ី ចេង) is a shoe manufacturer in Takeo with about 2,800 workers; 481 of them transferred their claims to the Cambodian Democratic Garment Workers' Union (C.CAWDU). [Para 50][Para 51][Para 52] On 12 May 2015 the union notified the employer by EMS that five workers were candidates in a union leadership election set for 24 May 2015, and the employer dismissed all five between 12 and 19 May. [Para 90][Para 126][Para 128] Of ten original demands, five were withdrawn and two merged, leaving candidate reinstatement, private hospital certificates, cleaning duties and strike-period wages. [Para 22][Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate the five union election candidates and pay their back wages? [Para 53][Para 112]
2. Whether the employer must accept medical certificates from private hospitals recognised by the Ministry of Health? [Para 222]
3. Whether the employer must stop assigning line workers to perform cleaning duties? [Para 231]
4. Whether the employer must maintain wages, attendance bonus, travel, meal, technical and health allowances during the 19–26 May 2015 strike? [Para 250]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The five were dismissed without fault, warning or valid reason and solely because they stood as union candidates; public hospitals cannot treat every illness and are often far from workers' homes, so recognised private certificates should be accepted; line workers are made to clean three times a day on rotation; and wages and benefits must continue through the strike. [Para 54][Para 95][Para 99][Para 108]
**Employer (respondent):** Two candidates performed poorly and one was warned twice for poor stitching and talking; the other two incited the strike and were dismissed for serious misconduct under Article 83; only state hospital certificates count under the NSSF scheme; the company employs its own cleaners; and Article 332 removes any obligation to pay during a strike, which was called without notice. [Para 58][Para 74][Para 96][Para 101][Para 111]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must reinstate Mr T, Mr K and Ms L with back wages from dismissal to reinstatement, and must accept private hospital certificates from facilities recognised by the Ministry of Health; reinstatement and back pay are refused for Mr S and Ms N, who receive instead the statutory severance of their respective contract types; and the cleaning-duty and strike-wage demands are dismissed. [Para 288][Para 289][Para 290][Para 291][Para 292]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 293(1), under which dismissing a workers' delegate or a candidate requires the labour inspector's prior approval, read with Article 3 of Prakas No. 305: candidates enjoy protection from 45 days before the election to 45 days after. [Para 112][Para 115][Para 116] Following earlier awards it applied three cumulative conditions — protected status, dismissal inside the protection window, and notice of candidacy to the employer by any reasonable means — and found the first two met for all five, since the dismissals fell 13 days and 7–8 days before the poll. [Para 120][Para 129][Para 131] The third condition failed for Mr S and Ms N because their dismissals at 09:00 and 15:35 on 12 May preceded the employer's receipt of the EMS notice that day, whereas the other three were dismissed on 18 and 19 May after notice. [Para 136][Para 139][Para 144]

Because the employer produced no evidence of seeking the labour inspector's approval, the dismissals of the three were procedurally invalid, and reinstatement with back pay followed. [Para 161][Para 163] For Mr S the panel found no valid reason and no notice, but preferred compensation over reinstatement given the broken relationship. [Para 183][Para 189][Para 191] For Ms N, on a fixed-duration contract running to 9 July 2015, Article 73(3) permits early termination at the employer's will against damages at least equal to wages to the contract's end, plus 5% end-of-contract compensation, final wages and unused leave, so reinstatement was refused but those sums are owed. [Para 196][Para 199][Para 217]

On issue 2 the panel held "official doctor" in Article 71(3) includes a licensed practitioner at a private clinic or hospital holding a Ministry of Health diploma and practice permit, so such certificates must be accepted. [Para 224][Para 226][Para 229] On issue 3 the workers' duty roster was undated and carried no company mark, and the employer's evidence that workers only tidy their own workstation was unrebutted, so no breach was proved. [Para 240][Para 244][Para 248] On issue 4 Article 332 suspends the contract during a strike and removes wages whether the strike is lawful or not; the attendance bonus counts as wage under Article 103 and Prakas No. 230, but since no work was performed none of the six heads was payable — and the strike itself was called without notice. [Para 251][Para 257][Para 266][Para 281]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union election candidate is protected from dismissal for 45 days before and after the poll, and a dismissal inside that window without the labour inspector's prior approval is invalid and attracts reinstatement with back wages — unless the union failed to notify the employer of the candidacy before the dismissal, in which case the protection does not attach. [Para 116][Para 139][Para 163] A "official doctor" certificate under Article 71(3) includes a licensed private practitioner recognised by the Ministry of Health. [Para 229] Wages and wage-based allowances are not payable for strike days, irrespective of whether the strike was lawful. [Para 257][Para 266]

**Obiter dicta:** That even where a dismissal is unlawful, the panel may prefer compensation to reinstatement where the working relationship has broken down. [Para 191]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel's constitution date is printed "28 ខេត្ត សភា ឆ្នាំ ២០១៥" (line 19), where the month is lost to OCR. The reasoning at paragraph 268 refers to workers not working "01 April 2013 to 06 May 2013", dates unrelated to the 2015 strike under consideration. [Para 268] The workforce figure of about 2,800 and the 481 claimants are given without reconciliation to the eight workers who attended. [Para 51][Para 52] No monetary figure is stated for the back wages or for Ms N's damages, and Mr S's severance is described only by its statutory heads, so the sums cannot be checked against the stated base wage of USD 128 and average wage of USD 200. [Para 65][Para 191][Para 288]
