# Award No. 268/15 — Hung Tak Garment (Arbitration Council, 11 November 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom chosen by the employer party, An Non chosen by the worker party, Aing Eng Tong chair) · Doc ID 139449

## 1. Facts
A Phnom Penh garment factory employing about 319 workers pays a monthly attendance bonus (ប្រាក់រង្វាន់ធ្វើការទៀងទាត់) of USD 10. [Para 38][Para 42] By an agreement of 1 January 2015 signed by the union president and vice-president, absence with permission of under three days costs USD 5 of the bonus, absence of three days or more forfeits it entirely, and unauthorised absence forfeits it outright. [Para 3][Para 45][Para 48] The workers, represented by the union president, who is also a staff delegate, ask that the bonus instead be reduced in proportion to the days of authorised absence. [Para 41][Para 44][Para 51]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pro-rate the attendance bonus to the number of days a worker is absent with permission, rather than deducting USD 5 a day or cancelling the bonus altogether? [Para 42][Para 56]

Two of the three original claims were settled before the award; this was the only issue left. [Para 11][Para 12]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** earlier awards have ordered pro-rating, many factories apply it, and the claim serves both the workers' benefit and the incentive to attend regularly. [Para 44] They accept that the union president signed the 2015 agreement but say the workers did not know how the deduction worked, so they do not accept its validity. [Para 47]
**Employer (respondent):** the practice follows the agreement of 1 January 2015 signed by the worker representative and union president; the employer keeps it as it stands, and no law requires pro-rating. [Para 45][Para 46]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to reduce the attendance bonus in proportion to the days a worker is absent with the employer's permission, and held the agreement of 1 January 2015 unenforceable. [Para 105][Para 106][Para 107]

The panel began with Notification No. 230 K.B/S.CH.N of 25 July 2012, paragraph 2, which repealed paragraph 1 of Notification No. 041 of 7 March 2011 and requires a worker who attends on every working day of the month "without absence" to receive at least USD 10 a month, raised from the earlier USD 7. [Para 56][Para 58][Para 59][Para 62] It treated that notification as a general rule binding on all garment, textile and footwear employers, not a bonus the employer is free to design, so the bonus is an obligation of public-order character rather than a discretionary incentive. [Para 61][Para 62]

It then asked what "without absence" covers. No competent body has interpreted the phrase, and the panel declined to assume it reaches both absence with permission and absence without it. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67] It divided absences into those recognised by law — annual leave, special leave, maternity leave, public holidays and weekly rest — which cannot reduce the bonus at all, so the worker keeps 100 per cent; and absence for personal reasons with the employer's permission, which is not a statutory entitlement and therefore raises the question of full payment, pro-rating, or forfeiture. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 70][Para 72]

On that question the panel reasoned from Articles 71(6) and 72(1): authorised absence with the employer's consent suspends the contract, and a suspension relieves the employer of the duty to pay wages for those days. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 91][Para 92][Para 93] Since Article 103 treats a sum paid as a bonus as part of wages, the same reasoning applies to the attendance bonus. [Para 86][Para 87][Para 88] Paying the full bonus while no work was done would be unjust to the employer, but neither could the employer withhold the whole bonus: it may deduct only the amount corresponding to the days actually absent. [Para 85][Para 94][Para 95]

Finally, Article 13(1) makes the provisions of the Labour Law public order and voids any unilateral decision, contract or convention that does not comply. [Para 100][Para 101] The 2015 agreement gave the workers less than the pro-rated standard the panel drew from Notification No. 230, and was therefore unenforceable. [Para 104][Para 105]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The regular-attendance bonus required by paragraph 2 of Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 is a wage, not a discretionary incentive, and cannot be cancelled in full for authorised absence: absences that the law itself provides for never reduce it, and absence taken with the employer's permission — which suspends the contract under Article 71(6) — permits only a deduction proportionate to the days missed. Any collective agreement or practice allowing total forfeiture is void under Article 13(1) of the Labour Law. [Para 62][Para 69][Para 93][Para 101][Para 105]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's refusal to construe the phrase "without absence" as covering either kind of absence, on the ground that no authority has settled it, and its remark that paying a full bonus for unpaid authorised leave would be unjust to the employer, are interpretive observations not necessary to the order. [Para 67][Para 85]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The trigger for total forfeiture is inconsistent: the practice and the order use three days or more [Para 3][Para 97], while the quoted agreement says four days or more [Para 48], and the same agreement is described as allowing three days at USD 5 in the same passage.
- Notification No. 230 is dated 25 July 2012 in most citations but "ឆ្នាំ ២០២៣" at [Para 59]; the amount is printed as "១០ (ជប់)" instead of ដប់ at [Para 58].
- The company name appears as ហ៊ុង តាក and once as ហ៊ុង គក [Para 7]; the internal-rules number is printed both as "លេខ ០៩ ៥" and "លេខ ០១៩" in one passage [Para 25].
- The chair's name is rendered អាំង អេង ថុង but the signature block adds stray Latin text ("mpmoss work") and the year "2017" beside two signatures [Para 108].
- The award records no monetary total, and the individual claimant is identified only as the union president, whose name recurs throughout; she is referred to here as Ms Y.
