# Award No. 012/15 — Nich T I Pearl (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 06 February 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Virak for the worker party, Kuy Nam chair) · Doc ID 139123 · Khmer original, with a dissenting opinion by Ing Sothy annexed

## 1. Facts
Nich T I Pearl (Cambodia) Co., Ltd cuts and sews garments at Mor village, Dangkao district, Phnom Penh, with approximately 900 workers. [Para 1][Para 25][Para 26] A worker (name redacted) began on 20 January 2014 in the sewing section, served two months' probation, then worked on successive three-month fixed duration contracts, the last due to expire on 20 January 2015. [Para 36][Para 76][Para 103] On 30 November 2014 she was elected president of the newly formed Cambodian union at the factory; on 01 December 2014, at about 10 a.m., the administration called her in and terminated her, telling her the contract had expired, without notice and without seeking the labour inspector's permission. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 51][Para 98][Para 119] The union had not yet applied to register. [Para 30]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take the worker back to serve normally again and pay her back pay from the date of dismissal to the date of return? [Para 2][Para 32][Para 75]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissal had no valid reason and was connected to the forming of the union, since she was a union leader elected the day before; she was never warned and committed no fault, and the employer refused to accept the notification letter of the elected leadership when it was presented on 01 December 2014. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 46][Para 52]
**Employer (respondent):** she committed serious misconduct by quarrelling with another worker and throwing cloth at her during working hours, and used working time for her own purposes, such as selling phone cards, walking to other sections for about ten minutes at a time; the administration had warned her orally more than once in November 2014. [Para 35][Para 41][Para 42][Para 43][Para 49]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to take the worker back into work and to pay her back pay from the date of dismissal to the date of return. The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification. [Para 127][Para 128][Para 129]

Before the merits the panel had to decide whether Mr H, who appeared for the employer, could represent it. [Para 61] Article 19 of Prakas No. 099/04 allows a party to appear through a representative only with that party's written authorization, as interpreted in Awards No. 161/09, No. 43/10 and No. 144/12 and in the reasoning of Award No. 71/08 River Rich on unauthorized signatories. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 65] Mr H admitted he was the company's administrative officer and had no transfer-of-rights letter, promising to file one later, but none had arrived by the 26 January 2015 document deadline. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 71] The panel therefore held he had no legal standing and disregarded his submissions. [Para 73][Para 74]

On the first question the panel applied Article 293 paragraph 1 of the Labour Law, which bars dismissal of a shop steward, staff representative or candidate without the labour inspector's agreement, extended to unions by Article 3 paragraph 3 and Article 4 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001: candidates in a union leadership election are protected as staff representatives for 45 days before the election and 45 days after it, and founders and members are protected from the registration application until 30 days after registration. [Para 77][Para 78][Para 79][Para 80][Para 83][Para 84][Para 85] Following Award No. 40/10 Meng Yan, and Awards No. 103/12 and No. 191/12, the panel required three conditions: the worker belongs to a protected category, the dismissal falls inside the protection period, and the union notified the employer of the names by any practicable means. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 95][Para 98] All three were met — she was elected president on 30 November 2014 and so a protected candidate; she was dismissed one day after the election, while no registration had been filed, so the protection under Award No. 40/10 continued; and the letter of 01 December 2014 notifying the three elected leaders was submitted and unopposed, the employer filing no objection by the deadline, so notice was taken as given. [Para 99][Para 100][Para 103][Para 104][Para 105][Para 106][Para 113][Para 114][Para 117][Para 118]

On the second question the panel held Article 293 requires the employer to obtain the labour inspector's prior authorization before dismissing a protected worker, and the employer produced no evidence of any request. [Para 119][Para 120] The dismissal was therefore procedurally unlawful, and consistent with Awards No. 71/09, No. 09/10 Chung Hao and No. 91/11, the remedy was reinstatement and back pay. [Para 121][Para 122][Para 123][Para 127]

**Dissent (Ing Sothy, employer-party arbitrator):** he agreed with reinstatement but dissented on back pay, because her contract was one of fixed duration: she was entitled to wages from the dismissal only until the contract's expiry date, and whether to renew a fixed duration contract is the right of the parties. [Para 132][Para 133][Para 134][Para 135][Para 136]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker elected to union office is a protected person under Article 293 of the Labour Law and Article 3 of Prakas No. 305: dismissal during the protection period — which, for a person elected before the union is registered, continues at least 45 days past the election and until the registration application under Award No. 40/10 — is unlawful unless the employer first obtained the labour inspector's authorization, and the remedy is reinstatement with back pay from dismissal to return. A representative who appears for a party without written authorization under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099/04 has no standing and his submissions are disregarded. [Para 62][Para 73][Para 93][Para 119][Para 122]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's treatment of the employer's misconduct allegations — the quarrel of 17 November 2014, the earlier cloth-throwing of 17 September 2014 and the oral warnings entered only in the administration's private notebook — became unnecessary once it found the dismissal procedurally barred, and the dissent's view that back pay should stop at the contract's expiry date was not adopted. [Para 48][Para 50][Para 134][Para 135]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The employer's name is printed only in Khmer, in several variants (ក្រុមហ៊ុនីច ធី អី ភែរីល, ក្រុមហ៊ុនិច ធី អី ភែរឹល, and a corrupted ក្រុមហ៊ុនីច ធី ជីវិត អី ភែរឹល at [Para 102]); the transliteration above is not from the face of the award.
- The contract end date is given as 20 January 2015 at [Para 36], [Para 76] and [Para 103] but as 20 December 2014 at [Para 40]; the dismissal date is 01 December 2014 at [Para 37] and 29 November 2014 at [Para 41] and [Para 44].
- The quarrel is dated 17 November 2014 at [Para 41] and [Para 50] but 17 September 2014 at [Para 48], where the panel also finds the cloth was thrown unintentionally.
- Signature and composition names do not match: the worker-party arbitrator is An Virak in the composition [Para 1] while the signature block prints "អាន ឌីន" alongside An Virak, and the chair is Kuy Nam at [Para 1] but "ក្មួយ នាម" at [Para 130].
- The panel's constitution date is corrupt — "ថ្ងៃទី ១៦ ខែមិត្ត រាង ២០១៥" [Para 11] — and the quoted notification letter breaks into nonsense, "ក្រុមហ៊ុន ទទួល ក្រដាស់ កូពី វិញ អត់ យក ក្រដាស់ ស្រី ន ទេ" [Para 59][Para 111].
- The panel states three conditions at [Para 95] but refers at [Para 98] to "all 4 03 conditions", a duplicated numeral.
