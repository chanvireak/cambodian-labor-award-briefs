# Award No. 098/15 — Royal Cambodian Limousine Service Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 1 July 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phalha presiding; Seng Vouch Hun, appointed by the employer; An Virak, appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 139190

## 1. Facts
Royal Cambodian Limousine Service Co., Ltd ("the company"), on Russian Federation Boulevard, Teuk Thla, Sen Sok, Phnom Penh, provides passenger transport and hotel-based driving services and employed about 250 staff. [Para 83][Para 84] The Tourism and Service Workers' Union at the company, registered 24 November 2014, filed thirteen demands on 25 March 2015; conciliation on 8 April 2015 settled four and left nine, and at the hearings the workers withdrew issues 2, 3, 5, 7, 8 and 9, leaving three. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 32][Para 33][Para 84] Sixty-seven drivers signed a transfer of rights to the federation on 12 March 2015; before 2015 they worked twelve-hour schedules and from 1 January 2015 the company moved them to eight hours. [Para 85][Para 110][Para 118] The award is non-binding and becomes final 8 days after notification. [Para 259]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise the base wage of the 67 drivers by USD 30 a month? [Para 171]
2. Whether the employer must pay a health allowance of USD 15 a month to each of the 67 drivers? [Para 191]
3. Whether the employer must pay back overtime of 4 hours a day to hotel-based drivers and 2 hours a day to warehouse-based drivers for the last three years, 2012 to 2014? [Para 197][Para 204]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** base wages of USD 120 to 230 a month do not meet their and their families' needs, the company stopped the 3,000 riel lunch payment and the USD 200 a year health money from 1 January 2015 after the union was formed, new staff are treated better than old, and union members face discrimination; drivers sat at the warehouse through the two-hour lunch break because the employer could call them out at any moment. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 106][Para 144][Para 158]

**Employer (respondent):** the company lost USD 300,000 in 2014, the lunch payment was discretionary and was stopped because some staff got food and others did not, the health money was a one-year trial that invited invoice forgery, and first-aid boxes and Infinity accident insurance are provided instead; drivers receive tips and were never required to stay during the lunch break. [Para 96][Para 98][Para 103][Para 108][Para 109][Para 147][Para 149]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the USD 30 wage increase and the USD 15 health allowance are refused consideration, and the 2 hours a day claim for warehouse drivers is refused; the employer is ordered to pay back overtime for warehouse drivers who actually drove guests during the 11:00–13:00 lunch break, and for hotel drivers 4 hours a day, at 50 percent of the normal rate for day overtime and 100 percent for night overtime between 22:00 and 05:00, for the three years from April 2012 to December 2014. [Para 251][Para 252][Para 253][Para 254][Para 255][Para 256]

On the union's standing the panel held, under Article 268 of the Labour Law and Prakas 305, that the local union was properly registered and could represent its members, but that on issues 1 and 4 it was not the most representative union in the enterprise, so those interest claims could not be heard — following Awards 81/04, 98/04, 202/14, 02/11, 66/11 and 206/12. [Para 164][Para 169][Para 186][Para 188][Para 190]
On issue 6 the panel read Article 137 with Prakas 80 and the amended Articles 139 and 144: normal hours are 8 a day and 48 a week, work beyond the employer's set schedule is overtime at 50 percent, and night work between 22:00 and 05:00 at 100 percent. [Para 204][Para 208][Para 210][Para 212] For warehouse drivers it found the 10-hour schedule included a genuine two-hour lunch break, evidenced by signed in/out sheets, and that the workers had not proved they were confined on site, so the 2 hours were not working time. [Para 218][Para 223][Para 227] Where a warehouse driver did drive during that break, however, the panel treated it as overtime payable at 50 percent against actual vouchers. [Para 230][Para 231] For hotel drivers it found a 12-hour schedule with no designated break, freedom of movement or sign-in, and held the whole 12 hours was working time, so 4 hours a day were overtime. [Para 238][Para 240][Para 243][Para 244] On the limitation period it applied Article 120 (three years) and Article 103 (overtime is wages), and because the complaint was filed on 3 April 2015 it awarded from April 2012 to December 2014. [Para 246][Para 247][Para 249]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the employer sets a schedule that includes a designated meal break and records entry and exit, the break is not working time and no overtime is owed for it merely because the worker chose to remain on site; but where the schedule is a continuous twelve hours with no defined break and the worker must hold himself available for immediate dispatch, the whole period is working time and the hours beyond eight are overtime at 50 percent by day and 100 percent at night, recoverable for the three years before the complaint under Article 120. [Para 227][Para 230][Para 240][Para 244][Para 249]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that a worker's decision not to leave the premises during the lunch break is his own right and that the risk of being called out was only his apprehension, the employer having stated he could decline; it also observed that transport work cannot realistically follow a fixed eight-hour schedule. [Para 224][Para 225][Para 161]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The number of claimants is printed three ways: 67 at paras 85 and 171, 58 at para 115, and 55 at para 203, with no reconciliation. [Para 85][Para 115][Para 203] The wage range is "120 to 230 dollars" at paras 90 and 174 but "120 to 2300 dollars" at para 175. [Para 90][Para 174][Para 175] The complaint is dated 3 April 2015 at para 26 and "03 April 2019 2015" at para 248, and a company notice is dated 24 December 2015, after the award. [Para 26][Para 70][Para 248] The conciliation point count is internally inconsistent, four settled of thirteen leaving nine, and the panel records nine unresolved at paras 27, 28 and 32. [Para 27][Para 28] The worker-appointed arbitrator appears as "An Virak" in the panel list only, and the employer's representatives and the individual drivers named in the awards are not reproduced here.
