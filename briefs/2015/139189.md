# Award No. 097/15 — UDS Gaman (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 27 May 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phalha presiding; Seng Vouch Hun, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 139189

## 1. Facts
UDS Gaman ("UDS"), at Bak Khaeng, Chroy Changvar, Phnom Penh, sews garments and employed about 600 workers at the time of the award. [Para 45][Para 46] The Free Workers' Union at UDS, registered with the Ministry of Labour on 31 October 2014, filed eight demands on 24 March 2015; conciliation on 8 April 2015 settled some and left seven, of which the workers withdrew the demand to dismiss the administrator Mr S at the hearing, leaving six for decision. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 27][Para 28][Para 47] The disputed acts are the employer's practice of cutting the USD 10 monthly attendance bonus, its refusal of a 2,000 riel daily lunch allowance, its piece rates, its monthly rather than immediate payment of holiday and Sunday wages, the absence of a bicycle shelter, and a USD 10 transport and accommodation allowance. [Para 50][Para 139][Para 159][Para 193][Para 217][Para 226] The award is non-binding and becomes final 8 days after notification. [Para 29][Para 241]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must deduct the regular attendance bonus pro rata to the number of days a worker was absent with the employer's lawful permission? [Para 97]
2. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of 2,000 riel a day to each worker? [Para 139]
3. Whether the employer must raise piece rates ("តម្លៃ ឡូត៍") across all sections by 28 percent to match the 2015 minimum wage increase? [Para 159]
4. Whether the employer must pay wages for work done on public holidays or Sundays immediately at the end of that shift or the following day? [Para 193]
5. Whether the employer must build a shelter for workers' bicycles and motorbikes within two months? [Para 217]
6. Whether the employer must pay a transport and accommodation allowance of USD 10 a month to workers who do not use the company's transport? [Para 226]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** a one-dollar cut for a single day's authorised absence exceeds a pro-rata deduction and workers regret the lost money; the absences were agreed by the employer, who had already granted the leave, and under Article 103 and Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 the attendance bonus is wages. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 56] They also argued daily wages do not cover their needs, that piece rates are too low for most sewers to reach the minimum wage, and that fuel and rent have risen. [Para 61][Para 64][Para 85]

**Employer (respondent):** it applies the law, and it already shows understanding by not deducting the whole bonus at once. [Para 58][Para 59] It said the Labour Law does not require a lunch allowance and the company was newly opened with weak finances; it sets piece rates by garment difficulty through the production manager; separate holiday payroll would be costly and no accounting staff work on holidays; it plans a shelter within four months, needing surveys and local-authority approval, and already pays USD 7 a month plus transport. [Para 62][Para 67][Para 78][Para 82][Para 83][Para 86]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer is ordered to deduct the attendance bonus pro rata to the days of authorised absence; the 28 percent claim is refused but the employer is ordered to set piece rates so that a worker of average skill working normally earns at least the guaranteed minimum wage; the lunch allowance, immediate holiday payment, bicycle shelter and USD 10 transport allowance claims are refused consideration. [Para 234][Para 236][Para 237][Para 238][Para 239][Para 240]

On issue 1 the panel held that Notification No. 230 (25 July 2012) point 2 obliges every employer in the textile, garment and footwear sector to pay at least USD 10 a month as attendance bonus, so the bonus is not a discretionary gift but a legal norm; it is nonetheless a form of wage under Article 103. [Para 104][Para 130][Para 131] An absence authorised by the employer suspends the employment contract under Articles 71(6) and 72, so no wage is earned for those days and no bonus either — but the employer may therefore deduct only an amount proportional to the days absent, not the whole bonus. [Para 132][Para 133][Para 135][Para 136][Para 137] The panel followed its earlier awards (112/11, 136/11, 154/11) but held that the new words "without absence" in Notification 230 gave it a fresh legal basis to depart from them, and it declined to do so. [Para 100][Para 101][Para 268][Para 271][Para 272]

On issues 2, 5 and 6 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, contract or past practice creating the claimed entitlements, so each was an interest dispute; because the claimant union was not the most representative union in the enterprise, the panel refused to consider them, following Awards 81/04, 98/04, 202/14, 02/11, 66/11 and 206/12. [Para 140][Para 142][Para 156][Para 157][Para 218][Para 228]
On issue 3 the panel refused a flat 28 percent because difficulty varies between piece types, but held that Article 108 requires piece rates to be set so an average-skill worker earns at least the minimum wage, and that the employer had failed to discharge its evidential burden by not producing the rate formula and wage tables it had been ordered to file. [Para 184][Para 186][Para 189][Para 192] On issue 4 it found no law requiring payment of holiday or Sunday wages at the end of the shift, Article 116 requiring only two payments a month. [Para 214][Para 215]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer has authorised a worker's absence, the employment contract is suspended for those days under Articles 71 and 72 and no wage is earned, so the employer may reduce the statutory USD 10 monthly attendance bonus only in proportion to the days absent, and may not forfeit the whole bonus; a claim for a uniform percentage increase in piece rates is an interest dispute the panel will not hear unless the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise, but the employer still bears the burden of showing that its rates let a worker of average skill reach the minimum wage under Article 108. [Para 136][Para 137][Para 156][Para 184][Para 186]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that it generally respects its earlier decisions and will depart from them only for a new legal basis or good reason, and noted that the employer's practice of not deducting the whole bonus at once was more generous than the law and could continue. [Para 266][Para 270][Para 272]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel president, Kong Phalha, filed a dissenting opinion as an annex, arguing that a worker absent with permission has not met the "without absence" condition and so forfeits the pro-rata bonus as well; the majority's order stands but the dissent contradicts it on issue 1 and is reproduced here rather than resolved. [Para 242][Para 258][Para 273] The bonus is printed as USD 10 a month at paras 50 and 104 but as "100 dollars" at para 97, and the 28 percent claim appears as "1 28 percent" at para 236. [Para 50][Para 97][Para 236] The hearing is dated 29 April 2015 at para 19 but 29 April 2014 at para 24, the panel is said to have been constituted on "22 April 2019 2015" at para 23, and stray years (2016, 2017, 2019) intrude into paras 57, 226 and 242. [Para 19][Para 23][Para 24] Conciliation is said to have settled "09 points" of eight demands at para 21, leaving seven at para 22. [Para 21][Para 22] The withdrawal request is dated 29 April 2015 at para 40, and the company name varies between "UDS Gaman" and "UDS". [Para 40][Para 45] Individual workers, union officers and the employer representative are identified only by partial OCR names and are not reproduced.
