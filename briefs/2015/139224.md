# Award No. 147/15 — Song Foung (Arbitration Council, 13 July 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry, chosen by the employer; An Vira, chosen by the workers; Kong Phallak, chair) · Doc ID 139224

## 1. Facts
Song Foung (សុង ហ្វូង), a garment factory at Samrong Tong, Kampong Speu with about 380 workers, some 90 per cent of them women, dismissed Ms K from the sewing line on 23 May 2015, before her fixed-duration contract had run its term. [Para 40][Para 49][Para 66] She had been dismissed once before and rehired on 30 December 2014 under a new staff number, on a three-month contract renewed twice. [Para 47][Para 48] The Federation of Unions for Workers' Development took up the claims of 49 workers; nine non-conciliated points reached the Secretariat on 17 June 2015. [Para 13][Para 41] At the hearing the workers withdrew four points and merged a fifth into the dismissal claim, leaving three. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 27]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take Ms K back into normal employment after dismissing her on 23 May 2015, and whether that dismissal amounted to anti-union discrimination? [Para 44][Para 96][Para 111]
2. Whether the employer must pay 50 per cent of wages and allowances to women workers for the three months before they stop work for maternity leave? [Para 154]
3. Whether the employer must provide a nursery and a breastfeeding room in the factory? [Para 163]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The dismissal had no valid reason and was aimed at a worker who regularly came forward to resolve other workers' problems, having led the Khmer Workers' Friendship Union at the factory; the employer refuses to renew the contracts of workers who join any union other than that one. [Para 45][Para 46] Women who have just given birth are not strong enough to come and collect maternity pay and need the money beforehand to meet delivery costs. [Para 62] Women with small children cannot leave them at home alone and the factory has no nursery. [Para 68]
**Employer (respondent):** It did not appear, informing the Secretariat by telephone that it would not attend because the union in the case is not its factory's local union. [Para 23][Para 91]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement refused; the employer ordered to pay Ms K damages, the 5 per cent end-of-contract indemnity, payment in lieu of unused annual leave and her final wages, and to provide a nursery; the maternity-pay claim refused. [Para 173][Para 174][Para 175][Para 176]

Three preliminary points were decided first. On standing, Article 268 of the Labour Law makes registration with the Ministry the moment a professional organisation acquires the rights the Law recognises; the factory's local union was unregistered, so it could not represent its members. [Para 70][Para 73][Para 78] The panel nevertheless held, under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, that the 49 workers who had signed a letter transferring their rights and the workers present at the hearing were parties, counting 59 in all. [Para 79][Para 82][Para 83] On the employer's absence, Article 21 of the same Prakas allows the panel to proceed where a party duly invited fails to appear without valid reason; the invitation had been sent by EMS, and the union's status was no valid reason. [Para 85][Para 89][Para 92][Para 94]

On the dismissal, the three-month contract began on 30 December 2014 and was cut short on 23 May 2015, which the panel characterised as termination by the employer. [Para 96][Para 99] Because the employer did not appear, it was treated as having abandoned its right of reply and the workers' account was accepted. [Para 100][Para 101] The anti-union discrimination claim failed on evidence: Articles 12 and 279 forbid using union membership or activity as a ground for dismissal, but the burden lies on the party alleging it, and no election record, no letter and no Ministry recognition showed Ms K held any union office, while no evidence at all was filed by the deadline. [Para 111][Para 112][Para 103][Para 109] The dismissal was therefore held unrelated to anti-union discrimination. [Para 121] That did not save the employer: under Article 73 it may end a fixed-duration contract before term of its own will, but must then pay damages of at least the wages due to the contract's end. [Para 127][Para 130] The panel ordered those damages, the 5 per cent indemnity under the same article, payment in lieu of unused annual leave under Articles 166 and 167, and final wages under Article 116. [Para 138][Para 142][Para 147][Para 150]

The maternity-pay claim was refused for want of a claimant: the only woman identified, Ms S, was neither on the letter transferring rights nor present at the hearing, and no evidence was filed for her. [Para 158][Para 160][Para 161]

The nursery claim succeeded. Article 186 requires any enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a breastfeeding room and a nursery on or near the premises, and where it cannot, to bear the cost of an outside nursery on actual invoices for children over 18 months. [Para 163][Para 166] The factory had 380 workers, about 90 per cent women, and no nursery; the absent employer was taken to have accepted the claim, and the panel ordered a nursery provided. [Para 168][Para 171]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An unregistered local union cannot represent its members, but the panel may still hear the individual workers who transferred their rights to a federation in writing; and where a fixed-duration contract is ended before term, the employer may lawfully do so yet owes damages equal to the wages to the contract's end, the 5 per cent indemnity, unused annual leave and final wages — while an anti-union motive is made out only by evidence, filed in time, that the dismissed worker actually held union office. [Para 82][Para 109][Para 121][Para 130]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observations that women who have just given birth find it difficult to travel to collect maternity pay, and that 1,200 riel a month was deducted from members' wages as union dues, played no part in the orders made. [Para 55][Para 62]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The claim states Ms K's staff number as B0842 and the reasoning as B0843. [Para 2][Para 44] The final contract is said to expire on 30 June 2014 although employment began on 30 December 2014, which is impossible and is plainly meant as 2015; the same slip recurs in the reasoning. [Para 98][Para 136][Para 47] The invitation to the employer is dated 24 July 2015 for a hearing held on 1 July 2015, and the panel set an evidence deadline of 3 June 2015 at that same later hearing. [Para 88][Para 29] The panel is said to have been constituted on 23 June 2019. [Para 19] The number of transferring workers is printed as "៣០ ៤៩", and although 15 attended and only 5 of them had signed the transfer letter, 10 attendees are counted as parties. [Para 41][Para 43][Para 81][Para 82] The company appears as សុង ហ្វូង and សុង ហ្វុង, and its Latin form here is a transliteration, not a printed rendering. [Para 1][Para 39][Para 78] The chair is printed គង់ ផល្នៈ in the header and គង់ ផល្លៈ in the signature block. [Para 1][Para 178] The renumbering table lists eight old numbers for nine new issues, and the operative paragraph prints "៥០ ខែ ភាគរយ" (50 month per cent). [Para 24][Para 175]
