# Award No. 022/15 — Double U N D (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 24 February 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Pen Bunchea (president) · Doc ID 139133

## 1. Facts
The respondent is a garment factory in Stung Mean Chey, Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, with about 1,804 workers. Nineteen demands followed a strike; conciliation settled 13 and left 6 unresolved, which reached the Council on 21 January 2015. The Federation of Workers' Unions took up the file, but only 12 workers were complainants — 6 named in a letter of authority of 14 January 2015 and 6 who attended the hearing — against a workforce of 1,804. [Para 17][Para 21][Para 45]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must build a canteen where workers can eat lunch? [Para 4]
2. Whether the employer must pay a travel and accommodation allowance of 10 USD per month? [Para 6]
3. Whether the employer must pay an attendance bonus of 15 USD per month? [Para 8]
4. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of 2,000 riel per day? [Para 10]
5. Whether the employer must reduce the attendance bonus proportionally to the days a worker was absent on properly authorised leave? [Para 12]
6. Whether the employer must issue pay slips two days before each payday? [Para 13]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** workers eat lunch standing on the pavement and under trees, where rain, sun, dust and falling branches spoil their food and one worker was injured by a falling branch; other factories pay 2,000 riel for lunch and provide pay slips two days early so that errors can be raised. [Para 55]

**Employer (respondent):** it pays 1,000 riel a day in lieu of a canteen under point 6(a) of the internal regulations and by a 2008 agreement with the Free Workers' Union; it applies Notification No. 230 on travel and the 10 USD attendance bonus; it cannot pay 2,000 riel for lunch; and payslips are issued with the wages twice a month, with a day allowed to challenge errors. [Para 5][Para 7][Para 9][Para 11]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 5 granted (proportional deduction ordered); issues 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 dismissed. [Para 210][Para 214]

On issue 1 the panel found the internal regulations expressly substitute 1,000 riel a day for a canteen, that both parties acknowledged this, and that no labour law, prakas, collective agreement or practice obliged the employer to provide a dining hall. The claim therefore exceeded the legal minimum, was an interest dispute, and the 12 complainants were not the most representative union, so it was dismissed for want of jurisdiction. [Para 109][Para 111]

Issues 2, 3 and 4 were disposed of the same way. The employer paid the 7 USD travel and accommodation allowance fixed by Notification No. 230 and the 10 USD attendance bonus it sets, and no instrument required 10 USD or 15 USD or a 2,000 riel lunch allowance; each claim therefore exceeded the minimum and failed on the representativeness ground. [Para 132][Para 140]

Issue 5 succeeded because it was a claim of right. The panel held that the 10 USD attendance bonus in point 2 of Notification No. 230 is a general legal rule binding garment employers, not a discretionary reward, and that an attendance bonus is a form of wage. Leave authorised by the employer suspends the contract under Articles 71(6) and 72(1), so no wage is due for those days and the bonus may be reduced — but only in proportion to the days taken, not forfeited in whole. The panel followed its earlier awards, including Nos. 071/14, 309/14 and 346/14. [Para 194][Para 195]

On issue 6 the panel read Article 112 of the Labour Law as requiring the employer to make the components of wages clear and readily understandable for each pay period. Since the employer attaches an itemised payslip to each payment, the article was satisfied; it does not require the slip to be handed over two days early. [Para 204][Para 208]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus fixed by ministerial notification is a component of wages, so where the employer has authorised a worker's absence the contract is suspended for those days and the bonus may be reduced proportionally to the days taken, never forfeited in full; a demand for more than a statutory minimum remains an interest dispute that only the most representative union may bring. Article 112 requires clear wage information at each pay period, not advance payslips. [Para 194][Para 120]

**Obiter dicta:** although the canteen claim failed for want of jurisdiction, the panel observed that the employer ought nonetheless to attend to hygiene and safety at mealtimes, since workers are the key human resource in its production chain. [Para 127]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award text is heavily degraded. The claimed lunch allowance is printed as "42000 3 riel" in one place and as 2,000 riel elsewhere. The quotation of point 6(a) of the internal regulations is broken mid-sentence by scanner noise at lines 108 and 150. The paragraph constituting the panel reads "23 January 2023 2015", and the record of the hearing gives its date as 4 February 2014 where the face of the award and the summonses give 2015. The signature block prints the worker-appointed arbitrator as "An Non" twice and leaves the employer-appointed signature line blank. The award also carries an annexed dissent by arbitrator Ing Sothy against issue 5, arguing that an attendance bonus is a conditional reward rather than wage and may therefore be withheld in full.
