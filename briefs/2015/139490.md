# Order No. 317/15 — Suy Yat (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 23 December 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Hem Hu Narith, chosen by the employer party; Toun Si Phan, chosen by the worker party; Pen Bun Chhea, chair chosen by the two) · Doc ID 139490

## 1. Facts
Suy Yat (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a garment factory at Chbar Mon with about 417 workers, faced a claim from workers represented by the Free Workers' Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia, registered on 27 April 2015 under No. 3268 with roughly 250 members at the plant. [Para 33][Para 35][Para 36] A strike was reported on 20 November 2015 on six demands; conciliation failed, the non-conciliation report No. 2855/15 of 30 November 2015 reached the Council on 1 December 2015, and the Council suspended the strike on 2 December 2015, the workers returning on 3 December. [Para 14][Para 12][Para 17][Para 18] The employer attended the hearing of 17 December 2015 and then withdrew, saying it had settled the points in-house, so the panel heard the workers alone. [Para 19][Para 102]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must take back the two dismissed union leaders? [Para 2][Para 45]
2. Whether the company must pay the overtime meal allowance regularly? [Para 3][Para 60]
3. Whether the company must stop compelling workers to work overtime? [Para 4][Para 65]
4. Whether the company must grant leave when a worker has a necessary personal matter? [Para 5][Para 71]
5. Whether the administration staff and interpreters must use appropriate language towards workers? [Para 6][Para 76]
6. Whether the company must dismiss team leader No. 1, bear the consequences when the fingerprint scanner fails, and raise the travel and accommodation allowance to USD 15? [Para 7][Para 8][Para 9][Para 85]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the two leaders were dismissed because they held union office and the dismissal was linked to their union activity [Para 53][Para 54]; the meal allowance used to come on the 1st and 15th and now arrives on the 5th or 18th [Para 61]; team leaders shift overtime onto workers, scold those who refuse and threaten to terminate them [Para 68]; leave is refused even for illness or a relative's accident and needs three approvals [Para 71][Para 72]; staff shout, intimidate and point at workers, one refuses to speak to them, and interpreters call them brainless [Para 77][Para 80][Para 81]; the team leader should go, the company should bear the cost of scanner failures, and the travel allowance should rise to USD 15. [Para 85][Para 93]
**Employer (respondent):** it withdrew from the hearing and offered no answer to any of the workers' contentions, relying instead on the settlement minute of 7 December 2015. [Para 19][Para 58][Para 59]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay the overtime meal allowance on the 1st and 15th of each month; to apply the voluntary principle to overtime; to grant leave for necessary personal matters; to have its staff and interpreters speak appropriately; and to install a working scanner, cross-checked against the daily attendance lists; it refused the two leaders' reinstatement, team leader No. 1's dismissal and the USD 15 allowance. [Para 254][Para 255][Para 256][Para 257][Para 259][Para 253][Para 258][Para 261]

On the employer's absence, Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 allows the panel to proceed where a properly invited party fails to appear without good reason, so it decided on the workers' evidence. [Para 97][Para 105] On who was claiming, the local union could not itself be the claimant: none of its office-holders attended and the authorisation letter of 20 November 2015 named only six workers, so with the four other workers who attended the panel treated eight individuals as the claimants. [Para 113][Para 117][Para 122][Para 123] That disposed of issue 1 — the two leaders were not claimants, had signed an agreement of 7 December 2015 resigning and accepting their entitlements, and did not appear — so reinstatement and back-pay were refused. [Para 118][Para 124][Para 125]

On the meal allowance, point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 requires that a worker who volunteers for overtime be given a free meal or paid 2,000 riel a day, and the panel held that payment must be regular enough for the worker to buy food for overtime. [Para 128][Para 132][Para 133] The employer had paid on the 1st and 15th and now delayed to the 5th and 18th, so payment on the 1st and 15th was ordered. [Para 141][Para 147]

On overtime, Article 4 of Prakas No. 80 of 2 March 1999 and Notification No. 030/15 of 27 February 2015 both rest the arrangement on the worker's consent: the employer may ask, the worker decides, and no disciplinary measure may follow a refusal. [Para 148][Para 151][Para 156] Scolding workers, moving them to a group without overtime and threatening termination to secure it were therefore improper, and the voluntary principle was ordered applied. [Para 167][Para 168]

On leave, Article 2 of the Labour Law gives the employer full authority to manage its establishment, including the procedure for granting leave, provided that authority is lawful and reasonable; refusing leave for illness and family emergencies was not. [Para 175][Para 181][Para 182]

On language, the panel followed its order in case 301/15 that expressions such as "you have no brain" are improper for anyone to use, and Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 lets it order an immediate end to prohibited conduct. [Para 188][Para 190][Para 191]

Dismissing team leader No. 1 was refused: transfer or dismissal is the employer's managerial right under Article 2, and the panel may order it only where a worker endangers others' health or safety, which was not shown. [Para 202][Para 206][Para 209] The scanner point was granted: having chosen fingerprint scanning, the employer must keep the machine working and legible, a malfunction is its own fault and not the worker's, and cross-checking against the team attendance lists makes verification accurate. [Para 219][Para 226][Para 227] The travel allowance was refused as an interest dispute: point 1 of Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 fixes USD 7 a month where no transport or accommodation is provided, the company pays that, no evidence showed neighbouring factories pay USD 15, and no agreement covered it — and an interest claim lies only for a most representative union. [Para 228][Para 237][Para 247][Para 251]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Overtime rests on the worker's consent: an employer may request it but may neither compel it nor discipline, reassign or threaten a worker who declines, and the meal allowance attached to overtime must be paid on a rhythm regular enough for the worker to buy food. [Para 151][Para 133][Para 167] Where an employer installs a fingerprint scanner, its malfunction is the employer's fault, not the worker's, and no penalty may follow; but choosing and disciplining team leaders stays the employer's managerial right unless a worker endangers others' safety. [Para 219][Para 227][Para 206]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that a party which stays away forfeits its chance to answer, and its account of how the eight claimants were identified, are procedural observations rather than grounds of decision. [Para 105][Para 122]
