# Award No. 88/06 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូឌា អឹប ភែរេល (Cambodia Apparel) (Arbitration Council, 2 November 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៉ីង សុទ្ធី, វង្ស វណ្ណា, គង់ ផល្លៈ) · Doc ID 140732

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូឌា អឹប ភែរេល (Cambodia Apparel) runs a garment factory on National Road 4, Chambok village, Samrong Tong district, Kampong Speu, employing about 643 workers [Para 28]. The claimant is សហព័ន្ធឋ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ (Cambodian Youth Labour Confederation) together with its enterprise union at the factory [Para 2]. Of twelve demands raised, the parties settled ten in conciliation; only issues 3 and 12 remained for the panel [Para 23][Para 24]. Issue 3 sought the dismissal of the administration chief, Ms H, and repayment of USD 20 she allegedly took from a worker during training [Para 47]. Issue 12 sought reimbursement of a 10,100 riel health-check fee for all workers [Para 53].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 3) Whether the company should dismiss Ms H from her post and repay the USD 20 she is accused of taking from a worker during training? [Para 47]
2. (Issue 12) Whether the company must reimburse the 10,100 riel health-check fee to all workers? [Para 53]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ:** Ms H took USD 20 from a worker when training her child; some workers paid 10,100 riel themselves at the clinic in 2002 and 1999, while others had that sum deducted from their wages [Para 49][Para 55][Para 56]. The union conceded it could not produce evidence of the USD 20 payment [Para 51].
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូឌា អឹប ភែរេល:** Ms H denies taking the money and asks the workers to show proof [Para 50]. The company refuses to reimburse the health-check fee because payment was a condition of recruitment [Para 54], and it challenged the legal standing of the union's newly elected leadership to represent the workers [Para 60].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel refused the demand to dismiss Ms H and to repay USD 20 [Para 116], and ordered the employer to pay the 10,100 riel health-check fee to the workers from the date the award takes effect [Para 117].
On issue 3, the panel noted that under Article 65 of the Labour Law a labour contract binds the parties, and that hiring and dismissal are the employer's prerogative as a party to that contract [Para 83][Para 90]. Following Award No. 73/04-Yen Yuen, workers may demand a colleague's dismissal only with evidence that the person is dangerous and would cause labour insecurity [Para 92]. The only allegation was the USD 20 payment, and no such evidence was produced [Para 94], so the demand had no legal basis and was refused [Para 95]. The separate demand for repayment of USD 20 also failed for want of clear evidence [Para 97][Para 98].
On issue 12, the panel agreed with earlier awards that Article 247(c) of the Labour Law obliges the employer to bear health-check costs [Para 99][Para 100], and ordered repayment of 10,100 riel [Para 102]. However, Article 120 imposes a three-year limitation on wage-related claims [Para 104], so only payments made on or after 22 August 2003 could be recovered [Para 115].
On representation, the panel found the new committee's election invalid: only 60 members voted, candidates were at work during the ballot, the vote preceded the secretary's resignation, and it coincided with the hearing [Para 72][Para 74][Para 75][Para 76]. The committee therefore cannot represent the union, but may represent the 40 workers who transferred their rights to it, and the award covers only those 40 [Para 79][Para 80][Para 81].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A workers' demand that an employer dismiss a colleague succeeds only where evidence shows the person is dangerous and would create labour insecurity; the employer holds the contractual prerogative to hire and dismiss [Para 92][Para 90]. Health-check fees are the employer's obligation under Article 247(c), but reimbursement reaches only payments made within three years before the claim, under Article 120 [Para 99][Para 104].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observation that the ballot was suspect because travel from Phnom Penh to Kampong Speu takes at least an hour, so candidates could not have been both at the factory vote and at the hearing [Para 77].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company name is printed in several variant spellings across the file (ខេមបូឌា អឹប ភែរេល, អ៊ែប តែ រេល, អឺប ភរេល) [Para 2][Para 25]. The demand at Para 13 states the health-check fee as "១០០ រៀល (ដប់ ពាន់ លាន មួយ រៀល)" while the reasons and operative order use 10,100 riel [Para 13][Para 102]. Para 42 dates a hearing "ថ្ងៃទី ២៤ ខែតុលា ឆ្នាំ ២០០៤" where 2006 is meant [Para 42]. The union statutes are quoted as requiring more than 100 members at Para 43 but 100 members at Para 74 [Para 43][Para 74]. The signature block is badly garbled by OCR and one arbitrator's name appears in two forms [Para 119].
