# Award No. 85/07 — Vivatino Design (Cambodia) Pty. Ltd v Cambodian Labour Union Federation and Cambodian Labour Union (Arbitration Council, 20 September 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Hem Hour Naryth (employer-appointed), Anan (worker-appointed), Kong Phallack (chair) · Doc ID 140634

## 1. Facts
Vivatino Design (Cambodia) Pty. Ltd runs a factory in Khan Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, with about 1,000 workers and six unions, none of which holds most representative status [Para 2][Para 19][Para 20].
The claimant, the Cambodian Labour Union (CLU) at the factory, affiliated to the Cambodian Labour Union Federation (CLUF), has about 120 members [Para 3][Para 21].
Conciliation settled 10 of 14 issues and the remaining four were forwarded to the Council on 27 August 2007; Issue 2, severance for four dismissed workers, was settled at the hearing, leaving Issues 1, 3 and 4 [Para 11][Para 12][Para 14][Para 15].
On 11 April 2007 workers struck over annual leave refused around Khmer New Year, and on 19 April 2007 the union president, Ms S, was punched twice by two bodyguards of the factory's administrative manager, Mr L, as she stopped them seizing the strikers' loudspeaker [Para 23][Para 25][Para 43][Para 44].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must compensate Ms S US$500 for injuries inflicted by the administrative manager's bodyguards during a strike in front of the factory? [Para 5][Para 23]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay workers an additional US$5 a month instead of building a day care centre? [Para 7][Para 77]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the company must reimburse the 1,000 riel monthly union contribution for January to June 2007, where members had authorised a deduction the company never made? [Para 8][Para 81]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), Cambodian Labour Union Federation (CLUF) and Cambodian Labour Union (CLU):** Ms S said the bodyguards acted on Mr L's instruction to take the loudspeaker by any means, so the company must answer for its manager's men; she was two months pregnant, was medically examined, and miscarried days later [Para 37][Para 40][Para 49][Para 53]. On Issue 3 the union sought US$5 extra rather than a day care centre, and on Issue 4 reimbursement of the 1,000 riel because the company had ignored the workers' request [Para 77][Para 85].
**Employer (respondent), Vivatino Design (Cambodia) Pty. Ltd:** the company said it had no bodyguards, had not ordered anyone beaten, and that accountability lay with the culprit, not the company; its investigation had produced no result [Para 45][Para 67][Para 73]. On Issue 3 it pointed to its agreement with another union providing US$5 a month for one year, and on Issue 4 it said it had never deducted the 1,000 riel and so owed nothing [Para 79][Para 83].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the US$500 compensation claim, declined to consider the additional US$5 demand, and rejected the 1,000 riel reimbursement claim [Para 135][Para 137].
Issue 1 turned on Article 248. The panel held the assault was not a work-related accident because during a strike the labour contracts are suspended under Article 332, so the workers were not working for the employer even though the company was operating [Para 88][Para 106]. It read "during working hours" as time the worker is working or should be working, and rejected a broad reading covering any accident on company time [Para 109]. Liability under Article 249 therefore did not attach, and the panel distinguished Award 23/07 You Ming, which concerned medical fees for a genuine work accident [Para 90][Para 91][Para 114]. It added that nothing prevented Ms S from seeking other legal solutions, or the company from defending the claim [Para 118].
On Issue 3 the panel applied Article 186, requiring a nursing room and creche where 100 or more women are employed, with reimbursement of creche fees where no creche can be built [Para 119][Para 120]. Because the company already paid US$5 a month for one year under an agreement with another union, and that sum was not inconsistent with Article 186, the demand for an extra US$5 went beyond the agreement and was an interests dispute [Para 122][Para 124][Para 127]. As the CLU lacked most representative status under Article 277, the panel declined to consider it, following Awards 53/04 Kong Hung, 79/07 Teratex, 57/04 Siu Quinh and 33/07 Gold Fame [Para 120][Para 128][Para 129].
On Issue 4 the panel set Article 129(2) and Prakas 305 Article 5, allowing a written request 15 days ahead, against the Article 281 ban on employer deduction [Para 130][Para 131][Para 132]. Both sides agreed the company never deducted the 1,000 riel, so the money stayed in the members' wages and the company took no benefit; the union's remedy lay against its own members [Para 133][Para 134][Para 135].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An injury suffered by a worker during a strike is not a work-related accident under Article 248 of the Labour Law, because Article 332 suspends the labour contract and the worker is not working for the employer, so the liability of managers under Article 249 does not arise [Para 106][Para 109][Para 113].
A union without most representative status under Article 277 cannot bring an interests dispute, so a demand for benefits going beyond an existing collective agreement is declined rather than decided [Para 128][Para 129].
Where a union contribution the worker authorised was never deducted, the employer owes no reimbursement, the wages having remained with the worker, and the claim lies within the union [Para 134][Para 135].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that the incident was a bad and tragic accident that should not have happened, and that its rejection of the claim barred no other legal route for the claimant [Para 118][Para 119].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
This is an unofficial English translation in which the letter "t" is dropped throughout ("he" for "the", "hat" for "that"), occasionally fusing words; meaning is recoverable but quotations of the Labour Law are unreliable in detail. Dates conflict: the employer is recorded as accepting a strike on 19 September 2007 while every other reference is to 11 and 19 April 2007 [Para 33]. The employer's evidence list includes internal work rules dated 10 October 2007, after the 5 September 2007 hearing [Para 16]. Passages repeat verbatim, notably the definition of "during working hours" and the citation of Gold Fame, Issue 2 [Para 109][Para 129]. The issue list opens with a stray "1. Ms." fragment before the first demand is stated [Para 3], and the claimant's late documents of 11 September 2007 were excluded because no reason was given for the delay [Para 75][Para 76].
