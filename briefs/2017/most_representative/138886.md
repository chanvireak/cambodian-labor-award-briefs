# Award No. 001/17 — Diamond City Bavet Limited (Arbitration Council, 7 March 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phallak presiding; Chhiv Phirom, employer nominee; An Nonn, worker nominee) · Doc ID 138886

## 1. Facts
Diamond City Bavet Limited — called Grand Diamond City throughout its own documents — runs a hotel, an entertainment club and a casino at Kbal Spean village, Poipet, O'Chrov district, Banteay Meanchey, and told the panel it employed 1,800 workers. [Para 1] On 14 December 2016 its workers complained to the provincial labour department over eight points, the first being the dismissal of their union president; conciliation on 29 December 2016 settled one point and left seven, which reached the Council on 2 January 2017. [Para 13] The panel sat at a Poipet hotel on 16 January 2017; issue 5 was settled, issues 4 and 7 withdrawn, leaving issues 1, 2, 3 and 6. [Para 12]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate the dismissed union president in his former post with his former role, wage and benefits, and pay his wages and benefits from the date of dismissal until his return? [Para 117]
2. Whether the employer must pay 130% of the day wage for night shifts? [Para 151]
3. Whether the employer must pay a food allowance of 3,000 baht a month? [Para 167]
6. Whether the 20-minute breaks taken after every 80 minutes of work must be counted as working time? [Para 180]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the president was elected on 12 October 2016 and dismissed on 19 November 2016 without the labour inspector's agreement and because of his union activity, so he must be reinstated with back pay; night work between 22:00 and 05:00 carries 130%; 50 baht a day cannot buy the four meals the company used to provide; and the 20-minute breaks are too short to leave the workplace. [Para 124]
**Employer (respondent):** he was suspended for seven days after security found him in a prohibited room, reinstated, then dismissed when he publicised the affair on Facebook and Radio Free Asia, breaching confidentiality and its internal rules; the monthly wage of 3,876 baht for hotel staff and 4,875 baht for casino staff already includes the night premium; the meal claim has no legal basis; and breaks are genuine rest, with overtime paid beyond eight hours. [Para 129]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must reinstate the union president with his former role, wage and benefits and pay his wages and benefits from the dismissal to his return; must pay 130% of the day wage for night shifts; the 3,000 baht food allowance is refused but meals must continue under the internal rules; the claim on rest periods is dismissed. [Para 369]
On standing, the panel held that the enterprise union was unregistered and its president could not represent the claimants in that capacity, but 111 workers had delegated their claims to the Cambodian Labour Confederation and four workers recognised by the provincial labour department attended; under clause 1 of Prakas 362 they could represent the collective dispute. [Para 202]
On issue 1, Article 67 of the Law on Trade Unions of 17 May 2016 protects every candidate in a union leadership election for 45 days before and 45 days after the ballot, and Article 43 forbids dismissing a protected worker without the labour inspector's agreement. [Para 219] The panel applied its Award 169/16 test of three conditions: the worker must be a protected person, the dismissal must fall inside the protection window, and the union must have notified the employer, copying the ministry. [Para 223] The president had been elected on 12 October 2016, dismissed on 19 November 2016 — about 38 days later — and the employer had received the election result; all three were satisfied. [Para 243] The employer admitted it never sought the inspector's authorisation, so the dismissal was unlawful and the panel expressly declined to examine his alleged misconduct. [Para 259] Reinstatement with back pay followed under Article 34 of Prakas 099. [Para 261]
On issue 2, Article 138 lets the employer organise rotating shifts, but Article 144-new defines night work as at least eleven consecutive hours including 22:00 to 05:00 and sets the rate at 130%; a regular rotation is night work, not overtime. [Para 276] The employer asserted that its single monthly sum already contained the premium but produced no payroll or calculation, so it failed to discharge the burden of proof and 130% was ordered. [Para 290]
On issue 3, no law, agreement, internal rule or practice created a 3,000 baht allowance, so the claim was one of interest, which only the most representative union may bring; the panel declined to consider it. [Para 294] It nevertheless found that the employer had replaced the canteen meals promised in chapter 10 of its internal rules with about 50 baht a day without following the amendment procedure under Article 30, so the rules continued to bind and meals had to be provided. [Para 329]
On issue 6 the panel again distinguished rest from standby: workers could leave the premises or use the rest rooms, were not checked, and were not required to return at once, unlike the twelve-hour standby held to be working time in Award 098/15. [Para 355] The eight-hour cap was respected and extra hours were paid as overtime, so the arrangement was lawful and reasonable and the claim failed. [Para 360]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union leader elected to office is a protected worker under Article 67 of the Law on Trade Unions for 45 days after the ballot, and a dismissal inside that window without the labour inspector's prior agreement under Article 43 is unlawful however serious the alleged misconduct; the panel will not examine the fault at all and will order reinstatement with wages and benefits from the date of dismissal. [Para 219]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel treated the union's copy of the election notice to the ministry as evidence supporting the worker's case rather than as a further condition of protection, so that notification to an employer who already knew the union leadership suffices. [Para 235]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer is named "Diamond City Bavet Limited" on the face of the award but "Grand Diamond City" in the findings, in the evidence list and in the workers' own annexes, and one delay letter addresses "Grand Diamond City and Poipet Resort" together. [Para 1] The letter recognising the four worker representatives is dated 29 December 2017 in the findings and 29 December 2016 elsewhere; only the 2016 date is possible in a case heard on 16 January 2017. [Para 109] The number of claimants is given as 200 in the employer's objection and as 111 in the panel's finding. [Para 189] The meals entitlement is printed three ways: three meals a day in the recruitment notice, and three for day shift against four for night shift in the internal rules, while the sum paid is described as 1,500 to 1,800 baht a month and as about 50 baht a day. [Para 177] The appendix of 111 thumbprints is unrecoverable OCR and is not relied on; the dismissed president and the other workers are referred to here by office and initial, and their names are redacted by policy. [Para 388]
