# Award No. 004/17 — Furs Gawont Apparel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 3 February 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Sin Kim Sean, worker-appointed; Kong Phalha, presiding) · Doc ID 138891

## 1. Facts
Furs Gawont Apparel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a garment-cutting and sewing enterprise employing about 460 workers, represented by the Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers' Democratic Union (សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា). [Para 76][Para 75] The union filed a collective dispute on 10 November 2016 covering twelve claims over working conditions; conciliation failed on all twelve and the case reached the Council on 04 January 2017. [Para 20][Para 22] At hearing the parties settled seven claims (2, 3, 5, 7, 8, 9 and 12) and the workers withdrew three (4, 10 and 11), leaving claims 1 and 6 for adjudication. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 27] The disputed acts were the employer's refusal to restore pre-registration start dates after workers regularised their identity documents with the National Social Security Fund, and its division of the monthly minimum wage by the actual working days of each calendar month. [Para 81][Para 90]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must implement Ministry of Labour and Vocational Training Notification No. 127 K.B/S.J.N of 02 May 2016 by keeping each worker's original date of entry into employment, and therefore seniority, where the worker had originally been hired under a borrowed identity document — the panel's issue 1? [Para 81][Para 114][Para 117]
2. Whether the employer's method of computing wages and benefits — dividing the monthly minimum wage by the actual number of working days in the calendar of each month (25, 26 or 27) instead of by 26 — was correct, and whether shortfalls for the last three years must be repaid — the panel's issue 6? [Para 90][Para 130][Para 150]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Notification No. 127 obliges the employer to preserve employment seniority during re-registration whatever identity documents were used before; the employer instead reset the first day of work to the date the genuine documents were produced, and one worker was refused hospital costs because the name on the NSSF card differed from the company card. [Para 86][Para 87][Para 88]
**Employer (respondent):** It could not comply with claims 1 and 6 because it was awaiting a decision from the company director, and it did not say when that decision would be taken; by letter of 20 January 2017 it asked the Council to reject points 01, 05, 06, 12 and filed no evidence on how wages had been computed. [Para 84][Para 89][Para 41][Para 101][Para 126]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — on issue 1 the employer was ordered to preserve seniority and the date of first entry into employment for workers who brought genuine personal documents for NSSF registration; on issue 6 it was ordered to repay the wages and other benefits actually lost through its incorrect computation for 2014, 2015 and 2016, per the number of months in the annexed worker lists, the parties to verify the figures together within three months of the award. [Para 127][Para 171][Para 172][Para 173] The award is non-binding (សេចក្តីបង្គាប់មិនជាប់កាតព្វកិច្ច). [Para 28][Para 174]

On representation, the panel applied Article 54(1) of the Law on Trade Unions of 17 May 2016, which reserves the right to bring and settle collective disputes, including before the Arbitration Council, to the most representative union, following Award No. 180/16 (Cement Cambodia Chakrey Ting) and Awards 171/16, 188/16, 220/16 and 234/16. [Para 103][Para 106][Para 107][Para 108] It found the union held a most-representative certificate, No. 302 K.B/V.B of 12 November 2015, valid to 12 November 2017, and that its leaders attended the hearing, so the claim was properly before it. [Para 110][Para 111][Para 112]

On issue 1 the panel read Notification No. 127 as acknowledging that workers had borrowed relatives' names and dates of birth, ordering that this not be treated as the workers' fault, and requiring that on re-registration they keep their rights, including employment seniority and their role in the professional organisation. [Para 117][Para 118][Para 119] It followed its reasoning in Award No. 209/16 (Hing Jing Tech Style Factory), that the employer must keep the original date of entry unchanged even where identity documents change. [Para 121][Para 125] Noting that the employer had no date for the director's decision, it held the employer had simply failed to comply with the notification. [Para 126][Para 127]

On issue 6 the panel found that neither the Labour Law nor related regulations expressly fix the daily-wage divisor. [Para 130] It nevertheless took point 4 of Notification No. 230 K.B/S.J.N of 25 July 2013, which uses 26 working days in a month as the reference even for new hires, and held 26 days to be the general standard for all workers, since applying one divisor to new and long-serving workers alike reflects equal treatment. [Para 131][Para 132][Para 133] It read point 4 of Prakas No. 10 S.K.B.Y. of 04 February 1999 on holiday pay, which computes holiday work by dividing total monthly wage by 26, as confirming that 26 denotes the working days of a month, and followed its earlier reasoning in Award No. 194/15 (Furs Gawont Apparel). [Para 134][Para 139][Para 140][Para 142] Applying Article 120 of the Labour Law, it held the three-year limitation did not bar the claim. [Para 154][Para 156] Applying Article 118 and prior Awards 361/14 (Ken Sport) and 097/15 (U.S. Gamen), it held the employer bears the burden of proving wages were paid, and that the employer's failure to file any computation evidence, and its silence on the workers' loss figures, meant that burden was not discharged. [Para 157][Para 158][Para 160][Para 163][Para 165] It therefore found the losses real, under Prakas No. 099 Article 34, and set out the per-day shortfalls claimed — USD 0.13 for workers on USD 90 a month, USD 0.14 on USD 100 or 104, USD 0.18 on USD 123 or 128, USD 0.198 on USD 135 and USD 0.20 on USD 140 — for 97 workers in 2014, 113 in 2015 and 120 in 2016. [Para 96][Para 98][Para 100][Para 150][Para 167]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer has for years hired workers who registered under borrowed identity documents, the Ministry's regularisation policy does not permit the employer to reset their service: the original date of entry, and the seniority attached to it, survives the production of genuine documents. [Para 125][Para 119] For wage computation, the daily wage is the monthly wage divided by 26 days, not by the variable number of working days in each calendar month, because the flat divisor is the only one that treats new and long-serving workers equally; and once a worker pleads a shortfall, the employer must produce the payroll evidence or the claim is taken as proved. [Para 133][Para 146][Para 165]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remark that the three-month reconciliation window exists so the employer can retrieve records held for the previous three years and both sides can check the calculation together is guidance on how the order is to be worked out, not a condition of liability. [Para 169][Para 170]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award date is printed as 03 February 2017 in the header and again at the date-of-issue paragraph, but the deadline paragraph sets 16 January 2016 for filing evidence, which cannot precede the 12 January 2017 hearing and is presumably 2017. [Para 1][Para 29][Para 30] The respondent's name is OCR'd in at least five forms across the document (ហ្វឺស, ហ្វឹស, ហ៊ឹស, ហ្វីលី, "First Gawon Apparel"), and the brief uses "Furs Gawont Apparel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd" as printed on the face of the award. [Para 1][Para 40][Para 94] Article 120 is quoted with an unexplained "បី ឆ្នាំ ២០១៥" inserted into the three-year period, and Prakas No. 099 is dated "21 April 2019 2004". [Para 154][Para 167] The holiday-pay worked example is garbled ("52$ +(2- x_1 ) = 54$"). [Para 137] The annexed worker lists are duplicated and heavily corrupted by OCR, with running numbers and identity numbers merged, so the counts of 97, 113 and 120 claimants cannot be reconciled line by line; the figure "USD 100" appears once without a stated basis. [Para 158][Para 176][Para 50]
