# Award No. 023/17 — Thien Sung Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 7 June 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; Sin Kim Sean, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, chair appointed by both) · Doc ID 138927

## 1. Facts
The company is a garment manufacturer with about 1,153 workers [Para 87]. Four workers — Ms T, Mr C, Ms Y and Ms K — had each been engaged on successive three-month fixed-duration contracts, their cumulative service running from 2013 or 2014 to 2017; at the end of every contract the employer paid 5 per cent of wages as the end-of-contract allowance [Para 101][Para 105][Para 109][Para 112][Para 163]. Between 2016 and 2017 each of the four signed a thumbprinted request to keep using a fixed-duration contract notwithstanding more than two years' service [Para 100][Para 181]. The employer gave each written notice of non-renewal between 7 and 13 days before their contracts expired, and terminated them [Para 103][Para 107][Para 114][Para 212]. Only one of the four original issues survived: the second and fourth were settled at the hearing and the third, alleging anti-union discrimination, was withdrawn [Para 17][Para 18][Para 19].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take the four workers back into work and pay them wages from the date of termination to the date of return? [Para 2][Para 162]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the four had served more than two years, so their contracts had become indefinite-duration under Article 67 of the Labour Law and their non-renewal was a dismissal; they had committed no fault and received no warning, and the employer's citation of the Labour Law against them was wrong in law [Para 115][Para 116][Para 118][Para 120][Para 166].
**Employer (respondent):** the fixed-duration contracts simply expired; the workers themselves asked in writing to continue on fixed-duration terms even after two years, notice was given before expiry, and the 5 per cent end-of-contract allowance was paid each time [Para 121][Para 122][Para 123][Para 167].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand for reinstatement and back wages was dismissed, but the panel ordered the full severance package for dismissal without valid reason on an indefinite-duration contract: notice pay under Articles 75 and 77, the Article 89 dismissal indemnity by actual seniority, payment for unused annual leave under Articles 166 and 167, moral damages under Article 91, and final wages under Article 116 [Para 252][Para 253][Para 254][Para 255][Para 256][Para 257].

On standing, there was no most-representative union at the factory, so under Article 54(1) of the Trade Union Law and Award 180/16 the workers could act themselves or through a mandated representative; the three union officers named in departmental letter No. 007/17 of 4 May 2017 appeared and were entitled to represent them, and the case was decided for the four workers directly concerned [Para 95][Para 155][Para 157][Para 160][Para 161].

On the contract type, the panel held that Article 67(2) and Article 73(5) of the Labour Law convert a fixed-duration contract into an indefinite-duration one once successive renewals bring total duration beyond two years, and that the reference to the Article 67 cap is to the cumulative maximum rather than to each renewal; it read ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982 as confirming that fixed-term contracts are not for long-term work [Para 169][Para 171][Para 172][Para 176][Para 177][Para 178][Para 180]. Facing the workers' signed requests to stay on fixed-term contracts, the panel applied Article 13 of the Labour Law, under which agreements giving less than the law allows are void; the requests therefore could not be given effect, and the panel declined to treat them as a waiver [Para 183][Para 184][Para 187][Para 188]. It followed that all four held indefinite-duration contracts and that their termination was a dismissal, not a non-renewal [Para 190][Para 191][Para 198][Para 199].

On the reason for dismissal, Article 74 requires a reason related to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the needs of the enterprise [Para 192][Para 194]. The employer's assertion that the four "did not work well" was not substantiated, and the workers had not raised union discrimination, so the panel found no valid reason and no basis for reinstatement as of right [Para 165][Para 200][Para 203]. Because the employer declined to take them back but agreed to pay severance computed on an indefinite-duration contract, the employment relationship was no longer viable, and the panel dismissed reinstatement and back pay while awarding the statutory entitlements [Para 201][Para 202][Para 204]. It fixed the notice period at one month under Article 75, the four having between two and five years' service, and computed the indemnities on the average of the last twelve months' wages [Para 210][Para 211][Para 216][Para 218].

Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented in the annex, considering that the four contracts remained fixed-duration because the workers had requested renewal on those terms and the 5 per cent allowance had been paid, so they were entitled only to the fixed-term settlement under Articles 73, 115, 116 and 167 and not to the Chapter 11 protections [Para 267][Para 271][Para 273][Para 274].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive fixed-duration contracts whose cumulative duration passes two years become an indefinite-duration contract by operation of Articles 67(2) and 73(5) of the Labour Law, and a worker's written request to remain on fixed-duration terms cannot prevent that conversion, because Article 13 voids any agreement that gives less than the law allows [Para 180][Para 184][Para 188][Para 190]. Termination of such a converted contract is a dismissal requiring a reason related to aptitude, conduct or the needs of the enterprise under Article 74, and where the employer will not reinstate, the panel awards the indefinite-duration severance package instead [Para 194][Para 200][Para 204].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's reading of ILO Recommendation No. 166 as context for Article 67 was expressly non-binding, the recommendation having no compulsory force [Para 179]. Its remarks that the employer's unparticularised claim of poor work was unsubstantiated were not necessary to the finding, which rested on the absence of any Article 74 reason [Para 165][Para 200].
