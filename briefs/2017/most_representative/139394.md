# Award No. 242/16 — Cam Bo Uni Sole Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 4 January 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Tang Try (chair) · Doc ID 139394

## 1. Facts
Cam Bo Uni Sole Co., Ltd. is a garment manufacturer employing about 1,873 workers. [Para 35] It has no most representative union. [Para 53] The Ministry's labour disputes department certified 15 machine technicians as worker representatives by letter 022/16 of 8 December 2016; one, Mr S-H, did not appear, so the claim covers the 14 who attended. [Para 36][Para 37] A machine technician maintains and sets the machines for two sewing teams, and receives a USD 2 target bonus for each team that meets its weekly target, while each sewing worker in a team that meets target receives USD 3. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 65]

## 2. Issues in dispute
Seven points were referred; the workers withdrew points 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 7 at the hearing, leaving one. [Para 20][Para 21]
1. Whether the employer must pay the 14 machine technicians a target bonus of USD 3 per sewing team for which they are responsible, equal to that of the sewing workers? [Para 38][Para 64]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the technicians carry heavy responsibility and fatigue, servicing two teams each, and the sewing workers only reach their bonus because of the technicians' cooperation; on a model change the technician alone strips and resets the machines and answers for sewing quality. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46]
**Employer (respondent):** the company faces economic difficulty and cannot raise the target bonus; it also gives benefits above the statutory minimum. [Para 47][Para 48]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed on jurisdiction — the panel declined to consider the claim for want of a most representative union, so no sum was ordered. [Para 85][Para 87]

The panel first settled who could bring the case. Under Article 54 of the Trade Union Law of 17 May 2016 the most representative union has the exclusive right to conclude collective agreements and to settle collective disputes, and the panel found no such union at the company. [Para 49][Para 53] Article 1 of Prakas No. 362 K.B/Pr.K of 29 August 2016 lets the labour disputes department and provincial labour departments certify worker representatives, and workers so certified may represent the worker party when no most representative union exists. [Para 54][Para 57] The 14 technicians therefore had standing to bring the dispute. [Para 62]

On the merits the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, contract, agreement or past practice requiring a target bonus of USD 3 per team for machine technicians. [Para 66] The claim is thus for a benefit above the legal floor, which makes it an interest dispute. [Para 67] Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 provides that an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement and applies to every worker in the company, and removes their right to strike over that interest. [Para 78][Para 79] An interest dispute can therefore be decided only where the claimant is the most representative union, or several local unions together holding that character under Articles 54 and 72 of the Trade Union Law. [Para 80][Para 84] Since the claimants were neither, the panel did not consider the claim. [Para 85]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an enterprise has no most representative union, workers certified as representatives under Prakas No. 362 may bring a collective labour dispute, but an interest claim — a benefit above the legal minimum — cannot be entertained from them, because an interest award operates as a collective agreement binding every worker in the enterprise. [Para 62][Para 80][Para 84]

**Obiter dicta:** that a union attains most representative status at 30 per cent membership or support in the enterprise, or by a ballot where that threshold is not met [Para 73]; and that the existing scheme already pays technicians USD 2 per team and double where both teams meet target, a point recited but not weighed because the claim was not reached on the merits [Para 65].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is dated 4 January 2017 but cites its own reconciliation report as dated 17 January 2014 in one passage and 12 December 2016 elsewhere [Para 16]. The complaint is described as received on 8 December 2016 and simultaneously as "complaint 2015" [Para 14]. The certification letter names 15 workers while the award treats the claimant group as 14, and one name appears in two spellings in the party list [Para 36][Para 59]. The panel is said to have been appointed under Prakas No. 222 of 14 June 2016, mandate 14, while other awards of the same period cite mandate 13 [Para 9]. Individual workers appear under initials by convention; the source prints their full names.
