# Award No. 015/17 — Eminen Garment (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 4 April 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy appointed by the employer, An Nan appointed by the workers, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 138908

## 1. Facts
Eminen Garment (Cambodia) Ltd, a garment factory in Saang district, Kandal province employing about 652 workers, did not renew the fixed-duration contract of Ms V, a sewing worker engaged on 4 May 2016 and re-engaged on successive three-month contracts, the last running from 1 November 2016 to 31 January 2017, with one week's notice. [Para 1][Para 56][Para 62][Para 64][Para 65] On 11 December 2016 she was elected deputy president of the Khmer Workers' Heart Union local; the non-renewal took effect on 1 February 2017 and she had not collected her last wages, including a USD 146.46 contract-end payment. [Para 66][Para 82] Four demands were filed; two were settled at the hearing and one was withdrawn, leaving only the claim that she be reinstated with back wages. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take Ms V back to work as normal, preserve her benefits and pay her wages from the date of non-renewal until her return? [Para 3][Para 61][Para 126]
2. Whether the employer must cease discriminating against her as deputy president of the union and apply the 1997 Labour Law and the Trade Union Law? (withdrawn by the workers) [Para 5][Para 24]
3. Whether the employer must recognise the Khmer Workers' Heart Union? (settled) [Para 7][Para 23]
4. Whether the employer must stop terminating the workers who voted to establish the union? (settled) [Para 9][Para 23]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the contract was not renewed because Ms V was active in the union, and as an elected deputy president she enjoys protection from dismissal under Article 67 of the Trade Union Law; the employer never issued her any written warning, and a company interpreter told her that if she left the union she would be promoted from sewer to team leader. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 70][Para 73][Para 74]
**Employer (respondent):** it had no buyers' orders and had to cut its workforce, so Ms V' contract simply expired; the decision rested on her declining evaluation score and had nothing to do with union discrimination, of which it was unaware because it never received the election notice. [Para 77][Para 78][Para 79][Para 92][Para 154]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to take Ms V back to work and to pay her wages from the date of non-renewal until the date of her return; no sum was quantified in the order. [Para 189][Para 190][Para 191]

On standing, the panel held that the provincial Department of Labour's letter of 1 March 2017 certifying Ms O and Ms V as worker representatives gave them authority to represent the claimants under Article 1 of Prakas No. 362 K.B/Pr.K of 29 August 2015, including before the Council, where no most-representative union yet exists. [Para 116][Para 121][Para 123]

On the first branch of issue 1, the panel set out Article 67 of the Trade Union Law of 17 May 2016 (protection of election candidates and union leaders as staff delegates from the date of the registration application) and Article 43 (no dismissal of a staff delegate or candidate without the labour inspector's prior consent), and accepted that the employer would have needed that consent. [Para 127][Para 128][Para 132] But it followed its earlier awards (Nos. 169/16, 181/16, 229/16, 007/17, 34/05, 53/06, 094/16) holding that this special protection cannot apply to the expiry of a fixed-duration contract, and both parties accepted the case was a non-renewal, not a dismissal — so Article 67 protection was unavailable. [Para 133][Para 135][Para 136][Para 137]

On the second branch, the panel applied Articles 12 and 279 of the 1997 Labour Law, which forbid an employer to use union membership or activity as a ground for hiring, discipline or termination decisions, and Article 62 of the Trade Union Law, which extends "termination of an employment contract" to cover dismissal or non-renewal of successive fixed-duration contracts made contrary to procedure. [Para 138][Para 140][Para 143][Para 148] Weighing the evidence, it found the employer's security guards had refused the 16 December 2016 notice of the election result at the administration's direction, and that the same notice sent by Cambodia Post was refused and returned on 23 December 2016; the workers' postal receipt corroborated a 16 December 2016 dispatch. [Para 87][Para 97][Para 157][Para 173] The employer's competing receipt and a 15 February 2017 confirmation letter from postal officer Mr S were rejected as inconsistent — the receipt was for the Federation of Trade Unions, not this local union — so the panel presumed the employer knew Ms V had been elected deputy president. [Para 167][Para 169][Para 172][Para 174] It also noted the employer admitted non-renewing about 20 workers at the same time but never stated how many of its 652 workers it needed to cut, that the 21 January 2017 evaluation letter scoring her 2.44 was unsigned, that the employer did not contest her explanation that her output fell because she was made to substitute for other sewers, and that the union secretary Ms P was likewise not renewed after about one month. [Para 105][Para 175][Para 176][Para 180][Para 182][Para 183] The employer therefore failed to show the non-renewal was unconnected to union discrimination, and the order to reinstate with back wages followed. [Para 184][Para 185][Para 189]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer does not renew the fixed-duration contract of an elected union leader, the special protection from dismissal in Articles 43 and 67 of the Trade Union Law does not apply, because that protection does not survive the expiry of the contract; but Article 62 of the Trade Union Law, read with Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law, makes such a non-renewal unlawful where union activity was a ground for it, and once the worker shows the employer knew of the election and the employer's own explanation is unsupported by credible evidence, the burden of showing the non-renewal was unconnected to union discrimination falls on the employer. [Para 133][Para 137][Para 143][Para 148][Para 184]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that the employer never specified how many of its 652 workers it needed to reduce, and that the dismissal of the union secretary and the simultaneous non-renewal of about 20 workers were consistent with a pattern, are supporting context rather than independent grounds; the remarks on the standing of certified worker representatives in the absence of a most-representative union were also decided in the alternative, since only one claimant remained. [Para 121][Para 175][Para 176][Para 183]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award date is printed as 04 April 2017 in the header [Para 1] but the closing line reads "Phnom Penh, 04 December" with no year [Para 206]. Both are reported; the header date is used above.
- The employer's name appears in several OCR forms — "Eminen", "Emenen", "Eminen Garment", "Aninen" [Para 1][Para 13][Para 55]; the form on the face of the award (015/17) is used.
- A block of the reasons at [Para 185] is replaced by untranslated, corrupted Latin text ("sublawy compa ianing on abomen botte inquaring pudes"), so the sentence identifying which article the non-renewal breached is partly lost; the surrounding citations to Articles 12, 279 and 62 were used instead.
- The award is expressly non-binding [Para 26][Para 192], and the employer-appointed arbitrator Mr I Sothy filed a dissenting annex: he accepted reinstatement but dissented from the back-wage order, citing Articles 73(3) and 73(6) of the Labour Law and the absence of evidence of damage [Para 195][Para 197][Para 198][Para 204][Para 205]. That reasoning is his, not the panel's.
- Internal dates conflict on when the election notice reached the employer: postal records are said to show 16 December 2016 [Para 97][Para 173], 10 February 2017 [Para 94][Para 163] and 15/16 February 2017 [Para 162][Para 168]; one passage dates the postal return "23 December 2019" [Para 160] where the reasons elsewhere say 2016 [Para 87][Para 98].
- Attorney and representative names are inconsistently transliterated (Mr A Peng / "អាល់ប៉ឹត ផេង" [Para 2][Para 29]); the arbitrator's name is printed both "អ៊ីង សុទ្ធី" and "អ៉ីង សុទ្ធី" [Para 1][Para 193].
