# Award No. 003/17 — Hoeng Jin Textile Factory (Arbitration Council, 7 February 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Seng Vouch Hun (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (president) · Doc ID 138890

## 1. Facts
Hoeng Jin Textile Factory, a garment cutter and sewer in Phnom Penh employing about 90 workers, faced a collective dispute filed on 17 November 2016 by the Cambodian Apparel Workers Democratic Union Federation (សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា). Conciliation on 28 December 2016 settled one of seven points and the hearing settled two more by agreement (issues 1 and 3), leaving four: deductions from union leaders attending a federation meeting, deductions from two workers attending a Council hearing, maternity pay timing and base, and deductions during certified sick leave. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 21][Para 26][Para 27]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Panel's issue 2) Whether the employer must reimburse the 31 USD deducted from each of three union leaders — 15 USD of attendance bonus plus three days' wages — for the three days of authorised personal leave they took to attend a federation meeting in Preah Sihanouk on 22–24 September 2016? [Para 4][Para 70]
2. (Panel's issue 4) Whether the employer must reimburse the 15 USD deducted from each of two workers who attended the Arbitration Council hearing of 5 September 2016 — 5 USD bonus, 5 USD wages and a 5 USD penalty? [Para 8][Para 81][Para 194]
3. (Panel's issue 5) Whether the employer must pay 50% of wages and allowances for the full 90 days of maternity leave, at least one day before the leave begins, calculated on the average of total wages over the previous 12 months? [Para 100][Para 224]
4. (Panel's issue 6) Whether the employer must restore wages and the attendance bonus deducted from workers who took sick leave supported by a medical certificate? [Para 108][Para 247]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the three leaders applied for leave correctly and were granted it, yet lost the whole bonus after more than 20 days' attendance, which is unreasonable; attendance bonus is a form of wage under Article 103 and must be deducted proportionally, like wages; those who attended the hearing told their line chief, who said he would inform administration; maternity pay should come as a lump sum before leave because most workers live far away. [Para 74][Para 75][Para 76][Para 94][Para 105]
**Employer (respondent):** proportional deduction is not permitted, because the leave was not for attending a meeting or invitation of the Ministry or the Council and past practice was to deduct the whole bonus for any leave; the two workers did not apply in writing and were not named in the invitation, while the three leaders did notify administration; it cannot pay maternity money in one lump sum for lack of means, and the sick-leave certificates were irregular or purchased. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 92][Para 107][Para 115]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must reimburse the remainder of the attendance bonus after a proportional deduction for the three days' authorised leave; repay 15 USD to each of the two workers who attended the hearing; pay 50% of wages and allowances for 90 days at least one day before maternity leave, on the average of total wages over the last 12 months; and restore wages and bonus for the two workers whose sick leave was certified. The claim for the third worker in issue 6 was not considered. [Para 193][Para 223][Para 246][Para 263][Para 265][Para 266][Para 267]

On representation, under Article 54(1) of the Union Law and Article 11 only a most representative union may bring a collective dispute; none existed here, and the company union's registration certificate and letter recognising three leaders, two of whom attended, sufficed. [Para 124][Para 128][Para 143]

On issue 2, the panel read Notification No. 230 K.B/S.J.N of 25 July 2012, point 2 — at least 10 USD a month for workers attending every working day "without absence" — as a legal norm binding all textile, garment and footwear employers, not a discretionary bonus. [Para 151][Para 155] The authorities have never defined "without absence"; the panel declined to assume the phrase covers authorised and unauthorised absence alike regardless of lawfulness, and decided case by case. [Para 158][Para 159][Para 163] Absence arising from annual leave, special leave, maternity leave, public holidays and weekly rest is provided for by law and permits no deduction — the worker keeps 100% of the 10 USD. [Para 160][Para 161] For absence outside the statute but authorised by the employer, Articles 71(6) and 72(1) suspend the contract and relieve the employer of paying wages for those days, so no bonus is owed for them either. [Para 183][Para 184][Para 187] The employer may not deduct the whole bonus but only an amount proportional to the days of leave. [Para 188] Its existing 15 USD a month is more favourable than Notification 230 and stands, but deducting 5 USD for a single day's leave was not appropriate. [Para 189][Para 190]

On issue 4, Prakas 099 Articles 20 and 14 require the parties to attend hearings and require the employer to let invited workers attend during working hours without any negative effect on their rights. [Para 196][Para 198][Para 199] Three union leaders who also attended without written applications were not deducted, so the asserted procedure was applied inconsistently, and failing to publicise any procedure was the employer's oversight, not the workers' fault. [Para 216][Para 217][Para 219] Repayment of the 15 USD withheld from each was ordered. [Para 222][Para 223]

On issue 5, Articles 182 and 183 give 90 days' maternity leave at half pay including allowances but do not fix the payment date; Article 115(3) requires wages not to be paid on a rest day and, if paid on a leave day, to be paid one day earlier, and Article 103 treats maternity-period pay as wages. [Para 224][Para 225][Para 227][Para 230] The employer's two-stage practice therefore breached Article 115, and payment must be made at least one day before leave at 50% for the full 90 days on the 12-month average. [Para 238][Para 239][Para 245][Para 246]

On issue 6, the parties agreed the company's practice is to keep wages and bonus for sick leave certified by a state or private hospital. [Para 252] The employer alleged the certificates were purchased but produced no document, witness or authority report; the certificates bore proper stamps and signatures, so the allegation remained a bare assertion. [Para 259][Para 260][Para 261] One worker had thumbprinted a letter disowning the union's claim and the workers never contested it, so the panel decided only for the other two. [Para 248][Para 249][Para 250][Para 251]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The attendance bonus under Notification 230 is a legal minimum, not a discretionary reward, and absence the law provides for — annual, special, maternity, public holiday and weekly rest — may not reduce it at all; for other absence the employer authorises, the contract is suspended so no bonus is owed for those days, but deduction must be proportional to the days taken and never the whole monthly bonus. An employer may not withhold wages, bonus or impose a penalty on a worker attending a Council hearing on invitation, and maternity pay must be paid in full at least one day before the leave starts. [Para 161][Para 187][Para 188][Para 199][Para 239]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel could not obtain a clear interpretation of "without absence" from any competent institution and therefore declined to interpret it generally, reserving each case to its facts. [Para 158][Para 163]
