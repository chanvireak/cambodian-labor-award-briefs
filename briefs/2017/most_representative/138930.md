# Award No. 024/17 — Cambo Uni Sole Ltd (Arbitration Council, 14 July 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Kong Palha, president) · Doc ID 138930

## 1. Facts
Cambo Uni Sole Ltd (ខេមបូ យូនី សូល អិលធីឌី) is a garment-cutting and sewing factory in Kandal province employing 1,840 workers, of whom about 90% are women [Para 93][Para 153]. The Khmer Workers' Heart Union, registered on 29 December 2015, brought 15 demands after a strike in March 2017 [Para 36][Para 72][Para 74]. Seven demands were settled in conciliation and demand 12 was withdrawn, leaving six for the panel: the reinstatement of three suspended union leaders, canteen and food-storage facilities, a childcare payment, the overtime meal allowance and motorcycle parking [Para 42][Para 43][Para 45]. The three leaders — the union's president, vice-president and secretary — were suspended on 24 March 2017 for serious misconduct, dismissed on 6 May 2017 after the labour inspector approved, and their appeal to the Minister was lodged on 24 May 2017 [Para 107][Para 108][Para 236].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the three dismissed union leaders and pay their wages and bonuses from dismissal until reinstatement? [Para 217]
2. Whether the employer must extend the canteen and provide extra tables and chairs so that every worker can sit to eat? [Para 241]
3. Whether the employer must build food-storage cabinets, with locks if possible, for workers' food brought from home? [Para 269]
4. Whether the employer must raise the childcare service payment to USD 50 per month? [Para 277]
10. Whether the employer must pay the overtime meal allowance once a week rather than once a fortnight? [Para 284]
14. Whether the employer must extend the motorcycle shed? [Para 300]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the three leaders were dismissed for their union activity and must be reinstated with back wages [Para 99]; the canteen's 150 tables seat only 1,800 of 1,840 workers, so people eat pressed together and rain blows onto the tables, while the site has room to build more [Para 131][Para 132]; the food racks are too small, food is hung on fences and lost, and wind and rain spoil it [Para 140][Para 142][Para 143]; USD 6 a month is far too little to hire childcare [Para 154]; one weekly meal-allowance payment is needed because workers have no money to buy food when they work overtime [Para 180][Para 296]; the motorcycle shed is cramped [Para 193].
**Employer (respondent):** the three were dismissed for serious misconduct, with the inspector's approval and after two applications, and the company is trading normally with no wish to dismiss anyone [Para 100][Para 102][Para 107]; tables are never fully used because many workers eat at home or outside, and the head office will not fund a new canteen [Para 135][Para 136]; existing racks suffice and workers ignore instructions [Para 144][Para 148]; it already pays USD 6 in lieu of a nursery [Para 155]; paying meal allowance weekly is impractical alongside weekly bonuses, fortnightly meal money and monthly wages [Para 181][Para 297]; the parking area is adequate but used untidily [Para 195][Para 196].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 10 was granted and the employer ordered to pay the overtime meal allowance once a week to workers who work overtime; issues 1, 2, 3, 4 and 14 were refused and no other sum was ordered; the award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent written objection to the Minister [Para 308][Para 309][Para 310][Para 311][Para 312][Para 313][Para 314].

Issue 1 was not decided on its merits. Under Article 43 of the Trade Union Law 2016, dismissal of a workers' delegate or union leader requires the labour inspector's prior approval, with a two-month window to appeal an approval to the Minister [Para 218][Para 219][Para 225]. The workers appealed on 24 May 2017 and that two-month period had not run out at the hearing, so the matter was still within the Minister's procedure and the panel declined to consider it, leaving it to the Minister's decision [Para 237][Para 239][Para 240].

Issues 2, 3 and 4 were classified as interest disputes (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍) because no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, contract, agreement or past practice imposed the duty claimed [Para 246][Para 247][Para 272][Para 273][Para 277]. The panel then applied its standing rule: under Article 54 and Article 72 of the Trade Union Law 2016, and Article 43 of Prakas 099, an interest award displaces the collective agreement for a year, so only a most-representative union may bring such a claim [Para 252][Para 260][Para 263]. Neither party asserted that a most-representative union existed at the factory, and the claimant was not one [Para 204][Para 266]. The recognised worker representatives were nonetheless competent to conduct the case under Article 1 of Prakas 362, so the panel heard them, but it refused to consider the three interest claims [Para 214][Para 215][Para 267][Para 274][Para 280].

Issue 10 succeeded. Point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 (7 March 2011) requires 2,000 riel a day, or a free meal, for workers who volunteer for overtime at the employer's request [Para 284]. The panel held the employer obliged to provide the free meal regardless, and that paying 2,000 riel in lieu is permitted, but the notification fixes no payment interval [Para 285][Para 288][Para 289]. Because the meal must be supplied on each day of overtime, paying the allowance daily is the reasonable reading, and the panel ordered weekly payment [Para 290][Para 298][Para 299]. Issue 14 failed on the same interest-dispute reasoning as issues 2 to 4 [Para 303][Para 307].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the two-month period for appealing a labour inspector's approval of a union leader's dismissal to the Minister has not yet expired, the Council will not examine the dismissal, because the statutory procedure is still live and the Minister's decision must come first [Para 237][Para 239]. An interest claim that rests on no statute, agreement, internal rule or past practice may be brought only by a most-representative union; recognised worker representatives can conduct the hearing but cannot supply that standing [Para 263][Para 266]. The overtime meal entitlement in Notification No. 041/11 accrues on each day of overtime, so payment at fortnightly intervals does not satisfy it and the panel will order weekly payment [Para 290][Para 299].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that women make up about 90% of the workforce, that workers eat at home or outside so the tables are under-used, and that food is hung on fences — contextual findings that did not decide any issue [Para 135][Para 141][Para 153].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The signature block names the worker-appointed arbitrator as អន លាន while the face of the award names him អន ណន, and the president appears as គង់ ផល្ហៈ on the face and គង់ ផល្លៈ at the signature [Para 1][Para 315]. The issue numbering runs 1–4, 10 and 14 with 5–9, 11–13 disposed of in conciliation or withdrawn, which makes the reasons hard to follow without the recitals [Para 42][Para 45]. The employer's name is spelled consistently in Khmer only; no Latin form appears. A stray "Johnshing" and a corrupted "ឱ្យND" appear in the dispositive reasoning, and several amounts are double-printed ("៥០០ ដុល្លារ" beside "៥០ ដុល្លារ") in the framing of issue 4, though the order itself uses USD 50 [Para 277][Para 307][Para 311]. Dismissal dates are printed as 24 March for the suspension letters, 5 May for the inspector's approval and 6 May for the dismissals; these are reconcilable but not stated in one place [Para 107][Para 108][Para 236].
