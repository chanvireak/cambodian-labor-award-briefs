# Award No. 011/17 — Bes Profit Wing (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 21 March 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom, employer-appointed; Tuon Si Phan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chha, president) · Doc ID 138902

## 1. Facts
Bes Profit Wing (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. weaves elastic band and employs about 60 workers, where the Cambodia Asia Union has held a registration certificate since 17 July 2015 [Para 36][Para 37][Para 39]. The union filed nine demands on working conditions — food storage, a clinic room, treatment of a pregnant worker, transfers, sick leave, overtime, posting of internal rules, hot water and toilet hygiene [Para 3][Para 11]. Conciliation failed on all nine because the employer did not appear, and it did not appear at the hearing either, though it first told the Secretariat it would come and then said the director had not delegated authority [Para 18][Para 26][Para 99].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must provide a hygienic cabinet or other means for workers to store their food? [Para 120]
2. Whether the employer must provide a properly equipped clinic room and the medicines required by Prakas No. 330? [Para 147]
3. Whether the employer discriminated against Ms N, a pregnant worker, by refusing her transfer from the rubber-yarn carding section to packing? [Para 180]
4. Whether the employer must stop threatening workers who are moved between buildings with dismissal or 50% wages? [Para 202]
5. Whether the employer must allow workers to request leave by telephone or through another worker when they are ill or have an emergency? [Para 206]
6. Whether the employer must organise overtime in the new building on a voluntary basis? [Para 231]
7. Whether the employer must post the internal regulations on the notice board? [Para 245]
8. Whether the employer must repair the broken hot-water dispenser? [Para 255]
9. Whether the employer must assign a regular toilet cleaner and supply sufficient soap? [Para 280]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** About 30% of workers bring food from home but may not take it into the production building, so it is left on motorbikes or tied to trees and eaten by the employer's cows, pigs and dogs; the clinic is an unhygienic converted guard room with no medicine or curtain; the pregnant worker was told to resign when she asked for lighter work; and overtime, leave and toilet facilities fall short of the Prakas [Para 44][Para 46][Para 56][Para 61][Para 90].
**Employer (respondent):** Absent, and therefore treated as having waived its right of reply; earlier it had only told the Secretariat it would attend [Para 98][Para 124][Para 176].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issues 2, 5 (in part), 6, 7, 8 and 9 were granted and orders made to comply with Prakas No. 330, to accept leave requests by telephone or through another worker for illness, to make overtime voluntary, to post the internal regulations, to repair the hot water dispenser and to clean the toilets with soap provided; issue 1 was granted in substance by ordering a food cabinet or an equivalent measure; issues 3 and 4 were dismissed [Para 294][Para 295][Para 297][Para 298][Para 299][Para 301][Para 304].

On the employer's absence the panel applied Article 21 of Prakas No. 099, holding that a party duly invited who fails to appear without good reason cannot block the proceedings, and that it had been invited by email on 3 March 2017 with full particulars of time, place and contact details [Para 94][Para 97][Para 100]. On who could claim, it found no union certified as most representative, held that the union's registered leadership could represent its own members under Article 11 of the Law on Unions, and treated the claimants as the union's members at the factory, without accepting the asserted figure of 54 members, which was unproven [Para 107][Para 112][Para 118][Para 119].

On food storage the panel held the ban on taking food into the production building lawful and reasonable as protecting the workplace atmosphere from smells, but held that ordering food to be left outside, while the employer let cows, pigs, dogs, pigeons and ducks roam and eat it, offended Article 229 of the Labour Law and lacked reasonableness; a cabinet or equivalent measure was ordered [Para 138][Para 141][Para 143][Para 146]. On the clinic it applied Articles 238 and 242 and Prakas No. 330: any enterprise with at least 50 workers must maintain a permanent infirmary of at least 20 square metres with the listed furniture, equipment and medicines; the converted guard room was about 2m by 3.5m, had no medicine or curtain, and fell short [Para 149][Para 164][Para 169][Para 170][Para 179].

On the pregnant worker the panel applied Article 46(2) of the Constitution and Prakas No. 307, but placed the burden of proof on the party alleging discrimination: the worker had always worked in the carding section, no document or witness showed the refusal was because of pregnancy, and transfer is the employer's prerogative; the claim failed [Para 182][Para 187][Para 194][Para 197][Para 201]. The threat claim failed for the same evidential reason [Para 203][Para 205]. On leave, the panel accepted that notice is needed to protect the production line but held that insisting a sick worker come in person so the employer can see the illness is not reasonable, and ordered telephone or third-party requests to be accepted for illness, while rejecting the emergency limb for want of particulars [Para 217][Para 218][Para 222][Para 229].

Overtime was ordered to be voluntary under Article 4 of Prakas No. 80, since denying future overtime to those who decline is a form of pressure [Para 231][Para 239][Para 244]. Posting the internal regulations was ordered under Article 29 [Para 245][Para 252][Para 254]. The hot water dispenser was ordered repaired because supplying hot water had become a past practice recognised by both sides and no agreement had replaced it [Para 263][Para 273][Para 279]. Toilet cleaning and soap were ordered under Articles 1 and 6 of Prakas No. 052 [Para 280][Para 284][Para 293].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer's managerial order is lawful only if it is both legal and reasonable: barring food from the production floor is reasonable, but leaving it where the employer's own livestock destroy it is not, and the employer bears the consequences of animals it keeps [Para 138][Para 143]. A benefit actually and consistently provided and accepted by both sides becomes a past practice the employer must continue until it is lawfully ended, and a party alleging discrimination carries the burden of proving it [Para 187][Para 273][Para 279].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that overtime is offered at the employer's initiative and accepted or refused by the worker, and that a refusal may never attract discipline [Para 234][Para 236].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company name is printed as Bes Profit Wing, Sen Brohvt Vong, Bes Prohfit and Bes Profit across Paras 1, 32 and 36. The hearing is dated 13 March 2017 at Paras 16 and 28 but 13 March 2016 at Para 26. Prakas No. 330 is dated 06 December 2000 in the text but 16 December 2000 where reproduced at Para 307. The worker at issue appears as Ms Na Chan Na with several OCR variants (Paras 59, 180, 192). The union's claimed membership of 54 is recorded as unproven at Para 117, and the claimant group is left undefined beyond "members" [Para 119]. Paragraphs 306–319 reproduce Prakas No. 330 and its equipment annex as OCR noise with French terms garbled.
