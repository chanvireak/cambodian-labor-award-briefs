# Award No. 029/17 — AM3 Cambodia Garment Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 10 October 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ouk Ri (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chea (president) · Doc ID 138933

## 1. Facts
AM3 Cambodia Garment Co., Ltd. is a registered garment-cutting company in Phnom Penh employing close to 300 workers, more than 100 of them women, and recognising two unions with no single most-representative union. [Para 1][Para 52][Para 53][Para 57][Para 83] The Khmer Youth Union brought a 12-point collective demand to improve working conditions; conciliation settled 7 points, 5 remained unresolved, and issue 5 (occasional special leave) settled at the hearing, leaving four issues for the panel. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 22][Para 23] The company's standing practice was to issue fixed-duration contracts of 2 months and then an indefinite contract. [Para 3][Para 58]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to conclude fixed-duration labour contracts of a minimum of 6 months with its workers? [Para 109]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to pay wages and other benefits to worker Ms S for the 22 days of sick leave from 17 May 2017 to 09 June 2017 covered by a valid medical certificate? [Para 139]
3. Whether the employer is obliged to pay a meal allowance of 4,000 riel per day for overtime worked from 04:00 to 06:00 in the evening? [Para 155]
4. Whether the employer is obliged to provide a nursery (ទារកដ្ឋាន) and a breastfeeding room, or a monthly milk allowance, for female workers with small children? [Para 183]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** 2-month contracts give no job security, contravene the law, and cut workers off from seniority bonuses, maternity leave and annual leave; the internal regulations entitle a worker on certified sick leave to full wages, and the 15 further days at home followed the doctor's advice; 3,000 riel does not buy enough food and neighbouring factories pay 4,000 riel; and Article 186 requires a nursery. [Para 60][Para 62][Para 70][Para 72][Para 79][Para 82]
**Employer (respondent):** the company is small and takes subcontracted sewing work, so it cannot offer long contracts and long contracts make workers harder to manage; it cannot pay wages for the days spent at home; 3,000 riel already exceeds the legal minimum and is better than the law requires; and the company is small with only 2–3 women having small children, so it cannot build a nursery. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 71][Para 81][Para 86][Para 87]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 1 dismissed; issue 2 granted, ordering payment of wages and other benefits for 22 days from 17 May 2017 to 09 June 2017; issue 3 dismissed without consideration; issue 4 granted, ordering the employer to arrange a nursery and breastfeeding room. [Para 191][Para 192][Para 193][Para 194]
On issue 1 the panel held a labour contract arises from offer and acceptance (មាត្រា 65 Labour Law; មាត្រា 311, 336, 337 Civil Code) and that neither party can impose a form or term the other does not accept; the workers' demand was lawful but so was the employer's refusal, so the claim was dismissed, following earlier awards 56/06, 96/13, 204/13 and 219/16. [Para 113][Para 124][Para 129][Para 133][Para 135][Para 138]
On issue 2 the panel applied clause 4 of the company's internal regulations, approved on 03 March 2016, which grants full wages for the first month of certified illness; it found the regulations nowhere require the sick leave to be spent in hospital, that the 15 days at home followed medical advice, and that the employer itself accepted the medical certificate as valid. [Para 139][Para 147][Para 148][Para 146] Compensation was ordered for all 22 days, not only the 7 days in hospital. [Para 144][Para 152]
On issue 3 the panel found the 4,000 riel demand exceeded the 2,000 riel set by Ministry Notification No. 041/11 and that the employer already paid 3,000 riel, above the legal floor. [Para 156][Para 157] It characterised the claim as an interest dispute and held that the claimant was neither the most-representative union nor an organisation with standing to bring an interest dispute, so it declined to consider the claim. [Para 160][Para 179][Para 181][Para 182]
On issue 4 the panel applied មាត្រា 186 of the Labour Law, which requires any enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a breastfeeding room and a nursery, or to pay the cost of an outside nursery where it cannot provide one; it found more than 100 women employed and no nursery in place. [Para 183][Para 188][Para 189]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand to replace short fixed-duration contracts with longer ones is a proposal to contract, not an entitlement: because a labour contract rests on matching offer and acceptance, a panel will not compel an employer to accept contract terms it has refused, however lawful the workers' proposal. [Para 124][Para 137] Where more than 100 women are employed, the duty under មាត្រា 186 is satisfied either by providing a nursery and breastfeeding room or by meeting the invoiced cost of an outside nursery — the employer's small size and lack of government enforcement do not displace it. [Para 183][Para 188] A claim for benefits above the legal minimum is an interest dispute that only a most-representative union may bring. [Para 176][Para 181]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel recorded the Minister's remark that only about 28 per cent of Cambodian companies have arranged a lawful nursery, and the employer's view that the government does not yet compel companies to build one — observations that did not alter the order. [Para 88][Para 89] The panel also noted, without deciding anything on it, that the two workers present were validly recognised as worker representatives under Prakas No. 362 for the conduct of the proceedings. [Para 107]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Issue 2 is dated two ways in the reasons: "ថ្ងៃទី ១១ ទី១៧ ខែឧសភា" (11 and 17 May) at [Para 139], against 17 May 2017 everywhere else, including the order at [Para 192]. The Ministry letter at [Para 43] describes the union as the professional organisation of 4,700 workers, while the employer states the workforce is close to 300 [Para 53] — the figure is unreconciled on the face of the award. At [Para 185] the Labour Law is printed as "ឆ្នាំ ២០២១ ១៩៩៧" (2021 and 1997). The enacting Prakas is garbled at [Para 12] ("លេខ ១៦ ២០១៩៩ សកបយ"). A further tension is undisclosed by the panel itself: issue 3 was refused as an interest dispute for want of a most-representative union, while issues 1 and 4 — equally claims for benefits beyond existing entitlement — were decided on the merits. Individual names of the worker and of the employer's representatives are reduced to bare initials in the OCR and are redacted here.
