# Award No. 017/17 — Dream Toy International Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 20 April 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (គល់ វឌ្ឍនា, អន ណន, គង់ ផល្ហៈ) · Doc ID 138918

## 1. Facts
Dream Toy International Co., Ltd., a toy manufacturer in Kandal province employing about 260 workers, terminated Ms L on 09 January 2017 without notice, although her last fixed-duration contract was due to expire on 07 April 2017; she had worked on successive six-month contracts since October 2013. [Para 48][Para 55][Para 123] She and a second worker, Ms O, demanded reinstatement and back wages from the date of dismissal to the date of return, and the collective dispute that survived conciliation was referred to the panel on 22 March 2017. [Para 3][Para 18][Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back Ms L and Ms O and pay wages and benefits from the date of dismissal to the date of return? [Para 55]
2. Whether the employer must stop discriminating against the Khmer Workers' Heart Union and the company union? [Para 77]
3. Whether the employer must comply with the Labour Law and the Law on Trade Unions? [Para 79]
4. Whether the employer must refrain from suppressing workers' freedom to form the Khmer Workers' Heart Union? [Para 81]
5. Whether the employer must recognise the Khmer Workers' Heart Union and sign every document the union submits? [Para 83]
6. Whether the employer must refrain from dismissing workers who voted to create the Khmer Workers' Heart Union? [Para 14]

Points 2 to 5 were settled at the hearing and recorded as agreed; point 1 as it concerned Ms O, and point 6, were withdrawn, leaving only point 1 for Ms L. [Para 28][Para 29][Para 30]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Ms L was the elected president of the Khmer Workers' Heart Union and her dismissal was union discrimination; the chain of contracts exceeded two years, so she held an unfixed-duration contract and was dismissed, not simply not renewed. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60][Para 139]
**Employer (respondent):** this was non-renewal of a fixed-duration contract because there was no work for her; the workforce had fallen from about 400 to about 260, five percent severance was paid at every contract expiry, and the company did not know she was a union leader. [Para 61][Para 63][Para 64][Para 65]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to order the employer to reinstate Ms L or to pay her back wages and benefits from the date of dismissal. [Para 203][Para 204]

On the character of the contract, the panel applied Article 67(2) of the Labour Law: a fixed-duration contract may not run beyond two years and may be renewed only while the total stays within two years, and practice to the contrary converts it into an unfixed-duration contract. [Para 107][Para 108][Para 109] Reading Article 73(5) together with it, the panel held that the reference is to the aggregate duration, not to each renewal taken alone. [Para 111][Para 114] Because Ms L began in October 2013 and her last contract ran to 07 April 2017, the total exceeded two years and the contract became unfixed-duration. [Para 123][Para 124][Para 125] The termination was therefore a dismissal under an unfixed-duration contract, not a non-renewal at expiry as the employer argued. [Para 138]

On protected status, Article 43(1) of the Law on Trade Unions allows dismissal of a union delegate or candidate only with the labour inspector's agreement, and Article 67 protects election candidates for 45 days before and 45 days after the ballot. [Para 140][Para 142][Para 143] Following Award No. 169/16, the panel required three conditions: protected category, dismissal inside the window, and notice to the employer. [Para 146] The first was met — Ms L was elected president on 03 December 2016. [Para 164][Para 166][Para 167] The second was met — she was dismissed on 09 January 2017, thirty-seven days after the ballot. [Para 168][Para 169][Para 172] The third was not: the letter of 05 December 2016 carried only a handwritten refusal note signed by Mr H, a federation official and not a person on the company side, so it could not prove the employer received the notice. [Para 173][Para 180][Para 181] The postal receipt bore two Cambodia Post stamps, 07 December 2016 and 27 January 2017, and the workers could not explain the second. [Para 191][Para 194] The party asserting bears the burden of proof, and no precise date on which the employer learned of the candidacy could be fixed. [Para 174][Para 177][Para 196][Para 199][Para 200] The claim consequently failed. [Para 201][Para 202]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A succession of fixed-duration contracts whose aggregate duration passes two years converts into an unfixed-duration contract, and the five percent severance paid at each expiry does not prevent the conversion; but a union leader dismissed inside the 45-day protected window still fails unless the union proves the employer was notified of the candidacy before the dismissal, and a refusal note written by a federation official is not proof that notice reached the employer. [Para 121][Para 132][Para 137][Para 146][Para 181]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the Labour Law tilts toward unfixed-duration contracts for the job security it gives workers, and that ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982, though not binding, is useful context for reading Article 67. [Para 112][Para 113][Para 116] It also noted that where no most-representative union exists, workers involved in a dispute may represent themselves or mandate a local union or another person. [Para 91][Para 97]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The start date is printed two ways: paragraph 55 contains an interpolated run ("COVID-19 October 2019 2013") and paragraph 123 reads "25, 08 October 2013" with two day numbers. [Para 55][Para 123] Issue 1 is used for two different claims: paragraph 29 records the withdrawal of "issue 1 (the case of Ms O)" while paragraph 30 says the panel will consider only "issue 1 (the case of Ms L)". [Para 29][Para 30] The severance rate appears as five percent at paragraphs 57 and 128 but as "15 persons percent" at paragraph 137. [Para 57][Para 137] The handwritten refusal note is dated 06 December 2016 at paragraph 73 but "06 / [month lost] 2019" at paragraph 179. [Para 73][Para 179] The only money figures, 178.83 USD (Ms O) and 819.93 USD (Ms L), come from the employer's account of its own settlements and are not adopted by the panel. [Para 4][Para 5] The letterhead is badly garbled by OCR (telephone, fax and email strings), and internal page numbers are embedded mid-sentence. [Para 1][Para 25]
