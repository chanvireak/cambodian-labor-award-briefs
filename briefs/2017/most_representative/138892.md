# Award No. 006/17 — Crystal Martin (Cambodia) Limited (Arbitration Council, 8 February 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom, employer-appointed; Tuon Si Phan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, presiding) · Doc ID 138892

## 1. Facts
Crystal Martin (Cambodia) Limited is a garment-cutting and sewing enterprise in Khan S'ang, Kandal Province, employing approximately 5,169 workers. [Para 1][Para 53] The sample-sewing section (ផ្នែក ដេរ គំរូ) demanded a USD 13 increase on its 2016 base wage of USD 160 for 2017; the company granted USD 10, taking the base to USD 170, and the workers refused it. [Para 3][Para 4][Para 57] Kandal provincial Labour Department conciliation on 11 January 2017 failed on this single point, which reached the Council on 13 January 2017. [Para 12][Para 13] Four sample-sewing workers — Mr Kh Koeun, Mr C Phally, Ms K Borany and Mr P Vat — brought the claim on a transfer of rights signed by 50 workers. [Para 41][Para 82]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise the base wage of sample-sewing workers by USD 13 over the 2016 base of USD 160 for 2017, rather than the USD 10 it granted — and, as a prior question, whether these four workers were competent to bring the collective dispute at all? [Para 57][Para 84][Para 85]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** At the meeting of 16 December 2015 the employer promised orally that sample-sewing wages would stay USD 20 above the ordinary worker's base every year, a differential it had paid since 2013, and the workers did not want to lose that benefit. [Para 64][Para 65]
**Employer (respondent):** That meeting concerned the 2016 increase only; no promise of a permanent USD 20 differential was made, no such principle exists in practice or in the collective agreement, and the differentials of USD 5, 7, 10 and 13 paid to team leaders, technicians, cloth, warehouse and quality staff vary each year by the employer's own assessment; paying USD 10 over USD 160 is already more favourable than the law requires. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider the case (សម្រេច បដិសេធ ពិចារណា សំណុំរឿង លេខ ០០៦/១៧) without ruling on the USD 13. [Para 85][Para 86] The award is non-binding and becomes final if no written objection reaches the Minister of Labour through the Council secretariat within 08 days of notification. [Para 87]

The panel raised the standing question of its own motion before the merits. [Para 73] It applied Article 54(1) of the Law on Trade Unions of 17 May 2016, under which the most representative union alone holds the exclusive right to create a collective agreement and to settle collective disputes, including the right to bring the dispute and represent workers at every stage of the procedure, the Arbitration Council included. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 77] It followed its reasoning in Award No. 180/16 (Cement Cambodia Chakrey Ting), and referred to Award No. 171/16 (Jewelin Co., Ltd), that only a most representative union may represent workers before the Council. [Para 76][Para 79][Para 80]

On the facts, the Labour Union for Cambodia at Crystal Martin held most-representative certificate No. 018/15 K.B/V.B of 19 March 2015, valid to 19 March 2017, and both parties accepted this. [Para 56][Para 81] The four claimants had, however, taken a transfer of rights signed by 50 sample-sewing workers and a representative certificate from the provincial Labour Department, not authority from that union. [Para 82][Para 83] Because the enterprise had a most representative union, the panel held that the four named workers had no right to represent the workers in bringing the collective dispute, and dismissed. [Para 84][Para 85]

The panel had earlier noted, when summarising the parties' submissions, its decision in Award No. 233/15 refusing a comparable claim to add USD 28 to a base of USD 105. [Para 71][Para 72]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an enterprise has a union holding a valid most-representative certificate, a collective dispute can reach the Arbitration Council only through that union's own leadership; a transfer of rights signed by the affected workers, or a certificate naming ad hoc worker representatives, confers no standing, and the panel will decline to hear the merits however strong the underlying wage claim. [Para 77][Para 84][Para 85]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remarks that the USD 20 differential was never a rule or a term of the collective agreement, that the USD 5–13 differentials paid to other groups vary yearly by the employer's own assessment, and that USD 10 above the legal minimum is more favourable than the law requires, are observations on the merits that did not decide the case. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 70]
