# Award No. 240/16 — I-Cheng (Cambodia) Corporation (Arbitration Council, 4 January 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuth Hun, appointed by the employer; Tuon Si Phan, appointed by the workers; Pen Bunhea, chair) · Doc ID 139396

## 1. Facts
I-Cheng (Cambodia) Corporation, a garment factory at Tang Dong, Bati, Takeo, employed about 2,732 workers. [Para 1][Para 50] The company suspended its employment contracts four times between February and September 2016, each time for two months and each time with the written authorisation of the Takeo provincial labour inspectorate; it paid no wages during those periods but kept attendance bonuses, travel allowance and accommodation allowance in place. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 62] A CYWU union activist, Mr S, was dismissed on 14 September 2016 after three warnings and unauthorised absences in July and August; 108 workers transferred their rights to the union confederation, 88 of whom claimed the suspension wages. [Para 53][Para 68][Para 97][Para 112]

## 2. Issues in dispute
Of four original demands one was withdrawn before conciliation and a third at the hearing, leaving two. [Para 13][Para 18][Para 19]
1. Whether the employer must pay 100% of wages for the periods of contract suspension in February, March, June and July 2016 that the labour inspector had authorised? (issue 1) [Para 56][Para 114]
2. Whether the employer must take Mr S back and pay his wages from the date of dismissal until reinstatement? (issue 2) [Para 68][Para 135]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the Labour Law permits an employer to suspend a contract for two months only, and the suspensions here were unlawful, so full wages are owed for the affected months; Mr S was dismissed for union activity, his absences were family-related and notified, and the employer's account of his disobedience is unsupported. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 80][Para 83][Para 128]
**Employer (respondent):** each suspension was discussed with team leaders, the labour office and the union beforehand, was announced to the workers in advance and was authorised under Article 71(k), which the panel should not revisit; Mr S was frequently late, ignored his team leader and was absent without leave on 20 and 22 August 2016, and was given a month's notice on 14 August 2016. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 62][Para 81][Para 139][Para 140]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — both claims were dismissed, but the employer was ordered to pay Mr S notice compensation under Articles 75 and 77, dismissal damages under Article 89, moral damages equal to the dismissal damages under Article 91, outstanding annual leave under Article 166, and final wages under Article 116. No figure is printed; each head is ordered by reference to the statutory formula. [Para 216][Para 217][Para 218][Para 219][Para 220][Para 221][Para 222]

On issue 1, Article 71(k) allows suspension of enterprise activity for no more than two months where serious economic or material difficulty prevents continued operation, subject to labour inspector supervision, and Article 72(k) excuses the wage obligation where the suspension is properly made. [Para 114][Para 115][Para 121][Para 122] The panel found the four suspensions covered by two written authorisations of the Takeo labour office dated 1 February and 3 June 2016, that both parties accepted those authorisations, that the workers had never objected to them at the time, and that the panel has no competence to set aside an inspector's authorisation — a dissatisfied worker must challenge it before the inspector. [Para 125][Para 126][Para 129][Para 130][Para 131] The claim therefore failed. [Para 133][Para 134]

On issue 2, Article 74 permits termination by notice for conduct-related reasons, but the employer bears the burden of proving the misconduct and that dismissal was the appropriate and procedurally correct sanction; Article 27 requires the penalty to be proportionate. [Para 121][Para 135][Para 141][Para 143][Para 144] Mr S admitted unauthorised absences and three written warnings, but the employer produced no internal disciplinary rules from which the panel could find dismissal to be the prescribed penalty for that misconduct, and produced no evidence of the alleged disobedience to his team leader. [Para 146][Para 147][Para 156][Para 157][Para 158] The notice of termination dated 14 August 2016 was unsigned and was issued while he was on authorised leave, and the panel found he never received it. [Para 165][Para 166][Para 167] With no valid notice and no proportionate cause, the dismissal was without proper grounds, so Articles 77, 89 and 91 compensation followed; seniority of two years eight months was rounded up to three years for the Article 89 calculation. [Para 168][Para 169][Para 180][Para 181][Para 182][Para 195]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A contract suspension carried out with the labour inspector's written authorisation under Article 71(k), and unchallenged by the workers before the inspector at the time, relieves the employer of the wage obligation under Article 72(k); the arbitration panel will not review the merits of that authorisation. [Para 122][Para 130] Separately, an employer that cannot produce internal disciplinary rules showing dismissal as the sanction prescribed for the misconduct it has proved, and cannot prove the remaining allegations, has not discharged its burden, and the dismissal is without valid grounds whatever the worker's admitted absences. [Para 143][Para 158][Para 159]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel held that the factory has no most-representative union, so under Articles 54 and 11 of the Labour Union Law the CYWU local may represent its own members, and a federation officer could represent the 108 workers who transferred their rights under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 89][Para 102][Para 105][Para 110] The award is non-binding and becomes final if no objection is filed within eight days. [Para 20][Para 223]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The postponement paragraph prints the original award date as "23 December 2019 2016", two years in one phrase, while the header and the closing orders give 4 January 2017 [Para 1][Para 22][Para 224]; both variants are reported, not corrected. A second inconsistency runs through the paragraph numbering: paragraph 114 asks the question for "February, March and July 2016" while the claim and the order name February, March, June and July [Para 112][Para 114][Para 216]. The claimant's name is rendered as three different strings (Sum Ben, Sum Beng, and An Sum on the death certificate of his grandfather) and is given here as Mr S [Para 1][Para 41][Para 68]. The panel composition is printed twice, and in the signature block the employer-appointed arbitrator's name is left blank [Para 1][Para 224]. Large OCR losses affect paragraphs 86 to 108, where Khmer statutory text and Latin fragments ("Ling", "Whiver Which Cambodia") are interleaved, and the equipment and party details at paragraph 1. No monetary amounts are printed anywhere in the operative part.
