# Award No. 036/17 — Quinn Th Major Industral Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 6 November 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel: Yu Soun Ty (employer-appointed), Sin Kim Sean (worker-appointed), Kong Phalla (president) · Doc ID 138939

## 1. Facts
A garment manufacturer at Ang Snuol, Kandal, employing about 3,952 workers. [Para 58][Para 59] Its enterprise union held a most-representative certificate valid to 8 June 2018, but the union's leadership mandate had expired by the time of the hearing. [Para 60][Para 62] The provincial labour department certified three workers as worker representatives by letter No. 010 of 10 October 2017, two of whom attended. [Para 63][Para 111] Nineteen candidates, proposed by two other unions, stood for the sixth-mandate employee-delegate election; all were being prosecuted for public defamation on a complaint by the claimant union. The election set for 15 September 2017 was suspended after the union's objection, then held on 13 October 2017 with the same nineteen candidates, its result unannounced at the hearing. [Para 70][Para 72][Para 73][Para 74]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must withdraw the nineteen candidates from the list of candidates for election as full-rights employee delegates and assistant delegates? [Para 65]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** employee delegates enjoy the same protection as union leaders under Article 269 of the Labour Law, so a candidate must not have been convicted of any criminal offence; the nineteen are under prosecution and cannot stand; in any event the employer has no power to withdraw candidates proposed by a union. [Para 75][Para 79][Para 82]

**Employer (respondent):** the criminal proceedings are separate from the employment relationship; it applies only the Labour Law and the Union Law; no final conviction exists and no law in force bars a convicted person from standing. [Para 78][Para 80][Para 81]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the claim was dismissed and the panel declined to consider it, holding that disputes over the right to stand in an employee-delegate election fall to the Labour Court. [Para 122][Para 124]

The panel dealt first with standing, because the employer objected that the two union officers present could not act. Under Article 54(1) of the Union Law only a most-representative union has exclusive rights of representation, including before the Arbitration Council; the claimant union holds that certificate, but its leadership mandate had expired, so the two officers could not appear as union leaders. [Para 87][Para 105] Prakas No. 362 Article 1, however, delegates to the Labour Disputes Department and provincial labour offices the power to certify worker representatives, and those certified may represent workers even where no most-representative union exists. Letter No. 010 certified three workers and two attended, so standing was satisfied. [Para 108][Para 109][Para 113]

On the merits the panel read Article 47 of the Union Law as channelling every complaint about an election, about the right to stand, and about the regularity of an employee-delegate election to the Labour Court, and, where no competent labour court exists, to the ordinary courts. [Para 115][Para 117][Para 118] Because the claim was precisely that nineteen candidates should be removed from the list, it was a dispute about the right to stand, not a collective labour dispute within the panel's competence. [Para 120][Para 121] The panel therefore dismissed the claim without examining whether the prosecutions had produced any conviction. [Para 122][Para 123]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A complaint that named candidates are ineligible to stand in an employee-delegate election is a dispute about the right to stand, which Article 47 of the Union Law reserves to the Labour Court, and the Arbitration Council has no jurisdiction to order their withdrawal. [Para 115][Para 117][Para 122] Where a union's leadership mandate has expired, its officers cannot represent the union before the panel, but workers certified by the Labour Disputes Department under Article 1 of Prakas No. 362 may represent the workforce. [Para 105][Para 108][Para 113]

**Obiter dicta:** none identified.
