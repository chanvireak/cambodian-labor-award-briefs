# Award No. 020/17 — Eminen Garnment (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 27 April 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Nan, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, chair appointed by both) · Doc ID 138923

## 1. Facts
Ms P was a sewing worker at Eminen Garnment (Cambodia) Ltd from 3 November 2015, on successive fixed-duration contracts of three months, the last expiring on 28 February 2017 [Para 58][Para 59]. On 11 December 2016 she was elected secretary of the Khmer Workers' Heart Union at the factory [Para 82]. On 21 February 2017 her team leader told her the employer would not renew her contract, and on 28 February she was called to collect wages and severance, which she did not collect [Para 60][Para 61]. The union's registration application was still pending with the Kandal provincial department of labour [Para 62].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take Ms P back into work and pay her wages from the date of termination until reinstatement, because she held office as union secretary? [Para 4][Para 57]
2. Whether the employer discriminated against her as a union officer, contrary to Article 62 of the Trade Union Law? [Para 8]
3. Whether the employer must recognise the Khmer Workers' Heart Union? [Para 11]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Ms P was elected union secretary on 11 December 2016, the union had applied for registration and the 30-day period had run, so registration was complete; she had committed no breach of internal rules, and non-renewal was discrimination contrary to Article 62 of the Trade Union Law [Para 62][Para 63][Para 64][Para 65].
**Employer (respondent):** Under Article 73 of the Labour Law an employer may decline to renew a fixed-duration contract when it expires; the company did not single her out but stopped renewing many contracts because work had fallen away, and never refused any lawfully registered union [Para 6][Para 66][Para 68].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the claim that the employer take Ms P back and pay wages from the date of termination to the date of return; no sum was ordered [Para 155][Para 156].

The panel first settled who could act. Because Eminen Garnment had no most-representative union (សហជីព មានភាពជាតំណាងបំផុត), and Article 54(1) of the Trade Union Law reserves exclusive bargaining and dispute-representation rights to such a union, the panel followed Awards 180/16 (Siemens Cambodia) and 171/16 (Jewelry Line) to hold that in the absence of a most-representative union the workers in dispute may represent themselves or mandate another [Para 93][Para 96][Para 100][Para 101]. The three workers confirmed by the Kandal provincial department of labour on 29 March 2017 therefore had standing, under Prakas 362 [Para 108][Para 109][Para 110].

On issue 1 the panel asked whether Article 67 of the Trade Union Law protected Ms P. Article 67 extends the protection given to employee delegates to candidates, founders and voluntary members, and Article 43 requires the labour inspector's prior authorisation for dismissal of such persons [Para 114][Para 117][Para 121]. The panel held that authorisation applies to dismissal, not to the expiry of a fixed-duration contract, following Awards 169/16 (Morning Glory), 181/16 and 229/16 (U Tong Development) and 007/17 [Para 123][Para 124]. Since both parties accepted that what happened was non-renewal, not dismissal, contract relations ended on 28 February 2017 by operation of the term and no protection attached [Para 126][Para 127][Para 154].

On issue 2 the panel applied Article 12 and Article 279 of the Labour Law and Article 62 of the Trade Union Law, the latter expressly reaching non-renewal of successive contracts [Para 128][Para 130][Para 133][Para 138]. The employer did not dispute knowing of her union office, but produced records showing the workforce fell from 782 in December 2016 to about 500 in March 2017, that contracts were suspended at 50 per cent wages in January to March 2017, and that 18, 15 and 20 contracts were not renewed in January, February and March respectively [Para 72][Para 76][Para 87][Para 88]. Fourteen other workers were not renewed at the same time as Ms P, and the workers did not contest the suspension evidence [Para 81][Para 153]. Finding the workers had not discharged the evidential burden on discrimination, the panel dismissed the allegation [Para 145][Para 147][Para 148].

Issues 2 and 3 (anti-discrimination order and union recognition) were withdrawn by the workers at the hearing, leaving only issue 1 for decision [Para 24][Para 25].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The statutory protection attaching to union office-holders and to candidates, founders and members under Articles 43 and 67 of the Trade Union Law — including the requirement of the labour inspector's prior authorisation — governs dismissal and does not extend to the lawful expiry of a fixed-duration contract or to an employer's decision not to renew it; where such non-renewal is alleged to be anti-union discrimination under Article 62, the worker bears the burden and must show more than the coincidence of union office and non-renewal [Para 123][Para 125][Para 147].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remarks that a most-representative union holds exclusive representation rights under Article 54(1), and that absent one the workers may represent themselves or mandate a representative, were made to establish standing and were not necessary to the dismissal of the claim [Para 96][Para 101]. Arbitrator An Nan filed a dissenting opinion in the annex, considering that the workers had given sufficient evidence and that the employer had not, and that reinstatement with back wages should have been ordered [Para 159][Para 171][Para 174].

The award is dated 27 April 2017 in the header and in the closing paragraph, consistent with the parties' agreed extension from 21 April [Para 1][Para 28][Para 175]. Paragraph 104 twice names Kêv province before correcting to Kandal ("ខេត្ត កែវ ខេត្ត កែវ កណ្តាល").
