# Award No. 008/17 — Top World Garment (Cambodia) Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 13 February 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kol Vuthana, employer-appointed; Sin Kim Sean, worker-appointed; Kong Phallob, president) · Doc ID 138900

## 1. Facts
Top World Garment (Cambodia) Ltd. is a garment manufacturer with 495 workers, where the Democratic Union of Workers in Garment had been registered since 23 July 2009 [Para 45][Para 46]. From 15 September 2016 the company stopped giving work and suspended the employment contracts in three instalments, paying only fragments of wages, then from 20 November 2016 suspended them altogether without pay until the hearing on 1 February 2017 [Para 56][Para 63][Para 119]. No labour inspector authorisation was obtained for any suspension, and the last one ran longer than two months [Para 120][Para 123]. The employer did not attend the hearing despite a properly served invitation [Para 20][Para 70].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay 100% of wages and other benefits for the period of unlawful suspension from 20 November 2016 to 1 February 2017? [Para 55][Para 107]
6. Whether the employer must pay 100% of base wage, travel and accommodation allowance, attendance bonus and seniority bonus for the suspension from 15 September 2016 to 15 November 2016? [Para 58][Para 127]

Issues 2 to 5 (October wages, November wages, removal of the factory manager, and pay for 1–2 December 2016) were withdrawn by the workers at the hearing and not decided [Para 24][Para 25].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** From 20 November 2016 the employer gave no work and suspended all contracts without paying wages or benefits, and none of the suspensions carried labour inspector authorisation; before suspension the base wage was 140 USD with 8 USD travel and accommodation allowance and a 10 USD attendance bonus, plus seniority pay by years of service, all of which must be paid in full [Para 56][Para 60].
**Employer (respondent):** Absent. It neither answered the claims nor gave any reason for not attending, and the panel treated it as having waived its right of reply [Para 20][Para 121].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to pay 100% of wages and other benefits for 20 November 2016 to 1 February 2017, and to make up the shortfalls for the three earlier suspension periods so that base wage, travel and accommodation allowance, attendance bonus and seniority bonus reach 100% [Para 140][Para 141][Para 142].

The panel first disposed of the employer's absence, holding that under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 it may proceed when a party duly invited fails to appear without good reason, and that the invitation had been served at the company gate after security refused it [Para 67][Para 72][Para 75]. On who could bring the claim, it found no union certified as most representative in the enterprise and applied Article 54 of the Law on Unions with Prakas No. 362: where no most-representative union exists, workers may represent themselves or delegate to a local union or other person, and representatives recognised by the Labour Disputes Department may act, including before the Arbitration Council [Para 79][Para 84][Para 89][Para 90]. Two of the three workers present were named in the Department's letter of 13 January 2017; the third, Mr S, had no thumbprint in the delegation but was present and was accepted as a representative, giving 281 claimants in total [Para 93][Para 103][Para 105].

On the merits the panel applied Article 71(11) of the Labour Law: suspension is permitted only for serious economic or material difficulty, must be under labour inspector supervision and may not exceed two months [Para 107][Para 108]. Following Award No. 58/07 it held those conditions cumulative, and following Award No. 08/07 that supervision means prior notification and prior authorisation from the labour inspector [Para 109][Para 110]. It then read Article 72(1) — which relieves the employer of wage obligations during suspension — as applying only to a suspension carried out lawfully; where the procedure is not followed the employer owes 100% of wages and benefits even though it has no work to give [Para 114][Para 115][Para 117]. The suspension from 20 November 2016 to 1 February 2017 exceeded two months and had no authorisation, so issue 1 was granted [Para 123][Para 124][Para 126]. For issue 6 the panel compared each period with what was paid: $15 base wage only for 15–30 September and 1–15 November, and $10 base wage plus 50% of the allowances and bonuses for October, and ordered the balance to bring every head to 100% [Para 132][Para 135][Para 137].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The wage exemption in Article 72(1) of the Labour Law protects only a suspension that satisfies Article 71(11) — serious economic or material difficulty, prior labour inspector authorisation, and a duration not exceeding two months; a suspension that fails any of those conditions is unlawful and obliges the employer to pay full wages and benefits for the whole period, whether or not work was available [Para 112][Para 117][Para 124].

**Obiter dicta:** With no most-representative union in the enterprise, the panel observed that workers may represent themselves or delegate to a union or other person, and that recognition by the Labour Disputes Department suffices for appearance before the Council [Para 84][Para 90][Para 100].

The ordered shortfall is printed as "10 ភាគរយ" at Para 134 and "1000 ភាគរយ" at Para 137, and "2 100 ភាគរយ" at Para 141, against 100% elsewhere. The president's name appears as Kong Phallob at Para 1 and a different final consonant at Para 144.
