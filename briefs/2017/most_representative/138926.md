# Award No. 019/17 — Pheng Sing Hong Enterprise Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 20 April 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Kong Phalla, chair appointed by both) · Doc ID 138926

## 1. Facts
The company is a garment manufacturer employing 400–450 workers, mostly women [Para 44][Para 84]. Its factory union filed a collective dispute on 3 March 2017 listing twenty demands on health facilities, hours, wages and severance [Para 28][Para 60]. On 7 March 2017 the employer dismissed two union activists, Ms S and Mr I, for distributing complaint leaflets outside working hours, paying them neither notice nor final February wages [Para 105][Para 107]. The employer did not attend the hearing; its administrative head came only to hand in documents, without written authority to represent it [Para 33][Para 35].

## 2. Issues in dispute
Of the twenty original demands, eleven were withdrawn at the hearing, leaving nine [Para 36][Para 37]:
1. Whether the employer must provide an infirmary with a permanent nurse, a doctor on duty and adequate medicines? [Para 3][Para 148]
2. Whether the employer must stop releasing workers at 10:45 and taking overtime from the midday break? [Para 4][Para 181]
3. Whether the employer must pay 50 per cent of wages and allowances for the 90 days of maternity leave before it begins? [Para 6][Para 198]
4. Whether the employer must issue labour books to workers on dismissal? [Para 10][Para 216]
5. Whether the employer must allow special leave to be offset against annual leave? [Para 14][Para 228]
6. Whether the employer must pay monthly wages no later than 16:00? [Para 16][Para 240]
7. Whether the employer must pay wages in full and stop carrying arrears? [Para 20][Para 254]
8. Whether workers with three years' seniority must receive 19 days of annual leave? [Para 21][Para 270]
9. Whether the employer owes severance to Ms S and Mr I, dismissed for distributing complaint leaflets? [Para 22][Para 286]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the factory lacks an infirmary, doctor and medicines and compresses lunch to fifteen minutes to extract overtime; maternity pay comes late and excludes allowances; there are no labour books, no special leave, delayed wages and arrears; annual leave is capped at 18 days; and the two activists were dismissed for exercising their right to complain [Para 76][Para 88][Para 105][Para 210].
**Employer (respondent):** it produced a letter of 15 March 2017 with 398 thumbprints in which workers reportedly agreed to claim nothing, and one of 8 April 2017 with 406 thumbprints asking that the owner not negotiate with the union; it answered no demand on the merits, and conceded that thumbprints on the March letter sat against the wrong names, including the union president's [Para 65][Para 67][Para 140].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — eight of the nine demands were granted and the labour-book demand dismissed; the sums were left to be computed from actual seniority and average wages [Para 347][Para 355].

**Procedure and standing.** The employer was duly summoned by letter No. 143 of 30 March 2017 and gave no reason for its absence, so the panel proceeded under Article 21 of Prakas 099; it had abandoned its right of reply [Para 111][Para 160]. With no most-representative union, Article 54(1) of the Trade Union Law and Award 180/16 let the workers act through a mandated representative [Para 45][Para 122][Para 126]. Departmental letter No. 005/17 named six representatives; three attended, mandated by 185 workers' thumbprints, so those 185 were the claimants [Para 134][Para 146]. The employer's "support the factory" letters could not be relied on, and withdrew no claim anyway [Para 141][Para 144].

**Issue 3 (infirmary).** Articles 238 and 242 of the Labour Law and Article 1 of Prakas 330 require any enterprise with at least fifty workers to keep a permanent infirmary with a nurse on duty, a doctor, dressings and medicines at the employer's cost [Para 148][Para 153]. With 400 workers the obligation applied; the panel ordered those facilities [Para 179].

**Issue 4 (working hours).** Article 4 of the company's internal rules sets 07:00–11:00 and 12:00–16:00, giving one hour for lunch [Para 82][Para 182]. Releasing workers at 10:45 and recalling them at 11:00 left fifteen minutes and used the rest of the break as overtime, contrary to those rules and to the voluntary principle in Article 4 of Prakas 80 [Para 187][Para 195]. The panel ordered the internal schedule restored [Para 197].

**Issue 6 (maternity).** Articles 182 and 183 give 90 days' leave on half wages including allowances [Para 198][Para 199]. They do not fix the payment date, but Article 115(3) forbids payment on a rest day and requires it the day before, and Article 103 treats maternity pay as wages [Para 206]. The panel ordered half wages and allowances for 90 days, paid at least a day before the leave begins [Para 215].

**Issue 9 (labour books).** Article 32 of the Labour Law and Articles 1 and 4 of Prakas 414 require a labour book for every Khmer wage-earner and its return on departure [Para 216][Para 221]. The workers had never been issued books, so the employer held none to hand back; the demand was dismissed [Para 224][Para 227].

**Issue 12 (special leave).** Article 171 and Prakas 267 permit special leave for family events and allow it to be set against annual leave where annual leave remains unused [Para 228][Para 233]. The panel ordered the employer to permit the offset [Para 239].

**Issues 14 and 18 (payment time and arrears).** Article 115(3) requires wages to be paid within working hours and Article 137 caps the day at eight hours [Para 240][Para 242]. Wages were paid between 17:30 and 18:00 although work ended at 16:00, so the panel ordered payment no later than 16:00 [Para 253]. On arrears, Articles 102 and 116 require full payment at least twice a month; paying part of the wage a week or ten days late breached them, and the panel ordered full payment for days actually worked [Para 269].

**Issue 19 (annual leave).** Article 166 adds one day of annual leave per three years of service, reaching 19 days in years four to six and 20 in years seven to nine [Para 270][Para 278]. The employer gave 18 days regardless of seniority; the panel ordered 19 days from three years' seniority [Para 285].

**Issue 20 (the two activists).** Article 74 permits dismissal at the employer's will only for a reason related to aptitude, conduct or the needs of the enterprise [Para 289][Para 290]. Distributing a complaint outside working hours is the exercise of a worker's right, not such a reason, so the dismissal lacked valid cause [Para 294][Para 295]. The panel awarded notice pay under Articles 75 and 77, the Article 89 dismissal indemnity by actual seniority, moral damages under Article 91, unused annual leave under Articles 166 and 167, and the withheld February 2017 wages under Article 116 [Para 302][Para 335][Para 345]. No reinstatement was ordered; both were treated as holding indefinite-duration contracts since 19 October 2015 [Para 288][Para 311].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer is properly summoned and stays away without reason, the panel decides on the workers' evidence alone under Article 21 of Prakas 099 [Para 118][Para 160]. A consent document the employer admits carries misplaced thumbprints cannot establish that any named worker waived a claim [Para 141]. Dismissing workers for distributing a complaint outside hours is not a reason related to aptitude, conduct or enterprise needs under Article 74, and attracts notice pay, the Article 89 indemnity, moral damages under Article 91, unused annual leave and withheld final wages [Para 294][Para 295].

**Obiter dicta:** The labour-book demand failed because no books had ever been created, so nothing is decided about reconstructing one for past service [Para 224]. The remarks on authenticating consent letters were unnecessary, the panel also finding they withdrew no claim [Para 144].

## 6. Source defects disclosed

The workforce is given as about 400 in some places and about 450 at paragraph 59.
