# Award No. 243/16 — Seng Hong Yan Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 12 January 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; On Non, appointed by the workers; Pen Bunhea, chair) · Doc ID 139397

## 1. Facts
Seng Hong Yan Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd cuts and sews garments at Prey Pring South, Chom Chao, Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh, and employed about 250 workers. [Para 1][Para 49][Para 50] The company had no work for its workers from 23 to 28 September 2016 and stopped them attending without seeking the labour inspector's authorisation, paying 50% of wages for those days. [Para 57][Para 119] It then gave two further unpaid days off, 29 September and 4 October 2016, on either side of the Pchum Ben national holiday. [Para 67][Para 134] Thirty-one workers transferred their rights to the Federation of Cambodian Apparel Industry Workers' Unions and the Labour Dispute Department recognised three worker representatives, giving 34 original complainants. [Para 55][Para 103]

## 2. Issues in dispute
Five demands were conciliated; the workers withdrew issue 1 and issue 5 at the first hearing and issue 4 at the second, leaving two. [Para 14][Para 20][Para 22][Para 23]
1. Whether the employer must pay the remaining 50% of wages for the days from 23 to 28 September 2016, when it suspended the contracts without the labour inspector's authorisation? (issue 2) [Para 57][Para 106]
2. Whether the employer must pay 100% of wages for 29 September and 4 October 2016, the two extra days off it granted around Pchum Ben? (issue 3) [Para 67][Para 125]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the absence of work was not their fault, so they should not lose half their wages; the employer suspended the contracts with no inspector supervision and without agreement from the worker representatives, which is unlawful, and the two holiday-adjacent days were the employer's decision, not theirs. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 62][Para 69][Para 70][Para 71]
**Employer (respondent):** it had no work to give and told the workers, through their team leaders, in advance; the workers had already accepted the 50% payment for 23–28 September on 9 October 2016, and it granted the two extra days so that workers could rest longer over the holiday, which cannot be paid because no work was done. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65][Para 72][Para 73][Para 74]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to pay a further 50% of wages for 23 to 28 September 2016, and 100% of wages for 29 September and 4 October 2016. No money figure is printed. [Para 143][Para 144]

On issue 2, Article 71(k) permits suspension only for serious economic or material difficulty, for no more than two months, and subject to labour inspector supervision; Article 72(1) relieves the employer of wages only where the suspension is properly made. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 110][Para 111] Both parties accepted that no request was ever made to the labour inspector and that the employer notified workers only through team leaders. [Para 119] The panel held the suspension procedurally invalid for that reason alone, so Article 72(1) did not apply and the employer could not escape the 100% wage obligation; the 50% already paid did not satisfy the spirit of Articles 71 and 72. [Para 120][Para 121][Para 122][Para 123] It declined to treat the workers' acceptance of the 50% payment as a waiver. [Para 124]

On issue 3, the panel found the two days off fell immediately before and after the Pchum Ben holiday and were contiguous with the 23–28 September suspension, in a period when the employer had no work for anyone. [Para 134][Para 135] The employer could not rely on the holiday as a reason to lay workers off without pay for days on which it had no work to give, so those two days were themselves suspension days and, being unauthorised in the same way, carried the same 100% wage obligation. [Para 136][Para 137][Para 140][Para 141][Para 142]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A contract suspension that the employer imposes without first notifying and obtaining the labour inspector's authorisation under Article 71(k) is procedurally invalid, and the employer remains liable for 100% of wages under Article 72(1) even where it has paid half and the workers accepted that payment. [Para 120][Para 121][Para 123] Days of no work that the employer attaches to a public holiday, and that run on from an invalid suspension, are suspension days and not unpaid holiday leave, so they carry the same full wage obligation. [Para 136][Para 137][Para 141]

**Obiter dicta:** because the factory had no most-representative union, the panel applied Articles 54 and 11 of the Labour Union Law and Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 to hold that the federations' three recognised representatives could act for the 31 workers who transferred their rights, and that the employer had validly delegated authority to its administration head, Mr S. [Para 76][Para 88][Para 93][Para 99][Para 100] The award is non-binding and becomes final if no objection is filed within eight days. [Para 24][Para 145]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The reasons on issue 3 contradict their own conclusion: paragraph 129 records that the workers knew of and did not object to the two unpaid days, and paragraph 130 concludes that they "agreed" to take them unpaid, yet paragraphs 136 to 141 hold the same two days to be unpaid suspension days carrying full wages. [Para 129][Para 130][Para 136][Para 141] Both are reported; the operative order follows the latter. [Para 144] Dates are printed inconsistently in the same paragraph — "23 September 2019" alongside "2016" [Para 118] — and the panel's arithmetic on the original complainants is unstable, reading "4 out of 31", then "34" [Para 102][Para 103]. The employer's name is rendered as សេង ហុង យាន, សេង ហុង យាន ហ្គាមេន and "Seng Hong Yan Garment" variously; the Latin form used here is a transliteration of the Khmer face of the award. [Para 1][Para 49] The signature block is largely lost, with the employer-appointed arbitrator's name replaced by OCR noise and the worker-appointed arbitrator's name left blank in the header of the closing page. [Para 146] Paragraphs 9, 54 and 125 contain interleaved Latin gibberish where Khmer text was lost. No monetary amounts are printed in the operative part.
