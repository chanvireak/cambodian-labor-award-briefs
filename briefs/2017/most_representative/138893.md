# Award No. 005/17 — Wai Ful Garmen (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 9 February 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ri, employer-appointed; An Vireak, worker-appointed; Kong Phalha, presiding) · Doc ID 138893

## 1. Facts
Wai Ful Garmen (Cambodia) Ltd is a garment-cutting and sewing enterprise in Ang Snuol district, Kandal Province, employing about 1,050 workers, of whom 260 were directly affected by this dispute. [Para 1][Para 60][Para 61] The Solidarity Union of Cambodia at the company (សហជីព សាមគ្គីភាព ការងារ កម្ពុជា), registered on 08 February 2016 under certificate No. 3513 K.B/VK, filed three claims after conciliation settled five of eight points on 29 December 2016. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 124] The disputed acts were the employer's USD 8 monthly lunch allowance against a claimed USD 15, its use of three-month rather than six-month fixed-duration contracts, and its non-renewal of substitute staff delegate Mr V Chhoeun, who had worked there since 13 July 2012 on successive three-month contracts. [Para 69][Para 74][Para 78][Para 83]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of USD 15 per worker per month, rather than the USD 8 it has paid since July 2015? [Para 69][Para 135]
2. Whether the employer must conclude six-month fixed-duration contracts with the workers instead of the three-month contracts it uses? [Para 74][Para 158]
3. Whether the employer must take back Mr V Chhoeun and pay his wages from the date of termination to the date of reinstatement? [Para 78][Para 184]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer promised before hundreds of workers that the lunch allowance would rise step by step once the company was stable, and the company is now stable; longer six-month contracts are wanted so the employer cannot easily dismiss them, and dismissal of Mr V Chhoeun was unlawful because he was a substitute staff delegate on an indefinite contract. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 75][Para 85][Para 96]
**Employer (respondent):** It cannot pay USD 15 because the law does not require it and it already pays USD 2 above the minimum wage, plus the USD 8 lunch allowance; it cannot move to six-month contracts because most workers who are not union members want three-month contracts to collect the 5% end-of-contract severance sooner; and Mr V Chhoeun's contract simply expired, he absented himself from the workplace without permission and breached internal rules. [Para 73][Para 77][Para 89][Para 92]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issues 1 and 2 refused, issue 3 granted: the employer must take Mr V Chhoeun back and pay his wages from the date of termination to the date of reinstatement. No sum was fixed in riel or dollars beyond that formula. [Para 232][Para 233][Para 234] The award is non-binding and becomes final 08 days after notification if no written objection is lodged with the Minister. [Para 235]

On representation the panel reviewed Article 54(1) of the Law on Trade Unions of 17 May 2016 and Article 72, following Award No. 180/16 (Cement Cambodia Chakrey Ting), and held that only a most representative union may carry a collective dispute to the Council. [Para 104][Para 107][Para 108] No union at the company held a most-representative certificate. [Para 62] The panel first observed that the three named workers therefore had no standing, then, applying point 1 of Prakas No. 362 K.B/P.K of 29 August 2016 on delegation, held that workers recognised by the provincial Labour Department do have standing where no most-representative union exists, as certified by letter No. 051 of 29 December 2016; it followed Awards 223/16, 224/16 and 244/16. [Para 127][Para 129][Para 130][Para 132]

On issue 1 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, contract, agreement or past practice obliging the employer to pay USD 15, and classified the claim as one exceeding the legal minimum, that is a dispute of interests (វិវាទ អំពី ផលប្រយោជន៍). [Para 136][Para 137] Reading point 1 of Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, an interest award replaces the collective agreement and binds all workers for one year; the panel therefore held such a claim admissible only from a most-representative union, and since the claimant union was not one, it declined to consider the claim. [Para 150][Para 152][Para 153][Para 156]

On issue 2 the panel applied Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the 2007 Civil Code and Award No. 001/16 (Strong Hold International), that a contract is formed and altered by the parties' consent, so neither side can be forced into a six-month term; it also cited Award No. 115/10 (G-Fashion). [Para 163][Para 167][Para 169][Para 173] The claim was refused. [Para 182][Para 183]

On issue 3 the panel held that Mr V Chhoeun's successive three-month fixed-duration contracts had run past two years, so under Article 67(2) and Article 73(5) of the Labour Law the relationship became an indefinite contract, following Award No. 10/03 (Zacksintek) and the ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982; paying 5% severance at each renewal did not keep the contract fixed-term, per Award No. 91/10 (New Way) and Award No. 179/16. [Para 187][Para 201][Para 203][Para 216][Para 217] His case was therefore a dismissal, not an expiry. [Para 218] As a substitute staff delegate elected in the third term with 25 votes, he enjoyed protection under Article 43 of the Law on Trade Unions, which requires the labour inspector's prior authorisation before dismissal; the employer sought none, so the dismissal was procedurally unlawful and reinstatement with back wages was ordered under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 103][Para 219][Para 224][Para 227][Para 228][Para 231]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive fixed-duration contracts whose combined duration exceeds two years convert into an indefinite contract as a matter of law, and the 5% severance paid at each renewal neither prevents the conversion nor preserves the fixed-term character. [Para 216][Para 217] A staff delegate — including a substitute delegate elected by the workforce — may not be dismissed at all before the labour inspector has authorised it, so a dismissal effected without that authorisation is unlawful and reinstatement with back pay follows. [Para 224][Para 227] Separately, a claim for a benefit above the statutory floor is an interest dispute that only a most representative union may bring. [Para 153][Para 156]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remarks that the Labour Law shows a deliberate tilt towards indefinite contracts because job security serves both worker and employer, and that fixed-term contracts should not be used for long-term work under ILO Recommendation No. 166, are explanatory context rather than holdings. [Para 194][Para 197][Para 198]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's name is printed in at least six forms (វ៉ៃ ហ្វ៊ុល ហ្គាមេន, វ៉ៃ ហ៊ល ហា មេន, វែ ហ្វ៊ុ, វើ ហ្វ៊ុ); the brief uses "Wai Ful Garmen (Cambodia) Ltd" as on the face of the award. [Para 1][Para 16][Para 55][Para 60] The panel contradicts itself on representation: para 127 holds the three workers have no legal right to represent the workers, while paras 130–132 hold that they do; both passages are reported above. [Para 127][Para 132] The dispute summary speaks of two workers where the reasoning addresses one, and the fourth paragraph of the issue-1 section adds a stray figure "២" beside USD 15. [Para 135] The worker's name is OCR'd as វ៉េង ឆវ័ន្ត, វេង ជីវ័ន, វេង ជវ័ន្ត and វ៉េង ឆវ័ន across the document. [Para 44][Para 231] Article 73(5)'s notice period is quoted as ten days for contracts over six months and corrected to fifteen days for contracts over one year in the same passage, and the labour-inspector decision deadline appears as "@(មួយ)ខែ". [Para 188][Para 189][Para 220]
