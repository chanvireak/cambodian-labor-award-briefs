# Award No. 022/17 — Sportex Industry Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 9 May 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; Tun Si Phan, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, chair appointed by both) · Doc ID 138924

## 1. Facts
Sportex Industry Co., Ltd is a garment manufacturer employing about 1,600 workers [Para 34][Para 35]. The company's practice was to engage workers on fixed-duration contracts of six months at a time, and once a worker's total contract duration passed two years the company moved that worker onto an indefinite-duration contract; 160 workers were on such contracts [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47]. The Cambodian Workers' Rights Promotion Support Union at the company held a registration certificate and a most-representative-union certificate No. 052/16 dated 15 July 2016, valid until 15 July 2018 [Para 36][Para 37]. Conciliation on 3 April 2017 failed on a single point [Para 12][Para 13].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must continue to apply six-month fixed-duration contracts to the same workers even though their total contract duration exceeds two years? [Para 4][Para 43]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the union asked that the same workers keep receiving six-month fixed-duration contracts, notwithstanding that their cumulative contract duration exceeds two years; the stated reason was that the workers want the end-of-contract severance payment equal to 5 per cent of wages earned during the contract period [Para 44][Para 49].
**Employer (respondent):** it cannot keep issuing six-month contracts to workers whose duration exceeds two years, because ILO guidance treats such workers as holding indefinite-duration contracts and because the buyers apply the same rule and would stop placing orders, leaving the factory without work [Para 50][Para 51][Para 52][Para 89].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the claim that the employer continue applying six-month fixed-duration contracts to the same workers whose total duration exceeds two years; no sum or order was made [Para 95][Para 96].

On standing, the panel applied Article 54(1) of the Trade Union Law, under which only the most-representative union in an enterprise may bring and represent a collective dispute, following Awards 180/16 (Siemens Cambodia), 171/16 (Jewelry Line), 188/16 (Green Summit), 220/16 and 234/16 [Para 52][Para 55][Para 56][Para 57]. Because the claimant union held certificate 052/16 valid to 15 July 2018 and its officers appeared at the hearing, it was entitled to represent all the workers of Sportex in the case [Para 58][Para 60][Para 62].

On the merits the panel treated the demand as a request to create or change the type of employment contract, and grounded the analysis in Article 65(1) of the Labour Law and Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code: a contract arises from matching offer and acceptance, and no party may be forced to accept a form or content it did not agree to [Para 66][Para 71][Para 75][Para 77][Para 79]. It noted its own earlier holdings that an indefinite-duration contract may be converted to a fixed-duration one only where both parties freely and genuinely agree [Para 85][Para 87]. Applying that, the panel held the workers' demand was not contrary to the spirit of the law, since proposing a change of contract type is their right, but the employer's refusal was equally lawful, since accepting or declining the offer is its right [Para 90][Para 91]. Finding no agreement between the parties on changing the type or form of contract, the panel dismissed the claim [Para 94][Para 95].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that an employer keep renewing six-month fixed-duration contracts for workers whose cumulative duration exceeds two years is a proposal to create or change the type of employment contract; because Article 65 of the Labour Law and the Civil Code require matching offer and acceptance, neither the workers nor the employer can impose a contract type or form on the other, and absent free and genuine agreement the panel will dismiss a claim to compel continued fixed-duration renewals [Para 79][Para 92][Para 93].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observations that an indefinite-duration contract can be converted back to a fixed-duration contract where both parties freely agree, and that the employer equally could not force an indefinite-duration contract on an unwilling worker, were not necessary to dismissing the claim [Para 87][Para 93]. Its remarks on ILO guidance and buyer requirements were recited as the employer's position, not adopted as findings [Para 51][Para 52].
