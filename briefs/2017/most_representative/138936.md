# Award No. 031/17 — Wai Ful Garment (Cambodia) Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 24 October 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ouk Ri (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chea (president) · Doc ID 138936

## 1. Facts
Wai Ful Garment (Cambodia) Ltd. is a garment-cutting and sewing company in Ang Snuol district, Kandal, employing about 876 workers; four unions operate there but none holds most-representative status. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 50] Two workers, Ms O and Ms D, joined on 6 June 2014 on successive three-month fixed-duration contracts, the last expiring 5 August 2017, and each renewal was accompanied by a 5 per cent severance payment. [Para 52][Para 53] On 22 July 2017 the employer told them their contracts would not be renewed, paid wages to 5 August 2017, 5 per cent severance and 15 days' notice compensation, which the two refused to accept. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 56] Of the 10 demands, five settled at the hearing and issues 1 and 4 were merged, leaving four matters for the panel. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the ending of the two workers' contracts was a non-renewal of a fixed-duration contract or a dismissal, given that their cumulative contract period exceeded two years? [Para 105][Para 138]
2. Whether the employer's termination was connected to union discrimination, contrary to មាត្រា 12 and 279 of the Labour Law and មាត្រា 62 of the Law on Trade Unions? [Para 139][Para 144]
3. Whether the dismissal of the two workers complied with the procedure of the Labour Law and the company's internal regulations under មាត្រា 74? [Para 161]
4. Whether the employer must add $2 to the monthly transport and accommodation allowance, making $10 in total? [Para 179]
5. Whether the employer must add $5 to the monthly attendance bonus, making $15 in total? [Para 200]
6. Whether the employer must maintain the attendance bonus in full when a worker is absent on sick leave covered by a valid medical certificate? [Para 209][Para 220]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the administration told them the contracts were not renewed because Ms O had joined and helped found the Khmer Workers' Spirit Union, and Ms D was cut off because she shared a house with her — plain anti-union discrimination; in 2017 they were absent only three days, to take a sick child to hospital, with their team leader's permission; and the travel allowance, bonus and wage payments were established past practice that cannot simply be withdrawn. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60][Para 61][Para 68][Para 69][Para 74][Para 79]
**Employer (respondent):** both workers were absent five or six times, disrupting production, and under company practice three unauthorised absences amount to serious misconduct; the team leader and the administration jointly assessed them and decided not to renew; it has no obligation to pay above the legal minimum, and its current travel allowance of $8 and attendance bonus of $10 already exceed Notification No. 230; on the bonus it denies any change of practice. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 66][Para 76][Para 81][Para 85][Para 203][Para 211]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to take both workers back; refused to consider the travel-allowance and bonus-increase claims; and ordered the attendance bonus maintained at 100 per cent during certified sick leave. [Para 244][Para 245][Para 246][Para 247]
On the character of the termination the panel applied មាត្រា 67(2) of the Labour Law: a fixed-duration contract may not run beyond two years and renewal beyond that converts it into an indefinite-duration contract. [Para 105][Para 107] It held that the two-year limit refers to the cumulative maximum, not to each renewal in isolation, and followed its earlier reasoning in Award 133/16. [Para 116][Para 134] Because service from 6 June 2014 to 5 August 2017 exceeded two years, both contracts had become indefinite. [Para 124][Para 136][Para 137] The 5 per cent severance paid at each renewal did not prevent that conversion, following Award 91/10 (Nhu Y). [Para 129][Para 135] The ending was therefore a dismissal, not a non-renewal. [Para 138]
On union discrimination the panel set out មាត្រា 12 and 279 of the Labour Law and មាត្រា 62 of the Law on Trade Unions, which together bar an employer from using union membership or activity as a ground for decisions on hiring, discipline or termination, including non-renewal of successive contracts. [Para 140][Para 142][Para 144][Para 149] It held the claimant bears the burden of proving discrimination, and that a bare assertion unsupported by evidence must be rejected. [Para 151][Para 155][Para 157] The workers offered nothing beyond their own account, so the panel found no link to union discrimination. [Para 159][Para 160][Para 161]
On lawfulness the panel applied មាត្រា 74: an indefinite contract may be terminated by either party, but not without a valid reason relating to the worker's aptitude or conduct or the necessities of the enterprise. [Para 161][Para 163] The employer produced no document showing the alleged absences, no evidence of their effect on production, and never supplied its internal regulations; the panel therefore found no valid reason for dismissal. [Para 172][Para 173][Para 174] Under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 it ordered reinstatement. [Para 175][Para 178]
On the allowance and bonus increases the panel found the employer already paid $8 travel allowance and $10 attendance bonus, above the $7 and $10 floors in Ministry Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012, and no instrument obliged it to pay more; those demands were interest disputes, which only a most-representative union may bring, and there is none. [Para 179][Para 182][Para 183][Para 197][Para 198][Para 204][Para 205] On the sick-leave bonus the panel adopted the Full Bench decision of 3 October 2017: the bonus must be paid in full where the worker attended every working day, counting legally entitled, contractual and legally mandated absence days as attendance, and an employer owes nothing for other absences. [Para 223][Para 225] Certified sick leave is a legal right; an employer's permission is not a favour, and an incentive scheme may not be used to suppress exercise of legal rights. [Para 233][Para 237][Para 238][Para 241] The claim was therefore granted at 100 per cent. [Para 242][Para 243]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive fixed-duration contracts whose cumulative length passes two years become an indefinite-duration contract by operation of មាត្រា 67(2), and the 5 per cent severance paid at each renewal does not prevent that conversion; the ending of such a contract must therefore be justified as a dismissal under មាត្រា 74 with a valid reason the employer can evidence. [Para 116][Para 135][Para 174] An attendance bonus must be paid in full where the only absences are legally entitled ones, including certified sick leave, because an incentive may not penalise the exercise of a statutory right. [Para 225][Para 237][Para 243]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's wider statement that any means used by an obligation-holder to obstruct the exercise of a right is contrary to law, citing មាត្រា 11 of the Civil Code, goes beyond what the order required. [Para 235][Para 236] Its table of the statutory leave entitlements (weekly, holiday, annual, sick, maternity and special leave) is explanatory background rather than a ruling. [Para 227][Para 232]

[Para 15][Para 41][Para 46] The provincial labour department is described at [Para 100] as "ខេត្ត កែវ - កណ្តាល" (Kaev and Kandal), though the company sits in Kandal. [Para 1] The demand list is mis-numbered, with item 8 rendered "១៨-" at [Para 11]. Issue 3 is quoted at [Para 200] as a demand for "$56" where the figure is $5 everywhere else. [Para 77][Para 246] The two workers are recorded with the same employee number (10639) in the payroll slips summarised at [Para 87], which cannot be right. Worker names are redacted here.
