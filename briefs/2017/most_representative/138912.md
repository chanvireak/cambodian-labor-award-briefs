# Award No. 016/17 — Sansom Mlup Prey (SMP) (Arbitration Council, 2 May 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom appointed by the employer, Liv Sovanna appointed by the workers, Kong Phally chair) · Doc ID 138912

## 1. Facts
Sansom Mlup Prey ("SMP"), a conservation NGO based in Chamkar Mon district, Phnom Penh and working with WCS, employs about ten staff. [Para 1][Para 422] Nong Chinta, its sales and operations manager, was engaged on 15 April 2010 on an indefinite-duration contract at USD 766 a month and was dismissed on 24 January 2017 for serious misconduct under Article 83 of the Labour Law: taking 22 bundles of confidential documents out of the organisation, and renting her own and her family's house to it without declaring a conflict of interest. [Para 3][Para 324][Para 325][Para 385] Five colleagues — Mil Samoeun, Loeum Chan Tra, Chea Mesa, Oung Norendna and Sem Thearith — stopped work from 25 January 2017 demanding intervention, returning never; the employer treated them as having abandoned their jobs and dismissed them on 10 and 13 February 2017. [Para 385][Para 396][Para 426] A five-point complaint was filed on 9 February 2017; three points settled at conciliation on 16 March 2017, leaving reinstatement and payment of wages from dismissal to reinstatement. [Para 9][Para 10][Para 11]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the dispute is collective, the employer objecting that the claims of Nong Chinta and Sroung Sochetra are individual? [Para 270][Para 281][Para 282]
2. Whether the dismissals were for serious misconduct and proportionate under Articles 27 and 83? [Para 326][Para 365][Para 384]
3. Whether the five colleagues' absence was a strike or an abandonment of employment? [Para 385][Para 399][Para 420]
4. Whether the six claimants are owed notice pay, indemnity, moral damages, unused annual leave, holiday and weekly-rest pay, 13th-month pay, phone allowance and bonus? [Para 429][Para 453][Para 519]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissal punished Nong Chinta for pressing her team's grievances, after her salary was cut from USD 766 to USD 430 and a new recruit, Sun Hul, was brought in over her; the five colleagues were protesting, not deserting, and each claimant sought USD 100,000 in contract-end compensation. [Para 183][Para 184][Para 197][Para 212]
**Employer (respondent):** the documents were confidential financial and payroll records, disclosure of which the contract forbids; the house was let at USD 250 a month when comparable premises let at USD 130 — self-dealing; the five stayed away 10 to 13 working days and ignored two emails recalling them, so they abandoned their employment; nothing is owed. [Para 331][Para 351][Para 219][Para 226]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — no reinstatement was ordered and the dismissals were upheld as proportionate; notice pay, indemnity, unused annual leave and the telephone allowance were ordered, while moral damages, holiday and weekly-rest pay, 13th-month pay, the bonus and Sroung Sochetra's claims were refused. [Para 384][Para 428][Para 519][Para 523][Para 533]

On jurisdiction the panel applied the three conditions of Article 302 as interpreted in *Zassintex* No. 10/03, and the presumption that every demand in a non-conciliated report is collective, which the objecting employer must displace. [Para 276][Para 279][Para 282][Para 284] Conditions (a) and (b) were met, and condition (c) was met because the five colleagues' protest disrupted a ten-person organisation; Sroung Sochetra's claim failed condition (c) because she had left on 31 December 2016 and no other worker was affected, so her dispute is individual and was not considered. [Para 289][Para 293][Para 297][Para 300][Para 303][Para 319] Four representatives certified by the Phnom Penh Department of Labour on 16 February 2017 were accepted. [Para 312][Para 318]

On the conflict of interest, the house had been leased at USD 250 a month since 10 November 2012 under a contract the employer itself signed knowing it belonged to her family, no evidence showed overcharging, and SMP had no internal regulation on conflicts of interest at all — so the panel could not identify the rule breached or the penalty prescribed, and found no serious misconduct. [Para 335][Para 337][Para 344][Para 345][Para 346] On the documents, clause 7 of her contract bars showing private, salary or financial information to non-staff and Sroung Sochetra had left only 15 days earlier, but the employer proved neither disclosure nor that Chinta permitted it; removing 22 bundles without telling Sun Hul or management was nonetheless improper. [Para 350][Para 356][Para 360][Para 362]

Article 27 requires the penalty to match the fault, and the panel held dismissal proportionate to that impropriety. [Para 365][Para 382][Para 384] For the five, the third element of a strike under Article 318 — stopping work while remaining in or before the enterprise — failed because they stayed away altogether. [Para 387][Para 389][Para 393][Para 394] Nor had they abandoned their jobs: with no internal regulation in force the six-day practice had no legal force, and they showed no intention to abandon. [Para 405][Para 406][Para 424][Para 425] Their absences of 10 and 13 working days nevertheless disrupted a ten-person organisation and breached Article 69, and after two emails the employer was entitled to act; dismissal was proportionate. [Para 417][Para 419][Para 422][Para 428]

Compensation was then fixed: notice pay by seniority under Articles 75 and 77, indemnity under Article 89 at 15 days per year capped at six months, unused annual leave under Articles 166 to 168, and telephone allowance of USD 40 for Chinta and USD 10 for Sem Thearith. [Para 462][Para 473][Para 494][Para 516] Moral damages were refused because the employer had proper reason to dismiss, holiday and weekly-rest pay because the evidence was in English only and article 23 of Prakas No. 099 requires Khmer, 13th-month pay because the workers did not serve until April, and the bonus for want of evidence. [Para 485][Para 501][Para 504][Para 509][Para 518] Claims first raised in post-hearing evidence were not entertained under Article 312 and article 33 of the Prakas. [Para 432][Para 445]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand recorded in a non-conciliated report is presumed to be a collective dispute; the employer bears the burden of displacing that presumption, and the third Article 302 condition turns on actual disruption, which workers who have already left can no longer cause. [Para 276][Para 279][Para 297][Para 300] An employer that has adopted no internal regulation on conflicts of interest cannot characterise self-dealing as serious misconduct under Article 83, having the burden of proving the misconduct charged. [Para 329][Para 344][Para 346] Removing documents in breach of a confidentiality clause is misconduct, but Article 27 requires the sanction to match the fault, so dismissal is open only where the breach is proportionate to that sanction. [Para 362][Para 365][Para 384] Absence without remaining on the premises is not a strike under Article 318, and absent an internal regulation the six-day abandonment rule has no legal force; prolonged unauthorised absence still breaches Article 69 and may justify dismissal. [Para 389][Para 394][Para 406][Para 422][Para 428] A panel will not entertain claims raised for the first time in post-hearing evidence. [Para 432][Para 445]

**Obiter dicta:** the survey of Article 54(1) on the most-representative union, and the discussion of Prakas No. 362 on the certification of worker representatives, are general statements made while confirming the panel's composition of the parties, not findings necessary to the orders. [Para 305][Para 311][Para 316]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The order is expressed to decide "issue 2" only, although the two surviving demands were reinstatement and back wages; no reasoned order addresses reinstatement, which can be inferred solely from the finding that dismissal was proportionate. [Para 3][Para 4][Para 526]
- The arbitrators' names differ between the header and the signature block, which adds a garbled "Asian Okas 2017" line. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 535]
- The workforce is given as about ten at [Para 422] but as more than eight at [Para 411] in a passage about Article 31.
- The employer's name appears in corrupt variants including "Sansom Mlop Prey" and "Sop Mlop Prey", and the Wildlife Conservation Society is rendered "WSC" at [Para 376]. [Para 1]
- Long Latin-script corruption interrupts the quotations of Articles 74 and 168 and the case citations at [Para 449], [Para 450], [Para 499] and [Para 518].
- The claimant group is given as seven at [Para 286] and as six at [Para 322], without reconciling Sroung Sochetra's exclusion.
- The award is non-binding, becoming final absent written objection within 08 days. [Para 534]
