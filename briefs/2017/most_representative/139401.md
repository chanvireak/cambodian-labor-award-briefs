# Award No. 248/16 — Hung Tak Garment Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 20 January 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun appointed by the employer party, An Non appointed by the worker party, Kong Phalha chair) · Doc ID 139401

## 1. Facts
Hung Tak Garment Co., Ltd. is a garment-cutting and sewing enterprise at Dangkor, Phnom Penh, employing about 430 workers; it moved its operations from Khan Stung Meanchey to Khan Dangkor. [Para 34][Para 35] On 11 November 2016 the Union of Free Workers of the Hung Tak Garment Factory, registered with the Ministry of Labour under No. 2571 of 20 July 2012 and holding 173 members, filed eight demands; conciliation settled four and the remaining four were forwarded to the Council on 29 December 2016. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 16][Para 36][Para 37] The four unresolved demands were $5 more travel and accommodation allowance, 1,000 riel more overtime meal money, a 2,000 riel daily lunch allowance, and a nursery with a permanent caregiver (or $5 more milk money). [Para 40][Para 44][Para 47][Para 51] The hearing was held on 9 January 2017. [Para 14]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must add $5 to the $7 monthly travel and accommodation allowance already paid? [Para 40][Para 86]
2. Whether the employer must add 1,000 riel to the existing overtime meal allowance, making 3,500 riel for overtime from 4 p.m. to 6 p.m.? [Para 44][Para 112]
3. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of 2,000 riel per day? [Para 47][Para 122]
4. Whether the employer must build a nursery with a permanent caregiver, or failing that pay $5 more milk money per month for one and a half years? [Para 51][Para 129]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** relocation lengthened the commute — workers must wait for the bus from 6:30 a.m. — and nearby factories pay $10 to $12 a month in travel money. [Para 41][Para 42] Food has become dearer, so 2,500 riel does not buy a meal, and wages are low; most other factories pay a lunch allowance. [Para 45][Para 48][Para 49] The employer built no nursery at the new site, and $5 milk money is not enough. [Para 53][Para 54]
**Employer (respondent):** on each demand the employer has no capacity to pay. [Para 43][Para 46][Para 50] It keeps its existing practice: $7 travel and accommodation money plus transport from the old to the new site, 2,500 riel overtime meal money and $5 milk money for mothers of children under 18 months. [Para 40][Para 52][Para 55]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider all four demands, so no money or nursery was ordered. [Para 148][Para 149][Para 150][Para 151]

The panel first fixed the claimant. There was no most-representative union at the enterprise and the Labour Disputes Department had issued no letter recognising worker representatives for this dispute, [Para 39][Para 69][Para 71] so it could not rely on the delegation route it had accepted in other cases. [Para 67][Para 81] It nevertheless held that the registered local union was the claimant, and that under Article 11 of the Law on Trade Unions of 17 May 2016 a registered union acquires the rights the law confers, including the right to represent its members before the Council; the union's president attended the hearing and could therefore bring the case for the members. [Para 73][Para 75][Para 76][Para 82][Para 84][Para 85]

It then classified the demands. Each asked for more than the law, a collective agreement, internal rules, the contract or past practice gave, so each was an interest dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍) rather than a rights dispute. [Para 90][Para 91][Para 115][Para 116][Para 117][Para 123][Para 124] Under Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099 an award on an interest dispute takes the place of a collective agreement, binds every worker in the enterprise for one year and strips them of the right to bargain collectively and to strike, so the panel held it could decide such a dispute only where the claimant is the most-representative union or a local union with capacity to conclude a collective agreement. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 105] Where the claimant union is not most representative, the Council has refused to consider interest disputes. [Para 106][Para 109]

On the individual demands: Notice No. 230 of 25 July 2012 fixes travel and accommodation money at $7 a month, the employer pays that and also provides transport, and nothing in law, agreement or practice requires $5 more. [Para 86][Para 87][Para 89][Para 90] Notice No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 fixes overtime meal money at 2,000 riel a day; the employer pays 2,500 riel, which is more favourable, and no instrument requires 3,500 riel. [Para 113][Para 114][Para 115] No instrument requires a 2,000 riel lunch allowance at all. [Para 123] The nursery demand was barred: the same parties had litigated it in Case 98/12-Hung Tak, decided on 19 June 2012 with 305 claimants, where the panel ordered the employer to provide a nursery, and all three conditions of res judicata (អាជ្ញាអស់ជំនុំ) were met. [Para 129][Para 133][Para 136][Para 139][Para 141][Para 145][Para 147]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a benefit exceeding what the Labour Law, a collective agreement, internal rules, the contract or established practice already provides is an interest dispute, and a panel will decline to decide it where the claimant union does not hold most-representative status: under Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099 an award on an interest dispute operates as a collective agreement binding every worker in the enterprise, so deciding it on a non-representative union's application would impose terms on workers who were not represented. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 105][Para 109] A demand already brought by the same parties and decided to finality by the Council is barred by res judicata, provided the parties, the issue and a complete decision on the merits all coincide. [Para 133][Para 136][Para 146]

**Obiter dicta:** the observations that a claim concerning a nursery touches general working conditions so that every worker in the enterprise could use it, and that registration under Article 11 of the Law on Trade Unions is what confers a union's rights, are not necessary to the dismissals. [Para 75][Para 138]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award records eight demands but conciliation resolved four without numbering them, so only the four surviving demands can be matched to the panel's numbering at [Para 40]–[Para 51]; the settled four are undescribed.
- The union president's name is printed four ways — "យឹម ចាន់" [Para 1], "យីម ចាន់" [Para 36], "ឃឹម ចាន់" [Para 82] and "ឃីម ចាន់" [Para 83] — and the employer-party arbitrator appears as "សេង វូច ហ៊ុន" [Para 1][Para 153]. Names are redacted here rather than guessed.
- The 2,000 riel lunch figure is printed "២០ រៀល" in the issue statement at [Para 122] and correctly as "២០០០ រៀល" at [Para 47] and [Para 123].
- [Para 66] is badly corrupted, running two different propositions together ("...មាន តួនាទី បញ្ជូន សំណុំរឿង ... លោក នឹង សុខា ..."), so the panel's statement of the Prakas No. 362 delegation power is unreliable as printed; the cleaner statement is at [Para 67].
- The final order is garbled and partly duplicated: [Para 150] omits the figure "២០០០" and [Para 151] repeats the nursery and milk-money claim twice with variant wording ("ប្រាក់ ថ្ងៃ ទឹកដោះគោ", "របាក់").
- Citation lists at [Para 107], [Para 133] and [Para 135] preserve mangled case numbers ("លេខ ៤៥០០៧-វិ", "លេខ ១៤០៨ ឃ្លីក ស៊ូ", "លេខ ៩៥០៩-ពាក ហ្វាត") that cannot be resolved from the text; the workforce figure is 430 at [Para 35] against 173 union members at [Para 37].
