# Award No. 013/17 — Sayam International Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 24 March 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mar Samborana (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Pen Bunhea (president) · employer absent from the hearing · non-binding award · Doc ID 138906

## 1. Facts
The company sells beverages in Khan Chamkarmon, Phnom Penh, and employed about 45 workers; it had no local union and no most-representative union. [Para 1][Para 53][Para 54][Para 55]
Fifteen workers filed a collective claim on 25 January 2017 and transferred their rights to four recognised representatives. [Para 56][Para 58][Para 124]
The workers said the company had paid a 13th-month payment every year since they joined, delivered in an envelope at the New Year party, and stopped in 2016; the company said the payment was a profit-share incentive given only when it made a profit. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 63][Para 66][Para 67]
Ten of the workers had been dismissed in December 2016 and January 2017; the company did not appear at the hearing despite proper invitation. [Para 92][Para 98][Para 105][Para 106]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the 13th-month salary for 2016 that it did not pay? [Para 59][Para 126]
2. Whether the employer must apply annual leave and paid public holidays under the Labour Law and pay the annual leave and holiday days it failed to grant? [Para 71][Para 151]
3. Whether the employer must pay statutory severance to the ten workers it dismissed without cause — payment in lieu of notice, dismissal indemnity, moral damages, unused annual leave, holiday pay, final wages for one worker — and pay the incentive bonus owed to two sales workers? [Para 84][Para 203][Para 219]

Issues 3 and 4 of the original claim were merged by the workers' letter of 10 March 2017, so the panel decided three issues. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 29]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The company paid a 13th-month sum equal to a month's wage every year, one worker receiving as much as 400% of monthly wage in 2010; it provided only 15 paid public holidays and never told them of the 18-day annual leave entitlement, so leave was never used; and the ten dismissed workers held indefinite-term contracts, were given no reason and no written notice. [Para 61][Para 64][Para 65][Para 72][Para 76][Para 85][Para 86]
**Employer (respondent):** The 13th-month sum is not a wage but an incentive paid out of profit, and 2016 brought no profit, so nothing was payable. [Para 3][Para 4][Para 66][Para 67]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issue 1 dismissed; Issue 2 granted (remaining annual leave carried into the next year for five serving workers, public holidays applied per ministerial Prakas, and holiday wages repaid for 2014, 2015 and 2016); Issue 3 granted for the ten dismissed workers (payment in lieu of notice, dismissal indemnity, moral damages, unused annual leave, holiday pay, and final wages for Mr S alone), with the incentive-bonus claim dismissed. Monthly wages are printed per worker, from 180 USD to 1,330 USD. [Para 92][Para 298][Para 302][Para 308]

On Issue 1 the panel first held, under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099, that it may proceed when a properly invited party fails to appear, following Awards No. 53/04, 63/04, 148/07 and others. It found the 13th-month payment to be a share of profit (ប្រាក់ភាគកម្មនៃប្រាក់ចំណេញ), applying Article 2 of the Labour Law and earlier awards holding that an employer may set the conditions of a profit share provided they are lawful and reasonable. No law, regulation or agreement obliged payment in a loss-making year, so the practice did not breach Article 2. [Para 99][Para 101][Para 130][Para 135][Para 136][Para 140]

Had the company in fact made a profit, the claim might have succeeded, but the panel could not establish profit or loss. The workers bore the burden of proof, yet the profit evidence lay only with the employer, which had abandoned its right to respond by absenting itself. Weighing that against the finding that the workforce had fallen from 45 to about 10, the panel declined to presume either profit or loss and dismissed the claim for want of clear facts. [Para 141][Para 144][Para 145][Para 147][Para 149][Para 150][Para 151]

On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 166 (18 days of paid annual leave after a year's service, rising by one day per three years) and Article 167(4): unused leave may be carried forward, but not for more than three consecutive years and only for the portion above twelve working days. It ordered the remaining leave carried into the next year for the five workers still employed. On public holidays it applied Articles 161 and 164 and Article 4 of Prakas No. 10 of 4 February 1999: a worker who works on a paid public holiday earns a further day's wage, i.e. 200%. Since only 15 holidays were granted and no holiday wage was paid, it ordered compliance with the ministerial Prakas and repayment for 2014, 2015 and 2016, the three-year limit under Article 120. [Para 151][Para 156][Para 175][Para 180][Para 184][Para 194][Para 200][Para 202]

On Issue 3 the panel applied Article 67(1): a fixed-term contract must state a precise end date, and a contract that does not is an indefinite-term contract. The ten workers had signed once, with no end date, so all held indefinite-term contracts. Following Award No. 047/14 Trou Tham Phnom Penh, it applied Article 74: dismissal without a reasonable ground relating to the worker's aptitude or conduct, or to the needs of the enterprise, is unlawful. Because the employer never stated any ground and never gave written notice, it awarded Article 75 and 77 payment in lieu of notice, Article 89 dismissal indemnity at 15 days per year, Article 91 moral damages, Article 167(2) and (4) unused annual leave, and Article 116 final wages to Mr S. [Para 208][Para 216][Para 217][Para 221][Para 223][Para 237][Para 246][Para 258][Para 266][Para 279]

The incentive-bonus claim failed: the two sales workers' supporting documents were in English, untranslated, and so inadmissible under Article 23 of Prakas No. 099 and Award No. 148/13; their own signed statements, without a witness, could not found the claim. [Para 282][Para 283][Para 286][Para 288][Para 289][Para 290]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A recurring 13th-month payment that the employer treats as a share of profit creates no obligation in a year when no profit is shown; the worker who claims it bears the burden of proving profit, and where the profit evidence lies solely with an absent employer and is unclear either way, the claim fails rather than being presumed. Separately, a contract signed once with no stated end date is an indefinite-term contract, and dismissal under it without any stated ground or written notice attracts the full statutory severance set. [Para 144][Para 150][Para 216][Para 223]

**Obiter dicta:** A worker who works on a paid public holiday is entitled to a second day's wage — 200% in all — and not to time off in lieu. Where a party bears the burden of proof but the evidence lies wholly with its opponent, which has absented itself, the panel will not fill the gap by presumption in either direction. [Para 145][Para 184][Para 185]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company name appears variously as Sayam, Sayang and Saya International. The workers' letter is dated "ថ្ងៃទី ១០ ខែមីនា ឆ្នាំ ២០១៩ ឆ្នាំ ២០១៧" at Para 27, printing two years. The wage table at Para 92 is badly corrupted by OCR: rows are scrambled, the monthly wage for Preung Samith and Khun Va is missing, and workers' names attach to the wrong columns. The header lists nine worker-side attendees (Para 1 and Para 2) while the panel records nine workers present and four accredited representatives (Para 57 and Para 121). The award is dated 24 March 2017 at Para 1 and Para 32.
