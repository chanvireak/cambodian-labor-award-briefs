# Award No. 043/17 — Pou Yien (Cambodia) Enterprise (Arbitration Council, 26 December 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chhea presiding; Liv Sovannara, worker nominee; Ing Sothy, employer nominee, dissenting) · Doc ID 138947

## 1. Facts
Pou Yien (Cambodia) Enterprise makes shoes at Slaeng Roloeung, Tuek Thla, Sen Sok, Phnom Penh, and told the panel it employed about 2,002 workers; the workers' own list named 1,000 claimants. [Para 1][Para 130][Para 132] Since its registration the company had used fixed-duration contracts of three months, six months and one year, paying 5% severance at each expiry, and from 11 and 14 October 2017 it notified every worker that renewals would be for three months only, ending the contract of anyone who refused. [Para 137][Para 138][Para 139][Para 140] One conciliation issue remained unsettled: conversion of long-serving workers to undetermined-duration contracts and the terms offered to shorter-serving workers. [Para 12][Para 136]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must convert the fixed-duration contracts of workers whose total service exceeds two years into undetermined-duration contracts? [Para 3][Para 136][Para 176]
2. Whether the employer must issue six-month or one-year fixed-duration contracts to workers with less than two years' service, rather than three-month contracts? [Para 4][Para 136][Para 204]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Articles 67(2), 67(8) and 73(5) of the Labour Law forbid fixed-duration contracts, or renewals, exceeding two years in total, so long-serving workers are already on undetermined-duration contracts and must be treated as such; the 5% payment at each expiry cannot cut the relationship, because work never stopped and no notice of termination was ever given at the earlier expiries; the switch to three-month contracts strips benefits and exposes everyone to non-renewal. [Para 141][Para 142][Para 144][Para 148][Para 149]
**Employer (respondent):** it terminated each fixed-duration contract at its expiry and paid the 5% severance, which is all Article 73(6) requires, and it renewed on terms the workers accepted; because orders have fallen it can now offer only three-month contracts. [Para 145][Para 146][Para 147]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to convert to undetermined duration the contracts of workers whose total service exceeds two years (issue 1); the claim that it must issue six-month or one-year contracts to workers under two years was dismissed (issue 2). [Para 235][Para 236]

*Representation.* The panel found no most-representative union at the company and recognised the three workers certified by the Department of Labour Disputes under Prakas 362. [Para 160][Para 170][Para 175]

*Issue 1 (conversion).* Article 67(2) caps a fixed-duration contract and its renewals at two years in total, and Article 73(5) converts to undetermined duration any contract renewed beyond that cap; the panel followed Jacqsintex (10/03) and Manhattan (70/10), reading the cap as applying to cumulative duration rather than to each renewal. [Para 176][Para 182][Para 186] Article 73(6) severance is owed when the contract is terminated and the employment relationship cut off; here the workers kept signing new contracts and kept working in the same factory, so the relationship was never severed and the 5% payments did not prevent conversion. [Para 192][Para 194][Para 200][Para 201] The panel also relied on ILO Recommendation 166 as context for reading Article 67. [Para 187][Para 188]

*Issue 2 (contract terms).* Article 65 leaves the form of the contract to the parties' agreement, and Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code require a matching offer and acceptance; neither party may impose a contract type or term on the other. [Para 204][Para 211][Para 220] The workers' request for six-month or one-year terms was consistent with the law, but so was the employer's refusal, and the workers could no more compel a one-year contract than the employer could compel a three-month one. [Para 230][Para 231][Para 232][Para 233] The claim was therefore dismissed, the existing three-month practice being left in place. [Para 234]

*Dissent.* The employer-nominated arbitrator recorded a dissent, reasoning that the employer alone chooses the contract type, that payment of 5% at every expiry keeps each contract fixed-duration, and that conversion would oblige the employer to pay severance twice over on the same period of service. [Para 240][Para 241][Para 242][Para 256][Para 257]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-duration contract renewed without interruption so that total service passes two years becomes an undetermined-duration contract under Articles 67(2) and 73(5), and the payment of 5% severance at each expiry does not prevent that conversion, because severance cannot sever a relationship that in fact continued. [Para 186][Para 194][Para 200][Para 202] The length and type of a renewed contract is a matter of agreement between the parties; neither the workers nor the employer may force the other to accept a duration it has not accepted, so a demand for six-month or one-year terms cannot be granted against the employer's refusal. [Para 220][Para 232][Para 233]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that short fixed-duration contracts expose workers to loss of benefits and to the risk that the employer simply will not renew, which is why the law leans toward undetermined duration. [Para 144][Para 185]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is dated 26 December 2017 on its face, yet the parties are recorded as agreeing to postpone issue from 13 to 28 December 2017. [Para 1][Para 21][Para 258] The invitation letters are dated for a hearing on 23 November 2017 while the award records the hearing as held on 30 November 2017. [Para 10][Para 127][Para 128] The employer-nominated arbitrator appears as "Ing Sothy" on the face, "Ing Sothy" in the signature block and "Aing Sothy" in the annex heading. [Para 1][Para 238][Para 240] The company's registration number is given as MOC-10574494 and as 200014263 for the same certificate. [Para 23][Para 129] Worker counts conflict across the award: 2,002 employees, 1,000 claimants, and lists of 27, 117 and 996. [Para 130][Para 132][Para 151] The reasoning at paras 229–231 is corrupted by OCR, losing the employer's stated position, and the order at para 236 repeats itself. [Para 229][Para 231][Para 236] The dissent is annexed without page numbers and its arithmetic examples are incomplete. [Para 253][Para 254] Individual workers, union officers and employer representatives are named in the source and are redacted here.
