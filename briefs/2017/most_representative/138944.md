# Award No. 038/17 — Park Shuny Thing Fake Thiring Limited (Arbitration Council, 14 December 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chhea presiding; Tuon Si Phan, worker nominee; Yu Soun Ty, employer nominee, dissenting on issues 1 and 5) · Doc ID 138944

## 1. Facts
Park Shuny Thing Fake Thiring Limited ("Park Shun") is a knitting and garment factory at Prek Ta Long, Chak Angre Krom, Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, registered in 1999. [Para 1][Para 87] Workers on short fixed-duration contracts, renewed annually after a signed termination agreement and a 5% severance payment each year, were let go section by section from June 2017 for want of orders; about 423 workers were affected and only some 115 remained in the automatic-machinery section. [Para 107][Para 277][Para 350] The dispute covered ten claims, of which the workers withdrew four (issues 3, 7, 8 and 9) at the hearing; conciliation had failed on 10 October 2017 and a strike was suspended by Council order 003/17. [Para 21][Para 26][Para 27]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back union activists and members and pay their wages and benefits from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 2][Para 96]
2. Whether the employer must allow workers to take their annual leave in accordance with the law? [Para 3][Para 176]
3. Whether the employer must preserve the attendance bonus and other benefits where workers clock in up to five minutes late? (withdrawn) [Para 4][Para 26]
4. Whether the employer must pay a 4,000-riel daily lunch allowance and a 3,000-riel meal allowance for overtime worked between 5 and 7 pm? [Para 5][Para 185]
5. Whether the employer must stop using short fixed-duration contracts and convert contracts totalling more than two years into undetermined duration? [Para 6][Para 191]
6. Whether the employer must provide a room and materials for the union? [Para 7][Para 207]
7. Whether the employer must pay the overtime meal allowance on the 25th of each month, separately from monthly wages? (withdrawn) [Para 8][Para 26]
8. Whether the employer must pay a US$20 monthly milk allowance for children aged 18 months to three years? (withdrawn) [Para 9][Para 26]
9. Whether the employer must pay the seniority bonus and reimburse it where applied incorrectly? (withdrawn) [Para 10][Para 26]
10. Whether the employer must pay 50% of wages to top up to 100%, with attendance, travel, accommodation and seniority bonuses, during periods when contracts were suspended without the Labour Inspector's authorisation? [Para 11][Para 211]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** contracts renewed without any break for more than two years became undetermined duration under Article 67, and the annual 5% payment did not interrupt service; the closure affected 80 to 90 percent of the workforce, including delegates, union leaders and pregnant women, as a collective dismissal carried out with no notice to the delegates or the Labour Inspector; the five-minute-late deduction, the absence of lunch allowance and of a union room were breaches of the law and of the 19 May 2016 conciliation agreement. [Para 104][Para 111][Para 113][Para 120][Para 186][Para 207][Para 288]
**Employer (respondent):** every worker was on a fixed-duration contract that expired by its own terms, each renewal preceded by notice, a 5% severance payment and the worker's signed agreement, so no conversion occurred; the company had no orders and no work, keeping only 115 staff on machines; it did not discriminate against union members, and it paid 50% wages during lay-offs as a practice more generous than the law, which the workers accepted orally. [Para 115][Para 117][Para 119][Para 218][Para 220][Para 277]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement was refused for every claimant, but the employer must pay the 146 workers whose contracts had become undetermined duration (and the six delegates, two pregnant women and seven union activists with over two years' service) notice compensation under Articles 75 and 77, dismissal damages under Article 89, moral damages equal to two months' wages, and any unpaid final wages; it must also pay 50% of wages to bring suspended workers to 100% for the unlawful suspension periods. Claims on annual leave, meal allowances, the union room and contract conversion were dismissed. [Para 410][Para 411][Para 460][Para 462][Para 544][Para 560]

*Jurisdiction and representation.* Applying the three conditions of Article 302, the panel held the dispute collective, since 320 workers faced a common act and a strike had already occurred, and it recognised the three representatives certified by the Department of Labour Disputes under Prakas 362, no most-representative union existing. [Para 231][Para 242][Para 253][Para 258][Para 259] Under Article 312 and Prakas 099 it could decide only the ten conciliation items and their direct consequences: the six delegates dismissed after the 10 October minutes fell inside its jurisdiction, the eight dismissed earlier did not. [Para 416][Para 427][Para 431][Para 433]

*Contract type (issues 1 and 5).* Articles 67(2) and 73(5) convert a fixed-duration contract to undetermined duration once renewals bring total service past two years, as the panel had held in Jacqsintex (10/03); the 5% payment and signed termination each year did not sever the relationship, because work continued without a break. [Para 276][Para 305][Para 307][Para 308][Para 335] A fixed-duration contract, by contrast, expires by its own terms and neither party can force renewal absent agreement under Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code, so workers under two years had no claim. [Para 315][Para 318][Para 328][Para 330]

*Collective dismissal and compensation.* Termination of 146 workers across sections for lack of orders was a collective dismissal by reduction of activity under Article 95, and the employer gave no notice to the delegates or the Inspector. [Para 352][Para 355] Because the notice given fell short of Article 75, the workers were owed compensation equal to the wages and benefits of the unserved notice period under Article 77, plus Article 89 dismissal damages and final wages under Article 116. [Para 365][Para 368][Para 378][Para 409] Following its award in Organisation for Cambodian Women in Crisis (21/10), the panel added two months' wages as moral damages for the Article 95 breach, since the omission deprived workers of the Minister's intervention. [Para 403][Para 404][Para 406]

*Suspension (issue 10).* Article 71(11) allows suspension only under the Inspector's supervision and for no more than two months, and Article 72 relieves the employer of wages only where the suspension was lawful; no authorisation existed here, so 100% wages were owed, the 50% already paid being set off. [Para 521][Para 528][Para 541][Para 544]

*Claims dismissed.* Issues 2, 4 and 6 were not considered on the merits because every claimant had already left the company; issue 5 was already resolved within issue 1. [Para 512][Para 513][Para 515][Para 519]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive fixed-duration contracts renewed without any break, so that total service passes two years, become a contract of undetermined duration under Article 67(2) even where the employer pays 5% severance and obtains a signed termination agreement at each expiry; ending such contracts in a staff reduction without the Article 95 procedure is a dismissal, entitling each worker to notice compensation, Article 89 damages and two months' wages as moral damages. [Para 305][Para 308][Para 352][Para 404] Suspending contracts without the Labour Inspector's prior authorisation is unlawful, and Article 72's release from wage obligations does not apply, so full wages are owed for the suspension period. [Para 528][Para 541]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel doubted it could order reinstatement where the employer has no work left and preferred compensation as the remedy; the withdrawn lateness claim was not decided. [Para 454][Para 456]
