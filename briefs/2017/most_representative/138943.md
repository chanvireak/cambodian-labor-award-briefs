# Award No. 040/17 — Khol Cherl Village Resort (Phumiv Pheap Thmey) (Arbitration Council, 26 December 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chhea presiding; Seng Vouch Hun, employer nominee; An Non, worker nominee) · Doc ID 138943

## 1. Facts
The employer is Khol Cherl Village Resort (Phumiv Pheap Thmey), a cultural and recreation centre at Svay Dangkum, Siem Reap, which its enterprise-opening declaration of 2 December 2016 records as employing 397 workers, 204 of them women. [Para 1][Para 60] The Tourism Employees' Union of the company, registered under No. 3512 K.B/VK, brought five claims through the Siem Reap Provincial Department of Labour and Vocational Training; conciliation on 27 October 2017 failed on all five, and the dispute reached the Council on 16 November 2017. [Para 15][Para 19][Para 61] At the hearing the parties settled issues 1, 2 and 3 by agreement, leaving only issues 4 and 5, and the panel records that no most-representative union exists at the company. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 64][Para 103]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay the outstanding public-holiday pay owed to all staff? [Para 3]
2. Whether the company must provide a meal allowance to staff taking maternity leave? [Para 5]
3. Whether the company must pay overtime and night-shift premiums and set working hours in accordance with the Labour Law? [Para 7]
4. Whether the employer must stop deducting the meal allowance when workers take their annual leave? [Para 9][Para 65]
5. Whether the employer must pay the annual bonus to workers in every section of the company? [Para 11][Para 84]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the US$25 monthly meal allowance is wage, because the social-security registration form requires the basic wage plus allowances as the contribution base, so the employer may not cut it; wages are so low that the deduction is intolerable, and the director promised at the 15 January 2016 meeting that no deduction would be made. [Para 68][Para 71][Para 72][Para 81] On the bonus, the company is itself a "project/site" (komrong-kandael), as its own request and opinion-survey forms show, so HR Decision 331/15 entitles every section — art, security, massage, restaurant, garden, sanitation, market and sculpture — to the annual bonus. [Para 86][Para 91][Para 92]
**Employer (respondent):** the allowance is an incentive for attendance, not wages, and is not paid to workers who are absent or on annual leave; the US$1-per-day deduction is the very rule laid down in HR Decision 213/15, and the NSSF base is reduced to US$24 for a worker absent one day, which the workers did not contest. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 76][Para 77][Para 78] The company is not a construction project site — it has only maintenance staff — and the forms bearing the words "project/site" are used for requests, not in administration; it also makes a loss every month, so it cannot pay bonuses. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 93]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both remaining claims were rejected: the employer is not ordered to stop deducting the meal allowance during annual leave, and is not ordered to pay an annual bonus to all sections. The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification if no party objects to the Minister. [Para 142][Para 143][Para 144]

*Representation.* Because no most-representative union exists at the enterprise, the panel applied Article 54(1) of the 2016 Trade Union Law and its earlier awards, and held lawful the representation of the workers by the four persons certified by the Provincial Department of Labour and Vocational Training under Prakas 362. [Para 101][Para 105][Para 107][Para 111][Para 113][Para 115]

*Issue 4 (meal allowance).* Article 2 of the Labour Law gives the employer the right to manage the enterprise where it acts lawfully and reasonably, and the panel followed its earlier awards holding that an employer may attach conditions to allowances it grants beyond the law. [Para 117][Para 118][Para 119][Para 120] The allowance was found not to be wage but a benefit above the Labour Law, given to encourage attendance rather than for workers who are absent; the deduction of US$1 per day of absence therefore fell within HR Decision 213/15 and was lawful and reasonable, and no duty to stop it arose. [Para 125][Para 126][Para 127][Para 128] The panel noted the workers never contested the employer's account of how the NSSF base is calculated per individual. [Para 78][Para 90]

*Issue 5 (annual bonus).* HR Decision 331/15 grants the bonus to staff "at every project/site" who have served one year or more, and the panel accepted that the employer was bound by that decision for genuine project sites. [Para 94][Para 130][Para 131] But the request and opinion-survey forms that carry the words "project/site" are used only when workers make a request, not in administration — a point the workers did not dispute — and such forms cannot establish that a resort is a construction site. [Para 133][Para 135][Para 136] The company's sections do no construction work at all, so workers there have no right to the bonus under HR Decision 331/15. [Para 138][Para 140][Para 141]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A meal allowance that the employer grants above the statutory minimum, and that is calculated per day of attendance, is not wage: the employer may withhold or reduce it for days of absence, including paid annual leave, provided the rule is set out in a written decision and applied consistently. [Para 125][Para 126][Para 127][Para 128] A bonus scheme confined by its own terms to employees of "projects/sites" does not reach service sections of an establishment that does no construction work, and the appearance of the phrase "project/site" on internal request forms does not prove the establishment is one. [Para 136][Para 138][Para 140]

**Obiter dicta:** an employer may unilaterally establish bonus and allowance schemes beyond those the Labour Law requires, in order to reward productivity; where there is no most-representative union, workers may be represented by persons recognised for that purpose by the Provincial Department of Labour. [Para 111][Para 119]
