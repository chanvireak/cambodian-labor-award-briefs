# Award No. 042/17 — Khen Sbot Su Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 27 December 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Aing Eng Thong presiding; Seng Vouch Hun, employer nominee; An Non, worker nominee) · Doc ID 138945

## 1. Facts
Khen Sbot Su Co., Ltd. manufactures shoes at Svaay Po, Sethey commune, Samaki Mean Chey district, Kampong Chhnang, and told the panel it then employed about 7,400 workers. [Para 1][Para 54][Para 55] Since 2010 it had paid a "function allowance" of US$2, US$4 or US$6 a month according to the post held, from six months' service, though no written agreement recorded the scheme. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63][Para 64] In about the last three or four months before the dispute it cut that allowance by roughly 50% for part of the workforce — US$6 becoming US$4 and US$4 becoming US$2 — without informing or consulting the workers, their representatives or the union, while the workers kept the same posts. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67][Para 68] Four claims went to conciliation on 16 November 2017; one was settled and three were referred to the Council. [Para 11][Para 12][Para 21]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must keep the function allowance unchanged when a worker is moved to another post? [Para 2][Para 59]
2. Whether the employer must pay for transport taking a sick worker from the factory to hospital, even where the illness is neither a work accident nor an emergency? [Para 3][Para 86]
3. Whether the employer must stop requiring one worker to operate two or three machines at the same time? [Para 4][Para 107]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the allowance had been paid at US$2, US$4 or US$6 a month since 2010 and workers arranged their lives on it; point 2.1.4 of the internal regulations forbids arbitrary deductions from wages and allowances, so the 50% cut, made with no notice and no discussion with the union, was unlawful; the employer owes a duty of care to workers who fall ill during working hours, since a 30,000-riel ambulance trip is a small cost to the company, and two workers running three machines is overwork that has driven colleagues to resign. [Para 69][Para 81][Para 84][Para 91][Para 92][Para 111][Para 113]
**Employer (respondent):** it studied the difficulty of each post and revised its management policy, which it is entitled to do because the allowance is a benefit above the law and shoe models differ by season; it pays NSSF contributions, keeps a doctor and three nurses on site and already pays transport for work accidents and emergencies, so it owes nothing for ordinary illness; the extra machines merely reorganise the production line and give workers more skill, and it wishes to reduce headcount. [Para 74][Para 75][Para 76][Para 88][Para 89][Para 96][Para 115][Para 117][Para 119]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must keep the function allowance unchanged while the worker performs the same post or role (issue 1) and must again allow one worker to use one machine as before (issue 3); the claim that it pay hospital transport for ordinary illness (issue 2) was dismissed without consideration. [Para 228][Para 229][Para 230]

*Preliminary.* The panel found no most-representative union at the company and recognised the three workers certified by the Provincial Department of Labour and Vocational Training under Prakas 362 as the workers' representatives. [Para 58][Para 136][Para 147][Para 149]

*Issue 1 (function allowance).* Article 2 of the Labour Law gives the employer the right to manage and organise the enterprise only where it acts lawfully and reasonably. [Para 156][Para 159] The internal regulations registered as No. 511 K.B/K.Ch of 10 June 2016 provide at point 2.1.4 that no deduction may be made from workers' wages and allowances at the employer's whim; the panel read that as barring a unilateral decision affecting wages or allowances. [Para 161][Para 163] Workers had a settled expectation of the allowance each month after six months' service, calculated by post, and the employer neither gave notice nor consulted. [Para 162] The 50% reduction was therefore an unlawful and unreasonable exercise of managerial power rather than a valid policy change, and the employer was ordered to maintain the allowance wherever the post is unchanged. [Para 164][Para 165][Para 167]

*Issue 2 (hospital transport).* The panel found no provision of the Labour Law, no collective agreement, no agreement between the parties, no internal rule and no past practice obliging the employer to pay for transporting a worker who falls ill (as distinct from a work accident or an emergency) to hospital; the claim was one for a benefit superior to the law. [Para 171][Para 172] That made it an interest dispute (vivieth ampi phal prayoch), and under Article 96(2) of the Labour Law and Articles 54 and 72 of the 2016 Trade Union Law read with Prakas 43 only a most-representative union, or unions with the right to conclude a collective agreement, may bring an interest dispute; the claimant unions had neither status, so the panel declined to consider the claim. [Para 174][Para 185][Para 189][Para 192][Para 193]

*Issue 3 (machines).* Under Article 2 and Articles 65 and 311 the parties are bound by the working conditions agreed when the contract was made. [Para 200][Para 201][Para 203] Since 2010 the employer had assigned one machine per worker; adding machines without extra pay or benefits, and while cutting the allowance, imposed work beyond what was agreed and beyond the worker's contractual duty, and could only be done with the workers' consent. [Para 217][Para 218][Para 220][Para 223][Para 224] The arrangement was therefore neither lawful nor reasonable, and the employer was ordered to restore one machine per worker. [Para 226][Para 227]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that has consistently paid a post-based allowance above the statutory minimum may not cut it unilaterally where its registered internal regulations forbid arbitrary deductions from wages and allowances and the workers' duties are unchanged; such a cut is neither lawful nor reasonable under Article 2 and the allowance must be maintained for as long as the post is. [Para 159][Para 161][Para 164][Para 167] Requiring a worker to operate more machines than were agreed at hiring is a unilateral change of working conditions that needs the worker's consent and is unlawful without it. [Para 218][Para 224][Para 226] An interest dispute may be brought only by a most-representative union, or by unions entitled to conclude a collective agreement; otherwise the panel will not consider it. [Para 185][Para 189][Para 192]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that a 30,000-riel ambulance trip is a small cost to a company of this size and that the employer should take care of its workers, while holding that no legal duty to pay it exists. [Para 92][Para 93][Para 94]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The internal-regulations point is printed as "2.1.4" in the reasoning but garbled at paras 69 and 85, and the article quoted at paras 156–158 is truncated mid-sentence. [Para 69][Para 85][Para 158] The claim is recorded as a flat US$6 allowance at para 2 but as US$2, US$4 or US$6 by post at paras 61–64. [Para 2][Para 61] Issue 3 is dated "since 2010 … in the year 2023" in one paragraph, a year after the award. [Para 195] A meeting minute relied on is dated 9 August 2018, later than the award, and Prakas 099 is dated "21 April 20 2024". [Para 40][Para 166] The hearing time is printed "08 and 20 30" and the signature block loses the arbitrator's name to OCR noise. [Para 18][Para 232] Individual workers and union officers are named in the source and are redacted here.
