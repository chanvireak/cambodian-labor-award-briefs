# Award No. 044/17 — Goy Vanna Tran Sport & Press Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 21 December 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Uk Ri (appointed by the employer), Sin Kim Sean (appointed by the workers), Pen Bun Chhea (president) · Doc ID 138950

## 1. Facts
The respondent is a passenger transport company employing about 60 workers, of whom 21 signed the claim. On 28 September 2017 the workers notified the employer of candidates for a union leadership election held on 29 September 2017; the employer refused to accept the notice and the workers served it by post. Four candidates were dismissed on 10 and 12 October 2017, and two further drivers on 26 September and 11 October 2017. Conciliation on nine points failed on 24 November 2017. The employer did not attend the hearing of 12 December 2017 and gave no reason. [Para 16][Para 100][Para 298][Para 92][Para 24]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must grant annual leave and public-holiday leave as required by the Labour Law and pay arrears of annual leave and public-holiday leave not previously granted? [Para 2][Para 161]
2. Whether the employer must pay National Social Security Fund (ប.ស) contributions for all the workers? [Para 3][Para 203]
3. Whether the employer must pay wages on a regular basis and pay overtime? [Para 4][Para 218]
4. Whether the employer must reinstate six dismissed workers in their original posts and pay them wages from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 5][Para 265]
5. Whether the employer must stop deducting fuel costs from drivers on routes fixed by the company? [Para 6][Para 338]
6. Whether the employer must stop taking deposits from workers and return deposits already taken? [Para 9][Para 356]
7. Whether the employer must pay 130% of the day wage for night work and overtime beyond eight hours? [Para 10][Para 370]

Issue 7 was withdrawn and issues 4 and 6 consolidated, leaving seven matters decided under the panel's own numbering. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 28]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** They had never been allowed annual leave or public-holiday rest, including Khmer New Year and Pchum Ben, and were paid nothing extra for working those days; the employer had unilaterally moved payday from the 1st–2nd to the 10th and then to between the 15th and 20th; drivers worked 13-hour days including night runs to Poipet on the day rate; and the six dismissed workers were union election candidates dismissed without the labour inspector's authorisation or prior warning. [Para 65][Para 78][Para 81][Para 110][Para 257]
**Employer (respondent):** It attended neither the hearing nor the pre-hearing stage and filed no documents, answering none of the claims; under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 the panel proceeded in its absence and treated it as having waived its right of reply. [Para 24][Para 32][Para 137][Para 170]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted. The panel ordered the employer to apply annual leave and pay in lieu of leave not granted; to apply public-holiday leave under the Ministry's Prakas and pay arrears of holiday-work wages for the last three years, 2014, 2015 and 2016; to pay wages on the 10th of each month; to fix working time at eight hours a day with voluntary overtime at 50% and 100% uplifts; to reinstate all six dismissed workers in their original posts with wages from dismissal to reinstatement; to stop deducting fuel costs; to return the deposits taken; and to pay 130% of the day wage for night work. The NSSF claim was dismissed for want of jurisdiction. [Para 390][Para 392][Para 394][Para 396][Para 397][Para 398][Para 399][Para 400][Para 393]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 166§1 and §4 — 1.5 days of paid annual leave per month worked, plus one day per three years of service, giving 18 days after a year — and Articles 161 and 164 with Article 4 of Prakas No. 10 (4 February 1999), under which work on a public holiday earns a further day's wage on top of holiday pay, i.e. 200% in total. The employer having not answered, the panel accepted the workers' account. [Para 161][Para 166][Para 180][Para 183][Para 193]

On issue 2 the panel held it had no jurisdiction: by Article 31 of the Law on Social Security (2002) and Articles 1 and 2 of Prakas No. 177 (18 August 2010), disputes over NSSF contributions lie with the NSSF dispute-resolution committee and, failing that, the courts. [Para 206][Para 212][Para 216]

On issue 3 the panel applied Article 116§2, requiring at least one payment a month, and its earlier awards holding that the employer may fix the payday so long as the law is respected; the move to the 10th and then to between the 15th and 20th was a unilateral variation with no adequate reason. On hours it applied Article 137 (eight hours a day, 48 a week) and Article 139 new of the 2007 amendment, granting 50% for ordinary overtime and 100% for night or weekly-rest-day overtime. [Para 223][Para 233][Para 243][Para 245][Para 262]

On issues 4 and 6 the panel applied Article 67§7, under which a fixed-duration contract not reduced to writing becomes undetermined in duration, and so treated all six dismissed workers as permanent. It applied Article 43§1 and Article 67 of the Law on Trade Unions (17 May 2016), protecting worker delegates and election candidates for 45 days before and after the ballot and requiring the labour inspector's prior agreement to dismiss; the four dismissed candidates met all three conditions — candidacy, the protected window, and notice to employer and Ministry — and no authorisation had been sought, so those dismissals were unlawful. For the two drivers who were not candidates it applied Article 74, permitting dismissal only for a reason relating to capacity or conduct, and found the stated grounds unproved and unaccompanied by any prior warning; those dismissals likewise lacked valid reason. Both groups were reinstated. [Para 266][Para 277][Para 289][Para 308][Para 313][Para 317][Para 319][Para 333][Para 337]

On issue 5 the panel applied Article 28, prohibiting disciplinary fines, and held that deducting wages where a driver exceeds the fixed fuel quota is a pecuniary penalty that must stop; since the workers asked only for full tanks rather than cash for surplus fuel, the employer need not keep paying a bonus for fuel saved. On issue 8 it applied Article 44, forbidding any cash bond as a condition of employment, and ordered the USD 300 deposits returned. On issue 9 it applied Article 144 new of the 2007 amendment, under which night work — at least 11 consecutive hours including 22:00 to 05:00 — attracts 130% of the day wage, and found the obligation undischarged. [Para 343][Para 349][Para 353][Para 356][Para 369][Para 372][Para 374][Para 387]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker who is a candidate in a union leadership election is protected for 45 days before and after the ballot, and dismissal within that window without the labour inspector's prior agreement is unlawful whatever the stated ground, so the worker must be reinstated with wages from the date of dismissal. Where the employer fails to appear after proper notice, the panel proceeds in its absence. Deducting wages for excess fuel use is a prohibited pecuniary penalty, and a hiring deposit must be returned. [Para 277][Para 313][Para 137][Para 349][Para 367]

**Obiter dicta:** The remarks that the Article 144 new night-work premium is separate from and additional to the overtime uplift, and that an employer barred from penalising excess fuel use need not keep paying a bonus for fuel saved, were not necessary to the orders. [Para 379][Para 353]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is inconsistent on the number of claimants: 21 workers signed the claim [Para 64][Para 158], yet only four representatives are recognised [Para 62] and three attended [Para 63]. The employer's name is printed in at least six variants, including "Goy Vanna Tran Sport & Press", "Grov Vanna Tran Sport" and "Huoy Vanna". Figures are garbled: [Para 256] reads "over 8000 hours" for eight hours, [Para 338] inserts "100" into the fuel-quota sentence, and [Para 131] prints "2021 2004" for the Prakas date. [Para 334] uses a stray letter for a sub-paragraph marker. Individual workers and union officials are identified here by role only; the dismissed candidates' names are printed inconsistently at [Para 299] and [Para 310], with a given name and family name transposed between the two, and have not been reconciled.
