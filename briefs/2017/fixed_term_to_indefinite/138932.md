# Award No. 026/17 — C.I.K. Cambo Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 31 July 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; Sin Kim Sean, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chheam, president) · Doc ID 138932

## 1. Facts
C.I.K. Cambo Co., Ltd is a garment-cutting and sewing factory at Chom Chao, Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh, whose workforce had fallen from 393 to about 239 as orders declined and sewing lines were cut from eight to six [Para 70][Para 81][Para 91]. Three workers — Ms T, a sewing-group head, Ms S, a sewing-section head, and Ms K, a group head — stood as candidates in the election to found a union at the factory, notified to the employer by EMS on 24 April 2017 and held on 17 May 2017 [Para 112][Para 198][Para 205]. The employer ended their employment on 6 May, 17 May and 20 May 2017 respectively, without seeking the labour inspector's authorisation [Para 205][Para 230]. Of seven demands, five were settled in conciliation and two remained: reinstatement with back pay, and advance payment of maternity wages [Para 12][Para 19].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back Ms T, Ms S and Ms K — candidates in the union-founding election — and pay their wages from dismissal until reinstatement? [Para 156]
2. Whether the employer must pay 50% of wages, including allowances, for the 90 days before a woman starts maternity leave? [Para 236]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the three were candidates in the union election and so enjoyed the protection against dismissal in Article 67 of the Trade Union Law 2016; they should not have been dismissed at all [Para 76][Para 77]. The employer dismissed them without any prior authorisation from the labour inspector [Para 78]. On maternity pay, payment monthly in arrears forces women living in the provinces to travel to collect it, at cost and risk, when they need the money before the birth for medicine and delivery costs [Para 131][Para 132].
**Employer (respondent):** the contracts were not continued because orders fell, the workforce was cut from 393 to 239 and sewing lines from eight to six, and group and section heads had to be reduced too [Para 3][Para 79][Para 81][Para 82]. Ms T had herself applied to resign; Ms S had been told on 17 March 2017 that her contract would end on 17 May 2017; Ms K's contract was fixed-term and simply not renewed [Para 85][Para 86][Para 87]. The employer denies receiving any notice of candidacy [Para 128][Para 210].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to take back all three workers and pay their wages from the date of dismissal until the date of return, and to pay 50% of wages including allowances for 90 days before maternity leave begins; the award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent written objection to the Minister [Para 256][Para 257][Para 258].

On representation, the panel held that the three recognised delegates could act for the 126 claimant workers because 123 of them had signed a written transfer of rights and authority; Prakas No. 362 and Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 permit representation by any person holding written authority [Para 153][Para 154][Para 155].

Issue 1 turned on the contract type and on Article 67. For Ms K the panel found that her indefinite contract had been converted into successive three-month fixed-duration contracts after she was required to correct the name she had used at hiring; Notification No. 127 of the Ministry of Labour requires that a worker who regularises an irregular identity keeps seniority, position and contract, so the conversion was contrary to it and void under Article 13 of the Labour Law, leaving her on an indefinite contract [Para 109][Para 110][Para 175][Para 183][Para 184]. On the employer's claim that it had already paid her severance, Article 118 puts the burden of proving payment on the employer, no evidence was produced, and the claim was rejected [Para 165][Para 169][Para 171][Para 172]. The panel then applied Article 67: candidates in a union leadership election enjoy the same protection against dismissal as workers' delegates, for 45 days before and 45 days after the election, provided the union notifies the employer and copies the Ministry of Labour by any reliable means [Para 189][Para 190][Para 191]. It found all three conditions met — candidacy, dismissal inside the protected window (11 days before the poll, on the poll date, and three days after), and notice given by EMS, which counts as effective service even where the employer refuses delivery [Para 198][Para 205][Para 206][Para 217][Para 219][Para 221]. Dismissal of such a worker also requires the inspector's prior approval under Articles 43 and 67; the employer sought none and did not contest that, so the dismissals were unlawful [Para 227][Para 229][Para 230]. The panel ordered reinstatement with back pay under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 [Para 231][Para 235].

Issue 2 was also granted. Articles 182 and 183 give 90 days' maternity leave and half wages including allowances, but fix no payment date [Para 236][Para 238][Para 241][Para 242]. Article 103 lists maternity pay as wages, and Article 115(3) forbids paying wages on a rest day and requires payment a day early where the payday falls on a holiday [Para 243][Para 246][Para 248]. The panel accordingly held that maternity pay must be released at least one day before the leave begins; the employer's practice of paying monthly, in step with ordinary payroll, did not comply, and it was ordered to pay the 90 days in advance [Para 251][Para 253][Para 254][Para 255].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union election candidate who is dismissed inside the 45-day window around the poll, without the labour inspector's prior approval, must be reinstated with wages from dismissal to return; notice of candidacy sent by registered post counts as effective notice even if the employer marks the envelope "refused", and a name-correction regularisation cannot be used to convert an indefinite contract into a fixed-duration one [Para 183][Para 219][Para 230]. Maternity pay is wages, so under Article 115 it must be paid at least one day before the leave begins rather than monthly in arrears [Para 248][Para 253].

**Obiter dicta:** the recital of the employer's falling orders and the reduction of sewing lines from eight to six, which the panel expressly declined to weigh once it found the dismissals unlawful [Para 81][Para 226].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The number of claimant workers is given as 123 signatories, 126 claimants and "20" in one summary line, and the workforce as 239, 393 or 410 depending on the passage [Para 13][Para 81][Para 89][Para 154][Para 155]. Years are corrupted in several places ("ឆ្នាំ ០១២" for 2012, "២០១" for 2017), and a sentence on the EMS envelope has been replaced by untranslated OCR noise [Para 31][Para 47][Para 180]. Ms K's dismissal date is printed as 20 May in the reasoning but her contract is said to expire on 21 May, and the employer's notice to her is dated 20 May [Para 105][Para 106][Para 185]. The president's name appears as ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈាម on the face and again at the signature, but as ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា in the body; the worker-appointed arbitrator is Sin Kim Sean throughout [Para 1][Para 148][Para 259]. Individual workers have been anonymised in this brief, not in the source.
