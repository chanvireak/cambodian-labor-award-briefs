# Award No. 046/17 — Meng Da Footwear Industrial Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 18 December 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Chhiv Phirom (appointed by the employer), An Virak (appointed by the workers), Pen Bun Chhea (president) · Doc ID 138949

## 1. Facts
The respondent operates a footwear factory in Phnom Penh. In early December 2017 its workers struck to press for improved working conditions, and on 1 December 2017 the Fourth Labour Dispute Office of the Department of Labour Disputes received their complaint by telephone. Conciliation on 5 December 2017 failed on three points, and the case reached the Council on 6 December 2017. The employer asked the Council to order the strikers back to work; on 7 December 2017 the Council issued order No. 004/17 suspending the strike with effect from the morning of 8 December 2017. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 18][Para 19]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay the 5% entitlement to the workers in a single payment covering the period from the company's opening up to 2018? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company must pay a regular attendance bonus (ប្រាក់រង្វាន់ធ្វើការទៀងទាត់) of 15 US dollars per month? [Para 4]
3. Whether the company must pay 15 US dollars per month as travel and accommodation allowance? [Para 6]

The panel did not reach any of these three claims. It recast the determinative question as whether the arbitration proceedings could continue at all, given the state of the strike. [Para 41]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The workers struck to obtain improvement of a number of working conditions, and pressed the three monetary claims above before the conciliator. Their two designated representatives did not attend the pre-hearing meeting, but reported by telephone beforehand that about 100 workers were still on strike. [Para 14][Para 22][Para 49]
**Employer (respondent):** The company stated it could not grant the claims and that it was applying the law on each of them, and asked the Council to issue an order requiring the strikers to return to work immediately. At the pre-hearing meeting its representative said most workers had already returned and only some 200 or 300 remained on strike. [Para 3][Para 5][Para 7][Para 26][Para 24]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction to proceed — the panel terminated the arbitration proceedings in case No. 046/17 without determining the merits, and made no monetary order. [Para 52][Para 53]

The panel applied Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council (21 April 2004), which requires the parties to a dispute to suspend all dispute measures — strike, lock-out, or any other measure capable of aggravating the dispute — throughout the arbitration, and to attend every meeting to which the panel summons them. It followed its earlier awards (Nos 59/06, 39/08, 137/09, 138/09, 110/11, 104/13, 322/14, 071/15, 021/16) in holding that the Council will not continue arbitration while workers are striking or the employer is locking out. [Para 42][Para 45][Para 46]

On the facts the panel found that the strike was still running when the case reached it, that order No. 004/17 of 7 December 2017 had directed the workers to stop striking and return to normal work from the morning of 8 December 2017, that the workers had not complied with that order, and that they also failed to attend the pre-hearing meeting of 13 December 2017, sending word by telephone instead that about 100 workers remained on strike — which corroborated the employer's account. Because the strike continued, the panel held it could neither continue the procedure nor hold a hearing, and closed the file. It recorded expressly that, no hearing having taken place, it had made no findings of fact on the three disputed issues. [Para 19][Para 47][Para 49][Para 50][Para 40]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a collective labour dispute has been referred to the Arbitration Council and the workers continue a strike after the Council has ordered it suspended under Article 20 of Prakas No. 099, the Council will not hear the dispute on its merits and will terminate the arbitration proceedings; the claims are left undetermined rather than dismissed, and may be pursued afresh once the strike has ended. [Para 44][Para 50][Para 51]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's recording that most workers had already returned to work and only about 100 remained on strike played no part in the outcome, which turned solely on the fact that a strike was still in progress. [Para 22]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The number of workers still striking is printed three ways: "about 100" at [Para 22] and [Para 49], and "about 200 or /3 300" at [Para 24]. The conciliation date is printed as "5 December 2021 2017" at [Para 15], and the meeting venue at [Para 11] carries "year 2027" where street number 327 belongs. [Para 36] is badly mangled by OCR, mixing Latin fragments into the letter reference, and [Para 54] prints "2016 2017" after the employer-appointed arbitrator's signature. The employer's name appears in at least five slightly different Khmer spellings across the document (Meng Da Footwear Industrial / Meng Da Foot Wiet In Das Stri). The two worker representatives are referred to here as Ms C and Mr S rather than by their printed names. Nothing in the award indicates the sums claimed were verified, since no hearing was held.
