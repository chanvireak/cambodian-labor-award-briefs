# Award No. 012/17 — Frist Gawon Apparel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 5 April 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Yim Sothy (employer-appointed), Tuon Si Phan (worker-appointed, dissenting on Issue 6), Pen Bunhea (president) · non-binding award · Doc ID 138905

## 1. Facts
The company sews garments in Khan Meanchey, Phnom Penh, and employed about 500 workers; the factory union was registered on 7 September 2015, held a most-representative certificate (No. 302, 12 November 2015, valid to 12 November 2017), and had 407 members. [Para 1][Para 94][Para 98]
Mr K (Khim Karya) had worked as a sewing-machine mechanic since 15 February 2012 on an indefinite-term contract, on 196 USD per month plus attendance and other allowances. [Para 101][Para 102]
Workers struck on 24 and 25 November 2016 to demand payment for unused annual leave. The company suspended Mr K without pay from 24 to 29 November 2016 pending the labour inspector's decision, dismissed him on 29 November 2016, and lodged its authorisation request that same day. [Para 103][Para 105][Para 106]
Of six claims, Issue 2 and the 100% wage claim within Issue 5 were withdrawn at the hearing, leaving Issues 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 156]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must end its anti-union discrimination by reinstating Mr K, the union president, and paying his wages from the date of suspension until reinstatement? [Para 2][Para 186]
2. Whether the company must pay piece-rate and bonus payments to workers in the ironing, packing, QC and transport sections? [Para 3]
3. Whether the company must install a proper electrical system in the factory? [Para 4][Para 216]
4. Whether the company must restore a proper motorcycle and bicycle parking area? [Para 5][Para 234]
5. Whether the company must restore the annual leave days deducted from the two worker representatives for the days they attended dispute settlement? [Para 256]
6. Whether the company must supply the union with the monthly list of members from whose wages union dues were deducted? [Para 7][Para 288]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr K is a union leader; the labour inspector never answered the company's request of 29 November 2016, which must be treated as a refusal, and the parties had already agreed on 16 December 2016 to await the inspector's decision. The employer must also guarantee safety, since the wiring is old, there is no earthing system, and workers are shocked merely by touching one another. [Para 111][Para 112][Para 113][Para 122][Para 123][Para 124]
**Employer (respondent):** It cannot reinstate Mr K because it filed a second request to dismiss him, dated 27 February 2017, with the Minister, and is awaiting that decision; it lacks the budget to replace the whole electrical system because orders are scarce, though it has banned-access signage and a safety committee and will keep repairing; and it moved the parking area outside only to expand the canteen, the compound being too small. [Para 114][Para 125][Para 129][Para 139][Para 150]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issue 1 granted: reinstatement of Mr K and payment of his wages from 24 November 2016 until the day he returns; Issue 3 granted: the electrical system must be made safe to technical standards; Issue 4 dismissed; Issue 5 granted: annual leave restored to Mr K and Ms H (Hun Sara); Issue 6 dismissed. No global sum was computed; his base wage is printed as 196 USD per month. [Para 102][Para 310][Para 311][Para 312][Para 313][Para 314]

On Issue 1 the panel applied Articles 43 and 45 of the 2016 Law on Trade Unions: a delegate or candidate may not be dismissed without the labour inspector's prior consent, and where the employer suspends a worker pending that decision, a refusal by the inspector renders the suspension void, its effects erased, and reinstatement immediate. Following Award No. 181/16 U Tong Development, it held Articles 43, 45 and 67 apply equally to workers protected under Article 67. [Para 187][Para 193][Para 199][Para 202][Para 203]

On the facts, the company never received a reply, so its request of 29 November 2016 was deemed refused. Its asserted appeal to the Minister of 27 February 2017 was unsupported: the panel found no such letter and held the document of that date was in truth a second request to the labour inspector. The procedure in Articles 43 and 45 was therefore not followed, the suspension was void, and reinstatement with back pay from 24 November 2016 was ordered. [Para 205][Para 208][Para 209][Para 210][Para 211]

On Issue 3 the panel applied Articles 230 and 250 of the Labour Law and Article 2 of Prakas No. 307 of 14 December 2007: every enterprise must arrange premises, machinery and equipment so as to guarantee workers' safety. Both parties accepted there was no earthing system and the wiring was old, and a worker, Ms S (Sim Chanly, printed once as Sam Savon), suffered burns from an electric shock and an exploding kettle on 24 January 2017. Signage and instructions were not a durable solution for 500 workers, so the system was ordered brought up to technical standard. [Para 217][Para 224][Para 226][Para 232][Para 233]

On Issue 4 the panel applied Article 2 of the Labour Law, which gives the employer the right to organise and manage the enterprise lawfully and reasonably. It found no provision imposing a duty to provide parking inside the compound and no evidence of danger from crossing the road beyond congestion and lateness; the move enlarged the canteen and two guards, with two more promised, were provided. The claim was dismissed. [Para 237][Para 240][Para 253][Para 255]

On Issue 5 the panel applied Article 306 of the Labour Law and Articles 20, 14 and 19 of Prakas No. 099: representatives must attend every conciliation and hearing, parties must take no measure prejudicial to the dispute during proceedings, and an employer must allow attendance during working hours without negative effect on the worker's rights. Since the parties agreed the company had deducted annual leave in lieu of wages for the two hearing days, the panel ordered those days restored. [Para 257][Para 264][Para 276][Para 287]

On Issue 6 the panel applied Article 129 of the Labour Law and Article 7 of the Law on Trade Unions: a worker may authorise deduction of union dues in writing and may withdraw at any time, but the duty to notify withdrawal rests on the worker, not the employer, and no provision obliges the employer to disclose which members have left. The claim was dismissed. [Para 288][Para 303][Para 307][Para 309]

Arbitrator Tuon Si Phan dissented on Issue 6 only: the union had supplied a proper membership list, the employer cut the deducted headcount from 407 to 383 in February 2017 on the bare assertion that some members had resigned, and only the employer holds the names, identity numbers and workplaces, so it must show that evidence to the union rather than stop deductions of its own motion. [Para 318][Para 320][Para 328][Para 334]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer suspends a protected union leader for serious misconduct and seeks the labour inspector's authorisation, silence by the inspector is a refusal in law; the suspension is then void, and reinstatement with wages from the date of suspension follows, and a later request to the Minister cannot cure the defect if it was never in fact made. [Para 210][Para 211]

**Obiter dicta:** Attendance by worker representatives at conciliation or arbitration hearings on proper invitation may not be charged against their wages, bonuses or annual leave, because Article 306 and Prakas No. 099 treat it as an incident of the dispute procedure rather than an absence. [Para 285]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is dated 05 April 2017 in the header at Para 1 and again at Para 335. The worker injured by electric shock is named Sim Chanly at Para 120, Para 226 and Para 250 but Sam Savon at Para 144. The company is rendered as Frist, Firist, Fos and Firs Gawon, and once in English as "Frist Gawon Apparel Co., Ltd" at Para 68. The minutes recording the 16 December 2016 agreement are dated 16 February 2017 at Para 113. The operative reference to the January 2017 dues list prints a corrupted year, "ឆ្នាំ ២០១៩៤", at Para 309, and Para 17 carries embedded English fragments where the adjournment record should read.
