# Award No. 002/17 — Poipet Resort Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 7 March 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phallak presiding; Chhiv Phirom, employer nominee; An Nonn, worker nominee) · Doc ID 138885

## 1. Facts
Poipet Resort Co., Ltd runs a hotel, an entertainment club and a casino at Kbal Spean village, Poipet commune, O'Chrov district, Banteay Meanchey, and employed about 1,059 workers. [Para 75] On 14 December 2016 its workers complained to the provincial labour department over seven points; conciliation on 29 December 2016 settled one and left six, which were referred to the Council on 2 January 2017. [Para 13] Because the workers could not afford to travel, the panel sat at a hotel in Poipet on 16 January 2017. [Para 18] Of the six, issue 4 was then settled and issues 3 and 6 withdrawn, leaving issues 1, 2 and 5. [Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay night-shift work at 130% of the day wage to workers whose shifts rotate? [Para 87]
2. Whether the employer must pay a food allowance of 3,000 baht a month to all workers? [Para 102]
5. Whether the 20-minute breaks taken after every 80 minutes of work must be counted as working time? [Para 115]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the law requires 130% of the day wage for work between 22:00 and 05:00, and the employer pays a single monthly sum whoever works nights; 50 baht a day cannot buy the four meals the company used to provide, hence the 3,000 baht claim; and the 20-minute breaks are too short to leave the workplace, so they spend about ten hours at the company to complete eight hours of work. [Para 91]
**Employer (respondent):** the monthly wages of 3,876 baht for hotel staff and 4,875 baht for casino staff already include the night premium, and the shift rotation was explained before hiring; the 3,000 baht allowance has no basis in law or agreement; the breaks are genuine rest during which workers may return to their rooms or leave the premises, and any hours beyond eight are paid as overtime. [Para 93]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must pay 130% of the day wage for night shifts; the 3,000 baht food allowance is refused, but the employer must keep providing meals under its internal rules until those rules are lawfully amended; the claim on rest periods is dismissed. [Para 259]
On standing, the panel found that the enterprise union was unregistered, but that 163 workers had delegated their claims to the Cambodian Labour Confederation and the provincial labour department had recognised four workers as representatives; under clause 1 of Prakas 362 those recognised workers may represent a collective dispute, and all four attended. [Para 138]
On issue 1, Articles 137 and 138 let the employer organise shift work, but Article 144-new defines night work as at least eleven consecutive hours including 22:00 to 05:00 and fixes the rate at 130%; rotating shifts are a regular pattern, not overtime, so the night rate applies to them. [Para 165] The employer asserted that the lump sum already contained the premium but produced no payroll or other evidence, and a party asserting a fact bears the burden of proving it; the premium was therefore unpaid and 130% was ordered. [Para 179]
On issue 2, the panel found no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice creating a 3,000 baht allowance, so the claim was one of interest; an interests dispute may only be brought by the most representative union, which this claimant was not, and the panel declined to consider it. [Para 183] It nevertheless found that the employer had replaced four canteen meals with 1,500 or 1,800 baht a month without following the procedure for amending internal rules under Article 30, so the unamended rules continued to bind and meals had to be provided. [Para 218]
On issue 5, the panel distinguished standby from rest: here workers could leave the premises or use the rest rooms, were not checked on, and were not required to be available for immediate recall, unlike the twelve-hour standby held to be working time in Award 098/15. [Para 244] Since the day was capped at eight hours and extra hours were paid as overtime, the arrangement was a lawful and reasonable exercise of the employer's management right, and the claim failed. [Para 250]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that pays a single undifferentiated monthly wage cannot be taken to have paid the 130% night premium under Article 144-new of the Labour Law; absent payroll evidence separating day and night hours, the premium remains owing for every hour worked between 22:00 and 05:00, even under a regular rotation. [Para 172]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that short breaks of 20 minutes every 80 minutes, though they give little chance to leave the company, suit the character of casino work because they spare workers four continuous hours on their feet. [Para 255]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The printed list of issues in dispute starts at item 2; the wording of item 1 survives only in the panel's later statement of it as the night-pay claim. [Para 3] The food figures are printed inconsistently: 3,000 baht in the claim, "300 baht" in the panel's question, a 30 baht meal price in the employer's evidence and 50 baht a day in the workers' argument. [Para 102] The letter recognising the four worker representatives is dated 29 December 2017 in one place and 29 December 2016 in another, and only the 2016 date is possible in a case heard on 16 January 2017. [Para 81] The employer appears as "Poipet Resort", "Poipet Rizot", "Poipet Wisot" and "Poipet Risot", and one report reference names a different casino altogether. [Para 15] The wage figure of 4,875 baht is once printed with a stray word after it. [Para 90] The appendix of 163 thumbprints is unrecoverable OCR and is not relied on; individual workers and representatives are referred to here by initial and their names are redacted by policy. [Para 278]
