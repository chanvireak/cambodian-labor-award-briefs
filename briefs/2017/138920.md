# Award No. 018/17 — Khembo Uni Soul Ltd (Arbitration Council, 19 April 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៉ីង សុទ្ធី, អន ណន, ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា) · Doc ID 138920

## 1. Facts
Khembo Uni Soul Ltd, a garment factory employing about 1,778 workers, dismissed three local union leaders — its president Mr N, vice-president Mr C and secretary Mr T — on 24 March 2017, during or just after a strike the workforce held from 17 to 22 March 2017. [Para 2][Para 60][Para 89][Para 125] At the hearing the parties settled the reinstatement claim, leaving three points: the weekly target bonus, pay for the strike days, and the removal of a supervisor, Mr H. [Para 28][Para 29] The award is not binding. [Para 30][Para 220]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the three dismissed union leaders and preserve their wages and benefits? [Para 2]
2. Whether the employer must pay the target bonus to sewing workers who reached the goal on 4 days of the disputed week, though it gave them no work on the other 2? [Para 140]
3. Whether the employer must pay daily wages, the attendance bonus and the lunch allowance for the strike days of 17 to 22 March 2017? [Para 165][Para 167]
4. Whether the employer must dismiss Mr H from his post and from the company for insulting and threatening workers? [Para 193]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the three leaders did not incite the strike, which the workers chose freely, so their dismissal was unjustified; the target bonus had always been paid for the days actually worked; and the strike was a response to the employer's failure to answer their demands. [Para 2][Para 72][Para 93]
**Employer (respondent):** the target bonus is calculated weekly and not daily, so a week below the goal earns nothing; no wages, attendance bonus or lunch money are owed for strike days, though travel money was offered; and it could not act against Mr H because he leads another union in the factory. [Para 76][Para 95][Para 100]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must pay a target bonus of 8,000 riel to workers who reached the goal on 4 days in the week of 06 to 11 March 2017; the strike-day pay and the removal of Mr H were refused. [Para 218][Para 219][Para 220]

On the target bonus, the panel found that the scheme paid 12,000 riel a week at 100 percent of the goal, rising by 1,000 riel for each percentage point above 90 percent. [Para 66][Para 78] The workers did not dispute that the bonus is assessed weekly rather than daily, and they accepted that a week short of the goal earns nothing. [Para 141][Para 142] But in the disputed week the employer supplied work for only 4 days, and the 2 missing days were its own failure, not the workers'. [Para 160][Para 161] The panel held the employer bore the burden of showing how the scheme applied when no work was given, and its representative admitted he did not know; it therefore ordered the 8,000 riel bonus for the 4 days worked. [Para 157][Para 159][Para 164]

On strike pay, the panel applied Articles 320(4), 324 and 332 of the Labour Law: a strike requires at least seven days' prior notice and the exhaustion of conciliation and arbitration, and during a strike the contract is suspended so wages are not paid. [Para 167][Para 181][Para 182] The workers struck on 17 March 2017 without notice and without awaiting the panel's own conciliation, so the strike was unlawful. [Para 183][Para 184] The attendance bonus was refused for the same reason, following the Notice No. 230 line of awards. [Para 179][Para 184] The lunch allowance was refused because no law, collective agreement or internal rule requires it during a strike, even though the employer pays 2,000 riel a day in normal work. [Para 185][Para 187][Para 191] The panel noted that Article 334 would have obliged the employer to pay wages had it hired replacements, which it did not. [Para 173][Para 174][Para 176]

On Mr H, the panel held that only the parties to a contract of employment may terminate it, so workers cannot obtain the dismissal of a third person; removal is the employer's managerial prerogative under Article 2. [Para 196][Para 197][Para 204] The panel may order removal only where the person endangers the health or safety of other workers, and the workers produced no evidence or witnesses of the insults and threats alleged. [Para 201][Para 209][Para 211] The claim therefore failed, though the panel observed that the employer should still have investigated the complaint under its internal rules rather than leave it unanswered because Mr H leads another union. [Para 215][Para 216]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A strike called without the seven days' notice and without exhausting conciliation and arbitration is unlawful, and because Article 332 suspends the contract of employment during a strike no wages, attendance bonus or meal allowance accrue for those days — unless the employer hires replacements, when Article 334 requires the strikers to be paid; separately, where an employer withholds work in a bonus week it bears the burden of proving how the scheme applies and cannot rely on its own silence. [Para 174][Para 182][Para 184][Para 157][Para 159]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that a worker's demand to have a supervisor dismissed can never succeed on its own, since it asks the panel to rewrite someone else's contract, and that an employer who receives such a complaint should still investigate it under its own disciplinary rules. [Para 197][Para 215]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer is printed both as "Khembo Uni Soul Ltd" and, at paragraph 139, as "Cambodia Uni Soul Co., Ltd", and the party heading is corrupted throughout. [Para 1][Para 139] The settled point 1 is recorded as agreed but its terms are nowhere reproduced, and the parties' answers at paragraphs 3 to 11 survive only as fragments. [Para 28][Para 3][Para 7] The number of workers appearing is unstable: paragraph 122 reads "100 workers, all 06 persons", paragraph 135 lists 06 plus 06, and paragraph 139 counts "09 other workers". [Para 122][Para 135][Para 139] Two key passages are badly damaged — paragraph 188 carries stray English and numeric fragments in place of the reasoning, and paragraph 209 mixes an English sentence into the burden-of-proof holding. [Para 188][Para 209] The award date appears as 19 April 2017 in the header but the extension clause at paragraph 32 loses the month of the original date. [Para 1][Para 32]
