# Award No. 014/17 — International Fashion Royal (Arbitration Council, 27 April 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ouk Ri (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Pen Bunhea (president) · non-binding award · Doc ID 138907

## 1. Facts
The company cuts and sews garments in Khan Porsenchey, Phnom Penh, and told the panel it employed about 1,170 workers, of whom only 82 were men. [Para 1][Para 39][Para 72]
It had no most-representative union; 363 workers transferred their rights to four representatives recognised by the Phnom Penh Department of Labour on 22 February 2017. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 93][Para 99]
The parties agreed the company pays 1,500 riel a day as a midday meal allowance, paid monthly with wages, and pays 10 USD monthly as a sewing-skill allowance alike to workers who run two machines and those who run three. [Para 43][Para 67][Para 145]
Seven claims were filed; Issue 7, seeking removal of the administration head for interfering in union affairs, was settled by agreement at the hearing, leaving six. [Para 8][Para 22][Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must provide a midday meal or add 1,000 riel a day to the present meal allowance, so that each worker receives 2,500 riel? [Para 2][Para 101]
2. Whether the company must pay the full three months' maternity allowance, 50% of wages and benefits for 90 days, to women who take maternity leave? [Para 3][Para 126]
3. Whether the company must pay the midday meal allowance and the overtime meal allowance every week rather than monthly? [Para 4][Para 143]
4. Whether the company must pay a 10 USD monthly incentive to workers in the thread-trimming (កាត់ព្រុយ) section? [Para 5][Para 180]
5. Whether the company must raise the skill allowance from 10 to 15 USD for workers who can run three machines? [Para 6][Para 187]
6. Whether the company must pay 20 USD a month in lieu of building a nursery? [Para 7][Para 194]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** 1,500 riel a day is not enough to buy a meal, so the allowance should rise to 2,500 riel; maternity pay should be handed over in one sum because women on leave are short of money and find it hard to travel to collect it monthly; every section receives an incentive except the trimmers; and workers running three machines work more than the others and should be paid more. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 51][Para 55][Para 62][Para 69][Para 73][Para 74]
**Employer (respondent):** It already gives many other benefits; these are additional requests that no law requires; its payroll system cannot pay wages for a 90-day block at once or be changed to weekly meal payments; and it pays 5 USD a month in place of a nursery. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 52][Para 58][Para 59][Para 76]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issues 1, 4 and 5 refused as interest disputes over which the panel had no power to adjudicate; Issue 2 granted (50% of wages and benefits for 90 days to be paid before maternity leave begins); Issue 3 partly granted (the monthly midday meal allowance claim dismissed, but the overtime meal allowance ordered paid weekly); Issue 6 dismissed as claimed, with the employer ordered instead to build a nursery or pay external nursery fees on actual invoices. [Para 214][Para 215][Para 216][Para 217][Para 218][Para 219][Para 220][Para 221]

On Issues 1, 4 and 5 the panel first found each claim went beyond the legal minimum, so each was an interest dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍). It applied Article 96(1) and (2) of the Labour Law on collective agreements, and Articles 54 and 72 of the 2016 Law on Trade Unions: only a most-representative union, or several local unions together entitled to conclude a collective agreement, may bring an interest dispute. Following Awards No. 81/04, No. 02/11 and No. 203/16, it held that an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement and binds all workers for one year, so a union lacking representative status cannot invoke it. The claimants here were individual workers transferring rights, not such a union, so the panel declined to consider these three claims. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 116][Para 117][Para 119][Para 120][Para 124][Para 183][Para 189]

On Issue 2 the panel applied Articles 182 and 183 of the Labour Law — 90 days' maternity leave with half wages and benefits paid by the employer — and Article 103, which lists maternity pay within the definition of wages. It then applied Article 115(3): wages may not be paid on a day the worker is entitled to rest, and if the pay date falls on a rest day it must be paid a day earlier. The panel held Article 115 applies to maternity pay, so the monthly instalment practice was incompatible with the law, and ordered the full 50% for 90 days paid at least one day before the leave begins, following Awards No. 57/06, No. 139/08 and others. [Para 126][Para 129][Para 134][Para 136][Para 138][Para 140][Para 141][Para 142]

On Issue 3 the panel split the claim. As to the midday meal allowance, Article 2 of the Labour Law gives the employer the right to manage the enterprise lawfully and reasonably, and no law or agreement requires payment more often than the present monthly practice. As to overtime meal money, Article 2 of Ministry of Labour Notice No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 entitles a worker doing voluntary overtime to 2,000 riel a day or a free meal; the employer was paying that monthly with wages. Because the notice fixes no payment date, the panel held a weekly payment is the reasonable reading, since the money must be available to buy food when the overtime is worked, and ordered weekly payment. [Para 151][Para 154][Para 160][Para 164][Para 166][Para 168][Para 178][Para 179]

On Issue 6 the panel applied Article 186: an enterprise employing at least 100 women must provide a nursing room and a nursery, and where it cannot take children over 18 months it must pay a nursery of the worker's choice on production of invoices. The company employs over 90% women, yet has no nursery and pays 5 USD a month instead. The panel held Article 186 does not authorise a flat 20 USD payment in lieu, so the claim failed, but the 5 USD payment does not discharge the statutory duty: the employer must build a nursery, or where it cannot, pay the actual cost of an outside nursery against invoices. [Para 194][Para 202][Para 208][Para 209][Para 210][Para 212][Para 221][Para 222]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Maternity pay is wages for the purposes of Article 115(3), so the 50% payable over 90 days must be handed over at least one day before the leave begins and cannot be released in monthly instalments. Conversely, a claim that merely improves on the statutory minimum is an interest dispute, which the panel cannot adjudicate unless it is brought by a most-representative union or by local unions collectively entitled to conclude a collective agreement. [Para 120][Para 136][Para 141]

**Obiter dicta:** Paying a flat monthly sum in place of a nursery does not discharge the Article 186 duty, since the article contemplates a nursery on the premises or reimbursement of actual outside fees against invoices. On overtime meal money the panel observed that the timing of payment matters as much as the amount, because the allowance exists to let the worker eat while working overtime. [Para 164][Para 209]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is dated 27 April 2017 at Para 1 and the parties agreed that extension at Para 25. The file's closing pages, from Para 224 onward, are almost entirely destroyed: the 363 individual transfer-of-rights letters are reproduced as OCR noise, with dates printed as fragments ("ខែ .0/..", "ថ្ងៃទី 1LL") and names and identity numbers reduced to digit strings, so the identity of most transferors cannot be verified from the source. The company name appears as International Fashion Royal, In Tha Na Syaunal Fe Sen Royal, and In Tha Na Syaunal Fe Sen. Para 21 prints the adjourned hearing date as "ថ្ងៃទី ១០ ខែធ្នូ មេសា ឆ្នាំ ២០១៧", merging two month names. The worker count is given as 1,170 at Para 39, Para 72 and Para 206.
