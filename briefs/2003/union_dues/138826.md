# Award No. 05/03 — Top One Factory (Arbitration Council, 26 June 2003)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mr V Vanna for a party, Mr O Ry, Kong Phallak chair) · Doc ID 138826

## 1. Facts
Top One Factory, on National Road 5 at Kilometer 9, Phnom Penh, employed 559 workers who were members of the Solidarity Worker Union and had signed deduction forms and membership cards. [Para 3][Para 7][Para 13] The union asked on 21 March 2003 that union fees be withheld from members' wages; the employer refused, believing the deduction unlawful under Article 281 of the 1997 Labour Law, and separately disputed how breast-feeding time should be allocated. [Para 11][Para 13] The employer already allowed half an hour's nursing in the morning and half an hour in the afternoon on factory premises, without fixed times and without distinguishing workers living near from those living far. [Para 14]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must withhold the union fee for the union from the salary of workers who are union members? [Para 3][Para 9]
2. Whether the breast-feeding time should be divided, and how, between morning and afternoon? [Para 3][Para 10]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the union requested withholding for its 559 members and asked for a day care centre and a breast-feeding room, producing a list with thumbprints and membership cards as written authorisation. [Para 9][Para 10][Para 13]
**Employer (respondent):** it refused to withhold because it feared the deduction was illegal and contrary to Article 281 of the Labour Law; it accepted a child care centre and nursery room but disputed the allocation of breast-feeding time. [Para 11]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — on issue 1 the union must give the employer a list of consenting workers at least 15 days before pay day, with completed deduction forms, and the factory must deduct and transfer the fees to the union between the 10th and 12th of the following month and certify the list to the Ministry of Labour; on issue 2 the panel fixed the nursing times by the parties' agreement. [Para 4][Para 5][Para 6]

On issue 1 the panel relied on the second paragraph of Article 129, which allows a worker to authorise the employer in writing to withhold union membership fees, and on Article 5 of Prakas No. 305 of MSALVY, which requires a written request within 15 days before pay day; Article 112 also requires the employer to give precise and comprehensible notice at each payment so the worker can refuse a deduction not of their will. [Para 15] The employer's objection under Article 281 was answered by reading that article with Article 280: Article 281 forbids employers to deduct union dues and to pay them for workers, but its purpose is to protect workers and to bar employer interference tending to place a union under employer control, so a deduction made at the worker's own written request is not contrary to it. [Para 15][Para 17][Para 18][Para 19]

On issue 2 the panel relied on Article 184 of the Labour Law and on the agreement reached at the hearing: workers whose home is far from the workplace get 30 minutes in the morning (7:00 a.m. to 7:30 a.m.) and 30 minutes at lunchtime (12:30 p.m. to 13:00 p.m.); workers whose home is near get 30 minutes in the morning and 30 minutes in the afternoon, taken in the factory at any time within those periods. [Para 6][Para 15]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The prohibition in Article 281 on an employer deducting union dues does not prevent an employer from withholding them at a worker's own written authorisation under Article 129; the prohibition targets interference with union independence under Article 280, not a facility the worker requests. Such a withholding is lawful only on a written request made at least 15 days before pay day under Article 5 of Prakas No. 305, and the employer must give clear notice at each payment so the worker may withdraw consent. [Para 15][Para 17][Para 18]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's account of Article 280 — that interference includes measures tending to create an employer-dominated workers' organisation or to support one financially so as to control it — explains the purpose of Article 281 but was not itself applied to any order. [Para 19]
