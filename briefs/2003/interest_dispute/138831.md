# Award No. 14/03 — Chusing Garment (Cambodia) Co. Ltd (Arbitration Council, 03-10-2003)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, Vong Vanna, chair Sa Sovan as printed in the header) · Doc ID 138831

## 1. Facts
Three unions at Chusing Garment (Cambodia) Co. Ltd, a garment factory at National Road 6A, Sangkat Prek Leap, brought a collective dispute that had run since July 2003, after three conciliation meetings at the company and a labour-inspector conciliation on 04/09/2003 failed to settle it. [Para 22] The disputed acts were a fight on 25 July 2003 between two Chinese managers (the cutting-and-stores manager and the head of the marking section, also known as "The Gecko") and a production manager's snatching of a worker's ID card on 13 August 2003; the workers also contested piece rates, production targets and a wage cut. [Para 22] [Para 24]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer should dismiss Mr S and Mr C, the two managers who fought at the factory, and Mr U, the production manager who pulled a worker's shirt and snatched her ID card? [Para 11]
2. Whether the employer must give prior notice of the piece rate and raise the piece rate, and whether it may compel workers to meet the factory's production targets? [Para 12] [Para 13]
3. Whether the factory's decision to reduce the wage and responsibilities of Mr K from $65 to $45 (per month) was lawful? [Para 14]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that the two managers had fought at the factory and a production manager had roughed up a worker, and that there was discrimination, because Khmer employees were dismissed without compensation for small mistakes while Chinese employees were merely warned; that no piece-rate schedule was ever shown to them in advance. [Para 22] [Para 29]
**Employer (respondent):** that both managers had already been warned by letter of 29 July 2003 and that Khmer workers who erred had also been warned and kept on, so there was no discrimination; that the production manager was enforcing an orderly exit queue for safety; that piece rates were given to section leaders; and that Mr K's grievance had been settled by agreement of 13 September 2003. [Para 23] [Para 24] [Para 28] [Para 33]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim to dismiss the three managers was rejected, but the employer was ordered to warn and educate them; by agreement it must provide a schedule of piece rates with advance notice of changes and calculate the piece rate reasonably; it must not force overtime and must pay overtime according to law; and Mr K's duties and salary are maintained at US$65 per month. [Para 48] [Para 49] [Para 50]
On dismissal, the panel followed Case #04/03 (Lida Garment): Article 65 of the Labour Law makes the employment contract one between the parties, so hiring and firing is the employer's right, and Article 83(2)(4) together with Article 35(e) of the internal rules confers a right to dismiss without notice or severance, not an obligation to do so. That right is lost if the employer does not act within 7 days of learning of the misconduct (Article 26), and the warning letters of 29 July 2003 were issued under Articles 25 and 35 of the internal rules. [Para 33] [Para 35] [Para 36] [Para 37]
On the piece rate, Article 112(a) requires the schedule and any amendment to be given in advance, which the employer accepted. On the demand for an increase, Article 108 only requires piece work to yield at least the minimum wage for a worker of average ability; the wages actually paid here equalled or exceeded the minimum wage, so there was no legal basis, and the further demand for an above-minimum rate is an interests dispute under Chapter 6 of the Prakas which the workers supported with no evidence. [Para 39] [Para 40] [Para 41] [Para 43]
On production targets, setting reasonable targets that a worker of usual ability can meet in an 8-hour day is the employer's prerogative; hours may not exceed 8 per day and 58 per week (Article 137), overtime is voluntary under Prakas #80 of 01 March 1999 and payable at 50% or 100% under Article 139, and an employee who misses reasonable targets may be corrected or warned for poor performance. [Para 44] [Para 45] [Para 46] [Para 47] [Para 48]
On Mr K, the parties reported that salary and duties would be maintained on his undertaking to respect the internal rules, per the letter of 13 September 2003, and the order gave effect to that agreement. [Para 33] [Para 50]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that warns an employee for serious misconduct instead of dismissing him does not act unlawfully that the panel will correct: Article 83(2)(4) is permissive, and the right to dismiss without notice expires 7 days after the employer learns of the misconduct (Article 26), so a warning issued inside that window exhausts the incident. Article 108 fixes only a floor for piece work — a wage at least equal to the minimum wage — so a demand for a higher piece rate is an interests dispute that fails without evidence. [Para 35] [Para 37] [Para 40]

**Obiter dicta:** that the panel could order an employer to transfer, or in the most serious case dismiss, a worker who is a dangerous type posing a health and safety risk to other workers, though no such evidence existed here; and that an employer may correct or warn an employee who fails reasonable production targets. [Para 37] [Para 48]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel is composed differently in the two places it is printed: the header gives the employer's arbitrator as Mar Samborana, the workers' as Vong Vanna and the chair as Sa Sovan, while the signature block gives Mar Samborana, Tuon Siphann and chair Ang Eng Thong. [Para 16] [Para 50] The hearing is 14.00–19.00 in the header and 9.00 in the summary of the case, and the non-conciliation report is dated September 12, 2003 in the header but 04/09/2003 in the summary. [Para 16] [Para 22] The issue 2 heading is lost and its sub-issues (a)–(c) survive only inside the reasons; the worker's name appears as Koy Bonyeun, Koy Bunyin and Koy Bunyuen. [Para 33] [Para 38] OCR drops "th" throughout ("themployer", "the" for "the"), and the order prints the maintained salary as US$65 per month against "$65 to $45" in the statement of issues. [Para 49] Individual names have been reduced to initials per the briefing convention.
