# Award No. 03/03 — Tonga Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 11 June 2003)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry for the employer party, Tuon Siphann for the worker party, Matthew Rendall chair) · Doc ID 138825

## 1. Facts
Tonga Garment Co., Ltd, a Phnom Penh garment factory with 660 employees, classified part of its workforce as "temporary workers" paid 700 riel per hour with no overtime, Sunday, holiday or leave entitlements, keeping workers on that status for up to two years. [Para 28][Para 45][Para 48][Para 50] The CCAWDU-represented workers raised ten demands, all refused; conciliation failed after the employer twice failed to attend, and it then neither appeared at the hearing nor sent a written postponement request. [Para 29][Para 32][Para 36][Para 44]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether to grant a postponement of the hearing to a later date in accordance with the employer's request? [Para 4]
2. Whether the "temporary workers" at the factory should change their status to that of "normal workers"? [Para 4]
3. Whether the employer should enlarge the hospital room and provide a total of 12 hospital beds? [Para 4]
4. Whether the employer should divide the hospital room into male and female sections by the provision of a dividing wall? [Para 4]
5. Whether the employer should provide toilets and washrooms in the hospital room? [Para 5]
6. Whether the employer does, and if not should, supply the hospital room with medicines as listed in Prakas No. 330? [Para 5]
7. Whether the employer should provide a child care centre and a place for breast feeding? [Para 5]
8. Whether a sufficient number of toilets is provided with sufficient water in each? [Para 5]
9. Whether the company should reinstate the practice of withholding union fees and passing them to the union? [Para 5]
10. Whether the company is discriminating against Ms M by refusing to allow her to work overtime? [Para 5]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** temporary status left workers on 700 riel an hour with no overtime, Sunday, holiday or leave pay; the factory had 12 beds at most, no divider, no toilet in the hospital room, only four medicines, no child care or breastfeeding room, and 15 toilets, three for office staff, short of water. [Para 29][Para 48][Para 52][Para 61][Para 70][Para 71] The employer stopped withholding union fees and withdrew Ms M's overtime after the April 2003 strike. [Para 74][Para 79]
**Employer (respondent):** it did not attend the hearing and argued only in writing — that the workforce was 660, that its hospital room and medicines complied, and that the unions' figures were observation-based estimates. [Para 41][Para 45][Para 60] It gave no reason for the postponement request telephoned on 4 June. [Para 42]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the hearing proceeded; the employer was ordered to cease the temporary worker classification, place all workers past three months on fixed or undetermined duration contracts at full rates, pay minimum wage and leave compensation, and complete the hospital room, toilet, medicine, child care, breastfeeding, union-fee and overtime remedies within one month. [Para 8][Para 9][Para 16][Para 24]

On issue 1, under Prakas No. 338 of 2002 (Annex Rules 4.8 and 4.9) a postponement may be granted only where a party shows good reason for absence or both parties agree, and an extension of the 15-day limit needs both parties' consent; none was shown and the workers refused to extend. [Para 80][Para 81] On issue 2, Article 66 allows only fixed duration or undetermined duration contracts, Article 68 caps probation at three months, and Articles 10 and 104 guarantee the minimum wage whatever the classification; "temporary worker" is not a status known to the Labour Law, and hourly hiring cannot circumvent it. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 88][Para 95] Compensation was one day's pay per accrued day of leave, to be agreed within one month or fixed by an employer-paid accountant. [Para 13][Para 14]

On issues 3 to 6, Prakas No. 330 of 2000 requires a room of at least 20 square metres, beds for 2% of staff — 13 beds for 660 workers — and the listed medicines at all times; Articles 229 and 242 and Article 38(2) of the Constitution require dignity and hygiene, so a 2-metre opaque divider and a toilet within 20 metres were ordered. [Para 100][Para 101][Para 105][Para 113] On issue 7, Article 186 requires a nursing room and child care centre where more than 100 women work, failing which the employer pays for external care. [Para 114][Para 117] On issue 8, Prakas No. 52 of 10 February 2000 requires 16 toilets, each permanently supplied with water, ending the practice of fetching water from a pump. [Para 120][Para 122]

On issue 9, Article 129 and Article 5 of Prakas No. 305 bind the employer to withhold union fees once a worker requests it; it had done so before the strike and was ordered to resume from the next pay day and certify the list to the Ministry. [Para 123][Para 128] On issue 10, Article 12 prohibits discrimination for union activity; the withdrawal of Ms M's overtime, previously regular if not daily, was a direct response to her role in the April 2003 strike. [Para 129][Para 133]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer cannot create a contractual status outside the Labour Law to reduce statutory entitlements: every worker past a probation of no more than three months must hold a fixed duration or undetermined duration contract and receive the minimum wage, overtime and holiday penalty rates and proportionate leave, and hourly hiring does not avoid that. A benefit the employer has been granting, such as withholding union fees on written request or assigning regular overtime, cannot be withdrawn as reprisal for a strike. [Para 88][Para 95][Para 128][Para 133]

**Obiter dicta:** the closing remark that most issues were simple matters of law resolvable at conciliation, and the cultural observation about dignity in infirmaries, are comments rather than grounds of decision. [Para 105][Para 134]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award date is "11-6-2003" with no day/month order stated [Para 1]; the hearing is 05-06-2003, the non-conciliation report 16-05-2003, submitted 23-05-2003 [Para 8][Para 26].
- Figures are printed two ways: the workers asked for 12 beds and 15 toilets [Para 29], while the orders require 13 beds and 16 toilets, computed from 2% of 660 and from Prakas No. 52 [Para 16][Para 101][Para 120]; the issue list itself says 12 beds [Para 4].
- Worker numbers appear as "more than 600" in evidence and 660 in the findings, the panel preferring the employer's records [Para 45][Para 46].
- Dates and words are corrupted by OCR: "May 192003", "97 May", "16h May", "3th June", "5h June", "47 June" [Para 29][Para 31][Para 33][Para 26][Para 80][Para 42]; the text breaks off at "adequately contain" [Para 102] and at a bare "It was ." [Para 127].
- Two passages are duplicated mid-sentence at [Para 58] and [Para 132]; the composition at [Para 8] runs the employer arbitrator's and chair's names together.
- Individual workers and representatives are reduced to initials here; the award prints Ms M's name in full throughout [Para 5][Para 79].
