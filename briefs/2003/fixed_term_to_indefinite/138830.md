# Award No. 17/03 & 18/03 — Ho Hing Garment (Arbitration Council, 11-2003)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, employer-appointed; Tuon Siphann, worker-appointed; Sa Sovan, chair) · heard as non-binding award · Doc ID 138830

## 1. Facts
Ho Hing Garment suspended two work groups, B-1 and B-2, by letter of 8 August 2003, paying the affected employees 50% of wage; among them were the two shop stewards of the Free Union of Khmer Workers, Mr M (president) and Mr S (secretary). When the company called the groups back it reassigned staff to other sections and shifts, but the two stewards refused and were held in the store section on night shift for an unspecified period. Case 17/03 covered that dispute; case 18/03 added the maternity payment, childcare centre and dismissal demands, and the panel heard both together. [Para 31][Para 32][Para 33][Para 38][Para 29]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company lawfully changed the work and shift of the two workers, Mr M and Mr S, without their agreement? [Para 4]
2. Whether the company must pay pregnant workers 50% of three months' salaries before maternity leave? [Para 6]
3. Whether the company must provide a childcare centre? [Para 6]
4. Whether the company must dismiss the head of administration, nicknamed "Charly"? [Para 7]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** There was sufficient sewing work, some of it sub-contracted out, so the refusal to return the two stewards to the sewing section was retaliation for their union roles; they also pressed the maternity advance, the childcare centre, and the removal of the administration head, a letter said to bear the thumbprints of 460 employees listing his failures on sick workers, security and hygiene, and relations with the union. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 44][Para 45]
**Employer (respondent):** The suspension was because there was no work, no sewing was sub-contracted (only cutting, with goods returned), and the two were offered the choice of cooperating in available work at the same wage or resting at home at normal wage pending recall with first priority; it had already agreed in writing to the maternity advance and to build a temporary then permanent childcare centre, and its new director would review but not dismiss the administration head. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43][Para 49][Para 50]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to provide immediately the same work as before to Mr S and Mr M (meaning it must begin accepting them back in their original skill from the date the award takes effect, with 2 or 3 weeks allowed for the arrangement); to pay three months' wage to women workers before maternity leave under Article 183, on 7 days' written notice; and to create at once a temporary childcare centre under the control and recognition of the Labour Health Officer, with a full regular centre within one year. The demand to dismiss the administration head was rejected. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 87][Para 88]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 293, under which dismissal of a shop steward requires the labour inspector's authorisation and any reassignment or transfer ending the steward's term is subject to the same procedure. Though Article 293 does not mention suspension, a suspension of undetermined duration has the same effect as a transfer that prevents the steward from performing his duties, so the employer violated Article 293; the panel also noted the suspensions lacked any official document showing the labour inspector's approval, and that the employer had by now had enough time to reassign the two to sewing. [Para 59][Para 61][Para 62][Para 65][Para 34][Para 66]

The panel set limits on managerial discretion: the employer may change work where wage is not reduced, the workplace is not moved far, day and night shifts are not swapped, and the specific professional skills are unaffected, since otherwise the employment contract is violated; similar, non-complex skills may be used, but training must precede the change, and for shop stewards termination and work change must follow the legal procedure as well. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70]

Issues 2 and 3 were decided on the parties' agreement: the company had already accepted both in writing, and the panel recorded the maternity payment, the 7-day written notice, and the one-year timetable for the permanent centre. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 43][Para 86]

On issue 4 the panel followed its own Awards No. 04/03 (Lida Garment) and No. 14/03: under Article 65 only the parties to the contract may terminate it, so hiring and firing is the employer's prerogative, and Article 83 B(4) does not require an employer to dismiss anyone — it merely preserves the right to dismiss without notice where the 7-day action requirement of Article 26 is met. The panel may order transfer, or in the most serious case dismissal, only where an employee is dangerous or a hazard to others' health and safety, and it found no such evidence against the administration head despite finding some of his acts deficient. [Para 73][Para 76][Para 78][Para 79][Para 80][Para 82][Para 84]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An open-ended suspension of a shop steward, or any reassignment or shift change that effectively stops the steward performing his duties, is a violation of Article 293 unless the labour inspector has authorised it; and an employer changing an employee's work or shift lawfully only where wage, workplace distance, shift direction and specific professional skill are unaffected, with prior training for any new skill required. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 66][Para 69]

**Obiter dicta:** The new director's undertakings to review the administration head's conduct, correct any mistakes found, improve employee–employer relations and secure more purchase orders; and the workers' complaint that internal rules and Article 83 are applied to ordinary workers but not to managers, which the company denied. [Para 49][Para 52][Para 55][Para 58]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The date of award is printed only as "11-2003", with no day. The employer is named "Ho Hing Garment." with the company suffix lost, and the dispute is framed by reference to a non-conciliation report dated September 12, 2003 sent on 15-09-2003. The administration head's name is printed inconsistently — the surname appears only in the issue list and the given names are spelled "Kem" in one place and "Kim" in another — and is redacted here. The number of employees who thumbprinted the dismissal demand is given as 460, though the workforce figure elsewhere in the file is not stated consistently. Sentences are fused where OCR dropped breaks, e.g. "For the decision issue 1, Article 293 of the Labour Law is relied on." [Para 1][Para 10][Para 33][Para 45][Para 58]
