# Award No. 11/03 — Cambodiana Hotel (Arbitration Council, 29-08-2003)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, employer-appointed; Tuon Siphann, worker-appointed; Ang Eng Thong, chair) · Doc ID 138829

## 1. Facts
Cambodiana Hotel, operating since 1990 and employing about 440 workers, adds a mandatory 10% "service charge" to guest bills and has long distributed 30% of the amount collected as a monthly service collection bonus, alongside seniority bonuses, discretionary year-end bonuses and benefits in kind. The Cambodiana Employees Union claimed the hotel owed 100% of the service charge collected under the Labour Law; conciliation failed and the non-conciliation report No. 1213 dated July 18, 2003 went to the Secretariat on 21-07-2003. [Para 23][Para 26][Para 10]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the hotel must pay employees 100% of the service charge collected? [Para 8]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** With no clear distribution mechanism they had received only 30% of the service charge, not the 100% the law requires, and the union's complaint to the Minister of Labour of April 25, 2003 also alleged threats against union leaders. [Para 28][Para 20]
**Employer (respondent):** The 10% added to bills is a service charge but the sum distributed must be read together with the wage package: 30% paid monthly plus seniority and year-end bonuses and benefits in kind said to total about 172,20% of the service charge collected, so nothing further is owed. [Para 55][Para 30][Para 29]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim for back-payment of prak thonianikruah was refused, but the panel found the hotel had breached the Labour Law by its method of calculation and ordered it to pay US$50 in damages to every employee who received the 30% service bonus and worked during the past three years (including workers whose employment ended in 2003), on or before 31 October 2003, paid directly and in full unless the parties agree otherwise; the hotel must also, within 2 months, establish a clear and transparent distribution method in consultation with representative unions, publicise it, and give monthly accounts of service charge collected and distributed. [Para 57][Para 61][Para 62][Para 63][Para 64]

The panel applied Article 134, which requires a mandatory percentage added to a client's bill "for service" to be collected and distributed in full to personnel in contact with clientele — the Khmer term being prak thonianikruah. It rejected the argument that the English word "Tip" in translation meant prak tik tae rather than service charge: although the bills are not marked somrap seva, any percentage added to guests' bills as a service charge is prak thonianikruah within Article 134, and the 10% must therefore be paid in full. [Para 42][Para 44][Para 46][Para 48]

On method, Article 136 leaves distribution to "professional practice" and Article 135 requires a clear account of sums received and distributed. Treating prak thonianikruah as part of wages under Articles 102 and 103, the panel held it must be paid in full every month under Article 116(2) and in cash under Article 113. An employer may nevertheless comply either by paying it on top of a basic wage or by a system, agreed or accepted by practice, folding all or part of it into a wage package, provided wages are paid in full monthly in cash, the method is clear and transparent, monthly accounts are given, and the amount actually paid is not less than a guaranteed minimum. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51][Para 52][Para 54]

Weighing the evidence, the panel found that the amounts distributed (30% monthly plus 85% of the amount collected as service charge paid as seniority and year-end bonuses) exceeded the employees' legal entitlement, so back-payment could not be granted; it was not decisive that the employer labelled as "bonuses" sums that should have been attributed to prak thonianikruah. The breach lay instead in the opaque method, the absence of monthly statements, payment of part of the share as discretionary yearly bonuses, and the mislabelling. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 58]

On remedy the panel invoked Article 34 of the Prakas on the Arbitration Council (#338 of 2002), which confers full power to grant any civil remedy or relief it deems just and fair, and compensated the economic loss from delayed payment and the uncertainty of discretionary bonuses. [Para 60][Para 61]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A mandatory percentage added to a guest's bill as a "service charge" is prak thonianikruah under Article 134 whatever its label, and must reach staff in full, monthly and in cash; an employer may satisfy this through a wage package rather than a separate payment, but only if the total paid is not less than the guaranteed entitlement, the distribution method is clear and transparent, and monthly accounts are given — a deficient method is itself a breach sounding in damages even where the sums actually paid were sufficient. [Para 48][Para 52][Para 58]

**Obiter dicta:** The ministerial letter of February 14, 1994 and the hotel's account of its pre-1997 practices; the observation that no law in other tourism markets fixes the method and time for distributing service charge, employers there acting under collective bargaining agreements or contracts; and the panel's several attempts at conciliation and mediation, which ended on August 25, 2003. [Para 28][Para 42][Para 64][Para 66]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Several figures are corrupted or internally doubtful: the benefits in kind are said to total "172,20%" of service charge collected; the sum paid through seniority and year-end bonuses appears as "85%" of the amount collected; the monthly bonus is described as "a tvariable Monthly Service Collection Bonus". Sentences are truncated throughout (for example "The confusion regarding this point seems to", "Thus in the opinion of the added to guests\" bills"), and the passage on mediation is printed twice in near-identical form. The employer-appointed arbitrator is printed "Mar Samborana" on the face and in the signature block. [Para 30][Para 55][Para 54][Para 44][Para 66][Para 10]
