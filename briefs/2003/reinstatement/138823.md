# Award No. 02/03 — Chou Sing Garment C/L (Arbitration Council, 11 June 2003)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mr An Nan for the worker party, Mr K Leong Lee for the employer party, Yim Yeat Dararith chair) · Doc ID 138823

## 1. Facts
Chou Sing Garment C/L, a garment factory on National Road 6A, Phnom Penh, deducted Riels 15,000 from the wage of each of six workers to cover a medical check carried out at the factory, and had done so twice. [Para 2][Para 8][Para 10] A list of workers refused the deduction and demanded reimbursement; the employer relies on an "Application Form" in which each worker is said to have accepted the charge. [Para 8][Para 17] The union says the workers were never told they would pay and would have refused the check had they known. [Para 15]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reimburse the wage deducted from each worker for the employee's medical check? [Para 2][Para 16]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the check was conducted at the factory with no notice that the workers would bear the cost; when the union raised the matter the employer refused to agree to reimbursement. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 15]
**Employer (respondent):** a check at the Labour Hospital Department costs only Riel 10,100 per worker, but calling the doctor to the factory costs Riel 15,000, which the workers should pay; the deduction was already negotiated with the union. [Para 9][Para 11][Para 12]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to reimburse the amounts deducted for the medical check from the salaries of the workers who are party to this case (Riels 15,000 per worker, deducted twice). [Para 29][Para 8]

The panel began with the hierarchy of laws: a Prakas cannot be less favourable than the Labour Law, and Prakas No. 09 of 19 January 1994 remains in force until replaced, so it is still enforceable. [Para 23] Article 13(1) of the Labour Law makes its provisions public order, so any rule arising from a unilateral decision, contract or convention that does not comply is null; the worker's agreement to pay for the check is therefore not valid. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 25] Articles 13(2) and 390 preserve benefits more favourable than the law granted by contract or practice, and the panel found the employer had paid for the checks of workers recruited before 1997, as recorded in the conciliation minute of 07.06.2000 and the work-condition minutes of 21.07.2001 and 08.05.2000 — so that more favourable practice continued to apply. [Para 18][Para 25][Para 26][Para 28] Article 391 required any clause not conforming to the law to be modified within six months of promulgation, which had not been done. [Para 28][Para 29] Finally Article 247(a) and (c) and Article 7 of Prakas No. 09 place the cost of the medical examination on the owner of the enterprise, and the panel found that Prakas not objectionable to Article 395. [Para 29]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may not shift the cost of a statutory medical examination onto the worker, even where the worker signed a form accepting the charge: Article 7 of Prakas No. 09 of 19 January 1994 obliges the enterprise owner to pay, and any contractual or unilateral arrangement less favourable than the Labour Law is null under Article 13(1). Where the employer had in fact been paying for such checks and that practice is recorded in a collective minute, the more favourable benefit survives under Articles 13(2) and 390 and must be modified under Article 391 before it can be withdrawn. [Para 23][Para 25][Para 28][Para 29]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks on the continued validity of a 1994 Prakas despite Article 395 and on the Ministry of Labour's continued recognition of it are general observations on the hierarchy of laws, not necessary to the order to reimburse. [Para 23][Para 29]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- A sentence embedded in the reasons at [Para 29] orders the employer to "cease such discriminatory practice and reinstate its previous practice of offering overtime work to Ms M Ai" — an overtime matter that belongs to a different award and is inconsistent with this case, which concerns only medical-check deductions. It was disregarded.
- The panel's names are broken across lines: the employer-party arbitrator appears as "Mr." at [Para 5] and "Kim Leong Lee" at [Para 6], the worker-party arbitrator as "Mr." at [Para 6] and "An Nan" at [Para 7], repeated without surnames at [Para 30]–[Para 33].
- The award date is printed only as "11-6-2003" [Para 1], with day/month order unstated; the hearing is recorded as 13.05.2003 and the non-conciliation report as 16-05-2003, submitted 23-05-2003 [Para 5][Para 7].
- The union could date the medical check only as "16-27-28 but not remember the month and year" [Para 13] — unusable.
- Minutes are dated 08.05.2003 in the evidence list [Para 7] but 08.05.2000 in the findings [Para 18]; amounts appear both as "Riels 15,000" and "Riel 10.100" (period used as thousands separator) [Para 9]; the Prakas reference is garbled as "Prakas #09 of 1994SKA" [Para 29].
