# Award No. 10/03 — Jacqsintex Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 23-07-2003)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mr You Suon Ti, employer-appointed; Mr An Nan, worker-appointed; Mr Kong Phallack, chair) · Doc ID 138828

## 1. Facts
Jacqsintex Garment Co., Ltd employed workers on six-month fixed-duration contracts renewed repeatedly, and the Democratic Union of Jacqsintex brought four collective claims, including the conversion of those contracts and the reinstatement of three dismissed union activists. Two of them, Mr W (#716, hired 1 June 2000, renewed 5 times) and Mr S (#967, hired 18 June 2001, renewed 3 times), had their contracts ended on 1 June 2003 and 18 June 2003 respectively; the third worker's (Mr T) claim was withdrawn. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 21][Para 10]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the Company must transform fixed-duration contracts to undetermined-duration contracts for employees whose employment lasts two years or where renewal occurs four times? [Para 5]
2. Whether employees may not be terminated for reasons other than serious misconduct? [Para 5]
3. Whether the Company must pay a $3 bonus to workers working more than 10 days overtime without setting production targets? [Para 5]
4. Whether the Company must reinstate the workers Mr T, Mr W and Mr S? [Para 5]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Successive six-month renewals and two years' service should convert to undetermined-duration contracts; the $3 overtime bonus was an entitlement that the Company had no right to condition on production targets; and the two dismissals followed their union activity and candidacy for union office. [Para 14][Para 46][Para 76]
**Employer (respondent):** It denied all four requests, said it would follow the 1997 Labour Law, claimed the right to terminate any worker at the end of the contract, said it did not know whether any workers were union members, and refused to produce the company report said to contain its reasons. [Para 15][Para 77][Para 78][Para 79]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel found that fixed-duration contracts convert to undetermined duration where total employment exceeds 2 years; made no order on issue 2 for want of evidence; allowed production targets for the overtime bonus subject to a clear, fair method achievable without endangering health; ordered reinstatement of Mr W (#716) and Mr S (#967) from 1 August 2003 on undetermined-duration contracts with accumulated seniority, with compensation of $96 (Mr W, monthly wage ($48), 2 months out of work) and $70.50 (Mr S, monthly wage ($47), 1.5 months out of work). No order was made for Mr T, whose claim was withdrawn. [Para 9][Para 10]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 67(2), which caps a fixed-duration contract at two years, and Article 73(5), under which a contract becomes undetermined-duration where the total length exceeds that limit. Finding the Khmer text unclear between "each renewal" and "total length", it read Article 67(7) and (8)'s bias toward undetermined-duration contracts, the original French text ("ne peut être conclu pour une durée supérieure à deux ans"), and ILO Recommendation 166 of 1982 as confirming that the two-year limit is a maximum total duration. [Para 24][Para 27][Para 32][Para 39][Para 40][Para 34]

On issue 2 the panel held that the Arbitration Council resolves existing disputes, not hypothetical ones, and the workers produced no evidence of any dismissal for a reason other than serious misconduct, so no decision was made. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44]

On issue 3 the panel held that an employer cannot unilaterally reduce salary, but that changing the work system and setting incentive targets lies within employer discretion in the garment industry, especially where workers had accepted the conditions for about two years; it therefore rejected the claim while requiring a fair allocation method. [Para 48][Para 50][Para 52][Para 53][Para 54]

On issue 4 the panel first held the dispute collective under Article 302, deferring to the Minister and Labour Inspectorate's classification, and noted it lacks jurisdiction over individual disputes. On the merits, worker #716's relationship exceeded two years and was therefore undetermined-duration, so termination required a valid reason under Article 74 with damages under Article 91 if absent; Article 12 and Article 279 forbid termination or non-renewal for union membership or activity. The employer conceded the workers were good, had hired replacements, and refused to disclose its report despite warning of the consequences, so the panel accepted the workers' testimony that the dismissals were tied to union activity and ordered reinstatement under Article 34 A of the Prakas. [Para 58][Para 57][Para 70][Para 72][Para 73][Para 75][Para 81][Para 82]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-duration contract converts to an undetermined-duration contract once any renewal pushes the total employment relationship past the two-year ceiling of Article 67(2); such a worker may only be dismissed for a valid reason under Article 74, and neither non-renewal nor termination may rest on union membership or activity under Articles 12 and 279 — an employer who withholds its stated reasons after being warned does so at the cost of the panel accepting the workers' account. [Para 41][Para 70][Para 73][Para 81]

**Obiter dicta:** The observation that undetermined-duration contracts benefit employers through greater employee commitment; that ILO Recommendation 166 is persuasive but not binding; and the conflict-of-interest discussion, where the panel found the Company had waived its objection to Mr An Nan after being told of his CLO work at the 3 July 2003 hearing. [Para 33][Para 36][Para 85][Para 87]
