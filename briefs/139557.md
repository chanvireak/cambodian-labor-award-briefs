# Award No. 099/14 — Unite Five Sen (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 May 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Vireak, appointed by the workers; Tang Tri, president) · Doc ID 139557

## 1. Facts
Unite Five Sen (Cambodia) Co., Ltd carries on business at Anhchanh village, Ponhea district, Kandal province. A strike led by the Free Trade Union of Workers of the Kingdom of Cambodia produced nine collective demands: a USD 12 midday meal allowance, a USD 12 transport allowance, wage payment three days before payday and on the 10th of the month, no deduction on the 17th, no deduction from workers who take sick leave supported by a medical certificate, pro-rata deduction only for annual leave, no deduction during the strike, and pay slips issued in Khmer. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 5][Para 8] Conciliation at the Kandal provincial department of labour on 23 April 2014 settled three of the twelve points raised; the nine unresolved points were forwarded to the Council on 25 April 2014 by non-conciliation report No. 465/14. [Para 18][Para 19] The panel was constituted on 28 April 2014 and sat on 5 and 12 May 2014; the workers attended the first hearing only. [Para 20][Para 15][Para 24]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether, where the claimant workers were duly summoned but failed to appear at the second hearing without valid reason, the panel should continue the arbitration in their absence or terminate it by award? [Para 39][Para 52]
2. Whether the three conditions attached to that choice — proper notification, non-appearance, and absence of a valid excuse — were satisfied in this case? [Para 47][Para 51]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The workers advanced the nine demands recorded in the non-conciliation report and appeared at the first hearing, but filed an authorisation letter the panel found defective and were directed to correct it; they did not appear at the adjourned hearing. [Para 1][Para 22][Para 62]
**Employer (respondent):** Appearing through Ms T under an authorisation signed by the company director on 12 May 2014, the company maintained its refusal of all nine demands and asked that the dispute be referred to the Arbitration Council for resolution. [Para 26][Para 18]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** terminated — the panel closed the arbitration of case No. 099/14; no order was made on the merits and no sum was awarded [Para 76][Para 77]

The panel began from Article 18 of Prakas No. 099 (21 April 2004), which obliges it to summon the parties to state their claims in person and to supply the necessary documents, and from Articles 20 and 21, which require parties to attend every meeting and permit the panel, where a properly summoned party fails to appear without valid reason, either to proceed in its absence or to close the arbitration by award. Rule 4.7 of the annexed procedural rules was read the same way. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41][Para 42] Reading these together, the panel held that termination is available only on three cumulative conditions, and it adopted without dissent the three-condition test it had applied in earlier awards. [Para 47][Para 51]

On notification, the secretariat issued invitation No. 486 dated 29 April 2014 for the first hearing, telephoned the workers at 09:34 that day, and for the second hearing issued invitation No. 525 of 5 May 2014, which was hand-delivered at the factory on 6 May 2014 to four staff delegates and two workers who received it but declined to sign. The panel held that receipt, not signature, established notification. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 57][Para 58] It therefore found condition one met. [Para 59]

On appearance, the panel gave "appearing before the panel" a substantive meaning: the party must both be present and take part in the arbitration from beginning to end, through the four stages of introduction and conflict-of-interest disclosure, explanation of procedure and issues, conciliation if the parties agree, and hearing on the substance. The workers were absent at the second hearing and had appointed no authorised representative for it. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 62] Condition two was met. [Para 63]

On valid reason, the only explanation came from Mr K, deputy secretary-general of the union, recorded on 12 May 2014: the union president was busy and had not delegated authority to him, and in any event no workers had come. The panel held that this was not a valid reason for non-appearance. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 67] All three conditions being satisfied, and having found itself unable to establish the facts of the claims because of the absence, the panel treated the claimant as having abandoned its claims and closed the proceedings without further hearing. [Para 38][Para 74][Para 75]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the claimant party has been properly notified of the date, time and place of a hearing, fails to appear and participate, and offers no valid reason, the panel may treat the claims as abandoned and terminate the arbitration by award without ruling on their merits. [Para 47][Para 74][Para 75]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed more generally that a respondent's duty to adduce reasons and evidence arises only after the claimant has first discharged its own, and that both parties owe a duty to attend every meeting the panel convenes. [Para 45][Para 72]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is a Khmer original whose OCR is uneven. The signature block at the foot of the award is badly corrupted, so the arbitrators' names are legible only in the composition paragraph in the header and not from their signatures. [Para 78] The list of prior awards relied on for the three-condition test is heavily garbled, with case numbers and party names run together and partially lost, so those citations cannot be verified from this text. [Para 50] The letterhead address block carries the year "2017" although the award is dated 22 May 2014. [Para 4] The third demand (pay slip three days before payday) is printed inside that letterhead block rather than with the other demands, and the demand numbered 1 sits at the end of the header line, indicating that OCR has reordered the list. [Para 1][Para 4] The procedural narrative states that twelve points went to conciliation and nine remained unresolved, but only nine demands appear in the text. [Para 18] The employer's village is printed once as "Tonleap Teuk" and once as "Tonleap Tik", and the final order misprints "Arbitration Council" as "អាជ្ញាធរកណ្តាល". [Para 1][Para 76]
