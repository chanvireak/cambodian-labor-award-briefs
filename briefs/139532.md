# Award No. 047/14 — True Time Phnom Penh Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 28 March 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kav Thach for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139532

## 1. Facts
True Time Phnom Penh Co Ltd, an office-based company on Street 217 in Phnom Penh employing about 200 staff, dismissed Mr H, an office employee engaged on 1 June 2011 under a written contract with no end date; the company says he was repeatedly late, drunk at work, absent without leave and, on the day of dismissal, incited colleagues to demonstrate for a higher wage. [Para 2][Para 40][Para 41][Para 50][Para 62] The Cambodian Unions Confederation, which had no local union at the company, filed for his reinstatement with back pay and for an end to threats and anti-union discrimination; at hearing the two demands were merged into one. [Para 3][Para 43][Para 46] The parties joined issue on whether the panel had jurisdiction at all, the employer calling it an individual dispute. [Para 91][Para 92]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the dismissal of Mr H was lawful, and whether the employer must reinstate him with back pay or, failing that, pay statutory compensation? [Para 45][Para 48]
2. Whether the panel has jurisdiction over the claim as a collective labour dispute under Article 302 of the Labour Law? [Para 94][Para 100]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr H is a good employee and did none of what is alleged; he acknowledges being late because of traffic but says he telephoned the personnel chief in advance, he did not incite anyone to strike and no strike in fact occurred, he only wanted the company to respect the Labour Law, and he had been elected president of the factory union on 18 January 2014. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 70][Para 76]
**Employer (respondent):** the company cannot take him back because of repeated lateness, a 2011 warning, coming to work intoxicated, leaving for two or three hours without the shift leader's permission, delaying work and watching video during working hours, and incitement of colleagues to demonstrate although he already earned $210 a month; the dismissal was preventive. [Para 4][Para 62][Para 73]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to reinstate Mr H and pay his wages from the date of dismissal to the date of return and, if reinstatement is impossible, to pay one month's wages in lieu of notice, 45 days' wages and allowances as dismissal damages, payment for unused annual leave, moral damages equal to 45 days' wages, and any unpaid final wages. [Para 191][Para 192]

The panel first fixed the date of dismissal. The conciliation record and the employer's own reports put it at 20 January 2014, while one filing said 20 February 2014; the panel noted the inconsistency and adopted 20 January 2014. [Para 55][Para 58][Para 61]

On jurisdiction it applied Article 302 of the Labour Law, which defines a collective dispute as one between an employer and some of its workers about working conditions, the exercise of rights conferred on professional organisations, recognition of such organisations, or relations between employer and workers, where the dispute may disturb the operation of the enterprise or harm social peace. [Para 96][Para 100][Para 101] It found the first condition met even though only one person was dismissed, because the right to strike is a collective right that would involve several workers and the dismissal was prompted by an anticipated strike, not by the conduct of one man alone. [Para 104][Para 106][Para 110][Para 111] The second and third conditions were also met, and the panel held it had jurisdiction. [Para 112][Para 114][Para 119]

On the merits the contract had no end date and so was of indefinite duration under Article 67, and Article 74 allows dismissal only with a valid reason related to the worker's capability or conduct or the needs of the enterprise. [Para 120][Para 123][Para 127] The employer bore the burden of proving serious misconduct. [Para 130][Para 131] Article 83(b)(5) makes incitement of other workers to commit serious misconduct a ground of serious misconduct, but the panel followed earlier awards holding that incitement to strike is not serious misconduct unless the employer proves the worker incited violence during a strike. [Para 135][Para 136][Para 141] No strike had occurred, and the employer admitted it dismissed him preventively on hearsay that he planned one. [Para 73][Para 139][Para 140] The dismissal was therefore without valid reason. [Para 142][Para 185]

Compensation followed the statutory heads: one month's wages and benefits in lieu of notice under Articles 75 and 77, since he had about 2 years 7 months' service; dismissal damages of 45 days under Article 89; payment for unused annual leave under Articles 166 and 168, the panel finding he was entitled to 18 days a year rather than the 6 granted; and moral damages under Article 91 equal to the dismissal damages. [Para 147][Para 155][Para 170][Para 174][Para 186]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A dismissal prompted by an anticipated strike is a collective labour dispute, not an individual one, and falls within the panel's jurisdiction under Article 302 even where a single worker is dismissed, because the right to strike is exercised collectively and the employer's motive concerns the workforce. [Para 106][Para 110][Para 114] Incitement to strike is not serious misconduct under Article 83(b)(5) unless the employer proves that the worker incited violence in the course of an actual strike; an employer that dismisses preventively on hearsay has no valid reason under Article 74 and owes notice, dismissal damages and moral damages. [Para 136][Para 141][Para 185]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that even if the dismissal affected only one man its cause lay in a planned collective walkout, and its observation that a strike in a company of 200 could disturb operations and social peace, are explanation of the jurisdictional finding rather than rules of decision. [Para 105][Para 118]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The dismissal date is given as 20 February 2014 in the demand statement [Para 11] and as 20 January 2014 in the conciliation record and the employer's shift reports [Para 53][Para 57]; the panel recorded the contradiction and adopted 20 January 2014 [Para 58][Para 61].
- The commercial registration appears as No. Co.0667 E/2011 dated 17 December 2012 in the findings [Para 40] and as certificate No. 7491 dated 27 December 2012 in the evidence list [Para 33].
- The internal rules are cited in three versions: dated 22 January 2013 [Para 32], dated 11 November 2013 and endorsed on 11 December 2013 [Para 81], and quoted with the annual-leave clause reading "18 days [of] 2" [Para 165].
- The claimant's name is printed inconsistently, appearing as "Hem Thu" in most of the award and as "Hem Joo" in the conciliation extract [Para 55]; it is redacted here as Mr H.
- He is described throughout as a "បុគ្គលិក" (staff employee) rather than a "កម្មករ" (worker), yet the collective-dispute analysis turns on the rights of workers; the award does not address that classification.
