# Order No. 110/11 — Shimano (Cambodia) Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 August 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Eang Sothy, employer-appointed; Ven Pov, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · order under Article 313 of the Labour Law · Doc ID 140070 [Para 1]

## 1. Facts and nature of the dispute

Shimano (Cambodia) Co Ltd is a garment and bicycle-parts factory at Trapeang Roka village, Trapeang Kong commune, Samrong Tong district, Kampong Speu province. The workers were represented by the Free Workers' Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia and the Free Workers' Union of Shimano Factory. [Para 1]

On 11 August 2011 the Kampong Speu Provincial Department of Labour received a complaint from the union alleging that the company did not respect the Labour Law and discriminated against the union. [Para 11] Conciliation was convened for 18 August 2011; the company did not attend, taking the position that it did not recognise the union because it had never received any document from it. [Para 13] The union then served strike notice to press its demands. [Para 5]

This document is not a merits award. It is an interim order on whether the panel may continue to arbitrate at all while the strike is running. [Para 37][Para 45]

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. Whether the workers' party may lawfully continue its strike while the Arbitration Council's proceedings are pending? [Para 37]
2. Whether the panel should continue to arbitrate the collective labour dispute, or decline to proceed and close the case? [Para 44][Para 46]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** The union asked the company to stop union discrimination, to take back the three workers it had dismissed, and — if the company refused — to pay each of them USD 3,000 in compensation. [Para 2][Para 3][Para 4] At the pre-hearing meeting the workers stated that they would not end the strike unless the employer allowed the three dismissed union leaders to return to normal work. [Para 21]

**Employer (respondent):** The company said it would allow all strikers to return to work according to their shifts, except for the three union leaders. [Para 22] It had already refused to attend conciliation on the ground that it did not recognise the union. [Para 13]

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** no further arbitration — the panel decided and ordered that arbitration in case No. 110/11 — Shimano (Cambodia) not continue. No monetary award was made. [Para 46]

The panel first set out the legal frame. Prakas No. 099 SKBY of 21 April 2004 on the Arbitration Council, Article 20, requires that during the Council's proceedings the parties suspend all dispute measures such as strike or lockout under Article 318 of the Labour Law, and any other measure that could aggravate the dispute; the parties must attend every meeting the panel convenes. [Para 37][Para 38]

On the facts, the Council had already ordered the workers on 24 August 2011 to suspend the strike immediately and to return to work on their shifts from 25 August 2011 until the arbitration was lawfully completed. [Para 16] The workers did not return and continued the strike. [Para 17] At the pre-hearing meeting on 26 August 2011 both sides appeared, the workers maintained they would not stop until the three leaders were reinstated, and the employer maintained its exception for those three. [Para 19][Para 21][Para 22] The panel gave the parties until 29 August 2011 to return to work, but was told on that date that the workers had still not returned and the employer still would not accept the three leaders. [Para 26][Para 27]

The panel then reviewed its own prior jurisprudence. In Order No. 04-IMSI of 29 January 2004 it had held that a party to a labour dispute has no right to strike during the arbitration proceedings, and that the Council cannot resolve or issue an order on a collective dispute referred to it in those conditions; it may take the dispute up again once the union stops the strike. [Para 40][Para 41] In Case No. 77/05 — Franco Nid Ting Garmen, the order of 24 December 2005 stated that where the workers continue to strike the Council will not proceed with the collective dispute. [Para 42] In Case No. 28/06 — GDM and Case No. 34/06 — Goldfame the Council likewise closed the files because the workers kept striking during its proceedings. [Para 43]

Applying that line, the panel found that in Case No. 110/11 the workers' party had continued the strike throughout, in disregard of the Council's order and request. [Para 44] It therefore decided not to continue the arbitration. [Para 45]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

Where a party to a collective labour dispute keeps striking after the Arbitration Council has ordered suspension of the strike under Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY and Article 318 of the Labour Law, the Council will not proceed with the arbitration; it may resume the case only once the strike has ended. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 45]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that both parties in this case are signatories to the 28 September 2010 Memorandum of Understanding on improving professional relations in the garment industry, which encourages peaceful dispute resolution and permits strike or lockout only at the last resort after the peaceful procedures are exhausted. [Para 23][Para 24] That observation did not drive the outcome.
