# Award No. 26/11-June Textile — June Textile Garment Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 9 March 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phyrum, Ven Pov, Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 140007

## 1. Facts
June Textile Garment Co., Ltd. operates a garment factory on Russian Federation Boulevard, Teouk Thla Commune, Sen Sok District, Phnom Penh. Its workers are organised in the Free Trade Union of Workers of the Kingdom of Cambodia (FTUWKC) local union. The workers demanded that the employer dismiss two named co-workers, Meng Kry and Ing Sokret Yuthea; the employer replied that the workers and union leaders must first provide evidence of those individuals' misconduct before it could dismiss them. Conciliation on 17 February 2011 failed and non-conciliation report No. 210 KB/RK/VK was sent to the Arbitration Council on 21 February 2011. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 5]

A strike followed; the Council issued an interim order on 24 February 2011 and the striking workers returned on the morning of 26 February 2011. The hearing was fixed for 3 March 2011 and both parties were summoned by letters dated 28 February 2011. The employer attended; the union and workers did not attend at all. [Para 7][Para 8][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must dismiss Meng Kry and Ing Sokret Yuthea as the workers demand, and whether the workers and FTUWKC leaders must first provide evidence of the two individuals' misconduct before any dismissal may occur? [Para 2]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** demanded the dismissal of Meng Kry and Ing Sokret Yuthea, but did not attend the hearing, did not select an arbitrator, and gave no reason for their absence. [Para 2][Para 21][Para 24]

**Employer (respondent):** attended the hearing and maintained that the workers and union leaders must provide evidence of the alleged misconduct before the company could dismiss the two employees. [Para 2][Para 25]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed (case closed) — the panel ordered "Close case 26/11-June Textile" without ruling on the merits of the dismissal demand. [Para 32]

Rule 4.7 of the Arbitration Council's Procedural Rules (Annex to Prakas No. 099, 21 April 2004) allows the panel, where a party fails to appear in person or by representative, to proceed in that party's absence or to terminate the proceedings by award. Clause 21 of the same Prakas permits the same course where a duly invited party fails to appear without good cause. [Para 13]

Following Awards 16/07-Lotus and 27/07-M&V (Branch 3), the panel applied a three-condition test for closing a case: the party was properly notified, the party did not appear, and the party gave no proper reason for non-appearance. [Para 13][Para 14]

On the first condition, the Secretariat issued official notice of the 3 March 2011 hearing by letter No. 170 KB/RK/VK/LKR dated 28 February 2011, and the workers were absent on the day without informing the Council why. The condition was fulfilled. [Para 14][Para 15]

On the second condition, the panel read "appear before the arbitration panel" as requiring both presence at the hearing and participation in the whole process, which comprises four stages: introduction and disclosure of conflicts of interest; explanation of the process and confirmation of the issues; conciliation if the parties agree; and arbitration. The union and workers attended none of the four stages and also failed to attend the selection of an arbitrator. The condition was fulfilled. [Para 16][Para 20][Para 21][Para 22][Para 23]

On the third condition, the union and workers gave no proper reason for not attending, while the employer attended as summoned. The condition was fulfilled. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 26]

The panel further noted that the FTUWKC local union had been given ample opportunity to argue the workers' claim under the Labour Law but opted for non-appearance; that the worker party appeared not to intend to resolve the dispute lawfully; and that, although a claimant is obliged to argue its claim with reasons and evidence, this claimant did neither and so lost the opportunity. The panel concluded that the worker party had dropped its claim and closed the case. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 29][Para 30][Para 31][Para 32]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a party has been properly notified of an arbitration hearing, fails to appear and to participate in any of the four procedural stages, and offers no proper reason for its non-appearance, the panel may treat the claim as dropped and close the case by award under Rule 4.7 and Clause 21 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 32]

**Obiter dicta:** the observations that non-appearance signalled an unwillingness to resolve the dispute through the Council and that the claimant had forfeited its chance to present reasons and evidence explain the closure but are not part of the three-condition test. [Para 28][Para 30]
