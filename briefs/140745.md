# Award No. 106/06 — Kwik Su (Cambodia) Ltd and the Khmer Youth Union of Kwik Su Factory (Arbitration Council, 1 December 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Yoo Sounty, Venh Pov, chair Tang Tri) · Doc ID 140745

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឃ្វីក ស៊ូ ខេមបូឌា អិលធីឌី (Kwik Su (Cambodia) Ltd) opened on 05 August 2005 with 433 workers and by the hearing employed 800–900 under one union, សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ នៃ រោងចក្រ ឃ្វីក ស៊ូ (Khmer Youth Union of Kwik Su Factory). [Para 31] [Para 53] The labour dispute department received the federation's eight demands on 09 October 2006; conciliation failed on 23 October 2006 and report No. 1605 of 27 October 2006 sent the case to the panel, which heard it on 16 and 17 November 2006. [Para 22] [Para 20] The factory's 37 toilets are closed from 07:00 to 07:15. [Para 33] [Para 36]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must open the toilet doors at 07:00 rather than 07:15? [Para 123]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must provide the union a meeting room and a place to post news of union activities and leaders under Circular No. 40 SKBY of 01 October 1998? [Para 4]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must hold a fresh election of worker delegates because the delegation leaves seats unfilled and does not act for the workers? [Para 167]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must cancel its three-month fixed-duration contracts, or make them contracts of one year or more? [Para 212]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the company must reinstate a dismissed union leader, Mr D, and pay him back wages? [Para 217]
6. (Issue 6) Whether the company must keep in place one day of unpaid and three days of paid leave? [Para 227]
7. (Issue 7) Whether the company must deduct 1,000 riel from the wage of each worker joining the Khmer Youth Union as dues? [Para 230]
8. (Issue 8) Whether the company must remove the military police officer posted inside the company? [Para 244]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that workers arriving at 06:30 need the toilets at 07:00, the locked quarter-hour plus a 100-metre walk being unreasonable. [Para 38] The delegates do nothing for them and are too few for a workforce nearing 800, and the three-month contracts began only after the union formed, easing dismissal of members. [Para 57] [Para 77] The guard pulled cards, showed his weapon and poked a worker's waist with his antenna. [Para 105]
**Employer (respondent):** that the closure lets cleaners scrub the toilets and team leaders allocate work, and any worker in urgent need may ask her team leader to be let out. [Para 37] [Para 43] It lends the delegates a room two hours each Saturday and says no law requires a union meeting room in a rented building. [Para 48] [Para 49] Three-month contracts cover 90 per cent of the garment sector and let it refuse renewal to weak probationers; it refused the dues deduction for want of a law, and defended the guard as the director's protector. [Para 79] [Para 231] [Para 245]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — demands 1, 3, 4, 5 and 8 were rejected; demand 2 was rejected as to a meeting room but granted as to a place to post the union leaders' names, photographs, functions and activities; demand 6 was recorded as settled by the agreement of 08 August 2006; and on demand 7 the employer must deduct 1,000 riel per member within 15 days of receiving the requesting file. [Para 279] [Para 280] [Para 284] [Para 285]
On the toilets, Article 2(2) of the Labour Law gives the employer full right to manage the enterprise and its workers' time, and Article 4 of the internal rules sets 07:00–12:00 and 13:00–16:00. [Para 130] [Para 132] Article 229 requires clean premises and Article 6 of Prakas No. 52 of 10 February 2000 requires water, soap and daily cleaning, so the closure performs those duties rather than breaching them. [Para 134] [Para 145]
On accommodation, Prakas No. 286 SKBY of 05 November 2001 Article 5 obliges the employer to give worker delegates a place to meet, and neither the Labour Law nor that Prakas requires a place for union meetings. [Para 146] [Para 147] Circular No. 40 SKA of 01 October 1998 does require a suitable place to post delegates' and leaders' names, photographs, functions and activities, which was missing. [Para 149] [Para 152]
On the delegates, Articles 283, 285 and 292 fix the right to elect delegates, the ratio to workforce and the duty to organise elections; the election of 28 November 2005 returned five full and five alternate delegates for 416 workers. [Para 153] [Para 156] [Para 159] Article 7 of Prakas No. 286 allows removal only for failure to perform duties and Article 292 paragraph 2 allows a fresh election only at expiry of the mandate. [Para 168] [Para 172] As the workers sought dissolution of the whole delegation rather than removal of a named delegate, and no law provides for adding delegates as the workforce grows, the demand was refused on equity. [Para 176] [Para 190]
On the contracts, Article 67.7 of the Labour Law converts an unwritten fixed-duration contract into one of indefinite duration, applied to the 35–40 per cent still on oral contracts. [Para 191] [Para 192] Under Article 65 only the company and each worker are parties, so the union and the 124 workers who thumb-printed the demand had no standing, and Articles 1, 3.1 and 22 of Kram No. 38 of 28 October 1988 confine a contract to its parties. [Para 199] [Para 203] [Para 214] No evidence showed the term punished membership. [Para 206] [Para 210]
On the dismissal, the claim had been decided by Issue 1 of Award No. 62/06 of 18 August 2006, and the panel refused to reopen it, applying res judicata as in Awards 18/04, 08/06, 10/06 and 24/06. [Para 219] [Para 222] [Para 223]
On the guard, Article 65 and Article 1 of the Labour Law place him under an employment contract, but Article 14 keeps the Royal Gendarmerie outside it, the relationship being private only. [Para 246] [Para 253] Assigning tasks is the employer's prerogative and no specific injury was proved, so removal was refused. [Para 264] [Para 277]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A short daily closure of toilet access, justified by the cleaning and inspection duties of Article 229 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 52, is management prerogative, not a hygiene breach. [Para 137] [Para 145] An employer must give a registered union a place to post its leaders' names, photographs and activities under Circular No. 40, but must provide a room only for worker delegates to meet, not for union meetings. [Para 149] [Para 152] A delegation elected under Articles 283 to 292 cannot be dissolved early at the workers' request, and the absence of a topping-up mechanism makes additional elections fail on equity. [Para 171] [Para 186] A union and members who are not parties to the individual contract cannot compel a change in its duration; only the contracting worker can, and only Article 67.7 converts an unwritten term into an indefinite one. [Para 192] [Para 214] A decided matter will not be reheard, and where the union holds its registration certificate and documented consents the employer must make the agreed dues deduction. [Para 222] [Para 238]

**Obiter dicta:** that hygiene complaints should go to the worker delegates and the labour inspector, and that workers may complain at any time about threats to their safety even though no removal order issued. [Para 140] [Para 278]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company's position is summarised as reopening the toilets at 07:15, but the reasons print "17:15", an OCR slip reported as printed. [Para 3] [Para 124] The finding on arrival times prints "10 06:30", and the delegate count appears as 416 against 433 at start-up. [Para 125] [Para 31] The company's name is OCR'd three ways;  the reasoning refers twice to Award No. "462/06" where every other reference is to 62/06. [Para 232] [Para 235] Order 7 repeats its own operative sentence, and the signature block garbles the arbitrators' names. [Para 285] [Para 288] The worker delegate and union officers are reduced to initials per convention. [Para 283]
