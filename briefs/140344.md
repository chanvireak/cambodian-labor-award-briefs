# Award No. 141/10 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន អាយ ប៊ើរ៉ាស៊ា ខូ អិលធីឌី (I Brasserie Co., Ltd.) (Arbitration Council, 10 January 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia (ក្រុមប្រឹក្សាអាជ្ញាកណ្តាល) · 3 arbitrators — យូ សួន ទី (employer-appointed), អាន វីរៈ (worker-appointed), chair ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា · Doc ID 140344

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន អាយ ប៊ើរ៉ាស៊ា ខូ អិលធីឌី (I Brasserie Co., Ltd.), a weaving and garment plant that opened in 2009 with about 250 workers and two unions, faced a collective dispute over working conditions brought by សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ នៃ ព្រះរាជាណាចក្រ កម្ពុជា, the Free Workers' Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia, through its factory branch. Conciliation on 29 November 2010 settled nine of eleven demands; the three unresolved points reached the Council on 15 December 2010 [Para 14][Para 15]. At the hearing of 23 December 2010 the workers withdrew the first demand — reinstatement of six workers whose contracts had not been renewed — so only the second and third demands were decided [Para 18][Para 19].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must state clear reasons and evidence before ending or not renewing a worker's fixed-duration contract? [Para 55]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must convert its six-month fixed-duration contracts into two-year terms? [Para 64]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ:** the company never explained why it declined to renew the contracts of the 114 weaving-section workers, although an agreement between the union and the company obliged it to give reasons; workers should be told whether their work was good or bad and what fault was held against them [Para 59][Para 61].
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន អាយ ប៊ើរ៉ាស៊ា ខូ អិលធីឌី:** the Labour Law requires no reason when a fixed-duration contract is allowed to expire, renewal is the contracting party's own right, and in fact the company gave seven days' notice plus two paid days to look for new work [Para 62][Para 56].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both remaining demands rejected; the instrument is a non-binding order (សេចក្តីបង្គាប់មិនជាប់កាតព្វកិច្ច) that becomes final eight days after notification if no party objects in writing [Para 106][Para 107][Para 108].
On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 73(a) khann 1, 2 and 5 of the 1997 Labour Law: a fixed-duration contract lapses automatically at its stated date, may be cut short only by written agreement made before the labour inspector or for serious misconduct or force majeure, and where the term exceeds six months the worker must be notified ten days before expiry [Para 71][Para 74]. The contracts at issue ran six months, the company gave seven days' notice with two paid days, and the agreement the workers invoked was never produced in evidence, so no reason had to be stated [Para 89][Para 87][Para 90].
On Issue 3 the panel applied Article 65 of the Labour Law and Articles 1 and 22 of Kret-Law No. 38 on contracts and non-contractual liability: a labour contract is born of consent, and no one outside the parties may impose its terms [Para 92][Para 97]. A two-year term therefore required the employer's agreement, which was absent, and a six-month term was in itself lawful [Para 103][Para 104].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-duration labour contract terminates by itself at expiry, and an employer that gives the notice the law prescribes commits no breach by declining to renew without stating reasons or proving fault; equally, a demand that the agreed term be rewritten into a longer one fails unless both contracting parties consent, since their consent is what constitutes the contract.

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the parties ordinarily know the start and end dates of a fixed-duration contract and can prepare for renewal or non-renewal, and it recorded that union membership had fallen from roughly 150 to about 20, the employer saying it never received a membership list [Para 83][Para 51][Para 52].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The reasoning at one point calls the respondent "ក្រុមហ៊ុន AKP", a name that appears nowhere on the face of the award, where the employer is consistently I Brasserie; the brief follows the face of the award. The number of affected workers is printed two ways: three workers' non-renewals are listed among the company's documents, while the reasons speak of 114 workers in the weaving section. The six individual workers dismissed under the withdrawn demand are named in full in the source; they appear here only as roles. Panel signatures are partly OCR noise ("modeon", a stray "Hom. Zeak"), and the arbitrator's name is printed both as យូ សួន ទី and, in the signature block, with a trailing digit.
