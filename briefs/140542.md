# Award No. 103/08 — Vivatino Design (Cambodia) Garment Pte. Ltd (Arbitration Council, 8 September 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Liv Sovanna, Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 140542

## 1. Facts
Vivatino Design (Cambodia) Garment Pte. Ltd, on International Street, Phum Trapeang Chuk, Sangkat Tekthlar, Khan Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, began operating in 2000 and employs about 1,200 workers in five unions [Para 2][Para 31][Para 32]. The complainant is the Democratic Thoamear Union Federation and its local union at the factory, with about 100 members [Para 4][Para 32]. On 27 March 2008 the company issued a notice promising an extra US$ 5 on all wages from 1 April 2008; on 4 April 2008 the Labour Advisory Committee declared a US$ 6 living allowance for garment and shoe workers, and at a meeting on 10 April 2008 the company said the US$ 5 notice was a technical error and that the US$ 6 allowance replaced it [Para 33][Para 34][Para 35][Para 36]. Of five demands, three were settled in final conciliation on 31 July 2008, leaving the extra US$ 5 and a US$ 20 milk payment in lieu of a daycare centre [Para 18][Para 19][Para 12].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay the additional US$ 5 wage promised in its notice of 27 March 2008, separately from the US$ 6 living allowance required by the Labour Advisory Committee declaration of 4 April 2008? [Para 32][Para 45]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay US$ 20 a month for milk in lieu of building a daycare centre? [Para 42][Para 55]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The Democratic Thoamear Union Federation and its local union argued that the notice of 27 March 2008 was a binding promise of US$ 5 on top of basic wages, that the union's refusal to sign the 10 April 2008 record meant it did not accept that agreement, and that the US$ 6 allowance came from a different source, so the US$ 5 was still owed [Para 36][Para 40][Para 41]. On Issue 2 it asked US$ 20 a month because US$ 5 was not enough for milk or external care, falling back to more than US$ 5, and said it would drop the demand if the company built a daycare centre [Para 42][Para 43][Para 45].
**Employer (respondent):** Vivatino Design said the US$ 5 notice was issued by mistake on wrong information, that it apologised at the 10 April meeting attended by Ministry officials and union representatives, and that it now adds the US$ 6 living allowance to basic wages paid on the 7th of each month, which no worker has complained about [Para 35][Para 37][Para 39]. On Issue 2 it said it could not afford more than the US$ 5 it already paid in lieu of a daycare centre [Para 44].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand for a separate US$ 5 wage increase was rejected, and the demand for US$ 20 in lieu of a daycare centre was also rejected; the company was ordered to build a daycare centre and, during construction, to keep paying US$ 5 a month under the agreement [Para 70][Para 71]. The award is binding immediately, the parties having chosen a binding award on 22 August 2008 [Para 71][Para 72].
On Issue 1 the panel found the notice of 27 March 2008 genuine and intended to help workers with rising prices, and that the US$ 5 was in fact added before the declaration of 4 April 2008 [Para 45][Para 46]. But it accepted the company's account that the notice rested on wrong information, that the error was explained at a meeting with the Ministry and the union, and that the US$ 6 living allowance had since been folded into basic wages [Para 47][Para 50][Para 52]. Since both payments answer the same purpose, the rising cost of living, requiring a further US$ 5 on top would be unreasonable pressure on the company, which had a reasonable reason for not paying it [Para 49][Para 51][Para 53][Para 54].
On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 186 of the Labour Law, under which an enterprise employing at least 100 women must set up a nursing room and creche, and must pay for an external creche where children over 18 months cannot be kept on site [Para 57][Para 58][Para 59]. Following Awards 63/04 Shine Well and 68/04 City New, it held that the company, with 1,200 workers and no daycare centre, is under that obligation [Para 31][Para 58][Para 60][Para 62]. A demand for money in place of building is contrary to the law [Para 61]. The panel then tested the US$ 5 in lieu agreement against Article 13 of the Labour Law, which makes the law public order and voids any contract or unilateral act inconsistent with it [Para 62][Para 63]. It held the purpose of the rule to be keeping mothers close to their children and enabling breastfeeding rather than milk in the first six months [Para 65], so the agreement did not absolve the employer of the duty to build [Para 66]. Where the employer cannot build for children over 18 months, it may instead pay an external creche against receipts [Para 67][Para 68].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Two rules emerge. A company that issues a wage notice on wrong information, promptly convenes the union and the Labour Ministry to correct it, and pays the statutory living allowance in its place is not required to pay both, because two payments serving the same purpose cannot be cumulated [Para 51][Para 54]. And an agreement to pay cash in lieu of a daycare centre does not discharge the duty Article 186 imposes on an employer of 100 or more women; the panel will order the centre built, allowing cash only during construction or for external care of children over 18 months [Para 66][Para 67][Para 69].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remark that workers and a representative union are free to sign an agreement whether its terms are above or below the law, while a later passage holds the in-lieu agreement contrary to the law's purpose, sits uneasily and was not necessary to the order [Para 62][Para 65].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The translation is unofficial and badly duplicated: several sentences appear twice within one line, including the account of conciliation and of the parties presenting their case [Para 17][Para 21], and the decision line runs Issue 2 into the middle of the Issue 1 order [Para 70]. Line 45 calls the body the "Arbitration Council Foundation" instead of the Arbitration Council. The company's address is printed twice in slightly different forms, and one address label is rendered with mixed Khmer and Latin characters [Para 3][Para 4]. A cited precedent is mangled from 77/08 into "77108" [Para 65], and the currency is variously printed "US$", "USD$", "UD$" and "$". The award date is 8 September 2008, but the record of the 10 April meeting and the 27 March notice are described in terms that cannot be reconciled with the exhibit list's description of a "minute on the meeting dated 27 March 2008" [Para 26][Para 33]. Individual representatives and officials are named in the award; they are given here by role only.
