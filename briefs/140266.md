# Award No. 45/10 — Yung Wai Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន យុង វា អ៊ិន ដាស្រៀល) (Arbitration Council, 4 June 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirem; Liv Sovanna; chair Kong Phally) · Doc ID 140266

## 1. Facts
Yung Wai Industrial (Cambodia) employs about 1,700 workers at Ta Khmao, Kandal Province. [Para 1] [Para 35] The claimant is the factory union of the Cambodian Workers' Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា), registered on 18 September 2007 with about 400 members; it holds no most-representative status, which it accepts. [Para 30] [Para 36] [Para 37] [Para 130] Of eleven demands, five were settled in conciliation on 26 April 2010 and six came before the panel, which heard them on 20 May 2010; at the hearing the union withdrew Issues 4 and 6, leaving Issues 1, 2, 3 and 5. [Para 20] [Para 24] [Para 25] The remaining demands concerned the payday, medical certificates, recognition of the second-term leadership and an annual staff party. [Para 38] [Para 51] [Para 59] [Para 65]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay each month's wages between the 3rd and 6th day of the following month? [Para 38] [Para 72]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must accept ordinary medical certificates, and not only certificates from a state hospital, when a worker is on unpaid sick leave? [Para 51] [Para 92]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must recognise the letter of the Department of Labour Disputes, No. 1025 KB/AK/VK of 5 November 2009, recognising the second-term union leadership, even though it bears no ministry seal? [Para 59] [Para 105]
4. (Issue 5) Whether the company must hold a staff party once or twice a year for all workers? [Para 65] [Para 117]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that pay must arrive by the 3rd to 6th of the next month because rent, water, electricity and food fall due at the start of the month, and that a party for section heads and union officers alone was unfair when the 13th-month salary was cancelled for economic reasons; that ordinary medical certificates should be accepted; and that the leadership letter is a ministry document the union cannot explain the missing seal of. [Para 39] [Para 64] [Para 66]
**Employer (respondent):** that wages are computed after month-end closing, approved by the parent company in Singapore, transferred and then drawn and distributed, so payment falls between the 7th and 10th and has never exceeded the 10th; that it accepts only state hospital certificates; and that the leadership letter carries no ministry seal, so it is not recognised, letters of the Royal Government requiring a seal. [Para 40] [Para 43] [Para 44] [Para 58] [Para 62] [Para 63]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issue 1 is refused; on Issue 2 the company is ordered to accept medical certificates from doctors trained under the Ministry of Health framework and properly licensed, whether from a state or a private facility; on Issue 3 the company is ordered to recognise the second-term union leadership named in the letter of the Department of Labour Disputes; Issue 5 is refused. [Para 134] [Para 135] [Para 136] [Para 137]

On Issue 1 the panel found that no law, prakas, notification, circular or other norm fixes the day on which wages must be paid, and that the parties had no agreement on it. [Para 74] [Para 85] Article 116 paragraph 2 requires payment at least once a month but does not prescribe the day. [Para 75] [Para 76] [Para 77] Article 2 paragraph 2 gives the employer the right to organise and direct the enterprise, provided that right is exercised lawfully and reasonably, and setting the monthly payday falls within it, as held in Awards 14/07, 17/07 and 116/09. [Para 78] [Para 81] [Para 82] [Para 84] The company explained the sequence from closing the payroll to remitting funds from Singapore, and the union did not contest it. [Para 41] [Para 42] [Para 46] Payment between the 7th and 10th was lawful and reasonable, and the claim had no legal basis. [Para 87] [Para 88] [Para 90]

On Issue 2 the panel read Article 71 paragraph 3, which suspends the contract for absence on illness certified by an official doctor, with Awards 102/07, 72/08 and 39/09 holding an official doctor to be one trained at a school recognised by the Ministry of Health, holding a valid diploma and licensed to practise. [Para 94] [Para 96] [Para 97] It noted that earlier awards had expressly required acceptance of certificates from such doctors in public or private practice. [Para 98] [Para 99] [Para 100] The company's practice of refusing everything except state hospital certificates, and certificates from two named private clinics for gynaecological cases, was therefore wrong. [Para 101] [Para 102] [Para 103]

On Issue 3 the panel applied Article 268 of the Labour Law and Prakas 021 of 15 February 2006, and followed Award 96/09 in holding that union leadership is lawful once the Ministry of Labour recognises it, even where the recognition letter is issued by the Department of Labour Disputes, because that department is part of the Ministry and the competent authority. [Para 106] [Para 108] [Para 112] The union's registration No. 1235 KB/VK of 18 September 2007 and the recognition letter of 5 November 2009 were therefore valid, and the company had to recognise the names in the letter. [Para 113] [Para 114] [Para 116]

On Issue 5 the panel found neither an agreement nor any legal norm requiring a staff party, treated the demand as one exceeding what the law provides, and so as an interest dispute. [Para 118] [Para 119] [Para 120] An interest award stands in place of a collective agreement for a year under clause 43 of Prakas 99, and the panel follows its consistent practice of declining interest disputes brought by a union without most-representative status; this union had conceded it had none. [Para 124] [Para 125] [Para 131] [Para 132] [Para 133]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Article 116 of the Labour Law requires payment at least monthly but fixes no day, so the employer's choice of payday within the month is an exercise of the management prerogative in Article 2 paragraph 2 and is lawful where it is reasonable and explained — a demand for a particular date therefore fails for want of legal basis. A certificate from a doctor trained under the Ministry of Health framework and licensed to practise satisfies Article 71 paragraph 3 whether the doctor works in a public or a private facility, and an employer that accepts only state hospital certificates acts unlawfully. A union leadership recognition letter issued by the Department of Labour Disputes is valid without a ministry seal, that department being part of the Ministry of Labour, and the employer must recognise the officers it names. A claim for a benefit neither law nor agreement provides is an interest dispute, which the panel will not hear from a union lacking most-representative status. [Para 77] [Para 84] [Para 103] [Para 112] [Para 133]

**Obiter dicta:** that haste in paying wages can itself cause calculation errors, and that the union's fairness argument about a party for supervisors when the 13th-month salary had been cancelled created no entitlement. [Para 49] [Para 66] [Para 67]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is a Khmer original; the Latin arbitrator names in the Forum line are transliterations, and the chair's name is printed twice in slightly different forms at the signature block. [Para 1] [Para 139] The company's name is unstable (យុង វា អ៉ិន ដាស្រៀល, យ៉ុង វ៉ា អ៊ិន ដាស្វ្រៀល, យុង វាំ), and the union is called both the factory union of Yung Wai I and, in one line, a "free workers' union". [Para 25] [Para 26] [Para 28] [Para 129] The demand list is misnumbered: the US$5 attendance incentive is printed as item "២៦". [Para 12] OCR corrupts the Article quotations ("រោត្រា", "ហត្រា", "មាត្រា" for the same word), and a garbled Latin fragment interrupts the reasoning on the annual party. [Para 75] [Para 117] The procedural section gives conciliation as 26 April 2010 but the non-conciliation report as 4 May 2010, forwarded on 11 May. [Para 16] [Para 20] Individual workers, union officers and employer representatives are described by role only.
