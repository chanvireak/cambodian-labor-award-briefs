# Award No. 061/15 — Angkor Hem Meas (Arbitration Council, 19 March 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Hem Hor Narith, chosen by the employer; An Non, chosen by the workers; Aing Eng Thong, chair chosen by the two) · Doc ID 139155

## 1. Facts
Angkor Hem Meas (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អង្គរ ហែម មើរ) provides massage services at Thnal village, Srongae commune, Siem Reap city, and told the panel it employs 66 staff. [Para 1][Para 33][Para 34] On 23 January 2015 the Federation of Food and Service Workers' Unions of Cambodia (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ ចំណីអាហារ និង សេវាកម្ម កម្ពុជា) complained to the Siem Reap provincial Department of Labour; the federation has no local union at the company. [Para 10][Para 35] Conciliation of 20 demands on 11 February 2015 settled 17 and saw one withdrawn, leaving two; the hearing was held on 06 March 2015 and settled the first of those, leaving only the wage demand. [Para 11][Para 16][Para 17][Para 9] The disputed act is not a dismissal but a refusal: 40 massage staff, having transferred their rights to the federation, demanded a base wage of USD 177 a month where they are paid between USD 45 and USD 70 plus tips. [Para 36][Para 38][Para 39]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a health allowance of USD 100 a year to all workers? [Para 2] (settled in conciliation at the hearing [Para 16])
2. Whether the employer must raise the base wage to USD 177 a month for every massage employee? [Para 38][Para 46]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The base wage of USD 45 to USD 70 does not cover monthly living costs, so the staff cannot live in dignity as Article 104 of the Labour Law requires; tips of only about USD 50 to USD 100 a month vary with the season; the base wage must therefore be raised to USD 177 for all massage staff. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43][Para 40]
**Employer (respondent):** No law or Prakas requires the massage sector to pay the base wage that the garment sector pays; the staff also receive tips from clients on top of the base wage, and social security contributions were already paid. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 3][Para 52]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider the demand altogether and awarded nothing, the earlier health-allowance demand having been settled. [Para 66][Para 67]

The panel noted that the claimants were 40 massage staff who had transferred their rights to the federation, and that they were the original complainants. [Para 36][Para 37] On the substance, Article 104 of the Labour Law requires wages at least equal to a guaranteed minimum wage sufficient for a dignified standard of living, and Article 107(2) leaves the fixing of that minimum to a Prakas of the Ministry of Labour after consulting the Labour Advisory Committee. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49] The panel found that no Prakas fixes a minimum wage for the massage sector, and that no agreement, contract, collective agreement or law obliges the employer to pay USD 177. [Para 50][Para 53] It therefore classified the demand as a dispute over interests rather than rights. [Para 54]

On standing, Article 96 of the Labour Law defines the collective agreement, and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 SAK BY of 22 November 2001 confers the right to request one on the most representative union. [Para 55][Para 58] Under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 SAK BY of 21 April 2004 an award on interests replaces the collective agreement, applies to all workers in the company and extinguishes their right to strike on that subject, so the panel may decide an interest dispute only where the claimant is the most representative union, or several unions together holding more than half the workforce. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63] Following awards 81/04 Evergreen, 98/04 Great Union, 202/14, 02/11 Puyen, 66/11 Inhansung and 206/12 Velico Garment, it held that the 40 masseurs were not a union possessing that character, and that the claimant therefore lacked the legal capacity to bring an interest dispute. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 66]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand to raise base wages above the statutory minimum is a dispute over interests, and the panel will decline to consider it unless the claimant is the most representative union in the enterprise — a body of individual workers holding transfers of rights is not such a union, and the size of that group does not supply the standing it lacks. [Para 54][Para 63][Para 65][Para 66]

Where no Prakas has fixed a minimum wage for the sector concerned, no legal obligation arises to pay any particular base wage, and the panel will not convert a wage aspiration into a right. [Para 50][Para 53]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observations that tips are a separate and seasonal addition to the base wage, and that the parties agreed the base wage range of USD 45 to USD 70, were recorded but played no part in the outcome, which turned on standing. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 43]
