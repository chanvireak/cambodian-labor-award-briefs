# Award No. 046/19 — Bou Kher Garment Factory (Cambodia) Company Limited (Arbitration Council, 12 July 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Eing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Virak, worker-appointed; Aing Eng Thong, presiding) · Doc ID 139065

## 1. Facts
The Cambodia Development Union (សហជីព អភិវឌ្ឍន៍ កម្ពុជា) lodged a collective dispute with the Kandal Provincial Department of Labour and Vocational Training on 21 May 2019 on behalf of workers at Bou Kher Garment Factory, a garment employer with 4,600 workers. Of six claims, conciliation settled one and five were referred to the Council on 11 June 2019; at the hearing of 19 June 2019 the parties reached agreement on points 2, 3, 4 and 5, leaving only point 1 for decision. [Para 15][Para 51][Para 21][Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must apply, to the team leaders and section chiefs of six sections (inspection, cutting, materials warehouse, panel opening, fabric warehouse and embroidery), the same percentage bonus formula that the packing section applies? [Para 55][Para 116]

Before reaching that question the panel had to decide a threshold objection: whether it had jurisdiction, the employer arguing that the claim pleaded at the hearing differed from the original complaint and from the non-conciliation report. [Para 103][Para 104]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the six sections have no clear bonus policy and their leaders receive no percentage of the piece-rate earnings of the members they supervise, while the packing section's leaders do; at times a team leader earns less than the workers in his own team, although he directs the same work, so the packing formula should apply equally. [Para 58][Para 69][Para 70]
**Employer (respondent):** the point does not appear in the complaint or the non-conciliation report, so the Council has no jurisdiction; in any event the six sections never had a percentage bonus, the employer has now introduced a new one on a standard-minute (SMV) formula that is more favourable than the law, section chiefs receive a USD 40 and team leaders a USD 20 monthly allowance, and existing benefits are retained. [Para 59][Para 66][Para 72]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the claim that the employer apply the packing-section percentage bonus formula to the six sections was rejected. [Para 127][Para 128]

Jurisdiction: Article 312(1) of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 99 Article 33 confine the panel to the issues recorded in the non-conciliation minutes, but the panel found the parties had in fact discussed the bonus formula during conciliation and that the report recorded the six sections had no clear bonus policy and the employer would adopt one. The hearing claim was therefore a clarification of an existing issue, not a new one, and jurisdiction existed. [Para 104][Para 113][Para 114][Para 115]

Merits: Article 2 of the Labour Law, read with Awards 62/06 and 029/15, gives the employer the right to organise and manage the enterprise, including unilateral bonus schemes outside those fixed by law, provided they are lawful and reasonable. Both parties accepted that no percentage bonus had ever applied to the six sections; the employer introduced one at the workers' request but on a formula different from packing's, and the workers produced no evidence that the new formula was unlawful or unreasonable. No agreement or policy obliged parity with the packing section. The panel therefore held the scheme a lawful management measure, more favourable than the statutory minimum. [Para 118][Para 120][Para 124][Para 125][Para 126]

Standing: the panel first found under Article 54(1) of the Trade Union Law 2016 and Prakas No. 303 that no most-representative union existed at the company, so the three workers certified by the Kandal Provincial Department under Prakas No. 362 were competent to represent the claimants. [Para 88][Para 90][Para 101]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may create and calculate a bonus scheme for team leaders and section chiefs unilaterally, on a formula of its own design, where no statute, collective agreement or existing policy requires parity with another section; the panel will not substitute its own formula unless the claimant shows the scheme is unlawful or unreasonable, and the mere fact that a comparable section is paid differently does not discharge that burden. [Para 123][Para 126]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that where output reaches 63 per cent or more, team leaders and section chiefs receive the new bonus on top of their wages, and that the formula will pay them more than before once the company's order situation recovers. [Para 77][Para 82]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company's Latin-script name is not printed anywhere in the surviving text — only the Khmer transliteration — so the registered English name cannot be confirmed from this document. [Para 50] The panel's constitution date is printed as "13 June 2023 2019 2019" and is unrecoverable as written. [Para 18] The reasoning line numbering jumps (items 19 to 20 in the employer's evidence list), and the list of wage tables for May 2019 is truncated mid-entry. [Para 41] The procedural summary says five points were unresolved and that four were then agreed, but the dispute list on the face of the award shows only five numbered claims in total, so the sixth settled claim is never identified. [Para 17][Para 21] The employer's jurisdictional objection recites the conciliation report as covering only incorrect piece-rate calculation, which conflicts with the report's recorded text quoted later in the award. [Para 59][Para 113] The names of the workers' representatives survive, but the notes of the hearing are partly overwritten by stray OCR characters ("all", "to", "of").
