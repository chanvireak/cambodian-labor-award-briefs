# Award No. 25/03 — Advanced Industry Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, Monday December 29, 2003)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, Tuon Siphann, chair Kong Phallak) · Doc ID 138832

## 1. Facts
Advanced Industry Co., Ltd, an imitation-jewellery producer in Building No. 215 St. 164, Sangkat Tek Laak 2, Khan Toul Kok, employed 138 workers on wages of US$38.00 per month during probation and at least US$45.00 per month thereafter, with a probationary period of up to five months. [Para 19] [Para 30] [Para 31] [Para 32] [Para 33] On November 28, 2003 the labour inspector received a complaint; conciliation on December 02, 2003 settled 12 of the 14 demands, leaving the attendance bonus and the seniority bonus for the panel, which held a hearing on December 18, 2003 and issued a non-binding award by agreement of the parties. [Para 20] [Para 22] [Para 23] [Para 26]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise the regular-attendance bonus from US$2.00 to US$5.00 per month and pay the increase back for previous months, as provided by Notification No. 017 of MoSALVY dated July 18, 2000? [Para 8] [Para 36]
2. Whether the employer must pay each employee a US$2.00 per month employment-seniority bonus under Article 5 of the same Notification? [Para 8] [Para 38]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that Notification No. 017 entitles them to a US$5.00 attendance bonus and, under Article 5, to a seniority bonus, and that the increase should carry back pay for earlier months. [Para 8] [Para 36] [Para 39]
**Employer (respondent):** that the company is small and earns little profit, that no law obliges it to pay a US$5.00 attendance bonus, that Notification No. 017 does not apply to imitation-jewellery companies, and that it could not follow the demands for that reason. [Para 36] [Para 39] [Para 47]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — by agreement during the panel's mediation the employer pays a regular-attendance bonus of US $4.00 per month from January 01, 2004 (not the US$5.00 claimed and no back pay), and a seniority bonus from January 01, 2004 of US$1.00 per month at one year, US$2.00 at two years, US$3.00 at three years and US$4.00 at four years, built by adding US$1.00 for each completed year; the order also says the seniority payment is effective from August 01, 2000. [Para 37] [Para 53] [Para 54] [Para 55] [Para 56] [Para 57]
On the attendance bonus the panel recorded only the settlement the parties reached under its mediation and treated it as valid and official, so it did not have to decide the legal force of Notification No. 017 for that head of claim. [Para 37] [Para 38]
On seniority the panel found that Notification No. 017 of July 18, 2000 was made for textile, garment and footwear factories and enterprises and does not apply to an imitation-jewellery company of its own force; but Article 312 paragraph 2 of the 1997 Labour Law lets the panel resolve labour disputes on the principle of equity, and the Notification contains a common principle applicable to any enterprise with working conditions similar to those sectors. [Para 41] [Para 42] [Para 43] The similarity was established on the evidence: the same working conditions, a probationary period of up to four or five months against a maximum of three months elsewhere, and a probationary wage of US$38.00 below that of other enterprises. [Para 44] [Para 45] [Para 46] [Para 47] The company promised on the panel's request to file evidence of its profits by Monday 22 December 2003 but never submitted its balance sheet, which led the panel to conclude that it earned more profit than it reported. [Para 48] [Para 49] [Para 50] The panel therefore awarded an equitable seniority bonus rather than the full Notification rate. [Para 44] [Para 53]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a MoSALVY notification is limited by its terms to one sector, the panel will not apply it directly to an enterprise outside that sector, but it will apply the notification's underlying principle by equity under Article 312 paragraph 2 where the evidence shows substantially similar working conditions — notably a longer probation and a lower probationary wage — and will set the benefit at a compromise rate rather than the notification's full scale. An employer that promises financial records to resist an equity claim and then fails to produce them is taken to have earned more profit than it asserted. [Para 42] [Para 44] [Para 50]

**Obiter dicta:** that increased skill from long service and the loyalty a seniority bonus buys make the bonus fair to both sides, and that a settlement reached in the panel's mediation is valid and official even where the legal basis for the claim is contested. [Para 38] [Para 51] [Para 52]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award orders the seniority bonus to run from January 01, 2004 in the operative paragraph but states at the end that the payment "is effective from August 01, 2000", a backdating the reasoning never explains; both are reported as printed. [Para 53] [Para 57] The employer's proposed scale omits the amount for employees of over three years, leaving that tier blank. [Para 40] The registration certificate is dated "June 31, 1995", a date that does not exist, and the Ministry appears as both MoSALVY and MSALVY. [Para 15] [Para 36] OCR duplicates sentences and drops "th" throughout ("themployees", "the" for "the"), and the company is called Advanced Industry Co., Ltd in the style of cause but "Advanced Industry Co Ltd" and "Advanced Industry Co., Ltd." in the body. [Para 16] [Para 27] Individual union officers have been reduced to initials per the briefing convention.
