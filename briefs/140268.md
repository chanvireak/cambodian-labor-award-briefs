# Award No. 42/10 — Tak Fat Garment (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 11 June 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tai Seng, An Vireak, Kong Phalla presiding) · Doc ID 140268

## 1. Facts
Tak Fat Garment (Cambodia) Ltd, a Phnom Penh factory of about 1,300 workers, was respondent to a collective dispute filed on 29 March 2010 by the Free Trade Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia and its factory branch; six of ten demands remained unresolved after conciliation and the panel heard the parties on 21 May 2010 [Para 21][Para 24][Para 105]. The dispute centres on the suspension and dismissal of the factory union president, Ms Y L, on 23 March 2010 after a fight with a Chinese section supervisor, and on the employer's transfer of 87 finishing-section workers to sewing and its refusal to let them enter the factory from 27 April 2010 [Para 111][Para 181]. Demands for maternity pay, July 2009 suspension wages, annual leave and an order barring future inducements to resign were also referred [Para 8][Para 13][Para 16].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the dismissal of the union president without labour-inspector authorisation was lawful, and whether she must be reinstated with wages from the date of dismissal? [Para 108]
2. (Issues 2 and 4, merged by agreement) Whether the panel should rule on the demand that the employer cease inducing resignations by paying USD 150 to workers and USD 500 to team leaders? [Para 119]
3. (Issue 3) Whether four women who resigned on 8 and 9 February 2010 may claim 50% wages for the 90-day maternity leave falling in March and April 2010? [Para 211]
4. (Issue 5) Whether the employer owes 100% wages for the 17-day July 2009 suspension of 115 finishing workers imposed without inspector authorisation, the annual-leave days deducted at that time, and 15 days' July 2009 wages to 72 sewing workers? [Para 227]
5. (Issue 6) Whether the termination of 87 finishing workers who refused transfer to the sewing section was valid, and whether their April and May 2010 wages are owed? [Para 271]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Ms Y L was not the aggressor on 23 March 2010 — she went to the section head to have a document signed and was bitten and held by him and his colleagues; her dismissal therefore lacked the inspector's authorisation the law requires [Para 115]. The transfer to sewing was a device, preceded by promises the employer refused to put in writing, to make workers resign; the July suspension was unlawful and annual leave was cut wholesale [Para 142][Para 164][Para 284].
**Employer (respondent):** Ms Y L committed serious misconduct by fighting with the section head, and both were dismissed; the request to dismiss her was sent to the labour inspector on 23 March 2010 and no reply had come, and the suspension was announced without a fixed term [Para 112][Para 113][Para 149]. Workers who resigned did so voluntarily, the transfer to sewing was a reasonable redeployment because finishing work had run short, and the absent workers abandoned their jobs under Article 6 of the internal rules [Para 121][Para 168][Para 176].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must reinstate Ms Y L and pay her wages from 23 March 2010 until she returns; pay 100% wages for the 17-day July 2009 suspension and restore the annual-leave days deducted, for the 115 finishing workers who thumbprinted the demand; and reinstate the 87 finishing workers to the sewing section, except those elderly or infirm workers whom it must return to finishing or dismiss with statutory benefits. The maternity, incitement, 15-day sewing-wage and April–May 2010 wage claims were dismissed [Para 309][Para 312][Para 313][Para 315].

On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 293 of the Labour Law and Article 4 of Prakas No. 305 (22 November 2001): a union leader may be dismissed only with the labour inspector's consent, and silence for one month is deemed a refusal [Para 182][Para 185]. Because the request of 23 March 2010 had gone unanswered for more than a month by the hearing date, the dismissal was unlawful, and the earlier awards cited (Nos 79/06, 74/08, 107/08, 03/09) were followed [Para 189][Para 196].

On Issues 2 and 4 the panel held the claim premature: the 20 claimants still worked at the factory, alleged only a fear of future inducement, and showed no present act or loss; an arbitration panel exists to resolve existing disputes, not future ones [Para 201][Para 203][Para 206]. On Issue 3, Articles 182 and 183 give 50% wages during 90 days' maternity leave to women with one year's service, but Article 65 ties that right to a live employment relationship; the four had resigned on 8 and 9 February 2010, before the leave period began [Para 213][Para 219][Para 224].

On Issue 5 the panel applied Article 71(11) and Article 72: a suspension for economic difficulty must be notified to and supervised by the labour inspector, and absent that, full wages are owed even if no work is given [Para 229][Para 241][Para 244]. The 72 sewing workers who refused the fifth-floor reassignment for 15 days were held to have acted like strikers under Articles 318 and 332, so no wages were due for that period [Para 260][Para 267].

On Issue 6 the panel applied Article 74 (unfixed-duration contracts may be terminated at either party's will but lawfully) and Article 2 (the employer's right to manage and redeploy). The transfer itself was reasonable — training was promised, no benefit lost — but the terminations were not, because the workers' absence flowed from the employer's own broken promise to sign the no-further-transfer agreement, and the dispute was still live [Para 288][Para 301][Para 284]. The panel warned against repeated transfers and against moving elderly or infirm workers into sewing [Para 302][Para 303].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer seeks to dismiss a union leader, the statutory protection is procedural and self-executing: absent the labour inspector's decision within one month, the request is deemed refused and the dismissal is unlawful, whatever the underlying misconduct, so reinstatement and back pay follow [Para 191][Para 197]. Relatedly, a suspension of contracts for economic difficulty that bypasses the inspector's notification and supervision obliges the employer to pay full wages even when it provides no work [Para 243].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's caution that transfers must not be repeated so often as to burden workers, and that elderly or infirm workers should not be moved into physically demanding sewing work, goes beyond the order made [Para 302][Para 303].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR frequently corrupts the employer's name ("Tack Pat", "តាក ហ្វាត", "តាក ហ្វាត ហ្គាមិន") and the section supervisor's name appears in three spellings; the four maternity claimants' names are likewise printed inconsistently, and are given here only by role. The Prakas 305 date is printed as "២៤ ២២ ខែវិច្ឆិកា ឆ្នាំ ២០០១" in one passage and as 22 November 2001 elsewhere [Para 185][Para 186]. The April–May wage demand is dated 2010 in the issue statement but 2011 in the reasoning, and the award's own reasons refer to "ឆ្នាំ ២០១៦" inside a 2009 agreement reference [Para 90][Para 173][Para 271]. Several lines carry untranslated OCR debris ("League", "inther", "So") with no recoverable meaning [Para 120][Para 181].
