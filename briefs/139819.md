# Award No. 128/13 — Property Care Service (Cambodia) Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 August 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Kong Phalja, president) · Doc ID 139819

## 1. Facts
Property Care Service (Cambodia) Co Ltd ("PCS") provides cleaning and security services and employed about 860 staff. Of thirteen claims taken to conciliation, two remained unresolved and came to the panel. The employer dismissed Ms S Sila, a service delivery manager on USD 700 base wage plus USD 180 allowance employed since 3 March 2008, on 21 May 2013; and it treated Mr R Phara, head of patrol employed since 27 July 2009, as having resigned with a last working day of 30 May 2013. [Para 78][Para 80][Para 91][Para 149][Para 290]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take Ms S Sila and Mr R Phara back into normal work and pay them back wages from the date of dismissal to the date of return? [Para 88]
2. Whether the employer must supply each cleaning employee with two sets of uniform a year, one free of charge and one at USD 15? [Para 191]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The dismissals were without valid reason and, in Ms S Sila's case, tainted by union discrimination; she was a full-rights workers' delegate protected by Article 293, and Mr R Phara never resigned, having withdrawn his application. Uniforms are the employer's obligation because it compels their wearing. [Para 95][Para 106][Para 153][Para 202]
**Employer (respondent):** The dispute is individual, not collective, under Article 302, so the panel lacks jurisdiction. Ms S Sila committed three serious faults — misappropriating company money, trying to pull 70 staff to a rival, and taking client money directly — and Mr R Phara resigned validly. The uniform practice is lawful and no law requires a free second set. [Para 88][Para 108][Para 174][Para 214]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must pay Ms S Sila two months' wages and benefits in lieu of notice, dismissal damages of 75 days' wages and allowances, payment for unused annual leave, moral damages equal to the 75 days' dismissal damages, and unpaid final wages; the dismissal of Mr R Phara is void, and the employer must reinstate him and pay back wages from dismissal to return; the uniform claim is dismissed. [Para 438][Para 439][Para 440]

On jurisdiction the panel applied the three conditions of Article 302 — several workers against one employer, a subject-matter of working conditions or union rights, and a dispute capable of disrupting the enterprise — and found all three met, since two employees were dismissed and other staff demonstrated for their return. The employer asserted an individual dispute but offered no reasons, and so did not discharge the burden the panel places on a party resisting collective characterisation. [Para 228][Para 229][Para 232][Para 235][Para 237]

On Ms S Sila's delegate status, Article 291 gives a full-rights delegate a two-year mandate. Her mandate ran from 18 May 2011 and had therefore expired by 21 May 2013; a name and signature on the internal rules of 18 May 2011 could not by itself prove a current mandate, and no other evidence was produced. The Article 293 protection was refused. [Para 244][Para 251][Para 253]

On union discrimination, the panel cited Articles 12 and 279 and held the accuser bears the burden. Two union-formation meetings were proved, the second at Ms S Sila's own home, but she conceded she did not know whether the employer knew of them, and no specific evidence of discriminatory motive was filed. The claim was rejected. [Para 255][Para 264][Para 267][Para 271]

On serious misconduct the panel turned to Article 26: an employer that knows of a serious fault must dismiss within seven days, or impose discipline within fifteen, and otherwise forfeits the right. The employer learned of the alleged USD 1,703 (baht) — USD 57.66 — misappropriation on 2 May 2013 but dismissed only on 21 May 2013. Because the right was already lost, the panel expressly declined to decide whether the misappropriation occurred or was a serious fault. [Para 285][Para 287][Para 292][Para 297]

The poaching allegation rested on an email of 9 May 2013 containing the phrase "my staff ... about 70 people". The panel held that phrase too ambiguous to establish intent to transfer PCS staff, and noted the employer never identified a single solicited employee or showed that anyone followed her to Master Service Cambodia. [Para 309][Para 310][Para 312]

The client-money allegation failed on evidence: the employer's own collection witness could not recall when the conduct was said to have happened, and the employer could not say when it learned of it. [Para 318][Para 321][Para 322]

For Mr R Phara the panel found the resignation was withdrawn before the employer received it, and that the letter of 30 May 2013 announcing he was no longer an employee operated as a dismissal. He had been elected union president on 27 May 2013; under Article 293 and Article 3(k)3 of Prakas No. 305 a candidate for union office enjoys the same protection as a delegate, for 45 days before and 45 days after the poll, and the federation had notified the employer by letter of 29 May 2013. No prior authorisation from the labour inspector was sought, so under Article 4(k)4 of the Prakas the dismissal is null and void. [Para 364][Para 374][Para 377][Para 384][Para 408][Para 414][Para 417]

On uniforms, the panel treated the choice of uniform as the employer's management prerogative under Article 2, reviewable for legality and reasonableness. Because the employer requires uniforms, it must supply enough free of charge for the work; but it did supply one new set each year from the second year, the second set at USD 15 was optional and paid by payroll deduction over three months, and the workers showed no reason why one free set was insufficient. [Para 423][Para 426][Para 433][Para 436]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that knows of an employee's serious fault must dismiss within seven days or discipline within fifteen under Article 26; once that period passes the right to rely on the fault is forfeited and the dismissal cannot be justified on it, whatever the fault's merits. Separately, dismissing an elected union leader or candidate for union office without prior authorisation from the labour inspector, contrary to Article 293 and Prakas No. 305, renders the dismissal null and void and entitles the employee to reinstatement and back wages. [Para 287][Para 297][Para 412][Para 417]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel recorded that it need not, and did not, decide whether Ms S Sila actually misappropriated the money or whether that act would have amounted to serious misconduct, and it observed that the dispute was in any event capable of disrupting the enterprise's normal operation. [Para 233][Para 297]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The dismissal date appears as 21 May 2013 in the reasons and award but as "the 26th" in one passage. The Thailand hotel cost is printed as USD 220 in the misconduct narrative and USD 200 in the expense analysis; the dinner cost appears as USD 18 and as 500 baht. The header party list is badly mangled, with duplicated numbering (items 11 to 13 repeated), unclosed brackets and names run together, and the arbitrators' signatures at the foot are lost to OCR except the president's name. The worker's name is printed variously as Sam Sila and Sam Seyla, and the second claimant as Rim Phara and Rim Theara. One line of the text is a stray fragment.
