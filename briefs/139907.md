# Award No. 260/13 — Long Lead (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 13 January 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun, employer-chosen; Liv Sovanna, worker-chosen; Ang Eng Thong, chair) · Doc ID 139907

## 1. Facts
Long Lead (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a footwear manufacturer in Chamkar Svay Village, Sethei Commune, Samaki Mean Chey District, Kompong Chhnang, employing about 1,691 workers. [Para 15][Para 19] From May 2013 to the third week of October 2013 it paid 1,500 riel in lieu of lunch to workers who attended work on Sundays and public holidays, weekly and against payslips signed by its Executive Director; in November 2013 it stopped. [Para 24][Para 49] The Free Trade Union of Workers of the Kingdom of Cambodia (FTUWKC) brought the dispute on behalf of 132 authorising workers. [Para 19][Para 23] The Council found the local union was not yet registered, so the award is non-binding. [Para 12]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must continue (and back-pay) the 1,500 riel payment in lieu of lunch for work performed on Sundays and public holidays? [Para 24]
2. Whether the employer must provide an additional 500 riel payment in lieu of lunch, making 2,000 riel per day? [Para 29]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the 1,500 riel payment was regular from May to October 2013; the employer stopped it without prior notice, any meeting or the workers' agreement, and should be ordered to keep paying it. [Para 25] The extra 500 riel was needed because inflation made a nutritious lunch unaffordable and because better nutrition reduced fainting at work. [Para 31]
**Employer (respondent):** the 1,500 riel payment was an accounting error — a Chinese-language document approved by the Executive Director was misapplied by the Accounting Department, whose correct formula covered only Monday-to-Saturday workers; the Executive Director was too busy to review payslips and discovered the error in October 2013. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 29] It gave notice to union leaders but not to the workers, and said it could not afford an increase but would consider the demand in early 2014. [Para 29][Para 31]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issue 1 ordered the employer to continue providing the 1,500 riel payment in lieu of lunch to workers who attend work on Sundays and public holidays; Issue 2 declined to consider the demand for an additional 500 riel. [Para 67]

On Issue 1 the panel applied the "past practice" doctrine, grounded in Paragraphs 2 and 3 of Article 65 of the Labour Law, under which a verbal contract is a tacit agreement between employer and worker. [Para 32][Para 35] A past practice is a custom or practice recognised and enforced by the parties, which need not be written and is formed when enforced consistently and regularly. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38] The panel followed Case 95/09-Tak Fat and earlier awards applying past practice to annual leave, wage calculation, bonuses and meal allowances. [Para 39][Para 40] It accepted that a past practice can legitimately end through genuine negotiation or because the circumstances on which it rested have changed. [Para 41][Para 42] The employer's accounting-error defence failed because the party making an allegation bears the burden of proof and no supporting evidence was submitted by the deadline. [Para 51][Para 53] The panel found the payment had run weekly for six months and that the Executive Director signed every payslip, so any error would likely have been noticed early, not six months later. [Para 49][Para 50] It held the 1,500 riel payment was a specific past practice, expected weekly by the workers, and that the abrupt stop could not be treated as a new practice replacing it. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 56]

On Issue 2 the panel found no provision in the Labour Law, a collective agreement, an internal work rule or any past practice obliged the employer to add 500 riel, and characterised the demand as an interests dispute. [Para 59][Para 60] Under Article 96 and Clause 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, only a union with most representative status (MRS) may negotiate a collective agreement and bring an interests dispute; Clause 43 of Prakas No. 099 makes such an award stand in place of a collective agreement for one year. [Para 61][Para 63][Para 64] Because the local union did not hold MRS, the panel declined to consider the demand. [Para 66][Para 67]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A payment made weekly over roughly six months to a defined group of workers, accepted and expected by them, is a past practice under Article 65 that the employer must continue; an employer asserting that the payment was an accounting error must prove it, and a unilateral stop does not by itself establish a new practice. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 56] An interests dispute seeking an increase above an existing entitlement may be entertained only when brought by a union holding most representative status. [Para 63][Para 66]

**Obiter dicta:** Some practices are not past practice — for example, the employer's management and supervision of the production line. [Para 40]
