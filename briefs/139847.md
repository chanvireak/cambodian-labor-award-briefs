# Award No. 166/13 — Long Bright Electric Enterprise (Arbitration Council, 10 September 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phalha presiding; Chhiv Phirom, employer nominee; An Non, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139847

## 1. Facts
Long Bright Electric Enterprise makes decorative light bulbs in the Manhattan Special Economic Zone, Svay Rieng, and employs about 550 workers; its union was registered on 2 July 2013 under No. 2940 K.B/W.K. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 71] On 2 August 2013 the employer suspended the contracts of the union's vice-president and secretary and, the same day, applied to the Labour Disputes Department for authorisation to dismiss them along with a team leader. [Para 88][Para 154][Para 155] The labour inspector had issued no decision by the date of the hearing and the one-month period for deciding had not expired. [Para 156] The team leader, who had joined on 14 November 2012 on a fixed-duration contract, was dismissed on 2 August 2013 before her contract expired. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 108]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the two union leaders and continue their fixed-duration contracts, they having committed no fault? [Para 74]
4. Whether the employer must take back the team leader dismissed on 2 August 2013? [Para 164]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the two leaders committed no fault and their contracts had not run their term, so they must be reinstated on their existing terms; the team leader was a union activist who was dismissed for acts she did not commit, and the employer never gave her a written warning or paid her any termination compensation. [Para 74][Para 135][Para 136]
**Employer (respondent):** the two leaders led a half-day strike on 5 July 2013 against tax withholding, blocked other workers from working, and forged the thumbprints of five workers on a union deduction form; the team leader falsified an attendance mark for another worker, drank alcohol during working hours, signed in place of the section head on a clinic slip, and threatened the nurse. [Para 90][Para 91][Para 92][Para 110][Para 118][Para 120][Para 123]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel declined jurisdiction over Issue 1, and on Issue 4 refused reinstatement but ordered two payments to the team leader: damages of at least the wages she would have earned to the end of her contract, and a contract-end indemnity of at least 5 percent of the wages she received during it. [Para 191][Para 192][Para 193]

On Issue 1 the panel set out Article 293 of the Labour Law, which forbids dismissal of a staff delegate or candidate without the labour inspector's prior consent and gives former delegates three months' protection after their term and unsuccessful candidates three months after the result is declared. [Para 141][Para 142][Para 143] It read clause 2 of Article 4 of Prakas No. 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001 as extending that protection to the three union leaders named under Articles 282 and 283. [Para 148][Para 149] The employer must therefore apply to the inspector, who must decide within one month of receiving the application, and silence for that month is deemed a refusal; the employer and worker then have two months to appeal to the Minister, who may reverse or vary the decision. [Para 150][Para 151][Para 158][Para 159] Article 295 allows immediate suspension only in cases of serious misconduct, and such a suspension becomes void if the inspector refuses authorisation. [Para 152][Para 153] Because no inspector's decision had issued and the month had not run, the panel held the matter still within the inspector's competence and not yet final, and refused to consider the reinstatement claim. [Para 156][Para 157][Para 160][Para 161][Para 163]

On Issue 4 the panel applied Article 73, under which a fixed-duration contract ends at its term but may be dissolved early only by written agreement before the labour inspector, or for serious misconduct or an act of God; a unilateral early termination by the employer outside those grounds opens the worker's right to damages of at least the wages due to the end of the contract, and Article 73(6) adds a contract-end indemnity of at least 5 percent of wages received. [Para 166][Para 167][Para 168][Para 169][Para 184][Para 185] The employer relied on four allegations, and the panel recalled its consistent position that the party making an accusation bears the burden of proving it. [Para 173][Para 175]

The only evidence offered was an audio recording of a conversation said to show the team leader falsifying the attendance mark. [Para 176][Para 177] The panel refused to act on it because the identity of the woman speaking was never disclosed or verified, so the recording could not be received as a witness's account; and the employer produced no other specific evidence to support the remaining allegations, which the team leader denied. [Para 179][Para 180] The panel therefore rejected all four accusations as unfounded and characterised the dismissal as a unilateral termination by the employer before the contract's term. [Para 181][Para 182] Reinstatement was refused, but the two statutory payments were ordered. [Para 191]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer has applied to the labour inspector for authorisation to dismiss a protected union leader and the inspector has neither decided nor allowed the one-month period to expire, the panel has no jurisdiction: the dismissal is not yet final and the reinstatement claim must be refused as premature, leaving the parties to the inspector's decision and to the two-month appeal to the Minister. [Para 157][Para 160][Para 161]

Separately, an employer that terminates a fixed-duration contract early for alleged misconduct must prove the misconduct with identified, admissible evidence; an anonymous audio recording whose speaker cannot be identified proves nothing, and the termination then stands as a unilateral act attracting damages to the end of the term plus the 5 percent contract-end indemnity. [Para 179][Para 181][Para 183][Para 190]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's findings that the employer knew of the two leaders' union positions, and that their contracts had not reached their term, were not necessary once jurisdiction over Issue 1 was declined. [Para 76][Para 140]
