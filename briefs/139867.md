# Award No. 189/13 — Sin Chang Shing Garmen (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 2 October 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Virak, worker-appointed; Tang Try, president) · Doc ID 139867

## 1. Facts
Sin Chang Shing Garmen (Cambodia) is a garment factory employing about 700–800 workers. [Para 55] A free trade union speaking for roughly 40 of them filed a collective dispute on 6 August 2013 raising 11 claims over working conditions; conciliation failed on all 11 and the file reached the panel on 2 September 2013. [Para 20][Para 56] The disputed act is the 1 August 2013 dismissal of Ms L, the union's elected secretary, who held a permanent contract from 11 February 2011 and was given no prior notice. [Para 65][Para 67] One claim was settled at the hearing and one withdrawn, leaving nine issues. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 28]

## 2. Issues in dispute
Following the panel's own numbering, issues 3 and 6 having fallen away:
1. Whether the employer had to take back Ms L, an elected union leader dismissed on 1 August 2013? [Para 57]
2. Whether the employer had to provide proper tables for workers eating inside the factory? [Para 75]
4. Whether the employer owed an additional USD 5 health allowance on top of the USD 80 basic wage? [Para 84]
5. Whether the employer had to raise the milk allowance from USD 10 to USD 15 a month and pay it from childbirth rather than from return from maternity leave? [Para 89]
7. Whether the employer discriminated against the union in dismissing its leaders? [Para 94]
8. Whether the employer had to add a second exit gate and more thumbprint machines? [Para 113]
9. Whether annual-leave indemnity had to be calculated on total wages? [Para 136]
10. Whether the employer had to supply two work shirts a year? [Para 144]
11. Whether the employer had to take back the 25 workers whose employment it had suspended? [Para 151]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Ms L was dismissed because she had been elected; the union sent the list of elected leaders to the employer on 20 June 2013 and again by post on 24 June, and the employer refused to accept it. [Para 58][Para 69][Para 71]

**Employer (respondent):** no document confirming the election result was ever received, and Ms L was dismissed because she incited workers to stop work on 1 August 2013, a fact shown on video. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement refused; the employer was ordered to pay severance equal to 30 days' wages plus benefits, one month's wages and benefits in lieu of notice, final wages and unused annual leave, and to provide meal tables within six months and two shirts a year; the remaining claims were dismissed. [Para 409][Para 410][Para 411][Para 417]

On issue 1 the panel began with Article 293, under which an employee delegate or candidate may not be dismissed without the labour inspector's agreement, a rule extended to union leaders by Prakas 305. [Para 162][Para 163] Following award 40/10 it held elected candidates protected from election day until the union is registered [Para 174][Para 182] and set three cumulative conditions. [Para 186] The first two were met: Ms L was an elected candidate and was dismissed on 1 August 2013, while registration was still pending. [Para 188][Para 192] The third failed: the union proved only that it wrote and posted a notice, not that the employer received the names by any reliable means, so the claim to protected status collapsed. [Para 194][Para 199][Para 200] Reinstatement was accordingly refused. [Para 203]

The panel then tested the dismissal directly. Articles 12 and 279 forbid anti-union discrimination [Para 204][Para 205] and the worker carries the burden of proving it [Para 210]; the evidence did not discharge that burden, so no discrimination was found. [Para 216] Under Article 74 an employer may dismiss at will only with notice and a reason tied to the worker's capacity or conduct or the needs of the enterprise. [Para 217] The video showed Ms L's section not working and her saying that the whole group should be taken out, conduct the panel called improper [Para 227] but which the employer had never pleaded as serious misconduct. [Para 230] Severance of 30 days' wages under Article 89 was therefore due, her service being 2 years 6 months. [Para 237] Moral damages under Article 91 were refused because a proper reason existed. [Para 245][Para 246] One month in lieu of notice under Articles 75 and 77, final wages under Article 116 and unused annual leave under Article 166 were ordered. [Para 252][Para 254][Para 261]

Issue 2 was granted: the employer conceded it had a canteen project and had promised the tables, so it was allowed a maximum of six months to complete the work. [Para 274][Para 277][Para 281]

Issues 4, 5, 9 and 11 were dismissed, the first two on jurisdiction. The panel read Prakas 103 as folding the USD 5 health allowance into the USD 80 minimum wage. [Para 285] Because the claimant union was not the most representative union in the factory, it declined to hear claims about interests — wages and allowances — at all. [Para 304][Para 305] More generous practice in other factories does not bind this employer. [Para 290] On milk, Article 311 of the Civil Code meant the parties' existing arrangement could not be changed by one side alone. [Para 313][Para 320] Annual-leave indemnity in cash under Article 167 arises only on termination, and the contracts continued. [Para 386][Para 391] The 25 suspended workers had given the union no written mandate and did not appear, so their claim could not be considered. [Para 403][Para 404][Para 406]

Issues 7 and 8 were dismissed on evidence: nothing showed discrimination against the other two leaders, and although gate and machine numbers fall within management's prerogative under Article 2, the union proved nothing about waiting time at the gate. [Para 330][Para 359][Para 372][Para 377] Issue 10 was granted, since article 6 of Prakas 307 requires two garments a year free of charge. [Para 393][Para 394][Para 399]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An elected union leader's protection against dismissal runs from the day of election until the union obtains registration, but is forfeited where the union cannot prove it brought the protected names to the employer's attention by some reliable means; the dismissal is then judged only on ordinary Article 74 grounds, which require notice and a reason the employer actually pleads. [Para 182][Para 200][Para 217]

A claim about interests may be entertained only where the referring union is the most representative in the enterprise. [Para 302]

**Obiter dicta:** practice in other factories that is more favourable than the statutory minimum creates no obligation for this employer. [Para 290]
