# Award No. 142/12 — Apsara Garment Factory (Arbitration Council, 13 August 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Ang Eng Tong (president) · Doc ID 140153

## 1. Facts
Apsara is a garment factory at Prek Pring South, Chom Chao, Porsenchey, Phnom Penh, employing about 500 workers [Para 1][Para 36]. The Federation of Unions for the Promotion of Workers' Rights filed on 22 June 2012 for the unpaid wages of some 50 workers; conciliation on 12 July 2012 failed on that single point and the file reached the Council on 20 July 2012 [Para 9][Para 10][Para 11]. The company was invited to the hearing of 30 July 2012 and to choose an arbitrator, and did not appear at either stage [Para 25][Para 34][Para 66]. Only four workers attended: Ms Y, Ms L, Ms P and Mr P [Para 39]. All four had been hired through a labour contractor (ការិយភារី), Mr K, without any written contract, and were paid by the day or by the month [Para 44][Para 47][Para 50][Para 53].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the federation could represent the workers at all, given that no local union had yet been formed and the letters transferring the workers' rights to the federation were never filed? [Para 15][Para 20]
2. Whether the panel could continue the arbitration in the employer's absence? [Para 25][Para 27]
3. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay the wages that the labour contractor failed to pay the workers he had supplied? [Para 38][Para 67]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** they came to work as usual, but wages arrived irregularly — about ten workers went unpaid in February and all of them in March — and when they asked, the answer was that the employer had already paid the contractor [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43]. Ms Y says she is owed a full month of March and five days of April on a USD 300 monthly wage; Ms L USD 170 for February, USD 150 for March and USD 60 for April; Ms P USD 150 for March; Mr P USD 30, 70, 140 and 70 for January to April [Para 45][Para 46][Para 49][Para 52][Para 56].

**Employer (respondent):** the company entered no position at all; it stayed away from both the conciliation of 12 July 2012 and the hearing of 30 July 2012, and filed no evidence [Para 3][Para 30][Para 66].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the company was ordered to pay the wage arrears owed to the four workers who appeared: USD 357.69, USD 380, USD 150 and USD 310 respectively [Para 75][Para 76].

The panel first fixed who was before it. Although the company and the federation are both signatories of the Memorandum of Understanding on professional relations in the garment industry, no local union had been created at the factory, and a federation's appearance does not by itself make individual workers parties to that memorandum [Para 14][Para 15][Para 17][Para 18]. Under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 a party may be represented only by a lawyer or by a person holding written authority, and the transfer letters were never produced [Para 19][Para 20]. One woman who came for her absent husband was therefore excluded, and the panel decided only the claims of the four workers present [Para 23][Para 24].

Because the employer had been properly summoned and gave no valid reason, the panel continued in its absence under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099, accepting the claimant's uncontradicted account and treating the interest part of the order as non-binding since the employer had not chosen that option [Para 26][Para 27][Para 28][Para 29].

On the merits the question was whether an employer owes wages to workers engaged by a contractor. Article 45 defines the contractor as a person who recruits workers himself to perform work for an entrepreneur at a lump price, and Article 48 provides that where the contractor has no money or has not performed his obligations, the entrepreneur or head of the enterprise must take his place and satisfy the obligations towards the workers, who may sue the entrepreneur directly [Para 67][Para 68][Para 69]. The panel read Article 48 as making the entrepreneur answerable for the contractor's workers even though there is no direct relationship with them [Para 70][Para 71]. On the facts the company gave the work to Mr K, who recruited the four, and Mr K never paid them; the company was therefore bound to pay in his place [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an entrepreneur gives work to a labour contractor who recruits the workforce and then fails to pay wages, the entrepreneur is substituted for the contractor and owes the workers their wages directly, regardless of the absence of any contract between them [Para 68][Para 70][Para 74]. A federation that appears for workers must prove its authority in writing; absent a local union and absent filed transfer letters, the panel hears only the workers who attend in person [Para 19][Para 21].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's decision to treat the interests part of its order as non-binding, because the employer had not chosen that option, was not needed to dispose of a pure wage claim [Para 29].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The memorandum of understanding is dated 28 September 2012, a month after this award issued; the year is evidently 2010 but the text says otherwise [Para 14]. The contractor's name is printed both as "គឹម លី" and "គីម លី" [Para 63][Para 73]. The award nowhere shows the arithmetic behind the USD 357.69 figure, which does not appear in the itemised findings [Para 46][Para 75]. The list of attendees includes a fifth person who was then held not to be a party, so the count of four is stated three times against a list of five [Para 22][Para 23][Para 39]. OCR corruption is heavy throughout: lines 32, 43 and 57 carry broken Latin fragments, and the operator and signature block are unreadable apart from the printed names [Para 32][Para 43][Para 78]. Individual workers, the contractor and the union officers appear only by initial or role.
