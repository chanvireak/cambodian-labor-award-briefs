# Award No. 79/08 — New Mek Garment Co Ltd (Arbitration Council of Cambodia, 10 July 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of three: លី តៃ សេង (employer-appointed), អន ណន (worker-appointed), គង់ ផល្ហៈ (president) · Doc ID 140528

## 1. Facts
The employer, ក្រុមហ៊ុន ញូមេ ក ហ្គាមេន ខូ,អិលធីឌី (New Mek Garment Co Ltd), a garment factory of about 1,200 workers at Steung Meanchey, Meanchey district, Phnom Penh, faced a collective dispute filed by telephone and taken up by សហព័ទ្ធ សហជីព.សហជីព កម្ពុជា ដើម្បី សិទ្ធិ កម្មករ ប្រចាំ រោងចក្រ ញូ មេក, a registered union of about 500 members in a plant with seven unions and none holding most-representative status [Para 1][Para 28][Para 30]. Of ten demands, conciliation settled seven; the remaining three came to the panel, which saw issue 2 settled by agreement and issue 3 withdrawn, leaving only issue 1 [Para 14][Para 18]. Workers are paid a monthly wage rather than by output, so the dispute concerns the incentive bonus of 1,200 riel a day, not the base wage [Para 46].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must restore the 2007 sewing quotas and crew sizes, or set quotas that most workers can actually meet? [Para 31][Para 47]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** in 2007 a crew of 32 to 33 workers sewed 950 small or 850 large shirts in eight hours and 70 to 80 per cent of crews earned the 1,200 riel daily bonus; in 2008 the quota rose to 1,150 and 1,050 shirts with crews cut to 27 or 28, so only about three of 34 crews could meet it and the bonus was earned on only 7 or 8 days a month [Para 31][Para 33][Para 35]. Workers who fell short were reprimanded or warned, and many resigned to join factories paying better [Para 36]. The union asked either for the 2007 quotas or for quotas proportional to crew size [Para 42][Para 43].

**Employer (respondent):** the company cannot return to last year's figures and applies its own policy [Para 3]. It admitted some workers had been warned but said it does not punish those who try [Para 38][Para 39]. It conceded it has no clear written policy on setting quotas, that this is a management matter needing time, and that its own lawyer agreed the practice should be corrected [Para 40][Para 45].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the demand that the company apply the previous year's sewing quotas was rejected, with no order made against the company [Para 68][Para 69].

The panel began from Article 2 of the Labour Law: an enterprise's workforce is employed under the management and direction of the employer [Para 48]. Following Awards 24/05, 18/08, 20/06, 108/06 and 116/07, it held the employer holds the right and authority to manage its human resources, including setting output volumes and incentive levels, provided that management is lawful and reasonable [Para 49][Para 51]. It added, from Award 10/03, that changes in production are ordinary in the Cambodian garment industry and that an employer has discretion over incentive pay according to circumstances, so varying a quota is not in itself a breach of the employment contract [Para 52][Para 53]. Award 116/07 was followed for the proposition that an employer may change the number of garments to be cut but must ensure the change is reasonable [Para 56].

Turning to the facts, the panel found the 2008 quota had no clear basis: the company had no stated policy for fixing quotas, and the new figures were attainable by only a small section of the workforce — three of 34 crews against 70 to 80 per cent in 2007 [Para 61]. It therefore held the setting of the quota to be unreasonable and, citing Article 312(2) of the Labour Law, said the Council may decide other disputes in equity and may order an employer to act lawfully and fairly [Para 65][Para 66]. It observed that the employer must establish a clear policy on output so that the quota is equitable and most workers can meet it [Para 63][Para 67]. That said, the union's specific demand — reinstating the 2007 quota — was not lawful either, because fixing output volume is the company's prerogative, so the claim was rejected [Para 64][Para 68].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may lawfully change production quotas and incentive thresholds from one year to the next, since setting output is a management prerogative and such variation is ordinary in the garment industry; but the prerogative must be exercised under a stated policy that a reasonable proportion of the workforce can meet, and a panel will not substitute a previous year's figures for the employer's own.

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that refusing a worker's claim does not endorse the employer's practice, and that the employer remained obliged to adopt clear and equitable quota-setting rules for the future [Para 63][Para 67].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The president's name is printed as គង់ ផល្ហៈ on the face of the award and as គង់ ផល្លៈ in the signature block. The employer-appointed arbitrator's surname is printed in a lengthened form in the signature block than in the panel heading, and the worker-appointed arbitrator's signature appears under a different name altogether from the one in the heading. The factory's name appears in at least three spellings. The award records seven unions in the plant but states the parties could not recall their names, so no union other than the claimant can be identified. Paragraph 64 is garbled at the point where the panel states its conclusion and its text repeats itself, and several quota tables have lost their column structure so the figures in §1 above are reconstructed from the surrounding prose.
