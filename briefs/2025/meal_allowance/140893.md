# Award No. 004/25 — Ry Ling (Cambodia) Apparel Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 September 2025)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Chiv Phirum (employer-appointed), Toun Si Phan (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (chair) · Doc ID 140893

## 1. Facts
The employer, Ry Ling (Cambodia) Apparel Co., Ltd, operates a garment factory in Thpong district, Kampong Speu province [Para 1]. On 28 July 2025 the Labour Disputes Department received a complaint from the workers' representatives raising 17 points on working conditions [Para 10]. Conciliation on 14 August 2025 settled 15 points, leaving 2 unresolved, which were forwarded to the Arbitration Council on 20 August 2025 [Para 12]. The panel was constituted and heard both parties, present and represented, on 28 August 2025 [Para 15]. At the hearing the workers withdrew the sub-claim seeking a copy of the employment contract [Para 16]. The two remaining issues were the demand for fixed sewing targets and the demand for six-month fixed-duration contracts [Para 17]. The parties chose a non-binding award [Para 18]. The workers are represented by the Khmer Youth Voice union, registered and recognized by the Ministry of Labour on 9 July 2025 [Para 49]. The panel recorded 15 workers with thumbprints plus 3 representatives present, 18 persons in total [Para 103]. The employer submitted company registration documents, internal rules, time-study tables, bonus tables and a worker-delegation agreement; the workers submitted union registration, a complaint letter and a letter objecting to that agreement [Para 22]. The panel found the company's main activity was garment sewing, with a stated workforce of 120 workers in total and 432 female workers [Para 47].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer is obliged to set a fixed sewing target for workers in the sewing teams [Para 104]?
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer is obliged to conclude fixed-duration employment contracts of at least six months with the 18 complainant workers [Para 131]?

## 3. Positions of the parties
The workers said targets were raised every week — 130 trousers per hour in week 1, 140 in week 2, 150 in week 3 and 160 in week 4 — while the target bonus stayed at 2,000 riel per day [Para 57]. Workers missing targets were called in by team and section heads, warned orally and made to thumbprint warning letters, causing discouragement and resignations [Para 64]. On contracts, the workers said three-month fixed-duration contracts are too short and create anxiety about non-renewal [Para 83], relying on Article 73 of the 1997 Labour Law and Instruction No. 050 of 17 May 2019 to argue that workers should receive contracts of at least six months [Para 87]. The union said it sought application as a principle for all workers, not only the 15 signatories [Para 94].

The employer said targets are set per garment model by its industrial-engineering (IE) department [Para 3]; they start at 60–80 pieces per hour in the first week, rise by 10 pieces per hour where a team meets them, and are held constant where a team cannot [Para 68]. It denied warnings, discipline or terminations for missing targets [Para 72]. It submitted time-study and bonus tables showing bonuses from 2,000 to 4,500 riel [Para 76]. On contracts, the company uses three-month fixed-duration contracts only [Para 81], giving seven days' notice and paying termination indemnity [Para 82]. It said most workers prefer this because benefits under the Labour Law are received sooner, that its approved internal rules require fixed start and end dates [Para 90], and it cited Article 65 to say contract form may follow custom [Para 91].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Dismissed. Both claims were rejected — the demand that the employer fix sewing targets [Para 161], and the demand for six-month fixed-duration contracts for 18 workers [Para 162]. The award is non-binding and becomes final if no party objects within 08 days; no payment in riel or USD was ordered [Para 163].

Before the merits, the panel applied paragraph 19 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 on representation, and accepted the 15 thumbprinted workers plus the 3 representatives present at the hearing as the complainants [Para 103].

On Issue 1, the panel held that Article 2 of the 1997 Labour Law gives the employer the right to manage and organise the enterprise, but that right must be exercised in accordance with law and reason [Para 105]. Employers may unilaterally establish bonus schemes beyond statutory bonuses to encourage productivity [Para 106]. The party making a claim bears the burden of proof [Para 112]; the workers produced no evidence of the alleged warnings and resignations [Para 115], and both parties accepted that base wages are not cut when targets are missed [Para 116]. The employer's measurement and bonus tables showed targets were studied and evaluated [Para 125]. The panel found the setting and raising of targets lawful and reasonable and not affecting workers' rights [Para 127], so the employer has no duty to fix targets [Para 128].

On Issue 2, Article 73 of the 1997 Labour Law addresses termination only, not contract duration [Para 133]; the six-month illustration in Instruction No. 050/19 of 17 May 2019 is explanatory and creates no obligation [Para 134]. Under Article 65 of the Labour Law and Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the 2007 C. C., a contract arises from matching offer and acceptance [Para 138]. Neither party can force the other to accept terms [Para 144]; the workers cannot compel a six-month contract [Para 157], and equally the employer cannot force a three-month contract on an unwilling worker [Para 158].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
**Ratio:** An employer's unilateral setting and periodic raising of production targets and a target bonus is an exercise of managerial prerogative under Article 2 of the 1997 Labour Law, and will be upheld where the employer shows a studied, graduated method, base wages are unaffected, and the claimant produces no evidence of unlawful warnings or discipline [Para 127]. A demand that an employer conclude fixed-duration contracts of a particular minimum length cannot be granted, because contract formation requires matching offer and acceptance under Article 65 and C. C. Articles 311, 336 and 337, and Instruction No. 050/19 sets no mandatory minimum [Para 134]. Representation before the Council under paragraph 19 of Prakas No. 099 requires written authorization or direct participation [Para 98].

**Obiter dicta:** The employer likewise cannot impose a three-month term on workers who do not agree [Para 158].
