# Award No. 005/25 — ផូគី ថរ៉ា រី សត & ស្ប៉ា (ខេមបូឌា) (Arbitration Council, 30 October 2025)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · សេង រួច ហ៊ុន · អន ណន · គង់ ផល្លៈ · Doc ID 140892

## 1. Facts
The employer operates a hotel in Phnom Penh and employs 350 workers, of whom 110 are women. [Para 61] The workers were represented by four employee representatives designated by the Department of Labour Disputes on 20 June 2025, and by a union registered on 13 September 2021. [Para 62] The union's leadership of three included a president, a vice-president and a secretary. [Para 63] A collective claim bearing 173 workers' names and thumbprints was submitted. [Para 64] The workers' representatives filed a collective labour dispute over service charges; conciliation resolved two of three points, and the one unresolved point was sent to the Arbitration Council on 3 September 2025. [Para 18] The panel was formed on 5 September 2025. [Para 21] The hearing was postponed from 15 September 2025 to 6 October 2025 at the employer's request, with the workers' consent. [Para 24] Both parties attended. [Para 25] Before 2018 the employer retained 10% of monthly service charges as a breakage reserve; agreements of 16 July 2018 and 21 January 2021, each for a two-year term, reduced this to 7.5%. [Para 68] Those agreements expired on 31 December 2022, but the employer continued the 7.5% deduction without a new agreement. [Para 69] At the hearing the parties settled Issue 1 (posting of detailed service-charge data on the union or hotel notice board), leaving Issue 2 for the panel. [Para 27]

## 2. Issues in dispute
The panel recorded two issues, of which the parties settled the first. [Para 26]
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must post detailed data on service charges collected from customers on the union or hotel notice board? [Para 2]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay all service charges collected from customers to the workers and stop deducting 7.5% of total service charges as a breakage reserve, with effect from the date of the award? [Para 66]

## 3. Positions of the parties
The workers' representatives argued that under Article 134 of the Labour Law service charges are a charge added for service that the employer must collect and pay to staff in contact with customers, and that the 7.5% breakage deduction violated Article 134. [Para 76] They relied on Article 127, which governs wage deductions but does not authorize deductions for future breakage. [Para 80] They relied on Article 13, arguing that a mechanism below the law is void, and that a unilateral deduction is void. [Para 82] They cited the earlier award in case No. 11/03 H. C. treating service charges as part of wages. [Para 83] They added that some breakage was caused by overtime or outside hired workers yet charged to the reserve, and that the employer should deduct from the wages of those who actually caused damage. [Para 98]
The employer argued the 7.5% reserve followed the 16 July 2018 agreement, point 3, and the 21 January 2021 agreement, point 2. [Para 6] It said the deduction followed professional custom under Article 136, absent any Prakas on distribution, and that most Phnom Penh hotels retain 10%, with only three retaining none. [Para 86] It relied on internal rule 18 making workers responsible for property they personally damage, and said the proportional reserve was a better mechanism than individual wage deductions that could exceed the subsistence minimum under Article 127. [Para 90] It stressed the deduction was taken proportionally from all sections, not from any individual worker or section that caused breakage. [Para 94]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Granted; the panel ordered the employer to pay all service charges collected from customers to the workers and to stop the 7.5% deduction as a breakage reserve with effect from the date of the award. [Para 146]
The panel held that under Article 134 of the 1997 Labour Law service charges are an incentive paid by customers to staff of hotels and similar enterprises, which the employer must collect and pay in full to employees who served the customers. [Para 116] The employer's duty is to collect the charges and pay them to the workers who dealt with the customers. [Para 117] The panel endorsed the interpretation in award No. 11/03 H. C., followed in award No. 042/16 Victoria Angkor, that the service charge must be paid to the workers in contact with customers. [Para 121] It also endorsed the interpretation in award No. 096/19 that service charges form part of wages under Articles 102 and 103. [Para 138]
The panel found that from 31 December 2022 no agreement existed, yet the employer continued the 7.5% deduction unilaterally. [Para 112] It held the unilateral deduction unlawful. [Para 122]
On Article 136, the panel held that distribution by professional custom or Prakas concerns who participates in distribution, and contains no authorization for a proportional deduction from all sections regardless of who caused breakage. [Para 126] Internal rule 18 makes the individual worker responsible for loss or damage that worker personally causes during work; the proportional deduction from all sections was inconsistent with that rule. [Para 129]
On Article 127, the panel held the employer may deduct only for materials the worker is responsible for, and never beyond the amount necessary for the worker's and family's subsistence. [Para 139] The panel found no evidence that any claimant worker caused the losses charged to the reserve. [Para 141] The deduction was therefore inconsistent with the internal rules and contrary to the law. [Para 143] Under Article 34 of Prakas 099, the panel may order immediate cessation of unlawful conduct, and so ordered the employer to pay all service charges and stop the 7.5% deduction immediately. [Para 145]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Service charges collected from customers are part of wages and must be paid to the employees who provided the service, not retained for general hotel expenses. [Para 117] An employer may not maintain a unilateral proportional breakage reserve after the agreement authorizing it expires; a deduction is lawful only under Article 127 where the individual worker is responsible for the lost or damaged items, and it may not exceed the subsistence minimum. [Para 139]
**Obiter dicta:** The parties chose a non-binding award. [Para 29] Prior Council awards were treated as persuasive comparators. [Para 121]
