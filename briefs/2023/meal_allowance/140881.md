# Award No. 010/23 — Ji Fei Sen (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ជី ហ្វេ សិន (ខេមបូឌា) ឯ.ក) (Arbitration Council, 19 May 2023)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer, An Non for the workers, Kuy Nam presiding; An Non filed a dissenting opinion) · Doc ID 140881

## 1. Facts
Ji Fei Sen (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ជី ហ្វេ សិន (ខេមបូឌា) ឯ.ក) runs a garment-cutting and sewing factory on National Road 4 at Trapeang Toul, Kamboul, Phnom Penh, employing 1,780 workers, 1,302 of them women [Para 1][Para 62]. The workers' representatives filed 11 demands on 22 March 2023; conciliation on 2 April 2023 settled six, and five unresolved points reached the Council on 10 April 2023 by report No. 228 [Para 25][Para 26][Para 27]. The hearing was held on 20 April 2023 [Para 24].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company's decision to stop work on Fridays and Saturdays in January 2023 was an unlawful suspension of the employment contract, entitling workers to the missing 75 per cent of wages [Para 64][Para 174]?
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must reimburse nursery fees, against actual invoices, to women workers who place children over 18 months outside the factory [Para 101][Para 215]?
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must provide a sweetened food supplement, as a drink or a cake, at every overtime session, in addition to overtime pay [Para 119][Para 226]?
4. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must stop setting production quotas above the workers' capacity [Para 126][Para 236]?
5. (Issue 5) Whether the employer must supply chilled, hygienic drinking water and orange juice to the workers [Para 144][Para 249]?

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** cutting Fridays and Saturdays amounted to a suspension of the contract carried out without the labour inspector's oversight, so the 75 per cent shortfall was owed; the agreement accepting 25 per cent was void because the company exploited their fear of losing work [Para 71][Para 73]. On Issue 3 they relied on Notice No. 003 of 10 February 2004, which requires a sweetened supplement during overtime [Para 121].

**Employer (respondent):** there was no suspension, only an agreed cut in working days under Article 65 of the Labour Law, and the company agreed to the union's own proposal [Para 173][Para 186]. On Issue 3 it argued it had complied with instructions by paying 2,000 riel of overtime meal money instead of a sweetened snack [Para 232].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to provide a sweetened food supplement, such as a sweetened drink, at every overtime session in line with Notice No. 003 of 10 February 2004, and rejected the other four claims [Para 235][Para 265][Para 267].

On Issue 1 the panel held that the parties changed the working days by agreement, which Article 65 of the Labour Law and Articles 311 and 336 of the Civil Code permit, so the arrangement was a reduction of days rather than a suspension [Para 181][Para 186][Para 187]. It found no evidence of exploitation, and so rejected the 75 per cent claim [Para 208][Para 209][Para 213].

On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 186 of the Labour Law, which requires employers with 100 or more women workers to set up a nursing room and a nursery [Para 215]. Because the employer had in fact built a nursery and engaged child-minders, following Awards No. 63/04 and 103/08, the workers had no further right to outside fees [Para 219][Para 223][Para 224].

On Issue 3 the panel read Notice No. 003 as imposing a duty to give a sweetened supplement at each overtime session, following Award No. 031/22 (A Plus A Su) and Awards No. 035/22, 041/22 and 006/23 [Para 229][Para 231]. It held that payment of 2,000 riel of overtime meal money under Notice No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 did not discharge that separate duty [Para 233][Para 234].

On Issue 4 the panel applied Article 2 of the Labour Law and held that setting quotas falls within the employer's managerial right where it is lawful and reasonable; the workers produced no evidence of penalty or coercion, so the claim failed [Para 236][Para 241][Para 246][Para 248].

On Issue 5 the panel applied Article 1 of Prakas No. 054 of 10 February 2000 and Award No. 173/14, read as requiring sufficient hygienic drinking water rather than chilled water and orange juice specifically [Para 249][Para 250][Para 251]. A laboratory analysis of 4 April 2023 showed the water in order, and the claim was rejected [Para 256][Para 258][Para 262].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An agreed reduction of working days is a modification of the contract under Article 65 of the Labour Law, not a suspension, so no statutory suspension wage is owed, and the agreement stands unless the party alleging coercion proves it [Para 186][Para 187][Para 208]. Separately, the sweetened-supplement duty in Notice No. 003 is distinct from, and not discharged by, overtime meal money under Notice No. 041/11 [Para 233][Para 234].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the Labour Law sets only a maximum number of working days and does not forbid an enterprise from reducing them [Para 190][Para 191].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel-constitution sentence prints "ឆ្នាំ ២០១ ២០២៣" at [Para 28] and a duplicated year at [Para 35]. The findings at [Para 183] name "ក្រុមហ៊ុន A" rather than the respondent. The three worker representatives are named in full at [Para 63] but reduced to fragments at [Para 164], and one is later referred to by an unexplained single letter. The company name is printed as "ជី ដី-ហ្វេ សិន" at [Para 95]. The dissenting opinion of arbitrator An Non is largely unrecoverable, [Para 286] to [Para 288] being strings of digits and Latin fragments, though its operative request — an order to pay the remaining 75 per cent for January 2023 — survives at [Para 285].
