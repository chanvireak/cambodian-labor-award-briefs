# Award No. 190/13 — S.H. International Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 2 October 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mar Samborana (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Pen Bunchhea (chair) · Doc ID 139862 · unofficial English translation of the Khmer original

## 1. Facts
S.H. International Co., Ltd, a Phnom Penh garment factory employing 700 workers, declined on 31 May 2013 to renew the three-month fixed-duration contracts (1 March to 31 May 2013) of Mr K, President of the Cambodian Labour Power Union (CLPU), and Mr L, its Assistant. [Para 19][Para 27][Para 30] CLPU had been formed by twelve workers on 9 May 2013 and was registered on 5 August 2013; the union is the claimant. [Para 20][Para 34]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr K and Mr L and pay them back pay from the date of dismissal to the date of reinstatement? [Para 4][Para 21]
2. Whether the employer must pay wages for May 2013 owed to Mr K and Mr L? — resolved by agreement at the hearing. [Para 5]
3. Whether the employer must pay 4,000 riel in lieu of lunch per day to each worker? — withdrawn by the workers at the hearing. [Para 5][Para 11]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the non-renewals were union discrimination — the employer knew Mr K had left FTUWK to form CLPU and asked him why he had done so, and Mr L was targeted as Mr K's assistant; the June 2013 notification letter proves the employer was told of the union leadership. [Para 31][Para 32][Para 40]
**Employer (respondent):** the same claim against the same employer was already decided in Award 122/13, which rejected reinstatement and back pay; the award is final and enforceable under the MoU, so the panel must not rehear it. The employer declined to answer the merits. [Para 23][Para 33]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider the workers' demand that the employer reinstate Mr K and Mr L and pay back pay from dismissal to reinstatement. [Para 74] The award is binding and enforceable by the parties under the Memorandum of Understanding on Improving Industrial Relations in the Garment Industry (MoU). [Para 75]

The panel first classified the claim. Under paragraph 2 of Article 312 of the Labour Law and clause 43 of Prakas 099, disputes over the interpretation and enforcement of law, regulations or a collective agreement are rights disputes decided according to law; all others are interests disputes decided in equity. [Para 41][Para 43] Because reinstatement is provided for by the Labour Law, the demand was a rights dispute, and the MoU makes arbitration of rights disputes binding on these parties. [Para 12][Para 45]

The panel then applied res judicata, taking the three conditions from Award 153/08 Hytex Garment: the same parties, the same issue, and a final ruling. [Para 52] All three were satisfied — the parties in Case 122/13 were the same, the demand was the same reinstatement and back-pay claim (which had also covered Mr C), and the panel there had rejected it on the merits. [Para 57][Para 62][Para 64]

The panel accepted that the doctrine yields where the earlier process was irregular or the earlier decision rested on false bases seriously affecting a party's right to justice. [Para 66] It found no such exception here: in Case 122/13 the workers filed a dark, unreadable EMS receipt on 8 July 2013 and, when ordered on 18 July 2013 to produce the original for comparison, failed to do so by the deadline, although the panel had given them full opportunity. [Para 68][Para 70] That failure was a failure to prove the claim, and the late arrival of a clean receipt from the Ministry did not excuse it. [Para 71][Para 72]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a party was given a full opportunity to file evidence in the earlier case and missed the deadline, the later production of that evidence does not open an exception to res judicata: the same claim between the same parties, already decided on the merits, will not be reconsidered. [Para 66][Para 72]

**Obiter dicta:** the MoU creates binding arbitration only for rights disputes; for interests disputes the parties may choose non-binding arbitration and may object to the resulting award without affecting their duty to implement a rights award. [Para 12][Para 14]
