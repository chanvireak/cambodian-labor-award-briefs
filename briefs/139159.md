# Award No. 066/15 — Im Vey Ang Thenal Manu Fac Cheu Ring L T D (Branch 1) (Arbitration Council, 31 March 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mr Mar Sombor Na for the employer party, Mr Tun Si Phan for the worker party, Mr Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139159

## 1. Facts
The respondent is a garment and knitting enterprise at House 754, National Road 2, Sangkat Choeng Roeum, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employing about 280 workers. [Para 1][Para 37] On 19 January 2015 it applied to the Ministry of Labour for permission to suspend all employment contracts for one month, and on 29 January 2015 it issued a notice suspending every contract from 1 February to 28 February 2015 while offering USD 15 as travel and accommodation allowance. [Para 56][Para 60][Para 77] Of the 228 workers who transferred their rights to the Democratic Union of Khmer Garment Workers, three attended the hearing; the union demands 100% of wages and other benefits for the suspension month. [Para 39][Para 41][Para 42][Para 43]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to pay wages and other benefits in full, at 100%, during the period in which it suspended the employment contracts from 1 February 2015 to 28 February 2015? [Para 43][Para 61]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Article 71(11) places any suspension under the supervision of the labour inspector, and Article 72(1) preserves the wage obligation unless a contrary provision applies; the employer received no written authorisation from the labour inspector, who came to the factory and merely wrote a report. [Para 44][Para 46][Para 54][Para 58] The words "seen and agreed" ("បាន ឃើញ និង ឯកភាព") on the inspector's minute mean only that the inspector agreed with his own report, not that the suspension was authorised; they also sought USD 45. [Para 58][Para 79]
**Employer (respondent):** it applied to the Ministry of Labour and Vocational Training in accordance with the law, and Article 71(11) requires only that a suspension be under the inspector's supervision; under Article 71(1) its only remaining obligation during suspension is to provide accommodation, and the USD 15 per month it paid exceeds the USD 7 required by Instruction No. 029/14. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 52][Para 57]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to provide wages and other benefits in full, at 100%, for the period of suspension from 1 February 2015 to 28 February 2015. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 87]

The panel read Article 71(11) as requiring two cumulative conditions: a genuine state of economic difficulty, and that the suspension be placed under the supervision of the labour inspector. [Para 61][Para 63] Following its earlier awards (No. 027/08 AK Khit, issue 6; No. 136/08 Su Per Thich, issue 2; No. 51/09 Yong Va, issues 1 and 2), it held that a suspension must be notified to the labour inspector and inspected and confirmed by him as truly in conformity with the real economic situation. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 66]

Applying Article 72(1), the panel restated the established rule: where the suspension procedure has been properly followed, the employer owes no wages; where it has not, the employer owes 100% of wages even though there is no work for the workers to do (following Awards No. 60/06, 74/07, 28/08, 53/08 and 71/09). [Para 67][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70]

On the facts, the employer did apply on 19 January 2015 and ministry officials inspected the factory on 27 January 2015, but no written authorisation to suspend was ever issued; the officials only signed a report bearing the words "seen and agreed", and that phrase is an administrative acknowledgement of the inspection, not an authorisation. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 78] The panel noted that the same words were signed on the report recording the workers' own demand for USD 45, which confirmed the phrase carried no approval effect, and that no other document showed any authorisation. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 81] The suspension was therefore not in accordance with the procedure of the Labour Law, and the wage obligation revived in full. [Para 82][Para 83]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of employment contracts is lawful only if the employer both notifies the labour inspector and obtains an inspection and written confirmation that the enterprise genuinely faces the economic difficulty relied on; a bare minute recording that the inspector "saw and agreed" with his own factual report is not such a confirmation. Where that procedure fails, the employer must pay 100% of wages and benefits for the whole suspension period even though no work was performed. [Para 64][Para 68][Para 69][Para 78][Para 83]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that Article 71(1) leaves the employer with an accommodation obligation during a lawful suspension, and its comparison of the USD 15 paid with the USD 7 required by Instruction No. 029/14, were not necessary to the outcome once the procedure was found defective. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 63]
