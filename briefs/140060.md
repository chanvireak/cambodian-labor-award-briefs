# Award No. 99/11 — Shoe Premier II (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 25 August 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ri, An Nan, Pen Bun Chhea) · Doc ID 140060

## 1. Facts
Shoe Premier II (Cambodia) has manufactured shoes since 1998 and employed 2,700 workers at the date of the award. The Free Workers' Union of the Shoe Premier factory, with about 1,050 members, was the claimant but was not yet the most representative union. The dispute concerned Ms K Eng Liang, the team leader of sewing line I, whom the workers accused of habitually insulting and verbally abusing them. On 22 June 2011 the workers petitioned the employer, and 96 workers in line I thumbprinted a demand that she be transferred to another section. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46][Para 54][Para 37]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the demand to transfer the team leader out of sewing line I is a collective labour dispute within the panel's jurisdiction? [Para 78]
2. Whether the employer must transfer Ms K Eng Liang out of sewing line I to another section? [Para 78][Para 96]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Ms K Eng Liang constantly used improper and insulting language towards the workers in her team, reducing some to tears, and the conduct had continued for two or three years. Complaints through the suggestion box brought no action, and the workers sought only her transfer, not dismissal. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58][Para 61]
**Employer (respondent):** The dispute was not a collective labour dispute because it arose between a group of workers and a team leader who is not an employer, and the plant continued to operate normally. Transfer was the company's managerial right; the team leader's words were merely education for workers who did not work well, and she had already been warned. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 65][Para 67][Para 70]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the workers' demand that the company transfer Ms K Eng Liang out of sewing line I. The rights-dispute decision was binding immediately under the 28 September 2010 Memorandum of Understanding; the interests-dispute decision would become final eight days after notification absent a written objection. [Para 121][Para 122][Para 123][Para 124]
The panel first classified the demand as a rights dispute because it concerned a request for transfer provided for in the Labour Law. It then examined whether the dispute was collective under Article 302. It held that the three conditions were met: the dispute was between a group of workers — union members and 96 line-I workers — and the employer; it concerned the relationship between employer and workers, namely the team leader's manner of supervising them; and although the enterprise still functioned normally, the dispute was supported by many workers and could affect their morale and workplace order. It rejected the employer's argument that the team leader was a stranger to the employment relationship, because she acted in the employer's name and protected its interests, making her the employer's representative. [Para 76][Para 78][Para 88][Para 89][Para 91][Para 92][Para 94]
On the merits, the panel accepted that the team leader's abusive language was wrongful and unethical, and that a supervisor should apply the internal rules and warn workers rather than insult or humiliate them. It nevertheless held that workers cannot order or demand the dismissal or transfer of another worker unless they prove that the person is dangerous and cannot be retained in the enterprise. The panel found that transferring Ms K Eng Liang was not a good solution, because if she did not change her conduct she could mistreat workers in another section just as she had in line I. The proper remedy was enforcement of the internal rules. The employer had already questioned her and issued a written warning, and it had stated that a second warning or dismissal would follow if she reoffended; the panel considered that response reasonable and just. [Para 103][Para 106][Para 107][Para 108][Para 109][Para 110][Para 112][Para 114][Para 116][Para 117][Para 118]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Workers cannot compel an employer to transfer or dismiss a supervisor or fellow worker merely because of abusive conduct; they must prove that the person is dangerous and cannot be retained in the enterprise, and where the conduct falls short of that, the appropriate remedy is enforcement of the employer's internal disciplinary rules through warnings and, on repetition, dismissal. [Para 103][Para 107][Para 114]

**Obiter dicta:** Abusive language that does not cause physical harm can still damage workers' morale and mental health, and the employer must act promptly if it continues. [Para 115][Para 120]
