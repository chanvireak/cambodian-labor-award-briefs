# Award No. 48/07 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន កាត់ដេរ អ៉ីថី នីធី v សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ នៃ រោងចក្រ អ៊ីចឹង សុទ្ធ សាអនុភាព (Arbitration Council, 9 July 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phalla, president; Kau Thach; Tuon Si Phan) · Doc ID 140611

## 1. Facts
The employer, ក្រុមហ៊ុន កាត់ដេរ អ៉ីថី នីធី, runs a garment factory at Building 646, National Road 2, Sangkat Chake Angre Krom, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, with 630 workers [Para 1][Para 36]. The claimant is សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ នៃ រោងចក្រ អ៊ីចឹង សុទ្ធ សាអនុភាព, which holds a most-representative certificate dated 9 August 2005 [Para 1][Para 38]. Of sixteen demands, seven were settled in conciliation on 30 May 2007 and nine came to the panel [Para 27][Para 28]; 404 of the 630 workers joined the complaint [Para 37].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must deduct union dues of 1,000 riel a month from members who authorise it in writing? [Para 40]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay wages for the half hour of rest before the overtime shift starts? [Para 48][Para 97]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay three months of maternity wages before the leave begins and US$15 a month in lieu of a crèche for children from 18 months? [Para 54][Para 113]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must add US$3 a month to the attendance bonus on top of the existing US$5? [Para 62][Para 63]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the company must add US$5 a month to seniority pay for workers with more than five years' service? [Para 64]
6. (Issue 6) Whether the company must add US$10 a month for the ten security staff? [Para 71]
7. (Issue 7) Whether the company must let workers use annual leave for personal errands such as identity documents? [Para 78]
8. (Issue 8) Whether the attendance bonus must be kept where a worker is thirty minutes late in a month for a broken bicycle or traffic? [Para 80]
9. (Issue 9) Whether the company must reduce time-punching to three times a day, or instead install more machines? [Para 85][Para 204]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The union relied on Prakas 305 for the check-off and Article 186 for the crèche allowance [Para 2][Para 55]. It argued other factories pay for the overtime break, that annual leave should cover obtaining identity papers, and that two working punch machines for over 600 workers cost 15 to 25 minutes at lunch [Para 49][Para 86][Para 93].
**Employer (respondent):** It refused the check-off because it deducts dues for no union at all, including this one [Para 41], and refused the rest-break pay because overtime is voluntary and the break matches the unpaid lunch break [Para 51][Para 53]. It refused extra bonuses and seniority as not required by law [Para 62][Para 65], and called the security staff contractors paid US$70 to US$90 a month [Para 73].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to pay three months of maternity wages before the leave begins, to pay US$15 a month in lieu of a crèche, to install two more time-punch machines and repair the broken one; the other six demands were refused [Para 222][Para 223][Para 230][Para 220][Para 228].
On Issue 1 the panel applied res judicata: award 94/04 had already decided the same dues check-off between the same parties [Para 94][Para 95], so it declined to consider the demand [Para 96][Para 220].
On Issue 2 the panel found that the Labour Law does not require wages during rest hours, the only exception being breastfeeding under Article 184 [Para 98][Para 99], following award 57/04 that the thirty-minute lunch break is not working time [Para 100][Para 101]. The overtime rest break was likewise not working time [Para 102], so the demand raised a benefit question rather than a right, and only a most-representative union may bring such a claim [Para 103][Para 104]; it was refused [Para 112][Para 221].
On Issue 3 the panel applied Article 183, which entitles a woman to half pay during maternity leave [Para 117]. Read with Article 115(3), it is payable before the leave starts, as earlier awards held [Para 124][Para 127]. On the crèche, Article 186 obliges an enterprise with at least 100 women to provide a nursery and crèche, or to pay for care where it cannot [Para 128][Para 130]; the company had no space to build [Para 131], and the workers proved actual outside care costs of 60,000 to 80,000 riel [Para 137][Para 138], so US$15 a month was ordered [Para 139][Para 223].
On Issues 4 and 5 the panel held that an attendance bonus beyond the US$5 fixed by Notification 017 and a seniority increment beyond the statutory scale were claims above the legal minimum [Para 141][Para 146]; the union was most representative and could bring them [Para 142][Para 147], but gave no evidence of fairness or of the employer's capacity, so both failed [Para 143][Para 151][Para 224][Para 225].
On Issue 6 the panel held the security staff were employees, following award 04/05 [Para 156][Para 158], but they already earned above the US$50 minimum including seniority of about US$55 [Para 159][Para 166], so the extra US$10 was a benefit claim it refused [Para 168][Para 169][Para 226].
On Issue 7 the panel accepted that obtaining birth and identity papers is a civic duty and that the demand was reasonable [Para 174][Para 175], but held that annual leave timing requires agreement between employer and workers, following awards 21/05 and 45/05 [Para 178][Para 179], and told the parties to agree dates [Para 180]; the demand as framed was refused [Para 181][Para 228].
On Issue 8 the panel read point 3 of Notification 745, preserving the benefits under Notification 017 [Para 187][Para 188], and award 33/07, holding the bonus is not a penalty on lawful leave [Para 191][Para 192], so it accrues pro rata [Para 193]; but no credible evidence of unfairness was produced, and the demand was refused [Para 199][Para 200][Para 229].
On Issue 9 the panel held that the employer's right to manage under Article 2 covers how attendance is recorded, and that four punches a day are lawful and reasonable [Para 201][Para 208]. It refused to order three punches [Para 205][Para 207], but found that only two working machines for more than 600 workers endangered safety under Articles 230 and 250 [Para 209][Para 212][Para 214], and ordered two more machines plus repair of the broken one [Para 219][Para 230].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Time spent resting before or during an overtime shift is not working time and carries no statutory wage, so a demand to be paid for it is a benefit claim that only a most-representative union may bring, and it fails without evidence of fairness [Para 98][Para 103][Para 104][Para 111]. Maternity pay under Article 183 is due before the leave begins, and where an employer cannot build a crèche, Article 186 requires it to pay for care at the rate the workers actually pay [Para 122][Para 127][Para 130][Para 138]. An employer's statutory right to manage attendance recording does not extend to leaving too few working machines for the workforce: a shortage that costs workers their lunch break breaches the safety duties in Articles 230 and 250 [Para 208][Para 214][Para 216].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that obtaining a birth certificate or identity card is a duty every Cambodian must perform, a finding it balanced against the need for agreement on leave dates [Para 174][Para 176].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is dated 9 July 2007 and the hearing 15 June 2007, but the file was reportedly sent to the secretariat on 07 May 2007, before the 30 May conciliation it follows [Para 24][Para 27][Para 28]. The union's membership is given as about 4,535 in a factory of 630 workers, which cannot be right [Para 36][Para 38]. The number of unresolved points is printed as 209 in one paragraph and nine elsewhere [Para 28][Para 31]. Issue 4's claim is stated once as US$3 and once as a figure of 55 dollars [Para 140]. The employer is named អ៉ីថី នីធី in the parties block and the union's factory name is rendered differently again [Para 1]. The employer-side arbitrator's name is reduced to fragments of Latin script on the signature page, and several statutory quotations are truncated mid-sentence [Para 107][Para 232].
