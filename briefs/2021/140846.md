# Award No. 036/21 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ម៉េង ដា ហ្វូត វៀ អ៊ិន ដាស ស្ទ្រីល (Arbitration Council, 15 December 2021)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៊ីង សុទ្ធី for the employer, អន ណន for the workers, ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា presiding) · Doc ID 140846

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ម៉េង ដា ហ្វូត វៀ អ៊ីន ដាស ស្ទ្រីល manufactures footwear and employed several thousand workers [Para 29]. On 28 October 2021 twenty workers of the company filed three demands on working conditions; conciliation on 15 November 2021 settled two, and the single unsettled point — holiday pay — came to the Council on 23 November 2021 under conciliation report No. 761 [Para 9][Para 10][Para 11]. The claimants were employee delegates of the company rather than a union [Para 31]. Only ten workers had thumbprinted support for the demand [Para 39]. The Council heard the parties online on 2 December 2021 and neither objected to procedure [Para 8][Para 16]. The award is non-binding [Para 19].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether an employer must pay 300 per cent of the daily wage — 100 per cent plus a further 200 per cent — to workers who work on a paid public holiday? [Para 32][Para 43]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Workers already receive 100 per cent of their wage for a paid public holiday, so when they come in and work that day they should receive a further 200 per cent, making 300 per cent [Para 36]. They added that neighbouring factories pay twice plus one, and that the employer had asked them to work those holidays when urgent work arose, so the employer should reciprocate [Para 37][Para 38].

**Employer (respondent):** The company applies the law: workers working a paid public holiday receive 100 per cent plus a further 100 per cent holiday allowance, totalling 200 per cent, and are also given a 4,000 riel meal allowance for attending [Para 41][Para 42]. It noted the factory has 40 full and 40 alternate employee delegates and 14 unions, none of which has most-representative status, and there is no collective agreement [Para 40].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider the demand for 300 per cent holiday pay, leaving the employer's 200 per cent practice in place [Para 72][Para 73].

The panel applied Article 161 paragraph 1 of the Labour Law, requiring the ministry in charge of labour to fix paid public holidays for all enterprises, and Article 164, entitling a worker who works on a holiday to the wage of the work done plus a compensatory allowance at the level set by ministerial Prakas [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45]. Article 4 of Prakas No. 10 SKBY of 4 February 1999 sets that allowance at one day's ordinary wage [Para 46]. Both parties accepted that the employer pays 200 per cent in total, and the panel held this already complied with the Prakas, following Awards 205/13 and 179/14 (Meng Da Footwear) among others [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 51].

Because no internal regulation, contract, collective agreement or past practice provides 300 per cent, the panel characterised the demand as one going beyond the law — an interest dispute rather than a rights dispute [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54]. It then applied Article 96(2) of the Labour Law on collective agreements, Article 54 (new) of the amended Trade Union Law and Article 72 of that law: only a most-representative union, or unions together entitled to conclude a collective agreement, may bring an interest dispute [Para 55][Para 57][Para 63][Para 64]. Since the claimants were employee delegates and not such a union, the panel held it could not consider the demand and refused it [Para 70][Para 71][Para 72].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Wage for work performed on a paid public holiday is fixed by Article 4 of Prakas No. 10 SKBY at one day's ordinary wage in addition to the wage for the work done, so a demand for 300 per cent exceeds the law and is a dispute of interest, not of rights [Para 46][Para 48][Para 54]. The panel will only consider an interest dispute where the claimant is a most-representative union or a union entitled to conclude a collective agreement; employee delegates have no standing to bring one [Para 64][Para 70].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that an award resolving an interest dispute substitutes for a collective agreement, applies to all workers in the company for one year, and causes them to lose the right to conclude a collective agreement or to strike over that interest claim in future [Para 65][Para 66].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The workforce figure is given as 3,760 at para 29 but as 4,147 at para 40, and neither is corrected [Para 29][Para 40]. The percentage claimed is printed inconsistently: "៤៣០០ ភាគយ" at para 52 and "២០០០ ភាគរយ" inside the operative paragraph at para 73, against 300 per cent everywhere else [Para 52][Para 73]. The demand is numbered as claim 3 in the conciliation report but argued and decided as "Issue 1" [Para 2][Para 32]. The company name appears in at least three different spellings across the face of the award, and one signature block is garbled into stray words [Para 75].
