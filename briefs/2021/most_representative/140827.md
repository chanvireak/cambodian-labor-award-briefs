# Award No. 066/20 — Cambodia Airports Services (CAMS) (Arbitration Council, 17 February 2021)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborna appointed by the employer party, Tuon Si Phan appointed by the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 140827

## 1. Facts
Cambodia Airports Services (CAMS) (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូឌា អ៊ែរ ផត មែនី ជ្ជមែន សឺវីស (CAMS)) manages the international airports at Phnom Penh, Siem Reap and Sihanoukville and employed 1,269 workers. [Para 177][Para 178] Three unions filed four demands on 16 November 2020, and conciliation on 23 December 2020 settled none of the three that reached the panel; the fourth, concerning workers said to enjoy special protection, was kept by the conciliator for handling in the capacity of labour inspector. [Para 47][Para 49][Para 50][Para 51] Faced with a fall of more than 90 per cent in flights and passengers, the employer ran a three-phase restructuring through 2020 and offered two rounds of voluntary resignation and one round of early retirement, paying the four statutory heads of severance plus a lump sum it called a voluntary payment. [Para 5][Para 7][Para 187][Para 188]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer's voluntary resignation and early retirement programme was concluded by the free consent of the parties or procured by bribery, inducement and coercion? [Para 181][Para 342]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must take back the union members and activists who resigned, retired early or were dismissed, and pay them wages from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 210][Para 370]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer was obliged to negotiate with the three unions before making 161 workers at Siem Reap International Airport collectively redundant? [Para 325][Para 487]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, ភាគី តំណាង កម្មករ និយោជិត):** the employer gave lump sums of about USD 4,000 to push workers into resigning or retiring early, made them sign undertakings not to complain elsewhere, paid severance on only 80 per cent of wages, and served warning letters selectively on union activists; the 161 redundancies at Siem Reap were announced to the unions without any real negotiation, contrary to Article 95 of the Labour Law. [Para 193][Para 196][Para 216][Para 223][Para 338][Para 339]
**Employer (respondent, ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូឌា អ៊ែរ ផត មែនី ជ្ជមែន សឺវីស (CAMS)):** the programme was voluntary, public and explained to every worker in advance with calculations, gave benefits above the law and was cancelled once the situation eased; no worker was coerced, and the unions were notified and met twice, on 4 and 13 November 2020, before the Siem Reap list was settled. [Para 202][Para 204][Para 230][Para 340][Para 491][Para 495]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — every claim was rejected, and the award issued as a non-binding order. [Para 498][Para 499][Para 505][Para 506]

On issue 1 the panel began from Article 65 of the Labour Law and Articles 664, 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code: a contract, and its dissolution, rests on the concurring will of the parties, and neither party nor a third party can impose terms on an unwilling party. [Para 342][Para 344][Para 347][Para 353] Applying the rule that the party asserting coercion bears the burden of proof, it found that the workers had produced nothing beyond their own statements; the programme had been publicly announced, explained and calculated with each worker beforehand. [Para 359][Para 360][Para 363][Para 365][Para 368] The bribery claim was therefore dismissed. [Para 369]

On issue 2 the panel held that even the most representative union needs a specific mandate from each worker, or a written transfer of the right to claim, before it can bring individual reinstatement claims. [Para 374][Para 378][Para 383] No such lists or authorisations were filed, and most of the named workers did not appear at the hearing, so the claim was not considered for them. [Para 389][Para 391][Para 392][Para 394][Para 396] Of the individual cases, the long-serving baggage handler's request for a recalculation of severance failed because he never specified the sums or heads of compensation said to be short. [Para 416][Para 417][Para 418] The claim of the worker dismissed on 23 October 2020 failed on the merits: under Article 74 and Article 27 of the Labour Law, and clauses 19.3.1 and 19.3.2 of the internal regulations, repeated failure to answer the employer's calls and to attend work justified the dismissal, which the panel found proportionate. [Para 433][Para 438][Para 450][Para 451] The security officer who signed a resignation and settlement on 4 and 7 September 2020 was found to have resigned voluntarily, without evidence of coercion, so no moral damages were owed. [Para 465][Para 471][Para 475]

On issue 3 the panel read Article 95(1) and (3) of the Labour Law as requiring the employer to notify the workers' representatives in writing and to propose measures limiting the impact of the layoff, but not to negotiate until agreement is reached; the employer had met the unions and staff delegates twice and had also met the strike-resolution committee. [Para 487][Para 491][Para 495][Para 496] The claim was dismissed. [Para 497]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that offers a voluntary resignation or early retirement package above the statutory minimum is not taken to have coerced or bribed the workers who accept it: consent is presumed from a publicly announced, explained scheme, and the worker who alleges bribery or duress must prove it. Article 95 obliges the employer to inform and consult workers' representatives about a collective layoff, not to obtain their agreement. [Para 363][Para 368][Para 496]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that only the most representative union may bring a collective interest dispute, and that it may nonetheless represent individual workers where they authorise it in writing, are general observations on representation. [Para 378][Para 383]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The employer's name is printed in at least five different spellings across the award, and its registration certificate number appears both as MOC-22109205 and, in the findings, as 09862. [Para 62][Para 177]
- The year 2020 is truncated to "20" throughout, including in every date of the restructuring phases and of the individual dismissals. [Para 7][Para 241][Para 425]
- One dismissal notice records a period running to 30 November 2024, three years after the award, and another sets a 2020 seniority period ending in 2024. [Para 257]
- The list of claimants is inconsistent: 12 named workers are claimed, 8 attended, and the panel mentions "85" and "09 per cent" of them in passages that OCR has destroyed. [Para 236][Para 237][Para 393]
- Workers, union officials and representatives are named in full at dozens of points; they are identified here by role and issue only. [Para 60][Para 234]
