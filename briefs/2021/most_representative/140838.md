# Award No. 023/21 — Horizon Outdoor (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហឹរ៉ាយ ហ្ស៊ិន អៅដរ (ខេមបូឌា)) (Arbitration Council of Cambodia, 13 October 2021)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: You Sounthy (employer-appointed), An Vireak (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chha (president) · Doc ID 140838

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហឹរ៉ាយ ហ្ស៊ិន អៅដរ (ខេមបូឌា) makes bags at Village Phsar Trach, Kampong Tralach, Kampong Chhnang, and employs 8,224 workers [Para 83]. The claimant is សហជីព កម្ពុជា នៃ ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហឹរ៉ាយហ្សិន អៅដរ (ខេមបូឌា), a minority union registered on 17 February 2021, while សហជីព សិទ្ធិ ប្រយោជន៍ កម្មករ holds the certificate of most representative status of 28 November 2019 [Para 146][Para 145]. Of five demands filed on 2 August 2021, one was settled at conciliation on 16 August 2021 and four reached the Council, where points 2 and 4 were merged, leaving three issues [Para 17][Para 18][Para 25]. The disputed acts are the employer's refusal to deduct union dues for the claimant union, its conduct of the staff-delegate election of 31 July 2021, and alleged anti-union discrimination [Para 85][Para 101][Para 109].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must deduct union dues from members' wages for សហជីព កម្ពុជា and remit them, under Article 49 of the Trade Union Law, from the date of the award? [Para 85]
2. (Issues 2 and 4) Whether the Council has jurisdiction over the demand that the staff-delegate election of 31 July 2021 be rerun lawfully and the election not recognised? [Para 101]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer discriminated against and threatened the leadership and activists of សហជីព កម្ពុជា? [Para 109]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The union argued that it had given notice with consent letters bearing workers' thumbprints, that the employer nonetheless refused to deduct and instead demanded resignation letters from the old union, which is interference in union affairs, and that the delegate election was unlawful because union leaders were told of it only on 28 July 2021 and the employer fielded only outgoing delegates [Para 87][Para 88][Para 103][Para 104]. It added that the employer dealt only with the most representative union and punished no manager who threatened its vice-president [Para 110][Para 113].
**Employer (respondent):** ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហឹរ៉ាយ ហ្ស៊ិន អៅដរ (ខេមបូឌា) replied that clause 10 of the collective agreement governs deductions and only the most representative union may bring such a dispute, that the election was lawful and notified to the provincial department on 14 July 2021 with candidates posted and announced three times, and that it knew nothing of any threat and discriminated against no union [Para 91][Para 106][Para 116].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to deduct union dues from members' wages and remit them to សហជីព កម្ពុជា under Article 49 of the Trade Union Law from the date of the award, declined to consider issues 2 and 4 for want of jurisdiction, and dismissed issue 3 [Para 215][Para 216][Para 217].
On jurisdiction over the claimant the panel applied the first paragraph of Article 54 of the amended Trade Union Law, which reserves exclusive bargaining and dispute-resolution rights to the most representative union, but read it with the eighth paragraph of Article 59 and Article 14 of Prakas No. 303 of 2 July 2018, which let a minority union represent its own members in collective disputes not arising from the collective agreement [Para 135][Para 140][Para 142][Para 150]. It followed Award 180/16, Award 171/16 and Award 014/20 [Para 139]. Clause 10 of the agreement was held to bind only the rights-and-benefits union, and so not to bar this claim [Para 148][Para 149].
On the merits of issue 1 the panel relied on point 4 of Article 49 of the Trade Union Law 2016 — a worker may belong to one union at a time, and the later union notifies the employer, upon which membership transfers — and on Article 26(1) and Article 129 of the Labour Law of 1997, which void deductions not authorised in writing by the worker [Para 153][Para 155][Para 161][Para 166]. Demanding a resignation letter from the old union went beyond what Article 49 requires [Para 161]. It found the formalities satisfied: the union gave notice and attached consent letters with thumbprints, as Article 4 of Prakas No. 300 of 2 July 2018 requires, with at least 10 days' notice [Para 175][Para 176][Para 180].
On issues 2 and 4 the panel held that Article 47 of the Trade Union Law sends every complaint about delegate elections, candidacy and their validity to the labour court, and that it therefore had no jurisdiction [Para 183][Para 186][Para 190]. On issue 3 it applied Article 12 of the Labour Law and Article 62 of the Trade Union Law and held that the workers bore the burden of proving discrimination; beyond one worker's written account, no specific evidence was produced, so the claim failed [Para 198][Para 199][Para 204][Para 210].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A minority union may bring a collective dispute to the Arbitration Council in its own name where the dispute does not arise from the collective agreement, because the eighth paragraph of Article 59 of the amended Trade Union Law and Article 14 of Prakas No. 303 preserve its right to represent its own members; and once it gives the employer the notice and written consents required by Article 4 of Prakas No. 300 with at least 10 days' lead, the employer must deduct dues under Article 49 even though a different union holds most representative status [Para 150][Para 180].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that the most representative union may leave the workplace during working hours to recruit members while a minority union may not, but treated that difference as outside the dispute [Para 201]. It also observed that a second, unauthorised deduction would be void under Article 129 of the Labour Law [Para 163].
