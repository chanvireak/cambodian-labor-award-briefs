# Award No. 027/21 — Din Han Enterprise Bright Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឌីន ហាន អេនធើ ប្រាយ ខូអិលធីឌី) (Arbitration Council of Cambodia, 15 November 2021)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Kol Vuthana (employer-appointed), An Vireak (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhem (president) · Doc ID 140836

## 1. Facts
The employer, ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឌីន ហាន អេនធើ ប្រាយ ខូអិលធីឌី, is a garment-cutting company in Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh employing 2,728 workers [Para 45]. The workers' representative filed a collective dispute on 27 August 2021 raising five working conditions [Para 10]; conciliation on 20 September 2021 settled three, and the two unresolved points reached the Council on 29 September 2021 [Para 11][Para 12]. At the hearing the workers' side withdrew issue 2, leaving issue 1 alone for decision [Para 18][Para 19]. The disputed act is the employer's payment of 50 per cent net wage, and no bonuses, to workers who were treated for COVID-19 and quarantined between 11 April and 14 May 2021 [Para 51].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the period from 11 April 2021 to 14 May 2021, during which 700 workers were treated for COVID-19 and quarantined, must be treated as sick leave under the company's internal rules, so that full wages, the attendance bonus and the seniority bonus are payable? [Para 48][Para 78]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** ភាគី តំណាង កម្មករ និយោជិត argued that clause 4 of the internal rules gives 100 per cent wages during sick leave, that all 700 workers held proper medical certificates, and that workers infected in February and March 2021 were paid in full, so the later group should be treated alike [Para 49][Para 56][Para 54]. They added that COVID-19 is not force majeure because it can be foreseen and prevented [Para 58].
**Employer (respondent):** ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឌីន ហាន អេនធើ ប្រាយ ខូអិលធីឌី replied that nearly half its workforce was infected and it suspended operations entirely, suffering heavy losses, so it lacked the capacity to pay 100 per cent; it had followed Prakas 043/21 by paying 50 per cent net wage, 100 per cent seniority bonus and a pro-rated attendance bonus [Para 63][Para 65]. It argued COVID-19 is a global epidemic limiting employer liability, and that the internal rules could not be applied [Para 66][Para 67].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the workers' claim for a further 50 per cent net wage, the attendance bonus and a 100 per cent seniority bonus for the 700 workers who were treated for COVID-19 and quarantined from 11 April 2021 to 14 May 2021 under the internal rules [Para 117].
On the suspension question the panel applied point 3 of Article 71 of the Labour Law of 1997, which makes absence certified by an official doctor a ground for suspending the employment contract, together with point 1 of Article 72, under which suspension relieves the employer of the duty to pay wages unless a contrary rule provides otherwise [Para 78][Para 80]. It followed the reasoning in Award 171/13 (Bouygues), and in Award 016/15 (San Dong Fu Wei Wood) and Award 204/16 (Vonami), holding that certified sick leave suspends the contract and neither side owes performance [Para 82][Para 85]. On that basis the absence of the 700 workers from 11 April 2021 to 14 May 2021 was a suspension, not paid sick leave [Para 86][Para 111].
The panel then examined whether the internal rules of 8 January 2013 supplied the "contrary provision" — they give full wages for the first month of certified sick leave and 60 per cent for months two and three [Para 90][Para 91]. It held they could not be applied to this period, because the employer was obliged to comply with the health measures and instructions of the competent authorities, and the factory at Village Thmei, Sangkat Stueng Mean Chey lay inside the "red zone" closed by government decision [Para 114][Para 103]. Prakas 043/21, dated 19 April 2021, required wages only for 1 to 14 April 2021 and left 15 to 30 April to voluntary support, so it created no obligation for the later period; the panel adopted the reading already given in Award 015/21 [Para 105][Para 108][Para 109]. It also found that the Ministry's question-and-answer document on COVID-19 pay was not a legal normative instrument [Para 77]. Since the employer had in fact paid 50 per cent net wage, the further claim failed [Para 113][Para 115].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a factory's operations are suspended on the order of the competent authorities during a lockdown, a worker's absence for COVID-19 treatment and quarantine suspends the employment contract under point 3 of Article 71 and point 1 of Article 72 of the Labour Law of 1997; the employer owes no wage for that period, the "contrary provision" exception does not revive an internal-rules sick-leave entitlement that conflicts with mandatory health measures, and a Prakas that fixes wages only for the first half of a month creates no obligation for the second half [Para 111][Para 114][Para 108].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that COVID-19 is a new, globally transmissible disease in which the employer's liability is limited and that employers must respect health measures set by the authorities; these remarks explain the context but do not carry the order [Para 96][Para 99].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR duplicates years in several places: the panel's constitution is printed as "1 October 2023 2021" [Para 13] and the internal rules as "8 January 2023 2013" [Para 90]. It also presents the claim amount as "4100 per cent" and then "1100 per cent" in the same passage, and the figure relied on in the reasoning and the order is 100 per cent [Para 56][Para 115]. One sentence in the reasons carries a stray worker count ("700 workers 3 persons") and the operative recital renders the date of the Prakas as "9 May 19, 2021", which the panel elsewhere dates 19 April 2021 [Para 86][Para 100][Para 104]. The hearing was held by Zoom for COVID-19 reasons and neither party objected to procedure [Para 16][Para 17].
