# Award No. 008/21 — Vai Full Garment (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 23 June 2021)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy appointed by the employer party, An Virea appointed by the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair; An Virea dissented in an annexed opinion) · Doc ID 140826

## 1. Facts
Vai Full Garment (Cambodia) Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ៃ ហ្វ៊ុល ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) អិលធីឌី), an export garment factory at Pong Teuk, Ang Snuol district, Kandal, employed 620 workers, of whom 332 put their thumbprints to the claim. [Para 59] The negotiating team filed 13 demands, and conciliation on 1 March 2021 settled 11 of them, leaving two for the panel. [Para 11][Para 12][Para 13] At the hearing the workers withdrew issue 2, so only issue 1 remained. [Para 22][Para 23] The proceedings were then suspended at the parties' request during the February 2021 COVID-19 outbreak and resumed on 28 May 2021. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 19]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must compute each worker's seniority from the first employment contract to the date of dismissal when paying notice compensation, seniority indemnity and moral damages? [Para 61][Para 94]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, ភាគី តំណាង កម្មករ និយោជិត):** the employer paid only 15 days of notice compensation and 14.5 days of seniority indemnity, whereas seniority must be counted from the first contract so that at least 15 days accrue; the employer instead counted seniority only from the end-2019 conversion of fixed-duration contracts into unlimited-duration ones, which strips them of benefits the Labour Law guarantees, and it paid an unexplained "adjustment" which it says is moral damages. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 75][Para 76][Para 78][Para 79]
**Employer (respondent, ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ៃ ហ្វ៊ុល ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) អិលធីឌី):** it paid all five heads of compensation provided by law, converted the contracts at the end of 2019 and paid 5 per cent severance at that point, and computed seniority from the conversion exactly as Notification No. 023/19 and Letter No. 070 of the Labour Inspectorate require; the factory has closed and the claim is unclear and already settled. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 64][Para 87][Para 88][Para 102]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel declined to consider the claim, holding that the conflict between the ministerial instruments and Article 73(7) of the Labour Law is a conflict of laws outside its competence, and the order issued as non-binding. [Para 118][Para 119][Para 120][Para 121]

The panel first narrowed the claimants: although 332 workers had thumbprinted the demand, only two could be matched to a first hiring date and a dismissal date, and it therefore considered the claim for those two only. [Para 89][Para 92][Para 93]

On the substance it set Article 73(6) of the Labour Law, which fixes severance at 5 per cent of wages paid during the contract, against Article 73(7), under which seniority is counted by adding the duration of the fixed-duration contract to that of the unlimited-duration contract replacing it. [Para 94][Para 95][Para 96] The employer had relied on Notification No. 023/19 of 8 July 2019 and on Letter No. 070 of 21 February 2021 from the Labour Inspectorate, which count seniority only from the date of conversion. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 106][Para 109]

The panel found that Notification No. 023/19 remains open to conflicting readings and does not state clearly how seniority is counted for severance, and that Article 47 of the Law on the Organisation of the Council of Ministers forbids a ministerial instrument from contradicting a law. [Para 105][Para 112][Para 113][Para 114] It concluded that it has no power to rule whether the Notification or the Letter conflicts with the Labour Law, and that the parties should seek an authoritative interpretation from the competent body or a court. [Para 116][Para 118] The claim was therefore not considered. [Para 119][Para 120]

**Dissent:** arbitrator An Virea recorded that the panel has full power under Article 312 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 099 to decide the issue put to it, that the workers never asked for a ruling on the validity of any instrument, and that the panel should not have declined to consider the dispute. [Para 127][Para 131][Para 132][Para 135]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the resolution of a claim turns on whether a ministerial notification or inspector's letter contradicts Article 73 of the Labour Law, the Arbitration Council will not choose between them: such a conflict of laws lies outside its jurisdiction, and the panel declines to consider the claim rather than compute seniority on either basis. [Para 111][Para 114][Para 118]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that paying 5 per cent severance while both parties continue to perform the contract is not a termination of it is an observation on the 2019 conversion, not the ground for declining the claim. [Para 97]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The number of claimants is printed as 332 in the findings and in the workers' argument, but as 330 in the list the panel analysed, and the employer refers to more than 400 dismissed workers. [Para 59][Para 66][Para 83][Para 89]
- One dismissal date is printed as 30 December 2022, a year after the award itself, and another as 28 December with the year cut to "20". [Para 89]
- The conciliation paragraph gives the date as 1 March 2021 in one place and as a garbled Khmer month with a truncated year in another. [Para 12][Para 51]
- The Labour Inspectorate's letter is cited under three different reference numbers and two different dates across the award. [Para 33][Para 79][Para 102]
- The four worker representatives are named in full in the procedural findings; they are identified here by role only. [Para 60]
