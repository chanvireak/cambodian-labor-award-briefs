# Award No. 003/21 — Hong Va (Cambodia) Garment Manufacture Fash Co., Ltd, Branch 1 (Arbitration Council, 26 February 2021)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy appointed by the employer party, Tuon Si Phan appointed by the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 140824

## 1. Facts
Hong Va (Cambodia) Garment Manufacture Fash Co., Ltd, Branch 1 (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហុង វ៉ា កម្ពុជា ហ្គាមិន ម៉ាញូ ហ្វាក់ ឆឺរីង លីមីធីត (សាខា ទី១)), a garment factory at Tuol Pongro, Khan Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh, employed 1,291 workers. [Para 43] On 15 January 2021 the Labour Disputes Department received a complaint from the union Khmer Workers' Livelihood Enhancement Federation raising five demands, three of which were settled at conciliation on 28 January 2021, leaving two for the panel. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 17] At the hearing the parties agreed issue 1, the conversion of fixed-duration contracts into unlimited-duration ones, so only issue 2 was decided. [Para 21][Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the workers may require the employer to remove a Chinese staff member from the company because he exceeded the authority given to him over Khmer workers? [Para 46][Para 69]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, ភាគី តំណាង កម្មករ និយោជិត):** the staff member was engaged only to distribute orders, but moved goods out of the factory as he pleased, gave orders directly to workers, reduced order volumes so that piece-rate bonuses fell, and dismissed and hired workers, none of it with the director's approval; other Chinese staff in the same post do not hold such power, and the workers fear the company will close. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 54][Para 55]
**Employer (respondent, ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហុង វ៉ា កម្ពុជា ហ្គាមិន ម៉ាញូ ហ្វាក់ ឆឺរីង លីមីធីត (សាខា ទី១)):** the staff member holds three roles — merchandiser, order distributor and assistant to the company director — so he is entitled to take decisions; the director, once told he was giving direct instructions to workers, told him to stop and he has; he is a company employee who has committed no fault, and the workers cannot demand his removal. [Para 56][Para 63][Para 64][Para 65][Para 66]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the demand that the employer remove the staff member, and the award issued as a non-binding order. [Para 81][Para 82]

The panel framed the question as whether the workers hold a right to require the removal of another employee. [Para 69] It relied on Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 311 of the Civil Code, under which a contract is created, modified or extinguished by the will of the parties to it, and drew from them the proposition that only the employer and the worker who are parties to a given employment contract may terminate the obligations under it, never a third party. [Para 69][Para 71][Para 73][Para 74] It followed earlier awards, including No. 116/10 and No. 83/12, holding that workers may not demand the dismissal or transfer of another worker unless they prove that the person is dangerous and cannot be kept in the enterprise, or that keeping him endangers the safety of others. [Para 75] Here the complainant workers were third parties to the contract between the employer and that staff member, and they produced no evidence that he was such a danger; keeping him, the panel found, did not threaten other workers' safety. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 80]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker or group of workers who are not parties to another employee's employment contract cannot require the employer to dismiss or remove that employee; such a demand succeeds only where the claimants prove that the employee is dangerous and that his continued presence endangers the safety of the workforce. [Para 73][Para 75][Para 80]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's acceptance that the staff member had somewhat exceeded his role in giving instructions directly to workers, and that the director had already corrected this, is an observation on the facts and was not the ground of decision. [Para 63]
