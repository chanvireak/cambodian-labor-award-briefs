# Award No. 020/21 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេន ស្ព័ត ស៊ូ (Ken Sport Shoe) (Arbitration Council, 28 September 2021)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · order under Article 313 of the Labour Law · panel: អ៊ីង សុទ្ធី (employer-appointed), អាន វីរៈ (worker-appointed), កុយ នាម (president) · Doc ID 140833

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេន ស្ព័ត ស៊ូ makes footwear in Kampong Chhnang with 8,311 workers [Para 48]. The workers' representatives filed 13 demands on 25 June 2021 [Para 19]; conciliation on 20 July 2021 settled seven, and report No. 513 K.B/AK/VK of 28 July 2021 referred six unresolved points to the Council [Para 20][Para 21]. At the hearing the workers withdrew Issue 2, on deductions for issuing new labour books, leaving five to be decided [Para 27][Para 28]. The five concerned food stalls inside the factory compound, union membership lists, equal benefits across the three plant unions, postponement of the sixth-term employee-representative election, and overtime for union leaders [Para 50][Para 64][Para 74][Para 79][Para 82].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer had to restore the food stalls in front of the canteen as before? [Para 93]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the employer had to disclose each union's membership list to the leaders of the three unions? [Para 110]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the employer was entitled to apply to members of other unions the conditions and benefits won by the three plant unions? [Para 123]
4. (Issue 5) Whether the employer had to postpone the sixth-term employee-representative election held on 29 June 2021? [Para 132]
5. (Issue 6) Whether the employer had discriminated against union leaders by refusing them overtime? [Para 141]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** ភាគី តំណាង កម្មករ និយោជិត ក្នុង ការដោះស្រាយ វិវាទ ការងារ រួម នៃ ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេន ស្ព័ត ស៊ូ argued that outside food stalls used to stand in front of the canteen and that only a Chinese catering concessionaire now sells expensive food, so workers cannot eat before overtime [Para 52][Para 53]; that the employer should show how many members each union has, since unions are losing members [Para 6][Para 70]; and that three union leaders were denied overtime for three years because of their union roles [Para 83][Para 85].
**Employer (respondent):** health rules bar outside vendors from the compound and the canteen meals are inspected by the provincial health department [Para 58][Para 61]; each union has already been given its own check-off record and membership list [Para 68][Para 118]; conditions and benefits are applied equally to all workers [Para 75]; and overtime follows actual need, voluntarily and without discrimination [Para 144].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — every claim was rejected: Issue 1 for want of jurisdiction, Issues 3, 4 and 6 on the merits, and Issue 5 for want of jurisdiction because electoral disputes belong to the Labour Court [Para 155][Para 156][Para 157][Para 158][Para 159].
On Issue 1 the panel began with jurisdiction: Article 32 of Prakas No. 099 confines it to collective labour disputes [Para 93], and Article 302 of the Labour Law requires the dispute to concern working conditions, the exercise of union rights or employer-worker relations [Para 95]. Applying the three conditions of Award No. 10/03-Zaksin Tech [Para 96][Para 97], it found 750 workers had thumbprinted the demand [Para 99], so the first condition was met, but no legal provision governs where food is sold, so the second was not [Para 103][Para 104][Para 105]; it therefore had no jurisdiction [Para 108]. On Issue 3, Article 61 of the Trade Union Law and Article 8 of Prakas No. 300 require the employer to keep dues-deduction records and to copy them on request [Para 110][Para 112]; the employer had done so, and a union may only request its own records [Para 118][Para 120]. On Issue 4, Article 2 leaves management of the company to the employer [Para 123], and applying one union's gains to all workers protects equal rights and professional harmony rather than breaching any rule [Para 128][Para 129]. On Issue 5, Article 47 of the Trade Union Law sends all complaints about representative elections to the Labour Court [Para 132], a position the panel had taken in Award No. 036/17 [Para 133][Para 136]. On Issue 6, Articles 12 and 62 of the Labour Law and Trade Union Law bar using union membership or activity as a ground for decisions on work allocation and benefits [Para 141][Para 142], but the complainant bears the burden of proving discrimination [Para 148], and no evidence supported the claim [Para 152][Para 153].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that no legal provision governs — such as where food may be sold inside a factory — fails the second condition of Article 302 of the Labour Law and lies outside the panel's jurisdiction, however many workers support it [Para 103][Para 107]. A complaint about an employee-representative election must go to the Labour Court under Article 47 of the Trade Union Law, not to arbitration [Para 139]. An employer that keeps dues records and gives each union its own list complies with Article 61, and need not disclose one union's members to another [Para 120][Para 121].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted the employer's undertaking to allow outside vendors back once the COVID-19 situation improves, without making it an order [Para 63].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The reasoning's summary on Issue 1 both refuses to consider the claim and orders the employer to arrange the stalls, contradicting the operative order that rejects it [Para 109][Para 155]. The workforce appears as 8,311 in the findings and 750 as the number of claimants [Para 48][Para 99]. The file mentions 13 demands, seven settled and six referred, but one of the six was withdrawn and Issue 2 has no decision [Para 20][Para 27][Para 156]. The hearing is dated 23 August 2021 while the invitations are dated 16 August 2021 [Para 18][Para 46], and the award date was postponed from 1 September to 28 September 2021 [Para 30]. Stray OCR fragments ("Ama", "fama", "Lin") interrupt several paragraphs [Para 93][Para 106][Para 136].
