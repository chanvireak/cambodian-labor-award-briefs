# Award No. 026/21 — Ken Sport Shoe (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេន ស្ព័ត ស៊ូ) (Arbitration Council of Cambodia, 1 December 2021)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Kong Phalla (president) · Doc ID 140842

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេន ស្ព័ត ស៊ូ makes shoes at Village Chamkar Svay, Sethey, Samaki Mean Chey, Kampong Chhnang, and employs 8,311 workers, about 80 per cent of them women [Para 54][Para 173]. The workers' representative filed six demands on 3 September 2021; conciliation on 22 September 2021 settled two, and four unresolved points were referred to the Council on 29 September 2021 [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20]. At the hearing the workers withdrew issue 1, leaving issues 2, 3 and 4 [Para 27][Para 28]. The disputed acts are the payment of wages through Wing and ACLEDA accounts rather than in cash, the employer's failure to deduct union dues for សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ for May, June and July 2021, and the absence of a nursery [Para 56][Para 81][Para 105].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether paying wages through Wing and ACLEDA accounts of each worker satisfies Article 113 of the Labour Law, or whether the employer must pay in cash by hand again? [Para 119]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay to សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ the dues it did not deduct from members' wages in May, June and July 2021? [Para 142]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must build a nursery and provide a breastfeeding room to the standard of the Labour Law? [Para 164]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The workers' representative argued that cash payment had been easy and trouble-free, that the single ACLEDA machine near the factory forces long queues, and that some workers lost wages for two to three months, received short payments or counterfeit and damaged notes, with the employer refusing responsibility [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61][Para 62]. On dues it said the union had more than 3,000 members paying 2,000 riel a month, yet received only about 4,000,000 riel, losing more than 2,000,000 riel a month after over 1,000 members disappeared from the list [Para 84][Para 85][Para 86][Para 87].
**Employer (respondent):** ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេន ស្ព័ត ស៊ូ replied that account payment is safer and more modern, that the employer transferred each worker's wage into that worker's own account and bears the risk of holding large cash sums, and that card problems are the worker's and the agents' responsibility [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67][Para 68]. On dues it said it deducted only from lists with complete details and thumbprints, and that since July 2021 a worker may belong to one union only, so payment goes to the last union joined [Para 93][Para 97][Para 100].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to build or arrange a nursery for women workers with small children, and dismissed the claims for cash payment of wages, for the union dues of May, June and July 2021, and for a breastfeeding room [Para 186][Para 187][Para 188][Para 189].
On issue 2 the panel applied Article 113 of the Labour Law of 1997, which requires wages to be paid directly to the worker unless the worker agrees to another method, and followed Award 274/13 in holding that "paid directly" means paid to the worker himself and not to a proxy or creditor, while payment through an ATM into the worker's own account is still direct payment [Para 119][Para 121][Para 122]. It found that the employer transferred each worker's wage into that worker's own Wing or ACLEDA account, never into another person's, and that it had answered the workers' complaints and had accounts reopened within a week [Para 124][Para 129][Para 132][Para 133]. The claim for a return to cash payment therefore failed [Para 136][Para 137].
On issue 3 the panel applied point 1 of Article 26 of the Trade Union Law 2016 and points 1 and 2 of Article 2 and Article 4 of Prakas No. 300 K.B/Pr.K.K.B.K of 2 July 2018: after written consent from the worker the employer must deduct and transfer the dues into the union's bank account, and the union must give at least 10 days' written notice with dated consents or thumbprints [Para 142][Para 144][Para 146][Para 147]. It followed Award 032/18, Award 054/18 and Award 104/19 [Para 150]. Examining the union's deduction list, the panel found no clear year, no statement of how many workers it covered and no signed or thumbprinted consent, so it could not credit the claim and dismissed it [Para 161][Para 162][Para 163]. On issue 4 it applied Article 186 of the Labour Law, which requires any enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a breastfeeding room and a nursery, and ordered a nursery since the factory has none [Para 164][Para 173][Para 174]. The breastfeeding-room claim failed: the employer produced photographs of an existing room and the workers, who bore the burden of proof, offered no evidence that it was unavailable or unfit, though the panel noted the employer must keep it clean [Para 175][Para 181][Para 182][Para 183].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Paying each worker's wage by transfer into that worker's own Wing or bank account is payment "directly" to the worker under Article 113 of the Labour Law, so an employer need not revert to cash; but an employer may deduct union dues only on a list that carries the year, the number of workers and each worker's dated written consent or thumbprint as Article 4 of Prakas No. 300 requires, and a union that cannot produce such a list loses its claim for the dues [Para 122][Para 147][Para 161]. An enterprise with at least 100 women must provide a nursery under Article 186 even where it pays a child allowance in substitution [Para 164][Para 174].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the employer must keep the breastfeeding room clean and usable, although Article 186 fixes no standard for it [Para 183]. It also noted that the workers' complaint list of nine names was met by the employer's explanation that three Wing accounts had been frozen for repeated wrong PIN entries and were reopened within a week [Para 128][Para 129].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR is heavily damaged. Several statutory and Prakas citations garble the phrase "after written consent", and one passage in the reasoning collapses into the stray words "Ling Lon" inside the Khmer sentence [Para 142][Para 148]. The award prints the panel's constitution date as "30 September 2019 2021" and the extension of the deadline as "26 June October 2021" [Para 21][Para 31]. Wage figures in the union's loss calculation are inconsistent: the union states dues of 2,000 riel per member and expected receipts of more than 6,000,000 riel, but the passage then gives 4,000,000 riel and elsewhere 20,000 riel from 13 members [Para 85][Para 86][Para 90]. The list of prior awards in the nursery reasoning breaks into untranslatable Latin fragments [Para 176]. Individual workers, union officers and employer representatives are named in the source and appear here by role only.
