# Award No. 150/16 — Sky Night International Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 26 July 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Kuy Nam, president, dissenting on issue 3) · Doc ID 139057

## 1. Facts
A garment factory employing about 1,400 workers faced a 16-point demand brought after a strike, of which 12 were settled at conciliation on 21 June 2016, leaving four for the panel [Para 11][Para 12][Para 18][Para 39]. The plant union was elected on 5 June 2016 but had not yet been registered by the Ministry; 419 workers transferred their rights to the federation and 422 were treated as complainants [Para 41][Para 42][Para 44]. On 13 June 2016 the employer signed agreements dissolving the unlimited-duration contracts of almost all workers, paying USD 100 per year of service and re-engaging them on six-month fixed-duration contracts with seniority reset [Para 52][Para 64][Para 67]. One worker, a union committee member, took the payment, continued for five days, and was excluded from work on 18 June 2016 [Para 54][Para 146].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate the worker or pay her termination compensation under the Labour Law? [Para 45][Para 99]
2. Whether the employer must preserve the workers' seniority bonus and their original start dates? [Para 64][Para 167]
3. Whether the employer must reduce the regular attendance bonus pro rata to the days of leave authorised by the employer? [Para 82][Para 214]
4. Whether overtime pay must be calculated by adding the seniority bonus and the position allowance to the basic wage? [Para 91][Para 256]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The 100 USD-per-year settlement gave less than the Labour Law allows, so seniority bonus and start dates should be preserved, since the workers had served six to ten years [Para 70][Para 71][Para 72]. The attendance bonus is part of wages and should be reduced only in proportion to authorised absences [Para 84][Para 86], and seniority and position allowances are wages that belong in the overtime base [Para 94][Para 95].
**Employer (respondent):** The worker left of her own accord by resignation letter and cannot be reinstated [Para 57][Para 114]. The settlement was voluntary and most workers accepted it, and workers who refused kept their seniority and unlimited contracts [Para 73][Para 74]. The attendance bonus exists to secure punctual attendance and is not wages, and the company maintains the basic wage as the overtime base [Para 87][Para 97][Para 98].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay seven days' notice compensation, outstanding annual leave pay and final wages to the dismissed worker, to pro-rate the attendance bonus, and to include the seniority bonus and position allowance in the overtime base; the claim to preserve seniority and start dates was dismissed [Para 295][Para 298][Para 299][Para 300].

On issue 1 the panel first classified the contract. Article 67(7) requires a fixed-duration contract to be in writing, failing which it becomes a contract of unlimited duration [Para 101][Para 102], and the panel followed its earlier reasoning to that effect [Para 103][Para 106]. No written six-month contract was produced by either side, so the relationship after the settlement was of unlimited duration [Para 110][Para 112]. On whether the worker resigned or was dismissed, the burden lay on the employer, which alone holds the resignation letter [Para 120]; nothing was filed by the deadline [Para 121], so the panel found a dismissal [Para 123]. No valid reason relating to aptitude, conduct or the needs of the enterprise was given, contrary to Article 74 [Para 126][Para 132]. On remedy, because the new unlimited contract had run only five days, Article 75 gave seven days' notice [Para 133][Para 139][Para 146]; Article 89 severance was not owed because service under that contract was under six months [Para 149], and for the same reason no moral damages under Article 91 [Para 156]. Annual leave under Article 166 and final wages under Article 116 were ordered [Para 159][Para 162][Para 166].

On issue 2 the panel held the 13 June 2016 agreements valid: voluntary, uncoerced, and consistent with Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code on the formation of contracts by offer and acceptance [Para 192][Para 193]. The workers did not explain how the settlement fell below the statutory floor, so the panel could not find it void under Article 13 of the Labour Law [Para 202][Para 204]. Seniority was therefore cut and start dates run from the new contracts [Para 210][Para 212], though the employer must keep paying the half-seniority amount as a separate allowance as agreed [Para 211].

On issue 3 the majority found the word "without absence" in Article 2 of Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 ambiguous and declined to presume it covered authorised as well as unauthorised absence [Para 219][Para 225][Para 230]. Authorised leave suspends the contract under Article 71(6), and Article 72(1) removes the wage obligation for those days [Para 249][Para 251][Para 252], so no attendance bonus accrues for them either; but the employer may deduct only the pro-rata amount, not the whole USD 10 [Para 253][Para 254][Para 255]. Arbitrator Kuy Nam dissented, reasoning that if authorised absence does not make attendance irregular then nothing may be deducted at all [Para 308][Para 309][Para 310].

On issue 4 the panel noted that neither Article 139 nor Article 5 of Prakas No. 80 Sakbey defines "wages during normal working time" [Para 259][Para 264]. Reading Article 102 (the wage strictly due for work performed) against the wider list in Article 103 [Para 266][Para 268][Para 270], it held that the seniority bonus rewards experience and loyalty and the position allowance rewards responsibility, so both are additional components of normal wages and belong in the overtime base [Para 277][Para 282][Para 287][Para 294].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer disposes of a resignation letter and fails to produce it, it has not discharged the burden of proving that the worker resigned, and the separation is treated as a dismissal requiring a valid reason under Article 74 [Para 120][Para 123][Para 132]. Where the parties freely agree to dissolve an unlimited-duration contract against a severance payment and to restart on a new contract, seniority runs from the new contract and is not preserved, provided the settlement is not shown to fall below the statutory minimum [Para 204][Para 210]. And the seniority bonus and position allowance are part of normal wages for the calculation of overtime [Para 287][Para 294].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observations that a contract procured by coercion may be treated as void [Para 188], and that an attendance bonus exists to encourage punctual attendance and may not be equated with wages generally, even though the USD 10 statutory bonus is a legal obligation [Para 222][Para 247].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company's name is printed in several forms (ស្កាយ ណាយ, សាយ ណាយ, ស្កាយ ណារ៉ុប), so the transliteration above is a best reading [Para 35][Para 42][Para 53]. The constitution of the panel is dated "២០២៣ ២០១៦" [Para 14], and the attendance-bonus practice is dated to 2011 in one place and 2012 in another [Para 223][Para 219]. The seniority scale in the table at Para 167 is destroyed by OCR (figures fused into "០៨២៤ ៣ ៤៤៥ ៦ ៧"). Money figures are printed inconsistently — the position allowance appears as 45 USD and as "១៥៦ ដុល្លារ" alongside the basic wage [Para 93][Para 279]. Paragraph 189 contains unreadable Latin text in place of a citation, and the closing date of the last page is cut off [Para 189]. The dismissed worker's name appears throughout the source; it is redacted here, as are the union officials' names [Para 59][Para 38].
