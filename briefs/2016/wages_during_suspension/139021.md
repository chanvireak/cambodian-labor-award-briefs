# Award No. 091/16 — M V Garment International Co., Ltd (Branch 3) (Arbitration Council, 17 May 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambor Na appointed by the employer, Liv Sovanna appointed by the workers, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · non-binding award · Doc ID 139021

## 1. Facts
M V Garment International Co., Ltd (Branch 3) cut and sewed clothing at Prek Ta Long, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, with about 2,300 workers. [Para 53][Para 54] Its local union of the Democratic Union of Cambodian Garment Workers was registered under certificate No. 1009 of 13 September 2006, and 714 workers of the branch transferred their rights to the federation by two letters dated 13 January and 16 February 2016. [Para 55][Para 57] The employer announced on 17 July 2015 a 500 riel daily meal subsidy for workers who came to work, stopped giving work on stretches in 2015 and early 2016, suspended contracts from 16 February to 31 March 2016, and let a six-month fixed-term contract held by Ms S expire on 30 October 2015 while she was on maternity leave. [Para 70][Para 71][Para 74][Para 87][Para 88]

## 2. Issues in dispute
Five claims reached the panel; issues 3 and 4 were withdrawn at the hearing, so it decided the remaining three in the panel's own numbering. [Para 22][Para 23]
1. Whether the employer must pay the 500 riel daily meal allowance from 01 August to 30 October 2015 and from 02 January to 07 February 2016, when it gave the workers no work? [Para 60]
2. Whether the employer must pay 100% wages, a 10 USD attendance bonus, a 07 USD travel allowance, the 500 riel meal allowance and a seniority bonus for the suspension from 16 February to 31 March 2016? [Para 74]
3. Whether the employer must take Ms S back into work and pay her back wages from the day she was stopped until the day she returns? [Para 82]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** living costs are high, the absence of work is the employer's own fault, and the 500 riel benefit is more favourable than the law and so must continue under Article 13; the suspension exceeded two months, so the labour inspector's authorisation should be annulled as void; and Ms S was never at fault, was on maternity leave, and a woman may not be dismissed during that leave even by prior notice. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 76][Para 77][Para 83][Para 84][Para 85]
**Employer (respondent):** it never paid a meal allowance before and introduced the 500 riel subsidy voluntarily to lift living standards, on the express condition of attendance, and it always gave prior notice when work was unavailable; the suspension of building A from 16 February to 31 March 2016 was properly authorised and only 50 USD a month was paid in lieu of accommodation; and Ms S's contract simply reached its term, which is non-renewal under Article 73, not dismissal. [Para 66][Para 68][Para 69][Para 74][Para 91][Para 92][Para 93]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three claims were rejected. [Para 173][Para 174][Para 175]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 2(2) of the Labour Law: the employer may manage and direct the enterprise, provided the exercise is lawful and reasonable. [Para 97][Para 101][Para 105] It read the announcement of 17 July 2015 as a benefit additional to the law, tied to attendance on a working day, and found the employer had on each occasion given prior notice to the workers, promising the allowance where no notice had been given. [Para 106][Para 112][Para 113] It found the employer did not pay the allowance during the stoppages but did pay it whenever a worker came and scanned in. [Para 114] It therefore held the employer had applied its announcement correctly, that withholding the allowance in those circumstances did not offend Article 13, and dismissed the claim. [Para 123][Para 125][Para 126]

On issue 2 the panel applied Article 71 of the Labour Law, which allows suspension for economic difficulty for no more than two months under the supervision of the labour inspector, and Article 72, under which an unlawfully suspended contract leaves the employer owing full wages. [Para 127][Para 128][Para 129][Para 134][Para 135][Para 136] It found the suspension of 16 February to 31 March 2016 was authorised by the labour inspector and that both parties agreed on that, so the suspension was procedurally regular and no wages or benefits were owed. [Para 138][Para 143][Para 144][Para 145] On the workers' request that the authorisation be declared void, the panel held that a worker who disagrees with an administrative letter should complain to the authority that issued it, and it found no evidence of any such complaint or refusal. [Para 140][Para 141]

On issue 5 the panel applied Article 73(5), which requires ten days' prior notice only for fixed-term contracts longer than six months, and held that no notice is required for a contract of six months or less. [Para 158][Para 160][Para 163] Ms S's last six-month contract expired on 30 October 2015 and the employer gave notice on 19 October 2015 — an act the law did not require. [Para 154][Para 162] On Article 182, relied on for the maternity bar, the panel followed Award No. 117/11 in reading it as prohibiting dismissal of a woman during maternity leave, but not as prohibiting the non-renewal of a fixed-term contract. [Para 165][Para 166][Para 168][Para 169][Para 170]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A meal subsidy granted by unilateral announcement and expressly conditioned on attendance is lawfully withheld from workers given prior notice that no work is available; the employer's management right under Article 2(2) permits a benefit more generous than the law to be granted on terms, and applying those terms does not breach Article 13. A contract suspension of two months or less that the labour inspector has authorised raises no obligation to pay wages under Articles 71 and 72. A fixed-term contract of six months or less expires at its term without any prior-notice obligation under Article 73(5), and Article 182's bar on dismissing a woman during maternity leave does not prevent such a contract from expiring. [Para 101][Para 123][Para 144][Para 160][Para 170]

**Obiter dicta:** the direction that a worker disputing the inspector's authorisation letter should lodge a complaint with the issuing authority rather than with the panel is a remark on remedies, not a ground of the dismissal. So is the panel's statement that it decides only for members of this union and workers who transferred their rights to the federation. [Para 59][Para 140]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The panel's constitution date is incomplete: "07 April, year" is printed with the year lost [Para 20].
- The meal-allowance claim is stated at the outset as July to December 2015 and 02 January to 15 February 2016 [Para 2], but the issue and the order confine it to 01 August to 30 October 2015 and 02 January to 07 February 2016 [Para 60][Para 173].
- Issue 2 fixes the suspension at 16 February to 31 March 2016 [Para 74][Para 138], but elsewhere the same suspension is treated as running from 08 February and workers argue it exceeded two months [Para 65][Para 139].
- The operative part opens twice, at [Para 172] and again at [Para 173], the first attempt being truncated.
- The number of workers appears as about 2,300 [Para 54] while 714 transferred rights [Para 57] and 08 attended the hearing [Para 58]; the initial claim is put at 11 conditions in one place and the conciliation at 06 points with 01 settled [Para 18][Para 19].
- The employer is printed variously as អឹម វ៉េ, អ៊ឹម វ៉េ, អ៊ឺម វើ, អ៊ីម វេ and "M&V" [Para 1][Para 14][Para 28][Para 53]; the chair's signature line is overlaid with debris ("ថៃ ខុសm CAZey") [Para 177].
- The award's own citation of Prakas No. 305 and of the earlier awards at [Para 102], [Para 119], [Para 132], [Para 136] and [Para 152] carries OCR-damaged numbers, some unusable.
