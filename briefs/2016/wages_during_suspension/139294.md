# Award No. 011/20 — Jin Yi Garmen (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 12 March 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Seng Vouch Hun (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Run Saray (president) · Doc ID 139294

## 1. Facts

Jin Yi Garmen (Cambodia) is a garment-cutting and sewing factory in Samakki Mean Chey district, Kampong Chhnang, employing 1,174 workers; 312 workers, including five representatives, brought the collective complaint. [Para 40] [Para 41] [Para 42]
From November 2019 the employer had no purchase orders and gave the workforce no work; it suspended the employment contracts for November and December 2019 with the labour inspector's authorisation. [Para 45] [Para 62]
From 1 January 2020 it neither sought further authorisation to suspend nor paid wages or other benefits, and the workers demanded the base wage of USD 190 per month until work resumed. [Para 43] [Para 46] [Para 63]

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. Whether the employer was obliged to pay the base wage of USD 190 per month to the workers from 1 January 2020 until it again provides work, given that the suspension of the employment contracts from that date was not submitted to the labour inspector? [Para 57]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** The suspension from 1 January 2020 onwards does not comply with Article 71 of the Labour Law, so the employer must pay 100% of wages for the period of the suspension. [Para 47] [Para 48]

**Employer (respondent):** Since March 2019 the company has faced a financial crisis for want of purchase orders and cannot pay; the minutes of the 30 December 2019 meeting record that workers who volunteer to wait for work claim no other benefits, so this is not a suspension at all, the employment relationship subsisting and normal operations expected in March. [Para 49] [Para 51] [Para 52] [Para 54]

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay the base wage of USD 190 per month to the workers from 1 January 2020 until it provides them with work as normal. [Para 76] [Para 77]

Article 71(11) of the Labour Law permits suspension of enterprise activity on serious economic or material difficulty, but only where the suspension is placed under the supervision of the labour inspector and does not exceed two months. [Para 57] [Para 58] Following its earlier awards (No. 58/07-8, 72/05, 22/05, 198/13, 160/14, 08/07 and 008/17), the panel read that supervision requirement as an obligation to notify and obtain the labour inspector's prior authorisation. [Para 59] [Para 60] [Para 61]

On the facts, both parties accepted that no work had been available since November 2019 because there were no orders. [Para 62] The two-month suspension of November and December 2019 had been authorised, but from 1 January 2020 the employer neither requested authorisation nor paid wages. [Para 45] [Para 63] [Para 70] The panel held that an employer's failure to provide work for want of orders is a suspension of the employment contract. [Para 64]

Article 72(1) provides that suspension affects the principal obligations of the contract, including the employer's obligation to pay wages, but the panel held that this applies only where the suspension is carried out in accordance with the legal procedure. [Para 65] [Para 66] It followed its previous awards to the effect that a company which has properly suspended under the labour inspector's supervision owes no wages, whereas a company which has not respected the law owes 100% of wages together with other benefits even though it has no work to give. [Para 67] [Para 68] [Para 69]

The employer relied on the minutes of the 30 December 2019 meeting, which registered workers volunteering to wait for work without claiming other benefits. [Para 55] [Para 71] The panel found those minutes concerned "other benefits" and were not relevant to the claim for the base wage, so it did not take them into account. [Para 72] [Para 73] It concluded that the suspension from 1 January 2020 was procedurally improper and that the wage obligation revived for the whole period of no work until work resumes. [Para 74] [Para 75]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

Article 72(1) relieves an employer of the wage obligation during a suspension of the employment contract only when the suspension itself satisfies Article 71 — prior notification to, and authorisation from, the labour inspector, and a duration not exceeding two months. Where the employer continues a suspension beyond the authorised period, or suspends without authorisation at all, the suspension is unlawful and the employer owes 100% of the base wage for the entire unauthorised period until work is again provided, even though no work is available. [Para 64] [Para 68] [Para 75]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the employment relationship between the parties is preserved throughout the suspension [Para 53]; it noted the 30 December 2019 minutes set a registration window of 11 to 21 January 2020 for volunteers [Para 55]; and it recorded the parties' agreement to extend the deadline for the award to 12 March [Para 19].

The header prints the award date as 12 March 2016, which cannot stand with reasons describing events from October 2019 to February 2020 and an extension of the deadline to 12 March in the same year [Para 1] [Para 19]. The hearing date is printed as 21 February 2019 although the surrounding procedural history places it in 2020 [Para 7] [Para 18]. The signature block loses the employer-side arbitrator's name entry to "ឈ្មោះ ៩--" [Para 79], and Para 44 opens with a garbled fragment ("-៥- និង ០").
