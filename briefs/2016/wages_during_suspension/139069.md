# Award No. 166/16 — Aerosoft Samit Footwear Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 22 July 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Vireak, appointed by the workers; Kong Phallha, chair appointed by both) · Doc ID 139069

## 1. Facts
The workforce of Aerosoft Samit Footwear Co., Ltd., a factory at Veal Vong village, Tasda commune, Sampeou Loun district, Battambang, raised ten collective demands, including prim (attendance) money of ten dollars (10$), travel and accommodation of seven dollars (7$), conversion to permanent status after two months and payments during the strike. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 10] Conciliation at the provincial Department of Labour on 07 July 2016 failed on all ten points and the file reached the Council secretariat on 11 July 2016. [Para 15][Para 16] The workers struck; the panel, constituted on 12 July 2016, issued Order No. 013/16 on 14 July 2016 suspending the strike and requiring the workers back at their shifts from the afternoon of 15 July 2016. [Para 17][Para 18] The workers complied, but the employer refused them entry and by letter of 14 July 2016 asked the Council to reject its own order. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay prim money of ten dollars (10$) as required by the Cambodian Labour Law? [Para 1]
2. Whether the employer must pay travel and accommodation of seven dollars (7$)? [Para 2]
3. Whether workers employed for two months or more must be converted to permanent status? [Para 3]
4. Whether the employer may deduct wages from a worker who is sick and holds a valid medical certificate? [Para 4]
5. Whether the employer must pay wages for public holidays according to the number of days in the Ministry's declaration? [Para 5]
6. Whether the employer must bear liability under the Labour Law for workplace accidents? [Para 6]
7. Whether prim money must be deducted at the old rate under the previous principles? [Para 7]
8. Whether a woman worker with one year of service who takes maternity leave must receive maternity allowance? [Para 8]
9. Whether prim money and attendance money must be preserved during the strike? [Para 9]
10. Whether the employer must repay prim, travel and accommodation money already paid to the workers? [Para 10]
11. Whether the panel should continue the arbitration at all, the employer having refused to comply with the order to readmit the workers? [Para 30]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** they decided to return to work in obedience to the Council's order but the employer would not let them enter; they asked the Council to issue an order suspending the strike and restoring them to work. [Para 17][Para 20]
**Employer (respondent):** its representative confirmed that the employer does not permit the workers to enter and work as the order requires, and by letter of 14 July 2016 formally requested that Order No. 013/16 be rejected. [Para 21][Para 23][Para 25]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel ordered the termination of the arbitration proceedings in case No. 166/16 Aerosoft Samit Footwear Co., Ltd., without adjudicating any of the ten demands. [Para 40]

The panel noted at the outset that no hearing was held, so it made no findings of fact on the demands; it decided the file on the documents alone, which were the workers' letter of 13 July 2016, the employer's letter of 14 July 2016, the non-conciliation report and conciliation minutes from the Ministry, and its own Order No. 013/16. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 26][Para 27][Para 28][Para 29]

On the only question it reached — whether to continue — the panel applied Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY of 21 April 2004, which requires the parties to suspend every dispute measure, such as a strike or lock-out under Article 318 of the Labour Law, or any other measure capable of aggravating the dispute, throughout the panel's work, and requires both parties to attend every meeting convened. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 32] Earlier awards had read that provision to mean the Council will not carry on with arbitration while the workers are striking or the employer is locking out, and the panel cited a line of its own decisions to that effect (5906-Sang Vo, 3908-Chong Youv, 13709 & 13809-Phrom Kanh, 110/11-Simanou, 104/13-Sabrina (Cambodia), 11913-Pou Yue, 01914-Dong Du Thek Stai (Cambodia) and 322/14-Jimly (Cambodia) Pte Ltd). [Para 33][Para 34][Para 35]

The panel found that in this case the employer had acted in disregard of the Council's order to readmit the workers while the strike was on, and that this conduct aggravated the dispute during the arbitration — a conclusion rested on both parties' telephone confirmations of 18 July 2016 and the employer's letter requesting rejection of the order. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38] Applying Article 20 and following that jurisprudence, the panel decided not to proceed with the arbitration, and ordered the file closed. [Para 39][Para 40]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a party refuses to comply with the panel's order suspending a strike or lock-out and restoring the status quo — here, the employer barring workers who had obeyed the order from re-entering — that refusal is itself an aggravating dispute measure under Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY, and the panel will decline to continue the arbitration rather than adjudicate the underlying demands. [Para 32][Para 38][Para 39]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's general statement that the Council will not proceed whenever workers are striking or an employer is locking out, and its recital of eight earlier awards, are broader than the facts needed here, where it was the employer's defiance of a specific order that aggravated the dispute. [Para 33][Para 34]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award decides only the procedural question and expressly records that no hearing took place and no findings of fact were made, so the ten demands were never examined on the merits. [Para 29]
- The signature block names only the employer-appointed arbitrator (Ing Sothy) and the chair (Kong Phallha); the worker-appointed arbitrator, An Vireak, appears in the composition at [Para 1] but is missing from the signatures at [Para 41], so his signature cannot be confirmed.
- The non-conciliation report is numbered 841 in one passage [Para 13] and 842 in two others [Para 16][Para 26], both dated 08 July 2016.
- Both the order suspending the strike and the employer's letter asking that it be rejected are dated 14 July 2016 [Para 18][Para 25].
- The employer's name is printed in several forms across the document — "Aerosoft Samit Footwear Co., Ltd." in the title block, "Aerosoft Sam Footwear" and "Aerosoft Samit Hut Wear" in the procedural narrative [Para 17], and "Aerosoft Fu Samit Footwear" in the workers' letter as recited [Para 23].
- The heading date is given as 22 July 2016 [Para 1], while the panel was constituted on 12 July 2016 and the last recorded step is 18 July 2016 [Para 17][Para 19].
