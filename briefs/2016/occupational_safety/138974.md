# Award No. 020/16 — G-Foremost (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council of Cambodia, 25 February 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bunchhea presiding; Ing Sothy appointed by the employer, An Nan appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 138974

## 1. Facts
G-Foremost (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a shoe manufacturer at Ta Sek Village, Kombol Commune, Por Sen Chey District, Phnom Penh, employing about 1,028 workers, with a local union of the Labour Development Union Federation (LDUF) claiming registration from the Ministry. [Para 40][Para 42][Para 43]
The LDUF filed complaint No. 450 of 29 December 2015 seeking improved working conditions; of fifteen issues raised, nine remained unresolved after conciliation and were referred by report No. 116 of 25 January 2016. [Para 33][Para 34]
At the hearing of 5 February 2016 five issues were settled and three withdrawn, leaving issue #1 alone: the position of the waste disposal area next to the place where workers eat. The panel inspected the site on 17 February 2016. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39][Para 52] The award is non-binding. [Para 66]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1 of the panel's numbering) Whether the employer must move the waste disposal area to a place away from the dining area where the workers have their meals? [Para 21][Para 46][Para 54]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must move the waste disposal area away from the dining area — recorded in the conciliation list and answered by the same reasoning as issue 1? [Para 21]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must pay full wages when it has insufficient work for the workers to do? — not decided; no longer among the remaining issues. [Para 22][Para 39]
4. (Issue 11) Whether the employer must pay wages within 48 hours of a worker notifying resignation? — not decided. [Para 23]
5. (Issue 12) Whether the employer must provide payslips three days in advance? — not decided. [Para 24]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the waste disposal area abuts a wall of the dining area about one metre high and holds fabric, leather, glue and other shoe scraps whose smell reaches both the eating place and the work stations; the employer collects only half the waste, leaving old waste and odour; moving the canteen to the box warehouse would force a five-minute walk out to buy food and an 8 to 10 minute walk back, cutting into the lunch break. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 51]
**Employer (respondent):** it accepts the area used to smell but now classifies dry and wet waste and disposes of it regularly, which has significantly reduced the smell; it will not move the waste area because it sits by the security guard station, where guards can prevent loss of shoes; instead it will move the dining area to the box warehouse, a former dining area 50 to 60 metres away. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the workers' demand that the waste disposal area be moved was rejected, and the employer was ordered to move the dining area to the box warehouse so that cleanliness is ensured and worker health unaffected. [Para 66]
On the legal standard the panel applied Article 229 of the Labour Law, which requires every establishment to be kept clean and to maintain hygiene, sanitation and the working conditions necessary for workers' health, and read it as obliging the employer to keep the premises clean and hygienic, including the area next to where workers eat. [Para 54][Para 55]
On the facts the panel relied on its own site visit: almost all waste had been collected but some scraps and some smell remained, the waste area abuts the dining area, and the distance to the box warehouse is 50 to 60 metres as the employer had stated. [Para 53][Para 58][Para 59][Para 60]
On the managerial prerogative the panel applied Article 2, paragraph 2, as interpreted in Awards 62/06 (Quicksew Cambodia), 108/06 and 84/08 (Trinunggal Komara), 33/07 (Goldfame Enterprises), 106/07 (M & V3), 08/09 (Global Apparel), 02/10 (Siem Reap Airways International-CAMS) and 218/12 (New Archid Garment): the employer may manage the enterprise, but only lawfully and reasonably. [Para 61][Para 62]
Applying that test the panel found relocation of the dining area lets workers eat and rest away from the waste, and that an extra 50 to 60 metres, or 8 to 10 minutes, is not an unreasonable burden; the decision to move the canteen rather than the waste was therefore neither unlawful nor unreasonable. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The duty in Article 229 to keep the workplace clean and hygienic can be met by separating workers from a nuisance instead of removing the nuisance itself: where an employer relocates the eating area away from a waste disposal area, and the added distance is modest, that is a lawful and reasonable exercise of the Article 2 managerial prerogative and the demand to move the waste is refused. [Para 62][Para 65]
The prerogative is not unlimited — it must be exercised lawfully and reasonably, and the hygiene duty survives the relocation. [Para 61][Para 66]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that even after the dining area is moved, the employer remains obliged to manage and collect waste to a standard of cleanliness and hygiene that does not affect worker health. [Para 66]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
This document is an unofficial English translation; the authoritative Khmer original is not in the library, and the award itself records that the documents, exhibits and other evidence considered were omitted from the English version, so the evidentiary basis of the fact-finding cannot be checked. [Para 39][Para 68]
OCR has systematically dropped the letters "h" and "t" at word boundaries throughout ("themployer" for "the employer", "thearing" for "the hearing", "ding area" for "dining area", "lssue" for "Issue", "remaing" for "remaining"); the sense is recoverable but no quotation from this text can be treated as literal.
The worker count behind the claim is inconsistent: thirteen workers are said to have attended the hearing, five have names and thumbprints on the authorisation letter of 18 December 2015, yet the panel says it will settle the dispute for fourteen workers, six who authorised and eight who attended unnamed — 13, 14, 5 and 6 cannot all be right. [Para 44][Para 45]
Names of the same individuals are printed differently within the award (Prok Siem / Prok Seam; Khem Houk / Khem Pok / Khim Pok; Mom Vuthy / Mon Vuthy), and the header's list of representatives omits or misplaces several initials, so no individual is named here. The issue numbering in the conciliation list is not sequential (3, 4, 11, 12, 14, 15, 6, 10) and issue 3 as listed duplicates the substance of issue 1; the panel decided only issue 1.
