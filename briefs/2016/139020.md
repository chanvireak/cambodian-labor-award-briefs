# Award No. 089/16 — Khin Thy Nhu Ehs Lak Ky (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 2 May 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kol Vathana appointed by the employer, An Non appointed by the workers, Kong Phally chair) · non-binding award, employer absent · Doc ID 139020

## 1. Facts
The company cut and sewed footwear on National Road 4 at Trapeang Ampil, Samrong Tong, Kampong Speu, and employed about 4,293 workers. [Para 27][Para 28] Its local union, of the Democratic Union of Cambodian Garment Workers, was registered with the Ministry of Labour and Vocational Training by certificate No. 3292 of 03 June 2015, with Mr C as president, Mr V as vice-president and Ms S as secretary. [Para 29] On 30 October 2015 the employer and that union signed an agreement whose point 9 provided that the employer agreed to deduct workers' wages to pay union dues, subject to valid written authorisation from each member. [Para 31][Para 33] 249 members signed and fingerprinted a request for a deduction of 01 USD per month, which the employer received on 17 November 2015 but never acted on. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 78]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must implement point 9 of the agreement of 30 October 2015 concerning deduction of wages to pay union dues? [Para 56][Para 30]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the union is registered with the Ministry of Labour, has placed before the panel the names and fingerprints of 249 members who agreed in writing, and the employer signed for and accepted that request on 17 November 2015 yet did not implement it — instead it deducted dues from all workers and distributed a percentage to a different union in the company. [Para 34][Para 36][Para 37][Para 38]
**Employer (respondent):** absent. The company's head of administration replied by telephone that it would not attend because the case had already been decided once and it had nothing further to say. [Para 1][Para 16]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to implement point 9 of the agreement of 30 October 2015 on deducting wages to pay union dues. [Para 80][Para 81]

The panel first dealt with the employer's absence. Under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council, a party properly summoned who fails to appear without a valid reason may have the proceedings continue or be concluded by an award in its absence. [Para 40][Para 41] The panel followed its earlier awards on that provision, including 63/04, 148/07, 99/09, 173/11, 144/12, 109/13 and 163/14. [Para 42] It found the employer had been properly invited by letter No. 514 of 08 April 2016 with the date, place and contact details, was reminded on the day, and had told the secretariat it would not come; it had therefore waived its right to defend. [Para 15][Para 43][Para 44]

On the claimant's standing the panel applied Article 268 of the Labour Law: a professional organisation acquires the rights the law recognises only once its statutes and the list of responsible officers are deposited and it is registered with the Ministry. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 50] The union here held certificate No. 3292 and its three officers were present at the hearing, so it had the lawful right to represent all its members in the dispute. [Para 46][Para 54]

On the merits the panel held that a contract arises from the concurring will of the parties under Article 311 of the Civil Code, takes effect through matching offer and acceptance under Articles 336 and 337, and that an employer is bound to perform an agreement it has made with its workers — the position it had taken in Award No. 271/14. [Para 56][Para 58][Para 60][Para 63][Para 64] It found as fact that on 30 October 2015 the employer and the union did sign point 9 in those terms. [Para 65] Turning to the deduction itself, Article 129(2) of the Labour Law allows a worker to authorise deduction of wages to pay dues to the union of which he is a member, the authorisation to be in writing and revocable at any time. [Para 69][Para 70] Article 5(5) of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 requires members to request the deduction in writing at least 15 days before pay day, and requires the employer to comply. [Para 74][Para 75] The panel found 249 workers had signed and fingerprinted such a request for 01 USD per month, and that the employer owed the deduction. [Para 78][Para 79]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a collective agreement obliges the employer to deduct union dues from the wages of members who have authorised it in writing, the employer must implement that clause: Article 129(2) of the Labour Law and Article 5(5) of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 make the deduction mandatory once each member has requested it in writing at least 15 days before pay day, and the authorisation stays revocable at any time. A party properly summoned who stays away without valid reason waives its right to defend, and the panel may hear the case and issue an award in its absence under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 41][Para 44][Para 64][Para 70][Para 74][Para 79]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's general remarks that the union's registered status also entitles it to represent its members in future disputes, and that a member may revoke the deduction at any point, go beyond what the order required. [Para 54][Para 76]
