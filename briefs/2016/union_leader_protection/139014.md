# Award No. 076/16 — Star Light Apparel Manufacturing Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 27 April 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer, dissenting on issue 3; An Non, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, chair appointed by the two) · Doc ID 139014

## 1. Facts
The Samakki Kamkar Kampuchea Federation filed six collective demands for the roughly 1,042 workers of Star Light Apparel Manufacturing Co Ltd, a garment factory; three were settled at conciliation, one was withdrawn at the hearing, leaving issues 1 and 3 [Para 15][Para 21][Para 55]. The disputed acts are the dismissal of seven elected union leaders on 22 January 2016, said by the employer to follow violence and an unlawful strike on 21 January 2016, and the non-renewal of an eighth leader's six-month fixed-duration contract when it expired on 15 January 2016 [Para 29][Para 63][Para 114][Para 166]. The award is expressly non-binding: it becomes final eight days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Minister of Labour [Para 22][Para 234].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer was obliged to take back the eight union leaders — seven dismissed and one whose fixed-duration contract expired — and specifically whether the seven were protected from dismissal under Article 293 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 305, and whether the non-renewal of the eighth was tainted by anti-union discrimination? [Para 111][Para 127][Para 175]
2. Whether the employer must deduct the regular attendance bonus proportionally to the number of days of special leave it had authorised, rather than deducting the $10 in full? [Para 191]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** None of the eight committed any fault, the employer gave no prior notice, and the dismissals were anti-union discrimination; on 21 January 2016 the seven had come to work normally but security guards and the company driver barred them at the gate [Para 61][Para 115][Para 117]. The attendance bonus is part of wages and should be cut pro rata like wages, not forfeited for a single authorised day's absence [Para 105][Para 107].
**Employer (respondent):** The seven were dismissed on 22 January 2016 because on 21 January 2016 they blocked other workers from entering, switched off the electricity in the sewing building, shouted slogans and threatened workers who wanted to work — conduct shown by photographs and video and punishable under the internal rules [Para 63][Para 77]. The eighth was not dismissed at all; his contract simply reached its term [Para 62]. The attendance bonus exists to encourage punctuality and is payable only where there is no absence [Para 108][Para 110].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to take back the seven dismissed leaders and, separately, to deduct the $10 attendance bonus pro rata to authorised days of absence; it refused reinstatement for the eighth worker whose contract had expired [Para 231][Para 232][Para 233].
On the dismissal date the parties' accounts conflicted — the workers pleaded 21 January 2016, the employer 22 January 2016 — and the panel, applying the rule that the party asserting a fact bears the burden of proof, found the workers had produced no specific evidence of a bar at the gate and accepted the employer's date [Para 118][Para 119][Para 123][Para 126].
On protection, the panel applied Article 293 of the Labour Law and Articles 3 ¶3 and 4 of Prakas No. 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001, which shield election candidates for 45 days before the ballot and founders and members during registration and for 30 days after it [Para 127][Para 129][Para 133][Para 137]. It adopted its own three-condition test from earlier awards [Para 141][Para 142] and found each met: the seven belonged to the protected category as leaders notified to the employer [Para 144][Para 149][Para 150]; the dismissals of 22 January 2016 fell while the registration application of 29 December 2015 was pending, the Ministry's refusal issuing only on 29 January 2016 [Para 151][Para 153][Para 154][Para 155]; and the employer itself admitted receiving two notices dated 10 November 2015 naming all seven [Para 156][Para 157][Para 158]. No request for the Labour Inspector's prior authorisation was shown, so the dismissals breached Article 293 and reinstatement followed, as in earlier awards [Para 159][Para 160][Para 161][Para 162][Para 164].
For the eighth worker the panel held Article 73(1) of the Labour Law makes a fixed-duration contract end of itself at its term, and Article 73(5) requires ten days' notice only where the contract exceeds six months; his was for six months and expired on 15 January 2016, so no notice was owed [Para 167][Para 168][Para 169][Para 170]. Protection against dismissal, it held, does not extend to expiry of a contract [Para 171][Para 174]. His anti-union discrimination claim failed for want of evidence, the burden lying on the worker [Para 179][Para 185][Para 187][Para 188].
On issue 3 the panel read Prakas No. 230 K.B/S.CH.N of 25 July 2012 as fixing a $10 monthly attendance bonus but found the phrase "without absence" uninterpreted by any competent body, and declined to assume it covered both lawful and unlawful absence [Para 194][Para 197][Para 200][Para 205]. It held authorised special leave suspends the contract under Articles 71(6) and 72(1), so no wages are owed for those days [Para 224][Para 225][Para 227]; since Article 103 treats a gratification as wages [Para 221], the bonus may be reduced but not extinguished [Para 229][Para 230].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a union has notified the employer of its elected leaders and its registration application is pending, dismissal of those leaders without the Labour Inspector's prior authorisation is unlawful and reinstatement follows, even where the employer alleges violent strike conduct; but that protection does not reach the natural expiry of a fixed-duration contract, which ends without notice or reinstatement [Para 159][Para 171][Para 174]. An attendance bonus forming part of wages may be reduced in proportion to authorised days of absence but not forfeited in full [Para 229][Para 230].

**Obiter dicta:** Ing Sothy's annexed dissent argues that a conditional attendance bonus (ប្រាក់រង្វាន់) is not wages at all, so proportionality is the wrong remedy and total forfeiture correct; it draws a distinction between a conditional bonus and a gratification (ប្រាក់ដែលបើកជារង្វាន់) under Article 103 [Para 237][Para 245][Para 246]. The panel also noted the Ministry's refusal of registration on 29 January 2016 without ruling on its validity [Para 92][Para 146].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
OCR damage is extensive and the employer's name is unstable across the file: "Star Light Apparel Manufacturing Co Ltd" appears also as "Eiperil Menu Fecturing", "Eiperil Menufecturing" and "Ei Phaeril Menufek Chhering" [Para 1][Para 54][Para 148]; it is abbreviated to "Li" at one point [Para 165]. The record is internally contradictory on the number of union leaders: the workers claim eight, but the election minutes list only three candidates — Mr B, Mr M and Mr N — and name only those three as elected president, vice-president and secretary [Para 58][Para 144][Para 145], while the notice letters are said to bear two different leadership slates [Para 90][Para 100][Para 148]. The dismissal date is pleaded as both 21 and 22 January 2016 [Para 117][Para 152]. The bonus amount is printed variously as "$10" and, in one instance, as "៤ ១០ ដុល្លារ" [Para 191]. Large block quotations of Prakas 305 and the award number in the annex are truncated mid-sentence where the OCR lost columns [Para 130][Para 141][Para 235], and the final signature line preserves only a fragment of the third arbitrator's name [Para 251].
