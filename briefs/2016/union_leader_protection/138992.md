# Award No. 045/16 — Global A Parallel Limited (Arbitration Council, 26 February 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phalluk, chair; Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Vireak, appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 138992

## 1. Facts
Global A Parallel Limited (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ក្លូប័ល អា ប៉ារែលីមីធីត), a company on National Road 4 in Khan Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh, faced a twelve-point collective complaint brought by the Cambodian Labour Solidarity Trade Union Federation; conciliation on 12 February 2016 left ten points unresolved and they reached the Council on 18 February 2016. [Para 1][Para 16][Para 17][Para 18] The demands included a demand that the company not dismiss 500 workers as it had announced on 18 January 2016, payment of unused annual leave, a 10-dollar monthly transport allowance or a staff bus, and maintenance of wages and benefits during a strike. [Para 2][Para 3][Para 4][Para 10] On 24 February 2016, the day before the scheduled hearing, the panel learned that a strike had begun and issued Order No. 006/16 requiring the strike to stop at once and all workers to return to their shifts from the morning of 25 February 2016. [Para 20][Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
The panel reached no merits question: it framed the only issue as a procedural one — whether the arbitration should continue at all. [Para 38] The ten demands it did not decide were:
3. Whether the company must refrain from dismissing the 500 workers it announced on 18 January 2016? [Para 2]
4. Whether unused annual leave must be paid out at the workers' average contractual wage? [Para 3]
5. Whether the company must hire a bus, or pay 10 dollars a month per worker for transport? [Para 4]
6. Whether wages and daily bonuses must be left untouched when a worker takes leave to obtain an identity card in their home district? [Para 5]
7. Whether the attendance bonus may be deducted according to actual attendance? [Para 6]
8. Whether the 10-dollar monthly attendance payment must be kept where a worker produces a medical certificate for sick leave? [Para 7]
9. Whether the company must deduct union dues from members' wages and remit them to the union? [Para 8]
10. Whether, when there is no work, the company must give leave with wages and benefits instead of dismissing workers? [Para 9]
11. Whether the company must settle all disputes without discrimination against union leaders? [Para 10]
12. Whether wages and other benefits must be maintained during a strike? [Para 10]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the workers chose to continue the strike as their means of resolving the dispute, and they said so at the pre-hearing meeting; they did not return to work as ordered. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 51]
**Employer (respondent):** it wrote on 23 February 2016 asking the panel to order the striking workers to stop and resume work, and both sides confirmed at the pre-hearing meeting that the strike was still running. [Para 21][Para 26][Para 28]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel terminated the arbitration proceedings in Case 045/16 without deciding any of the ten demands, and awarded nothing in riel or dollars. [Para 53][Para 54]

The panel began from Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004: while a panel is hearing a case, the parties must suspend all dispute measures such as a strike or a lockout, as well as any other measure capable of aggravating the dispute, and must attend every meeting the panel calls. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40] It read that provision as meaning the Council will not run an arbitration while workers are striking or an employer has locked out its staff. [Para 41]

It then reviewed its own practice and found it consistent: in Case 04/04 (IMSI) a 2004 notice held that where the parties had no right to strike during the proceedings the Council could neither resolve nor issue an award on the dispute, and could take the case up again only once all workers stopped striking; in Case 18/04 (the hotel group) the panel's second order required all workers to stop strike action and demonstrations in the hotel grounds and reserved the right to close the file if a party breached any term; in Case 77/05 (Franco Nith Ting) and again in Cases 110/11 (Simano) and 17/13 (Internal Fashion Yarl) the panel closed proceedings because the strike continued during the arbitration. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45][Para 46][Para 47][Para 48]

Applying that to the facts, the panel recorded that its Order No. 006/16 of 24 February 2016 had ordered the workers to stop striking and return to normal work, that by 25 February 2016 the strike was still running, and that the workers' representatives confirmed both that the strike continued and that the workers had chosen the strike, not arbitration, as the way to settle the dispute. [Para 51][Para 22] It concluded that the workers had no intention of submitting the collective dispute to arbitration, and on that ground terminated the proceedings under Article 20. [Para 52][Para 53] No finding of fact was made on any of the ten demands. [Para 37]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where workers keep a strike running, or an employer keeps a lockout in place, after the panel has ordered that dispute measures be suspended for the duration of the arbitration, the panel will terminate the proceedings and decide nothing on the merits, because Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 makes suspension of strikes and lockouts a condition of the arbitration going forward. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 52][Para 53]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted, without deciding anything by it, that the Council may take the same collective dispute up again once all the workers stop striking — a statement about a future application, not part of the order made here. [Para 44]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The number of unresolved points is printed two ways: two of twelve at [Para 17] but ten at [Para 18], and the ten listed demands are those numbered 3 to 12. [Para 2][Para 10] The company letter that opened the file is dated 6 February 2016 and the conciliation 12 February 2016, yet the conciliation report is also dated 17 February 2016 and reached the secretariat on 18 February. [Para 13][Para 16][Para 17] The agreement annexed is labelled Case No. 024/16 though this file is 045/16. [Para 29] The company name appears as ក្លូប័ល អា ប៉ារែលីមីធីត, ក្លូប័ល អា ប៉ារែល ល្មម ផត and "Jប័ល អា ប៉ារែលី មីធីត". [Para 13][Para 16][Para 30] The chair's name is printed គង់ ផល្បៈ on the face and គង់ ផល្លៈ in the signature block, and a line of English OCR noise plus a stray reference to Indonesia precede the signatures, which are themselves lost. [Para 1][Para 51][Para 54] The heading of [Para 37] states that the panel made no findings of fact on the issues, so the file contains no reasoning on any of the ten demands.
