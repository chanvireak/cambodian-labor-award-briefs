# Award No. 121/16 — Vallong (Cambodia) Outsole In Das Street Ltd (Arbitration Council, 23 June 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Uok Ry, appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna, appointed by the workers; Pen Bunhea, chair) · Doc ID 139395

## 1. Facts
Vallong (Cambodia) Outsole In Das Street Ltd manufactures shoe components at Ta Sen village, Cheung Prey, Kampong Cham, and employed about 300 workers when the award issued. [Para 1][Para 45][Para 46] A collective dispute over fifteen demands was conciliated by the provincial labour office on 27 May 2016; no settlement was reached and the file was referred to the Council Secretariat on 31 May 2016. [Para 18][Para 19] The complainant was the Workers' Union for Development of the company, registered with the Ministry of Labour on 19 May 2016; the employer did not appear at the hearing of 10 June 2016. [Para 47][Para 26][Para 27]

## 2. Issues in dispute
At the hearing the workers withdrew most of the fifteen claims, and the panel considered three only. [Para 29][Para 30]
1. Whether the employer may make payment of the monthly wage, and of overtime worked on Saturdays, conditional on workers volunteering for overtime on pay day? (issue 6 of the panel's numbering) [Para 52][Para 82]
2. Whether the employer must provide a permanently staffed company infirmary, with a nurse or doctor on duty and sufficient medical equipment and medicines for the workforce? (issue 10) [Para 62][Para 98]
3. Whether the employer must issue pay slips for workers to check, as its own notice of 14 March 2016 promised? (issue 13) [Para 65][Para 114]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** overtime must rest on the voluntary principle, yet on Friday 9 June 2016 the employer told the sole (E.V.A) section that wages would be paid that day only if they worked overtime, while the general section was paid at 1:30 pm without any overtime. [Para 54][Para 87][Para 91] The company kept a clinic room and a medicine box but no duty nurse, doctor, equipment or medicines, stocking them only when labour inspectors visited. [Para 62][Para 63] Pay slips formerly came on the 3rd to 5th of the month, then were enclosed with the wage envelope, and from May–June 2016 arrived a day after pay day. [Para 65][Para 66]
**Employer (respondent):** absent, although invited twice by post and telephoned on the hearing day; the panel treated it as having forfeited its right to reply. [Para 27][Para 78][Para 89]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered (i) to conduct pay-day and Saturday overtime on the voluntary principle and to apply in full its notice of 14 March 2016 on pay-day overtime; (ii) to provide a nurse and a doctor on duty, with sufficient medical equipment and medicines, under Article 242 of the Labour Law and Article 3 of Prakas No. 330; and (iii) to implement its notice of 14 March 2016 on issuing pay slips for checking after the work break. No sum of money was awarded. [Para 134][Para 135][Para 136][Para 138]

On issue 6, Article 4 of Prakas No. 80 of 9 March 1999 forbids an employer to compel overtime or to impose any discipline on a worker who declines it, and the panel followed its reasoning in Award No. 150/14 (Lian Fa (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd) that the worker alone decides. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 85] Paying the E.V.A section only when it agreed to overtime, while paying the general section at 1:30 pm though it worked none, was unequal treatment that put pressure on workers to obtain the monthly wage they needed. [Para 91][Para 92] The panel held the practice contrary to the spirit of Article 4 of Prakas No. 80, and separately noted that the employer had not honoured its own notice. [Para 95][Para 96]

On issue 10, Article 238 requires first-aid provision, Article 242 an infirmary with a doctor in charge and one or more nurses plus materials for immediate treatment, and Article 3 of Prakas No. 330 of 6 December 2000 fixes staffing by headcount — for 50 to 300 workers, one full-time nurse and a doctor for one or two hours. [Para 99][Para 100][Para 101][Para 145] On the employer's own account it had a clinic room but no duty nurse or doctor, equipment or medicines, and the panel found the Prakas unsatisfied, following Award No. 62/08 (Pao Da). [Para 105][Para 106][Para 111]

On issue 13, Article 112(b) obliges the employer to state wages clearly for each pay period when they vary, but the panel read it as not requiring notification before pay day. [Para 115][Para 116][Para 125] The claim succeeded on the employer's unilateral notice of 14 March 2016, which promised a monthly wage table for workers to check after the work break; because the workers asked only for that notice to be applied, the panel ordered it implemented. [Para 126][Para 131][Para 132][Para 133]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Conditioning the payment of the monthly wage — or of Saturday overtime pay — on a worker's volunteering for overtime on pay day is pressure that defeats the voluntary principle in Article 4 of Prakas No. 80, whatever form the pressure takes, and is unlawful even where the employer's own notice promises voluntariness; the test is whether a reasonable worker could decline without losing wages already due. [Para 91][Para 93][Para 96] Independently, an employer that issues a written notice granting workers a wage table to check after the work break must implement it, although Article 112(b) of the Labour Law does not itself require pre-pay-day notification. [Para 125][Para 131]

**Obiter dicta:** the award decides only for members of the complaining union, who were the original complainants [Para 51]; the panel may proceed in the employer's absence once invitation is proved, under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 [Para 73][Para 80]; and this is a non-binding award that becomes final if no objection is filed within eight days [Para 31][Para 139].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The original is Khmer, so the Latin spellings of the company and of the three arbitrators are transliterations; the company name appears variously as វ៉ាឡុង, វ៉ាលុង and វ៉ា ឡេង, and is given here in one form only. [Para 1][Para 45] The disciplinary rules are dated "15 April 2916" [Para 36]. The operative paragraph on issue 6 prints the notice date as "14 March 2019" and, in the same sentence, "2014 March 2016", while the reasoning and the final orders use 14 March 2016 [Para 97][Para 133][Para 135]; both variants are recorded here rather than corrected. The withdrawal count is inconsistent: paragraph 29 says twelve issues were withdrawn but lists eleven numbers (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 12, 14, 15) [Para 29][Para 30]. The union president's name is rendered under two different spellings at [Para 1] and [Para 48] and is given as Mr S throughout. The Prakas No. 330 annex tables listing equipment and medicines survive only as French OCR fragments [Para 158][Para 159].
