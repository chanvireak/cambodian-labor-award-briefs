# Order No. 323/15 — En J (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 20 January 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom, chosen by the employer party; An Non, chosen by the worker party; Aing Eng Thong, chair chosen by the two) · Doc ID 139489

## 1. Facts
En J (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a bicycle-assembly plant in the Tai Seng Bavet special economic zone with about 1,888 workers, dismissed a worker in its goods-pulling section on 6 November 2015 without notice, four weeks before his sixth-month fixed-duration contract was due to expire. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 54][Para 55][Para 56] The same worker had been elected president of the newly founded democratic union at the factory in a ballot held on 30 August 2015, and notice of the candidate list had been given to the company on 28 August 2015 and signed for by its security guard, with a second notice of the result handed over on 31 August 2015 and refused. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 77][Para 50] The union was not yet registered with the Ministry of Labour. [Para 50] The Cambodia Labour Union brought the claim on 17 November 2015, conciliation on the single point failed on 7 December 2015, and the hearing was held on 5 January 2016. [Para 8][Para 9][Para 7]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must take the worker back and pay him back-pay from the date it dismissed him? [Para 2][Para 53]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the worker committed no serious fault warranting dismissal; he was the president of the union and therefore enjoyed legal protection on dismissal; and the dismissal was connected with union discrimination. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 59] He signed the first two warnings only because the employer threatened to dock his pay, and denied the later allegations of not counting goods and losing eight bicycle inner-tube caps. [Para 69][Para 70]
**Employer (respondent):** it issued four written warnings — 29 August, 10 September, 26 September and 6 November 2015 — for card-scanning failures, not following the supervisor and short-counted goods, and under clause b-5 on ordinary faults in Chapter 5 of its internal rules it was entitled to dismiss; it accepts it never sought the labour inspector's authorisation, having written to the zone labour office on 5 November 2015 about not renewing contracts, which office agreed on 20 November 2015. [Para 60][Para 62][Para 64][Para 81]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to take the worker back into normal work and to pay him back-pay from the day of dismissal until the day he returns. [Para 134][Para 131]

The panel split the case into three questions: whether the worker enjoyed protection from dismissal at all, whether the dismissal was connected with his union activity, and whether his conduct warranted dismissal. [Para 85] It answered the first and stopped there.

On protection, Article 293(1) of the Labour Law requires the prior agreement of the labour inspector before an employee delegate or any candidate may be dismissed, and the panel read that as a prior authorisation in every such case. [Para 85][Para 86] Article 3(3) of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 extends the same protection to every worker who is a candidate for union leadership, for 45 days before the ballot and 45 days after it where the candidate is not elected, and requires the union to notify the employer of the candidate by every available means. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 91]

The panel then applied the three conditions it had set out in earlier orders for the protection to attach: that the worker falls within Prakas No. 305, that the dismissal occurred within the protected period, and that the employer was notified of his name. [Para 97]

On the first, the worker had been elected president of the factory union on 30 August 2015, which brought him within the Prakas. [Para 99][Para 100][Para 101] On the second, the panel noted that the Prakas fixes no end date for the protection of a candidate who is in fact elected, and followed its order in case 40/10 (Meng Yan) that such protection cannot run forever but must last at least 45 days after the ballot, or for a reasonable period such as until the union applies for registration. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 95][Para 96] Measuring from the ballot of 30 August 2015 to the dismissal of 6 November 2015 gave 68 days, about two months and eight days, which it held to be a reasonable period. [Para 103][Para 105][Para 106][Para 107] On the third, the notice of 28 August 2015 listing the candidates had been received and signed for, and although the employer refused the 31 August 2015 notice, it admitted knowing the letter probably concerned the union, so it had been told by every available means. [Para 108][Para 113][Para 114][Para 115][Para 117] All three conditions were satisfied. [Para 118]

Since the employer conceded that it had not sought the labour inspector's authorisation, and Article 4(4) of Prakas No. 305 makes any measure taken without that authorisation void, the dismissal was contrary to Article 293. [Para 120][Para 121][Para 125][Para 126][Para 129] Under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 the panel may order a dismissed worker reinstated in his former or a suitable post, and it did so, with back-pay running from dismissal to return. [Para 127][Para 130][Para 131] Having reached that result it declined to consider the union-discrimination ground or the merits of the alleged misconduct. [Para 133]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker who is a candidate for, or has been elected to, union leadership cannot be dismissed without the labour inspector's prior authorisation under Article 293(1) of the Labour Law, and a dismissal made without it is void and must be undone by reinstatement with back-pay — the protection of an elected leader running beyond the 45-day post-ballot window for as long as is reasonable, including until the union applies for registration. [Para 89][Para 95][Para 121][Para 130]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's decision not to examine the union-discrimination allegation or the merits of the four warnings, once the procedural breach was established, is an observation about the order of analysis rather than a finding that the employer did not discriminate. [Para 133]
