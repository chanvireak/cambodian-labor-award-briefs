# Award No. 040/16 — Ken Sport Shoe Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 1 April 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phalluk, chair; Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Vireak, appointed by the workers, dissenting) · Doc ID 138989

## 1. Facts
Ken Sport Shoe Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេន ស្ព័ត ស៊ូ), a shoe manufacturer in Samaki Meanchey district, Kampong Chhnang province, employed about 6,560 workers. [Para 49][Para 50] The Free Workers Union of Cambodia and its enterprise union, registered with the Ministry of Labour on 6 February 2013, filed a four-point collective complaint on 2 February 2016; conciliation on 12 February 2016 settled none of the four, but at the hearing the parties agreed issues 1, 2 and 4, leaving only issue 3. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 19][Para 20][Para 51] The surviving dispute concerned the company's practice, introduced on 2 July 2014, of requiring job applicants to produce a certificate of good conduct (លិខិតបញ្ជាក់កិរិយាមាយាទ). [Para 55][Para 65]

## 2. Issues in dispute
Only issue 3 survived; issues 1, 2 and 4 were settled by agreement at the hearing and were not decided. [Para 19][Para 20]
3. Whether the employer must stop requiring a certificate of good conduct when it recruits new workers? [Para 55][Para 70]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** obtaining the certificate costs applicants time and money; a police officer who is ill-disposed or unpaid may refuse it or write that the applicant "used to fight", which causes the employer to reject them; no other company imposes the requirement and Ken Sport Shoe itself only began it in 2014. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58][Para 59] Chapter 2, Article 4 of the collective agreement on recruitment contains no such condition, and one former union member, Ms C, abandoned an application because of it. [Para 61][Para 68][Para 69]
**Employer (respondent):** the certificate is part of the basic documents every applicant must submit, so it concerns would-be workers and not the benefits of current workers; selecting staff is the employer's own right. [Para 62][Para 63] The practice began on 2 July 2014 because the company had started exporting to the United States and American buyers demanded the condition, and previously certificates were received without difficulty and used only for information. [Para 64][Para 65]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider the demand; no sum in riel or dollars was awarded. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 97]

The panel held that a union's statutory right of representation runs only to its own members. Reading Article 266 of the Labour Law, which guarantees workers and employers the right to form professional organisations to study, promote and defend their collective and individual rights and interests, together with Article 5 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, it concluded that an enterprise union may defend the rights and interests of its members only. [Para 71][Para 72] Under Article 268 the rights and benefits the Labour Law recognises vest in a professional organisation once its statutes and the list of its responsible officers are registered with the Ministry. [Para 73][Para 74] It followed its earlier awards in Cases 023/14 (Wolbum Co. Ik Vll (Cambodia)), 32/14 (YSing Plastic), 079/15 (Sonando Too Am Ltd) and 176/15 (NJ (Cambodia)), and older ones such as 62/06, 30/08, 31/08 and 94/09. [Para 75][Para 77]

Applying that rule, the panel found the claimant union lawfully registered and entitled to represent its members before the Council, but not entitled to represent the interests of people who had merely applied for work: applicants are not union members and have no employment relationship with the employer. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 81] It cited Cases 018/16 (Hong Sen Tek Stay (Cambodia)) and 031/16 (Hou Sin Yai Limited) for the same proposition and declined to consider demand 3. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 85]

Arbitrator An Vireak filed a dissenting opinion. [Para 87][Para 89] He agreed with the majority that the union cannot represent applicants, but added a distinct ground: the disputed condition concerns the period before any employment relationship exists, so it falls outside the Labour Law, which by Article 1 governs only relations arising from the employment contract; the Council therefore has no jurisdiction at all and the workers should take the matter to a competent institution such as a court. [Para 92][Para 94][Para 96][Para 97]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union may bring a collective dispute to the Council only on behalf of its own members; it has no standing to claim on behalf of job applicants, who are neither members nor parties to an employment relationship, and a demand about recruitment conditions will therefore be declined for want of representation, however real the burden the condition imposes on applicants. [Para 72][Para 81][Para 82]

**Obiter dicta:** the dissent's further proposition — that pre-employment recruitment conditions fall outside Article 1 of the Labour Law and so outside the Council's jurisdiction altogether, leaving the courts as the proper forum — is not the majority's ground and did not decide the case. [Para 94][Para 96]
