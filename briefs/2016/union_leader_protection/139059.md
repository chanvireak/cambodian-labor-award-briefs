# Award No. 153/16 — Dai Yang Cambodia Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 27 July 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, President) · Doc ID 139059

## 1. Facts
Dai Yang Cambodia Co., Ltd runs a garment cut-and-sew operation employing approximately 615 workers, represented by the Democratic Union of Garment Workers of Dai Yang Cambodia, registered with the Ministry of Labour [Para 38][Para 40]. The company suspended the workers' employment contracts from April to May 2016 with valid authorisation from the labour inspector, then continued the suspension on a section-by-section basis from 1 June to 10 July 2016 without any authorisation, paying only 40% of wages [Para 45][Para 46][Para 48]. The collective dispute reached the Arbitration Council after conciliation at the Ministry failed, and the workers withdrew their second claim at the hearing [Para 14][Para 6].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to pay in full (100%) the wages, regular attendance bonus, seniority bonus measured by actual seniority, position allowance, skill allowance and travel and accommodation allowance for each worker's actual days of suspension from 1 June 2016 to 10 July 2016? [Para 67]
2. Whether the employer suspended the employment contracts from 1 June 2016 to 10 July 2016 in accordance with the required procedure? [Para 67]

The panel recorded that the workers withdrew the second original claim (that the company find work for them, or failing that pay 100% wages and benefits, or failing that pay lawful severance), leaving only claim one for consideration [Para 14][Para 15].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The suspension from 1 June 2016 had no valid authorisation from the labour inspector; under Articles 71 and 72 of the Labour Law a suspension may not exceed two months, and the employer had already exceeded that limit since April 2016, so full wages and allowances are owed for the actual suspended days [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54].

**Employer (respondent):** The company faced serious economic difficulty and a lack of orders; it applied to the labour inspector on 10 March 2016 for a three-month suspension, was granted only two months, applied again on 12 May 2016 for June and received no reply; it did not want the suspension either, since it pays wages and receives nothing in return [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay back, in full at 100%, wages, the regular attendance bonus, the seniority bonus according to each worker's actual seniority, the position allowance, the skill allowance, and the travel and accommodation allowance, for the number of days each worker was actually suspended from 1 June 2016 to 10 July 2016. No sum in riel or USD is stated in the award [Para 87][Para 88].

On the procedural question, the panel read Article 71 §11 of the Labour Law (suspension of enterprise activity on serious economic or material difficulty may not exceed two months and must be placed under the supervision of the labour inspector) together with its earlier interpretation in Award No. 58/07 (Star Sport Wear) and Award No. 08/07 (Seav Kinh) that supervision means prior notification and prior authorisation [Para 67][Para 69][Para 70]. The panel followed Award No. 72/05 (Nok Geaty/Nat Garment), where a suspension covered by an inspector's letter was characterised as a lawful *de jure* suspension, and held that the April–May suspension was authorised but the June–July suspension was not [Para 73][Para 76][Para 77].

On the wage question, Article 72(1) relieves the employer of the wage obligation only where the suspension was itself lawful [Para 78][Para 79]. Because the June–July suspension was not in accordance with procedure, Article 72(1) could not be applied, and the employer could not escape the obligation to pay 100% of wages even though it had no work to give and had already paid 40% [Para 80][Para 84][Para 85]. The panel relied on Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council for its power to order immediate payment of sums due [Para 86].

As a threshold matter the panel considered standing: under Article 11 of the Trade Union Law of 17 May 2016, a union acquires the rights and benefits recognised by that law once its founders register with the competent ministry, and those rights include representing members before the Arbitration Council. The three union leaders present therefore had lawful authority to represent the membership [Para 59][Para 62][Para 63][Para 66].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of employment contracts is lawful only where the employer has obtained prior authorisation from the labour inspector and the suspension does not exceed two months; where the employer suspends contracts without that authorisation, Article 72(1) of the Labour Law does not apply, and the employer owes 100% of wages and of the contractual bonuses and allowances for the days actually suspended, notwithstanding that no work was provided and that a partial wage was paid [Para 70][Para 76][Para 80][Para 84].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that the company now has work again for all its workers, which was not necessary to the order [Para 50]; it accepted the employer's statement that it did not wish to suspend, describing the suspension as driven by the economic situation and absence of orders [Para 57][Para 58]; and it observed that the union's majority-representative status ran to 10 May 2018, a point going to standing rather than to the merits [Para 42].

## 6. Source defects disclosed

The ministerial letter recognising the union leadership, No. 021/15, is dated 09 March 2015 at Para 26 and 09 March 2016 at Para 41. Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council is dated 21 April 2004 in the jurisdiction section but printed as "21 April 2015 2004" at Para 86 [Para 4][Para 86]. Para 76 prints the year as "២០ ២០១៦". Para 28 is badly corrupted OCR, mixing an unrelated numeric string into the inspectorate letter. The prior awards cited at Paras 72 and 81 are partly illegible (e.g. "១៩៨/១៣ ស៊ូ អី កែរីល", "២៨ ០៨-ហ្វាញ ជិស"), so their numbers are not reproduced here. The union president's name appears as ហេង សួភា and ហេង សូភា in different passages; individual workers are referred to by role only. The signature block at Para 90 is largely lost to OCR.
