# Award No. 170/16 — Chai Yu (Cambodia) Im Peril Manufacture Charging Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 23 August 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, chair appointed by both) · Doc ID 139072

## 1. Facts
Chai Yu (Cambodia) Im Peril Manufacture Charging Co., Ltd., a garment factory at Sambo village, Chbar Mon, Kampong Speu, with about 280 workers, faced seven collective demands filed on 28 June 2016, to which three more were added at the conciliation meeting of 05 July 2016. [Para 1][Para 19][Para 10][Para 66] Conciliation failed and ten unresolved points reached the Council on 13 July 2016. [Para 16][Para 22] A union election on 04 June 2016 made Ms S Saret president and Ms K Lon secretary of the Cambodia Union at the factory, which was not yet registered with the Ministry. [Para 67][Para 91] Kim Lon was dismissed on 21 June 2016 and Siek Saret on 01 July 2016, shortly after the conciliation meeting. [Para 72][Para 95] The parties chose a non-binding award. [Para 28]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back Ms K Lon, union secretary, without conditions, and pay her wages and benefits from dismissal until return? [Para 70][Para 105]
2. Whether the employer must take back Ms S Saret, union president, in her original post and pay her wages and benefits from dismissal until return? [Para 93]

*(The other eight demands — overtime until 8 pm, dismissal without reason, doubled wage deductions for unauthorised absence, wage deductions despite a medical certificate, workplace-accident liability and factory medical staffing, and anti-union threats and pressure to resign — were settled by agreement between the parties and not decided.)* [Para 25][Para 27]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** both women were dismissed without any clear reason after the union election results were announced, which is anti-union discrimination; Kim Lon had never been warned, and her lateness came only from a broken staff bus. [Para 75][Para 82][Para 83][Para 98][Para 99]
**Employer (respondent):** Kim Lon was dismissed for irregular attendance, lateness, leaving early, ignoring her team leader, telephoning and playing music during working hours; Siek Saret was dismissed because as a team leader she led badly, let her team joke at work and failed to meet output. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 101][Para 102] It also said it did not receive the union's notices of the election. [Para 89][Para 137]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer is ordered to take back Ms K Lon and Ms S Saret and to pay each of them wages and benefits from the date of dismissal until the date of return. [Para 158][Para 183][Para 184]

Seven of the ten demands were settled by agreement between the parties at the hearing, so the panel decided only the three reinstatement claims. [Para 25][Para 27]

On the legal framework, Article 43 of the Trade Union Law of 17 May 2016 allows dismissal of an employee delegate or of any candidate only with the prior agreement of the Labour Inspector; Article 67 extends the same protection to candidates in a union leadership election for 45 days before and 45 days after the poll, provided the union notifies the employer of the candidacy and copies the Ministry. [Para 108][Para 115][Para 116][Para 117] The panel drew the three conditions from Prakas No. 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001 and its earlier awards: that the worker is a protected person, that the dismissal falls inside the protection window, and that notice was given. [Para 120][Para 124]

For Kim Lon the panel found she was elected secretary on 04 June 2016 and dismissed on 21 June 2016, seventeen days later, so she was inside the window; the union delivered the notice by hand on 07 June, where security refused it, and by post on 08 June, where it came back stamped refused on 10 June. [Para 131][Para 134][Para 86][Para 87] Following Article 246 of the Civil Procedure Code and award No. 283/14 Lian Fa (Cambodia) Garment, the panel held service by post effective once the employer refuses it, so the employer is deemed to know, and the copy to the Ministry is not an extra condition where the employer already knows. [Para 141][Para 142][Para 146][Para 129][Para 147] No request for Labour Inspector approval was shown, so the dismissal breached Articles 43 and 67, and reinstatement with back pay follows under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 151][Para 152][Para 153][Para 158]

For Siek Saret the panel applied the same reasoning: elected president on 04 June, dismissed on 01 July, twenty-seven days later and inside the window; the employer knew her position because she attended the 01 July conciliation and was listed there as union president. [Para 167][Para 171][Para 172][Para 173] No Inspector approval was shown, so her dismissal was equally unlawful and she was reinstated with back pay. [Para 177][Para 178][Para 183]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker elected to union office, or standing as a candidate, is protected under Articles 43 and 67 of the Trade Union Law for 45 days either side of the poll, and dismissal inside that window is unlawful without the Labour Inspector's prior agreement; that protection does not depend on the local union having registered with the Ministry. [Para 67][Para 115][Para 116][Para 152]

Notice of candidacy counts as given where the union delivers it by hand and by registered post and the employer refuses both, the employer then being deemed to know; a copy to the Ministry is not a precondition in that situation. [Para 146][Para 147]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the Ministry copy serves only as further proof of the worker's standing where the employer contests the mandate, and its remark that a candidate's protection is renewed at each union election, are general statements beyond the two orders. [Para 118][Para 130]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The operative paragraph reinstating Ms K Lon orders the employer to take her back but omits the back-wage order that the reasoning at [Para 158] had directed; the same order for Ms S Saret does include back pay [Para 183][Para 184].
- The employer-appointed arbitrator's name is missing from the signature block, which is left blank after the employer label, although Seng Vouch Hun appears in the composition at [Para 1][Para 186].
- The employer's name appears as "Chai Yu" in the body and "Bai Yu" in the header and title block, with several Khmer spellings of the trading name [Para 1][Para 65].
- The number of unresolved demands is given as ten [Para 22] although the file began with seven and three were added, and seven of those ten were then settled by agreement [Para 19][Para 25].
- Paragraphs [Para 141]–[Para 145] are heavily corrupted, with stray numerals and fragments replacing the reasoning on service by post; the operative finding at [Para 146] was read with the cleaner statement at [Para 139].
- Dates are misprinted in several places: the year 2106 for a resignation letter [Para 59], "04 October June 2016" for the election [Para 133], and 2019 for 2015 at [Para 159].
- The rights-transfer is said to cover 28 workers at [Para 68], with an unreadable "2308" inserted in the same line.
