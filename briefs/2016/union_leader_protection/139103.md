# Award No. 206/16 — Cosmo Textile Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 12 September 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Seng Vouch Hun (employer-appointed), An Nan (worker-appointed), Kong Phallnu (chair) · Doc ID 139103

## 1. Facts
Cosmo Textile Co., Ltd, a garment factory at Svay Chrom, Bek Chan, Ang Snuol, Kandal, employed about 770 workers [Para 106]. Its Cambodian Industrial Sewing Workers' Union was registered on 15 June 2016, with Mr C as president, Mr O as secretary and a third officer, Mr T, as an activist [Para 107][Para 137]. On 25 July 2016 the employer suspended the contracts of all three and on the same day asked the labour inspector for permission to dismiss them, alleging serious misconduct — that they had incited a canteen worker to accuse the employer's representative, Mr Li Jehou, of assaulting her [Para 111][Para 112][Para 118]. The inspector replied on 15 August 2016 that the three were in the hands of the police investigating a complaint the employer had filed on 18 July 2016, so the department could not proceed under the Labour Law; no further decision issued and the employer did not appeal to the Minister [Para 113][Para 117][Para 174]. Of nine points in dispute seven were settled at the hearing, leaving the demand for reinstatement and the demand that the police complaint be withdrawn [Para 29][Para 30].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the three union leaders whose contracts it suspended without the labour inspectorate's authorisation, and withdraw the defamation complaint it lodged with the police against them on 18 July 2016? [Para 111][Para 129]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the three did nothing wrong; the canteen worker herself came to them saying Mr Li Jehou had slapped her shoulder and raised his foot to kick her, so they incited no one [Para 116][Para 120][Para 121][Para 130].
**Employer (respondent):** it maintains the suspension while awaiting the Minister's decision, and says the three committed serious misconduct under Article 83 of the Labour Law by inciting the canteen worker to damage Mr Li Jehou's reputation; it will not withdraw the police complaint because the case may already be before the Kandal court [Para 118][Para 119][Para 131].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to end the suspension and take back Mr C, Mr O and Mr T; the demand that it withdraw the police complaint was dismissed for want of jurisdiction [Para 222][Para 223][Para 224].

On the two union officers the panel applied Articles 43 and 45 of the Law on Trade Unions: dismissing a staff delegate, or a candidate for union office, requires the labour inspector's consent, and where serious misconduct is alleged the employer may suspend at once but only pending the inspector's decision — if consent is refused, the suspension is void and the worker must be reinstated immediately [Para 152][Para 158][Para 163]. The inspector here never consented; his letter said only that the matter lay with the police, and no other decision existed [Para 171][Para 172][Para 173]. The panel rejected the suggestion that the pending police case justified continued suspension, and held the suspension contrary to Articles 43 and 45 [Para 175][Para 176].

On Mr T the panel noted he was not a union officer, so Article 43 did not apply [Para 178]. The employer nevertheless relied on serious misconduct under Article 83 and on Article 71(7), which permits suspension as a disciplinary measure [Para 180][Para 193]. The panel held that the employer bears the burden of proving serious misconduct [Para 187], and that it produced neither evidence nor a witness that Mr T did what was alleged [Para 190]. Article 71(7) also presupposes internal regulations providing for that penalty, and the employer never put its regulations before the panel, so the provision could not be applied [Para 194][Para 195][Para 196]. The suspension therefore rested on no ground recognised by Article 71 and had to end [Para 197][Para 203][Para 210].

On the police complaint the panel held it had no jurisdiction: a labour dispute under Article 302 of the Labour Law concerns working conditions, the exercise of professional-organisation rights and relations between employer and workers, whereas a demand that an employer withdraw a defamation complaint from the police is not such a dispute [Para 213][Para 218][Para 219][Para 221].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union officer's contract may be suspended only pending the labour inspector's decision on dismissal, and where that consent is never given the suspension is void under Article 45 of the Law on Trade Unions, whatever criminal complaint the employer has lodged; the employer must end the suspension and reinstate, at least until the Minister rules on any appeal. For a worker who is not a union officer, Article 71(7) permits suspension as a disciplinary measure only where internal regulations provide for that penalty, and an employer that neither produces its regulations nor proves the alleged serious misconduct under Article 83 cannot justify the suspension. A demand that an employer withdraw a criminal complaint it filed with the police is not a labour dispute within Article 302 of the Labour Law, and the panel has no jurisdiction over it. [Para 163][Para 173][Para 190][Para 196][Para 218]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that the employer retains the right to appeal the inspector's refusal to the Minister, the appeal period not having expired, is an observation on a route it did not need to take [Para 174].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The panel's finding at [Para 139] reads, as printed, that the union leadership has *no* right to represent the union's members, which contradicts [Para 150], where the same union is treated as the proper claimant; the negative particle is an OCR intrusion and the reasoning from [Para 145] onwards was followed.
- The union is variously named "កម្មករ ឧស្សាហកម្ម គាត់ ដេរ" [Para 137], "ខូស ម៉ូថេក ស្តាយ" [Para 150] and "ខូស ម៉ូ តិច ស្តាយ" [Para 105]; the catalogue title "Cosmo Textile Co., Ltd" is used here.
- The two decisive letters are described inconsistently: the inspector's letter of 15 August 2016 is cited as No. 1456/16 at [Para 98] and again at [Para 117], but the same letter is said to refuse the dismissal at [Para 117] and to decline jurisdiction at [Para 113], and the panel later notes it "did not decide" [Para 171].
- Mr O's last fixed-term contract is said to expire on "24 September 2016" in corrupted form ("ថ្ងៃទី ២៤ ខែកញ្ជូ") [Para 126], and Mr T's on "309 April 2017" [Para 128].
- The worker-side arbitrator's name is missing from the signature block, which reads only "ខ្លះ ឈ្មោះ៖ អន ណន" [Para 226]; the employer-side signature is blank.
- Paragraph [Para 205] is heavily corrupted, with stray years and symbols embedded in the text, and was disregarded.
