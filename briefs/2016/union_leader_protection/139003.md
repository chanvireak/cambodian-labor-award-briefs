# Award No. 058/16 — Great On Neak Stai Factory Terry Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 March 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (អ៊ីង សុទ្ធី), appointed by the employer; Sin Kim Sean (ស៊ីន គឹម ស៊ាន), appointed by the workers; Kong Phalha (គង់ ផល្ហៈ), chair · Doc ID 139003

## 1. Facts
Great On Neak Stai Factory Terry Ltd, a weaving and garment plant at Svay Rolum, Sa'ang, Kandal, employed about 1,200 workers and recognised a C.CAWDU local union. [Para 57] From 5 September 2014 to 10 November 2015 the employer suspended weaving-section contracts on rotating days, and did the same in the packing section from January to June 2015 and again across seven sections from 2 February 2016, paying 50 per cent of base wage under a 2011 collective agreement and never seeking the labour inspector's authorisation. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 78][Para 94] Separately, on 5 October 2015 the employer dismissed employee representative Mr C (ជា វឿន) for sleeping on duty; the labour inspector refused to authorise the dismissal. [Para 84][Para 88][Para 91] One of ten claims settled at conciliation and two more at the hearing, leaving issues 2, 3, 4 and 7 for adjudication; issues 5, 8 and 9 were withdrawn. [Para 23][Para 28][Para 29][Para 30]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (panel issue 2) Whether the employer must pay the 50 per cent shortfall to 100 per cent for weaving-section workers suspended from 5 September 2014 to 10 November 2015 without labour-inspector authorisation? [Para 65]
2. (panel issue 3) Whether the employer must pay the same shortfall for packing-section workers suspended between 1 January 2015 and June 2015? [Para 78]
3. (panel issue 4) Whether the employer must reinstate Mr C to the packing section on piece rates and pay him wages and allowances from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 82][Para 127]
4. (panel issue 7) Whether the employer must pay the shortfall for weaving from 2 to 6 February 2016 and for seven other sections from 2 February 2016 to the date of the claim? [Para 94]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** a lack of work is the employer's own fault, so wages must be paid in full while contracts are suspended; the employer never obtained inspector approval, and "until present" in issue 7 reaches to the hearing. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 143]
**Employer (respondent):** productivity fell year on year as workers slowed down, so it suspended contracts and paid 50 per cent as agreed with the union in 2011; paying more would trigger claims from other sections and disrupt production. [Para 71][Para 73][Para 74]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay the 50 per cent shortfall to 100 per cent for weaving-section workers suspended 5 September 2014 to 10 November 2015 and packing-section workers suspended 1 January 2015 to June 2015; to pay Mr C's wages and other allowances from dismissal until reinstatement; and to pay the same shortfall for weaving from 2 February 2016 to 6 February 2016 and for the packing, ironing, pressing-inspection, trimming, shoulder-sewing and panel-inspection sections from 2 February 2016 to 10 March 2016, in each case for the days actually suspended. [Para 151][Para 152][Para 153]

On issues 2 and 3 the panel read Article 71 (point 11) of the Labour Law — suspension of enterprise activity must sit under the labour inspector's supervision — together with Article 72 (point 1), under which suspension relieves the employer of the wage obligation only where the law has been followed; otherwise 100 per cent wages are owed even with no work provided. [Para 100][Para 101][Para 107][Para 109] The employer admitted it never requested authorisation, and the panel found a suspension of three days a week followed by normal work to be irregular and outside Article 71. [Para 111][Para 112] It followed prior awards 27/08, 136/08, 51/09, 60/06, 40/07, 28/08, 53/08 and 71/09 on the same reading. [Para 106][Para 110]

Because the employer relied on the 23 August 2011 agreement to justify the 50 per cent rate, the panel tested that agreement against Article 13 of the Labour Law, which makes the Law's provisions mandatory and voids any arrangement contrary to them; it held the 50 per cent clause void and ordered the top-up. [Para 114][Para 115][Para 116][Para 117]

On issue 4 the panel applied Article 293: an employee representative may be dismissed only with the labour inspector's consent. [Para 127] The inspector had refused consent by letter 1935/15 of 27 October 2015 for want of evidence of serious misconduct, and the employer never appealed to the Minister within the two months allowed, so it had lost that right; reinstatement with back pay and allowances was ordered. [Para 134][Para 135][Para 136][Para 138]

On issue 7 the employer did not deny the February 2016 suspensions and no authorisation was produced; the panel treated the claim's "until present" as ending at the hearing of 10 March 2016 and ordered the shortfall to that date. [Para 146][Para 147][Para 148][Para 144][Para 150]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of employment contracts that lacks the labour inspector's prior authorisation under Article 71 does not relieve the employer of wages: 100 per cent of wages is owed for the suspended days, and any collective agreement fixing a lower rate such as 50 per cent is void under Article 13 as contrary to the mandatory provisions of the Labour Law. [Para 109][Para 112][Para 116]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that a rotating three-day suspension is "irregular" was not needed once the absence of authorisation was admitted; and its reading of "until present" as closing at the hearing date is an interpretive aside rather than an operative ruling. [Para 111][Para 143][Para 144]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is dated 30 March 2016 on its face and the reasons record deferral from 24 March 2016 to 30 March 2016, but the panel is said to have been constituted on "03 March 2015" and one passage prints "10 March 2019" for the hearing date. [Para 1][Para 25][Para 33][Para 144] The conciliation report is numbered 358/16 in one place and 354/16 in two others. [Para 19][Para 24][Para 53] The employee representative's staff number appears as "Dan 2061" in the claim list and "AB 2576" in the inspector's letter. [Para 8][Para 39] Claim percentages are corrupted in the reasoning, issue 3 being printed as "450 per cent" and issue 2 as "5000 per cent" where 50 per cent is meant. [Para 79][Para 100] The company name is OCR'd four different ways (ហ្គ្រេត / ហ្វ្រេត / ហ្គេក ធើ / ហ្គ្រេត អនធើ), and the employer-appointed arbitrator's signature on the last page is an illegible Latin-script scrawl. [Para 58][Para 155]
