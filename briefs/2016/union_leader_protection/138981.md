# Award No. 028/16 — Kamchay Mar Trading (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 24 February 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Kong Phally, president) · Doc ID 138981

## 1. Facts
Kamchay Mar Trading (Cambodia) Co., Ltd weaves garments on National Road 8 in Kamchay Mar district, Prey Veng province, and employs about 2,000 workers [Para 35][Para 36]. The National Federation of Unions of the Textile and Garment Industry of Cambodia filed six demands on behalf of the workers on 04 January 2016; conciliation on all six failed on 22 January 2016 and the file reached the Council on 02 February 2016 [Para 13][Para 14][Para 15]. At the hearing the workers withdrew four points (2, 3, 4 and 5), leaving points 1 and 6 [Para 20][Para 21]. Only two workers attended — Ms H, the union president, and Mr C, a union activist — and the panel treated them alone as the claimants [Para 37][Para 63][Para 64]. The employer did not appear, but sent an unsigned-numbered letter of 12 February 2016 answering each demand [Para 58][Para 46].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the workers may require the employer to dismiss Mr D, the chief of A1 building, "phong" section, because of his improper words and conduct towards workers — telling a worker who sewed a garment wrongly that she could not open her eyes, telling a checker he was talking to a person and not to a dog, demanding a medical certificate before allowing sick leave, threatening to end the contract after two or three sick-leave requests, refusing overtime to workers who take leave, and reprimanding two workers on 24 December 2015? [Para 2][Para 38][Para 43][Para 66]
2. Whether the employer must stop assigning piece-rate sewers (sewing by CILOT) to repair garments with missing buttons and to sew assembly panels, work for which they are not paid? [Para 7][Para 47][Para 88]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr D abuses workers verbally and in conduct, and the union has advised him twice without correction; they said they would file supporting documents with the Council for its consideration [Para 2][Para 45][Para 79]. Piece-rate sewers are made every day to repair button-less garments and assembly panels without pay, costing each worker roughly 03 to 04 lots and some USD 06 to 07 a month [Para 48][Para 49].
**Employer (respondent):** In its letter of 12 February 2016 the company said it would educate and guide Mr D on the use of appropriate language and gestures towards workers, and that it would select a separate person to repair the defective garments instead of using CILOT sewers, while denying that it failed to allow time for that work [Para 46][Para 51][Para 91].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — point 01 dismissed and point 06 not considered, the panel finding the parties no longer in dispute on it while ordering the employer to keep performing what it had undertaken [Para 99][Para 98][Para 100].

On point 01 the panel first had to decide whether workers may demand a supervisor's dismissal at all. Under Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 311 of the Civil Code only the parties to a contract may extinguish its obligations, so a third party cannot dissolve the employment contract between employer and worker, and following Awards No. 116/10 and No. 83/12 the panel held that only the employer may end Mr D's contract [Para 66][Para 69][Para 70][Para 71][Para 72]. A worker may nonetheless demand removal where the supervisor is shown to be a dangerous person who cannot be kept in the enterprise without affecting the safety or peace of other workers, but the party making the allegation bears the burden of proving it with clear evidence [Para 73][Para 75][Para 77]. The workers never specified which worker was addressed or when, promised documents, and filed none by the 18 February 2016 deadline, so the panel found the burden unmet and dismissed the claim [Para 45][Para 79][Para 80][Para 82][Para 87]. It added that the employer's offer to educate Mr D was a reasonable and acceptable response [Para 83][Para 84].

On point 06 the panel noted the employer's written undertaking of 12 February 2016 to stop assigning CILOT sewers to repair defective garments, that the workers raised no objection to that document by the deadline, and that the parties therefore no longer disputed the point; since the employer had already conceded the demand, the panel declined to consider it further but held the employer bound to continue performing as stated [Para 90][Para 92][Para 93][Para 95][Para 96][Para 97][Para 98].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Workers have no standing to demand the dismissal of a supervisor or manager, because only the parties to the employment contract may terminate it; the panel will nonetheless order removal where the claimants prove, with specific dates, named workers and documents filed within the time limit, that the supervisor is a danger to others — and an unsupported allegation, however serious, fails for want of evidence. Where the employer concedes a demand in writing before the panel and the workers do not contest that document, the point is no longer a dispute and the panel will not decide it, though the concession binds the employer. [Para 70][Para 73][Para 82][Para 98]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the employer's absence did not prevent it from proceeding, since a party properly invited who fails to appear without valid reason may have the case heard in its absence under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099; it also recorded, without deciding, the workers' estimate that the unpaid repair work cost them USD 06 to 07 a month. [Para 53][Para 60][Para 49]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer was absent from the hearing, so the record of its position rests solely on one letter. Its incorporation certificate is numbered 2052 KH/2013 at [Para 35] and 5760 at [Para 28], and the company name appears as Kamchay Mar, Kamchay Mar Trading and "Ramchay Mar". The finality period at [Para 101] is printed as 18 days, where comparable awards print 08. The point 6 heading is corrupted ("ចំណុច វ វាទ ទា ង៖") at [Para 88], the promised evidence deadline is garbled at [Para 80], and the presiding arbitrator is named Kong Phalbeh on the face and Kong Phally at signature, while the workers' arbitrator appears as "An Non" in the header and "An Lean" at signature [Para 102]. The two claimants are identified only by their printed Khmer names and by role [Para 37].
