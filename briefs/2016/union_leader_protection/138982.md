# Award No. 031/16 — Ho Sin Tay Limited (Arbitration Council, 14 March 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, appointed by the employer; Tuon Si Phan, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 138982

## 1. Facts
Ho Sin Tay Limited manufactures footwear and employs about 1,968 workers [Para 47][Para 48]. The Khmer Workers' Pride Union held an election on 19 December 2015 and its three leaders — Mr P as president, Mr C (employee No. 00813) as vice-president and Ms Y (employee No. 02911) as secretary — were notified to the company by post on 22 December 2015 and by hand on 23 December 2015 [Para 80][Para 81][Para 82]. The union was not yet registered with the Ministry of Labour [Para 49]. Both Mr C and Ms Y were told on 24 December 2015 that their fixed-duration contracts, expiring 31 December 2015, would not be renewed [Para 55][Para 73][Para 90]. Of four referred points, three were settled at the hearing (points 2, 3 and 4), leaving only point 1 [Para 21][Para 22].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the two Khmer Workers' Pride Union leaders — Mr C (employee No. 00813) and Ms Y (employee No. 02911), said to have been dismissed on 24 December 2015 — and pay them their wages for the period they were out of work? [Para 2][Para 55]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The company ended Mr C's contract because it knew he was a union leader, not for the work-related reasons given; he signed the warning letters under pressure, and the 24 December warning was in fact thumbprinted later, not on its stated date [Para 56][Para 59][Para 61][Para 62]. He did not abscond on 23 December 2015 — he was at the Ministry of Labour giving information on the dispute — and he collected the termination payment on 01 January 2016 only out of fear after the company telephoned repeatedly and warned him about "illegal strikes" in Bavet and Svay Rieng [Para 63][Para 69][Para 70].
**Employer (respondent):** Both contracts were 03-month fixed-duration contracts that simply reached their end on 31 December 2015; the company notified non-renewal on 24 December 2015 and had already warned Mr C twice (04 and 24 December 2015) for poor performance and half a day's unauthorised absence [Para 3][Para 55][Para 57][Para 58][Para 60]. The terminations have nothing to do with union discrimination, and the company likewise did not renew other workers in November and December 2015 for work reasons [Para 57][Para 71][Para 72][Para 112].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the claim for reinstatement and back wages was refused for Mr C, and refused consideration for Ms Y [Para 122][Para 123][Para 124].

The panel first asked whether Mr C enjoyed protection from dismissal. Under Article 73(1) a fixed-duration contract expires by its own terms when the term is reached, and his last 03-month contract ran to 31 December 2015 [Para 86][Para 87]. Article 73(5) requires ten days' advance notice of expiry only for contracts longer than six months, so the notice of 24 December 2015 was not even required — a practice, not an obligation [Para 88][Para 89][Para 91]. Turning to the candidate protection in Article 293 and Article 3(3) of Prakas No. 305, the panel held that protection for a candidate who wins office lasts at least 45 days after the election, or such reasonable period as until the registration application is filed, and that the protection bites only on dismissal — the act that requires the labour inspector's authorisation [Para 93][Para 98][Para 99][Para 100][Para 101]. No provision requires an employer to seek authorisation when a fixed-duration contract simply runs out, so Mr C, though an elected vice-president, was not protected against non-renewal [Para 102][Para 103][Para 104].

On discrimination the panel applied Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law, which forbid using union membership or activity as a basis for hiring and job-assignment decisions, and placed the burden on the workers to prove discrimination [Para 105][Para 106][Para 107][Para 110]. Mr C produced no specific evidence tying non-renewal to his union role, while the company showed it had equally declined to renew two other workers — a cleaner and a worker in the water section — in the same period for lateness, absenteeism and poor performance; the panel therefore could not find a union motive and dismissed the claim [Para 111][Para 112][Para 113][Para 114].

Ms Y's case failed on standing. She did not attend the hearing and her name was not on the 24 December 2015 mandate; the panel invited the union to file her written authorisation and none was filed, so under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 she was not a claimant and the claim was refused consideration, following Award No. 014/16 [Para 53][Para 54][Para 115][Para 116][Para 118][Para 119][Para 120][Para 121].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The protection against dismissal given to union election candidates and elected leaders by Article 293 of the Labour Law and Article 3(3) of Prakas No. 305 does not extend to the natural expiry of a fixed-duration contract: only a dismissal requires the labour inspector's prior authorisation, so an employer may let a short fixed-duration contract lapse without it, even where the worker is an elected union officer. A union officer who claims the non-renewal was anti-union must prove it, and that proof fails where the employer shows it declined to renew other workers for the same work-related reasons in the same period. [Para 101][Para 103][Para 104][Para 112]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the ten-day notice under Article 73(5) is confined to contracts over six months, and that giving notice of non-renewal for a three-month contract is a practice the law does not require; it also noted, without deciding, the worker's account that he collected his termination payment only after the company warned him about illegal strikes elsewhere. [Para 89][Para 91][Para 69]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is short and several key passages are garbled. The panel's establishment date reads "09 February 2021 2016" at [Para 18]; Prakas No. 305 is dated "22 November 2023 2001" at [Para 97]; and the extension of the award date at [Para 25] is legible but sits beside a duplicated heading. The mandate is described as covering 08 workers [Para 50] while the employer's objection letters name 07 of them [Para 51]. The company is called Ho Sin Tay Limited and, once, "Ho Sun Tay" [Para 66]; the union is called both Khmer Workers' Pride Union and "Khmer Workers' Spirit Union" in the membership lists at [Para 39]. The finality period is 08 days [Para 124]. Claimants' individual names appear in the document only as Mr C, Mr P and Ms Y for the purposes of this brief.
