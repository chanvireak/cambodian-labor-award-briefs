# Award No. 094/16 — Ever Well Knitting Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 25 May 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chiv Phirom, appointed by the employer; Sin Kim Sean, appointed by the workers; Kong Phalla, chair appointed by both) · Doc ID 139024

## 1. Facts
Ever Well Knitting Co., Ltd, a garment-sewing factory at Tuol Key village, Traey Sla commune, Sa'ang district, Kandal, employed about 400 workers. [Para 86] The Democratic Union of Khmer Garment Workers at the company received registration certificate No. 3533 of 14 March 2016 from the Ministry of Labour. [Para 87] The union complained to the Kandal provincial department of labour on 24 March 2016 on eleven demands; conciliation on 5 April 2016 settled none of them and the file was referred on 7 April 2016. [Para 15] At the hearing of 21 April 2016 the workers withdrew issues 4 to 8, 10 and 11, leaving issues 1, 2, 3 and 9 for decision. [Para 24][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the non-renewal of the fixed-duration contracts of the union president and vice-president was a dismissal, so that the company must take them back and pay wages from the date of the non-renewal? [Para 245]
2. Whether the company must take back the four candidates who stood in the union leadership election and pay their wages from the date of the non-renewal? [Para 309]
3. Whether the three union members — two whose contracts expired and one barred from the factory on 2 February 2016 — were dismissed or merely not renewed, and whether they were entitled to the protection against dismissal? [Para 328]
9. Whether the company must issue piece rates (តម្លៃឡូត៍) for every new style within 7 days after giving the workers a new PN (ម៉ូតសម្លៀកបំពាក់)? [Para 416]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the two leaders, the four election candidates and the members were all union activists whose contracts were cut during the 45-day protected period around the election, without the labour inspector's prior agreement and without notice; they should be reinstated with back wages, and piece rates should be published within 7 days of each new style because the company lowers rates once workers become fast. [Para 166][Para 215][Para 323]
**Employer (respondent):** it refused to take anyone back because there was not enough work and the workers had poor evaluations and warnings; a fixed 3-month contract simply expires, and the law requires no notice for contracts shorter than 6 months; its piece rates are already higher than other factories' and are set in line with the Ministry's Prakas. [Para 219][Para 291][Para 321]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement and back wages refused on issues 1, 2 and 3; the company was ordered to pay the one worker dismissed before her contract expired her 5% termination indemnity, final wages, unused annual leave and damages at least equal to wages to the contract's end; on issue 9 the company must issue piece rates for every style within 7 days after a new PN is given out. [Para 431][Para 435][Para 439]

On issues 1 and 2 the panel applied Article 73(1) of the Labour Law: a fixed-duration contract expires at its term, the employment relationship ends automatically, and whether to renew is a matter for the parties, neither of whom can force the other. [Para 245][Para 250] Article 73(5) requires 10 days' notice only where the contract exceeds 6 months; their contracts ran 3 months, so no notice was owed. [Para 259] Protection under Article 293 and Prakas No. 305 attaches only to a dismissal, so an expired contract cannot attract it, and the employer owed no notification to the labour inspector. [Para 272][Para 276][Para 278] The anti-union discrimination claim failed because the party alleging it carries the burden of proof and the workers' evidence — an assertion that a union president asked one candidate to resign, unsupported by any witness — did not discharge it. [Para 303][Para 305]

On issue 3 the panel split the three: for the two whose contracts ran out, the same non-renewal reasoning applied and reinstatement was refused. [Para 345][Para 347] The third was barred from the factory on 2 February 2016 although her contract ran to 31 March 2016 — a dismissal before term. [Para 339] She had been a union member since 1 January 2016 and the union held its registration receipt from 27 January 2016, so the dismissal came 7 days later, inside the protection window of Article 4 of Prakas No. 305, satisfying the first two conditions. [Para 355][Para 360] The third condition — the union's duty to notify the employer of the names of protected members by any available means — was not proved, so the protection was never acquired. [Para 362][Para 365][Para 366] The alleged serious misconduct under Article 83(5), that she toured the factory urging colleagues to thumbprint in support of the dismissed ironing-section leaders, was not made out. [Para 388] Because the company ended a fixed-term contract unilaterally before its term, Article 73(3) damages, the Article 73(6) 5% indemnity, final wages under Article 116 and unused annual leave under Article 166 were ordered. [Para 393][Para 398][Para 404][Para 409][Para 411]

On issue 9 the panel applied Article 112, which requires the employer to give clear and readily understood notice of wage conditions whenever they change, and followed earlier awards fixing 3 to 7 days for piece rates; it found the company breached that duty when rates shifted with each new style. [Para 421][Para 429][Para 430]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Non-renewal of a fixed-duration contract at its expiry is not a dismissal: under Article 73(1) the relationship ends automatically, no notice is owed where the contract is 6 months or shorter under Article 73(5), and the protection against dismissal in Article 293 and Prakas No. 305 does not attach, so reinstatement and back pay cannot be ordered. [Para 259][Para 276]

Where an employer instead ends a fixed-term contract before its term without the worker's agreement, the worker recovers Article 73(3) damages of at least the wages due to the contract's end, whether or not the Prakas No. 305 protection was validly claimed. [Para 391][Para 393]

**Obiter dicta:** once the protection had failed for want of the union's notification, the panel's further findings — that urging colleagues to thumbprint in support of dismissed leaders is not serious misconduct under Article 83(5), and that a strike is not itself serious misconduct — went beyond what the orders required. [Para 386][Para 388]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The issues are numbered 1, 2, 3 and 9 because 4 to 8, 10 and 11 were withdrawn at the hearing; the award records no reasoning on the withdrawn items. [Para 24][Para 25]
- The complainant group is counted inconsistently: 12 workers transferred rights of whom 6 were not union members, 8 workers represented, 9 present at the hearing, and 7 listed in the attendance passage. [Para 89][Para 91][Para 239][Para 240]
- Article 73(5) is garbled in quotation, reading "over 2 six months" at one point and "06 months" at another. [Para 252][Para 256]
- The worker dismissed before term appears as Huon Heun, Houn Heun and Huon-Heun; she is reduced to an initial here.
- The union's registration dates are given as a receipt of 27 January 2016 and a certificate of 14 March 2016, which the reasoning uses interchangeably. [Para 358]
- The award issued 25 May 2016 after the parties agreed to extend it from 4 May 2016. [Para 28]
- The signature block names only the worker-appointed arbitrator and the chair; the employer-appointed seat is left blank. [Para 441]
