# Award No. 185/16 — Tung Choi Fashion Technology Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 18 August 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Hem Hunarith, employer-appointed; An Virak, worker-appointed; Ang Eng Thong, president) · Doc ID 139086

## 1. Facts
Tung Choi Fashion Technology Co., Ltd ("Tung Choi") prints and finishes garments at Prey Boeng village, Ang Snuol district, Kandal province, with about 120 workers [Para 35][Para 36]. Mr T had worked there since 13 November 2014 as head of the ironing and image-printing section on successive three-month fixed-duration contracts; he had been a staff representative (ប្រតិភូ បុគ្គលិក) since 25 December 2014, with a mandate running to 12 December 2016 [Para 38][Para 42]. His last contract ran from 13 April 2016 to 13 July 2016; the company notified non-renewal on 9 July 2016, he worked to 13 July 2016, and was paid wages, severance and annual-leave compensation on 18 July 2016 [Para 39][Para 40][Para 55]. The employer did not appear at the hearing [Para 14][Para 62].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take Mr T back into work as before? [Para 38][Para 96]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The company ended his contract without valid reason and out of resentment that, as a staff representative, he had reported to the Kandal labour department the administration's statement that staff representatives were no longer required — conduct that brought a labour official to the factory in June 2016 [Para 43][Para 49][Para 50].
**Employer (respondent):** In its written statement of 1 August 2016 the company said the ironing and printing section had had little work for several months and the whole team, including Mr T as its head, was dissolved; it did not attend the hearing [Para 57][Para 58].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the claim that the employer take Mr T back into work; no compensation was ordered beyond the sums already paid [Para 95][Para 96].
The panel first held the hearing could proceed in the employer's absence, applying Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 (a party properly summoned who fails to appear without valid reason does not stop the panel), and decided the case on the workers' evidence and submissions [Para 59][Para 65][Para 66].
On the merits it applied Article 73(1) of the Labour Law: a fixed-duration contract terminates by operation of law when its term expires, and renewing or not renewing is the parties' own choice, since no party can be compelled to continue a contract without agreement [Para 70][Para 72]. It followed its earlier awards including No. 100/07 (Hoyea Cambodia), No. 64/09 (Sinomek 2) and No. 98/14 (Wai Full Garment) [Para 73][Para 74]. Because Mr T worked to 13 July 2016 and the relationship then ended automatically, the case was one of non-renewal, not dismissal [Para 75][Para 76].
It then asked whether the special protection in Article 43 of the Law on Trade Unions of 17 May 2016 — no dismissal of a staff representative or candidate without the labour inspector's agreement — covered non-renewal, and held it did not: that protection applies only to dismissal, which is the act for which the law requires the employer to seek authorisation [Para 77][Para 78][Para 79].
Finally, on the discrimination allegation, the panel applied its consistent rule that the party making the allegation bears the burden of proof [Para 83], examined the evidence, and found nothing showing the employer acted to rid itself of staff representatives; the other staff representative kept working [Para 90][Para 92][Para 93]. It rejected the workers' ground [Para 94]. The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent written objection to the Minister [Para 18][Para 97].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a fixed-duration contract runs to its stated term and is not renewed, there is no dismissal: the relationship ends by operation of law under Article 73(1) of the Labour Law, the Article 43 Trade Union protection requiring the labour inspector's authorisation does not apply, and a staff representative who alleges the non-renewal was anti-union bears the burden of proving it. [Para 72][Para 79][Para 90]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted its earlier ruling in Award No. 64/09 (Sinomek 2) that an employer may decide not to renew a contract at its expiry unless discrimination is the basis of the decision, and endorsed that reasoning without needing it, having already found no evidence of discrimination [Para 80][Para 82].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The workers' account dates the call to the administration "the evening of 9 June 2016" [Para 53], while the contract expiry, the non-renewal notice and the final working day are all given as 9 and 13 July 2016 [Para 39][Para 40]; both dates are reported as printed. The worker's name appears as both "ទូ ចាន់ ណារិទ្ធ" and "ទូច ចាន់ ណារិទ្ធ" [Para 48][Para 76]. The employer's name is printed in several forms ("តុង ឆៃ", "តុង ឆាយ", "តុង ឆៃ ខូអិលធីឌី"), and only the Latin form in the evidence list fixes it as Tung Choi Fashion Technology [Para 23][Para 24][Para 35]. Stray year fragments ("2017", "២០២២") and untranslated OCR noise interrupt several paragraphs, including the reasoning on burden of proof and the signature block [Para 47][Para 75][Para 98].
