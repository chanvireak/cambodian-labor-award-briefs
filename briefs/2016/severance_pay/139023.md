# Award No. 093/16 — Kem Vanna Fashion Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 10 May 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Tang Tri, chair appointed by both) · Doc ID 139023

## 1. Facts
Kem Vanna Fashion Co., Ltd, a garment-sewing enterprise at Vihear Suor village, Khsach Kandal district, Kandal, employed about 107 workers, of whom 21 gave the Khmer Workers' Heart Union Federation (សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព បេះដូង កម្មករ ខ្មែរ) a written mandate by letter of 24 February 2016. [Para 41] The union complained to the Kandal provincial department of labour on 7 March 2016 on four demands; conciliation on 1 April 2016 settled none of them and the file was referred to the Council secretariat on 7 April 2016. [Para 12] At the hearing of 22 April 2016 the parties reached agreement on issues 1 and 2, leaving only issues 3 and 4 for the panel. [Para 16]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must install cameras in the shed where workers park their motorcycles? [Para 1]
2. Whether the company must instruct its administration chief, Mr Y, to stop insulting workers and banging on the table when they protest? [Para 2]
3. Whether the company must bear liability for workers' motorcycles damaged inside the factory — by nails, stones, salt and sharp objects put into engines and tyres — and reimburse the repair costs incurred in February 2016? [Para 3]
4. Whether the company must pay the final month's wage owed to a dismissed worker, Mr H, and pay wages no later than 48 hours after any dismissal? [Para 4]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the damage was deliberate sabotage carried out in the company's parking shed, so the employer must replace the motorcycle or pay the USD 245 repair bill; and Mr H's final month's wage, about 18 days, had been unpaid since his dismissal on 18 December 2015 although Article 116 required payment within 48 hours. [Para 46][Para 70]
**Employer (respondent):** it cannot accept the demands — the machine may have broken down after the worker left the premises, no witness saw the damage happen, the worker reported it two days late and could not produce the invoice when asked, and the invoice's USD 35 service charge and USD 100 repair charge were abnormal; the law does not require the final wage to be paid separately from severance. [Para 56][Para 63]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 3 dismissed; on issue 4 the panel ordered the company to pay the final month's wage owed to Mr H after his dismissal. [Para 103][Para 104]

On issue 3 the panel held that the party asserting a fact carries the burden of proving it, following earlier awards it listed by number (101/08-GDM, 16809 Tek Thla Plaza, 115/10-Fourmos, 148/11-Diamond, 276/15-Mitpheap Gamin, 63/04-Sun Well, 99/06-Sao Thmey Bay, 33/07 and 51/07-Gold Fame). [Para 79][Para 80] The worker conceded that no witness knew of the damage, and his own account placed the first failure after he left the factory and a second failure mid-route, the machine dying finally at his home. [Para 84] Without direct evidence that the damage occurred while the motorcycle was under the company's control, the panel could not infer employer fault, and it refused to presume liability from the worker's assertion alone. [Para 87][Para 92]

On issue 4 the panel applied Article 116 of the Labour Law, which requires wages and all indemnities to be paid within 48 hours after the day work stops. [Para 93] The company's refusal to release the final wage until severance was settled was improper, because Article 116 fixes the deadline regardless of how the sums are packaged; paying wage and severance together or separately is only a matter of form. [Para 96][Para 98] A worker who claims only the final month's wage, leaving severance aside, does not act against the spirit of Article 116. [Para 101]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker who claims that the employer is liable for property damaged on the employer's premises must prove that the damage occurred while the property was within the employer's control; an uncorroborated account, a report made days later and an invoice produced only afterwards do not discharge that burden, however plausible the loss. [Para 79][Para 87]

An employer may not hold back the final month's wage until severance is agreed: Article 116 fixes a 48-hour limit running from the end of work, and whether the two sums are paid jointly or separately does not affect that deadline. [Para 96][Para 100]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that no case of motorcycle damage had previously arisen at the company, and its observation that paying wages and severance jointly or separately is "only a matter of form", were not necessary to either order. [Para 62][Para 98]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The damage is dated February 2016 in the claim and the reasons but February 2015 in both dispositive orders. [Para 3][Para 92][Para 103]
- Article 116's period is printed as 48 hours at [Para 93] and [Para 100], but the "eight" is lost at [Para 97], which reads as forty-five hours.
- The dismissed worker's name appears variously as Hout Va Cha, Huot Va Cha and Hout-Va Cha; he is reduced to an initial here. [Para 4][Para 104]
- The repair date is printed twice at [Para 51] as "23 February 2017 ... 2016".
- Company identifiers conflict: commercial registration Inv. 0627 E/2012 of 2 March 2012, versus statutes No. 0745/2012 and a registration certificate No. 4502 of 24 April 2015. [Para 23]
- The signature block names only the two party-appointed arbitrators, not the chair. [Para 106]
- OCR loss breaks the reasoning into fragments and garbles the list of prior awards ("148@1", "ចំណុច អាច" for ចំណុច វិវាទ). [Para 80]
