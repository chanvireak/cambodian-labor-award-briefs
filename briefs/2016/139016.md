# Award No. 079/16 — British American Tobacco Cambodia Ltd (Arbitration Council, 27 May 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom, chosen by the employer; An Non, chosen by the workers; Kong Phalma, chair chosen by the two) · Doc ID 139016

## 1. Facts
The Cambodia Labour Union filed six demands for the roughly 200 workers of British American Tobacco Cambodia Ltd, a cigarette manufacturer; two were settled at conciliation, one more at the hearing, leaving issues 1, 2 and 4 [Para 23][Para 28][Para 29][Para 76]. The individual at the centre of the case, Mr H, joined on 9 January 2009 as a territory market representative (TMR) on successive fixed-duration contracts, the last of them for nine months, expiring 31 December 2015 [Para 86][Para 162]. He worked until 5 January 2016 and the employer treated the relationship as having ended by expiry of a fixed-term contract [Para 87][Para 181]. The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent written objection to the Minister [Para 30][Para 299].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay Mr H the termination entitlements of an indefinite-duration contract — pay in lieu of notice, dismissal compensation, moral damages, unused annual leave and final wages — because his successive fixed-duration contracts had exceeded two years in total? [Para 150][Para 165]
2. Whether the employer must obtain the individual worker's consent before transferring him from one province to another? [Para 236]
3. Whether the employer must convert to indefinite-duration contracts those TMR staff whose successive fixed-duration contracts total more than two years? [Para 283]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Because the aggregate duration of Mr H's contracts exceeded two years, the contract became indefinite under Article 67(2) of the Labour Law, so every entitlement of an indefinite contract is owed; the employer gave no valid notice and no proper reason for termination [Para 89][Para 90]. On transfer, the employer must obtain the worker's consent before moving him between provinces, as in Mr B's case of a move from Prey Veng to Kampong Cham [Para 107][Para 110].
**Employer (respondent):** Mr H's contract simply reached its term on 31 December 2015, the employer cannot renew past that date, and it has already paid his final wages, 5% contract-end compensation and unused annual leave, with a further 1,978 as a goodwill gesture; the Labour Law does not prohibit successive fixed-duration contracts, and TMR staff are a special category without fixed hours who work at least 48 hours a week [Para 7][Para 92][Para 94][Para 128]. Transfers are reserved to the employer by clause 1(2) of the contract [Para 113][Para 258].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay Mr H pay in lieu of notice under Article 75, dismissal compensation under Article 89, moral damages under Article 91, unused annual leave under Article 166 and final wages under Article 116, all "in the case that" they have not yet been paid; the transfer-consent demand (issue 2) was dismissed; and the employer was ordered to convert to indefinite contracts all TMR staff whose fixed-duration contracts total more than two years (issue 4) [Para 292][Para 297][Para 298].
As a threshold matter the panel held the local union's leaders could not represent the members, because no union registration certificate, leadership recognition letter or Ministry receipt had been produced, and Article 268 of the Labour Law makes registration the source of representational rights [Para 136][Para 142][Para 143][Para 144]. Under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 the nine workers present at the hearing were therefore the claimants in person, and the decision binds only them [Para 145][Para 148][Para 149].
On issue 1, the panel read Article 67(2) as capping at two years the total duration of a fixed-duration contract including renewals, with any excess converting the relationship to indefinite duration, and applied its earlier interpretation in Award No. 10/03 — Zacksintek [Para 150][Para 151][Para 152][Para 153]. Measuring from 9 January 2009 to 31 December 2015, the total plainly exceeded two years, so the contract was indefinite [Para 162][Para 163][Para 164].
Two obstacles then arose for the employer. First, the payment records it filed were in English and untranslated, and Prakas No. 099 Article 23 makes Khmer the language of proceedings, so the panel refused to admit them and could not find that the 1001.48 dollars covered anything beyond final wages [Para 170][Para 171][Para 172][Para 173]. Second, the notice letters and the three witness statements from the sales manager, the regional sales manager and the HR officer were unsigned by Mr H and were contradicted; even if a notice letter were accepted, Article 75 required two months' notice for service beyond five years, which was not given [Para 196][Para 201][Para 204][Para 205].
The panel also held the 5% contract-end payment is due only on expiry of a fixed-duration contract and is not owed on termination of an indefinite one, so the employer may deduct the 5% it had already paid, following Award No. 067/16 — Cambodia Yon Sing Garment [Para 226][Para 228][Para 229][Para 230].
On issue 2 the panel refused to lay down a general consent requirement, since Article 2 of the Labour Law gives the employer a management right exercisable lawfully and reasonably, and provincial-boundary moves may be short or expressly agreed [Para 267][Para 269][Para 282]. It nonetheless observed that clause 1(2) of Mr B's contract was too vague to show genuine consent to a long-distance move, and that the employer should have discussed the Kampong Cham transfer with him first [Para 263][Para 266][Para 281]. On issue 4 it applied the same Article 67(2) reasoning to order conversion for all TMR staff over two years, the employer having raised no objection [Para 284][Para 287][Para 288][Para 290].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive fixed-duration contracts whose aggregate duration exceeds two years convert the relationship into an indefinite-duration contract, and the employer then owes the full indefinite-contract entitlement set; a boilerplate clause reserving the right to change the workplace does not by itself prove the worker's consent to a transfer between distant provinces, and documents submitted only in a foreign language cannot discharge the employer's burden of proving payment [Para 163][Para 172][Para 266].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982, though not binding, supports reading Article 67 as hostile to repeated short-term contracting for long-term work [Para 158][Para 160]. It also observed that the transfer-consent rule cannot apply uniformly, since nearby cross-border postings may need no consent at all [Para 282].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
OCR damage is severe and the award contradicts itself in several places. The employer's name appears as "British American Tobacco Cambodia", "British Tobacco Cambodia" and "ថ្នាំជក់អង់គ្លេស អាមេរិកកាំង កម្ពុជា" [Para 75][Para 77][Para 98]. The notice of termination is dated 1 December 2015 in the document list, 5 December 2014 in the reasoning, and 5 March 2015 in another entry, and the contract expiry is given as both 31 December 2015 and 31 December 2016 [Para 41][Para 46][Para 86][Para 101][Para 197]. The amount paid is stated as "about 1,001 dollars" by the workers, "1,001.48 dollars" by the employer, and the goodwill offer is printed only as "1,978" without a currency [Para 7][Para 82][Para 166][Para 167]. Statutory references are garbled by OCR — "Article 664" for Article 664 of the Civil Code, "Article 336"/"337" of the Civil Code, and "Article 63" where Article 67 is meant [Para 153][Para 238][Para 244]. The workers' conciliation demand was reinstatement of Mr H, but the panel decided only a compensation claim without recording the change [Para 5][Para 150]. The signature block at the end preserves no names at all [Para 300].
