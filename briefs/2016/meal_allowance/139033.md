# Award No. 116/16 — Yong Sing (Yong Xing) Company (Arbitration Council, 22 June 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sodhy, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Kong Phallha, president) · non-binding award · Doc ID 139033

## 1. Facts
Yong Sing is a small garment workshop in Prey Veng that takes partly finished goods from other factories and sews them on, employing about 100 workers, of whom 56 signed a mandate transferring their rights to three federations. [Para 1][Para 31][Para 32][Para 37] Of the 12 demands raised at conciliation on 12 May 2016, 8 were settled and 4 were referred to the panel. [Para 12][Para 13] The four residual claims concern a monthly travel-and-accommodation allowance, a minimum-wage top-up for piece-rate workers, holiday and Sunday pay, and a meal allowance for overtime. [Para 38][Para 42][Para 48][Para 54]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to pay a travel and accommodation allowance of 07 USD per month to the workers? [Para 59]
2. Whether the employer must top up piece-rate workers to the minimum wage of 140 USD per month where their output falls below it? [Para 63]
3. Whether the employer must pay holiday work at the normal day's wage and Sunday overtime at twice the normal day's wage, in accordance with the Labour Law? [Para 69]
4. Whether the employer must pay a meal allowance of 2,000 riel for overtime worked from 04:00 to 06:00 pm, and pay that overtime at 1.5 times the normal day's wage? [Para 88]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Ministry of Labour notification No. 230 K.B/ស.ជ.ណ of 25 July 2012 obliges the employer to pay 07 USD a month, and the posted company plan itself concedes an entitlement; piece-rate earnings in April 2016 were only 20 to 30 ម៉ឺន, below the 140 USD floor; holiday and Sunday work must attract the statutory premiums. [Para 39][Para 44][Para 51]
**Employer (respondent):** The company is still a small workshop that receives work from others and sometimes has no work to hand out, so it cannot pay the 07 USD rate and has instead posted a plan paying 05 USD from May 2016; of roughly 70 piece-rate workers about 20 already reach the minimum; it pays a flat 01 USD a day for holiday and Sunday work and 2,000 riel for two hours of overtime, and resists any higher rate. [Para 38][Para 40][Para 46][Para 47][Para 52][Para 58]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to pay the 07 USD monthly travel and accommodation allowance; to top up piece-rate workers to the 140 USD minimum wage whenever output falls short; to pay holiday work at the normal day's wage plus Sunday overtime at a 100 per cent premium (200 per cent of the normal day's wage in total); and to pay a 2,000 riel meal allowance for overtime plus overtime at a 50 per cent premium. No aggregate sum in riel or USD was fixed. [Para 62][Para 68][Para 87][Para 94][Para 95][Para 97][Para 99]

On issue 1 the panel read clause 1 of Notification No. 230, which sets the allowance at 7 USD a month, found the employer paying only 5 USD from May 2016, and held that the notification had not been complied with. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61]

On issue 2 the panel applied clause 2 of Prakas No. 409/15 K.B/ប្រ.ក of 08 October 2015, which requires 135 USD for probationary and 140 USD for full-rights workers where piece-rate output falls below the floor; both parties conceded the shortfall, and the panel held the prakras not applied. [Para 63][Para 65][Para 66][Para 67]

On issue 3 the panel applied Article 164 of the Labour Law and clause 4 of Prakas No. 10 SKBY of 04 February 1999, adopting the reading in Award No. 113/14 (San Jin) that a worker already paid for the holiday receives an additional sum equal to a normal day's wage for working it. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 71][Para 73] It treated Sunday work as overtime beyond the 8-hour/48-hour week under Article 137, and applied the 100 per cent premium in the new Article 139 for weekly-rest-day overtime, rejecting the employer's flat 01 USD. [Para 77][Para 82][Para 86]

On issue 4 the panel applied clause 2 of Notification No. 041/11 K.B/ស.ជ.ណ of 07 March 2011, which gives 2,000 riel a day or a free meal, found no meal allowance had ever been paid, and applied the 50 per cent premium in new Article 139 to the 04:00–06:00 pm overtime already compensated at 2,000 riel. [Para 88][Para 89][Para 90][Para 93]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a prakas or ministry notification fixes a minimum entitlement — a monthly travel allowance, a minimum-wage floor for piece-rate workers, a holiday supplement, or an overtime meal allowance — an employer's unilateral posted plan paying a lesser amount does not discharge the obligation, and the panel will order the statutory amount. [Para 61][Para 67][Para 75][Para 90] Work on a weekly rest day is overtime beyond the normal 8-hour/48-hour week and attracts the 100 per cent premium under the amended Article 139, so the worker must receive 200 per cent of a normal day's wage in total. [Para 82][Para 86]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted the company's character as a small sub-contracting workshop taking work from other factories, and the parties' agreement that about 100 workers are employed, but treated neither as altering the statutory entitlements. [Para 31][Para 32]
