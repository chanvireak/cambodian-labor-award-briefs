# Award No. 147/16 — Sin Ji Tian (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 21 July 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (In Sothy, appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna, appointed by the workers, dissenting on issue 2; Kong Polha, president) · Doc ID 139055

## 1. Facts
A garment factory employing about 540 workers faced an 11-point demand from the Khmer Workers' Consciousness Union Federation for improved working conditions [Para 3][Para 19][Para 54]. Conciliation on 17 June 2016 failed; six points were later settled by agreement, leaving five in dispute — the timing of the midday meal allowance, the pro-rating of the regular attendance bonus, the overtime meal allowance, a skills allowance and the pay consequences of work stoppages [Para 20][Para 25][Para 26]. The plant-level union held elections on 23 May 2016 but had not yet obtained its registration certificate from the Ministry of Labour, and the federation held written transfers of rights from 84 workers dated 31 May 2016 [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to pay the midday meal allowance weekly rather than monthly? [Para 26][Para 100]
2. Whether the employer must reduce the regular attendance bonus pro rata to the days of leave lawfully authorised by the employer? [Para 26][Para 113]
3. Whether the employer is obliged to pay an overtime meal allowance of 4,000 riel for work from 16:00 to 18:00? [Para 144]
4. Whether the employer is obliged to pay a skills allowance of USD 20 for operators of three machines and USD 15 for operators of two machines per month? [Para 174]
5. Whether the employer must pay 100 per cent wages when it gives no work without labour inspector authorisation, and repay the 50 per cent withheld for two days in June 2016? [Para 181]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The meal allowance is a legal entitlement that should be paid weekly because the workers live in hardship and need it to buy food, and the attendance bonus forms part of wages, so it should be reduced in proportion to any lawfully authorised absence rather than forfeited [Para 61][Para 67]. The 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance is not enough to buy a meal, and the 50 per cent wage paid during stoppages was unlawful because no suspension was authorised [Para 71][Para 83].
**Employer (respondent):** No law fixes when the meal allowance is paid, and the company, which began operating only in February 2016, cannot afford weekly payment; the attendance bonus deductions follow the agreement signed with the union, and the skills allowance cannot be paid because wages are being moved to piece rates [Para 63][Para 69][Para 73][Para 78].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay 100 per cent wages for periods when it gave no work without labour inspector authorisation; every other claim was dismissed [Para 214][Para 215].

On a threshold point the panel held that under Article 11 of the Trade Union Law of 17 May 2016 a union acquires legal rights and benefits, including the right to represent members before the Arbitration Council, only once its founders register with the Ministry [Para 87][Para 90][Para 91]. The plant union had no certificate yet, so its leaders could not represent members [Para 92][Para 93]. Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 Sakbey, however, allows a party to be represented by any person authorised in writing, so the panel accepted the federation's representation of the 84 workers who had signed transfers of rights [Para 96][Para 97][Para 99].

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 2 of the Labour Law, which gives the employer the right to manage and direct the enterprise so long as that right is exercised lawfully and reasonably [Para 100][Para 104]. No law or regulation requires a midday meal allowance at all; the 1,500 riel paid daily is a practice more favourable than the law, and paying it with the monthly wage is neither unlawful nor unreasonable, so the claim failed [Para 108][Para 110][Para 112].

On issue 2 the majority found that Notification No. 041 of 7 March 2011 was replaced by Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012, which added the words "without absence" and raised the minimum bonus to USD 10 [Para 117][Para 118][Para 119]. Because the panel will ordinarily follow its earlier jurisprudence unless a new legal basis or good reason exists, but here the earlier awards rested on the superseded notification, the majority declined to follow them [Para 120][Para 121][Para 122]. Treating lawful permission to be absent as distinct from the obligation to pay the bonus, and since the workers were absent on the days in question, the majority dismissed the claim [Para 130][Para 135][Para 137]. Arbitrator Liv Sovanna dissented, reasoning that the minutes of the 11 July 2012 Labour Advisory Committee meeting recorded agreement on a USD 10 bonus without the "without absence" condition, that a notification may not contradict the Committee's will, and that the panel should therefore keep its long-standing pro-rata approach [Para 219][Para 223][Para 225][Para 234][Para 244].

On issue 3 the panel found Notification No. 041 itself sets the overtime meal allowance at 2,000 riel per day or one free meal, and that the employer pays exactly that, so no legal or contractual basis supports 4,000 riel [Para 144][Para 146][Para 148][Para 149].

On issues 4 and 5(b) the claims were treated as interests disputes. Under Article 54 and Article 72 of the Trade Union Law and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099, the panel may decide an interest dispute only where the union bringing it is the most representative in the enterprise; this complainant was not, so the claims were not examined [Para 155][Para 168][Para 171][Para 172]. The back-pay claim also failed on evidence: the workers could not identify the two days in June 2016 or the workers affected, and filed no list by the deadline [Para 204][Para 207][Para 208].

On issue 5(a) the panel held that Article 72(1) removes the wage obligation only for suspensions under Article 71, and Article 71(11) permits suspension only for serious economic or material difficulty, under labour inspector supervision and for no more than two months [Para 181][Para 183][Para 185][Para 188][Para 189]. The employer had sent workers home on 50 per cent pay without seeking the inspector's authorisation, so the suspension was unlawful and Article 72(1) did not apply [Para 193][Para 194][Para 195], and full wages were ordered [Para 196][Para 214].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that sends workers home for lack of work without the prior authorisation and supervision of the labour inspector has not effected a lawful suspension under Article 71(11), so the exemption from wage payment in Article 72(1) does not apply and full wages are owed for those days [Para 189][Para 195][Para 196]. Separately, a union not yet registered under Article 11 of the Trade Union Law cannot represent its members before the panel, but a federation holding individual written authorisations under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 may bring those workers' claims [Para 93][Para 96].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's statement that it will ordinarily follow its own earlier jurisprudence unless a new legal basis or good reason justifies departure [Para 120], and its remarks that an interest-dispute award substitutes for a collective agreement and removes the right to strike on that subject [Para 165][Para 167].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is heavily degraded by OCR. The date of the panel's constitution prints as "២០២៣ ២០១៦" [Para 22], and the meal-allowance practice is dated "ខែវិច្ឆិកា ឆ្នាំ ២០១៩" where 2016 is plainly meant [Para 107]. Paragraph 138 is almost entirely corrupted into Latin nonsense strings, destroying part of the attendance-bonus calculation [Para 138]. Sums are printed several ways: the overtime meal allowance appears as 2,000 and 4,000 riel and the shortfall as "៥០០ ភាគរយ", "៤៥០ ភាគរយ" and "៤៥០០០ ភាគរយ" [Para 85][Para 181][Para 215], and the skills allowance as "២០១៦ ដុល្លារ" [Para 175]. The award says 11 points were settled at conciliation while elsewhere six were settled and five remain [Para 25][Para 26]. Union officials' names shift between renderings (កយ សំអុល / គយ សំអុល) and are redacted in this brief [Para 55][Para 98]. The registered address and telephone lines of the Secretariat are garbled at the head of the document [Para 2][Para 3].
