# Award No. 190/16 — Thob Sil Ver Limited (Arbitration Council, 14 September 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tai Seng, employer-appointed; An Nan, worker-appointed; Kong Phalla, chair) · non-binding award · Doc ID 139090

## 1. Facts
Thob Sil Ver Limited (formerly the O'Rang company) cuts and sews garments at Ampil Ta Pok, O'Rang district, Tboung Khmum province, and the employer states it employs about 1,299 workers. [Para 32][Para 33] A single union, the National Union Federation of the Textile and Garment Industry of Cambodia at Thob Sil Ver, holds registration and has roughly 700 members; there is no most representative union in the enterprise. [Para 34][Para 36] On 2 May 2015 the parties signed an incentive-bonus agreement at the provincial labour department: workers earning USD 150 to USD 168 receive a 6% bonus and those earning USD 169 or more receive 8%, calculated on actual monthly wage for a normal 8-hour day, with no expiry date stated. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40] The employer applied it until 31 May 2016 and then introduced a new bonus scale from 1 June 2016 without the workers' agreement. [Para 45][Para 95]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must apply the agreement of 2 May 2015 and reimburse the incentive bonus the workers should have received while the employer was not applying it? [Para 38][Para 78]
2. Whether the employer must pay a midday meal allowance of at least 1,000 riel a day to the workers? [Para 50][Para 115]

The workers withdrew their third demand — maternity leave pay for women whose contract ended while pregnant — at the hearing, so the panel considered only these two. [Para 18][Para 19]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer breached the 2 May 2015 agreement, which states no expiry date and so continues to bind, rather than lapsing after one year on 31 May 2016; under the new scale workers earning from USD 150 receive no bonus at all, and those at USD 153 receive only 5% instead of 6%; the whole point of the demand is to recover the bonus lost; neighbouring factories pay a midday meal allowance and long-serving workers deserve one. [Para 41][Para 47][Para 52][Para 53]
**Employer (respondent):** it did perform the agreement, but the agreement was valid only to 31 May 2016 because it was made to match a one-year purchase order; it has since issued a new one-year bonus policy from 1 June 2016 with tiers of 5% above USD 153, 6% above USD 164 and 8% above USD 185; the absence of an expiry date was the translator's mistake, not its true intention, which was one year only; it cannot pay a meal allowance. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 45][Para 54]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must apply the agreement of 2 May 2015 and reimburse the incentive bonus the workers should have received while it was not applying the agreement; the meal-allowance claim was refused consideration. [Para 140][Para 141]

On representation the panel held that under Article 54(1) of the Trade Union Law of 17 May 2016 only the most representative union may conclude a collective agreement or settle a collective dispute, following Award No. 180/16 (Cement Cambodia Chakrey Ting), and that where none exists workers may act in person or transfer their rights; Article 11 of the same law gives a registered union the rights the law confers only once its founders have registered. [Para 55][Para 57][Para 66][Para 69][Para 72][Para 76] It found the union here registered, its three officers present at the hearing, and the employer not disputing their standing, so it could represent its own members in this case. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 37][Para 77]

On issue 1 the panel applied Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code: a contract arises from the meeting of offer and acceptance and binds the parties, who may only alter or extinguish it by agreement. [Para 78][Para 81][Para 82][Para 85][Para 88] It followed Award No. 271/14 that an employer must perform a wage agreement it made with its workers, and Award No. 307/15 (Niu Ark Kit Garment) that a party wishing to change a collective agreement must first negotiate with the other party. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 91] On the facts, the employer applied the agreement only until 31 May 2016 and then imposed a new policy from 1 June 2016 without any discussion with the workers. [Para 95][Para 96] The panel rejected the argument that the missing expiry date was a translation error: following Award No. 180/16 (issue 2) and Award No. 05/16 (YLT International), translation is the employer's own responsibility because the employer engages and pays the translator, and a translation defect cannot let an employer escape a valid agreement. [Para 98][Para 100] Since the 2 May 2015 agreement states no expiry date it remains in force, and any change requires the workers' prior agreement. [Para 101][Para 102] Exercising its power under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 to order immediate payment of sums due, and following Award No. 196/15 (Long Lead (Cambodia), issue 2), the panel ordered performance and reimbursement of the bonus lost during the period of non-application. [Para 103][Para 105][Para 107][Para 113][Para 114]

On issue 2 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, employment contract, agreement or past practice creating a meal allowance, so the demand was an interest dispute. [Para 116][Para 117] Under Article 96(2) of the Labour Law, Articles 54 and 72 of the Trade Union Law and Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099, only a most representative union may bring an interest dispute, since such an award becomes a collective agreement binding all workers in the company; the claimant was not one, so the panel declined to consider the claim. [Para 127][Para 130][Para 132][Para 133][Para 137][Para 138]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A collective agreement that states no expiry date remains binding until the parties agree otherwise; an employer may not replace its bonus terms by unilateral policy, and a translation error in the text the employer's own translator produced is no ground for escaping the agreement, because responsibility for translation rests on the employer. [Para 98][Para 100][Para 102] A demand for a benefit no law, agreement or practice grants is an interest dispute that only a most representative union may bring before the panel. [Para 117][Para 133]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that the absence of a most representative union can obstruct workers' representation and touch their constitutional right to complain under Article 39 of the Constitution are general remarks on the Trade Union Law, not the basis of either order. [Para 63][Para 64]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award is dated 14 September 2016 in the header but the parties' extension of the deadline runs "to 15 September 2016" [Para 22]; both are reported as printed.
- The union's membership of about 700 against a stated workforce of about 1,299 is above the 30% threshold in Article 54, yet the panel recorded that no most representative union existed [Para 36][Para 75]. The finding is reproduced as made; the arithmetic tension is unresolved in the text.
- The employer's name is printed as ថប ស៊ីល វើរ លីមីធីត, លីបីធីត and លីមីតធីត, and the union as ថប ស៊ូលវេរ at [Para 34].
- Amounts are corrupted: [Para 111] prints the wage band as "១៥៣០ ដុល្លារ" (USD 1,530) for USD 153; [Para 45] carries a garbled "មួយ ខែកុម្ភៈ ឆ្នាំ ៦- ០០០" in tier 2; [Para 43] states the intended duration as "០១៤ ឆ្នាំ"; [Para 142] gives the objection period as "១៨៦៥" days.
- Names: the worker-appointed arbitrator is An Nan in the header but the signature block reads "បន្ទាយមា 19 09862" [Para 1][Para 142]; the employer's representative appears only as a bare name and is reduced to an initial here.
- Several quoted provisions break off into a bare "។" ([Para 65], [Para 71], [Para 80], [Para 106], [Para 120], [Para 128]), and [Para 110] and [Para 132] carry stray page markers mid-sentence.
