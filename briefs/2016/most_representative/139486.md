# Order No. 318/15 — Yee Pan Rok S.E.A. (Phnom Penh) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 6 January 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, chosen by the employer party; Toun Si Phan, chosen by the worker party; Pen Bun Chhea, chair chosen by the two) · Doc ID 139486

## 1. Facts
Yee Pan Rok S.E.A. (Phnom Penh) Co., Ltd, a garment factory in the Phnom Penh special economic zone, faced a collective claim from the Cambodia Workers' Union at the factory, registered on 3 July 2015 under No. 3312 and confirmed by the Minister of Labour's letter No. 089/15 as a lawfully registered workers' organisation. [Para 46][Para 47] The complaint reached the zone labour office on 15 September 2015; conciliation on 16 points left 5 in dispute, and the non-conciliation report No. 456/15 of 30 November 2015 was transmitted to the Council on 3 December 2015. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20] The hearing was held on 16 December 2015 and the order printed as a non-binding order (សេចក្តីបង្គាប់មិនជាប់កាតព្វកិច្ច). [Para 17][Para 129]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must keep paying wages and allowances when the federation invites the base union to meetings or seminars? [Para 2]
2. Whether the employer must move the clinic from an upper floor to the ground floor? [Para 4]
3. Whether the employer must provide a nursery (ទារកដ្ឋាន) and a suitable breastfeeding room? [Para 6]
4. Whether the employer must pay a multi-machine sewing-skill bonus of USD 4, 7 or 10 a month? [Para 8]
5. Whether the employer must withdraw its complaint to the Phnom Penh court against three union leaders? [Para 10]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** wages and allowances should continue whenever the federation calls the base union to a meeting or a seminar, including ILO events and attendance at conciliation or arbitration [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54]; the clinic should move downstairs for easier transport of patients [Para 4]; a nursery and breastfeeding room are required by law, since Article 186 obliges the employer to provide them [Para 67]; other companies such as Y Po and Malin pay multi-machine skill bonuses [Para 71]; the court complaint against three union leaders should be withdrawn as a gesture of cooperation [Para 77].
**Employer (respondent):** it would follow whatever the law requires [Para 3]; it cannot move the clinic because there is no free room [Para 5]; it awaits the Ministry's Prakas on nurseries [Para 69]; it has a clear policy of recruiting workers with real skills and never agreed to a skill bonus [Para 73]; the court case concerns forged thumbprints collected for union membership and is an individual matter already before the court [Para 78][Para 119].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to arrange a nursery and breastfeeding room for women workers with children, and, where it cannot, to let those with children over 18 months place them in an outside nursery at the employer's expense on actual invoices; it refused to consider the wage-continuity demand, the skill bonus and the court complaint. [Para 124][Para 125][Para 123][Para 126][Para 127]

On wage continuity the panel asked whether it had jurisdiction at all, and answered by quoting its own order in case 10/03: the Council exists to resolve labour disputes, not problems that have not yet arisen. [Para 80] It had consistently refused claims whose object was a future dispute and required workers to bring a specific event; here the union conceded that no law or practice required payment for such absences and that the request was only "kept for the future". [Para 55][Para 82][Para 84] It declared itself without competence over disputes that do not exist. [Para 85][Para 86]

The clinic demand was disposed of without reasoning: both parties agreed it was no longer pursued, the employer having set up a First Aid team to treat workers taken ill during working hours. [Para 60][Para 62]

On the nursery, the panel applied Article 186 of the Labour Law, which requires any enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a breastfeeding room in or near the establishment and a nursery as well, and, where it cannot take children over 18 months, to let mothers place them in an outside nursery at the employer's cost. [Para 88][Para 89] It read the article as imposing a principal obligation to establish the nursery and the breastfeeding room, the payment of care costs being an alternative only for children over 18 months where the employer cannot provide them. [Para 90][Para 91] Both parties accepted that no nursery or breastfeeding room existed, and the employer relied on the USD 10 a month milk allowance it pays women returning from maternity leave until the child is one year old. [Para 95][Para 63] The panel held that the allowance did not comply with Article 186: paid in place of the statutory facilities it did not meet the purpose of the law, did not release the employer from the duty, and was not an option in lieu of reimbursing actual invoices for children over 18 months. [Para 96][Para 97]

The skill bonus was treated as an interest dispute, since no provision of the Labour Law, no collective agreement, internal rule or past practice obliged the employer to pay it. [Para 102][Para 103] Under Article 96 and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 the right to bring an interest dispute belongs to the most representative union, because Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 makes an award on such a dispute a substitute collective agreement binding for one year; this union was not yet certified as most representative. [Para 104][Para 105][Para 107][Para 112][Para 113]

On the court complaint the panel set out Article 302's definition of a collective labour dispute, noted that only collective disputes fall within its jurisdiction, and held that a demand to withdraw a complaint against named individuals over forged thumbprints was not such a dispute, the matter already being before the court. [Para 115][Para 116][Para 121][Para 122]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An enterprise that employs at least 100 women must set up a nursery and a breastfeeding room under Article 186 of the Labour Law, and a monthly cash allowance in place of those facilities does not discharge the obligation; the allowance is an alternative to reimbursement of actual nursery invoices only for children over 18 months and only where the employer cannot provide the facilities. [Para 90][Para 96][Para 97][Para 124] A demand whose object has not yet arisen is not a dispute the panel can decide, and a demand to withdraw a court complaint against individual union leaders is not a collective labour dispute, whatever the motive behind it. [Para 85][Para 120]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's statement that it "always" examines a union's most-representative status in an interest dispute, and its explanation that an interest award would bind the workforce for a year and bar future claims, are observations about its own practice rather than steps needed to refuse this bonus. [Para 106][Para 108]

## 6. Source defects disclosed

- The order is dated 6 January 2016 [Para 1], but the extended issue date is recorded as "06 January 2015" [Para 25]; the non-conciliation report is dated 30 November 2015 yet described as transmitted on 30 December 2015 in the same paragraph [Para 15].
- Issue 4's figures are printed USD 4/7/10 at [Para 8] but "04/07/1,000" at [Para 100]; the sum for three machines must be read from the operative order [Para 126].
- The order calls itself final after "08 years" without objection [Para 129] where "08 days" is meant.
- OCR corruption is heavy: the seal line is symbols only [Para 1]; page tags ("AKP-19", "ADBC", "2017 )*") sit inside sentences at [Para 22], [Para 34], [Para 87]; [Para 57] carries a stray "1700 នាក់" and an unclosed bracket; the Article 302 quotation breaks off mid-sentence [Para 116][Para 117].
- The employer is "យេ ផេន រ៉ក អេស អ៊ី អេ" but "យេន ផេន រ៉ក" at [Para 47]; the panel is "ក្រុមប្រឹក្សា អាជ្ញាកណ្តុល" at [Para 50]. Managers, union officers and the three leaders named in issue 5 are reduced to initials or roles.
