# Award No. 220/16 — SL Garment Processing (Cambodia) Ltd II (Arbitration Council, 8 November 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Seng Vuoch Hun (employer-appointed), Sin Kim Sean (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (presiding) · Doc ID 139374

## 1. Facts
Mr S (Sim Sokha), a sewing-machine mechanic (employee no. 14541), was hired by SL Garment Processing (Cambodia) Ltd II ("SL2") on 5 August 2013 on an indefinite-duration contract, paid a base wage of USD 160 plus a USD 10 attendance bonus, a USD 3 seniority bonus and USD 7 transport and accommodation allowance per month. [Para 80][Para 83] The company dismissed him on 30 July 2016; he had never been disciplined or warned. [Para 81][Para 82] The union demanded his reinstatement in post with wages and benefits restored and paid to the day he returned; the company refused reinstatement but agreed to pay statutory severance and terminal benefits. [Para 2][Para 3][Para 85]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer was obliged to take Mr S back into work and to pay his wages and other benefits up to the date of his return? [Para 130]

The dispute reached the Council with 19 points, of which 13 were unresolved after conciliation. [Para 34][Para 35] At the hearing the parties settled point 9, the workers withdrew the enumerated remainder, and only point 1 remained for adjudication. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The dismissal had no valid reason — Mr S had committed no fault and received no warning — so the company must reinstate him and pay wages and benefits until he resumes work. [Para 86][Para 87]
**Employer (respondent):** The sewing groups D and E that Mr S serviced no longer exist, the remaining workers were merged into other groups, and the factory now carries too many mechanics for the machines left; dismissal is the employer's managerial prerogative and it will pay compensation rather than reinstate. [Para 89][Para 91][Para 92]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement and back pay refused, but the company was ordered to pay severance under Article 89, moral damages under Article 91, outstanding annual leave under Articles 166 and 167, and final wages under Article 116; no sum was computed in the award, and payment in lieu of notice was refused. [Para 208][Para 209][Para 211][Para 212]

Standing. Before the merits the panel addressed whether the claimant union could bring the case at all. Under Article 54 of the Law on Trade Unions (2016) only the most representative union in an enterprise may conduct collective dispute resolution, and the panel followed its earlier reasoning in Case 180/16 (Cement Cambodia Chakrey Ting) and Award 171/16 (Jewellyn Khou Ltd): the local union held a most-representative certificate valid until 20 June 2018, so it alone could represent SL2's workers before the Council. [Para 120][Para 124][Para 125][Para 129]

The dismissal ground. Article 74 permits termination of an indefinite-duration contract at one party's will, on written notice, but never without a reason relating to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the operational needs of the enterprise. [Para 131][Para 133] The panel held that the party asserting a fact bears the burden of proving it, following a line of earlier awards. [Para 138][Para 139] The company's allocation table was drawn up after the dismissal, listed only four mechanics and did not name Mr S, so it could not establish that he was a group D and E mechanic. [Para 142][Para 144] The workers' attestation carrying 46 thumbprints was equally inconclusive: the signatories admitted they did not know how mechanics were allocated, and the company showed that most of them were union activists. [Para 112][Para 114][Para 150][Para 151] Neither side discharged the burden of showing which group Mr S actually serviced, so a claim to reinstatement in groups B and C had no clear evidential basis. [Para 152][Para 153]

Consequences. Because the company also could not show any valid operational reason, and admitted Mr S had never been at fault, the dismissal was an exercise of will unrelated to the grounds Article 74 allows, and the employment relationship had broken down. [Para 156][Para 158][Para 179] Severance was therefore due under Article 89, calculated on average wages over the 12 months before dismissal. [Para 180][Para 181][Para 186]

Notice. The company produced a written notice dated 1 July 2016 running to 31 July 2016, unsigned by Mr S, plus CCTV stills of the meeting at which it was handed over in the presence of his group leader. [Para 105][Para 107][Para 110] The workers alleged only an oral notice given on 30 June 2016 but offered no evidence to support it. [Para 170] The panel accepted the written notice as compliant and refused the payment in lieu of notice under Articles 75 and 77. [Para 172][Para 173][Para 174]

Moral damages and leave. Article 91 allows damages where a contract is terminated without valid reason, a head of claim distinct from both notice and severance payments; the panel awarded an amount equal to the severance. [Para 187][Para 188][Para 192] Unused annual leave was payable under Articles 166 and 167. [Para 196][Para 198]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A dismissal on the employer's own will under Article 74 stands or falls on the reason given: the employer must prove a ground tied to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the enterprise's operational needs, and where it cannot, the dismissal is arbitrary and attracts both Article 89 severance and Article 91 moral damages — even though a contested reinstatement claim fails for want of proof [Para 133][Para 139][Para 156][Para 180][Para 187]. A written notice of termination actually delivered, evidenced by CCTV footage, satisfies Articles 74 and 75 despite the worker's refusal to sign, defeating any claim for payment in lieu [Para 172][Para 173][Para 174].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel remarked that the employer ought to have produced the mechanic-allocation table as it stood before the dismissal, so that the panel could see which group Mr S serviced [Para 143], and noted that the union's most-representative status ran until 20 June 2018 [Para 127].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The procedural narrative says the workers withdrew 11 points but enumerates only ten (2–8, 10, 12 and 13) [Para 40], and the figure of 19 conciliated points [Para 34] does not reconcile with 13 unresolved plus one settled plus those withdrawn. The dismissal is dated 30 July 2016 [Para 81] while the notice letter is said to run to 31 July 2016 [Para 106]. Long stretches of the source are corrupted OCR: Latin and English noise intrudes at [Para 117], [Para 129] and [Para 148], and the panel signature block at [Para 219] garbles the presiding arbitrator's name and title. The company is rendered variously as SL Garment, SL2 and "SL Gament" [Para 59], and the claimant as Sim Sokha, Sim Sena and Sim Mony Sokha [Para 109][Para 147][Para 152].
