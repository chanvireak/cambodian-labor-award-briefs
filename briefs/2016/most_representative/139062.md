# Award No. 156/16 — King Fuse In Das Steel Co., Ltd (2) (Arbitration Council, 5 August 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed, dissenting on issue 1; Kuy Nam, President) · Doc ID 139062

## 1. Facts
King Fuse In Das Steel Co., Ltd (2), a garment cut-and-sew factory in Ang Snuol district, Kandal province, employs approximately 1,300 workers organised by the Free Women Workers' Union of the company [Para 38][Para 39][Para 41]. Of four demands filed with the provincial labour department on 30 May 2016, one was settled and three remained: the pro-rata deduction of the USD 10 attendance bonus when a worker takes necessary leave, the grant of a daily target bonus to the 110 workers of the cutting section, and an increase of the travel and accommodation allowance from USD 7 to USD 12 a month [Para 11][Para 12][Para 2][Para 3][Para 4]. The panel heard the case on 13 July 2016 with both sides present and issued an award with a dissent annexed by the worker-appointed arbitrator [Para 10][Para 16][Para 249].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must deduct the regular attendance bonus pro rata to the number of days on which a worker took necessary leave with proper authorisation from the employer? [Para 44][Para 197]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to pay a daily target bonus to the 110 workers of the cutting section? [Para 198][Para 222]
3. Whether the employer must pay a travel and accommodation allowance of USD 12 a month? [Para 223][Para 247]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** A worker who works 26 days a month earns the USD 10 bonus, but when he takes only three days of permitted leave the whole bonus is cut, although everyone has necessary family obligations such as funerals and weddings, and other companies deduct proportionally [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49]. The cutting section is treated unfairly because it alone receives no daily target bonus [Para 60][Para 61].

**Employer (respondent):** The employer applies the Labour Law and its registered internal rules: a full bonus for workers who attend regularly, no bonus when they do not, and a graduated deduction of USD 1.5 for one day, USD 3 for two days and total loss from two and a half days [Para 50][Para 119][Para 120]. The cutting section needs high concentration and is paid a monthly bonus of USD 3 to USD 50 (USD 50 to USD 80 for head cutters) instead [Para 56][Para 58][Para 207][Para 208].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Dismissed in full — the claim for pro-rata deduction of the attendance bonus was refused, the claim for a daily target bonus for the cutting section was refused, and the claim for a USD 12 allowance was declined consideration; no sum was ordered [Para 245][Para 246][Para 247].

On issue 1 the panel traced Notification No. 041 of 07 March 2011 (minimum USD 7) and Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012, which raised the minimum to USD 10 and required the worker to have attended "without absence" [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74]. It held the USD 10 is a legal rule of general application that binds every employer once the worker satisfies its conditions, not a bonus within the employer's gift [Para 96]. Reading "without absence" it built a taxonomy of leave: absence as a legal right (weekly rest, public holidays, annual leave, sick leave, maternity, special leave up to seven days), absence as a contractual right, absence as a legal obligation (for example a summons to testify), and all other absence [Para 97][Para 98][Para 129][Para 130][Para 144][Para 157]. Attendance counted as regular where the worker attended every working day, including days of legal-right, contract-right and legal-obligation leave; the panel reasoned that an incentive must not be used to obstruct the exercise of a statutory right [Para 141][Para 169][Para 170][Para 195]. Absence for other personal reasons, even where the employer permits it as a favour, is not counted; the employer owes no bonus at all in that case unless it has adopted a more generous policy of its own [Para 166][Para 172][Para 196][Para 197]. The panel therefore rejected the argument that the bonus is a wage earned proportionally to days worked: it is a fixed incentive that does not vary with output [Para 183][Para 184][Para 186][Para 194].

On issue 2 the panel held that under Article 2 of the Labour Law the employer holds the managerial prerogative to design incentive schemes beyond those fixed by law, provided they are lawful and reasonable [Para 203][Para 209][Para 211]. Cutting demands sustained concentration, so a speed-based daily target bonus would provoke mis-cuts and losses; a monthly bonus keyed to quality is a reasonable exercise of that discretion, all the more so because the bonus table shows both sections earning broadly comparable monthly totals [Para 215][Para 218][Para 220][Para 221].

On issue 3, Notification No. 230 fixes USD 7 and the employer pays USD 7; no law, collective agreement, internal rule, contract or practice requires USD 12, so the claim exceeded the law and was a dispute of interest [Para 223][Para 224][Para 226][Para 227]. An interest dispute may only be brought by the most representative union under Article 96(2) of the Labour Law, Article 54 of the Trade Union Law and Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099, and the claimant was not such a union, so the panel declined to consider it [Para 240][Para 243][Para 244].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Notification No. 230's attendance bonus is payable at 100% only where the worker attended every working day of the month, counting as attendance days of leave that are a legal right, a contractual right or a legal obligation; any other absence, including leave the employer merely permits as a favour, forfeits the bonus entirely, because the bonus is a fixed incentive within the employer's managerial discretion and not a wage accruing in proportion to days worked [Para 172][Para 186][Para 195][Para 196]. An employer may lawfully reserve a daily target bonus for one section and pay another only a monthly quality-based bonus where the work demands differ and total reward is comparable [Para 211][Para 220].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's survey of leave categories, its reading of "ធុរៈ" from the Chuon Nath dictionary, and its remark that the parties' own competing definitions of "necessary" and "ordinary" errands show how unworkable a case-by-case inquiry would be, are explanatory rather than operative [Para 83][Para 84][Para 115][Para 117]. It also noted that a benefit scheme may not be used to obstruct the exercise of a statutory right, a proposition not needed once the three counted categories were defined [Para 139][Para 141].

**Dissenting opinion (An Non):** The worker-appointed arbitrator recorded a dissent on issue 1, invoking Awards Nos 223/2012 (Star Sport Wear), 216/2012, 214/2012, 211/2012 and 204/2012, in which panels ordered pro-rata deduction for leave taken with the employer's permission, and concluded that the majority departed from that jurisprudence without adequate reason and should have ordered the pro-rata deduction [Para 253][Para 254][Para 255].
