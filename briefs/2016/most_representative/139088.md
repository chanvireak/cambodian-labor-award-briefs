# Award No. 187/16 — Fes Gaunt E Pheril (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 31 August 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, employer-appointed; Tuon Si Phan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 139088

## 1. Facts
Fes Gaunt E Pheril (Cambodia) Co., Ltd ("Fes Gaunt") cuts and sews garments on National Road 2 in Mean Chey district, Phnom Penh, with about 470 workers [Para 71][Para 73]. Its factory union, the Democratic Garment Workers' Union, is registered with the Ministry of Labour and holds the most-representative certificate (No. 302, 12 November 2015, valid to November 2017) [Para 74][Para 76]. Seven demands were conciliated without success on 21 July 2016; two were withdrawn at the hearing, leaving five — notice-period job-search leave, annual leave during maternity leave, a half-day suspension on 29 March 2016, breastfeeding hours, and annual leave deducted for sick leave [Para 23][Para 28][Para 29].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must let a worker who resigns with notice take two paid days a week off to look for new work during the notice period? [Para 132]
2. Whether the employer must stop converting 4.5 days of annual leave into pay during maternity leave and refund the 2.25 days already paid? [Para 145]
3. Whether the employer must pay the missing 50 per cent of wages to the 87 workers suspended for half a day on 29 March 2016? [Para 165]
5. Whether the one hour a day for breastfeeding must be counted from the worker's return to work after maternity leave rather than from the birth? [Para 190][Para 193]
6. Whether the employer must restore the 10 days of annual leave deducted when a worker took medically certified sick leave? [Para 201]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Article 79 gives two paid days a week to look for work to whichever party terminates the contract, so a resigning worker is entitled to them; annual leave and maternity pay are distinct and cannot be offset; the half-day suspension was imposed without the labour inspector's authorisation, so full wages are owed; and the sick leave was properly certified [Para 80][Para 92][Para 95][Para 103].
**Employer (respondent):** The two days were given only where the company itself terminated, since the company had work available; the 4.5 annual-leave days were paid at half value and added to maternity pay under its practice; the suspension followed a genuine half-day shortage of goods and workers signed for 50 per cent; and the breastfeeding hour runs from the birth [Para 84][Para 86][Para 99][Para 105].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to allow two paid job-search days a week during a resigning worker's notice period, to stop offsetting 4.5 annual-leave days against maternity pay and to recover the 2.25 days already paid, to pay the outstanding 50 per cent of wages to the 87 workers suspended on 29 March 2016, and to restore 10 days of annual leave; only the breastfeeding claim was refused [Para 212][Para 213][Para 215][Para 216][Para 217].
On standing, the panel held that only a most-representative union may represent workers in a collective dispute before it, following Award No. 180/16 (Cement Cambodia Chakrey Ting), and found this union held that status [Para 124][Para 126][Para 131].
On issue 1 it read Articles 78 and 79 together: the duty to give notice applies to either party terminating alone, and Article 79 says nothing to confine the two paid job-search days to dismissals, so the employer's practice of granting them only on dismissal was unlawful [Para 135][Para 136][Para 139][Para 142].
On issue 2 it applied Article 167(3), which voids any agreement to pay out annual leave, and Article 182, under which the contract continues through maternity leave; annual leave is therefore separate and cannot be cashed out mid-employment. The employer's conversion practice was unlawful, and because it had paid 2.25 days' wages the workers concerned must repay that sum [Para 150][Para 152][Para 159][Para 164].
On issue 3 it applied Articles 71 and 72: a suspension of work requires the labour inspector's authorisation, and where that is missing the employer owes 100 per cent of wages even if no work was provided. Consent to 50 per cent is void under Article 13(1) of the Labour Law as contrary to a mandatory provision, so the employer could not rely on the signed forms [Para 173][Para 174][Para 180][Para 182][Para 187].
On issue 5 it applied Article 184 as interpreted in Award No. 31/11 (Quin Tha Major In Das Steel): the one hour a day runs from the birth, not from the return to work, and the employer was already complying [Para 196][Para 197][Para 199].
On issue 6 it found the internal rule required only a proper medical certificate; the worker produced two certificates from Bangkek Chey Chumnit hospital bearing the hospital's stamp and the treating doctor's name and signature, and no rule required the extra documents the employer demanded, so the 10 deducted annual-leave days had to be restored [Para 207][Para 208][Para 209]. The award is non-binding [Para 30][Para 218].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer's established practice cannot narrow a mandatory entitlement: the two paid job-search days under Article 79 belong to whichever party terminates the contract and so survive a resignation; annual leave may not be converted into cash while the contract subsists, including during maternity leave, because Article 167(3) voids such agreements; and a suspension of work without the labour inspector's authorisation leaves the employer owing full wages, the workers' written consent to half pay being void under Article 13. [Para 139][Para 152][Para 174][Para 187]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that the workers who had already received the 2.25 days' maternity offset must repay it, an adjustment neither party had claimed but which follows from the illegality of the practice [Para 161][Para 164].
