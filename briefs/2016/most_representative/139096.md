# Award No. 201/16 — Eternity Global Sporting (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 14 September 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chhea presiding; Seng Vouch Hun, employer nominee; Tuon Si Phan, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139096

## 1. Facts
Eternity Global Sporting (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, on Street 51 at Damnak Ampil, Ang Snuol district, Kandal, makes sports gloves and baseballs and employs about 741 workers. [Para 87][Para 88] Mediation of 11 demands left 10 unresolved, but at the hearing the parties agreed point 9 and part of point 10, and the workers withdrew points 1 to 8, leaving only point 10. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 31][Para 32][Para 33] The claimants are four workers who had left and were re-employed on 8 June 2016 under Award No. 032/16 of 10 March 2016; they claim that the grade (ចំណាត់ថ្នាក់), skill and incentive bonuses they once received were not paid after their return. [Para 8][Para 95] The only union holding most-representative status, the Khmer Workers' Pride Union, saw its certificate expire on 6 February 2015 and was reapplying, so no representative union existed. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 161][Para 162]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must repay a grade bonus of USD 5, a skill bonus of USD 5 and an incentive of USD 5 per month to Mr O, Mr P and Mr S for June to August 2016 (the panel's point 10, first limb)? [Para 95][Para 167]
2. Whether the employer must repay a grade bonus of USD 4.5, a skill bonus of USD 5 and an incentive of USD 13 to Mr L for the same months (point 10, second limb)? [Para 113][Para 168]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** all four had received these bonuses before and should receive them again as before; the employer raised the production targets after their return — from about 200 to 320 gloves for one worker and from about 400 to 700 for another — so they could not reach the target, while other workers in their groups also failed; Mr L was made to use an old, frequently broken machine and was stripped of his machine duties. [Para 98][Para 101][Para 102][Para 106][Para 107][Para 109]
**Employer (respondent):** grade, skill and incentive payments depend on each worker's output, cooperation, conduct and punctuality; the targets were raised generally after discussion with section and team heads and applied from July 2016, and 60 to 70 per cent of workers still earned them; Mr L withheld cooperation and refused meetings, and the USD 10 within his USD 13 incentive was a union-leadership allowance that ended with his post. [Para 115][Para 117][Para 122][Para 124][Para 127][Para 130]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel refused the claims of Mr O, Mr P and Mr S, and ordered the employer to pay Mr L his grade bonus of USD 4.5 and skill bonus of USD 5 for June to August 2016, plus USD 3 of the USD 13 incentive; the USD 10 union-leadership portion was refused. [Para 214][Para 215][Para 216][Para 217]

The panel first settled who could appear. Article 54(1) of the Trade Union Law reserves the exclusive right to bargain and to settle collective disputes to a most-representative union, and the panel noted the gap that opens where none exists. [Para 142][Para 143][Para 147][Para 151] Applying Article 19 of Prakas No. 099, it held that workers in an enterprise without a most-representative union may appear in person or transfer their rights to a local union or another person, and that the four claimants had validly transferred their rights and attended. [Para 155][Para 158][Para 159][Para 164][Para 165]

On the merits the panel treated all three bonuses as rewards distinct from basic wage that the Labour Law does not fix, so that setting their conditions falls within the employer's right to manage the enterprise under Article 2, provided it is exercised lawfully and reasonably. [Para 96][Para 171][Para 175][Para 179][Para 181] For the three sewing workers the panel found it agreed on both sides that they did not reach the production targets in the three months, that the targets were set for all workers rather than aimed at them, were discussed with section and team heads, and were met by others in their groups who received the bonuses. [Para 183][Para 185][Para 189] Withholding was therefore a lawful exercise of Article 2 and the claims were refused. [Para 190][Para 191][Para 192][Para 212]

Mr L was treated separately because his role as team leader of the machine and electrical section differed. [Para 182] Here the employer alleged lack of cooperation, refusal to attend meetings and delay in repairing machines, but produced no evidence beyond argument to show he failed the bonus conditions. [Para 206][Para 207] The panel held the party alleging a fact bears the burden of proof and rejected the employer's position, so the withholding was not a lawful exercise of Article 2. [Para 201][Para 208][Para 210] It ordered the grade and skill bonuses and, of the USD 13 incentive, only the USD 3 that was not tied to union office, because the USD 10 was an allowance for his position as leader of the most-representative union and ceased when he left it. [Para 198][Para 199][Para 200][Para 213]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A bonus that the Labour Law does not require is set by the employer under its Article 2 power to manage the enterprise, so the panel will not disturb its withholding where the criteria were fixed in advance, applied to the whole workforce, discussed with supervisors, and met by other workers doing the same job. [Para 171][Para 181][Para 185][Para 189] But where the employer justifies withholding by misconduct, it must prove that misconduct: an unproven allegation leaves the withholding outside the lawful exercise of Article 2, and the bonus becomes payable. [Para 201][Para 206][Para 208][Para 210] Where no most-representative union exists, workers may bring a collective dispute themselves or by transferring their rights in writing. [Para 155][Para 159]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the absence of a most-representative union can obstruct workers' representation and may affect the constitutional right to complain and claim compensation under Article 39 of the Constitution — an observation that did not decide anything, since the claimants had standing anyway. [Para 152][Para 153] It also noted that the employer's unconditioned payment of the USD 10 was not disputed. [Para 197]
