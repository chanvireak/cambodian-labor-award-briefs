# Award No. 204/16 — Vonammy (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 13 September 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Hem Hounarith (employer-appointed), Tun Si Phan (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chea (chair) · Doc ID 139102

## 1. Facts
Vonammy (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd, a garment factory at Phnom Touch, Bati, Takeo, told the panel it employed about 600 to 700 workers and that six unions existed there, none of them most representative [Para 59][Para 60][Para 62]. The Khmer Youth Union of the company was registered with the Ministry by certificate No. 3318 of 20 July 2015, with Ms S M as president, Ms R S as vice-president and Ms S T as secretary [Para 61]. Of seven points raised, six were settled in conciliation and one remained: whether workers on sick leave with a valid medical certificate keep their wages and bonuses [Para 1][Para 8][Para 9]. The union is not most representative, but the panel found it entitled to represent its own members and 80 workers who had transferred authority to the Khmer Youth Federation [Para 105][Para 110][Para 112].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must keep wages according to the actual number of days written in a valid medical certificate, and keep the regular attendance bonus in full rather than deducting it pro rata, for workers absent on sick leave? [Para 113][Para 148]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** a worker cannot know in advance how many days of sick leave the doctor will order, so the employer should pay wages for the actual days on the certificate rather than cap them at three days a month; the Labour Law requires wages to be kept during sick leave [Para 68][Para 69][Para 70]. They also ask that the attendance bonus not be cut pro rata during certified sick leave [Para 150].
**Employer (respondent):** it has applied a practice of keeping wages for only three days per month since the factory opened in mid-August 2014, the practice was announced to the workers, and its finances do not allow more; the internal regulations do not deal with sick-leave pay [Para 71][Para 72][Para 73][Para 74].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to keep full wages for the first month of certified sick leave and 60 per cent in the second and third months; the claim to a full, undeducted attendance bonus was dismissed as an interest dispute [Para 172][Para 173].

On wages the panel applied Article 71(3) and Article 72(1) of the Labour Law: absence certified by an official doctor suspends the employment contract, the worker need not work and the employer need not pay, "unless there is a contrary provision" requiring wages to be paid [Para 113][Para 115][Para 116][Para 118]. It found such a provision in Article 4 of the company's internal regulations: full wages for the first month of certified illness, 60 per cent in months two and three, no wages from month four to six but retention of the post [Para 120][Para 121][Para 122][Para 140]. The employer's daily practice of keeping only three days per month was therefore contrary to its own regulations and deprived workers of a benefit, so the regulations govern [Para 145][Para 146]. Because Article 4 fixes entitlement by month rather than by the days on the certificate, the panel could not read it as requiring payment for every day the doctor ordered; the order follows the regulation's own scale [Para 141][Para 142][Para 147].

On the attendance bonus the panel found no statute, collective agreement or agreement between the parties requiring the bonus to be paid in full during certified sick leave, so the claim was "more than the law provides" and an interest dispute [Para 148][Para 151][Para 152]. Under Article 96(2) of the Labour Law, Article 54 of the Law on Trade Unions and Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099, an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement and binds all workers for a year, so only a most-representative union may bring one [Para 153][Para 162][Para 164]. This union is not most representative, so the claim was not examined [Para 169][Para 170][Para 171].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Certified sick leave suspends the employment contract under Article 71(3) of the Labour Law, and the employer owes no wages unless a contrary provision applies. Where the enterprise's internal regulations themselves provide sick-leave pay — full wages for the first month, 60 per cent for the second and third — that regulation is the applicable provision, and an established practice of paying less (three days per month) cannot displace it. The regulation's own scale governs, not the number of days written on the medical certificate. A claim to keep a discretionary attendance bonus undeducted during sick leave is an interest dispute and may be arbitrated only by a most-representative union. [Para 116][Para 118][Para 140][Para 145][Para 146][Para 164]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's direction that an employer whose sick-leave clause is meant to cover only long illness should say so expressly, to avoid divergent interpretations, is guidance not required by the order [Para 137].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The conciliation record is dated three different ways: 27 August 2016 in the procedure [Para 8], 17 August 2016 where the report is identified [Para 4][Para 9], 12 August 2016 in the employer's evidence list [Para 40] and 27 July 2016 in the Ministry's list [Para 54].
- The deadline for objecting to documents (8 August 2016) is set earlier than the deadline for filing them (5 September 2016), which cannot be right [Para 16].
- The internal regulation is cited as Article 4 at [Para 75] and [Para 120] but as "Article 44" at [Para 146]; its text is printed twice, in nearly identical terms [Para 75][Para 120].
- Worker numbers conflict: 80 workers transferred authority at [Para 105] and [Para 110] but 83 at [Para 111]; the workforce is only an employer estimate of "about 600 to 700" [Para 60].
- The legislative citation at [Para 165] is badly corrupted and intrudes an unrelated personal name into the text of the Prakas; it was disregarded.
- Prior awards are cited as "188/19" at [Para 85] and "188/16" at [Para 94] for what appears to be the same case.
- The employer-side arbitrator is printed "ហែម ហូណារិទ្ធ" on the face and "ហែម ហួ ណារិទ្ធ" in the signature block, and the worker-side arbitrator's name is missing from that block altogether [Para 1][Para 175].
