# Award No. 218/16 — Bouv Kher Gameen Factory (Cambodia) Com Peni Ltd (Arbitration Council, 11 October 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouchhun, employer-nominated; Tun Si Phan, worker-nominated; Tang Tri, president) · Doc ID 139371

## 1. Facts
The company operates a garment factory at Pong Tuek, Baek Chan, Ang Snuol, Kandal, employing about 3,125 workers [Para 53][Para 54]. Three workers — two in pressing (Rang Thana, Long Pich) and one in sewing (Peang Nhou) — were dismissed on 30 July 2016 for exchanging counted bundles of finished garments between themselves on 26 and 27 July 2016, so that one would be paid piece rates above the basic wage while the other received only the basic wage; all three admitted the conduct [Para 63][Para 64][Para 69]. The factory union, registered on 29 April 2015 with recognised leaders, transferred five members' rights to the Cambodia Union Confederation and filed nine claims; four remained unresolved after conciliation and the parties settled the third at the hearing, leaving three for adjudication [Para 55][Para 56][Para 19][Para 20]. No union at the factory holds most-representative status [Para 58][Para 113].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back and reinstate the three dismissed workers? [Para 131]
2. Whether the employer must give workers a payslip at least one day before payday, itemised by wage and by piece-rate output? [Para 236]
4. Whether the employer must issue a separate, itemised statement of annual-leave compensation distinct from the monthly payslip, while the workers are still employed? [Para 256]

Dispute 3, on removing an administrative officer, was resolved by agreement at the hearing and is not adjudicated [Para 97][Para 98].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The three should be reinstated because the new internal rules were never agreed by the worker representatives — only 17 of the 20 representatives fingerprinted them, the three union leaders refusing — and the old rules required three warnings before dismissal; the sums gained were trivial and the conduct a minor breach [Para 3][Para 4][Para 72][Para 73][Para 81]. They also sought payslips one day early so wages could be checked and corrected on payday itself, and a stand-alone annual-leave statement so each worker could see the exact amount and avoid loss [Para 89][Para 102].

**Employer (respondent):** The conduct was serious misconduct — falsifying piece-rate counts to take the company's money — under Article 83 of the Labour Law, clause 10 of the internal rules and clause 5 of each contract, all of which permit immediate dismissal; the new rules were properly consulted on and endorsed by 17 of 20 representatives and visaed by the Kandal labour department, so the old rules no longer applied [Para 75][Para 76][Para 82][Para 84]. It could not issue payslips before payday because it has too few accountants and it would disrupt the production line, and the law imposes no such duty [Para 92][Para 93][Para 96].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three claims were rejected: reinstatement of the three workers, pre-payday itemised payslips, and a separate annual-leave compensation statement [Para 271][Para 272].

On jurisdiction, the employer argued this was an individual dispute about each worker's misconduct [Para 143]. The panel applied Article 302 of the Labour Law and the three-condition test in Award 10/03 — Zaksintex, finding that the dispute lay between a union (with its members) and the employer, concerned the employment relationship, and could provoke a strike affecting the factory and social peace, so it was collective and within jurisdiction [Para 145][Para 147][Para 158][Para 161].

On contract type, Article 67(2) caps fixed-duration contracts at two years and converts any arrangement exceeding that into an indefinite-duration contract, a rule the panel read together with Article 73(5) and ILO Recommendation 166 [Para 162][Para 169][Para 172]. Rang Thana (from 21 December 2015) and Long Pich (from 1 October 2014) held successive three-month contracts whose total, up to 31 August 2016, did not exceed two years, so they remained fixed-duration; Peang Nhou, employed since 20 December 2013, had renewed past two years and therefore held an indefinite-duration contract [Para 174][Para 178][Para 182][Para 184][Para 186].

On misconduct, Article 83(b)(1) lists theft, misappropriation and fraud as serious misconduct, and clause 10 of the internal rules and clause 5 of the contracts repeat that such conduct permits immediate dismissal [Para 188][Para 190][Para 194]. The panel found the swapping of counted bundles to inflate piece-rate pay was precisely a fraud on the company's money, and that all three had admitted it [Para 204][Para 205]. It rejected the challenge to the new internal rules: they were visaed by the Kandal labour department on 1 April 2016 as Article 24 requires, were approved by 17 of 20 employee representatives, and no law demands unanimity, so they governed from 1 April 2016 [Para 199][Para 200][Para 201][Para 202].

For the two fixed-duration workers, Article 73(1) and (2) allow early termination only by written agreement before the labour inspector or, absent agreement, only for serious misconduct or force majeure; serious misconduct being established, the employer could terminate [Para 207][Para 209][Para 211][Para 213]. For Peang Nhou, on an indefinite-duration contract, Article 74 requires a valid reason, which his admitted fraud supplied [Para 223][Para 225][Para 230]. On timing, Article 26(2) forfeits the right to dismiss if the employer does not act within seven days of learning of the misconduct; the acts occurred on 26–27 July and the dismissal on 30 July 2016, inside the period [Para 214][Para 219][Para 220][Para 232].

On dispute 2, Article 112 requires the employer to inform workers clearly and understandably of all wage components for each pay period, but sets no deadline before payday; following Award 05/06, payslips issued on payday with a right to object and have errors corrected satisfy it [Para 236][Para 238][Para 246][Para 254].

On dispute 4, Article 167(2) makes annual-leave compensation payable on termination before leave is taken, and Article 167(3) voids any agreement to forgo annual leave; the panel held compensation may be paid only when the employment relationship ends, so a separate statement during employment has no legal basis [Para 256][Para 258][Para 260][Para 269]. It added that paying such compensation twice a year alongside monthly wages is itself contrary to Article 167, though the claim before it was only for a separate statement [Para 266][Para 268].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a union brings a reinstatement claim for members dismissed for conduct that all admit, the panel has jurisdiction as a collective dispute once the Article 302 conditions are met, but reinstatement fails if the conduct amounts to fraud on the employer under Article 83(b)(1) and the dismissal issued within the seven days allowed by Article 26(2); fixed-duration contracts renewed within the two-year ceiling of Article 67(2) stay fixed and may be cut short for serious misconduct under Article 73, while renewal beyond two years converts them to indefinite-duration contracts judged under Article 74. [Para 161][Para 205][Para 220][Para 178][Para 184]

**Obiter dicta:** Article 112 does not oblige an employer to hand out payslips before payday; itemisation on the day, with a right of correction, satisfies it [Para 246]. An employer's practice of paying annual-leave compensation twice a year with monthly wages runs against Article 167 even though no claim for repayment was before the panel [Para 268].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR is badly degraded and internally inconsistent. The employer's name appears as Bouv Kher Gameen Factory (Cambodia) Com Peni Ltd, but also as Bouv Kheang, Bouv Kiri and "Bouv Kher Gameen" in shortened form, and its commercial-register number is truncated to "Inv. 1637 E/2012" [Para 25][Para 29][Para 53]. The conciliation report number is printed as 1598/16 in one recital and as "៥៩៨/១៦" in another [Para 10][Para 15]. The number of employee representatives who approved the new internal rules is given as 17 of 20 at Para 200 but as 7 at Para 195, a discrepancy the panel does not address. Rang Thana's start date is 21 January 2015 at Para 59 and 21 December 2015 at Para 174. Peang Nhou's employee number is 5384 at Para 271 but "ICC043" on the face of the claim [Para 2]. The dismissal date for Peang Nhou is printed as 30 July 2023 with 2016 alongside [Para 231]. Dispute numbering skips from 2 to 4 in the operative part, following the withdrawal of dispute 3, and the reasoning at Paras 262–265 repeats itself where text was lost.
