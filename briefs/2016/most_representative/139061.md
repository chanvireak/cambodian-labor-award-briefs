# Award No. 159/16 — Pacific Apparel Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 3 August 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed, dissenting; Liv Sovannara, worker-appointed; Ang Eng Thong, President) · Doc ID 139061

## 1. Facts
Pacific Apparel Co., Ltd is a garment cut-and-sew factory employing approximately 1,050 workers, the claimant workers having transferred their rights to three federations of Khmer workers' unions (180 workers signed the transfer of rights of 28 June 2016) [Para 41][Para 45][Para 47]. Of 15 demands conciliated on 28 June 2016, 13 were settled and two remained: the pro-rata deduction of the USD 10 regular attendance bonus for days of leave taken with the employer's permission, and the overtime meal allowance of 2,000 riel [Para 13][Para 5][Para 6]. The panel heard the case with both sides present and issued an award with a dissenting opinion annexed by the employer-appointed arbitrator [Para 17][Para 142].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to deduct the regular attendance bonus pro rata to the number of days the worker took leave for personal reasons with the employer's permission (the demand numbered 1(4))? [Para 63]
2. Whether the employer must pay an overtime meal allowance of 2,000 riel per hour, and pay it once a week, for overtime worked from 16:00 to 18:00 (the demand numbered 2(10))? [Para 102][Para 56]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer pays a USD 10 monthly attendance bonus, but a worker who takes three days of permitted leave in a month loses the whole USD 10, even though the worker has worked hard all month; the deduction should be proportional to the days taken [Para 48][Para 49][Para 52]. On meals, 2,000 riel per day is too little, since one meal costs 3,000 to 5,000 riel, and other companies pay 2,000 riel per hour plus a can of Coca-Cola [Para 58][Para 59].

**Employer (respondent):** The attendance bonus is by definition for workers who attend regularly without absence, as the Ministry's notification provides, so a worker who is absent is not entitled to it; the employer has already shown leniency by deducting it only progressively [Para 55]. On meals, the employer already provides a 2,000 riel lunch allowance daily and cannot also pay weekly because it is busy administering the weekly target bonus [Para 61].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Partly granted — the employer was ordered to deduct the regular attendance bonus pro rata to the number of days of leave taken with the employer's permission, and to pay the overtime meal allowance once a week; the claim for 2,000 riel per hour was refused consideration [Para 138][Para 139][Para 140].

On issue 1(4), the panel traced the bonus through Notification No. 041 of 07 March 2011 (minimum USD 7) and its replacement by Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012, point 2 of which raised the minimum to USD 10 and added the words "without absence" [Para 65][Para 67][Para 68]. It held that the USD 10 attendance bonus is not a discretionary bonus within the employer's gift but a legal rule of general application binding all employers in textiles, garments and footwear, and that Article 103 of the Labour Law makes a sum paid as a bonus part of wages [Para 70][Para 92][Para 93][Para 94]. The difficulty was the phrase "without absence": the Labour Advisory Committee had not explained whether it covers lawful absence, and the panel declined to presume that it covers both absence with and without permission [Para 73][Para 74]. It separated absences the law itself authorises — annual leave, special leave, public holidays — for which no deduction may be made and the worker receives the full USD 10, from absences for personal reasons permitted by the employer, which the law does not enumerate [Para 75][Para 76][Para 77]. For the latter it held that leave granted at the worker's request and agreed by the employer is a suspension of the contract under Article 71(6) and Article 72(1), so no wage is due for those days and therefore no attendance bonus either; the employer may not forfeit the bonus entirely but may reduce it only in proportion to the days taken [Para 95][Para 98][Para 99][Para 100][Para 101].

On issue 2(10), the panel found that Notification 041 point 2 fixes 2,000 riel per day of overtime or one free meal, and that no law, collective agreement, internal rule, contract or past practice entitles a worker to 2,000 riel per hour; the claim therefore exceeded what the law requires and was a dispute of interest rather than of rights [Para 104][Para 106][Para 107]. Under Article 96(2) of the Labour Law, Article 54 of the Trade Union Law and Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099, an interest dispute may only be brought by the most representative union, and the three unions here had held elections on 01 July 2016 but had not yet registered with the Ministry [Para 117][Para 119][Para 122]. The panel accordingly declined to consider the hourly claim [Para 123]. It nevertheless accepted that the daily meal allowance must actually reach the worker, and since meals are a daily necessity it ordered payment once a week rather than fortnightly or monthly [Para 125][Para 126][Para 136][Para 137].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A Ministry-fixed attendance bonus is part of wages, so absence the law itself authorises (annual leave, special leave, public holidays) may never reduce it; but where leave is granted at the worker's request with the employer's permission, the contract is suspended under Articles 71(6) and 72(1), no wage is earned for those days, and the employer may reduce the bonus pro rata to the days taken but may not forfeit it in full [Para 76][Para 98][Para 100]. A claim for a meal allowance above the notified rate is an interest dispute, which the panel will not hear unless it is brought by the most representative union in the enterprise [Para 107][Para 119].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that paying the bonus in full while the worker is on unpaid permitted leave would be unfair to the employer, since the worker did not come to work [Para 91]; that meals are a daily necessity every worker must be able to afford, and that overtime meal money should be paid in a cycle that lets the worker buy food when working overtime [Para 126][Para 129]; and that the meaning of "without absence" remains unclarified by the Labour Advisory Committee [Para 73][Para 74].

**Dissenting opinion (Ing Sothy):** The employer-appointed arbitrator recorded a dissent to the reasoning on issue 1(4), distinguishing "ប្រាក់រង្វាន់" (a conditional bonus, which he says is not wages) from "ប្រាក់ដែលបើកជារង្វាន់" (a bonus paid as of right, which is wages and cannot be apportioned), and concluding that a pro-rata deduction destroys the character of the bonus [Para 145][Para 148][Para 152][Para 155].
