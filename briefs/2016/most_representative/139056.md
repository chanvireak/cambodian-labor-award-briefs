# Award No. 151/16 — Yang In Neour (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 July 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (In Sothy, appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna, appointed by the workers; Aing Eng Tong, president) · Doc ID 139056

## 1. Facts
A garment factory making women's blouses, employing about 48 workers, faced a demand from the Khmer Workers' Consciousness Union Federation for improved conditions [Para 34][Para 35]. Twelve points went to conciliation on 11 June 2016 and nine were settled; the three remaining were sent to the Arbitration Council on 27 June 2016, and two of those were settled at the hearing itself, leaving only the travel-and-accommodation allowance [Para 9][Para 10][Para 11][Para 15][Para 16]. The employer already pays USD 7 per month, the figure set by Notification No. 230 and repeated in a written agreement with 44 workers dated 2 July 2016; the workers sought USD 10 [Para 42][Para 48][Para 60].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to pay a travel and accommodation allowance of USD 10 per month instead of the USD 7 it currently pays? [Para 42][Para 49]
   (This is the panel's issue 2 of the original twelve; issues 1 and 3 were settled and the rest were not pursued.) [Para 15][Para 16]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The USD 7 allowance is inadequate because workers pay USD 30 to USD 40 a month in rent and market prices have risen, so the allowance should be raised to USD 10 [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45].
**Employer (respondent):** The company already pays more than the law requires — the USD 7 allowance plus benefits such as a 2,000 riel midday meal allowance — and it maintains the USD 7 rate [Para 46][Para 47].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider the claim and made no order for payment; the USD 7 allowance stands [Para 82].

Before the merits the panel examined who was properly before it. Under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 Sakbey of 21 April 2004 a party may appear in person or be represented by a lawyer or by any other person holding written authority from that party [Para 49][Para 50]. Thirty workers had transferred their rights to the federation president on 13 June 2016, but the president did not appear; the federation's administration chief appeared instead and was ordered to file an instrument of appointment from the president [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53]. No such document was filed by the evidence deadline [Para 54][Para 56]. The panel therefore held that only the person holding the written transfer may represent the workers, that the officer present held no such authority, and that the four workers who attended in person were the complainants [Para 55][Para 57][Para 59].

On the substance the panel found that Article 1 of Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 requires a travel and accommodation allowance of USD 7 per month, that the 2 July 2016 agreement with 44 workers likewise records USD 7, and that the actual practice is USD 7 [Para 60][Para 61][Para 62]. No provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, contract, agreement or past practice obliges the employer to pay USD 10 [Para 64]. A claim above the statutory floor is therefore an interest dispute [Para 65]. Under Article 96(2) of the Labour Law, Article 54 of the Trade Union Law of 17 May 2016 and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099, an interest-dispute award substitutes for a collective agreement and binds all workers in the enterprise, so the panel will decide such a dispute only where it is brought by the most representative union [Para 66][Para 69][Para 70][Para 74][Para 76]. The complainants here were individual workers, not the most representative union, so the panel declined to consider the claim [Para 79][Para 80].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim for a benefit exceeding the minimum fixed by the Labour Law or by Notification No. 230 is an interest dispute, which the panel will only decide when it is brought by the union recognised as most representative in the enterprise; individual workers, or a body that has not filed the written authority required by Article 19 of Prakas No. 099, cannot put such a claim before the panel [Para 65][Para 76][Para 79].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's explanation that an interest-dispute award operates as a collective agreement for one year and removes the workers' right to conclude a collective agreement or to strike on that subject in future [Para 74][Para 75].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The number of demands is printed as twelve at the intake stage and as eleven in the federation's covering letter [Para 9][Para 24]. The date of the panel's constitution reads "២០២៣ ២០១៦" [Para 12], and the deadline for objecting to documents is given as 13 June 2016 although the deadline for filing them is 11 July 2016 [Para 18]. The presiding arbitrator is named Aing Eng Phong in the header and Aing Eng Tong at the signature block [Para 1][Para 84]. Worker counts vary across the file — about 48 employed, 30 transfers, 44 signatories to the 2 July 2016 agreement — and the figures are reproduced as printed [Para 35][Para 37][Para 48]. Individual workers, union officials and the employer's representatives are redacted in this brief; the employer's representative is named in the source with a variant spelling of her personal name [Para 36]. The Secretariat's telephone and email lines at the head of the document are corrupted [Para 2].
