# Award No. 139/16 — Cambodia Unisole Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 11 July 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, chosen by the employer party; An Nan, chosen by the worker party; Pen Bun Chea, chair chosen by the two) · Doc ID 139047

## 1. Facts
Cambodia Unisole Co., Ltd, a garment factory at Prey Chas village, Khsach Kandal district, Kandal province, employed about 1,800 workers. [Para 36][Para 37] It pays sewing workers a weekly target bonus by group: 7,000 riel where output reaches 90% of the quota set, 12,000 riel at 100%, and 22,000 riel at 120%. [Para 47] Machine mechanics each service two sewing teams, receive 3,000, 8,000 or 18,000 riel on the same thresholds, and receive the bonus twice over where both their teams qualify. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60][Para 84] Seven demands were referred; four were settled before the hearing (overtime, lunch allowance for late arrival, water coolers, and opening the gate early) and the demand to remove two named staff was withdrawn, leaving two. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute
The panel's own numbering is kept:
3. Whether the employer must pay the target bonus daily rather than weekly to sewing workers, and raise the bonus whenever it raises the production quota? [Para 67]
5. Whether the employer must raise the target bonus of the machine mechanics to the level of the sewing workers' bonus? [Para 86]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** a worker who meets the quota on four days but not on the last two loses the whole weekly bonus, and a change of garment model in the final days makes the quota unattainable; the bonus should therefore be calculated by the day, and raised whenever the quota is raised. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51][Para 52] Mechanics carry a heavy load, servicing two teams each and stripping and rebuilding machines alone at every model change, and their bonus is always 4,000 riel below the sewers'. [Para 62][Para 64][Para 85]
**Employer (respondent):** the weekly target bonus is an incentive only, on top of the monthly wage, and cannot be converted into a daily scheme; quotas are deliberately set low at first and raised as workers gain skill, so that both the workers' bonus and the company's profit are balanced. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 56][Para 57][Para 77] The bonus structure for mechanics is fixed in the company's internal system and will not be changed. [Para 65][Para 66]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both remaining demands were refused, and the panel declined to consider the mechanics' claim at all for want of a properly qualified claimant. [Para 107][Para 108]

Issue 3. Article 2(2) of the Labour Law gives the employer the right to organise and manage the enterprise under its own direction. [Para 67][Para 69] Following Award No. 24/05 (Wer Well), the panel held that an employer may by unilateral decision establish bonus schemes beyond those the Labour Law requires, in order to encourage greater and faster production, and such a scheme does not contravene the Law or infringe any right of the workers. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74] The weekly target bonus is more favourable than the statutory minimum, and the panel found nothing contrary to the spirit of the Labour Law in paying it weekly rather than daily. [Para 78] It also accepted that setting quotas from low to high, so as to balance the workers' bonus against the employer's profit as skill with a new model develops, is reasonable management. [Para 79][Para 80] The demand was therefore refused. [Para 81][Para 107]

Issue 5. The panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, contract, agreement or past practice obliging the employer to raise the mechanics' target bonus to the sewers' level, so the demand was for a benefit above the law — an interest dispute. [Para 86][Para 87] For an interest dispute, Article 96(2) of the Labour Law and Article 54 of the 2016 Trade Union Law reserve the right to the most representative union, and Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099 provides that an award on such a dispute replaces the collective agreement for one year and extinguishes the right to strike over the same interest. [Para 88][Para 92][Para 99][Para 100] The panel found that the claimants were not the most representative union, nor a union with the legal standing to bring an interest dispute, and declined to consider the demand. [Para 104][Para 105][Para 106][Para 108]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may by unilateral decision set an incentive bonus scheme above the statutory requirements, on whatever period and thresholds it chooses, and the panel will not replace it with the scheme the workers prefer: the choice falls within the management right in Article 2(2) so long as it is lawful and reasonable. A demand to raise one group's bonus to match another's, where no law, agreement or practice requires it, is an interest dispute, which individual workers cannot bring — it belongs to the most representative union alone. [Para 72][Para 78][Para 87][Para 104]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that a quota rising from low to high, balancing worker bonus against employer profit, is reasonable, and its remarks on the effect of an interest award in extinguishing the right to strike, are general observations not necessary to a dismissal for want of jurisdiction. [Para 79][Para 100]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The demand concerns 11 mechanics at [Para 61] but the panel records 10 at [Para 82]; neither figure is reconciled with the 1,800-strong workforce.
- The union's elected leadership is recorded as Mam Sambath, Chin Nen and Bhok Pheara in the election notice [Para 43], but as Mam Sambath, Yim Thea and Chin Nen in the Ministry's recognition of term 2 [Para 39]; the panel relies on the latter without noting the difference.
- The conciliation report is dated 16 June 2016 at [Para 11] and 15 June 2016 at [Para 16], and the panel is said to have been constituted on "21 June 2022 2016" [Para 17]; the hearing is recorded at "02 and 00" in the afternoon [Para 18] although the hearing date is given as 14:00 [Para 13].
- Conciliation failed because the worker party did not attend [Para 15], yet the award records both parties present at the hearing [Para 19].
- The workers relied on a prior award in a case named only "Ei Garment" with no case number given [Para 53].
- The claimant is described as three individual workers at [Para 46], but the representativeness test applied at [Para 104] is one that only a union can satisfy; the award does not explain how an individual claimant could ever meet it.
