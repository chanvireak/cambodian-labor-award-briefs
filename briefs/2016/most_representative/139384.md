# Award No. 230/16 — Va Long (Cambodia) Outsourcing Industry (Arbitration Council, 24 November 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of three — Seng Vouch Hun (employer-appointed), Tuon Si Phan (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (chair) · Doc ID 139384

## 1. Facts
The employer runs a shoe factory at Cheung Prey, Kampong Cham, with about 503 workers [Para 37][Para 38]. The Union of Workers for Development filed eight demands with the provincial labour department on 11 October 2016; conciliation failed because the employer never appeared, and the file reached the Secretariat on 28 October 2016 [Para 20][Para 22]. The employer also stayed away from the hearing of 11 November 2016, at which three union leaders attended [Para 25].

## 2. Issues in dispute
Eight demands were filed; the workers withdrew issues 1 and 8 at the hearing, leaving six, numbered as the panel kept them [Para 26][Para 27].
2. Whether the employer must pay holiday and weekly-rest-day overtime wages, and the overtime meal allowance, once a week? [Para 43]
3. Whether the employer must move the working hours from 07:00–12:00 and 13:00–16:00 to 07:00–11:00 and 12:00–16:00? [Para 50]
4. Whether the employer must stop requiring 1.5 days of annual leave to be used up each month and cancelling the remainder, and pay the unused balance at year end? [Para 56]
5. Whether the employer must provide transport for workers doing overtime on holidays and weekly rest days, and for night-shift workers leaving at 04:00? [Para 60]
6. Whether the employer must keep a company vehicle and driver to take workers to hospital when accidents occur? [Para 70]
7. Whether the employer must grant Ms P two further months of sick leave, October to November, after her 90-day maternity leave? [Para 76]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the union holds a registration certificate from the Ministry and 281 members, and its three leaders attended [Para 40][Para 41]. Monthly lump payment of overtime and meal money caused losses and left workers short of cash, so both should be paid weekly, as neighbouring factories do [Para 46][Para 49]. The 07:00–12:00 shift makes half-day leave impossible because Chinese supervisors refuse to sign the exit slip until noon [Para 53].
**Employer (respondent):** absent from conciliation and from the hearing, filing nothing; the panel treated this as a waiver of its right of reply [Para 21][Para 126].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim for weekly holiday/rest-day overtime pay is dismissed; the employer is ordered to pay the overtime meal allowance weekly; it must stop forcing 1.5 days of annual leave per month and cancelling the remainder; the working-hours, transport, vehicle and Ms P claims are dismissed [Para 235][Para 239].

Two threshold points came first. The hearing proceeded in the employer's absence under Article 21 of Prakas 099, service having been verified by email and telephone [Para 89]. On representation, the panel found no most-representative union at the factory and adopted Award 180/16, that where none exists workers may represent themselves [Para 95][Para 97]; but because no registration certificate or leadership acknowledgment was produced, Article 11 of the Trade Union Law left the three attendees representing only themselves [Para 109][Para 112].

On issue 2, Article 116 requires wages to be paid at least twice a month, and Article 103 makes overtime pay part of wages, so holiday and Sunday overtime can be discharged with the monthly wage, following Award 146/14 [Para 115][Para 124]. The weekly-payment demand therefore had no legal basis [Para 131]. The meal allowance is different: Notification 041/11 fixes 2,000 riel per day or a free meal but no payment date, so weekly payment was ordered to let workers buy food when working overtime [Para 132][Para 137].

On issue 3, the existing schedule totals eight hours a day, within Article 137, and is a lawful exercise of managerial authority; the real complaint was the exit-signature practice, which the panel urged the employer to ease but held did not make the schedule unlawful [Para 156][Para 161][Para 163].

On issue 4, Article 166 gives 1.5 days per month of service and Article 167(4) permits carrying leave forward, so compelling monthly consumption and deleting the balance contravened the law; the employer was ordered to stop and to preserve unused leave [Para 164][Para 181][Para 183]. Payment in lieu while the contract subsists is barred by Article 167(3), so that part was dismissed [Para 189][Para 201].

Issues 5 and 6 were dismissed without consideration of the merits: no specific claimant was identified, and the demands exceed the law and raise an interest dispute that only a most-representative union may bring [Para 203][Para 207][Para 227]. Issue 7 fell with the representation finding, the three attendees having no standing to act for Ms P [Para 231].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Holiday and weekly-rest-day overtime pay forms part of "wage" and may be paid with the monthly wage [Para 120][Para 122]; the Labour Law prescribes no weekly cycle, so a demand for weekly payment of it fails [Para 129][Para 131]. The overtime meal allowance under Notification 041/11 carries no statutory payment date, so a panel may order it paid weekly [Para 132][Para 137]. Annual leave accrues at 1.5 days per month of service and may be carried forward [Para 164][Para 181]; an employer may neither compel its monthly consumption nor cancel the unused balance, and may pay it out only when the contract ends [Para 183][Para 189].

**Obiter dicta:** an absent employer is taken to have waived its right of reply [Para 126]; and where no union is most-representative, workers may appear for themselves, but only for themselves [Para 97][Para 112].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's Khmer name is garbled in the OCR (អៅសូល អ៊ិន ដាស ស្ទ្រី) and the headcount appears as "៥០៣ ខែ នាក់" with a stray month word [Para 38]. The operative order prints the leave figure as "2 01 ថ្ងៃ កន្លះ" where the reasons say 1.5 days [Para 183][Para 238]. The dispositive text at paras 235–243 is heavily corrupted and repeats phrases, so the outcome above is taken from the reasoning sections. The award records the withdrawal of issues 1 and 8 without stating the workers' reasons [Para 26].
