# Award No. 329/15 — Vai Sin 2 (Orange Trading (Cam) Co., Ltd) (Arbitration Council, 14 January 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chhea presiding; Yu Soun Ty, employer nominee; An Nan, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139495

## 1. Facts
Vai Sin 2, registered as Orange Trading (Cam) Co., Ltd, knits sweaters at Kampong Thkol village, Sampeou Poun commune, Sa'ang district, Kandal province, and employed 306 workers, some 90 percent of them women. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 41] The Khmer Youth Voice Union Federation, which had organised a local union on 15 November 2015 that was not yet registered with the Ministry, took up the claims of fourteen workers under an authority of 14 November 2015. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 39] Conciliation of twelve demands on 7 December 2015 failed, and report No. 2267/15 reached the Council on 21 December 2015. [Para 20][Para 21] The employer did not attend the hearing of 29 December 2015 despite service of invitation No. 1666 at its premises on 23 December 2015, so the panel proceeded in its absence under Article 21 of Prakas 099. [Para 18][Para 24][Para 81][Para 85][Para 86]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must provide a nursery and breastfeeding room, or pay USD 25 a month to mothers who place their children elsewhere? [Para 3][Para 40]
2. Whether the company must keep a doctor present throughout working hours? [Para 4][Para 43]
3. Whether the company must pay Sunday overtime at the (1x2+1) rate plus a 4,000 riel meal allowance? [Para 5][Para 46]
4. Whether the company must send every worker for a fitness and health examination at the labour health department at its own cost? [Para 6][Para 49]
5. Whether the company must deduct 2,000 riel monthly from union members as dues for the federation? [Para 7][Para 51]
6. Whether the company must top up piece-rate workers' pay to the base wage when their output falls short? [Para 8][Para 53]
7. Whether the company must keep full wages and bonuses during sick leave certified by a doctor? [Para 9][Para 60]
8. Whether the company must organise overtime on a voluntary basis without compulsion? [Para 10][Para 66]
9. Whether the company must allow pregnant workers one paid day a month for antenatal checks? [Para 11][Para 70]
10. Whether the company must hold an election of worker delegates? [Para 12][Para 72]
11. Whether the company must comply with the Kandal labour department's conciliation minutes of 14 July 2015? (withdrawn.) [Para 13][Para 25]
12. Whether the company must pay an attendance bonus of USD 15, deducting only pro rata for authorised personal leave? [Para 14][Para 74]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company had no nursery or breastfeeding room though most workers were women half with children aged two or three; no infirmary or doctor; Sunday overtime was not paid at double rate and no meal allowance was given; no worker had ever been sent for a fitness examination; piece-rate workers took home only USD 60 to 80 a month against a base wage of USD 128; and supervisors blocked the gate to force overtime, threatening dismissal. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 46][Para 49][Para 55][Para 67][Para 224]
**Employer (respondent):** no position is recorded; the company was absent and the panel treated it as having abandoned its right to answer, while noting that an emergency first-aid box and some medicines had recently been provided.[Para 96][Para 112]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered a nursery and breastfeeding room, an infirmary with one nurse and a doctor for two hours a day, pre-employment health examinations at the employer's cost, voluntary overtime, an election of worker delegates, and pro-rata attendance-bonus deductions; it refused the USD 25 allowance, the Sunday overtime and meal claim, the union dues check-off, the base-wage top-up, full sick pay and the paid antenatal day. [Para 288][Para 291][Para 297]
On the nursery, Article 186 requires any enterprise employing at least one hundred women to provide a breastfeeding room and a nursery, and where it cannot take children over eighteen months it must pay the care costs of an outside nursery on actual invoices; the claimed flat USD 25 was refused because no law fixes such a sum and no invoices were produced. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 99][Para 100]
The infirmary followed Articles 239 and 242 and Article 3 of Prakas No. 330, whose staffing table requires one nurse and a doctor for two hours a day at an establishment of 306 workers. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 115][Para 116]
The health examination was ordered under Article 247 and Articles 1, 3, 5 and 7 of Prakas No. 09 SKA/94, which make a pre-employment fitness check compulsory and its cost the employer's, whatever the timing. [Para 123][Para 126][Para 130][Para 131][Para 138]
Three claims failed for want of evidence or jurisdiction. The Sunday overtime and meal allowance failed because the workers never identified the workers affected, the months or the amounts, and filed no payslips by the deadline. [Para 117][Para 118][Para 120][Para 122] The base-wage top-up failed on the same ground, the panel holding that a claimant must show the amount miscalculated, the month and the supporting proof, and that the employer's duty to prove payment arises only once the worker has done so. [Para 159][Para 164][Para 165][Para 167]
Union dues failed as a future dispute: the local union had no registration certificate, and the panel held it has no jurisdiction over a problem that has not yet arisen, since the check-off could only be claimed once the union was lawfully registered. [Para 141][Para 145][Para 146][Para 148][Para 149]
Sick pay and the paid antenatal day were treated as interest disputes, which the panel refused to decide because the fourteen complainants were not the most representative union in the enterprise; an award on interests would replace the collective agreement for a year and cost every worker the right to strike for such benefits later. [Para 187][Para 195][Para 196][Para 199][Para 200][Para 234][Para 236]
On sick leave it ordered the attendance bonus deducted only pro rata to the days absent with a proper medical certificate, rather than forfeited entirely, since full forfeiture defeats the purpose of the bonus and pressures sick workers to attend. [Para 207][Para 212][Para 216][Para 217] The same pro-rata rule was applied to authorised personal leave under Notification No. 230.[Para 286][Para 287]
Overtime was ordered on the voluntariness principle in Article 4 of Prakas No. 80: the employer may ask but not compel, and may take no disciplinary measure against a worker who declines. [Para 218][Para 221][Para 227][Para 230]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an enterprise employs at least one hundred women, Article 186 obliges it to provide a nursery and breastfeeding room or, for children over eighteen months, to pay the care costs of an outside nursery against actual invoices; the panel will not substitute a flat monthly sum the law does not fix. A demand whose precondition has not yet occurred — such as a union dues check-off before the union is registered — is a future dispute outside the panel's jurisdiction, and an interest dispute may be decided only at the instance of the most representative union. [Para 99][Para 146][Para 196]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that a worker absent with a doctor's certificate still deserves a proportionate part of the attendance bonus, because total forfeiture for more than three days of illness removes the incentive the bonus exists to create and pushes sick workers back to the line.[Para 210][Para 216]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The attendance bonus is printed as USD 18 a month in the workers' claim, USD 10 in the summary of practice, and USD 100 in the reasons. [Para 66][Para 74][Para 246] Prakas No. 330 of 6 December 2000 is dated "6 December 2019" at one citation, and Prakas No. 80 is dated "25 01 March 1999".[Para 218] The nurse required by the order is printed as "01" in the reasoning and "016" in the order. [Para 115][Para 290] The company appears as "Vai Sin 2 (Orange Trading (Cam) Co., Ltd)" on the face of the award and as "Orange Trading (Cam) Coultd" in the conciliation reference. [Para 1][Para 16] Point 11 alone was withdrawn, yet the panel's list of surviving points is printed with a duplicate and garbled numbering, and the reasons are interleaved with OCR debris that obscures several transitional sentences. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 229]
