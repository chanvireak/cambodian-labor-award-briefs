# Award No. 164/16 — Din Han Enterprise Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 10 August 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ri, employer-appointed; On Non, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, presiding) · Doc ID 139067

## 1. Facts
The Federation of Khmer Workers' Spirit Unions (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ស្មារតី កម្មករ ខ្មែរ) filed a collective dispute on 15 June 2016 seeking improvement of nine conditions at Din Han Enterprise, a garment manufacturer employing about 2,000 of the roughly 4,400 workers across the two companies the same owner runs. Sixty workers transferred their representation rights to the federation; 26 more attended the hearing. The local union had been elected on 24 May 2016 but had not yet received its registration certificate. [Para 15][Para 49][Para 53][Para 55][Para 50]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay monthly wages on the 8th of each month, or on the 7th where the 8th falls on a Sunday or public holiday? [Para 55]
2. Whether the employer must add three more first-aid stretchers, one for every three sewing groups? [Para 65]
3. Whether the employer must give two days' notice before requiring work on a public holiday? [Para 75]
4. Whether the employer must pay 4,000 riel as a meal allowance for overtime from 16:00 to 18:00? [Para 80]
5. Whether the employer must install three more time-card machines? [Para 84]

Points 5, 6 and 9 were settled at the hearing and point 8 was withdrawn, leaving the five above for decision. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 23]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** wages should fall on the 8th so that workers can repay loans, pay rent due on the 10th and travel to their home provinces with their families; one stretcher for 46 sewing groups of about 30 workers each is inadequate when three workers fainted on 17 July 2016 and colleagues had to carry them by hand; two days' notice of holiday work would let them plan; 2,000 riel does not buy a meal; and 11 card machines for 2,000 workers cause long queues. [Para 58][Para 70][Para 77][Para 81][Para 87]
**Employer (respondent):** paying two companies with about 4,400 workers takes from the 1st to the 10th for data collection, calculation, transfer, entry and counting, the 10th is what the employment contracts provide, and payment is brought forward whenever the 10th is a holiday; stretchers are not used in practice and the employer will relocate one to the centre of the sewing floor; holiday work is voluntary; and all workers can exit within about seven minutes. [Para 59][Para 63][Para 73][Para 79][Para 89]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed in full — the claims on the 8th-of-month pay date, the stretchers, the two-day holiday notice, the 4,000 riel meal allowance and the three extra card machines were all rejected; the award is non-binding. [Para 191][Para 192][Para 193][Para 194][Para 195][Para 196]

Standing and the rights/interest divide: the panel held that where a claim seeks a benefit superior to the law it is an interest dispute, and under Article 54 of the Trade Union Law 2016 read with Article 96(2) of the Labour Law and Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099 only a most-representative union may bring one, because the resulting award replaces the collective agreement and suspends the workers' right to strike over it. The claimant here was neither a most-representative union nor a union with the qualifying characteristics, so the panel declined to consider the interest claims at all. [Para 127][Para 143][Para 146][Para 147]

Pay date: Article 116 requires at least two payments a month with no more than sixteen days between them but fixes no calendar day, and Article 2 leaves the employer free to set the day provided it acts lawfully and reasonably. The employment contract stipulates the 10th, and the employer pays earlier when the 10th is a holiday. The claim therefore failed as a rights dispute. [Para 91][Para 102][Para 110][Para 112][Para 113]

Stretchers: Articles 238 and 242(4) and Article 4 of Prakas No. 330 require primary treatment, dressings and medicines, but no provision obliges the employer to provide stretchers. Because the claim exceeded the law it was an interest dispute and, with standing absent, was not considered. [Para 117][Para 124][Para 125][Para 127]

Holiday notice: Article 3 of Prakas No. 10 of 4 February 1999 makes holiday work voluntary, and no law, agreement or internal rule requires two days' notice. One day's notice, which the employer gives, allows a genuine and uncoerced decision, so the claim failed on the merits. [Para 155][Para 156][Para 160][Para 162][Para 163]

Meal allowance: Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 fixes 2,000 riel per day or one free meal for voluntary overtime, and the employer pays the 2,000 riel. The claim for 4,000 riel was an interest claim, refused for want of standing and in any event without legal foundation. [Para 164][Para 168][Para 171][Para 172][Para 173]

Card machines: the party asserting a fact bears the burden of proving it. The workers could not say how long the queue lasted, and the employer's account of a seven-minute exit was not displaced. [Para 178][Para 184][Para 187][Para 188]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim that seeks a benefit more favourable than the Labour Law is an interest dispute, and the panel will decline to decide it unless the claimant is a most-representative union or a union otherwise entitled to conclude a collective agreement under Articles 54 and 72 of the Trade Union Law; the same union status does not bar the panel from deciding the claimant's rights disputes on their merits. [Para 143][Para 146]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that a stretcher offers more safety for a fainting worker than being carried by colleagues, while still holding that no legal duty to supply one exists. [Para 150][Para 151]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The procedural summary states that four points were settled but lists only three (5, 6 and 9), which is the figure that reconciles with the nine claims, the withdrawal of point 8 and the five surviving points. [Para 21][Para 23] The panel's constitution date is printed as "13 July 2019 2016". [Para 18] The company's Latin-script name appears nowhere; only the Khmer transliteration survives, and it is rendered inconsistently (ឌីន ហាន អេនធើ ប្រាយ, ឌីន ហាន, ឌីន ហា), so the registered English name cannot be confirmed. [Para 48][Para 60] The number of workers who transferred rights is given as 60 in one line and the hearing attendance variously as 06 and 026. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 55] Article 116 is printed with stray text ("ពីរ ដង្ស៊ី ២០១៧"), and the reasoning on the meal allowance repeats "2017" and "2015" inside quoted passages. [Para 91][Para 166]
