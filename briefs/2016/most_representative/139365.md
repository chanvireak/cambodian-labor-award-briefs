# Award No. 209/16 — Hoeng Jin Textile Factory (Arbitration Council, 22 September 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry, Tuon Si Phan, Pen Bun Chhea presiding) · Doc ID 139365

## 1. Facts
Hoeng Jin Textile Factory ("Hoeng Jin Textile"), a garment plant in Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employed roughly 130 workers according to the employer. [Para 76] The factory had no most representative union; the Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers Democratic Union (C.CAWDU) held a registration certificate and its factory leadership appeared at the hearing. [Para 78][Para 179] Of nine non-conciliation demands, three were settled in conciliation and six came to the panel; one more was settled at the hearing, leaving five. [Para 21][Para 27] Three workers dismissed on 19 May 2016 for being under 18 — Mr H, Ms M and Mr N — had been reinstated under a written agreement of 22 June 2016, but no back wages were paid. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 91]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay back wages to Mr H and Ms M for 19 May 2016 to 1 July 2016 under point 9 of the 22 June 2016 minutes? [Para 183]
2. Whether the employer must take Mr N back and pay his back wages from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 215]
3. Whether the employer must convert fixed-duration contracts to undetermined-duration contracts for workers whose total service exceeds two years? [Para 234]
5. Whether the employer must take Ms T back and pay her back wages from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 261]
6. Whether the employer must establish a proper infirmary (គិលានដ្ឋាន) in the factory? [Para 296]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer signed point 9 of the conciliation minutes, which promised reinstatement and back wages on production of age documents, and must honour it; Mr N was under 18 only because he had borrowed another person's identity card, which Notification 127/MoSALVY says is not the worker's fault; contracts never put in writing are undetermined-duration by law; Ms T's absence was illness covered by medical certificates and leave approved by the administration head; and a factory of this size must have its own infirmary. [Para 94][Para 108][Para 118][Para 142][Para 151]
**Employer (respondent):** the administration head who translated for the director did not notice the back-wage clause, so the company cannot pay it; there is no vacant post for Mr N in the checking section and he was still under 18 when he produced his documents; fixed-duration contracts remain fixed-duration; Ms T stopped work more than seven days without notifying the employer; and the company is already preparing an infirmary while workers may use the SLJ company's facility about 100 metres away. [Para 97][Para 109][Para 121][Para 145][Para 154]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted in full on all five issues — the employer must pay back wages to Mr H and Ms M for 19 May to 1 July 2016; take Mr N back and pay his back wages from dismissal to reinstatement; apply undetermined-duration contracts wherever a fixed-duration contract was not put in writing or total service exceeds two years; take Ms T back and pay her back wages from dismissal to reinstatement; and establish an infirmary with medicines and a duty nurse or doctor. [Para 313][Para 314][Para 315][Para 316][Para 317]

As a preliminary matter the panel held that although Article 54 of the Trade Union Law 2016 reserves exclusive representational rights to the most representative union, and no such union existed at the factory, the absence of one cannot bar workers from collective dispute resolution; a registered union may represent its own members, following Awards 171/16, 180/16, 184/16 and 197/16. [Para 158][Para 165][Para 181]

On issue 1 the panel applied Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code: a contract arises from offer and acceptance, and a party is bound by the agreement it made. [Para 183][Para 186][Para 194] A translation failure by a translator the employer itself engaged is the employer's own responsibility and cannot excuse performance. [Para 208][Para 212] The employer having reinstated the two workers but withheld the wages, the order followed the agreement. [Para 202][Para 214]

On issue 2 the panel relied on Notification 127/MoSALVY of 2 May 2016, which provides that identity irregularities — borrowed names, wrong birth dates, under-age status — are not to be treated as the worker's fault and that employers retain no child-labour liability for them; the correct response is to regularise the documents while preserving seniority and the contract. [Para 218][Para 219][Para 225] Mr N had corrected his documents and reached 18 by the date of the award, so he was entitled to his contract and seniority, and the employer's lack of a vacancy did not defeat the point 9 agreement. [Para 229][Para 230][Para 232]

On issue 3 the panel applied Article 67(7) of the Labour Law: a fixed-duration contract must be in writing, failing which it becomes undetermined-duration; and Article 67(2), under which renewals may not push total duration beyond two years. [Para 234][Para 237][Para 244] The employer admitted the three-month contracts were never written and that cards were simply reissued every three months with 5% contract-end pay, so every such contract was already undetermined-duration. [Para 240][Para 241] Of the 29 workers listed, the panel found 25 had more than two years' total service. [Para 124]

On issue 5 the panel noted that the burden of proving misconduct lies on the party alleging it. [Para 274] Both sides accepted that Ms T asked the administration head by telephone on 14 July 2016 for one week's sick leave and was granted it, and that the head failed to tell the director; medical certificates from Preah Ang Duong hospital and a Thai hospital were filed and unchallenged. [Para 281][Para 285] The employer showed no reason relating to her capacity or conduct or to the needs of the business, so the dismissal lacked a valid reason and reinstatement with back wages was ordered. [Para 286][Para 295]

On issue 6 the panel applied Article 242 of the Labour Law, requiring an on-site permanent infirmary in any enterprise employing at least 50 workers, and Articles 3 and 4 of Joint Prakas 330 SKBY of 6 December 2000 on staffing and equipment. [Para 296][Para 299] With about 1,300 workers and no infirmary, and only the neighbouring SLJ facility available, the employer was not compliant. [Para 308][Para 310]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a conciliation agreement has been signed and the employer has performed part of it, a claim that its own translator failed to draw a clause to the director's attention is no defence to the unpaid balance; the employer bears the translation risk of an agreement it drafted and signed. [Para 208][Para 212] Separately, a fixed-duration contract never reduced to writing is undetermined-duration from the outset, and successive renewals that carry total service past two years convert it regardless of what the parties called it. [Para 237][Para 258]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remarks that undetermined-duration contracts promote job security and also benefit employers by encouraging commitment, and its reference to ILO Recommendation 166 as non-binding context, were explanatory rather than necessary to the order. [Para 250][Para 253][Para 255]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
OCR damage is extensive: words are duplicated across lines (Paras 196, 239, 257, 279–280), and the panel's own reasoning is sometimes printed twice in one paragraph. [Para 196] The workforce figure conflicts: the employer said about 130 workers at Para 76, while the panel's infirmary reasoning states about 1,300 at Para 308, and only the latter is consistent with Article 242 and the Prakas staffing table. The factory name is printed as Hoeng Jin Textile Factory, Heong Jin Textile Factory and Hoing Jin. [Para 22][Para 32] The union is described as C.CAWDU ("ស៊ី ខៅ ឌូ") and the employer representative's transliteration varies. [Para 179] Claimed back pay is stated as US$257.4 for 41 days, but 41 days at the printed monthly wage of US$140 plus US$15, US$7 and US$3 allowances does not reconcile on the face of the award. [Para 88][Para 96]
