# Award No. 227/16 — Ever Reach Technology Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 21 November 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Hem Honarith appointed by the employer, An Non appointed by the workers, Kuy Nam chair) · Doc ID 139380

## 1. Facts
Ever Reach Technology Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. makes toys at National Road 4, Suon Way Seven 1, Romdual village, Samrong Tong district, Kampong Speu, and had about 147 workers. [Para 1][Para 38][Para 39] The workers last worked normally on 10 August 2016; from 11 August the employer shut the gate and let no one in, with a single security guard on the front, and from September neither reopened nor paid wages. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49] The employer never stated whether the contracts were suspended or the company closed for good, and could not be contacted. [Para 50] The panel's invitations of 25 and 28 October 2016 were refused by the guard and simply posted at the premises, and the employer did not appear. [Para 17][Para 20][Para 62] Thirty-two workers gave written, thumbprinted mandates to the Khmer Youth Voice federation, and one more, Mr S, attended in person. [Para 44][Para 111]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay wages for September and October 2016, when it provided no work? [Para 117]
2. Whether the employer must pay wages and other benefits under the Labour Law on the footing that the company has closed down? [Para 142]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company shut its gate on 11 August 2016 and paid nothing for September or October although no notice of suspension or of closure was ever given, so wages are owed for the period they were kept out. [Para 47][Para 53][Para 54] They ask, in the alternative, for full statutory settlement if the company has truly closed. [Para 55][Para 56]
**Employer (respondent):** no position was taken; the employer did not attend the hearing and filed no documents, so the panel treated its right to reply as waived. [Para 16][Para 26][Para 94]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay September and October 2016 wages to each worker whose contract was in force during those two months, and declined to consider the claim for a closure settlement for want of sufficiently specific facts. [Para 152]

The panel first held that it could proceed in the employer's absence. Article 21 of Prakas 099 allows the Council to continue where a duly invited party fails to appear without valid reason, and the panel found every reasonable means of invitation had been used — two visits, refused letters, posted notices. [Para 58][Para 65][Para 66] On representation it followed Article 54(1) of the Law on Trade Unions: the union's recognised leaders had lost their mandate and the new leaders produced no recognition letter from the Ministry, so they could not act; but the federation held written mandates from 32 workers, and Article 19 of Prakas 099 let a party be represented by any person authorised in writing, so those 32 plus Mr S were the claimants. [Para 105][Para 106][Para 114][Para 116]

On issue 1 the panel started from Article 664 of the Civil Code 2007: a labour contract is a promise of work against a promise of wage, so a worker who does not perform is not owed wages. [Para 117][Para 120][Para 121] It found, however, that the failure to work in September and October 2016 did not originate with the workers but with the employer's own act in closing the gate, without any notice or information about its intention. [Para 133][Para 134][Para 135] Following Awards 065/15 (Khmer Thub Sport Thing Goods) and 002/16 (Kon Tek), where non-performance flows from the employer's own failure, the worker keeps the right to wages; the panel therefore ordered payment and, under Prakar 34 of Prakas 099, cast it as compensation. [Para 122][Para 123][Para 136][Para 137][Para 138]

On issue 2 the workers could not say whether the closure was temporary or permanent, and gave no particulars from which the company's legal situation could be fixed; following Award 150/14 (Lean Fa Garment) the panel held the facts insufficiently precise and declined to decide, leaving any closure settlement to be claimed if and when closure is established. [Para 143][Para 146][Para 149][Para 150][Para 151]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer shuts the workplace and gives no work while remaining silent about whether the contracts are suspended or terminated, the resulting non-performance is attributable to the employer, and the workers' right to wages for that period survives under Article 664 of the Civil Code; the claim is treated as one for compensation under Prakar 34 of Prakas 099. A claim that presupposes the permanent closure of the enterprise, by contrast, requires specific facts about the company's legal status, and without them the panel will decline to decide rather than guess. [Para 134][Para 137][Para 149]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that the absence of a most-representative union obstructs representation and may engage Article 39 of the Constitution, and its survey of awards on proceeding in default, are general remarks not needed for the orders. [Para 74][Para 75] The award is expressly non-binding. [Para 23]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The employer's arbitrator is printed as "Hem Honarith" on the face of the award [Para 1] and "Hem Huonarith" at the signature [Para 154].
- The company name is transliterated six ways in the same document — Ever Reach/Ever Rai/Everri/Everi Technology Garment — including in the style of cause itself [Para 1][Para 8][Para 32][Para 38].
- Year digits are corrupted where the text was re-typed: the panel is said to have been constituted on "27 October 2019 2016" [Para 14], Prakas 099 is dated "21 April 2019 2004" [Para 58], and the operative order reads "September and October 2022 2016" [Para 140].
- Invitation letter 1282 to the workers is dated "27 month 27 year 2016" with the month lost [Para 37], and the conciliation minute is dated 14 October 2016 [Para 33] while the report is dated 21 October 2016 [Para 8].
- The section of reasons on issue 2 repeats whole sentences (an entire clause about settlement on closure appears twice at [Para 149] and again in the operative part at [Para 152]), and a block of untranslated Latin OCR noise sits at [Para 143].
- The number of mandate-giving workers is given as 39 in one place and 32 (those who actually thumbprinted) later [Para 44][Para 111]; the panel works from 32 plus one, i.e. 33 claimants. [Para 116]
