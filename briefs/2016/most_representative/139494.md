# Award No. 326/15 — Ren Heng Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 13 January 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chhea presiding; Hem Honarith, employer nominee; An Nan, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139494

## 1. Facts
Ren Heng Garment Co., Ltd, at Krang Angkrang village, Krang Thnong commune, Sen Sok district, Phnom Penh, sews garments and employed about 200 workers. [Para 37][Para 38] The National Federation of Unions of the Cambodian Textile and Garment Industry took up the claims of eleven workers under an authority of 6 November 2015, raising sixteen demands on working conditions. [Para 39][Para 24] Conciliation on 11 December 2015 produced nothing because the employer did not attend, and report No. 1600 reached the Council on 18 December 2015. [Para 25][Para 26] The employer was again absent at the hearing of 28 December 2015, invitation No. 1649 having been served on 22 December 2015; the panel therefore proceeded in its absence under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 23][Para 78][Para 79][Para 82]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay a midday meal allowance of 2,000 riel a day? (withdrawn.) [Para 2][Para 31]
2. Whether the company must let pregnant workers leave 15 minutes early, or at least 10 minutes before the bell? [Para 3][Para 44]
3. Whether the company must allow one paid day a month for antenatal checks, keeping wages and benefits? [Para 4][Para 47]
4. Whether the company must provide an infirmary, medicines and a doctor present during working hours? [Para 5][Para 50]
5. Whether the company must stop compelling workers to work overtime? [Para 6][Para 53]
6. Whether the company must supply hygienic drinking water in a clean place? [Para 7][Para 56]
7. Whether the company must keep the toilets clean, with water and soap for hand-washing? [Para 8][Para 59]
8. Whether the company must grant leave for valid personal reasons and deduct the attendance bonus only pro rata for the days taken? [Para 9][Para 63]
9. Whether the company must pay 50 percent of wages and allowances for the three months before maternity leave? [Para 10][Para 67]
10. Whether the company must, on dismissing any worker, pay wages and benefits according to the Labour Law? (withdrawn.) [Para 11][Para 31]
11. Whether the company must stop insulting Khmer workers? [Para 12]
12. Whether the company must stop forcing workers to sew above a fixed quota? (withdrawn.) [Para 13][Para 31]
13. Whether the company must pay the overtime meal allowance once a week? [Para 14][Para 70]
14. Whether the company must bear full responsibility for workplace accidents? [Para 15]
15. Whether the company must pay 5 percent on each termination of contract? (withdrawn.) [Para 16][Para 31]
16. Whether the company must refrain from dismissing workers for union activity? (withdrawn.) [Para 17][Para 31]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company was giving no work and paying irregularly, forcing overtime from 16:00 to 18:00 and beyond and penalising those who refused, supplying drinking water from a tank beside the foul toilets, withholding soap and clean water, refusing leave to workers who had taken it before, and paying the maternity wage only after the worker returned. [Para 52][Para 55][Para 57][Para 60][Para 67][Para 184]
**Employer (respondent):** no position is recorded; the company did not appear and the panel treated it as having abandoned its right to answer the workers' contentions. [Para 86][Para 106]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered an infirmary with medicines and a doctor, voluntary overtime, clean drinking water, hygienic toilets with soap, leave for valid reasons with only a pro-rata attendance-bonus deduction, the 50 percent maternity wage for 90 days paid before leave, and weekly overtime meal allowance; it refused the 10-minute early exit and the monthly paid antenatal day. [Para 268][Para 270][Para 276]
Because the complainant federation was not the most representative union in the enterprise, the panel refused to decide the two points that were interest disputes — a new entitlement to leave early and a new paid day for antenatal checks — since an award on an interest dispute displaces the collective agreement for a year and would strip every worker of the right to claim such benefits later. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 99][Para 100][Para 103][Para 113]
Even so, it ordered the company to stagger pregnant workers' exit and thumbprint scanning so they leave before the crowd, finding the existing practice of holding them until the bell exposed them to being jostled. [Para 101][Para 102][Para 104][Para 269]
On the infirmary, Article 242 requires any enterprise with at least fifty workers to keep a permanent infirmary with dressings, equipment and medicines, Article 239 requires a medical service led by a doctor, and Prakas No. 330 of 6 December 2000 fixes the staffing ratio; the company met none of this. [Para 114][Para 117][Para 118][Para 120][Para 123][Para 129]
Overtime was measured against Article 4 of Prakas No. 80 and Notification No. 030/15 of 27 February 2015: two hours a day at most, ten hours' total work, prior ministry authorisation, and no discipline or threat for refusal. Blocking the gate and threatening dismissal were held to breach the voluntariness principle. [Para 131][Para 137][Para 139][Para 151][Para 152]
Drinking water fell under Article 1 of Prakas No. 054 and toilets under Article 229 of the Labour Law and Articles 1 and 6 of Prakas No. 052, which require clean water and soap at every toilet. [Para 153][Para 163][Para 165][Para 166][Para 175][Para 176]
The attendance bonus was the most closely reasoned point. Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 requires at least USD 10 a month for workers present on every working day; the panel found the word "absence" undefined, held that absences authorised by law or by the employer suspend the contract under Articles 71 and 72, and concluded that the bonus is a form of wage which the employer may deduct only in proportion to the days actually taken. [Para 200][Para 225][Para 226][Para 229][Para 230][Para 231][Para 233]
On maternity pay, Articles 182 and 183 give 90 days at half wage including allowances, and Article 115 read with Article 103 makes that sum a wage payable before the leave begins, not in instalments afterwards. [Para 234][Para 235][Para 239][Para 246][Para 247][Para 276]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the complainant union is not the most representative union in the enterprise, the panel will not decide an interest dispute, because an award on interests stands in place of a collective agreement for one year and would extinguish every worker's right to claim the benefit later; it will still enforce existing statutory duties on safety, health and wages. On the attendance bonus, absence authorised by law or by the employer suspends the contract and is a form of wage, so the employer may deduct it only in proportion to the days absent, not forfeit it entirely. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 215][Para 231]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the term "absence" in Notification No. 230 is undefined by the competent authority and that it could not itself read it as covering both authorised and unauthorised absence without regard to lawfulness and reasonableness, so each case must be assessed on its facts. [Para 203][Para 204][Para 207]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel's list of surviving issues is printed as "four points, namely points 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9 and 13" and it omits point 6, which the panel then decides and orders. [Para 32][Para 153][Para 272] Points 11 (insults to Khmer workers) and 14 (liability for workplace accidents) are neither withdrawn nor decided anywhere in the award. [Para 12][Para 15][Para 31] The hearing is dated 28 December 2015 throughout, but the invitation records service at 15:14 on 22 December 2015 and the award is dated 13 January 2016. [Para 23][Para 78][Para 1] Notification No. 230 is dated 25 July 2012 in the reasons but 25 July 2016 in one repetition, and the older Notification No. 041 is dated 7 March 2011 in one place and cited as "041/11" elsewhere. [Para 197][Para 198][Para 195][Para 248] The company name is printed as "Ren Heng Garment Co., Ltd" on the face, "Ren Heng Garment Go" in one reference and "Ren Heng Garment-Co" in another. [Para 1][Para 39] The claimant group is given as eleven workers throughout while the factory employs 200; the 111 names on the authority are not reproduced here. [Para 39][Para 41][Para 124]
