# Award No. 171/16 — Jewelin Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 16 August 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, appointed by the employer; An Nan, appointed by the workers; Kong Phallha, chair appointed by both) · Doc ID 139073

## 1. Facts
Jewelin Co., Ltd., a metal-processing enterprise in Sihanoukville with about 60 workers, ran a day shift and a night shift. [Para 1][Para 62][Para 63] A collective agreement of 09 May 2015 provided at point 1 that "the company agrees to divide work into two shifts". [Para 70][Para 158] On 27 November 2015 the company closed the night shift and moved all its workers to the day shift; of the twenty on nights, eleven accepted and nine refused. [Para 71][Para 72] The company then sent letters granting part of them temporary leave and, on 12 January 2016, seven-day notice requiring the objectors back at work by 19 January 2016 on pain of being treated as having resigned. [Para 73][Para 76][Para 95][Para 96] Eight workers did not return, and the panel treated their indefinite contracts as terminated by the employer on 19 January 2016. [Para 83][Para 226] The award is non-binding. [Para 17][Para 285]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to reopen the night shift (shift 2) and take the eight workers back into it, failing which to pay them termination compensation under the Labour Law? [Para 145][Para 149]
2. Whether the employer must pay severance, moral damages, notice in lieu and unused annual leave to those eight workers? [Para 166]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** closing shift 2 breached the 09 May 2015 agreement and the transfer to the day shift was made without their consent; they never received the notice requiring them back by 19 January 2016, and the employer had their telephone numbers and could have called them. [Para 146][Para 159][Para 213][Para 102]
**Employer (respondent):** the Labour Law does not require two shifts, so opening or closing a shift is the employer's exclusive managerial right; the eight abandoned their jobs by refusing to follow the shift assignment in the internal rules. [Para 87][Para 148][Para 179][Para 180]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer is not ordered to reopen shift 2 or reinstate the workers, but must pay each of the eight (LONG Sokba, KOUCH Somony Prasith, OUN Ngoal, KONG Phi, SOUNG Det, SENG Sopheak, RUN Ren and PEN Channa) severance under Article 89, moral damages under Article 91 equal to the severance, payment in lieu of notice under Articles 75 and 77, and unused annual leave under Articles 166 and 167. [Para 284]

On jurisdiction the panel applied Articles 309 and 312 of the Labour Law and Article 32 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY, holding it could decide only the disputes recorded in the non-conciliation minutes; the employer raised no objection, so jurisdiction was assumed. [Para 113][Para 114][Para 119][Para 124]

On standing, no union in the company was most representative, so nineteen workers had transferred their rights in writing to the Cambodian Labour Confederation; following award No. 140/16 Phnom Penh International School and Article 39 of the Constitution, the panel held the workers could be represented rather than left without a forum. [Para 67][Para 132][Para 133][Para 139][Para 143]

On issue 1, the panel read point 1 of the 09 May 2015 agreement as merely permitting two shifts, not obliging the company to keep the night shift open, and held that opening or closing a shift falls within the employer's managerial authority under Article 2 of the Labour Law, exercised lawfully where the company had lost orders. [Para 152][Para 160][Para 161][Para 162] Since shift 2 no longer exists, the panel could not order reinstatement into it. [Para 163][Para 164]

On issue 2, the panel first classified the contracts: the six workers whose written fixed-term contracts expired in 2014 and were not renewed in writing had indefinite contracts under Article 67(7), and the remaining two had no written fixed-term contract at all, so were also indefinite. [Para 167][Para 175][Para 176][Para 177][Para 178] It then found that the shift worked was itself an agreed working condition, that the employer changed it unilaterally without consent, and that this was a misuse of managerial authority. [Para 205][Para 208][Para 210][Para 211] Finally it held the employer bore the burden of proving that each worker received the recall notice, and that handing the letter to one union leader with instructions to pass it on did not discharge that burden; the eight therefore did not abandon their jobs, and the employer had terminated them without a valid reason. [Para 216][Para 222][Para 223][Para 225][Para 226][Para 235]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An agreement that the company "divides work into two shifts" permits two shifts; it does not oblige the employer to keep a shift open, and the panel will not order reinstatement into a shift that no longer exists, since shift allocation is a lawful managerial decision under Article 2 of the Labour Law. [Para 160][Para 161][Para 163]

Where the shift actually worked has become an agreed working condition, a unilateral transfer to another shift breaches it; and if the employer then treats the workers' non-attendance as abandonment, it must prove that the recall notice reached each worker — service on one union leader for onward transmission is insufficient. Failing that proof the separation is a termination without valid reason, attracting severance, moral damages, notice in lieu and unused annual leave. [Para 210][Para 223][Para 225][Para 226][Para 235][Para 256]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the absence of a most representative union must not deprive workers of the constitutional right to bring a claim, and its discussion of Article 39 of the Constitution, are general propositions going beyond the compensation order. [Para 132][Para 133]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The workforce in shift 2 is given as twenty, of whom eleven accepted the day shift and nine refused [Para 72], yet only eight workers appear in the claim and the order [Para 83][Para 284]; the ninth is never accounted for.
- The signature block is badly damaged: the worker-appointed arbitrator's name is replaced by the words "បញ្ហា លេខា" ("issue number") and the chair's name is lost behind stray numerals, although both appear in the composition at [Para 1][Para 286].
- The rights-transfer instrument is dated 27 June 2015 at [Para 67] but the demand letters of the same series are dated 27 June 2016 at [Para 46][Para 47].
- The award date is 16 August 2016 in the header [Para 1], after an agreed extension from "08 August 2019 2016" [Para 19].
- Paragraphs [Para 88]–[Para 91], [Para 177] and [Para 205] contain unreadable insertions — stray French and English fragments and numerals — which were read around, using [Para 202] and [Para 208] for the substance.
- The company's name is spelled "Jewelin", "Jewel in" and, once, as an undecipherable Latin string at [Para 82].
