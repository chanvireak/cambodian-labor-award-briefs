# Award No. 172/16 — I Son Kable Cambodia Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 18 August 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun for the employer party, Houn Chun Dy for the worker party, Pen Bun Chea chair) · Doc ID 139075

## 1. Facts
I Son Kable Cambodia Co Ltd makes phone cases at Samrong Tong, Kampong Speu, and employed about 1,200 workers. [Para 78][Para 79] The Free Workers Union of Cambodia, a base union registered with the Ministry of Labour on 13 October 2014, led a strike from 13 July 2016 on 17 collective demands; the panel issued order No. 014/16 on 19 July 2016 suspending the strike, and the workers returned. [Para 22][Para 26][Para 27][Para 62][Para 80] The employer had dismissed Mr T, the union's vice-president, on 20 June 2016 for leaving the workplace during working hours without permission, relying on three warning letters he had refused to sign. [Para 93][Para 99][Para 107] Four demands were settled, eight withdrawn, two merged, leaving five for decision. [Para 31][Para 32][Para 33][Para 34]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr T and pay his wages and benefits from the date of dismissal to the date of reinstatement? [Para 174][Para 314]
2. Whether the employer must allow a woman who has given birth one hour per day for breastfeeding, and provide a nursing room and nursery for workers' young children? [Para 255][Para 274]
3. Whether the employer must provide lunch with 40 kilograms of meat per meal? [Para 287]
4. Whether Mr T enjoyed special protection against dismissal, and whether the dismissal was lawful? [Para 177][Para 180]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr T committed no serious misconduct; as union vice-president he could only be dismissed with the labour inspector's prior authorisation, and he was dismissed on the spot; the warning letters bear no signature acknowledging his fault; the $13 per month paid in place of the nursing hour does not discharge the statutory duty. [Para 95][Para 114][Para 190][Para 263]
**Employer (respondent):** Mr T had already resigned his union post by letter of 20 February 2016 bearing his thumbprint, so no special protection applied; he left the workplace during working hours without permission on 13, 14 and 20 June 2016, which is serious misconduct once warnings accumulate. [Para 82][Para 100][Para 135][Para 176]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement and back wages refused; the employer must pay Mr T notice pay, dismissal compensation, moral damages, outstanding annual leave and last wages (figures lost to OCR), and provide the one-hour breastfeeding time, nursing room and nursery; the 40 kg meat demand and the sick-leave demand of a non-complainant were not heard. [Para 314][Para 315][Para 316][Para 318][Para 319]

On standing, a union derives its rights from Article 11 of the Trade Union Law only on registration, and registration also carries the right to represent members before the Council; the claimant was registered and held written delegations from 13 workers, so it could act for those 13 only. [Para 145][Para 148][Para 171][Para 172][Para 173] Mr T's letter of 20 February 2016 resigning the vice-presidency carried his thumbprint and was found genuine; Article 273 of the Labour Law guarantees freedom to leave a union at any time without the union's consent, and the claim that an ex-official incited him was a matter of the union's internal management on which the employer owes no enquiry. [Para 155][Para 157][Para 165][Para 166][Para 168] Special protection for union leaders therefore did not apply at the date of dismissal. [Para 178][Para 179]

On the dismissal, the panel applied the internal rules (Article 9 on leaving the workplace; Article 10 making two medium faults or four repeated light faults a serious fault) and held the employer bears the burden of proving serious misconduct, following Award No. 107/04. [Para 181][Para 183][Para 184][Para 191] The 13 and 14 June incidents rested only on warning letters with no acknowledgement signature, so they were rejected; only the 20 June absence of about five minutes was proved, which is not a serious fault under the rules. [Para 195][Para 198][Para 199][Para 205][Para 206] The dismissal lacked a valid reason: under Article 91 the employer owes moral damages, and under Articles 89, 75/77, 166–168 and 116 notice pay, dismissal compensation, unused annual leave and last wages. [Para 207][Para 210][Para 217][Para 228][Para 238][Para 252]

On the nursing demands, Article 184 gives one hour per day for one year from birth and Article 186 requires a nursing room and nursery where the enterprise employs at least 100 women — here about 1,000. [Para 256][Para 274][Para 280] The agreement of 25 February 2016 substituting $13 per month for those entitlements is an exchange the law does not permit, and is void under Article 13(1). [Para 264][Para 269][Para 271]

On the 40 kg of meat, no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, contract or past practice requires it, so the demand was a benefit above the statutory floor, hence an interest dispute. [Para 289][Para 290][Para 291] Because Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099 makes an award on an interest dispute a collective agreement binding all workers and removing their right to strike, only the most representative union under Articles 54 and 72 of the Trade Union Law may bring one; with 13 complainants against 1,200 workers this union is not, so jurisdiction was declined. [Para 303][Para 304][Para 307][Para 308][Para 309] The sick-leave claim for Ms S failed because she is not among the 13 complainants. [Para 310][Para 312][Para 313]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union official who has resigned his post by a signed or thumb-printed letter is no longer a protected union leader and needs no authorisation to be dismissed. Warning letters for earlier absences that carry no acknowledgement by the worker and no corroborating evidence cannot be aggregated into serious misconduct, and a short unauthorised absence classified by the internal rules as only a light or medium fault does not justify dismissal with cause — the employer may still dismiss but owes notice pay, dismissal compensation, moral damages, accrued leave and last wages. An employer cannot buy out the statutory nursing hour and nursery with a cash allowance, and only the most representative union may bring an interest dispute, because an award on it becomes a collective agreement binding the whole workforce. [Para 165][Para 195][Para 205][Para 210][Para 269][Para 271][Para 304][Para 308]

**Obiter dicta:** that the employer owes no duty to investigate a union's internal disputes about alleged incitement, and that its meal provision already exceeds the Labour Law, were not necessary to the orders. [Para 135][Para 166]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The compensation heads in the dispositive order carry no figures: notice pay, dismissal compensation, moral damages, annual leave and last wages print as "0]" and "on" at [Para 254] and [Para 315], so no amount in riel or USD could be reported.
- The dismissal date is 20 June 2016 in the findings and the claim [Para 93][Para 174] but is printed "20 July June 2016" in the notice-pay reasoning [Para 216].
- The union's registration number is "3206" at [Para 62] but the Ministry's confirming letter is No. 098/14 [Para 63]; the registration year prints as "20148" at [Para 80] and as 13 October 2014 elsewhere [Para 63][Para 151].
- The panel was constituted on 19 July 2016 [Para 25]; the two hearing invitations of 20 July 2016 bear different numbers for the same party [Para 74][Para 75]; the second hearing is 01 August 2016 [Para 21] and the extension runs from 08 to 18 August 2016 [Para 37].
- The number of women workers is printed as "4 workers ... approximately 1,000" [Para 123], with a stray "4" prefix also at [Para 119]; the headcount is "about 1,200" [Para 79] against 13 complainants [Para 90][Para 307].
- Mr T's name is printed four ways — "Tat Kim Cheng", "Tat Kim Cheang", "Kim Cheng" and the family name alone [Para 90][Para 152][Para 178][Para 199]; two of the 13 complainants' names are duplicated in the delegation list [Para 90].
- The security minute of 20 June 2016 records only that a union representative stepped out for five minutes and the guards did not know what was discussed [Para 53]; it cannot support the employer's account of the 13 and 14 June absences.
