# Award No. 148/16 — Royal Crowntex International Inc (Arbitration Council, 18 July 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ri, appointed by the employer; Sin Kim Sean, appointed by the workers; Kong Polha, president) · Doc ID 139058

## 1. Facts
A garment factory at Preah Sihanouk employing about 747 workers stopped operating on 10 June 2016, when management told the section and line leaders that it could not pay May wages or any wages thereafter and posted a notice at the gate announcing the closure from that date [Para 52][Para 67][Para 68]. The employer did not attend either hearing, and by telephone said only that it accepted whatever the panel decided [Para 20][Para 25][Para 77][Para 86]. The registered plant union, with 395 members, claimed termination compensation and benefits for those members; two of its three original claims were withdrawn at the hearing, leaving only the compensation claim [Para 57][Para 29][Para 30][Para 61].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to pay termination compensation and other lawful benefits to the 395 union members whose contracts were of unlimited duration, on the footing that the company could no longer operate? [Para 61][Para 98]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer terminated every contract on 10 June 2016 and owes five heads of payment — pay in lieu of notice under Articles 75 and 77, severance under Article 89, moral damages equal to severance under Article 91, outstanding annual leave under Articles 166 and 167, and final wages under Articles 116 and 124, including May 2016 wages less the USD 50 already paid and ten days' wages for 1–10 June 2016 [Para 61][Para 75].
**Employer (respondent):** It could not pay severance at present but would pay after selling its assets or the business, calculated to 10 June 2016 according to legal procedure [Para 8][Para 69][Para 71].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to pay all five heads of compensation to the 395 workers, calculated as set out below [Para 169][Para 170][Para 171][Para 172][Para 173].

The panel first confirmed it could proceed in the employer's absence: Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 Sakbey allows a hearing to continue where a party duly summoned fails to appear without good reason, and the employer's statement that it accepted any outcome was not a clear position and did not excuse non-attendance [Para 78][Para 80][Para 86][Para 87].

On standing, Article 11 of the Trade Union Law of 17 May 2016 gives a union rights, including the right to represent members before the panel, once it is registered [Para 89][Para 93]. This union held certificate No. 3309 of 2 July 2015, and its vice-president attended, so its leaders could represent all members [Para 94][Para 96][Para 97].

On the date of termination the panel weighed the posted closure notice and the employer's own statement in the 20 June 2016 conciliation minutes that it would pay from 10 June 2016 onward, and found termination on 10 June 2016 [Para 104][Para 105][Para 108]. Because the employer never appeared it waived its right of response [Para 116], and it gave no valid reason relating to aptitude, conduct or the needs of the enterprise as Article 74 requires [Para 112][Para 117]. On that basis the panel ordered, for unlimited-duration contracts: pay in lieu of notice under Articles 75 and 77, measured by the average monthly wage over the preceding twelve months divided by 26 [Para 118][Para 124][Para 128]; severance under Article 89 at 15 days' wages and allowances per year of service, capped at six months [Para 129][Para 133]; moral damages under Article 91 equal to the severance, the worker being relieved of proving loss [Para 141][Para 144][Para 146]; outstanding annual leave under Article 166 on the same twelve-month average [Para 147][Para 150][Para 160]; and final wages under Article 116 [Para 161][Para 163].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A posted notice that the factory is closing from a stated date, coupled with the employer's refusal to pay wages from that date, is a termination of the contracts on that date; an employer that stays away from both hearings waives its right of response and cannot later dispute the absence of a valid reason, so Article 89 severance, Article 91 moral damages equal to that severance, and Article 75 notice pay all fall due [Para 108][Para 116][Para 117][Para 146].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observation that the union's lack of most-representative status does not bar it from bringing a rights dispute on behalf of its own members, since the restriction applies to interest disputes [Para 57][Para 96].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company name appears in at least five forms (Royal Crowntex International Inc, Royal Crowntex Intemational Indi, Royal Crowntex L International Inc, "Royal Crowntex Lindon"), so the name above is taken from the operative paragraphs [Para 34][Para 54][Para 94]. The date the panel was constituted prints as "២០២១ ២០១៦" [Para 18]. The employer's request to postpone the 8 July hearing is dated 30 July 2016, which cannot be right [Para 32], and the conciliation minutes are dated 20 July 2016 although the conciliation report is dated 21 June 2016 and the first hearing took place on 8 July 2016 [Para 44]. The Article 89 cap is printed as six months at Para 130 and as "ប្រាំ មួយ ខែ" at Para 133 [Para 133]. Headcounts differ across the file — 747 employed, a list of 615, of whom 602 had over two years' service, and 395 claimants — and are reproduced as printed [Para 52][Para 63][Para 64]. Individual workers and union officials are redacted in this brief; the employer's negotiating counterpart is named in the source as "Mr M Jones" and in one place as "Mark Jones" with a stray given name [Para 39][Para 61].
