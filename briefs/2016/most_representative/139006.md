# Award No. 065/16 — Aqin Tech PTE Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 March 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (អ៊ីង សុទ្ធី), appointed by the employer; An Non (អន ណន), appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chha (ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា), chair · Doc ID 139006

## 1. Facts
Aqin Tech PTE Co., Ltd (អេឃីន ថិច ភីធី អ៊ី អិលធីឌី), a garment manufacturer at Damnak Thom, Stung Meanchey, Meanchey, Phnom Penh, employed about 1,350 workers and recognised a local union of the Democratic Union of Lucky Workers federation. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38] The federation complained on 18 December 2015 seeking better working conditions across eight points; three were settled at the last conciliation on 29 February 2016 and five came to the Council, where issue 5 was withdrawn, leaving issues 1 to 4. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 17][Para 21][Para 22] The company already paid 3,000 riel meal money for two hours of overtime, USD 7 a month for transport and accommodation, and an USD 11 monthly attendance bonus, but calculated maternity pay on the last month's basic wage, regular bonus, seniority bonus and travel allowance without overtime. [Para 43][Para 49][Para 61][Para 62]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay 2,000 riel an hour as overtime meal money for overtime worked between 15:00 and 17:00? [Para 43][Para 66]
2. Whether the employer must pay USD 10 a month for transport and accommodation? [Para 49][Para 82]
3. Whether the employer must pay women on maternity leave 50 per cent of wages and allowances for three months, calculated on the average wage of one year? [Para 57][Para 90]
4. Whether the employer must raise the regular attendance bonus to USD 12 a month? [Para 62][Para 103]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** neighbouring factories such as Sin Chou and Makalot 1, 2 and 3 pay 2,000 riel an hour, USD 11 for transport and lodging, and higher attendance bonuses; food, rent and transport have all risen; and other factories calculate maternity pay on the average of a year's wages, as the union learned in ILO training, which would leave women better off. [Para 44][Para 50][Para 51][Para 57][Para 58][Para 64]
**Employer (respondent):** each company's economics differ and comparisons are invalid; before Prakas 041/11 it paid 1,000 riel and now pays 3,000 riel for two hours, above the 2,000 riel required; it pays the USD 7 fixed by Prakas 230/12 and the government has already addressed housing and utilities; the USD 11 bonus exceeds the USD 10 minimum; and its long-standing maternity calculation was checked orally with the Ministry, though it holds no written confirmation. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 52][Para 53][Para 60][Para 61][Para 65]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — on issue 3 the employer was ordered to calculate maternity pay on the average of wages and allowances over the last 12 months; issues 1, 2 and 4 were refused without consideration of their merits. No sum in riel or dollars was fixed. [Para 111][Para 112][Para 113][Para 114]

On issues 1, 2 and 4 the panel found no Labour Law provision, collective agreement, internal rule, contract or past practice granting 2,000 riel an hour, USD 10 or USD 12, and classified each as an interest dispute. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 85][Para 86][Para 106][Para 107] Applying Article 96(2) of the Labour Law, Article 9 of Prakas 305 of 22 November 2001 and Article 43 of Prakas 099 — under which an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement and binds all workers for a year — it held that only a union with most-representative status may bring such a claim, found this union registered but not most representative, and declined to consider the three claims. [Para 70][Para 72][Para 73][Para 75][Para 79][Para 80][Para 108][Para 109]

On issue 3 the panel applied Articles 182 and 183 of the Labour Law (90 days' maternity leave, half pay including allowances, for women with a year's service) and then asked what counts as wage, answering from Article 103, which includes basic wage, overtime pay, commission, bonuses and payments in kind. [Para 90][Para 91][Para 92][Para 93][Para 95][Para 96] It followed awards 06/08, 17/11, 79/12, 52/12, 186/13 and 084/15, which take the last 12 months' wages, divide by 12 for a monthly average, halve it, and multiply by three for the 90 days; the company's use of the last month alone, omitting overtime, was held contrary to Article 183. [Para 98][Para 99][Para 100][Para 101][Para 102]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Maternity pay under Article 183 is half of the wage, and "wage" under Article 103 includes overtime pay; the entitlement must therefore be computed on the average of the wages and allowances actually received in the 12 months before leave, not on the last month alone — an employer's settled practice to the contrary does not satisfy the Law. [Para 96][Para 98][Para 101][Para 102] Separately, where a claim for more than the statutory minimum is an interest dispute, a union that is registered but not most representative cannot put it to the panel, even if the employer's current practice already exceeds the Prakas floor. [Para 75][Para 79][Para 109]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's account of how an interest award displaces the collective agreement for one year was background to its jurisdictional ruling and not itself decisive. [Para 73][Para 74]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is dated 30 March 2016 and the hearing 18 March 2016, but the conciliation report numbered 303 KAP/AK/VK is dated 7 March 2016 in two places and 8 March 2016 in a third. [Para 1][Para 14][Para 12][Para 17] The company name is OCR'd as អេឃីន ថិច, អេយីន ថិច and អេឃីន ថិន, so the Latin form used here is a transliteration of a Khmer-only name. [Para 1][Para 12][Para 29] Its registration appears both as certificate 5108 of 27 June 2014 and as Inv. 0286 E/2010 of 5 February 2010. [Para 27][Para 36] The first claim is stated both as 2,000 riel per hour and, in the Prakas quoted, as 2,000 riel per day; the fourth claim's dispositive prints "1 12 dollars" where USD 12 is meant. [Para 66][Para 68][Para 114] The paragraph describing the union leaders' attendance is garbled beyond reconstruction, and the signature block gives no name for the worker-appointed arbitrator. [Para 41][Para 116]
