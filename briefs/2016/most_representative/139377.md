# Award No. 222/16 — Fou Hou (Arbitration Council, 26 October 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ouk Ry (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (presiding) · Doc ID 139377

## 1. Facts
Fou Hou is a rubber-stamping and printing enterprise employing 36 workers, with no most representative union; a local union had been elected but never applied for registration. [Para 33][Para 34] Five workers brought eight claims on working conditions — first-aid medicine, the attendance bonus, meal allowances, overtime, sick leave, payslips and toilets — which the Phnom Penh labour department conciliated on 13 September 2016 in the employer's absence. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 39] The employer neither attended the hearing of 12 October 2016 nor filed any document. [Para 19][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer had to provide sufficient first-aid medicine for the workers? [Para 125]
2. Whether the employer had to reduce the regular-attendance bonus pro rata for days of permitted occasional absence? [Para 133]
3. Whether the employer had to pay a 2,000 riel meal allowance to workers doing overtime from 16:00 to 18:00? [Para 157]
4. Whether the employer had to organise overtime on a voluntary basis? [Para 182]
5. Whether the employer had to refrain from deducting wages for sick leave covered by a proper medical certificate? [Para 195]
6. Whether the employer had to issue payslips written in Khmer? [Para 207]
7. Whether the employer had to pay a 2,000 riel daily lunch allowance? [Para 223]
8. Whether the employer had to keep the toilets clean and supplied with soap? [Para 228]

A ninth, added at the hearing, sought reinstatement of four workers said to have been dismissed for their role in the dispute. [Para 238][Para 246]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The company kept no medicine, no nurse and no sick room, so workers had to wait for the break and buy their own medicine; it docked the whole USD 10 attendance bonus for a single day of permitted absence; it forced overtime from 16:00 to 18:00 and warned those who refused; and it issued payslips only in Chinese. [Para 39][Para 45][Para 50][Para 57][Para 64]
**Employer (respondent):** Absent. It filed no evidence and made no submission, so the panel treated it as having waived its right of response on each claim. [Para 25][Para 129][Para 191][Para 217]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to stock first-aid medicines to the standard of the appendix to Prakas No. 330, to run overtime on a voluntary basis, to issue payslips in Khmer, and to keep the toilets clean and supplied with soap; the bonus, meal-allowance, sick-leave and reinstatement claims were refused. [Para 255][Para 258][Para 260][Para 262]

Jurisdiction and standing. Article 312(1) of the Law on Labour confines the panel to the issues recorded as unresolved at conciliation, or to issues arising afterwards as a direct consequence of that dispute. [Para 198][Para 240][Para 243] On interest disputes, Prakas No. 099 Article 43(1) provides that an award replaces the collective agreement and binds all the enterprise's workers, so the panel held it may decide such a claim only where the claimant is the most representative union, or where the multi-union mechanism of Article 72 of the Law on Trade Unions applies. [Para 174][Para 176][Para 177] Here the local union was unregistered, so its officers could not represent members [Para 114][Para 115]; the transfer-of-right letter bore only employee numbers and fingerprints with no names, so the panel could not identify who had transferred anything [Para 36][Para 37]. The five workers who appeared were therefore treated as claimants in person, as Prakas No. 099 Article 19 permits. [Para 122][Para 123]

First aid. Prakas No. 330 Article 7 requires an enterprise of 20 to 49 workers to maintain a dressing room and a nurse; Fou Hou has 36 and had neither medicine nor a sick room, so the claim succeeded. [Para 125][Para 126][Para 128][Para 131][Para 132]

Bonus and allowances. The USD 10 attendance bonus is a benefit more generous than the law, and the 2012 ministerial notice fixing it applies only to textiles, garments and footwear; Fou Hou's rubber printing falls outside it. Article 2 of the Law on Labour leaves the employer free to manage the enterprise lawfully and reasonably, so the condition attached to the bonus stood. [Para 134][Para 135][Para 140][Para 141][Para 152][Para 154][Para 155] The 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance rested on a 2011 notice limited to the same sectors, and no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice established it; the claim was an interest dispute, which an unregistered union could not bring. [Para 160][Para 162][Para 163][Para 176][Para 181] The lunch allowance failed for the same reason, the panel finding no instrument creating it. [Para 224][Para 225][Para 227]

Overtime and payslips. Prakas No. 80 Article 4 requires overtime to rest on the worker's consent and forbids compulsion or discipline for refusal; the panel found the employer compelled overtime and extracted thumbprints on warnings from those who declined, and ordered voluntary overtime. [Para 182][Para 185][Para 193][Para 194] Article 112 of the Law on Labour requires wage information to be clear and easily understood, and payslips in Chinese defeated that for Khmer-reading workers, so Khmer payslips were ordered. [Para 207][Para 213][Para 219][Para 222]

Sick leave and reinstatement. The sick-leave claim named no worker and was advanced expressly for future application; being neither a present dispute nor in the conciliation record, it was refused. [Para 195][Para 196][Para 197][Para 203][Para 205] The reinstatement claim was also outside the record and was not shown to be a direct consequence of the dispute: only one of the four had attended the conciliation, the employer was not present there, and no evidence linked the dismissals to anti-union motive. [Para 246][Para 249][Para 253][Para 254]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An interest dispute — a claim to a benefit that no law, collective agreement, internal rule or established practice yet guarantees — may be adjudicated only where the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise or the parties have used the multi-union mechanism, because such an award replaces the collective agreement and binds every worker; an unregistered local union cannot supply that standing, though the workers themselves may appear in person. Absence of an established entitlement is fatal to the claim, not a reason to create one.

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that itemised Khmer payslips remove workers' suspicion about their wages and improve employer-worker relations [Para 214][Para 221], and that an employer may lawfully attach a no-absence condition to a bonus that exceeds what the law requires, provided it acts reasonably [Para 152][Para 153].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's name appears as Fou Hou, Fou Huo and, at [Para 119], as "Huar Oun"; the union is named in truncated form at [Para 1] and never in full. OCR has merged or split sentences across [Para 11], [Para 12] and [Para 16], and the conciliation date is printed both as 13 September 2016 [Para 15][Para 27] and in a garbled form at [Para 14]. The award reproduces Prakas No. 330 in full as an annex from [Para 264] to [Para 278]; that annex is heavily corrupted, its staffing table at [Para 267] is unreadable as a table, and the French and Latin drug names interleave with Khmer. No monetary total is computed anywhere; the sums in issue are the USD 10 attendance bonus and 2,000 riel allowances printed in the claims.
