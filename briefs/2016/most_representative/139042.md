# Award No. 131/16 — YKK MAS (Cambodia) Garment MFG Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 14 July 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun for the employer party, Toun Si Phan for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139042

## 1. Facts
YKK MAS (Cambodia) Garment MFG, a garment-cutting factory in Trapeang Chrey village, Kakab commune, Pur Senchey district, Phnom Penh, employed about 737 workers and had a local union of the Cambodian Labour Solidarity Union with 258 members, which was not the most-representative union in the company; the Khmer Youth Power Union Federation also appeared for the workers. [Para 1][Para 55][Para 56][Para 59][Para 61] Workers struck from 31 May 2016; conciliation on 2 June 2016 failed on all six points and the report reached the Secretariat on 6 June 2016. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 16] The panel issued Order No. 011/16 on 8 June 2016 requiring the strike to stop and workers to return from 9 June 2016; it continued, but on 15 June 2016 the panel was told the workers had returned. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 22][Para 23] Three of the six issues (2, 3 and 5) were settled by agreement at the hearing, leaving 1, 4 and 6. [Para 26][Para 27]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a transport and accommodation allowance of USD 12 per month? [Para 63][Para 91]
4. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of 6,000 riel per day for work on public holidays and on Sundays? [Para 70][Para 113]
6. Whether the employer must suspend the employment contracts of the four workers who used violence against a fellow worker on 26 May 2016? [Para 76][Para 145]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** workers spend about USD 15 to USD 20 a month on rent, and other companies pay more than the USD 7 the Labour Law notification provides; workers who work on holidays still need to eat, so 6,000 riel a day is owed for holiday and Sunday meals; and under Article 83 of the Labour Law violence against a fellow worker is serious misconduct, so the four who pushed and kicked Ms T must have their contracts suspended, the union asking for the same for Ms T for equality. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 66][Para 71][Para 72][Para 84][Para 86]
**Employer (respondent):** orders have fallen since 2015 and the company faces financial difficulty; it will negotiate the allowance with all unions in August 2016; it pays 3,000 riel for Sunday overtime meals although only 2,000 is required, and no holiday meal money because workers already receive holiday wages; and it cannot judge who was at fault in a fight, which is for the police and courts, so it has referred the matter to the Ministry. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 69][Para 73][Para 74][Para 75][Para 87][Para 88][Para 89][Para 90]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three remaining demands were refused; the order is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Minister of Labour. [Para 161][Para 162][Para 163][Para 164]

*Issue 1.* Point 1 of Notification No. 230 K.B/S.J.N of 25 July 2012 requires USD 7 per month as transport and accommodation allowance for workers in the textile, garment and footwear sector, and the parties agreed the employer pays exactly that. [Para 91][Para 92][Para 93] No provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, contract, agreement or past practice obliges the employer to pay USD 12, so the demand went beyond the law and was an interests dispute. [Para 94][Para 95] On an interests dispute the panel again applied Article 96(2) of the Labour Law, Articles 54 and 72 of the Law on Trade Unions of 17 May 2016 and Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099, which reserve the right to conclude a collective agreement, and hence to bring such a claim, to the most-representative union; following its award in case 118/16 and finding this union lacked that status, it declined to consider the claim. [Para 96][Para 102][Para 106][Para 107][Para 110][Para 111][Para 112]

*Issue 4.* On holiday meal money, Articles 137 and 164 of the Labour Law and Articles 2 and 4 of Prakas No. 10 SKBY of 4 February 1999 mean that a worker who works on a holiday earns one extra day's wage; no rule requires a separate meal payment, and none obliges 6,000 riel. [Para 113][Para 116][Para 119][Para 120][Para 125] The claim was therefore beyond the law and an interests dispute, and was not considered. [Para 126][Para 128] On Sunday meals, Notification No. 041/11 K.B/S.J.N of 7 March 2011 requires 2,000 riel or one free meal for voluntary overtime, and the employer pays 3,000 riel, which is better than the notification requires. [Para 129][Para 131][Para 134][Para 136][Para 138][Para 139] The claim for 6,000 riel was again beyond the law and an interests dispute, and the panel relied on its reasoning under issue 1. [Para 141][Para 142][Para 144]

*Issue 6.* Article 2 of the Labour Law gives the employer the right to organise and manage the enterprise, exercised lawfully and reasonably. [Para 145][Para 149] Earlier awards held that an employer may decide whether and when to impose discipline, because discipline is a management prerogative, provided the decision is lawful and reasonable. [Para 151][Para 153] No law compels an employer to impose a particular sanction when a worker commits a fault, and here the employer did not single out any worker unequally: it contacted the Ministry on the day of the incident, cooperated with the competent authority, and is waiting to see who caused the violence to avoid punishing the wrong person. [Para 156][Para 157][Para 158][Para 160] The panel therefore found no unlawful or unreasonable exercise of discretion and dismissed the demand for suspension of the four workers' contracts. [Para 160][Para 161][Para 163]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a notification or Prakas fixes a minimum allowance and the employer already pays at least that minimum, a demand for a higher figure rests on no legal, contractual or practice obligation, is an interests dispute, and will not be considered unless brought by the most-representative union, which alone may conclude a collective agreement and obtain an award replacing it. [Para 91][Para 94][Para 106][Para 126] An employer's decision whether and when to discipline workers who fought at the workplace is an exercise of management prerogative under Article 2; referring the matter to the Ministry, police and courts and waiting for their finding before sanctioning is neither unlawful nor unreasonable, and no law obliges the employer to suspend contracts. [Para 149][Para 151][Para 156][Para 159]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the employer's 3,000 riel Sunday meal payment is better than the 2,000 riel required, and that waiting for the competent authority does not mean the employer will impose no discipline at all, are observations on the evidence rather than grounds of the order. [Para 138][Para 139][Para 159]
