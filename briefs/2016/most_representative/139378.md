# Award No. 223/16 — Unique Ta Technology (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 November 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mar Samborna (employer-appointed, dissenting), An Non (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (presiding) · Doc ID 139378

## 1. Facts
Unique Ta Technology (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a wire and cable manufacturer at Ang Snuol employing about 427 workers, barred five union leaders — Mr K (Kim Visal), Mr B (Pan Bora), Mr T (Sreng Thearith), Mr C (Mao Chit) and Mr S (Phea Kosal) — from entering the plant from 27 September 2016. [Para 81][Para 85][Para 98] A union had been elected on 11 September 2016 with these five as its officers, and the plant then had no most representative union. [Para 82][Para 193] The workers had struck from 26 to 28 September and again from 4 to 14 October 2016 over the size of Pchum Ben loans; the Council had ordered the strike suspended. [Para 31][Para 104]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether Mr K and Mr B were dismissed, or their fixed-duration contracts were simply not renewed? [Para 161]
2. Whether the five were protected union candidates under Article 67 of the Law on Trade Unions? [Para 193]
3. Whether the dismissals amounted to anti-union discrimination contrary to Article 62? [Para 213]
4. Whether the employer dismissed the five lawfully under the Law on Labour? [Para 233]
5. Whether the employer owed a 2,000 riel daily lunch allowance? [Para 274]
6. Whether the employer had to cut the attendance bonus pro rata for permitted absences? [Para 297]
7. Whether the employer owed a 2,000 riel travel allowance for work on holidays and Sundays? [Para 317]
8. Whether the workers were entitled to wages and benefits during the strike? [Para 325]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The five were barred from 27 September 2016 because they had stood for union office and led the strike; they had committed no fault and sought reinstatement with back pay. [Para 85][Para 103][Para 224]
**Employer (respondent):** Mr K and Mr B were on fixed-duration contracts that expired on 28 September and 5 October 2016 and were simply not renewed; the other three were dismissed for serious misconduct on 23 September 2016, having switched off machines and halted the production line in breach of the internal rules and Article 83. [Para 91][Para 99][Para 235]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to reinstate all five with wages and benefits from the date of dismissal to the date of return; the lunch allowance, the pro-rata bonus, the holiday travel allowance and the strike-wage claims were refused. [Para 344][Para 345][Para 347][Para 348]

Dismissal or non-renewal. Article 73(3) allows fixed-duration contracts to end by expiry, but the employer barred Mr K and Mr B from the premises on 27 September, before their contracts ran out on 28 September and 5 October 2016. [Para 87][Para 89][Para 90][Para 161] Following Case 096/16 (Tak San Enterprise), the panel held that ending a fixed-duration contract before its expiry at the employer's sole initiative is a dismissal, not a non-renewal. [Para 163][Para 164]

Protected status. Article 67 of the Law on Trade Unions protects candidates in union elections for 45 days before and 45 days after the ballot, provided the union has notified the employer and copied the notification to the Ministry. [Para 174][Para 175][Para 176] The election was on 11 September 2016 and the dismissals on 27 September, sixteen days later, so the first two conditions were met. [Para 198][Para 199][Para 201] The third was not: the employer denied receiving the notice, the workers could not produce proof of delivery, and their claim to have copied the provincial labour department on 11 September failed because that date was a Sunday and no other document supported it. [Para 203][Para 207][Para 209][Para 210][Para 211] The five were therefore not protected employees. [Para 212]

Anti-union discrimination. Article 62 of the Law on Trade Unions and Articles 12 and 279 of the Law on Labour forbid an employer from using union membership, office or activity as the ground for discipline or termination. [Para 213][Para 215][Para 216] The workers relied on the timing alone, but produced nothing showing the employer knew of the election or of their offices; the panel rejected the claim. [Para 229][Para 230][Para 232]

Lawfulness of the dismissal. The employer invoked Article 83 serious misconduct, but offered no specific evidence that the five had caused the stoppage. [Para 245][Para 246] The panel found the real ground was their participation in the strike and their role in demanding larger Pchum Ben loans. [Para 247][Para 248] The right to strike is guaranteed by Article 37 of the Constitution and by the Law on Labour, Article 332(2) requires workers to recover their jobs when a strike ends, and Article 333 forbids any sanction for taking part. [Para 249][Para 253][Para 254][Para 256] No court had ruled the strike unlawful as Article 337 requires. [Para 264][Para 267] Dismissal for strike participation was therefore unlawful, and following Case 337/14 (Jin Chen Yan) the panel ordered reinstatement with back pay. [Para 261][Para 270][Para 274]

The benefit claims. The lunch allowance, the pro-rata attendance bonus and the holiday travel allowance were all found to rest on no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice. [Para 274][Para 320] The panel treated them as interest disputes, which under Prakas No. 099 Article 43(1) an award would convert into a collective agreement binding all 427 workers, so they may be decided only where the claimant union holds most representative status or Article 72 applies — which it did not. [Para 288][Para 291][Para 292][Para 294]

Strike wages. Article 332(1) suspends the contract during a strike and states that no wages are paid; Article 334 restores wages only where the employer hired replacements, which was not alleged. [Para 325][Para 326][Para 337][Para 339] The panel also noted the strike had not been notified in advance. [Para 341] The claim failed. [Para 342][Para 343]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Barring workers from the plant before their fixed-duration contracts expire converts non-renewal into a dismissal [Para 163][Para 164]. Such a dismissal is unlawful where its real ground is participation in a strike: the right to strike is guaranteed, Article 333 voids any sanction imposed for taking part, and only a court may declare a strike unlawful under Article 337 [Para 253][Para 254][Para 267] — the Council will not assume that characterisation for itself. Union-candidate protection under Article 67 depends on the union proving that it notified the employer and copied the Ministry [Para 174][Para 176]; a delivery the evidence cannot support leaves the candidate unprotected even inside the 45-day window [Para 211][Para 212].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that interest claims reaching beyond the law can only be adjudicated through a most representative union, since the resulting award binds every worker and displaces their right to bargain or strike over the same demand [Para 291][Para 292], and that striking workers are not paid because the contract is suspended, not terminated [Para 326].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award carries a signed dissent by the employer-appointed arbitrator at [Para 349]–[Para 372], which is part of the document but not the panel's reasoning: it holds the strike unlawful for want of the seven-day notice under Article 324 and for the machine stoppages, and would have refused reinstatement to Mr T, Mr C and Mr S outright and treated Mr K and Mr B as non-renewals [Para 364][Para 366][Para 369]. The point numbering is internally inconsistent: 19 points were conciliated [Para 27][Para 29], 14 are recorded as settled and two withdrawn [Para 37][Para 38], leaving five — but points 5 and 14 each appear twice with different content, once as settled and once as outstanding [Para 37][Para 39]. Claimant numbers also vary, with 12 authorised representatives [Para 83] and nine attending [Para 84][Para 157]. OCR damage is severe throughout: "កីមា កី សាល" for Kim Visal and duplicated clauses at [Para 158], [Para 262] and [Para 292], while the company appears both as Unique Ta Technology and as "Unixta Technology Co., Ltd" in the claimants' own documents [Para 65][Para 69]. No monetary total is computed; the sums in issue are the 2,000 riel allowances and the USD 20 attendance bonus [Para 126][Para 301].
