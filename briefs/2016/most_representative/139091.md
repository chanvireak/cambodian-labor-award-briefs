# Award No. 191/16 — E Cho Bes Factory (Arbitration Council, 1 September 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, employer-appointed; An Nan, worker-appointed; Aing Eng Tong, chair) · non-binding award · Doc ID 139091

## 1. Facts
E Cho Bes Factory sews shirts at Prek Ta Pring, Sitbo commune, Sa'ang district, Kandal province, with about 595 workers. [Para 44] The Democratic Union of Khmer Garment Workers at the factory is registered and holds a most-representative certificate from the Ministry of Labour dated 26 August 2015, but its leadership's term had expired. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 49] In February 2016 the employer told some workers to stop work for lack of orders, paid 50% of wages and full other benefits, and did so without seeking the labour inspector's authorisation. [Para 52][Para 55][Para 105] Ninety-five workers transferred their rights to the union federation by letter of 3 February 2016. [Para 48][Para 82] The employer was absent at the first hearing because neighbouring factory workers were striking and blocking the road, and the hearing was moved to 16 August 2016. [Para 16][Para 17]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a further 50% of wages so that the suspended workers receive 100% for the period of suspension in February 2016, which was carried out without the labour inspector's authorisation? [Para 3][Para 52]

The workers withdrew their second demand — that the employer accept a binding award — at the hearing, so the panel considered only issue 1. [Para 19][Para 20]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer suspended the employment contracts without the labour inspector's permission; the 17 October 2014 agreement limiting pay to 50% during a suspension is contrary to Article 13 of the Labour Law because it gives a benefit lower than the law, and the failure to provide work is the employer's own fault under Article 83. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 59]
**Employer (respondent):** it did not suspend the contracts at all, because a suspension would cost the workers their wages and other benefits; instead it gave them leave with 50% pay, under an agreement with the workers that was registered with the Ministry of Labour. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 62][Para 63]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer must pay a further 50% of wages so that the 95 claimant workers receive 100% for the suspension period in February 2016, calculated on the actual number of days each worker was suspended. [Para 135][Para 136]

On representation the panel held that Article 54(1) of the Trade Union Law of 17 May 2016 reserves exclusive standing to the most representative union, following Award No. 180/16 (Cement Cambodia Chakrey Ting). [Para 83][Para 85][Para 87] It found the factory union was the most representative union, but that the workers themselves admitted its leadership's term had expired, and that they had produced no evidence by the filing deadline that they had applied to the Ministry to recognise a new leadership. [Para 49][Para 51][Para 77][Para 80][Para 81] The president therefore had no right to represent the union, since a union only holds the rights the law confers while its registration and office-holders are current under Articles 11 and 12(2) of the Trade Union Law. [Para 66][Para 71][Para 72][Para 82][Para 90] A federation official, however, held a letter of appointment of 25 July 2016 and attended the hearing, so under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 — which allows a party to be represented by another person holding written authority — he could represent the 95 workers who had transferred their rights. [Para 47][Para 97][Para 98][Para 102]

On the substance the panel applied Article 71(11) of the Labour Law: a suspension of enterprise activity for serious economic or material difficulty must be placed under the supervision of the labour inspector and may not exceed two months. [Para 107][Para 108] Following Awards No. 58/07 (Star Scot Wear) and No. 08/07 (Seav Kinh), it held that this supervision means the employer must give notice and obtain the inspector's prior authorisation before suspending. [Para 109][Para 110][Para 112] Both parties agreed there was genuinely no work in February 2016 and that the employer never sought authorisation, so the suspension was procedurally unlawful. [Para 113][Para 114][Para 115]

The panel then held the 17 October 2014 agreement void: point 2, under which the union leadership agreed that the company pay 50% base wage on days when there was no work, falls below the Labour Law, and Article 13(1) makes any rule arising from a unilateral decision, contract or convention that does not comply with the law null by operation of law. [Para 116][Para 119][Para 120][Para 121][Para 125] Article 72(1), under which a suspension relieves the employer of the duty to pay wages, applies only where the suspension was carried out lawfully; where it was not, the employer owes 100% of wages and the other benefits even though no work was provided. [Para 126][Para 127][Para 128][Para 130][Para 132][Para 133] The panel accordingly ordered the top-up. [Para 134][Para 135]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that suspends work for lack of orders without first notifying the labour inspector and obtaining authorisation acts unlawfully, and cannot rely on Article 72(1) of the Labour Law to withhold wages; a collective agreement fixing 50% pay for such a suspension is null under Article 13(1) because it falls below the law, so the employer owes full wages for the days of suspension. [Para 110][Para 121][Para 128][Para 130]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the absence of a functioning most representative union can obstruct workers' representation and touch their constitutional right to complain under Article 39 are general observations on the Trade Union Law, not the basis of the order, which rested on the federation official's written authority. [Para 91][Para 92]
