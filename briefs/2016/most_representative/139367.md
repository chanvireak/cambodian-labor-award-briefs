# Award No. 213/16 — C.P. Cambodia Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 13 October 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tai Seng, Tuon Si Phan, Pen Bun Chhea presiding) · Doc ID 139367

## 1. Facts
C.P. Cambodia Co., Ltd. ("C.P.") operates an animal-feed mill at Chak village, Baek Chan, Ang Snuol, Kandal, employing about 258 workers. [Para 81][Para 82] Its registered union, the Free Workers' Union of C.P. Factory, holds certificate No. 679 dated 14 December 2004, with leadership recognised for a sixth term on 3 November 2015. [Para 86] Four demands went to conciliation on 26 August 2016 without agreement, and the union withdrew the second at the hearing, leaving three. [Para 18][Para 27][Para 28] The plant distinguishes daily-rated "workers" from monthly-rated "staff", with different benefit entitlements. [Para 91][Para 92]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reclassify four workers from the CS-ton section to the silo section as monthly-rated staff paid like the premix preparers? [Para 153]
3. Whether the employer must pay wages equal to 48 hours' work where workers perform 24-hour holiday duty, as in previous years? [Para 178]
4. Whether the employer must restore the annual medical allowance of 3,000,000 riel and the funeral allowance of 1,200,000 riel for the 37 workers claiming them? [Para 216]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the four CS-ton workers have worked in the silo section for nearly two years doing the same tasks as the skilled silo staff, so they should be classified and paid as staff; 24-hour holiday duty has always drawn 48 hours' pay, and the 2014 declaration reducing it breaches the internal rules because no staff delegation was consulted; and from 2011 to 2015 the 37 claimants received the full 3,000,000 riel medical and 1,200,000 riel funeral allowances, which were cut only in 2016. [Para 97][Para 113][Para 124]
**Employer (respondent):** the four requested the move themselves and remain daily-rated cleaners of machinery; promotion to staff requires an internal evaluation of three to five years' seniority, output and skill, which they have not passed, and the silo section is fully staffed; holiday duty is standby, not overtime, since workers may sleep and are not required to work; and these allowances are benefits above the law, which the company may set by its own policy. [Para 99][Para 101][Para 116][Para 127]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the reclassification and the 48-hour holiday pay claims were rejected; the employer was ordered to apply Notification No. 030/15 on overtime, and to pay 3,000,000 riel annual medical allowance and 1,200,000 riel funeral allowance to 16 named workers, while the same claim by the other 21 was rejected. [Para 238][Para 239][Para 240][Para 241]

On representation, the panel applied Articles 54 and 11 of the Trade Union Law 2016 and found that, although no most representative union exists at C.P., the registered union's leadership attending the hearing could represent its own members, following Awards 180/16, 171/16, 148/16 and 153/16; the panel therefore considered claims only for union members. [Para 133][Para 146][Para 152]

On issue 1 the panel recognised the employer's right under Article 24 to manage the enterprise, including which sections are paid by output and which by fixed monthly wage, provided the right is exercised lawfully and reasonably. [Para 157][Para 159] Because it had ordered the employer to produce the internal evaluation records for the four workers and the employer produced none by the evidence deadline, the panel declined to presume the refusal lawful. [Para 170][Para 172] It nevertheless found the workers had asserted only orally that they performed staff-level work and had supplied no evidence of their conditions or of the silo staff's conditions to compare; on the burden of proof the claim failed. [Para 175][Para 176][Para 177]

On issue 3 the panel held that working on a public holiday is not overtime: under Article 164 and Articles 2 and 4 of Prakas No. 10 SKBY of 4 February 1999 a worker who works a holiday earns one additional day's wage, not a doubled day. [Para 184][Para 186][Para 187][Para 190] But 24 hours of duty against a normal 8-hour day leaves 16 hours, which is overtime under Article 137. [Para 202] Article 137 caps the normal day at 8 hours, and point 1 of Notification No. 030/15 of 27 February 2015 limits overtime to 2 hours a day and total daily hours to 10; a 16-hour overtime day is therefore unlawful, and the panel could not adopt the claimed 48-hour rate as a lawful baseline. [Para 204][Para 207][Para 209][Para 213] It rejected the demand and instead ordered compliance with the Notification. [Para 214][Para 215]

On issue 4 the panel split the 37 claimants. Sixteen appear in point 3 of the 5 April 2010 agreement between C.P. and the union, which upgraded 23 named workers to staff, and the employer's own list still classifies them as workers; the panel held they are staff and, following point 7 of the 28 October 2011 meeting minutes requiring equal treatment of old and new staff, entitled to the staff-level allowances. [Para 219][Para 221][Para 226][Para 227] The remaining 21 were treated by the employer as workers, and although they asserted they had historically received the same amounts, they produced no supporting evidence; on the burden of proof that claim was rejected. [Para 231][Para 234][Para 236][Para 237]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer's own written agreement with the union has upgraded named workers to staff, the employer cannot later rely on its internal payroll list to keep them on worker terms; the agreement fixes their category and with it the benefit scale applied to staff. [Para 219][Para 227] Separately, a claim that workers did staff-level work fails where the claimants offer only oral assertion and no comparative evidence of the two sets of working conditions, even if the employer has failed to produce the evaluation records it was ordered to file. [Para 175][Para 176]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's statement that working on a public holiday is not overtime, and earns one additional day's wage rather than double pay, was not necessary to the order, which rested on the unlawfulness of 16 hours of overtime in a day. [Para 187][Para 190]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
OCR damage is severe and structural: long Khmer passages are duplicated (Paras 121–123, 128, 216–217), name lists are mangled, and an English-language block of nonsense text appears inside the 37-name table. [Para 124] The award states the claimant group as 37 workers but the reasons and orders address 16 and 21, which sum correctly to 37 only if every listed name is counted once — several names differ between the table and the order. [Para 216][Para 229][Para 240] Paragraph 151 is internally contradictory: it reads that the union leadership "has the right" to represent members while ending with a negative particle, yet paragraph 152 proceeds on the basis that representation was accepted; the order follows paragraph 152. [Para 151][Para 152] The award date is given as 13 October 2016 and the panel composition prints the chair's name as Pen Bun Chhea and, in the signature block, Pen Bun Chheam. [Para 1][Para 243] The medical allowance appears as both "3,000,000 riel" and "03 million riel", and the funeral allowance as "1.2 million riel". [Para 121][Para 228]
