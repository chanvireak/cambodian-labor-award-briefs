# Award No. 184/16 — Quin Tha Major Industries Steel Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 August 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambor Nas, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Kong Phalla, president) · Doc ID 139085

## 1. Facts
Quin Tha Major Industries Steel Co., Ltd ("QMI") is a garment-cutting and sewing enterprise in Ang Snuol district, Kandal province, employing about 4,250 workers [Para 62][Para 63]. Three federations — the Khmer Workers' Spirit Federation (ស្មារតី), the Khmer Workers' Justice Federation (សច្ចធម៌) and the Khmer Workers' Hope Federation (ក្តីសង្ឃឹម) — held factory union elections on 24 May 2016 and received rights transfers from 285 workers on 22 June 2016 [Para 64][Para 65]. Conciliation of their fifteen demands on 25 July 2016 failed entirely and the file reached the Arbitration Council on 27 July 2016 [Para 23][Para 24].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer should pay a midday meal allowance of 4,000 riel, counted from 07:00 to 16:00? [Para 3]
2. Whether the employer should pay USD 15 per month as a milk allowance to mothers of children under 18 months? [Para 4]
3. Whether wages should again be paid on the Saturday falling on the 5th of the month? [Para 5]
4. Whether the employer should keep sufficient medicine for workers? [Para 6]
5. Whether music should be played for workers throughout the working day? [Para 7]
6. Whether the employer should provide hygienic drinking water and place hot-water dispensers for workers as it does for staff? [Para 8]
7. Whether workers should be able to have medical certificates endorsed in each building as before? [Para 9]
8. Whether the employer should apply annual leave plus additional days under Article 166 of the Labour Law? [Para 10]
9. Whether night-shift work should be paid at 200 per cent of the day rate? [Para 11]
10. Whether workers should be allowed to leave five minutes early to line up? [Para 12]
11. Whether the employer should build a roofed walkway from Building 1 to Building 3 for mealtimes? [Para 13]
12. Whether reworked defective garments should count toward the production bonus? [Para 14]
13. Whether the employer should pay in full for treatment when a worker falls ill during working hours? [Para 15]
14. Whether the employer should deduct 2,000 riel a month in dues from members of the three unions? [Para 16]
15. Whether the travel and accommodation allowance should be raised by USD 8 on top of the existing USD 7? [Para 17]

Issues 4 and 8 were settled by agreement at the hearing and recorded in the award; the panel stated it would consider the remaining thirteen [Para 28][Para 29].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Each demand mirrors conditions already provided by other factories — meal or food allowances at neighbouring plants, higher travel allowances elsewhere, and pre-2016 practice at QMI itself — and the company has the means to pay [Para 71][Para 79][Para 131].
**Employer (respondent):** The company applies the Labour Law and cannot meet the demands: no law requires a milk allowance, the pay date was moved to the 10th because banks cannot be drawn on Saturdays, and the company has only recently begun operating [Para 76][Para 80][Para 132].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed / no jurisdiction to consider — the panel refused to consider Case No. 184/16 in its entirety, issuing no order on the thirteen contested points [Para 144][Para 145].
Before reaching any individual demand the panel examined the standing of the original complainants and their representatives [Para 133]. It read Article 54(1) of the Law on Trade Unions of 17 May 2016 as giving only the most representative union the exclusive right to conclude collective agreements and to resolve collective labour disputes, including carriage of a dispute before the Arbitration Council [Para 134][Para 136][Para 137]. It followed the same interpretation it had adopted in Award No. 180/16 (Cement Cambodia Chakrey Ting), Award No. 171/16 (Jewellery Co., Ltd) and Award No. 183/16 (Yi Da Manufacturer) [Para 135][Para 138][Para 139].
On the facts, QMI already had a most representative union — the Democratic Garment Workers' Union (សហជីពប្រជាធិបតេយ្យកម្មករកាត់ដេរ), certified on 8 June 2016 and valid until 8 June 2018 [Para 141]. The 285 workers who transferred their rights to the three federations did not thereby give those federations representative status, and neither the workers individually nor persons authorised by them could represent the workforce in a collective dispute [Para 140][Para 143]. The panel therefore declined the case [Para 144].
The award is expressly non-binding: it becomes final eight days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Minister of Labour through the Council's secretariat [Para 30][Para 146].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A federation that has obtained rights transfers from individual workers cannot bring their collective dispute to the Arbitration Council where the enterprise already has a union certified as most representative under Article 54(1) of the Law on Trade Unions (2016); the panel will decline to consider the case before examining any of the demands on their merits, however many points are in dispute. [Para 137][Para 142]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel recorded the two points settled by agreement — medicine supplied in accordance with Prakas No. 330 dated 6 December 2000, and annual leave already exceeding Article 166 — without those agreements affecting its refusal to consider the case [Para 84][Para 103].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel states that thirteen points remain for consideration [Para 29] but enumerates only twelve, omitting issue 12, which it nonetheless discusses at [Para 115] to [Para 122]. The employer's name is printed inconsistently ("ឃ្វីន ធ៏", "ឃ្លីន ធ៍", "ឃ្ងីន ធ៍" for the same company) and is rendered here from the "QMI" abbreviation its own letters use [Para 20][Para 40][Para 62]. The employer's representative appears as "លោកុល វុត្ថា" in the header and "លោក Kol Voutha" in the evidence list [Para 1][Para 33]. Several paragraphs carry untranslated OCR fragments in place of Khmer text, including the signature block [Para 33][Para 90][Para 147].
