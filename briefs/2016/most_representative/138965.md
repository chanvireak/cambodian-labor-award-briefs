# Award No. 005/16 — YLT International Ltd (Arbitration Council, 11 March 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Pen Bunchhea, chair) · Doc ID 138965

## 1. Facts
YLT International Ltd, at Satho village, Rumchek commune, Preah Sdach district, Prey Veng, produces articles from imported and locally grown wood and employed about 200 workers [Para 56]. On 27 November 2015 the company and the workers signed an agreement at the Prey Veng provincial Department of Labour and Vocational Training covering 21 demands; by the hearing date the company had still not implemented it, and 18 of the 21 points were later settled, leaving agreement points 3 (wages paid by the 10th of the following month) and 8 (a company doctor, especially during overtime) [Para 60][Para 61][Para 66][Para 69][Para 70]. Separately, from November 2015 the company cut the daily wage of four union members from USD 8.50 to USD 6.00 and withheld the USD 13 monthly top-up given to other workers, and on 27 November 2015 it deducted one day's wage and a USD 10 attendance bonus from three union leaders who attended conciliation at the provincial department [Para 74][Para 75][Para 189].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must respect and implement the agreement concluded on 27 November 2015 at the Prey Veng provincial Department of Labour, in particular demands 3 and 8? [Para 91][Para 93]
2. Whether the reduction of the four workers' daily wage and the failure to add USD 13 to their monthly base wage were related to anti-union discrimination? [Para 117]
3. Whether the employer must repay and restore the daily wage it cut from November 2015 to date? [Para 132]
4. Whether the employer must add USD 13 to the monthly base wage of the four workers? [Para 150]
5. Whether the employer must restore the wages and the USD 10 attendance bonus deducted on 27 November 2015 from workers who attended conciliation? [Para 182]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer had already agreed to all 21 demands in writing at the labour department and must perform the agreement; the wage cuts and the withheld USD 13 came right after the employer learned of the union election on 20 October 2015 and were anti-union discrimination, and the 27 November deductions punished union leaders for attending conciliation, which Article 306 and Prakas 305 clause 10 forbid [Para 62][Para 77][Para 84][Para 192].
**Employer (respondent):** it could not yet perform the agreement because its interpreter had not translated accurately from Khmer to Korean and it needed more time; the wage reduction was a "technical matter" it would investigate and report back to the director; the USD 13 was not a general increase but applied only to workers whose base wage was below USD 143 per month [Para 63][Para 64][Para 79][Para 81].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to respect and implement agreement demands 3 and 8 [Para 196]; to repay and restore the daily wage of USD 8.50 for the four named workers from November 2015 to date [Para 197]; and to restore the wages and USD 10 attendance bonus deducted on 27 November 2015 [Para 199]. The claim for a USD 13 monthly top-up was dismissed [Para 198].

On issue 1, the panel applied Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code, following Awards 31/12, 271/14 and 307/17: a contract arises from mutual consent and binds the parties, who must consult before altering or extinguishing obligations [Para 93][Para 99][Para 104]. It held that a translation error is the employer's own responsibility, since the employer chose and paid the interpreter, and that needing time is no excuse for agreeing to terms and then not performing them [Para 109][Para 110]. The panel therefore ordered performance of the two outstanding agreement points rather than renegotiation [Para 114][Para 115].

On issue 2, the panel applied Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law and the standing rule that the party alleging discrimination bears the burden of proof [Para 117][Para 119]. Although the wage cut followed the election notice, the workers produced no documents showing the employer acted on union grounds, and the employer attributed the cut to a technical matter; the panel found no basis to infer anti-union motive [Para 130][Para 131].

On issue 3, however, the panel applied Articles 126 to 129, which prohibit wage deductions outright and make any agreement permitting them void [Para 132][Para 142][Para 143]. Finding that the daily wage was in fact cut from USD 8.50 to USD 6.00 for the four workers and that the employer never gave a specific explanation, the panel held the reduction improper and ordered repayment to at least USD 8.50 per day from November 2015 [Para 144][Para 148][Para 149].

On issue 4, the panel found no law, collective agreement, internal regulation, contract or past practice obliging the employer to add USD 13 for these four workers, and the workers adduced no evidence that the increase was general; the claim was an interests dispute, which only a most-representative union may bring, and this local union was not one [Para 163][Para 164][Para 174][Para 178][Para 180].

On issue 5, the panel applied Article 306 of the Labour Law and clause 10 of Prakas 305, which protect negotiating representatives from wage and benefit deductions during conciliation, following Award 135/10 [Para 182][Para 185][Para 186][Para 187]. Because the three were registered union leaders with a valid invitation, the deductions of one day's wage and the USD 10 bonus on 27 November 2015 were improper and were ordered restored [Para 191][Para 193][Para 195].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that signs a collective agreement at the labour department is bound by it, and defective translation or a claimed need for more time does not excuse non-performance; the remedy is performance of the outstanding points, not unilateral postponement [Para 109][Para 110][Para 114]. Wage deductions are prohibited except in the cases listed in Articles 126 to 128, so a reduction the employer cannot justify even on non-union grounds must be repaid at the original rate [Para 142][Para 148]. Union leaders attending conciliation on a valid invitation may not lose wages or bonuses for doing so [Para 193].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that although the timing of the wage cut suggested a link to the union election, absent documentary proof it would not infer anti-union motive — separating the discrimination question from the legality of the deduction itself [Para 130][Para 131]. It also noted that an interests award becomes the collective agreement for one year and extinguishes the workers' right to strike over the same demands [Para 173].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The list of issues in dispute is cut off mid-page at the head of the award ("បញ្ហា ក្នុង វិវាទ ... [truncated]"), so the numbering was reconstructed from the demands recited at Paras 3–9 and the panel's own headings [Para 2]. The award date is printed as 11 March 2016 on the face while the parties agreed to postpone issuance from 04 February 2016 to that date [Para 1][Para 28]. Amounts appear in mixed formats — "8.50", "8,50" and "៨.៥០" for the same daily wage — and the employer's own submission at Para 110 states the workforce as 900 where the findings state about 200 [Para 73][Para 84][Para 110]. Large passages are duplicated by OCR (the finding at Para 181 repeats Para 149, and Para 194 repeats Para 193), and the minutes of the 27 November 2015 agreement are cited under two different dates [Para 35][Para 48]. The company name is transliterated as "YLT International" in the English passages and variously in Khmer elsewhere [Para 19][Para 56].
