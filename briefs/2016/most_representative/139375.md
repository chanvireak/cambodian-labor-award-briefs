# Award No. 221/16 — Ly Ly Sea (Arbitration Council, 26 October 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Kol Vuthy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Aing Eng Thong (presiding) · Doc ID 139375

## 1. Facts
Mr V (Vun Mon), a sewing-machine mechanic and one of only two in the factory, was hired on 22 February 2016 with no written contract of employment. [Para 39][Para 44] On 27 August 2016 the employer gave him a very short deadline to complete the swap of rented sewing machines for newly purchased ones; he replied that the time allowed was too short to finish the work. [Para 42][Para 43] The employer told him that if he could not complete it on time he would be dismissed, and dismissed him that day without prior notice and without paying his final wages. [Para 40][Para 45]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the Council could proceed to hear the dispute in the absence of the employer? [Para 49]
2. Whether the employer owed Mr V wages and other benefits under the Law on Labour when it terminated his contract? [Para 90]

The panel framed the second as its sole numbered issue in dispute, having first disposed of the procedural questions of absence and representation. [Para 48][Para 90]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The dismissal had no valid reason — Mr V had never committed a fault or received a warning — and the claim was confined to the wages and benefits the Labour Law guarantees on termination of contract. [Para 46][Para 47] Fifty-two workers had transferred their right to be represented to the Workers' Spirit Trade Union Federation of Cambodia by letter of 1 September 2016. [Para 86]
**Employer (respondent):** The employer did not attend. Through a telephone call to the secretariat it said the local union was not registered with the Ministry and therefore could not bring a dispute to the Council, so it would not appear. [Para 19][Para 53] Its stated ground for the dismissal, recorded by the panel, was that Mr V could not complete the assigned work within the short deadline set. [Para 108]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to pay 15 days of wages and benefits in lieu of notice, 7 days of wages and benefits as severance, 7 days as moral damages, the outstanding paid annual leave, and the final wage; the award states entitlements in days only and computes no monetary total. [Para 146][Para 151][Para 115][Para 123][Para 129]

Absence and representation. Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 permits the Council to continue and issue an award where a party duly invited fails to appear without valid excuse. [Para 49] The invitation of 6 October 2016 particularised the date, place and contact numbers, was delivered by hand on 7 October 2016, and was accepted by the employer although it refused to sign; the secretariat also telephoned on the hearing day. [Para 16][Para 52][Para 53] The employer's unregistered-union objection was held not to justify non-attendance. [Para 54] Because Ly Ly Sea had no most representative union, the federation could not claim the exclusive right of representation under Article 54 of the Law on Trade Unions; but Article 19 of Prakas 099 allows a party to be represented by any person holding its written authority, and 52 workers had given that authority. [Para 57][Para 80][Para 82][Para 83][Para 86] The panel accordingly recognised 58 claimants. [Para 88][Para 89]

The contract. Article 67(7) of the Law on Labour requires a fixed-duration contract to be in writing and provides that, if it is not, the contract becomes one of indefinite duration. [Para 93][Para 94] The panel applied that rule, following Case 183/14 (San Long), and held Mr V to be on an indefinite-duration contract. [Para 96][Para 100]

The dismissal. Article 74 allows termination at one party's will on written notice, but never without a suitable reason relating to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the operational needs of the enterprise. [Para 101][Para 103] The panel found that the employer's ground — inability to complete the machine exchange within a short deadline the employer itself had set — was not such a reason, and that no notice had been given. [Para 108][Para 109][Para 114]

The heads of claim. Service from 22 February 2016 to 27 August 2016 was 6 months and 5 days, which under Article 75 attracted 15 days' notice, so payment in lieu was due under Articles 75 and 77. [Para 112][Para 113][Para 115] Severance under Article 89 for service between six and twelve months was 7 days of wages and benefits. [Para 116][Para 123] Moral damages under Article 91, a head distinct from notice pay and severance, were fixed at the same 7 days. [Para 124][Para 126][Para 129] Unused annual leave was payable under Articles 166 and 167. [Para 135][Para 140] Final wages and all allowances fell due within the period fixed by Article 116. [Para 142][Para 145]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker engaged without a written fixed-duration contract holds an indefinite-duration contract under Article 67(7) [Para 96][Para 100], and dismissal for failing to meet a short deadline the employer set unilaterally is not a reason of aptitude, conduct or operational necessity under Article 74 [Para 105][Para 109]; the employer then owes notice pay, Article 89 severance, Article 91 damages and terminal benefits [Para 113][Para 121][Para 129]. The Council may hear the case in the employer's absence once a particularised invitation has been delivered and received [Para 49][Para 52], and an objection that the claimant union is unregistered is no excuse for staying away [Para 54].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that where no most representative union exists, the absence of that status can obstruct workers' right to representation and so touch the constitutional right to complain and claim reparation under Article 39 of the Constitution [Para 65][Para 66], and that in such enterprises workers may represent themselves or authorise a local union or another person to act for them [Para 68].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
No monetary amount appears anywhere in this award, so the entitlements could only be reported in days. The panel gives 58 claimants at [Para 89] while the transfer letter carries 52 names and six workers attended [Para 88]. Article 116 is printed as "83 hours" in the quoted text at [Para 142] and as "48 (eighty-three) hours" at [Para 145]. OCR damage is heavier than usual: [Para 90] repeats a clause mid-sentence, [Para 101] renders "indefinite duration" as "ជីវវេលា ចិន កំណត់", and [Para 137] is a stray character. The three arbitrators appear only in Khmer script on the face of the award, so the Latin spellings above are transliterations, not printed forms; the claimant's name also is printed both as វុន ម៉ុន and with the final vowel lost, at [Para 144].
