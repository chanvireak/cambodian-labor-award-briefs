# Award No. 041/16 — Sun Bes Garment (Arbitration Council, 11 March 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ang Eng Tong, chair; Chhiv Phirom, appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna, appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 138988

## 1. Facts
Sun Bes Garment (ក្រុមហ៊ុន សាន់ បេស ហ្គាមេន), a garment factory in Ang Snuol district, Kandal province, employed about 1,560 workers, 95 percent of them women. [Para 31][Para 32] The Khmer Youth Union Federation and its enterprise-level union at the company, registered with the Ministry of Labour on 29 September 2010, filed a collective complaint seeking improved working conditions; conciliation at the Kandal labour office on 12 February 2016 settled four of six points and left two, which reached the Council on 16 February 2016. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 26][Para 33] The disputed acts were the refusal to raise the daily meal allowance and the refusal to pay a monthly milk allowance for workers' children. [Para 3][Para 5]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to pay an additional 500 riel a day for lunch, bringing the allowance to 2,000 riel, as the workers demand? [Para 54]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to pay 15 US dollars a month as a milk allowance for the children of workers aged 18 months to 3 years? [Para 68]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** 1,500 riel a day does not buy a lunch, since a plate of rice costs 2,000 riel and a dessert another 500; they asked for 1,500 riel more (3,000 riel in all), or failing that 500 riel more for 2,000 riel, and noted that no agreement governs the meal allowance. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39][Para 40][Para 41]
**Employer (respondent):** the lunch money is a voluntary contribution, not a statutory duty; the company already grants benefits above the law — 10 dollars a month in travel and accommodation money, a 17-dollar attendance bonus, 2,000 riel meal money for overtime, a 3-to-5-dollar skill allowance and a 30-to-80-dollar target bonus — and has built a nursery and breastfeeding room which the workers do not use. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 51]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider both demands and awarded nothing in riel or dollars. [Para 67][Para 75][Para 76]

On issue 1, the panel first established the facts: both sides agreed that the company pays 1,500 riel a day for lunch as a matter of daily practice. [Para 37][Para 54] It then found that no provision of the Labour Law, no collective agreement, no internal rule, no employment contract, no agreement between the parties and no past practice obliges the employer to pay an additional 500 riel. [Para 55] A demand for a benefit that no instrument and no practice supports is an interest dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍), not a rights dispute. [Para 56]

On issue 2, the panel reached the same finding on the milk allowance: neither the Labour Law, a collective agreement, internal rules, the contract, an agreement nor past practice obliged the employer to pay it, and both parties agreed the company had never paid it. [Para 44][Para 69] This too was classified as an interest dispute. [Para 70]

Having characterised both demands as interest disputes, the panel applied its own jurisprudence on Article 43 of Prakas No. 099: an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement and binds every worker in the enterprise for one year, so the Council may decide such a dispute only where the union bringing it is the most representative union, or several unions together represent more than half the workforce. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 62] It cited its earlier awards in Case 036/14 (Great Union Cambodia Garment), 011 (Pu Khien), 66/11 (In Han Sung), 169/11 (Fortune Thy V), and 024/15 (Wai Flowl Garment (Cambodia) Ltd) to the same effect. [Para 61][Para 64] The claimant union held a registration certificate from the Ministry of Labour but had no certificate of most-representative status. [Para 34][Para 65] The panel therefore held that the complainant lacked the legal standing to bring an interest dispute and refused to consider either demand. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 72][Para 74]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An arbitration panel will entertain an interest dispute — a demand for a benefit that no law, collective agreement, internal rule, contract or past practice requires — only where the union bringing it is the most representative union in the enterprise, or unions together representing more than half the workers; a union's registration certificate from the Ministry of Labour does not by itself confer that standing, and without it the panel declines to consider the demand. [Para 62][Para 65][Para 66]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel accepted as true the workers' account that bringing children to the company nursery is genuinely difficult and dangerous in crowded hired transport, and that no one would be responsible for a child hurt on the way, but held it could not weigh those reasons because the union was not most representative. [Para 72][Para 73]

[Para 56] The company's registration is given as certificate No. 2563 dated 30 April 2013 at [Para 23] but as Inv. 0529 E/2010 dated 10 March 2010 at [Para 31]; the headcount is rendered as "១៥៦០ នាក់" with a garbled sentence on the 95 percent female share. [Para 31][Para 32] The union certificate number appears as "១៩៦០ ភ.ប/វក" at [Para 26] and "១៩៦០ ក.ប/វក" at [Para 33]. The three union leaders' names are printed inconsistently across the party list, and the arbitration clause quoted at [Para 57] is corrupted ("ជាលាយ សត្វ លា អក្សរ").
