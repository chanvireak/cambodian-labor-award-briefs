# Award No. 037/16 — Kon Tek (Cambodia) Co. Precision Limited (Arbitration Council, 17 March 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Kong Phallu (president) · Doc ID 138986

## 1. Facts
Kon Tek (Cambodia) Co. Precision Limited (ក្រុមហ៊ុន គ័ន ថេក (ខេមបូឌា) ខប ភើរេសិន លីមីធីត) assembles computer and telephone electrical fittings and employed about 1,500 workers [Para 47][Para 48]. Conciliation settled 13 of 20 points; of the 07 unresolved points, point 07 was settled at the hearing, leaving 06 — points 02, 03, 05, 08, 12 and 13 — all of which are wage or welfare add-ons [Para 17][Para 22][Para 23]. The Khmer Workers' Heart union at the factory was registered on 19 October 2015; the rival Khmer Workers' Spirit union held its election on 26 December 2015 but had not applied for registration [Para 49][Para 50]. Two workers attended the hearing under transfers of rights covering 12 employees [Para 51][Para 52].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a midday meal allowance of 4,000 riel per day (against the 1,000 riel paid), or provide one free meal? [Para 55][Para 101]
2. Whether the employer must raise the regular attendance bonus to USD 15 per month (against USD 10 paid)? [Para 58][Para 119]
3. Whether the employer must fold the USD 25 team-leader role allowance into the base wage? [Para 63][Para 126]
4. Whether the employer must install two loudspeakers in each of its 10 buildings and play music one hour a day during working time? [Para 69][Para 166]
5. Whether the employer must pay a USD 10 monthly machine-skill allowance to workers operating machines? [Para 74][Para 173]
6. Whether the employer must pay a USD 10 monthly skill allowance to the hand-insertion team (Chinese technical term "ទៀន កាន")? [Para 78][Para 180]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** 1,000 riel does not cover a midday meal that in fact costs 2,000 to 3,000 riel; the minimum wage is low and living costs high, so the attendance bonus should rise to USD 15 as neighbouring factories pay USD 12–13; the role allowance must be merged into base wage so overtime is computed on it; machine and hand-insertion work is dangerous and exhausting and merits a skill allowance; loudspeakers would let the employer communicate and lift morale [Para 57][Para 60][Para 61][Para 66][Para 72][Para 76][Para 80].
**Employer (respondent):** It pays what the law requires and will maintain the current practice on meals, bonus and allowances; the role allowance is deliberately separate from base wage and the Labour Law does not require aggregation; machines are safe and easy to learn and the insertion work requires no great force, with gloves provided; playing music during work risks accidents [Para 57][Para 62][Para 68][Para 73][Para 77][Para 82].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed on all six points — every claim was refused consideration, four of them for want of a competent claimant and all of them because they are interest disputes (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍) beyond what the Labour Law guarantees [Para 118][Para 125][Para 165][Para 172][Para 179][Para 187][Para 188]. The award is non-binding [Para 194].

The panel first addressed standing. Under Article 268 a union acquires legal rights only on registration with the Ministry, so the Khmer Workers' Heart union qualified while the Khmer Workers' Spirit union did not [Para 84][Para 87][Para 92]. But the Heart union's three leaders were absent, and the panel held it was therefore not the complainant [Para 93][Para 94]. It then applied Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099: an award in an interest dispute substitutes for a collective agreement, applies to every worker in the enterprise and removes their right to strike over those benefits, so the panel may decide such a dispute only where it is brought by the most representative union, or by unions together holding more than half the workforce [Para 111][Para 112][Para 113]. Neither condition was met, so the complainant lacked the legal capacity to bring the interest claims forward [Para 116][Para 117].

On the meal allowance, attendance bonus, loudspeakers, machine allowance and insertion allowance, the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, contract, agreement or past practice creating the claimed entitlement, and classified each as an interest claim it could not entertain [Para 102][Para 121][Para 168][Para 174][Para 181][Para 188][Para 191][Para 192][Para 193].

On the role allowance, the panel held that Article 2 gives the employer the managerial right to organise pay, and that separating a team-leader allowance from base wage is lawful and reasonable because it reflects the higher responsibility of the post; no rule forbids it [Para 130][Para 133][Para 135]. It added, following Award No. 74/08, that role and skill allowances are nonetheless part of "wages during normal work" and must be counted when computing overtime [Para 156][Para 160][Para 161]. Because the workers had raised that only as a reason and had not claimed aggregation for overtime purposes, the panel confined itself to refusing the aggregation claim [Para 163][Para 164].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Because an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement, binds every worker in the enterprise and extinguishes the right to strike over those benefits, the panel has no jurisdiction to decide one unless it is brought by the most representative union at the enterprise or by unions whose combined membership exceeds half the workforce; a claim for a benefit above the Labour Law's floor, filed by a union that is unregistered or unrepresented at the hearing, fails for want of a competent claimant [Para 112][Para 113][Para 117]. Separately, an employer may lawfully pay a team-leader role allowance outside base wage, but must include it in the wage base used to calculate overtime [Para 135][Para 161].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observation that the employer could equally have folded the role allowance into base wage and that doing so would cost it more in overtime is an aside; it changes no order [Para 133]. Its remarks on the safety of the machines and the risk of playing music are findings on the merits of claims it had already declined to hear [Para 73][Para 77].
