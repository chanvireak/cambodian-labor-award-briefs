# Award No. 136/16 — Jung Faith Knitwear FTY Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 19 July 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, chosen by the employer party; Tuon Si Phan, chosen by the worker party; Tang Try, chair chosen by the two) · Doc ID 139045

## 1. Facts
Jung Faith Knitwear FTY Co., Ltd, a garment and knitting factory in Chak Angre Krom, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employed about 180 workers, all members of a single union that was not yet the company's most representative union. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 67] From November 2015 to May 2016, on days when orders ran short, the company had workers sign a voucher and go home on 50% of their wage; a list filed at the hearing named 75 workers paid at that rate. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 84] Workers paid by output (ស៊ីបុង, *sy bong*) were paid below the statutory minimum, which the employer did not dispute. [Para 86][Para 96] The union complained on 25 May 2016 and conciliation failed on 10 June 2016; eleven workers, three of them union leaders, attended the hearing. [Para 20][Para 22][Para 68]

## 2. Issues in dispute
Seven demands were referred. [Para 2][Para 4][Para 6][Para 8][Para 9][Para 11][Para 13] At the hearing two were settled (the demand to keep the same section, and the demand for wages during the strike of 17 May 2016) and two were withdrawn (sufficient work, and equal allocation of goods between the two factories), leaving three. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 29] The panel's own numbering is kept:
2. Whether the employer must pay the missing 50% so that wages reach 100% for the period November 2015 to May 2016, when it had no work for the workers? [Para 114]
4. Whether the employer must top up the wages of output-paid workers to the statutory minimum wage for June 2015 to May 2016? [Para 131]
6. Whether the employer must bear the cost of replacing work books it lost, repaying workers the fee they paid for them? [Para 158]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** about half the workforce had insufficient work and received only 50%; the company never agreed the 50% rate with them, they always obeyed reassignment to other sections, and paying long-serving workers half wages was intended to make them resign. [Para 73][Para 75][Para 79][Para 81][Para 82] The minimum wage is a statutory floor that cannot be conditioned on accepting a warning letter. [Para 88] Each had paid 10,000 riel for a work book around 2000–2002, and the books were lost when the factory moved. [Para 98][Para 101][Para 102]
**Employer (respondent):** orders were insufficient, especially at year end, so it reassigned workers to unskilled tasks (counting, sorting, measuring, cutting thread) at a full 100% wage, and the workers refused; only where there was no work at all did it take a signed voucher and pay 50%. [Para 80][Para 83] It will not top up the minimum wage because the workers refuse to accept warning letters. [Para 92][Para 144] It never received any money for work books, though it found and returned 20–30 books. [Para 105][Para 107][Para 167]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay the additional 50% needed to reach 100% of wage for November 2015 to May 2016 according to the actual days not worked, and to top up output-paid workers to USD 128 per month for June to December 2015 and USD 140 per month for January to May 2016, month by month and worker by worker; the work-book claim was dismissed. [Para 176][Para 177][Para 178]

Issue 2. Article 72(1) provides that suspension of the contract releases the principal obligations, including the employer's duty to pay wages. [Para 114] The panel held that this applies only to a suspension lawfully made under Article 71, which permits suspension solely for serious economic or material difficulty, for no more than two months, and subject to supervision by the labour inspector. [Para 117][Para 118][Para 121] Following Awards No. 58/07 and No. 08/07, supervision means prior notification and prior authorisation in writing. [Para 122] No such authorisation existed for November 2015 to May 2016, so the suspension was unlawful and Article 72(1) could not be invoked. [Para 127][Para 128][Para 129] The parties had never agreed a 50% rate, and the employer admitted applying it, so the shortfall was payable. [Para 73][Para 83][Para 130]

Issue 4. Article 104 guarantees a minimum wage sufficient for a decent living. [Para 77] Prakas No. 283 fixed USD 128 per month for 2015 and Prakas No. 409/15 fixed USD 140 per month for 2016; an output-paid worker whose production falls short must be topped up to those figures. [Para 131][Para 132][Para 138][Para 140][Para 143] The employer filed no objection to the workers' list of underpaid workers, so the list stood. [Para 96] Its refusal to top up unless workers accepted warning letters was a monetary penalty, which Article 28 prohibits; the employer's right to manage under Article 2 must be exercised lawfully and reasonably and cannot extend to deducting wages as discipline. [Para 145][Para 149][Para 151][Para 154][Para 156]

Issue 6. Article 32 requires every Khmer worker to hold a work book, Article 38 requires loss to be reported to the labour inspectorate, and Article 4(4) of Prakas No. 414 requires the employer to return the book on departure. [Para 158][Para 160][Para 163] The panel accepted that the employer is the custodian and must replace a book it loses, but held that the party alleging payment bears the burden of proof. [Para 166][Para 168] The two books produced carried no entry or exit visa and no company name, post or wage, and no receipt or other evidence of the 10,000 riel payment was filed; the claim was therefore dismissed. [Para 173][Para 174][Para 175]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that suspends work without the labour inspector's prior written authorisation, and for longer than the two months Article 71 allows, cannot rely on Article 72(1) to withhold wages: the full wage remains payable for the days not worked, and a 50% rate never agreed with the workforce is a recoverable shortfall. The statutory minimum wage is a floor that cannot be made conditional on a worker accepting a disciplinary warning, because withholding it on that condition is a prohibited monetary penalty under Article 28. A claim for reimbursement of work-book fees still fails where the claimant cannot prove the payment or that the employer lost the book. [Para 117][Para 128][Para 154][Para 156][Para 174]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that Article 72(1) releases the obligations of both sides, that Article 2 management rights are limited to lawful and reasonable exercises, and its observation that the union was not yet the company's most representative union, are general statements not necessary to the orders. [Para 67][Para 116][Para 149]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- Amounts are corrupted by OCR in several places: the 50% demand appears as "ប្រាក់ ៤០ ឈ្នួល ៥០ %" [Para 4], the minimum-wage figure as "ប្រាក់ ឈ្នួល ៤០ អប្បបរមា" [Para 144], the 50% top-up as "៥០ ខែ ភាគរយ" ("50 months percent") [Para 114], and the trial wage as "១២៣ (មួយ រយៈ ម្ភៃ ប៊ី)" [Para 136].
- The award date is 19 July 2016 on the face and in the reasons, the parties having postponed it from 5 July 2016 [Para 1][Para 32], but the signature block carries "2017" beside the employer-party arbitrator's name [Para 181].
- The same earlier award is dated 05 June 2015 in the evidence list [Para 41] and 05 June 2016 in the reasons [Para 89].
- Paragraph 136 prints the minimum wage as "១២៨ (មួយ រយ ម្ភៃ ប្រាំបី)" — the words read "one hundred twenty-eight" while the bracketed Khmer in the 2015 Prakas text is otherwise garbled; 128 and 123 are used for the same 2015 figure at [Para 132] and [Para 137].
- Paragraph 15 dates the appointment Prakas to 14 June 2014 term XIV while the file is a 2016 case; paragraph 24 prints "14 June 2 2016".
- Paragraphs 72, 81, 108, 172 and 176–179 repeat clauses verbatim, and paragraph 70 states that "both parties acknowledge" the employer did have work in some sections — a concession the employer contradicts at [Para 80].
- The workers who received the 50% rate are known only from an unverified list [Para 84].
