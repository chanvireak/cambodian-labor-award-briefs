# Award No. 060/16 — J&J Double U Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 29 March 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (អ៊ីង សុទ្ធី), appointed by the employer; An Non (អន ណន), appointed by the workers; Aing Eng Thong (អាំង អេង ថុង), chair · Doc ID 139005

## 1. Facts
J&J Double U Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a garment manufacturer on National Road 3 at Kraang Pongro, Dangkao, Phnom Penh, employed close to 500 workers. [Para 60][Para 61] About 30 workers struck from 24 February to 1 March 2016 after the company declined to renew the contracts of 21 probationary workers, without giving notice of the strike or following the legal procedure. [Para 77][Para 78][Para 129][Para 130] The five demands went to conciliation, where a memorandum of 11 February 2016 recorded that the employer would give the sewing section chief a final warning; two claims were settled at the hearing and issues 2, 4 and 5 remained. [Para 66][Para 18][Para 19] The panel heard 32 complainant workers: 30 who had signed mandates and 2 present in person. [Para 63][Para 64]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (panel issue 2) Whether the employer must dismiss the Chinese sewing section chief under the agreement of 11 February 2016? [Para 65]
2. (panel issue 4) Whether the employer must pay a midday meal allowance of 2,000 riel a day? [Para 73][Para 105]
3. (panel issue 5) Whether the employer must maintain the wages and other benefits of the 30 workers who struck from 24 February to 1 March 2016? [Para 77][Para 121]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the section chief had been abusive before the agreement, and after the final warning he kept discriminating against strikers and had 21 workers stopped from work on 24 February 2016, so he must go; meals and rent have risen and 1,000 riel a day cannot feed a worker; the strike was the employer's fault because it never explained the 21 terminations. [Para 69][Para 75][Para 81]
**Employer (respondent):** the chief has visibly changed and now almost fears the workers, and will not be allowed to stay if he causes trouble; the 21 were probationary contracts not renewed for lack of work, a decision of higher management; the company has few orders and cannot raise the meal allowance or pay wages for days not worked during an unnotified, irregular strike. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 73][Para 76][Para 81]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three remaining claims were refused: the demand to dismiss the section chief, the demand for a 2,000 riel meal allowance (which the panel declined even to consider), and the demand to preserve strike-period wages; no sum was ordered. [Para 104][Para 120][Para 132][Para 133][Para 134][Para 135]

On issue 2 the panel read Articles 65 and 664 (Civil Code) and Article 311 of the Civil Code on the formation of contracts, and held that only the two parties to a contract may vary or terminate it, so the 11 February 2016 memorandum binds the employer only to give a final warning — which both sides accepted had been done, and both observed a change of conduct. [Para 82][Para 84][Para 88][Para 94][Para 95] Following earlier awards 53/08, 124/10, 06/10, 256/14 and 15/15 and awards 79/05, 101/08, 168/09, 115/10 and 148/11, the panel put the burden on the party making the allegation, and found that the workers filed no document proving continued hostility or that the 21 were dismissed by the chief rather than by higher management. [Para 90][Para 97][Para 99][Para 101][Para 102][Para 103]

On issue 4 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, employment contract or past practice entitling anyone to 2,000 riel, and classified the demand as an interest dispute. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 108] It then applied Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas 305 of 22 November 2001, read with Article 43 of Prakas 099, under which an award on an interest dispute substitutes for a collective agreement and binds every worker for one year; only a union with most-representative status may bring such a dispute, and here the complainant unions held no registration certificate from the Ministry. [Para 109][Para 111][Para 113][Para 114][Para 115][Para 118][Para 62] It therefore declined to consider the claim at all. [Para 119][Para 120]

On issue 5 the panel applied Article 332(1) and Article 72(1) of the Labour Law and award 27/10, holding that no wages or bonuses are owed for the days on which workers strike, whether the strike was lawful or not; the employer had deducted only the strike days. [Para 121][Para 123][Para 124][Para 125][Para 131][Para 132]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Two rules decide future cases. First, a demand that an employer dismiss a supervisor under a settlement memorandum fails unless the demanding party proves, by documents and not assertion alone, that the person remains a danger or a source of workplace insecurity; the memorandum obliges the employer only to the step it records, and only the contract's parties may alter it. [Para 88][Para 94][Para 99][Para 103] Second, no wages are payable for days actually struck, irrespective of whether the strike complied with the legal procedure; and a claim for a benefit above the statutory floor is an interest dispute that the panel will not hear unless brought by a union holding most-representative status in the enterprise. [Para 115][Para 118][Para 125]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's account of how an interest award displaces the collective agreement and binds all workers for one year was explanatory background; it did not need to decide that question, having already found the complainant union unrepresentative. [Para 113][Para 114]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The conciliation report is dated 1 March 2016 in two places but 2 March 2016 in the summary of transmittal, all under the same number 291 KAP/AK/VK. [Para 9][Para 14] The document deadline is printed as "17 March 2017" and the deferral of issuance as "from 25 [month missing] 2016 to 29 March 2016". [Para 21][Para 22] The dispositive section labels the section-chief claim "issue 1", although the reasoning and the list of disputed points call it issue 2. [Para 65][Para 133] The workforce figures are unstable: 30 mandate signatories appear as "30 workers 0 workers", and the total is given as 32. [Para 63][Para 64] The company's registration appears both as certificate 1323 and as Inv. 0480 E/2014, and the section chief's name is OCR'd three ways (ថា វ ជីង ចឹ / ជីង ចឹង / ជីង ចឺ) with the section printed as "Ling". [Para 26][Para 60][Para 65][Para 70] The signature block names only the employer-appointed arbitrator and the chair; the name of the worker-appointed arbitrator is missing. [Para 137]
