# Award No. 180/16 — Siment Cambodia Chakrey Ting (Arbitration Council, 16 August 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ouk Ry (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Kong Phallha (chair) · Doc ID 139080

## 1. Facts
Siment Cambodia Chakrey Ting produces cement at Prey Thnong, Tuek Chhou, Kampot, and told the panel it employed about 330 workers [Para 54]. There was no most-representative union at the plant; a factory union was registered with the Ministry on 29 July 2015, and two workers transferred authority to the federation for the dispute [Para 55][Para 56][Para 58]. The union complained that the company had not applied the minimum wage promised in point 4 of a collective agreement signed on 1 September 2015 [Para 60][Para 61]. The parties chose a non-binding award [Para 18].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay the thirteenth-month salary regularly as provided in the agreement of 29 May 2015? [Para 3]
2. Whether the company must implement the agreement signed on 1 September 2015? [Para 4]

Issue 1 was withdrawn by the worker party, leaving issue 2, which the panel framed as the minimum-wage question [Para 16][Para 17][Para 126].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** point 4 of the 1 September 2015 agreement bound the company to apply the Ministry of Labour's minimum-wage prakas from June 2016, which meant at least USD 140 per month for every worker, and other cement firms had already done so [Para 66][Para 67].

**Employer (respondent):** the words "prakas of the Ministry of Labour" in the agreement referred to a prakas for the cement industry, not the garment-textile-footwear prakas, and no cement prakas had yet been issued [Para 68][Para 69]; any mismatch was a translation error made in a confused meeting [Para 72][Para 75].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim for USD 140 under Prakas No. 409/15 was rejected, and the employer was ordered to pay the minimum wage of USD 128 per month under Prakas No. 283 dated 12 November 2014, from June 2016, as agreed in point 4 of the 1 September 2015 agreement [Para 180][Para 181][Para 182].

On jurisdiction, Articles 309 and 312 of the Labour Law confine the panel to the unresolved points in the reconciliation record, and the employer raised no jurisdictional objection [Para 84][Para 85][Para 98]. On representation, because the plant had no most-representative union, the registered factory union's officers present at the hearing could lawfully represent its members under Articles 54 and 11 of the Trade Union Law [Para 102][Para 121][Para 124].

On the merits, the panel first fixed what the disputed phrase meant, since the agreement was made on 1 September 2015 when Prakas No. 409/15 of 8 October did not yet exist [Para 148][Para 166][Para 167]. The only minimum-wage prakas then in force was No. 283 of 12 November 2014, setting USD 128 per month for 2015 [Para 150][Para 168][Para 170]. Reading the agreement's two phrases together — "prakas of the Ministry of Labour" and "in June 2016" — the panel held the parties could only have fixed June 2016 as a start date for a prakas that already existed, so the reference was to Prakas 283 and not to the yearly-updated garment prakas [Para 151][Para 152][Para 173]. Article 337 of the Civil Code bound the employer to perform what it had agreed [Para 171].

The employer's translation defence failed: the employer chose and paid the interpreter, and error in translation is not a ground for escaping a valid agreement [Para 160][Para 162]. The interpreter's refusal to clarify point 4 further weakened the employer's case [Para 163]. The panel added that the parties should perform their obligations in good faith under Article 5 of the Civil Code [Para 175][Para 179].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a collective agreement promises to apply "the Ministry of Labour's prakas on minimum wage" from a named future month, the promise is read as referring to the minimum-wage prakas actually in force when the agreement was signed, not to prakas issued later; a party that chose the interpreter cannot escape the agreement by pleading that the translator got it wrong [Para 152][Para 160][Para 173].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that minimum-wage prakas are updated annually so the figure changes year to year, and its encouragement to both parties to act with good faith and honesty, were not necessary to the order [Para 172][Para 179].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Prakas No. 409/15 is dated "8 October 2016" in most passages but "8 October 2015" in others, and is also numbered "049/15" and "04 9/15" (paras 60, 74, 126, 146, 180, 181); the panel's own reasoning requires the 2015 date, and both are reported here [Para 60][Para 146][Para 180]. The company name appears as "ចក្រី ទី៦" at para 33, and the commercial-registration number is given as Inv.1097 E/2006 dated 13 July 2012 at para 53 but as No. 10698 of 22 September 2015 at para 23. The agreement date is printed "01 September" throughout but "016 September 2015" at para 139. Article 11 of the Trade Union Law is dated "17 May 2016" at para 115 and "17 July May 2016" at para 124. Paragraph 125 reads "40" before "workers' union"; paragraph 104 is largely unreadable OCR debris. A 2017 stamp fragment appears at para 21 and a stray "2016" inside the Article 311 quotation at para 126. No passage prints a total sum owed, only the monthly rates of USD 128 and USD 140.
