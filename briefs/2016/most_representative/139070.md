# Award No. 165/16 — Cambodian Sunrise Garment Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 02 August 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambor Na, appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, chair appointed by both) · Doc ID 139070

## 1. Facts
Cambodian Sunrise Garment Co., Ltd., a garment factory in the Sihanoukville Special Economic Zone employing about 230–300 workers, faced eight collective demands raised through the Cambodian Workers' Consciousness Federation. [Para 1][Para 13][Para 33][Para 52] Conciliation on 05 July 2016 settled four points and left four unresolved, which reached the Council secretariat on 11 July 2016. [Para 14] The panel sat on 19 July 2016; the workers attended through three representatives, but the employer did not appear despite an invitation sent by email and telephone and confirmed received, and filed no documents at all. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 21][Para 24] The award is expressly non-binding. [Para 19][Para 165]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must deduct the monthly attendance bonus (prim) pro rata to the days a worker was absent on leave authorised by the employer, rather than forfeiting the whole bonus? [Para 40]
2. Whether the employer must pay a further 05 USD attendance bonus on top of the existing 10 USD, making 15 USD a month? [Para 44]
3. Whether the employer must pay a food allowance of 3,000 riels a day to workers doing overtime from 16:00 to 18:00? [Para 47]
4. Whether the employer must provide a permanent nurse or doctor on duty and adequate medicine for the workers? [Para 50]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company pays a 10 USD monthly attendance bonus but cancels the whole of it when a worker takes a single day of authorised leave, which demoralises the workforce; they also sought 5 USD more, 3,000 riels of overtime food money and proper medical staffing, relying on daily practice in the company. [Para 41][Para 43][Para 45][Para 49][Para 53]
**Employer (respondent):** absent. By telephone on the hearing day its representative said only that the employer obeys the law and has no capacity to meet the demands; no written response or evidence was filed, so the panel treated the employer as having waived its right to reply. [Para 18][Para 21][Para 61][Para 67]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 1: the employer is ordered to deduct the attendance bonus only pro rata to the days of authorised leave; issue 2 dismissed (claim for a further 05 USD refused); issue 3 dismissed (3,000 riels overtime food allowance refused); issue 4 granted, the employer being ordered to provide one full-time nurse and one doctor or mid-level doctor present at least 2 hours during working hours, plus adequate medicine per Prakas No. 330 SKBY. [Para 160][Para 161][Para 162][Para 163]

On procedure, the panel held it could hear the case in the employer's absence under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY, since the employer had been properly invited and had given no valid reason for staying away. [Para 55][Para 63][Para 64]

On issue 1, the panel read Notification No. 230 K.B/S.CH.N of 25 July 2012, point 2, as requiring at least 10 USD a month for workers who attend every working day "without absence", and Article 103 of the Labour Law as treating a bonus as wages. [Para 71][Para 96][Para 97] Because the notification nowhere defines "absence", the panel refused to assume it covers authorised absence, and held under Articles 71 and 72 that leave authorised by the employer suspends the contract of employment and the corresponding wage obligation for those days only. [Para 78][Para 81][Para 99][Para 101][Para 102] The employer therefore had no right to cancel the bonus entirely and could deduct only in proportion to the days actually absent. [Para 104][Para 105]

On issues 2 and 3, the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, contract or past practice obliging the employer to pay more than the statutory 10 USD bonus, or 3,000 riels of overtime food money, and classified both claims as demands above the legal minimum — an interest dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍). [Para 111][Para 131][Para 132] Under Article 96 of the Labour Law, Articles 54 and 72 of the Trade Union Law of 17 May 2016 and Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099 SKBY, such a claim may be adjudicated only if brought by the most representative union, because the resulting award replaces the collective agreement for a year; the local union here had not even registered, so the panel declined to consider the claims. [Para 116][Para 119][Para 120][Para 122][Para 125][Para 134]

On issue 4, Article 238 required primary care for workers and Article 242 and Prakas No. 330 SKBY fixed staffing by headcount; with 300 workers the company needed one nurse plus a doctor or mid-level doctor, yet its infirmary had no permanent medical staff and its medicine stock was inadequate, the Chinese manager or interpreter merely handing out tablets. [Para 136][Para 141][Para 146][Para 149][Para 159]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A monthly attendance bonus is wages under Article 103 of the Labour Law, and where the employer has authorised a worker's leave the contract is suspended for those days under Articles 71 and 72; the employer may therefore withhold the bonus only in proportion to the days of authorised absence, not in full. [Para 96][Para 102][Para 104][Para 105]

A claim for a benefit above the statutory minimum is an interest dispute, and the panel will not entertain it unless the union bringing it is the most representative union in the enterprise under Articles 54 and 72 of the Trade Union Law, since an award on such a claim substitutes a collective agreement binding all workers for one year. [Para 120][Para 121][Para 122][Para 125]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that bonus amounts above the minimum vary by employer as an incentive within the employer's discretion, and that authorised leave is a mutual arrangement in which the worker forgoes wages and the employer forgoes discipline, go beyond what the four orders required. [Para 73][Para 88][Para 89]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- Workforce size is given as "about 230" at [Para 33] but as 300 at [Para 52] and [Para 145]; the medical staffing order turns on the 300 figure.
- The 15 USD total is printed as "១៥៦ ដុល្លារ" (156 dollars) in the issue 2 heading [Para 106] and as 14 USD at [Para 111], while the decision records 15 USD [Para 161].
- The panel's constitution is dated 13 July 2016 with a stray "2022" inserted [Para 15]; Prakas No. 099 is dated "21 April 2023 2004" [Para 55]; the award date appears as 02 August 2016 in the header [Para 1] and again at [Para 20].
- The employer's name is rendered variously as "Cambodian Sunrise Garment Co., Ltd.", "Cambodian Sunrise Garment Co., Ltd Digital" [Para 2] and, in the panel's summary, "Cambodian Sunrise" [Para 32].
- No employer documents were received and no employer position was recorded beyond a telephone statement, so the award rests entirely on the workers' submissions. [Para 21][Para 24][Para 60]
- The last 17 lines are the annexed Prakas No. 330 SKBY, heavily garbled, with table columns collapsed and French medicine names mangled [Para 166]–[Para 183].
