# Award No. 214/16 — Dong Du Textile (Cambodia) Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 28 September 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ri, employer-nominated; An Nan, worker-nominated, dissenting; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 139373

## 1. Facts
The company, a garment manufacturer on National Road 3 at Tik Thla, Krang Pongro, Dangkao, Phnom Penh, employs about 1,420 workers under a first-term collective agreement registered on 6 January 2016 [Para 194][Para 195][Para 207]. A strike ran from 1 August to 22 August 2016, with a lockout of the production building; the panel ordered both stopped by interim order No. 019/16 of 2 September 2016 [Para 30][Para 31]. Before that, on 1 August 2016, the company dismissed three union leaders (Mr K, Mr N and Mr M) for inciting the strike, and on 30 July 2016 it ended the contracts of four workers (Ms M, Mr Y, Mr S and Mr S) who had low assessment scores [Para 218][Para 230]. The factory union's leadership term had expired and recognition of its new leaders was suspended, so the federation relied on rights transferred by 38 workers plus 7 who attended, giving 45 claimants [Para 198][Para 201][Para 339].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate the seven union leaders and activists it dismissed? [Para 210]
2. Whether the employer must repay the "machine bonus" (profit share, ប្រាក់ភាគលាភ) it deducted for July 2016? [Para 254]
5. Whether the employer must allow food sellers back inside the factory compound? [Para 265]
6. Whether the employer must dismiss the C4 team leader? [Para 272]
7. Whether the employer must maintain wages and benefits during the strike of 1–22 August 2016? [Para 278]
9. Whether the employer must stop sending work to outside companies and transfer three workers from cutting back to sewing? [Para 283]

Disputes 3, 4 and 8 were withdrawn at the hearing [Para 41][Para 42].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** All seven were union leaders or activists, so dismissal was anti-union discrimination under Articles 12 and 279; their successive fixed-term contracts exceeded two years and had become indefinite-duration, no notice was given, and the four had never been warned [Para 235][Para 237][Para 238]. Deducting July's profit share because they struck in August punished them for striking, and the 40-minute lunch break is too short to eat outside the compound [Para 257][Para 267].

**Employer (respondent):** The three incited an unlawful strike, serious misconduct under Article 83, and the four were not rehired for genuinely low scores; each contract was only six months and 5% termination pay was paid at each expiry; it did not know the four were activists because the union never notified it [Para 239][Para 241][Para 243]. It deducted the July profit share only after workers ignored its offer to restore it on returning between 3 and 5 August; food sales inside raised hygiene and safety concerns [Para 260][Para 269].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must reinstate six of the seven workers and repay the July 2016 profit share to the 45 claimants; the claims on food sellers, the C4 team leader, strike-period wages and outsourcing were dismissed [Para 597][Para 598][Para 599][Para 600][Para 601][Para 602].

On standing, a union without most-representative status may act for workers who transferred their rights, and for those appearing in person under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099; the 45 claimants qualified, but Mr M's transfer letter post-dated the hearing and he was absent, so he was not a claimant [Para 333][Para 336][Para 339].

On contract type, Article 67(2) converts successive fixed-term contracts into an indefinite-duration contract once their combined duration exceeds two years [Para 377][Para 389]. The panel rejected the employer's argument that each six-month term be counted separately, so all six remaining claimants held indefinite-duration contracts and could only be dismissed for a valid reason under Article 74 [Para 391][Para 458].

On union protection, Article 43 of the Trade Union Law requires the labour inspector's consent before dismissing a shop steward, and Article 67 extends protection to election candidates, founders and members, but neither protects leaders whose term has expired; Mr K and Mr N therefore had no special immunity [Para 408][Para 423][Para 432][Para 434]. On discrimination, Articles 12 and 279 forbid using union membership or activity as a ground for dismissal, and Article 62 extends this to non-renewal, but the burden lay on the workers, who offered no evidence that the employer knew of their activism [Para 434][Para 441][Para 453][Para 457].

On the three leaders, Article 83(b)(5) treats incitement of other workers to serious misconduct as serious misconduct, but a strike is not itself serious misconduct, and following Awards 70/04 and 20/05 the panel held that incitement to strike is not serious misconduct absent violence; the video showed no specific act of incitement or violence, so no valid reason existed [Para 468][Para 471][Para 475][Para 481][Para 482]. On the four, the assessment tables did not disclose how scores were computed or even which end of the 1–5 scale was better, and no warning had been issued, so low performance was not established; the dismissals also lacked a valid reason [Para 492][Para 495][Para 497][Para 498]. It ordered reinstatement of all six [Para 486][Para 502].

On dispute 2, the panel classified the profit share as part of monthly wages and therefore a rights dispute [Para 503]. Article 333 prohibits any sanction against workers for taking part in a strike and voids such sanctions; because the deduction was expressly conditioned on returning to work between 3 and 5 August, it was a punishment for striking, and the employer's claim of loss was impossible for July, before the strike began [Para 510][Para 512][Para 513][Para 514]. Repayment was ordered, but only for the 45 identified claimants, since 159 listed names were not rights-transferees and 563 could not be identified [Para 507][Para 508][Para 518].

On disputes 5, 6, 7 and 9, the panel applied Article 2's managerial discretion, exercised lawfully and reasonably: no law or agreement required food sellers to be admitted, and hygiene and safety justified moving them outside [Para 523][Para 525]. Only the parties to a contract may dissolve it, so it could not order the dismissal of a team leader who was not a party, though it found his words to the pregnant worker improper and told the employer to correct his conduct [Para 535][Para 546][Para 555]. Articles 332 and 72(1) suspend the contract and the wage obligation on strike days, and the claim of replacement hiring under Article 334 was unsupported [Para 558][Para 565][Para 571]. Transferring work is the employer's right under Article 2 and clause 9 of the collective agreement provided no worker is left idle, which both sides accepted; the reassignment request was absent from the conciliation report and so outside Article 312 [Para 579][Para 584][Para 593].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive fixed-term contracts whose combined duration passes two years become an indefinite-duration contract, so ending them requires a valid reason under Article 74; incitement to strike is not serious misconduct under Article 83(b)(5) unless it is shown to have incited violence; and withholding a monthly profit share because workers struck is a sanction prohibited by Article 333, whatever the loss the employer claims. [Para 391][Para 475][Para 513]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that Article 43 of the Trade Union Law protects only shop stewards and election candidates, not leaders whose mandate has expired, and that the employer's assessment form was too imprecise to prove low performance even where scores were genuinely low [Para 432][Para 495]. The worker-nominated arbitrator dissented on one point, arguing that Article 67 protects union leaders like shop stewards, so expired leaders keep three months' protection [Para 607][Para 621].
