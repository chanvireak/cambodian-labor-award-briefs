# Award No. 144/16 — Naga Peace Corporation Limited (Arbitration Council, 21 July 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Kong Phalla chair) · Doc ID 139053

## 1. Facts
Naga Peace Corporation Limited is a garment factory at Prek Treng, Kien Svay, Kandal, with about 1,900 workers. [Para 53] The Cambodia Economic Workers' Union Federation brought ten demands; two were settled at conciliation, three were withdrawn, and five remained. [Para 18][Para 22][Para 23] Three elected union leaders were dismissed on 11 May 2016, eleven days after the union election of 30 April 2016, when the employer cut its sewing teams from 80 to 60 workers each after installing automated machines. [Para 102][Para 108][Para 200][Para 244] The federation had authorisations from 28 workers; 13 revoked their thumbprints, leaving 15 claimants. [Para 128][Para 132][Para 135]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a midday meal allowance of 2,000 riel a day? [Para 65][Para 136]
2. Whether the employer must raise the travel and accommodation allowance to USD 15 a month? [Para 70][Para 156]
3. Whether the employer must enlarge the canteens where workers eat? [Para 75][Para 161]
4. Whether the employer must raise the attendance bonus to USD 15 a month? [Para 85][Para 179]
10. Whether the employer must reinstate the three dismissed union leaders and pay their wages from dismissal until reinstatement? [Para 95][Para 185]

Issues 7 and 9 were settled at conciliation and issues 5, 6 and 8 withdrawn. [Para 22][Para 23]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** other factories pay 1,000 to 2,000 riel a day for meals, and a 1998 inter-ministerial Prakas requires a midday meal; transport costs USD 20 a month against the USD 7 paid; the canteens seat 500 each for 1,800 workers; bonuses elsewhere are USD 15 to 20. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 72][Para 79][Para 86] The three leaders were dismissed for union activity, with no fault and no warning, and were offered USD 200 each on 10 May to resign from the union. [Para 104][Para 230]
**Employer (respondent):** it has no money for more, no law requires these benefits, and the canteens with concrete floors and stone seats are adequate. [Para 69][Para 74][Para 82][Para 168] The dismissals followed automation and the need to cut staff, and the three were not known to be union leaders until conciliation. [Para 110][Para 118][Para 233]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement and back wages were refused, but the employer must pay each of the three 5 per cent contract-end compensation, final wages, unused annual leave, and damages equal to the wages due to the end of each contract; the four benefit claims were refused. [Para 274][Para 275][Para 282]

*The benefit claims.* No law, collective agreement, internal rule, contract or practice requires 2,000 riel a day, a USD 15 travel allowance or a USD 15 bonus, so each was a demand above the legal minimum, an interest dispute. [Para 136][Para 156][Para 179] An award there becomes a collective agreement binding every worker under Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099, which only a most-representative union may seek; the claimants were individual workers, so issues 1, 2 and 4 were refused. [Para 149][Para 154][Para 160][Para 184] On the canteens, no provision requires the employer to build one, and the two canteens with concrete floors and stone seats are a benefit above the law; the workers produced no evidence that 1,800 cannot be seated, and the panel found seats were being reserved by other workers, so the claim failed on the burden of proof. [Para 167][Para 168][Para 169][Para 171][Para 178]

*Protection.* Article 293 allows dismissal of a staff delegate or candidate only with the labour inspector's agreement, and the third indent of Article 3 of Prakas No. 305 protects candidates for 45 days before and after the poll; for those elected, no end date is fixed, and Award No. 40/10 read the protection as at least 45 days after the election or until registration is applied for. [Para 186][Para 187][Para 191][Para 192][Para 195] Three conditions must be met. [Para 196] The three were elected leaders and were dismissed 11 days after the poll, so conditions 1 and 2 were satisfied. [Para 198][Para 200][Para 202] Condition 3 — notice to the employer by any reasonable means — failed: no security guard with badge 051 appears in the security company's monthly rosters from January to May 2016, and the postal letter carried no red "refused" stamp, bore a handwritten note dated 3 May although posted on 4 May, and the postal clerk confirmed the branch never delivered it because the sender took it himself. [Para 203][Para 206][Para 209][Para 210][Para 214][Para 216] The workers therefore did not prove notification, and the three were not protected. [Para 217][Para 219]

*Discrimination.* Articles 12 and 279 forbid using union membership or activity as the ground for hiring, discipline or termination. [Para 219][Para 220] The employer had terminated fixed-duration contracts before expiry, without consent and without serious misconduct — unlawful in itself, but not proof of union discrimination. [Para 231][Para 232] The employer attributed the cuts to automation, and the workers' account of the USD 200 offer rested on their own statements with no witness or document, so the burden of proof was not discharged and no discrimination was found. [Para 233][Para 236][Para 237][Para 238] Reinstatement was refused. [Para 274][Para 281]

*Compensation.* Because the employer ended the contracts early by its own will, Article 73(6) required contract-end compensation of at least 5 per cent of the wages paid during each contract, Article 116 the final wage, and Articles 166 and 167 payment for unused annual leave. [Para 249][Para 254][Para 256][Para 263] Article 73(3) also required damages equal to the wages each worker would have earned to the end of the contract — about six months for one, two months and 24 days for the second, and three months and 11 days for the third. [Para 264][Para 266][Para 269][Para 272][Para 267][Para 275]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Union protection under Prakas No. 305 Article 3 depends on the union actually notifying the employer of the candidacy by a means that can be proved: a delivery denied at the gate, and a postal item the branch never delivered and which lacks the refusal stamp, do not discharge that condition, however short the interval between the poll and the dismissal. [Para 209][Para 214][Para 216] Early termination of a fixed-duration contract without consent and without serious misconduct is unlawful and entitles the worker to the 5 per cent minimum, final wages, annual leave and damages to the contract's end, but illegality alone does not prove union discrimination, which the worker must evidence. [Para 232][Para 254][Para 267] Claims above the statutory minimum are interest disputes that individual workers cannot bring. [Para 154]

**Obiter dicta:** the remarks that the employer admitted not following the Article 95 procedure for staff reductions, and that canteen seats should not be reserved, grounded no order; the first because no discrimination was proved, the second as guidance only. [Para 113][Para 172][Para 245]

- [Para 19] dates the panel's formation "23 June 2023 2016"; [Para 265] dates a contract expiry to "11 October 2015" where [Para 97] and [Para 266] require 2016.
- [Para 179] states the claim as "២៥ ដុល្លារ" (USD 25); the demand and order are USD 15 [Para 85][Para 280].
- The three claimants and union officers are named throughout; they are referred to by role here, per the anonymisation convention.

- Whole clauses are duplicated by OCR in [Para 148], [Para 150] and [Para 176], and the panel is called "ក្រុមប្រឹក្សា អាជ្ញាធរ" in places where the Arbitration Council is meant.
