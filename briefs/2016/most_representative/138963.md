# Award No. 001/16 — Strang Health International Nally Mighty Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 28 January 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chev Phirum, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Kong Phalla, president) · Doc ID 138963

## 1. Facts
The company, a shoe manufacturer in Bati district, Takeo, employed about 4,900 workers and had a collective dispute over working conditions referred after conciliation failed on 09 of 16 points [Para 67][Para 25]. A union local of the Khmer Workers' Conscience Union was formed by election on 23 December 2015; the worker elected president, Nuon Borath, was dismissed on 24 December 2015 at about 15:40 without prior notice, the same day the election result was delivered to the company [Para 79][Para 97]. The union had not yet been registered with the Ministry of Labour, and the workers' side was represented by two federations holding 312 transfer-of-right letters plus 30 workers present at the hearing [Para 69][Para 155].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate the elected union president and preserve his benefits? [Para 74]
2. Whether the employer must raise the transport and accommodation allowance by USD 8 to a total of USD 15 per month? [Para 99]
3. Whether the employer must pay a meal allowance of 4,000 riel for overtime worked from 16:00 to 18:00 and pay it weekly? [Para 104]
4. Whether the employer must let pregnant workers leave 15 minutes early and take one day per month for antenatal checks on full benefits? [Para 111]
5. Whether the employer must issue fixed-duration contracts of six months to one year instead of three months? [Para 121]
6. Whether the employer must install additional fingerprint scanners? [Para 125]
7. Whether the employer must pay an attendance bonus of USD 15 per month and deduct it pro rata for authorised leave days? [Para 134]
8. Whether the employer must pay wages and other benefits for the strike period? [Para 139]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissal of Nuon Borath one day after the election result was delivered was anti-union discrimination, since without the union there would have been no dismissal; he had never been warned, and the company's appraisal evidence was never produced [Para 75][Para 85].
**Employer (respondent):** it did not renew the contract because the worker failed the company's performance appraisal, and it would pay his final wages, transport and accommodation allowance, attendance bonus, meal allowance, severance and unused annual leave; it denied the union had validly notified it [Para 86][Para 88].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to take Nuon Borath back and preserve his benefits [Para 358], to pay the overtime meal allowance once a week [Para 361], and to deduct the attendance bonus pro rata to authorised leave days [Para 366]. Claims 2, 7, 10, 11, 16 and 15(a) were dismissed [Para 359][Para 362][Para 363][Para 364][Para 367][Para 365].

On issue 1, the panel applied Article 293 of the Labour Law with Prakas 305: a candidate elected as union leader is protected for at least 45 days after the ballot, and dismissal requires the labour inspector's prior approval [Para 160][Para 171]. All three conditions were met — protected status, dismissal within the 45 days, and notification to the employer [Para 175][Para 178][Para 189]. Because the company produced no appraisal records, the panel rejected the performance explanation and found the dismissal unlawful [Para 185][Para 194].

On issues 2, 3(a), 7, 15(a) and the bonus top-up, the panel held each claim to exceed what the law and practice provide, making it a dispute over interests; such a claim may only be brought by a union with most-representative status at the enterprise [Para 199][Para 206][Para 216][Para 249]. The claimants were 312 individual workers and 30 attendees, not such a union, so the panel declined jurisdiction [Para 211][Para 219][Para 253][Para 298].

On issue 3(b), the 2,000 riel meal allowance for overtime is set by Notification 041/11; the panel found the allowance is not part of the overtime wage and need not be paid with it, but must be paid as the need arises, and ordered weekly payment [Para 227][Para 237][Para 246].

On issue 15(b), Notification 230/12 fixes USD 10 as a statutory minimum, not an employer's discretionary bonus, so the company could not simply keep the whole bonus when leave was authorised [Para 306][Para 333][Para 339]. Issue 10 was refused because neither party may force contract terms on the other under Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code [Para 275][Para 268].

On issue 11, the number and placement of scanners is a management prerogative under Article 2, and the workers produced no evidence of actual delay [Para 284][Para 288]. On issue 16, Article 332 suspends the contract during a strike so no wages are owed, and because the strike began without the seven days' notice required by Article 324, attendance bonuses were also forfeited [Para 348][Para 351][Para 357].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker elected as a union leader is protected from dismissal for at least 45 days after the ballot even before the union is registered, and a dismissal in that window without the labour inspector's approval is unlawful and nullifies the employer's stated reason where no supporting records are produced [Para 171][Para 191][Para 194]. A claim for a benefit exceeding the statutory or contractual floor is an interests dispute that the panel will not hear unless brought by a most-representative union at the enterprise [Para 206][Para 208].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that a strike's legality does not affect the wage consequence of Article 332, and that it would depart from its own earlier awards only where a new legal basis exists — here the word "without absence" added by Notification 230/12 — while nevertheless continuing to follow those awards [Para 348][Para 399][Para 400]. It also recorded that it could not construe "without absence" because the Labour Advisory Committee has not interpreted it [Para 309].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company name appears in at least five transliterations across the face and body (e.g. "Strang Helth", "Strang Hel", "Stang Hel"), so the English form above is a reconstruction [Para 1][Para 63][Para 67]. Workforce size is printed as about 4,900 in the findings and as 900 in the employer's submission on meal-allowance administration [Para 68][Para 110]. The panel constitution date is corrupted as "05 January 2022 year 2016" [Para 27]. The attendance bonus figure is printed as "១០០ ដុល្លារ" (USD 100) at one point where USD 10 is meant [Para 299]. Issue numbering in the original list is not continuous (items 5, 6, 8 and 9 having been settled or withdrawn), and the enforcement order is numbered 009/16 while the award is 001/16 [Para 2][Para 28][Para 32]. Arbitrator Kong Phalla's signed dissent on issue 15(b) is appended and is not the award of the panel [Para 369][Para 402].
