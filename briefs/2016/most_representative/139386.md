# Award No. 237/16 — Zina-Dai Dong (Cambodia) Electronics (Arbitration Council, 28 December 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of three — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed, dissenting), An Non (worker-appointed), Aing Eng Phong (chair) · Doc ID 139386

## 1. Facts
A shoe factory at Ang Snuol, Kandal, with about 179 workers, which announced on 7 November 2016 that it had no further orders and closed its doors on 10 November 2016 [Para 22][Para 40][Para 46]. Three workers — Mr V, Mr K and Mr T — pursued the single unresolved demand for wages and statutory benefits [Para 43][Para 88]. An earlier award, No. 142/16 of 28 July 2016, had ordered the employer to reinstate Mr V and to pay severance to Mr T, and had become binding on 5 August 2016 [Para 73][Para 97].

## 2. Issues in dispute
One demand survived conciliation; the panel examined it as three cases [Para 92].
1. Whether the panel has power to order the employer to execute Award 142/16 by paying Mr T the severance that award granted him? [Para 92]
2. Whether the employer must compute Mr K's severance from his first day of service, 14 March 2014, rather than from 1 August 2015 when his contract became indefinite-duration? [Para 107]
3. Whether the employer must pay Mr V severance and back wages from the date Award 142/16 became binding until the factory closed on 10 November 2016? [Para 137]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the three men were recognised by the provincial labour department as the workers' representatives under Prakas 362 and said they alone were claiming [Para 84][Para 87]. Mr K's seniority runs from 2014 and the 5% end-of-contract payments he received at conversion must not erase it [Para 61][Para 62]. Mr V was never taken back despite Award 142/16 and lost time waiting [Para 67][Para 68].
**Employer (respondent):** it agreed to pay severance according to each worker's contract type when the factory closed [Para 46]. For Mr K it maintained that the 5% payment and the unused annual leave paid at conversion settled the earlier fixed-duration period, and that it lacked the means to pay more [Para 63][Para 64]. For Mr V it said it wished to take him back but every section chief refused to accept him [Para 70].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must pay Mr K severance computed from 14 March 2014 to 10 November 2016; the claims of Mr T and Mr V are dismissed for want of jurisdiction [Para 147][Para 148][Para 149].

On standing, no most-representative union exists at the factory, but Prakas 362 of 29 August 2016 empowers the labour department to certify worker representatives, and the three certified men were the only claimants [Para 80][Para 84][Para 88].

On case 1, Article 353 of the 2006 Civil Procedure Code reserves compulsory enforcement of arbitral awards, domestic and foreign, to the courts through an enforcement order [Para 92]. The panel therefore held it has no power to compel compliance with its own earlier award and declined to consider the claim [Para 95][Para 101].

On case 2, Article 73(7) of the Labour Law provides that where an indefinite-duration contract replaces a fixed-duration one, seniority is counted by adding the two periods together [Para 107][Para 109]. Both parties accepted the 14 March 2014 start and the 1 August 2015 conversion [Para 110][Para 111]. The panel held that the 5% end-of-contract payment and the leave payout made at conversion could not release the employer from aggregating seniority, because such payments fall due only when the employment relationship ends, not while it continues [Para 123][Para 131][Para 132]. Severance was ordered on the full period [Para 134][Para 135].

On case 3, Mr V's claim too was an application to enforce Award 142/16; the employer had paid him to 10 August 2016 instead, and he had already petitioned the Kandal court on 6 October 2016 [Para 140][Para 144]. The panel held it must wait for that court's decision and dismissed the claim without consideration [Para 145][Para 146].

Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented on case 2, reasoning that the fixed-duration contract ended on 30 July 2015 with a settled 5% payment and leave payout, and that Mr K then freely chose an indefinite-duration contract, so severance should run only from 1 August 2015 [Para 153][Para 155][Para 159].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an indefinite-duration contract replaces a fixed-duration contract, seniority for severance is the sum of both periods, and the 5% end-of-contract payment and annual-leave payout made at the moment of conversion do not extinguish the earlier period, because those entitlements arise on termination of the relationship, not during it. The panel cannot enforce its own awards: compulsory execution belongs to the courts alone, so a fresh claim asking it to order compliance is inadmissible.

**Obiter dicta:** certification of worker representatives by the labour department under Prakas 362 supplies standing even where no most-representative union exists [Para 84]; and a party that lets the eight-day period pass without objection is bound by the award [Para 97].
