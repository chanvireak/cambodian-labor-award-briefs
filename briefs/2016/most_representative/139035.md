# Award No. 117/16 — MP International Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 June 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sodhy, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Kong Phallha, president) · non-binding award · Doc ID 139035

## 1. Facts
MP International ("MPI") is a Phnom Penh security-services company, registered since 2002, employing about 2,900 workers, including a cash-in-transit (CIT) section of escorts, drivers and supervisors paid a base wage of 110 to 130 USD a month. [Para 1][Para 87][Para 88][Para 122] The company dismissed Mr K, a CIT supervisor since 05 April 2000 and president of the enterprise union registered on 07 April 2016, on 12 April 2016 for alleged overtime fraud on 04 April 2016 and abusive words to his supervisor on 09 April 2016. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 104][Para 115] Of ten demands, six were settled at conciliation, two were withdrawn, and the panel took the remaining two. [Para 17][Para 24][Para 25][Para 26]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to take Mr K back into work and pay his wages from the date of dismissal until the date of reinstatement? [Para 139]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to raise the monthly base wage of CIT workers by 10 per cent every year? [Para 175]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr K was dismissed without the labour inspector's prior authorisation, which Article 293 requires for a union leader, and even on the serious-misconduct route the employer had to act within seven days of learning of the fault; the CIT section has long service but has never had a raise. [Para 98][Para 99][Para 124]
**Employer (respondent):** Mr K committed serious misconduct under Article 83 and clause 6 of section 3 of the internal rules — claiming six hours of overtime when the security camera shows the vehicle returned at about 18:46 and his supervisor authorised only three hours; a request for authorisation was sent to the labour inspector on 19 April 2016; and the market now has seven competitors, so a 10 per cent annual rise is impossible though overtime pay is already generous. [Para 100][Para 103][Para 114][Para 125]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 1 granted: the employer must take Mr K back and pay his wages from the date of dismissal to the date of reinstatement; issue 2 dismissed: the panel declined to consider the 10 per cent annual increase. No sum in riel or USD was fixed. [Para 192][Para 193]

On issue 1 the panel applied Articles 293 and 295 of the Labour Law, read with clause 4 of Prakas No. 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001, which extends the protection of the three union leaders to the dismissal of union officers. [Para 139][Para 144][Para 153][Para 154] The employer had been ordered at the hearing to file the authorisation request and never did, so the panel found no evidence that authorisation was sought; it added that even on the employer's own date of 19 April 2016, the one-month period for the inspector's decision had run by the hearing of 13 June 2016, which under Article 293 deems the request refused. [Para 166][Para 167][Para 168][Para 169] Lacking authorisation, the dismissal did not comply with Articles 293 and 295, and clause 34 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY empowered the panel to order reinstatement. [Para 170][Para 171][Para 174]

On issue 2 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, employment contract, agreement between the parties or past practice creating a duty to raise base wages by 10 per cent each year, and characterised the claim as a demand for a benefit above the statutory floor — an interest dispute. [Para 177][Para 178][Para 179] Under Article 96 and clause 9 of Prakas No. 305, and clause 43 of Prakas No. 099, an interest dispute may be brought only by the most representative union, or unions together holding more than half the workforce; this union was not the most representative in the enterprise and had received transfers of rights from only four workers, two of whom attended. [Para 180][Para 182][Para 184][Para 189][Para 190] The panel therefore declined jurisdiction over issue 2. [Para 191]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union officer's dismissal is unlawful unless the labour inspector has authorised it; where the employer cannot produce the request, or where the inspector's one-month decision period has expired, the request is treated as refused and the panel will order reinstatement with wages from the dismissal date to the date of return. [Para 168][Para 169][Para 174] A claim for a wage increase above the statutory floor is an interest dispute that the panel will not hear unless the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise or represents more than half the workers. [Para 184][Para 190]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that an arbitral award on an interest dispute substitutes for a collective agreement and binds all workers for one year, which is why the representative threshold is enforced. [Para 182][Para 183]
