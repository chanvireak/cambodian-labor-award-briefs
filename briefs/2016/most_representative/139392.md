# Award No. 057/16 — Lead Win Shoe (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 31 March 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Kong Phallha (chair) · Doc ID 139392

## 1. Facts
Lead Win Shoe (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. manufactures footwear and employs about 1,390 workers. [Para 53] Its Khmer Workers' Union was registered with the Ministry on 11 September 2015. [Para 55] A collective agreement between the company and the Cambodian Labour Workers' Union — the most representative union in the plant — had run since 1 January 2015 for two years. [Para 58][Para 59] The company deducted 2,000 riel a month from members' wages as dues for that union; 153 workers signed letters refusing any further deduction, which reached the employer only on 16 March 2016. [Para 67][Para 183]

## 2. Issues in dispute
Eight points were un-conciliated; the parties settled points 5 and 7 at the hearing and the workers withdrew point 6, leaving five. [Para 23]
1. Whether the employer must deduct union dues from members' wages for the Khmer Workers' Union? [Para 60]
2. Whether the employer must reply to or notify the Khmer Workers' Union when it submits letters seeking negotiation? [Para 72]
3. Whether the employer must stop deducting dues for the Cambodian Labour Workers' Union from the workers who have written refusing that deduction? [Para 75]
4. Whether the employer must dismiss Mr M, the company's adviser, from the company? [Para 99]
5. Whether the employer must pay 5,000 riel a day in meal money for work on public holidays and Sundays? [Para 103]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the union's letters went unanswered, members who refused the deduction were ignored, and the adviser had delayed settlement of the dispute and used offensive language. [Para 73][Para 78][Para 100]
**Employer (respondent):** it applies the collective agreement, which channels dues through the most representative union; it received no individual written authorisations; and it has no ground to remove its own adviser. [Para 66][Para 74][Para 102]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must stop deducting dues for the Cambodian Labour Workers' Union from 153 workers' wages from March 2016; the other four claims were dismissed. [Para 255]

**Dues for the claimant union (issue 1).** Article 129(2) allows a worker to authorise in writing that dues for the union of which he is a member be deducted from his wage, and to revoke that authorisation at any time; Article 5 of Prakas No. 305 requires 15 days' notice to the employer. [Para 122][Para 128] The two letters relied on came from the federation, not from the members, and merely asserted that members had thumbprinted their consent. [Para 137] No individual written authorisation existed, so no duty to deduct arose. [Para 141]

**Replies to union letters (issue 2).** A registered union has the right to put requests and complaints to the employer; neither the Labour Law nor Prakas No. 305 obliges the employer to answer them. [Para 159][Para 160] The claim was refused, the panel adding that an employer should nonetheless take such letters seriously to keep workplace relations sound. [Para 167]

**Stopping the deduction (issue 3).** The right to authorise or refuse a deduction belongs to the worker alone, is exercised by written letter, and is revocable at any time. [Para 172][Para 173] Of 155 letters filed, two were undated and so could not establish true intent, leaving 153. [Para 180][Para 181] Although those letters reached the employer only on 16 March 2016, the duty to stop runs from March 2016. [Para 185] Clause 8(4) of the collective agreement, which made stopping conditional on the local union's agreement, adds a requirement the law does not impose and is void under Article 13. [Para 193][Para 196]

**The adviser (issue 4).** Only a party to the contract may terminate it — Article 65(1) of the Labour Law and Article 311 of the Civil Code — so a third party cannot, and workers must prove the person is dangerous to others. [Para 204][Para 206][Para 217] Delay in conciliation and offensive words did not meet that test. [Para 224][Para 225]

**Meal money (issue 8).** Clause 9(7)–(8) of the collective agreement sets 3,000 riel for work on Sundays and public holidays, which the employer paid. [Para 228][Para 229] A claim above that figure rests on no law, agreement or practice, so it is an interest dispute; while a collective agreement is in force Article 96 bars further interest claims, and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 makes an interest award a collective agreement binding every worker. [Para 249][Para 251]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A payroll deduction for union dues requires the individual worker's own written authorisation and may be withdrawn by him at any time; a collective-agreement clause making withdrawal conditional on the incumbent union's consent is void under Article 13, and the employer must stop deducting from the month the withdrawal reaches it. [Para 173][Para 196]

**Obiter dicta:** that while a collective agreement is in force neither side may bring fresh interest claims [Para 248]; and that although no law compels an employer to answer a union's letters, sound industrial relations call for it [Para 167].
