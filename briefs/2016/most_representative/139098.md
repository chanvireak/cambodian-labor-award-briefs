# Award No. 202/16 — Mirae Apparel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 20 September 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ang Eng Thong presiding; Mar Sambor Na, employer nominee; Ven Pov, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139098

## 1. Facts
Mirae Apparel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a garment factory at Phnom Penh Thmei, Sen Sok, employs about 270 workers. [Para 51] On 6 April 2016 the company asked the labour inspector to authorise a two-month suspension of operations for lack of orders; the inspector visited and took a record on 26 May but never replied in writing, and the company suspended all contracts from 7 April to 9 June 2016 while paying USD 35 per month. [Para 27][Para 60][Para 62] Twenty-five workers transferred their rights to a lawyer, who brought the claim; no most-representative union existed in the factory. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 56] Workers struck from 9 to 15 June 2016 and the company closed on 15 June 2016 under an agreement providing USD 70 per year of seniority. [Para 68][Para 189][Para 197]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay 100 per cent of wages and benefits for the suspension period of 7 April to 9 June 2016, having paid only USD 35 per month? [Para 3][Para 59][Para 104]
2. Whether the employer must pay final wages for 10 to 15 June 2016, unused annual leave, notice compensation, damages, and health compensation, counting seniority from 2012? [Para 59][Para 136][Para 188]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the suspension was unlawful and wages must be paid in full for the whole period; the USD 35 paid was not what they accepted; they are also owed final wages for the days before closure, unused annual leave, notice pay, damages and health compensation, calculated on seniority from 2012. [Para 69][Para 75][Para 76]
**Employer (respondent):** it suspended only because it had no orders, sought the labour inspector's authorisation on 6 April, and the workers agreed to the suspension and to the USD 35; after the order came back it asked them to return on 9 June but they struck and demanded termination, so they, not the company, ended the contracts, and the 70 US-dollar-per-year settlement was the agreed exit. [Para 61][Para 71][Para 72][Para 73][Para 84]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered wages and benefits at 100 per cent for 7 April to 9 June 2016, plus unused annual leave and the severance agreed on 15 June 2016; it refused final wages for 10 to 15 June, notice compensation, damages and health compensation. [Para 210][Para 211][Para 212]

On issue 1 the panel set out Article 71(11) of the Labour Law: an enterprise in serious economic or material difficulty may suspend activity for no more than two months, and the suspension must be placed under the supervision of the labour inspector. [Para 106][Para 107] Following earlier awards, it held that supervision means the employer must give notice and obtain the inspector's prior authorisation in writing; a record of a visit is not an authorisation. [Para 108][Para 109][Para 111][Para 116] Here the inspector never issued a written reply, so the suspension did not follow the procedure and was unlawful. [Para 113][Para 118][Para 125] Since the suspension was invalid, the exemption from the wage obligation in Article 72(1) did not apply, and the employer owed 100 per cent of wages and benefits even though it had no work to give. [Para 120][Para 121][Para 124][Para 126] As for the USD 35, Article 13(1) makes Labour Law rules mandatory and voids any unilateral decision, contract or agreement that does not respect them, so even a worker's consent to the lower payment could not cure it; the panel found in any event that the employer produced no evidence of the agreement it alleged. [Para 128][Para 129][Para 132][Para 133][Para 134]

On issue 2 the panel split the claim. Annual leave: Article 166 gives paid annual leave, Article 167 requires payment for leave accrued but not taken when the contract ends, and the 15 June agreement itself promised it, so it was ordered. [Para 173][Para 176][Para 178][Para 180][Para 209] Final wages for 10 to 15 June: Article 332(1) suspends the contract during a strike and bars wages for strike days, whether the strike is lawful or not, so nothing was due for those days. [Para 190][Para 191][Para 192][Para 196] Notice pay, damages and health compensation: these follow only where the employer terminates of its own will, and here the workers demanded termination and refused to return, so the employer owed none of them. [Para 167][Para 170][Para 172] The panel also examined whether the 70 US-dollar-per-year severance offended Article 13 and found no basis to say so. [Para 201][Para 202] It further rejected the employer's assertion that the 25 workers had refused the 15 June agreement, found no conditions attached to the severance, and ordered payment under it. [Para 205][Para 206][Para 207]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of activity under Article 71 is lawful only if the employer obtains the labour inspector's prior written authorisation; without it the suspension is invalid, Article 72(1) does not release the employer from wages, and the full wage and benefits are owed for the whole period. [Para 109][Para 116][Para 126] Because the Labour Law's rules are mandatory under Article 13(1), a worker's agreement to accept a reduced payment during such a period is void. [Para 129][Para 130][Para 133] Wages are not payable for days of strike, lawful or not. [Para 192] And where it is the workers who demand termination, notice compensation, damages and health compensation do not arise. [Para 170][Para 172]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel declined to read the phrase "if the company reopens production" in isolation from the rest of the 15 June agreement, holding that a truncated quotation cannot support an inference that the employer terminated. [Para 159][Para 162] It also observed, without deciding anything on it, that the absence of a most-representative union can obstruct workers' representation. [Para 90]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel is said to have been constituted on "23 August 2022 … 2016", and the operative reasoning carries stray year tokens ("2017", "2016") inside quotations. [Para 13][Para 128][Para 176] The date the employer called the workers back is given as 10 June 2016 in the chronology and as 25 June 2016 in the reasoning. [Para 67][Para 155] The crucial severance finding reverses itself: paragraph 203-204 holds that the employer owes nothing under the 15 June agreement because the workers refused it, and paragraph 205-207 holds that there is no evidence of refusal and that the employer must pay; both are reported above and both are cited. [Para 204][Para 205][Para 207] The employer's Latin name is printed as "Mirae Apparel Co., Ltd" and "Mireapparel Co., Ltd" while the Khmer runs through several variants ("មីរ៉េ", "ម៉ីរ៉េ", "អី ភែរីល"). [Para 21][Para 31][Para 51] The hearing date line and the award date are consistent, but the extension from 12 to 20 September is recorded only in the procedure section. [Para 9][Para 19] The 25 claimant workers, their ID-card numbers and the union officials are named in full in the source and are redacted here. [Para 40][Para 54]
