# Award No. 183/16 — Yi Da Manufacturer Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 24 August 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Sin Kim Sean, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 139084

## 1. Facts
Yi Da Manufacturer Co., Ltd ("Yi Da") is a garment-cutting and sewing enterprise in Kandal province employing roughly 1,600 workers [Para 110][Para 111]. The C.CAWDU federation (សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីពប្រជាធិបតេយ្យកម្មករកាត់ដេរកម្ពុជា) notified the company on 23 May 2016 of an election on 26 June 2016 to create a factory-level democratic union, naming seven candidates [Para 153][Para 167]. Between 25 and 26 May 2016 the company dismissed four of those candidates; conciliation at the Kandal provincial department of labour settled two of seven points and left five unresolved, which were forwarded to the Arbitration Council on 25 July 2016 [Para 17][Para 18].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer should cease anti-union discrimination by taking back the dismissed election candidates and paying their wages from the date of dismissal to the date of reinstatement? [Para 2][Para 115]
2. Whether the employer should pay USD 10 per month in travel allowance to each of four workers? [Para 3]
3. Whether the employer should pay USD 5 per month in health allowance to each worker? [Para 5]
4. Whether the employer should raise the midday meal allowance from 1,000 to 2,000 riel per day? [Para 7]
5. Whether the employer should issue one-year contracts and convert the contracts of workers with two or more years' service into undetermined-duration contracts? [Para 9]

At the hearing the workers withdrew issues 2 to 5, and within issue 1 withdrew the claims of three of the seven candidates, leaving four — Mr T, Ms S, Ms S and Mr V — so the panel considered issue 1 alone [Para 22][Para 23][Para 116].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The four were dismissed without the labour inspector's authorisation precisely because they stood as union-election candidates; they had no written contract after probation, and the company pressured them to accept a lump sum and resign rather than proceed [Para 117][Para 119][Para 162].
**Employer (respondent):** The dismissals rested on serious misconduct under the internal rules — failure to perform work during working hours, and deceiving other workers that the company had promised life insurance — and the company never received the election notice, learning of the candidacies only at conciliation; there is no anti-union discrimination, since a most representative union and a collective agreement exist in the factory [Para 133][Para 155][Para 158][Para 161].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed / no jurisdiction to consider — the panel refused to consider Case No. 183/16 in its entirety; no reinstatement, back pay or allowance was ordered [Para 181][Para 182].
The panel did not reach the merits of the dismissals. It read Article 54(1) of the Law on Trade Unions of 17 May 2016 as reserving the exclusive right to conclude collective agreements and to resolve collective labour disputes to the most representative union within the enterprise [Para 170][Para 171]. Following its interpretation in Award No. 180/16 (Cement Cambodia Chakrey Ting) and Award No. 171/16 (Jewellery Co., Ltd), it held that this exclusivity extends to carriage of a dispute before the Arbitration Council itself, so that only the most representative union may represent workers there [Para 173][Para 174][Para 176].
On the facts, Yi Da had a most representative union, the Cambodia Workers' Union, holding certificate No. 041/16, and the 13 workers who transferred their rights to C.CAWDU did not thereby convert C.CAWDU into that union [Para 178][Para 179]. C.CAWDU therefore had no right to represent them in the collective dispute, and the panel declined to consider the case [Para 180][Para 181]. The award was declared binding and effective immediately on signature, under the parties' agreement of 3 August 2016 [Para 182].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an enterprise already has a union certified as most representative under Article 54(1) of the Law on Trade Unions (2016), a different federation to which individual workers have transferred their rights has no standing to bring a collective labour dispute to the Arbitration Council on their behalf, and the panel will decline to consider the case however strong the underlying dismissal claims appear. [Para 175][Para 180]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel recorded, without deciding, the employer's allegations of serious misconduct and the workers' account of a USD 4,000 payment made on 14 July 2016, and noted the employer's statement that 350 US dollars had been paid as severance [Para 133][Para 144][Para 162].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The most-representative certificate No. 041/16 is dated 20 June 2016 at [Para 34] but 20 June 2018 — with validity to 20 June 2018 — at [Para 114] and [Para 178]; both readings are reported as printed. The panel's constitution is printed as "27 July 2022 2016" [Para 19]. The list of 13 transferring workers at [Para 112] is numbered only to (12) before naming the thirteenth, and worker names appear in several spellings across the record (e.g. "ស្រ៊ុន លីម"/"ស្រ៊ុន លឹម"), with one reduced to a bare "ស្រ៊ី" [Para 113][Para 115].
