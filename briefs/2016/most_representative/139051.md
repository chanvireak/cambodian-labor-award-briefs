# Award No. 145/16 — Dali Garment International (Cambodia) Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 18 July 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Kong Phalla chair) · Doc ID 139051

## 1. Facts
Dali Garment International (Cambodia) Ltd. is a garment factory at Trapeang Kong, Samraong Tong, Kampong Speu, employing about 476 workers. [Para 67][Para 68] A strike over working conditions produced twelve non-conciliation issues, of which the parties settled ten by agreement on 21 June 2016, leaving only issues 9 and 12 for the panel. [Para 19][Para 26][Para 27] Four staff delegates elected in 2015, still within their term, appeared for the workers, and a Free Workers Union had been registered at the factory on 25 December 2015. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 72] The employer pays USD 10 a month as a midday meal allowance and, at each expiry of a fixed-duration contract, pays a 5 per cent contract-end allowance and annual leave money even where the contract is renewed. [Para 74][Para 81][Para 131]

## 2. Issues in dispute
9. Whether the employer must pay a midday meal allowance of USD 15 a month to the workers? [Para 74][Para 92]
12. Whether the employer must pay the 5 per cent contract-end allowance and the annual leave compensation on the monthly payday, while the employment relationship continues? [Para 80][Para 115]

Issues 1 to 8, 10 and 11 were settled by the parties before the hearing and are not restated in the order. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 147]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** other factories pay more than USD 10 a month for meals — San Bes pays USD 12, Sabrina and Ditley pay USD 20 — so USD 15 is reasonable. [Para 76][Para 77] The 5 per cent and the annual leave money should be paid with the monthly wage, because accounting has already computed them and only administrative re-checking causes delay, sometimes of two months. [Para 83][Para 85]
**Employer (respondent):** the law obliges it to pay only USD 10 a month for meals, and the factory has only just begun production so it cannot afford more. [Para 78][Para 79] The 5 per cent and annual leave money are deliberately shown on separate payroll lines for easier verification, and any delay was a technical error in entering four workers on the payroll. [Para 84][Para 86]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both remaining claims were refused; the panel declined to consider the meal allowance claim and rejected the payment-timing claim. [Para 146][Para 147][Para 148]

*Who were the claimants.* Trade Union Law Article 32 requires workers in an enterprise with at least eight employees to elect staff delegates to represent them, and the panel held the four elected delegates, whose term had not expired, were the claimants representing the workforce. [Para 88][Para 90][Para 91][Para 112]

*Issue 9.* The parties agreed and the panel found that the employer already pays food money above the statutory minimum, and that no provision of the Labour Law, a collective agreement, the internal rules, a contract, an agreement between the parties or past practice requires USD 15. [Para 92][Para 93] The claim was therefore a demand above the legal minimum, that is an interest dispute. [Para 94] Because an award in an interest dispute becomes a collective agreement binding every worker in the company under Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099, and Article 96(2) of the Labour Law with Articles 54 and 72 of the Trade Union Law reserve that power to a most-representative union, the panel examined whether the claimant had that status. [Para 95][Para 98][Para 106][Para 107] The Free Workers Union was registered, and neither party asserted it was not most representative, but the claimants were four staff delegates rather than that union, so they could not bring an interest dispute. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 112] The claim was refused without consideration. [Para 113][Para 114][Para 147]

*Issue 12.* First limb: Article 73 of the Labour Law ends a fixed-duration contract at its expiry and, under Article 73(6), requires a contract-end allowance equal to the wages due for the unexpired term, at least 5 per cent of the wages paid during the contract. [Para 117][Para 120][Para 121] Following Award No. 60/06, the panel read the provision as compensating the severing of the employment relationship: where the contract is renewed the relationship continues, and a worker may claim the 5 per cent only when the relationship is actually cut off. [Para 127][Para 128][Para 130] The employer here paid 5 per cent at each expiry even while renewing, so it owed nothing while the workers remained employed and the claim had no legal basis. [Para 131][Para 132][Para 133] Second limb: Article 167 requires annual leave compensation only where the contract ends before the worker has acquired the leave, and its third indent voids any agreement to waive that right. [Para 134][Para 136][Para 137] Paying annual leave money while the relationship continues is therefore not required, and the workers had no right to it before leaving. [Para 141][Para 142][Para 144] Both limbs were refused. [Para 145][Para 146][Para 148]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The 5 per cent contract-end allowance and the annual leave compensation under Articles 73(6) and 167 fall due only when the employment relationship is actually severed: an employer that renews a fixed-duration contract owes neither, and paying them early is a voluntary benefit that creates no obligation to keep paying on the monthly payday. [Para 128][Para 131][Para 142] A claim for a benefit above the statutory minimum is an interest dispute, which the panel may not decide when it is brought by staff delegates rather than by a most-representative union, since an award there would bind every worker as a collective agreement. [Para 94][Para 106][Para 112]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that the employer's early payment of annual leave money, though more generous than the law, was "not in conformity" with Article 167 was an observation on practice, not a finding that the practice was unlawful; nothing was ordered about it. [Para 143][Para 144]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The dispositive order on issue 9 prints the claim as "USD 150 a month" [Para 147]; the pleadings and reasons fix it at USD 15 [Para 74][Para 92], which the order must be read as meaning.
- [Para 92] records the current meal allowance as "២ ០៧ ដុល្លារ" (a stray 07), while [Para 74] and the employer fix it at USD 10.
- [Para 90] prints "ប្រតិភូ បុគ្គលិក មាន ៩៧៦ សិទ្ធិ", a stray number inside the sentence on delegates' right to represent.
- [Para 23] invites the parties to a hearing and conciliation on 23 June 2016, while [Para 18] dates the hearing 6 July 2016 and [Para 22] the panel's formation 24 June 2016.
- The evidence list loses its numbering, repeating items ("២០១១-" at [Para 41], a second item 8 at [Para 48], "៥- ២៨-" at [Para 57]) and including an unnumbered "18" [Para 48].
- The chair is printed "គង់ ផល្ហៈ" [Para 1], a variant of the "Kong Phalla" who chairs other 2016 awards.
- [Para 108] contains long non-Khmer OCR debris ("អាមេរិក អាច បញ្ហា រួម…១៧ ខែសីហា ឆ្នាំ ២០២៣"), including a date seven years after the award; it was disregarded.
- The four staff delegates and the workers named in the warning letters are not named here, per the anonymisation convention.
- No sum is ordered on any issue; the only money figures are the disputed USD 10 and USD 15 claims.
