# Award No. 098/16 — Crystal Martin (Cambodia) Limited (Arbitration Council, 10 June 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chiv Phirom, appointed by the employer; Sin Kim Sean, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, chair appointed by both) · Doc ID 139026

## 1. Facts
Crystal Martin (Cambodia) Limited, a garment-sewing factory at Vihear Suor Leu, Khsach Kandal, Kandal, had about 5,300 workers before the 2016 downturn and about 4,647 at the hearing. [Para 70] Its company union, registered in 2013, had its second-term leadership recognised by the Ministry of Labour on 17 March 2016, and the federation held mandates from 221 workers by letter of 29 February 2016. [Para 71][Para 72] On 18 February 2016 the company dismissed five table-cutting workers as the first stage of a planned collective lay-off of 472, paying each five heads of indemnity. [Para 77][Para 164] The union complained on 15 March 2016; conciliation on 6 April 2016 settled four of nine issues, and the workers withdrew issues 2 and 3, leaving issues 1, 4 and 5. [Para 18][Para 24]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must take back the five dismissed table-cutting workers and pay their wages from dismissal to return? [Para 152]
4. Whether the company must keep wages and other benefits unchanged while workers attend meetings of the federation or of the authorities, or attend seminars? [Para 210]
5. Whether the company must deduct the regular attendance bonus only in proportion to the days of leave it has authorised? [Para 249]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the five had no prior misconduct and worked well; the company dismissed them without following the collective-dismissal procedure, and in fact dismissed the longest-serving workers while keeping newer ones and hiring outside workers into their section; wages should not be cut when they attend union meetings or conciliation, and the attendance bonus should not be forfeited in full for authorised leave. [Para 81][Para 88][Para 144]
**Employer (respondent):** the company faced an economic crisis and lack of orders from December 2015, notified the labour inspector and the worker representatives, and ranked workers by professional merit first and seniority second; the two dismissed workers with warnings were properly selected, and those who remained were simply not renewed; the piece-rate section was staffed by another company's workers, and the 31 March 2016 agreement governs the bonus. [Para 95][Para 99][Para 146][Para 167]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 1 dismissed, reinstatement and back wages refused; on issue 4 the company must keep wages and benefits unchanged when workers attend conciliation or arbitration on proper invitation, but not for seminars or federation meetings; on issue 5 the company may deduct the attendance bonus only in proportion to the days of authorised leave. [Para 297][Para 298][Para 301]

On issue 1 the panel took Article 95 of the Labour Law as its frame: a reduction of activity requires an order of dismissal fixed by professional merit, seniority and family burden, notice to the worker representatives, and notice to the labour inspector. [Para 153] It found the company had notified the inspector and met the representatives, and that two of the five had genuine warnings, so their selection by professional merit was lawful. [Para 170][Para 172][Para 175] The other three, however, had entered the company before more than 100 of the 120 table-cutting workers earmarked for the later phase, yet were dismissed in the first phase, so the ranking was not fully compliant with Article 95. [Para 179][Para 184] That made the dismissal one without valid grounds, engaging Article 91. [Para 191][Para 193] Because the company genuinely had no work, an order to reinstate could not be performed, so compensation was the appropriate remedy, and since the five heads of indemnity including damages had already been paid, no further sum was ordered. [Para 200][Para 203] The panel nonetheless recorded the workers' priority right to re-employment for two years under Article 95(5). [Para 205]

On issue 4 the panel found no provision requiring wages to be maintained for federation meetings or seminars, so that part was an interest dispute which a non-most-representative union cannot bring. [Para 211][Para 212] Attendance at conciliation and arbitration is different: Article 306 of the Labour Law and Article 10 of Prakas No. 305 provide that representatives must attend when summoned and must not have wages, bonuses or other benefits cut for doing so. [Para 227][Para 233] The company's practice of charging such absences against annual leave or deducting wages was therefore wrong, and the order was limited to attendance at the provincial department of labour and the Council on proper invitation. [Para 241][Para 242]

On issue 5 the panel held that the 10 USD monthly attendance bonus set by Ministry Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 is a mandatory general rule for the garment and footwear sector, not a discretionary management tool. [Para 254] It is a wage, being a sum paid as a bonus. [Para 280] Leave authorised by the employer suspends the contract under Article 71(6), so no wage is owed for those days and the bonus may be reduced in proportion to them, but it may not be forfeited in full. [Para 284][Para 286] The 31 March 2016 agreement, which cancelled the bonus entirely for more than three days' authorised leave, gave less than the law allows and was void under Article 13. [Para 291][Para 295]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A collective lay-off under Article 95 is lawful only if the order of dismissal actually follows professional merit, seniority and family burden; where longer-serving workers are dismissed ahead of newer ones in an earlier phase, the selection is defective and the dismissal lacks valid grounds under Article 91, even though the economic reason for the reduction is genuine. [Para 179][Para 184][Para 193]

An employer must not deduct wages, bonuses or benefits from worker representatives for attending conciliation or arbitration when properly summoned; and an attendance bonus required by Ministry Notification No. 230 may be reduced only in proportion to authorised leave days, never forfeited in full. [Para 233][Para 286]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's statement that it could not order reinstatement because the factory had no work, and its observation that the workers retain a two-year priority right to re-employment under Article 95(5), were not necessary to the dismissal of the claim, the damages having already been paid. [Para 200][Para 205]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award moves between three findings on the same dismissal: procedure complied with, procedure defective for three workers, and no valid grounds — the dispositive order records only a refusal. [Para 184][Para 192][Para 297]
- The workforce is given as about 5,300 before the downturn and 4,647 at the hearing, while the lay-off plan covers 472 and the first phase about 30. [Para 70][Para 164]
- The complainant group is described as 221 mandate-holders and elsewhere as the union's members generally. [Para 73][Para 76]
- The regular attendance bonus is printed as both 10 USD and "4 10" USD per month. [Para 287]
- The 31 March 2016 agreement is dated variously and is described in one place as concluded on 01 September (year unclear). [Para 294]
- Several paragraphs are OCR fragments with repeated clauses and stray year strings ("2017", "2019", "2022"); the signature block for the employer-appointed arbitrator is illegible. [Para 303]
