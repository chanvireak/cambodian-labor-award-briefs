# Award No. 130/16 — Khem Tekang Sport Tek Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 28 June 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Ang Eng Thong chair) · Doc ID 139041

## 1. Facts
Khem Tekang Sport Tek Co., Ltd., a glove manufacturer in Prey Chas village, Vihear Suor commune, Khsach Kandal district, Kandal, employed about 845 workers and had one local union, the Khmer Workers' Heart Union, with 400 members but no recognition as most-representative. [Para 1][Para 28][Para 29][Para 33] Conciliation of seven points on 27 May 2016 left two in dispute: a demand that pregnant workers be allowed to leave 15 minutes early (the company allowed 5), and a demand that annual leave deducted in September 2015 be restored. [Para 3][Para 4][Para 12][Para 13] The employer did not attend the hearing of 15 June 2016, telling the Secretariat it would not come because the demands were beyond the law. [Para 10][Para 17]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must allow pregnant workers to leave fifteen minutes before the end of the shift? [Para 34][Para 53]
2. Whether the employer must restore the annual leave days it deducted in September 2015? [Para 38][Para 77]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** five minutes is too short for a pregnant worker to walk the roughly 60 metres from the production building to the food stalls, and in practice no pregnant worker has yet been injured leaving work; in September 2015 the employer gave time off at full wages because there was no work, then deducted that time from annual leave without the workers' agreement, and the union president refused to thumbprint the deduction. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 37][Para 39][Para 42][Para 79]
**Employer (respondent):** the demands are beyond the law; on the second point it has the right to have workers use annual leave at times when there is no work for them. [Para 17][Para 48]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 1 dismissed; on issue 2 the panel ordered the employer to restore to 19 workers the annual leave days actually deducted in September 2015. [Para 100][Para 101][Para 99]

The panel first had to decide whether it could hear the case at all, the employer being absent. [Para 44] Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 allows it to continue where a party duly invited fails to appear without valid reason, and earlier awards had read that as permitting a hearing in the party's absence. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46] The Secretariat had emailed and telephoned the employer with the date and time of the hearing, and the employer knew of it. [Para 47][Para 48] The panel found no valid reason for non-attendance, held that the employer had thereby abandoned its right to respond to the workers' submissions, and accepted those submissions. [Para 49][Para 51][Para 52][Para 55][Para 56][Para 80][Para 81]

*Issue 1.* No provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, employment contract, agreement between the parties or past practice obliges the employer to release pregnant workers fifteen minutes early, so the demand was an interests dispute. [Para 57][Para 58] On an interests dispute the panel considered the union's representativeness: Article 96(2) of the Labour Law, Articles 54 and 72 of the Law on Trade Unions of 17 May 2016 and Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099 confine the right to conclude a collective agreement — and hence to bring an interests dispute — to the most-representative union, whose award replaces the collective agreement for one year. [Para 59][Para 61][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70][Para 71] Following its earlier awards, the panel declined to consider an interests dispute brought by a union without that status, and found this union had not yet acquired it. [Para 72][Para 74][Para 75][Para 76]

*Issue 2.* Article 170 of the Labour Law requires annual leave in principle to be granted during the Khmer New Year period unless another time is agreed, with the employer notifying the labour inspector in advance, and it allows an employer to fix remaining days elsewhere only for workers under 18. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 84] Earlier awards read Article 170 as permitting annual leave outside Khmer New Year only by agreement between the workers and the employer, notified to the labour inspector. [Para 86][Para 87][Para 88] One earlier award stated squarely that an employer cannot use workers' annual leave for periods when the company has no work for them. [Para 89][Para 91][Para 92] The panel held that even if workers did not attend on the days the employer told them to stay away, that did not mean they agreed to spend annual leave, precisely because there was no work, and they had not thumbprinted any agreement. [Para 95][Para 96] Deducting annual leave in those circumstances without any agreement was contrary to Article 170, so the employer had to restore the days actually deducted in September 2015. [Para 97][Para 98]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may not charge a workless period against workers' annual leave: under Article 170 leave is taken at Khmer New Year unless another time is agreed with the workers and notified to the labour inspector, and the employer's unilateral direction to stay home when there is no work is not such an agreement, whether or not the workers attended. [Para 86][Para 89][Para 95][Para 97] A demand that goes beyond any legal, contractual or practice-based obligation is an interests dispute, which the panel will not consider unless brought by the most-representative union. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 71][Para 74] A party duly invited who stays away without valid reason abandons its right to respond, and the panel may proceed in its absence. [Para 44][Para 49][Para 51]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that employers must notify the labour inspector before fixing leave outside Khmer New Year, and that leave arrangements must be clear and set for a period other than the Khmer New Year, are general guidance going beyond the two orders made. [Para 83][Para 87][Para 91][Para 92]
