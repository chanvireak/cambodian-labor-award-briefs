# Award Nos. 006 & 010/16 — Kem Vanna Fashion Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 10 March 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Ven Vou (worker-appointed), Pen Bunchhea (chair) · Doc ID 139390

## 1. Facts
Kem Vanna Fashion Co., Ltd. is a garment manufacturer employing about 1,079 workers. [Para 134] The Khmer Workers' Heart Union Federation organised a union election at the company on 2 November 2015; the union had not yet received its registration certificate from the Ministry. [Para 135] Between 17 and 19 December 2015 the company terminated four union figures, paying each statutory termination indemnity, which none of them collected. [Para 166] Two failed conciliations produced reports 149/16 of 14 January 2016 and 165/16 of 18 January 2016, which the panel consolidated. [Para 77]

## 2. Issues in dispute
Of 25 points, 13 were settled by agreement and point 18 was withdrawn, leaving eleven for adjudication. [Para 80]
1. Whether the employer must restore three meals a day to workers as before? [Para 140]
2. Whether the employer must take back the four union leaders into normal work with benefits preserved? [Para 151]
3. Whether the employer must dismiss the administration chief, Mr S, from the company? [Para 204]
4. Whether the employer must build a nursery and a breastfeeding room? [Para 219]
5. Whether the employer must pay 100% wages during work suspension and repay the 50% withheld in March, April and August 2015? [Para 227]
6. Whether the employer must raise the regular attendance bonus to USD 15 a month and pro-rate deductions by days of lawful absence? [Para 246]
7. Whether the employer must pay 2,000 riel meal money for work on public holidays? [Para 255]
8. Whether the employer must pay 1,500 riel an hour for overtime work, 3,000 riel in total? [Para 261]
9. Whether the employer must restore holiday bonuses as before? [Para 268]
10. Whether the employer must give notice before ending three-month fixed-duration contracts? [Para 278]
11. Whether the employer must pay 50% wages and allowances across the 90 days at least one day before maternity leave? [Para 283]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the terminations were union discrimination against elected leaders; the meal, bonus and allowance claims rested on established past practice; and the company owed a nursery and timely maternity pay under the Labour Law. [Para 196]
**Employer (respondent):** it had no capacity to raise benefits; the suspensions were authorised by the Labour Inspector; the dismissals rested on misconduct — fabricated sick leave, incitement to strike, leafleting during working hours — and none of the four was a protected worker. [Para 202]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must take back Mr H, Ms P and Ms S into normal work with benefits preserved; provide a nursery; and pay 50% wages and allowances for the 90-day maternity period at least one day before leave begins. All remaining claims were dismissed. [Para 545]

**Past practice (issue 1).** The panel defined past practice as a consistent, long-established practice acknowledged by both parties, which need not be written, Article 65 permitting oral contracts. [Para 292] It ends, however, where the parties agree a fresh arrangement. [Para 303] Three meals ran from 2012 to June 2014; from 17 June 2015 the parties agreed a USD 10 monthly meal allowance in substitution, which the workers accepted and received. [Para 309] The claim was therefore refused. [Para 316]

**Reinstatement (issue 2).** Article 293 bars dismissing a workers' delegate or candidate without the Labour Inspector's prior consent. [Para 318] Article 3(3) of Prakas No. 305 protects candidates for 45 days before the poll and 45 days after it; for candidates actually elected the Prakas fixes no end date, and following Award No. 40/10 the panel held protection runs at least 45 days and until application for registration. [Para 325] Applying the settled three conditions [Para 326]: on status, Mr H, Ms P and Ms S were elected president, vice-president and secretary, but no evidence showed Ms L to have been a candidate, so condition one failed for her alone [Para 331]; on timing, the dismissals fell 46, 45 and 47 days after the poll, a reasonable period [Para 339]; on notice, the union announced the election on 5 November 2015 in person and by post, receipt being admitted [Para 346]. No authorisation was ever sought from the Labour Inspector, so reinstatement was ordered under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 357]

Ms L's own dismissal failed separately: she proved no discrimination under Articles 12 and 279, the burden lying on the complainant. [Para 366] Her fixed-duration contract having been cut short, Article 73(3) gives compensation to the contract end only, so reinstatement was refused. [Para 383]

**Administration chief (issue 3).** Only a party to the contract may terminate it — Article 65(1) of the Labour Law and Article 311 of the Civil Code — and workers must prove the person is dangerous to others; neither was shown. [Para 388] Refused. [Para 404]

**Nursery (issue 4).** Article 186 requires any enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a breastfeeding room and nursery, or to pay actual invoices for outside care. [Para 412] The internal-rule USD 5 allowance contradicts Article 186 and is void under Article 13. [Para 421] The employer was ordered to provide the nursery or reimburse actual invoices. [Para 424]

**Suspension (issue 5).** Article 72(1) lifts the wage obligation during a lawful suspension, and Article 71(11) requires Inspector supervision and a maximum of two months. [Para 444] Letter 627/15 authorised suspension of 800 workers over May and August 2015, and the employer paid 50% while preserving other benefits. [Para 242] No repayment was owed. [Para 452]

**Interest disputes (issues 6, 7, 9, 14).** Bonus and meal-money claims resting on no legal rule, agreement or practice are interest disputes; under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 an award on interest becomes a collective agreement binding every worker, so only the most representative union may bring one. [Para 466] With 429 workers the claimant union was not most representative. [Para 469] Issue 9 also failed because notification 041/11 already secures 2,000 riel or a meal for overtime, which the employer honoured [Para 494]; issue 14 failed for want of any evidence [Para 517].

**Notice (issue 23).** Article 73(5) requires ten days' notice only for fixed-duration contracts exceeding six months, measured on the actual contract term and not accumulated seniority, so nothing was owed on three-month contracts. [Para 525]

**Maternity pay (issue 25).** Articles 182 and 183 give 90 days at half pay but fix no payment date; Article 115(3) bars payment on a rest day and requires it the day before, and Article 103 counts maternity pay as wages. [Para 536] Monthly payment breached Article 115, so the employer must pay at least one day before leave. [Para 542]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A past practice, however consistent, is extinguished once the parties agree a replacement benefit and the workers accept it; and the Article 186 duty of an enterprise employing 100 or more women to provide a nursery cannot be discharged by a cash allowance in the internal rules, because Article 13 voids rules contrary to the Labour Law. [Para 315]

**Obiter dicta:** that an award resolving an interest dispute operates as a collective agreement for one year and removes the right to strike over that interest [Para 465]; and that elected union leaders, unlike defeated candidates, have no fixed end to their protection under Prakas No. 305 [Para 324].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The face of the award joins two file numbers, "006 & 010/16", and the panel postponed the award date from 8 February 2016 to 10 March 2016 at the parties' request [Para 82]. The decision on issue 5 prints the claim as "950 per cent" where the reasons say 50 per cent [Para 550]; 429 workers are described both as the complainant group and as "10 claimants" [Para 468]. The meal-practice change is dated to June 2014 in one passage [Para 146] and to 17 June 2015 in another [Para 150]. Suspension letter 627/15 of 23 April 2015 authorises stoppages in May and August 2015, two months apart from the letter [Para 242]. Individual workers, the administration chief and union officers appear under initials by convention; the source prints their full names.
