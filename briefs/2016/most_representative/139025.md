# Award No. 097/16 — Best Sources (Cambodia) Factory Ltd (Arbitration Council, 26 May 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer, dissenting in part; An Non, appointed by the workers; Kong Phalla, chair appointed by both) · Doc ID 139025

## 1. Facts
Best Sources (Cambodia) Factory Ltd, a garment-sewing factory at Trapeang Thleung, Chom Chao, Pur Senchey, Phnom Penh, employed about 605 workers. [Para 88] The Federation of Trade Unions for Labour Rights organised a company-level union, whose leadership election was held on 19 January 2016; the federation received a receipt for its registration application from the Ministry of Labour on 28 January 2016, and no registration certificate had been issued by the hearing date. [Para 89] The union complained on 7 March 2016; conciliation settled six of twelve issues on 8 April 2016, and the workers withdrew issues 2, 3 and 4 and part of issue 1, leaving issues 1, 8 and 9. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 29] The hearing was held on 2 May 2016; the award issued 26 May 2016. [Para 20]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must take back the union president, Ms P, and pay her wages from the date of dismissal until she returns to work? [Para 153]
8. Whether the company must raise the midday meal allowance to 4,000 riel per day? [Para 191]
9. Whether the company must issue employment contracts of 6 months' duration instead of 3? [Para 208]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the president was dismissed on 29 February 2016, two days before her contract expired and within the 45-day protection around the union election, without the labour inspector's prior agreement and without notice; she should be reinstated with back pay, the meal allowance should rise to 4,000 riel because market prices have risen, and 6-month contracts should replace 3-month ones, which the employer uses to end employment easily. [Para 101][Para 120][Para 127]
**Employer (respondent):** the contract was not renewed but simply came to its term, the company had no orders from buyers, the union was not validly registered because its leaders lacked a year's service under Article 269, and it cannot raise the meal allowance while facing economic difficulty or be forced into 6-month contracts, which are the director's decision. [Para 106][Para 117][Para 121][Para 129]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — on issue 1 the company was ordered to take Ms P back and pay her wages from the date of dismissal until she returns; on issue 8 the panel declined to consider the meal allowance; on issue 9 the demand for 6-month contracts was refused. [Para 242][Para 243][Para 244]

Before the merits the panel addressed the union's standing: the registration application had been pending more than two months, from the receipt of 28 January 2016 to the hearing, so under Article 268 of the Labour Law the union is deemed registered and may represent its members. [Para 143][Para 144] The federation held mandates from 23 valid thumbprints; one of the 24 bore no name or employee number. [Para 92][Para 94]

On issue 1 the panel found that Ms P's contract ran from 1 September 2015 to 1 March 2016, but she was called in and told of its end on 29 February 2016 — before the term — so this was a dismissal, not a non-renewal. [Para 97][Para 158] She had been elected union president on 19 January 2016 and the union notified the administration chief of the result and of the elected names on 20 January 2016, so all three conditions for protection were met: she was a protected person, the dismissal fell inside the 45 days after the election, and the union had notified the employer by an available means. [Para 160][Para 169][Para 173][Para 178] The company had not obtained the labour inspector's prior agreement, as Article 293 and clause 3 of Article 3 of Prakas No. 305 require, so the dismissal was unlawful and reinstatement with wages from dismissal to return followed. [Para 184][Para 189]

On issue 8 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, contract or past practice obliging the company to pay 4,000 riel; the demand exceeded the legal minimum and was therefore an interest dispute, which the panel may decide only where the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise. [Para 191][Para 193] This union was not, so the panel refused to consider the claim. [Para 203][Para 207]

On issue 9 the panel held that a contract is formed by offer and acceptance, and neither party can impose terms the other does not accept; the workers' request for 6-month contracts was lawful, but so was the company's refusal, and the change from 6-month to 3-month terms from early 2016 was the director's decision. [Para 224][Para 233][Para 237] The claim was refused. [Para 241]

**Dissent:** Ing Sothy agreed that Ms P should be reinstated but dissented on back wages, holding that her fixed-term contract ended on 29 February 2016, that the company had already paid the 5% indemnity, and that Article 73(3) entitles her only to wages up to the contract's term; no law fixes damages at the daily wage for the whole period, and she submitted no evidence of loss. [Para 250][Para 252][Para 253][Para 258]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker elected as a union leader is protected against dismissal from the date of the election for 45 days, provided the union has notified the employer of the elected names by any available means; a dismissal inside that window without the labour inspector's prior agreement is unlawful, and the remedy is reinstatement with wages from the dismissal until return — the employer's view that the contract merely expired does not answer a termination effected before its term. [Para 158][Para 173][Para 184][Para 189]

An interest dispute over a benefit above the legal minimum can be entertained only where the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise; a minority union's demand is outside the panel's reach even where the underlying grievance is real. [Para 201][Para 203]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's treatment of the pending registration as effective registration after two months under Article 268 was decided as a threshold matter and was not itself the basis of any order; the dissent's view that damages cannot exceed wages to the contract's end is a minority opinion, not the panel's holding. [Para 144][Para 253]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The union's registration status is described both as unregistered at the hearing and as deemed registered; the employer's refusal letter cites Article 269 and a one-year service requirement that the panel never rules on directly. [Para 117][Para 141][Para 144]
- The panel dates the pending period's end at 26 May 2016 as "the hearing date", but the hearing was held on 2 May 2016 and 26 May is the award date. [Para 20][Para 143]
- The mandate count is given as 249 thumbprints, then 24, then 23 valid, and separately as 23 throughout the analysis. [Para 92][Para 93][Para 149]
- Ms P's name appears as Pa Sarim, Pa Sarym and Pa Sarim with a stray diacritic; she is reduced to an initial here. The vice-president appears as Bou Sophatra and Bou Sophaktra. [Para 91][Para 176]
- Article 73(3) is invoked in the dissent but the majority never states the damages provision it applies when ordering back wages. [Para 189][Para 253]
- The employer's account of an oral notice on 22 February 2016 and a notice of indemnity on 26 February 2016 is recorded but not resolved against the 29 February date. [Para 111]
