# Award No. 210/16 — Great Honor Textile Factory Limited (Arbitration Council, 15 September 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, employer-nominated; Sin Kim San, worker-nominated; Ang Eng Thong, president) · Doc ID 139369

## 1. Facts
The company, a weaving and garment enterprise at Village 4, Svay Rolum, Sa'ang, Kandal, employed about 982 workers, of whom 720 belonged to the Democratic Union of Garment Workers of Cambodia at the factory [Para 42][Para 43][Para 48]. On 6 August 2016, a wage-payment day, the workers attended in the morning, were sent home for half a day at 50% pay for lack of work, and found the employer gone in the afternoon with July 2016 wages unpaid; the employer never returned and the courts were executing a protective order over the site [Para 55][Para 56][Para 14]. The union, holding the most-representative certificate No. 057/15 dated 17 August 2015 and valid to 17 August 2017, brought a collective dispute claiming termination compensation for all 720 named workers [Para 46][Para 51]. The employer never appeared at the hearing despite service by registered post, which the postal agent returned marked "company suspended operations" [Para 13][Para 69].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. When did the employer terminate the employment contracts of the 720 workers? [Para 84]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to pay the five heads of termination compensation claimed (notice, severance, moral damages, unused annual leave, final wages) to those 720 workers? [Para 92]

The workers withdrew their second original dispute at the hearing, leaving only dispute 1 for adjudication [Para 17][Para 18].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** All 720 had indefinite-duration contracts and 10 to 18 years' seniority; the employer's afternoon disappearance on 6 August 2016, without notice and without a valid reason, was a unilateral termination entitling each of them to notice pay under Article 75, severance under Article 89, moral damages under Article 91, 19 days of unused annual leave under Article 166, and July wages plus six days of August wages under Article 116 [Para 52][Para 57][Para 60].

**Employer (respondent):** Filed no evidence and did not attend, so it advanced no contention on the merits and is treated as having abandoned its right to reply [Para 90][Para 22].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay termination compensation to all 720 workers (listed in the annex) under five heads: (1) notice pay under Articles 75 and 77; (2) severance under Article 89; (3) moral damages under Article 91; (4) unused annual leave under Articles 166 and 167 where not already paid; and (5) final wages under Article 116 [Para 161][Para 156]. No aggregate sum is printed; the award states the heads of liability only, with per-worker figures left to the annex.

On procedure, the panel applied Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council: where a party has been properly summoned and fails to appear without valid excuse, the Council may proceed or close the case by award [Para 62]. It found service had been attempted at the factory on 24 August 2016 and then by registered post on 29 August 2016, that the post was returned because the company had suspended operations, and that the employer had shown no valid reason for absence; it therefore heard the case in the employer's absence, on the workers' evidence [Para 67][Para 70][Para 71].

On standing, the panel applied Article 54 of the Law on Trade Unions (17 May 2016), under which only the most representative union holds the exclusive right to represent workers in collective bargaining and collective dispute resolution, including before the Arbitration Council, following Award 180/16 — Cement Cambodia Chakrey Ting [Para 72][Para 75][Para 77]. It found the claimant union held certificate No. 057/15 valid to 17 August 2017 and that its leaders attended the hearing, so the union lawfully represented all the workers [Para 81][Para 83].

On the date of termination, the panel accepted the workers' uncontradicted account that the employer left the factory on the afternoon of 6 August 2016 and never paid July wages, and held the contracts terminated that day [Para 85][Para 88][Para 91].

On liability, Article 74 permits unilateral termination of an indefinite-duration contract only on notice and only for a valid reason relating to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the operational needs of the enterprise [Para 93][Para 95]. The panel found the employer gave no reason of that kind and made no internal reorganisation, so the termination was unlawful, following Award 029/15 — British American Tobacco Cambodia [Para 97][Para 101]. It then computed each head: notice pay equal to wages for the statutory notice period fixed by seniority under Article 75, read with Article 77, using average monthly pay over the preceding 12 months [Para 107][Para 113]; severance of 15 days' wages and allowances per year of service under Article 89, capped at six months, the panel finding no serious misconduct [Para 116][Para 120][Para 121]; moral damages under Article 91 equal to the severance, the employer having shown no valid reason [Para 129][Para 135]; unused annual leave under Articles 166 and 167 on the same 12-month average [Para 142][Para 147]; and final wages to the last working day under Article 116 [Para 154].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that closes its factory and disappears mid-pay-period, leaving wages unpaid and giving no reason connected to the workers' aptitude, conduct or the operational needs of the enterprise, effects a unilateral termination without valid reason; every affected worker on an indefinite-duration contract is then entitled to notice pay, statutory severance, moral damages equal to that severance, payment for unused annual leave, and final wages, and the panel may determine those entitlements ex parte once proper service has been attempted. [Para 101][Para 111][Para 135][Para 71]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that only the most representative union may carry a collective dispute to the Council, so a minority union's claim would fail on standing even where the merits were strong; it also noted that the employer's non-appearance forfeited its right of reply rather than shifting any burden of proof onto it. [Para 77][Para 90]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR is heavily degraded. The employer's name appears in at least five spellings (ហ្គ្រេត អន នើរ ថេក ស្តាយ ហ្វេក ធើរី and variants), rendered on the face as "Great Honor Textile Factory Limited" per the annex letter [Para 27]. No riel or USD figure survives for any head of compensation: the operative paragraphs order the five heads and refer to the annex, whose per-worker table is reduced to fragmented rows of names and employee numbers, so amounts cannot be reported [Para 161][Para 167]. Certificate No. 057/15 is dated 2015 in the text but the same sentence prints "ឆ្នាំ ២០១៩ ឆ្នាំ ២០១៥" (2019 then 2015); the panel elsewhere relies on 17 August 2015 to 17 August 2017 [Para 46][Para 81]. The letter at Para 32 is dated "21 May 2915". The workers claimed 19 days of unused annual leave but the panel's finding reads 20 days [Para 60][Para 142]. The number of employees is given as approximately 982 while claimants are 720 [Para 43][Para 48]. The list of issues at Para 84 is garbled and loses item numbering, and the two-dispute framing survives only in the procedural recitals.
