# Award No. 022/16 — At Ta Khes (Cambodia) Garmen (Arbitration Council, 17 February 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Kong Phally, president) · Doc ID 138979

## 1. Facts
At Ta Khes (Cambodia) Garmen is a garment-cutting enterprise employing about 200 workers [Para 39][Para 40]. Two federations — the Federation of Unions for the Protection of Workers' Rights and Freedoms of Cambodia and the Kampuchea Workers' Union Federation — filed a complaint on 02 January 2016 covering 22 workers who had given written mandates to be represented [Para 18][Para 42]. Conciliation on the 11 points failed on 22 January 2016 and the file reached the Council on 26 January 2016; at the hearing the workers withdrew points 3, 6 and 9 and merged points 1 and 10, leaving 08 points for decision [Para 19][Para 20][Para 26][Para 28]. The employer did not attend, and its representative told the Secretariat by telephone that "the boss does not want to settle with the union" [Para 24][Para 25].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must provide an on-site infirmary with a permanent nurse and doctor, and sufficient medicines (points 01 and 10, merged)? [Para 52][Para 110]
2. Whether the employer must let workers take special leave under the Labour Law, deducted from annual leave and without loss of wages? [Para 55][Para 146]
3. Whether the employer must provide a room where union leaders can meet workers and give them 02 hours each week for such meetings? [Para 57][Para 158]
4. Whether the employer must stop assigning workers to tasks outside their role and skill without their agreement? [Para 60][Para 174]
5. Whether the employer must pay a meal allowance of 3,500 riel per hour for overtime worked between 16:00 and 18:00? [Para 65][Para 188]
6. Whether the employer must provide a day-care nursery, or USD 35 per month to women with small children? [Para 68][Para 198]
7. Whether the employer must pay 50% of wages and allowances for the 90 days of maternity leave before the worker actually stops work? [Para 70][Para 205]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The company has no sick room, no doctor and no first-aid medicine, so a worker who falls ill at work has nowhere to rest and no treatment; the company also withholds wages and annual-leave credit when special leave is taken, and pays only 2,000 riel for overtime meals instead of the 3,500 riel claimed [Para 52][Para 54][Para 65][Para 153]. It has no nursery for its more than 100 women workers and pays maternity wages only when the worker returns, not before she stops work [Para 202][Para 215][Para 216].
**Employer (respondent):** Did not appear and called no evidence; its only recorded position, given to the Secretariat by telephone, was that the owner would not negotiate with the union, so it forfeited its right to answer the workers' allegations [Para 24][Para 25][Para 79][Para 124].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to set up an infirmary with one nurse on duty and a doctor or medium-level doctor present at least 02 hours per 8-hour shift, to supply sufficient medicines per Prakas No. 330, to allow special leave under the Labour Law, to provide a nursery or pay childcare costs on invoices, and to pay 50% of wages and allowances for the 90 days of maternity leave at least one day before leave begins; points 04, 05 and 07 were refused consideration [Para 222][Para 223][Para 224][Para 228][Para 229][Para 230][Para 225][Para 226][Para 227].

On points 01 and 10 the panel held that Articles 238 and 242 of the Labour Law require any enterprise with at least 50 workers to maintain a permanent infirmary staffed and stocked, and Prakas No. 330 of 06 December 2000 fixes the staffing ratio, so the employer's obligation was established; because the employer did not attend, the workers' uncontested assertions were accepted [Para 110][Para 111][Para 118][Para 121][Para 125][Para 126]. On the doctor, Article 239 and Article 3 of Prakas No. 330 set a minimum presence of 02 hours per 8-hour shift for an enterprise of this size [Para 127][Para 132][Para 137]. On special leave, Articles 171 and Prakas No. 267 of 11 October 2001 permit deduction from annual leave only where annual leave has not yet been used, so deducting wages as well was unlawful [Para 146][Para 149][Para 151][Para 156][Para 157].

Point 04 failed at the threshold: no law, collective agreement, internal rule, contract or past practice obliges the employer to provide a union meeting room, so the claim was an interest dispute; under Prakas No. 099 Article 43(1) such a dispute may only be brought by the most representative union, and the claimants were not that union [Para 159][Para 160][Para 166][Para 168][Para 171][Para 173]. Point 05 failed on evidence: the workers never specified when, how often or to how many workers the loading of cartons was assigned, nor produced contracts, so the panel could not find the management right under Article 2 was exercised unlawfully [Para 178][Para 183][Para 184][Para 186][Para 187]. Point 07 was also an interest claim, since no instrument set a 3,500 riel overtime meal rate and the employer had already paid the 2,000 riel required by Notification No. 041/11 [Para 191][Para 192][Para 193][Para 195][Para 197]. Point 08 succeeded under Article 186, which requires a nursing room and nursery where at least 100 women are employed [Para 198][Para 199][Para 203]. Point 11 succeeded under Articles 182, 183 and 115: maternity wages are wages and must be paid before leave begins, not on return [Para 205][Para 208][Para 213][Para 219][Para 220].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the employer fails to appear after proper invitation, the panel may proceed in its absence and will treat the claimant's uncontested factual assertions as established, but it still applies the statute itself; a claim that merely restates a statutory minimum (infirmary, doctor, medicines, nursery, maternity wages payable before leave) is granted, while a claim for a benefit above the statutory floor is an interest dispute that only the most representative union in the enterprise may bring, and fails on standing where the claimant is not that union. [Para 125][Para 168][Para 194]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel explained at length why Mr Va Sit, general secretary of one federation, could not represent the 22 workers: the 02-month registration period under Article 268 had not run from the 18 December 2015 leadership notification, and no certificate showed his federation was a lawful professional organisation [Para 105][Para 106][Para 108][Para 109].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's absence leaves no respondent evidence at all. The company name is printed at least five ways (At Ta Khes, At Ta Khi S, At Ta Khee S, At Ta Di S, At Ta Khi S Garmen). Worker numbers conflict: about 200 at [Para 40] and [Para 122], but "១០ ២០០" at [Para 133]. The leadership election minutes are dated 02 December 2015 at [Para 49] and 12 December 2015 at [Para 32]. Issue numbers are corrupted in places: "១៤-" at [Para 5], "ចំណុច អាច ១០៨" at [Para 198] and "ទី 99" at [Para 205]. The panel composition is printed "Kong Phalbeh" on the face and "Kong Phally" at signature [Para 232].
