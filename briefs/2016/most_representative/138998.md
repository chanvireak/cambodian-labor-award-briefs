# Award No. 050/16 — UD-S Garment Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 16 March 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mr Ing Sothy (employer-selected), Mr Tun Si Phan (worker-selected), chair Mr Pen Bun Chhea · Doc ID 138998

## 1. Facts
UD-S Garment Co., Ltd., a garment-cutting and sewing enterprise at Bak Khaeng, Prek Liep, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, employed approximately 698 workers and recognised the Free Workers' Union of UD-S Garment, registered with the Ministry of Labour on 31 October 2014 under certificate No. 3211. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 35] The union filed nine collective demands in January 2016 covering meal allowances, piece rates, overtime, meeting space and dismissals; conciliation failed on all nine and the file reached the Secretariat on 23 February 2016. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 16] At the hearing of 29 February 2016 the parties settled four issues (2, 6, 7 and 8), the workers withdrew issue 1, and the panel decided the remaining issues 3, 4, 5 and 9 by a non-binding award issued on 16 March 2016. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 21][Para 22][Para 24]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a midday meal allowance of 2,000 riel per day to each worker? [Para 48][Para 83]
2. Whether the employer must raise piece rates (តម្លៃ បុង) by 28% on the old rates? [Para 55][Para 101]
3. Whether the employer must give union representatives 1 or 2 hours of meeting space every Saturday under Prakas No. 313? [Para 65][Para 142]
4. Whether the employer must reinstate the worker Ms H (name redacted), dismissed from the QC and labelling team (employee No. 02796)? [Para 71][Para 160]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Other companies pay a midday meal allowance, so UD-S must too, and the employer promised "some" allowance at a meeting of 1 October 2015; piece rates are so low that only 2 of 30 workers in a team reach the minimum wage, and the employer never disclosed the rates in writing; Prakas No. 313 gives union leaders the same rights as shop stewards, including meeting time; and Ms H's absence on 10–12 January 2016 warranted only a warning, not dismissal. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 56][Para 57][Para 66][Para 72]
**Employer (respondent):** It cannot afford the meal allowance having just raised wages to US$140 per worker per month, and it never promised the allowance, only to raise the issue with management; piece-rate increases must pass production trials, which lifted some sections and not others, and the rate tables are confidential internal documents; the Labour Law gives the two weekly hours to shop stewards only; and Ms H was dismissed during a valid probation contract under the evaluation policy, having taken three unauthorised absences and thumbprinted her acknowledgement. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 58][Para 61][Para 70][Para 76][Para 78][Para 80]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed in part and ordered in part — the meal allowance, the 28% piece-rate increase, the Saturday meeting space and the reinstatement were all refused, but the employer was ordered to set piece rates so that a worker of average skill working normally earns at least the guaranteed minimum wage. [Para 171][Para 172][Para 173][Para 174][Para 175]

On the meal allowance the panel examined res judicata (អាជ្ញា អស់ ជំនុំ) against Award No. 097/15-UD-S Garment, issue 2, which had refused the same 2,000 riel claim. Conditions 1 and 2 were satisfied, but condition 3 was not, because the earlier panel had refused to consider the claim on the ground that it was an interest dispute rather than deciding it on the merits. [Para 84][Para 89][Para 91][Para 93][Para 95][Para 96] Res judicata therefore did not bar the claim; the panel nevertheless refused it because the union still lacked most-representative status, as it had in Award No. 097/15, and so had no standing to bring an interest dispute. [Para 97][Para 98][Para 99][Para 100]

On the piece rates, the panel again found res judicata inapplicable, this time because the 2016 claim concerns the 2016 minimum wage, which differs from 2015, so condition 2 failed and no further condition needed examination. [Para 105][Para 107][Para 109][Para 110][Para 111] On the merits it applied Article 1 of Prakas No. 409/15 of 8 October 2015 fixing the 2016 minimum wage at US$140 per month, Article 2 of the same Prakas on piece-rate workers, Article 108 of the Labour Law and Article 137 on the eight-hour day, and followed prior awards holding that the employer may set or change piece rates but must calibrate them so an average-skill worker reaches the minimum wage. [Para 112][Para 113][Para 115][Para 117][Para 118][Para 119][Para 120] The burden of proving the rates were not too low lay on the party making the assertion, yet the employer withheld the rate tables as confidential, so it failed to discharge that burden and the panel accepted the workers' claim that the rates were set too low. [Para 127][Para 131][Para 133][Para 134] The flat 28% rise was refused because difficulty varies from one operation to another and the percentage must vary accordingly, but the employer must recalculate rates to meet Article 108. [Para 137][Para 138][Para 139][Para 140][Para 141]

On meeting space, Article 6 of Prakas No. 286 of 5 November 2000 grants two hours a week to shop stewards (ប្រតិភូ បុគ្គលិក) only and does not extend that right to unions, and no statute, collective agreement, internal rule, contract or past practice required Saturday meeting time, so the demand was an interest dispute the non-representative union could not bring. [Para 143][Para 145][Para 146][Para 147][Para 157][Para 158][Para 159]

On the dismissal, the panel applied Articles 68 and 82 of the Labour Law, found Ms H held a two-month probation contract from 15 December 2015 and was dismissed on 13 January 2016 while it was still valid, and held that an employer need give no reason for a dismissal during probation, so it declined to analyse the grounds and refused reinstatement. [Para 160][Para 161][Para 162][Para 163][Para 167][Para 168][Para 169][Para 170]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Res judicata bars a repeated claim only where the earlier panel decided it on the merits; a refusal to consider because the demand is an interest dispute leaves the claim open, but a union without most-representative status still cannot pursue an interest dispute. Where an employer fixes piece rates, it bears the burden of showing the rates let an average-skill worker earn the guaranteed minimum wage, and withholding the rate tables as confidential discharges nothing, so the panel will order recalibration under Article 108 rather than a flat percentage rise. [Para 96][Para 99][Para 133][Para 139]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that percentages of increase must differ by type of operation because each operation's difficulty varies, and that Article 6 of Prakas No. 286 confers meeting time on shop stewards alone without any equivalent union entitlement. [Para 137][Para 145]
