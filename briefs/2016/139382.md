# Award No. 226/16 — Vico Handbag (Cambodia) Industrial Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 21 November 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Uk Ri appointed by the employer, An Non appointed by the workers, Run Saray chair) · Doc ID 139382

## 1. Facts
Vico Handbag (Cambodia) Industrial Co., Ltd. sews handbags on National Road 4 at Trapeang Sang Chiek, Samrong Tong district, Kampong Speu, and employed about 1,017 workers, 114 of them men. [Para 1][Para 42][Para 43] From 01 March 2016 the employer suspended the contracts for two months for lack of work and told the workers to return on 01 May 2016; when they came back on 03 May the gates were shut, the employer had gone, and no wages or termination payments were made. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 116] The workers obtained a protective injunction over the company's property from the Kampong Speu Provincial Court on 25 October 2016, with a release figure of $795,015.16. [Para 29][Para 55][Para 56] The employer did not appear and filed no documents. [Para 15][Para 22][Para 119]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay termination compensation and the other benefits due under the Labour Law to the workers? [Para 115]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer closed the factory, abandoned it and paid no termination compensation, so the statutory entitlements are owed; they also asked the court to preserve and sell the company's assets to fund payment. [Para 51][Para 54]
**Employer (respondent):** no position was taken; the employer did not attend the hearing and filed no documents, so its right of reply was treated as waived. [Para 22][Para 119]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered termination payments for the workers on fixed-duration contracts (final wages, unused annual leave, moral damages, and the 5% end-of-contract indemnity) and for those on undetermined-duration contracts (severance, moral damages, notice compensation, unused annual leave and final wages); it refused notice compensation for the fixed-duration group. [Para 204][Para 208][Para 209]

The panel first established it could proceed in the employer's absence under Article 21 of Prakas 099, the invitation of 27 October 2016 having been posted at the gate when no representative could be found. [Para 60][Para 65][Para 66] On representation the panel found none of the bodies before it could act: the Samleng Kamkar union's recognised leadership had expired and its new leadership was unrecognised, and no Independent Labour union leader attended, only one federation official with no authority. [Para 96][Para 98][Para 100][Para 101] The authority letters held by the two workers present were limited to proceedings in the provincial court, so they could not be used at the Arbitration Council; but both were elected full staff delegates on 15 November 2014 and, under Article 32 of the Law on Trade Unions, staff delegates represent the workers of the enterprise — they were therefore the claimants. [Para 107][Para 109][Para 110][Para 114]

On the substance the panel found the contracts could no longer be performed: a two-month suspension with no work, followed by closure, three months without any contact, and a court injunction over the assets, meant the employment relationship was at an end. [Para 121][Para 123][Para 124] It fixed 03 May 2016 as the date of termination. [Para 125]

It then split the workforce by contract type. For the 229 workers on fixed-duration contracts it applied Article 116 (final wages), Articles 166 and 167 (payment for unused annual leave), Article 73(3) (moral damages at least equal to wages to the end of the term) and Article 73(6) (an end-of-contract indemnity of at least 5% of wages paid over the term). [Para 127][Para 132][Para 138][Para 145] It refused notice compensation for this group: Article 73(5) governs notice of expiry or non-renewal only, and the sanction for giving none is that the contract continues, not a payment. [Para 152][Para 153][Para 154][Para 159] For the 708 workers on undetermined-duration contracts it awarded severance under Article 89, moral damages under Article 91, notice compensation under Articles 75 and 77, unused annual leave and final wages. [Para 166][Para 181][Para 191][Para 197][Para 199]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer suspends contracts and then abandons a closed factory without notice, the contracts are treated as terminated on the date the workers were last able to present themselves, and the statutory termination payments fall due. The measure of those payments depends on contract type: fixed-duration workers receive final wages, unused annual leave, moral damages to the end of the term and the 5% end-of-contract indemnity but not notice compensation, because Article 73(5) attaches no payment to a failure to give notice of expiry; undetermined-duration workers receive severance, moral damages and notice compensation. [Para 125][Para 152][Para 154] A written mandate to represent workers before a court does not authorise representation before the Arbitration Council, but an elected staff delegate may represent the workforce under Article 32. [Para 109][Para 114]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's survey of awards on proceeding in default and its remarks on the absent most-representative union are general observations not required by the orders. [Para 62][Para 75] The award is expressly non-binding. [Para 19]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The termination date is reasoned inconsistently: the panel records no contact "from 03 August 2016" and then presumes 03 May 2016 as the date of termination, three months earlier [Para 125]. The operative orders carry no date.
- The suspension is described as running 01 March 2016 to 01 May 2016 at [Para 52] but as "01 March 2012" at [Para 115].
- Headcounts do not reconcile: 1,017 workers at [Para 43], but 708 on undetermined-duration plus 229 on fixed-duration contracts, i.e. 937, at [Para 58].
- The document carries a corrigendum of the same date which deletes a passage on page 14 and substitutes the annual-leave calculation of Article 168 [Para 215][Para 217][Para 223]; the superseded text still appears in the body at [Para 192].
- The company appears as Vico, Victco and Vikco Handbag — including "Victco Handbags (Cambodia) Industry Co., Ltd" in the seniority list [Para 32][Para 42][Para 211]; the second union's name is truncated at [Para 84].
- Moral damages for fixed-duration workers are attributed to Article 73(3) [Para 138] while the same remedy for the other group is attributed to Article 91 [Para 176]; the operative summary of the fixed-duration award refers to "Article 90" at [Para 201], which appears to be a misprint.
