# Award No. 181/16 — Ou Thong Development Cambodia Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 12 August 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Inq Sothy (employer-appointed), Tuon Si Phan (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (chair) · Doc ID 139083

## 1. Facts
Ou Thong Development Cambodia Co Ltd makes foam slippers at Prey Pieng, Kantork, Phnom Penh, with about 69 workers [Para 66][Para 321]. A Cambodia Asia union local was registered with the Ministry on 23 July 2015, with Mr E as president and two other officers [Para 68][Para 69]. On 31 May 2016 the company refused the three union officers entry and stopped paying Mr E, its union president, without stating a reason [Para 78][Para 79][Para 84]. It dismissed Mr N, a union activist in packing, on 28 June 2016 [Para 97][Para 107]. The union raised eleven demands; four were withdrawn and the panel decided seven [Para 29][Para 30]. The parties chose a non-binding award [Para 31].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back Mr E, the union president dismissed on 31 May 2016, pay his May 2016 wages and preserve his wages and benefits during the suspension? [Para 73]
2. Whether the employer must pay monthly wages before the 10th of each month, or a day earlier where the 10th falls on a Sunday or public holiday, and complete payment within working hours? [Para 93][Para 196]
3. Whether the employer must take back Mr N, the union activist in packing dismissed on 28 June 2016, with back wages? [Para 97]
4. Whether the employer must adopt internal rules under Articles 22 to 31 of the Labour Law? [Para 111]
5. Whether the employer must hold an election of worker delegates? [Para 115][Para 324]
6. Whether the employer must deduct 2,000 riel monthly from the 51 workers who volunteered to pay union dues? [Para 118][Para 336]
7. Whether the employer must provide cleaning staff and adequate water and soap in the toilets under Prakas No. 052? [Para 127][Para 365]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the president was a protected union leader dismissed without the labour inspector's prior authorisation and without any stated reason; the activist's playful behaviour caused no damage and was not serious misconduct; wages were paid late and after working hours; the company had no internal rules, no worker delegates, no dues deduction and no hygienic toilets [Para 83][Para 108][Para 114][Para 117][Para 121][Para 132].

**Employer (respondent):** it could not take Mr E back because he had committed serious misconduct — repeated threats, inciting strikes, arguing with the security guard, flooding the furnace on 17 May 2016 and blocking the gate during the 8 June strike [Para 85][Para 86][Para 87][Para 91]. It did not attend the hearing; its manager's assistant asked for a postponement to 15 August 2016 because the director was meeting buyers [Para 25][Para 27].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted in substance on all seven points — Mr E was reinstated with his May 2016 wages and back wages, Mr N was refused reinstatement but awarded severance, and the employer was ordered to pay wages on time and in working hours, adopt internal rules within the law, hold worker-delegate elections within 45 days, deduct 2,000 riel monthly in union dues, and stop assigning production workers to clean toilets while supplying water and soap [Para 400][Para 401][Para 402][Para 403][Para 404][Para 408][Para 409][Para 410][Para 411][Para 412][Para 413].

The panel first held the hearing could proceed without the employer, since the employer had been properly summoned, its postponement request would have pushed the award past the 15-day limit in Article 313, the workers refused any extension, and Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 allows the panel to continue in a party's absence [Para 134][Para 149][Para 150][Para 154][Para 155][Para 165][Para 166]. The request was not a valid excuse because the company could have sent a representative under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 [Para 162][Para 163].

On Mr E, Article 43 of the Trade Union Law forbids dismissing a worker delegate or protected worker without the labour inspector's consent, and Article 45 permits only a suspension pending that decision, with the suspension void if consent is refused [Para 167][Para 175][Para 179][Para 180]. Even accepting the six allegations in the employer's letter of opposition, the employer neither sought nor obtained authorisation [Para 187][Para 188][Para 189]. The dismissal was therefore procedurally unlawful, and under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 the panel ordered reinstatement with the unpaid May wage and back wages to the date of return [Para 190][Para 191][Para 195].

On Mr N, Article 27 requires the sanction to match the fault and Article 74 requires a valid reason [Para 230][Para 237]. The panel found the fire-hose and container-kicking incidents did not amount to serious misconduct under Article 83, because he had received only one written and one oral warning and the company had no internal rules grading repeat offences [Para 258][Para 259][Para 260]. It nevertheless held the frequent horseplay showed a character defect going to his work, which was a valid reason under Article 74, so reinstatement was refused but notice pay under Articles 75 and 77, Article 89 dismissal indemnity, unused annual leave under Article 166 and final wages under Article 116 were ordered [Para 261][Para 262][Para 268][Para 278][Para 294][Para 304].

On wages, Article 115(3) forbids paying on a rest day, so payment falling on a Sunday or public holiday must be made a day earlier, and payment must be completed inside working hours [Para 206][Para 207][Para 214][Para 221][Para 226].

On the remaining points, Article 22 requires internal rules for establishments with at least eight workers, subject to consultation and labour-inspector endorsement under Article 24 [Para 310][Para 313][Para 315]. Article 32 of the Trade Union Law and Article 8 of Prakas No. 286 require worker delegates, and Article 35 makes the employer responsible for holding the election within 45 days of a request [Para 324][Para 325][Para 327][Para 335]. Article 129 of the Labour Law and Article 26 of the Trade Union Law require deduction of union dues on written request; as no new implementing prakas exists, Article 5 of Prakas No. 305 still governs [Para 340][Para 343][Para 348][Para 349]. Finally, Prakas No. 052 requires clean toilets with adequate water and soap, and Article 229 of the Labour Law requires hygienic working conditions [Para 365][Para 369][Para 371]; assigning packing workers to clean toilets was neither lawful nor reasonable [Para 388].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Dismissing a registered union's president without the labour inspector's prior consent is unlawful even where the alleged misconduct is grave, because Article 45 allows the employer only to suspend the contract while consent is sought, and refusal voids the suspension [Para 175][Para 180][Para 189]. Separately, persistent horseplay that causes no damage is not serious misconduct where the employer has no internal rules grading repeat offences, but it can still be a valid reason for dismissal attracting severance rather than reinstatement [Para 259][Para 261].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that an employer may send a representative rather than attend in person, and that a request for postponement cannot be used to escape the statutory 15-day limit, were background to the procedural ruling rather than part of the substantive order [Para 150][Para 162].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company's name is printed four ways — "Ou Phong Development" in the header, "Ou Thong Aphivat" in the body, "Ou Thong Development" in the report and findings [Para 1][Para 51][Para 65][Para 321]. The report number is 3916 at para 17 but 2916 at para 22. Dates carry duplicated or impossible years: "25 July 2019 2016" at para 23, "22 31 May 2016" at para 184. Paragraph 188 cites "Article 67 of the Trade Union Law" then "Article 57" for the same rule, and para 228's reproduction of Article 83 is largely unreadable. The reasoning is internally inconsistent on Mr N: para 260 holds the dismissal could not rest on fault, while para 261 finds a valid reason under Article 74; both are reported and the order follows para 261 [Para 260][Para 261]. Headcount is 69 at paras 66 and 321 but 62 workers with 51 union members at para 118. No total sum in riel or USD is stated; only monthly figures (USD 160 base, USD 320 average for Mr E, USD 140 and USD 200 for Mr N) and the 2,000 riel dues appear.
