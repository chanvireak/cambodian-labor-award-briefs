# Award No. 051/16 — New Ark Khit Garment Factory Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 21 March 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mr Seng Vuch Hun (employer-selected), Mr An Non (worker-selected), chair Mr Kong Phalha · Doc ID 139000

## 1. Facts
New Ark Khit Garment Factory Co., Ltd., a garment-cutting and sewing factory at Trapeang Village, Peuk, Ang Snuol, Kandal, registered under Inv. 1390 KH/2008, employed approximately 3,880 workers and recognised the Free Women Workers' Union of the company, registered on 27 April 2015 under No. 3267. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47] Its two activists, Ms O (name redacted), a hygiene worker since 12 January 2009, and Mr N (name redacted), a cutter since 2 January 2009, had each been kept on successive six-month fixed-duration contracts until the employer ended their employment on 1 December 2015 after notice given on 2 November 2015. [Para 51][Para 53][Para 55][Para 67][Para 104] Of four demands, two were settled at the hearing of 2 March 2016 and issue 2 was merged into issue 4, leaving reinstatement and back pay to be decided by a non-binding award issued 21 March 2016 after the parties agreed to extend the date from 17 March. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 22][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the two workers' successive fixed-duration contracts had become contracts of unspecified duration? [Para 88][Para 90]
2. Whether the employer's termination of those contracts was lawful under the Labour Law, and if not, whether reinstatement and back pay are owed? [Para 89][Para 119]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Both had long service, no record of fault or warning, and difficulty finding new work; their repeated six-month contracts made them workers of unspecified duration, so dismissing them without proper notice and without valid reason was unlawful. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 59][Para 60][Para 61] They also answered the employer's cost-cutting claim by pointing out that it continued recruiting new workers daily and that buyers kept placing orders. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 126]
**Employer (respondent):** There was no dismissal, only the non-renewal of fixed-duration contracts that had reached their term, because the company had to cut costs and had no orders amid a serious economic and financial crisis, and both Khmer and Chinese staff were reduced; it agreed to pay statutory indemnities but refused reinstatement. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 64][Para 65][Para 66][Para 133]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement and back pay were refused, but the employer must pay both workers the indemnity in lieu of notice under Articles 75 and 77, the dismissal indemnity under Article 89, unused annual leave under Articles 166 and 167 and moral damages under Article 91, and must pay Mr N his unpaid final month's wage. [Para 160][Para 161][Para 162][Para 163][Para 164][Para 166][Para 167][Para 171]

On standing, the panel applied Article 268 of the Labour Law and its earlier awards, holding that a union acquires the recognised rights only once its statutes and officers are registered, found this union registered and its two officers present, and confined the award to the workers who were original claimants. [Para 73][Para 75][Para 84][Para 85]

On the contract type, Article 67(2) caps a fixed-duration contract at two years, and Article 73(5) provides that without notice of expiry the contract continues for an equal term or becomes one of unspecified duration once total duration passes the Article 67 limit; Article 67 also expresses the Law's deliberate preference for unspecified-duration contracts because they give workers security and reward employer commitment. [Para 90][Para 92][Para 93][Para 95][Para 97][Para 98] Both workers had served more than six years on renewed six-month contracts, so the panel held their contracts had become of unspecified duration. [Para 101][Para 104][Para 105][Para 106]

On the 5% end-of-contract indemnity, Article 73(6) requires it whenever a fixed-duration contract ends, but the panel followed Award No. 91/10-Pyu Vy and held that paying it and renewing the contract does not prevent conversion, because the Law aims at total duration exceeding two years, and the successive renewals here left no fair gap between contracts. [Para 107][Para 109][Para 111][Para 112][Para 114][Para 116][Para 117]

The termination was therefore a dismissal under a contract of unspecified duration, governed by Article 74, which permits dismissal at the employer's will only by written prior notice and for a valid reason related to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the necessity of running the enterprise. [Para 118][Para 120][Para 122][Para 123] The employer showed no such reason and produced no evidence for its claim of no orders, and its assertion was contradicted, so the dismissal was unlawful. [Para 124][Para 127][Para 129][Para 131][Para 132] Because relations were no longer cooperative and the employer refused reinstatement, the panel preferred full statutory compensation to forcing the workers back. [Para 133][Para 134][Para 135] It found notice of 2 November 2015 for a 1 December 2015 dismissal — 29 days — short of the Article 75 minimum for their seniority, and awarded the indemnity in lieu, plus the Article 89 dismissal indemnity, outstanding annual leave and Article 91 moral damages. [Para 137][Para 138][Para 139][Para 140][Para 145][Para 149][Para 155]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive fixed-duration contracts whose total duration passes the two-year cap in Article 67 convert into a contract of unspecified duration, and neither the payment of the 5% end-of-contract indemnity nor a short renewal gap prevents that conversion; termination of such a converted contract is a dismissal that must satisfy Article 74 with written prior notice of the length Article 75 requires for the worker's seniority and a valid reason proved by evidence, failing which the employer owes notice, dismissal and leave indemnities and moral damages even where the panel declines to order reinstatement. [Para 95][Para 111][Para 117][Para 123][Para 131][Para 145]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that Cambodia's Labour Law shows a deliberate preference for unspecified-duration contracts because job security matters to workers and long service encourages commitment, which also benefits the employer. [Para 97][Para 98]
