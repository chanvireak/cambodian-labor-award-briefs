# Award No. 197/16 — Shin Jitian (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 15 September 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Chhiv Phirom (employer-appointed), An Nan (worker-appointed), Aing Eng Thong (chair) · Doc ID 139099

## 1. Facts
Shin Jitian (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a garment factory employing about 445 workers, with no union yet recognised as most representative [Para 63][Para 64]. On 30 July 2016 the employer terminated three sewing workers — the president, the vice-president and the secretary of the Khmer Workers' Conscience Union (សហជីព ស្មារតី កម្មករ ខ្មែរ), elected on 23 May 2016 — before their fixed-term contracts expired, paying severance only up to each contract's end date [Para 70][Para 75][Para 92]. The union had not yet applied to register with the Ministry [Para 94]. After a strike from 1 August 2016 and Council order 017/16, eight of the sixteen points in dispute reached the panel; point 7 was settled at the hearing [Para 21][Para 26][Para 31].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the three dismissed union leaders and pay their wages and other benefits from the date of dismissal to the date of reinstatement? [Para 70]
2. Whether the employer must deduct the regular attendance bonus pro rata for days of lawful special leave? [Para 98]
3. Whether the employer must pay a skill allowance of USD 20 for three-machine skills and USD 15 for two-machine skills per month? [Para 107]
4. Whether the employer must pay 100 per cent wages when it has no work, and back-pay the 50 per cent shortfall? [Para 110]
5. Whether the employer must provide hot-water and cold-water containers for the workers? [Para 114]
6. Whether the employer must place a stretcher (ប្រង់កា) in each sewing team? [Para 120]
7. Whether the employer must deduct Riels 2,000 from members' wages as union dues? — settled at the hearing and not considered [Para 127][Para 128]
8. Whether the employer must provide a lockable cabinet for the union's documents? [Para 130]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissal was union discrimination — it rested on no fault, followed no warning and no notice, and struck only the three leaders while their own teams were working overtime until 18:00 or 20:00 [Para 76][Para 229][Para 237].
**Employer (respondent):** it had few orders and had to reduce staff, so it cannot take the three back; it says it has no capacity to pay the skill allowance or install the facilities claimed [Para 77][Para 109][Para 133].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to take back Mr H, Mr S and Mr Y and to pay their wages and other benefits from the date of dismissal to the date of reinstatement; points 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 8 were dismissed [Para 248][Para 338][Para 343].

On the Article 67 protection the panel applied the three conditions drawn from its earlier awards: the worker must be of the protected category, the dismissal must fall within 45 days of the election, and the union must have notified the employer and copied the Ministry [Para 185][Para 194]. Mr H and Mr Y satisfied the first, having been elected president and secretary on 23 May 2016 [Para 204][Para 205]; Mr S did not, because his name is absent from the election notice and nothing showed he had replaced the elected vice-president [Para 207][Para 209]. The second condition failed for all three: the dismissal came two months and seven days after the election and the union had still not applied to register, giving no reason, which the panel held was not a reasonable period to keep the protection alive [Para 212][Para 213][Para 216].

The panel then turned to discrimination independently of Article 67. Article 62 of the Law on Trade Unions and Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law forbid decisions taken on account of union office or activity, and Article 62 expressly covers the non-renewal of successive fixed-term contracts [Para 217][Para 225]. The claimant carries the burden of proof [Para 231], but here the employer's account was contradicted by facts both sides admitted: the teams worked overtime until 18:00 or 20:00 before the dismissal [Para 237][Para 238]; the employer filed no evidence of an orders shortage or of the teams it says it suspended [Para 240]; only the three leaders were dismissed, none had a fault or a warning, and they had longer seniority than their team-mates [Para 242][Para 243]. The panel found union discrimination and ordered reinstatement with back wages under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 [Para 244][Para 245].

Points 2 and 4 were barred as res judicata: the same claims against the same employer had been decided in Award 147/16 of 21 July 2016 between the same parties, and all three conditions of that doctrine were met [Para 250][Para 262][Para 293]. Points 3, 6 and 8 the panel classified as interest disputes; because an award on an interest dispute stands in place of a collective agreement and binds the whole workforce for one year [Para 280][Para 282], only a union able to conclude such an agreement may bring one [Para 283]. This union was neither registered under Article 11 of the Law on Trade Unions nor most representative, so those claims were not examined [Para 156][Para 157][Para 286]. On point 5 the panel held that Prakas No. 054 requires sufficient hygienic drinking water, not hot-water and cold-water containers [Para 296][Para 299][Para 306].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that dismisses precisely the union's office-holders, without fault and at a time when the same teams are working overtime, is taken to have discriminated on the ground of union office under Article 62 of the Law on Trade Unions (2016) and Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law, even where the 45-day protection in Article 67 has lapsed; reinstatement and wages from dismissal to reinstatement follow. The Article 67 protection lapses where the union delays its registration application beyond a reasonable period — two months and seven days without explanation is not reasonable — and a worker not named in the election notice the employer received cannot invoke it at all. An interest dispute may be arbitrated only at the instance of a union capable of concluding a collective agreement, because the resulting award binds the entire workforce. [Para 183][Para 213][Para 216][Para 244][Para 283]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the absence of a most-representative union can obstruct workers' right to complain under Article 39 of the Constitution, and that transferring representation rights to a federation does not itself confer that status, are general observations not necessary to the order [Para 143][Para 144].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award is dated 15 September 2016 in the header and where the deadline was extended [Para 1][Para 35], but the reasons record all workers ending the strike and returning on "17 September 2016", after the 24 August hearing, while order 017/16 set the return for the morning of 15 August [Para 26][Para 27].
- A date is printed "31 February 2016" [Para 86]; the same sequence reappears as "21 February July 2016" [Para 113].
- The strike order is numbered 017/16 in the procedural recital and 017/17 in the evidence list [Para 26][Para 57].
- Worker counts conflict: 231 transferred representation rights [Para 65] but 126 (printed "125>") [Para 163], and the total is stated as 307 and then 308 [Para 165][Para 166].
- Point 3 is pleaded as "3 dollars for 3 skills and 15 dollars for 02 machines" [Para 6] but reasoned and decided at USD 20 for three machines and USD 15 for two [Para 107][Para 265].
- Point 6 is internally contradictory: the reasoning says the employer "should" place stretchers where workers can reach them [Para 326][Para 329], yet the operative paragraph dismisses the claim [Para 343].
- Names: the secretary is printed variously "កុយ" and "កយ" (Koy/Kay) [Para 68][Para 216], the elected vice-president appears as "នៅ ផាន ណា" [Para 92][Para 204] although Mr S is claimed as vice-president, and the employer-side arbitrator's signature line is blank [Para 346].
