# Award No. 141/16 — Yi Da Manufacture Garment Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 1 August 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Hem Hour Narith for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Sok Mothoeun chair) · Doc ID 139049

## 1. Facts
Ms S was a sewing worker at Yi Da Manufacture Garment Co., Ltd. from 19 February 2016 on a two-month probation contract. [Para 60] On 8 May 2016 the Cambodia Union held an election at the factory, 19 workers voting, and Ms S was elected union president with Ms N as secretary. [Para 81][Para 94] The employer's administration dismissed her on 10 May 2016, two days later, after the end of working hours, and paid no severance. [Para 61][Para 62] The union took the result to the factory gate on 9 May, where security refused it, and posted it the same day in an envelope marked "REFUSED"; the union had not yet applied for registration. [Para 76][Para 80][Para 95]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the factory must take Ms S back into work as union president, because her dismissal was union discrimination? [Para 1][Para 63]
2. Whether the factory must pay her wages from the date of dismissal until the date she returns to work? [Para 2][Para 60]
3. Whether the factory must refrain from intimidating other union leaders into resigning from the union? [Para 3]

Issue 3 was settled at conciliation and issues 1 and 2 were merged, so the panel decided one combined issue. [Para 15][Para 17]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissal was union discrimination; Ms S committed no fault and always followed her supervisor's instructions, and after the probation ended the employer never gave her a written contract, so her contract became indefinite. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65]**Employer (respondent):** there was no discrimination or intimidation; Ms S performed poorly and should have been released at the end of probation but was continued through a technical error of the company; a written three-month fixed-duration contract existed, though administration probably never gave her a copy; and the dispute is individual, because she lacked the seniority to stand. [Para 66][Para 68][Para 69][Para 72]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to take Ms S back into work and to pay her wages from the date of dismissal until the date she returns; no sum in riel or dollars is stated in the order. [Para 189][Para 190]

*Who was the claimant.* Article 268 gives a professional organisation the rights the Law recognises only once its statutes and list of responsible officers are registered with the Ministry, as the panel held in Awards Nos 023/14, 032/14, 079/15 and 176/15; Prakas No. 099/04 Article 19 allows representation only by a lawyer or a person holding written authority. [Para 88][Para 89][Para 97][Para 99] The union was elected but not registered, so Ms S and Ms N could not represent members; no representative had been authorised, and the two workers present at the hearing were the claimants in person. [Para 96][Para 101][Para 102]

*Jurisdiction.* Prakas No. 099 Article 32 confines the panel to collective labour disputes, and Article 302 of the Labour Law requires three conditions. [Para 104][Para 119] All three were met: the claim was between workers and an employer; it concerned working conditions, namely the dismissal and the demand for reinstatement; and it could disturb the enterprise or social peace — following Award No. 029/15, obstruction of production short of a full stoppage suffices, and 19 workers had voted for the union and could protest. [Para 121][Para 122][Para 125][Para 130] The dispute was therefore collective and within jurisdiction. [Para 132]

*Type of contract.* Article 67(7) requires a genuine fixed-duration contract to be in writing, failing which it becomes a contract of indefinite duration; the panel followed Award No. 183/14. [Para 133][Para 135] The party asserting a fact bears the burden of proving it, and the employer asserted the three-month written contract. [Para 141][Para 145] No such document was produced, so Ms S held an indefinite-duration contract. [Para 147][Para 148]

*Protected status.* Article 293 of the Labour Law allows a staff delegate or candidate to be dismissed only with the agreement of the labour inspector, and the third indent of Article 3 of Prakas No. 305 (22 November 2001) extends that protection to candidates for union leadership for 45 days before and 45 days after the poll; Award No. 40/10 read the protection as running at least 45 days after the election or until registration is applied for. [Para 149][Para 150][Para 153] The panel applied three cumulative conditions. [Para 155] First, protected category: Ms S was elected president; the employer's objection that she lacked the seniority to stand had to be raised with the Ministry, which is exclusively competent to rule on a candidate's eligibility, and the employer proved neither an objection to the Ministry nor any Ministry refusal. [Para 159][Para 161][Para 165][Para 166] Second, timing: the dismissal on 10 May 2016 fell two days after the poll. [Para 168] Third, notification: service through Cambodia Post, on a stamped envelope bearing the mark (v), is a valid means, and the employer's denial of receipt was rejected. [Para 173][Para 176][Para 178] All three were satisfied. [Para 180]

*Consequence.* Dismissal of a protected worker requires the labour inspector's prior authorisation, and none was sought or shown. [Para 184][Para 185] Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 lets the panel order civil compensation, including reinstatement and immediate payment. [Para 186][Para 187]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker elected to union office is protected against dismissal from the day of the poll for 45 days under the third indent of Article 3 of Prakas No. 305, and a dismissal inside that window is unlawful unless the employer first obtained the labour inspector's agreement under Article 293; the panel does not review the candidate's eligibility, which belongs to the Ministry. [Para 150][Para 161][Para 184] A fixed-duration contract the employer cannot produce in writing is converted by Article 67(7) into an indefinite-duration contract, so the worker cannot afterwards be treated as a probationer who may simply be released. [Para 135][Para 148] Reinstatement with wages from the date of dismissal to the date of return is the remedy under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 186][Para 190]

**Obiter dicta:** the observations that a union acquires its statutory rights only on registration, and that a representative needs written authority under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099, were not necessary to the order, since the two workers sued in person. [Para 96][Para 99]
