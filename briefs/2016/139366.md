# Award No. 212/16 — Gab Bo Tek Trading Khmer Pheny Limited (Arbitration Council, 16 September 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuth Hun, An Nan, Run Saray presiding) · Doc ID 139366

## 1. Facts
Gab Bo Tek Trading Khmer Pheny Limited ("Gab Bo Tek") is a garment-cutting factory at Ta Du village, Rokar Krao, Daun Keo, Takeo, employing about 992 workers. [Para 53] The Khmer Workers' Spirit Trade Union Federation elected a factory union on 15 August 2016, but no application for registration had yet been filed with the Ministry of Labour, so no most representative union existed at the plant. [Para 54][Para 58] Nine demands went to conciliation on 20 August 2016; seven were settled and two were referred to the panel. [Para 14][Para 19] 209 workers signed letters of mandate and transfer of rights to the Federation on 18 July 2016, and five more appeared at the hearing without such letters, making 214 complainants. [Para 55][Para 130][Para 131]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must dismiss Mr P, the section chief, from his job? [Para 132]
2. Whether the employer must maintain the base wage of US$170 per month for Ms Y after she was demoted from team leader to ordinary sewer? [Para 162]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr P verbally abuses workers in the section, bullies them, plays favourites, watches and scolds women going to the toilet, threatens strike participants, and fails workers after three written warnings; the demotion of Ms Y was accepted but the cut in her wage from US$170 to US$140 was not, so the wage term of her contract stands. [Para 60][Para 62][Para 67][Para 68][Para 85][Para 92]
**Employer (respondent):** dismissal of a section chief requires a specific incident, an administrative investigation, and evidence, and no worker ever filed a complaint through the internal mechanism the company published on 27 July 2016; Ms Y repeatedly took unauthorised leave and led her team badly, so pay follows the post held. [Para 70][Para 72][Para 75][Para 86][Para 94]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand that Mr P be dismissed was rejected, and the employer was ordered to maintain Ms Y's base wage of US$170 per month. [Para 198][Para 199]

As a preliminary matter, the panel applied Article 54 of the Trade Union Law 2016, which reserves exclusive representational rights to the most representative union, and Article 11, under which a union acquires the rights recognised by that law only on registration. [Para 102][Para 116][Para 118] Because the factory union was unregistered, its leadership had no right to represent members in this case. [Para 122] The panel nevertheless proceeded, because Article 19 of Prakas 99/04 lets a disputing party appear in person or be represented by any person holding that party's written authorisation; the 209 mandate letters plus the five workers present in person therefore gave it jurisdiction over 214 individual complainants. [Para 123][Para 125][Para 130]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 311 of the Civil Code: a contract binds only its parties, and a third party has no right to dissolve it. [Para 132][Para 134][Para 135][Para 137] Whether a supervisor stays in post is internal management, which Article 24 leaves to the employer provided it is exercised lawfully and reasonably. [Para 143][Para 145] Following Awards 14/03, 18/03 and 50/07, the panel held it may only order removal where the worker is a dangerous person whose retention endangers others' safety. [Para 147][Para 149] On the evidence — verbal abuse, favouritism, threats, and dismissal of workers without reason — the panel found no act endangering safety, and dismissed the claim. [Para 153][Para 154][Para 161] It added, following Award 29/08, that the employer should impose internal disciplinary measures where abuse is established. [Para 155][Para 159]

On issue 2 the panel accepted that Ms Y consented to the demotion, since the employer proved repeated unauthorised absences and poor team leadership against her, and Article 27 permits discipline only in proportion to the fault. [Para 162][Para 164][Para 170][Para 172] But the demotion did not carry consent to a wage cut: her last fixed-duration contract named the post of team leader without stating a wage, yet through performance she received US$170 for that post, making the wage a term of the contract. [Para 177][Para 178] Under Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code no party can impose a change of contractual terms unilaterally. [Para 180][Para 183][Para 186] The reduction to US$140 was therefore a one-sided variation made without her agreement and could not stand. [Para 195][Para 196]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer lawfully demotes a team leader for proven misconduct but the contract fixed pay by reference to the post actually performed, the employer cannot cut the wage to the ordinary-worker rate without the worker's consent; the wage is a term of the contract and any unilateral variation is ineffective. [Para 178][Para 196] Separately, the panel will not order an employer to dismiss a supervisor for abusive language, however proven, because removal is internal management and is available only against a worker shown to endanger others' safety. [Para 145][Para 149][Para 154]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observation that the employer ought to apply its internal disciplinary rules to a supervisor found to have used improper language, and that workers should channel such complaints through the internal mechanism, did not affect either order. [Para 159][Para 160]
