# Award No. 071/16 — Cambo Chotab Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 25 April 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mr Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer and dissenting; Mr An Non, appointed by the workers; Mr Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · Doc ID 139011

## 1. Facts
Cambo Chotab Co., Ltd cuts and sews garments under registration Inv. 0158E/2012 of 17 January 2012 and employed about 1,290 workers. [Para 65] [Para 66] Its union was registered on 04 December 2012, and its new leadership — Mr R as president, Mr A as vice-president and Mr P as secretary — was recognised by the Department of Labour Disputes by letter of 03 November 2015. [Para 54] [Para 67] On 16 December 2014 the employer suspended the contracts of five union leaders, the same day that it asked the Department for permission to dismiss them, and in January 2015 it sued in the Phnom Penh court for termination of their contracts. [Para 85] [Para 76] Award No. 239/15 had already ruled on their reinstatement and on suspension pay for two of them. [Para 79] [Para 84] By the hearing the company had not closed and the court had not ruled. [Para 88] [Para 90]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the five union leaders and pay them their five termination entitlements together with back pay for the period of suspension? [Para 70] [Para 86]

(The panel recorded five unresolved issues at conciliation but the workers withdrew issues 1, 2, 3 and 5, leaving only issue 4. [Para 25] [Para 26])

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that the company has closed, so they no longer seek reinstatement but do seek the five termination entitlements paid to other workers, and repayment of wages withheld during the suspension, because the employer suspended them unlawfully — without notice to, or authorisation from, the labour inspector. [Para 71] [Para 72] [Para 73]
**Employer (respondent):** that the company has not closed and has not been struck off — it only paused production for a period and paid severance to other workers; that the five were suspended because they are union leaders who committed serious faults, and that the employer is entitled to wait for the court's decision before dismissing them, so no termination entitlements are owed yet. [Para 74] [Para 75] [Para 76] [Para 87] [Para 88]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim for the five termination entitlements was refused consideration, as was the back-pay claim of Mr A and Mr P; the employer was ordered to pay 100% wages to Mr R, Mr T and Mr D throughout the suspension pending the court's decision. [Para 160] [Para 161] [Para 162]
On the termination entitlements the panel found the decisive fact to be that no dismissal had yet occurred: the employer was still suspending the five while it awaited the court, so the contract had not ended and severance could not yet be assessed. [Para 89] [Para 90] [Para 91]
On Mr A and Mr P the panel applied res judicata (អាជ្ញា អស់ ជំនុំ): the same parties, the same point — wages during suspension — and a complete decision on it in Award No. 239/15, which had ordered 100% wages for those two. [Para 95] [Para 98] [Para 101] [Para 103] [Para 108] [Para 109] It noted that the arbitral procedure contains no rule displacing that principle, and that refusing a re-hearing avoids contradictory outcomes and secures finality. [Para 96] [Para 99]
On Mr R, Mr T and Mr D the panel held that awaiting a court ruling is not among the eleven grounds in Article 71 or the strike ground in Article 332, so the suspension was unilateral and outside the statute. [Para 122] [Para 126] [Para 129] It nonetheless followed earlier awards — No. 28/04 Raffles, No. 18/04 Sunway and No. 014 — that an employer may suspend to await an investigation or court ruling but must keep paying wages monthly for a reasonable period. [Para 135] [Para 137] [Para 141] [Para 143] [Para 144] Because these three could not earn during the suspension, and the loss flowed from the employer's unilateral decision, full wages were owed. [Para 153] [Para 154] [Para 155] [Para 156]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union leader's contract may be suspended so that the employer can await a court ruling on termination, even though that reason is not listed in Article 71, but a suspension of that kind obliges the employer to pay full wages each month for the whole period; withholding them because the worker could look for other work is not permitted. Where a prior award has already decided the same point between the same parties, the panel will not decide it again, and no severance is payable while the contract has not been terminated. [Para 109] [Para 141] [Para 155]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that Article 2 of the Labour Law gives the employer the right to organise and manage the enterprise only so long as that right is exercised lawfully and reasonably; and that suspension awaiting a court ruling must end within a suitable period. [Para 133] [Para 137]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award carries a dissenting opinion by the employer-appointed arbitrator as an annex, and the dissent dates the suspension and the employer's court filing to 16/12/2015 and 18/12/2015, where the panel and the documentary evidence place them on 16 December 2014 and 05 January 2015. [Para 85] [Para 168] [Para 169] The panel itself mixes the years, recording the dismissal request lodged with the Department as 16 December 2016. [Para 85] Only the Khmer form of the company name is printed (ខេមបូ ខូថប អិលធីឌី); the Latin rendering in the title is a transliteration, not a form appearing on the face of the award. [Para 1] [Para 65] OCR losses are severe in the evidence schedules, the article quotations and the operative part, where the word "ប្រាក់" and the claimants' numbers drop out. [Para 111] [Para 123] [Para 158] The number of claimants is given as five throughout, but the reasons twice refer to a group of five that includes workers not among the named five. [Para 70] [Para 151] Individual workers and union officers are reduced to initials per convention; the dissent names three of them.
