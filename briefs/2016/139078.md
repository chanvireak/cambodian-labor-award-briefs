# Award No. 176/16 — Graceful (Cambodia) Textile Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 31 August 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Non for the worker party — dissenting on issue 07 — and Kong Phalla chair) · Doc ID 139078

## 1. Facts
Graceful (Cambodia) Textile Co Ltd ("Graceful") is a garment factory in Tram Knga, Takeo province, employing about 720 workers. [Para 59][Para 60] Three registered base unions operate there, the largest being the Independent Workers' Union with about 90 per cent of the workforce; a branch of the Cambodian Workers' Union and the Khmer Youth Voice Union were newly formed and unregistered. [Para 61][Para 63][Para 162] In April and June 2016 the employer ended the fixed-term contracts of five workers who were leaders or candidates of the unregistered union; one contract ran to 28 April 2016 and another to 3 June 2016. [Para 93][Para 185][Para 222] During periods without work it paid only 50 per cent of wages, without the labour inspector's approval for a suspension. [Para 117][Para 254] Of 11 demands, four were settled (4, 8, 9, 11) and one withdrawn (10), leaving six for decision. [Para 25][Para 27]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate the five dismissed union leaders and candidates with back pay and benefits? [Para 177][Para 178]
2. Whether the employer must top up to 100 per cent the wages of Mr S during his one-month contract suspension? [Para 235]
3. Whether the employer must pay 100 per cent of wages for periods when there was no work, and restore two days of annual leave used at Chinese New Year? [Para 245]
4. What type of contract the four workers held, and whether the employer must conclude fixed-term contracts of six months to one year? [Para 265][Para 274]
5. Whether the employer must provide a free lunch or Riel 4,000 a day? [Para 299]
6. Whether the employer must deduct the attendance bonus pro rata for authorised leave days? [Para 324]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissals happened right after the union election was notified and were not based on any fault, so they are anti-union discrimination; the workers are entitled to reinstatement and to their unpaid wages; the employer used their annual leave to cover a work shortage. [Para 77][Para 79][Para 201][Para 121]
**Employer (respondent):** the contracts simply ran out and non-renewal is not dismissal; the individual workers were poor performers or had discipline problems, evidenced by two written warnings; during the stoppage the company and Mr S agreed a 50 per cent suspension wage, and the two leave days were taken under a written agreement with the workers' representatives and registered union. [Para 80][Para 83][Para 119][Para 203]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay 100 per cent of wages for the periods when it had no work, and rejected the reinstatement, top-up, contract-term, lunch and attendance-bonus demands. [Para 356][Para 357][Para 358]

On standing, the two claimant unions were unregistered, so under Article 11 of the Trade Union Law they could not represent members as unions; the panel therefore proceeded only for the six workers who had given written authorisation under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 and attended the hearing. [Para 163][Para 168][Para 169][Para 176] On reinstatement, Article 73(1) makes a fixed-term contract expire at its term, and the expiry of a contract is not a dismissal; the panel found the five contracts had run their course, four of them on 28 April 2016 with the workers recalled and paid to that date, and one on 3 June 2016. [Para 178][Para 186][Para 218][Para 231] Special protection cannot attach to the expiry of a contract. [Para 187][Para 190] On the discrimination allegation under Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law and Article 62 of the Trade Union Law, the panel applied the rule that the party alleging discrimination bears the burden of proof, found no evidence linking the non-renewals to union activity, and noted that the employer had continued the contracts of the other elected leaders; the employer's stated reasons and the two warning letters were treated as a plausible ground. [Para 191][Para 205][Para 213][Para 215][Para 216]

On the 50 per cent top-up, Article 71(6) permits suspension by agreement and Article 72(1) removes the wage obligation during a lawful suspension; employer and Mr S had agreed the 50 per cent so that he could train on the weaving machines. [Para 235][Para 241][Para 243] But Article 71(11) allows suspension without work only for serious economic or material difficulty, for no more than two months, and under the labour inspector's supervision, which the panel read as prior authorisation; none was sought, so the suspension was unlawful and full wages were due. [Para 246][Para 250][Para 255][Para 256] The two annual leave days taken at Chinese New Year were upheld, since Article 170 lets the parties agree in writing when leave is taken and the workers' representatives and registered union had signed. [Para 259][Para 270][Para 271]

On the contract-term demand, Articles 65 and 67 of the Labour Law and Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code require mutual consent; neither party can force a contract type or term on the other, so the demand failed. [Para 285][Para 293][Para 296][Para 298] The lunch demand was a benefit not fixed by the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules or practice, hence an interest dispute that this unregistered union could not bring; the panel declined to hear it. [Para 300][Para 302][Para 321][Para 323] The attendance-bonus demand was refused: Notification No. 230, which replaced No. 041/11, adds the words "without absence", so a day of leave disqualifies the bonus, and the pro-rata deduction is the lawful exercise of that condition. [Para 328][Para 331][Para 341][Para 355]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The expiry of a fixed-term contract at its stated term is not a dismissal and attracts neither reinstatement nor special protection for union leaders; to establish anti-union discrimination the complainant must produce evidence linking the non-renewal to union activity, and the employer's documented performance and discipline grounds will answer the allegation. Where an employer suspends work for lack of orders it must obtain the labour inspector's prior authorisation and keep the suspension within two months; failing that, Article 72(1) does not relieve it of wages and the full wage is owed for the idle period. [Para 216][Para 250][Para 255][Para 256]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that an unregistered union's absence of standing could obstruct workers' constitutional right to complain, and that workers may instead delegate their representation under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099, are general observations on access to the Council rather than part of the order. [Para 148][Para 156][Para 166]

[Para 362][Para 364][Para 367][Para 368]
- The award date is 31 August 2016 in the header and at [Para 369]; the panel's constitution is printed "19 July 2019 2016" [Para 21], and the extension runs from 08 to 31 August 2016 [Para 30].
- The employer's name is printed indifferently as "Graceful", "Graceful (Cambodia) Textile", "Fres Full" and "Pratom (Cambodia) Textile" [Para 1][Para 37][Para 52]; the claimant union appears under three variant names [Para 33][Para 162].
- The number of demands is 11 [Para 18] but the list at [Para 1]–[Para 12] contains twelve numbered items, and the panel calls the remaining six "01, 02, 03, 05, 06, 07" [Para 27] while issue 04 was settled, not withdrawn.
- Six complainants are stated after [Para 169], but the delegation list at [Para 65] includes Mr S, who was not among the five dismissed.
- Article 73(1) and Article 67(7) are quoted with OCR losses ("ថិរ វេលា" for "ថិរវេលា", "អតិបរមា" truncated) at [Para 178][Para 196], and the operative part at [Para 358] refers to authorisation from the "labour inspector for agriculture", a phrase that does not appear in the reasoning.
