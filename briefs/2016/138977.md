# Award No. 019/16 — Jewelry Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council of Cambodia, 23 February 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Run Saray presiding; Seng Vouchhun appointed by the employer, An Nan appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 138977

## 1. Facts
Jewelry Co., Ltd (ជេវើលីន ខូ អិលធីឌី) makes industrial steel tools at Sangkat 4, Sihanoukville, and employed about 77 workers. [Para 1][Para 91][Para 92]
It ran a day shift and a night shift from 2010. By an agreement of 9 May 2015 with the union representative it accepted that work be divided into two shifts; on 27 November 2015 it closed the night shift, moved all its workers to the day shift, and issued letters telling six, then four, workers to stay home on temporary permission with their wages undecided. [Para 98][Para 99][Para 100][Para 101][Para 104]
Five demands went to conciliation and were referred by report No. 008/16 of 20 January 2016; three were settled at the hearing and the first two were merged, leaving one issue. [Para 14][Para 25][Para 26][Para 27] The award is non-binding. [Para 28]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issues 1 and 2, merged and decided) Whether the employer must honour clause 1 of the 9 May 2015 agreement by reopening the night shift and pay 100% wages and benefits to the shift-2 workers given no work from suspension until their return? [Para 2][Para 4][Para 27]
2. (Issue 3, settled) Whether the employer must repay wage deductions made when workers took sick leave with proper medical certificates it refused to accept because the certificate did not state the days allowed? [Para 6][Para 7][Para 25]
3. (Issue 4, settled) Whether the employer must repay a deduction from Mr L (employee No. 008, mechanics) made without clear reason, which it attributed to three days' absence in November 2015 and exhausted annual leave? [Para 8][Para 9][Para 25]
4. (Issue 5, settled) Whether the employer must repay four days' annual-leave compensation deducted from Mr C (employee No. 036, mechanics, union secretary), which it justified by his leave for union work? [Para 10][Para 11][Para 25]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer breached the 9 May 2015 agreement by closing the night shift and moving workers to the day shift without their consent; the shift-2 workers were given no work and told to stay home pending a legal solution, so they should receive 100% wages and benefits; the deductions for sick leave, for Mr L and for Mr C were unjustified. [Para 111][Para 113][Para 128]
**Employer (respondent):** closing shift 2 was its exclusive managerial right; orders had fallen because oil prices dropped, the night shift produced more defects and less output, its workers took more leave and came and went freely, and one manager could not supervise it; it gave written notice on 12 November 2015 that shift 2 would close on 27 November, and it suspended no one — the nine who refused the day shift were given temporary permission to go home to avoid trouble. [Para 116][Para 120][Para 121][Para 124][Para 129]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand to reopen the night shift was rejected; the employer was ordered to pay Mr L dismissal damages under Article 89, moral damages under Article 91, notice pay under Articles 75 and 77 and unused annual leave under Articles 166 and 167, plus 100% of wages for the suspension period from 27 November 2015 to 19 January 2016; the other eight workers were not considered. No figure is printed. [Para 260][Para 261][Para 262][Para 263]
On standing, the panel applied Article 268 of the Labour Law and clause 2 of Article 3 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001: a union leadership mandate lasts no more than two years, the recognition of these three leaders expired on 4 September 2015 and no new mandate was evidenced, so they could not act as recognised leaders — but Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 lets a party act through anyone holding its written authorisation. Of the nine workers, only Mr L was named in the transfer-of-rights letter of 18 December 2015 and attended the hearing, so the panel decided the issues for him alone. [Para 141][Para 143][Para 146][Para 148][Para 150][Para 152]
On the agreement, the panel applied Article 2, paragraph 2, as read in Awards 62/06, 108/06, 33/07, 106/07, 84/08 and 08/09 — the employer may manage the enterprise, but only lawfully and reasonably — and Awards 54/06, 53/08, 08/09 and 22/09 on shift changes. It found the 9 May 2015 agreement fixed no date for performance and only recorded consent to dividing work into two shifts; it did not oblige the employer to keep shift 2 open indefinitely, so closing it did not breach the agreement. [Para 157][Para 159][Para 162][Para 165][Para 166]
The panel then took a different route on the individual contract: the written contract said nothing about shifts, but Mr L had worked shift 2 for more than a year with the employer's agreement, which under Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code amounts to matching offer and acceptance, so the night shift had become a contractual working condition; moving him to the day shift alone therefore breached an agreed condition and was not a proper exercise of the managerial right. [Para 167][Para 176][Para 187][Para 189][Para 191]
Because the employer's own conduct, not abandonment, ended the relationship, the panel held the employer had terminated the contract by its own will and owed statutory termination payments. Mr L's service exceeded two years, so under Article 67(2), as interpreted in Award 410/03 and consistent with ILO Recommendation No. 166, his successive fixed-duration contracts had become an indefinite-duration contract. [Para 194][Para 195][Para 196][Para 200][Para 205][Para 210][Para 211]
On the "temporary permission" letters the panel applied Articles 71 and 72: a contract may be suspended only on the grounds listed there, and a suspension of enterprise activity must be placed under the inspection of the labour inspector; this suspension matched no listed ground and appeared nowhere in the internal rules, so it was unlawful and 100% wages were owed for the whole period. [Para 237][Para 248][Para 251][Para 252][Para 253][Para 258][Para 259]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer's power to reorganise shifts is part of the Article 2 managerial prerogative and is lawful where it is reasonable, but a shift pattern that the parties have actually performed for a sustained period becomes an agreed condition of the individual contract even when the written contract and a later collective agreement are silent; ending that pattern for one worker by unilateral decision is a breach of the contract and amounts to termination by the employer, carrying dismissal damages, moral damages, notice pay and annual-leave compensation. [Para 188][Para 189][Para 191][Para 195]
Placing workers on indefinite "temporary permission" to stay home, with wages left undecided, is not a suspension permitted by Articles 71 and 72 and obliges the employer to pay full wages for the period. [Para 253][Para 258][Para 259]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that a fixed-duration contract cannot lawfully run beyond two years, and that ILO Recommendation No. 166, though not binding, is useful context for reading Article 67. [Para 206][Para 207]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The suspension date is printed two ways: the conciliation list says workers were allowed to rest from 27 December 2015, while the findings, the letters and the order all use 27 November 2015. Both are reported; the operative date is 27 November 2015. [Para 4][Para 100][Para 259][Para 262]
The award prints no amounts: every head of compensation is named by article only and the wage base is never stated, so no sum can be given. [Para 261][Para 262]
The remaining-issue count is inconsistent: five issues were referred, three were settled at the hearing and issues 1 and 2 were merged, yet the panel records that "one" issue remains and then names both 1 and 2. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 27]
The company name appears in several spellings across the award (including ជេវើលី and ដេវើលីន), and the worker and union secretary are printed under variant forms of their names; they are given here as Mr L and Mr C. [Para 1][Para 149]
Paragraph numbering is unreliable mid-reasoning: paras 137–139, 143–144, 150–152 and 255–256 repeat or truncate the same sentence, and stray "2016", "1979" and "777" tokens interrupt paras 132, 254 and 135. [Para 135][Para 254]
