# Award No. 096/16 — Tak Sun Enterprise (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 26 May 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer, dissenting in part; Sin Kim Sean, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, chair appointed by both) · Doc ID 139027

## 1. Facts
Tak Sun Enterprise (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a garment-sewing and laundry company on National Road 3, Dangkao, Phnom Penh, employed about 600 workers. [Para 110] Its company union had not yet received a registration certificate, and the federation held mandates from 108 workers through letters of 9 March and 31 March 2016. [Para 111][Para 112] The union complained on 21 March 2016 on twelve demands; conciliation settled six and the workers withdrew three, merging issues 1 and 11 and issues 2 and 12, leaving four matters for decision. [Para 19][Para 26][Para 28] On 28 March 2016 the company closed its laundry section outright and ended the contracts of the workers there, most of which ran to 31 March 2016. [Para 210]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. and 11. Whether the company must take back the ten founders of the company union and pay their wages from dismissal to return? [Para 176]
2. and 12. Whether the company must take back four union members — two whose contracts expired and two whose contracts were cut short — with back wages? [Para 283]
9. Whether the company must take back the three union leaders, one of whom was elected and two appointed or standing as candidates? [Para 363]
10. Whether the company must take back the ten candidates who stood in the union election? [Para 434]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the ten founders, the four members, the three leaders and the ten candidates were all union people dismissed on 28 March 2016, inside the protected period, without the labour inspector's prior agreement; the laundry section was still working and new workers were hired, so the closure was a pretext; they should be reinstated with back wages. [Para 163][Para 173]
**Employer (respondent):** the laundry section was losing money and closed for good, the workers concerned were told on 28 March and paid to the end of their contracts, and the company has the right to choose fixed-term contracts and not to renew them; the union never registered and never notified the protected names properly. [Para 130][Para 132]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement refused in every case because the laundry section no longer exists; compensation ordered for the nine founders whose contracts were cut short, for two of the four members, for the three leaders and for the ten candidates; the claim of one founder and two members whose contracts simply expired was dismissed entirely. [Para 282][Para 302][Para 404][Para 456]

The panel first classified each termination. Where a 2-month contract ran its course and was not renewed, as with one founder and two of the members, it held that the relationship ended automatically under Article 73(1) and that no protection against dismissal attaches to expiry. [Para 185][Para 193][Para 286] Where the company called workers in on 28 March and ended contracts that ran to 31 March, it held that this was a dismissal before term by the employer alone. [Para 212][Para 297]

On protection, the panel applied Article 4 of Prakas No. 305: a founder or member is protected from the date the union applies to register until 30 days after registration, provided the union has notified the employer of the protected names. The founders' protection failed because the union produced no receipt showing it had applied to register. [Para 229][Para 230] The three leaders and the ten candidates succeeded: the election was held on 15 February 2016, they were dismissed 42 days later and so inside the 45-day window, and the union's letter No. 088/16 of 8 February 2016 listed all twenty candidates, a notification the company admitted receiving. [Para 374][Para 377][Para 387]

On the closure, the panel found it was a genuine reduction of activity under Article 95, evidenced by orders falling from 4,467,725 trousers to 111,391 and by the building being let to another company. [Para 248] Because the whole section closed, the panel held there was no duty to rank workers by merit, seniority and family burden, following its earlier award on a fully closed building. [Para 250] That did not excuse the manner of termination: for contracts cut short, the employer owed the 5% termination indemnity under Article 73(6), damages under Article 73(3) of at least the wages due to the contract's end, final wages under Article 116 and unused annual leave under Article 166. [Para 258][Para 272]

One member stood differently: she had been renewed repeatedly from 2 January 2014, so that under Article 67(2) her total service exceeded two years and the contract became one of indefinite duration. [Para 318] Her termination was therefore a dismissal of an indefinite contract; the closure gave a valid ground under Article 74, so no damages were owed, but notice indemnity under Articles 75 and 77, dismissal indemnity under Article 89, unused leave and final wages were ordered. [Para 330][Para 343]

**Dissent:** Ing Sothy dissented on the last member, arguing that successive renewals each remained fixed-term because the 5% indemnity was paid at every expiry, and that a rule converting them into indefinite contracts would make the employer pay twice; she should receive the Article 73(6) indemnity only, not the Article 75, 77, 89 and 91 payments. [Para 473][Para 480][Para 490]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer closes a section entirely for genuine economic reasons, that is a reduction of activity under Article 95 and no order of dismissal by professional merit, seniority and family burden is required; but ending a fixed-term contract before its term still obliges the employer to pay the 5% indemnity, damages of at least the wages to the contract's end, final wages and unused leave. [Para 250][Para 258][Para 272]

Successive renewal of fixed-term contracts beyond a total of two years converts the relationship into one of indefinite duration under Article 67(2), so that a later termination is a dismissal governed by Articles 74 and 89 rather than an expiry. [Para 318][Para 330]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's statement that the union's failure to produce a registration receipt defeated the founders' protection, and its reasoning that a closed section leaves no room for a merit ranking, were threshold findings not tied to any particular sum ordered. [Para 229][Para 250]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award is heavily damaged by OCR: the contract tables at [Para 124] and [Para 161] are columns of dates without headers, and several passages are fragments ("អ្នកស្រី នៅក្នុង សេចក្តីបង្គាប់") with no content.
- The number of workers dismissed varies within the award: nine, ten, thirteen and "1,000" are used for the same group. [Para 205][Para 437][Para 442]
- The union's status is stated both as unregistered and as having notified the employer of twenty candidates, and the notification address is held to be wrong at one point and adequate at another. [Para 111][Para 383][Para 387]
- Contract lengths are given as 1 month, then 3 months, then 2 months, without a clear date for each change. [Para 118][Para 120]
- The worker whose successive contracts are at issue appears as Phan Sophak, Phan Sophakr and Phan Sophakre; she is referred to by role here.
- The award date is printed as 26 May 2016 in the header and again at the end, after an agreed extension from 6 May 2016. [Para 31][Para 491]
