# Award No. 194/16 — Ri Ying Shu Footwear (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 26 August 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ang Eng Thong presiding; Seng Vouch Hun, employer nominee; An Non, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139094

## 1. Facts
Ri Ying Shu Footwear (Cambodia), a shoe manufacturer in the Sihanoukville Special Economic Zone employing about 190 workers, faced 17 demands from the Federation of Khmer Workers' Spirit Unions; conciliation on 3 August 2016 settled 13 and left 4 unresolved. [Para 12][Para 39] The federation had held an election to create a factory-level union on 13 March 2016 but that union had not yet been registered with the Ministry of Labour, and 29 workers transferred their rights to the federation by letter of 14 July 2016. [Para 40][Para 41] The four remaining demands were referral to the Council on 5 August 2016. [Para 12][Para 13]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to pay a regular attendance bonus of USD 15 per month, i.e. USD 5 above the USD 10 now paid? [Para 3][Para 46][Para 68]
2. Whether the employer must deduct the regular attendance bonus proportionately to the number of days a worker is absent with the employer's permission, rather than forfeiting it entirely? [Para 4][Para 53][Para 91]
3. Whether the employer must pay a travel and accommodation allowance of USD 15 per month, i.e. USD 5 above the USD 10 now paid? [Para 5][Para 58][Para 128]
4. Whether the employer must pay an overtime meal allowance of 1,500 riel per hour, i.e. 3,000 riel per day, instead of the 2,000 riel per day now paid? [Para 6][Para 64][Para 139]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the extra USD 5 is a modest sum that other factories already pay, while the cost of living leaves workers short; van fares have risen from USD 7 to USD 20 and about 90 per cent of workers commute by van; the council has previously ordered proportional deduction of the attendance bonus. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 56][Para 61][Para 62]
**Employer (respondent):** the company already grants many benefits and follows Ministry of Labour notification practice; deducting only proportionately would encourage absenteeism and disrupt the production line; it pays USD 3 above the legal travel allowance and already meets the legal meal rate. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 57][Para 63][Para 67]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to deduct the regular attendance bonus proportionately to the days of absence covered by the employer's permission, and refused to consider the claims for USD 15 attendance bonus, USD 15 travel and accommodation allowance, and 1,500 riel-per-hour overtime meal money. [Para 149][Para 151]

The three refused claims were disposed of on a single jurisdictional ground. Each is a demand above what the law requires, hence a dispute of interest (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍). [Para 73][Para 135][Para 144] Under Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council, an award on an interest dispute takes the place of a collective bargaining agreement and binds all workers for one year, which would extinguish their right to bargain and to strike over the same subject. [Para 82][Para 83] Only a union with most-representative status under Articles 54 and 72 of the Trade Union Law of 17 May 2016 may therefore bring such a dispute. [Para 81][Para 84] The federation's local union was unregistered and the 29 claimants out of roughly 190 workers were not a most-representative union, so the panel declined to consider those claims. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 87][Para 88]

Issue 1 additionally failed on the merits: Article 2 of Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 requires at least USD 10 per month for workers with regular attendance, the parties agreed USD 10 was paid, and no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice obliged the employer to pay USD 15. [Para 68][Para 70][Para 71][Para 72]

On issue 2 the panel treated the demand as a dispute of rights and reached the merits. The phrase "without absence" in Notification No. 230 was left unclarified by the Labour Advisory Committee, and the panel refused to construe it as covering both authorised and unauthorised absence. [Para 99][Para 100][Para 104] Absence authorised by the employer is not an absence "provided by law", so the worker keeps the full USD 10 for lawful leave. [Para 102][Para 111] But the attendance bonus is a wage under Article 103 of the Labour Law, and leave granted at the worker's request with the employer's consent suspends the contract under Article 71(6), releasing the employer from the wage obligation for those days under Article 72(1). [Para 119][Para 121][Para 122][Para 123][Para 125] The employer could therefore not forfeit the whole bonus, only deduct the portion corresponding to the days of permitted absence. [Para 126][Para 127][Para 128]

Issue 3: Article 1 of the same notification requires USD 7 per month, and the employer paid USD 10, so it had already exceeded the legal minimum. [Para 128][Para 131][Para 132] Issue 4: Article 2 of Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 requires 2,000 riel per day of overtime, which the employer paid. [Para 140][Para 141]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim for a benefit above the statutory minimum is a dispute of interest, and because an award on such a dispute replaces the collective agreement and binds the whole workforce for a year, the panel will not consider it unless the claimant is a most-representative union under Articles 54 and 72 of the Trade Union Law; an unregistered local union representing a minority of the workforce has no standing. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 87] Separately, where an employer grants unpaid leave at the worker's request, the attendance bonus — being a wage — is payable only for days actually worked, so the employer must deduct it in proportion to the days absent and may not forfeit it in full. [Para 121][Para 125][Para 127]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that it still had no authoritative interpretation of "without absence" from the Labour Advisory Committee and that each claim must therefore be decided on its own facts, and it observed that the statutory USD 10 bonus is a legal obligation rather than a discretionary reward. [Para 96][Para 99][Para 104][Para 120]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The first dispute point is headed "ចំណុច វិវាទ ទី 09" (point 09) although it is point 01 in the numbering used everywhere else. [Para 46] The panel's constitution is dated "08 August 2019 2016", giving two years, and the hearing time is corrupted to "ម៉ោង ឆ្នាំ ២០២" where the date line says 14:00. [Para 14][Para 15] The signature block carries "2016" and a stray "2017", against the header and reasons date of 26 August 2016. [Para 1][Para 20][Para 153] Notification No. 230 appears as "២៣០០", "២៣០ ក.ប/ស.ជ.ណ", "២៣០ ក.ម/ស.ជ.ណ" and "២៣០ ក.ប/ សេ.ជ.ណ". [Para 71][Para 93][Para 104] Amounts are duplicated in the OCR ("៤ ១០ ដុល្លារ", "១០ ដុល្លារ អាមេរិក ៤"). [Para 46][Para 131] The meal allowance is stated per hour in the demand list but per day in the reasoning. [Para 6][Para 64] The employer's Latin-script name is nowhere printed, so the transliteration above follows the Khmer only, and the two workers attending the hearing appear by full name in the source and are redacted here. [Para 42]
