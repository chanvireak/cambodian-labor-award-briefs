# Award No. 004/16 — Ken Sport Shoe Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 2 February 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Aing Eng Thong (president) · Doc ID 138962

## 1. Facts
Ken Sport Shoe Co., Ltd, a shoe manufacturer in Kampong Chhnang province employing 5,800 workers, was asked by the Ken Sport Shoe branch of the Khmer Free Women Workers' Union to deduct 2,000 riel per month from the wages of 172 members as union dues from September 2015. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 44] The union had been registered by ministerial letter No. 116 ក.ប of 11 August 2015 and certificate No. 3339 of the same date, and its leadership was recognised. [Para 26][Para 33][Para 41] The federation filed the complaint with the provincial Labour Department on 23 December 2015; conciliation on 6 January 2016 failed and non-conciliation report No. 020 កប/កឆ of 8 January 2016 reached the Secretariat on 11 January 2016. [Para 8][Para 9][Para 5] The panel was constituted on 13 January 2016 and heard the single issue on 20 January 2016 at 14:00. [Para 11][Para 7][Para 12]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must deduct 2,000 riel per month from the wages of the 172 workers who volunteered to pay union dues to the Khmer Free Women Workers' Union at Ken Sport Shoe, starting September 2015? [Para 44][Para 60]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The union is registered and legally recognised by the Ministry, and the 172 members gave written consent to the deduction, submitting a list with their names and thumbprints on 22 September 2015, which the employer refused to accept. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 56]

**Employer (respondent):** It cannot comply because the federation president herself wrote on 1 April 2015 asking the Minister to cancel the branch union's registration over internal problems, and the union and its leadership (Mr S) are split and incapable of managing members; deducting dues could provoke unrest in the factory. It denies receiving the 22 September 2015 list and maintains its refusal pending the panel's decision. [Para 47][Para 49][Para 50][Para 57][Para 58]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to deduct 2,000 riel every month from the wages of the 172 workers who volunteered to pay union dues to the Ken Sport Shoe union; the order is non-binding (មិនជាប់កាតព្វកិច្ច) and becomes final eight days after notification absent written objection to the Minister. [Para 81]

The panel applied Article 268 of the Labour Law, which confers the rights and benefits the law recognises on a professional organisation once its statutes and the list of its responsible officers are deposited and registered with the Ministry; it found that registration was completed by certificate No. 3339 of 11 August 2015. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 77]

It then applied Article 129(2), which lets a worker authorise deduction of wages to pay dues to the union of which he or she is a member, the authorisation to be in writing and revocable at any time, together with Article 5 of Prakas No. 305 សកបយ of 22 November 2001, which requires the request at least 15 days before pay day and obliges the employer to act on it. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 67] It followed Awards 74/11-Forever Me Speed Footwear and 110/13-Cambodian Hoy Fu, which read those provisions as imposing a duty on the employer once a valid written request is made. [Para 65][Para 68][Para 69]

On the employer's objection, the panel found that the Minister never replied to the 1 April 2015 deregistration request and that the Ministry instead issued the registration certificate on 11 August 2015; a deregistration request and the employer's anxiety about the union's internal affairs cannot therefore ground a refusal to deduct dues from members who consented. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 78]

The panel relied on the 22 September 2015 letter and the thumbprinted list to find that the 172 workers had in fact consented. [Para 72]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a registered union and 172 consenting members make a written request at least 15 days before pay day, the employer is under a legal duty to deduct the union dues from those members' wages; an unanswered request to deregister the union, or the employer's concern about the union's internal disputes, is no defence. [Para 67][Para 78][Para 79]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remark that members may revoke the deduction authorisation at any time, which preserves their freedom to stop the check-off. [Para 63][Para 69]
