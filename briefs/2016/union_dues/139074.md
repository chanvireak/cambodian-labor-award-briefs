# Award No. 174/16 — A Khin Tech PP E Ltd (Arbitration Council, 17 August 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Aing Eng Phong chair) · Doc ID 139074

## 1. Facts
A Khin Tech PP E Ltd ("A Khin Tech"), a garment factory employing about 700 workers, had two unions inside the plant: the Democratic Samnang Kamkar union and the Chalana Kamkar union. [Para 31][Para 39] In December 2015 some 640 workers signed letters withdrawing from the Democratic Samnang Kamkar union and asking the employer to stop deducting that union's dues from their wages; the employer kept deducting. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 46] Separately, the Chalana Kamkar union's leaders tried to hand over 640 letters authorising deduction of its own dues for remittance to its federation, and the company's administration refused to accept them. [Para 53][Para 54] The dispute reached the Arbitration Council after conciliation failed on 13 July 2016. [Para 13]

## 2. Issues in dispute
The conciliation report listed three demands; at the hearing the workers merged issues 1 and 3, leaving two for the panel. [Para 18][Para 19]

1. Whether the employer is obliged to stop deducting union dues from the wages of the 640 workers who submitted written letters withdrawing from the Democratic Samnang Kamkar union, in accordance with the agreement of 02 December 2015? [Para 38][Para 58]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to deduct union dues every month from the 640 workers who are members of the Chalana Kamkar union and remit them to the Chalana Kamkar union federation? [Para 52][Para 107]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the 640 letters are in writing, carry each worker's identity details, thumbprint and the date 03 December 2015, and a worker who withdraws ceases to be a member automatically, so the employer must stop deducting; the union also submitted its own 640 consent letters on 15 August 2015 and again to company security on 27 August 2015, and the administration refused them. [Para 46][Para 53][Para 54]
**Employer (respondent):** the company does not interfere in union affairs and acts according to law; it did not stop the deductions because it did not want problems arising in the company, especially with the Democratic Samnang Kamkar union, and it does not know how to resolve the dues question between the two unions; it considers the 02 December 2015 agreement to have been skewed by interference in union affairs. [Para 3][Para 6][Para 42][Para 56]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to stop deducting union dues from the wages of the 640 workers who had asked to cease paying dues to the Democratic Samnang Kamkar union, and rejected the demand that it deduct dues monthly for the Chalana Kamkar union federation, while ordering deduction from the 640 workers who voluntarily pay dues to the Chalana Kamkar union once individual written requests are received. [Para 133][Para 134][Para 135]

On issues 1 and 3 the panel worked from Article 114 of the Labour Law, which forbids an employer from restricting a worker's freedom to use his or her own wages, and from Article 7(2) of the Trade Union Law of 17 May 2016, under which a worker may withdraw from a union by a signed or fingerprinted letter delivered to the union and the employer, after which he or she is automatically no longer a member and the employer must stop the dues deduction; nobody may obstruct the right to join or leave a union. [Para 58][Para 63][Para 64][Para 65][Para 67][Para 68] Article 129 of the Labour Law permits deduction of wages for union dues only where the worker has authorised it in writing, and that authorisation may be refused at any time. [Para 69][Para 73][Para 74] Because the letters carried identity details and thumbprints dated 03 December 2015, the panel held the withdrawal effective and the employer's duty to stop the deduction engaged; it followed its own reasoning in Award No. 013/16 — Kampuchea Th... and the Civil Code, Article 337, on the binding force of an agreement. [Para 46][Para 60][Para 89][Para 105] The employer's stated fear of trouble with the other union could not release it from that duty. [Para 103][Para 104]

On issue 2 the panel held that the duty to deduct dues is owed only to a base union registered with the Ministry of Labour and Vocational Training. It read Article 11 of the Trade Union Law as conferring rights only on registration, Article 129(2) of the Labour Law and Article 26 of the Trade Union Law as requiring the member's own written consent, and Article 5 of Prakas No. 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001 as requiring a written request at least 15 days before the deduction. [Para 107][Para 115][Para 116][Para 118] Following Award No. 289/15 — EM V... International Garment, Kampong Chhnang, it concluded that the statutory duty runs to a registered base union only and does not extend to a federation, so the employer owed nothing to the Chalana Kamkar federation as such; the Chalana Kamkar union is a registered base union in the company, so deduction is owed once each worker's written request is in the employer's hands. [Para 127][Para 129][Para 130]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker's written, signed or fingerprinted notice of withdrawal from a union takes effect on delivery, and from that moment the employer must stop deducting that union's dues from the worker's wages; the employer's wish to avoid friction with another union in the plant is not a lawful reason to continue. Conversely, the statutory duty to deduct union dues is owed to a base union registered with the Ministry of Labour, not to a federation above it, so an employer deducts for a federation only where each member has given a written request at least 15 days before the payroll deduction. [Para 64][Para 67][Para 118][Para 129][Para 130]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that Prakas No. 305 SKBY remains in force until a new Prakas on dues-deduction procedure is issued, and that the 15-day period protects both the worker against deduction contrary to his will and the company against error, are general remarks on the regulatory gap rather than steps in the order. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 117]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award date is 17 August 2016 in the header [Para 1], but the panel's constitution is recorded as "20 July 2019 2016" [Para 15] and the extension of the issuing date is printed as "09 August 201 2016" to 17 August 2016 [Para 22]. Only the header date is used above.
- The 02 December 2015 agreement is dated "02 December 2014" in the list of documents received from the workers [Para 25] and 02 December 2015 everywhere else [Para 38][Para 47][Para 103][Para 105]; the 2014 date is evidently wrong but both are reported.
- The number of withdrawal letters is 640 in the claim and findings [Para 38][Para 89] but 160 in the list of documents filed by the workers [Para 44]; the panel reasoned and ordered on 640.
- Prakas No. 305 SKBY is dated "22 November 20019" in the reasons [Para 117] and cited as 2001 [Para 118]; Article numbers in the copied text are garbled, e.g. "Article 268" alongside Article 129 and Article 26 [Para 127], and Article 129 is printed as "Article 129(bis)" style fragments [Para 109].
- The chair's name appears as "Aing Eng Phong" on the face of the award [Para 1] and as a variant spelling at the signature block [Para 137]; the employer company's name is printed variously "A Khin Tech PP E Ltd", "A/khin Tech" and "Akhin Tech 1" [Para 1][Para 31][Para 37].
- The citations to prior awards are lossy: "Award No. 60/05 — Ave Green", "46/10", "13/09 — Chun Teak Tha", "15/11", "154/11 — BN" and "289/15 — EM V..." appear with digits and Latin fragments interleaved in the Khmer [Para 77][Para 85][Para 127]; the union-federation name is printed with a stray "15" in the demands [Para 4].
- The award is headed as a collective dispute order under Article 313 of the Labour Law and is expressly non-binding, becoming final only if no party objects within 08 days of notification [Para 1][Para 20][Para 136].
