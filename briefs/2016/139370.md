# Award No. 217/16 — Indochine Apparel (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 12 October 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouchhun, employer-nominated; An Nan, worker-nominated; Sok Mathuong, president) · Doc ID 139370

## 1. Facts
The company, a garment manufacturer at Prey Chas, Vihear Suor, Khsach Kandal, Kandal, employed about 1,944 workers [Para 39][Para 40]. Facing a shortage of orders, it asked the Kandal labour department on 2 August 2016 to suspend the contracts of 1,845 workers, held a meeting with union and worker representatives on 1 August 2016 at which most attendees fingerprinted their agreement to the benefits offered during suspension, and received the labour inspector's authorisation letter No. 1325/16 dated 3 August 2016 [Para 25][Para 58][Para 55]. The suspension ran from 6 to 18 August 2016, during which the employer paid 50% of wages and of the USD 11.5 monthly meal allowance, but kept the USD 10 attendance bonus and USD 15 travel and accommodation allowance at 100% [Para 48][Para 49][Para 51]. Five workers transferred their rights to the president of the Federation of Labour Unions for Cambodia to sue; neither the factory union nor any union there held most-representative status [Para 45][Para 102].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to maintain 100% of basic wage, attendance bonus and meal allowance for 6–18 August 2016, when it had no work to give? [Para 114]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to pay a meal allowance of 3,000 riel for overtime worked from 16:00 to 18:00? [Para 151]
3. Whether the employer is obliged to raise the basic wage of the eight chief cutters (មេកន្ត្រៃ) from USD 155 to USD 160 a month to match a woman hired later? [Para 177]

Two of the five original disputes were settled in conciliation; these three remained [Para 15][Para 20].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The suspension was unlawful because the labour inspectorate never inspected the factory, approved the request on the very day it was received, and the employer's letter was dated 2 August while the authorisation issued on 3 August, so the procedure was irregular; the employer therefore had to pay full wages and the full meal allowance for the suspended days [Para 54][Para 55][Para 56]. They also sought 3,000 riel of overtime meal allowance to ease daily costs, and parity at USD 160 for long-serving cutters whom a new arrival had leapfrogged [Para 65][Para 70].

**Employer (respondent):** It notified the labour department by telephone before acting, held the 1 August meeting, obtained authorisation on 3 August, and paid 50% of wages and meals plus full bonus and travel allowance during the suspension, which already exceeded the statutory minimum; it could not meet the 3,000 riel claim, and the new cutter earned USD 160 because she had years of experience elsewhere, better output, and made USD 160 a condition of accepting the job [Para 57][Para 66][Para 71][Para 72].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three claims were rejected: 100% wages and meal allowance for 6–18 August 2016, the 3,000 riel overtime meal allowance, and the wage increase for the eight cutters [Para 184][Para 185][Para 186].

On standing, the panel applied Article 54 of the Law on Trade Unions (17 May 2016) that only the most representative union holds the exclusive right to represent workers in collective disputes, and found the factory had no such union [Para 75][Para 79][Para 102]. The factory union's leadership mandate had expired, and although new leaders were elected on 25 June 2016, the union had not notified or applied for recognition of the new leadership to the Ministry of Labour, so under Articles 11 and 12 of that law the three leaders could not represent members [Para 104][Para 105][Para 106]. The panel nevertheless held, under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099, that a party may be represented by any person holding its written authorisation, and that the five workers who had signed transfers of rights on 30 July 2016 were properly claimants [Para 107][Para 109][Para 113].

On dispute 1, Article 72(1) provides that suspension of the contract removes the employer's obligation to pay wages, and that provision applies only to suspensions falling under Article 71 [Para 114][Para 117]. Article 71(11) allows suspension for serious economic or material difficulty for no more than two months, subject to labour inspector supervision [Para 118][Para 119]. Following Awards 58/07 and 08/07, the panel read supervision as requiring prior notification to and authorisation from the labour inspector, which the employer had [Para 121][Para 123]. On the workers' attack on the authorisation letter, the panel followed Award 088/16 — M W Garment: a party disputing an administrative letter must complain to the body that issued it, and having failed to do so could not have the suspension annulled collaterally; the suspension was therefore lawful [Para 127][Para 131][Para 132]. Cutting the meal allowance by half was within the employer's managerial prerogative under Article 2, was reasoned by the absence of work, and breached no article [Para 137][Para 148][Para 149]. The claim was dismissed [Para 133][Para 150].

On dispute 2, the panel found the employer already paid 2,500 riel, above the 2,000 riel set by Ministry Notification No. 041/11, and that no law, collective agreement, internal rule or practice required 3,000 riel [Para 151][Para 154][Para 156]. It further characterised the claim as an interest dispute, which only a most-representative union may bring under Prakas No. 099 Article 43, read with the Trade Union Law [Para 158][Para 171][Para 172]. The claim failed on both grounds [Para 175].

On dispute 3, none of the eight cutters attended the hearing and none appeared in the transfer-of-rights letter, so under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 the federation president had no authority to represent them; the panel declined to consider the claim [Para 178][Para 179][Para 181][Para 183].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of contracts that the labour inspectorate has authorised in advance under Article 71(11) extinguishes the employer's wage obligation under Article 72(1) for the suspended days, and a worker who wishes to challenge the authorisation must complain to the issuing authority, not ask the panel to disregard it; separately, a union whose leadership has changed without notification to the Ministry of Labour cannot represent its members, and a claim may proceed only for workers who personally authorised the representative in writing. [Para 117][Para 131][Para 106][Para 109]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that a collective award on an interest dispute substitutes for a collective agreement and strips all workers in the enterprise of the right to bargain and strike over that subject, which is why such claims are reserved to the most representative union [Para 171]. It also noted that an employer's practice more generous than a ministry notification is lawful and cannot be used to ground a higher claim [Para 155][Para 157].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR is poor throughout. The employer's name appears as "Indochine Apparel (Cambodia) Ltd" and in Khmer transliterations that vary between ឥណ្ឌូចិន អី ភែរីល and ឥណ្ឌូឆាយ ហ្គាវ័ន្ត, and the union is attached to a differently transliterated factory [Para 31][Para 39]. Workforce figures conflict: the suspension request covers 1,845 workers while the panel finds approximately 1,944 employees [Para 25][Para 40]. The conciliation report number is printed as "១៥ថត ១៦" in the procedural recital and as 1577/16 elsewhere [Para 11][Para 15]. At Para 132 the panel states the suspension ran from 6 to 8 August 2016, whereas the operative paragraphs and the parties use 6 to 18 August 2016 [Para 132][Para 184]. Dispute 3 is pleaded as a rise to USD 160 but one recital renders the figure as "២ ១៦០" [Para 176]. Several sentences are truncated mid-clause where OCR dropped text, including the recital at Para 114 and the reasoning at Para 129–130, which repeats itself without content.
