# Award No. 043/16 — T&K Garment Industry 2 (Arbitration Council, 11 March 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-selected), An Non (worker-selected, dissenting), Kong Phallha (president) · Doc ID 138993

## 1. Facts
Six workers of T&K Garment Industry 2, a Phnom Penh garment factory employing about 838 workers, pressed four demands through a strike notice of 13 January 2016: a midday meal or 2,000 riel in lieu, proper allocation of work by section and role, incentive bonuses on the former footing, and reinstatement of a dismissed colleague, Mr Y [Para 41][Para 42][Para 3][Para 9]. Conciliation at the Ministry of Labour left four of seven demands unresolved and the file reached the Arbitration Council on 18 February 2016 [Para 17]. Mr Y had worked since 11 December 2013 under a probation contract and then two successive one-year fixed-duration contracts, the last running from 11 February 2015; the employer notified non-renewal on 28 January 2016 and he ceased work on 11 February 2016 [Para 70][Para 71].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to provide a midday meal or its equivalent of 2,000 riel per day to the workers? [Para 3]
2. Whether the employer must allocate workers' duties correctly by section and role? [Para 5]
3. Whether the employer must pay an incentive bonus to all workers on the same basis as its former practice? [Para 7]
4. Whether the employer must take Mr Y back into employment? [Para 9]

At the hearing the parties settled issue 2 by agreement that duties would be allocated lawfully, so the panel reasoned only on issues 1, 3 and 4 [Para 21][Para 22][Para 57].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer already pays 2,000 riel meal money at its large branch at Toul Pongro and should apply the same rule here [Para 54]; incentive bonuses were paid for over two years and were withheld from workers who joined the union, notably those in the ironing, cutting-table and electrician sections [Para 61][Para 63]; Mr Y was a union adviser with no fault, so non-renewal was anti-union discrimination under Article 12 [Para 75][Para 76].
**Employer (respondent):** it lacks the means to provide meals or the 2,000 riel, and GDM is not a branch of the company but only a commercial counterparty [Para 55][Para 56]; the bonus depends on each team leader's assessment of individual output and there is no rule requiring it, the 2016 wage increase having absorbed its capacity to pay [Para 65][Para 67]; Mr Y's fixed-term contract simply expired, compensation would be paid according to the Labour Law, and reinstatement is refused [Para 76][Para 77].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed in full — the panel declined to consider the meal claim and rejected both the bonus claim and the reinstatement claim; no sum was ordered [Para 221][Para 222][Para 223]. The award is non-binding and became final eight days after notification absent objection to the Minister [Para 224].

On issue 1 the panel found no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice obliging the employer to supply a meal or 2,000 riel, and classified the demand as an interest dispute [Para 111][Para 112]. Because an interest dispute award replaces the collective agreement for one year and removes the right to strike over it [Para 119], only a most-representative union, or unions together representing over half the workforce, may bring such a claim [Para 120]. The six complainants were not such a union, so the panel refused to consider the claim [Para 122][Para 123][Para 124].

On issue 3 the panel held that Article 2 of the Labour Law leaves the employer free to arrange incentive bonuses unilaterally, subject to legality and appropriateness, following earlier awards [Para 132][Para 133][Para 134]. The party asserting unequal or discriminatory payment bears the burden of proof [Para 136]; beyond oral assertion the workers produced nothing showing the bonus was withheld because of union membership [Para 139], so no impropriety in the employer's allocation was established [Para 140][Para 141].

On issue 4 the panel applied Article 67: a fixed-duration contract may not exceed two years and renewals beyond that convert the contract to indefinite duration [Para 142][Para 143][Para 144]. Mr Y's total service of 2 years 2 months therefore made his contract indefinite in duration, so the cessation was a dismissal, not a non-renewal [Para 161][Para 162][Para 163]. He was a union adviser, but the panel found the third condition for protected status unmet: the postal envelope sent to the employer on 19 January 2016 contained no notice naming him, so protection could not be presumed [Para 185][Para 186][Para 188]. Nor was there evidence of anti-union discrimination; no other union leader was dismissed at the same time [Para 196][Para 197][Para 199]. The employer could therefore dismiss, but had given only 15 days' notice where Article 75 requires one month for over two years' service, and had shown no proper reason under Article 74 [Para 212][Para 213][Para 216]. Since the workers claimed reinstatement only and expressly waived compensation, the panel left compensation aside and dismissed the reinstatement claim [Para 218][Para 219][Para 220].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive fixed-duration contracts whose aggregate duration exceeds two years convert by operation of Article 67 into a single indefinite-duration contract, so that the employer's subsequent "non-renewal" must be treated as a dismissal governed by Articles 74 and 75 rather than as the expiry of a term. Separately, an interest dispute may be arbitrated only where the claimant is the most-representative union in the enterprise, or unions together representing more than half the workforce. [Para 144][Para 161][Para 162][Para 120]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that no other union leader or activist was dismissed at the time Mr Y was, and that an interest-dispute award operates as a collective agreement binding all workers in the company for one year — observations that explain the panel's caution but do not carry the outcome [Para 197][Para 119]. Arbitrator An Non filed a dissenting opinion on issue 4, reasoning that Article 74 forbids dismissal without a proper reason, that none was given, and that the panel should therefore order reinstatement plus back pay from dismissal to return [Para 231][Para 232][Para 235].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is dated 11 March 2016 in the header and in the closing signature block [Para 1][Para 236], but the paragraph recording constitution of the panel prints the date as "18 February 2023 2016", carrying both years [Para 18]. The union election is given as 10 January 2016 throughout [Para 43][Para 178] but as 10 January 2015 at the point where the panel assesses Mr Y's membership [Para 173]. The passage describing the dismissal date is garbled and repeats itself: "the employer dismissed him on 11 February 2016 … on 11 February of this year 2016" [Para 179]. Paragraph 47 states the union held "022" leadership elections, an evident OCR corruption of a two-digit figure. Several arbitral award numbers cited as authority are mangled past recognition, e.g. "លេខ ៣៣VO៧+ហ្គោល រហូម" [Para 138]. Individual workers' names survive in the source but are rendered here as bare initials per briefing convention.
