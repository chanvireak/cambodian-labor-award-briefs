# Award No. 081/16 — Juhui Footwear Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 10 May 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, chosen by the employer; Sin Kim Sean, chosen by the workers; Pen Bunchhea, chair chosen by the two) · Doc ID 139015 · unofficial English translation of the Khmer original

## 1. Facts
Following a strike on 1 September 2014 against Award No. 190/14, the Kampong Cham Court of First Instance issued injunction order No. 87 on 3 September 2014 requiring the workers to return to their teams and duties within 48 hours and not to disturb other workers, under Article 337 of the Labour Law [Para 30][Para 72]. The workers returned, then stopped work again at 12:30 pm on 12 September 2014, and on 15 September 2014 the employer terminated about 400 workers [Para 31][Para 32][Para 77]. Twelve demands went to conciliation; eleven were withdrawn and only Issue #11 survived — reinstatement of the striking workers with back pay — and the employer now employs about 3,566 workers [Para 16][Para 19][Para 28].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Preliminary question decided within Issue #11) Whether the C.CAWDU local union vice-president had the right to represent the claimants, given that recognition of the local union leadership had expired on 17 January 2016, or whether representation rested instead on the written authorisation signed by 96 workers? [Para 54][Para 61][Para 64]
2. (Issue #11) Whether the employer must reinstate the 96 striking workers and pay them back wages from the date of termination to the date of reinstatement, or whether their non-compliance with the court's injunction order was serious misconduct justifying termination? [Para 65][Para 72][Para 82]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The strike was lawful, having been elected and notified to the employer on 25 August 2014, and it was called to press an interest dispute under Award No. 190/14; the injunction was therefore improper, workers enjoy protection under Article 37 of the Constitution and Articles 318, 319, 324 and 333 of the Labour Law, the employer recruited 72 new workers during the strike, and the workers did return to work as ordered before striking again [Para 36][Para 38][Para 39][Para 48].
**Employer (respondent):** The strike was violent, breached the injunction order and was procedurally irregular, so only the court may rule on its legitimacy; the employer never terminated anyone — the workers abandoned their jobs by not returning to work as the injunction required, and the employer has already offered termination compensation, outstanding wages, 5% severance and payment in lieu of unused annual leave, which the workers refused [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45][Para 50].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the demand that the employer reinstate the 96 workers and pay back wages from termination to reinstatement; the award is non-binding and becomes binding eight days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Ministry [Para 82].
On representation, the panel applied Clause 3, Paragraph 2 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, under which a local union leader's term may not exceed two years, and found that the recognition granted by Ministry letter No. 82 of 17 January 2014 expired on 17 January 2016 [Para 54][Para 25]. It held, following Award No. 62/06 — Quicksew Cambodia and others, that registration under Article 268 of the Labour Law is what confers the right to represent members before the Council, so the vice-president, Ms M, could not act for the members [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61][Para 62]. Article 19 of Prakas No. 99 nonetheless lets a party be represented by any person expressly authorised in writing, and 96 workers had signed such an authorisation dated 2 January 2016, so the Council decided the case for those 96 only [Para 63][Para 64][Para 67][Para 68].
On the substance the panel first rejected the employer's characterisation of the facts: applying Article 318 of the Labour Law, as interpreted in Award No. 36/06 — Mondotex and later awards, it found the three elements of a strike — a group of workers, a stoppage, and a demand for solutions — present on 12 September 2014, so the absence from work was not job abandonment [Para 68][Para 69][Para 70][Para 71].
It then held the injunction legally effective: under Articles 550 and 554 of the Code of Civil Procedure an objecting debtor must move the court that issued the order to modify or cancel it, and under Article 557 to extinguish it where the creditor files no principal suit; no such motion or request was shown [Para 73][Para 74][Para 75][Para 76]. Since the workers had returned for only two and a half days before stopping again, they failed the 48-hour return obligation, which is serious misconduct under Article 337, Paragraph 2 [Para 77][Para 78][Para 79]. Finally, under Article 26, Paragraph 2, an employer renounces the right to dismiss for serious misconduct if it does not act within seven days of learning of it; the employer learned of the non-compliance on 12 September 2014 and terminated on 15 September 2014, about three days later, so the terminations were lawful [Para 81].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union's right to represent its members before the Arbitration Council depends on current registration and unexpired leadership recognition, not on the individual officer's office; but any party may be represented by a person holding its express written authorisation [Para 59][Para 63][Para 64]. Where a court injunction orders strikers back to work and no objection or extinction motion is brought under the Code of Civil Procedure, continued stoppage is serious misconduct under Article 337, and a termination effected within seven days of the employer learning of it is lawful [Para 76][Para 79][Para 81].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that the workers had protected their position by the statutory route of objecting to the injunction and did not use it, which explains why their argument that the order was "improper" could not be entertained [Para 73][Para 74]. It also recorded, without deciding anything on it, the workers' assertion that the employer hired 72 new workers during the strike [Para 38].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
This is an unofficial English translation with systematic OCR losses of the letter "t" at word starts ("thearing", "themployer", "trike", "hissuance"), so quoted statutory language is unreliable in places. The document contradicts itself on facts that matter: the injunction is said to require return "within 48 hours" and also to require return "on 10 September 2014", while the workers are said to have resumed on both 9 and 10 September [Para 30][Para 68]; the claim is pleaded for 107 workers but decided for 96 [Para 33][Para 65][Para 66][Para 67], against 400 terminated [Para 32]; and the withdrawal list at Para 16 says eleven issues were withdrawn while enumerating only nine numbers. Party names are also unstable — the union is written "C.CAWDU" and "C.CAWDU Local Union" and the employer "Juhui Footwear Co", "Co., Ltd." and "Co Ltd." [Para 1][Para 19][Para 72]. The evidence section states that all documents and exhibits were omitted from the English version [Para 18], and the award number of the injunction is given as both 87 and 51 [Para 40][Para 50].
