# Award No. 142/16 — Zhena Dai Dong (Cambodia) Electronic Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 28 July 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Kong Phalla chair) · Doc ID 139050

## 1. Facts
Zhena Dai Dong (Cambodia) Electronic Co., Ltd. makes earphone cables with about 360 workers. [Para 55][Para 56] The federation held a leadership election on 17 February 2016, 12 workers voting, and notified the company on 25 March 2016, which the employer admits. [Para 57][Para 112][Para 114] Mr B, an electrician since 9 March 2013 on an indefinite-duration contract, was dismissed on 12 March 2016 after four light faults. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67] Ms V, a production worker since 21 May 2013 and elected union vice-president, was dismissed on 23 May 2016 without notice. [Para 93][Para 95][Para 221]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay severance, notice compensation, annual leave and final wages to Mr B, and take Ms V back into work? [Para 3][Para 151]
2. Whether the employer must stop deducting wages and stop requiring workers to record absences of 10 to 30 minutes in the leave book? [Para 6][Para 115]
7. Whether the employer must raise the attendance bonus to USD 15 a month? [Para 16][Para 126]
8. Whether the employer must raise the midday meal allowance to 4,000 riel a day? [Para 18][Para 132]

Issues 4, 5 and 6 were withdrawn; issues 3 and 9 were settled at conciliation. [Para 30][Para 32]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Ms V was dismissed with no fault of her own and should return to work. [Para 96] Wages should not be cut for short absences, since the previous administration never cut them and the new leave book makes leaving the line difficult; 30 minutes' absence now costs USD 0.44. [Para 117][Para 119] Other factories pay bonuses of USD 11 to 20 a month, and 2,000 riel does not cover a meal. [Para 127][Para 133]
**Employer (respondent):** Mr B disrupted other workers, breached the internal rules and ignored his supervisor, receiving an oral and two written warnings before a fourth fault justified dismissal. [Para 69][Para 85] Ms V caused problems with her section head and was absent without leave for more than six consecutive days. [Para 97][Para 98] Wages are cut only for time not worked; the leave book merely records rest days, and the USD 10 bonus and 2,000 riel meal money already exceed the law. [Para 121][Para 128][Para 134]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must pay Mr B notice compensation, severance, unused annual leave and final wages, and must take Ms V back into work; the claims on short absences, the attendance bonus and the meal allowance, and Mr B's emotional damages, were refused. No sum is stated: the order lists heads of payment only. [Para 334][Para 335][Para 336][Para 337][Para 339]

*Who could claim.* Trade Union Law Article 11 gives a union its rights only on registration, and the panel held the three unregistered elected leaders could not represent the membership. [Para 141][Para 144] Under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099/04 a representative needs written authority, so the 23 workers who thumbprinted an authorisation to the federation were the claimants. [Para 145][Para 149]

*Mr B.* Article 74 allows dismissal of an indefinite-duration contract only for a valid reason going to aptitude, conduct or the needs of the enterprise. [Para 152][Para 154] The internal rules rank light faults as oral education, written warning, unpaid suspension, then dismissal; four faults were handled in that sequence, so the reason was valid. [Para 89][Para 172] No prior notice was given, so Article 75 required notice compensation; the misconduct was not serious, so Article 89 required severance on the average of the last twelve months' wages including overtime and bonuses; Articles 166 and 167 required unused annual leave and Article 116 the final wage. [Para 179][Para 186][Para 208][Para 218] Emotional damages under Article 91 were refused, the dismissal having proper grounds. [Para 201][Para 202]

*Ms V.* Article 293 requires the labour inspector's agreement to dismiss a staff delegate or candidate, and the third indent of Article 3 of Prakas No. 305 protects election candidates for 45 days before and 45 days after the poll; Award No. 40/10 read that as at least 45 days after, or until registration is applied for. [Para 222][Para 223][Para 224][Para 228] The poll was on 17 February 2016 and the dismissal on 23 May 2016, three months and six days later, with no evidence of a registration application, so the second condition failed. [Para 239][Para 243][Para 246][Para 247] Her dismissal still had to satisfy Article 74. [Para 253] The employer bore the burden of proving the six days' unauthorised absence, and its team leader and administration head gave conflicting evidence on whether leave to 19 May was granted; proving nothing, it showed no valid reason and reinstatement followed. [Para 257][Para 260][Para 263][Para 264]

*Short absences.* Article 664 of the Civil Code makes wages the price of work and Article 102 ties wages to work performed, so none is owed for time not worked. [Para 269][Para 271][Para 275] Article 2 gives the employer the right to manage the enterprise lawfully and reasonably; requiring a record in the leave book fell within it, the form was not complex and the workers proved no particular hardship. [Para 284][Para 294][Para 295][Para 297] Both limbs of issue 2 were refused. [Para 278][Para 298]

*Bonus and meal allowance.* On the bonus the panel examined res judicata: case No. 191/15 had different claimants (six workers authorising another federation), so the first condition failed. [Para 305][Para 306][Para 308] On the merits, the USD 10 bonus and 2,000 riel meal allowance already exceed the law and no instrument requires more. [Para 309][Para 311][Para 330][Para 331] Both were demands above the legal minimum, that is interest disputes; Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099 makes an interest award a collective agreement binding all workers, and Articles 54 and 72 of the Trade Union Law reserve that to a most-representative union, which this one was not. [Para 322][Para 323][Para 324] Both were refused. [Para 327][Para 333]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The 45-day protection of Prakas No. 305 Article 3 is a fixed window measured from the poll: a union officer dismissed months later, with no registration application filed, is an ordinary worker judged only under Article 74, and the employer must prove the misconduct it alleges, failing which reinstatement follows. [Para 246][Para 257][Para 263] Wages are earned by work, so an employer may withhold pay for minutes not worked and require those absences to be recorded under Article 2. [Para 275][Para 297] A demand above the statutory minimum is an interest dispute, which the panel cannot decide unless the claimant union is most representative, since an award there binds every worker as a collective agreement. [Para 311][Para 324]

**Obiter dicta:** the res judicata discussion, and the statement that an unregistered union's elected leaders may not represent members, were not necessary to the outcome, since the 23 individual workers were the claimants and the earlier case had different parties. [Para 144][Para 149][Para 305]
