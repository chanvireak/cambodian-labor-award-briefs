# Award No. 179/16 — Dali Garmen International (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 25 August 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Seng Vouch Hun (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Kong Phallha (chair) · Doc ID 139081

## 1. Facts
Dali Garmen International (Cambodia) Ltd, a garment factory at Trapeang Kong, Samrong Tong, Kampong Speu, employed about 450 workers [Para 106]. After a strike on 16 June 2016, the company dismissed two elected worker delegates, Mr N and Mr S, and two ordinary workers, Mr D and Ms T, and also sued workers and their union in court [Para 117][Para 129][Para 202][Para 244][Para 245]. Some 83 workers transferred authority to four federations, 39 of whom later repudiated their thumbprints, leaving 44 claimants [Para 108][Para 111][Para 243]. The parties chose a non-binding award [Para 22].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the two worker delegates and the two dismissed workers and pay their wages from the date of dismissal to the date of reinstatement? [Para 244]
2. Whether the employer must pay a skill allowance of USD 5 to workers who can sew on one machine and USD 10 to those who can sew on two? [Para 197]
3. Whether the employer must let pregnant workers leave 15 minutes before the end of the shift? [Para 199]
4. Whether the employer must withdraw the complaint it filed against the workers and the union in court? [Para 202]
5. Whether the employer must pay overtime to electricians who work more than eight hours a day? [Para 207]

The panel numbered the dispute as eight points; points 3 and 4 were settled in conciliation and point 2 was withdrawn, leaving points 1, 5, 6, 7 and 8, which are the five issues listed above [Para 19][Para 20][Para 21].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** none of the four had committed any fault or been warned; the two delegates were dismissed without the labour inspectorate's prior authorisation; the two ordinary workers were dismissed for joining the strike; and the employer owed them reinstatement, back wages, the skill allowance, the extra five minutes for pregnant workers and overtime for electricians [Para 119][Para 120][Para 121][Para 197][Para 212].

**Employer (respondent):** the four committed serious misconduct under Article 83 — insulting and threatening supervisors and workers, refusing instructions and inciting others — so it could not take them back [Para 122][Para 249][Para 276]; the skill allowance was unaffordable after the minimum wage rose, the 10 minutes already given to pregnant workers sufficed, and the court complaint was its own right after strike damage [Para 198][Para 200][Para 206].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to reinstate the two delegates with back wages until the Minister authorises their dismissal, and to pay severance to the two ordinary workers; the claims on skill allowance, pregnant workers' early exit and electricians' overtime were declined for want of jurisdiction, and the claim to withdraw the court complaint was rejected [Para 443][Para 444][Para 445][Para 446][Para 447][Para 449][Para 450].

On the delegates, Article 43(1) of the Trade Union Law of 17 May 2016 forbids dismissing a worker delegate without the labour inspectorate's consent [Para 246]. The employer notified the Kampong Speu department on 14 June 2016 but never received a reply, and more than one month had passed by the hearing, so under Article 43(2)–(4) the request is deemed refused, and a dismissal carried out without authorisation is procedurally unlawful [Para 254][Para 259][Para 263][Para 266]. Following Award No. 169/16, the panel ordered reinstatement with back wages until the Minister decides otherwise [Para 267][Para 270].

On Mr D, the panel found the alleged offences — shouting at a security guard, switching off a fan early, and telling workers the administration had sent him to collect thumbprints — did not amount to serious misconduct, and his internal-rules breach was not classified as such [Para 292][Para 296][Para 297][Para 298]. Because his fixed-term contract had expired and he kept working without a new written contract, Article 67(7)–(8) converted it into an indefinite-term contract [Para 306][Para 311]. His conduct was still a valid reason to dismiss under Articles 74 and 27 [Para 318], but he received only one day's notice where Article 75 required 15 days, so notice pay, Article 89 indemnity, unused annual leave and final wages were ordered [Para 323][Para 324][Para 333][Para 346][Para 349].

On Ms T, the three warnings were proved, but the third offence was inattention to work, a medium fault under the internal rules [Para 353][Para 361]. A fixed-term contract may be cut short only by agreement, serious misconduct or force majeure under Article 73 [Para 367]. As her fault was not serious, she was owed moral damages equal to wages to the end of her contract, about one month and 14 days, plus the 5% contract-end indemnity, annual leave and final wages [Para 374][Para 382][Para 383][Para 386].

On the skill allowance and the pregnant workers' exit, the panel found no provision of law, collective agreement, internal rules or past practice creating the entitlement, so these were interest disputes [Para 391][Para 414]. Only a most-representative union may bring an interest dispute, and the claimants were not one, so the panel declined to consider them [Para 410][Para 411][Para 417][Para 418]. Withdrawing a court complaint falls outside Article 302 altogether [Para 424][Para 425]. The overtime claim failed because no electrician other than the already-dismissed Mr D was named [Para 437][Para 442].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A dismissal of an elected worker delegate carried out without the labour inspectorate's prior authorisation is procedurally unlawful, and where the inspectorate has not answered within one month the employer's request is deemed refused, so the delegate must be reinstated with back wages until the Minister rules [Para 259][Para 266][Para 270]. Separately, a fixed-term contract that expires while the worker keeps working becomes indefinite-term, and if the employer later dismisses for a valid but non-serious reason it still owes statutory notice and Article 89 indemnity [Para 311][Para 318][Para 333].

**Obiter dicta:** the observations that the panel will normally defer to the labour inspectorate's and the Minister's characterisation of a dispute as individual or collective, and that an award on an interest dispute stands in place of a collective agreement for one year, were not necessary to the orders made [Para 240][Para 404].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award date is 25 August 2016 in the header [Para 1]. Dates inside the text carry duplicated years: "21 July 2019 2016" at para 16, "14 June 2023 2016" at para 322 and "16 June 2022" at para 164. The number of claimants is given as 83 transfers with 39 repudiations (para 111) but 35 repudiations (para 219), and the panel settled on 44 [Para 243]. The company's registration appears as MOC-31110976 of 11 February 2015 at para 59 and as No. 408237 (old Inv.0587 E/2015) of the same date at para 105. Personal names are printed inconsistently throughout — the two delegates appear as "Nun Sophon", "Nun Sopho" and "Nun Sophal", and as "Sok Thuy" and "Sok Phuy"; "Deth Meach" appears as "Di Meach", "Deth Meach" and "Dim Meach"; those names are reduced here to initials. Paragraph 386 still names the wrong worker in the final-wages order for Ms T. The signature block at para 452 loses the chair's name to OCR, leaving only a numeral. No total sum in riel or USD is stated anywhere; only the unit rates of USD 5, USD 10 and USD 200 appear.
