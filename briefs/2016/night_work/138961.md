# Award No. 002/16 — Kon Tek (Cambodia) Co., Pen? Limited (Arbitration Council, 12 February 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Tuon Si Phan (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (president) · Doc ID 138961

## 1. Facts
The company, which manufactures electronic components and employs about 3,800 workers, faced 13 demands filed by the Khmer Free Workers' Union Federation with the Takeo provincial Labour Department on 24 December 2015. [Para 19][Para 58][Para 59] Conciliation on 5 January 2016 settled nothing, and non-conciliation report No. 011 កបវ/តក of 6 January 2016 reached the Secretariat on 11 January 2016; the panel was constituted the next day and heard the case on 18 January 2016. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 18] At the hearing the parties settled demands 5, 6 and 12, the workers withdrew 1, 2, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9 and 13, and demands 10 and 11 were merged, leaving two issues. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 27][Para 28] The parties agreed to extend the award date from 1 to 12 February 2016. [Para 31]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must comply with point 22 of the agreement of 27 November 2015, under which no armed force may be stationed inside the factory compound? [Para 28][Para 143]
2. Whether the employer must (a) take back Mr S and Ms C, (b) renew the fixed-duration contracts of Mr C and Ms N, and (c) reimburse withheld wages and restore the three transferred workers to their original workplaces? [Para 164]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Armed guards remain inside the compound and line the entrance at shift changes, contrary to point 22; the transfers of Mr C, Ms C and Ms T were to work outside their skills and were punishment for union activity, the employer pressed them to thumbprint resignation from the Khmer Free Workers' Union, and wages for November and December 2015 went unpaid. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 78][Para 105][Para 108][Para 121][Para 182]

**Employer (respondent):** No armed force has been present since the agreement was signed — guards were previously used only when accountants carried cash, and now the bank delivers directly. [Para 73][Para 74] The four contracts ended at their term because the workers performed badly or disobeyed instructions, the transfers affected many workers across buildings and had nothing to do with union membership, and no wages are owed for days not worked. [Para 81][Para 82][Para 83][Para 139][Para 140][Para 197]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both remaining issues were refused; the order is non-binding (មិនជាប់កាតព្វកិច្ច) and becomes final eight days after notification absent written objection to the Minister. [Para 244][Para 245][Para 248]

On issue 1 the panel applied Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code: the agreement of 27 November 2015 is a contract binding the employer not to permit armed force on the premises, so a duty existed. [Para 143][Para 145][Para 149][Para 153] But the party asserting breach carries the burden of proof, following Awards 78/05-Evergreen, 51/08-SD, 101/08-GDM, 168/09-Tuek Thla Plaza, 115/10-GF Mos and 147/15-Song Fong; beyond argument at the hearing the workers produced no evidence that armed guards were actually deployed, so the claim failed. [Para 157][Para 158][Para 159][Para 160][Para 163]

On issue 2(a) and (b) the panel held under Article 73 of the Labour Law that a fixed-duration contract ends by operation of law at its term, and renewal is the choice of the parties, which no one can compel without agreement (Awards 100/07-Zea Cambodia, 68/09-Sinomac 2, 98/14-Way Garment, 172/14-Forever, 178/14-Suon Usaha). [Para 166][Para 168][Para 169][Para 173] Mr S's contract expired on 2 January 2016 and Ms C's on 31 December 2015; Mr C's ran to 25 January 2016 and Ms N's to 20 January 2016, with notice already given. [Para 170][Para 171][Para 172]

On the discrimination allegation the panel cited Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law and held the workers bore the burden of showing the non-renewals and transfers were prompted by union membership; they offered no such evidence, and 33 transfer letters covering 33 workers showed transfers were general. [Para 175][Para 176][Para 185][Para 190][Para 192][Para 194]

On the transfers themselves, Article 2 lets the employer manage and move staff subject to four conditions — no wage cut, no day/night shift switch, no severe change of skill, no distant relocation — and the workers only contested the skill condition, yet none reported to the new building and none proved the new work differed in skill. [Para 210][Para 212][Para 225][Para 232][Para 235]

On wages, Article 664 of the Civil Code makes wages the price of work done; since the absence did not result from any wrongful act of the employer, no wages were owed and the transfer-back claim also failed. [Para 198][Para 200][Para 204][Para 241][Para 242][Para 243]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A party asserting that the employer breached a collective agreement, or that a non-renewal or transfer was anti-union, carries the burden of proof and fails if it offers only argument at the hearing without evidence of the specific act alleged. [Para 158][Para 185] A fixed-duration contract ends automatically at its term, and the panel will not order its renewal, because renewal requires the agreement of both parties. [Para 168][Para 173]

**Obiter dicta:** The four conditions the panel uses to test the lawfulness of a transfer, drawn from Awards 170/03, 18/03-Ho Hing, 63/06-FY, 108/06-Trung Gal and 90/07-GVM. [Para 210][Para 211]
