# Award No. 192/16 — Arin Manufacturing (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 13 September 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ri, employer-appointed; Tuon Si Phan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · non-binding award · Doc ID 139092

## 1. Facts
Arin Manufacturing (Cambodia) Co., Ltd cuts and sews garments on National Road 8 at Tien Phleng village, Smong Cheung commune, Kamchay Mear district, Prey Veng, and employs about 2,117 workers. [Para 71][Para 72] The Democratic Union of Khmer Garment Workers at the company was registered with the Ministry of Labour on 20 January 2016 with its leadership recognised the same day, and 197 workers transferred their rights to the federation on 5 March 2016. [Para 73][Para 75] The company issues fixed-duration contracts of three months at a time and pays 5% of the contract value when each expires. [Para 80][Para 81] Of seven demands, two were settled in conciliation; demand 4 was withdrawn and demands 2 and 3 were merged, leaving three issues for the panel. [Para 15][Para 20][Para 21][Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must convert the contracts of workers with more than two years' seniority into contracts of indefinite duration (គ្មានកំណត់ថិរវេលា)? [Para 2][Para 78]
2. Whether the employer must take back the union vice-president, Mr E, and all union activists, and pay their wages from the date of termination until reinstatement? [Para 3][Para 4][Para 95]
3. Whether the employer must pay USD 15 a month as a milk allowance for women workers with newborn children until the child reaches 18 months? [Para 6][Para 116]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** workers with more than two years' service should hold indefinite contracts rather than being renewed every three months; the company forced Mr E and the two others to resign because there was no work, promising to take them back when work resumed, and a group leader signed a letter to that effect with Mr E; mothers cannot use the factory crèche because they travel by crowded bus. [Para 86][Para 109][Para 110][Para 117]
**Employer (respondent):** each fixed-duration contract is under two years and successive contracts never total more than two years, so no conversion is owed, and 5% is paid at each expiry; the three workers resigned of their own accord and were paid termination allowances, and no agreement to rehire exists — the group leader has since left the company; it already provides a crèche, a breastfeeding room and carers, and cannot pay a milk allowance. [Para 90][Para 91][Para 98][Para 101][Para 108][Para 118]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must issue indefinite-duration contracts to the 13 workers whose fixed-duration contracts total more than two years; the reinstatement and back-pay claim for Mr E, Mr L and Mr S was dismissed; the milk-allowance claim was refused consideration. [Para 198][Para 199][Para 200]

On representation the panel applied Article 54(1) of the Trade Union Law of 17 May 2016, following Award No. 180/16 (Cement Cambodia Chakrey Ting), and Article 11 of the same law, under which a union acquires the rights the law confers once its founders register. [Para 119][Para 121][Para 131] It found the factory union registered on 20 January 2016 with its leadership recognised, that its vice-president Ms H attended the hearing, and that the employer did not dispute its standing, so it could represent its members. [Para 136][Para 137][Para 138][Para 139]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 67(2) of the Labour Law, which caps a fixed-duration contract at two years, and Article 73(5), under which a fixed-duration contract becomes one of indefinite duration where there is no prior notice and the total duration exceeds the limit in Article 67; it followed Award No. 10/03 (Zaksintek) that successive renewals totalling more than two years produce an indefinite contract. [Para 144][Para 145][Para 146][Para 147] It treated ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982 as context only, since it is not binding. [Para 151][Para 152][Para 153] The employer had renewed three-month contracts without notice for years, and the panel held it can no longer issue fixed-duration contracts where the total exceeds two years. [Para 155][Para 157][Para 158] On the evidence it verified 13 workers with more than two years' service: the workers claimed 17, the list showed 14, and one name was withdrawn, and the employer did not contest their seniority. [Para 92][Para 93][Para 159][Para 160][Para 161][Para 162]

On issues 2 and 3 the panel applied the rule that the party asserting a fact bears the burden of proof, following Award No. 63/04 (San Well) among others, and that a claim unsupported by specific evidence must be refused. [Para 167][Para 169][Para 170] Resignation letters signed by each of the three were on file. [Para 115] Mr E had resigned on 30 December 2014 to go and work in China; Mr L and Mr S resigned when their contracts expired, and all three received termination allowances. [Para 97][Para 98][Para 100][Para 101][Para 164][Para 165] A letter between the group leader, Mr A, and Mr E existed but was too unclear for the panel to conclude that the employer had agreed to rehire anyone, and the employer denied any such agreement. [Para 172][Para 173] The panel therefore found the three resigned voluntarily, without coercion, and dismissed the claim. [Para 174][Para 175][Para 176][Para 177]

On issue 5 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, agreement, internal rules or past practice requiring a milk allowance, so the demand was a benefit above the law and an interest dispute. [Para 179][Para 180][Para 181] Under Article 96(2) of the Labour Law, Articles 54 and 72 of the Trade Union Law and Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099, only a most representative union may bring an interest dispute, and the claimant was not one, so the panel declined to consider it. [Para 189][Para 190][Para 192][Para 195][Para 196]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive fixed-duration contracts that, taken together, exceed two years convert into a contract of indefinite duration under Articles 67(2) and 73(5) of the Labour Law whether or not the employer gave notice, so an employer who has renewed short contracts for years must issue an indefinite contract; seniority is measured by the total of the successive contracts, not by the length of any one of them. [Para 145][Para 147][Para 150][Para 157]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's use of ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982 as an interpretive aid, while noting it is not binding, and its general remarks on the law's preference for indefinite contracts as a source of job security, are context rather than grounds of the order. [Para 151][Para 153]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award is dated 13 September 2016 in the header, matching the extension at [Para 25]; but [Para 17] records the panel as constituted on "5 August 2014".
- The company name is printed in at least eight variants (អរិន មេនូហ្វេ ក ធឹរីង, ធីរីង, ធឺរីង, ធើរីង, ឆឺរីង, ផឹរីង, ជីវីង, ធើរីង), and [Para 82] carries a fragment of a former name ("Trading (Cambodia)") with no sentence around it.
- Numbers are corrupted: [Para 108] prints "03 8" and [Para 115] "403" for the three workers; [Para 178] prints the milk allowance as "១៥៦ ដុល្លារ" (USD 156) against USD 15 elsewhere; [Para 72] gives the workforce as 2,117 from the conciliation report.
- The count of claimants is stated three ways: 17 claimed [Para 92], 14 on the list after one name was withdrawn [Para 93], and 13 finally considered [Para 159]. The panel's operative figure of 13 is used here.
- Worker names are spelled inconsistently (អិន ចិន / អ៊ិន ចិន; ឡេង ភារុន / ភារុនិ; ហ៊ិន ស្រី អាំ) and are reduced to initials here; the group leader appears as អាចែ ភីង and អាចែ ភីង សុខ.
- Long stretches of the reasoning are garbled or truncated, including the quotes of Articles 54 and 72 at [Para 187] and the citation list at [Para 194], where statute names are replaced by fragments.
