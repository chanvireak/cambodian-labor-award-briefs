# Award No. 105/16 — Siu Quinh Garment (MFG) Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 2 June 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (employer-appointed ឈីវ ភិរម្យ; worker-appointed អន ណន; chair អាំង អេង ថុង) · non-binding award · Doc ID 139030

## 1. Facts
Siu Quinh Garment (MFG) Ltd. ("Siu Quinh") cuts and sews garments and employs about 500 workers; the Cambodian Workers' Union Federation holds authorisations from 147 of them dated 1 April 2016. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 45] The union filed ten demands on 5 April 2016; conciliation failed on all ten, and further conciliation at the hearing of 17 May 2016 settled demands 1 to 6 and 8, leaving demands 7, 9 and 10. [Para 17][Para 23][Para 24] Demand 10 concerns Mr K (worker no. K48), an ironing-section union committee member hired on 1 December 2015 on successive two-month fixed-duration contracts, the last running to 31 March 2016. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 76]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must provide a union space and two hours per week for union leaders and members to meet? [Para 77]
2. Whether the employer must provide premises and tents for a party before Khmer New Year and Pchum Ben? [Para 95]
3. Whether the employer must take Mr K back into his former job? [Para 104]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the meeting space and two hours a week are needed so leaders and members can discuss union business and resolve members' problems, and other employers provide the New Year and Pchum Ben parties even without a legal duty; Mr K is one of five union committee members protected by the Labour Law, his non-renewal was discrimination contrary to Article 12, and the employer rehired the other casual workers terminated with him but not him. [Para 50][Para 54][Para 62][Para 63][Para 67][Para 69]
**Employer (respondent):** the Labour Law requires a meeting room only for staff delegates, not for unions; it applies its old practice and will hold a party in November only in years without a strike; the case is a non-renewal of an expired fixed-duration contract that also affected about ten other casual workers, and had nothing to do with union membership. [Para 51][Para 55][Para 56][Para 64][Para 65][Para 66]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three remaining demands were refused; the award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification unless a party lodges written objection with the Minister. [Para 94][Para 101][Para 142][Para 146]

**Demand 7.** The panel found no provision of the Labour Law, a collective agreement, internal rules, the employment contract, an agreement between the parties or past practice obliging the employer to provide premises and two hours a week; the demand therefore went beyond what the law requires and was an interest dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍). [Para 78][Para 79] Under Clause 43(1) of Prakas No. 099 an award on an interest dispute substitutes for a collective agreement and binds all workers for one year, and under Article 96 and Clause 9(1) of Prakas No. 305 only the most representative union may conclude such an agreement. [Para 80][Para 83][Para 85][Para 86][Para 87] An interest dispute may therefore be arbitrated only where the claimant is the most representative union, or several unions together, representing more than half the enterprise's workers. [Para 88] Here the claimant was a group of workers, not the most representative union, so the panel had no basis to consider the demand and refused it. [Para 92][Para 93][Para 94]

**Demand 9.** The panel applied the same analysis: no legal, contractual or practice-based duty to provide premises and tents for the two festivals, so the claim was an interest dispute, and the claimant lacked the representative character required to bring one. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 98][Para 99][Para 100][Para 101]

**Demand 10.** Article 73(1) provides that a fixed-duration contract expires naturally at its term; whether to renew is the parties' right, and neither party can force the other to continue without agreement. [Para 104][Para 106][Para 108][Para 111] Article 293 protection, which requires Labour Inspector authorisation, covers dismissal (ការបញ្ឈប់), not non-renewal, so it did not apply here. [Para 113][Para 117] Following Award 64/09 (Sino Mek 2), an employer may decline to renew at expiry unless discrimination is the basis of the decision. [Para 118][Para 120] Articles 12 and 279 forbid using union membership or union activity as the ground for hiring, discipline or termination decisions. [Para 121][Para 122][Para 123] The burden of proving discrimination lies on the party alleging it. [Para 125][Para 134] The workers produced no evidence that the employer rehired all the other casual workers except Mr K, and their own account showed the union leaders held six-month contracts that the employer kept renewing. [Para 136][Para 137][Para 138][Para 139] The panel therefore found the non-renewal unconnected to union discrimination and refused reinstatement. [Para 140][Para 141][Para 142]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a benefit that no law, collective agreement, internal rule, contract or established practice grants is an interest dispute, which the panel will not arbitrate unless the claimant is the most representative union (alone or with others) representing more than half the workforce; and the expiry of a fixed-duration contract is not a dismissal, so reinstatement follows only if the worker proves that union discrimination, not the expiry, caused the non-renewal. [Para 88][Para 113][Para 118][Para 140]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the employer's November party was offered on a condition — that no strike occurred that year — but did not decide whether such a condition is permissible, the demand having failed on representativeness. [Para 55][Para 56]
