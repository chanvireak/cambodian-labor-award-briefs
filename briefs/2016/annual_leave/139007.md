# Award No. 066/16 — Galey Global (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 19 April 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mar Sambo Na (ម៉ារ សំបូរ ណា), appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna (លីវ សុវណ្ណា), appointed by the workers; Aing Eng Thong (អាំង អេង ថុង), chair · Doc ID 139007

## 1. Facts
Galey Global (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a garment manufacturer in the Sihanoukville special economic zone, employed about 884 workers and recognised a CFLM local union registered on 25 December 2014. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 28] The union complained on 1 March 2016; conciliation on 7 March 2016 settled three of four points, leaving one: that the company replace the full-time general manager, Mr E, whom the CEO had hired three to four months earlier. [Para 8][Para 9][Para 10][Para 30][Para 31] Thirteen workers, including a union vice-president, attended the hearing of 29 March 2016. [Para 7][Para 29]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must replace the full-time general manager, Mr E, and remove him from the company? [Para 30][Para 56]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr E changed company practice in ways that cut workers' benefits and behaved negatively — requiring overtime to 21:00 without overtime pay or transport, deducting annual leave when leave was requested contrary to the agreement of 13 September 2014, dropping the USD 50 wedding gift until complaints were made, and leaving a supervisor promoted seven months earlier USD 300 a month short of the supervisor rate; grievances sent to the CEO went unanswered, and he refused to meet the workers. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 37][Para 38][Para 39][Para 40][Para 44][Para 45][Para 46]
**Employer (respondent):** appointing and replacing the general manager is the CEO's right, not the workers'; the earlier practice existed but the new manager wished to revoke it; Mr E joined only days after the former manager left, so the handover was incomplete; and no act of his endangered anyone. [Para 48][Para 53][Para 54][Para 55]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the demand that the employer replace and remove the full-time general manager from the company. [Para 73][Para 74]

The panel began with Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 311 of the Civil Code, holding that only the parties to a contract may create, vary or extinguish its obligations, so a third party cannot dissolve or amend the employment contract. [Para 56][Para 58][Para 59][Para 61] It then read Article 2 of the Labour Law as conferring on the employer the right to arrange and manage the enterprise so long as that right is exercised lawfully and reasonably, and followed awards 62/06, 09/xx, 3307, 119/09, 156/13 and 201/14 in holding that the panel cannot order an employer to remove a worker, supervisor or section chief, because staffing is the company's internal arrangement for keeping production running. [Para 62][Para 66][Para 67][Para 68]

It identified one exception in awards 18/03, 17/03, 08/03, 14/03, 15/04, 16/04 and 50/07: the panel may order removal where the person has beaten someone or used violence, creating danger and a risk to other workers' safety. [Para 68] Testing the evidence, it found that the workers alleged only changes to conditions and benefits and a negative attitude, not any act capable of endangering others, and that the flexing of the agreement remained a plan that had not been implemented. [Para 70][Para 71][Para 72][Para 51] It therefore found no basis to characterise Mr E as a dangerous person whom the company could not keep, and dismissed the claim. [Para 72][Para 73]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The panel will not order an employer to dismiss or replace a manager, supervisor or worker at the demand of the workforce: the choice and deployment of staff is the employer's right of internal management under Article 2 of the Labour Law, and only the parties to the employment contract may alter it. Removal is ordered only on evidence that the person has used violence or otherwise poses a concrete danger to the safety of other workers; grievances about changed benefits must be brought as benefit disputes, not as a demand for removal. [Para 61][Para 66][Para 68][Para 72]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the manager's intention to revoke the 2014 arrangements was still a plan, never implemented, was recorded as background; the outcome rested on the absence of any dangerous act. [Para 48][Para 51][Para 52]
