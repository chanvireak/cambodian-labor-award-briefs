# Award No. 160/16 — Ken Sport Shoe Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 19 July 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Sin Kim Sean, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, President) · Doc ID 139060

## 1. Facts
Ken Sport Shoe Co., Ltd operates a shoe factory at Samaki Mean Chey district, Kampong Chhnang province, and was the respondent to a collective dispute filed by two federations, the Federation of Cambodian Workers' Unions and the Khmer Youth Workers' Federation [Para 1][Para 11]. The federations raised three demands: refund of wages the company deducted on 09 April 2016, proper timing of the entry and exit bell, and an end to deducting annual leave in place of strike days [Para 3][Para 4][Para 5]. Conciliation at the provincial labour department on 04 July 2016 failed, and the three unresolved points were transmitted to the Council's secretariat on 06 July 2016 [Para 8][Para 13]. Neither the workers nor any representative appeared at the 14 July 2016 hearing; the employer did [Para 16][Para 17].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must return to the workers the wages it deducted on 09 April 2016? [Para 3]
2. Whether the company must arrange the entry and exit bell to sound at the correct times each day? [Para 4]
3. Whether the company may deduct annual leave entitlement in place of days taken as strike? [Para 5]
4. Whether the panel should continue the arbitration in the absence of the claimant worker party? [Para 27]

The panel reached no view on issues 1 to 3: it found it could not establish the facts relating to them precisely because the claimant did not attend [Para 26].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Neither federation attended, by telephone the Cambodian Workers' Unions official said he was busy in a meeting, and the Khmer Youth Workers' Federation said it would not attend because the workers had not asked the Ministry to send case No. 160/16 to the Council; no written defence or evidence was filed [Para 19][Para 21][Para 43]. Their position survives only in the conciliation record: the deductions, the bell timing and the leave substitution are unlawful [Para 3][Para 4][Para 5].

**Employer (respondent):** The employer appeared through its representatives and did not agree to refund the money deducted on 09 April 2016 [Para 1][Para 3][Para 16]. Beyond that denial the record contains no substantive defence, because the claimant never appeared to put its case [Para 26].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Proceedings terminated — the claimant was treated as having abandoned its claim, and the arbitration of case No. 160/16 was closed by award with no order on any of the three demands and no sum awarded [Para 52][Para 56][Para 57].

The panel identified Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council (21 April 2004) and Rule 4.7 of the procedural rules annexed to it as permitting the panel either to continue in a party's absence or to close the file by award, provided the absent party was properly notified of the date, time and place [Para 27][Para 29][Para 30]. Following earlier awards (Nos 16/07, 27/07, 138/08, 11/11, 131/12, 108/13 and 332/14), the panel distilled three cumulative conditions for declining to continue: proper invitation, non-appearance before the panel, and absence of a valid reason [Para 31][Para 33].

It then tested each. Condition one was met: invitation letters No. 865 (to both federations) and No. 864 (to the company) issued on 11 July 2016, stating the hearing date of 14 July 2016 at 14:00, the venue, and contact telephone and fax numbers [Para 34][Para 37][Para 38]. Condition two was met: "appearing before the panel" was read to require both physical presence at the hearing and participation through all four stages — introduction and conflict-of-interest explanation, explanation of procedure and definition of issues, conciliation if agreed, and the evidentiary hearing — and the claimant did neither [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41]. Condition three was met: the reasons given by telephone at 13:48 on the hearing day (a conflicting meeting; the absence of a Ministry referral) were not valid reasons for non-attendance [Para 42][Para 44][Para 45]. All three therefore being satisfied, the panel treated the claim as abandoned, held it unnecessary to continue, and closed the arbitration [Para 46][Para 50][Para 52][Para 53].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the claimant party has been properly invited with clear notice of the hearing date, time and place, has neither appeared before the panel nor taken part in all four stages of the hearing, and has offered no valid reason for its absence, the panel may treat the claim as abandoned and terminate the arbitration by award without examining the merits; "appearing" means presence at the hearing and participation throughout, not merely notification or a telephone response [Para 39][Para 40][Para 45][Para 52].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's general statement that both claimant and respondent bear a duty to appear in person to explain their assertions and supply the documents and information needed for the panel to decide is an observation on party conduct, not a condition of the ruling [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 is printed as "ប្រការ ២១៦" at Para 27, and the same Prakas is dated "ថ្ងៃទី ៦១ ខែមេសា ឆ្នាំ ២០០៤" at Para 46 against 21 April 2004 everywhere else [Para 27][Para 46]. Para 29 repeats a block of text twice mid-sentence, and Para 31 carries a stray "2017". Para 14 prints "ឆ្នាំ ឆ្នាំ ២០១៦" and Para 1 renders "ក្រុមប្រឹក្សា" as "រួមប្រឹក្សា". The worker-side arbitrator is "ស៊ីន គីម ស៊ាន" on the face of the award and "ស៊ីន គឹម ស៊ាន" in the signature block, and the employer-side arbitrator's name is missing altogether from that block [Para 1][Para 58]. Paras 47–49 and 54 are badly corrupted by OCR, with fragments ("Lax", "Land", "អ្នកគ្រូ") intruding into the reasoning. No individual worker is named in the text.
