# Award No. 012/16 — Cambodia Beer (Khmer Brew), Cambodia Beer Co. (Arbitration Council, 19 February 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — You Soun Ty (employer-appointed), Houn Chhun Dy (worker-appointed), Ang Eng Thong (president) · Doc ID 138970

## 1. Facts
The brewery employs about 539 workers, including some 261 women sales assistants (ជំនួយការលក់, beer promoters) deployed in shops and restaurants in Phnom Penh [Para 73][Para 74][Para 86]. On 8 January 2016 the employer had them thumbprint new contracts moving their shift from 14:00–21:00 to 16:00–23:00, effective 15 January 2016; 24 refused, and on 19 January 2016 a notice was posted declaring 11 of them no longer employees [Para 197][Para 148][Para 155]. A collective dispute over two demands — retention of the old hours and an end to short fixed-duration contracts — went through conciliation on 18 January 2016 and was referred to the Council on 19 January 2016 [Para 8][Para 10][Para 11][Para 13]. At the hearing the workers added a third demand: reinstatement of the 11 with back pay [Para 18][Para 251].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer was obliged to keep the old hours, 14:00–21:00, for the sales assistants? [Para 161]
2. Whether the employer was obliged to stop using short fixed-duration contracts? [Para 241]
3. Whether the Council had jurisdiction over the added demand to reinstate the 11 dismissed sales assistants? [Para 252][Para 253]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer used its managerial power in breach of the contract and the Labour Law; a change to a term of the contract needs the agreement of both parties, yet the employer relied on its own needs alone [Para 93][Para 94]. The new contracts were thumbprinted without the workers knowing the hours had changed and under pressure from the sales supervisor, who said the contract would not be renewed otherwise, so consent was vitiated under Articles 348 and 349 of the Civil Code [Para 95][Para 117][Para 219].

**Employer (respondent):** The hours were changed for market reasons — restaurants and events now start receiving guests from 16:00 [Para 115]. The contracts were read out and explained, notice was posted before 31 December 2015, and 234 sales assistants and the staff delegates and union supported the change [Para 111][Para 120][Para 128][Para 211]. Not signing simply meant the fixed-duration contract ran to its end under Article 74, which is not coercion [Para 122][Para 220]. The added reinstatement demand was not in the non-settled report and lies outside the Council's jurisdiction [Para 141].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand to restore the old hours is dismissed, but the employer is ordered to put in place safety measures for workers waiting for the company vehicle at night and for their transport home; the demand on short-term contracts is dismissed and the added reinstatement demand is not considered [Para 239][Para 240][Para 250][Para 264][Para 269][Para 270]. The award is non-binding and becomes final if no party objects in writing within 08 days [Para 271].

On the frame of the case, the 11 workers whose contracts had been terminated no longer held contracts and were no longer parties to point 1, which the panel examined only for the 11 who had signed the new contracts [Para 158][Para 160].

On the type of contract, it applied Article 67(2) of the Labour Law, which caps a fixed-duration contract at two years, and Article 73(5), under which a contract with no notice of termination and a total duration beyond that cap becomes a contract of undetermined duration; it followed Award No. 10/03 (Zaksin Tik) and ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982 as interpretive context, and found all 11 had worked over two years on successive one-year contracts, so they held undetermined-duration contracts [Para 163][Para 166][Para 171][Para 175][Para 177].

On the change of hours, it treated the shift as a term of the contract and applied Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code: a contract is formed and varied by matching offer and acceptance, and neither party may impose terms on the other [Para 184][Para 189][Para 199]. The claim of vitiated consent failed: the burden lay on the workers, the supervisor had read the contract, notice was posted, and the pay in the new contract was the old-hours rate, which the panel held could not prove they were unaware of the change [Para 204][Para 210][Para 212][Para 215][Para 223]. Nothing established fraud under Article 348 or abuse of a dominant position under Article 349 [Para 202][Para 216][Para 224].

On safety, Article 666 of the Civil Code requires the employer to safeguard workers' health and safety, and the panel found the 23:00 finish leaves women waiting up to two hours outside shuttered, unlit shops because the company vehicle cannot enter their alleys [Para 232][Para 233]. Because the employer had not consulted the workers' representatives about these risks before imposing the change, the change did not comply with Article 666, and the employer was ordered to arrange protective measures, in line with Awards Nos 56/06, 96/13, 154/13, 168/13, 188/13, 204/13, 016/14 and 191/15 [Para 237][Para 238][Para 240].

On short-term contracts, the panel held that a demand to stop them is not against the law, being an offer, but the employer's refusal is equally lawful, and neither side may force a contract type on the other; the demand was dismissed [Para 244][Para 246][Para 250].

On the added demand, Article 312(1) of the Labour Law and Article 33 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 confine the panel to issues in the non-settled record and to issues arising later as a direct consequence of the dispute [Para 253][Para 254]. The employer had already stated at the 18 January conciliation that the contracts ended on 31 December 2015, so the terminations were not a later consequence, and the panel declined to consider them [Para 261][Para 262][Para 263][Para 264].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a fixed-duration contract is renewed so that the total duration exceeds the two-year cap in Article 67(2), it is a contract of undetermined duration, and a change to its terms — including shift hours — requires genuine agreement, judged on offer and acceptance, with the claimant bearing the burden of proving vitiated consent [Para 166][Para 175][Para 199][Para 215]. An employer may lawfully change shift hours for business reasons and may decline to convert short-term contracts, but a change that exposes workers to a foreseeable safety risk at night without prior consultation of their representatives breaches the duty of care and requires protective measures [Para 238][Para 244][Para 250]. A dismissal already disclosed at conciliation is not a later consequence of the dispute, so the panel cannot take it up [Para 263].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remarks that undetermined-duration contracts encourage commitment, and that ILO Recommendation No. 166 is not binding but useful context, are explanatory rather than operative [Para 168][Para 173].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel's own composition date is printed as "ថ្ងៃទី ២០ ខែមករា ឆ្នាំ ២០២២ ឆ្នាំ ៦០១៦" and the award-date extension as "ថ្ងៃទី ០៩ ខែកុម្ភៈ ឆ្នាំ ២០ ២០១៦", though the face date of 19 February 2016 is consistent [Para 1][Para 14][Para 21]. The number of complainants shifts: the claim names 22, two more were added at the hearing for a total of 24, the employer recognised only 22, and the panel accepted 22 as the original complainants [Para 77][Para 78][Para 79][Para 81][Para 85]. The effective date of the new contracts is given as 15 January 2016 in the body but the shift was described as beginning the same day and the thumbprinting as occurring on 8 January 2016, so the panel's account of when the change took effect is only partly recoverable [Para 88][Para 91][Para 197]. The operative paragraphs are the most degraded in the file: the names in them are cut and duplicated, the second list is printed with "ចំនួន ២១ នាក់" for eleven workers, and one name appears as "សូស្រី នាគ" against "សូស្រី នាត" earlier [Para 160][Para 265][Para 266]. The employer's name appears as "ស្រាបៀរ កម្ពុជា ខេម ប្រ៊ូវ", "ស្រាបៀវ កម្ពុជា ខេម ប្រ៊ីវ" and "ស្រា មៀរ កម្ពុជា" in successive passages [Para 5][Para 32][Para 73]. Staff delegate names are lost, and the union supporting the change is printed as "សហជីព បុគ្គលិក កម្មករ ជឿនលឿ", distinct from the registered "សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ" [Para 32][Para 56][Para 129]. Numbers are unreliable in the safety reasoning, where "២៦១", "២៤" and "១១" cover overlapping groups [Para 86][Para 89].
