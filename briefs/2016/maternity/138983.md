# Award No. 034/16 — Kuy Tov Outhong Factory (Arbitration Council, 3 March 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Yoo Soun Ty (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Ang Eng Thong (president) · Doc ID 138983

## 1. Facts
Kuy Tov Outhong Factory (រោងចក្រ គុយ ទាវ អ៊ូថុង) manufactures shoes for export and employs roughly 50 workers, over half of them women [Para 31][Para 32][Para 40]. A collective dispute was filed by the Federation of Trade Unions for Worker Development, which held transfers of rights from 18 workers, after conciliation at the Phnom Penh Department of Labour settled 13 of 14 points [Para 8][Para 9][Para 33]. The single unresolved point (ចំណុច មិនសះជា) concerns the timing of the 50% maternity wage for women who stop work to give birth [Para 10][Para 36]. The employer did not appear at the 19 February 2016 hearing despite written and telephone summons [Para 14][Para 45][Para 49].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the panel may proceed to hear the case in the employer's absence, the employer having been duly summoned and having given no valid reason? [Para 42][Para 43]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to pay 50% of wages and allowances for the three months preceding maternity leave in a single advance payment rather than in monthly instalments? [Para 36][Para 51]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer's standing practice is to pay the 50% maternity wage once a month, between the 8th and 10th of each month; because the women live far from the factory and come from Takeo, Kampong Speu, Kampong Chhnang and Kampong Cham, travel to collect it is difficult and exposes them to danger, and the employer requires each woman to collect it in person and bars her from delegating [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38][Para 39].
**Employer (respondent):** Absent from the hearing and filed no documents; by not appearing and not answering the claim before the panel, the employer abandoned its right to respond [Para 25][Para 26][Para 61].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer is ordered to pay 50% of wages and allowances (ប្រាក់ឈ្នួល និងប្រាក់បន្ទាប់បន្សំ) for the three-month period, at least one day before the day the pregnant worker stops work for maternity leave [Para 65][Para 66]. The award is non-binding (សេចក្តីបង្គាប់មិនជាប់កាតព្វកិច្ច) and becomes final if no party objects in writing within 08 days of notification [Para 22][Para 67].

On the procedural threshold, the panel applied Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004: where a party has been duly summoned and fails to appear without valid reason, the panel may continue and issue an award in that party's absence. Having verified the summons of 12 February 2016 and the two telephone attempts on 19 February, the panel sat in the employer's absence and decided on the workers' evidence and explanations alone [Para 42][Para 44][Para 48][Para 50].

On the substance, the panel read Article 182 of the Labour Law (right to 90 days' maternity leave) and Article 183(1) (right to half wages, including allowances, paid by the employer) and found that neither fixes the moment of payment [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53]. It then applied Article 115(3), which forbids paying wages on a day the worker is entitled to rest and requires payment one day earlier, holding that "wages" in that article covers the 50% maternity payment because Article 103 lists sums payable during maternity rest within the definition of wages [Para 54][Para 55][Para 57]. It followed its own reasoning in Award No. 154/11-B & N on point 4 and the earlier awards cited there (Nos 97/06, 91/07, 58/11, 81/11, 180/14, 277/15, 309/15) [Para 57][Para 58][Para 59]. The employer's monthly instalment practice was therefore held contrary to Article 115, and the panel accepted the workers' uncontested account of that practice [Para 62][Para 63].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The 50% maternity wage under Articles 182 and 183 is "wages" for the purposes of Article 115(3); an employer must therefore pay the whole 50% of wages and allowances for the three-month maternity period at least one day before the worker's leave begins, and a practice of releasing it in monthly instalments during the leave does not comply [Para 57][Para 64]. A party duly summoned who stays away without valid reason loses its right to answer and the panel may decide the case on the other side's evidence [Para 43][Para 61].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remarks that most of the workforce is female and that the women travel from distant provinces go to the practical hardship of the instalment practice, not to the legal test, which turns solely on Article 115(3) [Para 37][Para 40].
