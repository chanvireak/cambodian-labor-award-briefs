# Award No. 143/16 — IK Apparel Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 4 August 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborna for the employer party, Toun Si Phan for the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 139052 · dissenting opinion of Mar Samborna annexed

## 1. Facts
IK Apparel Co., Ltd. is a garment factory at Anlong Romeat, Kandal Stung, Kandal, with about 650 workers. [Para 44][Para 45] The Khmer Workers' Spirit Union Federation brought eleven demands; seven were settled at conciliation on 16 June 2016 and four were referred, one of which was withdrawn at the hearing. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 20] At the hearing three workers appeared and the federation held written authorisations from the workforce. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49] The disputed practices were the employer's refusal to let a worker who takes morning leave return for the afternoon shift, its deduction of the whole attendance bonus for leave taken with permission, its payment of USD 7 a month travel and accommodation allowance, and its payment of annual leave money at each expiry of a fixed-duration contract. [Para 51][Para 73][Para 79][Para 87]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must allow a worker who has taken half a day's leave in the morning to return to work in the afternoon? [Para 51][Para 92]
2. Whether the employer must cut the attendance bonus only in proportion to the days of leave the worker took with the employer's permission? [Para 73][Para 126]
3. Whether the employer must pay a travel and accommodation allowance of USD 12 a month? [Para 79][Para 164]
4. Whether the employer must pay compensation for unused annual leave at each expiry of a fixed-duration contract, while the employment relationship continues? [Para 87][Para 185]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** workers living near the factory need only half a day off for personal business and want to return for the afternoon to earn wages. [Para 58][Para 99] The attendance bonus is part of the wage, so it should not be cut for leave the employer has authorised. [Para 77] USD 7 does not cover the 10 to 12 dollars a month they spend on transport, and the sick-leave and annual-leave sums should be paid out at each contract expiry as other factories in the Vattanac industrial park do. [Para 81][Para 89]
**Employer (respondent):** workers are paid by output; each morning the team leader assigns the day's work and explains new styles, so a worker absent in the morning cannot work the afternoon without disrupting the line and costing the team its target bonus. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 63] The attendance bonus exists to encourage punctuality, and the travel allowance of USD 7 is exactly what Prakas No. 230 requires. [Para 79][Para 83][Para 164]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must allow the afternoon return and must cut the attendance bonus only pro rata to authorised leave; the transport allowance and the annual-leave payment claims were refused. [Para 205][Para 206][Para 207]

*Issue 1.* Article 2(2) gives the employer full power to organise work and the production line, including the procedure for granting leave, provided the exercise is lawful and reasonable. [Para 92][Para 95] Article 71(6) treats absence authorised by the employer as a suspension of the contract, and Article 170 requires annual leave to be agreed between the parties, so any leave rests on agreement. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 104] The panel found the employer's blanket refusal inconsistent with those provisions, since the worker's consent to a full day off was not truly given, and it also found — weighing the morning production meeting, the re-setting of machines and style changes — that the employer had not proved a real disruption on the facts. [Para 108][Para 110][Para 111][Para 123] The refusal was therefore neither lawful nor reasonable under Article 2, and the panel ordered the employer to permit the return to work. [Para 124][Para 125][Para 205]

*Issue 2.* Article 103 includes bonuses in wages, and Article 71(6) with Article 72(1) suspends the wage obligation during authorised absence. [Para 155][Para 157][Para 158] The attendance bonus of USD 10 under Ministry notification No. 230 is therefore a wage the employer cannot withhold for days worked, but it owes nothing for days not worked; the panel held the employer may deduct only the amount proportional to the days of authorised leave, not the whole bonus. [Para 162][Para 163][Para 205]

*Issue 3.* Point 1 of notification No. 230 fixes the travel and accommodation allowance at USD 7 a month, and the employer pays exactly that. [Para 164][Para 165][Para 166] No law, collective agreement, internal rule, contract, agreement or past practice requires USD 12, so the claim is a demand above the legal minimum, an interest dispute. [Para 167][Para 168] An award in an interest dispute becomes a collective agreement binding every worker, which under Article 96(2) of the Labour Law and Articles 54 and 72 of the Trade Union Law only a most-representative union may seek; the claimant was not shown to be one. [Para 172][Para 177][Para 180][Para 182] The panel therefore refused to consider the claim. [Para 183][Para 184][Para 206]

*Issue 4.* Article 166(1) gives paid annual leave and Article 167(2) requires compensation only where the contract ends before the leave is taken; Article 167(3) voids any agreement to waive the right. [Para 185][Para 187][Para 188][Para 191] The panel held the compensation is payable on termination, not while the worker is still employed, and that the employer's practice of paying it each March, though generous, is contrary to Article 167; instead of paying out, the employer should let leave accumulate, up to three years. [Para 189][Para 190][Para 193][Para 198][Para 200][Para 202] The claim was refused. [Para 203][Para 204][Para 207]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Leave under Articles 71(6) and 170 requires the agreement of both parties, so an employer may not refuse outright a request for half a day while insisting on a full day: a refusal that leaves the worker no genuine choice is neither lawful nor reasonable under Article 2, and the worker may return for the remaining shift. [Para 104][Para 108][Para 124] Where absence is authorised, the contract is suspended, so an attendance bonus may be reduced only in proportion to the days not worked, never forfeited in full. [Para 158][Para 162] Annual leave money falls due only on termination, and a demand for a benefit above the Prakas minimum is an interest dispute that staff delegates or a non-majority union cannot bring. [Para 189][Para 180]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the employer's early annual-leave payments are "contrary to Article 167" was not the basis of any order, and its suggestion that the parties show mutual accommodation — allowing the afternoon return where no style change is scheduled — is guidance, not a ruling. [Para 117][Para 202]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- [Para 20] records that the workers withdrew issue 2, yet the panel reasons on and orders under issue 2 at [Para 126]–[Para 163] and [Para 205]; the withdrawal covered only the demand to raise the bonus to USD 15, and the pro-rata point survived. Both are reported here.
- Headcounts conflict: "about 650 workers" [Para 45], authorisations from 830 workers [Para 47], and 83 claimants in the same paragraph's reasoning [Para 50] — a tenfold discrepancy in the last figure.
- [Para 98] prints the leave entitlement as "ចំនួន ១១៤ ថ្ងៃ" (114 days), where the context requires one day.
- The signature block [Para 209] omits the worker-party arbitrator, Toun Si Phan, and runs the employer arbitrator and the chair together; the annexed dissent is signed only by Mar Samborna [Para 224].
- The panel found the refusal "not in conformity" with Articles 71(6) and 170 [Para 108] yet also found the employer's concern for the line genuine [Para 109]; the order rests on the former, leaving the tension unresolved.
- [Para 172] and [Para 174] repeat Article 54 and the most-representative criteria twice over, with OCR doubling whole phrases; [Para 176] embeds non-Khmer debris inside the quotation of Article 72.
- The company name is spelt six ways across the award (អាយ ខេ អី ភែរឹល / អ៊ីភែរីល / អ៊ីភែវីល / អ៊ីភែវីល), and [Para 44] contains stray Latin text ("memon in 1800, under verement").
- Workers and union officers are not named here, per the anonymisation convention.
