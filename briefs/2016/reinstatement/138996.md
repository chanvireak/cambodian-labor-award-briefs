# Award No. 049/16 — Hong Sen Thek Stai (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 3 March 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-selected), An Non (worker-selected), Aing Eng Thong (president) · Doc ID 138996

## 1. Facts
The case concerned a garment or industrial enterprise of Hong Sen Thek Stai (Cambodia) at Ta Du village, Roka Krau commune, Don Keo town, Takeo, brought by the Cambodia Labour Solidarity Confederation on behalf of its local union [Para 1]. The Takeo provincial labour office received the union's complaint of 5 February 2016, conciliated four points on 16 February 2016, and referred the one point left unresolved to the Arbitration Council on 23 February 2016 [Para 9][Para 10][Para 11]. The union's representatives were recorded as absent; its president told the secretariat by telephone that he had forgotten the hearing date [Para 1][Para 17][Para 43].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must take back Mr C (staff number S 3431) and pay him back wages from the date of dismissal to the date of return, the company having given him three days' notice before his contract expired? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company must grant the demands numbered 02 and 04, which it resists on the ground that the workers produced no clear documents? [Para 3]

Only one of these points was referred as unresolved [Para 11]. The panel therefore decided a threshold question before any merits issue: whether it should continue the arbitration at all in the absence of the complainant [Para 25].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the union filed on 5 February 2016 seeking improved conditions and the reinstatement of Mr C with back pay; at the hearing it advanced nothing, no representative appearing and no documents being filed [Para 9][Para 1][Para 20].
**Employer (respondent):** the company refused to reinstate Mr C or to pay back wages, stating that it had notified him three days before his contract reached its term; it also refused demands 02 and 04 for want of clear supporting documents from the workers [Para 2][Para 3]. Its administration head attended the hearing [Para 1][Para 14].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** withdrawn — the panel treated the complaint as abandoned and terminated the arbitration, making no order on the merits and awarding nothing [Para 56][Para 60][Para 61][Para 62].

The panel applied Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council, which allows it either to continue in a party's absence or to close the file by award, and Rule 4.7 of the Council's procedural rules, which requires that the absent party have been properly notified of date, time and place [Para 25][Para 26][Para 27]. Earlier awards had distilled three conditions: proper invitation, non-appearance, and no valid reason for it [Para 28][Para 29][Para 30].

On condition one, the secretariat issued invitation No. 260 Kb/Ak/Vk/LkA on 24 February 2016 to the confederation president, giving the date of 1 March 2016 at 14:00 and the venue, and telephoned the workers' side the same day to confirm [Para 31][Para 32]. On condition two, the panel read "appearing before the panel" as requiring both attendance at the hearing and participation through every stage — introduction and conflict-of-interest explanation, explanation of procedure and identification of the issues, re-conciliation if the parties agreed, and the hearing to the end — and found neither element satisfied [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39][Para 40]. On condition three, the only explanation offered was that the president had forgotten the date, and the panel waited until 15:00 before proceeding; it held no valid reason shown [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45][Para 46].

Because a complainant bears the duty to appear, state its case orally and supply the documents needed for the panel to decide, and the union did none of these, the panel treated the claim as abandoned and closed the file [Para 50][Para 51][Para 52][Para 54][Para 55][Para 57][Para 58][Para 59].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a complainant has been properly notified of the hearing and neither appears nor participates, and offers no valid excuse, the panel is entitled to treat the claim as abandoned and terminate the arbitration by award, rather than deciding the merits in the complainant's absence and on the respondent's case alone.

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's four-stage description of an arbitration hearing — introduction, explanation of procedure and issues, optional re-conciliation, and the hearing proper — is explanatory rather than necessary to the outcome, as is its observation that the respondent's evidential burden arises only after the complainant has discharged its own [Para 38][Para 53].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The paragraph constituting the panel prints the date as "24 February 2023 2016", carrying both years [Para 12]. The conciliation report is numbered "209 កបវ/ឥក" at first mention and "209 កបវ/តក" at the others, and is dated 18 February 2016 although conciliation is recorded as occurring on 16 February [Para 6][Para 11][Para 20]. The panel cites "Article 212" of Prakas 099 where the provision applied is Article 21, and dates Rule 4.7 to "26th 21 April 2004" [Para 25][Para 26]. The record states that four points were conciliated and one remained, yet two demands are described at the head of the file, and demands 02 and 04 are referred to only by number, their content never set out — so the substance of issue 2 cannot be recovered from this award [Para 10][Para 2][Para 3]. Two passages, the telephone contacts and the panel's findings on them, are duplicated almost word for word [Para 33][Para 42]. The dispositive paragraphs are themselves corrupted, the second reading "បញ្ឈប់ ជា ណា អាជ្ញារ" [Para 62], and one arbitrator's signature line is reduced to stray characters [Para 63].
