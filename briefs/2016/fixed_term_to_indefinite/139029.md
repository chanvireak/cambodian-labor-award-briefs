# Award No. 108/16 — FCF Inspection (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 3 June 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (employer-appointed Ing Sothy — dissenting on Issues 1 and 3; worker-appointed Tuon Siphann; chair Pen Bunchhea) · non-binding award · Doc ID 139029

## 1. Facts
FCF Inspection (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. ("the employer") provides quality-check services and employs about 350 workers; the C.CAWDU local union was registered on 9 September 2015 with leader recognition from MoLVT on 27 October 2015. [Para 24][Para 25] Its president, Ms K (ID 05), began work on 1 November 2014 under an unwritten contract, was moved onto three-month contracts from mid-2015, and was notified of termination on 22 October 2015, leaving on 31 October 2015. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 41][Para 52] Six demands were conciliated, three settled there and a fourth at the hearing, leaving the reinstatement claim and the contract-conversion claim. [Para 16][Para 18][Para 19]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate Ms K, president of the C.CAWDU local union, and pay her back wages from termination to reinstatement? [Para 35][Para 81]
2. Whether the employer must convert to undetermined-duration contracts (UDC) the 69 workers whose seniority exceeds two years? [Para 59][Para 81]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Ms K was a UDC worker because she was hired without a written contract; the three-month contracts were signed unwillingly and without UDC termination compensation, so they are contrary to Article 13; as a union leader she also needed Labour Inspector authorisation to be dismissed. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 46][Para 62][Para 106]
**Employer (respondent):** the case is individual, not collective, because the termination had no effect on the production chain or social peace; it is a non-renewal of a three-month contract, not a dismissal, justified by poor performance and a shortage of work; the workers signed the fixed-duration contracts (FDC) freely, and each renewal was under two years with 5% severance paid at the end. [Para 29][Para 31][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49][Para 65][Para 66]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to reinstate Ms K with back pay from the date of termination to the date of reinstatement, and to implement and apply UDC for the 69 workers. The award is non-binding and becomes binding eight days after notification unless a party lodges written opposition. [Para 124][Para 136][Para 137]

**Jurisdiction.** Clause 32 of Prakas No. 099 confines the panel to collective disputes, and the panel normally follows the Labour Inspector's classification absent explicit reason to object. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 71] Applying the three Article 302 conditions, it found the parties and subject conditions met, and held the consequence condition met too: the local union holds 300 of the 350 workers, so it can take action that could disrupt production in future. [Para 76][Para 81][Para 82][Para 88][Para 90] It expressly declined to follow Award 26/16 between the same parties, which had found the opposite on the same facts. [Para 86]

**Issue 1.** Article 67(7) requires an FDC to be in writing, failing which the contract is of undetermined duration, so Ms K's original engagement was a UDC. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 95] A UDC may be converted to an FDC only with genuine and free consent: the employer must state its intention to end the UDC and offer the FDC, and give the worker time — at least the seven days of Article 75 — to decide. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 107] The panel found no evidence of such notification, no clause in the three-month contract recording agreement to terminate the UDC, and no UDC termination compensation paid before the conversion. [Para 108][Para 109][Para 110][Para 111] The conversion was therefore against the Labour Law and the Council's jurisprudence, and her contract remained a UDC; the case is consequently a dismissal, not a non-renewal. [Para 113][Para 114][Para 115][Para 117] Article 74 also failed for want of prior notice and of any valid reason. [Para 112] As a recognised local union leader she enjoyed the Article 293 protection extended by Clause 4 of Prakas No. 305, so dismissal required Labour Inspector authorisation, which was never sought. [Para 118][Para 120] Under Clause 34 of Prakas No. 099 the panel ordered reinstatement and back pay. [Para 123][Para 124]

**Issue 3.** The contracts of the 69 workers had the same history: unwritten commencement, three-month contracts introduced in 2015, severance paid at each contract end, continuous service over two years. [Para 126][Para 127][Para 128][Para 129] No evidence showed the employer stated an intention to convert, or that the workers agreed to end their UDCs, and no UDC termination compensation was paid. [Para 129][Para 130][Para 131][Para 132] The panel therefore held the 69 workers to be on UDC and ordered the employer to apply UDC to them. [Para 134][Para 136]

**Dissent (Ing Sothy).** He agreed that Ms K must be reinstated but objected to back pay: during the grievance period she was free to work elsewhere and no law fixes her loss at the daily wage, so an award of wages for that period exceeds a fair discretionary remedy. [Para 138][Para 142][Para 144][Para 146] On Issue 3 he would not order conversion, because the employer paid the 5% severance at every renewal and no single renewal exceeded two years, so Article 73 was satisfied. [Para 147][Para 157][Para 158]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A conversion from a UDC to a fixed-duration contract is ineffective unless the employer has notified its intention to end the UDC, allowed the worker time to decide, and paid the UDC termination compensation; absent those steps the contract stays a UDC, and dismissing a protected local union leader under it without Labour Inspector authorisation requires reinstatement and back pay. [Para 104][Para 110][Para 114][Para 118][Para 124]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that a union with a large share of the workforce can satisfy the Article 302 consequence condition on the strength of its future capacity to disrupt production, even where no disruption has yet occurred. [Para 88][Para 90]
