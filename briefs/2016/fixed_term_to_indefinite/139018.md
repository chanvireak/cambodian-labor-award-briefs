# Award No. 088/16 — M V Garment International Co., Ltd (Branch 1) (Arbitration Council, 6 May 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambor Na appointed by the employer, An Virak appointed by the workers, Sok Mathoeung chair) · non-binding award chosen by the parties · Doc ID 139018

## 1. Facts
M V Garment International Co., Ltd (Branch 1) cut and sewed clothing at Prek Ta Long, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, with about 204 workers. [Para 42] The employer suspended the workers' contracts in stretches across late 2015 and early 2016 and, on 17 July 2015, announced a 500 riel daily meal subsidy for workers who came to work. [Para 69][Para 95] 86 workers transferred their rights to the Democratic Union of Cambodian Garment Workers, which brought two unresolved claims after conciliation failed on 25 March 2016. [Para 49][Para 13][Para 9]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the workers the 500 riel daily meal allowance for 01 to 30 October 2015 and 01 January to 07 February 2016, periods when it had no work for them but held no authorisation from the labour inspector? [Para 82]
2. Whether the employer must pay 100% wages, a 500 riel meal allowance, an attendance bonus of 10 USD per month, a travel allowance of 07 USD and a seniority bonus covering the suspension of contracts from 16 February to 31 March 2016? [Para 113]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the 500 riel meal payment is a benefit more favourable than the law and must continue under Article 13 of the Labour Law; the October 2015 and January 2016 suspensions were never authorised by the labour inspector, and contractual suspension is the employer's fault, not the workers'. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 58] "Prior notice" in the company announcement must mean notice to the labour inspector, not to the workers. [Para 67]
**Employer (respondent):** the announcement of 17 July 2015 grants the subsidy only to workers who come to work and expressly excludes those absent on sick, maternity, personal or annual leave or given prior notice of a stoppage; no law, agreement or Prakas obliges it to pay meals. [Para 60][Para 65][Para 69][Para 70] The 08 February to 31 March 2016 suspension was inspected and authorised and did not exceed two months, and 100% wages were already paid for 01 January to 07 February 2016. [Para 77][Para 78][Para 129]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected both claims: the meal allowance of 500 riel per day for the October 2015 and January–February 2016 stoppages, and the 100% wages, 500 riel meal allowance, 10 USD attendance bonus, 07 USD travel allowance and seniority bonus for the February–March 2016 suspension. [Para 138][Para 139]

On issue 1 the panel started from Article 2(2) of the Labour Law: the employer's right to manage and direct the enterprise is recognised, but only where exercised lawfully and reasonably. [Para 83][Para 86][Para 90] It read the announcement of 17 July 2015 as a voluntary benefit conditioned on attendance, and held that the employer had in fact given prior notice to the workers through their team leaders on each occasion when work was unavailable. [Para 95][Para 96] The workers' contrary reading — that the notice contemplated was notice to the labour inspector — was rejected as inconsistent with the text. [Para 100] The panel therefore found the employer had applied its own announcement and had not breached Article 13. [Para 101][Para 108][Para 106] It followed its earlier reasoning in Awards 62/06, 10806, 33/07, 106/07, 8808 and 08/09. [Para 87] It distinguished Award 271/15 (FCF Ink Steksen (Cambodia) Co Ltd), where the employer had imposed no conditions on the meal payment at all, so the situations were not alike. [Para 109][Para 110]

On issue 2 the panel applied Article 71 of the Labour Law, which permits suspension of enterprise activity for no more than two months and only under the supervision of the labour inspector. [Para 113][Para 114] It found that the suspensions of 01 to 30 October 2015 and 01 January to 07 February 2016 had no inspector authorisation and were procedurally unlawful, so wages and benefits were owed for those periods under Article 72. [Para 124][Para 125][Para 126] The claim as pleaded, however, covered 16 February to 31 March 2016; both parties agreed that suspension ran from 08 February to 31 March 2016 and was authorised by the labour inspector, and it did not exceed two months. [Para 71][Para 134][Para 135] For that authorised period the employer owed nothing. [Para 136] The workers had also conceded that 100% wages were paid for 01 January to 07 February 2016. [Para 130]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer grants a meal subsidy by unilateral announcement made conditional on attendance and on prior notice of any stoppage, withholding it from workers who received that notice is a lawful and reasonable exercise of the management right under Article 2(2) and does not offend Article 13; the notice condition is satisfied by notice given to the workers, not to the labour inspector. Conversely, a contract suspension that lacks labour inspector authorisation, or that exceeds two months, obliges the employer to pay 100% wages and benefits for the unauthorised period under Articles 71 and 72, while a suspension of two months or less that the inspector has authorised carries no such obligation. [Para 86][Para 96][Para 106][Para 126][Para 136]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that a worker who disagrees with an administrative authorisation letter should lodge a complaint with the authority that issued it, rather than with the panel, is a general observation on remedies and was not needed for the dismissal. [Para 131]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The panel is said to have been constituted on "06 April 2019 2016" [Para 15], two years printed in one date.
- The conciliation report states the claim period as 01 October 2015 to 15 February 2016 [Para 3], but the reasons address 01 to 30 October 2015 and 01 January to 07 February 2016 [Para 82]; the second claim is pleaded as 16 February to 31 March 2016 [Para 5][Para 71] while both parties are recorded as agreeing on 08 February to 31 March 2016 [Para 71][Para 134], and the reasons once compute 01 January to 31 March 2016 as "03 months" [Para 74].
- The meal allowance appears as 500 riel throughout, as "50 riel" in the operative order [Para 138], and as "4500 riel" in the reasoning [Para 107].
- The employer is printed variously as អឹម វ៉េ, អី ម វ៉េ, អឹម វេំ, អឹម វើ and "M&V" [Para 1][Para 34][Para 46]; the union's name likewise varies.
- The workers'-side arbitrator is legible as An Virak in the header [Para 1] but reduced to "unnumne it Ammlicaks" in the signature block [Para 141].
- Stray OCR debris interrupts the text at the start of paragraphs ("To និយោជក" [Para 52], "The Stot" [Para 111], "Ol និយោជក" [Para 64]).
