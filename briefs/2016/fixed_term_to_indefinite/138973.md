# Award No. 018/16 — Khem Paper In Dash Stri Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council of Cambodia, 15 February 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ang Eng Tong presiding; Ly Tai Seng appointed by the employer, An Non appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 138973

## 1. Facts
Khem Paper In Dash Stri Co., Ltd, a paper manufacturer at Street 371, Sangkat Stung Mean Chey, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, stopped giving work in January 2014 and thereafter paid only 50% of each worker's monthly wage, released by telephone about 20 minutes beforehand within a window of 1 hour 30 minutes. [Para 39][Para 51][Para 85]
Thirteen workers with 5 to 15 years' service and wages of USD 150 to 300 a month, represented by the Cambodia Labour Union (សហភាព ការងារ កម្ពុជា), had never been given a written contract; they sought termination and the statutory termination payments. [Para 49][Para 50]
Conciliation report No. 161 of 19 January 2016 referred two unsettled issues; at the hearing the workers withdrew issue 1, leaving issue 2 alone for decision. [Para 12][Para 25][Para 26] The award is non-binding. [Para 27]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must restore full-time work to all the workers as before? — withdrawn by the workers at the hearing, so not decided. [Para 3][Para 25]
2. Whether, the employer not having restored that work, it must pay the termination compensation claimed — moral damages, dismissal damages, unused annual leave, holiday pay, final wages, weekly-rest-day pay, notice pay and overtime? [Para 4][Para 47]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** they had no written contract from the day they were hired, so their contracts were of indefinite duration; the employer suspended work for two years without any stated reason or date of resumption, paying 50% wages and letting a 1 hour 30 minute collection window leave some with nothing at all; in 2013 they worked normally, including public holidays and Sundays, without statutory holiday pay or overtime, and received only 9 days of holiday a year (Khmer New Year, Pchum Ben, Water Festival). [Para 48][Para 49][Para 58][Para 61]
**Employer (respondent):** absent. It received the invitation of 27 January 2016, asked by telephone for a postponement but never filed a written request in Khmer as required, submitted no documents, and was treated as having waived its right to reply. [Para 69][Para 76][Para 86]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — dismissal damages under Article 89, moral damages under Article 91, unused annual leave under Articles 166–167, holiday pay for 2013 and Sunday overtime for 2013, each computed on the actual seniority of every worker; the claim for notice pay was dismissed. No riel or USD sum is printed: the panel refused the workers' compensation table as a mere draft. [Para 164][Para 167][Para 109]
On the employer's absence the panel applied Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, which allows it to continue and decide once a party has been properly summoned and fails to appear without good reason, following a list of earlier awards including Nos. 53/04, 63/04, 148/07, 98/09, 173/11, 142/12, 109/13, 163/14, 240/15 and 333/15. [Para 65][Para 67][Para 71]
On the contract the panel held that Article 67 of the Labour Law makes a fixed-duration contract written or it is not fixed-duration at all; the workers had none in writing, so all thirteen held indefinite-duration contracts and could seek termination by their own will. [Para 73][Para 78][Para 79]
On liability the panel applied Article 90: where the employer's own conduct drives the worker to end the contract, the worker still receives dismissal damages and moral damages. It followed Award No. 84/11 (Soupertech) and No. 286/15 (KTC Cabull), finding that two years of open-ended suspension, half wages and the 1 hour 30 minute pay window were the conduct that provoked the terminations. [Para 88][Para 89][Para 96]
On quantum, Article 89 was applied by seniority, with the average of the last 12 months divided by 12 and then by 26 as the daily base; the panel expressly declined to adopt the workers' table because a draft table could not satisfy it that the Article 89 calculation was correct. [Para 100][Para 109][Para 111] Moral damages under Article 91 were fixed at an amount equal to the dismissal damages. [Para 113][Para 116]
Annual leave was granted under Articles 166 and 167 for leave unused at termination. [Para 122][Para 129] Notice pay under Articles 75 and 77 was refused, because those provisions reach only a worker dismissed by the employer without notice, not a worker who terminates under Article 90 — the same reading as Awards 84/11 and 286/15. [Para 133][Para 135]
On holiday pay and Sunday overtime the panel applied the three-year limitation in Article 120 and found a right to claim for the last three years. [Para 139][Para 153] It then refused the two most recent years, from January 2014 to the hearing, because no work was given in that period and the workers had therefore already rested on those days. [Para 145][Para 160] For 2013 it ordered payment for the public holidays and Sundays actually worked, at the 100% rate of Article 139 (new) for weekly rest days, excluding the Khmer New Year, Pchum Ben and Water Festival days on which no one worked. [Para 149][Para 163]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that leaves its workers in suspension with no fixed resumption date, pays half wages and makes collection of even that practically impossible has by its own conduct driven the termination, so workers who then end the contract are entitled to dismissal damages and moral damages under Article 90 — but not to notice pay under Articles 75 and 77, which is confined to terminations the employer itself effects. [Para 89][Para 96][Para 135]
The absence of a written fixed-duration contract makes the employment relationship one of indefinite duration under Article 67, whatever the parties may have intended, and an indefinite-duration contract may be ended at the will of one party. [Para 73][Para 79]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that a 1 hour 30 minute pay window places a burden on workers living far away, that the employer's promise of an early resumption had not materialised over two years, and that it could not assess whether the company could return to normal operation because the employer was absent. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 95]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The decision prints no amounts at all: every compensation head is stated as "according to actual seniority" with no figure, because the panel rejected the workers' draft table, so no sum can be reported. [Para 109][Para 111][Para 164]
The award states 4 disputed points at para 25 while the conciliation report and paras 12 and 26 give 2, of which one was withdrawn — the panel's own numbering is followed above. [Para 25][Para 26]
Worker counts are inconsistent: 15 workers transferred their rights to the union at para 41 but the union represents 13, para 117 reads "កម្មករ និយោជិត ២០ ចំនួន ១៣ នាក់" and para 163 reads "ដល់ ១០ កម្មករ ... ១៣ នាក់". The employer's name is printed in at least four forms (ខេម ផេភើរ អ៊ិន ដាស ស្ទ្រី, ខេម ផេបផើ អិន ដាស្ទ្រី, ខេមរៈ) and its own evidence table is captioned "Cam Paper" and once "Gam Paper"; the company is named here as on the face of the award. Where the award first cites the case in English "Cam Paper" appears to be a trading name, not the registered one. [Para 1][Para 41][Para 109][Para 117][Para 163]
Amounts of time and money that do survive are garbled: Article 120's limitation period is printed as "បី ឆ្នាំ ២០១" at para 137 and "បី ឆ្នាំ ២០១៥" at para 151, where the law says three years. Stray "2017" tokens interrupt paras 6, 33, 142 and 171. The signing block names Ly Tai Seng but the worker-appointed arbitrator's and the presiding arbitrator's signatures are reduced to unreadable strings, so only the three names given in the composition at paras 1–2 are reported. Individual workers and the union representative are reduced to initials and are not reconstructed. [Para 6][Para 137][Para 151][Para 171]
