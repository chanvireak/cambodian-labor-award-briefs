# Award No. 236/16 — I-Dal Pacific (Cambodia) Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 16 December 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy appointed by the employer, An Non appointed by the workers, Kong Phalha chair) · Doc ID 139383

## 1. Facts
I-Dal Pacific (Cambodia) Ltd. operates at Trapeang Chheu Neang village, Peuk commune, Ang Snuol district, Kandal. [Para 1] Its workers filed a complaint of 24 November 2016 raising eleven demands, the first being that the director stop terminating contracts without cause. [Para 1][Para 18] Conciliation failed on 26 November 2016 because the employer refused to let a union with no members at the plant, and the federation, take part; eleven points remained unsettled and were sent to the Council. [Para 19][Para 20] After referral the workers went on strike; the panel directed at the hearing that the strike stop and work resume in shifts, and was later told that the workers had returned. [Para 21][Para 25][Para 26]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the director must refrain from terminating labour contracts without cause? [Para 1]
2. Whether a change of shareholding obliges the owner to settle with the workers? [Para 2]
3. Whether the administration must not warn workers without reason? [Para 3]
4. Whether the two administration staff should be dismissed? [Para 4]
5. Whether maternity leave must be 90 days on 50% wages plus allowances, calculated on the last salary, and whether the same applies to women with over two years' service? [Para 5]
6. Whether wages must be maintained during sick leave certified by a hospital? [Para 6]
7. Whether a card may be required to use the toilet? [Para 7]
8. Whether fixed-duration contracts must be for two years? [Para 8]
9. Whether the annual wage increment must be folded into the base salary? [Para 9]
10. Whether a returning mother is entitled to a monthly unpaid health check and one hour off for nursing? [Para 10]
11. Whether eight named workers must be reinstated in their former posts? [Para 11]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** they pursued the eleven demands through the Ministry and the Council; they did not appear at the hearing, telephoning instead to say the rain prevented travel, and asking for an adjournment. [Para 30][Para 50][Para 58]
**Employer (respondent):** it attended the pre-hearing meeting through its administration head and staff, filed photographs and a letter of 01 December 2016 asking the Council to order the workers back to work, and stayed away from the hearing once it learned the workers had sought an adjournment. [Para 1][Para 31][Para 32][Para 33]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** withdrawn — the panel closed the arbitration, treating the claimants as having abandoned their complaint, and made no order on any of the eleven issues. [Para 70][Para 71][Para 73]

The panel treated as the sole question whether it should continue at all in the absence of the claimant workers. [Para 43] Article 21 of Prakas 099 and Rule 4.7 of the procedural rules allow it either to proceed or to close the file where a party, duly summoned, fails to appear without valid reason, and earlier awards read that power as subject to three cumulative conditions: proper invitation, non-appearance, and no valid excuse. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 46]

Condition one was met: invitation 1352 of 09 December 2016 set out the date, time and place, and the secretariat also telephoned. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 52] Condition two was met: "appearing" under Article 21 means both attending and taking part through all four stages of the hearing — introduction, explanation of procedure and issues, conciliation, and argument — and the claimants did none of these. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 55][Para 57] Condition three was met: rain was not a valid reason, since it is no obstacle to travel, and a party genuinely seeking an adjournment would have contacted the secretariat beforehand rather than waiting to be telephoned. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60][Para 62]

Having found all three conditions satisfied, the panel held that a claimant bears the burden of presenting its allegations and evidence in person, and that a claimant who does neither cannot require the panel to continue; it accordingly recorded the complaint as abandoned and ended the proceedings. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 67][Para 69][Para 70] It also recorded that no findings of fact could be made on the eleven issues for that reason. [Para 42]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Article 21 of Prakas 099 permits the panel to close a file where three cumulative conditions are shown: the party was properly invited with the date, time and place; it did not appear and did not take part through the stages of the hearing; and it gave no valid reason. Weather is not a valid reason, and a party that wants an adjournment must ask for it before the hearing rather than when telephoned on the day. A claimant that satisfies these conditions is treated as having abandoned its complaint, and the panel will not decide the merits in its absence. [Para 44][Para 46][Para 53][Para 59][Para 70]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's review of earlier awards on waiver of appearance and its four-stage description of an arbitration hearing are explanatory, not necessary to the closure. [Para 46][Para 54] The panel declined to rule on the strike, which had already ended. [Para 25][Para 26]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The style of cause is heavily garbled: the list of the workers' fifteen representatives is printed inside the employer's entry, the Council's street address and e-mail are spliced into the party details, and OCR noise ("=>00> ==+=-") sits in the national heading. [Para 1]
- The panel is said to have been constituted on "30 November 2019 2016" [Para 21].
- The invitation is described as fixing the hearing for "12 November 2016" [Para 48], although the hearing date is 12 December 2016 and the pre-hearing meeting 06 December 2016 [Para 16][Para 17].
- The award records that neither party attended the hearing [Para 29], yet the reasoning treats only the workers as absent and records that the employer telephoned to explain its non-attendance [Para 31]; the employer had also appeared at the pre-hearing meeting [Para 1].
- Paragraph 32 prints the evidence preamble twice, paragraph 61 repeats a finding mid-sentence, and paragraph 42 states that no facts could be found, which is inconsistent with the detailed procedural findings that follow [Para 32][Para 42][Para 61].
- Issue 5 mixes two questions — the 90-day/50% maternity entitlement and a separate claim for women with over two years' service — inside a single numbered demand; it is reproduced as printed. [Para 5]
