# Award No. 338/15 — Foukhes Met (Cambodia) Leader Good Manufacture Charring Limited (Arbitration Council, 8 February 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Virak, worker-appointed; Kong Phalha, president) · Doc ID 139498

## 1. Facts

The Federation of Trade Unions of Workers' Hearts of Cambodia (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ទឹកចិត្ត កម្មករ កម្ពុជា) filed 23 demands for improved conditions at the company's factory in Ang Snuol district, Kandal province, on 28 December 2015; conciliation by the provincial labour department failed on all 23 points, which were sent to the Secretariat on 31 December 2015. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 31] The first hearing was set for 14 January 2016, but the panel found the workers were on strike and adjourned so they could return to work. [Para 33][Para 34] A second hearing was fixed for 02 February 2016; the employer attended, the worker party did not. [Para 35][Para 36]

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. Whether the panel must continue the arbitration and decide the 23 demands, or may terminate the proceedings by award, where the claimant party failed to appear at the hearing? [Para 52]
2. Whether the three cumulative conditions in Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council — proper summons, non-appearance, absence of a valid excuse — were satisfied, and whether non-appearance amounts to abandonment of the claims? [Para 60][Para 65][Para 86]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** The Federation sought 23 changes, among them an end to dismissal without valid reason, proper medical checks, maternity pay at 50 per cent of the 12-month average wage for three months, no discrimination against union leaders and members, USD 15 monthly housing or transport allowance, free or 5,000-riel midday meals, soap and water in the toilets, quadruple-plus wages for Sunday and holiday overtime, technical-skill allowances of USD 10 to USD 20, reinstatement of seven named union leaders and activists, and voluntary-only overtime. [Para 3][Para 5][Para 8][Para 13][Para 18][Para 20] The Federation did not appear on 02 February 2016 and, when telephoned, said its officer had forgotten the hearing date and asked for an adjournment. [Para 74][Para 75]

**Employer (respondent):** The employer attended both hearings, represented by its administration chief Mr C (Chea Vuthy Sok Theng), its human-resources officer and a GMAC representative, and filed a power of attorney, its statutes, its commercial registration and a summary of the dispute, submitting to the panel's process. [Para 1][Para 37][Para 39][Para 41]

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** withdrawn — the panel terminated the arbitration proceedings in case No. 338/15 without ruling on any of the 23 demands, holding that the claimant had abandoned its claims and rights. [Para 86][Para 88][Para 89]

The panel began from Article 18 of Prakas No. 099, which requires the panel to summon the parties to present their claims in person and to file the necessary documents, and from Articles 20 and 21 and Rule 4.7 of the Council's procedural rules, which allow the panel either to proceed in a party's absence or to close the case by award, provided the party was properly notified of the date, time and place. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54][Para 55][Para 56] It distilled three cumulative conditions from that framework and from its earlier awards: proper summons, failure to appear, and no valid reason for the failure. [Para 60][Para 64]

On condition one, the Secretariat had issued invitation No. 093 dated 21 January 2016 to the Federation president, stating the hearing date, venue and contact telephone and fax numbers, and had telephoned the Federation at 09:16 on 21 January 2016 to confirm the 02 February 2016 hearing; the panel found proper notification. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67] On condition two, it read "appearing before the panel" in Article 21 as requiring both presence at the hearing and participation throughout its four stages — introduction and conflict check, explanation of procedure and issues, optional re-conciliation, and the merits hearing; the workers were absent and had appointed no authorised representative under Article 19. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 71][Para 72] On condition three, the panel held that forgetting the hearing date is not a valid reason capable of justifying an adjournment. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 77]

Having found all three conditions met, the panel reasoned that a claimant bears the burden of presenting argument and evidence in person to establish its claim, and that a respondent's burden arises only once the claimant has done so; a claimant who does not appear therefore abandons its claims and rights, and the panel need not continue. [Para 81][Para 82][Para 84][Para 85][Para 86] It accordingly closed the file, and recorded that the award was a non-binding one becoming final eight days after notification absent written objection to the Minister of Labour through the Secretariat. [Para 87][Para 89]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

A claimant union that is properly summoned, fails to appear at the arbitration hearing, and offers no valid excuse — forgetting the date is not one — is treated as having abandoned its claims, and the panel will close the case by award without examining the merits, so that the 23 underlying demands are left undetermined rather than refused. [Para 77][Para 86]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the hearing has four distinct stages and that a party must be present through all of them, not merely at the opening, and that a respondent's evidential burden only crystallises after the claimant has discharged its own. [Para 70][Para 84]
