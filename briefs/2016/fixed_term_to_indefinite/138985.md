# Award No. 032/16 — Eternity Global Sporting (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 10 March 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed, dissenting in part), Sin Kim Sean (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (president) · Doc ID 138985

## 1. Facts
Eternity Global Sporting (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (អ៉ីធើនិធី ក្លូប៊ល ស្ព័ត ធីង (ខេមបូឌា) ខូអិលធីឌី) makes balls and gloves and employed about 969 workers [Para 96][Para 97]. A union election was held on 01 January 2016, in which two of the four claimants were elected as president and secretary and two stood but were not elected [Para 116][Para 119][Para 120]. Two of the 02 unresolved points went to the panel: the refusal to admit the four to work from 11 January 2016, and the employer's practice of issuing successive three-month fixed-duration contracts [Para 19][Para 113][Para 134]. The employer notified a "suspension" of the four on 11 January 2016 pending the labour inspector's decision on its request to dismiss them for gross misconduct [Para 126][Para 127].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must stop suspending the four workers, take them back to work and pay their wages from the date of suspension until they return? [Para 148][Para 221]
2. Whether the employer must convert fixed-duration contracts into indefinite-duration contracts (កិច្ចសន្យាមានថិរវេលាមិនកំណត់) for workers whose successive contracts total more than 02 years? [Para 222][Para 227]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer cut the four off after the election results were announced, in plain anti-union discrimination, and had asked five workers to resign from the union; they seek reinstatement with back wages, and conversion of rolling three-month contracts to indefinite ones for stability [Para 122][Para 139].
**Employer (respondent):** The four were not dismissed but suspended pending the labour inspector's ruling, after a request filed on 09 January 2016 for authorisation to dismiss them for gross misconduct under Article 83; most workers prefer fixed-duration contracts because of the 5% end-of-contract payment, and conversion is not required [Para 123][Para 142][Para 144][Para 197].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted on both points — the employer must stop the suspension, take the four back and pay their wages from the date of suspension until their return, and must convert fixed-duration contracts to indefinite ones for workers whose total service exceeds 02 years [Para 228][Para 229]. The award is non-binding and becomes final after 08 days without objection [Para 230]. Arbitrator Ing Sothy appended a dissenting opinion agreeing on reinstatement but opposing the back pay and the conversion order [Para 233][Para 235][Para 243].

On point 1, the panel applied Article 293 and Article 3(3) of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001: every candidate in a union leadership election enjoys the same protection against dismissal as a staff delegate, for 45 days before the election and 45 days after it if not elected [Para 163][Para 166][Para 167]. Following Award No. 40/10 and No. 154/13, it held that elected leaders are protected from the day of the election until the union is registered [Para 175][Para 176]. It found all three conditions met — the four were protected persons, the suspension fell 10 days after the election and so inside the protected window, and the union had notified the employer of the candidates by email on 30 December 2015 and of the results on 04 and 05 January 2016 [Para 185][Para 190][Para 198][Para 200]. Because the employer produced no evidence of its 09 January request to the labour inspector and no inspector's record existed, and because Article 293(4) deems a request rejected where no decision issues within one month, the panel held that the suspension was void under Article 295 and ordered reinstatement with back pay [Para 211][Para 213][Para 218][Para 221].

On point 2, the panel read Article 67(2) with Article 73(5): successive fixed-duration contracts totalling more than 02 years become indefinite-duration contracts, and Article 73(5) refers to the aggregate maximum, not to each renewal [Para 148][Para 151][Para 155]. It treated ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982 as context confirming that fixed-term contracts should not be used for long-term work [Para 156][Para 158]. Applying this, three of the four workers had totals over 02 years and their contracts had become indefinite; the fourth, whose total had not yet exceeded 02 years, remained on a fixed-duration contract [Para 160][Para 161][Para 162]. The general order nonetheless covers all workers whose aggregate service exceeds 02 years [Para 227][Para 229].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A candidate or elected leader in a union election is a protected person: suspending or dismissing them inside the 45-day window around the poll is void unless the labour inspector has authorised it, and where the employer cannot prove it applied and no decision issued within one month, the request is deemed refused, the suspension is void and wages run from the date of suspension to the date of return [Para 167][Para 218]. Successive fixed-duration contracts whose combined duration exceeds 02 years are converted by operation of law into indefinite-duration contracts [Para 159][Para 227].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remarks that most workers prefer fixed-term contracts for the 5% end-of-contract payment, and its observation that the employer is the party who loses by using fixed-term contracts for long-term work, explain the parties' incentives but do not qualify the statutory conversion rule [Para 144][Para 257].
