# Award No. 286/15 — K.T.C. Cable Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 6 January 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Run Saray (president), Chiv Phirom (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed) · Doc ID 139463

## 1. Facts
K.T.C. Cable Co., Ltd (KTCable) makes electrical cable and had about 21 employees by the hearing [Para 106][Para 107]. Production stopped from July 2015 and the company began shifting its administrative office to a new site; on 16 and 23 November 2015 it told seven named administrative staff to report to the new office from 27 November 2015, failing which they would be treated as having abandoned their jobs [Para 6][Para 211][Para 213][Para 214]. Twenty workers, represented by the Khmer Democratic Workers' Union Federation, claimed severance and other payments; the union at the company had been registered on 30 October 2015 [Para 217][Para 229][Para 230]. One of the five original claims was settled in conciliation and the workers agreed that claims 1 and 2 merely supported claim 4, leaving the panel to decide claims 3 and 4 [Para 26][Para 31].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the three sanitation workers are entitled to compensation for annual leave not taken? [Para 232]
2. Whether those three workers are entitled to 13th-month salary? [Para 232]
3. Whether the employer was entitled to require the seven administrative staff to work at the new office location, and whether they may claim severance and other payments before going? [Para 349]
4. Whether each of the 20 workers was bound by a fixed-duration or an indefinite-duration contract, and what severance is due under Articles 75, 77, 89, 91, 116, 166 and 167? [Para 278][Para 324]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer manufactured a situation of harassment — no work, duties outside their skills, unpaid overtime, delayed wages, removal of transport and of water and electricity, sale of the factory, and a unilateral move of the office — in order to make them resign and escape statutory compensation; the sanitation workers were denied annual leave and 13th-month salary enjoyed by other departments [Para 2][Para 124][Para 397].

**Employer (respondent):** it committed no harassment; it kept paying wages even without work and during a three-month strike, and the cleaning duties were only occasional help requested with the workers' agreement; the office move was to cut costs and affected only the administrative office, not the whole company, and the seven who refused had abandoned their jobs [Para 12][Para 187][Para 207][Para 208][Para 219].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — annual-leave compensation was ordered for the three sanitation workers and their 13th-month claim refused; the claim of Mr S (Sok Samoeun) was dismissed because his contract was only suspended; severance under Article 89, moral damages under Article 91, annual-leave compensation and final wages were ordered for the seven relocated staff, and severance, moral damages and annual-leave compensation for the nine who resigned under Article 90, with notice damages refused to those nine; no sum was fixed [Para 456][Para 457][Para 458][Para 459][Para 460][Para 461]

On issue 3 the panel held that Article 166 gives every worker, including hourly-paid and casual workers, paid annual leave of one and a half days per month of continuous service, with one extra day per three years, following Awards No. 47/06 and 250/15 [Para 233][Para 245][Para 246][Para 247][Para 251]. The three sanitation workers had received none [Para 252], so compensation was ordered for their actual seniority [Para 258]. The 13th-month salary was different: no law obliges an employer to pay it, it is a bonus the employer may grant by unilateral decision, and the employer had allocated it department by department rather than capriciously within one department [Para 265][Para 269][Para 270][Para 272][Para 276]; the claim was refused [Para 277].

On issue 4 the panel first classified the contracts. Fifteen workers had served more than two years on successive fixed-duration contracts, so under Article 67(2) and Article 73(5) their contracts became indefinite-duration [Para 281][Para 284][Para 293][Para 294]; the three sanitation workers had no written contract and so were indefinite-duration under Article 67(7) [Para 314][Para 315][Para 321]. Mr S's continuity was not proved, so he remained on a one-year fixed contract [Para 306][Para 311][Para 312]. On the transfers, Article 2 lets an employer reorganise, but only within four limits — no wage cut, no day/night shift change, no drastic change of skill, and no move to a distant place [Para 352][Para 353]. The panel found the new site far away, the roles unchanged in name but without work at the old site, and the production manager posted to an administrative office [Para 357][Para 358][Para 360], so the four conditions were not met and the employer should have terminated and paid statutory compensation rather than treat the staff as having abandoned their posts [Para 361][Para 382]. For those seven the panel ordered dismissal damages under Article 89, moral damages under Article 91, damages for want of notice under Articles 75 and 77, annual-leave compensation under Articles 166 and 167, and final wages under Article 116 [Para 366][Para 371][Para 384][Para 386][Para 452]. For the nine who resigned, the panel found that keeping the company running for over a year in an unclear state, without terminating and paying, was itself conduct that drove them to resign under Article 90 [Para 422][Para 424][Para 427]; they received dismissal damages, moral damages and annual-leave compensation, but not notice damages, since Articles 75 and 77 apply only to dismissal by the employer, not to resignation under Article 90 [Para 432][Para 444][Para 445]. Mr S's contract was suspended on 16 November 2015 pending the labour inspector's authorisation, so he was never terminated and Articles 116, 166 and 167 did not apply to him [Para 337][Para 343][Para 344][Para 346].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may reorganise or relocate work only within four limits — no reduction in wages, no switch between day and night shifts, no drastic change of skill, no distant relocation [Para 352][Para 353]; a transfer that breaks those limits is a breach of contract that obliges the employer to terminate and pay statutory compensation, and treating the refusal as abandonment does not cure it [Para 361][Para 382]. Continued operation for more than a year in an unresolved state, without terminating and paying, is itself conduct that drives resignation under Article 90, entitling the worker to dismissal damages, moral damages and annual-leave compensation, but not to damages for want of notice [Para 424][Para 427][Para 444][Para 445].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that it would not examine whether the suspension of Mr S's contract was itself lawful, because the claims before it concerned termination only [Para 345], and that documents submitted in a foreign language without Khmer translation are disregarded under Article 23 of Prakas No. 099 [Para 405][Para 406].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award date is printed as 06 January 2016 on the face of the award [Para 1] but the extension clause runs to "06 January 2015" [Para 35], and the panel's constitution is dated "12 November 2019 2015" [Para 28]. Head counts vary: 20 workers claimed, 15 attended the hearing, and later passages speak of 12, 9, 7 and 3 in overlapping lists [Para 109][Para 111][Para 221][Para 432][Para 446]. The final order is duplicated almost verbatim in two blocks [Para 452][Para 459] and one order names "40" sanitation workers before listing three [Para 454]. Individual names run through the text unredacted and are inconsistently spelled, so they are cited here as Mr S and Ms D; "Reynaldo M.P.Var" appears both as a party and as an author of documents [Para 134][Para 181]. Stray English fragments ("Augu", "Sangkay", "ungwere when 2017") and repeated case numbers in the lists of prior awards are OCR artefacts [Para 39][Para 236][Para 464].
