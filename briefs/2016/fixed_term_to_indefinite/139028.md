# Award No. 102/16 — Ou Tong Development (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 17 May 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (employer-appointed គល់ វឌ្ឍនា; worker-appointed អន ណន; chair ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា) · Doc ID 139028

## 1. Facts
Ou Tong Development (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. ("Ou Tong") makes kuy teav noodles and employed about 20 workers. It hired Ms N (worker no. 569) and Ms C (worker no. 560), both in the folding section, on 29 December 2015 at a USD 135 wage plus USD 10 incentive bonus and USD 7 travel and accommodation allowance per month, with no written contract. The employer terminated both on 28 March 2016 without notice. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 26][Para 27][Para 28]
Conciliation settled two of the three disputed points; the unresolved point, forwarded to the Arbitration Council on 22 April 2016, was the demand that the two workers be taken back. [Para 6][Para 11][Para 26]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the contracts of Ms N and Ms C were probationary (សាកល្បង) contracts or indefinite-term contracts? [Para 40]
2. Whether the employer terminated Ms N and Ms C lawfully under the Labour Law? [Para 51]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the contracts were indefinite-term, because at hiring the employer gave no written contract and fixed no duration; the alleged slow work was really one day of food poisoning that left the two weak at work. [Para 32][Para 33]
**Employer (respondent):** it did not renew the probationary contracts because both workers worked slowly, and it would not take them back; it was ready to pay contractual-severance payments under the Labour Law, to pay 7 days in lieu of notice, and to check whether annual leave remained outstanding. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 36]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement refused; the employer was ordered to pay each worker 7 days' wages and benefits in lieu of notice and 4.5 days of unused annual leave; severance and moral damages were refused. [Para 88][Para 89][Para 90][Para 91]

On issue 1 the panel read Article 68 of the Labour Law, under which a probation contract must be dated with a start and end date and cannot exceed three months, two months for specialised workers and one month for ordinary workers. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 42] It found no evidence that the employer ever set out a probation period with fixed dates for either worker; the parties agreed that no written contract existed at hiring and that only the hiring date and the dismissal date were known. [Para 48][Para 49] Article 67 requires a fixed-duration contract to be in writing, failing which the contract becomes one of indefinite duration. [Para 43][Para 44] The panel followed the reasoning of Award 57/07 on its first issue, where an oral hiring was held not to be probationary even though the personnel record described the worker as a probationer, because that record was not a formal document. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47] It therefore held both contracts to be indefinite-term. [Para 50]

On issue 2 the panel applied Article 74: an indefinite-term contract may be terminated at the will of one party, but the party terminating must give written notice, and no dismissal may occur without a valid reason relating to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the needs of the enterprise. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 54] It followed Awards 51/08 (third issue) and 160/13. [Para 55] The employer's sole reason was that the two worked slowly; it produced no evidence at all in support. [Para 56][Para 59] The panel held the reason did not satisfy Article 74 and the dismissal was therefore unlawful. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 60] Because the employer maintained it would not reinstate, the panel turned to the termination payments the workers had not yet received. [Para 61][Para 62]

On severance, Article 89 gives 7 days' wages and allowances for continuous service of 6 to 12 months, 15 days per year beyond 12 months, capped at 6 months' wages, with a part-year of 6 months or more rounded up. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65][Para 66][Para 67] Both workers had served only 3 months, fewer than 6, so neither was entitled to severance. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 70] Moral damages under Article 91 are calculated by reference to the severance amount and cannot be confused with notice pay, so the refusal of severance carried the Article 91 claim with it. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 73][Para 75][Para 76]

On notice, Article 75 sets a minimum of 7 days where service is under 6 months and Article 77 requires payment equal to the wages and all benefits for the unobserved notice period; the employer gave no notice but had offered 7 days. [Para 77][Para 78][Para 79][Para 80][Para 81] On annual leave, Articles 166 and 167 give 1.5 working days per month of service, with 4 days accruing only after a full year and payment on early termination; 3 months of service yielded 4.5 days. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 84][Para 85][Para 87] The award is binding and takes effect immediately under the agreement of 5 May 2016. [Para 94]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that never issued a written contract fixing the probation period's start and end dates cannot later characterise the engagement as probationary: the contract is indefinite-term, a dismissal then needs a valid reason under Article 74 proved by evidence, and a worker dismissed without such reason keeps entitlement to statutory notice pay and accrued annual leave even where service was too short to earn severance. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 60][Para 81][Para 87]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that notice pay, severance and moral damages are distinct heads that are neither interchangeable nor mutually exclusive, and that an employer's offer to pay 7 days in lieu of notice simply matched the statutory minimum for service under six months. [Para 72][Para 80]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The dismissal date is printed as 28 March 2016 in one paragraph and as "38 March 2016" in the next; the second is an impossible date and the first is used here. [Para 28][Para 29] The constitution of the panel is dated 26 April "2019 2016" in the same line, two years printed for one event; the award date is consistently 17 May 2016. [Para 1][Para 12][Para 17] The conciliation report number and the two hearing invitations are partly garbled by OCR, and the extract of the panel's earlier reasoning runs into corrupted citations ("49700", "ស៊ីរ៉ា តិច") before the same award is named as 57/07. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 43][Para 45] One worker's name appears as "ឆោម សុខ នា" in the claim and as "ឆោម សុខ ខា" in the issues paragraph, and a fifth attendee at the hearing is not among the named claimants.
