# Award No. 146/16 — Van Linh Hyung Heng (Cambodia) Garment Factory Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 15 July 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Hem Hunarith, appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna, appointed by the workers; Aing Eng Tong, president) · Doc ID 139054

## 1. Facts
Seven workers of a garment factory — three of them leaders of the Khmer Youth Union at the plant and four ordinary members — had worked since 1998. Their seniority was paid out on 12 April 2013, after which they were re-engaged on successive six-month fixed-duration contracts [Para 44][Para 45]. On 1 April 2016 the employer announced that the company, formerly Su Tongfang, was renamed Van Linh Hyung Heng, keeping seniority and benefits intact [Para 46]. In late April 2016 the employer sought to buy out seniority for USD 5, 12 and 18, later USD 50 per year of service, and on 17 May 2016 refused the seven entry to the plant, saying new team leaders had not yet been selected [Para 48][Para 50][Para 54].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the seven workers' employment relationship was governed by a fixed-duration or an unlimited-duration labour contract? [Para 69]
2. Whether the employer lawfully terminated the seven workers' employment under the Labour Law? [Para 90]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer dismissed all seven without valid reason, and dismissed the three union leaders without the authorisation of the labour inspector [Para 56][Para 57]. Their contracts had not yet expired, so any severance had to equal their wages multiplied by the five months remaining on the contract [Para 52].
**Employer (respondent):** Absent. The employer submitted no documents and did not appear at the hearing despite two attempts at service and a telephone call, so the panel treated its right of response as waived [Para 19][Para 62][Para 86].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to take back all seven workers and to preserve their wages and other benefits unchanged [Para 126]. The award is a non-binding arbitration order that becomes final eight days after notification if no party objects in writing to the Minister of Labour [Para 127].

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 67(2) of the Labour Law: a fixed-duration contract may not exceed two years, and may be renewed one or more times provided the maximum duration of the renewal does not exceed two years; performance contrary to this rule converts the contract into one of unlimited duration [Para 69][Para 70][Para 71]. Article 73(5) adds that absent prior notice, a contract continues for a term equal to the original, or is treated as unlimited where the aggregate duration exceeds the Article 67 limit [Para 72]. The panel followed its own reasoning in Award No. 10/03-Zacksintek that the aggregate duration of all successive contracts is what counts [Para 73], and read Article 67(2) as referring to the maximum total period rather than to each individual renewal [Para 77]. ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982, Article 3, was used as interpretive context even though non-binding [Para 78][Para 80]. On the facts — contracts of six months from April 2013 running past two years in aggregate — the seven held unlimited-duration contracts [Para 88][Para 89].

On issue 2(a), for the three union leaders, Article 293 of the Labour Law permits dismissal of a staff delegate only with the agreement of the labour inspector, and Article 4(2) of Prakas No. 305 Sakbey of 22 November 2001 extends that protection to three union leaders [Para 97][Para 98][Para 99]. The panel found the employer never sought the inspector's authorisation [Para 102], and ordered reinstatement at existing wages and benefits [Para 108].

On issue 2(b), for the four members, Article 74 allows either party to terminate an unlimited-duration contract on written notice, but no dismissal may occur without a valid reason relating to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the operational needs of the enterprise [Para 110][Para 112]. Refusing entry on 17 May 2016 because new team leaders had not been chosen, and then refusing to meet the workers, was a dismissal without such reason [Para 119]. Following its earlier awards on the same point, the panel ordered the four reinstated [Para 121][Para 124], and restated the order for all seven in the operative paragraph [Para 126].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer renews a worker on successive fixed-duration contracts so that their aggregate duration exceeds two years, the relationship is an unlimited-duration contract whatever the length of each individual renewal; dismissal then requires a valid reason under Article 74, and for a union leader additionally the prior agreement of the labour inspector under Article 293 [Para 77][Para 82][Para 99].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remark that ILO Recommendation No. 166 is not binding but is a useful interpretive context for Article 67, and its observation that unlimited-duration contracts serve both workers' security and employers' interest in long-service commitment [Para 75][Para 80]. The recital of Prakas No. 34 of 21 April 2004 conferring power to order reinstatement is framing rather than a separate holding [Para 101].
