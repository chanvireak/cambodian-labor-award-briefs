# Award No. 146/16 — វ៉ាន់ លីន ហ្យុង ហេង (ខេមបូឌា) ហ្គាមេន ហ្វេក ធើរី ខូអិលធីឌី (Arbitration Council, ថ្ងៃទី ១៥ ខែកក្កដា ឆ្នាំ ២០១៦)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Panel: ហែម ហូណារិទ្ធ, លីវ សុវណ្ណា, chair អាំង អេង ថុង · Doc ID 139054

## 1. Facts
The employer is a garment-cutting factory employing about 400 workers [Para 34]. It was formerly named Su Tong Fang and changed its name on 1 April 2016, telling workers their seniority and benefits would be preserved [Para 46]. The worker party was the federation and its local union, acting for seven employees: three union officials and four union members [Para 40]. The local union had 110 members, was registered in 2004, and had its sixth-term leadership recognised on 9 September 2015 [Para 36] [Para 37]. The seven had worked since 1998; seniority was paid off once in 2013, after which they held successive six-month fixed-term contracts with no probation contract, the last running from 1 April to 1 October 2016 [Para 44] [Para 45] [Para 85]. In late April 2016 the employer sought severance exits; after a strike over the offer it paid nine days of May wages, the seven took holiday from 13 to 16 May, and on 17 May they were refused re-entry because no new team leader had been chosen [Para 48] [Para 53] [Para 54]. The complaint was filed on 25 May 2016, conciliation on 16 June failed on the single remaining point, the panel was formed on 27 June, the hearing was held on 6 July 2016, and the non-binding award issued on 15 July 2016 [Para 8] [Para 9] [Para 11] [Para 7] [Para 17] [Para 18]. The employer's representative was listed as absent and it did not appear; two union leaders attended for the workers [Para 15] [Para 38].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Procedural) Whether, under Prakas 099, the panel could proceed in the employer's absence after proper summons [Para 59]?
2. (Issue 1) Whether the seven workers, on six-month fixed-term contracts renewed from April 2013, held fixed-term or indefinite-term contracts under Article 67 of the Labour Law [Para 69]?
3. (Issue 2) Whether the three union officials were dismissed without the labour-inspector approval required by Article 293 of the Labour Law, and whether the four other workers were dismissed without a valid reason under Article 74 of the Labour Law [Para 90] [Para 97] [Para 110]?
4. (Remedy) Whether the panel could order reinstatement to the former or a suitable position with wages and benefits preserved [Para 101]?

## 3. Positions of the parties
The worker side demanded that all seven be reinstated with wages and other benefits unchanged [Para 43]. It said three were union officials and four were union members, and that no union at the factory enjoyed majority representation [Para 42]. It rejected the severance offers because the current contracts had not expired, arguing any seniority payment should be wages multiplied by the five months remaining on the contracts [Para 52]. It characterised the refusals of re-entry as dismissal without proper cause and, for the union officials, without inspector approval [Para 56] [Para 57]. The workers relied on the union registration certificate, leadership recognition, the conciliation record, the April 2016 payroll and company cards [Para 19]. The employer filed no documents and did not appear; the invitation was accepted by company security but not signed, an email summons went unanswered, and calls on the hearing day went unanswered [Para 13] [Para 14]. The panel treated that silence as waiver of the right to answer [Para 86].

## 4. Award and reasoning
The panel first found the employer had been properly served and given no valid reason for absence, so under Prakas 099 it could continue in absentia [Para 64] [Para 67] [Para 68]. On contract status, it applied Article 67(2) and Article 73(5): fixed-term contracts whose renewals make the aggregate duration exceed two years convert to indefinite-term contracts [Para 82]. The workers' contracts from April 2013, with the last due to end 1 October 2016, exceeded two years; because the employer did not respond, the panel accepted the workers' account and held all seven held indefinite-term contracts [Para 85] [Para 87] [Para 88] [Para 89]. For the three union officials, Article 293 of the Labour Law and Prakas 305 require labour-inspector approval for any suspension or dismissal of union leadership; the panel found none had been sought, and held the employer must reinstate them with wages and benefits unchanged [Para 97] [Para 98] [Para 99] [Para 102] [Para 107]. For the four members, Article 74 of the Labour Law permits termination of an indefinite-term contract only with written notice and a valid reason tied to the worker's aptitude or conduct or the needs of the enterprise; an unexplained refusal of re-entry on 17 May 2016 and refusal to negotiate did not meet that test [Para 110] [Para 119]. Article 34 of Prakas 099 supplied the reinstatement power [Para 101]. The panel ordered the employer to take back all seven workers with wages and other benefits unchanged [Para 125] [Para 126].

**Outcome:** granted — reinstatement of all seven workers to their former work with wages and other benefits unchanged; no monetary sum awarded [Para 126].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Fixed-term contracts renewed so that their aggregate duration exceeds two years convert to indefinite-term contracts, after which the employer may terminate only with written notice and a valid reason tied to conduct, aptitude or enterprise needs [Para 82] [Para 112]. Renewals are measured by aggregate duration, not each renewal's length [Para 77]. A union leader may not be dismissed without prior labour-inspector approval; absence of approval defeats the dismissal [Para 99]. A party properly summoned who gives no excuse cannot halt the arbitration by absenting itself [Para 68]. This award is non-binding and becomes final only if no party objects in writing to the Minister within eight days of notification [Para 127].
**Obiter dicta:** The panel treated ILO Recommendation No. 166 as useful but non-binding interpretive context for Article 67 [Para 78] [Para 80].
