# Award No. 134/16 — Kin Tay Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 29 June 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambor Na, chosen by the employer party; Liv Sovanna, chosen by the worker party; Pen Bun Chhean, chair chosen by the two) · Doc ID 139044

## 1. Facts
Kin Tay Garment Co., Ltd, a garment factory on National Road 2, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employing about 500 workers, announced the closure of its operations on 16 November 2015 and stopped production the same day, without prior notice to the workforce and without stating any reason. [Para 32][Para 80][Para 81] On that date it paid termination compensation to the workers generally, including two other union leaders, but not to the three Cambodia Union leaders who are the claimants here. [Para 41][Para 44][Para 140] The three claimed notice compensation, dismissal damages, moral damages, unused 2015 annual leave and wages for 1–16 November 2015 under indefinite-duration contracts. [Para 42][Para 46][Para 52] The employer never appeared and filed no document; its factory gates were shut and postal invitations were returned. [Para 18][Para 39][Para 59]

## 2. Issues in dispute
The non-conciliation report listed four demands. [Para 3][Para 4][Para 5][Para 6] At the hearing the worker party withdrew demands 3 and 4 (wages and allowances for union leaders from November 2015, and 100% pay for five months of suspended work) and asked that demands 1 and 2 be joined, so the panel considered only the two remaining issues in its own numbering: [Para 19][Para 20][Para 21]
1. Whether the employer is obliged to pay compensation for termination of the employment contract to the three claimant workers? [Para 63]
2. Whether the employer discriminated against the union in connection with the payment of that termination compensation? [Para 132]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer closed on 16 November 2015 without notice, paid severance to every worker except the three union leaders, and told them that if they wanted the same payment they would have to file a complaint through the dispute procedure — conduct showing anti-union discrimination. [Para 45][Para 141][Para 142] Each held an indefinite-duration contract, none had taken 2015 annual leave (19, 18 and 19 days remaining), and wages for 1–16 November 2015 were unpaid. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 49][Para 51][Para 52]
**Employer (respondent):** no position on the merits. It did not attend the hearing despite proper invitation, gave no reason, and submitted no evidence, so it never answered the claims or the discrimination allegation. [Para 18][Para 57][Para 143] Its only recorded stance is that it did not agree to the four demands at conciliation. [Para 9][Para 12]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay the three workers notice compensation under Article 75, dismissal damages under Article 89, moral damages under Article 91, compensation for unused annual leave under Article 166, and final wages for 1–16 November 2015 under Article 116 of the Labour Law; the union-discrimination claim was dismissed. No figure in riel or dollars is printed for any head of payment. [Para 129][Para 148][Para 147]

Proceeding in the employer's absence: Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council allows the panel to continue where a party duly invited fails to appear without valid reason. Invitations were handed to the factory's unnamed security guard on 7 and 10 June 2016 and posted the same day, the post being returned marked that the company had closed; the panel held the employer duly summoned and continued. [Para 54][Para 56][Para 59][Para 61][Para 62]

Issue 1 — the obligation to pay. Article 87, third paragraph, provides that cessation of an enterprise's activity does not release the employer from the obligations of that Section; following Award No. 096/15 (Chang Kai Printing Co., Ltd), the panel held that a shutdown for want of orders cannot be pleaded to escape them. [Para 63][Para 64] The employer had terminated by its own will alone, the same day it announced the closure, with no reason given and no prior notice, which the panel classified as unilateral termination without valid reason or notice. [Para 80][Para 81][Para 85] Because the employer produced nothing, it did not discharge the burden under Article 118 of proving that wages had been paid. [Para 67][Para 69][Para 72] Notice compensation was fixed by the seniority scale in Articles 75 and 77, dismissal damages by Article 89 (15 days of wage and allowances per year of service, capped at six months, a part-year of six months or more counted as a full year), moral damages under Article 91 at the same amount as the dismissal damages since the termination was unjustified, unused annual leave under Article 166 on the 19/18/19 days found, and final wages under Article 116, which requires payment within 48 hours of the last working day. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 92][Para 96][Para 104][Para 109][Para 117][Para 125][Para 128]

Issue 2 — union discrimination. Articles 12 and 279 forbid an employer from using union membership or union activity as the basis of a decision on termination, pay or benefits. [Para 132][Para 133] The panel accepted that the workers' account, if proved, would establish discrimination, but found nothing beyond their own statements at the hearing — no document, no witness — and held the burden unmet, dismissing the claim notwithstanding the employer's default. [Para 144][Para 146][Para 147]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The closure of an enterprise does not release the employer from the termination obligations of Section 3 of the Labour Law, and a closure announced and carried out on the same day, without prior notice and without a stated reason, is a unilateral termination without valid reason, entitling each affected worker to notice compensation, dismissal damages, moral damages of like amount, unused annual leave and final wages. An employer's failure to appear does not relieve the worker of proving anti-union discrimination: an uncorroborated assertion at the hearing fails even where the respondent files nothing. [Para 64][Para 81][Para 87][Para 146][Para 147]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's survey of Prakas No. 099 Article 21 and its list of earlier awards on absent parties (Nos 42/05, 63/04-Sanh Vay, 148/07, 99/09, 173/01, 144/12-Apsara, 109/13, 163/14-Sin Chang Shing Garment) are general observations on procedure, as is its remark that the employer waived its right to reply. [Para 55][Para 71]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The closure is dated 16 November 2016 at [Para 85] but 16 November 2015 everywhere else, including the operative reasoning and the facts. [Para 80][Para 85]
- Paragraphs 67–69 import a passage from Award No. 361/15 (Ken Sport) and, on the strength of it, order repayment of a missing wage of "USD 100"; no such sum is claimed in this case and the operative order contains no USD 100. [Para 67][Para 69][Para 148]
- The order lists heads of compensation without any quantified amount, so the award cannot be enforced on its face without a calculation. [Para 129][Para 148]
- Hearing date is 16 June 2016 at [Para 11] but 29 June 2016 — the award date — at [Para 16].
- Garbled dates and numbering: the panel is said to have been constituted on "09 June 2022 2016" [Para 15]; the referral date reads "10/7 June 2016" [Para 14]; the remaining issues are printed as "09 and 02" instead of 01 and 02 [Para 40]; the annual-leave finding prints "2022" [Para 116].
- The employer's representatives are recorded only as "absent" on the face of the award [Para 1], and the security guards who received the invitations are unnamed [Para 56][Para 58].
- Paragraphs 138–139 and 145–147 repeat the same sentences, and paragraphs 3 and 150 are OCR noise (addresses, telephone numbers and stray numerals after the signatures).
