# Award No. 064/16 — MH Bio-Energy Group Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 19 April 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mr Seng Vuoch Hun (employer-selected), Mr An Nan (worker-selected), chair Mr Run Saray · Doc ID 139002 · unofficial English translation of the Khmer original

## 1. Facts
MH Bio-Energy Group Co., Ltd., an ethanol manufacturer at Doung Village, Prek Pnov, Phnom Penh, registered under Inv. 1171E/2007 of 6 February 2007, employed approximately 120 workers. [Para 22][Para 23] Mr S (name redacted), a mechanic controlling the motor system of the potato milling section, held a one-year contract from 6 February 2014, which the employer did not terminate at expiry, so he continued working on a renewed one-year term to 6 February 2016. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 59] On 16 January 2016 the employer moved him to the potato pressing section, which both parties agreed has an unpleasant smell, and after he refused to move it issued a termination letter on 2 February 2016 stating that he was terminated with effect from 21 January 2016. [Para 34][Para 36][Para 37][Para 72] The KWPFU local union was unregistered but held an unnumbered authorisation dated 2 February 2016 from 14 workers, and 6 workers were claimants; two of four demands were settled in conciliation and the drivers' demand (issue 2) at the hearing of 21 March 2016, leaving issue 1 for a non-binding award of 19 April 2016. [Para 17][Para 20][Para 21][Para 23][Para 24]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr S, terminated on 2 February 2016, and pay back wages and entitlements under the Labour Law? [Para 13][Para 25]
2. Whether the employer must pay termination compensation to drivers formerly employed by CJ Agro before entering new contracts? (settled at the hearing) [Para 14][Para 15][Para 20]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr S objected to being moved into a section whose unpleasant smell could affect his health and asked instead for termination compensation under the Labour Law — outstanding wages, payment in lieu of annual leave, compensation in lieu of notice, dismissal indemnity and damages; the union also disputed the termination date, maintaining he was terminated on 2 February 2016 and not 21 January 2016. [Para 26][Para 32][Para 35][Para 36]
**Employer (respondent):** It refused reinstatement and relied on Clause 6 of the contract giving it the right to allocate work between sections according to the company's plan, saying the change cost him nothing in wages, hours, travel or skill and was needed because the pressing section was short of workers; it treated his refusal as serious misconduct under Article 83 of the Labour Law and point 10.1 of the internal rules, and held that the termination took effect on 21 January 2016. [Para 14][Para 36][Para 37][Para 68][Para 69]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted in part — reinstatement was not ordered because Mr S claimed compensation instead, and the employer was ordered to pay him compensation in lieu of prior notice under Articles 75 and 77, dismissal indemnity under Article 89, payment in lieu of unused annual leave, damages at least equal to the dismissal indemnity, and all outstanding wages to 2 February 2016. [Para 110][Para 130][Para 131][Para 132][Para 133][Para 134][Para 135]

On the contract type, the panel applied Article 67(8), holding that a fixed-duration contract whose work "tacitly and quietly continues" past its end becomes one of undetermined duration when neither party expresses an intention to renew or not renew, and Article 67(7), under which a contract not made in writing becomes undetermined. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 42][Para 43] It read Article 73(5) with Article 67(2): notice of non-renewal is due 10 days ahead for contracts of up to six months and 15 days for longer ones, and without it the contract renews for an equal term or becomes undetermined once total duration passes two years, as Award No. 271/13-D & L held. [Para 46][Para 48][Para 49][Para 54][Para 57] Because the employer gave no notice at the 6 February 2015 expiry and no new written contract was signed while Mr S kept working, the panel found the relationship had renewed implicitly and he was a worker of undetermined duration. [Para 59][Para 61][Para 62][Para 63]

On the transfer, Article 2(2) of the Labour Law gives the employer the right to supervise and manage the enterprise, but following Award No. 62/06-Qicksew and later awards the panel held that right must be exercised lawfully and reasonably. [Para 65][Para 67] Article 229(1) requires workplaces to be kept clean and hygienic, and clause 1 of Prakas No. 125 of 15 June 2001 requires the employer to keep the atmosphere free of smells, gas and dust that endanger or hinder work. [Para 74][Para 75] The panel accepted that the odour comes from the potato raw material and cannot be removed entirely, but found the employer had said nothing to show it took any measure to protect workers in the pressing section, and held that moving a worker from a section with no smell to one with an unpleasant smell, without any protective measure, was a direction contrary to Article 229 and Prakas No. 125 and therefore unreasonable despite Clause 6. [Para 76][Para 79][Para 80][Para 81][Para 83][Para 84]

Because the direction was unlawful, his refusal was justified and he committed no serious misconduct under Article 83 or point 10.1 of the internal rules. [Para 91][Para 92][Para 93] On the date, the panel applied the rule that the party alleging must prove, found the termination letter of 2 February 2016 itself inconsistent with the employer's claim of a 31 January meeting, and held the termination took effect on 2 February 2016. [Para 98][Para 99][Para 101][Para 102] Article 74 requires a valid reason relating to aptitude, behaviour or the needs of the operation, and none was shown, but since Mr S sought compensation rather than reinstatement the panel awarded it. [Para 104][Para 106][Para 109][Para 110] It found one year and eleven months' service, counted the eleven-month fraction as a full year and awarded two years' dismissal indemnity, computing daily wages from the average of the last 12 months, and awarded damages under Article 91 equal at least to that indemnity. [Para 111][Para 116][Para 118][Para 120][Para 122][Para 128]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a fixed-duration contract ends without written notice of non-renewal and the worker quietly keeps working, the contract renews for an equal term or becomes one of undetermined duration, and the employer's contractual right to move a worker between sections is not unlimited: a transfer into a section whose smell or atmosphere endangers health, with no protective measure taken under Article 229 and Prakas No. 125, is an unlawful direction, so the worker's refusal cannot be serious misconduct and dismissal without a valid reason under Article 74 attracts notice, dismissal and leave compensation, damages and unpaid wages. [Para 41][Para 63][Para 84][Para 93][Para 106][Para 128]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the odour is inherent in the potato raw material and that the employer is unlikely to be able to remove it entirely from the plant, so the duty is to mitigate rather than eliminate it. [Para 78][Para 79]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The procedural narrative dates the assignment of a conciliation officer to "20 April 2016", one day after the award itself, where the complaint was received on 9 February 2016. [Para 17][Para 18] The award contradicts itself on notice of expiry: the findings record that the employer "did not give notification of the expiration" in 2015, while the next sentence records the workers accepting that it did notify him. [Para 27][Para 28] The panel speaks of "one year and eleven months" of service yet also of a duration of "eleven months" counted as an entire year for Article 89, and never states which Article 75 notice period (15 days or one month) it applied. [Para 111][Para 116][Para 117] The employer is described as holding a local union registration certificate in one line and as not having one in the next. [Para 23] The union's authorisation is cited as from 14 workers while 6 claimants are listed, and the claimant list is printed twice with a duplicated fragment. [Para 23][Para 24] The English text is a translation with systematic OCR loss of "th" ("themployer", "thearing", "termination"), and all evidence documents were omitted from this version by the translator. [Para 21][Para 37]
