# Award No. 325/15 — Cambodia Hoi Fu Garment & Knitting Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 20 January 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Run Saray presiding; Uk Ri, employer nominee; An Nan, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139493

## 1. Facts
Cambodia Hoi Fu Garment & Knitting Co., Ltd, a garment and knitting factory at Chrongrouk village, Trapeang Krasaing commune, Porsenchey district, Phnom Penh, employed about 230 workers at the time of the dispute. [Para 51][Para 52] The workforce complained of repeated suspensions through 2015 and the company's eventual closure; the Khmer Democratic Workers' Union Federation took up 111 workers' claims under an authority of 22 November 2015, at a factory where it had no local union. [Para 54][Para 53] On 16 November 2015 the company closed for lack of buyers' orders and terminated the contracts, paying severance on the seniority tiers recorded in that day's minutes; conciliation of the three surviving demands on 16 December 2015 failed and the file reached the Council on 18 December 2015 under report No. 1604. [Para 79][Para 74][Para 19][Para 20] At the hearing of 29 December 2015 the workers confirmed that points 1 and 2 were merely the reasons for point 3, so the panel decided point 3 alone. [Para 17][Para 23][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must stop the practices complained of — no work provided, no wages for September and October 2015, transfers to work outside the workers' skills, irregular payment, only USD 15 a month during suspension while overtime was forced and rest days paid at 50 percent, no response to flooding of the factory, unhygienic toilets and inadequate medicine, and refusal of statutory annual leave? [Para 2][Para 3][Para 5][Para 6][Para 8][Para 9]
2. Whether the employer may refuse to let the workers enter and work from 7 November 2015, saying there is no work, when the suspension period had ended on that date? [Para 10]
3. Whether the company must pay wages and the entitlements under Articles 75, 77, 89, 91, 116 and 166, computed by the Article 110 formula, because it permanently ceased production and dismissed the workers? [Para 11]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company terminated their indefinite contracts on 7 November 2015 without valid reason and without notice; severance is an individual right over a personal debt, so no one could sign away their entitlement in their place, and the settlement accepted by other workers does not bind the 111 who refused it; they asked for USD 30 per worker and USD 46 per team leader for the two suspended months, together with statutory severance, notice, illness and annual-leave payments. [Para 69][Para 75][Para 80][Para 57]
**Employer (respondent):** most workers accepted the severance fixed in the 16 November 2015 minutes of the all-fronts strike-settlement committee, which paid USD 70 per year of service to sewing-machine workers and USD 95 to daily-paid workers, or a tiered USD 200, 350, 500 or 700 by seniority; the company closed on 16 November 2015 for want of orders and the settlement should be applied. [Para 70][Para 72][Para 74][Para 79]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay dismissal damages under Article 89, moral damages under Article 91, notice compensation under Articles 75 and 77, annual-leave compensation under Articles 166 and 167, and USD 30 per worker and USD 46 per team leader for the suspension, but refused wages for the suspension period itself. [Para 165][Para 163][Para 159]
Before the merits the panel fixed who the claimants were, since a party may appear in person or through a representative holding written authority under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 85][Para 86] Of the 111 who signed the authority, two names were duplicated and seven appeared both in the authority and in the employer's list of workers already paid; the panel held those seven still entitled to claim the correct statutory severance despite having received a payment, and added one worker present at the hearing who had signed no authority, giving 110 claimants. [Para 88][Para 90][Para 91][Para 92][Para 96]
On the termination, the panel applied Article 74: an indefinite contract may be ended by either party, but not without a valid reason relating to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the necessity of the enterprise's operation. [Para 98][Para 99][Para 100] It found the employer had ended the contracts of its own will on the ground that there were no buyers and no work, which is not a reason of that kind, so the termination was unlawful. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 122] Dismissal damages under Article 89 therefore followed — 15 days' wages and allowances per year of service, capped at six months, with any period over six months counted as a full year. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 108][Para 109][Para 111] Moral damages under Article 91 were also awarded, the panel noting that this head is distinct from notice and dismissal compensation, is equal to the dismissal damages, and requires no proof of loss by the worker. [Para 118][Para 119][Para 120][Para 121][Para 123]
Because the employer gave no notice, compensation in lieu under Articles 75 and 77 was ordered. [Para 129][Para 130][Para 131] Since no annual leave had been taken, compensation under Articles 166 and 167 was ordered, calculated on the average wage of the last 12 months divided by 12 and then by 26. [Para 136][Para 137][Para 139][Para 140][Para 143] On the suspension, the panel found labour inspectorate letter No. 227 of 2 September 2015 had authorised the suspension from 7 September to 7 November 2015, so no wages were owed for it; the USD 30 and USD 46 offered were incentive payments the employer had agreed to pay and the workers had not collected, and were ordered paid. [Para 158][Para 159][Para 161][Para 162][Para 163]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer closes the enterprise for want of orders and ends indefinite contracts for that reason alone, the termination lacks the valid reason relating to aptitude, conduct or the necessity of the enterprise that Article 74 requires, and the workers recover dismissal damages under Article 89, moral damages under Article 91, notice compensation under Articles 75 and 77 and outstanding annual leave under Articles 166 and 167, each computed on the average wage of the twelve preceding months; wages are not owed, however, for a suspension the labour inspectorate has properly authorised under Article 71. A worker who has signed a receipt for severance is not thereby barred from claiming the statutory amount. [Para 92][Para 103][Para 111][Para 159]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the settlement in the strike-settlement committee's minutes bound only those who accepted it, and that a worker's severance entitlement is an individual right over a personal debt which no representative can waive on the worker's behalf. [Para 75][Para 80]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The strike-settlement minutes are dated 17 November 2015 in the employer's evidence list but 16 November 2015 wherever the panel quotes or relies on them, and the factory closure is put on 16 November 2015. [Para 30][Para 70][Para 79] The number of workers who refused the settlement is given as 92 in one place, as 111 in another, and the claimant group is finally fixed at 110. [Para 70][Para 80][Para 96] The company is printed as Cambodia Hoi Fu Garment & Knitting Co., Ltd on the face of the award, as "HOI FU Garment" in the conciliation report reference, and as "Huy Fu" in the closing paragraphs. [Para 1][Para 20][Para 78] The award orders heads of relief by article with no total sum in riel or USD anywhere; the only figures printed are the USD 30 and USD 46 suspension allowances and the USD 70, 95, 200, 350, 500 and 700 severance tiers. [Para 74][Para 163][Para 165] The caption contains stray OCR fragments ("01-", "2017") and the reasons at paras 145 and 146 repeat the termination date in corrupted form ("7 November 2015" printed as "07 November" with broken characters). [Para 1][Para 145][Para 146]
