# Award No. 128/16 — Seng Hong Yan Im Pro Dry Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 27 June 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kol Vathana, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Kong Phallha, president) · non-binding award · employer absent · Doc ID 139037

## 1. Facts
Seng Hong Yan Im Pro Dry is a sewing and embroidery company in Phnom Penh employing about 89 workers. [Para 1][Para 36][Para 37] Four federations received mandates from 64 workers, and the panel could identify only 20 of the 22 claimants by name, thumbprint and attendance at the hearing. [Para 40][Para 45][Para 46] Each of the 20 was on successive 06-month fixed-duration contracts, the last expiring on 31 May 2016; the employer gave oral notice on 23 May 2016 and shut the workers out from 24 May 2016. [Para 47][Para 96] The employer was properly summoned and did not appear, saying by telephone only that it was in the provinces. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 60]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must renew the 06-month fixed-duration contracts for a further 06 months for the 20 workers, or pay them 06 months' wages as damages under Article 73 of the Labour Law? [Para 47][Para 63][Para 84]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer failed to give lawful written notice of non-renewal, and although the final contract was for 06 months, the total duration of all their fixed-duration contracts exceeds one year, so the contracts must be deemed renewed for a further 06 months or compensated in that amount. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53]
**Employer (respondent):** Absent from the hearing, the employer had stated in writing on 31 May 2016 that every end-of-contract payment had been agreed voluntarily with the workers and that no one was coerced. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 55] It also argued that notice is owed only to workers whose contracts had reached their expiry date. [Para 4][Para 5]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the claim for renewal of the 06-month contracts or payment of 06 months' wages under Article 73 was refused for all 20 workers. No sum in riel or USD was ordered. [Para 108][Para 109]

The panel first held that it could proceed in the employer's absence under clause 21 of Prakas No. 099, the employer having been validly summoned without adequate excuse, and that by not appearing it had abandoned its right of reply, so the workers' account was accepted. [Para 56][Para 60][Para 61][Para 65][Para 66]

On the contract type the panel applied Article 67(2) of the Labour Law: a fixed-duration contract may not run beyond two years and renewal may not extend the total beyond two years, failing which the contract becomes one of undetermined duration. [Para 68][Para 70][Para 80] On the service table it found that six of the 20 — Ms Y, Ms N, Ms H, Ms S, Ms C and Ms R — had total service exceeding two years without interruption, so their contracts were already of undetermined duration and the Article 73 claim did not concern them. [Para 81][Para 82][Para 85]

For the remaining 14 the panel found total duration did not exceed two years and the final contract was for 06 months. [Para 83] Applying Article 73(5), it held that prior notice of non-renewal is required only for contracts longer than 06 months (ten days, or fifteen days where the contract exceeds one year), and that where no notice is given the contract continues for an equal term only if the total would then exceed the Article 67 limit. [Para 86][Para 88] The notice obligation attaches to each individual contract, not to the worker's total seniority, so the employer owed no notice on a 06-month contract. [Para 90][Para 91] It followed that the contracts were not renewed and no 06-month damages were payable. [Para 97][Para 98][Para 107]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The duty to give advance notice of non-renewal under Article 73(5) is measured against the length of each individual fixed-duration contract, not the worker's aggregate service, so an employer owes no notice and no renewal-term damages on a 06-month contract whose total service remains under two years. [Para 88][Para 91][Para 97] Where aggregate service has already passed two years, the contract is of undetermined duration by operation of Article 67(2) and the Article 73 renewal claim is inapplicable to that worker. [Para 80][Para 82][Para 85]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel endorsed ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982 as interpretive context for Article 67 while accepting that it is not binding. [Para 75][Para 77]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer did not appear, so all findings rest on the claimants' documents, and the panel itself could not identify two of the 22 claimants. [Para 42][Para 44][Para 45] The dates are printed inconsistently: the employer is said to have stopped work from 24 June 2016 at lines 49 and 64 but from 24 May 2016 at lines 96 and 106; the mandate letter is dated 24 May 2015 (line 40) although the dispute is from 2016; and the service table at line 54 is badly corrupted, including an entry for "ឆ្នាំ ២០១៨". The company name appears in at least five variants (lines 1, 8, 13, 25, 30). The president's name is printed as "គង់ ផល្អៈ" in the header and "គង់ ផល្លៈ" at the signature block (line 111). Most importantly, having found that six workers' contracts had become of undetermined duration, the panel dismissed the claim for all 20 without granting those six any remedy, leaving the effect of that finding unresolved on the face of the award. Individual workers are redacted here.
