# Award No. 027/16 — Hong Kong Winfair Garment (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 4 March 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; Sin Kim Sean, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 138980

## 1. Facts
Hong Kong Winfair Garment (Cambodia) Ltd cuts and sews garments and employs about 654 workers, 74 of them men [Para 71][Para 72]. A Democratic Union of Khmer Workers (Cambodia) local was elected at the factory on 10 January 2016 but had not yet been registered with the Ministry of Labour; 87 workers mandated the CCC federation on 19 January 2016 and 07 of them attended the hearing [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74][Para 76]. The company dismissed Ms K (employee No. 30098), a T1 team leader employed since 07 October 2011 on an indefinite contract, on 16 January 2016 for paying another worker's wages without a transfer letter and withholding USD 60 from her [Para 98][Para 101][Para 102]. Of the six points referred, four were settled at the hearing (points 1, 5 and 6 by agreement, point 4 withdrawn), leaving points 2 and 3 [Para 20].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must keep paying the regular-attendance bonus of USD 12 and the transport and accommodation allowance of USD 16 at their full rate while a worker is absent sick with a valid medical certificate (point 02)? [Para 77][Para 119]
2. Whether the employer must take back Ms K (employee No. 30098), a candidate in the union election, reinstate her and pay her wages from the date of dismissal until her return (point 03)? [Para 96][Para 165]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Both payments are better than the legal minimum and form part of wages under Article 103, so the employer may not deduct them day by day when a worker is sick with a proper certificate; the transfer and allowance notices the employer relies on bear only a thumbprint and an employee number, with no name of any worker or representative [Para 81][Para 84][Para 88][Para 94]. Ms K was a union election candidate, made an honest mistake at most, took nothing from the company and cost no worker a riel, and was dismissed without the labour inspector's permission [Para 103][Para 104][Para 106][Para 109].
**Employer (respondent):** The attendance bonus and the transport allowance are incentives that exceed the law and are paid to encourage regular attendance, and both notices authorise pro-rata deduction for days absent; the company nevertheless guarantees at least USD 07 a month, the Prakas No. 230 minimum [Para 90][Para 93][Para 157]. Ms K committed serious misconduct under Article 10(C)(11) of the internal rules by paying wages against a thumbprint without a transfer letter, and the company will not take her back [Para 110][Para 111].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — point 02 was dismissed and point 03 granted: the employer must take Ms K back, reinstate her and pay her wages from the date of dismissal until the date of her return [Para 207][Para 208][Para 209].

On point 02 the panel split the claim. The regular-attendance bonus, though wages under Article 103, is fixed separately from base wage and exists to encourage attendance; following Award No. 186/13 (Il C Garment), it held that deducting it pro rata for sick days is the most reasonable balance between the bonus's purpose and the law's protection of workers' health, so the claim failed [Para 119][Para 120][Para 127][Para 145][Para 148][Para 149]. The transport and accommodation allowance is not wages at all, since Article 103 expressly excludes travel costs, and the company already pays USD 16 against the USD 07 required by Prakas No. 230 of 25 July 2012; pro-rata deduction that never takes the worker below USD 07 therefore did not breach any statutory right, and that claim failed too [Para 83][Para 84][Para 86][Para 156][Para 159][Para 163][Para 164][Para 165].

On point 03 the panel applied Article 293 of the Labour Law and Article 3(3) of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001: every candidate in a union leadership election enjoys the protection against dismissal given to staff delegates, for 45 days before the election and 45 days after it where the candidate is not elected [Para 166][Para 168][Para 169]. Following Awards No. 012/15 and No. 267/15, it treated the protection as conditional on three cumulative elements — the worker's candidate status, a dismissal occurring inside the protected period, and notice of the candidacy given to the employer by any practicable means [Para 172][Para 177][Para 179]. All three were met: Ms K appears as a candidate on the 10 January 2016 election notice, the dismissal came on 16 January 2016, only 06 days later, and the notice reached the employer by post on 06 January 2016 and again by Khmer-language email on 13 January 2016, the employer's refusal to accept the postal item not counting against the workers [Para 179][Para 181][Para 184][Para 185][Para 186][Para 189][Para 192][Para 193]. The panel found no evidence whether she won or lost, and held that irrelevant — the 45-day protection runs from the election either way [Para 183][Para 184]. Because the employer dismissed her without seeking the labour inspector's prior authorisation, the dismissal was procedurally unlawful, and under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 the panel ordered reinstatement with back pay rather than deciding whether the misconduct itself was serious [Para 196][Para 198][Para 203][Para 204][Para 205].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A candidate in a union leadership election who is dismissed within the 45-day protected period and without the labour inspector's prior authorisation must be reinstated with wages from the date of dismissal, whether or not she was elected and whatever the merits of the alleged misconduct, because the defect is procedural and the panel reaches no further. By contrast, an attendance bonus and a transport allowance paid above the statutory floor are not protected in full during certified sick leave: the attendance bonus may be reduced pro rata for sick days, and the transport allowance, not being wages, may be reduced pro rata but never below the Prakas No. 230 minimum. [Para 184][Para 204][Para 127][Para 163]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel recorded the workers' contention that paying wages against a thumbprint without the company's transfer form was at most an intermediate fault causing the company no loss, and noted that no evidence showed Ms K won or lost the election — both observations were unnecessary to the outcome, which turned on the procedural defect. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 183]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Dates are repeatedly corrupted: the panel's establishment date reads "04 February 2023 2016" at [Para 17] and the award extension reads "25 February 2019 2016" at [Para 23]; a police report is dated 09 December 2016 for conduct described as occurring in January 2016 [Para 26]. The company name is printed in at least six variants (Hong Kong Winfair Garment, Hong Kong Win Fair, Hong Kong Win Fair Garmen, Hong Kong Winfair, Hong Kong Win Fai). The workers' federation is written both as CCC and C.CAWDU, and the union's registration status is stated once as not yet registered [Para 72] and once as an independent Siew Kao Du union being formed [Para 50]. Point 3 is restated almost verbatim within [Para 165], and the presiding arbitrator's name is partly lost to OCR at signature [Para 210].
