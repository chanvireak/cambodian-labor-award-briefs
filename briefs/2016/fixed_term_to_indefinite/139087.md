# Award No. 188/16 — Green Sam Mit (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 31 August 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambor Na, employer-appointed; An Virak, worker-appointed; Ang Eng Thong, president) · Doc ID 139087

## 1. Facts
Green Sam Mit (Cambodia) Ltd ("Green Sam Mit") sews glove liners in the Preah Sihanouk special economic zone with roughly 400 workers [Para 43][Para 44]. Two federations — the Cambodia Workers' Support Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព គាំទ្រ កម្មករ កម្ពុជា) and the Democratic Lucky Workers' Union Federation (សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ សំណាង កម្មករ) — took rights transfers from 120 workers on 12 July 2016 [Para 48][Para 128]. The factory union elected Mr B president on 14 July 2016; the employer dismissed him on 15 July 2016 [Para 45][Para 74]. Of eleven conciliated demands nine were settled and two went to the panel: the incentive-bonus calculation and Mr B's reinstatement [Para 10][Para 16].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must calculate the incentive bonus correctly for each worker according to the percentage of output achieved? [Para 51][Para 140]
2. Whether the employer must take Mr B back into work? [Para 72][Para 165]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The bonus scheme is chaotic — workers producing more receive less than slower colleagues, citing specific group comparisons — and Mr B was dismissed one day after his election, without the labour inspector's authorisation, because he had pressed the eleven demands; he was told he was dismissed for not knowing Chinese [Para 57][Para 84][Para 225].
**Employer (respondent):** Bonuses vary legitimately with the type of item and the volume of orders each month; Mr B's dismissal was decided on 9 July 2016, before the demands and the election, for not speaking Chinese and for slow, late work, and all termination payments are offered [Para 60][Para 91][Para 226][Para 243].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim to a recalculation of bonuses and the claim to reinstatement were both refused, but the employer was ordered to pay five statutory termination entitlements: pay in lieu of notice under Articles 75 and 77, severance under Article 89, moral damages under Article 91, unused annual leave under Article 166, and final wages under Article 116 [Para 277][Para 278][Para 279].
On standing, the panel noted the factory union was not yet registered with the Ministry of Labour, so under Article 11 of the Law on Trade Unions (2016) its officers could not represent members [Para 115][Para 121][Para 123]. It nonetheless proceeded under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099, since the two federations held written rights transfers and authorisations from the 120 workers plus two workers present at the hearing, giving 122 complainants [Para 124][Para 126][Para 139].
On issue 1, the panel applied its consistent rule that the party alleging a fact bears the burden of proof [Para 144][Para 146]. It found a worker can only be shown to have produced more than a colleague through specific comparative output data for the same period, and the workers produced none; the claim that the scheme was miscalculated was therefore rejected [Para 152][Para 154][Para 164].
On issue 2, it set out the three conditions for the special protection against dismissal in Articles 43 and 67: the worker must be a protected category, the dismissal must fall within the 45 days before or after the union election, and the union must have notified the employer and copied the ministry [Para 177][Para 182]. Mr B met the first two — dismissed one day after the election — but not the third: the workers could not prove the notice left with the company's security guard, and the employer only signed for the notice on 28 July 2016, after the dismissal [Para 205][Para 208][Para 211][Para 212].
Because Article 62 of the Law on Trade Unions and Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law bar anti-union discrimination including non-renewal, the panel then asked whether the dismissal was discriminatory and found it was not: the decision to dismiss was formed on 9 July 2016, before the eleven demands and before the election [Para 213][Para 232][Para 234]. The dismissal was nonetheless a lawful termination of an undetermined-duration contract under Article 74 for a reason related to the worker's aptitude — no Chinese and late work — so compensation, not reinstatement, followed [Para 227][Para 235][Para 237]. Mr B had between six months and two years' service, giving 15 days' notice pay [Para 252], 7 days' severance [Para 258], 7 days' moral damages [Para 263], plus unused annual leave [Para 270] and final wages [Para 274]. The award is non-binding [Para 17][Para 280].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The Article 43/67 protection of union leaders and election candidates against dismissal depends on all three conditions being met, and a union that cannot prove its notice of candidacy reached the employer before the dismissal fails the third condition however close the dismissal falls to the election; discrimination is then judged on when the employer formed the decision to dismiss, so a decision taken before the demands were made is not anti-union, and the dismissal stands as a lawful Article 74 termination compensated under Articles 75, 77, 89, 91, 116 and 166. [Para 198][Para 211][Para 232][Para 258]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that a union not yet registered cannot represent its members under Article 11, yet continued the case because the federations held individual written authorisations — a course it did not need to reach the merits [Para 123][Para 126].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel's constitution date is printed as "3 August 2021 2016" [Para 12], and the objection period in the final paragraph as "400" days where eight days is meant [Para 280]. The employer's name appears in several forms ("ហ្គ្រីន សាំ មីត", "ស្ត្រី ន សាំ មីត", "សាំ ហ្មីត"), and one evidence item attributes a bonus table to a differently named company [Para 22][Para 43]. The paragraph at [Para 211] is duplicated and heavily garbled, repeating the dismissal date three times, and the workers' own letter refers to eleven demands while only two reached the panel [Para 10][Para 76].
