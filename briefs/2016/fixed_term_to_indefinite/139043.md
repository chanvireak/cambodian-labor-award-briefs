# Award No. 133/16 — Nex.T Apparel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 22 July 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Kong Phallak chair) · a dissenting opinion by Ing Sothy is annexed · Doc ID 139043

## 1. Facts
Nex.T Apparel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd., a garment factory at Mal village, Dangkao district, Phnom Penh, employed about 917 workers. [Para 1][Para 63][Para 64] Mr N joined on 6 December 2013 on a two-month probation contract and then on successive three-month fixed-duration contracts; his last contract ran to 6 May 2016, and administration told him by telephone after working hours that it would not be renewed. [Para 70][Para 71][Para 72] He was elected deputy president of the Cambodia Union at the factory election of 23 April 2016; the union notified the employer of the result by post on 3 May 2016 and the employer refused delivery on 4 May 2016. [Para 65][Para 208][Para 217][Para 218] He had not received his last month's wage, his leave compensation or the 5% end-of-contract payment. [Para 73] Three points went to conciliation on 2 June 2016, none settled, and one was resolved at the hearing, leaving two — reinstatement and back wages. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 19][Para 20]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take Mr N back in his post as deputy president of the Cambodia Union at the factory and let him work normally again? [Para 2][Para 70][Para 135]
2. Whether the employer must pay him his wages from the date of termination until the date he returns to work? [Para 4][Para 245]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the termination was union discrimination; a member of another union told him on 6 May 2016 to resign from the Cambodia Union or his contract would not be renewed; he was elected deputy president on 23 April 2016 and the union notified the employer by post; he never slept at work but was ill with intestinal fever on 23 March 2016 and, refused leave, rested in the carton section. [Para 2][Para 75][Para 83][Para 84][Para 85][Para 88][Para 89][Para 90]
**Employer (respondent):** it discriminates against no union and cannot take him back because orders have fallen and his fixed-duration contract expired; the collective agreement keeps contracts at three months renewable indefinitely; and he committed serious misconduct — bringing alcohol in on 1 September 2015 and sleeping at work on 23 March 2016 — for which he was warned on 7 September 2015. [Para 3][Para 5][Para 77][Para 78][Para 80][Para 81][Para 35]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to take Mr N back to work and to pay his wages from the date of termination until the date of his return; because this is a rights dispute the order is binding and final immediately, without any right of objection. [Para 248][Para 249][Para 250][Para 124]

*Threshold.* Under Article 268 of the Labour Law a professional organisation acquires rights only when its statutes and officers are registered, and this union had not applied, so Mr N could not represent its members; under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 only the two workers present, Mr N and Ms C, were claimants. [Para 103][Para 108][Para 110][Para 112][Para 113] The claims turn on the application of legal provisions and the collective agreement, so they are rights disputes under Article 312, decided by law rather than equity, and the resulting award is binding. [Para 125][Para 133][Para 24] The employer argued the collective agreement of 18 February 2016 bound only the Union for Labour Rights' members; the panel found Articles 3 and 11 make it cover the whole company, both parties accepted no worker had opted out under Article 8, and the employer's own reliance on Article 9 confirmed that. [Para 115][Para 116][Para 118][Para 120][Para 123]

*Issue 1.* Article 67(2) forbids a fixed-duration contract exceeding two years and allows renewal only within that total; Article 73(5) converts the contract to undetermined duration where notice is absent or the total exceeds the Article 67 limit. [Para 136][Para 137][Para 140][Para 150][Para 152] The panel noted the law's preference for job security, citing ILO Recommendation No. 166 as context. [Para 142][Para 146][Para 147] Article 9 of the collective agreement — three-month fixed contracts renewable indefinitely with 5% each time — contradicts those provisions and is void under Article 13(1), which nullifies rules arising from unilateral decision, contract or agreement contrary to the law. [Para 158][Para 162][Para 163][Para 167] As Mr N's contracts aggregated more than two years, his contract had converted, so the ending of it was a dismissal, not a non-renewal. [Para 179][Para 180] Article 293 and Article 3 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 protect union leaders and candidates from dismissal without the labour inspector's prior consent, for 45 days before and after the election; following case 40/10, protection continues at least 45 days after the vote or until registration is applied for. [Para 182][Para 183][Para 186][Para 188] Mr N was dismissed on 6 May 2016, within 45 days of the 23 April 2016 election, and the union had notified his candidacy by post, a valid means the panel treated as effective although the employer refused delivery. [Para 208][Para 211][Para 214][Para 220][Para 224] No evidence showed the employer sought the inspector's authorisation, and under Article 295 such a dismissal is void; it also failed to prove that six participants were off the payroll or that the thumbprints were false, the panel declining to examine thumbprints as outside its competence. [Para 201][Para 203][Para 205][Para 206][Para 228][Para 240] Reinstatement was ordered under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099, and back wages with it, the second claim also being a rights dispute. [Para 241][Para 244][Para 246][Para 249]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive fixed-duration contracts whose aggregate exceeds two years convert by operation of Article 67(2) and Article 73(5) into a contract of undetermined duration, and a collective-agreement clause purporting to keep workers on three-month contracts indefinitely is void under Article 13(1); ending such a contract is a dismissal, not a non-renewal. Where the worker is a union leader or election candidate within the 45-day protection of Article 293 and Article 3 of Prakas No. 305, the dismissal is void without the labour inspector's prior authorisation, and the panel will order reinstatement with wages from dismissal to return. [Para 150][Para 152][Para 167][Para 180][Para 183][Para 228][Para 240][Para 247]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations on the law's preference for job security and on ILO Recommendation No. 166 as non-binding context are explanatory only, as is its refusal to examine the authenticity of thumbprints on the election record, which it put down to lack of competence rather than to the merits. [Para 143][Para 148][Para 205]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The document is not a single award: it prints the award, then an annexed dissent by arbitrator Ing Sothy under Article 37 of Prakas No. 099, objecting only to the reasoning on issue 2 — that the contract stayed fixed-duration under Article 9, that 5% was paid at each of the first eight endings, and that the worker had eight hours a day free to earn elsewhere, so no back wages were due. [Para 253][Para 254][Para 258][Para 259] Reported, not adopted.
- The award date is 22 July 2016 on its face [Para 1] and in the closing line [Para 263], yet the parties are recorded as postponing the award from 29 June to 14 July 2016. [Para 25]
- The panel is said to have been constituted on "០៩ ខែមិថុនា ឆ្នាំ ២០២១ ២០១៦" — two years for one event [Para 16]; Prakas No. 099 is dated "២០១៩ ២០០៤" at [Para 129].
- The worker is one person under many spellings — "ញ៉ាន តយ", "ញ៉ាន តន", "ញ៉ាន គយ", "ញ៉ាន ឥយ" — rendered here as Mr N. [Para 2][Para 73][Para 77][Para 180]
- The company name varies between "នីច ធី អី ភែរីល", "នីចធី អ៊ីភែរីល", "Nex.T Apparel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd" and "នីច-ធី". [Para 1][Para 41][Para 34]
- The election record shows 18 attendees at [Para 97] while the employer objected that six were absent from the payroll [Para 199]; the CBA registration number appears as "៥២៨" at [Para 21] and "៥២៨៩" at [Para 67]. Paragraphs [Para 89], [Para 123], [Para 222] and [Para 235]–[Para 237] contain long runs of untranscribable OCR text or substituted words.
