# Award No. 182/16 — Yida Menuver K Chery Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 23 August 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Inq Sothy (employer-appointed, dissenting in part), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Aing Eng Thong (chair) · Doc ID 139082

## 1. Facts
Yida Menuver K Chery Co Ltd, a garment factory at Chheu Teal, Kien Svay, Kandal, employed about 1,650 workers [Para 52]. A Cambodia Union Federation local was elected on 8 May 2016, with Ms U returned as secretary; the union had not yet applied to register with the Ministry [Para 53][Para 54]. The company ended Ms U's employment on 4 June 2016, and she claims the dismissal was anti-union discrimination and that no written contract of her own was ever given to her [Para 60][Para 66][Para 94]. Two workers attended the hearing and, having transferred authority to no one, were treated as the claimants [Para 103][Para 104]. The parties chose a non-binding award [Para 18].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the factory must take back Ms U as union secretary, because her dismissal was anti-union discrimination? [Para 2]
2. Whether the factory must pay her wages from the date of dismissal until the date she returns to work? [Para 3]

The panel recorded three points in dispute; point 3, that the factory must not pressure other union leaders to resign, was settled in conciliation and the parties asked for it to be written into the award, while points 1 and 2 were merged into a single question [Para 15][Para 16][Para 17][Para 97].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Ms U committed no fault; the company stopped her fixed-term contract because she stood for the union, and an administration officer told her on 24 May 2016 that she must resign from the union because she was "inciting workers to strike"; after the probation period no written contract was signed, so her contract became indefinite-term [Para 61][Para 62][Para 66][Para 92][Para 93].

**Employer (respondent):** her work performance had been poor over the last two or three months, so the company did not renew her contract; there was no discrimination, and it never forced her to resign from the union [Para 63][Para 64][Para 95][Para 206].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted on the claim as reformulated — the employer was ordered to take Ms U back and pay her wages from the date of dismissal until she returns to work; the protection against dismissal for union office was found not to apply, but the dismissal was still without a valid reason [Para 199][Para 215][Para 216].

On contract type, Article 67(7) requires a fixed-term contract to be in writing; where it is not, the contract becomes one of indefinite duration [Para 105][Para 106]. The three-month contract produced by the employer bore no thumbprint or signature of Ms U, and the employer conceded it had been signed unilaterally and that the worker may never have received it [Para 117][Para 118][Para 121]. The 5% contract-end payment made every three months did not convert the relationship back into fixed-term, since form, not payment, is what characterises such a contract [Para 122][Para 123][Para 124]. On her start date the panel preferred the payroll and identity-card evidence over the probation contract, and fixed it at 5 October 2015 [Para 131][Para 132][Para 133].

On discrimination, Article 62 of the Trade Union Law and Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law forbid an employer from using union membership or office as a ground of decision [Para 134][Para 136][Para 139]. The burden lay on the worker, and she offered only her own account; the witness to the resignation letter was not produced [Para 146][Para 155][Para 156]. The panel therefore found no anti-union discrimination [Para 157].

On protected status, Article 43(1) of the Trade Union Law and Article 3 of Prakas No. 305 require three cumulative conditions for a candidate or officer to be protected from dismissal [Para 159][Para 165][Para 168]. The first was met, as she was an elected secretary; the second was met, as the dismissal fell 28 days after the election and so within the 45-day window [Para 176][Para 177][Para 178][Para 179]. The third failed: the union's notice of the election result reached the company only on 30 June 2016, after the dismissal, because of a postal delay that was neither the worker's fault nor the employer's burden [Para 190][Para 191][Para 192][Para 197][Para 198].

Even so, a dismissal needs a valid reason related to the worker's aptitude or conduct or the needs of the enterprise under Article 74 [Para 200][Para 202]. The employer's assertion of poor performance was unsupported by any evidence, so no valid reason existed [Para 208][Para 209]. Under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 the panel ordered reinstatement and back wages [Para 211][Para 215][Para 216]. Arbitrator Inq Sothy agreed with the order but recorded a dissent, observing that the 5% contract-end indemnity is payable only on termination of a fixed-term contract and that the employer should be able to recover what it had already paid [Para 220][Para 224][Para 225].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-term contract that the employer alone signs, without the worker's signature or thumbprint and without delivering a copy, is not a written fixed-term contract at all, so the relationship is indefinite-term from the start and dismissal requires a valid reason under Article 74 [Para 106][Para 121][Para 209]. Union protection against dismissal, however, depends on the employer actually receiving notice of the candidate's or officer's status before the dismissal; a postal delay that delivers the notice afterwards defeats the third condition [Para 168][Para 192][Para 198].

**Obiter dicta:** the dissent's view that the 5% contract-end indemnity belongs only to fixed-term contracts and that an employer may reclaim it where the contract proves indefinite-term was not adopted by the majority and did not affect the order [Para 224][Para 225].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award carries a dissenting opinion by the employer-appointed arbitrator on its face, which is unusual and easy to miss [Para 1]. The union election is dated 8 May 2016 but paragraph 175 leaves the month to a duplicated "ខែ ខែឧសភា"; the dismissal is dated 4 June 2016 at paras 60 and 145 but the panel's summary at para 134 leaves the month as a single letter. The identity card is read as "10/05/2015" and construed as 5 October 2015, not 10 May [Para 69][Para 129]; the panel's comparison card shows "11/18/2015" and is read as 18 November 2015 [Para 128]. The claim that the union had not registered with the Ministry [Para 53] sits awkwardly with paragraph 175, which treats the election as complete; both are reported. Paragraph 224 prints the statutory rate as "២០ ០៥ ភាគរយ" (20 and 05 per cent) where Article 73(6) fixes 5 per cent. The company name is spelled four different ways across the document. No sum in riel or USD is stated; the order is expressed as back wages from dismissal to return.
