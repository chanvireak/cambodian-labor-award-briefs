# Award No. 115/16 — Cambodia Beer (Khmer Brew) (Arbitration Council, 30 June 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouchhun, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Kong Phallha, president) · non-binding award with a dissenting opinion by An Non on issue 2 · Doc ID 139036

## 1. Facts
Cambodia Beer (Khmer Brew) is a Phnom Penh brewery, registered since 1995, employing about 539 workers. [Para 93][Para 94] The Food and Service Workers' Federation brought five demands on 25 March 2016; the daycare demand was settled at the hearing, the demand to nullify a maternity-related notice was withdrawn, and three demands remained: conversion of fixed-duration contracts, reinstatement of 12 dismissed beer promoters, and maternity benefits for one promoter. [Para 22][Para 29][Para 30][Para 31] The 12 were dismissed after they declined new working hours (14:00–21:00 changed to 16:00–23:00) and took part in a strike from 15 to 20 January 2016. [Para 136][Para 150]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must convert fixed-duration employment contracts into contracts of undetermined duration for 25 workers who have worked more than 02 years? [Para 201][Para 206]
2. Whether the employer must take back the 12 dismissed workers and pay their wages from the date of dismissal to the date of return? [Para 229][Para 131]
3. Whether the employer must pay 100 per cent maternity leave pay for 90 days, and 400 USD from the Angkor Beer workers' fund for actual health-care invoices, to Ms S? [Para 395]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer renews one-year fixed-duration contracts indefinitely without paying the 5 per cent end-of-contract indemnity or outstanding annual leave, so the total duration exceeds two years and the contracts become indefinite; the 12 were dismissed for striking; Ms S, an illiterate promoter, thumbprinted a document she could not read and was then locked out. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 118][Para 150]
**Employer (respondent):** The company uses both contract types according to its needs and pays the 5 per cent indemnity and leave only where it itself ends the relationship; the 11 workers' contracts expired on 31 December 2015 and Ms S's on 08 January 2016, before the strike, so there was no dismissal, and the terminations were not retaliation for union activity. [Para 105][Para 107][Para 128][Para 160][Para 334]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 1 was granted for the 13 workers still employed and declined for the 12 no longer employed, the employer being ordered to convert their fixed-duration contracts to contracts of undetermined duration; issue 2 was dismissed as to reinstatement, with compensation ordered instead under Articles 75, 89, 91, 166 and 116; issue 3 was dismissed in full. [Para 228][Para 389][Para 390][Para 391][Para 392][Para 393][Para 394][Para 415]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 67(2) and Article 73(5) of the Labour Law: a fixed-duration contract may not exceed two years, renewals may not extend the total beyond two years, and an unlawful renewal converts the contract into one of undetermined duration. [Para 206][Para 208][Para 213] The employer did not dispute the start dates or total service of the 25 listed workers, and the panel found 13 still employed whose cumulative duration exceeded two years, ordering conversion for them; for the 12 whose employment had ended it declined to rule, since no relationship remained. [Para 109][Para 224][Para 228]

On issue 2 the panel accepted that the 11 promoters continued working from 01 to 14 January 2016 — a fact the employer had conceded in Award No. 012/16 — so their contracts became indefinite under Article 67(2), and the ending of employment on 18 January 2016 as recorded in the conciliation minutes was a dismissal, not expiry of a fixed term. [Para 243][Para 245][Para 249][Para 251] It found the dismissal unsupported by any valid reason relating to the workers' aptitude or the operation of the enterprise under Article 74. [Para 348][Para 349] Because the employer stated flatly that it would not reinstate them, the panel declined reinstatement and instead awarded notice pay, dismissal damages, moral damages, unused annual leave and final wages. [Para 350][Para 389][Para 390] The claims that the dismissals punished strike participation (Articles 332, 333) and amounted to anti-union discrimination (Articles 12, 279) were rejected for want of evidence. [Para 312][Para 313][Para 336]

On issue 3 the panel found that Ms S gave birth on 18 March 2016, after the employment relationship had ended, so she was no longer a worker entitled to maternity pay under Article 183 or to the internal-rules benefit of 100 per cent; by clause 13 of the Angkor Beer workers' fund statute, a member whose contract ends loses membership and the 400 USD claim. [Para 403][Para 404][Para 412][Para 414][Para 415]

**Dissent:** Arbitrator An Non dissented on issue 2, holding that where a dismissal is without valid reason the claim for reinstatement and back wages should be granted rather than replaced by compensation, and that the employer's refusal to reinstate could not itself justify refusing the remedy. [Para 428][Para 433][Para 441]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive renewals of a fixed-duration contract that bring total service beyond two years convert the contract into one of undetermined duration by operation of Article 67(2), whether or not the worker was paid the 5 per cent indemnity, and the conversion binds the employer for workers still employed at the date of the award. [Para 220][Para 224][Para 227] Where such a converted contract is ended, the ending is a dismissal requiring a valid reason under Article 74, and the panel will award the Article 75, 89, 91, 166 and 116 entitlements even where it declines reinstatement. [Para 277][Para 349][Para 390]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982 discourages fixed-duration contracts for work of long duration, while accepting that the recommendation is not binding. [Para 215][Para 217]

The union's registration date is 17 September 2007 (lines 97 and 193) but 12 March 2007 at line 76. The dismissal date is variously 31 December 2015, 33 August 2015 (line 231), 18 January 2016 (line 251) and "31 December 2016" (line 249). The group is described as 12 workers, 11 workers and once as "១២៦ នាក់" (line 416). The panel's own decision on issue 1 first "declines to consider" the claim for the 12 then orders conversion for 13, without reconciling the two lists. The service table at lines 108–109 is largely destroyed, and lines 300–304 quote a different award (No. 306/15, Sung Yin) inside the reasoning. Individual workers, including the maternity claimant, are redacted here.
