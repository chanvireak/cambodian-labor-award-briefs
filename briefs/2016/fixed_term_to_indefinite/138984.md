# Award No. 029/16 — Gret On Neor Tech Style Factory Limited (Arbitration Council, 22 March 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (president) · Doc ID 138984

## 1. Facts
Gret On Neor Tech Style Factory Limited (ហ្គ្រេត អន នើរ ថេក ស្តាយ ហ្វេក ធើរី លីមីធីត) weaves sweaters for export and employed about 1,200 workers at the relevant time [Para 70][Para 71]. Three federations brought a collective dispute; conciliation left 07 points unresolved, and one of those (point 2, the certificate of service) was settled at the first hearing, leaving six for adjudication [Para 19][Para 20][Para 25]. From 11 January 2016 the employer suspended part of the workforce by section without the labour inspector's prior authorisation, paying 50% wages; the workers also alleged unilateral transfers between sections and the dismissal of Mr S on 17 June 2015 for an alleged insult to a supervisor [Para 89][Para 112][Para 151][Para 153].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must terminate the workers' contracts and pay five heads of compensation (final wages, notice, dismissal damages, moral damages and unused annual leave)? [Para 80][Para 154]
2. (Settled at hearing — certificate of service; not adjudicated.) [Para 25]
3. Whether the employer must pay 100% wages during the unlawful suspension, refrain from deducting annual leave without the workers' agreement, and refund the January 2016 leave deductions? [Para 176][Para 177]
4. Whether the employer must add one day of annual leave for every three years of service and make that good retrospectively from 2007? [Para 213][Para 224]
5. Whether the employer must convert unused annual leave into cash each year while the workers remain employed? [Para 225][Para 237]
6. Whether the 240 workers suspended for three months in 2010 may still claim wages, the claim having arisen more than three years earlier? [Para 238][Para 243]
7. Whether the dismissal of Mr S was for a valid reason, and if not, what compensation is owed? [Para 248][Para 283]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer has no work to give, older workers cannot keep pace, and machines are being removed, so the workers have lost confidence and want their contracts terminated with the five statutory heads of compensation paid while they continue to work; the suspension was unlawful and annual leave was deducted without agreement, so full wages and a refund are due, plus the one-day-per-three-years increment [Para 82][Para 84][Para 104][Para 124][Para 234].
**Employer (respondent):** It has no plan to close and will not terminate contracts; the work shortage is seasonal in the weaving sector and suspensions were lawful business necessity; the suspension agreement of 2 December 2011 with another union governs annual leave, and the 2010 claim is stale — the dispute over Mr S is individual, not collective, and his pointing at a supervisor was gross misconduct under Article 83 [Para 85][Para 90][Para 119][Para 141][Para 146][Para 153].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the five-head termination claim (point 1), the cash-conversion of leave claim (point 5) and the 2010 suspension claim (point 6) are dismissed; the employer must pay 100% wages for the unlawful suspension from 11 January 2016, must not deduct annual leave without agreement and must refund the January 2016 deductions (point 3), must add one day of leave per three years of service retrospectively from 2007 without cash payment (point 4), and must pay Mr S notice pay (Art 75), dismissal damages (Art 89), unused leave (Arts 166–167), moral damages (Art 91) and final wages (Art 116) (point 7) [Para 312][Para 313][Para 314][Para 315][Para 316][Para 317]. The award is non-binding [Para 317].

On point 1 the panel held that Article 90 lets a worker claim dismissal damages and moral damages where the employer's bad faith drove the worker to resign, but Article 74 lets either party to an indefinite contract terminate on notice, and a worker cannot compel the employer to terminate; since the workers kept working and the employer never terminated, the claim failed [Para 156][Para 158][Para 165][Para 169][Para 175].

On point 3 the panel applied Article 71(11): suspension is lawful only under the labour inspector's supervision and for no more than two months; the authorised two-month suspension ended on 11 January 2016 and no further authorisation was possible, so the section-by-section suspension from that date was unlawful and the 50% arrangement void, entitling the workers to 100% wages [Para 179][Para 181][Para 187][Para 196][Para 197]. Separately, Article 170 requires the workers' agreement and notice to the inspector before leave is fixed outside Khmer New Year, and no evidence of either existed, so the January 2016 deductions had to be reversed [Para 199][Para 211][Para 212].

On point 4 the panel read Article 166(4) as granting one extra day per three years of service, keyed to actual seniority, so that leave rises to 19, 20, 21, 22 and 23 days by seniority band; it followed its earlier awards but ordered the increment in leave days, not money, because the workers are still serving [Para 219][Para 221][Para 224].

On point 5 the panel held Article 167(3) voids any arrangement converting leave to cash during employment, so the annual cash-out claim was contrary to the law's purpose of encouraging rest [Para 228][Para 229][Para 235][Para 237]. On point 6 it applied the three-year limitation in Article 120 to the 2010 suspension, which was by then more than five years old, and found the right extinguished [Para 243][Para 245][Para 246].

On point 7 the panel first held the matter a collective dispute, since three unions supported the claim and non-resolution could disturb the enterprise, so jurisdiction was sound [Para 261][Para 267][Para 269]. It then held the employer bears the burden of proving gross misconduct and produced no evidence that Mr S pointed at the manager rather than at goods, so the dismissal lacked a valid reason and all five heads were owed [Para 280][Para 282][Para 284][Para 311].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of work is lawful only if it stays under the labour inspector's supervision and within two months; once the authorised period expires, continued suspension by section is unlawful, the 50% wage arrangement is void, and full wages are payable for the whole unlawful period [Para 187][Para 194][Para 197]. Where an employer alleges gross misconduct under Article 83, it carries the burden and must produce evidence; absent evidence the dismissal is without valid reason and notice pay, dismissal damages, moral damages, unused leave and final wages all follow [Para 280][Para 282][Para 311].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observation that leave is meant to let workers spend time with family and in social activity, and that a cash buy-out defeats that purpose, explains but does not decide point 5 [Para 236]. It also declined to rule on whether the 2011 leave agreement with another union was lawful, because that union was not a party [Para 207][Para 208].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Names of the employer are OCR-unstable and appear in several transliterations within one document ("ហ្គ្រេត អន នើរ", "ហ្រ្វេត អន នើរ", "ហ្គ្រេតា អន នើរ"), and the panel itself notes the shorthand it will use [Para 70][Para 71]. Two internally inconsistent dates appear for the suspension: 11 January 2016 in the reasoning and operative order, but "11 December 2016" in one passage, and a stray "2017" fragment sits in the jurisdiction discussion [Para 185][Para 198][Para 266]. The award date is given consistently as 22 March 2016, with an agreed extension from 26 February 2016 recorded [Para 1][Para 35]. The 2010 suspension claimant count moves between 240 and 94 in the reasons and the order [Para 238][Para 247][Para 316]. Stray Latin and numeric fragments ("ال", "AVI", "15 ដូស") interrupt the signature and reasoning blocks [Para 301][Para 318]. The dismissed worker's name is printed as both "ស៊ិន គឹម ស៊" and "ស៊ិន គីម ស៊"; he is recorded here as Mr S.
