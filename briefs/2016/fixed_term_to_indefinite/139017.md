# Award No. 080/16 — Ubin Service Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 26 April 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborna, chosen by the employer; An Non, chosen by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, chair chosen by the two) · Doc ID 139017

## 1. Facts
Two federations — the Cambodian Labour Union Confederation and the Federation of Free Workers' Unions of Cambodia — plus the factory's local unions brought two demands for the workers of Ubin Service Co Ltd, a garment factory employing about 180 people [Para 1][Para 36]. The second demand, for workbooks (សៀវភៅការងារ), was settled at the hearing, leaving only the claim for termination entitlements and final wages for workers dismissed from early February 2016 [Para 5][Para 17][Para 18]. The company accepted that the employment contracts were of indefinite duration but said it had no further orders and no work to give, and that it could pay only final wages and unused annual leave, and only in instalments [Para 40][Para 47][Para 51].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the 32 claimant workers pay in lieu of notice, dismissal compensation, moral damages, unused annual leave and final wages after dismissing them in February 2016 for want of orders? [Para 65][Para 66]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer dismissed them on differing dates in February 2016 without paying all their wages, paying only partial sums in instalments — roughly US$95 per worker across three payments when the base wage alone was US$140, plus a US$10 attendance bonus, US$7 travel and accommodation allowance and up to US$11 seniority bonus — so the full statutory entitlements and the unpaid balance are owed [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45].
**Employer (respondent):** The company has no orders and no work, so it may pay only final wages and unused annual leave, and those only in stages because it is short of cash and has itself borrowed money to pay office staff; it asks for time to raise the rest [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50]. It also says it warned the slow workers orally, repeatedly, before dismissing them, though it issued no written notice [Para 52][Para 54][Para 72].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim for moral damages under Article 91 was rejected; the employer was ordered to pay all 32 claimants dismissal compensation under Article 89, pay in lieu of notice under Article 75, unused annual leave under Articles 166 and 167, and the balance of final wages, each according to the worker's actual length of service [Para 104][Para 105][Para 106][Para 107].
On who was a claimant, the panel applied Article 19 of Prakas No. 099/04, allowing a party to appear in person or through a person expressly authorised in writing [Para 60]. Twenty-eight workers had signed an authorisation letter dated 13 February 2016 in favour of the two federations [Para 37][Para 61]; four more attended the hearing and pressed claims of their own although their names were absent from the letter, so the panel joined them and decided the case for 32 workers [Para 39][Para 59][Para 64]. It noted the employer's January and February 2016 wage tables did not list those four at all [Para 57].
On the reason for dismissal, the panel held that having no buyer orders and no work to assign, together with the workers' slow performance, was a legitimate reason related to the operational necessities of the enterprise within Article 74 of the Labour Law, and that the workers had not contested the employer's account at the hearing [Para 70][Para 71][Para 73]. Because the termination was therefore not without a valid reason, no moral damages were due under Article 91 [Para 68][Para 74].
That finding did not displace the statutory entitlements. As the contracts were indefinite and the employer terminated them of its own will, Article 89 required dismissal compensation of 15 days' wages and allowances per year of service, capped at six months, and the employer had paid none [Para 75][Para 76][Para 79][Para 80]. Article 75 required written notice of one, two or three months according to length of service, and Article 77 substitutes pay for the period not observed; the employer conceded it gave no written notice and only told the workers orally on the day of dismissal, so pay in lieu of notice was owed [Para 81][Para 84][Para 85][Para 86]. Articles 166 and 167 entitled them to payment for annual leave accrued but not taken, only part of which had been paid [Para 92][Para 94][Para 95]. Finally, Article 116 requires wages and all allowances to be paid within 48 hours of the last working day; staged payments did not satisfy this, so the balance of final wages was ordered [Para 96][Para 97][Para 98][Para 99].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A dismissal grounded in the genuine operational needs of the enterprise, such as the loss of all buyer orders, is a valid reason under Article 74 and defeats a claim for moral damages, but it does not extinguish the entitlements that attach to termination of an indefinite-duration contract at the employer's will — dismissal compensation, pay in lieu of notice where no written notice was given, accrued annual leave and the full balance of final wages, which must be paid within 48 hours and cannot be spread over instalments [Para 73][Para 74][Para 86][Para 97].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel recorded, without resting the outcome on it, that the employer had itself borrowed money to meet the payroll of staff still working, and it acknowledged the employer's request for staged payment as a statement of cash difficulty rather than as a legal defence [Para 49][Para 50].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
OCR damage is heavy throughout, including in the company's own name, printed variously as "យូប៊ីន សឺវីស", "យូប៊ីន សីវីស" and "យូប៊ីន ស៊វី ស" [Para 1][Para 8][Para 24]. The number of claimants is unstable: the demand and the authorisation letter speak of 28, the panel decided for 32, and 10 attended the hearing with only 4 of them outside the letter [Para 3][Para 37][Para 39][Para 64]; the four joined workers do not appear in the employer's wage tables at all [Para 57]. The dismissal dates are not fixed — the award refers repeatedly to "differing dates" within February 2016 without listing them, and the table at Para 55 collapses into overlapping columns [Para 43][Para 55][Para 66]. Wage figures are internally inconsistent, with a stated average of about US$95 per worker set against a stated US$140 base wage and three separate allowances [Para 44][Para 45]. Article references are garbled in the decision section, where final wages are attributed to Article 166 instead of Article 116 [Para 103][Para 107], and the annual-leave provision is printed as "Article 167" in one place and "Article 167" with a misnumbered heading elsewhere [Para 89][Para 92]. The signature block is partly illegible [Para 109].
