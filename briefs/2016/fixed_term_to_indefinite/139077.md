# Award No. 178/16 — Ubin Service Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 18 August 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Tang Tri chair) · Doc ID 139077

## 1. Facts
Ubin Service Co Ltd is a garment factory at Steung Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employing about 160 workers. [Para 31][Para 32] In January 2016 the employer dismissed nine workers, all on indefinite contracts with long service — one had worked since 1998. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39] The employer's stated reason was that it had no work and could not continue their contracts, expressly not any fault of the workers, and it gave them no prior notice. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 55] The employer agreed to pay last wages and outstanding annual leave but only in instalments of USD 30 per worker per month, and had paid none of the other severance. [Para 3][Para 41][Para 42] There was no base union in the company, so the two federations acted on written delegations from the dismissed workers. [Para 24][Para 26][Para 36]

## 2. Issues in dispute
The workers advanced three demands; two were not conciliated and the workers then merged demands 1 and 2, so a single question remained. [Para 10][Para 11][Para 16]

1. Whether the employer must pay the nine dismissed workers their last wages and the various severance payments — notice pay, dismissal compensation, outstanding annual leave and moral damages? [Para 37][Para 48]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer dismissed them without any misconduct on their part and without the notice required by the Labour Law, so it owes the full statutory severance; the offer of USD 30 a month is not payment. [Para 45][Para 46]
**Employer (respondent):** it accepts that it dismissed the nine because it had no work and could not continue their contracts, and it does not allege fault; it says only that it is unable to pay the severance claimed. [Para 43][Para 47]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay all nine workers notice pay under Articles 75 and 77, dismissal compensation under Article 89, moral damages under Article 91, outstanding annual leave under Articles 166 and 167, and last wages under Article 116, within 48 hours counted from the date of dismissal. [Para 110][Para 113][Para 114][Para 115][Para 118][Para 119]

The panel began with Article 74: an indefinite contract may be terminated at the will of either party with written notice, but no dismissal may be made without a valid reason relating to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the needs of the enterprise. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51][Para 56] The employer had admitted that the dismissals rested on lack of work and not on any fault, and the panel treated that as the absence of a valid reason rather than a redundancy justifying termination. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 71]

Because no notice was given, Article 77 required pay in lieu equal to the wages and benefits for the notice period that Article 75 sets by seniority — 7 days below six months, 15 days from six months to two years, one month from two to five years, two months from five to ten years and three months above ten years — calculated on average monthly pay over the preceding 12 months divided by 26 for the daily rate. [Para 57][Para 60][Para 61][Para 62][Para 75] Article 89 then gives dismissal compensation of 15 days' wages and allowances per year of service, capped at six months' pay, with any period over six months counted as a full year; the panel applied that cap expressly. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 70][Para 72] Article 91 entitled the workers to moral damages equal to the dismissal compensation, and the panel noted these are not to be confused with notice pay or dismissal compensation. [Para 80][Para 85] On annual leave, Articles 166 and 167 give 1.5 working days per month of service plus one extra day for each three years, producing 19 days in years four to six, 20 in years seven to nine and 21 in years ten to twelve; the panel found the employer had begun paying but had not finished. [Para 86][Para 90][Para 92][Para 96][Para 98] Finally Article 116 requires every wage and allowance to be paid within 48 hours of the last working day, and the panel held that the USD 30 monthly instalments did not discharge that obligation. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 108][Para 109]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer ends an indefinite contract because it has no work, and expressly does not allege fault, it has shown no valid reason under Article 74 and must pay the full statutory package: notice or pay in lieu under Articles 75 and 77 according to seniority, dismissal compensation of 15 days per year of service capped at six months under Article 89, moral damages equal to that compensation under Article 91, accrued annual leave under Articles 166 and 167, and last wages within 48 hours under Article 116. An inability to pay, and a unilateral offer to pay by monthly instalments, does not vary those entitlements. [Para 56][Para 62][Para 72][Para 85][Para 108]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's arithmetic guidance — average monthly pay taken over the last 12 months and divided by 26 for the daily rate, and a 13-month worker being credited with 15 days — is illustrative of method rather than part of the order. [Para 74][Para 75][Para 79]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award date is 18 August 2016 in the header, but the panel's constitution reads "21 July 2019 year 2016" [Para 13]; the hearing was 02 August 2016 and the extension runs from 10 to 18 August 2016 [Para 9][Para 19].
- The number of complainants is stated as nine throughout the claim and the order [Para 37][Para 115], but the delegation list at [Para 33] names only eight, and [Para 36] says there are ten complainants; four attended the hearing, one of whom was not on the delegation letter [Para 34][Para 35].
- The names of the nine are printed inconsistently — "សរ សាម៉េន"/"ស សាម៉េន", "ហ៊ង ចិន្តា"/"ហ៊ន គ្រី", "សៀង ចិន្តា"/"សៀង វិជ្ជា" [Para 2][Para 33][Para 37][Para 48]; they are redacted in this brief.
- The employer's name appears as "Ubin Service", "Ubin Sivis" and "Ubin Sevis" in the same document [Para 1][Para 31], and the conciliation report number is 044 at [Para 7] but 944 at [Para 12][Para 27].
- The 48-hour period in Article 116 is written both as "48 (forty-eight) hours" [Para 108][Para 114] and as a garbled word form [Para 120]; the notice periods in Article 75 are also partly lost to OCR [Para 57][Para 59].
- The panel's dispositive order is printed twice, in slightly different wording, at [Para 110]–[Para 114] and [Para 115]–[Para 119].
