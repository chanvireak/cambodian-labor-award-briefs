# Award No. 113/16 — UD S Garmen Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council of Cambodia, 21 June 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chhea presiding; Inh Sothy appointed by the employer, Toun Si Phan appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 139034

## 1. Facts
UD S Garmen Co., Ltd (យូឌី អេស ហ្គាមេន ខូអិលធីឌី) is a garment factory at Bak Khaeng, Khan Chroy Changva, Phnom Penh, employing about 600 workers, with a company-level free workers' union registered as No. 3211 of 31 October 2014. [Para 1][Para 62]
Six claims by the union were conciliated without agreement and referred to the Council by report No. 661 of 17 May 2016; the hearing was held on 25 May 2016 and the parties chose a non-binding award.

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must repay two hours' wages to three workers and one full day's wages to two workers, deducted for their attendance at the arbitration hearing of 1 March 2016? [Para 68]
2. Whether the employer must stop photographing the union president inside the factory? [Para 87]
3. Whether the employer must allow workers to take hot water out of the infirmary for use at their work stations? [Para 98]
4. Whether the employer must pay wages and benefits for the half day of 9 April 2016, when it allowed Khmer New Year games? [Para 110]
5. Whether the fixed-duration contract of Ms T had become an indefinite-duration contract, so that the employer owes severance and other payments on ending it on 25 March 2016? [Para 121]
6. Whether the employer must pay six months' moral damages to Ms S? [Para 134]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the five workers had written leave for a full day to attend the hearing, and the employer could not cut wages after signing that permission or cancel it orally on the way out; filming the union president without her consent breaches her personality rights; the employer had previously allowed hot water to be taken out. [Para 79]
**Employer (respondent):** it kept wages for the morning of 1 March 2016 and deducted only the two hours not worked between 14:00 and 16:00; it filmed only because the union president's conduct required a record; Prakas 054 lets it set how drinking water is supplied, and taking hot water out caused litter on the roof; it announced work for the morning of 9 April by loudspeaker, which the workers admitted hearing; Ms T's contract simply expired and she was paid 5% on each expiry. [Para 83][Para 128]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 2 granted (employer ordered to stop filming Ms M); issue 5 granted (notice pay under Articles 75 and 77, dismissal damages under Article 89, moral damages under Article 91 at least equal to the dismissal damages, unused annual leave under Articles 166–167 and final wages under Article 116, with the 5% end-of-contract payments already made deducted); issues 1, 3, 4 and 6 dismissed. No riel or dollar figures are printed in the decision. [Para 337]
On issue 1 the panel applied Article 2 of the Labour Law and Article 118 (the employer proves wage payment); the wage table showed a deduction of only two hours, and under Article 664 of the Civil Code wages are the counterpart of work actually performed, so wages for hours not worked were not owed. [Para 184]
On issue 2 the panel held Article 10 of the Civil Code protects private life and personality, that being photographed is part of that right, and that filming a worker without consent is not a lawful or reasonable exercise of the management prerogative; it ordered the practice stopped under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 197][Para 205]
On issue 3 the panel read Articles 1 and 3 of Prakas No. 054 of 10 February 2000 as obliging the employer to supply safe drinking water while leaving it to fix the manner of supply, and accepted the hygiene justification for keeping hot water inside the infirmary. [Para 217]
On issue 4 the panel found Ministry Notification No. 080/16 of 31 March 2016 says employers "should" hold games "if possible", creating no duty to pay for a morning not worked; the workers admitted the loudspeaker announcement. [Para 233]
On issue 5 the panel applied Article 67(2) and Article 73(5) of the Labour Law, following Award No. 10/03: successive fixed-duration contracts whose aggregate exceeds two years become indefinite-duration. Ms T's aggregate service was about 2 years 8 months, so her contract was indefinite-duration and her ending was a dismissal, not an expiry; the employer gave 7 days' notice where Article 75 required one month, and showed no valid reason or warning, so notice pay, dismissal damages for three years' seniority, moral damages, annual leave and final wages were owed. [Para 235][Para 272][Para 288]
On issue 6 the panel found Ms S's last contract was exactly six months and expired on 23 April 2016; Article 73(5) requires notice only for contracts longer than six months, so no notice duty arose and no moral damages were due. [Para 335]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer's management prerogative under Article 2 extends to scheduling, to withholding wages for hours not worked, and to fixing how amenities such as drinking water are supplied, but only when exercised lawfully and reasonably; a measure that invades a worker's personality rights under Article 10 of the Civil Code, such as filming a worker without consent, falls outside it. [Para 172][Para 197]
Successive fixed-duration contracts whose combined duration exceeds two years become indefinite-duration by operation of Article 67(2), and ending them then requires a valid reason and the statutory notice period, failing which notice pay, dismissal damages and moral damages follow. [Para 256]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the employer had already met the hot-water concern by allowing half-hour rests in the infirmary on a section head's note, and that filming one worker alone could cause her psychological pressure. [Para 203]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The operative paragraphs print no amounts: the compensation items at paras 318 and 342 are followed by blank or "០០" placeholders, so no figure can be reported. Ms T's start date is 1 August 2013 at para 121 but "1 August 2016" at para 275, which cannot be right if she had 2 years 8 months' service by March 2016. The games day is 9 April 2016 at para 110 but "9 January 2016" later in the same paragraph. Worker counts are garbled: "០៥០០ នាក់" at para 160 and "១០៥ ខែកក្កដា នាក់" at para 183. Stray "2017" tokens interrupt paras 24, 60 and 83. The employer appears as "យូ អេស ហ្គាមេន" at para 20. Individual workers and union officials are reduced to initials; no name has been reconstructed.
