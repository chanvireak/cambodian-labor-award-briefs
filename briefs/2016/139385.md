# Award No. 234/16 — Grace Full (Cambodia) Tech Style Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 22 December 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of three — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Leav Sovanna (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (chair) · Doc ID 139385

## 1. Facts
A garment factory at Tramang, Takeo, employing about 882 workers [Para 42]. The plant union was registered on 26 February 2016 [Para 43]. On 10 September 2016 its president, Mr B, was warned in writing for sewing a wrong shirt, refused to sign the warning, and was sent home at the lunch break; from 15 September the employer would not let him in [Para 49][Para 50][Para 59]. The secretary, Mr V, had been dismissed on 6 July 2016 and then re-engaged on a second two-month probation contract, but no department would accept him [Para 68][Para 69][Para 71].

## 2. Issues in dispute
Eight demands were filed; at the hearing the workers withdrew issues 2 to 8, leaving one [Para 27][Para 28].
1. Whether the employer must take back Mr B (union president) and Mr V (union secretary) and pay their wages and other benefits from the date of dismissal until reinstatement? [Para 102]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer dismissed the two men without following the procedure in the Labour Law and discriminated against them because they led the union [Para 45][Para 46]. Mr B never abandoned his job: he tried to enter the factory between 11 and 22 September and was barred [Para 52]. Mr V is willing to work in any section the employer assigns except sanitation [Para 74].
**Employer (respondent):** Mr B was absent without leave for twelve days, and under the internal rules absence over three days amounts to abandonment [Para 53]. Mr V did no work under the second probation contract because no section would take him, and no decision to dismiss him had been taken [Para 71][Para 73].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer must reinstate Mr B and pay his wages and benefits from the dismissal until he returns, and must give Mr V suitable work in any section with wages and benefits from the date work was withheld [Para 206][Para 207].

On standing, the panel held the union registered under Article 11 of the Trade Union Law could act for its members, the employer having acknowledged the three leaders who attended, even though no most-representative union exists at the factory [Para 99][Para 100].

On Mr B's contract, the parties gave conflicting start dates, 16 March 2014 and 16 March 2015 [Para 118]. The panel applied the rule that the party asserting bears the burden, found the employer produced neither the contracts nor the internal rules it was ordered to file, and accepted 2014 [Para 125][Para 128]. Total service therefore exceeded two years, so under Article 67(2) the successive fixed-duration contracts became an indefinite-duration contract [Para 131][Para 132].

On the character of the separation, the panel noted that the Labour Law has no concept of abandonment and that abandonment must be shown from the actual circumstances, following Award 33/14 [Para 140][Para 141]. The employer filed no internal rules, so its oral assertion could not establish that the twelve-day absence was abandonment [Para 145][Para 147]. Since the employer had stated plainly that it would not take Mr B back, the panel found a dismissal effective 15 September 2016 [Para 149][Para 150].

That dismissal breached Article 43 of the Trade Union Law, which requires the labour inspector's consent before a union leader is dismissed, and Article 67 of the same law [Para 151][Para 163]. No request for consent and no decision were produced, so reinstatement with back pay was ordered [Para 164][Para 167].

On Mr V, the panel found the second probation contract was performed and the employment relationship came into being, the employer having admitted he came to the factory [Para 174][Para 177]. The failure to work came from the employer's side, which knew when it signed that it might have no post for him [Para 193][Para 196]. Under Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 664 of the Civil Code, and following Awards 65/15 and 2/16, wages stay payable where the employer withholds work [Para 188][Para 194]. Suitable work and wages from the date work was withheld were ordered [Para 205].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer withholds work for reasons within its own control, the worker's failure to perform does not extinguish the wage claim; the employer must provide work and pay. An employer that bars a worker from the premises and states it will not take the worker back has dismissed, not suffered abandonment, and a claim of abandonment cannot rest on internal rules the employer never produces. Dismissing a union leader without the labour inspector's prior consent is unlawful, and reinstatement with back wages follows.

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that successive fixed-duration contracts reflect a legislative preference for indefinite-duration employment, and that ILO Recommendation 166, though not binding, informs Article 67 [Para 111][Para 116].
