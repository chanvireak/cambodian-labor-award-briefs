# Award No. 072/16 — Seav Kinh Garment (IMF) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 April 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mr Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; Mr An Non, appointed by the workers; Mr Tang Try, chair) · Doc ID 139010

## 1. Facts
Seav Kinh Garment (IMF) cut and sewed garments and employed about 560 workers. [Para 34] A factory union received its registration receipt from the Ministry of Labour and Vocational Training on 05 February 2016, and the union federation held transfers of representation rights from 168 workers dated 01 February 2016. [Para 35] [Para 37] Conciliation settled 8 of 11 demands, leaving three for the panel. [Para 14] [Para 15] The three concerned the $10 monthly attendance bonus during certified sick leave, 100% wages when the employer sends workers home because there is no work, seniority recorded in the work books (សៀវភៅការងារ), and payslips issued before payday. [Para 40] [Para 42] [Para 52] [Para 58] In 2013 the parties had agreed to pay severance once and to restart seniority from 2013. [Para 52] [Para 101]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must keep paying the full attendance bonus when a worker is on certified sick leave, and pay 100% of wages when it has no work for the worker during a short period? [Para 40]
2. Whether the employer must not erase seniority from the work books and must repay the seniority compensation it underpaid? [Para 52]
3. Whether the employer must issue payslips 2 or 3 days before the monthly payday? [Para 58]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that the Labour Law forbids deducting wages during sick leave certified by a doctor — though the representative could not name the article — that attendance bonus should be preserved in full for the month, that seniority must be entered in each work book from the true first day of employment, and that advance payslips are a legal duty and are needed by workers who have already left. [Para 43] [Para 44] [Para 53] [Para 60] [Para 61]
**Employer (respondent):** that the $10 attendance bonus is conditional on regular attendance, so it cannot be paid in full when a worker is absent; that the accounting system is handwritten and payslips are issued at payment with an opportunity to complain afterwards; and that it does not know what each work book records, but would file copies for the panel. [Para 45] [Para 54] [Para 62] [Para 63]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim for the full attendance bonus during sick leave was dismissed, and so were the seniority and advance-payslip claims; the employer was ordered to pay 100% of wages, not 50%, whenever it sends workers home for lack of work in a short period without the labour inspector's authorisation. [Para 123] [Para 124] [Para 125] [Para 126]
On the attendance bonus, the panel treated Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 as an incentive to regular attendance rather than a wage, and held that a worker absent on certified sick leave should not receive the full bonus but should have it reduced in proportion to the days absent — the fairest balance between the purpose of the bonus and the law's protection of workers' health. [Para 64] [Para 66] [Para 70] [Para 71] [Para 72] It followed its earlier reasoning in Award No. 186/13 — LC Garment and the awards cited there. [Para 65] [Para 73]
On the 100% wage claim the panel applied Article 71(11) and Article 72(1): suspending a contract requires that the enterprise genuinely be in economic difficulty and that the suspension be placed under the labour inspector's supervision, which means written notice to the inspector and the inspector's verification. [Para 78] [Para 79] [Para 80] [Para 81] Sending workers home on 50% pay without that authorisation is an unlawful suspension, and 100% wages follow even though there is no work to do. [Para 85] [Para 89] [Para 92] [Para 93]
On seniority, Articles 32, 34 and 37 and article 04 of Prakas No. 414 of 23 March 2005 require the work book to record the date of first employment, position, wage and successive contracts. [Para 94] [Para 100] The 16 work books filed by the employer recorded no first-employment date at all, only issue dates in 2010, 2011 and 2012. [Para 56] [Para 105] The worker who asserts bears the burden of proof, and the 172 claimants produced nothing to contradict the books, so the claim was refused consideration. [Para 103] [Para 107] [Para 108] [Para 109]
On payslips, Article 112 requires the employer to inform workers clearly about their wages for each pay period; it does not require that information to precede payday. [Para 110] [Para 116] [Para 118] Payslips issued at payment, with a right to complain about shortfalls, satisfy the article. [Para 120] [Para 121] [Para 122]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus is a conditional incentive, not wages, so it may be reduced in proportion to days of certified sick leave without breaching wage protection. Sending workers home without work at half pay is a suspension of the employment contract, and a suspension is lawful only where the enterprise is genuinely in economic difficulty and the labour inspector has been notified and has verified it; without that authorisation the employer owes full wages. Article 112 requires clear wage information, not its delivery before payday. [Para 72] [Para 81] [Para 85] [Para 118]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that a payslip itemising the wage components is a transparency measure that removes workers' suspicion about their pay, and its encouragement of the employer's stated willingness to find suitable alternative work during slack periods. [Para 90] [Para 117]
