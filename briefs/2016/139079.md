# Award No. 177/16 — GHI Garmen (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 18 August 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Seng Vouch Hun (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Sok Martheng (chair) · Doc ID 139079

## 1. Facts
GHI Garmen (Cambodia) Ltd, a garment factory at Prek Ho, Ta Khmau, Kandal, told the panel it employed about 700 workers [Para 66]. It ended the contracts of its workforce in two phases — 426 workers on 30 June 2016 and 200 on 31 July 2016 — after buyers withdrew orders following a February–March 2016 strike and alleged sewing-quality failures [Para 76][Para 83][Para 147]. Some 196 workers transferred authority to two lawyers and claimed severance and benefits under the Labour Law [Para 67][Para 72]. The panel treated the dispute as a non-binding award chosen by the parties [Para 20].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must take back all the workers and employees it dismissed, and if not, whether it must pay their wages and other benefits under the Labour Law? [Para 3]
2. Whether the employer must pay severance and other benefits under the Labour Law to the 196 workers whose contracts it ended on 30 June 2016? [Para 72]

Issue 1 was withdrawn by the worker party at the hearing, so the panel decided only issue 2 [Para 18][Para 19].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** of the 196 claimants, 147 had served more than two years and therefore held indefinite-term contracts whatever the paper said; they were owed notice pay, dismissal indemnity, moral damages and final wages, and the July 2016 suspension of work was unlawful so full wages were due [Para 73][Para 74][Para 121][Para 214].

**Employer (respondent):** every contract was ended at its expiry date, not broken early [Para 79]; the company had already paid 5% contract-end indemnity, outstanding annual leave and seven days' notice in lieu [Para 81]; and the closure flowed from the loss of orders, not from any fault of the workers [Para 147].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — claims for notice pay and moral damages by the 49 fixed-term workers were rejected, and the employer was ordered to pay dismissal indemnity, moral damages equal to that indemnity, notice-in-lieu and final wages to the 147 indefinite-term workers, computed per the tenure annex [Para 280][Para 281][Para 282].

On contract type, the panel applied Article 67(2): a fixed-term contract cannot run beyond two years and successive renewals beyond that ceiling convert it into an indefinite-term contract [Para 123][Para 125]. Parties agreed pre-2014 contracts were six-monthly and thereafter three-monthly, with identity cards changed and a short break every two years [Para 89][Para 91]. Because wages continued and seniority was preserved across those breaks, the panel found no interruption of the employment relationship, and held the 147 workers who had served over two years to be indefinite-term [Para 140][Para 142]. The 49 workers under two years were fixed-term [Para 115][Para 122].

On the closure defence, Article 87(3) does not release an employer from Part 3 obligations except in force majeure [Para 143]. Loss of buyer orders is not force majeure, so the employer remained liable for severance [Para 148].

On the fixed-term group, Article 73 requires no advance notice for contracts shorter than six months [Para 163]; the seven days' notice pay already paid exceeded the legal minimum, so that claim and the related moral damages were refused [Para 168][Para 169][Para 180][Para 206].

On July 2016, the employer suspended contracts from 1 to 31 July and paid only 50% wages without labour-inspectorate authorisation [Para 212][Para 226]. Article 71 requires such suspension to be supervised by the labour inspector; the panel therefore ordered 100% wages for that month [Para 227].

For the 147, dismissal without a valid reason attracted Article 89 indemnity at 7 or 15 days' wages by seniority, capped at six months, plus Article 91 moral damages of the same amount, Article 75/77 notice pay and Article 116 final wages [Para 243][Para 257][Para 269][Para 271][Para 282].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive fixed-term contracts whose total duration exceeds two years, where the employer keeps paying wages and preserves seniority across the breaks between them, are indefinite-term contracts from the outset, so termination attracts dismissal indemnity, moral damages and statutory notice rather than simple expiry [Para 125][Para 142]. An employer that suspends work without labour-inspectorate supervision owes 100% of wages for the suspended month, and a shutdown caused by lost orders does not escape Part 3 severance obligations [Para 148][Para 227].

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that the Cambodian Labour Law shows a "soft" preference for indefinite-term contracts, and that ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982, though non-binding, is useful context for reading Article 67, was not necessary to the outcome [Para 127][Para 131][Para 133].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR is badly degraded. The award is dated 18 August 2016 in the header [Para 1], but paragraph 22 records an agreed extension "to 18 August 2016" from a date whose day and month were lost ("៣ ថ្ងៃទី ០៥ ខែ ឆ្នាំ ២០១៦") [Para 22]. Headcount is printed three ways: 450 workers terminated (paras 5, 30, 72), 426 plus 200 in two phases (para 76), and 196 claimants (para 72). Paragraph 231 speaks of a right to dismiss "40" workers where the analysis concerns 147. The employer representative appears as "Cao Bei", "Cao Bel" and "Cao Beiber" (paras 2, 24, 64), and is rendered as "Cao Bei" here. The labour-ministry reconciliation report is cited as No. 1197/16 with three different suffix forms ("ក.ប/កណ", "ក.ប/ភណ", "ក.ប/ភណ") (paras 9, 14, 59). Duplicated years ("២០១៩ ២០១៦") appear at paras 15 and 208, and "១៣ ខែ" at para 120 appears to be a corruption of three months. The name of the first-instance court at para 51 is garbled ("សាលាដំបង"). The tenure annex (paras 284–289) is largely unreadable, so no total sum in riel or USD can be reported; the award orders payment by reference to that annex [Para 281].
