# Award No. 283/15 — M&V International Garment (Branches 1 and 3) (Arbitration Council, 9 December 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kav Thach for the employer party, Tun Si Phan for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139459

## 1. Facts
M&V International Garment operates branches 1 and 3 at Prek Ta Loung, Chak Angre Krom, Phnom Penh, with 972 workers in branch 3 and 315 in branch 1. [Para 43][Para 54][Para 55] On 23 September 2015 the company asked the labour inspectorate for permission to suspend contracts for lack of work, and on 22 October 2015 the inspectorate authorised a two-month suspension from 1 November to 31 December 2015; notice went to the workers on 26 October 2015. [Para 21][Para 54][Para 57][Para 58] Under an agreement of 20 October 2015 with five unions the company paid USD 50 a month as an accommodation allowance during the suspension. [Para 56][Para 59]

## 2. Issues in dispute
One claim remained unresolved — holiday wages and the seniority bonus for the suspension period — which the panel split into two questions. [Para 8][Para 9][Para 48][Para 65]
1. Whether the employer suspended the contracts lawfully from 1 November to 31 December 2015? [Para 65][Para 75]
2. Whether the employer must therefore pay full wages for the five public holidays in that period (9, 24, 25 and 26 November and 10 December) and the seniority bonus? [Para 49][Para 79][Para 87]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** a worker is paid in full for a public holiday even without working it, and Prakas No. 10 of 4 February 1999 on pay for work on public holidays secures that right; art 72(3) provides that suspension time counts for seniority and no rule says otherwise, so the seniority bonus must be paid notwithstanding the suspension. The USD 50 covers accommodation only. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 59][Para 62][Para 78]
**Employer (respondent):** the suspension followed the procedure — application on 23 September 2015, authorisation on 22 October 2015, notice on 26 October — so under arts 71 and 72 the only duties left are the agreed travel and accommodation allowance of USD 50 a month, and neither holiday wages nor the seniority bonus is owed. [Para 54][Para 57][Para 60][Para 61][Para 63][Para 64]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the claim for public holiday wages and the seniority bonus over 1 November to 31 December 2015 was refused. [Para 95][Para 96]

On the lawfulness of the suspension the panel followed Award No. 08/07 — Siv Ky, reading art 71(11) to require both notice to and prior authorisation from the labour inspectorate before a suspension is lawful, and distinguishing a covered suspension, which is de jure. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 69] The authorisation letter of 22 October 2015 covered 315 workers in branch 1 and 972 in branch 3, in buildings A and C, for two months, and the requirement was met. [Para 75][Para 76] Article 72(1) then applies: the suspension removes the employer's duty to pay wages, so nothing was owed for the period, and the earlier awards cited confirm that a procedurally correct suspension leaves no wage obligation while a defective one leaves the employer owing 100%. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74][Para 77]

On holiday pay the panel read arts 1 and 2 of Prakas No. 10 as presupposing an enterprise operating normally, or at least one that cannot stop, and held they do not reach a factory whose activity is wholly suspended: where the workers need not work at all, the employer owes no further rest day, including paid public holidays, during the suspension. [Para 80][Para 82][Para 83][Para 84][Para 85] Holiday wages were therefore not due. [Para 86]

On the seniority bonus the panel accepted that art 72(3) keeps the suspension period counting towards seniority and does not interrupt it, but observed that the article says nothing about paying the bonus during the suspension. [Para 87][Para 88] Since the seniority bonus is a component of wages under art 103 and the earlier awards, and no wages are payable during a lawful suspension, the bonus was not owed either. [Para 90][Para 91][Para 94]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of contracts authorised in advance by the labour inspectorate under art 71(11), for a period within the two months the article allows and notified to the workers, is a de jure suspension: art 72(1) then extinguishes the employer's duty to pay wages for its duration, and the only sums owed are those separately agreed, such as an accommodation or travel allowance. [Para 67][Para 75][Para 77]
Pay for public holidays under Prakas No. 10 of 4 February 1999 is not owed during such a suspension, because the Prakas presupposes an enterprise running normally, and a wholly suspended factory owes no further paid rest days. [Para 82][Para 84][Para 86]
The rule in art 72(3) that suspension time counts towards seniority preserves the length of service for later calculation; it does not create a right to the seniority bonus during the suspension, since that bonus is part of wages and no wages are due then. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 94]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the purpose of Prakas No. 10 is directed at enterprises with a production line running normally, or unable to stop, is a general comment on the Prakas rather than a finding needed for the order. [Para 84]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The panel is said to have been constituted on "10 November 2019 2015" [Para 11], and the award date was extended by agreement from 3 December to 9 December 2015 [Para 16].
- The suspension end date is 31 December 2015 in the authorisation, the findings and the order [Para 57][Para 75][Para 95], but 30 December 2015 in one finding [Para 76].
- The company asked for two months in branch 1 but only one month in branch 3 [Para 24][Para 54][Para 55], while the authorisation and the award treat both branches as suspended for two months [Para 57][Para 75]; the discrepancy is unexplained.
- Branch 3 is said to hold 972 workers, which is also the figure given for the whole company [Para 43][Para 55], so the branch totals cannot both be right.
- The claim as recorded at [Para 2] is fragmentary, with stray characters and a broken sentence about "Sa workers", and the reasoning repeats whole clauses twice at [Para 87] and [Para 90].
