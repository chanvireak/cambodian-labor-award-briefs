# Award No. 67/11 — Huey Chuen (Cambodia) Corp., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 13 July 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Tuon Siphann, Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 140035

## 1. Facts
Huey Chuen (Cambodia) Corp., Ltd employed about 1,100 workers at Chroy Ampil, Phnom Penh; the claimant was the Local Union of the National Union Federation of Cambodian Workers (NUCW) [Para 1][Para 14]. Ty Samnang had worked since 15 January 2008 under a verbal contract as an interpreter and head of nine sewing lines, and had been an advisor to the local union since January 2010 [Para 16][Para 17]. A dispute arose over his departure on 27 May 2011: the workers said he had sought sick leave and torn up the refused form, while the employer said he had requested and obtained approval to resign [Para 18][Para 19][Para 21]. Some 167 workers endorsed his reinstatement and a strike followed [Para 33].

## 2. Issues in dispute
2. Whether the employer must reinstate Ty Samnang without conditions, or alternatively pay him a termination payment, following the end of his employment on 27 May 2011? [Para 14][Para 16]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Ty Samnang submitted a sick-leave form on 26 May 2011, the employer refused it, and he tore it up; he never resigned [Para 18][Para 19]. The dispute was collective because a large number of workers endorsed the claim and went on strike for his reinstatement [Para 33][Para 50].
**Employer (respondent):** Ty Samnang asked Sok Navy for a resignation form, the head of the factory approved it on 27 May 2011, and he later removed the approved letter from the office [Para 19][Para 21][Para 22]. He refused to apologise for slapping a table, and the dispute was not collective because the employer did not know he was a union advisor [Para 24][Para 33].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Dismissed — the panel rejected the demand to reinstate Ty Samnang or pay a termination payment, but ordered the employer to pay his outstanding wages and payment in lieu of untaken annual leave [Para 69][Para 70].

On jurisdiction, the panel applied Article 302 of the Labour Law and its three-condition test from Award 45/07-Wilson: the dispute was between an employer and a number of its workers, concerned working conditions (the non-renewal of Ty Samnang's contract), and could jeopardise the enterprise's effective operation or social peace, as the strike showed [Para 52][Para 56][Para 57][Para 58]. Because the Ministry of Labour's non-conciliation report is presumed to contain collective issues, and the employer objected, the employer bore the burden of proving the dispute was individual; it did not discharge that burden, so the panel had jurisdiction [Para 48][Para 49][Para 59].

On the merits, the panel found Ty Samnang's account not credible. His personal leave record was intact and unfilled, which suggested he had not submitted a sick-leave form, and he had gone to the officer responsible for resignation requests rather than leave requests [Para 63][Para 64]. Sok Navy testified that he filled in a resignation form on 26 May 2011 and that the head of the factory approved it the next morning; Suon Sothearith testified that he saw Ty Samnang take the approved letter from Sok Navy's table [Para 34][Para 36][Para 44][Para 45]. The panel therefore held that the employment relationship ended on 27 May 2011 when the approval was communicated, and that the employer was not obliged to reinstate him or pay termination compensation after his resignation [Para 67][Para 68]. It nevertheless applied Article 167, paragraph 2 of the Labour Law, under which a worker whose contract ends before using accrued paid leave receives an indemnity, and ordered outstanding wages and payment in lieu of untaken annual leave [Para 69][Para 70].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A dispute over the non-renewal of a union advisor's contract is collective under Article 302 where the claimant is represented by a local union, a large number of workers endorse the claim and strike, and the subject concerns working conditions; the Ministry of Labour's non-conciliation report is presumed collective, and an employer objecting to that presumption bears the burden of proof. [Para 49][Para 52][Para 56][Para 59]

**Obiter dicta:** The workers did not contest the employer's contention that, if Ty Samnang had torn up a leave form, his leave record would also have been torn up. [Para 30]
