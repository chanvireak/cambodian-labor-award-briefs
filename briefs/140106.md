# Award No. 160/11 — Huy Chien (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Man Chey branch) (Arbitration Council, 29 December 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ung Sothy, employer-appointed — dissenting; Tuon Si Phan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · Doc ID 140106

## 1. Facts
The Democratic Khmer Workers' Union at the Man Chey branch of Huy Chien (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a footwear plant employing 980 workers, filed a collective dispute on 21 October 2011 seeking improved working conditions; conciliation on 22 November 2011 settled one of five demands, leaving four, which reached the Council under non-conciliation report No. 1328 of 30 November 2011. [Para 1][Para 52][Para 23][Para 24][Para 25]

At the hearing of 14 December 2011 the first demand (implementation of the October 2011 agreements on a breastfeeding room, extra dining tables and roofing) had been performed and the second (a USD 5 base-wage increase) was withdrawn after the company granted it, so the panel consolidated the remaining third and fourth demands into a single issue: the open-ended suspension of Mr C R, the branch union president, on 18 October 2011 without pay and with no stated end date. [Para 28][Para 29][Para 55][Para 57]

The company said it suspended him for forging a medical certificate; he held an indefinite-term contract from 23 April 2008 as a machine operator and branch union president, and a post-hearing letter to the Labour Dispute Department was produced bearing a 24 October 2011 entry stamp although the company had told the hearing only one such letter existed. [Para 56][Para 58][Para 76][Para 77][Para 78]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the demand to end the suspension of Mr C R and reinstate him with back pay from the date of suspension is a dispute over rights or over interests? [Para 79][Para 85]
2. Whether the employer took the suspension measure against a union leader in accordance with the procedure the law prescribes? [Para 86]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the union treated the suspension as an anti-union act because Mr C R was branch president; it sought an order ending the suspension, his return to normal work and wage arrears from 18 October 2011 to the day he resumes, and it also asked the company to stop the interference and intimidation attributed to Mr C V. [Para 14][Para 16][Para 70][Para 82]

**Employer (respondent):** the suspension rested on the worker having forged a medical certificate — he had asked the clinic doctor to certify illness when in fact he was arranging his nephew's funeral after a drowning on 11 October 2011 — and the company said the suspension was meant to await the labour inspector's decision; it denied anti-union discrimination, interference or intimidation. [Para 11][Para 15][Para 58][Para 67][Para 71][Para 89]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to end the suspension of Mr C R and take him back to work, paying his wages from 18 October 2011 until the date he resumes; no order was made on any interest dispute. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 110]

On characterisation the panel followed its own earlier awards, holding that a rights dispute is one concerning an entitlement written in a law, agreement or collective convention. Because the case turned on the suspension of a union leader, governed by Articles 293 and 295 of the Labour Law and Article 4 of Prakas No. 305, the panel found the demand a rights dispute, decided with immediate final effect and not open to objection; the parties had already agreed in the Memorandum of Understanding of 28 September 2010 to make such decisions binding. [Para 79][Para 84][Para 85][Para 31][Para 109]

Article 293 forbids any dismissal of an employee delegate or candidate without the labour inspector's agreement, and Article 295 lets an employer suspend a worker it considers to have committed serious misconduct, but only while simultaneously asking the inspector to authorise dismissal; if the inspector does not agree, the suspension is void and the worker is entitled to wages for the suspended period. Article 4 of Prakas No. 305 extends that protection to three union leaders. [Para 91][Para 94][Para 96][Para 100][Para 101]

Applying that, the panel found the employer had asked for a suspension but never sought authorisation to dismiss, and no response had come from the inspector; a suspension that had run since 18 October 2011 with no pay at all was therefore procedurally wrong. The panel rejected the employer's reliance on the forged certificate as a justification for the form of the measure, noting it took no step other than an indefinite suspension, and it also recorded that the dismissal request supposedly sent to the inspector was never evidenced. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 105][Para 90][Para 78]

Arbitrator Ung Sothy dissented on the back-pay order, relying on Article 72/1, under which suspension displaces the mutual obligations of work and wage unless a contrary rule provides otherwise, and on Article 71/2, under which only the employer may end a suspension once its cause has lapsed; he found no rule requiring wages during suspension. [Para 113][Para 114][Para 115][Para 118]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may suspend a union leader it believes has committed serious misconduct, but the suspension is lawful only if the employer simultaneously asks the labour inspector to authorise dismissal; where no such request is made, or the inspector does not agree, the suspension is void and the worker must be reinstated with wages for the whole suspended period. [Para 96][Para 100][Para 101][Para 103]

**Obiter dicta:** the finding that a post-hearing suspension letter bearing a 24 October 2011 stamp contradicted the company's statement at the hearing that only one letter existed, and the observation that the panel received no evidence of any dismissal request to the inspector. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 78]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The worker's name carries one surname in the body of the award and a different one at the operative order; the surname is inconsistent and the name is reduced to initials here. The panel's own reasoning addresses "issues 3 and 4" as one, so the source no longer separates the reinstatement demand from the demand to stop interference and intimidation. Numbering in the file jumps ("-6-" before issue 3) and the annex to the award opens without a heading break. Arbitrator Ung Sothy's name is printed "អ៊ឹង សុទ្ធី" and "អ៉ីង សុទ្ធី"; the worker-appointed arbitrator's name is partly lost in the signature block, where it appears as "modezu". The clinic doctor who issued certificate No. 771/2011 is named in the source but is reduced here to role, and the citation to Prakas No. 305 carries the year 2001 in the text and 22 November 2001 as its issue date. [Para 55][Para 106][Para 29][Para 111][Para 94][Para 37]
