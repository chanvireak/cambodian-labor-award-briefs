# Award No. 046/16 — Jin Yi Garment (Cambodia) Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 5 April 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-selected), An Non (worker-selected), Run Saray (president) · Doc ID 138997

## 1. Facts
Jin Yi Garment (Cambodia) Co Ltd, a garment factory in Samaki Meanchey district, Kampong Chhnang, employing about 3,797 workers, paid its sewing workers by piece rate ("តម្លៃឡូត៍") on barcodes scanned per operation [Para 51][Para 52][Para 58][Para 62]. The 2016 minimum wage rose to 140 USD a month under Prakas 409/15, but piece rates were not adjusted, and the union claimed that some 90 per cent of the roughly 2,700 piece-rate sewers could not reach 140 USD in normal hours [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61][Para 92]. Conciliation on 15 February 2016 settled one of two points and referred the other; the panel heard the case twice, on 4 and 30 March 2016 [Para 9][Para 7][Para 8][Para 15].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise the old piece rates by a further 10 per cent for all sewing workers? [Para 58][Para 59]

The award records that the union leadership of two of the three unions at the factory had passed the end of its term, so those officers attended as staff delegates under Article 283 rather than as union representatives, while the Seri Kammakor union, with 876 members, retained a valid mandate [Para 55][Para 85][Para 88][Para 89].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the minimum wage rose 10 per cent to 140 USD in 2016 but piece rates stayed unchanged, so around 90 per cent of piece-rate sewers earn below 140 USD for eight normal hours regardless of effort; the employer's figures must therefore be wrong, since a worker paid by output has every reason to scan her barcodes [Para 60][Para 61][Para 63][Para 66][Para 67].
**Employer (respondent):** many workers earn less than 140 USD because they do not finish enough pieces or do not scan their barcodes — in a 35-person team only about 15 scan — and the company already tops up anyone below 140 USD to that figure, paying more where output exceeds it [Para 64][Para 65][Para 95][Para 105].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand for a flat 10 per cent increase was dismissed, but the employer was ordered to set the piece rates anew so that a worker of average skill working normal hours, excluding overtime, public holidays and weekly rest, earns at least the minimum wage of 140 USD a month [Para 119][Para 120][Para 121].

On representation the panel applied Article 268, holding that a union acquires the rights the Labour Law recognises only once its statutes and the list of its responsible officers are registered with the Ministry; leadership recognition for the Seri Kammakor and Moratok Cheat unions at Jin Yi had expired and fresh recognition was pending, so their officers could not represent members as union leaders, though the Seri Kammakor union's mandate stood and its officers could [Para 75][Para 81][Para 83][Para 84][Para 87][Para 88].

On the merits the panel set out the statutory rule for piece work: wages must be calculated so that a worker of average skill, working normally, earns at least the guaranteed minimum wage [Para 90]. Article 137 caps ordinary hours at eight a day and forty-eight a week, and Prakas 409/15 fixed the 2016 minimum at 140 USD a month, with employers required to top up any output-based wage that falls short [Para 91][Para 92][Para 94].

It then weighed the evidence. The employer admitted most workers fall below 140 USD but blamed unscanned barcodes; the panel rejected that explanation as unproven, since no evidence supported it and a piece worker has every incentive to scan [Para 105][Para 106][Para 107]. The employer had also not shown that it conducted a trial before fixing rates, to confirm that a worker of average skill could reach the minimum in eight hours, offering only its calculation formula, which the workers did not contest [Para 68][Para 69][Para 70]. The rates were therefore too low and the employer had failed its duty under Article 108 as the award cites it [Para 100][Para 110][Para 111].

The panel nonetheless refused the 10 per cent formula: operations differ in difficulty from one to another, so a uniform percentage cannot be applied, and the demand rested on no legal basis and could not be shown to confer a benefit beyond statutory entitlement [Para 114][Para 115][Para 116][Para 117][Para 118]. Hence a re-set of rates to the Article 108 standard rather than a fixed uplift [Para 120][Para 121].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where work is paid by output, the employer's freedom to fix piece rates is bounded by a duty to ensure, by trial or equivalent evidence, that a worker of average skill working ordinary hours earns at least the statutory minimum wage; a rate that leaves the majority below the minimum breaches that duty and must be re-set, and the panel will not substitute a uniform percentage increase for the employer's own recalculation.

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted, without deciding anything on it, that it could not compare the union's demand with what the law already guarantees, and so could not treat the 10 per cent claim as an interest dispute at all [Para 117][Para 118]. It also treated as settled that staff delegates elected under Article 283 may represent workers before the panel independently of any union mandate [Para 86][Para 88].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is dated 5 April 2016 in the header but records an agreed extension of the issuing date to 7 April 2016 [Para 1][Para 18]. The minimum-wage Prakas is printed as 409/15 in the reasoning and as "309/15" a few paragraphs later [Para 92][Para 96], and the article governing piece work is quoted at one point without a number and later cited as Article 108 [Para 90][Para 110]. The step-by-step findings on rates, the trial before fixing them and the employer's formula are stated twice in near-identical terms, and the dispositive section is likewise printed twice [Para 68][Para 107][Para 119][Para 120]. Several cited award numbers are illegible, e.g. "លេខ ៤GO៧-វ៉ឺន ណឺ ណែធីង ហ្វាក់ តូរី" [Para 99]. The claimant's identity is obscured by damage to two paragraphs that should name the transferring unions and the worker delegates present [Para 89][Para 53], and the party list at the head of the file is truncated mid-entry [Para 2].
