# Award No. 019/18 — Phnom Penh Soriya Passenger Transport (Arbitration Council, 23 July 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Vira for the worker party, Tang Tri chair) · Doc ID 138895

## 1. Facts
Phnom Penh Soriya Passenger Transport (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ភ្នំពេញ សូរិយា ដឹកជញ្ជូន អ្នកដំណើរ) is a Phnom Penh bus and coach operator employing about 308 workers, with a registered union that the employer recognises and whose vice-president, Mr K, it knew. [Para 102][Para 103] On 23 April 2018 the workers filed a 14-point claim for improved conditions; conciliation settled 5 points, the workers withdrew points 5, 8 and 9 at the hearing, and the panel took the remaining six. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 32][Para 33] The disputed acts are two dismissals (Mr A for misconduct, Mr K for loading unauthorised goods on a company bus), the closure of the Siem Reap "Old Market" branch and transfer of two ticket sellers to Phnom Penh, a USD 300 deposit taken from every driver, unpaid night-shift and overtime rates, and a claim to a USD 170 base wage. [Para 112][Para 232][Para 336][Para 444][Para 445] The parties chose a non-binding award. [Para 34]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr A and Mr K and pay their wages and benefits from the date of dismissal until reinstatement? [Para 107][Para 275]
2. Whether the employer must reinstate Ms P and Ms K at their original workplace after closing the branch where they worked? [Para 169][Para 336]
3. Whether the employer must pay a meal allowance of 5,000 riel for each of two meals a day, and overtime for work beyond eight hours a day? [Para 215][Para 376]
4. Whether the employer must stop taking deposits from drivers and return the deposits already taken? [Para 232][Para 418]
6. Whether the employer must pay night-shift wages at 130% of the day rate, for past night shifts and continuing? [Para 243][Para 429]
7. Whether the employer must raise the wages of the lowest-paid workers to a base of USD 170 a month? [Para 253][Para 445]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** both dismissals were union discrimination — Mr K was dismissed because he is the union's vice-president, and the company terminated him before the labour inspector decided; the internal rules invoked against the ticket sellers carried no Labour Ministry visa and so cannot be applied; night driving is dangerous and drivers get no adequate rest. [Para 164][Para 213][Para 248]
**Employer (respondent):** Mr A wrecked his bus, refused responsibility and insulted management in front of staff, a serious fault; the two ticket sellers abandoned their jobs by ignoring three transfer notices; and drivers already receive trip money (ប្រាក់ជើង) that covers meals and overtime, so no further payment is due. [Para 275][Para 337][Para 221][Para 414]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claims to reinstate Mr A, Ms P and Ms K, the meal allowance and the USD 170 base wage were refused; the employer was ordered to pay Mr K his wages and benefits for 23 November 2017 to 1 January 2018, to stop taking deposits and return those taken, to pay overtime at 50% and 100% surcharges under Article 139 (new), and to pay night-shift wages at 130% for past shifts and continue doing so. [Para 450][Para 451][Para 454][Para 455][Para 456][Para 457]

On issue 1 the panel split the two workers. Mr A had admitted using the words "អញ អ្ហែង" to the garage chief and slapping the table, which the panel held to be insulting a manager under Article 83(b)(4) of the Labour Law; the employer's repair report signed by the garage and technical chiefs with seven witnesses' thumbprints carried the burden, and dismissal came two days after the fault, inside the seven-day limit in Article 26(2). [Para 287][Para 297][Para 299][Para 301][Para 303][Para 300] Mr K's case turned on procedure: the company decided his dismissal on 23 November 2017 and only obtained the labour inspector's authorisation on 2 January 2018, so the termination violated Article 43 of the Trade Union Law, which protects union leaders under Article 67; because authorisation existed by the date of the award the panel refused reinstatement but ordered wages for the unauthorised interval. [Para 324][Para 325][Para 327][Para 329][Para 333] On issue 2 the panel held that a transfer changes a term of the contract and needs the worker's agreement under Article 311 of the Civil Code, but found the thumbprinted internal rules of 13 May 2014 — applied since 2014 without objection — to be that agreement, and the eight days' absence after the final notice to be abandonment under rule 6. [Para 352][Para 361][Para 369][Para 374] On issue 3 the meal allowance was refused as an interest dispute because the claimant union was not the most representative union under Article 54 of the Trade Union Law and Article 43 of Prakas 099; overtime survived because Article 137 and Article 139 (new) impose it as of right at 50% for normal days and 100% at night or on weekly rest days. [Para 393][Para 397][Para 407][Para 417] Issue 4 was granted outright: Article 44 forbids any cash security as a condition of employment, so the USD 300 deposit was unlawful and the panel used its power under Article 34 of Prakas 099 to order repayment. [Para 418][Para 426][Para 428] Issue 6 was granted on Article 144 (new), which fixes night work at 130% of the day wage. [Para 434][Para 441][Para 444] Issue 7 was refused on the same most-representative-union gate as the meal allowance. [Para 446][Para 449]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer terminates a union officer before the labour inspector has authorised the termination, the dismissal is unlawful under Article 43 of the Trade Union Law even if authorisation is granted later; the panel will order wages and benefits for the interval between the dismissal and the authorisation but will not order reinstatement once authorisation exists. [Para 329][Para 333] A cash deposit demanded as a condition of hiring is prohibited by Article 44 of the Labour Law whatever the worker agreed, and must be returned. [Para 423][Para 428] A claim that goes beyond what the Labour Law, a collective agreement, the internal rules or past practice already gives is an interest dispute, which the panel will not hear unless the claimant is the most representative union in the enterprise. [Para 393][Para 394][Para 397]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that drivers themselves bear civil liability for accidents they cause under the Road Traffic Law, and its observations that an employee should show appropriate conduct and cooperate with managers investigating damage, do not affect any order. [Para 242][Para 289][Para 291]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award lists nine issues but numbers 5, 8 and 9 were withdrawn, so the reasons and the order run 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7 with no issue 5; the numbering gap is the award's own and was preserved. [Para 32][Para 33]
- Paragraph 334 dismisses the claim for wages "from the date of dismissal until reinstatement" for Mr K while paragraphs 333 and 335 order payment for 23 November 2017 to 1 January 2018; the narrower order was treated as operative and both passages are reported.
- The internal-rules date appears as 13 May 2014 in the findings and evidence list but as 25 April 2014 and 5 April 2014 when the visa is discussed, and the visa itself is dated 01 May 2014. [Para 41][Para 279][Para 355][Para 359]
- Employer and workers give different termination dates for the two ticket sellers (11 May 2016 against 18 May 2016) and different start dates for Ms K (November 1996 against 2 October 1997); the panel adopted 18 May 2016 without resolving the start date. [Para 177][Para 178][Para 180][Para 367]
- OCR corruption: English filler text is embedded in the Khmer at paragraphs 291, 338 and 352 ("nvoreserfregiture for furgantroughtery…", "reserviewherporation commonomany…"); stray numerals and Latin letters intrude at paragraphs 253 ("Li"), 305 and 397 ("តំណាង ១០"), and 447 ("តំណាង ៤០"); the company name is printed both សូរិយា and សូរិ យា.
- The award date is given only as 23 July 2018 in the header, while paragraph 36 records an extension from 27 June 2018 to that date and repeats "២០២ ២០១៨".
