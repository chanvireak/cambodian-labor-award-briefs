# Award No. 023/20 — Bou Khier Garmen Factory (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 29 April 2020)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Aing Eng Thong, president, chosen by the two arbitrators) · Doc ID 139560

## 1. Facts
Bou Khier Garmen Factory (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a garment and export manufacturer at Pong Teuk village, Ang Snuol district, Kandal province, employing 4,385 workers. A 2014 collective agreement with the Union for Workers and Employees of Cambodia gave each worker taking special marriage leave a USD 10 wedding gift; the agreement ran two years and expired on 23 February 2016, but the company kept paying the USD 10 until the end of 2018 and stopped from 2019. [Para 12][Para 24][Para 50][Para 52][Para 54] Of seven demands conciliated on 3 April 2020, six were settled and one — restoration of the USD 10 wedding gift — was referred to the Council by report No. 275/20 of 6 April 2020. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 50]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must resume paying the USD 10 wedding gift to workers who take special marriage leave, as it did before? [Para 3][Para 50]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Although the collective agreement expired in 2016, the employer kept paying the USD 10 until 2018 and should continue; it stopped without notifying the workers, and the benefit had become an established entitlement. [Para 56][Para 57]
**Employer (respondent):** The collective agreement lapsed in 2016 and the payments made after that were a benefit better than the law, granted without any document requiring them; new management from late 2018 audited the benefit, found no legal basis for it, and stopped it pending a new agreement with a most-representative union — of which there is currently none — the cost having run to USD 1,000–1,500 a month for 100 to 150 marriages. [Para 60][Para 63][Para 64][Para 66][Para 69]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider the demand; no payment was ordered and no sum was awarded [Para 80][Para 81]

The panel did not reach the question whether an expired collective agreement's benefit survives as a practice. It began instead from the state of the evidence. Workers had asked at the hearing to file documents identifying the workers who had married and had not received the USD 10, and the panel set 20 April 2020 as the deadline for filing and 22 April 2020 for objections. No document was filed. [Para 59][Para 21][Para 78]

The panel relied on Award No. 181/14 (Syun Yin (Cambodia) Bike Style), where it had held that it cannot rule on the substance of a demand where there is no identified disputing party or claimant and no evidence to support the assertion; it stated that it agreed with that reasoning and with Awards No. 230/16 and No. 026/19. [Para 72][Para 74][Para 75]

Applying that test, the panel found that of the thirteen workers who thumbprinted the complaint of 18 March 2020, not one had married, and that no worker was identified as having married and been refused the gift. The demand therefore had no specific claimant before the panel who had married and was claiming, and there was no evidence to assess. On that basis the panel refused to consider the demand rather than deciding whether the employer was obliged to pay. [Para 49][Para 76][Para 79][Para 80]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a demand concerns a contingent benefit payable only to workers who fall within a stated event, the panel will decline to consider it if no claimant is identified as having suffered the event and no supporting evidence is filed within the time allowed; the merits of the entitlement are then left undecided. [Para 72][Para 79][Para 80]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's recital that the company currently has no union holding most-representative status, and the employer's account of the cost of the benefit, played no part in the outcome, which rested solely on the absence of an identified claimant and of evidence. [Para 55][Para 69]
