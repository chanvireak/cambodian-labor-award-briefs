# Award No. 020/20 — Ji Fei Sen (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 April 2020)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Kong Phalla chair) · Doc ID 139536

## 1. Facts
Ji Fei Sen (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a garment factory in Trapeang Tuol village, Khan Kambol, Phnom Penh, employs 1,721 workers. [Para 50] Its workers put eight demands; conciliation on 23 March 2020 settled three and left five, which reached the Council on 25 March 2020 under report No. 335 Kb/Ak/Vk. [Para 11][Para 12][Para 13] The hearing was held on 6 April 2020 and the panel issued a non-binding award on 22 April 2020. [Para 10][Para 19][Para 20] The employer runs six-month fixed-duration contracts renewed every six months, and has converted workers with more than two years' service to unspecified-duration contracts under Instruction No. 050/19. [Para 133][Para 134]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay in cash for the unused annual leave of workers who are still employed? [Para 54]
2. Whether the employer must let pregnant workers leave 15 minutes early from the first month of pregnancy, on production of a hospital antenatal book and an identification card? [Para 65][Para 109]
3. Whether the employer must give workers a choice between a fixed-duration and an unspecified-duration contract when their fixed-duration contract comes to an end? [Para 71][Para 118]
4. Whether the employer must refrain from deducting annual leave and other benefits when a worker takes special leave? [Para 76][Para 155]
5. Whether the employer must pay an incentive bonus ("ABC money") to workers in every department? [Para 83][Para 169]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** unused annual leave should be paid in cash each year to workers still working, as neighbouring factories do; pregnant women from their first month face pushing and shoving when boarding transport and should be released 15 minutes early; workers should choose their contract type; special leave should not be set off against annual leave; and an ABC bonus graded A 100 per cent for 15 dollars, B 80 per cent for 12 dollars and C 50 per cent for 8 to 10 dollars should reward attendance and good conduct. [Para 57][Para 67][Para 79][Para 88][Para 90]
**Employer (respondent):** it applies the law, paying unused annual leave only when a contract is not renewed or is terminated; it already releases women pregnant more than three months 15 minutes early with a badge, as Notification No. 294 requires; contract type is a matter of mutual consent; it offsets special leave against annual leave but keeps other benefits; and it has never had an ABC bonus scheme, which would be hard to grade. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 69][Para 75][Para 81][Para 87][Para 91]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all five demands were refused; the award is of the non-binding type and becomes final eight days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Minister. [Para 202][Para 203][Para 206][Para 207]

On annual leave, Article 167(2) requires payment in lieu only where the contract is cut short or ends, and Article 167(3) voids any agreement to waive the right to annual leave; the panel read the two together as prohibiting cash payment for unused leave while the worker is still serving, so the demand was contrary to the article. [Para 95][Para 97][Para 99][Para 100][Para 107]

On pregnant workers, the Labour Law is silent, but point 2 of Notification No. 294 Kb/S.J.N of 27 September 2017 requires owners to allow women pregnant more than three months to leave at least 15 minutes early; the notification sets the threshold at three months, the employer complied with it, and the workers showed no legal basis for a first-month entitlement. [Para 109][Para 110][Para 115][Para 116]

On contract type, the panel applied Article 65 and Article 67(1) of the Labour Law and Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code: a contract is the meeting of offer and acceptance, and neither party may impose a form the other does not accept. It accepted that successive fixed-duration contracts totalling more than two years convert to unspecified duration under Article 67(2), that the employer applies this, and that a worker may prefer fixed duration because the 5 per cent termination indemnity pays more than seniority indemnity — but conversion is automatic by law, not a menu. [Para 118][Para 123][Para 131][Para 134][Para 139][Para 149][Para 154]

On special leave, Article 171 and point 2 of Prakas No. 267 of 11 October 2001 let the employer set special leave off against annual leave where the worker has not used all annual leave, and require repayment where it has; the employer did exactly that and preserved other benefits. [Para 155][Para 156][Para 160][Para 165][Para 166]

On the ABC bonus, the panel found no law, collective convention, agreement, internal rule or past practice creating it, so the claim was an interests dispute; following Article 54 of the Trade Union Law and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099, an interests dispute may proceed only if the referring union is most representative, and it was not, so the panel declined to consider the demand. [Para 169][Para 170][Para 178][Para 194][Para 199][Para 200]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Article 167 does not permit an employer to buy out unused annual leave while the employment relationship continues; cash compensation for untaken leave is owed only when the contract ends. A statutory notification that fixes a threshold — releasing pregnant workers 15 minutes early from the third month — satisfies the employer's obligation, and a demand for the same benefit from the first month is outside the instrument and must fail. Whether a fixed-duration or unspecified-duration contract is concluded is a matter of mutual consent, and the statutory conversion after two years operates automatically rather than as a choice offered to the worker. [Para 99][Para 107][Para 115][Para 139][Para 154]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that fixed-duration contracts are less favourable to job security yet may pay a worker more through the 5 per cent indemnity, and that the employer's managerial prerogative under Article 2 covers bonus schemes not contrary to law, were not needed once the demands were refused. [Para 125][Para 134][Para 174][Para 176]
