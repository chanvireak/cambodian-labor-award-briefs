# Award No. 034/20 — WYP Trading Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ាយ វីភី ត្រេឌីង ខូអិលធីឌី) (Arbitration Council, 15 July 2020)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Kong Phalla (chair) · Doc ID 140474

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ាយ វីភី ត្រេឌីង ខូអិលធីឌី (WYP Trading Co., Ltd) is a garment-cutting enterprise in Phnom Penh with 127 workers. [Para 61] Workers represented by the Khmer Federation of Unions and the Khmer Workers' Union filed twelve demands on 26 May; conciliation on 8 June settled three, leaving nine referred to the Council, of which the parties then merged issues 2, 7 and 9 into one. [Para 27][Para 29][Para 35] Only sixteen workers' names and thumbprints appear on the authorisation transferring rights to the representatives, although the workers claimed 102 complainants. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 68] The company had already suspended contracts and paid out terminating fixed-duration contracts as work ran out during the COVID-19 period. [Para 44][Para 75]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether fixed-duration contracts running beyond two years must be converted into contracts of undetermined duration? [Para 70]
2. (Issues 2, 7 and 9) Whether the employer must take back workers whose fixed-duration contracts had ended and who had returned as daily-paid labour, and convert them into permanent workers? [Para 116]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the seniority bonus must be added to the base wage when calculating overtime pay? [Para 87]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must build a canteen for the workers? [Para 148]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the employer must pay a USD 20 monthly milk allowance to women workers with children under 18 months? [Para 169]
6. (Issue 6) Whether workers must be allowed a 15-minute rest break during overtime? [Para 178]
7. (Issue 8) Whether the employer owes seniority bonus unpaid since 2018? [Para 186]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** ភាគី កម្មករ និយោជិត, through the Khmer Workers' Union and the Khmer Federation of Unions, sought conversion of long-service fixed-duration contracts, reinstatement as permanent staff of workers re-hired by the day, a canteen because rain made hot meals unsafe, the USD 20 milk allowance, a fifteen-minute break in a ten-hour shift, and seniority bonus allegedly unpaid since 2018. [Para 77][Para 93][Para 103][Para 105][Para 108]
**Employer (respondent):** ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ាយ វីភី ត្រេឌីង ខូអិលធីឌី, through its administration head and a GMAC officer, answered that it had applied Instruction No. 050/19 on contract types, that the daily-paid workers were hired through a labour contractor and not by the company, that the contracts had expired and been paid out, that it had no work and no budget, and that the building owner would not permit a canteen. [Para 4][Para 81][Para 82][Para 95][Para 100]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — every demand was rejected or declined: issues 2, 7 and 9 and issues 4, 5, 6 and 8 were refused, and issue 3 was declined as already decided. [Para 192][Para 193][Para 194][Para 197]
On Issue 1 the panel recorded the parties' agreement in open hearing that fixed-duration contracts exceeding two years would be handled under Instruction No. 050/19, and declined to consider the point further. [Para 70][Para 71]
On Issues 2, 7 and 9 the panel first narrowed the claim: of the five named complainants only one, with a service number, appeared in the authorisation list, so it decided the issue for that worker alone. [Para 113][Para 115] Applying Article 73 §1 of the Labour Law, it held that a fixed-duration contract expires by operation of law at its term, that renewal is the decision of the parties to the contract, and that no party can force another to continue it. [Para 116][Para 118][Para 119] It found the last contract ended on 28 February, notice had been given on 1 February, final wages and 5% severance were paid, the employer had no wish to renew and had no work, and the worker's return as daily labour ran through a labour contractor, creating no direct employment relation with the company. [Para 122][Para 123][Para 125][Para 126]
On Issue 3 the panel applied the res judicata principle and asked whether the same issue between the same parties had been decided on the merits. [Para 131][Para 133] It found the same company and the same claimant workforce had litigated the identical seniority-bonus question in Award No. 168/16, where the demand was rejected, and that all three conditions were satisfied, so it declined to decide the point again. [Para 141][Para 143][Para 145][Para 147]
On Issues 4, 5 and 6 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, a collective agreement, internal regulations, an employment contract, an agreement between the parties or past practice creating the claimed canteen, milk allowance or rest break, and classified each as a demand above the legal minimum and therefore an interest dispute. [Para 148][Para 172][Para 180] Applying Article 96 §2 of the Labour Law, Article 54 (new) and Article 72 of the Trade Union Law, and Article 43 §1 of Prakas No. 099, it held that an award in an interest dispute replaces a collective agreement and binds all workers, so only the most representative union may bring such a dispute; the complainant union was not the most representative, and the panel declined jurisdiction over the three demands. [Para 154][Para 160][Para 163][Para 166][Para 167]
On Issue 8 the panel found no named or counted claimants and no specific dates showing non-payment, so no operative order could be made. [Para 186][Para 187]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-duration contract ends at its term by force of Article 73, and neither the panel nor a group of workers can compel its renewal or the re-engagement of the worker as permanent staff, especially where the return to work was through a labour contractor. [Para 118][Para 119][Para 125]
A demand for a benefit above the statutory minimum is an interest dispute, and the panel will decline it unless brought by the most representative union, because an award there would stand in place of a collective agreement binding every worker in the enterprise. [Para 162][Para 163]
An issue already decided between the same parties on the same facts will not be reopened. [Para 133]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that an award on an interest dispute takes effect for one year as a substitute for a collective agreement, and that reopening identical demands would risk contradictory outcomes and deny disputes a final resolution. [Para 135][Para 161]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Every year is truncated to "២០" (header, notices, award date), and the panel's constitution is printed as "15 June 2019 20," an impossible date. [Para 31] The conciliation report number is printed as 574 in the recitals and 474 in the referral paragraph. [Para 24][Para 30] Workforce figures conflict: 127 employees, 102 alleged complainants, 16 authorised representatives and 94 workers said to remain. [Para 61][Para 65][Para 68][Para 95] The milk-allowance sum is printed both as "20" and as "120 (twenty)" dollars. [Para 12][Para 97] Award 168/16 is dated 2016 in the text although the company's award list elsewhere cites it as 2016; the panel's citation of it is garbled by OCR. [Para 130][Para 141] Several individual workers are named in the source; this brief identifies them by role or service number only.
