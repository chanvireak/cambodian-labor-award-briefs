# Award No. 113/19 — I K Apparel Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 10 January 2020)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Leav Sovanna for the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 139279

## 1. Facts
I K Apparel is a garment-cutting and sewing enterprise in Anlong Romiet, Kandal, employing 1,027 workers. [Para 38] A collective dispute over five working conditions went to the Kandal Provincial Department of Labour, where one point was conciliated and four remained; the file reached the Council on 17 December 2019 and the hearing was held on 27 December 2019. [Para 14][Para 16][Para 17] The four unresolved points were holiday pay, the attendance bonus, the transport and accommodation allowance, and the overtime meal allowance. [Para 57][Para 108]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay wages of 300 per cent of the ordinary daily wage to workers who work on public holidays? [Para 69]
2. Whether the employer must add USD 5 to the USD 10 monthly attendance bonus, making USD 15 a month? [Para 78]
3. Whether the employer must add USD 3 to the USD 7 monthly transport and accommodation allowance, making USD 10 a month? [Para 100]
4. Whether the employer must add Riel 2,000 to the Riel 2,000 daily overtime meal allowance, making Riel 4,000, and pay it every week rather than monthly? [Para 108][Para 116]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** holiday work should be counted at 1x2+1, i.e. 300 per cent, to give workers heart for the job and to encourage cooperation when the employer needs extra output; the attendance bonus should reach USD 15 as at neighbouring factories; actual transport costs exceed USD 7 a month; and the overtime meal allowance is a daily living necessity that should be paid weekly. [Para 39][Para 41][Para 48][Para 54][Para 59][Para 60]
**Employer (respondent):** it already pays 200 per cent to workers who volunteer to work on holidays, USD 10 attendance bonus, USD 7 transport and accommodation allowance and Riel 2,000 a day for overtime meals, all as the Prakas require; it cannot go further because orders and revenue have fallen for two years, it has no budget, it faces monthly tax-declaration difficulties, and it already runs two wage payments a month. [Para 39][Para 44][Para 46][Para 53][Para 56][Para 62][Para 63][Para 64]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim for 300 per cent holiday pay was dismissed; the claims for USD 15 attendance bonus and USD 10 transport allowance were dismissed for want of jurisdiction; the claim for an extra Riel 2,000 a day was dismissed, but the employer was ordered to pay the Riel 2,000 overtime meal allowance to workers **every week** instead of monthly. [Para 133][Para 134][Para 135][Para 136][Para 137]

**Issue 1 — holiday pay.** Article 161 of the Labour Law requires the Ministry to fix paid public holidays, and Article 164 gives a worker who works on a holiday, in addition to wages for work done, a compensation fixed by Prakas. [Para 69][Para 71] Article 4 of Prakas No. 10 SKBY of 4 February 1999 fixes that compensation at one ordinary day's wage. [Para 72] Following Award No. 113/14 Sun Jin and the awards cited there, the panel held that a holiday is a day already paid, so a worker who works it receives the normal wage plus one day's compensation, i.e. 200 per cent; the employer had paid exactly that. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 76] The claim for 300 per cent was therefore refused. [Para 77]

**Issue 2 — attendance bonus.** Point 2 of Notification No. 230 K.B/S.Ch.N of 25 July 2012 requires at least USD 10 a month for workers with no absence, and the employer paid USD 10. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 80] No provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules or past practice obliged it to pay USD 15, so the claim exceeded the legal floor and was an interest dispute. [Para 81][Para 82][Para 83] Because an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement and binds all workers for one year under Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099 SKBY, only a most-representative union may bring such a claim, per Article 96(2) of the Labour Law and Articles 54 and 72 of the Trade Union Law of 17 May 2016. [Para 84][Para 90][Para 91][Para 94] The claimant was a workers' representative group, not a most-representative union, so the panel declined to consider the claim. [Para 97][Para 99]

**Issue 3 — transport and accommodation.** Point 1 of the same Notification No. 230 fixes USD 7 a month, which the employer paid. [Para 100][Para 101] Nothing in law, agreement, internal rules or practice required the extra USD 3, and the same jurisdictional bar applied, so the claim was not considered. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 107]

**Issue 4 — overtime meal allowance.** Point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 K.B/S.Ch.N of 7 March 2011 fixes Riel 2,000 a day, which the employer paid, so the extra Riel 2,000 was refused. [Para 108][Para 110] On frequency, the panel held that a free meal must be given on each overtime day, failing which Riel 2,000 a day replaces it, and that the payment cycle must simply let the worker buy food for overtime days — daily, weekly or monthly in principle. [Para 118][Para 126][Para 127] Because the employer paid monthly while the allowance answers a daily living need, and prices have risen, the panel ordered weekly payment. [Para 128][Para 130][Para 131]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Two rules decide future cases. First, a worker who works a public holiday is entitled under Article 4 of Prakas No. 10 SKBY to the ordinary day's wage plus one day's compensation — 200 per cent in total — and a claim for 300 per cent fails because the holiday wage is already earned. [Para 72][Para 74][Para 76] Second, a claim that a benefit already paid at the statutory minimum be raised above that minimum is an interest dispute; since an award on such a dispute substitutes for a collective agreement and binds every worker in the enterprise for a year, the panel has no jurisdiction unless the claimant is a most-representative union within Articles 54 and 72 of the Trade Union Law. [Para 83][Para 90][Para 91][Para 94] A subsidiary rule: an overtime meal allowance paid monthly satisfies the Prakas only if the worker can still buy food for overtime days, so where the daily living need is real, weekly payment is required. [Para 127][Para 130]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that a free meal gives workers added strength to work further overtime and that food is an unavoidable daily necessity the employer must supply go beyond the order. [Para 125] Its refusal to weigh the 3 January agreement on half-salary meal money, on the ground that midday meal money is a different subject from overtime meal money, is likewise explanatory only. [Para 65][Para 67][Para 68]
