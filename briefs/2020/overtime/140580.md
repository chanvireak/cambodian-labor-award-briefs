# Award No. 040/20 — Khmer Electric Call Power (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខ្មែរ អេឡិកទ្រី ខល ផៅវ័រ) (Arbitration Council, 21 July 2020)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — employer-appointed សេង វួច ហ៊ុន, worker-appointed អន ណន, president ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា · Doc ID 140580

## 1. Facts
The workers' representatives (ភាគី តំណាង កម្មករ និយោជិត) of Khmer Electric Call Power (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខ្មែរ អេឡិកទ្រី ខល ផៅវ័រ), an electricity-generation company on Street 217 in Phnom Penh with 54 employees, brought a collective dispute over night-shift pay [Para 30][Para 31]. Thirty-two workers signed the original complaint; twenty of them rotate through four teams on three shifts of 07:00–15:00, 15:00–23:00 and 23:00–07:00 [Para 32][Para 34]. The employer conceded that it pays the same 100% rate for the night shift as for the day shift, although it pays 200% for overtime and public-holiday work and gives eight rest days a month [Para 35][Para 36]. Talks on 20 February and a complaint to the Ministry on 23 March failed, and the single unsettled point came to the Council [Para 37][Para 38].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer is obliged to pay wages at 130% to workers who rotate onto the night shift from 22:00 to 05:00? [Para 49]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Relying on Article 144-new of the Labour Law and Circular No. 184 of 2007, the representatives argued that work between 22:00 and 05:00 must be paid at 130%, and that under Article 13 benefits already granted cannot be traded away against that entitlement [Para 39][Para 46][Para 47].

**Employer (respondent):** The company maintained that from the start of operations it has paid benefits above the statutory floor, and warned that if the 130% rate were imposed it would withdraw those extra benefits [Para 41][Para 45]. It also argued that Article 144-new defines night as eleven consecutive hours and that its workers never work eleven consecutive hours [Para 43][Para 44].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay 130% of the day-shift wage to workers who rotate onto the night shift from 22:00 to 05:00 [Para 65][Para 66].

The panel applied Article 144-new of the Labour Law, introduced by the amendment to Articles 139 and 144 promulgated by Royal Kram No. NS/RKM/0397/01 of 13 March 1997 and in force on 20 July 2007, which defines night as at least eleven consecutive hours including the period from 22:00 to 05:00 [Para 49][Para 50]. It read that article together with point 1 of Circular No. 185 of 14 August 2007, which grants 130% of the day wage to workers working at night between 22:00 and 04:00 whether they rotate shifts or work nights permanently [Para 52][Para 53].

It followed its earlier awards, No. 116/09, No. 062/10, No. 68/10 and No. 063/19, for the proposition that the eleven-hour definition fixes when the night period falls but does not mean that only work spanning eleven hours is night work, and that night work means work from 22:00 to 05:00 [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57][Para 58]. It confirmed that line with Awards No. 119/07, No. 119/09, No. 250/15, No. 001/17, No. 002/17, No. 010/18 and No. 081/19 [Para 60].

On the facts the panel held that paying 100% for a shift running from 22:00 to 05:00 was contrary to those provisions, and that the obligation to pay 130% applied even though the workers rotate between day and night rather than working nights permanently [Para 62][Para 63]. The employer's warning that it would withdraw other benefits did not answer the statutory duty, and the panel recorded that no agreement had ever linked those benefits to the night rate [Para 45][Para 46].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker who works any part of the 22:00 to 05:00 period is a night worker entitled to 130% of the day-shift wage, even if the worker rotates between day and night shifts and even if the shift is shorter than eleven hours; the eleven-hour definition in Article 144-new locates the night period rather than setting a minimum length of night work, and an employer cannot offset benefits it already grants against that rate [Para 57][Para 58][Para 62][Para 63].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted the employer's statement that it would withdraw its above-statutory benefits if the claim succeeded, but decided nothing on that threat beyond recording that no agreement had ever tied those benefits to the night rate [Para 45][Para 46].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Every year figure in the award is truncated to "20": the heading, the Prakas number, the hearing date and the appended erratum all lose their final two digits, so the 2020 year is inferred from the Prakas number 185/20 and the sequence of dates rather than read off the page [Para 1][Para 11][Para 82]. The company name is spelled several ways across the award, and the case number appears in two forms [Para 7][Para 30][Para 68]. One figure is corrupted in the operative reasoning, where 130% appears once as a four-digit number [Para 64]. The workers rely on Circular No. 184 of 2007 while the panel applies Circular No. 185 of 14 August 2007; the award does not reconcile the two references [Para 39][Para 53]. The document is an award of 21 July followed by a separate erratum of 04 September correcting the procedural paragraph only, and the erratum's own year is truncated [Para 68][Para 79][Para 82].
