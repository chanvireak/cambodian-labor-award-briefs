# Award No. 010/20 — Syung Yin (Cambodia) B Tech Style Ltd (Arbitration Council, 3 March 2020)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Tuon Si Phan (worker-appointed), Run Saray (president) · Doc ID 139293

## 1. Facts
Syung Yin (Cambodia) B Tech Style Ltd, a weaving enterprise in Ta Khmao, Kandal, faced a collective dispute filed by the factory's democratic union on 9 January 2020 over five claims, one of which was settled in conciliation. [Para 11][Para 12][Para 13][Para 34] A hearing was held on 14 February 2020. [Para 9][Para 17] After the hearing the employer sent photographs and video said to show workers blocking the company's entrance, which it characterised as a strike. [Para 20] The workers denied any strike, saying they acted only because the employer owed wages and seniority payments and that the trucks involved belonged to the company itself. [Para 21][Para 25][Para 26] A post-hearing meeting was convened on 24 February 2020, and the strike-resolution committee recorded on 25 February 2020 that workers had been sitting in the bicycle and motorcycle parking area in a strike. [Para 22][Para 27][Para 93]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay back seniority payments and seniority indemnity to the workers? [Para 2]
2. Whether the employer must pay wages and other benefits for the period of an unlawful suspension carried out without the labour inspector's authorisation? [Para 3]
3. Whether the employer must take the union's members back into work and pay wages and benefits from the date of dismissal until reinstatement? [Para 4]
4. Whether the employer must pay back wages and benefits to the twelve workers covered by case No. 033/18? [Para 5]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer owed wages, seniority indemnity and the pre-2019 seniority back payment, and that is why they acted as they did; their action was not a strike, and the trucks they stopped belonged to Syung Yin itself rather than to another company renting space on the site. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 92]
**Employer (respondent):** from 12 February 2020 until the post-hearing meeting the workers struck, blocking the entrance and preventing trucks carrying material for other companies renting premises inside the site from leaving. [Para 24][Para 90]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel terminated the arbitration proceedings and closed the file without ruling on any of the four remaining claims. [Para 95][Para 96]

The panel raised the question whether it could continue at all before examining the merits. [Para 83] Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 requires the parties, during the arbitration, to suspend every dispute measure — strikes, lockouts under Article 318 of the Labour Law, and any other step that could aggravate the dispute — and to attend every meeting the panel summons. [Para 83][Para 84] The panel read that provision as barring it from continuing arbitration whenever workers are striking or the employer has locked out. [Para 85][Para 86] It noted that it had consistently followed this reading in a long line of earlier awards, from 59/06 through to 008/19. [Para 87]

Applying it, the panel weighed the employer's post-hearing photographs and video, the workers' denial, and the record of the strike-resolution committee of 25 February 2020, which described workers sitting in the bicycle and motorcycle parking area as a strike. [Para 20][Para 27][Para 93] It found that the workers had indeed struck at the company during the arbitration, and accordingly declined to continue. [Para 94][Para 95] The panel expressly stated that it had not considered the facts the parties presented at the hearing of 14 February 2020. [Para 82]

The four claims — seniority payments, wages for the allegedly unlawful suspension, reinstatement with back wages, and the twelve workers' back wages in case 033/18 — were therefore left undecided. [Para 2][Para 3][Para 4][Para 5][Para 96]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A strike begun after the hearing still strips the panel of the ability to proceed: Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 obliges the parties to suspend all dispute measures for the duration of the arbitration, and where workers are in fact striking the Council must terminate the proceedings rather than decide the claims, leaving the dispute to be taken up afresh once the strike has ended. [Para 84][Para 86][Para 94]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel treated the strike-resolution committee's characterisation of the workers' conduct as determinative, while recording the workers' contrary account that the trucks belonged to the company and not to a third-party tenant — an account it did not resolve. [Para 26][Para 93]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification. [Para 96] OCR losses are heavy. The year is cut to "ឆ្នាំ ២០" throughout, the panel is said to have been constituted on "ថ្ងៃទី ៣ ខែកុម្ភៈ ឆ្នាំ ២០២៣ ២០", and the post-hearing notification is dated "ថ្ងៃទី ១៨ ខែកញ្ញា កុម្ភៈ". [Para 14][Para 88] The number of claims is inconsistent — five are said to have been filed, four are listed, and one is said to have been settled — and the claimant group is never counted. [Para 11][Para 13][Para 2] The employer's name appears as "ស្យុង យិន", "ស្យ៉ុង ឃឺន" and "តម្បាញ ស្យុង យិន" in different passages. [Para 27][Para 57] The reasoning at line 96 repeats its own opening clause verbatim, and Latin noise ("JT", "7775", "27") interrupts the text. [Para 35][Para 72][Para 96] Individual workers, union officers and the committee official are named in the source; they are not reproduced here.
