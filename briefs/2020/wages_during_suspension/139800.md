# Award No. 025/20 — Go Thon Travel Service (Arbitration Council, 25 May 2020)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, chosen by the employer party; Tuon Si Phan, chosen by the worker party; Pen Bun Chhea, chair chosen by the two) · Doc ID 139800

## 1. Facts
Go Thon Travel Service is a tourist transport company at Trapeang Ses village, Kok Chak commune, Siem Reap, which employed 21 workers, of whom 19 remained at the date of the hearing. [Para 46][Para 47] From late January 2020 the company had no revenue because no guests hired its vehicles; it applied to the provincial labour department on 25 February 2020 to suspend the contracts of 19 workers for March and April, and on 12 March 2020 the department authorised the suspension for the period 1 March to 30 April 2020. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 59][Para 60] The eleven drivers who remained claimed wages for March and April 2020; the union's demand for severance for the two workers already paid was settled at the hearing, leaving only that claim. [Para 18][Para 47][Para 50]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay wages for March and April 2020 to the eleven remaining drivers, or pay them severance instead? [Para 50][Para 65]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the suspension for March and April 2020 was procedurally wrong because the employer did not call the workers' representatives to discuss it before applying to the labour department, the labour inspector never came to ask the representatives about its effect on the workers, the employer had no legal ground for suspending and the company suffered no serious harm; 50 per cent of wages is not enough to live on. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 56][Para 61]

**Employer (respondent):** it could not pay the claim because from the end of January 2020 the company had no income at all, no guests having come for its services, which is why it applied to suspend; it applied twice, for 19 workers for March and April and again for May and June, and the department told it to apply online; it received no government support after the first application but was told of a 40 US dollar monthly grant after the second. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61][Para 62]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the claim for March and April wages, holding the suspension lawful, and the award is non-binding subject to the eight-day objection period. [Para 86][Para 87][Para 88]

The panel began with the procedural objection to the workers' written submission. It applied Article 25 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, which leaves the panel free to assess the admissibility, relevance, materiality and weight of evidence, and found that the submission denied facts the workers had themselves admitted at the hearing of 8 May 2020 about the application for suspension, the department's authorisation and the notification to the workers; it therefore declined to consider the submission. [Para 77][Para 78][Para 79][Para 81]

On the merits it set out Article 71(11) of the Labour Law, which permits suspension where an enterprise meets serious economic or material difficulty, for no longer than two months, under the supervision of the labour inspector, and Article 72(1), under which a suspension affects only the principal obligations of the contract, including the obligation to pay wages. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 70][Para 71] Following its earlier awards, it read the supervision requirement as demanding prior notification to, and authorisation from, the labour inspector before a suspension can be lawful, and held that only a procedurally lawful suspension attracts Article 72(1); an unlawful one leaves the employer owing 100 per cent of wages. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 69][Para 72][Para 73][Para 74]

Applying that test, the panel found that the parties agreed the employer had announced on 25 and 29 February 2020 that it was applying to suspend for March and April, and that the department had issued its authorisation of 12 March 2020 covering exactly 1 March to 30 April 2020. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 84] The suspension therefore ran for no more than two months, was notified and applied for before it took effect, and stood under the inspectorate's supervision, so the employer owed no wages for the period. [Para 84][Para 85][Para 86] The panel did not address the workers' complaint that the inspector never visited the factory, beyond recording the department's explanation at conciliation that too many tourism companies had applied for its officers to inspect each one. [Para 52]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of employment contracts is lawful, and the employer is released from the obligation to pay wages under Article 72(1) of the Labour Law, where the enterprise faces serious economic difficulty, the suspension does not exceed two months, and the employer has notified the labour inspector and obtained authorisation before the suspension takes effect; the absence of a physical inspection by the inspectorate does not by itself make an authorised suspension unlawful. [Para 67][Para 69][Para 72][Para 84]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's refusal to consider the workers' written submission, and its account of the department's explanation for not inspecting, are procedural and evidentiary observations rather than part of the rule on suspension. [Para 52][Para 81]
