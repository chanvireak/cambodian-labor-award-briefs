# Award No. 111/19 — Syong Yin (Cambodia) Bi Tek Stay Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 9 January 2020)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chheam presiding; Ieng Sothy, Liv Sovanna) · Doc ID 139277

## 1. Facts
Syong Yin (Cambodia) Bi Tek Stay Co Ltd weaves cloth at Prek Traeng village, Sitbo commune, Ta Khmau, Kandal, with 610 workers, 456 of whom signed the collective complaint. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48] On 2 December 2019 the provincial department received five demands; conciliation on 5 December 2019 failed on all five, and at the hearing the workers merged issues 2, 3 and 4, leaving three. [Para 10][Para 11][Para 16][Para 17] The employer had paid the October 2019 wage of USD 182 net only, with no allowances, on 3 December 2019, and had no work for the workers through October, November and December. [Para 49][Para 64]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the remaining October 2019 allowances and benefits to all workers? [Para 49][Para 75]
2. Whether the employer must pay wages twice a month, as Article 116 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 442 of 21 September 2018 require, rather than once? [Para 56][Para 88]
3. Whether the employer must pay 100% of wages for November and December 2019, when it had no work, and back-pay the missing 50% of the October 2019 wage to the 222 workers who received only half? [Para 62][Para 98]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer paid only the net October wage and no allowances; it promised the allowances for 16 December 2019 and then for the end of December, and paid nothing; it pays wages once a month although the law requires twice; and because it gave no work and never lawfully suspended the contracts, it owes 100% for November and December plus the 50% shortfall for October. [Para 51][Para 59][Para 63][Para 67][Para 68]

**Employer (respondent):** it promised the remaining October allowances, the 5% and the year-end bonus at the end of December 2019; on double payment it would review the payroll and revert; and it cannot pay 100% because some workers had work and some did not, so it pays according to each worker's actual situation. [Para 52][Para 61][Para 69][Para 70]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer is ordered to pay the remaining October 2019 allowances and benefits to all workers; to pay wages twice a month; to pay 100% of wages for November and December 2019 to all workers; and to pay the missing 50% of the October 2019 wage to the 222 listed workers. No sum is quantified; the award is non-binding and becomes final 8 (eight) days after notification. [Para 124][Para 125][Para 126][Para 127][Para 128]

On issue 1 the panel applied Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code: a contract is formed by offer and acceptance and binds the parties to perform it. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 77][Para 79][Para 80] It found a record of 4 December 2019, signed by both sides at the Kandal labour department, in which the employer undertook to pay the remaining October allowances, the 5% and the year-end bonus at the end of December 2019; that undertaking was binding, and the employer had not performed it by the hearing. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 84][Para 85][Para 86]

On issue 2 the panel read Article 116 of the Labour Law, requiring wages at least twice a month with no more than sixteen days between payments, and Article 1 of Prakas No. 442, which from January 2019 requires payment in the second and fourth weeks of each month. [Para 88][Para 90] Both parties admitted the company paid once a month, so the employer was in breach and was ordered to pay twice monthly, following award 085/19. [Para 95][Para 96][Para 97]

On issue 3 the panel set Article 71(11), which allows suspension of activity only for serious economic or material difficulty, under labour-inspector supervision and for no more than two months, against Article 72, which removes the duty to pay wages during a lawful suspension. [Para 98][Para 107][Para 109] Because the employer had no work in November and December and never sought the inspector's authorisation, the suspension was unlawful and the duty to pay survived; the panel followed its earlier awards 21/03, 01/04, 60/06, 74/07, 27/08, 53/08, 109/09, 008/17, 010/18 and 004/19. [Para 105][Para 111][Para 114][Para 115] The 50% paid in October for days without work was likewise not authorised, so the shortfall was owed to the 222 workers on the list. [Para 117][Para 118][Para 120][Para 121][Para 123]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that gives no work may stop paying wages only by suspending the employment contracts under Article 71, with the labour inspector's authorisation and for no more than two months; a suspension it never sought leaves the wage obligation intact, so full wages are owed for the idle months and the unpaid half of any month paid at 50%. [Para 106][Para 111][Para 114] A written record signed by both parties at conciliation binds the employer to the payments it promises there, independently of the statutory claim. [Para 84][Para 86]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that Article 72 disapplies the wage obligation only where the suspension itself was made in accordance with the law — a qualification the employer's case did not meet. [Para 109][Para 110]
