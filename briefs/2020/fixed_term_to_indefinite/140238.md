# Award No. 026/20 — Valong (Cambodia) Footwear Industry Steel Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 12 June 2020)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Tuon Siphan (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (president) · Doc ID 140238

## 1. Facts
Valong (Cambodia) Footwear Industry Steel Co., Ltd. makes shoes at Cheu Teal village, Pteah Jum commune, Cheung Prey district, Kampong Cham, and employed 1,862 workers when the file was opened [Para 1][Para 71][Para 72]. The plant union filed six demands on 24 April 2020; the provincial labour department conciliated between 28 April and 4 May and settled two of them, sending the remaining four to the Council on 7 May [Para 14][Para 15][Para 16]. At the first hearing the parties settled Issue 1 itself, leaving the panel to decide Issues 2, 4 and 5 [Para 20][Para 21]. The eight workers who figure throughout were all members of the union's committee, working under fixed-duration contracts renewed every three months [Para 5][Para 74].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must continue the employment contracts of the eight union committee members, or in the alternative pay them severance for termination? [Para 73][Para 110]
2. (Issue 4) Whether, in a period when there is no work, the employer must suspend the contracts rather than dismiss the workers? [Para 93]
3. (Issue 5) Whether the employer must distribute one mask per worker per day? [Para 100][Para 244]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the non-renewal letters were undated, unsigned and never acknowledged by the workers, so they were not official and the contracts ran on, becoming indefinite-duration [Para 78][Para 79][Para 81][Para 91]. Suspension rather than dismissal would preserve the USD 40 government and USD 30 employer wage support, and no worker could find new work during the crisis [Para 94][Para 95][Para 97]. The employer requires masks at the gate but pays too little for workers to buy them [Para 102][Para 104].

**Employer (respondent):** it did not dismiss anyone; the fixed-duration contracts simply expired and were not renewed [Para 82][Para 111]. Ministry guidance never obliged it to suspend contracts, and it may suspend or dissolve contracts to meet the company's actual needs [Para 98][Para 99]. On masks it already follows Ministry of Health guidance, taking temperatures at entry and issuing masks to anyone at 37 degrees or above [Para 106][Para 108][Para 109].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issue 2 was granted in three separate measures, Issue 4 refused, and Issue 5 granted in qualified form: sufficient masks whenever the employer keeps the mask requirement, but no fixed one-per-day entitlement [Para 266][Para 269][Para 270].

Issue 2 was split by contract history. For the two workers whose last fixed-duration contract expired on 20 April 2020 and who were let go on that same day, Article 73 paragraph 1 of the Labour Law made this ordinary expiry, not dismissal, so they received only the 5 per cent severance under paragraph 6, calculated on the last three months' wages [Para 112][Para 117][Para 118][Para 148]. For the two terminated two and three days before their contracts ran out, the panel held unilateral early termination to be dismissal, following Award 096/16-Tak San Enterprize and 223/16-Unicota, and awarded both the 5 per cent severance and damages under Article 73 paragraph 3 equal to wages to the contractual end date [Para 120][Para 121][Para 125][Para 150][Para 156].

The remaining four had three-month contracts renewed repeatedly. Where the total exceeded two years, or the contract was performed on after expiry with no written renewal, Articles 67(7) and 67(8) converted it into an indefinite-duration contract, as Award 271/13-D&L held; their earlier 5 per cent payments did not undo that [Para 126][Para 129][Para 130][Para 138][Para 139]. Dismissal from such a contract requires a valid reason under Article 74 and notice under Article 75; the 17 April letters carried no signature or thumbprint and were never acknowledged, so they were not official notice and pay in lieu was owed under Article 77 [Para 175][Para 186][Para 188][Para 190]. Seniority pay followed Article 89 (new) and Prakas 443, the panel applying the 2019 amendment prospectively but awarding 7 days for the first half of 2020, since service exceeded one month but fell short of six [Para 202][Para 214][Para 223]. Article 91 damages were refused: the COVID-19 shutdown was an external factor outside the employer's control and so a valid ground for dissolution [Para 228][Para 229][Para 231].

Issue 4 was refused because the eight were already dismissed and were already claiming severance on that footing; they no longer had any contractual relationship to suspend [Para 235][Para 236][Para 243]. On Issue 5 the panel read Article 229, Prakas 004/20 and the Ministry notification of 19 March as encouraging, not commanding, mask supply, and found no rule requiring a fresh mask each day since some masks can be reused; but an employer that imposes the requirement must bear its cost [Para 248][Para 249][Para 262][Para 264][Para 265].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-duration contract allowed to run to its own expiry date ends by non-renewal, not dismissal, and attracts only the 5 per cent contractual severance; terminating it even a few days early makes it a dismissal carrying damages for the unexpired term [Para 118][Para 125]. Short fixed-duration contracts renewed until they exceed two years, or performed on after expiry without a written renewal, become indefinite-duration contracts, from which dismissal requires a valid reason and statutory notice or pay in lieu [Para 129][Para 138][Para 190]. An employer that imposes a health requirement must supply what the requirement costs, but no per-day quantity is fixed by law [Para 261][Para 264].

**Obiter dicta:** the employer-appointed arbitrator dissented on the notice-in-lieu order for the four indefinite-contract workers, reasoning that the 5 per cent already paid at each renewal barred further payment; the majority's order stands notwithstanding [Para 274][Para 296][Para 297].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Every year in the text is truncated to two digits, printed "ឆ្នាំ ២០" for 2020, so no date can be verified from the digits alone [Para 10][Para 16]. The panel's constitution is dated "8 May 2019 2020" in one line, giving two years [Para 17]. The source's own issue numbering runs 1, 2, 4, 5 with no Issue 3 anywhere in the file, and the omission is never explained [Para 5][Para 6][Para 7]. Worker names are printed in several incompatible spellings across the document list and the reasoning, and the eight individuals are identified here by group rather than name for that reason [Para 32][Para 73][Para 153]. The notice letters are dated 14 April, 17 April and 20 April in different places [Para 83][Para 186]. The severance figure appears once as "154 per cent" where the rest of the award says 5 per cent [Para 210][Para 75].
