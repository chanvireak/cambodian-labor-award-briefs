# Award No. 024/20 — Starry Than Footwear (Arbitration Council, 28 April 2020)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Nan, worker-appointed; Kong Phalla, president) · Doc ID 139563

## 1. Facts
Thirty-one workers of Starry Than Footwear Co., Ltd. were dismissed on 18 March 2020, the day after a walkout at the plant; ten later accepted the payments offered and dropped their claims, leaving twenty-one claimants — three on three-month fixed-duration contracts and eighteen on contracts of indefinite duration. [Para 39][Para 41][Para 59] The company says the dismissals followed a 17 March stoppage in which a worker switched off the factory's power; the workers say they were at the Ministry for conciliation and were then refused entry by security. [Para 58][Para 61][Para 63]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must honour the fifteen points of the reconciliation agreement concluded on 16 March 2020 before the labour dispute officer? [Para 2] (withdrawn by the workers at the hearing — [Para 14])
2. Whether the company must pay the wages and other benefits owed to the thirty-one dismissed workers according to each worker's type of employment contract? [Para 39]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissals were made without valid reason and without notice, and severance was not paid within forty-eight hours; the worker accused of cutting the power was at the Ministry that morning, and on 18 March the workers came to work but security refused them entry. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 61][Para 63]
**Employer (respondent):** on 17 March the named worker and others demonstrated, shouted and cut the electricity, roughly fifty workers left the floor and thirty-one refused to return, so they were dismissed; the company had already offered final wages, five per cent severance and some damages, which the workers refused. [Para 47][Para 58][Para 60]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the company to pay the three fixed-duration workers damages equal to their wages to the contract end date, five per cent severance, outstanding annual leave and final wages, and the eighteen indefinite-duration workers damages in lieu of notice, outstanding annual leave, seven days' seniority indemnity for the first half of 2020, back seniority for pre-2019 service calculated on wages and allowances, damages under Article 91 and final wages. [Para 202][Para 203] No aggregate sum in riel or USD is printed; the only figure stated is 75 USD, the employer's own fifteen-day calculation for pre-2019 seniority. [Para 177]

On the fixed-duration contracts the panel read Article 73 of the Labour Law as allowing the employer to terminate unilaterally before the end date, but held that this opens the worker's right to damages of at least the wages due until the contract expires, following Awards 142/13, 243/13, 245/13, 067/14 and 205/15. [Para 74][Para 75][Para 77] It then added the five per cent severance under Article 73(6), following Award 096/16, annual leave under Articles 166 and 167, and final wages under Article 116. [Para 88][Para 94][Para 98]

On the eighteen indefinite-duration contracts the question was whether the dismissal was lawful under Articles 74 and 83. [Para 102][Para 109] The employer carried the burden of proving serious misconduct, following Award 107/04. [Para 115] The time-card showed the accused worker clocked in at 6.56 a.m. on 17 March, and the panel accepted he attended work that morning, but found the photographs undated and incapable of showing that he cut the power or that production was seriously disrupted, while the conciliation attendance list confirmed the workers were at the Ministry that afternoon. [Para 118][Para 119][Para 120][Para 121] No serious misconduct was established, so the dismissal lacked a valid reason. [Para 124]

The consequences followed article by article: damages in lieu of notice under Articles 75 and 77, scaled to each worker's actual seniority; outstanding annual leave under Articles 166 to 168; seven days' seniority indemnity for the first half of 2020 under new Article 89 and Article 2 of Prakas 443; unpaid pre-2019 back seniority under Article 3 of Prakas 443 and Instruction 057/19; damages under new Article 91, which the panel stressed is distinct from notice damages and seniority indemnity; and final wages under Article 116. [Para 134][Para 143][Para 161][Para 176][Para 187][Para 195][Para 198]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that ends a fixed-duration contract before its term owes the worker damages of at least the wages payable to the contract's end date unless the termination rests on the worker's serious misconduct, and where an employer dismisses a worker on an indefinite contract for alleged serious misconduct it must prove that misconduct — undated photographs and a time-card showing the worker's attendance do not discharge that burden. [Para 76][Para 119][Para 124]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that the present case is a termination rather than an exercise in paying back seniority, and that pre-2019 back seniority must be computed on wages and allowances rather than the basic wage alone, go beyond what the outcome required to be decided. [Para 170][Para 185][Para 186]
