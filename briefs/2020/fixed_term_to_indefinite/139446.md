# Award No. 015/20 — T & L (Cambodia) Han Bek In Das Steel Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 March 2020)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy chosen by the employer party, An Non chosen by the worker party — dissenting, Kong Phalla chair) · Doc ID 139446

## 1. Facts
Four workers at a Phnom Penh bag factory — the president, vice-president, secretary and treasurer of a newly formed workplace union — were elected on 4 February 2020, and the union posted notice of the result to the employer on 6 February 2020, the same day it applied to the Ministry for registration. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 78] The employer refused delivery of the notice and terminated all four on 3 March 2020, shortly before their renewed three-month contracts were due to expire, citing slow work and low productivity. [Para 71][Para 85][Para 92][Para 187] The workers seek reinstatement with back wages. [Para 75]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take the four workers back and pay their wages and other benefits from the date of dismissal to the date of return? [Para 65][Para 97][Para 231]

The other ten claims (pay day, pregnant workers, toilets, drinking water, clinic, new banknotes, forced overtime, transport, administrative language) were settled at conciliation, leaving this issue alone for decision. [Para 27][Para 28]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the four were union leaders; the employer summoned them on 3 March 2020 and told them to abandon union activity if they wanted their contracts renewed, then dismissed them without notice and without fault. [Para 80][Para 82][Para 83][Para 84]
**Employer (respondent):** the four worked slowly and their productivity was too low, causing late shipment; their contracts ran to 8 and 9 March 2020 and the employer had no wish to renew, and knew nothing of the union. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 88]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement and back wages were refused, but the panel ordered the employer to pay each of the four: damages in lieu of notice under Articles 75 and 77; seniority indemnity of 7 days for the first half of 2020 under Article 89 (new); damages under Article 91 (new) equal to that seniority indemnity; indemnity for unused annual leave under Articles 166 and 167; and the unpaid final month's wages under Article 116. No riel or USD figures are printed. [Para 212][Para 220][Para 227][Para 230][Para 232][Para 233]

The panel first fixed the contract type. Article 67(7) of the Labour Law requires a fixed-duration contract to be in writing, failing which it becomes a contract of unspecified duration; the employer had renewed orally every three months, so all four held indefinite-duration contracts. [Para 97][Para 98][Para 104][Para 106]

On protection, the panel applied Article 43(1) and Article 67 of the Trade Union Law 2016, which give election candidates the same shield as staff delegates — 45 days before and 45 days after the poll where the candidate loses, and, while a union is applying for registration, protection for founders and members until 30 days after the Ministry confirms registration. [Para 108][Para 111][Para 114] It applied the familiar three conditions: that the worker be one the law covers, that the dismissal fall inside the protected period, and that the employer and the Ministry have been notified of the protected names. [Para 115]

The first condition failed. Only Mr S Samon was shown by the registration receipt to be a candidate; for Mr O Samoeun, Mr M Samrong and Mr O Chhavorn the file held no election result proving that the result had been sent to the employer at all, so they were not protected workers. [Para 123][Para 124][Para 125] For Mr S Samon the dismissal of 3 March 2020 did fall inside the registration period, which on the authority of Award No. 010/17 would satisfy the second condition, but the panel then held the same evidential gap defeated it. [Para 132][Para 133][Para 134] The third condition also failed: the postal receipt of 10 February 2020 proved only that the employer refused delivery, no election document accompanied it, and the notice was never copied to the Ministry. [Para 135][Para 140][Para 141] All four therefore lacked dismissal protection. [Para 142]

On discrimination, Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law and Article 62 of the Trade Union Law forbid using union membership or office as a ground for termination; the claimant bears the burden, and the four offered no specific evidence beyond their own assertion. [Para 144][Para 146][Para 154][Para 159] The panel therefore found no union discrimination. [Para 161]

The dismissal still had to satisfy Article 74, which allows an employer to terminate an indefinite contract at will only with written notice and a valid reason going to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the needs of the enterprise. [Para 164][Para 167] The employer gave no prior written notice and relied on slow work, which the panel held did not meet Article 74. [Para 171][Para 172] Because the employer refused reinstatement and the relationship had lost mutual trust, the panel followed Award No. 023/17 and refused reinstatement while ordering compensation under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 173][Para 175][Para 181]

On quantum, Article 75 sets notice of 7 days for under six months' service and 15 days for six months to two years, and Article 77 requires wages and benefits for the notice period when it is not given; the four had worked from 8 July 2019. [Para 183][Para 184][Para 185][Para 190] Article 89 (new) and Prakas No. 443 of 21 September 2018 give 7.5 days per half-year, but paragraph 3 of Notification No. 023/19 bars a second claim where the employer already paid the 5 per cent termination indemnity every three months — which it had for 2019 — so only the 7 days for the first half of 2020 were owed. [Para 195][Para 197][Para 207][Para 209][Para 211] Article 91 (new) then adds damages equal to the seniority indemnity, capped at six months' wages. [Para 213][Para 217][Para 220]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-term contract renewed orally every three months is not a fixed-term contract at all: Article 67(7) makes writing a condition of that status, so the employment becomes indefinite and can be ended only under Article 74, with written notice and a reason tied to the worker's aptitude or conduct or the needs of the enterprise — an assertion of slow work unsupported by notice does not suffice. [Para 98][Para 106][Para 172] Union protection, by contrast, is not established by the fact of office: a claimant must produce the election record showing that the protected names were notified to the employer and copied to the Ministry, and without that document even an elected leader dismissed during the registration period is unprotected. [Para 124][Para 141]

**Obiter dicta:** the observations that the relationship had lost mutual trust, which justified refusing reinstatement rather than ordering it, and that payment of the 5 per cent indemnity forecloses a duplicate seniority claim, are reasoning about remedy and quantum rather than the rule of liability. [Para 179][Para 209]

**Dissenting opinion (Arbitrator An Non):** the employer told the four that if they did not resign from the union they would be dismissed, and had no reason to dismiss them; the award should therefore have ordered reinstatement with back wages from dismissal to return. [Para 240][Para 241]
