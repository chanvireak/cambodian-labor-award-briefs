# Award No. 112/19 — Go Ton Travel Service (Arbitration Council, 30 January 2020)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Tuon Si Phan for the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 139282

## 1. Facts
Go Ton Travel Service is a tourist-transport company at Trapeang Ses, Kouk Chak, Siem Reap, employing 33 workers. [Para 36][Para 37] Fourteen workers on indefinite oral contracts — the union's demand speaks of fifteen — asked the employer to terminate their contracts before the labour inspector and pay severance, because there was too little work. [Para 3][Para 38][Para 39][Para 40] Conciliation failed on 4 December 2019, the two original demands were merged into one, and the Council held a hearing on 17 January 2020; the award is **non-binding**. [Para 13][Para 19][Para 20]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to terminate the employment contracts of the 14 workers and pay them severance? [Para 57]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Article 83 of the Labour Law forbids hiring workers for whom there is no work, and Article 2 does not let the employer assign work outside a worker's skill — here workers were told to drive the owner's children to school, carry them food and wash cars; some 90 per cent of the tourist vehicles cannot run and the repair staff have all resigned, so there is no work left to give. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49]
**Employer (respondent):** the company is still operating and has no wish to terminate the contracts or close; wages have been paid twice a month without fail; the assignment of driving the owner's children and washing cars is within the workers' duties, not a misuse of skill; four tourist vehicles still run and others will be repaired when tourists return; if it ever does terminate, it will follow the Labour Law. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 50][Para 51][Para 52][Para 54][Para 55][Para 56]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the demand that the employer terminate the contracts of the 14 workers and pay severance. [Para 70]

The panel framed the single question as whether the employer is obliged to terminate and pay severance. [Para 57] It began with Article 311 of the Civil Code, under which a contract is the meeting of wills by which persons create, modify or extinguish obligations, and Article 65(1) of the Labour Law, under which the employment contract creates the working relationship and is governed by that Law in whatever form the parties agree. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 59] From these it derived that the creation, modification and extinction of contractual obligations must come from the will of the parties to the contract — employer and worker — so only those two parties hold the right to extinguish it, and one party cannot compel the other to extinguish obligations against that other's will. [Para 60][Para 61] It followed four prior awards to the same effect: Nos. 34/13, 138/13, 039/14 and 308/14. [Para 62]

On the facts, the employer had no intention of terminating, because it still needed the workers to run the company. [Para 62] Under an indefinite contract a party may terminate on reasonable grounds or for serious misconduct, but where a party has no wish to terminate, no law requires it to do so at the other side's demand. [Para 64][Para 65] The employer therefore had no obligation to terminate and pay severance. [Para 66]

The panel also addressed the workers' request at the hearing that it lay down a severance calculation formula. [Para 67] It declined, because the employer had not yet terminated any contract, so there was nothing to calculate. [Para 68][Para 69]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Termination of an employment contract is an act of party will, not a remedy one side can extract from the other: under Article 311 of the Civil Code and Article 65(1) of the Labour Law only the parties to the contract may extinguish its obligations, and neither may force the other to terminate against its will, still less to pay severance for a termination it did not choose. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 65] It follows that a union cannot obtain severance by asking the panel to order the employer to dismiss its own members; the remedy, if any, lies in the employer's own decision to terminate on reasonable grounds or for serious misconduct. [Para 64][Para 66] Nor will the panel fix a severance formula where no termination has occurred. [Para 68]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's acceptance that assigning workers to drive the owner's children to school, carry their food and wash cars was within their existing duties, and its findings on the four serviceable vehicles and the departed repair staff, are factual observations that did not decide the outcome, since the claim failed on the will of the parties alone. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 54]
