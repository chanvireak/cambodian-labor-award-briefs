# Award No. 056/20 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ាយ អូលែត អឹភែរឹល (ខេមបូឌា) (Arbitration Council, 12 November 2020)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៉ីង សុទ្ធី appointed by the employer, វ៉ែន ពៅ appointed by the workers, chair ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា) · Doc ID 140817

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ាយ អូលែត អឹភែរឹល (ខេមបូឌា), Y All Apparel (Cambodia), is a garment-cutting and sewing company at Trapeang Chhuk village, Tuek Thla sangkat, Sen Sok, Phnom Penh, employing 1,284 workers. [Para 1] [Para 66] The employer suspended the labour contracts for two months from 1 May to 30 June 2020 for lack of orders during COVID-19, told the workers on 26 June 2020 that the factory would close and what it would pay, and closed on 1 July 2020. [Para 69] [Para 70] [Para 71] The workers filed six demands; conciliation on 29 September 2020 failed on all six, and at the hearing the workers withdrew demands 2 to 5 after the parties agreed them, leaving demands 1 and 6. [Para 14] [Para 15] [Para 20] [Para 21] The parties chose a binding award, and the panel heard them on 8 October 2020. [Para 22] [Para 13]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must compute the seniority used for the payment in lieu of notice from the first fixed-duration contract, adding both contract periods together, rather than from 1 January 2019? [Para 76] [Para 104] [Para 110]
2. (Issue 1(b)) Whether the employer must pay damages (សោហ៊ុយជំងឺចិត្ត) for ending the contracts when it closed the factory? [Para 75] [Para 139]
3. (Issue 6) Whether the employer must pay a 100 per cent wage supplement to the 1,184 workers who worked on the five Khmer New Year days in April 2020? [Para 97] [Para 151]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that notice pay must be computed on actual seniority counted from the first employment contract, because Articles 73(7), 74, 75 and 77 fix seniority by reference to the first contract whatever 5 per cent payments were made at each fixed-term renewal, and that damages are due because the closure destroyed their jobs. [Para 76] [Para 77] [Para 78] They also sought the 100 per cent supplement for the five New Year days they worked. [Para 97] [Para 101]
**Employer (respondent):** that it calculated notice pay from 1 January 2019, when the fixed-duration contracts became undetermined-duration, and paid the 5 per cent termination allowance at each renewal as Notification No. 023/19 and the labour inspector's letters required; it also said the workers refused damages and had accepted 5 per cent. [Para 74] [Para 79] [Para 88] [Para 91] On the New Year days it said those April days had become ordinary working days because the holiday was moved to August. [Para 102] [Para 103]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to decide the notice-pay seniority question, and refused both the damages claim and the 100 per cent New Year wage supplement. [Para 162] [Para 163] [Para 164]
On notice pay the panel set out Article 73 paragraphs 5, 6 and 7 of the Labour Law and read paragraph 7 as requiring the two contract periods to be added where a fixed-duration contract becomes undetermined-duration; it also followed earlier awards holding the same. [Para 114] [Para 116] [Para 117] [Para 118] [Para 119] It then found that the employer had relied on Notification No. 023/19 of 8 July 2019 and on letters No. 070 and No. 295 of the labour inspectorate, which say seniority runs only from the date of conversion where the 5 per cent allowance was already paid. [Para 123] [Para 126] That put the panel on a question of law it held it had no power to decide: whether a ministerial notification contrary to Article 73 can be applied, and it said a party must seek an interpretation from the competent government institution, the competent court or the Constitutional Council instead. [Para 130] [Para 135] [Para 136] [Para 137] The claim was therefore declined without a ruling on the merits. [Para 138]
On damages the panel applied Article 91 (new) of the Labour Law, under which damages are due only for a termination without a valid reason, and found that the pandemic cancellation of European orders was a cause outside the employer's control, since the employer had already suspended the contracts for two months and still had no orders. [Para 142] [Para 146] [Para 147] [Para 148] [Para 149] The claim was refused. [Para 150]
On the New Year days the panel found that the four April days became ordinary working days under Sub-Decree No. 40 and Prakas No. 242/20, the holiday being moved to 17 to 21 August 2020, and that the company had closed on 1 July before those replacement days, so no 100 per cent supplement was owed. [Para 157] [Para 158] [Para 159] [Para 160] [Para 161]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a claim can succeed only if a ministerial notification is held contrary to the Labour Law, the panel will decline to decide it, because it has no power to rule on the validity of a notification or of a labour-inspectorate letter and the party must ask the competent institution, court or Constitutional Council for that interpretation. [Para 130] [Para 137] A shutdown caused by pandemic order cancellations, after the employer has already suspended contracts, is a valid reason for termination under Article 91 (new), so no damages are payable. [Para 142] [Para 149] Where the government moves public-holiday days to a later month and the employer closes before those replacement days arrive, no 100 per cent supplement is owed for work done on the original dates. [Para 157] [Para 159]

**Obiter dicta:** that the panel read Article 73 paragraph 7 as adding both contract periods together, an interpretation it recorded but did not apply because jurisdiction was lacking. [Para 118] [Para 130]
