# Award No. 004/20 — Park Hen Bag (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 17 February 2020)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Seng Vouch Hun (employer-nominated), An Nan (worker-nominated), Run Saray (president) · Doc ID 139288

## 1. Facts
Park Hen Bag (Cambodia) Co., Ltd makes bags on Street 130 in Samrong Tong district, Kompong Speu, and employed 1,326 workers. [Para 53][Para 54] On 4 January 2020 the company dismissed three workers before their fixed-duration contracts expired, without notice: two were elected workers' delegates and the third a union activist. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 71][Para 72] The workforce struck from 8 to 14 January 2020. [Para 64] On 13 January 2020 the provincial Labour Department wrote that the dismissal of the two delegates breached procedure and ordered their reinstatement, but the company appealed to the court instead of the Minister. [Para 65][Para 82] The Council decided one issue only, the claim for reinstatement, the other demands having been settled or withdrawn. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24] The award is non-binding. [Para 25][Para 157]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the Council has jurisdiction over issue 1 when the non-conciliation report of 13 January 2020 omits it? [Para 97][Para 99]
1(a). Whether the employer must reinstate the two dismissed workers' delegates? [Para 107][Para 113]
1(b). Whether the employer validly terminated the contract of the third worker, and what it owes him? [Para 121][Para 128]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The three dismissals were procedurally unlawful, because one of the three was an elected workers' delegate and another a union activist; the claims had never been withdrawn at conciliation. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 104]
**Employer (respondent):** The three incited workers to slow production so that goods would ship late and a manager named Kim would be blamed, left the premises without authorisation, ignored counselling, and used improper language; one forged workers' thumbprints and drank alcohol on site, both serious misconduct under Article 83 of the Labour Law and the November 2018 disciplinary policy. [Para 73][Para 76][Para 78][Para 79] It did not know they held delegate or union office because the company kept no delegate records. [Para 83][Para 84]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement was refused for all three workers; for the third, the panel ordered the employer to pay damages in lieu of notice and other statutory entitlements under Article 73, the amount to be computed from his wage to the contract's end. [Para 155][Para 156]

On jurisdiction the panel applied Article 312 of the Labour Law and Article 33 of Prakas No. 099, which confine it to the points in the non-conciliation minutes and their direct consequences. [Para 99][Para 100] The 13 January report excluded issue 1 on the ground that the representatives had withdrawn it, but the 8 January minutes, signed by ten participants, list five unresolved points including issue 1, and no passage records any withdrawal. [Para 101][Para 103][Para 105] The panel therefore held issue 1 to be an unresolved point already in the minutes, not a new demand, and took jurisdiction. [Para 105][Para 106]

On issue 1(a) it found both workers to be elected delegates, and applied Article 43 of the Trade Union Law 2016: no delegate or candidate may be dismissed without the labour inspector's prior agreement, and a party dissatisfied with that decision has two months to appeal to the Minister, who may cancel or amend it. [Para 108][Para 110][Para 113] The provincial department had refused permission on 13 January 2020 and the two-month window had not run, so the question sat within the Minister's competence and the panel declined to decide it. [Para 117][Para 118][Para 119][Para 120]

On issue 1(b) the panel held that Article 73 lets an employer end a fixed-duration contract before its term only by consent or for serious misconduct, and that a unilateral early ending otherwise entitles the worker to damages of at least the wages due to the contract's end. [Para 128][Para 130][Para 132][Para 150] The photographs and video showed workers entering and leaving and gathering, but nothing identified the third worker drinking alcohol or forging thumbprints, and the employer produced no witness to the forgery. [Para 147][Para 148][Para 149] Reinstatement was refused but the Article 73 compensation was ordered. [Para 152][Para 154][Para 156]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a non-conciliation report omits a demand that the signed conciliation minutes record as unresolved, and nothing in the minutes shows the workers withdrew it, the Council has jurisdiction over it as an existing point of disagreement rather than a new one. [Para 103][Para 105] The dismissal of an elected workers' delegate is outside the Council's competence while the labour inspector's refusal stands and the two-month period for appealing it to the Minister is still open. [Para 113][Para 119] An employer that ends a fixed-duration contract early without proved serious misconduct owes damages at least equal to the wages remaining to the contract's end. [Para 132][Para 150]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that the employer's own disciplinary policy grades sanctions progressively — oral warning, written warning, suspension, dismissal — which it did not follow before dismissing. [Para 93]

## 6. Source defects disclosed

[Para 9][Para 17] The award inverts the conciliation record: it says the report lists four unresolved points excluding issue 1, while reciting that the same report lists issues 2 to 5 and that issue 1 was withdrawn. [Para 55][Para 97] The number of unresolved points is given as four and as five in adjacent passages. [Para 16][Para 21] The first dismissed worker is named once as "ជូ ស្រៀង" and elsewhere "ផូ ស្រៀង", and the striking workers' names are given inconsistently. [Para 61][Para 71] The panel's signature block lost the employer-side arbitrator's name to OCR noise. [Para 158] Individual workers and union officers are reduced to initials here. [Para 61]
