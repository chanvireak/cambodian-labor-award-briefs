# Award No. 036/20 — Chhun Vey (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឈួន វៃ (ខេមបូឌា) ខូអិលធីឌី) (Arbitration Council, 20 July 2020)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · three-member panel: សេង រួច ហ៊ុន (employer-nominated), ស៊ីន គីម ស៊ាន (worker-nominated), គង់ ផល្លៈ (president) · Doc ID 140561

## 1. Facts
Chhun Vey (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឈួន វៃ (ខេមបូឌា) ខូអិលធីឌី) provides internet service from Phnom Penh and employed 23 workers at the time of the dispute. [Para 44] Four employees earning above US$800 had their May 2020 wages cut by 40 per cent after the company lost online-gaming and casino clients to COVID-19; they had not signed the reduction letter and continued to work full hours. [Para 50][Para 51] The workers' representatives raised three demands in conciliation; the parties settled Issues 1 and 3 (payment of wages twice monthly, and terminal benefits on dismissal), leaving only Issue 2 for the panel. [Para 19][Para 20]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer's 40 per cent reduction of the four workers' May 2020 wages was lawful, such that the deducted amount must be repaid and further deductions stopped? [Para 67]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), the workers' representatives of Chhun Vey (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (ភាគី តំណាង កម្មករ និយោជិត នៃ ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឈួន វៃ (ខេមបូឌា) ខូអិលធីឌី):** they never agreed to the cut, know nothing of the company's accounts, believe it can still pay in full, and worked full hours throughout. [Para 54][Para 56]
**Employer (respondent), Chhun Vey (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឈួន វៃ (ខេមបូឌា) ខូអិលធីឌី):** the ban on online gambling from 18 August 2019 and the closure of casinos left it with 75 per cent less revenue, it had already shed 20 per cent of staff, and 24-hour service obligations prevented shorter hours. [Para 57][Para 60][Para 64]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to repay the 40 per cent of wages deducted from the four workers in May 2020 and to stop deducting wages from workers with immediate effect. [Para 108]

The panel began from Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 664 of the Civil Code: a labour contract is formed by offer and acceptance, and Article 102 requires the employer to pay the wage agreed. [Para 68][Para 70][Para 74] On the Civil Code it cited Articles 311, 336 and 337 — a contract is created, modified or extinguished only by concurring wills, and no party may impose terms the other has not accepted; a contract made under compulsion may be void. [Para 79][Para 85][Para 90]

Applying this, the panel found the employer had unilaterally altered a wage term that the parties had fixed at a specific figure; the deduction letters were the employer's will alone, and none of the four workers signed them. [Para 78][Para 100] It also weighed the financial evidence and found the employer's income-and-expense table carried no date, signature or seal, so it proved nothing. [Para 65] Even accepting a genuine downturn, the panel held that economic crisis or falling revenue is not a lawful ground for cutting agreed wages. [Para 99] It followed Awards 56/06-Borik, 96/13, 154/13, 164/13, 188/13, 204/13, 016/14, 191/15 and 028/20-CAMS (Cambodia Airports). [Para 91] Under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 the panel has power to order immediate payment and to order cessation of unlawful conduct, and it did both. [Para 106]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may not reduce a contractually agreed wage by unilateral decision: a wage term is part of the contract and can only be varied by the worker's consent, and neither a documented fall in revenue nor a state of economic crisis supplies that consent. [Para 101] Unauthenticated internal financial tables do not discharge the employer's burden of proving the necessity of a cut. [Para 65]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the four workers continued to perform full working hours while paid 60 per cent, which it treated as evidence that the reduction was not a genuine reduction of work. [Para 77]
