# Award No. 008/20 — Dik Neathy Nit Ther Ltd & E Co Base Factory (Arbitration Council, 14 February 2020)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Seng Vouch Hun (employer-appointed), Sin Kim Sean (worker-appointed), Kong Phalla (president) · Doc ID 139289

## 1. Facts
Two related garment factories — Dik Neathy Nit Ther Ltd employing 877 workers and E Co Base Factory employing 172 — faced a collective dispute over three unpaid entitlements. [Para 66] The employer had paid December 2019 wages in two instalments of USD 50 (22 December 2019 and 1 January 2020) and still owed each worker between USD 20 and USD 100. [Para 69] Conciliation at the Kandal provincial department of labour failed on all three points, which were forwarded to the Council on 23 January 2020. [Para 12][Para 14] The workers' representatives were Mr S (local union president) and Mr P; the employer was represented by its director-general and administrative and compliance staff. [Para 2]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the remainder of the December 2019 wages to all workers on 8 February 2020? [Para 67]
2. Whether the employer must make the back payment (រំលឹក) of seniority indemnity for service before 2019 to all workers by 8 February 2020? [Para 75]
3. Whether the employer must pay the seniority indemnity (បំណាច់អតីតភាពការងារ) for the second semester of 2019 to all workers by 8 February 2020? [Para 79]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** they had performed their work for December 2019 and received no full wage, contrary to Article 116 of the Labour Law; Prakas 443 and Instructions 057 and 058 oblige the employer to pay seniority back pay for pre-2019 service and to settle the 2019 indemnity every six months. [Para 70][Para 77][Para 81]
**Employer (respondent):** it could not meet the claims because the novel coronavirus outbreak in China delayed transfers of money into Cambodia, buyers owed it money and refused to pay, no new orders had arrived and some orders were suspended. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted on all three issues — the employer was ordered to pay the remaining December 2019 wages, the seniority back payment and the second-semester 2019 seniority indemnity to all workers, in each case on the date the award becomes final. [Para 125][Para 126][Para 127]

On issue 1 the panel asked whether the employer owed the December 2019 balance. Reading Article 664 of the 2007 Civil Code (a contract of employment is a promise of work exchanged for a promise of wages), it held that wages are the counter-value of labour actually performed, and that where work has been done the employer's obligation stands. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 86] Both parties accepted that the workers had served through December, and that the employer had paid only two instalments of USD 50 while withholding the rest. [Para 87][Para 88] Article 116 of the Labour Law and Article 1 of Prakas No. 442 (wages twice a month, 50% in the second week and the balance in the fourth week) were applied; no provision permits deferral, even where workers had agreed to a postponement to 8 February 2020. [Para 91][Para 92][Para 98][Para 99] The panel followed its reasoning in Awards 33/15 and 065/15 that wages follow completed work, and in Award 085/19 on twice-monthly payment. [Para 86][Para 96]

On issue 2 (pre-2019 back pay) the panel relied on Article 3 of Prakas No. 443 and point 3 of Instruction No. 057: for textile, garment and footwear enterprises the employer must pay 30 days of cumulative seniority indemnity each year, 15 days in June and 15 days in December, calculated on each worker's actual seniority from 2008 onward. [Para 109][Para 111][Para 112] The employer had paid the first semester of 2019 but nothing for the second, and its coronavirus and buyer-default explanation was held to be no legal excuse. [Para 113][Para 114]

On issue 3 the panel applied Article 89 (new) of the Labour Law — 15 days of wages and allowances per year, half payable every six months — as interpreted in Award 071/19, and Article 2 of Prakas No. 443 (7.5 days in June and 7.5 days in December). [Para 104][Para 106][Para 118][Para 120] Payment of the first semester did not discharge the second. [Para 121]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer's contractual duty to pay wages is triggered by the worker's performance of the work, not by the employer's liquidity: delayed bank transfers, defaulting buyers and suspended orders are not recognised grounds for postponing wages, seniority back pay or the semi-annual seniority indemnity, and a worker's agreement to a deferral does not displace the statutory payment schedule. [Para 90][Para 99][Para 114]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's description of the two companies as garment enterprises with four main activities, and its account of the conciliation chronology, were recited as background and were not relied on for the orders. [Para 65]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is a non-binding award (សេចក្តីបង្គាប់មិនជាចំណុះកាតព្វកិច្ច), becoming final eight days after notification if no party objects to the Minister of Labour. [Para 128] OCR losses are extensive. The year is truncated throughout as "ឆ្នាំ ២០" in the header and reasons, though the reasoning fixes 14 February 2020; line 21 prints the month corruptly as "ខែម ភ្ជាំ". [Para 1][Para 21][Para 102] Issue 3's heading dates the claim "8 February 2015" while the panel's own question and every related passage say 2020. [Para 117] The employer companies appear under at least four spellings (Dik Neathy Nit Ther / Dik Neathy Nit Thear / E Co Base / E Coch Base), and Instruction 057 is quoted as requiring both 15 days and 14 days for the June instalment. [Para 110] Stray digits intrude into the text ("បើកប្រាក់ ៤ ឈ្នួល", "២៥ មាត្រា"), and individual worker and union names were reduced to initials. [Para 83][Para 104]
