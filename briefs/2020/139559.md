# Award No. 021/20 — Long Met Agri Call Cheur Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 24 April 2020)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 139559

## 1. Facts
Long Met Agri Call Cheur Co., Ltd is an agricultural enterprise at Trapeang Phleang village, Chhouk district, Kampot province, employing 778 workers. Of eleven demands taken to conciliation in early 2020, ten were settled and one remained: that the company reinstate Mr L, a union activist, to his former job and pay his wages from dismissal to reinstatement. [Para 3][Para 9][Para 10][Para 47] Mr L was hired on 13 May 2018 on an indefinite contract, was absent without permission from 4 September 2019, filed a leave request for 5 to 11 September 2019 citing personal reasons without supporting documents, returned on 11 September and was dismissed on 12 September 2019. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61][Para 63][Para 122]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the dispute is a collective labour dispute within the panel's jurisdiction, the employer objecting that it is an individual dispute concerning one worker? [Para 48][Para 88]
2. Whether the employer lawfully dismissed Mr L for six days of unauthorised absence? [Para 123][Para 135]
3. Whether the employer must reinstate him and pay wages from dismissal, and what termination payments are owed? [Para 121][Para 142]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The dispute is collective, since the labour ministry referred it and roughly 100 to 127 workers thumbprinted the original complaint, including thirteen union leaders; the dismissal was discrimination against a union activist, the absence was excused by his own leg injury and a sick wife, and he had filed a leave request. [Para 53][Para 56][Para 66][Para 72][Para 99]
**Employer (respondent):** The dispute is individual — only Mr L was still in dispute, the other demands having been settled and the settled points already applied — and it had already filed a complaint with the Kampot Provincial Court, received on 6 April 2020; the union local and nineteen workers had petitioned against his return; six days of unauthorised absence amounted to abandonment of employment under Article 6 of the internal rules. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 52][Para 77][Para 82]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim for reinstatement, back pay and moral damages was dismissed; the employer was ordered to pay wages in lieu of notice, pay in lieu of unused annual leave, seniority indemnity, and the final month's wages, with no sum quantified in the award [Para 209][Para 210]

On jurisdiction, the panel held that under Article 32 of Prakas No. 099 it decides only collective disputes, and that the labour inspectorate and minister ordinarily classify a dispute before referral, a classification the panel follows absent clear reason to depart from it. Applying the three conditions drawn from Article 302 of the Labour Law in Award No. 10/03 (Zaxsintech), it found the claim was brought by a group of workers against one employer, concerned the dismissal of their union activist, and could disturb the smooth running of the enterprise and social peace — 127 workers had thumbprinted the complaint and the ministry had referred it. The panel also noted that its own constitution on 31 March 2020 preceded the court's receipt of the employer's complaint on 6 April 2020, so that complaint had no effect at the time the arbitration began. [Para 88][Para 100][Para 104][Para 107][Para 110][Para 116][Para 119]

On the dismissal, the panel applied Article 74 of the Labour Law, which permits termination at the employer's will only with a valid reason relating to the worker's aptitude or conduct or the needs of the enterprise, and Article 6 of the internal rules, which classes under two days' absence in a month as a minor fault, two to six days as a medium fault, and six days in a month or six consecutive days as abandonment of employment. It held that the rules nowhere describe six consecutive days' absence as serious misconduct, so the employer had no clear legal basis for that characterisation; the true characterisation was abandonment, and the employer's decision to dismiss rather than record abandonment nonetheless rested on a valid reason under Article 74. [Para 123][Para 130][Para 133][Para 135][Para 141]

Because the dismissal was with a valid reason but without the written notice Article 74 requires, the panel ordered payment in lieu of notice under Articles 75 and 77, computed on the average wage of the last twelve months. It ordered pay in lieu of untaken annual leave under Articles 166 and 167, seniority indemnity under the amended Article 89 and Prakas No. 443 — fifteen days for 2018, the eight months worked counting as a full year, and seven days for the second half of 2019, roughly two months and twelve days — and the final month's wage under Article 116. Moral damages under the amended Article 91 were refused, the dismissal having a valid reason, and reinstatement with back pay was refused for the same reason. [Para 145][Para 148][Para 161][Para 189][Para 192][Para 201][Para 204]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an internal rule treats six consecutive days of unauthorised absence as abandonment of employment rather than serious misconduct, dismissal on that ground is justified under Article 74 but is not a dismissal for serious misconduct, so the employer still owes notice pay, untaken annual leave, seniority indemnity and the final month's wage. [Para 135][Para 141][Para 148]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remarks that it will ordinarily follow the labour inspectorate's classification of a dispute as collective or individual, and its citation of Article 87 of the 2006 Civil Procedure Code on when a court claim takes effect, were general statements not needed to decide the single remaining demand. [Para 91][Para 114]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The year of the award and of most procedural dates is printed only as "ឆ្នាំ ២០" (20), the final digit group being lost; the 2020 year is inferred from case number 021/20 and from the hearing and dismissal dates that are printed in full. [Para 1][Para 6][Para 19] The composition paragraph gives the president as "Pen Bun Chheam" while the signature block prints "Pen Bun Chhea", and the employer-appointed arbitrator's signature line carries no name. [Para 1][Para 212] The panel's own numbering is inconsistent: the single unresolved demand is numbered "ចំណុច វិវាទ ទី ១" in the reasons although only one demand was referred. [Para 3][Para 121] The worker's hire date appears as 13 May 2018 in the findings and as "13 May 2023 2018" in the seniority calculation. [Para 59][Para 186] Large runs of text are visibly corrupted, including the passages on the employer's court complaint and the final order, which repeats itself and mixes in stray figures. [Para 115][Para 210] The award states that the employer did not treat the absence as abandonment, then that the dismissal rested on abandonment under the rules, without reconciling the two. [Para 134][Para 139]
