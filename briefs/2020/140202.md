# Award No. 029/20 — HC Group (Arbitration Council, 11 June 2020)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phalla chair; You Soun Ty appointed by the employer; An Vireak appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 140202

## 1. Facts
HC Group — also printed as HCC and EnviroCam — supplied cleaning and waste-collection services at Cambodia's international airports and employed 163 workers. [Para 63] On 24 March the employer met all staff and announced that 116 contracts would be suspended from 25 March, seven workers being kept on; no work was given to the 116 afterwards, though March wages were paid in full. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67] The claimant is the Union of Workers of Cambodia International Airport of HC Group, acting for the workers in the collective dispute; its complaint of 26 March went to the provincial labour department, conciliation failed, and 40 workers pursued April and May claims for wages, food allowance and holiday-day pay. [Para 1][Para 68][Para 88] The panel also recorded two demands it never reached: the workers' original demands 1 and 2, that wages and all benefits be maintained without any reduction, and the employer's counter-request that the workers instead petition for a 50% wage to be forwarded to company management. [Para 2][Para 3]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the suspension of the 40 workers' contracts in April and May was carried out in accordance with Article 71(11) and Article 72(1) of the Labour Law, so that the employer owed them no wages? [Para 96]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must therefore pay 100% wages, the monthly food allowance and holiday-day wages to the 40 workers for April and May? [Para 64][Para 97]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers, through the Union of Workers of Cambodia International Airport of HC Group (claimant):** the suspension was invalid because the employer neither notified nor obtained authorization from the labour inspector before suspending, contrary to Articles 71 and 72; without that authorization Article 72(1) did not release the wage obligation, and the company had actual work for six workers only because the airport demanded it. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 73][Para 4]
**HC Group (respondent):** COVID-19 removed almost all air traffic — revenue fell below 10%, the airport telephoned on 23 March asking that staff be cut to six, and the company applied three times (30 March, 1 April, 13 April), following the department's advice on form; the inspector then postponed his inspection, so the delay was not the employer's fault, and the company would re-employ the workers with benefits and seniority preserved when conditions improved. [Para 77][Para 79][Para 81][Para 84][Para 87]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay 100% wages for April and May to the 40 workers; the claims for food allowance and for holiday-day wages were dismissed. [Para 139][Para 140][Para 141]

On wages, Article 71(11) permits suspension for serious economic or material difficulty, for no more than two months and under the supervision of the labour inspector; following Award 58/07 Star Sport Wear the panel read that supervision as requiring prior notification of, and authorization from, the inspector. [Para 97][Para 99][Para 100] Article 72(1) removes the wage obligation only where the suspension was itself lawful. [Para 102][Para 103] Both parties accepted that the suspension ran from 25 March without any authorizing letter, and the department's letter of 22 April said only that a new online filing system had prevented it from issuing a permit — a filing is not an authorization. [Para 108][Para 109][Para 111] Full wages were therefore owed. [Para 112]

On the food allowance, Article 103 defines wages, while clause 6(ឃ) of the internal regulations grants a monthly food allowance; the panel held that allowance to be a gratuity rewarding attendance rather than wages, following Award 040/17, so with no work performed in April and May nothing was payable. [Para 113][Para 115][Para 116][Para 119]

On holiday pay, Article 164 and Prakas No. 10 SKBY of 4 February 1999 give a worker who actually works a holiday one additional day's ordinary wage, following Award 52/12; no worker worked a holiday in those months, so the claim failed. [Para 123][Para 126][Para 129][Para 134][Para 135]

The employer-appointed arbitrator dissented, treating the administrative delay as outside the employer's control and the wage order as an unfair burden. [Para 144][Para 147][Para 159][Para 160]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension under Article 71(11) displaces the Article 72(1) wage obligation only where the employer obtained the labour inspector's prior authorization; a request filed but not authorized leaves the suspension procedurally invalid, and the employer owes 100% wages for the suspended months even though it provided no work. A monthly food allowance paid only to workers who attend is a gratuity, not a wage, and is not owed for a month in which no work was done. [Para 105][Para 111][Para 119]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's wider proposition that a worker who does work a holiday earns one extra day's wage on top of the monthly wage was not applied, no worker having worked. [Para 130][Para 134]
