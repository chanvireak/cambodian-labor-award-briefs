# Award No. 031/20 — Rusing Gamen (រូស៊ីង ហ្គាម៉ែន) (Arbitration Council, 1 June 2020)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Liv Sovanna, chair Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 139935

## 1. Facts
The workers of Rusing Gamen, a garment factory at Toul Sangke, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, filed a complaint with the Department of Labour Disputes on 15 April 2020 raising three points, the principal one being that the company take back five named union leaders and activists — Mr E, Mr S, Mr K, Ms C and Mr N — and let them work normally. [Para 1] [Para 19] Conciliation on 12 May 2020 failed, and non-conciliation report No. 474 KB/AK/VK dated 13 May 2020 reached the Secretariat with only two issues, the reinstatement demand having disappeared. [Para 12] [Para 14] [Para 15] [Para 20] The hearing was set for 25 May 2020 at 2:00 p.m., and both parties attended. [Para 17] [Para 18]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay statutory severance and correct wages to five dismissed workers? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company must stop discriminating against the Cambodian Union Federation (CATU) — warning worker Mr K for telephoning the union, seizing ten thumbprint sheets, ordering workers to withdraw their thumbprints, and refusing to check off union dues? [Para 4] [Para 5] [Para 6] [Para 7]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that the company paid wages unlawfully to five workers on dismissal, and that from 9 April 2020 it warned, interfered with and pressured union members, telling them to erase their thumbprints from CATU's sheets "or there will be problems". [Para 2] [Para 4] [Para 6] [Para 7]
**Employer (respondent):** that on dismissing any worker it had already paid wages and other benefits as the Labour Law requires, and that it did not discriminate against CATU at all. [Para 3] [Para 8]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** withdrawn — the panel terminated the arbitration proceedings in case No. 031/20, treating the claimant's walk-out as abandonment of its complaint; no order was made on either issue. [Para 54] [Para 56] [Para 57]

The panel never reached the merits. It first held that Article 312 of the 1997 Labour Law and Clause 33 of Prakas No. 099 confine it to the issues stated in the non-conciliation minutes, following Awards 115/06 and 27/07 and the line of awards listed at paragraph 46, so the reinstatement demand — though the main demand of the complaint — could not be examined. [Para 44] [Para 46] [Para 47] [Para 48]
It would therefore hear only the two issues transmitted by the Department. [Para 22] [Para 52] The workers objected, asked that all three points from the original complaint be taken up, and then left the hearing room. [Para 21] [Para 51] [Para 53]
Clause 18 of Prakas No. 099 requires the parties to appear and speak in person, Clause 20 requires attendance at every session, and Clause 21 with Rule 4.7 of the annex allows the panel either to continue in the party's absence or to close the case by award, provided notice was properly given. [Para 36] [Para 37] [Para 38] [Para 39] [Para 40] Departing before the proceedings begin counts as failure to appear. [Para 44] The panel chose the second course and treated the walk-out as abandonment. [Para 54] [Para 55]
It also recorded that it could find no facts on the disputed issues, the claimant having left. [Para 35]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claimant union that, having been duly summoned, walks out of the hearing because the panel declines to add an issue omitted from the non-conciliation minutes is taken to have abandoned its complaint, and the panel will terminate the arbitration rather than decide the transmitted issues in its absence. The panel's jurisdiction is fixed by the non-conciliation minutes: a demand in the original complaint that the conciliator did not transmit cannot be arbitrated, however central it is. [Para 46] [Para 54] [Para 55]

**Obiter dicta:** that walking out of the hearing room before the proceedings have begun may be treated as failure to appear or absence of representation under Rule 4.7, a proposition drawn from Award 115/06 rather than from this case's own facts. [Para 44]
