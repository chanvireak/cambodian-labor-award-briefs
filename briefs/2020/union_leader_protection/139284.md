# Award No. 117/19 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ធឿន ស៊ុង ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) ខូ អិលធីឌី (Arbitration Council, ថ្ងៃទី ២២ ខែមករា ឆ្នាំ ២០២០)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · អ៊ុង សុទ្ធី, លីវ សុវណ្ណា, president ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា · Doc ID 139284

## 1. Facts
The employer is a Phnom Penh garment manufacturer with about 1,182 workers, and the claimant was the collective employee representative body. [Para 1] [Para 79] A complaint on improvement of 13 working conditions was lodged with the Labour Dispute Department on 29 November 2019; conciliation on 18 December 2019 left 9 points unresolved, and the report was sent to the Secretariat on 27 December 2019. [Para 27] [Para 28] [Para 29] The panel was formed on 31 December 2019 and heard both sides, who appeared, on 8 January 2020. [Para 31] [Para 32] [Para 33] The worker side withdrew claims 2, 7, 8 and 9, the parties agreed on claim 6, and four claims remained. [Para 34] [Para 35] [Para 36] The parties chose a non-binding order. [Para 37] The order was issued on 22 January 2020. [Para 39]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer may refuse to allow union leaders to conduct union work in the production building during the lunch break? [Para 81] [Para 87]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the employer is obliged, under Prakas No. 442, to pay wages twice monthly on the 20th of the current month and the 5th of the next month? [Para 94] [Para 95]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must remove the step that production workers must step on while security checks them before leaving? [Para 105]
4. (Issue 5) Whether the employer may set high production-bonus output targets for workers who are not paid by piece rate? [Para 118]

## 3. Positions of the parties
On union activity, the workers said the canteen held only 200 to 300 people, some ate at home, and lunch-time organizing did not touch working hours; the employer said it did not discriminate but fingerprinting union papers in the production area stained garments and left workers no time to prepare for the 12:00 shift, relying on Article 2 of the Labour Law. [Para 82] [Para 85] [Para 86] [Para 87] [Para 88] [Para 89]
On wages, the workers relied on Prakas No. 442 and sought payment on the 20th and the 5th; the employer said it already paid twice monthly under a schedule notified a year in advance, on Saturdays, with the other union's agreement. [Para 7] [Para 8] [Para 95] [Para 97] [Para 101] A post-hearing pleading treated the fixed dates as a fallback. [Para 103]
On the step, the workers said it was slippery and dangerous after long hours; the employer said it had reduced it to 20 cm, fitted adhesive, offered a handrail and seen no accidents in two to three years. [Para 108] [Para 110] [Para 111] [Para 115]
On bonuses, the workers cited rising targets (1048 to 1272) and claimed rebukes and loss of overtime; the employer said engineers set reasonable targets from workforce, hours and machine timing, and the bonus was separate from base wages. [Para 119] [Para 120] [Para 121] [Para 154]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the remaining claims 1, 3, 4 and 5 and ordered no payment in riel or USD. [Para 160] [Para 161] [Para 162] [Para 163] The order is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notice absent a written objection to the Minister. [Para 164]
Issue 1: Under Article 66 of the 2016 Trade Union Law the employer may refuse access where it would obstruct normal operations; the panel found stained goods and production impact, permitting union work elsewhere but not in the production building. [Para 123] [Para 125] [Para 127] [Para 128]
Issue 3: Article 116 of the 1997 Labour Law and Prakas No. 442 require two payments a month, in the second and fourth weeks, which the employer had made; the workers showed no legal basis that the schedule was non-compliant. [Para 130] [Para 131] [Para 132] [Para 138] [Para 139] Arbitrator លីវ សុវណ្ណា dissented, finding the fixed dates a reasonable exercise of the wage right. [Para 168] [Para 177]
Issue 4: The alleging party bore the burden of proof and produced no specific evidence of injury; the panel found the lowered, adhesive-fitted step lawful and reasonable management. [Para 143] [Para 146] [Para 148] [Para 149] [Para 150]
Issue 5: Non-statutory incentive bonuses distinct from base wages fall within employer discretion; the claim was rejected. [Para 154] [Para 156] [Para 159]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Under Article 66 of the Trade Union Law, union access to a production area may be refused where the employer shows disruption or product-quality harm. [Para 123] Article 116 and Prakas No. 442 fix payment frequency and weeks, not worker-chosen dates, so two payments in the required weeks defeat a different-date claim. [Para 131] A party alleging an unsafe arrangement must prove the hazard before the panel displaces a reasonable management measure. [Para 143] Non-statutory incentive bonuses may be conditioned by the employer because they are separate from base wages. [Para 154] These are arbitral reasons, not binding precedent.

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The pay-date claim is stated inconsistently (20th of the current month versus 20th and 5th of the next), while actual payments fell on the 28th and the 8th/10th. [Para 95] [Para 98] [Para 99] The worker pleading cites Prakas No. 15442 where the panel applies Prakas No. 442. [Para 136] [Para 131] One line says the employer stopped the bonus targets although the order rejects that claim. [Para 158] [Para 163] OCR losses include garbled headers and the overtime meal-allowance fragment at [Para 17] and broken text at [Para 104] and [Para 159].
