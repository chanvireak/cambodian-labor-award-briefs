# Award No. 016/20 — Ken Sport Shoes Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 9 April 2020)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៉ីង សុទ្ធី appointed by the employer party, លីវ សុវណ្ណា appointed by the worker party, ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា chair) · non-binding award · Doc ID 139507

## 1. Facts
Ken Sport Shoes Co., Ltd., a shoe manufacturer at Chamkar Svay, Sethey, Samakki Mon Chey, Kampong Chhnang, employs 11,470 workers. [Para 49][Para 50] From 2013 to 2019 the employer paid the overtime meal allowance once a week, every Saturday; from 1 January 2020 it paid twice a month, on the 15th and the 25th, through a Wing account, and some workers say they lost the money or could not withdraw the remainder. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 56] On 26 July 2019 the employer dismissed Mr P Vanna, Mr K Sokvy and Mr Kh Vichey for serious misconduct; the other two were re-hired in February 2020, but Mr P Vanna — a former vice-president of the Free Cambodia Union — applied on 21 February 2020, passed the test and interview, attended the training on 24 February 2020, failed the trainer's evaluation, re-applied and failed again. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67][Para 72][Para 74]

## 2. Issues in dispute
Seven points were non-conciliated; four were settled in conciliation (points 2, 4, 5 and 7) and points 3 and 6 were merged, leaving two for decision. [Para 22][Para 23]
1. Whether the employer is obliged to pay the overtime meal allowance once a week, every Saturday, to the workers? [Para 51][Para 86]
2. Whether the employer's refusal to take back Mr P Vanna was connected to trade-union discrimination? [Para 64][Para 103]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the weekly payment is needed for daily living; the twice-monthly Wing payment loses money for some workers and leaves balances they cannot withdraw, and the employer does nothing about it; Mr P Vanna is capable, having worked at the factory nine years, and was refused only because he led a union that the administrative head, Mr S, dislikes. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57][Para 75][Para 77][Para 79]
**Employer (respondent):** paying twice a month suits both sides and saves time; it is hard to find 2,000-riel notes, and exchange-rate movement means the bank keeps no separate riel account, so a worker withdraws once the balance reaches USD 1; recruitment follows a fixed test, interview and training sequence and the refusal had nothing to do with discrimination. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 62][Para 63][Para 82][Para 85]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay the overtime meal allowance once a week, every Saturday; the demand to reinstate Mr P Vanna was rejected. [Para 154][Para 155]

On the meal allowance the panel relied on point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 of the Ministry of Labour, which entitles a worker who voluntarily works overtime at the employer's request to 2,000 riel per day or one free meal, without fixing how many overtime hours trigger it. [Para 86][Para 87] The notification is silent on when the money must be paid, so the panel reasoned from purpose: the allowance exists so the worker can buy food for the overtime shift, and a payment schedule is lawful only if it lets the worker do that; weekly payment therefore satisfies the notification, and the panel followed Awards 76/11, 146/12, 244/12, 007/18, 043/19 and 092/19 in so holding. [Para 88][Para 89][Para 91][Para 97][Para 101]

On Mr P Vanna the panel treated the demand as one about contract formation. Under Article 65 of the Labour Law and Articles 664, 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code a contract arises only from a matching offer and acceptance, and the employer's offer was conditional on passing the pre-contract training; he did not pass it, so no contract came into being and no reinstatement could be ordered. [Para 103][Para 105][Para 108][Para 125][Para 127][Para 128] On the discrimination claim, Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law and Article 62 of the Trade Union Law 2016 were cited, but the burden of proving discrimination lies on the party alleging it (Awards 015/17, 34/11, 182/16, 214/16), and the panel found no evidence beyond oral assertion: two other former union leaders were re-hired, two other candidates also failed the same training, and nothing showed Mr P Vanna was a union member or active in a union at the time. [Para 130][Para 143][Para 148][Para 150][Para 152]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The 2,000-riel overtime meal allowance under Notification No. 041/11 need not be paid daily, but any schedule the employer adopts must leave the worker able to buy food for the overtime shift, and weekly payment on a fixed day meets that requirement; a change to twice-monthly payment through a bank account that leaves workers unable to draw small sums does not. A candidate who accepts an employer's conditional offer of work but fails the pre-contract training has no contract to be reinstated to, and a refusal at that stage is not unlawful discrimination unless the claimant proves the union motive. [Para 89][Para 91][Para 128][Para 148]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that payment through Wing caused small losses and unwithdrawable balances, and that an employer should deal with such complaints through its administration office, go beyond the order to restore weekly payment. [Para 56][Para 61]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The year is systematically lost throughout: the award date prints as "9 April 20", and every date in the recitals, the evidence list and the reasoning is truncated to "២០" — 2020 is inferred from the Prakas of 23 May 2019 (seventeenth term) and the 2019–2020 facts. [Para 1][Para 13][Para 18][Para 53]
- The workforce figure is printed as 11,470, far larger than the 700-odd typical of the awards in this series; it is reproduced as printed but may be an OCR artefact. [Para 50]
- Mr P Vanna's name appears in at least five forms ("ផុន វណ្ណា", "ផុន វណ្ណៈ", "ធុន វណ្ណា", "ផុន ណ្ណោ", "ផុន វិ"); the deliverable at [Para 155] omits the name altogether. [Para 64][Para 103]
- The seventh demand asks that workers dismissed "under Article 83" be reinstated, but Article 83 concerns the internal work rules, not dismissal; the panel never addressed the mismatch and dismissed the merged demand without discussing Article 83. [Para 7][Para 22][Para 155]
- The employer-appointed arbitrator's name is missing from the signature block, which prints only "appointed by the employer party" with no name; the chair's name appears twice. [Para 157]
- The conciliation report is dated 04 March 2020 and the minute 03 March 2020, while the provincial office received the request on 12 February 2020 and the last conciliation is recorded as 3 March 2020. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 42][Para 43]
