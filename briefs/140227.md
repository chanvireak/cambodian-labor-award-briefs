# Award No. 238/12 — Horus Industrial Corp (Arbitration Council, 25 January 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy; Oun Non; Kong Phalla presiding) · Doc ID 140227

## 1. Facts
Horus Industrial Corp is a garment factory registered in 1996 employing about 200 workers [Para 103][Para 104]. Its workers' union was certified on 5 September 2012 [Para 106], and after a secret ballot of 32 members on 27 July 2012 the workers struck from 9 August 2012 [Para 164][Para 182]. They ended the strike on 19 October 2012 and tried to return, but the employer refused to readmit them and issued notices of 1 and 10 October 2012 treating them as having abandoned their jobs [Para 115][Para 125][Para 126]. Of 93 claimants, four had resigned, leaving 89 [Para 111][Para 112]. Three issues were referred; the workers withdrew issue 3, leaving issues 1 and 2 [Para 16][Para 17].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay the five statutory benefits — notice pay (Articles 75 and 77), dismissal damages (Article 89), moral damages (Article 91), payment in lieu of accrued annual leave (Articles 166 and 167) and the last month's wage — to the 89 claimants, given its contention that they abandoned their jobs? [Para 177][Para 191]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer owes wages for the strike period of 9 August to 19 October 2012 because it recruited replacement workers or sent work to other factories during the strike? [Para 209][Para 213]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The strike was lawful — a secret ballot was held and the employer and the Ministry were notified on 30 July 2012 [Para 165]. The employer's return-to-work notices were procedurally void, since only a court may suspend a strike, and its refusal to readmit the strikers amounted to dismissal [Para 123][Para 124][Para 126]. On issue 2, the employer advertised for new workers on the first day of the strike and trucked goods to another factory for sewing [Para 131][Para 145].
**Employer (respondent):** It never dismissed anyone; the workers abandoned their posts and, under Article 6 of the internal regulations, it was entitled to treat them as having resigned [Para 118][Para 187]. The strike ended on 10 October 2012 [Para 132]. The truck in the photograph carried fabric for collar binding only, because the factory has no machine of that type, and such outsourcing occurs with or without a strike [Para 147][Para 148].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay all five benefits, including notice pay, dismissal damages, moral damages, annual leave and the final month's wage, to the 89 claimants [Para 242], and the claim for strike-period wages was dismissed [Para 243].

On issue 1 the panel classified the demand as a rights dispute, because dismissal during a strike is regulated by the Labour Law [Para 179][Para 180]. It held that the legality of a strike is for the labour court alone to determine [Para 195][Para 200], so the employer could not rely on its own assessment that the strike was unlawful. The notice of 1 October 2012 ordering the workers back had no legal effect, because the workers were then exercising a guaranteed right and the employer had no power to order a strike to end [Para 201]. It followed that the internal regulations could not be applied and the workers could not be treated as having abandoned their jobs [Para 202][Para 204]. The severance of the employment relationship was therefore a dismissal [Para 205], which Article 333 renders void, entitling the workers to reinstatement [Para 206]; because they claimed compensation rather than reinstatement, the panel awarded the five statutory heads of benefit [Para 207][Para 208], limited to the 89 remaining claimants [Para 181].

On issue 2 the panel again found a rights dispute [Para 211]. Article 332(1) suspends the employment contract during a strike, so neither work nor wages are owed [Para 214][Para 215][Para 217]. Following Awards 27/10 and 178/12, wages become payable only where the employer hired replacements or diverted work elsewhere during the strike, which engages Article 334 [Para 216][Para 219]. Here the photographic evidence of recruitment banners could not be read, so replacement hiring was not proved [Para 223][Para 224], and the extra security guard was not a production replacement because no guard had joined the strike [Para 225]. The invoices the employer produced did not support its explanation, since they ran between the company and TA BINDING LIMITED rather than the company named in the workers' allegation [Para 236][Para 237]. A single photograph of a truck leaving on 10 September 2012 was nevertheless too thin a basis to find that work had been outsourced in substitution for strikers [Para 238][Para 239], so Article 334 was not engaged and the claim failed [Para 240][Para 241].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A return-to-work order issued while a lawful strike is still in progress has no legal effect: non-compliance cannot be converted into abandonment of employment, and a termination founded on it is a dismissal. Wages for strike days become payable under Article 334 only where replacement hiring or outsourcing to defeat the strike is proved by evidence, not merely alleged. [Para 201][Para 239]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that only a court may rule a strike lawful or unlawful, and that a worker becomes seriously at fault only by failing to return within 48 hours of such a ruling [Para 196][Para 200]. The employer arbitrator dissented in part, accepting liability for the other four heads but rejecting the award of moral damages under Article 91 to strikers on unfixed-duration contracts [Para 247][Para 250].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The scan is heavily corrupted: stray Latin and Khmer fragments ("Sa", "S.", "2016", "4142") interrupt sentences, and one paragraph gives the number of claimants as four of 93 where 89 is meant [Para 181][Para 212]. The strike's end date is printed inconsistently — the workers claimed until 19 October 2012, the employer said 10 October, and the panel's own finding states the strike was not ended until 10 October [Para 132][Para 183][Para 209]. The internal regulations are dated 16 December 1994 in the evidence list and 13 December 1994 in the reasons [Para 28][Para 119]. Names of the employer's representatives and of individual workers appear in full in the claimant lists; they are described by role here and not reproduced. The award was issued on 25 January 2013, after the parties agreed to postpone it from 9 January [Para 23].
