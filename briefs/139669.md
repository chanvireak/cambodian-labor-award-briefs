# Award No. 207/14 — Great Honour Tech Style Fashion Limited (Arbitration Council, 25 August 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mr Ing Sothy (chosen by the employer), Mr Tuon Si Phan (chosen by the workers), chair Mr Pen Bun Chea · Doc ID 139669

## 1. Facts
Great Honour Tech Style Fashion Limited, formerly Vin Nai Thiting, is a garment factory at Svay Rolum village, Saang district, Kandal, cutting, embroidering and printing clothes, socks, sleeves, pillows and scarves, and employed about 1,890 workers. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 56] The Cambodia Democratic Garment Workers' Union at the factory held a most-representative certificate, No. 129, dated 17 August 2012 and valid to 17 August 2014, and reported 1,157 members, of whom the employer deducted dues for 801. [Para 43][Para 58][Para 59][Para 60][Para 62] Five demands went unresolved at conciliation on 18 July 2014 and all five were refused. [Para 18][Para 19]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a midday meal allowance of 5,000 riel a day to each worker? [Para 64][Para 232]
2. Whether the employer must return Mr C, a union activist, to his former packing section with the same wage and benefits? [Para 75][Para 264]
3. Whether the employer must pay the wages and attendance bonus withheld from weaving-section workers for 1 to 7 February 2014? [Para 171][Para 354]
4. Whether the employer must pay a 6,000 riel meal allowance for work on Sundays and public holidays, that is 4,000 riel more than the 2,000 riel already paid? [Para 203][Para 408]
5. Whether the employer must comply with point 4 of Award No. 104/11 (Vin Nai Thiting) and issue lot prices 3 to 7 days ahead? [Para 214][Para 458]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Other factories pay midday meal money, and the gesture motivates workers and raises the employer's profit; wages are too low to guarantee health, causing fainting and nutritional deficiency. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 70][Para 71] Mr C's transfer was union discrimination because he is a union activist, he lost about half his monthly income, and other workers who made the same mistake were not transferred. [Para 276][Para 277][Para 133][Para 138] The 1–7 February absence was a protest, not a strike, and the employer broke its promise to give a new PN style. [Para 193][Para 194][Para 381][Para 383]
**Employer (respondent):** It has no capacity to pay more; no law, regulation or agreement requires a midday meal or a higher meal allowance, and it already pays 2,000 riel on holidays, which is better than the law. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 211][Para 212] The transfer was a disciplinary measure after repeated packing errors, applied under the internal rules and proportionate to the fault; Mr C accepted it and worked in the new section for over eighteen months. [Para 296][Para 343][Para 346][Para 351] The February absence was a strike, so no wages were owed, and no replacement workers were hired. [Para 196][Para 405]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all five demands are refused; the rights-dispute orders became final immediately under the memorandum of understanding of 3 October 2012, and the interest-dispute orders become final after 08 days if no party objects in writing. [Para 466][Para 467][Para 468][Para 469][Para 470][Para 471][Para 472]

On issue 1 the panel found no Labour Law provision, Prakas, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice obliging the employer to pay 5,000 riel a day, so the demand exceeded the law and was an interest dispute. [Para 233][Para 234][Para 235] Having found the union most representative and therefore competent to bring interest claims, it decided the claim on equity, and refused it because the union offered no evidence that other companies pay such an allowance, no evidence of the fainting and malnutrition alleged, and no evidence of the employer's capacity. [Para 244][Para 246][Para 253][Para 257][Para 259][Para 262]

On issue 2 the panel treated the transfer as a rights dispute and examined whether it was discrimination under Articles 12 and 279. [Para 269][Para 270] The union did not identify when Mr C became an activist, and the employer produced a written warning of 17 October 2011 — before any union activity — for the same packing error, so the panel found the "problems began after he became an activist" assertion unclear and insufficient. [Para 280][Para 281][Para 283][Para 284][Para 287] The transfer was instead a disciplinary measure under Article 27, which requires the penalty to be proportionate to the fault; the panel found the errors (four short-packing incidents, one inside the one-month period he had promised to be careful) justified it, and that he had accepted it by working there for over a year and a half. [Para 333][Para 342][Para 346][Para 351][Para 353]

On issue 3 the panel held the 1–7 February stoppage was a strike under Article 318: 150 weaving workers, a cessation of work, and a demand for a settlement. [Para 357][Para 359][Para 360][Para 362][Para 365] Under Article 332 paragraph 1 and Article 72 paragraph 1 the contract is suspended during a strike and neither work nor wages are owed, and no replacement workers were hired that would trigger Article 334. [Para 396][Para 397][Para 401][Para 405][Para 406]

On issue 4 the panel repeated its standing distinction: work on a public holiday is ordinary work carrying one extra day's wage under Article 164 and Prakas No. 10, not overtime, so no overtime meal allowance is owed beyond the 2,000 riel already paid; Sunday work is overtime under Article 139, and 2,000 riel satisfies point 2 of Notification No. 041/11. [Para 415][Para 420][Para 429][Para 437][Para 441][Para 442]

On issue 5 the panel held it has no power to enforce its own awards: under Article 353 paragraph 1 of the 2006 Civil Procedure Code only a court may order enforcement, so the claim was refused as outside its jurisdiction. [Para 458][Para 461][Para 462][Para 465]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim for a benefit that no law, agreement or practice provides is an interest dispute, and even a most-representative union must prove the practice it invokes and the employer's capacity, or the claim fails on equity. A transfer imposed as a disciplinary measure proportionate to repeated proven faults under Article 27 is a lawful exercise of managerial authority, not discrimination, particularly where the worker accepted the new post for an extended period. Work on a public holiday is not overtime, while the weekly rest day is; and the panel cannot enforce its own earlier awards, that being a matter for the courts. [Para 246][Para 262][Para 346][Para 420][Para 462]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that a warning letter need not be signed or thumbprinted by the worker, provided the worker is made aware of the fault, and that an employer's decision not to give other workers the same penalty does not entitle the penalised worker to escape discipline. [Para 310][Para 331]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The union's most-representative certificate is printed as valid to 17 August 2014, eight days before the award date of 25 August 2014; both dates are reproduced as printed. [Para 1][Para 59] The company's name appears in the heading as "Great Honour Tech Style Fashion Limited" and is acknowledged at the hearing to have been formerly "Vin Nai Thiting", with several Khmer variants ("Gret On Neour", "Fret On Neour") and machine-noise debris in the evidence list. [Para 1][Para 54][Para 55] The claimant worker's name is written inconsistently (Chea Veun, Chea Voen, Chea Ruen, Chea Vien) and is redacted here as Mr C. [Para 86][Para 150][Para 267] The file refers to the earlier award as both No. 104/11 and No. 140/11 for the same decision. [Para 214][Para 216] Date inconsistencies appear in the reasons (18 September 2012 written as 18 October in one passage, and 2012 rendered as 2019 or 2015 in others). [Para 314][Para 315][Para 420] Pages are heavily corrupted by OCR where the scanner captured page numbers and stamps mid-sentence ("-២៣-", "CAZ", "ZZ"). [Para 273][Para 395]
