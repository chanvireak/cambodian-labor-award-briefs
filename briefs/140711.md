# Award No. 56/06 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន បូរិក ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) / Borik Garmen (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 9 August 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (កៅ ថា ច; ហួន ជុន ឌី; គង់ ផល្លា presiding) · Doc ID 140711

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន បូរិក ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) operates the រោងចក្រ កាត់ដេរ ប៉ោហ្វីន (បូរិក) garment factory at Prey Boeng, Kandal, and the workers are កម្មករ និយោជិត represented by សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព លើក ត ម្កើង ជីវភាព កម្មករ និយោជិត ខ្មែរ [Para 1][Para 2]. Of a workforce of about 400, 176 workers had more than one year's service on six-month fixed-duration contracts, while 86 probationary workers on 40-day probation were made permanent by an agreement of 20 May 2006 following the strike of 18 May 2006 [Para 24][Para 26][Para 27][Para 28]. The company then issued those workers three-month contracts and they demanded six months [Para 25][Para 29]. Conciliation at the Kandal provincial Department of Labour on 14 July 2006 settled three of six demands; the three unresolved demands reached the Arbitration Council on 20 July 2006 and were heard on 26 July 2006 [Para 10][Para 12][Para 13].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must conclude fixed-duration labour contracts of at least six months for the workers concerned? [Para 25]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company may deduct the regular attendance bonus of USD 2 for one day's absence, USD 3 for two days, and the whole bonus for three days, where the absence is lawful or is sick leave authorised by the company? [Para 32]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay cash in lieu of annual leave? [Para 35]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), កម្មករ និយោជិត, represented by សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព លើក ត ម្កើង ជីវភាព កម្មករ និយោជិត ខ្មែរ:** a three-month term is too short, exposes them to easy dismissal and yields little benefit on dismissal, so contracts should run six months; and because most workers need money more than rest, accrued annual leave should be paid out in cash [Para 30][Para 42].
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន បូរិក ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា):** it cannot grant six-month contracts because it fears these workers, who have just struck and disregard the internal rules, will cause further problems; its bonus practice follows Notice No. 017 [Para 31][Para 33].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel declined to consider the six-month contract demand, ordered the employer to pay the regular attendance bonus pro rata to the number of days of lawful absence, and refused the demand for cash in lieu of annual leave [Para 115][Para 116][Para 117].

On Issue 1 the panel treated the demand as an interest dispute. Article 277 of the 1997 Labour Law requires a union to be registered and to satisfy further conditions for most-representative status [Para 74], and Article 96 with Prakas No. 305, Article 9 reserves the right to conclude a collective agreement for the whole factory to the union with the most members [Para 75][Para 76]. This union had about 400 members but had not applied for that status [Para 24][Para 72]. Following Prakas No. 099, Article 43 [Para 78] and its earlier awards [Para 80][Para 81], the panel held that a union lacking most-representative status cannot bring an interest dispute, and declined to consider the demand [Para 83]. It added that contracts rest on genuine consent: no party may force another to accept terms, and a contract so coerced is void under Article 7 of Decree Law No. 38 of 28 October 1988 [Para 58][Para 61][Para 65]; the workers could negotiate but could not compel six-month terms [Para 63][Para 66].

On Issue 2, Notice No. 017 of the Ministry of Social Affairs treats the regular attendance bonus as an incentive for attendance without unlawful absence and shows no intention to penalise lawful sick leave [Para 87][Para 88]. Following Case 63/04 (San Wel) [Para 86] and Case 62/04 (Eisin) [Para 98][Para 100], the panel reasoned that forfeiting the whole USD 5 bonus for lawful sick leave is unjust to the worker, while paying it for days not worked is unjust to the employer [Para 88][Para 89]. As Notice 017 is silent, it adopted an equitable solution: the bonus is paid pro rata to the days actually worked in the month [Para 90][Para 91][Para 103][Para 104].

On Issue 3, Article 167 of the Labour Law confers annual leave only after one year's service, and where the contract ends before entitlement compensation is paid under Article 166 [Para 106][Para 107]. Any collective agreement providing cash in lieu of annual leave, or waiving the right, is void [Para 108][Para 111]. The company had agreed to arrange annual leave in August 2006 for workers with over one year's service [Para 112], so the demand for cash contradicted Article 167 and was refused [Para 113][Para 114].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that has not obtained most-representative status under Article 277 cannot bring a collective interest dispute, including a demand for contract terms, before the Arbitration Council, and the panel will decline to consider it [Para 74][Para 83]. Where a worker's absence is lawful sick leave, an attendance bonus must be paid pro rata to days actually worked rather than forfeited in full [Para 90][Para 103].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that a worker dissatisfied with contract terms remains free not to renew, and that a coerced contract would in any event be void under the 1988 Decree Law, although the outcome on Issue 1 rested on representativeness [Para 65][Para 67].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Notice No. 017 is dated 17 July 2000 in the summary of the employer's position but 17 August 2000 in the reasons [Para 5][Para 33][Para 85]. Paragraph 105 prints the year of the company's August leave arrangement twice, as "ឆ្នាំ ២០១៩ ២០០៦" (2019 and 2006). Page-break artifacts interrupt sentences at paragraphs 34 and 52, and paragraph 52 is badly garbled OCR. The letterhead in paragraphs 1–2 merges the Council's Phnom Penh address with the factory's address, and the signature block at paragraph 119 carries OCR noise where the signatures were scanned.
