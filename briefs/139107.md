# Award No. 059/19 — Traks Apparel (Cambodia) Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 8 August 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Pen Bun Cham chair) · Doc ID 139107

## 1. Facts
Traks Apparel (Cambodia) Co Ltd, a garment factory on Russian Federation Boulevard, Teuk Thla commune, Sen Sok district, Phnom Penh, employed 2,853 workers. [Para 61] On 28 June 2019 the workers complained of six working conditions; five were not conciliated, and at the hearing the workers withdrew two, leaving three issues. [Para 19][Para 20] Both parties had agreed to extend the award date from 6 to 8 August 2019. [Para 29]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must restore the USD 5 incentive payment above the minimum wage and repay what it deducted from 2016 to date? [Para 65]
2. Whether the employer must raise the target bonus (រង្វាន់គោលដៅ, "ក្តាខៀន") to a more adequate level? [Para 84]
3. Whether the employer must pay a 2,000 riel meal allowance for each hour of overtime? [Para 90]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer paid USD 5 above the minimum wage for years, from 2012 to 2015, and cannot simply cut it; the number of production lots has risen and some styles are harder, while the target bonus has not moved; the meal allowance should be raised because other factories pay more than the legal minimum. [Para 72]
**Employer (respondent):** the USD 5 was an extra above the legal minimum paid while the factory was new and wages were low, not a deduction from basic wage; since 2016 the minimum wage has risen each year, the company issued annual declarations and notifications explaining the change, workers did not object, and it cannot now pay another USD 5; it already pays the 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance. [Para 77][Para 95]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three claims were refused; the award is non-binding and becomes final after 8 days without written objection. [Para 173][Para 176]

*Issue 1.* The panel first defined past practice (ការអនុវត្តកន្លងមក) as a practice actually applied in the enterprise, repeatedly and consistently, known and accepted by both parties, and which need not be written. [Para 120] It found that the USD 5 payment from 2012 to 2015 met that definition and was a benefit more favourable than the law. [Para 128] It then held, following Award No. 95/09, that a past practice may lawfully be brought to an end where the parties so agree, where a new practice replaces it, or where the original circumstances that grounded it have changed — and here the minimum wage had risen every year from 2016, the employer had explained the reduction to USD 3 in annual notifications, and the workers had not objected at the time, so the change was reasonable and accepted. [Para 134][Para 140] The claim to restore USD 5 and to recover arrears was therefore refused. [Para 141]

*Issues 2 and 3.* The target-bonus increase and the higher overtime meal allowance were both held to be interest disputes: no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rule, contract or past practice required a higher target bonus, and the employer was already paying the 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance required by Notification No. 041/11. [Para 142][Para 166] Because the claimant was not a most-representative union, the panel refused to consider them. [Para 163]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A past practice that confers a benefit above the legal minimum is not frozen forever: it may be ended lawfully where the circumstances that originally justified it have materially changed — here, successive statutory minimum-wage increases — provided the employer notifies the change openly, gives reasons, and the workers accept it in fact by not objecting; the practice then ceases to bind and arrears cannot be claimed for the period after the change. [Para 134]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's working definition of past practice — repeated, consistent, known and accepted by both parties, and capable of arising without any written instrument — is a definitional statement the outcome did not turn on, since the practice was found to exist and then held to have ended. [Para 122]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The employer's name is OCR-mangled into at least four variants in the same document ("ត្រាកស៍ អឹភែរឹល", "ត្រាក់ស៍ អ៊ីកែរឹល", "ត្រាភស៍ អឹកែរឹល", "ត្រាក់ស៍ អឺ ភែរឹល"). [Para 2]
- The key sum is printed inconsistently: the claim reads "៣$10" for what the reasons treat as USD 3, and the employer is described as having changed "៥០០ ដុល្លារ អាមេរិក" — USD 500 — where USD 5 is plainly meant. [Para 130]
- Notification No. 041/11 is dated "ថ្ងៃទី ០៧ ខែមីនា ឆ្នាំ ២០១១៧", a year that does not exist. [Para 165]
- The complaint is described as covering six points and elsewhere as eleven conditions, and the five claims listed at the head of the award are renumbered as three issues without explanation. [Para 45]
- The signature block names the employer-party arbitrator and the chair but leaves the worker-party arbitrator's name blank. [Para 177]
- The award is dated 8 August 2019 only because the parties agreed to extend it from 6 August, and that earlier date survives in the reasons. [Para 29]
