# Award No. 18/06 — GHG (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 31 March 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Ing Sothy (employer-chosen), An Nan (worker-chosen), Ang Eng Thong (chair) · Doc ID 140689

## 1. Facts
GHG (Cambodia) Ltd employs about 500 workers at Prey Tear village, Sangkat Steung Meanchey, Khan Meanchey, Phnom Penh [Para 24][Para 25]. The workers filed eight demands; two were conciliated, and the parties settled Issues 1, 4, 5 and 6 during the arbitration, leaving Issues 2 and 3 [Para 17][Para 19]. Issue 2 arises from the company's rule, adopted after it moved premises on 8 November 2005, that workers take off their shoes, carry them inside and put them back on at their own worksite [Para 28][Para 29]. Issue 3 concerns maternity pay: the company pays women on maternity leave 50 percent of the minimum wage of US$45 regardless of actual wage, plus seniority bonus, excluding attendance bonus, overtime and Sunday pay [Para 30][Para 37].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the company must allow workers to wear shoes into the workplace, rather than carry them to their worksite? [Para 9][Para 31]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay women on maternity leave 50 percent of their wage and perquisites, including overtime pay, rather than 50 percent of the minimum wage? [Para 30][Para 37]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), Cambodian Industrial Union Federation (CIUF) and its local union at GHG (Cambodia) Ltd:** the shoe rule endangers them — foot injuries from needles, slipping and electric shocks — and maternity pay should reflect each woman's actual wage and perquisites, not a flat 50 percent of the minimum wage, applying from now on [Para 34][Para 30].
**Employer (respondent), GHG (Cambodia) Ltd:** walking with shoes makes the floor and the cloth laid on it filthy, so workers must carry their shoes to their own worksite; and its maternity practice pays 50 percent of the US$45 minimum wage plus seniority bonus [Para 30][Para 33].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand to wear shoes into the workplace was rejected, and the employer was ordered to pay women on maternity leave an amount equal to 50 percent of their average monthly wage for the three months of leave [Para 43][Para 44].

On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 2(2) of the Labour Law, on enterprises and establishments operating under the supervision and direction of the employer, and its own consistent reading that the employer has the right and authority to manage and lead the workforce within the law [Para 31]. It followed Awards 28/04 — Raffles Grand Hotel d'Angkor, 49/04 — Ho Hing, 03/05 — Flying Dragon and 15/06 — Xing Tai [Para 32]. Managing the workplace being the company's right, the panel held the policy within that right and reasonable, since shoes would dirty the floor and the cloth [Para 32][Para 33]. The workers had alleged needle injuries, slipping and electric shocks but produced no evidence of any accident or its identity, date or severity, so the policy stood [Para 34][Para 35][Para 37]. The panel added that workers who meet an actual or potential safety problem must report it to the company for a solution [Para 36].

On Issue 3 the panel applied Article 183, under which a woman on maternity leave is entitled to half her wage including perquisites paid by the employer, and Article 103, which lists what wages include — actual wage, overtime, commissions, bonuses and indemnities, profit sharing, gratuities and benefits in kind [Para 37][Para 38]. It noted that the two articles refer to each other, making the text circular, and turned to purpose: the law protects a woman's job, gives her light work after leave and an hour a day for breastfeeding, and Article 46 of the Constitution guarantees that she keeps her job and takes maternity leave on full pay without losing seniority or social benefits [Para 39][Para 40]. On that basis the panel held that "half of their wage, including perquisites" means 50 percent of her average monthly wage, calculated by adding the twelve months' wages before the leave and dividing by twelve, and that a three-month leave therefore yields one half of the average monthly wage times three [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Under Article 183 read with Article 103, "half of their wage, including perquisites" means 50 percent of the worker's own average monthly wage — the twelve months preceding the leave summed and divided by twelve — paid for each of the three months of maternity leave, and not 50 percent of the minimum wage [Para 41][Para 43]. A workplace rule on footwear or dress falls within the employer's managerial right under Article 2(2) and will be upheld unless the workers show actual accidents or a concrete safety threat arising from it [Para 32][Para 35].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's direction that workers must report any actual or potential safety problem caused by such a policy so that the company can remedy it [Para 36]; and its observation that Articles 103 and 183 are circular in their cross-reference [Para 39].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Two workers are named in full in the evidence list and the facts — the woman injured on 29 December 2005 and a second worker whose hospital receipts are exhibited — and their names are not reproduced here; the injured worker's name is also spelled two different ways [Para 12][Para 21]. Several lines are duplicated by the OCR, including the passages on Article 2(2), on Article 183 and on Article 46 of the Constitution [Para 31][Para 37][Para 40]. The union's acronym is OCR'd inconsistently as "CIUF" and "ClUF" [Para 3]. The non-conciliation report is numbered both "331 KKBVIAK/VK" and "331 KKBV/AK/VK" [Para 15][Para 23]. The factory village and sangkat appear as "Prek Tea" / "Prey Tear" and "Steung Meanchey" / "Steng Meanchey" [Para 1][Para 24]. "Fax: N/A" is OCR'd as "NIA" [Para 1]. The procedural paragraph says two of an initial eight issues were conciliated while four more were settled at arbitration, but the arithmetic of the remaining list is not stated [Para 17][Para 19].
