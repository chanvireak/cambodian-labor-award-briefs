# Award No. 010/18 — FUMEY (Cambodia) Import Export Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 10 May 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phallak presiding; Seng Vuchhun, employer nominee; An Nonn, worker nominee, dissenting on issue 1) · Doc ID 138884

## 1. Facts
FUMEY (Cambodia) Import Export Co., Ltd makes yoghurt in Kandal province and employed about 106 workers. [Para 72] On 7 February 2018 the leaders and members of the Democratic Union of Workers at Fumey, registered on 12 December 2017, complained to the Kandal labour department over six points; conciliation on 26 March 2018 failed and report No. 514/18 was referred to the Council on 2 April 2018. [Para 22] The panel heard the case on 10 April 2018; issue 5 was settled by agreement and issue 6 withdrawn, leaving issues 1 to 4. [Para 21] The employer objected at the hearing that a union with under 30% of the workforce could not bring a collective dispute. [Para 75]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the union secretary and a member who stopped attending from 16 January 2018, and a third worker dismissed on 13 October 2016, with wages from the date of the disputed act? [Para 196]
2. Whether the employer must restore the worker demoted from head of the plastic-ironing team to deputy, with the original role pay? [Para 289]
3. Whether the employer must pay 130% of the day wage for night work? [Para 322]
4. Whether the employer must pay 100% wages for days on which it had no work for the workers but did not suspend the contracts according to law? [Para 334]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the two workers were dismissed, not absent of their own will — the administration told them "if you do it you are dismissed, if you don't you are dismissed" and had security escort them out without written warning, one of them a union leader who could not be dismissed without the labour inspector's agreement; the team head's demotion followed his union activity; night hours must carry 130%. [Para 86]
**Employer (respondent):** the two workers walked off the job for more than three consecutive days from 16 January 2018, which clause 3 of Article 6 of the internal rules treats as abandonment, so they cannot be taken back; the team head was demoted in 2014 for negligence causing a loss, and both shifts are already paid at the same rate; the company pays 50% and gives rest days when there is no work, and accepts it never sought the labour inspector's authorisation. [Para 87]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement refused for all three workers, but the employer was ordered to pay the dismissed worker severance, moral damages, pay in lieu of notice, pay in lieu of untaken annual leave and final wages; the employer must restore the demoted worker to head of the plastic-ironing team with the role pay, pay 130% for night hours actually worked, and pay 100% wages on days it gave no work without lawful suspension. [Para 354]
The panel first rejected the employer's standing objection: Article 54(1) of the Law on Trade Unions reserves the exclusive right to a collective dispute to the most representative union, but neither that article nor Prakas 362 imposes a 30% membership threshold on a complainant, and three workers recognised by the provincial labour department appeared; the claim was properly before the panel. [Para 182]
On issue 1(a), the panel noted that the Labour Law has no provision on abandonment and looked to the internal rules, which treat three consecutive days of absence as abandonment; both sides accepted the two workers were absent from 16 January 2018, they did not deny the employer's record, and they made no attempt to return until seven days later. [Para 197] Their account rested on their own statements and letters, contradicted by the administration and unsupported by any witness. [Para 207] Abandonment was found, and reinstatement with back pay refused. [Para 222]
On issue 1(b), the same burden applied to the employer, which offered only an undated "reminder letter" signed by unnamed administration with no thumbprint of the worker and no other document; the panel found the dismissal of 13 October 2016 had no valid reason under Article 74, but declined to order reinstatement because the worker waited roughly one year and four months before complaining and now works elsewhere. [Para 242] It substituted the statutory consequences: severance under Article 89, moral damages under Article 91, pay in lieu of notice under Articles 75 and 77, pay in lieu of annual leave under Article 166, and final wages under Article 116. [Para 257]
On issue 2, the employer bore the burden of proving a 2014 demotion and produced nothing, so the panel accepted that the demotion from team head to deputy occurred in 2016; no reason was offered for the second demotion to ordinary worker in March 2018, and Article 27 requires discipline proportionate to the fault. [Para 299] Both demotions being wrongful, the original post and role pay (USD 36 for a team head against USD 20 for a deputy) were restored. [Para 141]
On issue 3, Article 144-new of the 2007 amendment defines night work as at least eleven consecutive hours including 22:00 to 05:00 and sets the rate at 130% of the day wage; the company ran two shifts ending at 22:30 and paid both alike, which the panel held short of the law, ordering 130% for the night hours actually worked. [Para 327]
On issue 4, Articles 71 and 72 allow suspension of the enterprise's activity or of the contract only under the labour inspector's supervision, and the employer admitted it never sought authorisation while paying 50%; the suspension being unlawful, the wage obligation was never suspended and 100% wages were owed. [Para 342]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer stops providing work without the labour inspector's prior authorisation under Articles 71 and 72 of the Labour Law, the suspension is unlawful and the full wage remains due; and because reinstatement is a discretionary remedy, a worker who waits more than a year before complaining and has since found other work receives the statutory compensation under Articles 89, 91 and 75–77 instead of the job. [Para 249]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that a worker should not let a claim lie dormant after the dispute arises, since delay can leave a party unable to prepare or to obtain a good result; and it noted that Article 43 of the Law on Trade Unions protects against dismissal only, so it does not reach a worker who has abandoned the job. [Para 225]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award carries a dissenting opinion of the worker nominee, arbitrator An Nonn, who on issue 1 found a dismissal rather than abandonment and would have ordered reinstatement with back pay; the dissent prints his name once as "An Hen" and once as "An Nonn". [Para 1] The panel's constitution is dated only "4 September" with no year, in a case heard in April 2018. [Para 25] The loss said to justify the 2014 demotion is printed as approximately USD 5,000 in the employer's account and as USD 45,000 in the findings. [Para 143] The operative paragraph on issue 4 prints "10 percent" while the reasoning and the order both say 100 percent. [Para 352] The sums ordered under the compensation heads are lost to the OCR, the order listing the heads with blanks where the amounts belong. [Para 288] The employer is written variously as "FUMEY", "Fumey", "Houmi" and "Hu Mi" in Khmer, and "FUMEY CAMBODIA CO., LTD" appears in the exhibit list. [Para 24] Individual workers, union officers and the employer's representatives are referred to here by initial; their names survive in the source and are redacted by policy.
