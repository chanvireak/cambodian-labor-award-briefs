# Award No. 235/12 — Manhattan Textile and Garment Corp (Arbitration Council, 16 January 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mr Chhiv Phyrum for the employer party, Mr An Nan for the worker party, Mr Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 140222

## 1. Facts
Manhattan Textile and Garment Corp runs a garment factory at Ampil Leu, Kampong Cham, employing 3,400 workers, about 80 per cent of them on six-month fixed-duration contracts. [Para 1][Para 23][Para 26] The Local Union of ATU, registered on 18 September 2012 and representing some 2,000 workers without most representative status (MRS), brought four demands after conciliation resolved only one of five issues. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 11] The disputed act is a contractual practice: at each contract expiry the employer asks workers to take two days' unpaid leave before signing a new six-month contract, and treats that break as ending seniority, so fixed-duration workers are said never to reach one year's service and are refused the seniority bonus, maternity payment and maternity leave. [Para 26][Para 28][Para 30][Para 63] Workers submitted a list of 498 claimants on 20 December 2012; the employer answered with 243 contracts, of which 15 were permanent. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 37]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether workers on fixed-duration contracts with more than one year of service are entitled to the seniority bonus under Notification No. 041/11? [Para 57][Para 62]
2. (Issue 2) Whether female workers on fixed-duration contracts with more than one year of service are entitled to 50 per cent of 90 days' wages and perquisites as maternity payment, and to be rehired after maternity leave? [Para 83][Para 91]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay a monthly USD 3 incentive bonus to workers choosing to work overtime on public holidays? [Para 96]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must pay a 4,000 riel meal allowance per day? [Para 105]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the two days' leave does not break service, so they still have more than a year's seniority and are entitled to the bonus and to maternity payment; wages are too low to buy food and the employer had already paid the USD 3 bonus on 29 and 31 October 2012. [Para 31][Para 32][Para 48][Para 50]
**Employer (respondent):** fixed-duration workers never reach one year because each contract is broken by two days' leave, so no bonus or maternity entitlement arises; the leave follows irregular purchase orders, the USD 3 payment comes from buyers for their own products, and the meal allowance is unaffordable. [Para 30][Para 33][Para 48][Para 49]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to cease the practice and pay seniority bonuses to the fixed-duration workers with over one year of service named in the workers' list, except the 15 on undetermined-duration contracts, and to pay 50 per cent of 90 days' wages and perquisites to the 17 female workers on fixed-duration contracts with over a year's seniority; it rejected rehiring after maternity leave but ordered the employer to stop barring maternity leave; issues 3 and 4 were declined. [Para 83][Para 90][Para 96][Para 108][Para 109][Para 110]

The panel first sorted the demands into rights and interests disputes under Article 312 of the Labour Law and Clause 43 of Prakas No. 099, following Awards 119/09-SL, 86/10-New Mingda, 02/11 Pou Yuen, 05/11-M&V, 13/11 Gold Kamvimex, 14/11 GHG, 31/1 Quint Major and 62/11-Ocean. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 56][Para 57]

On Issue 1 the point 3 bonus in Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 made it a rights dispute, and the panel adopted the definition in Award 68/05-Gold Lida, followed in 75/05-Fortune Garment, 17/10 Zongtex, 60/10 Kin Tay and 101/10-Tripos: seniority ends only when the worker stops working. [Para 59][Para 62][Para 65] Because the two days' leave precedes a renewal the employer intends, it rests on the parties' agreement and does not stop work; and since the practice is regular, general and specific it cannot be explained by irregular purchase orders. [Para 68][Para 71][Para 75] Using short-term contracts to escape statutory duties offends Article 13(1) and the good-faith principle in Award 64/11-M&V, so the employer's practice was a misuse of law. [Para 76][Para 78][Para 81] Having found 283 fixed-duration workers claimed, the panel ordered payment per the workers' list. [Para 83]

On Issue 2, Article 183 gives half wages and perquisites for the 90 days to women with one year of uninterrupted service; seniority being unbroken, the 17 women qualified. [Para 86][Para 87][Para 89][Para 90] Rehiring was refused because the workers had already ended their contracts, but Article 182 gives 90 days' maternity leave irrespective of seniority, so the employer was ordered to stop denying it. [Para 92][Para 94][Para 95][Para 96]

Issues 3 and 4 were interests disputes: no law, collective agreement or Internal Work Rules provided for them. [Para 98][Para 99][Para 107] Since an interests award becomes a collective agreement binding all workers under Clause 43 of Prakas No. 099, the panel held it cannot decide such a claim unless the union holds MRS or represents over half the workforce, following Awards 81/04-Ever Green, 98/04-Great Union, 169/11-Fortune Teo, 02/11 Pou Yuen and 66/11-In Han Sung; the union had no MRS, so both were declined. [Para 100][Para 102][Para 103][Para 104][Para 108]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A break of two days' unpaid leave inserted at each contract renewal, on a practice that is regular, general and specific, does not interrupt seniority, because seniority ends only when the worker actually stops working for the enterprise; a fixed-duration worker who has renewed six-month contracts more than twice therefore has over one year of service and is entitled to the Notification No. 041/11 seniority bonus and to maternity payment under Article 183, and the device of short contracts used to avoid those obligations is a misuse of law contrary to Article 13(1). [Para 65][Para 68][Para 78][Para 87]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that non-members cannot later strike over an interests dispute already settled by award, and that interests awards bind all workers at the enterprise for one year, are general remarks on Clause 43 and were not needed to decline issues 3 and 4. [Para 101][Para 102]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The non-conciliation report is dated 26 November 2012 but is said to have been submitted to the Secretariat on 4 November 2012, before its own date; the referral is elsewhere given as 4 December 2012. All three dates are reported as printed. [Para 9][Para 12]
- The reasoning on Issue 4 reproduces the wording of Issue 3 (the USD 3 public-holiday bonus) instead of the meal allowance, so the interests-dispute analysis there does not match its own heading. [Para 107]
- The claimant group is 498 on the workers' list, 483 after the panel's count, and 283 fixed-duration workers in the operative finding; the gap is unexplained. [Para 35][Para 40][Para 83]
- Names of employer representatives and union officers are given in full in the source but are recorded here by role; the English translation also mangles the employer's corporate suffix and the words "the employer" throughout, and the word "trainee" appears as "Traing". [Para 1][Para 3][Para 7]
- The evidence section is expressly omitted from this English translation, so the documents weighed cannot be checked. [Para 20]
