# Award No. 282/13 — Shino Garment (Arbitration Council, 29 January 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (ហែម ហួរ ណារិទ្ធ for the employer, អន ណន for the workers, រុន សារ៉ាយ chair) · Doc ID 139929

## 1. Facts
The respondent is a garment factory employing about 700 workers. [Para 41][Para 42] A union at the factory, the Cambodia-ASEAN Union of Sino Garment, had lodged a registration application and held the Ministry's receipt dated 17 December 2013, but had not yet been registered when the award issued. [Para 43][Para 83] Nine demands went to conciliation, four were settled there and one more at the hearing, leaving four: a production bonus of 2,000 riel a day, meal money for holiday and Sunday work, an increase in the transport allowance to USD 15 a month, and a minimum wage of USD 120 a month. [Para 19][Para 24][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a bonus of 2,000 riel a day as promised to the workers? [Para 48]
2. Whether the employer must pay 2,000 riel in meal money to workers who volunteer for holiday or Sunday work? [Para 103]
3. Whether the employer must raise the transport and accommodation allowance to USD 15 a month, i.e. by USD 8? [Para 148]
4. Whether the employer must raise the basic wage to USD 120 a month, i.e. by USD 40? [Para 159]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the 2,000 riel bonus was promised orally by the employer and was paid to groups 1 to 4 whenever they met the target, but stopped after the group leaders changed; for holiday and Sunday work there is no food seller outside the factory, so meals cost more; market prices have risen and other factories pay USD 15 in transport and a USD 120 basic wage. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 62][Para 70][Para 75]
**Employer (respondent):** the 2,000 riel was paid only to groups 1 to 4 because their styles were especially difficult, and nothing was promised to group 9; holiday work carries a full day's extra wage under Article 164 and is not overtime, so no meal money is owed; and it cannot afford either the transport increase or a USD 120 basic wage. [Para 53][Para 55][Para 66][Para 72][Para 77]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — meal money of 2,000 riel was ordered for workers who volunteer for Sunday work; the claims for the 2,000 riel bonus, meal money for holiday work, transport of USD 15 and a USD 120 base wage were refused. The award is not binding and becomes final 8 days after notification absent written objection to the Minister. [Para 147][Para 169][Para 170][Para 171][Para 172][Para 173][Para 174]

The panel first settled who was before it. Article 268 of the Labour Law gives legal rights only to a registered professional organisation; the Cambodia-ASEAN Union had a receipt for its application but no registration, so it lacked legal personality and could not represent other workers at the Council. [Para 78][Para 83][Para 84] Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 nonetheless lets a party appear in person or through a representative holding written authority, and four workers — Ms K, Ms P, Ms H and Mr A — attended with mandates, so the panel could decide for them; three further workers had mandated the federation only on issues 1 and 2, and one more only on issues 3 to 5. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 87][Para 93]

On the bonus, the workers asserted an oral promise but the panel found the burden on them and no evidence of any commitment; the group 9 list showed 28 workers, not the 30 claimed, and the bonus appeared nowhere in the payroll. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 96][Para 100][Para 101] The claim was dismissed. [Para 102]

Issue 2 split in two. Work on a public holiday is not overtime: Article 164 and Prakas No. 10 of 4 February 1999 give a worker who works a holiday an extra day's wage on top of the monthly wage, and the panel had held in earlier awards that holiday work is not overtime. [Para 108][Para 110][Para 113][Para 115] No law, agreement or practice required meal money for holiday work, so that part was an interest dispute and, the claimants not being most representative, the panel declined it. [Para 119][Para 120][Para 131][Para 133] Sunday work is different: the normal week runs Monday to Saturday, 8 hours a day and 48 hours a week, so Sunday work exceeds normal hours and is overtime. [Para 135][Para 137][Para 141][Para 142] Point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 therefore required 2,000 riel in meal money for each day of voluntary overtime, without limiting the number of hours, and the employer had paid nothing; the panel ordered it. [Para 143][Para 146][Para 147]

On transport, point 1 of Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 fixed USD 7 a month and the employer paid exactly that; no law, agreement, internal rule or practice required USD 15, so the claim exceeded the law and, as an interest dispute brought by non-representative claimants, was declined. [Para 148][Para 150][Para 151][Para 152][Para 158] On wages, Notification No. 103 of 9 April 2013 set USD 80 a month for workers past probation, and the employer paid USD 80; the demand for USD 120 was likewise an interest claim that the panel declined for want of representativeness. [Para 160][Para 162][Para 163][Para 168]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Work on a public holiday is not overtime: it earns the extra day's wage provided by Article 164 and Prakas No. 10, so meal money conditioned on overtime is not owed for it. Work on Sunday, by contrast, falls outside the Monday-to-Saturday 48-hour week and is overtime, so the 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance under Notification No. 041/11 is payable for each Sunday worked. [Para 113][Para 137][Para 143] A union that holds only a receipt for a registration application, and not registration itself, has no legal personality and cannot represent workers before the panel; individual workers may still proceed on written mandates under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 84][Para 86][Para 88]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the group 9 list contained 28 rather than 30 workers, and that the bonus never appeared on the payroll, were findings on evidence that were not necessary once the oral promise had been found unproven. [Para 58][Para 59]
