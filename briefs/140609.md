# Award No. 47/07 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ជុង ហ្វៃ និត វៀរ អេហ្វ ធី វាយ v សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ រោងចក្រ ជុង ហ្វៃ (Arbitration Council, 27 June 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phalla, president; Kau Thach; An Norn) · Doc ID 140609

## 1. Facts
The employer, ក្រុមហ៊ុន ជុង ហ្វៃ និត វៀរ អេហ្វ ធី វាយ, operates a knitwear factory on National Road 2, Chake Angre Krom, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, with 800 workers [Para 1][Para 30]. The claimant is សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ រោងចក្រ ជុង ហ្វៃ, with about 100 members among the 300 workers who signed the complaint [Para 3][Para 31][Para 32]. Of fourteen demands, seven were settled in conciliation and three more — issues 1, 4 and 7 — were settled at the hearing itself; the panel decided issues 2, 3, 5 and 6 [Para 23][Para 27][Para 28]. A rival union, សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ នៃ ព្រះរាជាណាចក្រ កម្ពុជា, holds most-representative status in the factory [Para 33][Para 105].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the company must refund the medical check-up fee to every worker who paid it from 1998, the factory's first year of operation? [Para 35][Para 64]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the company must preserve the attendance bonus and the wage of a worker on certified sick leave, accepting any valid certificate and not only an occupational-medicine certificate? [Para 42][Para 102]
3. (Issue 5) Whether the meal allowance for overtime must be paid weekly rather than monthly? [Para 47][Para 129]
4. (Issue 6) Whether the company must refund the 500 riel it charged each worker for stamping a new identity card? [Para 53][Para 153]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Article 247 of the Labour Law and Prakas 09 of 1994 put the cost of the pre-employment medical check on the employer, so the 10,100 riel each worker paid must come back [Para 35][Para 64]. They asked that a certificate from any lawfully recognised doctor suffice, since the occupational-medicine clinic is far from their homes [Para 45]. They wanted the overtime meal allowance paid weekly because they lack cash between paydays [Para 48], and the card-stamping charge refunded, since the stamping served the insurer [Para 53][Para 154].
**Employer (respondent):** The pre-employment check is a condition the company sets before hiring, so it is not the company's cost [Para 36][Para 63]. It had agreed with the most-representative union in 2006 to accept occupational-medicine certificates only, and cannot change that [Para 43][Para 44]. It pays the meal allowance with the monthly wage, as it has since 1998 [Para 49][Para 12], and treats the 500 riel as a card-production cost [Para 14].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the medical-fee refund was refused; the company was ordered to accept state hospital certificates for sick leave, to pay the overtime meal allowance weekly, and to repay 500 riel to each of the 800 workers [Para 166][Para 167][Para 168][Para 169].
On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 247-A and 247-C, which place medical examination at the employer's expense [Para 65], and Prakas 09 of 19 January 1994 on fitness examinations [Para 66]; it held Article 247 of the 1997 Labour Law prevails over the Prakas [Para 69], so the employer owes the fee [Para 70][Para 72]. Under Prakas 305, however, only a union with an absolute majority may claim for the whole workforce, and otherwise a union represents its own members alone [Para 81][Para 83]; this union was not most representative [Para 84][Para 85], so the claim reached members only [Para 86]. Article 120 limits wage claims to three years [Para 88][Para 91], running from the date the employment contract began [Para 95], which left only members who started on or after 5 June 2004 [Para 97]. The union promised a list of those members and never produced it [Para 38][Para 99], so no worker was shown to be entitled [Para 100] and the demand was refused [Para 101].
On Issue 3 the panel found the collective agreement signed with the most-representative union covers all workers in the company [Para 107]. Article 71 suspends the contract during illness certified by a qualified doctor [Para 108], and the internal rules themselves accept state certificates [Para 124]. The company's practice of accepting only occupational-medicine certificates, and its 2006 agreement to that effect, were held incorrect because state medicine is not limited to occupational medicine [Para 126]; the term covers provincial, municipal, district and khan hospitals and health centres [Para 127][Para 128].
On Issue 5 the panel read Article 116 as requiring wages to be paid at least twice a month [Para 130][Para 132]. The meal allowance is not part of the overtime wage but a substitute for a meal the employer must provide [Para 140][Para 141]; Notification 017 fixes 1,000 riel a day or one free meal [Para 142][Para 145]. Since the allowance accrues on each day of overtime, weekly payment is reasonable and saves both sides time [Para 147][Para 151], and the company was ordered to pay weekly [Para 152].
On Issue 6 the panel applied Article 249, which makes the employer responsible for occupational accidents regardless of the worker's personal circumstances [Para 153]. The company bought insurance from Asia Insurance for 2006 to 2007 and, because the insurer required stamped cards, collected the old cards and charged each worker 500 riel for a new one [Para 154][Para 159][Para 160]. That charge was improper because insurance against occupational accidents is the employer's own legal duty [Para 161][Para 164], and the money was ordered repaid to the 800 workers [Para 165][Para 169].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A charge an employer imposes on workers to satisfy a duty the law places on the employer — here, insuring against occupational accidents and stamping the cards the insurer requires — is unlawful and must be refunded, whether or not the workers paid it without protest [Para 161][Para 164][Para 165]. Where the internal rules and the Labour Law accept a certificate from any qualified doctor, an employer's practice of accepting only occupational-medicine certificates, even when agreed with the most-representative union, is incorrect [Para 108][Para 124][Para 126]. A union without an absolute majority may claim only for its own members, and a claim that depends on identifying those members fails if the promised list is never produced [Para 83][Para 86][Para 99].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the company pays 4 dollars per worker for the insurance and that an injured worker would receive 1,000 dollars, facts it did not use to change the order [Para 61]; it also recorded the employer's position that the company itself bears the cost of the card materials [Para 14].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The medical-fee claim is printed as 100 riel per worker in the demand and the reasons but as 10,100 riel in the analysis and the order, with a corrupt "410.100 riel" appearing in the limitation discussion [Para 35][Para 64][Para 92]. The Prakas authorising majority representation is dated 22 November 2001 in one place and 22 November 2019 in another [Para 81][Para 106]. The procedural history repeats whole clauses, and the claim is described as covering fourteen demands although only seven went to conciliation [Para 22][Para 23]. The president's name appears as គង់ ជល្លៈ on the face and គង់ ផល្លៈ on the signature page, and the employer-side arbitrator's name is a fragment. OCR losses: several passages of reasoning are duplicated or truncated mid-sentence, one paragraph reduces to "Sta" before a quotation [Para 118], and the words on which the sick-leave ruling turns are partly unreadable [Para 121][Para 147].
