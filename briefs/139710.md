# Award No. 250/14 — Teng Sun Limithit Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 September 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Houn Chundy for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139710

## 1. Facts
Teng Sun Limithit Co., Ltd sews bags at Samrong Tong, Kampong Speu, and had been operating since January 2014 with about 760 workers. [Para 21][Para 46][Para 47] The Free Workers' Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia filed 7 demands on 1 September 2014; conciliation the same day failed on all 7, and 3 were settled at the hearing while the workers withdrew points 1, 2 and 3, leaving only point 5, the lunch allowance. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 18][Para 19][Para 20] Seven workers attended the hearing and are the claimants; 129 workers had given a written power of attorney to the federation president, who did not attend, and the local union was not registered with the Ministry. [Para 23][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 52][Para 53]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of Riels 2,000 per day, being an increase of Riels 1,000 over the Riels 1,000 already paid? [Para 5][Para 54][Para 55][Para 58]

(The panel's remaining points 1, 2 and 3 — reinstatement of two named workers, a motorcycle shelter, and a dining area — were withdrawn; points 4, 6 and 7 on a permanent nurse, hot and cold water, and additional toilets were settled at the hearing.) [Para 2][Para 3][Para 4][Para 6][Para 7][Para 18][Para 19]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Riels 1,000 a day cannot buy adequate food, and other employers — Vining, Vin Son and Simano — pay Riels 2,000 per day for lunch. [Para 56]
**Employer (respondent):** the company has only just begun operating and cannot afford the demand; it already pays Riels 1,000 per day, which is more favourable than the Labour Law requires. [Para 57][Para 59]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider the lunch allowance claim of Riels 2,000 per day, making no monetary order. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 80]

The panel found that the Riels 1,000 paid daily since the factory opened exceeds what the Labour Law requires, and that no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, employment contract, agreement between the parties or past practice obliged the employer to pay Riels 2,000. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60] The claim was therefore for a benefit above the statutory floor, that is an interests dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍). [Para 61][Para 62]

On interests disputes the panel applied Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001 on union representativeness, and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY, which makes an interests award a substitute collective agreement binding all workers for one year and extinguishes their right to strike on that interest. [Para 63][Para 66][Para 69][Para 70] It held it may decide such a dispute only where the union bringing it is the most representative in the enterprise, or where several unions together represent more than half the workers, following awards Nos 81/04, 98/04, 169/11, 228/13 and 172/14. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 73][Para 74][Para 75] Two facts defeated the claim: the local union had not been registered, so it had not yet become a lawful professional organisation, and it was not the most representative union in the company — and because only an unregistered local union and its federation were before it, the federation official who attended could not establish that the parties to the case were signatories to the Memorandum of Understanding of 3 October 2012 relied on. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 26][Para 76][Para 77]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim for a benefit above what the Labour Law and the applicable instruments require is an interests dispute, and the panel will decide it only where the union bringing it is the most representative in the enterprise or represents more than half the workers; a local union that has not received its registration certificate is not yet a lawful professional organisation and cannot supply that standing, so the claim is dismissed without consideration of its merits. [Para 24][Para 61][Para 71][Para 77][Para 78]

**Obiter dicta:** the observations on the Memorandum of Understanding of 3 October 2012 — that a federation may sign it while an unregistered local may not — are not necessary to the dismissal, which rests on standing alone. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 25][Para 26]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The dispositive finding at [Para 76] is missing its negation: the printed text reads that the complainant "is the most representative union", which contradicts [Para 77], [Para 24] and the order at [Para 78]; the sense has been taken from those paragraphs.
- The list of union demands is corrupt at [Para 2]: point 1 asks the company to "accept the names" of two workers, which the conciliation record elsewhere treats as a demand to reinstate them, and no reasoning on that point survives because it was withdrawn [Para 19].
- The workers' power of attorney is described as covering "72 points" at [Para 40] against 7 demands at [Para 13][Para 14]; 7 is used.
- The award is dated 30 September 2014 [Para 1], but the non-conciliation report is printed as submitted "03 September 2015 2017" [Para 10] and the panel's constitution as "05 September 2 2014" [Para 15].
- The union president is named only in the body of the award [Para 49] and the claimant association is spelled both "Teng Sun Limithit" and "Teng Sun Limit Hit"; individual workers and officials are rendered as (name redacted) here.
- The requirement for a permanent nurse is supported by a nursing diploma in the employer's evidence [Para 33], but the point was settled at the hearing [Para 18] and no reasoning on it appears.
