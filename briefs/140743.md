# Award No. 103/06 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន អ៉ឹម និង វ៉េ កាត់ដេរ អន្តរជាតិ សាខា ទី៣ (Im & V3 Garment International, Branch 3) (Arbitration Council, 21 November 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mâr Sambor Na, An Nonn, Kong Phalla) · Doc ID 140743

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន អ៉ឹម និង វ៉េ កាត់ដេរ អន្តរជាតិ សាខា ទី៣ (Im & V3 Garment International, Branch 3), a garment factory at house 1623, National Road 2, Sangkat Chak Angre Krom, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employed 2,974 workers [Para 1][Para 55]. The union, សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា (C.CAWDU) and its factory branch សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ នៃ រោងចក្រ អ៉ឹម និង វ៉េ, filed a complaint on 19 September 2006 raising four demands for improved working conditions [Para 1][Para 14]. Conciliation on 13 October 2006 settled one of the four points, and the three unresolved points were referred to the Council on 24 October 2006 [Para 15][Para 16]. At the hearing of 3 November 2006 points 1 and 2 were settled, so only point 3 was decided [Para 14][Para 19][Para 20]. Point 1 concerned 100% wages during short-time work, and point 2 the conversion of casual workers with two months' service into full-rights workers; both were settled and are not decided here [Para 2][Para 4][Para 19].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 3) Whether the Council has jurisdiction over the demand that the company take back the three dismissed group leaders, when the employer argues that this is an individual labour dispute? [Para 22][Para 46][Para 47]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា (C.CAWDU) and សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ នៃ រោងចក្រ អ៉ឹម និង វ៉េ:** The company should take the three dismissed group leaders back with full wages and compensation for the period they were kept outside the factory; they did not extort money from workers, because the workers collected money voluntarily for a monthly party [Para 6]. The union maintained that the dispute was collective and within the Council's jurisdiction [Para 46][Para 53].
**Employer (respondent) — ក្រុមហ៊ុន អ៉ឹម និង វ៉េ កាត់ដេរ អន្តរជាតិ សាខា ទី៣ (Im & V3 Garment International, Branch 3):** The three had threatened casual workers every month to extort money, forcing them under threat of dismissal [Para 7]. The company refused to conciliate this point, treating it as an individual dispute, and pointed out that the three had themselves admitted the extortion [Para 8][Para 9].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the Council declined jurisdiction and refused to consider the demand that the three group leaders be taken back [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65].
The panel first set out its general approach: where the labour inspector and the Minister of Labour have already decided, the Council will follow that decision unless there is a clear reason to object [Para 47]. It then turned to Article 302 of the Labour Law, which defines a collective labour dispute as one between one or more employers and a number of their employees over working conditions, the exercise of rights granted to professional organisations, recognition of a professional organisation in the enterprise, or relations between employer and workers, and which must be capable of making the enterprise operate poorly or disturbing social peace [Para 49].
Following its earlier Award No. 10/03 (Jackson Tic), the panel listed the three cumulative conditions: (a) a dispute between a number of workers and one or more employers; (b) a subject-matter concerning working conditions, the exercise of rights granted to professional organisations, recognition of such an organisation, or employer-worker relations; and (c) the dispute must be capable of disrupting the enterprise or social peace [Para 50][Para 51].
Applying them, conditions (a) and (b) were satisfied because the complaint was brought by a union representing a group of workers and concerned the employment relationship between the workers and the employer [Para 53]. Condition (c) failed: the three workers were a very small number compared with the 2,974 workers at the factory, they were not indispensable skilled workers even though they worked in the ironing section and were group leaders, and the union's members would not support them in a way that would disrupt the enterprise or social peace [Para 54][Para 55][Para 56]. The union also produced no evidence at the hearing that the dispute could disrupt the enterprise or social peace [Para 57].
The panel therefore held the dispute was not a collective labour dispute but an individual labour dispute between the three workers and the company, to be settled under the procedure of Article 301 [Para 60]. Because the Council's jurisdiction under Chapter 12, Part 2 of the Labour Law covers only collective labour disputes, it had no jurisdiction over this case and refused to consider it [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63][Para 64].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A dispute filed by a union is not automatically a collective labour dispute; it must satisfy all three conditions of Article 302, including that it can make the enterprise operate poorly or disturb social peace [Para 59][Para 60].
Three dismissed group leaders among 2,974 workers, who are not indispensable to production and whose fellow union members would not support them disruptively, do not meet that third condition, so their reinstatement demand is an individual dispute outside the Council's jurisdiction [Para 54][Para 55][Para 56][Para 61][Para 62].
**Obiter dicta:** The Council will ordinarily follow a decision already taken by the labour inspector and the Minister of Labour absent a clear reason to object [Para 47].
