# Award No. 24/04 — South Bay Enterprise (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 21 May 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mr Ouk Ry, employer-appointed; Mr An Nan, worker-appointed; Mr Sok Mathoeun, chair) · Doc ID 139305

## 1. Facts
South Bay Enterprise Co., Ltd., a garment factory at Chom Chao, Phnom Penh, employs 620 workers. [Para 20] The factory moved to a new location, and after the move the workers normally ate lunch in the factory compound. [Para 25] On Saturday 24 April 2004 the company directed them to eat outside the factory, and they struck the same day. [Para 25] Labour inspectors resolved sixteen of eighteen issues on 24 April and the workers agreed to return on 26 April, but the workforce kept striking, and the canteen question alone went to the Council. [Para 21][Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must set up a canteen equipped with suitable chairs and tables for the workers? [Para 9]
2. Whether, while that canteen is being built, the workers may take their lunch inside the factory? [Para 10][Para 11]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the canteen should be inside the factory compound or in any area suitable for rest and lunch; the company's proposed site beside the road is too small, dusty when there is traffic, hot and exposed to rain. [Para 27][Para 28]
**Employer (respondent):** it agreed to build the canteen outside the factory wall on a three-metre strip near the red gravel road, with the landowner arranging authority approval to use the footpath, but would not let the workers eat in the factory during construction, and maintained that no law requires it to provide a canteen at all. [Para 11][Para 26][Para 36][Para 37]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer must build a suitable canteen for the workers, equipped with tables and chairs, in the factory compound, with construction completed no later than 26 June 2004. [Para 37]

The panel began by holding that no legal requirement obliges an employer to provide a canteen [Para 29]. The dispute was therefore an interest dispute, not a rights dispute, and Article 312 of the Labour Law required it to be decided on the principles of equity. [Para 30] The panel explained that any award it made would operate as a collective bargaining agreement binding the parties for one year under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, and offered the employee party the choice between an award and further time to negotiate. [Para 31][Para 32] On 21 May 2004 at 9:00 am the employee party elected to have the award. [Para 33]

Turning to equity, the panel found that both parties agreed the site the company proposed was inadequate — small, next to the road, with no chairs, tables or suitable wall — and that it sat on the walkway, where a canteen should not be built. [Para 34][Para 35] It held the lunch break important to the workers' health and safety, noted that the employer gave no reason why it could not build inside the factory, and observed that garment factories generally do provide shaded places or suitable canteens without harm to health and safety. [Para 36][Para 37]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Although the Labour Law imposes no duty to provide a canteen, a dispute over one is an interest dispute to be decided on equity; where the employer has allowed workers to eat in the factory compound and the only alternative it offers is a roadside strip on a walkway, without shade, tables or chairs, the panel will order a canteen in the compound by a fixed date. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 34][Para 37]

**Obiter dicta:** the explanation that an award in an interest dispute becomes a one-year collective agreement under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099, and the general observation that garment factories usually provide shaded eating space, are explanatory only. [Para 31][Para 37]
