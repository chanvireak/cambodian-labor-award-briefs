# Award No. 61/09 — Cintri (Cambodia) Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 17 June 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kol Vathana for the employer, Ann Vireak for the workers, Kong Phallack chairing) · Doc ID 140388

## 1. Facts
Cintri (Cambodia) Ltd., a city cleaning company at No. 442D Monivong Blvd, Phnom Penh, employed about 1,050 workers. [Para 12] The claimant was the Local Union of TUFIKEL (Trade Union Federation for Increasing Khmer Employees Lifestyle), registered on 25 February 2008. [Para 3][Para 12] Two drivers — Mr C, the local union president and an employee since 14 December 2005, and Mr M, employed since 20 April 2004 — were transferred to Sihanouk Province on 4 April 2009, effective 23 April 2009, because the branch there had asked for three more drivers and the company wanted them as model staff. [Para 13][Para 24]

The union notified a non-violent strike on 23 April 2009, then cancelled it at a meeting at Phnom Penh City Hall on 28 April 2009; the company terminated both on 2 May 2009 for refusing to go, then on 21 May 2009 offered reinstatement on a six-month transfer with a salary increase, and the parties agreed to abide by the panel's decision. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 16][Para 17]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must maintain the positions of the two local union leaders in Phnom Penh, rather than transfer them to Sihanouk Province for six months? [Para 3][Para 18]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, Local Union of TUFIKEL):** the two men cannot leave Phnom Penh because of their families — Mr C has three children, two at school and one aged one, and Mr M has two young children — and the transfer is discrimination against the union, since it came soon after the union's registration, of which the company had been told. [Para 19][Para 22][Para 27][Para 30]

**Employer (respondent, Cintri (Cambodia) Ltd.):** the Sihanoukville branch requested three more drivers on 26 March 2009; both were chosen as good workers to serve as models, not as union leaders, a fact the company learned only from the strike notice; it offered transport to and from Phnom Penh, accommodation, a salary increase, a two-week trial and a six-month limit; and the matter is in any event an individual, not a collective, dispute. [Para 24][Para 26][Para 28][Para 32]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the demand that both drivers keep working in Phnom Penh was rejected, and the employer was ordered to transfer them to Sihanouk Province for a maximum of six months from the award taking effect, providing transport both ways, accommodation and a wage increase, with a two-week trial after which they may negotiate. [Para 57][Para 58]

The panel first settled jurisdiction. Under Article 302 of the Labour Law a dispute is collective where it arises between an employer and a number of staff, concerns working conditions or the exercise of recognised rights of professional organisations, and could jeopardise the effective operation of the enterprise or social peace. All three were met here, the third because a strike had been threatened and only suspended, and the Ministry had already classified the dispute as collective. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39]

On the employer's objection that this was an individual dispute, the panel held that the Labour Inspector and Ministry decide that classification, and that the panel follows it absent clear reason not to, as in Awards 10/03-Jacqsintex, 02/04-Cambodiana Hotel, 41/04-MiCas and 07/05-Coca Cola. It also held that each demand in a non-conciliation report is presumed collective, with the burden on the employer to prove otherwise — a burden not discharged by mere assertion, as Award 45/07-Wilson requires. [Para 36][Para 40][Para 41][Para 42]

On the merits, the panel set out the Article 2 managerial right to supervise and direct, subject to the four conditions from Awards 17/03 and 18/03-Ho Hing: no salary deduction, no transfer from a nearby to a remote place, no day-to-night shift change, and no transfer to a position requiring significantly different skills. Sihanouk Province is 200 km away, so the second condition was engaged; but the panel asked whether the employer had taken appropriate measures to make the change workable. Transport to and from Phnom Penh, accommodation, a pay increase, the six-month limit, the two-week trial and the negotiation opening satisfied it, since the workers' stated concern was family contact. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 49][Para 51]

On union discrimination, Article 12 forbids using union membership in deciding the definition and assignment of work, but the workers bear the burden of evidence, as in Awards 93/06-Evergreen, 112/06-River Rich and 01/07-Supreme. They offered none beyond timing, and did not document that they had told the company of the union's registration. [Para 53][Para 55][Para 56]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The distant-transfer condition is not breached by distance alone: a transfer to a remote site is lawful and reasonable where the employer neutralises it — return transport, accommodation, a wage increase, a fixed short duration and a trial period with a right to negotiate — so that the worker's family life is preserved. A union alleging that such a transfer is anti-union bears the burden of proving it, and timing alone will not do. [Para 46][Para 49][Para 56]

**Obiter dicta:** that the Labour Inspector's and Ministry's classification of a dispute as collective or individual will be followed unless there is clear reason to depart from it, and that every demand in a non-conciliation report is presumed collective until the employer proves otherwise. [Para 36][Para 40]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is an unofficial English translation with the Evidence section omitted, so the panel's evidentiary reasoning survives only in summary. OCR drops the "h" throughout ("themployer", "athearing", "tworks") and produces doubled or truncated clauses: Articles 12 and 2 of the Labour Law are quoted twice in near-identical, garbled form, and the sentence on the definition and assignment of work is split across three paragraphs. The employer's own answer at the conciliation stage is recorded as saying it cannot maintain the two positions "due to their good work performance", which contradicts its later reasoning that good performance was the reason for the transfer; the panel does not resolve this. The award prints all workers, union officers and employer representatives by full name, reduced here to initials and roles, and names a Phnom Penh City Hall official who chaired the settlement meetings, also reduced to a role.
