# Award No. 90/07-GDM — GDM Enterprise Company Limited v Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU) and Khmer Youth Trade Union (KYTU) at GDM Branch (Arbitration Council, 26 September 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Ann Vireak (worker-appointed), Kong Phallack (chair) · Doc ID 140639

## 1. Facts
GDM Enterprise Company Limited runs a laundry and dry-cleaning factory at Samaki Village, Sangkat Russey Keo, Khan Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, employing about 420 workers [Para 1][Para 20].
The claimants are the Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU) and the Khmer Youth Trade Union (KYTU) at the GDM Branch, which the panel found was not registered and which supplied no documents on the election of its leader [Para 4][Para 20][Para 21].
On 23 August 2007 the Department of Labour Disputes received a telephone complaint from the KYTU branch seeking improved working conditions, and nine of the ten demands were conciliated [Para 11][Para 12].
The remaining demand, on maintaining Mr S in his post, was forwarded with non-conciliation report No. 931 on 5 September 2007, after an unsuccessful conciliation on 4 September 2007 [Para 11][Para 13].
The hearing was held on 11 September 2007, and 346 of the 420 workers were recorded as involved in the dispute [Para 11][Para 20].
Mr S had been Chief of the Dry Unit, was transferred to the Laundry Unit and demoted on 21 August 2007, suspended for a week on 22 August 2007, and allowed back on 23 August 2007 after workers protested, without pay for the suspension day [Para 33].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must maintain Mr S in his position as Chief of the Dry Unit, given the workers' claim that his transfer to the Laundry Unit was invalid? [Para 5][Para 22][Para 44]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), KYFTU and KYTU at GDM Branch:** Mr S's transfer was invalid and he should be kept as Chief of the Dry Unit [Para 23][Para 24][Para 44]. Mr S said the company director asked him on 18 August 2007 to persuade workers to return to work because they feared a cut in night-shift pay from 200% to 130% after an amendment to the Labour Law, and that his subordinates then damaged about 40 trousers [Para 24][Para 25]. He said he never received a warning letter about garment damage and that the company raised the reason only on the day of dismissal [Para 36][Para 37]. He accepted the transfer brought no wage cut, the two units shared a warehouse and similar skills, and the shift pattern was unchanged [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41].
**Employer (respondent), GDM Enterprise Company Limited:** it could not keep Mr S in the same position and wanted to investigate his misconduct first [Para 6][Para 7][Para 8]. It said he chatted with other workers for an hour and a half instead of working on 18 August 2007 and that his team damaged several thousand garments, showing he did not follow company direction [Para 26]. His work had been evaluated by his supervisor [Para 28], and if he worked hard the company would consider promoting him to Chief of the Laundry Unit [Para 42]. Its representative at the hearing did not know the details of the dismissal [Para 30][Para 31].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** the demand to keep Mr S in the Dry Unit was rejected, but the company was ordered to maintain him in the position of Chief of Group [Para 70][Para 71][Para 72][Para 73].
The panel held that Article 2(1) of the Labour Law gives the employer the right to manage and direct the enterprise, including transferring workers, subject to four conditions laid down in Awards 17/03 and 18/03 Ho Hing Issue 1: no reduction in wages, no distant relocation, no change of shift from day to night, and a similar skill set [Para 50][Para 51][Para 52].
On the facts the transfer to the Laundry Unit satisfied all four conditions: pay was unaffected, both units sat in the same warehouse, the machines were similar, and the company ran half a month on day shift and half on night [Para 54][Para 55][Para 58][Para 59].
The demotion to ordinary worker was different. Article 27 of the Labour Law requires any disciplinary sanction to be proportional to the seriousness of the misconduct, a principle applied in Awards 54/04 Sportwear Issue 2 and 08/05 Winner Knitting Issue 2 [Para 60][Para 61].
The panel found no evidence to support the claim that Mr S damaged several thousand garments or chatted without working, and the company's representative could not refute the workers' account because the person who knew the case did not attend [Para 62][Para 63][Para 31].
Article 10 of the company's Internal Work Rules requires misconduct to be punished by graduated warnings and only dismissed where the worker reoffends, and Mr S had never received a warning about damaged garments, a point the company did not refute [Para 45][Para 48][Para 64][Para 65][Para 66].
The panel therefore held there was insufficient evidence that the demotion was proportional to the misconduct or consistent with the Internal Work Rules [Para 67][Para 68]. It rejected the demand to restore him to the Dry Unit but ordered the company to maintain him as Chief of Group [Para 71][Para 73].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer's right to manage and direct the enterprise under Article 2(1) of the Labour Law includes the power to transfer a worker, but only where the transfer leaves wages unchanged, does not move the worker to a distant location, does not switch the shift from day to night, and requires a similar skill set [Para 51][Para 52].
A disciplinary sanction must be proportional to the misconduct under Article 27 of the Labour Law, so a demotion unsupported by evidence and imposed without the warnings the Internal Work Rules require is unlawful [Para 60][Para 61][Para 66][Para 68].
The employer bears the consequence of sending a representative who cannot answer the case, since the panel cannot treat untested allegations as proved [Para 31][Para 63].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The case number is printed as "90107", evidently 90/07, and the award date of 26 September 2007 sits after the hearing date of 11 September 2007 but before the second listed hearing date of 18 September 2007 [Para 1][Para 11][Para 13]. The hearing date is given as 11 September 2007 from 8:00 to 11:00 a.m. in one place and as 2:00 p.m. in another [Para 11][Para 13]. The employer's three-letter abbreviation is transposed in the facts section, so the factory is called by the wrong initials [Para 20]. The panel composition is printed without punctuation, running the employer-side arbitrator's name into the worker-side name [Para 1]. The English translation drops the letter "t" throughout ("he" for "the", "hat" for "that") and several sentences are truncated mid-phrase, for example the statement that the Arbitration Council "considers that the" is repeated and then cut off [Para 53]. The order itself is internally inconsistent: the reasoning upholds the transfer while ordering the worker maintained as "Chief of Group", a rank not previously mentioned, rather than either Chief of the Dry Unit or Chief of the Laundry Unit [Para 69][Para 73].
