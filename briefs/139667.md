# Award No. 209/14 — Keep Top Sporting Goods (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 25 August 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mr Seng Vouchhun (chosen by the employer), Mr Tuon Si Phan (chosen by the workers), chair Mr Pen Bun Chea · Doc ID 139667

## 1. Facts
Keep Top Sporting Goods (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a sports-equipment manufacturer in Sangkhat 1, Preah Sihanouk town and province, employed about 200 workers, of whom 113 gave the Cambodia Democratic Garment Workers' Union Federation a thumbprinted authorisation to sue on their behalf; no local union at the company had yet been registered with the Ministry. [Para 28][Para 29][Para 30][Para 31][Para 32] Conciliation of 13 demands on 23 July 2014 settled 12 and left one unresolved: the deduction of the attendance bonus (ប្រាក់រង្វាន់ធ្វើការទៀងទាត់) for days of authorised absence. [Para 8][Para 9][Para 33] The employer pays a US$15 monthly attendance bonus and had applied an agreement of 23 April 2014 with seven worker representatives under which US$5 is deducted for one day of authorised absence, US$10 for two and all US$15 for three. [Para 33][Para 35][Para 36] Both parties chose a non-binding award. [Para 14]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must deduct the attendance bonus pro rata, according to the number of days on which a worker is absent with the employer's authorisation, instead of applying the flat US$5, US$10 and US$15 deductions in the 23 April 2014 agreement? [Para 33][Para 47]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The attendance bonus is part of wages under Article 103 of the Labour Law and may be cut only in proportion to the days of absence; the past deductions were therefore wrong, and every agreement contrary to the law is void, as Awards No. 086/14 and No. 166/14 held. [Para 40][Para 46][Para 47][Para 48]
**Employer (respondent):** It cannot grant the demand and will keep applying the 23 April 2014 agreement, which most workers accepted; it produced no evidence of that acceptance and did not submit the agreement to the panel within the deadline. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 45][Para 37]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer is ordered to deduct the attendance bonus pro rata to the number of days a worker is absent with the employer's authorisation; the award is non-binding until no party objects in writing within 08 days. [Para 98][Para 99][Para 100]

The panel began with Ministry Notification No. 230 K.B/S.J.N. of 25 July 2012, point 2, which requires a worker who comes to work regularly for the working days of each month "without absence" to receive an attendance bonus of at least US$10 a month, and which preserves higher bonuses already paid. [Para 47][Para 48] Because the employer pays US$15, the panel treated the payment as a benefit above the notification and held that the higher practice must be maintained. [Para 49][Para 50] It then found that neither the Ministry nor the Labour Advisory Committee had defined the words "without absence", and that it could not itself assume the phrase covers both authorised and unauthorised absence irrespective of legality and reasonableness; the claim therefore had to be decided case by case. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 64] Absences that flow from annual leave, special leave, maternity leave, public holidays and weekly rest are lawful and do not permit any deduction, so the worker keeps 100% of the bonus for those days. [Para 61][Para 62] The panel reasoned that absence with the employer's authorisation suspends the contract under Article 71 paragraph 6, and Article 72 paragraph 1 removes the wage obligation for the suspended days, so the employer may not cancel the whole bonus but may deduct only the amount proportionate to the days actually absent. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 86][Para 87][Para 88]

The 23 April 2014 agreement could not be applied. The workers had not been told of the benefits the law gave them, the employer filed no evidence that most workers accepted it, and the agreement gave less than the law allows, so under Article 13 paragraph 1 it was void, as the panel had held in Awards No. 115/08, No. 109/09, No. 10/10, No. 78/10 and No. 70/11. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 91][Para 92][Para 95][Para 96][Para 97] The panel also noted that the agreement of 23 April 2014 was made with seven ordinary workers before any union existed, and that the demand for proportional deduction was raised only once the union appeared. [Para 43][Para 44]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The attendance bonus is a wage, so it may not be forfeited in full for authorised absence; the employer may deduct only the part proportionate to the days for which the contract was suspended, and days of annual, special or maternity leave, public holidays and weekly rest attract no deduction at all. An agreement that gives workers less than the Labour Law and the Ministry notification, and that was concluded without informing them of their statutory entitlement or proving majority acceptance, is void under Article 13 and cannot justify a larger deduction. [Para 62][Para 88][Para 91][Para 95]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that bonuses take many forms and that their amount is a matter of the employer's own discretion for motivating staff, but that the attendance bonus fixed by Notification No. 230 is a general norm binding on all textile, garment and footwear employers rather than a management tool. [Para 55][Para 56]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel's own framing of the issue prints the bonus as "US$150" a month at para 47, while the evidence, the agreement and the order all use US$15; both are reproduced as printed, and the order follows US$15. [Para 36][Para 47][Para 49][Para 99] The company appears as "Keep Top Sporting Goods (Cambodia)" in the heading and as "Keeptop" in the union's letter, with further variants in the Khmer; the face of the award is followed here. [Para 1][Para 21] Para 10 dates the creation of the arbitration panel "29 July 2023 2014". [Para 10] Para 94 breaks off mid-sentence ("in the case …"), and the reasoning resumes at para 95. [Para 94][Para 95] The award date is 25 August 2014 in the heading, following an agreed extension from 18 August 2014. [Para 1][Para 16] The signature block carries no printed names for the employer-chosen and worker-chosen arbitrators, only the chair's, "Pen Bun Chea". [Para 101] Individual workers and representatives are recorded only by name and are not reproduced here.
