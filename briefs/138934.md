# Award No. 027/17 — Ken Sport Shoe Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 7 August 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Kong Phalla (president) · Doc ID 138934

## 1. Facts
Ken Sport Shoe Co., Ltd. manufactures shoes in Samaki Meanchey district, Kampong Chhnang, and employed roughly 770 workers at the time of the award. [Para 1][Para 68][Para 69] Workers from four unions submitted 18 demands for improved conditions; conciliation at the provincial Labour Department settled 14, four remained unresolved, and the first (card-slot gates) settled at the hearing, leaving three issues. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 20][Para 21] Both sides accepted that the company's practice was to issue pregnancy identification cards to women from three months of pregnancy so they could leave 10 minutes early. [Para 76][Para 151] The panel found no most-representative union at the company. [Para 75][Para 131]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to issue an identification card to pregnant workers from the moment they receive an antenatal examination book from the hospital, so that they may leave work 10 minutes early? [Para 146]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to pay a position allowance (ប្រាក់មុខងារ) to workers in the SCM and ME sections? [Para 165]
3. Whether the employer must permit workers to wear shoes without heel straps, or failing that, must itself supply the shoes it requires them to wear? [Para 189][Para 198]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the first three months of pregnancy are the most dangerous, so women need protection from the moment they hold a hospital antenatal book; two workers less than three months pregnant miscarried while crowding out of the factory gate on 18 May 2017 and 10 June 2017; SCM and ME workers do heavy work — cleaning toilets, clearing drains, removing waste — yet receive no position allowance; and the required shoes are expensive, tear easily, are never supplied, and workers were never told of the requirement at interview. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 80][Para 94][Para 102][Para 104]
**Employer (respondent):** the 10-minute card is a company policy already more generous than the law, which requires no such privilege, and the three-month threshold is written into the company's internal rules; the position allowance is by design only for production-line workers and is graded by the weight of the job at $2, $4 or $6; the company does not prescribe a shoe model, only a shoe protecting toes and heels, and the law does not require it to supply footwear. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 95][Para 96][Para 106][Para 108]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 1 granted, ordering pregnancy cards from receipt of the antenatal book; issue 2 dismissed without consideration; issue 3 granted, ordering the employer to stop requiring shoes with a front toe-cap and rear heel strap and to allow shoes without a heel strap. [Para 218][Para 219][Para 220][Para 221]
On issue 1 the panel read clause 1.7.2 of the internal regulations, approved by the provincial Labour Department under letter No. 511 dated 10 June 2016, which grants all pregnant workers the right to leave 10 minutes early and attaches no condition. [Para 146][Para 148][Para 149] The company's separate "procedure for protecting pregnant women" limits the card to pregnancies beyond three months, which the panel held narrows an entitlement already fixed in the approved internal regulations and so cannot stand; it also accepted, the employer having offered no contrary evidence, that women in their first three months face the same risk. [Para 155][Para 156][Para 158][Para 161][Para 163]
On issue 2 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal regulations or past practice requiring a position allowance for SCM and ME workers, and therefore classified the demand as an interest dispute. [Para 166][Para 167][Para 168] Applying មាត្រា 96(2) of the Labour Law and មាត្រា 54 and 72 of the 2016 Law on Trade Unions, it held that only a most-representative union may bring an interest dispute, that the claimant was not one, and declined to consider the claim. [Para 169][Para 183][Para 186][Para 187]
On issue 3 the panel accepted that an employer may organise safety measures under មាត្រា 249 and 250, but only where they are lawful and reasonable. [Para 194][Para 203] It found the internal regulations imposed no shoe requirement at all, that the employer produced no expert or other evidence that toe-cap and heel-strap shoes were a necessary safety measure, and that the labour inspector had never issued any report on footwear. [Para 109][Para 205][Para 206][Para 208] Requiring workers to buy the shoes themselves also placed the cost of the employer's safety duty on the workers, contrary to មាត្រា 229, 249 and 250. [Para 210][Para 215][Para 216]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a company's approved internal regulations grant a benefit unconditionally, a later internal procedure that adds a threshold condition (here, three months of pregnancy) impermissibly narrows that entitlement and will not be applied. [Para 156][Para 158] A footwear or uniform rule imposed for safety will be struck down where the employer can show no evidence that it is a necessary protective measure and shifts its cost onto workers, since safety at the workplace is the employer's duty and burden. [Para 208][Para 210]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the employer should look for some other appropriate measure to protect workers' safety rather than requiring toe-cap and heel-strap shoes — advice going beyond the order to stop the policy. [Para 211] It also recorded, without relying on it, that two workers miscarried while leaving the factory. [Para 160][Para 162]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The list of remaining issues is misprinted at [Para 21] as "ទី២, ទី២ និង ទី៤" (2, 2 and 4), where the second numeral should read 3; the reasoning and the order both run 2, 3, 4. [Para 218][Para 219][Para 220] The reasoning at [Para 209] cites មាត្រា ២៤៨ and ២៥០ of the Labour Law, while [Para 210] and [Para 215] cite ២៤៩ and ២៥០ for the same proposition — the award is internally inconsistent on the article number. The Prakas is printed as "លេខ ១៩៩ សកបយ" at [Para 180] but as 099 elsewhere, e.g. [Para 182]. OCR inserts stray numerals into the text at [Para 130] ("៤០"), [Para 159] ("២៤ នាទី") and [Para 186] ("១០"), and the company is once rendered "ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេត្ត ស្ព័ត ស៊ូ" (Khet for Ken) at [Para 51] and [Para 53].
