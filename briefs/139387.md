# Award No. 239/16 — Ken Sport Shoe (Arbitration Council, 28 December 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of three — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (chair) · Doc ID 139387

## 1. Facts
A shoe factory at Samakki Mon Chey, Kampong Chhnang, registered in 2010 and said at the hearing to employ about 7,000 workers [Para 58][Para 59]. Two plant unions filed five demands on 3 November 2016; three were settled in conciliation and two remained [Para 9][Para 10]. Mr K, a union activist, worked under a final fixed-duration contract running from 20 October 2015 to 19 October 2016, which the employer did not renew [Para 63][Para 149]. Ms T, a team leader, was demoted on 1 August 2016 after 300 pairs of shoes were damaged under her line [Para 109][Para 110].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back Mr K, a union activist, and let him work again? [Para 149]
2. Whether the employer must restore Ms T to the post of team leader and to the team-leader wage? [Para 207]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr K was a good worker with no warning, and the non-renewal was retaliation for his union activity, including his attendance at International Labour Day on 2 May 2016, after which he was moved from place to place and left idle [Para 67][Para 68][Para 73]. No written notice of non-renewal was ever given [Para 70]. Ms T never agreed to the demotion and did not know her wage and benefits would fall with it [Para 113][Para 120].
**Employer (respondent):** non-renewal is the employer's right, notice was given a month ahead through the group leader, and Mr K refused to sign [Para 71][Para 80]. It denied discrimination, noting four other activists attended the same event and kept their contracts [Para 74]. For Ms T, damaging 300 pairs is a serious fault permitting dismissal under the internal rules, and the employer showed mercy by demoting her instead; she agreed orally and in writing [Para 116][Para 117][Para 119].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must take Mr K back; the claim to restore Ms T's post and wage is dismissed [Para 244][Para 245].

On standing, the panel found no most-representative union at the factory and relied on Prakas 362 of 29 August 2016, under which the provincial labour department certified six worker representatives; the four who attended had standing to represent the workers [Para 138][Para 142][Para 146].

On Mr K, Article 73(5) requires written notice of non-renewal — fifteen days where the contract exceeds one year — and Article 67(2) caps successive fixed-duration contracts at two years [Para 152][Para 154][Para 157]. The panel compared the employer's evidence: a bare table ticking "non-renewal", which it held was an internal evaluation sheet and not a notice to the worker, plus meeting minutes produced after the hearing whose author gave inconsistent dates [Para 177][Para 178][Para 179]. The employer therefore failed to prove notice [Para 181]. The contract was renewed for an equal term, taking total service to three years, beyond the two-year ceiling, so it became an indefinite-duration contract [Para 182][Para 183][Para 186].

The separation was accordingly a dismissal of a worker holding an indefinite-duration contract, not a lawful expiry [Para 187]. Article 74 allows dismissal at will only with written notice and a valid reason going to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the needs of the enterprise [Para 188][Para 190]. The panel accepted that Mr K had signed an undertaking on 10 May 2016 to obey his supervisors and that he admitted the fault, but found the employer showed no later disobedience, and that the internal rules do not make dismissal mandatory for it [Para 198][Para 200][Para 201]. Reinstatement was ordered [Para 206].

On Ms T, rule 4.2.36("ង") of the internal rules treats deliberate waste causing loss of company materials as a serious fault, sanctionable first by final warning and second by dismissal [Para 208][Para 209]. Demotion is not listed among the sanctions, but the panel followed Award 156/11 (Golfem) in holding that an employer may impose a sanction not written in the internal rules where it is proportionate to the fault and agreed by the worker; restricting the employer to listed sanctions would trespass on its right to dismiss for serious misconduct under Article 26 [Para 213][Para 226][Para 229]. Demotion is milder than the dismissal the rules allow, and Ms T signed a request for demotion on 17 November 2016 [Para 230][Para 231]. The claim was dismissed [Para 243].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer fails to give the written notice of non-renewal that Article 73(5) and its own internal rules require, the fixed-duration contract is renewed for an equal term [Para 157][Para 182]; if the accumulated service then exceeds two years it becomes an indefinite-duration contract, and any later separation is a dismissal requiring a valid reason under Article 74 [Para 183][Para 186][Para 190]. A demotion imposed for a serious fault is lawful even though the internal rules list only warning and dismissal, provided it is proportionate and the worker agrees [Para 226][Para 229], since the employer's power to dismiss for serious misconduct cannot be narrowed to the listed sanctions [Para 213].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the eight-day rule in Article 26(2) gives the employer a right to dismiss for serious fault but imposes no duty to do so, so an employer may choose a lighter sanction [Para 217].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The certification letter lists six worker representatives, but only four of the six who attended appear on it, and the panel treated the four as the representatives without explaining the other two [Para 143][Para 145]. The employer's representatives are printed with a garbled name for the first of them [Para 1]. A paragraph describing the conciliation cites "ជំពូក ៤០ ទី១២", where the chapter number is corrupt [Para 5]. The closing paragraph on finality is badly corrupted and the signature block for the worker-appointed arbitrator is reduced to stray characters [Para 246].
