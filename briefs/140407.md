# Award No. 86/09 — Wil Sin Garmen (Cambodia) Co., Ltd v Cambodia Democratic Apparel Workers' Union (Arbitration Council of Cambodia, 24 July 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Chhiv Phirem ឈឺវ ភិរម្យ (employer-appointed), Tuon Si Phan ទួន ស៊ី ផាន់ (worker-appointed), Kong Phalla គង់ ផល្លៈ (president) · Doc ID 140407

## 1. Facts
Wil Sin Garmen (Cambodia) Co., Ltd — 1,016 workers — was a garment factory in Dangkao, Phnom Penh. On 28 February 2009 fire destroyed it almost entirely, and the company stopped operating and applied to the Ministry of Commerce, the CDC and the Ministry of Labour to close. [Para 36][Para 38] From 28 February to 5 June 2009 the company suspended work and, by agreement with the unions, paid workers USD 10 a month. [Para 41] On 5 June 2009 the company and two other factory unions (Khmer Youth Union and the Cambodia Workers' Labour Union) agreed to terminate contracts under Articles 89, 166 and 167 plus March–April arrears; the claimant union was expressly excluded from that deal. [Para 44]

In the non-conciliation report the union's members demanded notice pay (Art 75), dismissal damages (Art 89), moral damages (Art 91), unpaid wages (Art 116), wages owed (Art 124) and annual-leave compensation (Arts 166–167); the employer answered that the fire left it unable to pay anything, offering only 15 days per year under Art 89 and annual leave. [Para 3][Para 4] The claim was filed for 293 workers: 287 of them had already received Art 89 pay and sought Arts 75 and 91 in addition, while six others, not yet paid under Art 89, sought Arts 75, 89 and 91. [Para 46][Para 48] The Art 116, Art 124 and annual-leave demands were never reached in the reasons or the order; the union holds about 150 members and is not the most representative union at the factory. [Para 37]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether a registered union may lawfully represent, before the panel, workers who are not its own members? [Para 64]
2. (Issue 2a) Whether the employer owes notice pay under Articles 75 and 77 for terminating without prior notice? [Para 76]
3. (Issue 2b) Whether the employer owes moral damages under Article 91 for terminating without valid reason? [Para 85]
4. (Issue 2c) Whether the Article 89 severance of the six workers is computed on the 12-month average wage, including overtime and bonuses? [Para 99]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**ភាគីកម្មករនិយោជិត — Cambodia Democratic Apparel Workers' Union (សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា, claimant):** the company cut the contracts on 5 June 2009 at the meeting with the unions with no prior notice whatever, so Art 75 compensation is owed; the termination was also without clarity or valid reason, so Art 91 moral damages follow; and Art 89 severance must be averaged over the last 12 months' total wages, not a base-salary-plus-allowance formula. [Para 50][Para 55][Para 101]

**ភាគីនិយោជក — Wil Sin Garmen (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (respondent):** no notice was needed because the fire was broadcast on television and workers had known of the closure since the March 2009 discussions with all three unions; in any event the fire left the company without the means to pay the statutory benefits; on Art 89 it accepts liability and disputes only the arithmetic — base salary plus the USD 6 living allowance plus seniority bonus, divided by 26. [Para 42][Para 51][Para 60]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must pay notice compensation under Articles 75 and 77 to the workers who are members of the claimant union; the Article 91 moral-damages claim is dismissed; and Article 89 severance for the six unpaid workers must be recalculated on total wages including overtime and bonuses over the last 12 months, divided by 12 then by 26 and multiplied by the number of days owed. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 108]

On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 268: a professional organisation obtains the rights the Labour Law recognises only once registered and recognised by the Ministry of Labour, and those rights include representing its members before the Council — a position it had already taken in Awards 62/06 and 31/08. Registration does not extend representation to non-members, so the union could act for its 150 members only; because the list of non-member claimants arrived on 14 July 2009, one day after the deadline, it was not considered. [Para 66][Para 68][Para 71][Para 74]

On Issue 2a the panel read Articles 75 and 77 together: unilateral termination without notice, or without observing the notice period, obliges the employer to pay wages and benefits for the period not observed. The company produced nothing showing notice was given, and the panel had specifically ordered it to file such documents by 13 July 2009; discussions with the unions were not notice. Notice pay was therefore ordered for union members. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 84]

On Issue 2b the panel held that Article 91 compensation is distinct from notice pay and from Art 89 severance, and requires termination without valid reason. Here there was a reason: the workers themselves asked to be released and the fire made continued operation impossible while closure applications were pending — the same conclusion as in Award 84/08. The claim was dismissed. [Para 86][Para 91][Para 93]

On Issue 2c the panel followed Award 27/04 (Issue 1): severance is total wages over the 12 months before termination, including overtime and bonuses, divided by 12 and by 26, times the statutory days. The employer's base-salary formula was rejected and the six workers' severance was ordered on that basis, counted to 28 February 2009. [Para 103][Para 105]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union registered under Article 268 may appear before the panel, but only for the workers who are its members; where the claimant list includes non-members and the evidence identifying them is filed out of time, the panel examines the claims of members alone and the remainder fall away for want of a lawful representative. [Para 71][Para 74]

**Obiter dicta:** that prior discussions with unions and press coverage of a factory fire do not substitute for the written notice Article 75 requires, and that Article 91 moral damages are cumulative in principle with notice pay and Art 89 severance but are unavailable where the employer had a valid reason. [Para 83][Para 86]
