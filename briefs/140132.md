# Award No. 99/12 — Huy Chhean (Cambodia) Knit Co (Arbitration Council, 10 July 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-selected; Sin Kim Sean, worker-selected; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · Doc ID 140132

## 1. Facts
Huy Chhean (Cambodia) Knit Co, a Phnom Penh shoe factory of about 2,300 workers, dismissed 35 of its technical-section workers in the wake of a strike; the union, which had no registration certificate at the time, demanded severance and back wages for them. [Para 72][Para 74][Para 120] Separately, the union's secretary — the only woman on its executive — was stripped of her team-leader post and its USD 20 function allowance in May 2012, and the union recast that loss as a fresh claim at the hearing. [Para 76][Para 84][Para 85] At the first hearing the parties settled the check-off and seniority-bonus claims, leaving two claims for decision. [Para 23][Para 24]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the panel has jurisdiction over the new claim to restore the union secretary's team-leader post and the USD 20 function allowance? [Para 148][Para 151]
2. Whether the employer must pay severance and back wages to the 35 dismissed technical workers, counted from 20 December 2011 to 2 March 2012? [Para 163][Para 172]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the demotion was anti-union retaliation, coming after the secretary was elected on 7 July 2011 and was then watched by guards, denied leave and dismissed once before being taken back; the 35 were dismissed without severance after they tried to return to work on 2 March 2012 and were turned away by factory security. [Para 92][Para 94][Para 95][Para 133]
**Employer (respondent):** the demotion followed a steady decline in work quality, frequent unexplained absence and repeated warnings, and another worker was demoted the same month for the same reason; the 35 had already been paid severance in performance of Award No. 178/11 and the court injunction, and no worker presented himself on 2 March 2012. [Para 96][Para 98][Para 103][Para 135]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel held it had no jurisdiction over the demotion claim and refused both severance and back wages for the 35 dismissed workers, making no order for payment or reinstatement. [Para 162][Para 173][Para 174][Para 175]

On the demotion the panel began with Article 312(1) of the Labour Law, which confines it to the issues listed in the non-conciliation minute or to issues arising after that minute as a direct consequence of the original dispute. [Para 151] It applied a two-limb test: did the demotion occur after the minute, and was it a direct consequence of the original claim? [Para 152] The demotion took effect on 7 May 2012 and the minute was signed on 11 May 2012, so the first limb failed. [Para 153][Para 154][Para 155] The second also failed: the section head's request of 7 May cited quality, absenteeism and refusal to follow instructions since October 2011, the secretary had received written warnings in 2008, 2010 and 2011 which she declined to sign, and a second worker was demoted in the same month. [Para 115][Para 107][Para 111] The panel accordingly declined jurisdiction rather than deciding the discrimination question. [Para 161][Para 162]

On the 35 workers the panel accepted the figure of 35 over the 36 pleaded in the written submissions, because 35 was the number in the non-conciliation report and the number confirmed at the hearing. [Para 123][Para 124] The injunction of the Phnom Penh Municipal Court of 16 December 2011 had ordered the workers back to their posts within 48 hours; the appeal decision of 1 March 2012 upheld it in full and confiscated the appeal fee. [Para 131][Para 129][Para 130] The panel held the appeal decision did not create a new right to return: it confirmed the earlier injunction, whose third operative paragraph made it immediately enforceable. [Para 166][Para 168] Failure to return within those 48 hours was therefore serious misconduct, as the panel had already held in Award No. 178/11, and the employer owed neither severance nor back wages. [Para 170][Para 172] The workers' assertion of a blocked return on 2 March 2012 was unsupported by any document, and the employer's payment schedules were illegible and so disregarded. [Para 134][Para 139]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim that crystallised before the non-conciliation minute, and that is not a direct consequence of the demands recorded in that minute, falls outside the panel's jurisdiction under Article 312(1) even where it looks like retaliation and is pleaded as such; the panel will dismiss it on jurisdiction rather than examine the merits. [Para 151][Para 155][Para 161] A worker's failure to return to work within the 48 hours fixed by a court injunction prohibiting disruption constitutes serious misconduct, and the employer then owes no severance and no back wages for the period of absence. [Para 168][Para 170][Para 172]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that the employer's payment ledgers were too small in print to be read, and that it therefore disregarded the employer's claim that 26 workers had already collected, is an observation on evidence handling rather than a ground of decision. [Para 139][Para 143]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The number of technical workers involved is given as 67 at [Para 9] and 76 at [Para 120], while the operative part proceeds on 35. The prior award is cited as No. 178/12 at [Para 10] and No. 178/11 at [Para 125]. The non-conciliation report is numbered 423 at [Para 13] and 523 at [Para 18]. The header dates the award 10 July 2012, but the panel records that both parties agreed to postpone issuance from 20 June to that date. [Para 30] The worker whose demotion is in issue and the seven named employees are individuals; they are reduced to initials and roles here, and the union's lack of registration is recorded at [Para 74] while the same union is treated as a registered federation affiliate at [Para 73].
