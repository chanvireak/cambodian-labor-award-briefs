# Award No. 026/16 — FCF Inspection (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 24 February 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tai Seng, appointed by the employer; Sin Kim Sean, appointed by the workers; Kong Phally, president) · Doc ID 138978

## 1. Facts
FCF Inspection (Cambodia) Co., Ltd provides quality-inspection services and employs about 300 workers; a Democratic Union of Khmer Workers (Cambodia) local was registered at the company on 27 October 2015 with Ms K as its president [Para 34][Para 35]. Two workers — Ms K, the union president, and Ms H — brought claims after the company stopped engaging Ms K on 30 October 2015 and after Ms H left in October 2015 [Para 2][Para 3][Para 49]. The union's claims were filed with the Ministry of Labour, conciliation failed on 28 January 2016, and the two unsettled points were referred to the Council on 01 February 2016 [Para 9][Para 10]. The company objected at the outset that this was an individual, not a collective, labour dispute [Para 38].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company should take back the union president, Ms K (employee No. 05), reinstate her in her post and pay her wages from the date of the stoppage until her return? [Para 43]
2. Whether the company should pay Ms H (employee No. 0201) her October 2015 wages and other benefits — a USD 10 regular-attendance bonus, USD 01 per day in transport allowance, 6,000 riel per day in meal allowance, 50% of wages for the 10 days of suspension, and 07 days' wages in lieu of notice? [Para 55]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Ms K was a long-serving employee who had been made to sign successive 03-month fixed-duration contracts (three of them, unlike other QC heads), a practice contrary to Article 13 of the Labour Law and therefore void, leaving her on an indefinite contract; the company then terminated her without valid grounds, without the serious misconduct required by Article 83, and without the labour inspector's permission owed to a union president [Para 47][Para 48][Para 51]. Ms H's contract was likewise converted into a series of 03-month contracts contrary to Article 13, and the company suspended her for 10 days paying only 50% of wages and withheld her allowances and 07 days' notice pay [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58].
**Employer (respondent):** The case concerns only two individuals and is a personal dispute, not a collective one — Ms K's case is simply the non-renewal of a fixed-duration contract that expired on 30 October 2015, and no other worker, including the union's vice-president and secretary, supported or protested for the two claimants, nor was production disrupted [Para 38][Para 39][Para 50]. The company nonetheless stated it agreed to pay Ms H's October wages and benefits [Para 66].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — all claims in case No. 026/16 were refused consideration (បដិសេធ ពិចារណា); the award is non-binding and becomes final 08 days after notification [Para 95][Para 96][Para 97].

The panel began with jurisdiction. Article 32 of Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council confines it to collective labour disputes referred under Article 309 of the Labour Law, and by principle the labour inspector and the Minister of Labour decide whether a dispute is individual or collective, a classification the panel will follow absent clear reasons to depart from it [Para 68][Para 69][Para 70][Para 71]. Article 302 defines a collective dispute by three cumulative conditions, and the panel adopted the test stated in prior Award No. 10/03 (Zacksintic): a dispute between a number of workers and one or more employers, concerning working conditions or the exercise of professional-organisation rights, which can make the enterprise run badly or damage social peace [Para 80][Para 81][Para 82].

On the first condition the panel found that the dispute arose between 02 workers and the employer, so condition (a) was met [Para 84][Para 85]. On the second, the claims concerned working conditions, namely the workers' contracts, so condition (b) was met [Para 87][Para 88]. On the third, the panel weighed the evidence and found that no other worker supported the claims, that the union president's role did not by itself convert the matter into a collective dispute, and that the claimants themselves conceded that since Ms K's stoppage and Ms H's resignation no act had affected the production chain or damaged social peace [Para 88][Para 89][Para 90][Para 91]. Because condition (c) was not satisfied, the dispute was not a collective labour dispute and the panel had no jurisdiction to decide it, so the claims were refused consideration without examination on the merits [Para 92][Para 93][Para 94][Para 95].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim brought by a small number of workers, even where one of them is the enterprise's union president, is not a collective labour dispute within Article 302 unless the third condition is also proved — that the dispute is capable of making the enterprise run badly or of damaging social peace; absent that showing the panel has no jurisdiction and will not reach the merits. [Para 91][Para 92][Para 93]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the labour inspector and the Minister of Labour bear the responsibility for classifying a dispute before referral, and that where an employer challenges the Ministry's characterisation the employer carries the burden of proving its assertion; here the employer's challenge succeeded on the evidence of absence of worker support and of disruption. [Para 70][Para 76]
