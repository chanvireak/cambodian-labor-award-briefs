# Award No. 095/16 — Bill Ab (Cambodia) Menufore Kha Chiring Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 11 May 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Chhiv Phirom (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Koy Nam (chair) · Doc ID 139393

## 1. Facts
Bill Ab (Cambodia) Menufore Kha Chiring Ltd. manufactures hangers and employs about 152 workers. [Para 43] The claim was filed by the Khmer Youth Voice Union Federation and its plant union, but the two workers who appeared — Mr S and Mr J — did so as elected workers' delegates, a status both parties recognised. [Para 45][Para 46] The panel therefore treated the delegates, not the union, as the claimants, under Article 283 of the Labour Law. [Para 65] The company pays a USD 10 monthly attendance bonus and a USD 9 travel and accommodation allowance. [Para 47]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay an attendance bonus of USD 15 a month and a travel and accommodation allowance of USD 12 a month? [Para 47]
2. Whether the employer must terminate the workers' employment contracts and pay them five heads of benefit calculated on seniority? [Para 50]
3. Whether the employer must reduce the attendance bonus in proportion to the days of absence it has authorised? [Para 55]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the bonus and allowance should be raised above current levels; long-serving workers on indefinite contracts fear closure without severance and want their contracts ended with payment; and cutting the whole bonus for a single authorised absence day is unjust. [Para 48][Para 52][Para 59]
**Employer (respondent):** it has no capacity to raise the payments and already exceeds the legal minimum; it has no plan to close and will pay statutory severance if it ever does; the bonus exists to reward punctuality. [Para 49][Para 53][Para 60]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — on issue 3 the employer must reduce the attendance bonus in proportion to days of authorised absence; issues 1 and 2 were dismissed. [Para 156][Para 157][Para 158]

**Bonus and allowance (issue 1).** Notification No. 230 K.B/S.J.N of 25 July 2012 requires at least 7 dollars of travel and accommodation allowance and at least 10 dollars of attendance bonus a month. [Para 66][Para 69] The employer pays 9 and 10 dollars respectively, which is better than the floor, so nothing was owed. [Para 68][Para 72] Any sum above that floor is a claim to a benefit exceeding the law, hence an interest dispute; as the claimant was not the most representative union the panel declined to consider it. [Para 74][Para 86]

**Terminating the contracts (issue 2).** Only the parties to a contract may create, vary or extinguish its obligations — Article 65(1) of the Labour Law and Articles 311 and 407 to 414 of the Civil Code. [Para 93][Para 95] The workers showed no serious breach by the employer, which is the only route to unilateral termination, and the employer had no wish to terminate. [Para 105][Para 109] The claim had no legal basis and was refused. [Para 110][Para 113]

**Pro-rating the bonus (issue 3).** The attendance bonus is a sum the employer pays as a reward, and so is wages under Article 103. [Para 148][Para 149] An absence the employer has authorised suspends the contract under Article 71(6), and Article 72(1) removes the wage obligation for suspended days. [Para 150][Para 151] The employer may therefore deduct in proportion to the days absent, but has no right to cancel the whole bonus. [Para 155] The panel also held that the words "without absence" in Notification No. 230 have never been interpreted by the competent authority, so each case falls to be decided on its facts. [Para 126][Para 130]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus payable under Notification No. 230 is wages; where the employer has itself authorised an absence, that day suspends the contract, so the employer may reduce the bonus pro rata to the days absent but may not forfeit it altogether. [Para 149][Para 155]

**Obiter dicta:** that a demand for a bonus or allowance above the statutory floor is an interest claim which only the most representative union may bring [Para 74][Para 86]; and that workers cannot require an employer to terminate their own contracts so as to trigger severance [Para 112].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award's face names a union federation and plant union as the worker party, yet the reasoning recasts the two appearing workers as elected workers' delegates and the claimants accordingly [Para 61][Para 65]. The panel is said to have been constituted on "11 April 2019 2016" [Para 15], and Prakas No. 099 is dated "2019 2004" [Para 80]. The reasoning on issue 1 repeats its own opening sentence and prints the allowance figures inconsistently between paragraphs [Para 66][Para 67]. Individual workers appear under initials by convention; the source prints their full names.
