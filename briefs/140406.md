# Award No. 81/09-វនរេច — ក្រុមហ៊ុន វន រេច (ខេមបូឌា) ខូច រើល ធី ឌី (Arbitration Council, 31 July 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៉ឹង សុទ្ធី for the employer, ហូន ជុន ឌី for the workers, chair ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា) · Doc ID 140406

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន វន រេច (ខេមបូឌា) ខូច រើល ធី ឌី ("Van Reach (Cambodia) Corp") employs about 3,112 workers at Prey Pring Khang Cheung, Chom Chao, Dangkao district, Phnom Penh. [Para 43] The claimant is សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ (the Khmer Youth Union Federation), whose local union notified the company of its formation on 18 June 2009. [Para 34][Para 160] Workers struck from 22 June 2009 over 22 demands, later narrowed to 12; of those, Issues 4, 6, 7, 8 and 10 were settled at the hearing and Issues 2, 5 and 9 were withdrawn, leaving Issues 1, 3, 11 and 12. [Para 21][Para 25][Para 45][Para 46]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must dismiss the 20 named workers whom other workers accuse of threatening and intimidating them? [Para 56][Para 108]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the company must provide an adequate nursery and breastfeeding room? [Para 73][Para 122]
3. (Issue 11) Whether the company must notify the piece rate for each production series so that workers know it at least one week after doing the work? [Para 81][Para 138]
4. (Issue 12) Whether the company must stop discriminating against the union in assigning and allocating work? [Para 94][Para 155]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the members of សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ argued that the 20 members of a rival union roamed the factory intimidating workers and blocking any protest, that the factory has a nursery and breastfeeding room only on paper, that piece rates are disclosed only on the 9th of the month when wages are paid and are even cut from 7 to 5 cents without notice, and that two union leaders were moved to other jobs after the union was notified. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 82][Para 90][Para 94]
**Employer (respondent):** Van Reach said it cannot dismiss the 20 without evidence of any offence, that it had agreed with another union to pay USD 7 a month instead of building a nursery, that issuing rates in advance would let workers refuse difficult series, and that the transfers followed production needs, kept wages, shifts and conditions unchanged, and were also applied to two other workers. [Para 70][Para 77][Para 84][Para 104][Para 106]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel rejected the demand to dismiss the 20 workers, ordered the company to provide a breastfeeding room and a nursery inside the factory (with outside daycare paid on actual invoices where no nursery exists for children over 18 months), ordered it to notify the piece rate for each series after workers have worked 7 days, and ordered it to stop transferring workers because of union membership or activity. [Para 173][Para 174][Para 175][Para 177][Para 178]

On Issue 1, Article 65 of the Labour Law makes the employment contract binding between the worker and the employer alone, and Article 1 of the 1988 law on contracts confines the power to extinguish a contract to its parties; a third party cannot demand dismissal. [Para 110][Para 112][Para 113][Para 114] The panel added that it may order an employer to remove a worker only where that worker endangers the health and safety of others, following Awards 14/03 and 32/04, and here the workers produced no evidence of who was threatened, where or when. [Para 116][Para 120][Para 121]

On Issue 3, Article 186 requires an enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a breastfeeding room and a nursery, and permits outside daycare at the employer's cost where no nursery can be provided for children over 18 months. [Para 124][Para 125][Para 127] The panel held that paying USD 7 a month does not satisfy the purpose of the law, which is to keep mother and child close, so the agreement with the other union did not discharge the duty. [Para 134][Para 135][Para 136]

On Issue 11, Article 112 requires the employer to inform workers clearly of wage conditions before they take up the post and at every change; since piece workers' earnings vary with each series, the employer must notify the rate per series, and while Article 112 fixes no number of days, the usual period in earlier awards is 3 to 7 days. [Para 139][Para 141][Para 144][Para 152] Because the company disclosed only on the 9th and offered no other justification, the panel ordered notification within 7 days. [Para 149][Para 151][Para 154]

On Issue 12, Articles 12 and 279 forbid an employer to use union membership or activity in deciding assignments. [Para 155][Para 156] The transfer of the group leader kept the same skill and pay and was not shown to be retaliatory, but the transfer of the mobile worker to garden maintenance, made after he attended the first hearing and to a wholly different role, was an improper use of managerial power. [Para 167][Para 168][Para 171][Para 172]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union cannot obtain the dismissal of rival workers, since only the parties to a contract may end it and removal is ordered only where a worker endangers others' health and safety; paying an allowance instead of building the nursery and breastfeeding room required by Article 186 does not discharge that duty; and an employer that discloses piece rates only on payday, or moves a union activist to an unrelated job after the union is notified, breaches Articles 112, 12 and 279. [Para 114][Para 116][Para 134][Para 154][Para 172]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the two transferred workers had accepted the changes because they feared losing their jobs. [Para 98]
