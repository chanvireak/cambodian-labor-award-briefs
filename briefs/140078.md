# Award No. 122/11 — Dey Wall Bes (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 21 October 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom, employer-appointed; Tuon Si Phan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 140078

## 1. Facts
The Solidarity Union Federation filed 11 claims to improve working conditions at the Dey Wall Bes (Cambodia) garment factory; conciliation settled five of the six unresolved points at the first hearing [Para 24][Para 25][Para 27]. The parties attended the first hearing on 3 October 2011, but the worker party did not appear at the second hearing on 20 October 2011 and gave no reason [Para 28][Para 30][Para 88]. The employer attended and reported that the two union leaders had resigned and that Ms D, whom it had agreed to reinstate, had not returned to work [Para 89][Para 90].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must remove the section 05 team chief, Mr S, from the company? [Para 3]
2. Whether the employer must reinstate Ms D and pay her wages and regular bonuses until she recovers and returns to work? [Para 5]
3. Whether the employer must refrain from transferring the workplaces of union leaders? [Para 7]
4. Whether the employer may deduct the incentive bonus of union leaders or union members without reason? [Para 9]
5. Whether a worker taking one day's leave in a month may have three days' bonus deducted? [Para 11][Para 14]
6. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr C, a union activist, into work? [Para 16]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer must remove the section 05 team chief, reinstate Ms D after her workplace accident and keep her wages until recovery, stop transferring union leaders, stop deducting union members' incentive bonuses, stop triple deductions for a day's leave, and reinstate Mr C, who was dismissed because of anti-union discrimination [Para 3][Para 5][Para 7][Para 9][Para 16].
**Employer (respondent):** it cannot remove the team chief; Ms D resigned voluntarily; transfers are its managerial right; bonuses go to diligent workers; the old company rules on leave deductions stand; and Mr C's fixed-term contract simply ended with all legal payments made [Para 4][Para 6][Para 8][Para 10][Para 13][Para 17].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel closed case No. 122/11 and issued no order on the merits [Para 108][Para 109].

The panel treated the case as one of abandonment. Under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council, where a party has been duly summoned and fails to appear without a valid reason, the panel may continue in that party's absence or close the proceedings by issuing an award [Para 76]. Rule 4.7 of the Council's procedural rules is to the same effect [Para 77].

The panel applied the three conditions it had previously derived from Article 21 in Awards 16/07 and 27/07: the party was properly notified; the party did not appear; and the party gave no valid reason [Para 79]. On the first, the secretariat issued hearing notices No. 694 and 695 of 21 September 2011 and No. 749 and 750 of 18 October 2011, and the worker party received them [Para 69][Para 70][Para 79][Para 81]. On the second, the worker party was absent from the second hearing, while the employer attended [Para 88]. On the third, the worker party gave no valid reason, stating only that it could not attend and asking the panel to issue an award under the procedure [Para 96][Para 97].

Finding all three conditions met, the panel held that the claimant had chosen not to participate in the process and had not provided the reasons and evidence needed to support its claims [Para 99][Para 102][Para 104][Para 105]. It treated this as abandonment of the claim and closed the case [Para 106][Para 108]. The panel also observed that it had given the claimant a full opportunity to defend its claims under the Labour Law procedure [Para 100].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a claimant party has been duly summoned to a hearing, fails to appear without a valid reason, and supplies no reasons or evidence, the panel may treat the claim as abandoned and close the case under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099, without adjudicating the merits; the three-condition test is notification, non-appearance, and absence of valid excuse [Para 76][Para 78][Para 79].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that it had afforded the claimant every opportunity to present its case, and that non-attendance causes a party to lose the chance to give reasons and evidence in support of its claims [Para 100][Para 105].
