# Award No. 205/15 — Agile Sweater (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 4 September 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Tun Si Phan for the worker party, Taing Try chair) · Doc ID 139268

## 1. Facts

Agile Sweater (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a garment-weaving factory at Bory Kamkar village, Chbar Mon, Kampong Speu, employing about 887 workers. [Para 1][Para 59][Para 60] In June and July 2015 workers formed the Democratic Union of Garment Workers of Cambodia (CCAWDU) and notified the company on 8 July 2015 of eight candidates for its leadership election, set for 26 July 2015; the notice was receipted on 9 July 2015 and stamped in at the provincial labour department. [Para 153][Para 158][Para 159][Para 160] On 15 and 16 July 2015 the company dismissed nine workers — seven of the eight candidates and two ordinary members — each with a letter giving reasons such as inciting a strike, shutting off machines, or negligence. [Para 2][Para 151][Para 152][Para 177] The union sought reinstatement and back pay for all nine. [Para 62][Para 161]

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. Whether the seven dismissed candidates enjoyed protection from dismissal as candidates in a union leadership election? [Para 163][Para 172]
2. Whether their dismissal was carried out lawfully under Article 293 of the Labour Law, and whether they must therefore be reinstated with back pay? [Para 197][Para 205]
3. Whether the employer must also take back the two dismissed union members who were not candidates, and pay them back wages? [Para 219][Para 220][Para 237]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** the nine were dismissed without valid reason and had committed no fault; the seven were candidates in the election and the two members were their relatives, so the timing shows the dismissals were tied to the formation of the union. [Para 63][Para 136][Para 149][Para 222][Para 234]

**Employer (respondent):** the dismissals had nothing to do with union membership; each rested on grounds written in the dismissal letter, ranging from shutting off power and inciting a strike to negligence, absenteeism and insubordination, and some amounted to serious misconduct. [Para 64][Para 66][Para 73][Para 133][Para 145] It denied receiving the candidacy notice and denied that any security guard of the name on the receipt worked at the factory. [Para 154][Para 157]

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to take back the seven candidate workers and pay them back wages from the date of dismissal to the end of each worker's last fixed-duration contract, and dismissed the claim for the two members. [Para 208][Para 238][Para 239]

On protection, Article 293(1) requires the Labour Inspector's authorisation before a staff delegate or candidate is dismissed, and Article 3(3) of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 extends the same protection to every worker who is a candidate in a union leadership election, for 45 days before the poll and 45 days after it if not elected, provided the union has notified the employer of the candidacy by any practicable means. [Para 163][Para 166][Para 167][Para 168] The panel applied the three conditions it had used in earlier awards: the worker must be of the protected category, the dismissal must fall inside the protection window, and the union must have notified the employer. [Para 170][Para 171]

All three were met. The seven were candidates on the 8 July notice. [Para 172][Para 173] They were dismissed on 15 and 16 July 2015, eleven and twelve days before the 26 July election, and so inside the 45-day window. [Para 176][Para 177][Para 178] On notification, the panel found a signed and stamped receipt dated 9 July 2015 bearing the name and telephone number of a company security guard, and held that where the employer merely denies the guard's existence without producing any evidence — though it was in a position to obtain records from the security firm it engaged — the employer has not discharged its burden and the union's notification stands. [Para 182][Para 188][Para 190][Para 191][Para 194]

Because no application for the Labour Inspector's authorisation was ever shown, the panel found the dismissal of the seven contrary to Article 293. [Para 197][Para 198][Para 199] Under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 it ordered reinstatement and back pay to the end of the last fixed-duration contract of each. [Para 206][Para 207][Para 208]

For the two members the claim failed. Their contracts were fixed-duration and were ended before expiry, which Article 73(1) and (3) permits on the employer's own motion subject to damages. [Para 209][Para 210][Para 214][Para 219] The panel noted that the claim sought only reinstatement and back pay, not those damages, so it did not have to characterise the termination. [Para 220][Para 221] On discrimination, the workers bore the burden of proof and produced nothing showing the two were CCAWDU members or that their dismissals were union-related; neither name appeared on the candidacy notice. [Para 224][Para 233][Para 235][Para 236]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

A candidate in a union leadership election is protected exactly as a staff delegate: from 45 days before the poll until 45 days after it, the employer may not dismiss the candidate without the Labour Inspector's prior authorisation, and a dismissal inside that window without authorisation is unlawful whatever misconduct is alleged, entitling the candidate to reinstatement and back wages. Notification of the candidacy is effective when the union delivers a receipted and labour-department-stamped notice to the enterprise by any practicable means; an employer that merely denies receipt, while able to obtain records proving or disproving it, has not discharged its evidential burden. Membership alone, unaccompanied by proof of notification or of anti-union motive, attracts no such protection. [Para 167][Para 170][Para 194][Para 199][Para 236]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that it did not need to decide whether the dismissals rested on agreement, serious misconduct or otherwise, since only reinstatement and back pay were claimed, is an observation on the scope of the claim and not a finding on the merits of the alleged misconduct. [Para 220][Para 221]

## 6. Source defects disclosed

- The conciliation report carries number 2107/15 at [Para 6] and 6107/15 at [Para 12], both dated 28 July 2015 and sent on 30 July 2015.
- The dismissed seven are described as "3 on 15 July and 4 on 16 July" at [Para 177] but as dismissed on "២៤ ១៥ និង ទី ១៦ ខែកក្កដា" at [Para 198], an extra numeral intruding into the date.
- The candidacy notice lists eight candidates [Para 158] while seven candidates were among the dismissed [Para 151]; the eighth candidate was not dismissed, and the union's demand names nine workers with variant spellings of several [Para 2][Para 62].
- Individual names are printed inconsistently — "អូន ផានិត", "អ៊ូន ផានិត" and "អួន ផានិត"; "ស្រ៊ូ ជា" and "ស៊ូ ជា"; "ឃីម ថន" and "ឃឹម ថន", the last appearing as "ដើម ថន" in the operative order [Para 2][Para 238]; names are therefore redacted here.
- The Prakas under which protection is claimed is dated 22 November 2001 at [Para 164] but "១២ ខែវិច្ឆិកា ឆ្នាំ ២០០១" at [Para 165].

- The signature block is illegible ("mont", "uponson the Mastance", "20170") and no signature date survives [Para 241]; the award date is taken from the header only [Para 1].
- The company name appears in at least six transliterations, and [Para 208] writes "សង រំលឹក ប្រាក់ ឈ្នួល" for back wages while [Para 238] prints "រំលឹក ប្រាក់ ឈ្នួល" in the same order.
