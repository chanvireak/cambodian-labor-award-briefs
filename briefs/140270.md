# Award No. 47/10 — Maru Chun (Cambodia) Shoe Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 10 June 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (In Sothy, An Non, Pen Bun Chhea presiding) · Doc ID 140270

## 1. Facts
Maru Chun (Cambodia) Shoe Co., Ltd began operating in March 2010 and employed about 1,200 workers at Trapeang Rumchek, Dangkao, Phnom Penh; two unions exist at the factory, and the Cambodian Union Federation for Workers' Rights is the claimant [Para 28][Para 29][Para 30]. On 18 May 2010 that federation filed nine demands, of which only one remained unresolved at conciliation: reinstatement of three men — two electricians and a machine mechanic — whom the company had taken on 1 March 2010 on probation contracts of one to three months and dismissed on 5 and 6 May 2010 [Para 9][Para 10][Para 36][Para 39]. The workers said the three had already served five months, and the union had named them as its factory leadership; the company said they had simply failed probation [Para 2][Para 3][Para 40].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must take back the three dismissed men, given that their probation contracts ran one to three months and they had worked since 1 March 2010? [Para 35][Para 51]

Within that single issue the panel examined three sub-questions: what type of contract the three held after the probation period expired; whether, as union leaders, they enjoyed protection against dismissal; and whether the dismissals were in truth union discrimination [Para 51][Para 63][Para 75].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the three were union leaders and were dismissed because of their union role, which is discrimination; they had each worked from 1 March 2010, more than two months, so they were no longer probationers and could not be dismissed as such [Para 35][Para 47][Para 49].
**Employer (respondent):** the three were still within probation and were let go because their work was poor — a 4 May 2010 management evaluation found none of them had passed probation; two or three other probationers who were not union members were dismissed at the same time, and the union only told the company of its leadership line-up at 4:00 pm on 5 May, after two of the dismissals had already been carried out [Para 40][Para 41][Para 45][Para 81].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to order the company to reinstate the three men; it noted in passing that they would have a claim for damages under Article 73 of the Labour Law, but recorded that no such damages were claimed [Para 95][Para 96][Para 94].

On contract type, the panel applied Article 68 and Article 52 of Prakas practice: probation may not exceed three months generally, two months for specialised workers and one month for ordinary workers [Para 52][Para 53]. Following Award No. 62/06 (Quick Chew), it treated electricians and machine repairers as specialised workers, so the ceiling for these three was two months [Para 54][Para 57]. Since they began on 1 March 2010 and were dismissed on 5 and 6 May 2010, they had worked beyond two months and were therefore on fixed-term contracts under Article 67, not probation [Para 60][Para 61][Para 62][Para 58].

On protection, the panel applied Prakas No. 305 (22 November 2001), Articles 3(2), 3(3) and 4: only leaders chosen by secret ballot, and founders and members of a union whose registration application is pending, get shop-steward protection, for 45 days before and after the ballot, covering at most three leaders [Para 64][Para 65][Para 71][Para 72]. It found these three had been appointed, not elected, and that the union had never applied to register, so they fell outside the protected class [Para 73][Para 74].

On discrimination, Articles 12 and 279 forbid an employer from using union membership or activity in hiring and job allocation, but the workers bear the burden of proving it (Awards 56/09 and 61/09) [Para 76][Para 77]. The panel weighed the sequence: the evaluation of 4 May preceded the union's notification of 5 May at 4:00 pm; the two electricians were dismissed at 7:20 am on 5 May, before that notification; and two or three other probationers were dismissed with them [Para 80][Para 81][Para 84]. The workers produced nothing further, so discrimination was not established [Para 85][Para 86].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Work beyond the statutory probation ceiling converts the relationship into a fixed-term contract, so a later "failure of probation" is no answer; but protection for union leaders under Prakas No. 305 depends on election by secret ballot and on the union being registered or its application pending — appointees of an unregistered union are unprotected, and a dismissal carried out before the employer was told of the leadership, alongside non-union probationers, does not establish anti-union discrimination [Para 72][Para 74][Para 86].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that dismissal before the end of a fixed-term contract outside serious misconduct entitles the worker to damages at least equal to wages to the contract's end (Article 73) is stated although no damages were sought [Para 91][Para 93][Para 94].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The three claimants' names are printed in several inconsistent forms across the award, with the family name variously rendered and one given name appearing with three different spellings, so this brief uses initials only [Para 35][Para 73][Para 79]. A stray Latin-script personal name has been inserted by the OCR in the middle of a sentence in the discrimination reasoning [Para 78]. The panel chair's name is spelled differently in the header and the signature block, and the employer-appointed arbitrator's name likewise [Para 1][Para 98]. The employer's name alternates between "ម៉ារូ ឈុន" and "មាំ រូ ឈុន" [Para 33]. Finally, the closing formula says the award became final on 28 May 2010 by the parties' agreement, though the award is dated 10 June 2010 [Para 97].
