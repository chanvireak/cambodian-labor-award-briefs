# Award No. 009/21 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូ ហ៊ែន សាំ អិលធីឌី (Arbitration Council, 15 December 2021)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៊ុក រី for the employer, ទួន ស៊ី ផាន់ for the workers, ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា presiding) · Doc ID 140844

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូ ហ៊ែន សាំ អិលធីឌី is a garment manufacturer employing 2,073 workers [Para 53]. On 18 March 2021 the workers' representatives filed seven demands; conciliation on 6 April 2021 settled four, and the three unresolved points reached the Council on 9 April 2021 under conciliation report No. 243 [Para 14][Para 15][Para 16][Para 17]. At the hearing of 16 November 2021 the parties settled two of the three — the demand to unlock the scissors and the demand to replace the hot and cold water dispensers in buildings A and B — by agreement, so the panel considered only the remaining third point [Para 25][Para 26]. This award is binding on the parties [Para 27][Para 89].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 3) Whether every change of public-holiday dates requires the agreement of each individual worker? [Para 56][Para 74]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Any shift of a holiday to another day must follow a ballot of each worker, and in the past the employer changed holiday dates without consulting or discussing with workers at all; the practice is unlawful because the workers themselves did not agree, whatever the shop stewards or union leaders requested [Para 60][Para 61][Para 62][Para 63]. Holiday leave is the worker's own right to negotiate with the employer [Para 63].

**Employer (respondent):** Holiday swaps rest on a 15-year practice of meetings with the employee delegates and the in-house union, with no problem ever arising; changes originate in a request from four delegates or union leaders, the employer then convenes them and agrees, and where a minority disagrees the majority voice is applied [Para 66][Para 67][Para 68]. Polling every worker individually wastes time and blocks the production line [Para 71].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to obtain the agreement of each individual worker before changing public-holiday dates [Para 87][Para 88].

The panel applied Article 161 paragraph 1 of the Labour Law, which requires the ministry in charge of labour to fix by Prakas the paid public holidays for all enterprises, and Prakas No. 10 SKBY of 4 February 1999 [Para 74][Para 75]. It read Article 2 of that Prakas — an enterprise that cannot suspend operations on a holiday may arrange with its workers to work on those days — together with Article 3, which requires the work to be voluntary [Para 76][Para 77]. It followed Award No. 99/09 (King Lan), which held that although workers have a legal right to rest on holidays, an employer may seek their agreement to work, but that work must stand on the principle of voluntariness [Para 78][Para 79][Para 80]. On the facts, the panel accepted that the employer had convened the delegates and union leaders and decided by majority, but held this insufficient: delegates and union leaders must first obtain the agreement of the workers before putting a swap request to the employer, because holiday rest is the workers' right [Para 82][Para 84][Para 85]. It therefore found the employer's practice not yet lawful and ordered individual consent [Para 86][Para 87].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A change in public-holiday dates is not validly agreed by a majority vote of employee delegates or union leaders: because holiday rest belongs to each worker, the representative body must obtain the consent of the workers it represents before requesting the change, and the resulting work on a holiday must be voluntary under Article 3 of Prakas No. 10 SKBY [Para 77][Para 84][Para 86]. Delegates may act for workers only where the workers have already conferred that authority on them [Para 69].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that the employer may still arrange with workers to work on a holiday where the enterprise cannot suspend operations, and that changes have in practice concerned the Khmer New Year and Pchum Ben holidays [Para 76][Para 72]. It also recorded that the factory has five unions, one with most-representative status, and 21 employee delegates [Para 59].
