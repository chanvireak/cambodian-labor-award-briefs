# Award No. 055/15 — Du Hust (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 19 March 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, chosen by the employer; Tun Si Phan, chosen by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, chair chosen by the two) · Doc ID 139154

## 1. Facts
Du Hust (Cambodia) (ឌូ ហឹស្ត (ខេមបូឌា)) cuts garments at Tuol Pongro village, Chom Chao, Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh, with about 2,250 workers. [Para 1][Para 67] On 13 January 2015 the Federation of Democratic Unions of Cambodian Garment Workers (សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា) filed complaint No. 007/15 on six working conditions; conciliation on 13 February 2015 left four points unsettled, and report No. 166 of 19 February 2015 reached the Secretariat the same day. [Para 17][Para 18][Para 19] At the hearing of 26 February 2015 one further point was settled and one withdrawn, leaving two. [Para 16][Para 23][Para 24][Para 25] The disputed act is the dismissal on 17 July 2014 of Mr It Si Tha, a machine repairer hired on 16 December 2013 on a one-year fixed-duration contract expiring 15 December 2014, dismissed without prior notice for being drunk at work. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 133]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a weekly meal allowance to workers working overtime? [Para 2] (settled in conciliation at the hearing [Para 23])
2. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr It Si Tha and pay him back wages from the date of dismissal until the date he resumes work? [Para 130][Para 131]
3. Whether the employer must stop requiring workers to seek written permission to leave the factory during the 11:30–12:15 midday break? [Para 8] (settled in conciliation at the hearing [Para 23])
4. Whether the employer must add USD 28 to the existing base wage of USD 110, making USD 138 a month, from January 2015? [Para 106][Para 147]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The dismissal was unlawful: Article 83(b) of the Labour Law does not list drinking or being drunk on company premises as serious misconduct, the employer gave no prior notice as Article 73 requires, no severance was paid, and Mr It Si Tha is a union activist protected like a staff delegate. [Para 81][Para 93][Para 94][Para 95][Para 103] On wages, the USD 110 base was lawful, so USD 28 must be added to reach USD 138, as other companies do. [Para 107][Para 109]
**Employer (respondent):** The dismissal rested on serious misconduct under Article 37(a)(5) of the internal rules; three worker witnesses signed an acknowledgment that he came to work drunk, lost self-control and shouted, and 5% severance and final wages were already paid. [Para 82][Para 90][Para 92] On wages, Prakas No. 283 of 12 November 2014 sets only a USD 128 minimum, while the company already pays USD 133 base plus USD 16 attendance and USD 14 travel allowance. [Para 111][Para 112][Para 113] It also challenged the transfer-of-rights fingerprints as forged and said 35 signatories had already left. [Para 74][Para 75]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to order reinstatement or back wages, and refused to consider the USD 28 wage demand at all; no sum was awarded. [Para 146][Para 170][Para 171][Para 172]

On representation, the panel applied Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 SAK BY of 21 April 2004, under which a party may appear through a person holding written authorisation. [Para 114] The federation held 93 thumb-printed transfers of rights; the employer alleged forgery and that 35 signatories had left, but produced neither names nor resignation or dismissal letters, and no evidence of confusion or thumb-printing by copying, so the panel found the federation entitled to represent the 93, adding the two members who attended to reach 95 claimants. [Para 115][Para 124][Para 125][Para 126][Para 127][Para 129]

On issue 2, Article 73(b)2 of the Labour Law allows a fixed-duration contract to be terminated before expiry only for serious misconduct or force majeure. [Para 131] Article 83 does not itself name drunkenness, but Article 83(b)(3) makes a serious breach of disciplinary, safety and hygiene rules serious misconduct. [Para 134][Para 135][Para 143] Internal rule 37(a)(5) lists drinking or being under the influence on company premises during or after working hours, and the panel held that rule consistent with the law. [Para 136][Para 144] Following award No. 107/04 Zaksintec, the employer bore the burden of proving the misconduct; it discharged that burden with three signed witness statements, unopposed by the workers, plus the security chief's observation, and the panel therefore believed that Mr It Si Tha was drunk at work on 17 July 2014. [Para 137][Para 138][Para 139][Para 140] The dismissal was accordingly lawful and no prior notice was owed. [Para 145][Para 146]

On issue 4, Prakas No. 283 fixes the 2015 minimum at USD 128 a month from 1 January 2015. [Para 147][Para 148][Para 152] The employer was paying USD 133, more favourable than the Prakas, and the panel found no statute, collective agreement, past practice or internal rule obliging a further USD 28. [Para 153][Para 154][Para 155] It classified the demand as a dispute over interests, not rights. [Para 156] Because Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 makes an interest award a substitute for the collective agreement, binding all workers and extinguishing their right to strike on that subject, only the most representative union may bring such a claim. [Para 163][Para 164][Para 165] A claimant representing 95 of about 2,250 workers was not the most representative union, as the panel had held in awards 169/11 Gortune Thy, 02/11 Puyen and 66/11 Inhansung, so it declined to consider the demand. [Para 166][Para 167][Para 168][Para 169][Para 170]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Being drunk or under the influence on company premises during working hours is serious misconduct through Article 83(b)(3) of the Labour Law read with a valid internal rule, so a fixed-duration contract may be terminated before expiry under Article 73(b)2 without prior notice, provided the employer proves the fact — unopposed signed witness statements and an eyewitness will do — because the burden of proving serious misconduct lies on the employer. [Para 131][Para 137][Para 138][Para 140][Para 143][Para 144]

A claim to raise wages above the statutory minimum is a dispute over interests, and since an award on interests replaces the collective agreement and removes the right to strike, the panel will decline to consider it unless the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise; representing 95 of roughly 2,250 workers is not enough. [Para 156][Para 164][Para 165][Para 168]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observations that a machine repairer's drunkenness could endanger himself and stall the production line, and that Article 83 nowhere defines what being "under the influence" means, were not necessary to its finding, which rested on the signed statements. [Para 101][Para 142]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The wage demand is printed as USD 20 at [Para 10] but as USD 28 everywhere it is reasoned, and the total is given as USD 138 on a base of USD 110 [Para 106][Para 147][Para 170]; it is recorded here as printed in each place. The panel's own numbering calls the reinstatement claim ចំណុច វិវាទ ទី០២ [Para 130] but the operative order labels it ចំណុច វិវាទ ទី ១ [Para 171]. A demand to convert 20 fixed-duration contracts to undetermined-duration contracts appears at [Para 4] without a number and is never numbered, reasoned or ruled on. The worker-chosen arbitrator is printed as ទូន ស៊ី ផាន់ in the header [Para 1] and as "ម៉ៃខ ប៊ុន ឈាន" with an intruded numeral in the signature block [Para 173]. Paragraph 124 carries an intruded fragment ("ស្នាម លោក ហ៊ុន សែន") inside the reasoning. The company is spelled ឌូ ហឹស្ត, ឌូ ហឺស្ត, ឌូហឹស្ត and ឌូហិស្ត [Para 1][Para 61][Para 66][Para 105]. Paragraph 154 prints "១៣៣ ខែ ដុល្លារ" for the 133 dollar wage [Para 154], and paragraph 149 gives the probation minimum as "១២៣ (មួយ រយ ម្ភៃ ប៊ី)" [Para 149]. The union's own registration is said to be outstanding [Para 68] while the federation is held entitled to represent the workers [Para 127].
