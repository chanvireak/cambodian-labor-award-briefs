# Award No. 48/04 — Da Joo Cambodia (Arbitration Council, 14 July 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators: Mar Samborana (employer party), Tuon Siphann (worker party), Sok Mathoeung (chair) · Doc ID 139321

## 1. Facts
Da Joo Cambodia, National Road 4, Ang Snuol district, Kandal Province, employed 450 workers. On 11 June 2004 the Kandal Labour Inspector conciliated eight demands and six were settled; the two left over were a police profile contribution of 3,000 riels per worker, collected by the factory's administrator and passed to the police during profile-making in December 2002 and July 2003, and the cost of pre-employment medical examinations of 10,100 riels per worker. [Para 11][Para 12][Para 42][Para 55][Para 58]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay back 3,000 riels to each worker for the police profile fee? [Para 7][Para 19]
2. Whether the employer must reimburse workers for their medical examinations? [Para 7][Para 46]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** They gave thumbprints and paid without understanding the document, out of fear of the employer, and received neither receipt nor profile copy; the police receipt was signed by the factory administrator, so the factory is responsible, and at minimum should bear 100% of the 3,000 riels, or 50% (1,500 riels each) given the profile's common benefit. On medical examinations they claim the full 10,100 riels each, a total of $450. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 25][Para 28][Para 46]

**Employer (respondent):** The profiles are the personal responsibility of each worker and each employer, required by the Ministry of Interior for public security; the factory is not liable, did not deduct anything from wages, and merely relayed the police demand. It had already let workers attend examinations in working time without losing wages or bonuses and rented cars for them, but later agreed to repay 4,000 riels each. [Para 29][Para 34][Para 46][Para 48]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Partly granted — the Council rejected the workers' claim that the employer pay 3,000 riels to each worker; ordered the employer to stop collecting money from workers for third parties absent prior written agreement or a legal requirement; ordered the employer to pay 100,000 riels in compensation to the Khmer Youth Union in Da Joo factory by the end of July 2004; ordered the employer to contact the authorities and return the paid-for profile documents within 3 months; and, on the parties' agreement, to pay 4,000 riels back to each worker for medical examinations on 10 July 2004. [Para 87][Para 88]

On the profile fee the panel found no law or regulation allocating the contribution. Notification No. 993 of the Ministry of Interior (8 October 1997) requires the factory only to help officials make the profiles, and letter No. 17/03 (28 April 2003) fixes the fee at 3,000 riels per Cambodian worker and US$5 per foreign worker without saying who pays. Documenting profiles is a public service of a public authority, so in law neither side is clearly obliged. The decisive point was Article 114: the employer may not restrict a worker's freedom to use his wage. The factory had not deducted the money from payroll — workers received full salaries and then handed 3,000 riels over — but the panel held they would not have paid had they not feared the employer who was collecting it, so the collection amounted to an unlawful salary deduction and interference with that freedom. Collecting for a third party is therefore prohibited without prior written worker agreement or a legal duty, and facilitation means arranging communication, not handling money. [Para 65][Para 72][Para 76][Para 78][Para 82][Para 83][Para 85][Para 86]

On the medical examinations the panel noted that Article 247 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 09/99 place the examination on the employer, unlike the workbook entries under Articles 32 and 34 which are the worker's own obligation; it recorded the negotiated repayment of 4,000 riels per worker and the agreed payment date rather than fixing the full 10,100 riels claimed. [Para 68][Para 71][Para 88]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer who receives money from workers outside the payroll in order to pass it to a third party — here a police profile fee — effects a deduction from wages contrary to Article 114, because payment procured by the fear an employer's collection inspires is not consent; such collection is unlawful unless the workers agreed in advance in writing or the law obliges the employer to collect, and the employer's duty to facilitate a public authority's work does not extend to collecting its fees. [Para 79][Para 83][Para 85][Para 86]

**Obiter dicta:** That a profile is an identity document serving public security and the police's search for offenders throughout the province, not merely the factory's interest, and that where no statute allocates the fee neither party is clearly bound to pay it. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 78]
