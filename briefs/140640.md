# Award No. 92107 — Kin Tay Garment Co. Ltd (Arbitration Council, 27 September 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · ING SOTHY (employer-chosen), SIN KIM SEAN (worker-chosen), KONG PHALLACK (chair) · Doc ID 140640

## 1. Facts
Kin Tay Garment Co. Ltd, National Road No. 2, Sangkat Chak Angre, Khan Meanchey, Phnom Penh, employed approximately 1,000 workers [Para 1][Para 23]. The claimants were the Khmer Youth Trade Union (KYTU), the Cambodian Apparel Workers of Democratic Union (CAWDU), and workers belonging to neither union [Para 23]. A telephone complaint of 5 September 2007 produced a non-conciliation report No. 943 of 6 September 2007 covering four remaining demands: a higher seniority bonus, one-year fixed duration contracts, incorporation of a US$5 allowance into minimum wages, and return of old contracts and curriculum vitae [Para 12][Para 14][Para 24]. The hearing was held on 12 September 2007 and further conciliation failed [Para 12][Para 16].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must increase the seniority bonus by US$3 for workers with 5 to 10 years' service and by US$5 for those with more than 10 years' service? [Para 24]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must sign one-year fixed duration labour contracts instead of six-month contracts? [Para 33]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must incorporate the US$5 allowance into ordinary workers' minimum wages? [Para 41]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must return workers' old labour contracts and curriculum vitae? [Para 45]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimants, KYTU / CAWDU / non-members):** the seniority bonus should rise because the price of goods has increased and long service builds skill and exposure to occupational disease [Para 24]; six-month contracts are too short to claim annual leave and maternity leave goes unpaid [Para 33]; folding the US$5 allowance into the minimum wage would raise overtime, which is computed on that wage [Para 42][Para 44]; the old contracts are needed as evidence against future dismissal [Para 46].
**Employer (respondent, Kin Tay Garment Co. Ltd):** it would pay more only if the law required it and its notification already paid above the legal minimum [Para 28][Para 29]; the Labour Law fixes no length for fixed duration contracts, so six months stands [Para 34][Para 35]; the US$5 is an incentive, not wage [Para 41][Para 45]; the old contracts were lost through repeated changes of administrator, and it apologised and promised compliant new contracts [Para 47][Para 48].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issue 1 declined to consider, Issue 2 rejected, Issue 3 declined to consider; on Issue 4 the demand to return the lost contracts was rejected, but the company was ordered to write a letter of apology for losing them and the workers and company were ordered to sign new contracts per the 23 July 2007 agreement [Para 94][Para 95].
Issue 1 failed because Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 caps the seniority bonus at US$5 per month after four years, so the claim exceeded the law and was an interests dispute [Para 50][Para 51][Para 52]. The panel follows its practice of declining interests disputes where the claiming union lacks most representative status under Article 96 (2B) of the Labour Law and Article 9 (1) of Prakas 305; CAWDU had none [Para 55][Para 57][Para 58].
Issue 2 turned on Article 65 and Article 67: a contract is an agreement, Article 67 caps fixed duration contracts at two years but sets no minimum, and Decree No. 38 Article 22 makes the contract the law of the parties [Para 60][Para 67][Para 62]. Lacking evidence of start dates or of a legal bar, and noting Article 166 gives 1.5 days' leave per month only after one year, the panel rejected the demand [Para 69][Para 71][Para 72][Para 74].
Issue 3: Article 104 sets a guaranteed minimum wage, Notification No. 745 set US$45 probationary and US$50 thereafter, and the company's 25 January 2007 announcement already paid at least US$50 with US$52 for some [Para 74][Para 75][Para 76][Para 79]. The demand being above the law, it too was an interests dispute and was declined for want of most representative status [Para 82][Para 83][Para 86].
Issue 4: Article 67 (4) requires information, not a photocopy, and Article 65 (2) allows oral contracts [Para 87][Para 88]. Reading Decree No. 38 Article 2 as obliging honest cooperation, the panel held each party should keep a copy, but what was lost could not be restored; under Article 34 of Prakas 099/04 it ordered the apology and the new contracts [Para 91][Para 92][Para 93][Para 94].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a benefit beyond what a notification or the Labour Law already guarantees is an interests dispute, and the panel will decline to decide it where the union that brought the dispute does not hold most representative status in the enterprise [Para 52][Para 54][Para 55][Para 57]. Where a written contract has been lost and cannot be recovered, the panel will not order its return but will order an apology and the signing of replacement contracts on the terms already agreed between the employer and worker representatives [Para 93][Para 94].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that in principle each contracting party should keep a copy of a labour contract even though no article compels it, and that it would normally consider an interests dispute brought by a most representative union [Para 59][Para 92].

## 6. Source defects disclosed

The OCR drops initial letters throughout ("the" prints as "he", "they" as "hey", "employer" as "mployer"), which occasionally inverts meaning in the employer's submissions [Para 25][Para 52].
