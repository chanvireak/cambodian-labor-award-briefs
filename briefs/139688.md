# Award No. 73/13 — Yak Jin (Cambodia) Inc. (Arbitration Council, 3 May 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun for the employer, An Nan for the workers, Kong Phallach chair) · Doc ID 139688

## 1. Facts
Yak Jin (Cambodia) Inc. is a garment factory in Kambol, Phnom Penh, with about 2,800 workers; two C.CAWDU unions represent them, both registered, neither holding Most Representative Status. Of seventeen demands raised after a January 2013 strike and a settlement meeting of 21 January 2013, fourteen went un-conciliated; one was settled at the hearing and four withdrawn, leaving ten covering an administration officer's dismissal, tax withholding, bonuses, overtime currency, annual leave, the clinic, travel allowance, pregnancy cards and meal allowance. [Para 3][Para 42][Para 43][Para 83]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must dismiss the administration officer? [Para 86]
3. Whether the employer must pay the wage tax on workers' behalf? [Para 110]
4. Whether the employer must keep paying the 6,000 riel weekly bonus to workers who reach 58 hours Monday to Friday without working on holidays? [Para 116]
5. Whether the employer must pay incentive bonuses of US$15, US$10 and US$7 a month by A, B and C grade to the mobile inspection section? [Para 126]
7. Whether the employer must pay overtime wages in US dollars rather than riel? [Para 132]
8. Whether the employer must pay in lieu of unused annual leave, calculated on average wages and benefits, during the contract, and move the clinic to the ground floor? [Para 137]
9. Whether the employer must pay office staff the US$10 monthly attendance bonus, and the weekly bonus, as it does for production workers? [Para 147]
11. Whether the employer must pay 1,300 riel a day travel allowance for Sunday overtime? [Para 155]
12. Whether the employer must issue pregnancy cards and allow 15 minutes' early departure on a doctor's certificate? [Para 167]
13. Whether the employer must add 2,000 riel to the overtime meal allowance for work from 6:00 to 8:30 p.m.? [Para 175]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The administration officer obstructed them — delaying the complaint about Compliance ordering lunch boxes thrown on the ground, ordering factory doors shut on 16–17 January 2013 to stop protests and force overtime, and refusing to let workers on authorised leave collect January wages on 9 February 2013; office staff work as regularly as production workers and should get the same bonuses; pay in riel loses value against the dollar. [Para 89][Para 94][Para 96][Para 149]
**Employer (respondent):** All instructions came from the director, not the officer, who merely had a heavy workload; office staff were told at interview they would work ten hours a day for a monthly salary, so the bonuses do not apply; the travel allowance was replaced by raising the monthly allowance from US$7 to US$8; overtime in riel loses nothing. [Para 99][Para 316][Para 344][Para 262]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must stop paying in lieu of unused annual leave during the contract, and must pay office staff the US$10 monthly attendance bonus; all other claims were dismissed or declined. Rights-dispute orders bind under the MoU of 3 October 2012; interests-dispute orders become final eight days after notification absent written opposition. [Para 372][Para 374][Para 376][Para 383]

**Issue 1 — dismissal of the officer.** Classified a rights dispute and decided under Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 311 of the Civil Code: only the parties to a contract may create, vary or extinguish its obligations, so a third party cannot terminate the employment contract between the employer and its administrative employee; following 116/10-Whitex, workers may demand a dismissal only on evidence that the person is dangerous and cannot be kept without endangering others. The complaints described the officer's role, and each act alleged — the delayed response, the door closures, the wage payment — was ordered by the director; no specific danger was shown. Dismissed. [Para 190][Para 193][Para 195][Para 200][Para 211][Para 212]

**Issue 3 — wage tax.** No provision of the Labour Law, any collective agreement or any agreement obliges the employer to pay the tax on wages for the worker, so this was an interests dispute; because the unions hold no MRS, the panel declined to consider it, following 81/04-Evergreen, 98/04-Great Union, 02/11-Pou Yuen, 66/11-In Han Sung and 169/11-Fortune Teo. [Para 213][Para 218][Para 222][Para 224]

**Issue 4 — weekly bonus.** The workers asserted a changed practice over the last four months, the employer that it was unchanged and that 58 hours includes holiday work; the assertions were contradictory, the 2011 collective agreement (clause 7, point 6: 6,000 riel for 58 hours, 4,000 for 48) no longer in force, and no proof of change was offered. Following 63/04-Sun Well, 99/06-South Bay and 33/07 and 51/07-Gold Fame, a claim unsupported by evidence fails; the demand was an interests dispute and was declined. [Para 231][Para 233][Para 237][Para 240]

**Issues 5, 12, 13 — incentives, pregnancy cards, meal allowance.** Each rested on no statute, collective agreement or agreement, so each was an interests dispute, declined for want of MRS. [Para 245][Para 361][Para 367]

**Issue 7 — dollars or riel.** A rights dispute under Article 2, resolved as an exercise of managerial prerogative: the employer may pay overtime weekly in riel, following 62/06-Clique Studio, 108/06-Trung Gal Komara, 33/07-Gold Fame and 119/09-SL, provided the exercise is lawful and reasonable; no evidence showed riel payment reduced what workers received. Dismissed. [Para 250][Para 256][Para 259][Para 265]

**Issue 8 — annual leave.** Article 168 requires payment before leave of at least the average wage, bonus and benefits over the preceding twelve months; Article 167(3) voids any agreement to pay in lieu of, or waive, annual leave, and the panel held the law forbids cashing out unused leave while the contract continues, following 94/04-ETN and 45/05-BNN. It ordered the employer to stop that practice and dismissed the claim for an average-wage calculation. The clinic claim was a rights dispute on managerial prerogative; with no evidence of serious risk beyond carrying a fainting worker upstairs, the arrangement was lawful and reasonable. [Para 272][Para 284][Para 292][Para 305][Para 306][Para 308]

**Issue 9 — office staff bonuses.** A rights dispute under Point 2 of Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012. Article 3 defines a worker as anyone contracting to work for wages under another's direction, so office staff are workers in the garment sector and entitled to at least US$10 a month for regular attendance without absence; granted at US$10. The weekly bonus rested on no instrument, was an interests dispute and was declined. [Para 310][Para 322][Para 326][Para 328][Para 334]

**Issue 11 — Sunday travel allowance.** A rights dispute on managerial prerogative; the employer abolished the 1,300 riel daily allowance by agreement with worker representatives on 3–4 January 2013 and raised the standing monthly allowance from US$7 to US$8, better than the law, following 24/05-Well Well. Dismissed. [Para 344][Para 350][Para 353][Para 355]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Workers cannot compel an employer to dismiss another employee, because only the parties to that employment contract may terminate it and a third party must show the person is dangerous; unused annual leave cannot be cashed out while the contract continues, since Article 167(3) voids any such agreement; and office staff are "workers" under Article 3, so the US$10 attendance bonus in Notification No. 230 extends to them. [Para 195][Para 284][Para 326]

**Obiter dicta:** The remarks that paying overtime in riel, keeping the clinic upstairs, and replacing a daily travel allowance with a higher monthly one were lawful and reasonable rest on Article 2 rather than any instrument, and went beyond the orders. [Para 259][Para 307][Para 353]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Khmer original with heavy OCR corruption: duplicated and broken clauses (paras 185–186, 225), stray Latin fragments ("John", "AND", "Shan", "N") and garbled headings. The operative order gives the office bonus as US$10 while the reasoning at para 328 prints "១០០ ដុល្លារ" (100 dollars); the order and the notification are followed here. Issue 8 is listed as withdrawn at para 42 yet appears among the remaining issues at para 43 and is decided on the merits, and para 42's parenthetical for it is unreadable. The panel's formation date prints as "ឆ្នាំ ២០២៣ ២០១៣". Workers, union officers and the officer complained of are given bare initials. [Para 39][Para 42][Para 43][Para 328][Para 374]
