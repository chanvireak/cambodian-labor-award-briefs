# Award No. 144/15 — Cambodian Huy Fu Garment & Nithing Factory Kompeny Ltd (Arbitration Council, 14 July 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · Doc ID 139219

## 1. Facts
The respondent operates a garment factory registered under Inv. 635 E/1999 of 24 June 1999 and employing about 270 workers. [Para 44][Para 45] The Huy Fu factory workers' union, registered under certificate No. 217 of 10 October 2001, filed seven demands; conciliation on 2 June 2015 failed, and report No. 594 of 11 June 2015 reached the Council on 15 June 2015. [Para 46][Para 19][Para 24] At the hearing of 26 June 2015 the parties settled issues 3 and 6 and the workers withdrew issues 2, 4 and 7, leaving issue 1 (a 28 USD monthly wage increase) and issue 5 (3,000 riel daily meal money). [Para 21][Para 28][Para 29][Para 30]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must add 28 USD per month to the base wage of every daily-paid worker? [Para 2]
2. Whether the employer must pay the wage and the annual seniority indemnity to one worker (Mr M, badge PK7716) and must not replace him with a new worker? [Para 4]
3. Whether the employer must pay 30 USD per month as milk money, in place of building a nursery (ទារកដ្ឋាន), from a child's birth until the age of 18 months? [Para 6]
4. Whether the employer must add 3,000 riel to the overtime payment already made, for a total of 7,000 riel? [Para 8]
5. Whether the employer must pay 3,000 riel per day as midday meal money (ប្រាក់ថ្លៃបាយថ្ងៃត្រង់)? [Para 11]
6. Whether the employer must not deduct the regular attendance bonus (លុយព្រីម) when it suspends work for lack of orders? [Para 13]
7. Whether the employer must stop transferring workers arbitrarily to posts where they have no skill? [Para 15]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the daily-paid workers are long-serving and skilled, other factories have already raised the base wage by 28 USD per month, and the employer pays no midday meal money at all, whereas other companies do; on the meal money the union said it did not insist on exactly 3,000 riel and the employer could give something to ease the cost of food. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 57][Para 58]
**Employer (respondent):** it cannot meet the demands because it has no capacity, has few orders, and asks that the law simply be applied; daily-paid workers already receive between 4.92 and 6 USD a day, with a base wage of not less than 128 USD a month, plus seniority bonus, transport and accommodation allowances and the attendance bonus. [Para 3][Para 52][Para 55][Para 59][Para 60]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider both remaining claims: the demand for a 28 USD monthly increase and the demand for 3,000 riel daily meal money. Issues 3 and 6 were settled and issues 2, 4 and 7 withdrawn, so no order was made on them. [Para 84][Para 91][Para 92][Para 93]

On issue 1 the panel first applied Prakas No. 283 K.B/Pr.K of 12 November 2014: the minimum wage for the garment, textile and footwear sector for 2015 was fixed at 128 USD per month for fully qualified workers and 123 USD during probation, applicable from 1 January 2015. Both parties accepted that the base wage of the daily-paid workers was at least 128 USD, and the panel found the employer already compliant and that no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, employment contract, agreement between the parties or past practice obliged it to add a further 28 USD. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70] It characterised the demand as a claim to a benefit above what the law provides, and therefore as an interest dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍) rather than a rights dispute. [Para 71][Para 72]

That characterisation decided both issues, because the panel then turned to standing. Under Article 96 of the Labour Law a collective agreement is made between an employer or employers' organisation and one or more trade unions representing the workers, and under Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 the most representative union has the right to request that the employer conclude one. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 75] Under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 an award resolving an interest dispute stands in place of a collective agreement, applies for one year, and causes all workers to lose the right to strike on the interest claims concerned; the panel reasoned that it may therefore resolve such a dispute only where the union bringing it is the most representative in the enterprise or the unions together have more than half the workforce as members. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 80] It followed earlier awards to the same effect — No. 81/04 Evergreen, No. 98/04 Great Union, No. 169/11 Fortune Tea (with No. 02/11 Pou Yuen and No. 66/11 Inhansung), which had declined to hear interest disputes brought by a union lacking that status. [Para 80][Para 81]

On the facts the claimant was not the most representative union: the workers themselves said its representative status had expired, and the ministry's letter No. 961 of 23 August 2011 recognised only three office-holders for the third term. The panel held that the claimant accordingly lacked the legal character to bring an interest dispute and declined to consider the claims. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 82][Para 83] Issue 5 was disposed of on the same ground, the panel expressly cross-referring to its reasoning on issue 1 after finding no provision and no past practice requiring midday meal money. [Para 86][Para 89][Para 90]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a demand seeks a benefit beyond what the Labour Law, a collective agreement, the internal rules, the contract or established practice already secures, the panel treats it as an interest dispute; and because an award on an interest dispute displaces the collective agreement for a year and extinguishes the right to strike on those claims, the panel will only consider such a dispute when the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise or the claimant unions together represent more than half the workforce. A union whose representative mandate has expired, or which cannot show that majority, has no standing, and the panel will decline to consider the claim however reasonable its merits. [Para 72][Para 79][Para 80][Para 82][Para 83]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that the employer had already implemented Prakas No. 283 and that the workers' base wage of 128 USD matched the statutory minimum, which would in any event have disposed of issue 1; and its remark that a demand for exactly 3,000 riel was not insisted upon by the union itself. [Para 58][Para 69][Para 70]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel composition is printed two ways: the header and the recitals name the chair as Pen Bun Chhea (ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា), while the signature block at the end gives "ថៃ ប៊ុន ណា" (Thai Bunna) and adds stray text; the composition above follows the header. [Para 1][Para 95] The overtime figures do not reconcile: issue 4 asks for "30 riel" more on an existing total of 7,000 riel, while the employer's position states it already pays 4,500 riel. [Para 8][Para 10] The amended award date is printed as "from 09 July 2019 2015 to 14 July 2015", the year being corrected in the text. [Para 33] The employer's representatives are numbered 1, 2, 2, and their names are reduced to initials here. Latin-script OCR debris interrupts the reasoning ("Juses" at the union's registration, "King Alsa /ខ្ញុំ A" before letter No. 961, "Zes", "ភ្នំ គូលែង", "Whav"), and page markers are embedded mid-sentence. The company name appears in several transliterations across the award and the conciliation report, and the registration number is given as Inv. 635 E/1999 in the findings while the certificate of incorporation is cited as No. 9902 of 9 December 2014.
