# Award No. 065/15 — Kip Tha B Sport Thing Goods (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 27 April 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mr Mar Sombor Na for the employer party, Mr Sin Kim Sean for the worker party, Mr Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139162

## 1. Facts
Kip Tha B Sport Thing Goods (Cambodia), a sports-equipment manufacturer at Sangkat 1, Preah Sihanouk, employed about 215 workers. [Para 1][Para 43][Para 44] In July 2013 it moved its plant about 16 km, from the special economic zone at Bit Trang to Village Ou Diri, Sangkat 1, and promised to keep contracts, wages and conditions unchanged and to provide transport to workers who needed it; about 40 workers used the hired car. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 141] On 1 September 2014, the number needing transport having fallen to seven, the employer stopped the car; after a 30 August 2014 meeting at which it offered either termination with severance or USD 20 a month toward housing, the parties signed an agreement on 9 October 2014 under which the employer would hire a tuk-tuk for six named workers and deduct USD 15 a month from each. [Para 83][Para 85][Para 86][Para 87] The tuk-tuk ran for three days only, 15–17 October 2014. [Para 88][Para 111] Six workers transferred their rights to the union. [Para 45][Para 47]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to perform the agreement of 9 October 2014 by hiring a tuk-tuk to carry six workers to work and home again, deducting USD 15 a month from each worker? [Para 100][Para 101]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to repay wages and other benefits to those six workers for the periods in which it did not provide transport, from 1 September 2014 to 14 October 2014 and from 18 October 2014 until transport is again provided? [Para 89][Para 130]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer stopped the hired car just when the company had moved and workers needed transport, breaking its own oral promise; it also named a worker who had already left, Ms Y, as one of the six in place of Mr D, a deliberate evasion of the agreement. [Para 61][Para 90][Para 91] Mr D had resigned once but was called back by the company president with seniority, functions and benefits intact and continued to use the hired car until it stopped, so he is not a new worker. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 70] The new site costs about 20 riel a day in fares, which they cannot afford, so they could not come to work. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 144]
**Employer (respondent):** it stopped hiring the tuk-tuk because one of the six, Ms Y, had resigned, and under the agreement it owes nothing for the five who remain. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 95][Para 96] It also says the six did not come to work at all and the complaint was brought long after the events. [Para 97]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to perform the 9 October 2014 agreement by hiring a tuk-tuk for the six workers with a USD 15 monthly deduction from each, and to repay wages and other benefits for the six from 1 September 2014 to 14 October 2014 and from 18 October 2014 until transport is provided again. [Para 178][Para 179]

On issue 1, applying Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code, the panel held that a contract arises from offer and acceptance and binds the party who made it; following Award No. 271/14, an employer must carry out an agreement it has concluded with its workers. [Para 101][Para 102][Para 108][Para 109] The 9 October 2014 agreement did not name the six workers, which is why the parties disputed the sixth name. [Para 75][Para 115] Reviewing the attendance lists of the earlier case No. 255/14, the panel found that Mr D had attended and had transferred his rights, while Ms Y had been absent from work since early September 2014 and had not attended; the workers' account was therefore consistent with the record and was accepted. [Para 121][Para 122][Para 123][Para 124] The employer's argument that Mr D was a new worker with no right to the hired car had no proper basis, since his resignation and return in July 2013 coincided with the start of the car service and he used it throughout. [Para 125][Para 126][Para 127] The employer had performed the agreement for three days, which itself showed the conditions of the agreement were met. [Para 128][Para 129]

On issue 2, Article 664 of the Civil Code defines the employment contract as an exchange of work for wages, and under Article 398(1) a debtor who fails to perform owes damages unless it shows it was not at fault. [Para 130][Para 132][Para 136][Para 138] Article 2 of the Labour Law gives the employer the right to manage and to transfer workers, but subject to four conditions, the fourth being that workers are not moved to a distant place; the move here was 16 km and cost 20 riel a day in fares. [Para 147][Para 150][Para 152][Para 153] The promise of transport, accepted by the workers' conduct in travelling to the new site from July 2013, became a contract under Article 343 of the Civil Code. [Para 156][Para 157][Para 159][Para 160] Nothing in that contract made it conditional on how many workers used the service, and a fall from 40 to 7 did not entitle the employer to end it unilaterally; it breached the contract from 1 September 2014 to 14 October 2014 and again from 18 October 2014. [Para 163][Para 164][Para 165][Para 170] Because the workers' absence was caused by the employer's own failure to provide transport, it must pay their wages and benefits for those periods under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 174][Para 175][Para 177]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A transport undertaking given when an employer relocates its plant, accepted by workers who then travel to the new site, is a binding contractual term that cannot be withdrawn unilaterally because fewer workers use it. Where the employer's own breach of that undertaking is the reason the workers do not attend, the absence is attributable to the employer, which must pay wages and benefits for the period of the breach; and an employer may not defeat a settlement naming six beneficiaries by substituting the name of a worker who has already left. [Para 128][Para 159][Para 164][Para 174]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's survey of the four conditions on an employer's power to transfer workers, and its remark that the 30 August 2014 offer of USD 20 toward housing was never accepted, are general observations that did not decide either issue. [Para 85][Para 150]
