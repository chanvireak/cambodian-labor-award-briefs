# Award No. 22/11-Banjamat Construction — Banjamat Construction Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 21 March 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phyrum, An Nan, Kong Phallack) · Doc ID 140000

## 1. Facts
Banjamat Construction Co., Ltd, a civil construction and machine-maintenance subcontractor for K-Cement Co., Ltd at a Kampot Province site, dismissed Chhun Pov and Tep Mao on 17 December 2010 without prior notice, both being Maintenance Section workers on verbal undetermined-duration contracts since 1 January 2010 [Para 14][Para 23][Para 27]. Both had been elected leaders of the Local Union of BWTUC at K-Cement, whose registration was refused because K-Cement said they were not its workers [Para 31][Para 32]. The union's six demands went to conciliation on 11 January 2011; none was conciliated, and the non-conciliation report No. 024 MKBV/KP 011 dated 24 January 2011 reached the Secretariat on 14 February 2011 [Para 4][Para 5].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer should reinstate the union leaders to work with their skills? [Para 1]
2. Whether the employer should provide back pay to the union leaders from the date of dismissal to the date of reinstatement? [Para 1]
3. Whether the employer should refrain from discriminating against and threatening union members? [Para 1]
4. Whether the employer should implement the law on paid public holidays? [Para 1]
5. Whether the employer should implement the law on annual leave? [Para 1]
6. Whether the employer should calculate wages for night work in accordance with the Labour Law? [Para 1]
Issues 3 to 6 were resolved in conciliation at the hearing, and the panel combined Issues 1 and 2 as related into the single remaining issue it decided [Para 6][Para 7].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The two dismissed men were elected union leaders and had been dismissed for refusing to accept transfer from the Maintenance Section to the Construction Section and for protesting that transfer; the dismissal was unjust and they sought reinstatement with back pay [Para 25][Para 27][Para 34].
**Employer (respondent):** Banjamat said the two refused to follow its direction, that the 16 December 2010 protest by 20 workers was serious misconduct, and that the union was K-Cement's and not its own; it did not object that the dispute was non-collective and did not submit its Internal Work Rules [Para 24][Para 33][Para 34][Para 36][Para 41].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Banjamat Construction Co., Ltd was ordered to reinstate Chhun Pov and Tep Mao and to provide back pay from the date of dismissal to the date of reinstatement; Issues 3 to 6 had already been settled by conciliation and no amount was fixed in the award [Para 6][Para 59][Para 60].
On whether the dispute was collective, the panel followed its own prior awards (10/03-Jacqsintex, 02/04-Cambodiana Hotel, 41/04-MiCasa, 07/05-Coca Cola, 61/09-Cintri) that it will decide the issues in a non-conciliation report absent an explicit objection that they are not collective, and the employer had raised none [Para 40][Para 41][Para 42]. On special protection, Article 293 paragraph 1 of the Labour Law and Clause 3 paragraph 3 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 protect a union leadership candidate from dismissal from 45 days before the election, but only where the employer was duly informed of the candidacy by reliable means [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44]. Applying the three requirements from Award 122/09-YVP, the panel found the two men had notified K-Cement, not Banjamat, of the election, so they were leaders of K-Cement's union and not entitled to Article 293 protection against Banjamat [Para 45][Para 46][Para 48][Para 49]. On the dismissal itself, both held undetermined-duration contracts, and Article 74 lets either party terminate at will but only with written prior notice and a valid reason relating to the worker's aptitude or behaviour or the needs of the enterprise [Para 50][Para 51][Para 52]. The 16 December 2010 protest from 7:40 p.m. to 8:10 p.m. was a strike on the three elements of Article 318 — a group of workers, a work stoppage, and the aim of satisfying a demand [Para 53][Para 54]. Articles 332 paragraph 2 and 333 reinstate strikers in their jobs and prohibit and nullify sanctions for strike participation, and the panel had already held such dismissals illegal in 01/06-Goldtex Hing Shing, 125/09-Wincam and 148/09-Focus Footwear [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57][Para 58]. The dismissal was therefore contrary to Articles 332 and 333 and without reasonable grounds, and reinstatement with back pay was ordered [Para 59][Para 60]. The award is non-binding and becomes binding eight days after notification unless a party lodges written opposition [Para 60].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Dismissing workers because they took part in a strike is unlawful: participation in a strike may not found a sanction, and the sanction is null and the workers are reinstated. Special protection for union leadership candidates under Article 293 arises only against the employer that was duly informed of the candidacy by reliable means; where the notification went to another company, the candidate has no such protection against its own employer [Para 44][Para 57][Para 58].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's practice of deciding whatever the Ministry forwards in a non-conciliation report unless a party explicitly objects that the issues are not collective, and its three-element reading of Article 318, were stated as general principles rather than being necessary to the reinstatement order [Para 40][Para 53].
