# Award No. 055/16 — Thong Khang (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 22 March 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mr Ing Sothy (employer-selected, dissenting on issue 4-2), Mr Liv Sovanna (worker-selected), chair Mr Pen Bun Chhea · Doc ID 139001

## 1. Facts
Thong Khang (Cambodia) Co., Ltd., a shoe-components manufacturer at Thlok, Chongruk, Kong Pisei, Kampong Speu, employed approximately 64 workers. [Para 32][Para 33] The Khmer Workers' Spirit Union, which had held its leadership election on 25 December 2015, was not yet registered with the Ministry of Labour, and 22 workers had thumbprinted a transfer of rights to the federation's president dated 29 January 2016, of whom 3 attended the hearing. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 36][Para 37][Para 39] Of eight demands, three were settled at the hearing of 9 March 2016 (2, 5 and 8) and three withdrawn (3, 6 and 7), leaving the contract-length demand and the sick-leave bonus demand for a non-binding award of 22 March 2016, with a written dissent by the employer-selected arbitrator. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24][Para 108][Para 110]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must replace its three-month fixed-duration contracts with contracts of 6 to 12 months for all workers? [Para 40][Para 54]
2. Whether the employer must keep the whole US$10 monthly attendance bonus when a worker is absent on sick leave supported by a valid medical certificate? [Para 46][Para 80]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Three-month contracts are too short and leave workers afraid the employer will easily end their employment, and having accepted their work after probation the employer should commit to 6 or 12 months; on the bonus, sick workers must pay for medicines, injections and treatment, so cutting the US$10 makes their lives harder, and their absence is caused by illness, not choice. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 50][Para 51]
**Employer (respondent):** It cannot offer 6 to 12-month contracts because the company only opened in November 2015 and does not yet have enough orders to guarantee work for that long, and some workers often seek leave or do not attend to their work, so three-month contracts let it decide whether to renew; on the bonus, workers must attend regularly to earn it, and keeping it for absentees would demoralise those who do attend. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 52][Para 53]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed and partly granted — the demand to convert three-month contracts into 6 to 12-month contracts was refused, and the demand to retain the full US$10 attendance bonus during certified sick leave was refused, but the employer was ordered to reduce that bonus only in proportion to the number of days of certified sick leave. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 104][Para 105][Para 106]

On the contract length, the panel applied Article 65 of the Labour Law, which defines the employment contract and subjects it to the general rules on contract formation, and Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the 2007 Civil Code, under which a contract arises from matching offer and acceptance and no party can impose a form or term the other has not accepted. [Para 54][Para 56][Para 59][Para 61][Para 63] It held the workers' request lawful as an offer and the employer's refusal equally lawful as its answer, and that neither side may force a 6 to 12-month term or a three-month term on the other. [Para 74][Para 75][Para 76][Para 77] Once the parties agree a duration, both must perform to the expiry date unless they agree to amend. [Para 78] The demand was therefore dismissed. [Para 79]

On the bonus, Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 grants at least US$10 a month to workers who attend every working day without absence, and following Award No. 186/13-ILC Garment the panel accepted that an official medical certificate proves the illness and makes the absence legitimate, so the employer may not strip the entire bonus for certified sick leave. [Para 80][Para 83][Para 85][Para 87][Para 88][Para 89] It reasoned from Article 72 of the Constitution, Article 25 of the Universal Declaration and Article 12 of the ICESCR, which Cambodia ratified in 1992, that health protection covers workers, and that a rule forfeiting the whole bonus for certified illness defeats the bonus's purpose, destroys the incentive it creates and pushes sick workers to attend, damaging their health and eventually factory output. [Para 92][Para 93][Para 94][Para 96][Para 97][Para 99] But the worker absent sick should not receive the full month's bonus, so the panel chose pro-rata deduction by days of certified absence as the most reasonable balance between the bonus's purpose and the law's protection of workers' health. [Para 100][Para 101][Para 102]

The employer-selected arbitrator dissented on that order alone, arguing that a conditional "bonus" is not wages under Article 103, which covers only gratuities paid unconditionally, that a conditional bonus cannot be apportioned unless its terms say so, and that Article 169(2)-(3) already pays sick leave as if the worker had attended, so no deduction is justified. [Para 113][Para 114][Para 116][Para 119][Para 120][Para 121][Para 122]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The panel cannot rewrite the duration of an employment contract: a demand for a longer fixed term is only an offer, and the employer's refusal is a lawful exercise of its freedom of contract, so the terms both sides actually agreed bind them until expiry. Where an attendance bonus requires perfect attendance, an employer may not forfeit it entirely for absence covered by a valid medical certificate, because certified illness makes the absence legitimate, but it may reduce the bonus in proportion to the days of certified absence. [Para 76][Para 78][Para 88][Para 103]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that a rule forfeiting the whole bonus would make workers come to work ill, harming their health and, in the end, the factory's own output. [Para 99]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The union's election is dated 25 December 2015 in the panel's findings but 25 December 2016 in the union's own notification letter, a date after this award. [Para 27][Para 35] The conciliation report number carries two different suffixes, "កប/កស្ត" and "ភ.ប/កស្ត", both dated 23 February 2016 and transmitted on 26 February. [Para 13][Para 18][Para 28] The employer's name shifts between "ថុង ខាង" and "ក្រុម ហ៊ុន ថុង ខាង", once with "ខូអិលធីឌីជីថល" appended to the union's own name. [Para 1][Para 18] The dispositive text garbles the bonus as "១០ម៉ឺន ដុល្លារ" and "51 ដុល្លារ" where US$10 is meant, though the reasoning and the dissent both use US$10. [Para 102][Para 105][Para 106] The evidence section records no documents received from the employer at all, so the employer's account of its order book rests only on its oral submission. [Para 27] Individual workers, union officers and the employer's director are redacted here. [Para 35][Para 37]
