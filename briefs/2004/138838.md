# Award No. 18/04 — InterContinental Hotel (Arbitration Council, 10 January 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mr Mar Samborana (employer-selected), Mr An Nan (worker-selected), chair Mr Matthew Rendall · Doc ID 138838

## 1. Facts
The InterContinental Hotel on Mao Tse Toung Blvd, Phnom Penh, faced a collective dispute over service charges raised by the Union of Independent Solidarity of InterContinental Hotel (UISICH) with the tourism and service workers' federation; workers struck on 5 April 2004 and the Arbitration Council ordered the strike and lockout ended on 12 April 2004. [Para 1][Para 25][Para 26][Para 32][Para 33] By an agreement of 8 May 2004 the hotel reinstated 34 strikers but put six on paid suspension, and the parties deferred the service-charge arbitration pending a Prakas from the ministry. [Para 34][Para 44] On 15 October 2004 the hotel offered the five remaining suspended workers their old pay in lower positions on three months' probation, or dismissal with statutory compensation; they refused, wages stopped on 24 October 2004, and the union asked the Council to hear the case on 8 November 2004. [Para 35][Para 49][Para 50][Para 36]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the Arbitration Council retains jurisdiction although the fifteen-day period in Article 313 of the Labour Law expired, the parties having agreed to postpone the arbitration? [Para 66][Para 68][Para 71]
2. Whether the demotion of the five striking employees is a dispute arising directly out of the non-conciliation report and so within the Council's jurisdiction under Article 312(1)? [Para 73][Para 74][Para 76][Para 80]
3. Whether the employer may demote, or otherwise sanction, workers for participating in a lawful strike, and whether demotion is a punishment permitted by the internal work rules? [Para 81][Para 96][Para 97][Para 118]
4. Whether the employer must pay the five workers' wages from 1 October 2004 while the case was pending? [Para 123][Para 128]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The dispute is collective because it flows from the strike for service charges, as in the Sunway case; the five must be reinstated in their former positions; and Article 26 of the Labour Law bars punishment once fifteen days have passed since the employer learned of the misconduct. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40][Para 42]
**Employer (respondent):** The matter is an individual dispute outside the Council's jurisdiction, the five committed separate misconducts during the strike — forcing colleagues to join and leaving workstations without permission — and their leadership roles justified demotion on probation at unchanged pay or dismissal with compensation. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 46][Para 35]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer must reinstate all five workers to their previous duties and positions immediately on the award taking effect, may punish only one of them (the worker found to have incited colleagues) by written warning within 15 days, is not obliged to pay their wages from 24 October 2004 to date, and the service-charge arbitration is postponed until the ministry's Prakas. [Para 130][Para 131]

On jurisdiction, the panel held the fifteen-day limit in Article 313 does not extinguish jurisdiction where the delay came from a party agreement and the complainant made a reasonable request to resume, following the Sunway Hotel case, and that the employer did not object to resumption. [Para 69][Para 71][Para 72] Applying Article 312(1), it found the two requirements met: the demotion arose after the report and was a direct consequence of the unsuccessfully conciliated issue of service charges, since the suspension flowed from conduct during that strike. [Para 74][Para 76][Para 78][Para 80] On the merits, it held that leaving the workplace during a strike is a legal act under Article 318, that a court verdict of 30 June 2004 recognised the strike as lawful, that no prior notice of strike participation was owed, and that Article 333 prohibits sanctions imposed on the pretext of participation in a strike — so demotion, even at unchanged pay, is punishment and four of the five must be reinstated. [Para 91][Para 95][Para 96][Para 97] For the one worker seen entering the hotel and urging colleagues to join, the panel accepted the HR manager's uncontradicted written testimony (following the Jacqsintex and Raffle Le Royal cases), found the fifteen-day rule in Article 26 inapplicable because of the agreed suspension, but held the internal work rules allow punishment only by verbal warning, written warning, temporary suspension or dismissal, so the three-month probationary demotion was not a permitted sanction: reinstatement plus a written warning. [Para 104][Para 107][Para 110][Para 114][Para 118][Para 121] Because the workers had been paid for five months without working and declined the option to work while contesting, no wages were due for the period from 24 October 2004. [Para 128]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A dispute over sanctioning workers for conduct during a strike is a direct consequence of the non-conciliation report on the demand that gave rise to that strike, and so falls within the Council's jurisdiction under Article 312(1); participation in a lawful strike is not misconduct, demotion imposed for it is a sanction prohibited by Article 333, and any penalty the employer does impose must be one the internal work rules actually provide. [Para 80][Para 96][Para 118]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that the employer did not carry through its threat to dismiss the workers if they failed to return within 48 hours, and that the employer remains free to punish any worker who fails to return to work after the award takes effect. [Para 98][Para 127]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The non-conciliation report is dated 9 April 2004 and said to have been submitted that same day [Para 10], but the reasoning states it was written on 5 April 2004 [Para 76]. The hearing is listed at 8:00am [Para 14] and at 8:30am [Para 37]. The number of suspended workers is six in the 8 May 2004 agreement and the findings [Para 44][Para 45] but five in the claim, the letter of 15 October 2004 and the orders [Para 35][Para 49][Para 130], the sixth having apparently dropped out. The panel cites "Sunway Hotel Case #18/04" as its precedent [Para 71], which is this case's own number. Worker names are printed inconsistently ("Kov Bun An"/"Kov Bon An", "Prum Meas Molin"/"Prom Meas Malin") and are redacted here per briefing rules. OCR losses are heavy: "Themployer", "Themployee", "aring", and sentences duplicated mid-line throughout the findings. Attorney and witness names appear as printed.
