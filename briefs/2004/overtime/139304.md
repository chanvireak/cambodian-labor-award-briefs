# Award No. 21/04 — You Chheng Garment Factory (Arbitration Council, 11 May 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mr Ouk Ry, employer-appointed; Mr Tuon Siphann, worker-appointed; Mr Ang Eng Thong, chair) · Doc ID 139304

## 1. Facts
You Chheng Garment Factory, on National Route 4 in Kandal province, employed 495 workers, 20 of them in the ironing department. [Para 27][Para 28] Those 20 were paid by the piece under an agreed scale of USD1.73 for 170 shirts, rising to USD2.00 for 235, USD2.50 for 270 and USD3.00 for 300, with USD1.00 for each further 60 shirts and the same price for every sleeve type. [Para 33][Para 65][Para 66][Para 67] In March 2004 the employer introduced a capped-sleeve shirt, PO No. 18248, and for the March wage run paid USD1.73 for 450 shirts without telling the workers in advance, a rate fixed after the accountant resigned. [Para 34][Para 40][Para 52] The workers struck on 24 April 2004 and returned on 29 April; conciliation settled one of the three listed issues. [Para 15][Para 63][Para 68]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the 20 ironing workers the rate agreed and practised before — USD1.73 for 200 shirts — rather than the rate applied to the March 2004 order? [Para 15][Para 31][Para 42]
2. Whether their wages may instead be computed from the exact number of completed products at the rate the employer unilaterally set for the capped-sleeve order? [Para 48][Para 52]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** capped-sleeve shirts are not as easy as the employer claims, a worker could iron at best 450 in the eight-hour day and 700 with overtime, so the price must stay at USD1.73 for 200 shirts, or as offered in conciliation USD1.73 for 300; negotiating the price with the Chinese employer through the accountant was the company's job, and any adjustment needed renegotiation. [Para 38][Para 42][Para 43][Para 44][Para 47]
**Employer (respondent):** wages are properly calculated from the exact number of completed products, and capped-sleeve shirts are easier — a quick worker irons 1,200 a day and a normal one 700 — so USD1.73 for 450 is fair; it had already paid USD1.73 for 200 on older orders, offered 10% more once the dispute arose, but the demand for USD1.73 per 250 shirts exceeded the agreement and was unaffordable. [Para 48][Para 51][Para 52][Para 54][Para 55][Para 56]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must calculate the ironing wage at USD1.73 for 375 shirts on capped-sleeve order PO No. 18248 only, on a scale rising to USD4.00 for 640 shirts, and must apply the previously agreed rate to all other shirts. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74]

The panel built its award on the employer's own concession in conciliation that the rate be USD1.73 for 375 shirts, leaving the increment to the panel, rather than on a free assessment of the competing claims. [Para 69][Para 70]

On the ordinary shirts the panel left the established scale untouched: regardless of short or long sleeves, 200 shirts are worth USD1.73, because that scale was agreed and practised. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 71]

On the new shirt the panel weighed the output figures and found neither side documented: nothing proved the workers could iron 700 to 1,200 capped-sleeve shirts a day, while their own figure of 450 in normal hours was credible. [Para 43][Para 46] It accepted the workers' argument [Para 47] and reasoned that capped-sleeve shirts cannot be compared with ordinary shirts because they iron as quickly as trousers, so more shirts for the same money was reasonable. [Para 71][Para 72] It accordingly set a graduated scale from 375 shirts to 640. [Para 74]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer introduces a new product and unilaterally resets the piece rate for it without agreeing that rate with the workers, the panel will fix the price by comparing the difficulty of the new work with the established scale, and the employer's asserted output figures carry no weight unless documented; the agreed rate continues to govern the products it already covered. [Para 67][Para 71][Para 72]

**Obiter dicta:** the findings that motorbikes blocking the gate did not stop other workers entering, and the remarks on the employer's storage costs and lost customers, played no part in fixing the rate. [Para 47][Para 63]
