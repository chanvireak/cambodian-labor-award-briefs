# Award No. 10/04 — Eternity Apparel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 19 March 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mar Samborana (employer-appointed), An Nan (worker-appointed), Ang Eng Thong (chair) · Doc ID 139296

## 1. Facts

Eternity Apparel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd operates a garment factory at #646, National Road 02, Chak Angre Krom commune, Meanchey district, Phnom Penh, employing 700 people. [Para 18]
On 24 February 2004 the workers lodged a complaint with the Meanchey labour inspectorate listing eleven demands; four were settled in conciliation on 26 February 2004 and seven remained. [Para 19] [Para 20] [Para 21]
Twelve workers, nine of them ironing-section workers involved in the dispute, had already signed resignation letters and receipts for termination payments, and none of them appeared at either hearing to pursue reinstatement. [Para 11] [Para 29] [Para 32]

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. Whether the company must reinstate the workers of the ironing section? [Para 21]
2. Whether overtime work must be on a voluntary basis? [Para 21]
3. Whether workers who work overtime until 21h00 must receive a food allowance of 2,000 riels per day in addition to overtime wages? [Para 21]
4. Whether the company must hand over time cards before 16h00? [Para 21]
5. Whether the company must pay wages together with pay slips? [Para 21]
6. Whether the company must pay 50 percent of wages when it has no work for the workers? [Para 21]
7. Whether the company must grant leave when a worker is busy or has an urgent matter? [Para 21]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** Through the CFITU representatives, the workers pressed all seven demands, argued that the 6 March 2004 conciliation report was falsified because the named workers denied signing or fingerprinting it, and said the nine workers dared not appear because the employer or its associates had threatened them. [Para 34] [Para 35]

**Employer (respondent):** The company's attorney produced the resignation letters and severance receipts, said the fingerprints on the conciliation report were genuine but had photocopied poorly, denied any threats, and conceded six of the seven demands, offering 1,000 riels of food allowance under Prakas 017 rather than the 2,000 riels claimed. [Para 33] [Para 36] [Para 37] [Para 39]

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement of the nine ironing workers was refused; the company was ordered to respect voluntary overtime, not to require more than two hours of overtime per day, to pay wages plus a food allowance of 1,000 riels per day during overtime, to hand time cards to non-overtime workers before 16h00, to issue pay slips from the April 2004 payday, to pay 50 percent of wages when there is no work, and to grant leave on acceptable reasons. [Para 41] [Para 42] [Para 43] [Para 44] [Para 45]

On issue 1 the panel reasoned that the nine workers had accepted the indemnity, written their own resignation letters and then failed to appear to support their demands about the dismissal, the indemnity or their reasons for resigning; it therefore found that they had in fact resigned and abandoned the claim to reinstatement. [Para 39] It did not resolve the competing accounts of the conciliation report, resting on the workers' conduct rather than on the disputed fingerprints. [Para 34] [Para 36]

On issue 2 the employer accepted the claim, so the panel ordered voluntary overtime as agreed. [Para 39] [Para 41]

On issue 3 the panel applied Article 140(C) of the Labour Law, which caps working hours at ten per day, and Prakas 80 SALVY of 10 March 1999, under which the labour inspectorate permits only two hours of overtime per day for health reasons; it concluded that although the Law sets no express ceiling for irregular urgent work, the company may not require more than ten hours of work in a day including regular hours, and no more than two hours of overtime. [Para 39] [Para 40] [Para 41] For the allowance it applied Article 04 of Prakas 017 SALVY of 18 July 2000, which entitles workers who volunteer for overtime to 1,000 riels per day or a free meal, and ordered that sum in addition to overtime wages under Article 139 of the Labour Law — the 2,000 riels claimed was not awarded. [Para 40] [Para 41]

On issue 4 the employer agreed to release time cards before 16h00 to workers not doing overtime, and on issue 5 to issue pay slips stating each category of pay from the April 2004 payday, a timetable the CFITU accepted. [Para 41] [Para 43] On issue 6 the employer agreed to pay 50 percent of salaries when there is no work, and the panel recorded that this sum is not set off against the eighteen days of annual leave. [Para 41] [Para 44] On issue 7 the employer agreed to facilitate leave for workers who reasonably show they are busy or have an urgent matter. [Para 41] [Para 45]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

A worker who accepts a termination indemnity, signs a resignation letter and then fails to appear before the panel to pursue reinstatement is treated as having resigned and abandoned the claim, whatever the union later alleges about the authenticity of the underlying documents. [Para 39] Where the Labour Law fixes no overtime ceiling for irregular urgent work, the panel will read Article 140(C) together with the labour inspectorate's two-hour practice to hold that total working time may not exceed ten hours in a day and overtime may not exceed two hours. [Para 40] A volunteer overtime worker is entitled to the statutory food allowance of 1,000 riels per day under Prakas 017 SALVY in addition to overtime wages, and the panel will not award more than the Prakas provides merely because the union claims it. [Para 40] [Para 41]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that only CFITU representatives and defenders appeared, while the direct parties, including members of the Free Trade Union of Cambodian Workers and CCAWDU, did not attend [Para 23]; and it recorded the employer's explanation that fingerprints on the conciliation report were genuine but illegible after photocopying [Para 36].
