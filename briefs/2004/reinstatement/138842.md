# Award No. 05/04 — Thai Dou Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 19 February 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ang Eng Thong presiding; Mar Samborana, employer nominee; Liv Sovanna, worker nominee) · Doc ID 138842

## 1. Facts
Thai Dou Garment Co., Ltd, at Toek La'ak III commune, Tuol Kok district, Phnom Penh, employs some 60 workers who were party to this collective dispute. [Para 1][Para 17] On 26 January 2004 the Tuol Kok labour inspectorate conciliated ten issues at the factory and settled seven, but the employer — saying he does not read Khmer — refused to sign the conciliation report and did not implement the seven agreed points. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20] Three issues remained, the employer declined the inspectorate's invitation of 28 January 2004, and neither side attended the hearing of 11 February 2004. [Para 21][Para 24][Para 25][Para 26][Para 32] The workers' representatives then wrote withdrawing the complaint on 14 February 2004, followed by the Union Federation of Cambodian Workers' letter No. 0825 UFCW of 17 February 2004. [Para 17][Para 30][Para 32]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether, where a worker is absent without permission for one day, the enterprise may deduct only the wage earned that day, or may double the deduction to two days' wages as the employer demands? [Para 21][Para 22]
2. Whether, when the enterprise has no work, it must pay 50 percent of wages together with bonuses, or only USD 0.50 per day as the employer offers? [Para 22][Para 23]
3. Whether the two dismissed workers may be reinstated subject to a written warning, which the worker representatives refuse to allow? [Para 23][Para 24]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** a day's unauthorised absence should cost only that day's wage, not two; where there is no work the employer should pay 50 percent of wages and bonuses, as the workers had demanded since the conciliation of 26 January 2004; and reinstatement should not be conditioned on a written warning. [Para 4][Para 5][Para 6][Para 7]
**Employer (respondent):** absence without permission should attract a double deduction of two days' wages; where work is short he will pay only USD 0.50 per day; he does accept reinstating the two workers but insists on warning them in writing; and he told the Council he had already resolved everything with the workers and did not attend the hearing. [Para 5][Para 6][Para 7][Para 26]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** withdrawn — the panel terminated the arbitration and ordered Case 05/04 removed from the list of cases of the Secretariat, leaving all three issues undecided. [Para 34]
The panel applied Article 21 of Prakas 338 of MOSALVY, which allows it, where a party duly invited fails to appear without proper cause, either to proceed in that party's absence or to dismiss the proceedings by means of an award. [Para 32] It found that both disputing parties had been properly invited by the Secretariat and neither appeared at the hearing of 11 February 2004. [Para 32]
It then weighed the two withdrawal letters — the worker representatives' letter of 14 February 2004 and the Federation's letter No. 0825 UFCW of 17 February 2004 — which confirmed that some workers had received their wages, some had returned to their home provinces and others had been offered work by another company, and which asked that the case be concluded without any demand. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 32] On that basis the panel found the employee party had agreed to drop its complaint and that the arbitration over Case 05/04 must be terminated by removing it from the Secretariat's list. [Para 33][Para 34]
The two representatives who appeared for the Federation, its vice-president and a coordinator, had told the panel they did not know the true facts and that many of the claimant workers were no longer employed by Thai Dou, so no contradictor was left to press the three issues. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 29] Because the case ended on withdrawal, the panel gave no ruling on the double deduction, the 50 percent wage or the written warning. [Para 34]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the complainant party has withdrawn its demands in writing and neither party appears on due notice, the panel does not decide the merits: it treats the claim as abandoned and terminates the arbitration by award under Article 21 of Prakas 338, removing the case from the Secretariat's list, so the same demands may only be revived by a fresh complaint and conciliation. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 34]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that the employer had refused to sign the conciliation report of 26 January 2004 on the ground that he does not read Khmer, and that seven of the ten issues had in fact been settled at that conciliation — facts it did not need in order to dismiss the case. [Para 19][Para 20]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The withdrawal date is printed "On 17 2004" in para 30, the month having been lost to the OCR; the Federation's letter is elsewhere dated 17 February 2004. [Para 17][Para 30] The case number appears as "Case: 05/04" on the face of the award and as "Case 05104" in the order, and the Prakas on the Arbitration Council is cited as "Prakas 338 MOSALVY dated 11 December 2003" in the reasons, although it is dated 11 December 2002 in the jurisdiction section. [Para 1][Para 9][Para 32][Para 34] The employer is "Thai Dou Garment Co., Ltd" in the title and "Thai Dou Garment Enterprise" in the case summary. [Para 1][Para 17] The employee representatives' names in the header survive only as a broken run — "Sok Dara, worker representative Mom Saven, worker representative Mom sros, worker representative 18 Mam Ti" — so their roles cannot be reliably paired with the names; individual workers are not reproduced here, and the two workers the employer offered to reinstate are referred to only as named in the source. [Para 3][Para 6][Para 23] The respondent's name is given as "Wang Tiangguo" once and the panel's jurisdiction footnotes are interleaved into the body text. [Para 2][Para 7]
