# Award No. 51/04 — Sam Han Garment Company (Cambodia) Fabric Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 July 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Kao Thach (employer-chosen), Ven Pov (worker-chosen), Kong Phallack (chair) · Doc ID 139325

## 1. Facts
Sam Han Garment Company (Cambodia) Fabric Co., Ltd employed 7,300 workers. In January 2004 it dismissed four carpenters — Mr R, Mr Y, Mr B and Mr S — all on unspecified-duration contracts and none for serious misconduct. [Para 31][Para 43] Agreement No. 006 of March 2004 obliged the company to pay each of them a dismissal indemnity calculated by the Ministry of Social Affairs, plus March salaries, by the end of March; no calculation was ever made and no indemnity was paid. [Para 36][Para 37] A six-point complaint of 10 June 2004 was conciliated on 12 June 2004, four points settling, and the remaining two reached the Council on 1 July 2004. [Para 38][Para 39]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must reinstate the four dismissed carpenters or instead pay them indemnities for dismissal according to the Labour Law? [Para 17]
2. Whether the company must be held responsible for the loss of workers' motorbikes and bicycles parked inside the factory? [Para 18]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The four were dismissed for refusing to work overtime, not for the drinking and absences the company later invoked, and those grounds were stale events from long before. No prior notice was given: Mr R was told to take 18 days' annual leave from 24 January 2004 and learned on returning, on 14 February 2004, that he had been dismissed with effect from 24 January. His basic wage was $90, not the $45 the company used, because the company paid only 50% for March. The workers also claimed wages in lieu of annual leave for every year except 2003, saying leave was not customarily given. [Para 50][Para 53][Para 90]

**Employer (respondent):** It could not reinstate the four but would pay the Article 89 indemnity, calculating on a basic wage of $45 for Mr R and $55 for the others. It said the dismissals followed frequent drinking during working time and many absences, and that Mr R and Mr S had taken annual leave in 2000 and Mr R 15 days in 1996, so leave had been granted. Against the annual-leave claim it invoked the three-year limitation in Article 120 and the three-year deferment cap in Article 167(4). [Para 39][Para 48][Para 50][Para 100]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to pay each carpenter a dismissal indemnity under Articles 75, 89 and 166 of the Labour Law, calculated on the reasoning below and paid not later than 30 August 2004; the demand under Article 91 was rejected; and the company was ordered to perform the settlement reached at the hearing on parking (issuing parking cards for bicycles and motorbikes and reserving parking space, with the union registering workers and collecting photographs). [Para 90][Para 111][Para 112]

Because the parties agreed at the hearing that the company would pay the dismissal indemnity and that the panel would fix the amount, the panel did not decide whether to reinstate; it quantified. [Para 42][Para 111]

On notice, Article 75 requires prior notice where dismissal is for a reason other than serious misconduct. The company gave none to workers dismissed on 24 January 2004 and was therefore required to give two months' notice; having already paid half salary for two months after dismissal, it owed one further month. That month is the average wage: total wages including overtime and bonuses over the twelve months before dismissal, divided by twelve. [Para 70][Para 71][Para 72][Para 73]

On the indemnity, since no serious misconduct was committed and the dismissal was at the employer's sole will, the full Article 89 indemnity was due — fifteen days of wage and fringe benefits per year of service, following Award 27/04 (MS International). Wage means actual remuneration plus overtime and bonuses, excluding specified payments (Articles 102–103), averaged over twelve months per Article 168; the average daily wage is the monthly average divided by 26, multiplied by 15 days per year. Service of six months or more counts as a whole year. On that basis Mr R (seven years six months) received 120 days; Mr Y (five years two months) 75 days; Mr B (five years two months) 75 days; Mr S (six years six months) 105 days. [Para 75][Para 77][Para 80][Para 85][Para 88]

Article 91 damages were refused: although termination without valid reason entitles the other party to damages, the carpenters proved no loss beyond the entitlements already awarded under Articles 75, 89 and 166. [Para 89][Para 90]

On annual leave, Article 170 gives leave at Khmer New Year absent agreement, and the panel found no agreement, so the 2000 and 1996 absences the company relied on were not annual leave. [Para 94][Para 98] Leave already acquired survives termination under Article 167(2), and Article 120's three-year limitation runs from termination (24 January 2004), so neither bars the claim; Article 167 protects the right rather than forfeiting unused leave year by year, and Article 167(4)'s deferment cap presupposes an agreement to defer, which did not exist. Having found the company proved only the 2003 leave, the panel awarded the balance of accrued leave on the Article 166 scale of 18 days per year for years one to three, 19 days for years four to six and 20 days from year seven: 10 additional days for Mr R, 3.7 days each for Mr Y and Mr B, and 9.5 days for Mr S, at the average daily wage. [Para 100][Para 103][Para 105][Para 106][Para 111]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A dismissal for a reason short of serious misconduct, effected without the two months' prior notice Article 75 requires, obliges the employer to pay the notice-period wages it withheld, and a dismissal at the employer's sole will with no serious misconduct obliges it to pay the full Article 89 indemnity of fifteen days of wage and fringe benefits per year of service, computed on the twelve-month average of actual wages including overtime and bonuses rather than on a reduced basic wage the employer chose. Accrued paid annual leave is not forfeited by non-use: it becomes a payable indemnity on termination under Article 167(2), the Article 120 limitation running from the termination date, and the Article 167(4) deferment cap applies only where an agreement to defer exists. [Para 71][Para 74][Para 100][Para 103]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that employers violating Article 167 are liable to a fine of thirty-one to sixty days of base daily wage under Article 363 — a penal consequence not relied on for the order — and observed that Article 167 should not be read to extinguish leave a worker did not take in a given year. [Para 102][Para 103]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
OCR is extensively degraded ("theemployees", "athemployees", "withemployees", "annualeave"). The reasoning's lettering runs "a- Article 75" then "d- Article 166", so the sub-headings for Articles 89 and 91 are lost, and the annual-leave figures are printed without units in places. Mr S's seniority is stated three ways: five years two months with a start date of 8 November 1997, six years six months in the findings, and seven years in the indemnity computation; Mr B's start date of 8 November 1998 also yields five years two months, so one date is certainly wrong, and both are reproduced as printed. The basic wage is disputed on the face of the award ($90 claimed, $45 applied by the company) and is not resolved. The MS International precedent appears as "27/04" and, elsewhere, "27104". Mr R's dismissal date is given as 24 January 2004 while the notification to him came on 14 February 2004; both are recorded. [Para 43][Para 53][Para 63][Para 88][Para 99]
