# Award No. 14/04 — June Textile Company (Arbitration Council, 5 April 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ouk Ry (employer-appointed), Koy Neam (worker-appointed), Sar Sovann (chair) · Doc ID 139297

## 1. Facts

June Textile Company has operated on Russian Boulevard, Tek Thla, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh since 1994 and employs about 5,000 workers. [Para 14] [Para 15]
The roughly 30 workers of the mechanical section demanded on seven points in January 2004; six were settled in conciliation on 20 February 2004, leaving one: a US$20 monthly wage increase said to be owed because they had worked long for the company without any rise and were discriminated against as Khmer Youth Federation of Trade Union (KYFTU) members. [Para 15] [Para 16] [Para 17]
Wages are calculated on a daily rate of US$2, there is no clear payroll for basic wages, and increases depend on a supervisor's task evaluation — but the supervisor changed every year, so the section's performance had not been evaluated and its basic wage had not risen for years. [Para 20]

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. Whether the company must increase the wages of the workers of the mechanical section by US$20 each, because they have long service and are paid less than members of other unions for the same tasks contrary to Articles 12 and 106 of the Labour Law? [Para 8] [Para 17] [Para 21]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** Long service plus the training and skill acquired on the job entitle them to a rise; the same task must receive the same wage, and members of other unions receive more, which shows discrimination on grounds of union membership. [Para 17] [Para 26]
**Employer (respondent):** Wages follow an annual task evaluation by each section supervisor; these workers were never properly evaluated, but they already earn more than the minimum wage in Notification 17/2000, and raising their pay would prompt the other 5,000 workers to demand the same. [Para 27]

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** partly granted — the union's demand under Article 106 of the Labour Law for equal wages for the same task was denied, but the employer was ordered to establish a payroll for the mechanical section dividing job classifications and a wage ladder for each, to evaluate performance regularly against criteria printed and given to the workers at the start and end of each period, and to keep a file of documents on each worker's tasks. [Para 34] [Para 35]

On the discrimination claim, the panel read Article 106 (equal wage for equal working conditions, professional skill and output, regardless of origin) together with the general prohibition in Article 12, so that wages must be equal without discrimination based on origin, sex, age or union membership where conditions, skill and output are the same. [Para 21] It held that a claimant must give detailed information showing that comparable workers receive more — for instance women proving they are paid less than male colleagues for the same tasks — and that an assertion that other unions' members earn more is not enough. [Para 22] [Para 23] The workers provided no clear or specific evidence of discrimination, and the panel accepted that wages may legitimately differ with skill and output even among workers of equal seniority and training. [Para 24] [Para 25] The claim therefore failed. [Para 26]

On the separate argument from acquired skill, the panel accepted the company's policy of annual evaluation-based increases but found as fact that the mechanical section had not been evaluated correctly because supervisors kept changing, a failure the company itself recognised. [Para 27] [Para 28] [Para 29] Rather than fix a sum, the panel held it reasonable for the employer to apply its policy and evaluate the workers, and that the employer could then consider an increase based on the result; to make that fair it required the evaluation criteria to be disclosed in advance and the records kept. [Para 30] [Para 31] [Para 32]

The panel noted that under clause 43 of the Prakas on the Arbitration Council (No. 338) an award resolving a collective dispute takes the place of a collective bargaining agreement for one year, that neither party may strike or lock out to renew or change an agreement while it is in force under Article 321, and that it would not amend an agreement properly made and still effective (Award 11/04-CAMS). [Para 32] [Para 33] [Para 34] It accordingly stated that this award does not settle an interests dispute and does not replace a collective bargaining agreement. [Para 35]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

A claim that a wage is discriminatory under Articles 12 and 106 of the Labour Law fails unless the workers identify the comparators and show, with specific evidence, that workers in equal conditions, with equal skill and output, are paid more on a prohibited ground such as sex, age, origin or union membership; equal seniority and training do not by themselves prove unlawful inequality. [Para 22] [Para 24] [Para 25] Where, however, the employer's own evaluation-based wage system has broken down so that a section has gone years without assessment, the panel will not substitute its own figure: it will order the employer to build a classification payroll with wage ladders, to evaluate regularly against criteria disclosed to the workers before and after each period, and to keep records of each worker's tasks. [Para 29] [Para 32] [Para 35]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the employer's concern about the other 5,000 workers following any increase, and about other factories' reaction, formed part of its reasons for refusing the sum claimed [Para 27]; and it recorded that the conciliation resolved six of the seven demands, which are not reopened by the award [Para 16].

## 6. Source defects disclosed

The KYFTU demand letter is dated "December 11, 2004" although the dispute runs from January to April 2004, so the year is wrong in the text [Para 15]. The award date appears only as "April 05, 2004" in the header [Para 1]. The panel's statement that an award takes the place of a collective agreement for one year [Para 32] sits awkwardly with its later holding that the award does not replace a collective bargaining agreement [Para 35]; both are reproduced as printed. Article 106 and Article 12 are quoted only in fragments.
