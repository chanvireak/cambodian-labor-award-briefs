# Award No. 68/04 — City New Garment Factory (Arbitration Council, 6 September 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Dr Sok Mathoeung presiding; Mar Sambona appointed by the employer; Tuon Siphann appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 139338

## 1. Facts
City New Garment Factory, on Street 68 at Street 45 Phsa Toch, Don Penh district, Phnom Penh, has operated since 1995 and employs 1,057 workers, more than 900 of them women, of whom about 30 give birth each year. [Para 18][Para 23][Para 24] On 6 August 2004 the district labour inspectors received a six-point complaint by telephone, and conciliation settled nothing. [Para 19][Para 20] The six points concerned childcare, equal pay for quality control workers, sick leave wages, maternity benefits, medical check-up costs deducted from wages, and travel costs of dismissed probationers. [Para 9][Para 10]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must provide 3 cans of milk of 1 kg each and US$20 a month for childcare, or in the alternative US$27 a month in benefits and 18 months of childcare for women returning from maternity leave? [Para 9][Para 23]
2. Whether the company must pay the base wage equally among quality control (QC) workers? [Para 9][Para 55]
3. Whether the company must maintain payment of wages during medically certified sick leave? [Para 9][Para 67]
4. Whether the company must maintain the full seniority bonus and the US$5 regular bonus for female workers during maternity leave? [Para 10][Para 88]
5. Whether the company must pay each worker 10,100 riel for medical check-ups? [Para 10][Para 111]
6. Whether the company must pay transport costs when it dismisses probationary workers? [Para 10][Para 115]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** no breastfeeding room existed and the childcare space was small and unstaffed, with one worker paying US$15 a month outside; the 57 quality control workers on US$45 did the same work at the same speed as the 38 on US$50; public hospitals were too distant and costly and often refused certificates; and wages had been docked 10,100 riel for compulsory check-ups. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 31][Para 76][Para 77][Para 107]
**Employer (respondent):** the US$50 rate rewarded greater skill and fewer mistakes, the US$45 workers making errors that caused buyers to reject goods; its Internal Work Rules limited sick pay and recognised only designated public hospitals; and workers had to hold a health certificate before being hired. [Para 31][Para 33][Para 34][Para 41]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must open a breastfeeding room within two weeks and either establish a childcare centre under Ministry supervision or pay US$15 a month for children over 18 months looked after outside, from 30 September 2004; the demand for equal US$50 pay is rejected but skill tests must be held within one week for the US$45 workers, with wages raised according to expertise; the company must accept certificates from any legally recognised public or private doctor and pay full wages for one month of sick leave and 70 per cent for two to three months; maternity leave of 90 days must carry half of basic salary plus benefits averaged over the 12 months before leave, including seniority and regular bonuses; 10,100 riel must be repaid to every worker whose wage was deducted after 5 July 2001 or who paid out of pocket, within one week, and check-ups paid for workers not yet examined; the travel-cost demand is rejected. [Para 117][Para 118][Para 119][Para 120][Para 121][Para 123][Para 125][Para 116]
On issue 1 the panel applied Articles 186 and 187 of the Labour Law, following award 63/04 (Shine Well): an enterprise with at least 100 women must install a nursing room and childcare centre, and where it cannot, must pay the cost of outside care, which the evidence fixed at US$15 a month; the US$27 and milk demand exceeded what the law provides. [Para 47][Para 49][Para 50][Para 52][Para 54]
On issue 2 the panel took Article 106 to require four cumulative elements — equal working conditions, professional qualifications, productivity, and no discrimination — and found the workers proved only the first and third, since the employer's evidence of error rates justified the gap and Article 2 and Article 5(A) of the Internal Work Rules leave wage setting to the employer within the law, as in award 28/04 (Raffel Grand Hotel d'Angkor). [Para 55][Para 56][Para 61][Para 62][Para 63][Para 64] The company's own letter of 30 August 2004 offering room for reconsideration led to the skill-test order. [Para 65][Para 66]
On issue 3 the panel read Article 4(F) of the Internal Work Rules as granting full wages for the first month of sick leave and 70 per cent for two to three months, and held that limiting certificates to named public hospitals was inconsistent with Article 13, which permits better but not lesser benefits than the law, and unjust given Cambodian practice. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 82][Para 83][Para 86]
On issue 4 the panel applied Articles 182 and 183, Article 5 of Notification 017 of 18 July 2000 on the seniority bonus, and Article 3 on the attendance bonus, holding that Article 183 requires at least half of the benefits the worker would have earned, calculated by averaging all benefits over the 12 months before leave, dividing by 12 and halving, with Article 168 as a guide. [Para 88][Para 90][Para 93][Para 101][Para 103]
On issue 5 the panel followed 02/03 (Chou Sing) and 60/04 (United Arts) and held Joint Prakas No. 09/94 still valid and, independently, Article 247(c) sufficient: deduction of wages for a compulsory check breaches Article 127, and Article 120 limits claims to three years, so deductions from 5 July 2001 were recoverable. [Para 105][Para 106][Para 107][Para 109][Para 111]
On issue 6 the panel rejected a demand aimed at hypothetical future dismissals, which disclosed no actual circumstance, and held Article 68 inapplicable to workers who live in one province and find work in another. [Para 115][Para 116]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may not deduct from wages, or require a worker to pay in advance, for the medical examination Article 247 of the Labour Law obliges the employer to provide, and the worker may recover such deductions for three years under Article 120. [Para 107][Para 108][Para 109] An employer must honour medical certificates from any legally recognised doctor, public or private, and may not confine sick pay to named public hospitals. [Para 83][Para 86] Maternity benefits under Article 183 are at least half of the average of all wages and benefits, bonuses included, earned in the 12 months before leave. [Para 101][Para 103]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that most civil servants, NGO staff and private employees in Cambodia accept certificates from any legally recognised doctor, and that requiring public-hospital certificates would be unjust to workers. [Para 83][Para 85]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The repayment figure is printed "100 riel for each worker" at para 111 while paras 10 and 123 give 10,100 riel. [Para 111][Para 123] Conciliation dates conflict: the attempt is dated 11 June 2004 at para 10, the minutes 12 July 2004 at paras 17 and 35, and other minutes 6 August 2004 at para 17, while para 20 records that no point was agreed and para 35 records an agreement of 12 July 2004 on hospitals. [Para 10][Para 17][Para 35][Para 20] The workforce is 1,057 at para 18 and "more than 900 female workers" at para 23, and the quality control section is 95 workers of whom 38 are said to receive US$50 at para 28. [Para 18][Para 23][Para 28] Paras 96 and 97 repeat and truncate Article 183(2), para 118 breaks off mid-sentence, and paras 41 and 65 repeat their own text. [Para 96][Para 65] The company's name appears as "City New Garment Factory" and "City New Factory (Cambodia) Ltd". [Para 1][Para 15] Individual workers, union officers and the employer's representatives appear under full names in the source and are not reproduced here. [Para 2][Para 3]
