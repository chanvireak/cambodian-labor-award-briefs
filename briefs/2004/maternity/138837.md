# Award No. 29/03 — Raffles Le Royal (Arbitration Council, 2 February 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mr Mar Samborana (employer-selected), Mr An Nan (worker-selected), chair Mr Ang Eng Thong · Doc ID 138837

## 1. Facts
Raffles Le Royal, Building No. 98, Sangkat Watphnom, Phnom Penh, employed around 280 workers and was negotiating a first collective bargaining agreement (CBA) with the Union of Raffles Le Royal, registered on 23 May 2003. [Para 3][Para 22][Para 23][Para 6] The union notified MOSALVY of a strike on 16 December 2003, the hotel complained to the Labour Inspectorate on 17 December, and one of the demands was conciliated on 23 December; the union struck at 5h00 on 25 December and the Phnom Penh court declared the strike illegal that day, after which the parties returned to work on 26 December. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 27][Para 29][Para 31][Para 33] The panel heard the case on 30 December 2003 and 5 and 13 January 2004; neither party agreed to a binding award. [Para 18][Para 21][Para 36]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must provide the union with an exclusive room, six chairs and a whiteboard as a union library? (Article 2-h) [Para 8][Para 36]
2. Whether the employer must contribute US$10.00 per month to a union-managed social fund? (Article 5-j) [Para 42][Para 44]
3. Whether casual and temporary workers must be engaged on Labor Law contracts and paid no less than the minimum wage of a permanent employee with the same skills? (Article 5-3) [Para 46][Para 47]
4. Whether the employer must pay the statutory overtime, public holiday, weekly day-off and night work rates? (Articles 7-d, 7-i, 7-j) [Para 11][Para 12][Para 88]
5. Whether the employer must pay a seniority bonus of US$15.00 per month for 2004, a 13th month bonus, and continue collecting and distributing the service charge? (Articles 9-ជ, 9-i, 9-e & f) [Para 16][Para 63][Para 66]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The union sought a room for representatives to meet members and hold legal documents, a US$10.00 monthly employer contribution to a social fund for workers facing fire or family incidents, a US$15.00 monthly seniority bonus in 2004, a 13th month bonus paid half at Khmer New Year and half at Phumbin, 100 days' maternity leave at full pay, and continued collection of the service charge distributed to all staff. [Para 38][Para 42][Para 63][Para 68][Para 66]
**Employer (respondent):** The hotel refused an exclusive library because the union would use it for activities against the hotel, arguing a common library already existed; it rejected the social fund; it refused the seniority bonus and 13th month bonus because it had lost millions of dollars annually and the unlawful strike caused further damage; and it stopped collecting the service charge from 1 January 2004. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 44][Para 65][Para 68]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — both parties must apply the CBA annexed to the award; the employer must not sue the union or employees who joined the strike, and the union and employees must not claim the service charge already collected and distributed. [Para 121][Para 122]

On the library, the panel applied Article 5 of Prakas No. 286 of 5 November 2001 on shop stewards, which requires the employer to provide an office, meeting room and a place to post information, so the hotel must provide a room with sufficient supplies. [Para 74][Para 75] The US$10.00 social fund had no legal basis and was not reasonable, and in the then state of relations the employer was not ready to contribute. [Para 78][Para 79] On casual workers, the panel held a temporary contract must be one of the three Labor Law categories, that quiet continuation beyond three months converts it into a fixed or unspecified duration contract, and that wages must not fall below the minimum wage of a skilled permanent employee. [Para 80][Para 82][Para 84] Applying Articles 139, 164 and 166 and Prakas No. 10 of 4 February 1999 and Circulation No. 024 of 19 October 1999, it fixed overtime at an additional 50%, weekly day-off work at double, holiday work with an indemnity equal to a normal day, night work at double, night on a holiday at three times and night on a weekly day off at four times the normal daily wage. [Para 88][Para 93][Para 94][Para 96] Annual leave was set at 18 days plus one day per three years, with 1.5 days per month of service on termination. [Para 97][Para 100] Sick leave follows the hotel's internal rule — 100% of wages the first month, 60% for the second and third, none beyond three months, with no termination during the six months — which the panel found equitable. [Para 105][Para 107] Maternity stays at the statutory 90 days on half pay for women with one year's service. [Para 108][Para 110][Para 111] Because employees would receive 100% of the service charge, the panel refused the US$15.00 rise and the 13th month bonus, and ordered the hotel to continue collecting the 10% service charge and distribute it in full, long collection having established a legal custom; split shifts beyond two a day attract a round-trip travel allowance. [Para 112][Para 114][Para 118][Para 119]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A service charge collected over many years becomes a legal custom binding the employer to keep collecting and distributing it in full, and while it is distributed the panel will not also award a basic-wage increase or 13th month bonus; a temporary or casual contract must fall within the three Labor Law categories, converts to a fixed or unspecified duration contract after three months of quiet continuation, and may never pay below the minimum wage of a skilled permanent employee. [Para 82][Para 84][Para 118][Para 119]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that if relations between the parties improve they could discuss the social fund again, and that the hotel's disciplinary guideline table is reasonable given the standards of the international hotel sector. [Para 79][Para 120]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Prakas No. 243 on work-related accidents is dated "September 10, 2004" in the reasoning [Para 120], five months after this award, but "September 10, 2002" in the annexed CBA [Para 246]. The number of union demands is printed as 10 in the case summary [Para 24], eight in the conciliation narrative [Para 27] and nine in the panel's account [Para 35], while the issue list runs to 16 items [Para 16]. The strike is dated 24–25 December 2003 in the employer's submission [Para 65] but began at 5h00 on 25 December [Para 29]. The chair is "Ang Eng Thong" in the panel composition [Para 21] and "Ang Eng Tong" in the signature block [Para 126]. OCR losses are heavy throughout ("Themployer", "article", "eight" for "eight"), and the text of Article 10 of the annexed CBA is unrecoverable gibberish where the scan broke down [Para 204]. Individual union officials and representatives appear only by name and are not reproduced here.
