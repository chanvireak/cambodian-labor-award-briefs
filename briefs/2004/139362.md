# Award No. 113/04 — Jeou Star Company (Arbitration Council, 13 January 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Somborana, Liv Sovanna, chair Kong Phallack) · Doc ID 139362

## 1. Facts
Jeou Star Company, a 106-worker enterprise in Khan Daun Kor, Phnom Penh, dismissed seven of its forty fixed-duration workers in December 2004, among them the union secretary Phan Chanra, Ms E Ra and Sok Chanthorn, and the union demanded reinstatement of the three [Para 18][Para 38][Para 3]. The Labour Inspectorate conciliated two of three demands on 17 December 2004 and referred the reinstatement claim by non-conciliation report 2991/MoLVT of 22 December 2004 [Para 19][Para 5]. The hearing was held on 28 December 2004 before Mar Somborana, Liv Sovanna and chair Kong Phallack [Para 9].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the Council has jurisdiction over the termination of Ms E Ra, or whether she must bring an individual dispute to the Phnom Penh court within two months? [Para 55][Para 60]
2. Whether Phan Chanra's two one-year contracts plus his three-month probationary period made his employment an undetermined-duration contract? [Para 64][Para 97]
3. Whether the employer could end a union secretary's contract by non-renewal without the Labour Inspector's prior permission? [Para 102]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Phan Chanra worked more than two years, so his contract became undetermined-duration and, as union secretary, he enjoys specific protection; his performance matched other workers and his dismissal followed the October termination of the union president and the November termination of the vice-president [Para 30][Para 31][Para 32]. Ms E Ra, having first accepted dismissal, then rejected it and sought continued employment [Para 47].
**Employer (respondent):** the contracts simply expired, probation excluded, so non-renewal was lawful; Phan Chanra was reminded four or five times to work harder, the company genuinely needed to cut staff for lack of steady work, and there was no discrimination since the president resigned voluntarily and the vice-president still works [Para 33][Para 34][Para 36]. Sok Chanthorn accepted severance and withdrew on 3 January 2005 [Para 47].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel declined to consider Ms E Ra's complaint, Sok Chanthorn having withdrawn, and ordered the employer to reinstate Phan Chanra immediately and pay him all wages from 1 January 2005 onwards once the award took effect [Para 105].

On jurisdiction, the panel accepted that the Ministry decides whether a dispute is individual or collective, and applied the three conditions of Article 302: a dispute involving a group through their union, concerning working conditions or the exercise of professional-organisation rights, capable of jeopardising the enterprise's operation [Para 48][Para 50]. All three were met for Phan Chanra, the third because many workers supported him and a strike was possible; for Ms E Ra conditions B and C failed, both parties agreeing few workers supported her, so hers was an individual dispute under Article 300 and Article 301 required her to sue within two months of non-conciliation [Para 53][Para 58][Para 60].

On Phan Chanra, the panel held a probationary period is itself a labour contract, since Article 65 defines a contract by the working relationship and Article 68 places probation within the chapter on labour contracts [Para 73][Para 78][Para 79]; and, being limited to one, two or three months by skill level, probation is a fixed-duration contract [Para 96]. Reading Article 67(2) as capping the total contractual period, as in 10/03 Jacqsintex, 02/04 Cambodiana, 81/04 Ever Green and 98/04 Great Union, it found his employment — three months' casual work from 3 September 2002 plus contracts from 1 January 2003 to 1 January 2005 — ran two years and three months, so the contract converted to undetermined duration and non-renewal was in truth a dismissal [Para 85][Para 93][Para 97][Para 99].

That dismissal was unlawful: the employer had to follow Articles 75, 77, 89, 91, 116, 166 and 167, and because Phan Chanra was a union secretary it also had to obtain the Labour Inspector's permission and give a valid reason under Articles 74, 79, 279 and 293 and Prakas 305/2001 and 313/2000 [Para 102][Para 103].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A probationary period is a labour contract of fixed duration and counts toward the total length of employment; where successive fixed-duration contracts and the probationary period together exceed the two-year ceiling in Article 67(2), the contract converts to undetermined duration, so non-renewal operates as a dismissal and, for a protected union officer, requires the Labour Inspector's prior permission and a valid reason [Para 85][Para 97][Para 102].

**Obiter dicta:** that the Council generally defers to the Ministry's characterisation of a dispute as collective or individual unless there is clear reason to object, and that a strike threat alone can satisfy the third Article 302 condition [Para 49][Para 53].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The reasoning contains an unresolved tension: the panel notes that Article 67(7) requires fixed-duration contracts to be written while the probationary periods here were oral, then rests conversion on the two-year ceiling rather than on that informality, so two routes to the same result are left unreconciled [Para 94][Para 97]. Ms E Ra's first contract is dated 1 March 2003 to 1 December 2003 and described as eight months, while the evidence list describes it as ten months [Para 11][Para 44]. The second worker is "Sok Chanthorn" in the findings and "Sok Chanthon" in the party list, and "Eun Ra" appears as "Eurn Ra" [Para 3][Para 47][Para 62]. The award date is 13 January 2005 although the hearing was 28 December 2004 and severance for Sok Chanthorn was agreed on 3 January 2005 [Para 1][Para 47]. Paragraph divisions frequently split a worker's name across lines, and OCR loss is pervasive ("thearing", "bargaing", "unspecifieduration" for "undetermined duration").
