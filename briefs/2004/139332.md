# Award No. 60/04 — United Arts Garment Company (Arbitration Council, 16 August 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Kao Thach (chosen by the employer party), Sin Kim Sean (chosen by the worker party), Ang Eng Thong (chair) · Doc ID 139332

## 1. Facts
United Arts Garment Company, at Kbal Domrey Village, Dang Kau District, employs 1,068 workers. On 21 June 2004 the workers filed a complaint over nine demands; conciliation on 21 June and 13 July 2004 settled six, leaving the wage for workless days, the 100-riel medical check and a US$5 rise for the hygiene and packing sections. A settlement was reached with the Khmer Youth Union — 75 percent of the medical check, full wages from 14 July 2004, US$10 per worker on termination — but C.CAWDU returned the file to the Secretariat, saying it knew nothing of the settlement and that it did not bind its members. [Para 10][Para 11][Para 13][Para 14][Para 17][Para 18]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay the full basic wage when it has no work, rather than the 50 percent it has been paying? [Para 3][Para 4]
2. Whether the company must reimburse the full 100 riels each worker paid for the pre-employment medical check? [Para 4][Para 5]
3. Whether the company must raise wages by US$5 a month for workers in the hygiene and box packing sections? [Para 5][Para 6]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, C.CAWDU):** the 50 percent cut is unlawful because a suspension for lack of work was never placed under the Labour Inspector, so full wages are owed; the medical check is a legal obligation whose cost falls on the employer, the more so as workers lost time, transport money and bonus in attending; and the hygiene and packing sections, some there since 1996, get only US$45 a month and no overtime. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 23][Para 24]
**Employer (respondent):** the 50 percent practice is written into its internal work rules and was a kindness while business was slow; from now on it will suspend properly, paying 50 percent to fixed-duration workers and all benefits to those on undetermined-duration contracts; it will pay 75 percent of the medical check although the cost is the worker's; and the hygiene and packing staff do not attend to their tasks. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 27]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must pay the full wage even when there is no work, except where the contract is lawfully suspended under Article 71; must provide 50 percent of wages where a suspension is made under Article 71(11); and must reimburse the medical examination cost of 100 riels to each worker once the award takes effect. The US$5 increase was refused. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53]
On the first issue the panel applied Notification 017 of MoSALVY and Article 104, which guarantee a minimum wage of US$45 a month sufficient for a decent standard of living, and held that an employer in economic difficulty may suspend only under the Labour Inspectorate's supervision as Article 71(11) requires; Article 71(7) permits temporary layoff for valid reasons under internal regulations, but this factory's rules say nothing about temporary layoffs. It followed Hun Loyal Cambodia 21/03 of 8 December 2003 and M & A Cambodia Garment 46/04 of 5 July 2004, which require full wages where the suspension does not comply with Article 71(11). Where suspension is lawful, clause 7 of the fixed-duration contracts — 50 percent of wages — applies, and by equity it extends to workers moved to undetermined-duration contracts without a written contract, since they worked under the same conditions for a year. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 35][Para 36][Para 37][Para 38]
On the medical check the panel relied on Article 247(C-4) of the Labour Law and clause 7 of Joint Prakas No. 09/MoSALVA of 19 January 1994, under which the cost of an employee's medical examination is paid by the owner of the enterprise, and followed Chou Sing 02/03, Loyal Cambodia 21/03, Kbal Koh II 19/04 and Kong Hong 53/04. That the worker paid first and produced the receipt does not discharge the employer, and the Prakas survives because it contradicts nothing in the 1997 Law. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 41][Para 42]
On the US$5 rise the panel found the sections already at the minimum wage with seniority pay under Notification 17/00 and Article 104, held the demand to be an interests rather than a rights dispute, and refused to consider it because C.CAWDU lacks most representative status — a status it admitted lacking at the hearing. Only a union registered with the most members and meeting Article 277 may conclude a collective bargaining agreement for all workers, and an award on interests would bind the parties for a year under Article 43 of Prakas 99/2004. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44][Para 47][Para 49][Para 50]
The arbitrator chosen by the employer, Kao Thach, appended a dissent agreeing with the orders but rejecting the reliance on Joint Prakas 09 of 1994, which in his view fell with the 1992 Labour Law by implied abrogation, the Article 247(d)(4) reasoning being sufficient on its own. [Para 55][Para 57][Para 60]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Full wages are owed for days the employer simply has no work, unless it has suspended the contract under Article 71(11) with the Labour Inspectorate's supervision; a contractual 50 percent clause cannot substitute for that procedure, though once a lawful suspension exists the clause governs and, by equity, carries over to workers whose fixed-duration contracts became undetermined-duration ones without a written contract. The employer bears the cost of pre-employment medical examinations under Article 247(C-4) and Joint Prakas 09 of 19 January 1994, and a union without most representative status cannot bring an interests claim for the whole workforce. [Para 35][Para 38][Para 41][Para 50]

**Obiter dicta:** the 50 percent practice in the internal work rules was characterised as an attempt to help workers while business was slow, and the panel observed that an award settling an interests dispute would stand in place of a collective bargaining agreement for one year. [Para 25][Para 45]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The non-conciliation report is dated 13 January 2004 and said to have reached the Secretariat on 15 January 2004, months before the June complaint and July conciliations; both dates are reported as printed. The medical sum is irreconcilable: 100 riels is ordered and reimbursed, but the findings describe "75% of 100 riels equal to 7500 riels". The internal work rules are said to set the 50 percent practice at ¶25 and to say nothing about temporary layoffs at ¶34. The Prakas is dated 19 January 1994 in the reasoning and 19 January 2004 in the dissent; Article 395 appears as "Article 95"; Article 247 is printed "(C-4)", "(d)(4)" and "(6)". Two consecutive paragraphs repeat verbatim. The employer's manager, the union president and the union secretary are redacted here as Mr S, Mr M and Mr C. [Para 8][Para 25][Para 30][Para 34][Para 41][Para 42][Para 57]
