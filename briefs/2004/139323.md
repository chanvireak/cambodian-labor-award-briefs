# Award No. 47/04 — The Day Young (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 7 July 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators: Kao Thach (employer party), Vuong Vanna (worker party), Kong Phallak (chair) · Doc ID 139323

## 1. Facts
Day Young (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, National Road 5, Russey Keo district, employed 826 workers. A leave-request slip required workers to ask permission to leave at 4:00pm, which the union said forced overtime; overtime ran until 8:00pm or 10:00pm without transport home. After a strike from 16 to 23 June 2004 and unsuccessful conciliation by the district labour inspectorate, four of eight demands remained, including a demand to dismiss a supervisor and a former union president. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 53][Para 103]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must eliminate unconditionally the letter of request for leave at 4:00pm? [Para 29][Para 53]
2. Whether the factory must transport workers home when they work overtime until 8:00pm? [Para 30][Para 53]
3. Whether the factory must dismiss Ms K, the supervisor, and Ms S, the former union president, for serious misconduct? [Para 31][Para 53]
4. Whether the factory must maintain payment of full wages and bonuses for the period of the strike? [Para 34][Para 55]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The punch-out slip traps workers who finish at 4:00pm; they are pressed by supervisors to stay, so only an assurance that no one is forced to work overtime will do. Night travel is unsafe and other factories such as Seng Yong, Chu Tsing and City New provide transport. Ms K kicked, slapped and insulted three workers, on 23 May 2003 and 19 May 2004, and Ms S was corrupt and too close to management; both should be dismissed. Wages and bonuses should continue through the strike. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 68][Para 82][Para 83][Para 103]

**Employer (respondent):** The slip is company policy required by the buyer after 11 September 2001 and eases management of the line; no one is forced, and the owner proposed listing monthly volunteers for overtime. It pays 2,000 riels for food up to 6:00pm and 8:00pm, will transport workers leaving after 9:30 or 10:00pm, and is a new company losing USD30,000 to 40,000 a year. The dismissal claim is unproved, and no wages are due for an illegal strike. [Para 59][Para 67][Para 70][Para 72][Para 73][Para 103]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Partly granted — the company must follow the settlement agreement reached at the hearing, transport night workers who finish at 8:00pm, remove Ms K from her current position and move her to another as promised at the hearing, and the workers' claim for wages and bonuses during the strike is rejected. [Para 141][Para 142][Para 143][Para 144]

On the first issue the parties settled: the leave-request slip is kept, workers are listed each month as willing or unwilling to work overtime, a worker listed as unwilling may switch for the following month, and the group supervisor must give the slip to any worker who does not wish to work overtime. The panel recorded this as an agreement rather than imposing a rule. [Para 111][Para 141]

On transport the panel found a conflict of norms: paragraph (b) of Prakas No. 80 of 1 March 1999 fixes night work from 10:00pm to 5:00am, whereas Article 144 paragraph 1 of the Labour Law defines night as at least eleven consecutive hours including the interval between 10:00pm and 5:00am. By the hierarchy of norms the law prevails over a Prakas or Anukret, so the Labour Law governed; because the company had fixed no eleven-hour band, night work was counted from 7:00pm to 6:00am. Article 6 of Prakas No. 80 requires enterprises to transport night workers when they finish at night, so transport was owed to those finishing at 10:00pm as demanded — the order records 8:00pm. [Para 112][Para 114][Para 115][Para 117][Para 118][Para 119]

On the dismissals the panel held it has consistently found itself not entitled to order an employer to dismiss any worker (Lida #04/03, Chu Tsing #14/03, Huo Hing #17/03, Chou Tsing #06/04, Lucky Zone #15/04, Yada Printing #16/04, Y Tsing #32/04, Full Well U #34/04), the exception being an employee whose conduct endangers others' health or security (Chu Tsing #14/03). A labour contract is ordinary law under Decree No. 38 of 28 October 1988, Article 22 of which allows amendment only by consent; only the parties may terminate, and even proven serious misconduct under Article 83 gives the employer a right, not a duty, to dismiss. The employer had admitted Ms K was at fault and agreed to move her, and against Ms S there was no strong evidence beyond her resignation letter. [Para 123][Para 124][Para 129][Para 132][Para 134][Para 136]

On the strike the panel applied Article 332: a strike suspends the contract, allowance for work is not provided and salary is not paid. Article 334 would require payment where the employer recruits replacements, but the panel did not need to apply it because the strike was illegal, so withholding wages and bonuses was just. [Para 137][Para 138][Para 139]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An arbitration panel cannot order an employer to dismiss or remove a worker, because hiring and dismissal belong to the parties to the labour contract alone and Article 83 confers a right to dismiss rather than a duty; the exception is a worker whose conduct endangers the health or security of others, and short of that the panel may only record a disciplinary transfer the employer itself conceded. [Para 123][Para 124][Para 132]

**Obiter dicta:** That where a Prakas on night work contradicts Article 144 of the Labour Law the law prevails by hierarchy of norms, and that an employer's duty to transport night workers under Article 6 of Prakas No. 80 depends on the hours actually fixed as night. [Para 114][Para 118]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The reasons conclude that transport is owed to workers finishing at 10:00pm, but the order fixes 8:00pm — the same discrepancy appears in the reported demands, which shift from 8:00pm to 10:00pm [Para 30][Para 119][Para 141]; the decision refers to a settlement agreement made at a hearing "commencing on 1 July 2004" although the hearing sat on 25 June 2004 [Para 39][Para 141]; the supervisor's name is printed as "Mak Kuntheand", "Mak Kunthea" and, in the representatives' list, "Mak Sokunthea" [Para 4][Para 54][Para 77]; the header address block is destroyed by OCR, reduced to fragments such as "100 @-@ 100" and repeated bracket marks [Para 1]; the leave claim is described in the issues as "eliminate unconditionally" but in the summary as a request kept subject to conditions [Para 29][Para 64]; and one eye-witness is referred to as "Mr C Sony" in the findings though the party list records "Chhin Sony" [Para 15][Para 88].
