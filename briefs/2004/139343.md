# Award No. 78/04 — A.I.A Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 September 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun, employer-appointed; Tuon Siphann, worker-appointed; Kong Phallak, chairman) · non-binding award by the parties' decision at the hearing · Doc ID 139343

## 1. Facts
A.I.A Co., Ltd, Street 265, Sangkat Teuklark, Khan Tuol Kork, Phnom Penh, employed 330 workers and sub-contracted work from other factories since 1997. Its workers, through the Khmer Youth Union of A.I.A. Factory, complained to the Labour Inspection Department on 19 August 2004 on eight points; conciliation on 27 August 2004 settled four, and the non-conciliation report No. 2310 L.V.T/LI of 1 September 2004 sent four issues to the Council: medical check fees of 15,000 riel, proper overtime rates and back pay, equal benefits for casual workers, and full wages when the factory had no work. The hearing was held on 7 September 2004. [Para 16][Para 20][Para 5][Para 6]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay 15,000 riel for each worker's medical check? [Para 5]
2. Whether the company must pay 1.5 times wages for overtime on normal days and reimburse workers for past overtime payment? [Para 6]
3. Whether the company must give casual workers the same benefits as regular workers and reimburse past benefits? [Para 6]
4. Whether the company must pay full basic wages when there is no work and reimburse past wages? [Para 6]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The company should bear the medical check fee; overtime should be paid at the legal rate with three years' back pay, workers having understood that the law allowed claims within three years, while Notification 017/01 and Article 390 let them keep the better practice; casual workers, some having worked two days a month for a year, should receive regular-worker benefits of US$45 a month; and full wages were due when there was no work, since that was not their fault. [Para 28][Para 32][Para 34][Para 40]
**Employer (respondent):** About 248 workers had agreed to pay their own medical checks and the company followed the majority; it applied a one-plus-one formula paying many workers more than the law requires, asked for a list of underpaid workers, and said the overpaid should return thousands of dollars; casual status depended on skill and about ten casuals had already become regular; and it would pay full wages only to workers who attended and did other work such as cleaning other than toilets, half wages otherwise. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 29][Para 30][Para 36][Para 42]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to reimburse 15,000 riel of medical check fees to workers checked in 2004 by 10 November 2004, the 1997 claim being rejected; to pay overtime at not less than 1.5 times normal wage by day and double at night or on weekly days off, with three years' back pay from September 2001 to August 2004; to pay casual workers the regular minimum wage, bonuses and overtime pro rata, leave and holiday pay pro rata, and back pay at US$1.73 per day for those paid 5,000 riels a day; on no-work days, 100 per cent for workers who attend and do other work and half wages otherwise, plus 10,000 riel to each worker previously given days off at half pay; and rejected the claim for full back pay over those half-pay days. [Para 108][Para 110][Para 112][Para 113]

On issue 1 the panel held that Article 247(c) of the Labour Law 1997 and Prakas 09/94 on medical checks require the employer to pay, Article 7 of the Prakas obliging the owner of a factory to bear the fee. Prakas 09/94, though made under the 1992 law, does not contradict the 1997 law and was not abrogated under Article 395. Following Awards 02/03 (Chou Sing), 53/03 (Kong Hong) and 60/04 (United Art), the panel found Article 247(c) sufficient, so workers paying for themselves did not release the employer, and their self-payment agreements were void under Article 13 as contrary to public order. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46][Para 47][Para 49][Para 52][Para 61]

The 1997 fees failed on limitation: Article 135 of the Law on Contract and Responsibility Outside the Contract (No. 38 of 1998) allows three years to sue for damages, and under Article 93 of the Constitution everyone is taken to know the law once in force, so ignorance was no answer and that claim expired in 2000. [Para 54][Para 56][Para 58][Para 59]

On issue 2 the panel held Article 139 and Article 5 of Prakas 80/99 require 150 per cent for daytime overtime and 200 per cent at night from 10:00, and that pieceworkers, for whom no article prescribes a formula, are entitled to the 50 per cent increase: monthly wages divided by total hours gives the average hourly rate, half of which is multiplied by overtime hours and added to total wages, with a floor of US$45 plus overtime and the US$5 bonus, the employer paying whichever calculation gives more. For casual workers US$45 divided by 26 days gave US$1.73 a day and US$0.3245 an hour by day, US$0.4327 at night. Back pay ran three years under Article 120, and overpayments could not be reclaimed, since benefits better than the law are preserved under Article 13 and no miscalculation was shown. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 65][Para 66][Para 69][Para 77][Para 80][Para 86][Para 90][Para 91]

On issue 3 the panel applied Article 10, under which casual workers enjoy the same rights as regular workers save for separately stipulated clauses; nothing separately provided for them except the 5,000 riel daily wage, below the US$45 per 26 days minimum, so they were owed the minimum wage, attendance bonus, proportionate overtime, leave and holiday pay, following 26/04 (Sport Wear) and 30/04 (Honey Wear), plus three years' back pay. A hearing demand that casuals of more than two months' service be made regular fell outside Article 312-1, not being in the report. [Para 92][Para 94][Para 95][Para 96][Para 97]

On issue 4 the panel found the half-wage practice for no-work days was neither contractual nor in the internal rules but an industry practice, that the contract was not validly suspended under Article 71 and workers had no option to take other work, so wages were owed; but since workers had accepted the practice for years without complaint, full restitution would not be correct, and under Article 34 of the Prakas the just settlement was 10,000 riel per affected worker, the employer's undertaking to follow industry practice being reasonable. [Para 98][Para 102][Para 103][Para 105][Para 106][Para 107]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer must bear the cost of its workers' medical checks under Article 247(c) and Prakas 09/94, and an agreement that a worker pay for his own check is void under Article 13 as contrary to public order, the claim remaining subject to the three-year limitation running from payment. And where an employer pays pieceworkers or casual workers on a formula of its own, it must still guarantee that no worker receives less than the statutory calculation of overtime and minimum wage, and cannot later reclaim amounts by which it overpaid. [Para 47][Para 61][Para 54][Para 84][Para 90]

**Obiter dicta:** That the half-wage practice for no-work days is industry practice, not contract or internal rule, and that the workers' long acquiescence made full restitution inequitable, equity under Article 34 of the Prakas producing a 10,000 riel payment. [Para 101][Para 104][Para 105]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel composition does not match the signatures: the employer-appointed arbitrator is composed as "Seng Vuoch Hun" but signs as "Ouk Ry", and the chair appears as "Kong Phallak" here and "Kong Phallack" elsewhere. The dates are irreconcilable — the award is dated 22 September 2004 and the hearing was 7 September 2004, yet the non-conciliation report is said to be dated 1 September 2004 and submitted to the Secretariat on 29 September 2004, a week after the award, while the case summary gives submission on 1 September 2004. The signature block carries OCR debris, Khmer characters and the stray years "2016" and "2017" beside each name. Paragraphs 96 and 98 fuse citations into the prose ("Shine Well and 66/04", "52/04- Kong Hong, 55/04 - Yu Chheng"). The union president is named in full in the party list and the facts, and is redacted here as Mr K. [Para 9][Para 12][Para 20][Para 114][Para 115][Para 98][Para 96][Para 5][Para 26]
