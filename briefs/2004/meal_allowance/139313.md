# Award No. 32/04 — Cambodian Ecent Garment Factory Knitting Co., Ltd v Khmer Youth Federation (Arbitration Council, 8 June 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach, employer-chosen; Venn Pov, worker-chosen; Kong Phallack, chair) · Doc ID 139313

## 1. Facts
About 160 workers at Cambodian Ecent Garment Factory Knitting Co., Ltd, Phum AngKeo, National Route 4, Khum Kantauk, Ang Snuol, Kandal province, struck on 21 May 2004 behind a ten-point claim; nine points were settled at meetings on 21 and 22 May 2004 and one remained. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 14] The unresolved demand was that the company dismiss the weaving team leader, Mr Y, on the ground that he spoke and behaved improperly towards his subordinates, mocked them and kicked them in front of them. [Para 15][Para 16] The employer refused, saying there was no clear evidence or witness, and the panel ordered the strike stopped and the workers back at their shifts by 14:00 on 24 May 2004. [Para 17][Para 19] The parties agreed in the hearing that the award would not be immediately binding. [Para 11]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must dismiss the weaving team leader, Mr Y, for behaving and speaking improperly towards the workers in his team? [Para 4][Para 16]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the team leader mocks and kicks subordinates in front of them and asked workers for 2,000 riels when they asked him to repair a machine, so he should be dismissed. [Para 16][Para 30]
**Employer (respondent):** the allegations are unsupported by evidence or witnesses, the team leader denies any intention to harm, and the company will not dismiss him; the matter was referred to the competent authority. [Para 17][Para 26][Para 32]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel decided against the workers' demand that the company dismiss the weaving team leader, and ordered Mr Y to write a letter of apology and post it on the board, as he had promised before the panel at the hearing. [Para 41][Para 43]

The panel began with the evidence and found it wanting: the worker party produced no proof of improper behaviour, and the only witness from the weaving team, Ms S, said she had merely heard the rumour and had never been treated that way, and had put her fingerprint on the dismissal letter because she was ordered to. [Para 20][Para 23][Para 25] Mr Y admitted making fun of his team but said he did not realise they regarded it as misconduct, and denied meaning to demand money for repairs, which he carried out without payment; no contrary evidence was offered. [Para 26][Para 32][Para 33]

The panel then held that it has no power to order an employer to terminate a worker's contract, following Lida 04/03, Chou Sing 14/03, Ho Hing 17/03 and 18/03, Chou Sing 06/04, Luckyzone 15/04 and Yada Printing 16/04, subject only to the exception recognised in Chou Sing 14/03 where the worker's conduct affects the safety and health of others. [Para 36][Para 37] Under Art. 65 the employment contract binds the parties to it, so hiring and dismissal are exclusively the employer's, and even where a worker commits serious misconduct under Art. 83 the law confers a right to dismiss rather than an obligation. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40] The demand was accordingly unreasonable and contrary to the contract-termination procedure. [Para 35]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A workers' demand that an employer dismiss a colleague is not a claim the panel can grant: hiring and dismissal belong exclusively to the employer under Art. 65, and even serious misconduct under Art. 83 gives the employer a right to dismiss, never a duty. The panel will intervene only where the impugned worker's conduct endangers the safety or health of others, and otherwise will confine itself to remedies the employer itself has offered, such as a posted apology. [Para 36][Para 39][Para 40]

**Obiter dicta:** an apology posted on the notice board is an appropriate response to admitted mockery that the actor did not understand to be misconduct; and the panel recorded the absence of any witness who had personally suffered the alleged kicking. [Para 23][Para 26][Para 43]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is dated 8 June 2004 and the hearing ran 2 June 2004 from 2:00pm to 4:00pm. A registration certificate in the employer's evidence is dated 22 September 2004, more than three months after the award at line 8. The conciliation report appears as dated 31 May 2004 at line 9 but 21 May 2004 at line 10, and the non-conciliation report as dated 24 May 2004 in both places. The precedents are cited inconsistently, "Ho Hing - 17/03 and 18/03" at line 36 against "Ho Hing 17/03 and 18/04" at line 41. The sum demanded by the team leader is printed "2 000 Riels" with a space at line 30. Binding effect is stated two ways: the parties agreed the award is not immediately binding at line 11, while line 46 makes it binding after 8 days unless opposition is lodged and adds that a party may then go to court or strike or lock out. OCR damage is heavy ("the" to "he", "employees" to "mployees"), the worker witness is reduced to a bare initial at line 23, and the employer's representative list at lines 2 to 4 runs the name and post across separate lines.
