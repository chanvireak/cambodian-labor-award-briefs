# Award No. 64/04 — Mercury Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 27 August 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phallack presiding; Kao Thach appointed by the employer; Ven Pov appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 139334

## 1. Facts
Mercury Garment Co., Ltd ("MG") is a garment factory at No. 365, Tomnop Thmei Boeng Tompon Street, Dangkao district, Phnom Penh, six months in operation and employing 530 workers on contracts of undetermined duration. [Para 22][Para 25][Para 26] Workers complained to the Dangkao district Labour Inspection Office on 13 July 2004; conciliation on 30 July 2004 settled one of the two issues raised, leaving the reimbursement of medical check costs. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reimburse medical check costs to all workers, including the 230 workers who paid 10,100 riels each for a check taken before they were hired? [Para 13][Para 27]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** every worker should be reimbursed, including the 230 examined before employment who paid from their own money and handed the invoices to the administration manager in exchange for employment cards. [Para 13][Para 27]
**Employer (respondent):** it reimburses only checks carried out after employment began and will not reimburse pre-employment checks; Article 3 of its Internal Work Rules requires a check before service but does not say who pays. It offered to reimburse all workers not yet examined, and the workers consented. [Para 14][Para 29]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer must reimburse 10,100 riel to each worker who paid for his or her own medical check and must pay for the checks of workers not yet examined, within seven days after the award comes into effect. [Para 40][Para 41]
The panel grounded the obligation on Article 247(c) of the 1997 Labour Law, which requires the employer to establish and provide medical examinations at its own expense, and held that Article 247 alone is sufficient authority even though the Prakas contemplated by it had not been issued. [Para 31][Para 32][Para 33] Article 7 of Joint Prakas No. 09 of 19 January 1994 independently requires the enterprise to pay for its workers' checks, and the panel followed its earlier awards 02/03 (Chou Sing Garment), 21/03 (Loyal Garment), 19/04 (Kbal Koh), 53/04 (Kong Hong), 60/04 (United Art) and 63/04 (Shine Well). [Para 30][Para 35] Because the panel had recorded contrasting opinions on whether Joint Prakas 09/04 survived the 1997 Labour Law, it rested the operative holding on Article 247 rather than on the Prakas. [Para 34][Para 36] It held that a worker paying the clinic first does not discharge the employer's obligation, since the cost was the employer's from the outset. [Para 37][Para 38] Article 377, punishing breach of Article 247 by a fine of 120 to 360 days of base daily wage and one to five years' imprisonment, confirmed that the duty is a legal one and not a matter of the parties' preference. [Para 39]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer bears the cost of the medical examination required of a worker on recruitment; where the worker paid the clinic directly, the employer must reimburse the sum actually paid, and an internal work rule that makes the check a condition of hiring cannot shift that cost onto the worker. [Para 38][Para 40]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel left open whether Joint Prakas No. 09 of 19 January 1994 remained in force after the 1997 Labour Law, resting the decision on Article 247 alone, and its recital of the Article 377 criminal penalties was not necessary to the order to reimburse. [Para 34][Para 39]
