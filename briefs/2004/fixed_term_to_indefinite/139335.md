# Award No. 66/04 — Winner Garment (Arbitration Council, 10 September 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phallack presiding; Kao Thach appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 139335

## 1. Facts
Winner Garment, a factory of 300 workers on Luek Thmey Road, Kaab Commune, Dangkao District, Phnom Penh, faced a 12-point complaint lodged with the district Labour Inspection Office on 15 July 2004. [Para 26][Para 27] Conciliation on 05 August 2004 settled five of the twelve points; seven went to the panel, and five of those seven were then settled by agreement at the hearing of 26 August 2004, where both parties accepted the award as binding. [Para 28][Para 30][Para 26] Three claims remained: the shop steward elections, travel costs for terminated probationary workers, and the reinstatement of the union president. [Para 30][Para 58]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must send workers for health checks at its own expense? [Para 4][Para 5]
2. Whether the company must employ probationary workers for two months and repay past underpayments within one week? [Para 5][Para 6]
3. Whether the company must pay all benefits on termination of undetermined-duration contracts within seven days, failing which fixed-duration contracts must be discarded? [Para 8][Para 9]
4. Whether the shop steward elections of 26 April must be re-run because the union president was not informed of them? [Para 10][Para 30]
5. Whether the company must pay the return journey home of probationary workers whose contracts it terminated? [Para 11][Para 52]
6. Whether Mr S, the union president, must be accepted back at a basic salary of US$90 per month without a fixed-duration contract? [Para 12][Para 58]
7. Whether the company must pay full wages to workers on days when it has no work for them? [Para 15][Para 16]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company should bear the whole cost of health checks and of the return journey of terminated probationers; it underpaid probationers and owed severance on undetermined-duration contracts, to be paid within seven days or the fixed-duration contracts abandoned; new shop steward elections were needed because the union president knew nothing of the April elections; and Mr S should return at US$90 without a fixed-duration contract. [Para 4][Para 8][Para 11][Para 10][Para 12]
**Employer (respondent):** it could pay only 5 per cent of the health check procedures, having no means for more; it would repay underpaid wages within two months and severance within two months; it had held the shop steward elections lawfully with one week's notice to all workers including the union vice-president; it would pay travel costs only where it was required to, would take Mr S back only at US$60 on a fixed-duration contract, and would pay 100 per cent of wages only to workers who attend for eight hours. [Para 5][Para 8][Para 11][Para 14][Para 16]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim for new shop steward elections is rejected; the company is ordered to pay the travel cost of probationary workers it terminated where they were recruited far from the workplace and the company had taken responsibility for transporting them there; and it is ordered to accept Mr S back on an undetermined-duration contract at US$60 per month from July 2004. [Para 66] Claims 1, 2, 3 and 7 were disposed of by the parties' agreement at the hearing and were not decided. [Para 30]
On jurisdiction the panel held that Article 298 of the Labour Law, which sends disputes over the election, eligibility and fairness of shop steward elections to the Labour Court, does not give the court sole jurisdiction — unlike Article 337, which expressly reserves strike legality to the courts — and that neither the Arbitration Council nor a labour inspector is barred from resolving such a dispute before it reaches a court. [Para 40][Para 42][Para 46] It also relied on Article 12 of Prakas 99/04, on the collective character of the dispute under Article 302, and on the parties' unreserved acceptance of binding arbitration at the hearing. [Para 43][Para 39][Para 44]
On issue 4 the panel found the union had shown no procedural defect under Article 299 or Prakas 286/01, and that under that Prakas a company need only discuss shop steward elections with unions holding representative status, which under Article 277 requires registration with the Ministry — registration the union at Winner had not obtained. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 51]
On issue 5 the panel distinguished Article 68, which covers travel to and from work during probation, from Article 188, which governs the return trip at the end of a contract and applies to probationers as much as to regular workers, but only where the worker was recruited far away and the employer paid the original journey. [Para 52][Para 54][Para 55] The union supplied no names, identity numbers or numbers of such workers, so only the conditional order could be made. [Para 56][Para 57]
On issue 6 the panel held that Article 293 of the Labour Law and Article 7 of Prakas 313/02 protect the three main union leaders as they protect shop stewards, so dismissal required the Labour Inspector's authorisation; the company terminated Mr S and paid him severance without that authorisation, in breach of both instruments, so his undetermined-duration contract continued and he was to be reinstated. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65] The US$60 rate was fixed because that was the wage he actually received in June 2004 and had accepted at the hearing. [Para 36][Para 60][Para 66]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A dismissal of a union president, vice-president or shop steward without prior authorisation from the Labour Inspector is ineffective, and the worker's undetermined-duration contract continues with the wage actually being paid at the date of the unlawful termination. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 66] The panel may decide a shop steward election dispute notwithstanding Article 298, and a company need not consult a union on shop steward elections unless that union has registered representative status with the Ministry. [Para 46][Para 51] Return travel on termination is owed under Article 188 only where the worker was recruited at a distance and the employer paid the journey to the workplace, and the claimant must identify the workers concerned. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 57]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel declined to rule on the legitimacy of the company's wider strategy of terminating all workers, paying severance and rehiring them all on fixed-duration contracts, because that was not in dispute. [Para 61][Para 62]

[Para 19][Para 20][Para 30][Para 21][Para 35] The non-conciliation report is dated and sent 24 May 2004 at para 17, which precedes the 15 July 2004 complaint and the 05 August 2004 conciliation, and differs from the transmission date of 12 August 2004 at para 29. [Para 17][Para 27][Para 28][Para 29] The shop stewards Prakas is printed 266/01 at para 40 and 286/01 at paras 48 and 49. [Para 40][Para 48] Mr S's wage is US$90 per month at paras 34 and 58 but he is said at para 60 to have been paid US$60 for six months. [Para 34][Para 58][Para 60] The union vice-president appears as "Hong Sokhon" at para 4 and "Hong Sokha" at para 31, and the union president as Sim, Sem and "Some Phally". [Para 4][Para 31][Para 14] Paras 41 and 42 repeat their own text, paras 19 to 22 repeat evidence entries, and para 4 lists "4- Sier Son" twice. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 19][Para 4] The employer's own name appears as "Winner Garment" and "Winner Garments". [Para 26][Para 30]
