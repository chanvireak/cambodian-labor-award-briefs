# Award No. 01/04 — New Point Two Garment Industry (Arbitration Council, February 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Matthew Rendall presiding; Kao Thach, employer nominee; Liv Sovanna, worker nominee) · Doc ID 138839

## 1. Facts
New Point Two Garment Industry, a subcontractor for other garment factories with around 300 employees, was started about a year before the award by the owner of the adjacent New Point One Garment. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 18][Para 23] The employer regularly suspends groups of usually 20 to 30 workers for one or two weeks, always saying there is not enough work, and pays them nothing during the suspension; some 30 workers were suspended at the time of the claim. [Para 15][Para 21][Para 22][Para 24] The workers, members of the Coalition of Cambodian Apparel W.D.U., complained that they should receive 50% of salary and other benefits during suspension. [Para 1][Para 8] No worker had a written contract or a copy of the internal rules, and the employer did not appear at the hearing of 21 January 2004 despite notice. [Para 11][Para 18]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether employees suspended by the employer must be paid 50% of their salary during the period of suspension? [Para 8]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer suspends them in rotating groups for lack of orders and pays nothing at all; industry practice, including at New Point One Garment under the same owner, is to pay 50% of basic salary during suspension, and they want that plus other benefits. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 26][Para 34]
**Employer (respondent):** it stated that there will be no pay during suspension and that 50% is paid only after two years of employment; it did not attend the hearing, sent no evidence, and did not supply its internal rules or sample contracts when asked. [Para 25][Para 11][Para 29]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand for 50% of the basic wage during suspension was denied, but the suspensions themselves were declared invalid: the employer must cease suspending contracts except for the reasons in Article 71, reinstate all suspended workers from the date of the award, and pay 100% of wages and benefits for the suspension period running from December 19, 2003, with the amount fixed by agreement within one month or, failing that, by an accountant engaged at the employer's expense. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 51]
The panel treated the "floating worker" arrangement — regular unpaid suspension of groups when orders are short — as a status unknown to the Labour Law: because these workers were employed for long or undetermined periods they could not be casual workers under Article 9, and Articles 10 and 104 guarantee all employees, casual included, minimum wage and working conditions. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 39][Para 40] It followed its award in Tonga Garment Co., Ltd. v Union (Case 03/03) that such workers must be on probationary, fixed-duration or undetermined-duration contracts. [Para 40][Para 41]
Article 71 permits suspension only on its listed grounds; the first ten did not apply, Article 71.7 was unavailable because the employer had no internal rules as Article 29 requires, and Article 71.11 covers a serious economic or material difficulty suspending the enterprise's operation for no more than two months, not routine suspension of groups whenever orders fall short — especially as there was no evidence the suspensions were placed under Labour Inspector control. [Para 32][Para 41][Para 43][Para 44][Para 45] The panel held an employer may not circumvent employee protection by paying workers only when it has work, and ordered compensation as if the contracts had not been suspended. [Para 46]
On the 50% claim, the panel accepted that paying half wages during suspension is widespread in the industry, including at New Point One, but found no legal requirement and insufficient evidence to order it, and so denied that demand while granting full wages on the invalidity ground. [Para 34][Para 47][Para 48]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may suspend employment contracts only for a ground listed in Article 71 of the Labour Law; the absence of sufficient orders is not such a ground, and an unpaid rotating suspension used to keep a workforce available only when work exists is not a recognised contract status, so the suspended workers are entitled to their full wages and benefits as though no suspension had occurred. [Para 38][Para 45][Para 46]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that many employers, and New Point One Garment under the same owner, pay 50% of basic salary during suspension although this is not a legal requirement. [Para 26][Para 35]
