# Award No. 31/04 — Camintex Garment (Arbitration Council, June 28, 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Ouk Ry, Vong Vanna, Men Nimith · Doc ID 139316

## 1. Facts
Camintex Garment employed 700 workers and closed after strikes over public-holiday work and wage calculation; the panel found those issues caused the second strike [Para 20]. The first strike occurred on 14 February 2004 and the second on 24 and 25 March 2004 [Para 14]. The company closed on 27 March 2004, asserting that the strikes reduced production, caused buyer cancellations and significant profit loss [Para 14]. The panel found the employer closed for 26 days, terminated all 700 contracts, paid severance to all workers, and that the workers accepted the benefits [Para 21] [Para 23]. The factory reopened on 22 April 2004 [Para 15]. It accepted 450 of the former workers and proposed rehiring the rest by seniority and professional skill, except 12 workers alleged to have caused problems [Para 16] [Para 17]. The worker representatives identified those 12 as leaders of the Camintex Workers Solidarity Union, established on 13 February 2004 [Para 18]. The dispute was referred after failed conciliation [Para 8] [Para 12].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer was required to reinstate former union leaders and former workers after the factory reopened? [Para 8]
2. Whether the employer could refuse to rehire the 12 workers because they caused trouble and the factory closure? [Para 9]
3. Whether the refusal to rehire the 12 union leaders discriminated against union membership or activity? [Para 33]
4. Whether the employer proved misconduct justifying exclusion from rehiring rights after collective dismissal? [Para 28]
5. What rehiring priority and immediate reinstatement order should apply under Article 95 of the Labor Law? [Para 29]

## 3. Positions of the parties
The workers claimed that, because the factory reopened, former union leaders and former workers had to be reinstated [Para 8]. They argued that the 12 excluded workers were the elected union leadership and that the employer had agreed to rehire other remaining workers while excluding only those leaders [Para 33]. The employer refused to accept the union leaders, accusing them of making trouble and causing the closure [Para 9]. It said it was willing to reinstate former workers without discrimination based on union establishment [Para 10]. At hearing, the employer said it could accept all remaining workers as future need arose, except the 12 union leaders, whom it said caused many problems [Para 31]. Its representative conceded their union-leader status but denied discrimination [Para 33]. The employer offered no evidence of misconduct beyond repetition of the "problems" allegation [Para 34]. A later letter from workers at the factory rejected the 12, alleging incitement to demonstration and rule violations, but gave no details or proof [Para 36] [Para 41].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Partly granted. The employer must immediately reinstate 8 of the union leaders, list all remaining workers including the 4 remaining union leaders by seniority, and give them preference over new workers if it requires more workers within 2 years starting from April 1 2004 [Para 48].

The panel treated the closure and termination of all 700 contracts as a collective dismissal under Article 95 of the Labor Law [Para 24]. It read Article 95 as giving dismissed workers a two-year priority for the same position when the business improves and workers are needed [Para 25]. It accepted that workers who could lawfully have been dismissed for misconduct need not be rehired, but required proof [Para 26]. The permissible categories were serious misconduct within 7 days before collective termination, or misconduct for which dismissal would be proportionate within 15 days before that termination [Para 27]. The employer therefore had to prove the 12 fell into one of those categories [Para 28]. It did not [Para 34] [Para 43]. The workers' letter did not identify conduct proving serious misconduct under Article 83, gave no details, and did not show the matters were raised with the workers [Para 39] [Para 40] [Para 41]. The panel also held that Article 12 of the Labor Law bars taking union membership, leadership or activity into account in hiring decisions [Para 44]. Because two-thirds of the workforce had already been rehired, it ordered immediate reinstatement of at least 8 of the 12 and two-year priority for the remaining 4 [Para 45] [Para 46].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
After a collective dismissal, an employer that reopens and hires workers must apply the statutory rehiring priority; it may exclude a former worker only by proving disqualifying misconduct of the required kind and timing, not by bare allegations [Para 25] [Para 27] [Para 28]. Union leadership or activity cannot lawfully be used in the rehiring decision [Para 44]. Where exclusion is unproved but some workers have already been rehired, a panel may order immediate reinstatement of a proportion and preserve priority for the rest [Para 45] [Para 46].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that if workers committed acts for which the employer had the right to dismiss, they should not be required to be rehired after a collective layoff [Para 26].
