# Award No. 35/04 — Jacqsintex Industrial Cambodia Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 June 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ang Eng Thong presiding; Kao Thach appointed by the employer; Vong Vanna appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 139317

## 1. Facts
Jacqsintex Industrial Cambodia Co., Ltd employs 500 workers. [Para 21] On 17 May 2004 about 475 workers struck over working conditions — a canteen, permission to bring food into the factory, and the reinstatement of a worker dismissed that day — and the Labour Inspectors conciliated three of six issues. [Para 22][Para 23] The non-conciliation report No. 631/MoSALVY dated 27 May 2004 reached the Secretariat the same day and the hearing was held on 4 June 2004. [Para 17][Para 19] Before the hearing the company agreed to reinstate the dismissed worker, Mr R, and to warn him for the first time. [Para 24][Para 39]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must reinstate Mr R, whom it dismissed on 17 May 2004? [Para 8][Para 9]
2. Whether the company must build a proper canteen where workers can eat and rest? [Para 12][Para 13]
3. Whether the company must stop banning all workers from bringing cakes and food into the factory and act instead against the workers who litter? [Para 16][Para 14]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the narrow dusty road leaves them eating in dust, mud and bad smells, which affects their health and risks accidents, so a canteen is needed; they accept the ban inside the production building and promise to keep the grounds clean, and ask that only those who litter be sanctioned. [Para 25][Para 27][Para 29][Para 32][Para 34]
**Employer (respondent):** it has long planned a canteen but cannot afford the cost; food is allowed on the premises but not in the production areas, because scraps attract mice that destroy finished goods, and roughly 10 bins are provided. [Para 35][Para 37][Para 38]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must give the workers a clear date for building the canteen within three months of the award taking effect, must allow cakes and food onto the factory premises, and the workers must put waste and leftovers in the bins and keep food out of the production areas; the reinstatement demand is not decided because the parties settled it. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 42]
On issue 1 the panel declined to rule: an agreement of 17 May 2004 provided for reinstatement to the ironing section with a first warning, and dismissal on the third instance of misconduct. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40][Para 42]
On issue 2 the panel applied Article 229 of the Labour Law, which requires working conditions necessary for workers' health: with no canteen, meals are taken among dust, mud and traffic, producing disorder, bad sanitation and conditions contrary to that article. [Para 43] The employer's own account that the plan would be carried out when finances allowed showed it accepted the obligation, leaving only the timing undetermined, so the panel fixed a deadline of three months. [Para 44]
On issue 3 both parties agreed at conciliation and at the hearing that food may be brought onto the premises, that workers will not take it into production areas, and that the Internal Work Rules and the Labour Law apply to breaches; the panel recorded that as an agreement rather than deciding it. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer has no place for workers to eat and rest, so that meals are taken among dust, mud and moving trucks, the working conditions fall below those necessary for workers' health under Article 229 of the Labour Law; an employer that accepts the duty to build a canteen but names no date will be ordered to fix one within three months of the award taking effect. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 46]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the site has an unoccupied area where a canteen could be built without conflict, and that few and small bins had left workers recycling oil containers as bins. [Para 34][Para 35]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The case summary repeats itself: "Ltd employs 500 workers. On 17 May 2004 Jacqsintex ..." appears twice and the sentence breaks off before the strike. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 22] The non-conciliation report is dated 19 May 2004 in the evidence list and 27 May 2004 in the jurisdiction section, and the file is described as "No. 631/MoSALVY" but also as letter No. 631 from MoVLT. [Para 17][Para 19] The conciliation is said to have settled three of six issues, while only three issues are listed as in dispute. [Para 8][Para 23] The name "Jacqsintex" is used throughout but the award calls the parties' federation "C.CAWDU" and the union president is named only in the OCR. [Para 1][Para 5][Para 8] The heading "CASE SUMMARY" is followed by a fragment beginning "Ltd", losing the company name at the start of the sentence. [Para 20]
