# Award No. 55/04 — You Chheng Garment Factory (Arbitration Council, 23 August 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Kao Thach (chosen by the employer party), An Nan (chosen by the worker party), Kong Phallack (chair) · Doc ID 139329

## 1. Facts
You Chheng Garment Co., Ltd. (printed on the face of the award as "YOU CHHENGARMENT FACTORY") employed 650 workers, 410 of whom struck from 24 to 29 April 2004 demanding a higher ironing rate; an award of 11 May 2004 fixed that rate and was not opposed. On 4 June 2004 the company suspended 12 workers for two months — the union President and Vice-President and ten ironing workers — and on 2 August 2004 extended the suspension from 5 August onwards with no end date. The unresolved demands (regular status for long-serving casual workers, paid sick leave, the US$5 no-work bonus, and milk or a day-care centre) came to the panel after conciliation. [Para 19][Para 29][Para 30][Para 34]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company may suspend the 12 workers, including the union President and Vice-President, and extend that suspension indefinitely? [Para 29]
2. Whether casual workers employed for more than three months must be given regular-worker status? [Para 34]
3. Whether the company must pay wages during certified sick leave — 100 percent for the first month, 60 percent for the second and third, with employment maintained to the sixth month? [Para 34]
4. Whether the company must maintain the full US$5 monthly bonus whenever it has no work, regardless of how many days pass? [Para 36]
5. Whether the company must provide two cans of milk a month, or build a day-care centre, for workers with children 18 months or younger? [Para 37]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the 12 had neither incited the strike nor been lazy — the company simply had no work for them — and none had received any oral or written warning before suspension; casual workers past three months are regular workers; the US$5 bonus is owed for every workless day; two cans of milk cost US$6.30 each. [Para 30][Para 34][Para 36][Para 37]
**Employer (respondent):** the 12 were ringleaders of an illegal strike that cost US$35,000, and the casual contracts were short-term arrangements the workers themselves asked to resume; no law or internal rule supports paid sick leave, which it cannot afford; the bonus scale of US$5/US$3/US$1 follows the agreement of 8 May 2002; it can pay only for small cans costing under US$3, and would withdraw the breastfeeding hour if pressed. [Para 22][Para 33][Para 35][Para 37]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must reinstate the 12 workers on the award coming into force and pay them full wages from 4 June 2004 until they return; give regular status to all workers who have worked regularly for at least two months; insert paid sick leave clauses in its Internal Work Rules and negotiate the rates; maintain the full US$5 bonus regardless of workless days, except where the worker is absent; and build a day-care centre and breastfeeding room with one hour off, or pay day-care bills. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 84][Para 85][Para 86]
On suspension, Article 71 allows suspension for a lawful reason and Article 71(7) allows it to take disciplinary action under the employer's internal work rules, but these rules say nothing about suspension beyond seven days as a disciplinary measure, so 71(7) was unavailable; Article 27 requires any disciplinary action to be proportionate, and a suspension with no defined period is not. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43][Para 45]
On casual workers, Articles 66, 68 and 9 define casual work as work needing only a short period, and probation cannot exceed three months, so workers kept on beyond that period cannot be treated as casual; Articles 10 and 104 entitle all workers to minimum wage and lawful conditions, and the company's short-term contracts were not valid. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 53][Para 54]
On sick leave, Article 71(3) and Article 72(1) suspend the contract for up to six months without obliging wages, but Notification 14 of 16 August 2002 channels paid sick leave into internal work rules, and awards 26/03 (Sport Wear) and 24/03 set the pattern of 100 percent, 60 percent, 60 percent, then no wage with employment maintained. The company's rules being silent, it must add the clause; the rates were left to negotiation. [Para 65][Para 68][Para 71][Para 73][Para 74]
On the bonus, the 8 May 2002 agreement's US$5/US$3/US$1 scale cannot override Notification 017 of 18 July 2000, Article 3, which guarantees regularly working workers at least US$5; under Article 13 a contract contrary to the Law is invalid, and days without work are not a suspension of the contract. [Para 74][Para 75][Para 76][Para 77]
On milk and child care, Article 186 requires an enterprise employing at least one hundred women to set up a nursing room and day-care centre; where it cannot build one it must pay the cost of care elsewhere. [Para 80][Para 82]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may suspend a labour contract as a disciplinary measure only where its written internal work rules authorise that measure and the suspension has a defined term proportionate to the misconduct; an indefinite suspension is unlawful, and the remedy is reinstatement with wages from the date of suspension. Work performed beyond the maximum probation period is regular employment whatever label the contract bears, and a contractual bonus scale that pays less than the statutory minimum yield for regular attendance is invalid. [Para 43][Para 45][Para 53][Para 63][Para 76]

**Obiter dicta:** part-time work is permitted, and a part-time worker is entitled to full-time benefits in proportion to the work performed; the Ministry of Labour is not obliged to issue any Prakas on paid sick leave, that subject belonging in internal work rules. [Para 62][Para 67]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's name is OCR-damaged in the header ("YOU CHHENGARMENT FACTORY") against "You Chheng Garment Co., Ltd." in the body; "US$D3" appears for US$3, and one sentence about Article 66 is printed twice. The award states at one point that conciliation settled one of three issues with two unresolved, and elsewhere that 17 points were agreed and nine remained — both are reported. The reasoning requires "two months, and more than 21 days per month" for regular status while the operative order says only "at least two months". Probation is described as up to three months, then as not exceeding two months for specialised workers. The company's loss is given as US$35,000 in its submissions and US$83,086.98 in its court claim of 3 August 2004; casual workers are counted as 130–140 and, from the employer's testimony, as 110. The third hearing is timed "2:00-5:00 am". The Administration Manager appears as "Henng Chanthou" and "Heng Chanthou" and is redacted here as Mr H.
