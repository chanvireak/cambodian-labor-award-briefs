# Award No. 16/04 — Yada Printing Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 29 March 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ly Tayseng (employer-chosen), An Nan (worker-chosen), Ang Eng Thong (chair) · Doc ID 139300

## 1. Facts
Yada Printing Co., Ltd prints at National Road 3, Ang Snoul District, Kandal Province, and employed 135 workers [Para 20]. On 9 March 2004 the Yada Printing Employees' Union complained to the provincial labour office demanding the dismissal of the administration manager, Mr S, for obscene language, contemptuous treatment of workers, failure to resolve grievances and anti-union discrimination [Para 21][Para 29]. Mediation on 10–11 March 2004 failed, the workers struck, and they returned on 13 March 2004 under an order of the Arbitration Council [Para 12][Para 26]. Both parties agreed at the hearing to a binding award [Para 28].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must dismiss Mr S, the administration manager, because he used obscene language, looked down on employees, never resolved their requests and deterred them from joining the union? [Para 10]
2. Whether the company must pay wages and regular bonuses for the days the employees were on strike? [Para 11]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the union collected a complaint and thumbprints against Mr S, alleging obscene language, threats to force overtime, indifference to a work accident and threats against union membership, and asserted that goods were transported to P & E Company to have another firm do the work during the strike [Para 18][Para 30].
**Employer (respondent):** it did not object to the complaint and acknowledged the employees' dissatisfaction, undertaking at the hearing to examine the manager's conduct seriously and decide in good faith [Para 30][Para 44]. On the strike it argued no replacements were hired and no work transferred, the goods having been moved by their owner because of the strike [Para 31][Para 32].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to make a decision responding to the employees' demand for Mr S's dismissal before the end of April 2004, and denied the demand for wages and other regular bonuses during the strike [Para 49][Para 50].

On the first issue the panel found as fact that the administration manager had committed the misconduct alleged: deterring the union from resolving employees' issues, threatening employees to work overtime, using obscene language, looking down on employees, ignoring a work accident and threatening employees not to join the union [Para 29][Para 30]. It nevertheless held, following Award 04/03 (Lida Garment), that Article 65 of the Labour Law establishes relations only between the contracting worker and employer, so hiring and terminating are the employer's rights, and the employer may dismiss a worker for misconduct under Article 83 [Para 35][Para 37][Para 38]. Employees may complain about a manager, but dismissal or a strict disciplinary sanction remains the employer's prerogative [Para 40]. Because the employer itself recognised the discontent and its likely effect on production, and had promised at the hearing to act, the panel gave it until the end of April 2004 to decide rather than ordering dismissal [Para 41][Para 46].

On the second issue Article 332 provides that a strike suspends the labour contract, so salary and the work allowance are not paid [Para 46]. Article 334 creates an exception requiring wages where the employer recruited new employees to replace strikers, except recruitment to ensure minimum services [Para 48]. The panel found no replacements had been hired and no work transferred to another factory, the owner having removed goods because of the strike, and the employees had not proved otherwise [Para 31][Para 32][Para 49]. The employer therefore owed nothing for the strike days [Para 49].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A panel will not order the dismissal of a manager even where it finds the misconduct proved, because Article 65 leaves termination to the contracting employer under Article 83; the appropriate remedy is an order fixing a reasonable deadline by which the employer must decide on the demand, which keeps the disciplinary power where the law places it while preventing indefinite inaction [Para 40][Para 46]. Strike pay is owed only where the employer recruited replacements under Article 334; a suspension under Article 332 otherwise leaves the worker unpaid, and the burden of proving replacement or transfer of work lies on the employees [Para 48][Para 49].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel remarked that retaining a manager whom many employees dislike will harm the company and reduce production, and that the employer needs sufficient reason to terminate any employment contract [Para 42][Para 43].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The strike is dated 10 March 2004 in the case summary and 11–12 March 2004 in the findings [Para 24][Para 30]. The union's letter requesting conciliation is dated both 3 March 2004 and 1 March 2004 [Para 23][Para 24]. The complaint of 9 March 2004 is recorded as raising four matters, including permanent status after three months and permission for parents and siblings to meet employees, but only two issues appear in the issues-in-dispute list and neither of those two is addressed [Para 21][Para 10]. The findings at the misconduct passage repeat a broken sentence ("The administration manager deters misconduct as argued by the employees"), and Article 334 is printed with interpolated words that reverse its sense ("requires the employer salary is not paid to provide wages") [Para 30][Para 48]. The panel's composition line repeats "Arbitrator chosen by the Employer Party" twice before naming two employer-side arbitrators [Para 12][Para 13].
