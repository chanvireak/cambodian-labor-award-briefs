# Award No. 53/04 — Kong Hong Company (Arbitration Council, 26 July 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mar Samborana (employer-side, selected by lot), An Nan (worker-chosen), Kong Phallack (chair) · Doc ID 139327

## 1. Facts
Kong Hong Company, at Phum Beung Salang on National Road No. 5, Khan Reussey Keo, Phnom Penh, employed 430 workers, about 300 of them women. [Para 15][Para 69] On 17 April 2004 the Khan Reussey Keo Labour Inspection Office received a complaint seeking reimbursement of 10,100 riels per worker for medical check-ups and compliance with the Labour Law; conciliation on 17 June 2004 settled three of eight issues, leaving five for the Council. [Para 16][Para 18][Para 19] The employer refused to select its arbitrator, who was chosen by lot on 5 July 2004, and then failed to attend the hearing of 13 July 2004 without acceptable reason, so the panel proceeded in its absence under Article 21 of the Prakas. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 25][Para 26]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must provide floating and casual workers the same benefits as regular workers? [Para 19]
2. Whether the company must stop applying annual leave to periods when it has no work for the workers? [Para 19]
3. Whether the company must reimburse 10,100 riel to each worker for the medical check-up service charge? [Para 19]
4. Whether the company must set up a day-care centre with a nurse inside the factory as the Labour Law requires? [Para 20]
5. Whether the company must pay double wages for night work between 10:00 pm and 5:00 am? [Para 20]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Some 50 to 60 casual workers on two-month contracts at 800 riels a day received none of the benefits regular workers enjoyed — incentive bonuses, basic wage, overtime wages, meal fees — and some remained labelled casual after two years of service. When work ran out the company made them take annual leave, deducting half-days or whole days, although they still attended the workplace; the company had never scheduled annual leave. Most regular workers had paid the 10,100 riel check-up fee themselves and handed the receipt to the administrator. No day-care centre or caretaker existed despite 300 women workers; the workers refused the company's offer of USD$5 per month and proposed USD$12 for women with babies. Night workers had never received double pay. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 31][Para 36][Para 37]

**Employer (respondent):** It did not appear and offered no reasons, submitting no evidence at all. Its only positions on record are the offers made before conciliation: USD$5 per month in lieu of a day-care centre, which the workers rejected, and its refusal of the five demands. [Para 15][Para 20][Para 25][Para 36]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer must give floating and casual workers the same basic wage and benefits as regular workers (incentive bonuses, overtime wages, 1000 riels meal fees for overtime, leave, severance pay) and convert to regular status those who worked at least 21 days a month over two months; must not deduct from annual leave periods when it has no work; must make a clear annual-leave schedule for all workers; must reimburse 10,100 riel for check-ups already paid, within seven days of the award taking effect; must set up a nursing room and day-care centre under Ministry control or pay the invoiced charges of outside centres, in the first week of September 2004; and must pay double the day-time rate for work between 10:00 pm and 5:00 am, immediately on the award taking effect. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74][Para 76][Para 77]

On casual work, Article 9 distinguishes regular and casual workers, and Article 10 subjects casual workers to the same rules and gives them the same rights as regular workers save for separately stipulated clauses; Article 13 voids any contractual rule inferior to the Law, so the sample contract's clause paying only what the company determines "up to the actual outputs" could not strip those rights. The panel also held the contract must state the wage clearly, whether monthly, daily, hourly or piecework, with piecework rates issued monthly. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 43][Para 45]

Clause 2 of the sample contract, by which workers waived compensation on expiry, contravened Article 73(6), which requires severance pay proportional to wages and length of contract, and so fell under Article 13; clause 3 on voluntary overtime was consistent with Article 139, subject to overtime pay and 1000 riels meal fees under Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000. Following Sport Wear 26/04, and reading Article 68's probation limit as a guideline, the panel held that a garment worker should not be employed two full months as casual, and that a casual working at least 21 days a month for more than two months becomes regular. [Para 47][Para 49][Para 50]

On annual leave, Article 170 gives leave at Khmer New Year, shifting it requires agreement plus notice to the Labour Inspector, and Article 171 allows deduction only of special leave where leave remains untaken; nothing permits deducting days on which the employer simply has no work, so the panel required a schedule agreed and notified where it falls outside the New Year period. [Para 52][Para 56][Para 58][Para 60]

On check-ups, Joint Prakas No. 09 of 19 January 1994 requires the enterprise owner to pay the fee under Article 7, and paragraph 3 of Article 247 of the Labour Law stands behind it; the Prakas remains enforceable and was not abrogated by Article 395. Reimbursement was ordered for workers who paid after July 1999, within the five-year contract period of Article 25 of Decree No. 38. [Para 62][Para 65][Para 67][Para 68]

On child care, Article 186 requires any enterprise employing at least one hundred women to provide a nursing room and day-care centre, with Article 187 leaving conditions to a Prakas; with 300 women employed, the obligation applied, dischargeable either by provision or by paying outside-centre invoices. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 70]

On night pay, Circular No. 024 MoSALVY of 19 October 1999, supported by Article 139, requires double pay for work between 10:00 pm and 5:00 am. [Para 70][Para 71]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker hired on rolling short-term "casual" contracts for at least 21 days a month over more than two months is a regular worker, and casual workers enjoy the same statutory benefits as regular workers — basic wage, incentive and overtime pay, meal fees, leave and severance — any contractual clause to the contrary being void under Article 13. An employer may not charge annual leave against days on which it has no work to give, must schedule leave, and must pay the medical check-up fee, provide a nursing room and day-care centre once it employs one hundred women, and pay double for night work from 10:00 pm to 5:00 am. [Para 50][Para 58][Para 62][Para 70]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the employer's pre-conciliation offer of USD$5 per month instead of a day-care centre did not discharge Article 186, and that a party invited but absent without valid reason does not prevent the panel from issuing an award, following Tonga Co. 03/03; neither observation was necessary to the five orders. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 36]
