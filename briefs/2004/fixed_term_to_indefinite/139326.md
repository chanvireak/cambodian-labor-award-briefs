# Award No. 54/04 — Cambodia Sportswear Mfg Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 12 August 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Hem Hour Narith (employer-chosen), Tuon Siphann (worker-chosen), Kong Phallack (chair) · Doc ID 139326

## 1. Facts
Cambodia Sportswear Mfg Co., Ltd employed 5,140 workers. A strike ran from 3 to 6 May 2004 after the company refused to negotiate working conditions. The company laid off workers for want of orders — over 800 by July 2004 — and on 6 May 2004 sought permission from the Labour Inspection Department to dismiss eight union leaders and members for inciting the strike; at the hearing both sides acknowledged that those dismissals were cancelled. [Para 23][Para 92][Para 105][Para 121] On 29 May 2004 the company nonetheless suspended the contracts of twelve union leaders and members indefinitely and barred them from the factory, and it lodged an opposition on 29 May 2004 to Award 26/04 of 22 May 2004. [Para 24][Para 56][Para 106][Para 119]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must comply with Award 26/04 of 22 May 2004? [Para 13]
2. Whether the company must reinstate the fourteen suspended union leaders and members? [Para 13]
3. Whether the company must pay the allowances due to laid-off workers according to law? [Para 14]
4. Whether the company should be required not to bring guns into the company? [Para 15]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The company had to perform Award 26/04, and its refusal rested only on an opposition. The twelve were suspended for their union roles, not for misconduct: the suspension was indefinite, unpaid, and accompanied by a bar on entering the factory, which in their view also suspended their representative functions. On compensation, the parties had agreed in April 2004 that workers could choose between the last month's wage and the twelve-month average, yet the company decided the method itself; Miss M Chansrey said she was refused the average method and told the method "applied so far" would be used. They also complained that when conflicts arose the company brought guns, bombs, authorities and bodyguards into the factory. [Para 24][Para 62][Para 84][Para 127][Para 131]

**Employer (respondent):** The dismissals were lawful under Article 83 and the internal work rules, the workers had disturbed colleagues who wanted to work and refused to attend negotiations, and the lay-offs followed the cancellation of all orders by a buyer, not the strike. It said it had never brought arms in, that authorities intervening during a strike carry arms as a public service and cannot be prevented from doing so, and that the workers' public statements about guns on the table were defamatory — hence its court complaints. On compensation, it said it merely followed the workers' own choice of the last month's wage once they saw the average was lower, and that nobody had complained before this dispute. [Para 54][Para 74][Para 76][Para 87][Para 128]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel rejected the demand that the company apply Award 26/04 as outside its jurisdiction; ordered the employer to stop suspending the contracts of the twelve workers and to reinstate them as soon as the award took effect; ordered the company to recalculate dismissal compensation under Articles 75, 89 and 166–167 on the daily average wage explained below and to pay any remaining portion within 30 days of the award taking effect; and recorded the parties' agreement on arms. [Para 164][Para 165]

On the first issue, Article 40 of the Prakas on the Arbitration Council of 21 April 2004 makes an award unenforceable where a party lodges an opposition within eight days of issuance, and Article 319 of the Labour Law lets either party resort to strike or lock-out where an arbitral decision is rejected. Award 26/04 issued on 22 May 2004 and the employer opposed it on 29 May 2004 — within the legal period — so the award could not be applied and the panel had no jurisdiction to decide the issue again. [Para 132][Para 134][Para 135]

On the second issue, Article 71 allows suspension of a contract for appropriate reasons, and Article 71(7) allows an employer to suspend a worker for misconduct provided the internal work rules sanction that measure. No provision of these internal work rules provides contract suspension as a punishment, so Article 71(7) did not apply. Article 27 requires any disciplinary sanction to be proportional to the seriousness of the misconduct, and an indefinite, unpaid suspension with a bar on entry is not proportional; the panel therefore held that the law does not permit indefinite suspension as a punishment and ordered the twelve reinstated. [Para 153][Para 154][Para 158][Para 159]

On the third issue the panel confined itself, as the parties had agreed, to the method of calculation. Following MS International (27/04) and Sam Han (51/04), dismissal compensation comprises notice compensation (Article 75), dismissal compensation (Article 89) and unused annual leave (Articles 166 and 167(2)). Articles 102–103 define wages to include actual salary, overtime and allowances, so the monthly average is the total of those over the twelve months before dismissal divided by twelve, and the daily average is that figure divided by 26; the daily average, not the last month's wage, is the basis of the compensation. [Para 92][Para 162][Para 163][Para 164]

The fourth issue was settled by agreement in the hearing: no party or third party may take arms into the negotiation room or area, use of arms during a strike or demonstration is the authority's right, and any third party present must introduce itself to the parties. [Para 91][Para 165]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer cannot suspend a worker's contract indefinitely as a disciplinary measure: Article 71(7) permits suspension only where the internal work rules provide it as a sanction, and Article 27 requires proportionality, so an open-ended unpaid suspension with a bar on entering the factory is unlawful and the workers must be reinstated. Where an award has been opposed within the eight-day period, it is unenforceable and the panel will not re-decide the same dispute. Dismissal compensation is computed on the twelve-month average of salary, overtime and allowances, never on the last month's wage alone. [Para 135][Para 158][Para 163]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel recorded that the authorities may carry arms during a strike or demonstration and that the company cannot prevent them from doing so, an observation the parties adopted by agreement rather than as a ruling; it also noted that the company's defamation complaints were pending in court and expressed no view on them. [Para 90][Para 165]

The worker Mom Chansrey appears as "Mrs M Sreychan" at one point and "Miss M Chansrey" elsewhere; the eight dismissed workers include a "Mr Y Sopheap" absent from the twelve. The employee party's union statute is dated 8 December 2004, after the award, and is reproduced as printed. The decision's sub-numbering runs "(1) … (2)" under item 3 only, leaving item 4 as an agreement rather than an order. [Para 16][Para 13][Para 25][Para 106][Para 131][Para 164]
