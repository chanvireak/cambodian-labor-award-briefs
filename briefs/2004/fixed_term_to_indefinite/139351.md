# Award No. 92/04 — C.H.P. Garment Factory v Khmer Youth Union of C.H.P. Garment Factory (Arbitration Council, 1 December 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Hem Hour Narith, chosen by the employer party; Liv Sovanna, chosen by the worker party; Kong Phallack, chair) · Doc ID 139351

## 1. Facts
C.H.P. Garment Factory, at Prey Tea commune, Cham Chao quarter, Dankau district, Phnom Penh, employed 740 workers. [Para 35] Through August and September 2004 the company often had no work: workers punched their time cards and were sent home, and one union official worked only 17 days in August and 10 in September. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 47] The company never notified the Labour Inspector of these stoppages, sometimes did not warn the workers, and its Internal Work Rules say nothing about suspension of work. [Para 42][Para 48] On 27 October 2004 the Labour Inspectorate received a complaint; conciliation on 2 November 2004 settled eight of ten points, leaving wages and the dismissal of six union leaders. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38] Three of the six were terminated on 21 October 2004 and three — including the newly elected union president and secretary — on 27 October 2004, all on undetermined-duration contracts, the union's registration request having been filed on 18 October 2004. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 56][Para 63][Para 64][Para 95]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay full basic wages when it has no work for the workers, regardless of whether they attend, and pay the wages withheld in August and September 2004? [Para 16][Para 40]
2. Whether the employer must reinstate the six union leaders to the same place and positions? [Para 17][Para 20][Para 21]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** full basic wages are owed whether or not work is available, plus the arrears of August and September 2004; the six must be reinstated, and the employer must prove that their termination was not based on union membership. [Para 16][Para 87]
**Employer (respondent):** in the absence of work it applies the law in force, relying on a verbal agreement to pay a US$5 monthly bonus instead of full wages; the six were terminated according to the Labour Law, not for union reasons or misconduct, and it is ready to pay the notice, compensation, damages and annual leave due. [Para 17][Para 41][Para 87][Para 90]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer (1) to continue paying full wages to all workers whether or not work exists, unless the contract is suspended under Article 71, and to pay 50 percent of wages to workers required to attend during a lawful suspension; (2) to pay the remaining wages of August and September 2004; and (3) to reinstate the six workers pending the Labour Inspector's control and decision, paying them full wages from the date their contracts ended, or, if it refuses to reinstate, to pay those wages until the Inspector decides. [Para 97][Para 98][Para 99]

*Wages.* Article 71 permits suspension of the labour contract only on the grounds it lists — point 7, a temporary suspension for appropriate reasons set out in the Internal Work Rules, and point 11, serious financial difficulty rendering operation economically and materially impossible or unusually difficult — and a point 11 suspension must not exceed two months and is subject to review by the Labour Inspector. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 73][Para 74] The company's Internal Work Rules contain no suspension clause, and the company told the Inspector nothing, so neither route was open; where the employer does not suspend in conformity with Article 71 it must pay full wages under the contract, as held in Awards 21/03 (Wonrex), 46/04 (M.N.A) and 60/04 (United Art). [Para 76][Para 78] Were a lawful suspension in place, Article 72 would relieve the employer of wages, but where it nonetheless requires workers to attend, 50 percent of basic wages is acceptable. [Para 77]

*Reinstatement.* Article 74 allows termination of an undetermined-duration contract only with prior notice and a valid reason going to the worker's attitude or behaviour, or to the operational requirements of the enterprise; absent a valid reason the worker is owed damages under Article 91. [Para 84][Para 85] Articles 12 and 279 forbid terminating or hiring by reference to union membership, so the decision to terminate must be shown not to rest on union affiliation. [Para 86][Para 87] The panel found no attitude, behaviour or misconduct supporting the terminations; the employer's only explanation was that it had little work and might close the factory. [Para 88][Para 89][Para 90] If the terminations rest on lack of work, the selection must follow the Article 95 procedure (Award 01/03, Cambodiana), and the Inspector must be informed. [Para 91] More decisively, the six were specially protected: under Article 4 of Prakas 305/04, founders and members who join while a registration request is pending are protected like shop stewards from the date the request is filed until 30 days after registration, and Article 293 requires the Labour Inspector's authorisation before a shop steward or candidate can be dismissed. [Para 91][Para 92][Para 93] Since the request was filed on 18 October 2004, all six were protected and their dismissal had to be submitted for the Inspector's approval — even absent any discrimination. [Para 95][Para 96]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that cannot provide work must either suspend the labour contract through one of the routes in Article 71 — a clause in the registered Internal Work Rules, or a notified suspension of no more than two months on serious financial grounds subject to the Labour Inspector's review — or continue to pay full wages; it may not simply send workers home unpaid, and where it requires attendance during a lawful suspension it owes half the basic wage. A worker who joins a union while its registration request is pending is protected like a shop steward, so dismissal before the Labour Inspector authorises it is unlawful even where no anti-union motive is shown. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 91][Para 95][Para 96]

**Obiter dicta:** the remarks that an Article 95 selection procedure must be followed and the Inspector informed if the terminations truly rest on lack of work [Para 91], and that the employer may terminate more workers or close the factory [Para 90], are observations on a route the panel did not have to decide, since the protection under Prakas 305/04 was decisive.
