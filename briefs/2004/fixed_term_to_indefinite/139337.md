# Award No. 65/04 — New Point 2 Garment Company Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 31 August 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phallack presiding; Kol Vathana appointed by the employer; Ms Sin Kim Sean appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 139337

## 1. Facts
New Point 2 Garment Company Co., Ltd employed 193 workers at the corner of Street 48 and Street 68, Sangkat Sras Chork, Khan Daun Penh, and moved the factory to Tek Thla, seven kilometres away. [Para 29][Para 34] The workers struck against the move on 5 August 2004; conciliation on 5 and 16 August 2004 settled three of four issues, leaving the demand for a higher dismissal indemnity, which the company refused on the ground that dismissal caused by relocation is reasonable. [Para 30][Para 33][Para 15][Para 39] At the hearing of 17 August 2004 the workers fell into three groups — 23 with expired fixed-duration contracts, 60 with fixed-duration contracts still running, and 106 on undetermined-duration contracts — all refusing to work at the new site. [Para 41]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employees are entitled to the higher dismissal indemnity they demand, the company refusing it because a dismissal caused by a change of factory location is reasonable? [Para 15][Para 38]
2. Whether the employment contract is altered by a change of factory location, and if so who must repair the prejudice that change causes? [Para 55][Para 56]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** all three groups refuse to move because they fear the employer will take vengeance, citing past forced overtime and abusive language, and they want compensation: 5 per cent under the sixth point of Article 73 for the 23, premature-termination damages under the third point of Article 73 for the 60, and indemnity under Articles 75, 89, 91 and 166 for the 106. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 45][Para 50][Para 48]
**Employer (respondent):** the relocation is a valid reason, so no compensation is owed; it will not terminate the 60 or the 106 and will transport them to Tek Thla until their contracts end; it accepts indemnity under Articles 75, 89 and 166 for the 106 but not Article 91; for the 23 it offers the last month's wages and unused paid leave. [Para 46][Para 51][Para 43][Para 15] It also said the 5 August minutes were unclear and it would follow the 16 August minutes, and it offered no evidence-based answer on forced labour. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 49]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must pay the 23 first-group workers severance of 5 per cent of wages received over the length of their contracts, seven days after the award comes into force; must transport the 60 second-group workers to the new location until their contracts end, with time and place to be negotiated at once; must accept any third-group worker who chooses to work at the new site with his or her own transport; and must pay dismissal indemnity under Articles 75, 89 and 166 to the 106 third-group workers who decline to move, no later than 30 September 2004, the claim for compensation being rejected. [Para 99][Para 100][Para 101][Para 102][Para 103]
The panel found the 1997 Labour Law silent on a change in the place of performance, so it read the contract through Article 65 and Sub-Decree No. 38 of 28 October 1988: Article 29 requires obligations as to place to be performed regularly and properly and Article 32 requires performance at the debtor's domicile where no other place is named. [Para 57][Para 62][Para 63] It concluded that performance was due at the old site, that a transfer without the workers' agreement breaches the contract, and that the workers could stop work under Article 90 and claim what they would receive if dismissed, adding Article 91 compensation or Article 73 third-point damages where the employer had no sufficient reason. [Para 63][Para 64] It also held that an employer owes nothing for a worker's new residence or transport when the factory moves. [Para 65]
For the 23 expired contracts the panel applied the first and sixth points of Article 73: the contracts ended on their date, no collective agreement set severance, so 5 per cent of wages over the contract term was due. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 71] For the 60 running contracts it held Article 73's first and second paragraphs inapplicable, since the employer would not terminate; a worker who refused to move was taken to have resigned, retaining the 5 per cent severance but not damages, and the employer was entitled to rely on the clearer 16 August minutes. [Para 73][Para 77][Para 81][Para 82][Para 83] For the 106 it held Article 95 inapplicable and Article 74 the governing provision: the relocation was a valid reason based on the requirements of the operation of the enterprise, so Article 91 compensation was refused while the Articles 75, 89 and 166 indemnity was ordered. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 97][Para 98]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Because a labour contract must be performed at the place agreed, a factory relocation is a unilateral change of the place of performance and an employer may not impose it; a worker who declines to move is treated as dismissed or resigned but keeps the end-of-contract severance of 5 per cent of wages under Article 73, and keeps any Article 91 or Article 73 third-point damages only where the employer's reason for the move fails. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 83] An employer owes no allowance for a worker's new residence or travel after a relocation. [Para 65]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that it had to read the contract because the Labour Law does not address a change of the place of performance, and it treated the company's past overtime pressure and loud speech as unproven rather than as a ground of itself. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 49]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The workforce is 193 at para 29 but the three groups total 189 at para 41, and the third group is given as 105 workers plus a maid, then as 106 workers at para 84. [Para 29][Para 41][Para 84] At para 28 both parties are recorded as deciding that the award is "not bindinq", yet the closing clause makes it binding eight days after notification unless opposition is lodged. [Para 28][Para 103] The panel states at paras 63 and 64 that a worker transferred without agreement may claim Article 91 compensation, but refuses that compensation at paras 97 and 98 on the ground that the relocation was a valid reason. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 97][Para 98] The conciliation of 5 August 2004 is recorded as producing an indemnity agreement at para 36 while the employer is later recorded as refusing to follow it because those minutes are unclear. [Para 36][Para 79] OCR loss runs throughout — "the" is printed for "the employer", "noterminate" for "not terminate", "fixeduration" for "fixed duration" — and the case number prints as "65/04" with the Prakas on the Council as both 338/02 and 099/04. [Para 16] The maid in the third group and the individual workers, union officers and Legal Aid of Cambodia representative appear under full names in the source and are not reproduced here. [Para 41]
