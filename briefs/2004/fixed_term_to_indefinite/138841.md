# Award No. 02/04 — Hotel Cambodiana (Arbitration Council, 16 April 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phallack presiding; Ouk Ry, employer nominee, dissenting; Touch Siphann, worker nominee) · Doc ID 138841

## 1. Facts
Hotel Cambodiana, on Sisovaddh Blvd, Phnom Penh, has traded since 1990 and employed 317 workers. [Para 27] In June, July and August 2003 it did not renew the contracts of 71 employees — 51 in June, 13 in July and seven in August — citing losses after the SARS outbreak, the Iraq war and new direct flights to Siem Reap. [Para 55][Para 77][Para 112] Most had worked 11 to 13 years on repeatedly renewed two- or three-year contracts, each getting 15 days' notice and two or three months' severance on expiry. [Para 32][Para 84][Para 104][Para 116] Seven were later re-hired as casual staff at US$75 per month; the union vice-president, Ms H, was among the 71. [Para 37][Para 86][Para 172]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Does the Arbitration Council have jurisdiction over the dispute? [Para 15]
2. Are the employment contracts between Hotel Cambodiana and the 71 workers those of specified or unspecified duration? [Para 16]
3. Was the dismissal made en masse or on an individual basis? [Para 17]
4. Did the hotel discriminate against the union? [Para 18]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** 71 employees were dismissed within a short period for the same reason, which is a collective dismissal requiring Article 95 procedure; years of renewal had turned their contracts into undetermined-duration contracts; the hotel gave only 15 days' notice instead of three months and no two days a week to look for work; it then hired outsiders for gardening work the old staff had done, and the union vice-president was told to abandon union work if she wanted her job back. [Para 31][Para 33][Para 34][Para 35][Para 39][Para 53]
**Employer (respondent):** the case is an individual dispute over non-renewal of fixed-duration contracts, which the Council cannot hear; MoSALVY had ruled it individual on 20 June 2003 and the union let the two-month court deadline pass; guest numbers fell to 30 percent and the hotel lost an estimated US$3.50 million; there was no anti-union motive, and Ms H's contract simply expired. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 56][Para 61][Para 62][Para 68][Para 69][Para 72]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the Council held it had jurisdiction; the contracts had become undetermined duration; Ms H must be reinstated in her previous position with the same wages and benefits and paid for the period since July 2003; the other 70 workers' reinstatement claim was rejected; and the hotel was ordered to pay each of the 70, by 31 March 2004, two and a half months' wages and benefits for the notice shortfall and three months' wages and benefits as damages. [Para 202][Para 203][Para 204][Para 205][Para 206][Para 207]
*Jurisdiction.* Because MoSALVY's non-conciliation report of 13 January 2004 and the Minister's letter of 19 January 2004 submitted a collective dispute, Articles 309–310 and Article 31 of Prakas 338 obliged the panel to hear it; and the earlier service-charge award of 29 August 2003 had not decided reinstatement, so nothing was being decided twice. [Para 88][Para 89][Para 91][Para 92][Para 93][Para 98]
*Contract duration.* Applying Article 67.2 and its own award in Jacqsintex Industries Ltd (Case 10/03), the panel held that renewal beyond a two-year total converts the contract to undetermined duration, and that paying severance on expiry shows only how the employer classified the contract, not what it was. [Para 101][Para 102][Para 105][Para 107][Para 108]
*Grounds for layoff and notice.* Falling guest numbers and losses were a valid reason under Article 74, but Article 75 required three months' notice for staff of over ten years and only 15 days was given; Article 77 therefore made the hotel liable for the rest of the notice period, and Article 79 required 26 days off to seek work, which was not granted and was compensated in cash. [Para 112][Para 113][Para 115][Para 116][Para 121][Para 123]
*Collective layoff.* Dismissing 51, 13 and seven workers over three months to reduce staff was a mass layoff under Article 95, whose procedure — ordering layoffs by qualifications, seniority and family burden, informing workers' representatives, rehiring priority, notifying the Labour Inspector — was not followed; only MoSALVY was informed of eight dismissals. [Para 133][Para 137][Para 138][Para 140][Para 144]
*Damages.* No article fixes the sum for breaching Article 95, but Article 34 of Prakas 338 empowers the panel to grant any just civil remedy; taking Article 91 and the absence of bad intent into account, three months' wages and benefits was appropriate. [Para 146][Para 147][Para 155][Para 159]
*Union rights.* Article 279 forbids anti-union discrimination, and Articles 282 and 293 with Article 7 of Prakas 313 protect the president, first vice-president and first secretary, whose dismissal requires the Labour Inspector's prior approval; none was given, so Ms H's dismissal was null and void. [Para 161][Para 162][Para 163][Para 185][Para 186][Para 189] The three advisors were not leaders under Prakas 313 and had not designated a protected person in writing under Article 4 of Prakas 305, so they had no protection and no proof of anti-union motive. [Para 198][Para 199][Para 201][Para 202]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-duration contract that is renewed so that the total engagement exceeds two years becomes a contract of undetermined duration under Article 67.2, and letting such workers go on expiry for a single economic reason is a mass layoff that must follow Article 95; where it does not, the workers are entitled to damages, and a protected union leader dismissed without the Labour Inspector's prior approval is deemed never to have been dismissed. [Para 105][Para 107][Para 137][Para 163][Para 189]

**Obiter dicta:** a severance payment on each expiry does not prevent a contract being of unspecified duration, though it may reduce the compensation owed; the employer offered to recommend the dismissed workers for future vacancies. [Para 76][Para 108]
