# Award No. 22/04 — Raffles Hotel Le Royal (Arbitration Council, 07 June 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mr Mar Samborana, employer-appointed, dissenting; Mr Tuon Siphann, worker-appointed; Mr Kong Phallack, chair) · Doc ID 139306

## 1. Facts
Raffles Hotel Le Royal employs 219 people. [Para 35] From 13 February 2004 the union pressed the hotel to resume collecting the ten per cent service charge, and about 97 workers began a non-violent strike on 05 April 2004, having arranged minimum service with over 100 workers still working. [Para 36][Para 39][Para 68] The Phnom Penh Municipal Court declared the strike illegal by urgent injunction on 09 April 2004 at about 2.00 pm, but the injunction was not posted inside the hotel until 2.00 pm on 10 April. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 94] By letter of 19 April 2004 the hotel dismissed 97 striking workers; new worker delegates were elected on 29 April 2004 and signed a collective agreement with the hotel on 01 May 2004. [Para 37][Para 52][Para 81][Para 112]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate the 97 employees laid off in March 2004? [Para 15]
2. Whether the employer must maintain and pay the workers who took part in the strike from 05 March 2004 until they returned to work, on the ground that it recruited new workers to replace them? [Para 15]
3. Whether the selection of the new shop stewards and the collective bargaining agreement negotiated after the 05 April 2004 strike were unlawful? [Para 17]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** their strike was lawful, the termination notice was procedurally defective and out of time, the hotel used MPA security to bar their return on 12 and 13 April, and it replaced strikers with staff from Siem Reap and with trainees; the new delegate election and agreement were staged to bypass the union. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 51]
**Employer (respondent):** the 97 were dismissed for failing to return within 48 hours of the injunction declaring the strike illegal, though it had offered them registration to return; no new staff were recruited, only trainees and partner-hotel staff on preplanned training; and the delegate and agreement issues fell outside the non-conciliation report. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 57][Para 61]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must accept all 97 workers back from the day the order takes effect and pay their salaries in full from 11 April 2004 to the date they resume; the claim to be paid for the strike period is rejected; the employer is prohibited from dealing with the new worker delegates, and the collective agreement signed with them is suspended unless the representative union agrees. [Para 121][Para 122]

Issue 1. Article 337 reserves to the courts the determination whether a strike is illegal and requires strikers to return within 48 hours of the declaration, failing which they commit serious misconduct. [Para 86][Para 87] The panel treated the municipal injunction as such a declaration, held it could not review its correctness, and calculated the 48 hours from issuance at about 2.00 pm on 09 April, so return fell due by 2.00 pm on 11 April. [Para 90][Para 92] Because the injunction was posted at the hotel only at 2.00 pm on 10 April, the workers' ignorance of it for 24 hours was a valid reason for not returning. [Para 94] The panel further found they did try to return on 11, 12 and 13 April but refused to sign a register promising not to strike again, and held that condition unreasonable given the constitutional and statutory right to strike. [Para 98][Para 99] Article 337 was therefore not satisfied and the dismissals were unlawful, with salaries back-dated to 11 April 2004. [Para 100][Para 101]

Issue 2. A strike suspends the contract, and strike-period wages are owed only where the employer hired replacements. [Para 106] On the evidence, six staff and one casual worker came from the partner hotel and nine students came from a business school under a placement requested in March for three months at US$30 a month; those were preplanned activities unrelated to the strike, and the workers' summary table covered 18–24 April, after the strike had ended. [Para 102][Para 104][Para 105][Para 106]

Issue 3. Under Article 312 the panel may decide issues in the non-conciliation report and issues arising after it as a direct consequence of the dispute. [Para 107] It made an order on 18 May 2004 calling for evidence on the point; the employer objected, refused to produce documents and left the hearing with its lawyer, and the panel proceeded in its absence. [Para 109][Para 111] On the merits, Article 288 requires worker delegates to be chosen from candidates nominated by unions with representative status; the union held most-representative status under a certificate running to 30 December 2005, so the hotel had to consult it first and did not, which made the election illegal. [Para 112][Para 113][Para 115] Article 96(2) permits a collective agreement with worker delegates only where no union represents the workers, which was not this case; the hotel dealt with illegally chosen delegates in order to bypass the union holding the sole right to bargain. [Para 117][Para 118][Para 119]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A dismissal for serious misconduct under Article 337 fails where the employer neither brought the 48-hour deadline home to the workers nor removed an unreasonable condition — a written promise not to strike again — from their return; and a worker-delegate election, together with any collective agreement concluded with the delegates so elected, is unlawful where the employer did not consult the union holding most-representative status, as Articles 288 and 96(2) require. [Para 94][Para 99][Para 100][Para 115][Para 118]

**Obiter dicta:** the remarks that leaving a hearing breaches Article 20 of the Prakas, and that the hotel showed flagrant disregard for freedom of association and ILO Conventions C87 and C98, go beyond what the orders required. [Para 115][Para 120]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The lay-off is dated "March 2004" in the issues list [Para 15] but the notice and letters of dismissal are dated 19 April 2004 [Para 37][Para 52].
- The strike is dated "05 March 2004" in the issues list [Para 15] but 05 April 2004 throughout the findings [Para 36][Para 68].
- The injunction is cited as "#09.Ch on 9 April 2003" [Para 89] and as issued 09 April 2004 [Para 90]; the workers' return attempts are dated "11 April 2003" [Para 106].
- The collective agreement's validity is printed "01 May 2004 to 30 May 2005" [Para 81].
- The union's registration is "No. 462 of 23 May 2003" [Para 32] while its most-representative certificate is "#575" [Para 113].
- The panel rests its refusal of strike wages on Article 332 [Para 106] where its reasoning on replacement workers belongs to Article 334.
- The signature block records a dissent by the employer-appointed arbitrator "attached in the Annex" [Para 123]; no annex text survives in the OCR.
- Names are split across paragraphs and abbreviated inconsistently ("Mr de Cruz" for the human resources manager who is also the attorney) [Para 101][Para 104].
