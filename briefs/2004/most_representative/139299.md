# Award No. 15/04 — Lucky Zone Apparel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 2 April 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mar Samborana (employer-chosen), Toun Siphann (worker-chosen), Sok Mathoeung (chair) · Doc ID 139299

## 1. Facts
Lucky Zone Apparel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd employed 1,300 workers in Phnom Penh alongside a related cutting operation run by Belgian Company in the same building [Para 35][Para 187]. On 27 January 2004 Ms K, deputy president of the Free Trade Union of Workers, used abusive language towards Ms P, president of the Union of Workers, who complained to the company and asked that discipline be applied [Para 44][Para 60]. Conciliation failed on 29 January 2004; the workers struck from 6 to 12 March 2004 and stopped on receiving an interim order from the Arbitration Council [Para 170][Para 192]. Four demands remained unresolved [Para 40].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must resolve the dispute between Ms K and Ms P under the internal work rules and Article 83(b) of the Labour Law, by disciplining Ms K? [Para 40]
2. Whether the company must dismiss Mr C, the administration manager, because he was biased against the unions? [Para 42]
3. Whether the company must move the cutting section at Belgian Company from upstairs to downstairs at Lucky Zone so that the two workforces can work together? [Para 44]
4. Whether the company must pay wages for the strike days and the attendance bonus of US$5? [Para 44]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr C systematically favoured the Free Trade Union — excluding other unions' representatives from dispute resolution, suspending Ms P but not Ms K, and securing a US$6/7/8 wage scale for 40 Free Trade Union members — so he was indebted to that union and should be dismissed [Para 62][Para 67][Para 78]. Because the company moved work to Belgian and replaced strikers, Article 334 obliged it to pay wages and the bonus [Para 158].
**Employer (respondent):** Mr C was only a facilitator under his job description, the minute of 11 March 2004 was checked and signed by all representatives, and the wage increase was the company's own decision recognising hard work, not his [Para 85][Para 86][Para 112]. No one is paid unless they work; a strike suspends the labour contract, and the parties had agreed on 29 January 2004 that future strikes would not be paid [Para 155][Para 156][Para 157].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — all four demands were rejected, but the company was ordered to pay damages of US$5 to each worker who was a member of the Union of Workers of Lucky Zone or the Cambodian Independent Union at Lucky Zone and had paid union contributions up to March 2004, within 14 calendar days of the award taking effect, and the three unions were ordered to apply to the Ministry for an election of the most representative union, to be completed within three months [Para 237][Para 238][Para 239].

On the first issue the panel held that although the quarrel was individual in origin, the complaint and the demand for dismissal made it a collective dispute; under Article 65 of the Labour Law a labour contract binds only the contracting parties, so hiring and dismissal are the employer's right unless the worker is dangerous or may cause turmoil, and there was insufficient evidence that Ms K was dangerous [Para 198][Para 202][Para 205]. It therefore had no power to order a dismissal [Para 206].

On the second issue the panel treated the administration manager's acts as the employer's acts, and found that the wage increase benefited 47 workers who were mostly Free Trade Union members and none from the other unions, a fact the employer did not contradict [Para 182][Para 210]. As no reasonable ground was given for that selective increase, the acts contradicted Articles 12 and 279, which entitle members of all unions to appropriate compensation [Para 207][Para 215]. The dismissal demand was still refused on the Article 65 principle [Para 218].

On the third issue the panel found Belgian and Lucky Zone to be separate companies with separate production, documents, accounting and purchase orders, though one owner [Para 220][Para 221]. It could not impose a joint work arrangement on two distinct structures, and it declined to decide the half-wage practice because that was not an issue properly before it, while noting that paying workers half wages for lack of work contradicts the law [Para 222][Para 224][Para 225].

On the fourth issue Article 332 provides that a strike suspends the labour contract so that salary and the work allowance are not paid [Para 226]. Article 320(4) permits a strike only after all peaceful methods have been tried; the 6–12 March strike gave notice but the dispute had not yet been sent to the Arbitration Council, so the strike was procedurally unlawful and the panel did not need to decide the Article 334 allegation, the strikers being entitled to nothing either way [Para 229][Para 232][Para 234].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An arbitration panel cannot order an employer to dismiss or reinstate a named employee, because Article 65 confines termination to the contracting parties and the exception is limited to workers who are dangerous or may cause turmoil; where a manager's exercise of employer power favours one union's members in pay without reasonable grounds, the breach of Articles 12 and 279 is remedied by compensation to the excluded members, not by removing the manager [Para 204][Para 215]. A strike begun before the dispute has been referred to the Arbitration Council is unlawful under Article 320(4), and its participants earn no wages or attendance bonus regardless of whether the employer used replacement labour [Para 230][Para 233].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed, without deciding, that an employer may not keep workers on half wages merely because it lacks work for them, and that this practice is contrary to the law [Para 224]. It also urged the unions to cooperate with the company and not to affront one another [Para 116].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The number of workers receiving the disputed wage increase is printed as 40 in the workers' submission and as 47 in the findings [Para 67][Para 181]. The increase is described as US$6 then US$7 then US$8 in one passage and as a rise "from US$7 to US$8" in another [Para 68][Para 213]. The agreement not to pay future strikers is dated 29 January 2004 in one place and 19 January 2004 in another [Para 157][Para 196]. The third arbitrator's name appears as both "Toun Siphann" and "Tuon Siphann" [Para 20][Para 241], and the heading "Reason for the 4th decision" is duplicated mid-sentence at the Article 332 passage [Para 226]. One worker's name is given as both Keo Chinda and Keo Chinday [Para 25][Para 44].
