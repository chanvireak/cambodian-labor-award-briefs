# Award No. 24/03 — Top One Garment Factory (Arbitration Council, 31 January 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ms Y Suonty (employer-selected), Mr L Sovanna (worker-selected), chair Dr Sok Mathoeung · Doc ID 138836

## 1. Facts
Top One Garment Factory, #26B Street 5, Svay Pak Quarter, Reusey Keo District, Phnom Penh, employed 765 workers, of whom most were members of the Solidarity Labor Union of Top One, the most representative union at the factory. [Para 1][Para 26][Para 27] The parties began negotiating a first collective bargaining agreement (CBA); the negotiations failed and led to a strike in November 2003, the conflict was reported to MOSALVY on 13 November 2003, and its representative mediated part of it on 17 November 2003. [Para 28][Para 29] The remaining conflicts — the award says 52 in one place and 53 in another — were sent to the Arbitration Council, which heard the case on 11, 17, 19, 22 and 24 December 2003 and 8, 10 and 15 January 2004, after the employer withdrew from a mediation the chair had been assigned to conduct. [Para 3][Para 24][Para 29][Para 30][Para 32][Para 34]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must recognise union representation rights — accompaniment of workers in dispute resolution, maintenance of the representative's wages and bonus, two hours per week of union work, and access for outside advisers? (conflicts 1–6) [Para 4][Para 42]
2. Whether the employer must provide the infirmary, staffing and medical equipment demanded, by reference to Prakas No. 330? (conflicts 7–11) [Para 63][Para 66][Para 67]
3. Whether casual workers must receive the same wages, bonuses and benefits as permanent employees? (conflict 15) [Para 76][Para 82]
4. Whether wages, seniority bonus and inflation adjustment must be raised, and on what basis, the employer having declined to produce financial records? (conflicts 47–49) [Para 151][Para 155]
5. Whether the employer must pay wages during certified sick leave, grant 100 days' maternity leave at full pay, and maintain seniority? (conflicts 26, 28) [Para 107][Para 115][Para 120]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The union sought a CBA covering the 53 listed demands, including a US$5 monthly attendance bonus, a US$2 seniority bonus rising 5% a year, wages raised toward US$60, 19 days' annual leave, triple pay for holiday and night overtime, and a 400,000 riel monthly employer contribution to a well-being fund. [Para 9][Para 16][Para 18][Para 105][Para 169]
**Employer (respondent):** The employer objected that a casual worker's salary should include wages only, declined to attend mediation and sent an objection to the Secretariat, and did not answer the panel's request for audited financial statements or send a representative to explain its financial capacity. [Para 32][Para 78][Para 152][Para 155]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — both parties must comply with the conditions of the collective bargaining agreement annexed to the award as annex "A", which records every conflict, agreed or decided, in sequence. [Para 229][Para 230]

On representation, the panel held a union exists to protect members' rights and that Prakas No. 317 and No. 318 of 29 November 2001 let employees be assisted by a representative, so accompaniment was founded and wages and bonuses could not be cut; a most representative union gets two hours a week, as worker delegates do under the Labour Law, and seminar attendance requires the employer's prior permission with reasons if refused. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45][Para 47][Para 49][Para 52][Para 54] On health, it applied clauses 2 and 3 of Prakas No. 330 to order a medical room of at least 20 square metres with beds, two standby nurses and a doctor for three hours a day, plus four armchairs and a water cooler, while refusing equipment beyond the Prakas. [Para 65][Para 67][Para 68] It held casual workers are covered by the Labour Law and Notification No. 017, so they must receive bonuses and benefits like permanent employees, with attendance bonus pro rata to the days the employer sets. [Para 80][Para 81][Para 82][Para 83] On wages — an interests dispute — the panel reviewed national accounts, export and productivity data because the employer produced no audited records, found the wage share of hourly value added had fallen from 46% (2000) to 32% (2002), and raised the basic wage 5%, from US$45 to US$47.25, with inflation adjustment for 2002 only. [Para 174][Para 194][Para 196][Para 199][Para 200][Para 218] It ordered sick-leave wages under clause 4 of the internal work rule (full pay first month, 60% for the second and third, position kept to the sixth) and full salary and seniority bonus for three months' maternity leave. [Para 110][Para 111][Para 120] It denied 19 days' annual leave and seven-hour Saturdays unless an hour is made up, ordered pay slips in Khmer, 200% for Sunday and 100% for holiday overtime with the 1000 riel meal allowance, and held the well-being fund and annual party were matters for the employer's discretion. [Para 105][Para 107][Para 136][Para 141][Para 223]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a first CBA is in dispute and the employer withholds audited financial information, the panel will decide wage claims on law and equity using national industry data; a rise in the basic wage is justified where the workers' share of hourly value added has fallen over successive years, and casual workers are entitled to the same statutory bonuses as permanent employees, in proportion to the days the employer sets for them. [Para 83][Para 155][Para 196][Para 212]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that most workers do not take annual leave, so a radio or cassette player at work is appropriate and reduces fatigue, while an annual staff party depends on the employer's generosity; it also noted arbitrators may use discretionary powers to visit victims of sexual harassment but that the Council decides only collective disputes in the non-conciliation report. [Para 130][Para 133][Para 136]
