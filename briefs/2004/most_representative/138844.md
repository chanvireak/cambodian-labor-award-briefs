# Award No. 11/04 — CAMS (Cambodia Airport Management Services) and Union of Independent Employees of International Airport (Arbitration Council, 16 March 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, chosen by the employer; An Nan per the panel composition, Liv Sovanna per the signature block, chosen by the workers; Ang Eng Thong, chair) · Doc ID 138844

## 1. Facts
CAMS employs 645 workers at Pochentong (Phnom Penh) International Airport; five Lost and Found staff complained on 04 February 2004 that their monthly wage of USD160 was below the USD180 paid to Check-In staff for work they say uses similar techniques. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 23] A collective bargaining agreement signed with the most representative union on 11 February 2004 had already raised the Lost and Found rate from USD136 to USD160 while setting Check-In at USD180. [Para 25][Para 27] Conciliation failed on 27 February 2004 and the non-conciliation report of 01 March 2004 reached the Secretariat on 03 March 2004; the disputed act is the refusal of a further increase to USD180. [Para 12][Para 22][Para 45]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the Lost and Found staff are entitled to a wage increase from USD160 to USD180, equal to the Check-In rate? [Para 9][Para 23]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The two sections historically shared the same wage and use similar, largely computer-based techniques, and Lost and Found duties — handling passenger complaints over lost and damaged baggage and now caring for baggage itself — are no less onerous than Check-In duties; their representatives missed the final bargaining day of 22 January 2004 on urgent personal business, so the demand was never put. [Para 27][Para 29][Para 30][Para 32]
**Employer (respondent):** Lost and Found requires different techniques from Check-In, which now carries more skills and responsibilities; the 11 February 2004 agreement already lifted the section from USD136 to USD160 and, by its terms, bargaining continues but the wage issue may not be reopened. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 32][Para 34]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the Lost and Found workers' complaint for an increase from USD160 to USD180 is rejected. [Para 45]
The panel began from Article 96(2) and Article 97 of the Labour Law, under which a collective agreement binds the employer and all categories of workers in the establishments it covers, and from Article 9 of Prakas 305 SALVY of 22 November 2001, under which the union with most representative status may bargain for all workers it represents. [Para 34][Para 35] On the strength of MoSALVY letter No. 085 SALVY of 06 March 2003 recognising that status, it held the Union of Independent Employees of International Airport competent to sign for the whole workforce, so the agreement's wage terms reached the five complainants whether or not they sat at the table. [Para 35][Para 42] It traced the negotiation from December 2003, through the agreed postponement to 22 January 2004 signed by all sides and the 22 January session held before the head of Dangkor's Labour Inspection office with the union's leadership and activists present, to formal signature on 11 February 2004, and found the procedure lawful so that the agreement bound both parties and all represented workers. [Para 36][Para 38][Para 39][Para 41][Para 42] The demand therefore contradicted the agreement and could be entertained only if the agreement were shown null and void, and no such evidence was produced. [Para 43][Para 44] The panel recorded that the complaint was procedurally correct but had no legal basis, and dismissed it. [Para 45]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A wage demand that contradicts a collective agreement concluded by the most representative union under lawful procedure fails unless the claimants prove the agreement itself null and void: the agreement binds every worker the union represents, including workers of a section whose representatives were absent from the final bargaining session, and absence from bargaining is not a ground for reopening what was settled. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remark that the demand, "though procedurally correct, has no legal basis" separates the right to complain from the merits, and its observation that unequal treatment between two sections "would be unjust" assumes facts it did not need to decide. [Para 28][Para 45]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel is differently composed on its own face: the composition section names An Nan as the arbitrator chosen by the workers, while the signature block is signed by Liv Sovanna in that seat. [Para 14][Para 45] The postponement date is printed two ways — "15 January 2003 (2004?)". [Para 37] A document in the employer's exhibit list is dated 12 December 2004, after the award, and is presumably a mis-keyed 2003. [Para 18] OCR loss is constant ("Inon-conciliation", "JURISDICTIONT", "arises out of the outcome" for "arose", "the five employees claimed" for "five employees"), and the exhibit list runs unnumbered past item 13. [Para 9][Para 10][Para 27] Two different conciliation dates appear — 10 February 2004 in the jurisdiction section and 27 February 2004 in the case summary — and the 01 March 2004 non-conciliation report is consistent with the latter. [Para 11][Para 22][Para 12] Only USD amounts survive; no riel equivalent is printed. Employer representatives, union officials and the individual complainants are named in the original and are given here as (name redacted).
