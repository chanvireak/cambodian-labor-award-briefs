# Award No. 57/04 — Evergreen Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 9 August 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Kao Thach (chosen by the employer party), Tuon Siphann (chosen by the worker party), Ang Eng Thong (chair) · Doc ID 139330

## 1. Facts
Evergreen Garment Co., Ltd employs 1,000 workers. Until a new employer took over, the factory ran three eight-hour shifts; with less work it now runs a single shift, 6:00 a.m. to 2:30 p.m. with a 30-minute lunch break inside that span. The Independent Apparel Workers Union complained to the Labour Inspection Office of Khan Reussey Keo on 7 June 2004; conciliation on 30 June 2004 settled five of seven issues, and the two survivors were wages for workless days and the shift ending. After the hearing the parties settled the first issue themselves on 6 August 2004, leaving the panel the second, and both sides declared the award not binding. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 28][Para 30][Para 33]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay 100 percent of wages (US$1.73 per day) when it has no work for less than three days, and 50 percent when it has no work for more than three days? [Para 7]
2. Whether the 30-minute lunch break must count as working time, so that the shift ends at 2:00 p.m. rather than 2:30 p.m. when the company runs one or two shifts? [Para 34]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** wages are owed in full (US$1.73 per day) for short workless periods and at 50 percent for longer ones; and when there are one or two shifts the working day should run 6:00 a.m. to 2:00 p.m. with the 30-minute lunch break inside the eight hours. [Para 7][Para 34]
**Employer (respondent):** it pays 100 percent where a workless day comes without notice and 50 percent where it gives notice of more than two days; and the shift is 6:00 a.m. to 2:30 p.m. with a half-hour lunch, because scheduling is the enterprise's own right and the break is not hours worked. [Para 8][Para 9][Para 11]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the demand that 30 minutes of lunch break be included in working hours so as to end the shift at 2:00 p.m. when total work time is divided into one or two shifts; the wage question had already been agreed by the parties and was not decided. [Para 30][Para 41]
The panel took Article 137 of the Labour Law (1997), which caps work at eight hours a day and 48 hours a week, together with the first paragraph of Article 138, under which each enterprise sets its own work schedule according to the nature of its activity and its organisation of work. On that basis the 6:00 a.m. to 2:30 p.m. schedule does not exceed eight hours, and setting it is the company's legal right. [Para 36][Para 37]
The panel then held that a 30-minute lunch break is not working time as the Labour Law uses that term, and that the workers may take it within the eight hours only where the employer agrees. It found nothing in the employment contract or the company's Internal Work Rules obliging the company to give 30 paid minutes for lunch — although Article 4 of those rules fixes eight hours a day and 48 hours a week with a one-hour lunch break, a rule the panel noted but did not treat as creating the obligation claimed. [Para 36][Para 38][Para 39]
Finally the panel characterised the claim: with no legal provision requiring paid lunch, and with the union not entitled to be treated as the most representative union in the factory, the dispute is an interests dispute rather than a rights dispute, and the union is not legally entitled to represent all workers in it. It followed 31/03 Hong Wah Garment Cambodia. [Para 35][Para 39][Para 40]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A lunch break is not working time, and a claim to have it paid is an interests dispute sounding in negotiation, not a rights claim; a union that is not the most representative union in the enterprise cannot bring such a claim on behalf of the whole workforce. Within the eight-hour day and 48-hour week of Articles 137 and 138, the employer's choice of shift start and end is lawful and the panel will not rewrite it. [Para 36][Para 39][Para 40]

**Obiter dicta:** counting the 30-minute lunch break as paid working time would give the workers more than the law requires, since the law merely permits it where the employer agrees. [Para 38]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The non-conciliation report is said to have reached the Secretariat on 1 July 2004 in one place and on 16 July 2004 in another. The certificate of commercial registration is dated 15 October 2004, after the award date of 9 August 2004. Conciliation is recorded as settling five of seven issues, yet only the first issue is then shown as agreed, on 6 August 2004, with the hearing of 26 July 2004 having left two issues open. Two sentences in the findings are printed twice, "Signea.," stands for "Signed", and the worker-side representatives' titles are garbled ("President of Vice the Independent Apparel Apparel Workers Union"); the union appears both as the Independent Apparel Workers Union and as the Cambodian Independent Apparel Workers Union of Evergreen Garment Co., Ltd. The employer's answer on wages is stated as notice-based (one day without notice, more than two days with notice) while the workers' demand is threshold-based at three days. The award records that both parties declared it not binding. [Para 14][Para 22][Para 25][Para 28][Para 29][Para 30][Para 31]

Names of the employer's lawyer, the union officials and the workers are redacted here as the award prints them only as bare individuals.
