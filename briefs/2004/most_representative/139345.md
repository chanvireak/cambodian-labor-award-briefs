# Award No. 83/04 — June Textiles Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 15 October 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry, Tuon Siphann, Men Nimith chair) · non-binding award · Doc ID 139345

## 1. Facts
June Textiles Garment employed 4,000 workers, mostly women, on Russian Federation Boulevard, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, with no day-care centre in operation. On 3 September 2004 the workers complained through their union (KYFTU) after an accountant in the cutting section, Ms N, was said to have withheld part of their attendance bonus; conciliation on 4–7 September 2004 failed and eight demands reached the panel. The parties agreed the award would be non-binding. [Para 17][Para 18][Para 19][Para 16]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must give each mother who has just given birth three one-kilogram cans of milk powder per month and pay US$20 per month for a babysitter? [Para 9]
2. Whether the company must stop questioning workers who resign from the union and stop deducting union dues? [Para 9]
3. Whether the company must take measures under the Labour Law against the Chinese chief of the production department and Ms N, who exploited workers' wages? [Para 9][Para 10]
4. Whether the company must play tapes for the workers during working hours? [Para 11]
5. Whether the company must increase the wages of group chiefs and hygiene-section workers by US$10 each? [Para 11]
6. Whether the company must organise a place for apprentice training? [Para 11]
7. Whether the company must pay a thirteenth-month bonus? [Para 11]
8. Whether the company must organise an election for a new term of workers' representatives? [Para 11]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mothers needed milk and childcare money because no day-care centre existed; the company should stop interrogating union resignees; Ms N had hidden part of their pay and should be dismissed; wages of group chiefs should rise by US$10 and a thirteenth month be paid, as other factories such as Yung Wah did. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 25][Para 39]

**Employer (respondent):** The nursing room and day-care centre were already built and would open within three months; apprentices learn faster on the production line, which is standard practice; it could take no measure against Ms N because it had no evidence, and workers are paid according to capability. [Para 30][Para 34][Para 42][Para 56]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must pay US$15 per month to women workers with children over 18 months placed in outside care until its own centre meets Prakas standards, finish the nursing room within three months, and allow two hours a day for breastfeeding; must reimburse the money lost; the demands to dismiss Ms N, to raise wages and pay a thirteenth month, and to provide a separate apprentice area were rejected. [Para 116][Para 118][Para 119][Para 120][Para 121]

On issue 1 the panel applied Articles 184–186 of the Labour Law: a manager employing at least one hundred women must provide a nursing room and crèche, or pay the cost of outside care for children over 18 months, and must allow one hour a day for breastfeeding, which it held could not be replaced by formula milk. Article 186 gives two options, and where the buildings exist but are not yet usable the payment obligation runs in the interim. [Para 42][Para 71][Para 72][Para 74][Para 75]

The amount followed prior awards: in 68/04 the panel had found US$15 per month to be the cost of external care, and in 56/04 the parties settled at US$15. Although the law fixes one hour on the premises, it is silent on time for mothers who travel home, so the panel allowed two hours, one in the morning and one in the afternoon. [Para 43][Para 45][Para 78][Para 84][Para 85]

On issue 3 the panel found the company responsible for its representative's act: the employer admitted a loss of US$2 to US$5 per worker, and Article 114, which bars the employer from restricting a worker's use of wages, was engaged because Ms N acted as the company. Following awards 18/03 (Hu Hing Garment) and 70/04 (Hana), it held it has no power to order the dismissal of a manager or accountant, that right belonging to the employer, and ordered reimbursement instead. [Para 90][Para 91][Para 92][Para 97][Para 100][Para 101]

Issues 5 and 7 were held to be interests disputes: the workers already received minimum wages and the KYFTU lacked most representative status, the CLUF holding it, so the union could not conclude a collective bargaining agreement and the panel could not issue an award that would substitute for one. A thirteenth month likewise rested only on another factory's practice. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 104][Para 109]

On issue 6, Article 8 of the Annexure to Prakas 04/00 requires a place for training with both theory and practice, not a separate place; the panel accepted line training but warned the factory not to discipline, threaten or blame apprentices whose output is slower. [Para 110][Para 111][Para 115][Para 116]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an enterprise employs at least one hundred women but its nursing room and crèche are not yet in service, Article 186 obliges it to pay the going rate for external childcare — US$15 per month, as fixed in awards 68/04 and 56/04 — for each child over 18 months, until compliant facilities open; and an employer cannot escape liability for money an accountant withheld, because a manager's act in disbursing wages is the company's act, though the panel will not order that manager's dismissal. [Para 44][Para 86][Para 92][Para 100]

**Obiter dicta:** That mothers are entitled to two rather than one hour a day where they must travel home to feed, and that apprentices on a production line should not be blamed for slower output. [Para 85][Para 115]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The parties agreed a non-binding award (paragraph 17), yet the closing boilerplate states it binds after eight days unless opposed. The shop-steward election is dated "late October 2005" in the reasons but October 2004 in the case summary and decision. The accountant's name appears as both "Ngoev Lay Im" and "Ngeov Lay Im", and the union acronym is printed "KYFTUJT" on the face. The summary's statement that 4,000 women workers have "approximately 20 children between them" is evidently a lost figure. Decision numbering runs 1, 2, 3, 5 and 7, 6, with no 4. OCR losses are pervasive ("theemployer", "bargaing", "traing", "vidence"), and individuals are identified here by role or initial. [Para 1][Para 17][Para 41][Para 65][Para 121]
