# Award No. 81/04 — Evergreen Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 4 October 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, Sin Kimsean, Men Nimith chair) · non-binding award · Doc ID 139346

## 1. Facts
Evergreen Garment Co., Ltd, at #289 National Road 5, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, employed 1,150 workers and had two unions: CIWFTU with about 850 members and CCAWDU with about 350. A worker, Ms O, had signed five successive six-month contracts from 2002; the last expired on 19 August 2004 and the company did not renew it. On 23 August 2004 the company suspended 282 workers on three production lines for lack of orders. CCAWDU complained of discrimination and of the non-renewal. [Para 22][Para 18][Para 41][Para 44][Para 55]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must reinstate Ms O to the same position in accordance with the agreement of 19 February 2004? [Para 5]
2. Whether the company must give equal rights to the two unions in the factory, CCAWDU and CIWFTU? [Para 5]
3. Whether the company must finish paying wages to workers by 4:00 p.m.? [Para 5]
4. Whether the company must allow monitoring and thread-cutting workers to enter their workstations fifteen minutes earlier? [Para 5]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Five successive six-month contracts took Ms O past two years, so her contract became of undetermined duration and could only end for serious misconduct, economic reasons or an act of God under Articles 74, 82 and 83; the company favoured CIWFTU and suspended mostly CCAWDU members; and workers should be paid by 4:00 p.m. and allowed in fifteen minutes early. [Para 29][Para 33][Para 39][Para 40][Para 53][Para 62]

**Employer (respondent):** Ms O was let go at the expiry of a fixed-duration contract, the parties had agreed in writing on two years, and the company faced economic difficulty with too few orders; it denied bias, saying the suspension followed normal procedure on 13 lines with no goods and was notified to the Labour Inspector with 50 percent wages. [Para 46][Para 49][Para 54][Para 56][Para 99]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must reinstate Ms O by placing her on the list of workers whose contracts are suspended and pay her 50 percent wages from the date it decided not to renew; the discrimination complaint was rejected; the company must pay wages within working hours by 4:30 p.m.; the demand to enter workstations fifteen minutes early was declined. [Para 117][Para 119]

On issue 1 the decisive evidence was the five contracts, which the panel totalled at 6 months × 5 = 30 months, or two and a half years. Following award 10/03 (Jacqusintex), which reads Article 67(2) as converting a fixed-duration contract into an indefinite one once any renewal carries it beyond two years, the panel held the contract indefinite before termination, so the employer needed a ground under Article 74 (aptitude or behaviour), Article 82 (act of God) or Articles 82–83 (serious misconduct). [Para 68][Para 69][Para 70]

None was given. The panel rejected the employer's arithmetic of two years and three months, rejected the written two-year agreement as inconsistent with a contract that had already run two and a half years, and found the claim of economic difficulty unsupported by clear evidence. On law and evidence alone it would have ordered reinstatement with full wages. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 78]

It did not, because the company had suspended 282 workers for lack of orders; the panel reasoned that it would be very difficult to find Ms O a job, and on the particular facts and equity ordered her included among the suspended workers at 50 percent wages instead. [Para 81][Para 82][Para 85][Para 118]

On issue 2 the panel applied Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law, which forbid taking union membership or activity into account in hiring, assignment, promotion, pay, discipline or dismissal. CCAWDU produced no specific worker or act showing discrimination; the suspension had covered both unions' members, and a rejected leave request was refused because the form came late under procedures common to all. The complaint had no clear basis. [Para 90][Para 91][Para 98][Para 103][Para 104]

Issue 3 was settled by agreement at the hearing at 4:30 p.m. with prior written notice for delay. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 104]

On issue 4 the panel held the fifteen minutes to be the workers' own time, outside working hours and outside the employer's legal obligations, so the demand was an interests dispute. The panel may hear interests disputes, but under Article 43 of Prakas 099/2004 an award settling one replaces a collective bargaining agreement for a year; CCAWDU, with 350 members against CIWFTU's 850, lacked most representative status under Article 277 and so could not bargain for the whole factory. Following 31/03 (Hong Wah) and 60/04 (United Art), it declined to consider the demand. [Para 107][Para 109][Para 110][Para 112][Para 113][Para 115][Para 116]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where successive fixed-duration contracts carry a worker's total service beyond two years, the contract is of undetermined duration under Article 67(2) as interpreted in 10/03, and non-renewal is a dismissal that must rest on a ground in Articles 74, 82 or 83; but if the enterprise has since suspended a large part of its workforce for lack of orders, the panel may put the worker on the suspension list at 50 percent wages rather than order full reinstatement pay. [Para 69][Para 77][Para 85][Para 118]

**Obiter dicta:** That a cool, clean resting place benefits both productivity and health, that a worker's personal break time falls outside the employer's legal obligations, and that an award on an interests dispute bars strikes on other interests issues for a year. [Para 104][Para 105][Para 111]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The hearing is dated "Wednesday, 5 September 2004" in the hearing record, a date that fell on a Sunday, while the case summary puts the hearing on 15 September 2004 at 2:30 p.m. The complaint is said to have been received on "25 August 2005". The worker party computed total service at 29 months (two years five months) and the panel at 30 months. The non-conciliation report is numbered 2375 and dated 9 September 2004 in one place and 8 September 2004 in the document list. Issue 3 is pleaded at 4:00 p.m. and decided at 4:30 p.m. The evidence list mentions a suspension of 282 workers but a report on 255 suspensions and 37 terminations. The parties agreed the award would not be binding, yet the closing boilerplate says it binds after eight days. OCR losses are heavy ("theemployer", "undetermineduration", "bargaing"), and individual workers are identified here by initial. [Para 9][Para 20][Para 21][Para 24][Para 31][Para 43][Para 120]
