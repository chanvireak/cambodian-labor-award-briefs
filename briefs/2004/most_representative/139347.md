# Award No. 86/04 — Ho Hing Garment Factory (Arbitration Council, 10 November 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach, Tuon Siphann, Sok Mathoeun chair) · Doc ID 139347

## 1. Facts
Ho Hing Garment Factory at #266 Street 271, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, employed 470 workers and had two unions: the Free Worker Union, which held the certificate of most representative status (no. 380, 8 August 2003), and the Khmer Free Worker Union, registered 27 October 2003 with about 450 members and refused that status. The workers complained on 31 August 2004; conciliation on 21 September 2004 settled one of three disputes, and two pay-related demands reached the panel, which held hearings on 22 October and 6 November 2004. [Para 18][Para 22][Para 24][Para 19][Para 21][Para 17]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must apply the same cutting-section piece-work rate to the ironing section as to the cutting and sewing sections? [Para 11]
2. Whether the company must provide the same sewing-section daily bonus to workers in the finishing section? [Para 12]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Seven ironing workers received only the US$45 minimum wage for 26 or 27 days a month while piece workers did fewer days and earned more, so the piece rate should extend to ironing; and the finishing section should receive the sewing section's daily bonus, the company's own finishing bonus procedure of 24 July 2004 being unattainable. [Para 25][Para 27][Para 30]

**Employer (respondent):** Ironing cannot be paid by the piece because the volume of garments to iron varies from much to nothing, and ironing workers already receive US$45 or more; bonuses for the finishing section are set by a separate method in force from 24 July 2004 and tied to units achieved. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 28][Para 29]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel declined to issue an award on either interest dispute and ordered the employer and both unions to negotiate a collective bargaining agreement containing a solution to both disputes before 31 March 2005, meeting at least one hour a week. [Para 40][Para 43][Para 44]

On issue 1 the panel compared four months of payroll ledgers and found that piece workers generally worked fewer than 26 days but earned more than the ironing workers, who received the statutory minimum of US$45 for 26 or 27 days. The demand, however, was not grounded in the Labour Law 1997 or in Announcement 017/00, so the panel classified it as an interest dispute rather than a rights dispute. [Para 25][Para 31]

Issue 2 was classified the same way: the finishing workers also received the legal minimum wage, and the daily bonus they sought appears neither in the Labour Law nor in Notification 17/00. [Para 32]

Having found both to be interest disputes, the panel turned to Article 43 of Prakas 99/04, under which an award settling an interest dispute becomes the collective bargaining agreement for one year, and to Article 321 of the Labour Law, which would bar strikes and lock-outs on those issues for that year. Since issue 1 concerned only seven workers and issue 2 about 150 of 470, and the Khmer Free Worker Union lacked most representative status, an award would impose a factory-wide agreement on workers who were not parties to the dispute and strip them of their right to strike, which the panel held would be unfair, following awards 04/03 (Lida), 06/04 (Chou Sing), 24/03 (Top One), 61/04 (Best Novel) and 62/04 (Ecent), and 31/03 (Hong Var) and 60/04 (United Art) on unions without that status. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 34][Para 36][Para 37]

It referred the parties to 24/03 (Top One) as a model agreement under Article 34(e) and 34(g) of Prakas 99/04, and to Article 96(1) of the Labour Law on the purpose of negotiation, observing that the Khmer Free Worker Union's 450 members would satisfy Article 277(1-b) while the certificate is held by the Free Worker Union. It ordered immediate tripartite negotiation. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40][Para 43]

Separately, the panel held the company's finishing-section bonus scheme unreasonable because its weekly targets of 25,000 to 35,000 units were beyond the capability of a normal worker. [Para 30][Para 42]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A panel will refuse to decide an interest dispute, and will instead order the parties into collective bargaining, where the complainant union lacks most representative status and the workers affected are a minority, because an award would substitute for a collective bargaining agreement binding every worker in the enterprise and would extinguish the strike rights of workers who were never parties to the dispute. [Para 33][Para 35][Para 36][Para 40]

**Obiter dicta:** That the Khmer Free Worker Union's 450 members appear to satisfy Article 277(1-b) for most representative status, and that the finishing-section bonus targets are beyond a normal worker's capacity. [Para 39][Para 42]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award records that the two issues were submitted to the Council on 15 October 2004, yet the non-conciliation report is numbered 247, dated 26 October 2004 and submitted on 27 October 2004 — after the first hearing on 22 October. The MoLVT report in the document list is separately dated 6 October 2004. The statutory instrument is printed three ways: "Announcement 017/00", "Notification 17/00" and "Notification 17/00" again, and the Labour Law provision appears as both "Article 277 (1-b)" and, in other awards cited, "Article 277(b)". One prior award is named "Hong Var" here and "Hong Wah" elsewhere in the same series. The finishing-bonus table is badly garbled, its unit and bonus columns interleaved so the weekly quotas cannot be read with certainty. The parties agreed the award was not immediately binding, while the closing boilerplate says it binds after eight days. OCR losses run throughout ("theemployer", "bargaing", "containg", "Ho ing Company"), and individuals are identified here by role. [Para 17][Para 22][Para 31][Para 32][Para 39][Para 14][Para 30]
