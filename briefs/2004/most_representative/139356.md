# Award No. 99/04 — AIA Company (Arbitration Council, 15 December 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach, Ven Pov, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 139356

## 1. Facts
AIA Company, at #44 Street 265, Sangkat Tek lak, Khan Toulkok, Phnom Penh, employed 300 workers when the Khmer Youth Federation of Trade Union complained to the Labour Inspector on 27 October 2004 over eighteen demands. [Para 27] Five were conciliated on 16 November 2004 and thirteen reached the panel, which from the outset limited itself to rights disputes because the union held no most representative status. [Para 28][Para 29] The parties agreed to a non-binding award. [Para 26]

## 2. Issues in dispute
The panel kept the numbered order of the non-conciliation report and decided only the first six and the last seven as rights matters. [Para 29]
1. Whether the employer must install a nursing room and day care centre or pay the cost of outside child care? [Para 2][Para 31]
2. Whether the employer must pay the return transport of probationary workers it recruited from far away and dismissed? [Para 2][Para 34]
3. Whether the employer must take further measures to reduce workplace heat? [Para 3][Para 37]
4. Whether the employer must build a motorcycle and bicycle shelter and a canteen? [Para 4][Para 39]
5. Whether the employer must grant special leave beyond wedding days and without docking daily wages? [Para 6][Para 40]
6. Whether a new election of shop stewards must be organised? [Para 6][Para 41]
7. Whether the US$5 attendance bonus must survive arrival fifteen minutes late? [Para 8][Para 55]
8. Whether past piece rates below the minimum wage must be repaid? [Para 9][Para 56]
9. Whether the employer must negotiate with the union before any dismissal? [Para 10][Para 56]
10. Whether wages must rise for workers on heavy work? [Para 11][Para 56]
11. Whether the employer must notify the Labour Inspector of every recruitment and dismissal? [Para 12][Para 56]
12. Whether the employer must deduct 1,000 riel monthly for union dues? [Para 13][Para 56]
13. Whether the employer may deduct double wages for absence without permission? [Para 14][Para 57]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company should meet the eighteen demands, from a day care centre and cooler workplace to special leave for family illness and an end to double deductions, and should negotiate before dismissing anyone. [Para 2][Para 6][Para 14]
**Employer (respondent):** it could not afford the day care centre, milk, cooling, shelter or wage increases, said it paid piece rates lawfully and had already made up wages to US$45, and maintained that it had elected shop stewards and negotiated where required. [Para 3][Para 9][Para 11]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to organise a nursing room and day care centre under Ministry of Labour supervision, or to pay child care costs against invoices from women workers using outside centres, starting January 2005; to place a thermometer in the factory; to grant special leave under Prakas 267; and to deduct union contributions monthly between the 10th and 12th. All other demands were rejected. [Para 128][Para 129][Para 130][Para 131][Para 133]

On the day care centre the panel applied Articles 186 and 187: an employer with at least one hundred women must install a nursing room and child care centre, and for children over eighteen months must cover the cost of an outside centre; a cash payment in place of the room is not permitted, following 63/04 Shinewell and 68/04 City New. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 62] The employer offered no evidence of incapacity. [Para 33]
The transport claim failed for want of proof: Article 188 covers workers recruited far from the workplace whose trip the employer paid, but the union supplied neither names nor numbers of dismissed probationary workers, and its demand was expressly for the future. [Para 62][Para 65][Para 68] The panel held it resolves extant disputes, not hypothetical ones. [Para 71]
On heat it found Article 229 and Praks 125/01 and 147/02 impose a duty to keep workplaces at a suitable temperature, but rejected the claim because the workers produced no temperature readings; it nonetheless ordered a thermometer so that future claims could be evidenced. [Para 75][Para 79][Para 80][Para 129]
The shelter and canteen demand, the late-arrival bonus, the wage increase for heavy work and the demand to negotiate before dismissal were classified as interest disputes or beyond the law, and left to negotiation because the union lacked most representative status under Article 277 and so could not make a collective bargaining agreement that Article 43 of Prakas 99/04 would turn into a binding award for all workers. [Para 83][Para 85][Para 90][Para 89]
On special leave the panel applied Article 171 and Prakas 267/01 clause 1, which lists seven days a year for the worker's wedding, a wife's childbirth, a child's wedding and illness or death of close family; allowing leave only for weddings contradicted the Prakas, and deducting normal daily wages for it was not open to the employer. [Para 96][Para 98]
On the election, the panel held it had jurisdiction over a union election under Articles 302 and 312, Article 298 routing fair-election complaints to the Labour Court without excluding arbitration, but rejected the demand because the union's vice-president admitted he knew of and took part in the election, and because a letter of 24 March 2003 recognised three shop stewards whose mandate ran to March 2005. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 106][Para 107][Para 108]
Past piece rates failed for lack of evidence and because the company had made up pay to US$45; notification of recruitment and dismissal under Article 21 was left to the Labour Inspector's monitoring, the workers losing nothing by its breach. [Para 112][Para 119] On union dues the panel read Article 281 with Article 129 and Prakas 305 clause 5: deduction is the employer's obligation once workers consent in writing, so the company was ordered to deduct and remit. [Para 121][Para 133] Finally, double deduction for unauthorised absence was held to breach Article 27's proportionality rule and the company's own Internal Work Rules, which treat absence as minor, medium or serious misconduct according to its length. [Para 123][Para 127][Para 128]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer has at least one hundred women workers, Article 186 obliges it to provide a nursing room and day care centre or to pay the invoiced cost of outside care; paying cash to the mothers instead is not compliance, and a union without most representative status cannot bring an interest dispute to arbitration. [Para 60][Para 89][Para 90]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel remarked that the workers lose nothing where an employer fails to notify the Labour Inspector under Article 21, and that its jurisdiction covers elections although Article 298 sends fair-election complaints to the Labour Court. [Para 102][Para 119]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award fixes the non-conciliated issues as reaching the Council on 19 December 2004, after the 6 December hearing the same paragraph describes. [Para 29] It records the parties' agreement to a non-binding award, while the closing boilerplate makes it binding eight days after notification. [Para 26][Para 136] Shop stewards are named three ways — Keo Daline, Sem Sarom, Chouk Kunthea and Chhok Kunthea — and the union vice-president appears as both Ses Sophy and Sek Sophy. [Para 55][Para 105][Para 107] The establishing Prakas number is omitted and the traditional "Prakas" is printed "Praks" in places. [Para 16][Para 77]
