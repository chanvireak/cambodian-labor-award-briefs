# Award No. 98/04 — Great Union Company (Arbitration Council, 30 December 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach, Liv Sovanna, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 139355

## 1. Facts
Great Union Company, at building #646, National Road No. 2, Sangkat Chak Angre Krom, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employed 625 workers who filed a complaint with the Khan Mean Chey Labour Inspector on 7 September 2004 raising twelve working conditions. [Para 30][Para 31] Seven of the twelve were conciliated on 5 November 2004 and three more were settled at the hearing, leaving the medical check-up fee and the length of fixed duration contracts. [Para 22][Para 33][Para 38] The Cambodian Worker Union had roughly 300 members and no most representative certificate. [Para 39][Para 41]

## 2. Issues in dispute
The panel heard only the second and third of the five non-conciliated points. [Para 39]
1. Whether the employer must reimburse the medical check-up fee the workers paid themselves over the last three years? [Para 12][Para 45]
2. Whether the employer must make fixed duration contracts of twelve months, and whether workers whose successive fixed duration contracts exceed two years must be treated as employed under undetermined duration contracts? [Para 13][Para 50]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** payment of the medical check-up is by law the employer's obligation, so the fees must be reimbursed; the six-month contracts are too short and should be twelve months. [Para 12][Para 15][Para 46]
**Employer (respondent):** the check-up is a condition of recruitment, covered by a verbal arrangement that workers would pay, and it declined reimbursement; on contracts it said it would apply the Labour Law and explained that six-month terms let it control workforce capacity. [Para 13][Para 16][Para 47]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to pay a medical check-up fee of 10,100 riel to each worker employed since the beginning of 2002 whose certificate names Great Union, payment complete within seven days of the award taking effect; the demand for twelve-month contracts was refused, and the company was ordered to convert to undetermined duration contracts the contracts of workers who have more than two years' service. [Para 60][Para 86][Para 87][Para 88]

On the medical fee the panel relied on Article 247 of the Labour Law, holding that it supplies a sufficient legal basis for the employer's obligation even without a new Prakas, and noted that Joint Prakas 9/94 on medical check-ups also places the cost on the employer. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 53] It acknowledged its own conflicting opinions on whether Prakas 9/94 survives the 1997 Law, then rested the outcome on Article 247 alone, following 64/04 Mercury Garment. [Para 55][Para 56] That the workers paid first did not discharge the employer; Article 377 attaches a fine of one hundred twenty to three hundred sixty days of base daily wage and imprisonment of one to five years to breaches of Article 247. [Para 59][Para 60]

On the twelve-month term the panel held that Article 67(2) merely caps fixed duration contracts at two years and permits any shorter length, so the demand exceeded the employer's legal obligation and was an interest demand. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63] On conversion it read Article 67(2) with Article 73(5) and the French original of the Law, concluding that the two-year maximum refers to the total length of the employment relationship, so any renewal that carries the total beyond two years produces an undetermined duration contract; the union's status only mattered to the interest demand, not to this rights claim. [Para 69][Para 83][Para 85]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The cost of the pre-employment medical examination lies on the employer under Article 247 of the Labour Law even where the worker has already paid the clinic, and successive fixed duration renewals that carry the total length of the relationship beyond two years convert it into an undetermined duration contract. [Para 51][Para 85]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the Law favours undetermined duration contracts because they give work security, and used ILO Recommendation 166/1982 as context for interpreting Article 67 although it creates no obligation. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 79]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award records that both parties decided at the hearing that the award is not binding, while the closing boilerplate states it binds eight days after notification unless opposed. [Para 29][Para 88] The establishing Prakas is printed as 38/02 here, against 338/02 in parallel awards. [Para 21] The panel's own list of prior awards is garbled, repeating "Loyal Cambodia, 19/04" and pairing numbers with the wrong names. [Para 60] OCR also runs the fifth issue and the second issue into single paragraphs and prints "Inon-conciliation" for the non-conciliation report. [Para 9][Para 13]
