# Award No. 94/04 — Eternity Apparel Company (Arbitration Council, 7 December 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach, Tuon Siphann, Run Saray chair) · Doc ID 139357

## 1. Facts
Eternity Apparel Company, at building 646, National Road 2, Chak Angre Krom, Meanchey district, Phnom Penh, employs 1,160 workers, of whom 650 belong to the Democracy Union of Workers, a union without most representative status. [Para 15][Para 16] On 7 September 2004 the workers complained to the Meanchey Labour Inspector over twelve working conditions; after two conciliation sessions one issue was settled and eleven came to the panel. [Para 17][Para 18][Para 19] Most of the disputed acts concern conditions the company says internal management or safety requires, from the number of toilets to the wearing of trousers. [Para 33][Para 47]

## 2. Issues in dispute
The panel worked through the eleven non-conciliated issues in the order of the report. [Para 19]
1. Whether the employer must build five more toilets for the second floor? [Para 4][Para 20]
2. Whether workers with over one year and three months' seniority may take their annual leave, and be paid for unused days? [Para 4][Para 26]
3. Whether wages and seniority bonuses for ninety days of maternity leave must be paid in advance? [Para 4][Para 30]
4. Whether the employer must deduct union contributions from members' wages? [Para 4][Para 31]
5. Whether women workers must be allowed to wear skirts during working hours? [Para 4][Para 33]
6. Whether music must be played during working hours? [Para 5][Para 35]
7. Whether the roof must be raised and cooled because heat is excessive? [Para 6][Para 39]
8. Whether chairs must be installed in the ironing, quality control and trimming sections? [Para 6][Para 43]
9. Whether the attendance bonus may be withheld for five minutes' lateness? [Para 6][Para 50]
10. Whether seven days' wages may be deducted from workers who resign without notice? [Para 6][Para 55]
11. Whether a day care centre must be built and one hour a day allowed for breastfeeding? [Para 6][Para 58]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the factory lacks toilets, the roof makes the heat unbearable, standing all day swells their legs so they want chairs at least for rest, and they seek leave for family illness, union deductions, skirts and a day care centre with breastfeeding time. [Para 4][Para 6][Para 47]
**Employer (respondent):** it lacks space to build toilets and cannot raise the roof, only pregnant women are allowed to sit because sewers work at piece rate, trousers are required to prevent machinery accidents, and music would make workers sleepy and cause accidents. [Para 23][Para 34][Para 36][Para 49]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to build five more toilets on the second floor, to let workers with over one year's service take accrued annual leave, to deduct union contributions from 1 April 2005, to renovate the roof or workplace within two months, to provide chairs in the three sections within one month, to stop deducting seven days' wages, and to establish a day care centre with one hour a day for breastfeeding; the demands on maternity advance payment, music and the late-arrival bonus were rejected as interest disputes, and skirts were refused. [Para 122][Para 123][Para 124]

On toilets the panel applied Prakas 52/00: clause 1 requires sanitary latrines and clause 5 requires one high-seat toilet per set number of women workers where a hundred or more are employed. Six toilets for 800 women on the second floor failed that standard, so five more were ordered. [Para 61][Para 63][Para 64]
On annual leave the panel applied Articles 166 and 167: paid leave accrues at one and a half days a month and the right is acquired after one year, so senior workers may use it in the second year without waiting for the year's end, and unused days carry over but not beyond three years. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 69][Para 70] It held that an agreement converting leave into cash is void under Article 167, but declined to decide that point since the parties were not in conflict over it. [Para 70]
Maternity advance payment was treated as an interest dispute: neither the Law nor the Prakas regulates when wages and severance are paid, so the demand was above the employer's obligation and, the union lacking most representative status, could not be arbitrated under Article 43 of Prakas 99/04. [Para 74][Para 78][Para 79][Para 80]
Union contributions were ordered on the basis of Article 129 and clause 5 of Prakas 305: once workers consent in writing, deduction at the next payday is the employer's obligation, and the employer must also send the consent letters to the Ministry. [Para 84][Para 85][Para 86]
Skirts were refused: dress is the worker's right, but Articles 249 and 250 make the employer responsible for work-related accidents and entitle it to set internal rules for safety. [Para 88][Para 90][Para 91]
Music was left to negotiation as an interest matter, like the maternity demand. [Para 93][Para 94] Heat was treated differently: Prakas 14/02 clause 3 requires the employer to find a way to cool the workplace, and the low roof produced heat harmful to health, so renovation within two months was ordered. [Para 96][Para 97]
Chairs were ordered under Prakas 53/00, whose clause 2 requires sufficient chairs near workstations even where the work is done standing, because prolonged standing swells the legs. [Para 100][Para 101]
The attendance bonus demand was an interest dispute: neither the Internal Work Rules nor Notification 17/00 fixes the minutes of lateness that forfeits the bonus, and the employer's claim of a five-minute agreement was unsupported by evidence. [Para 103][Para 106][Para 108][Para 109]
On the seven-day deduction the panel applied Article 74, which requires written prior notice from either party terminating an undetermined duration contract, and Article 91 on damages; no article permits an employer to withhold wages for want of notice, and the employer showed no damage. [Para 112][Para 114][Para 116][Para 117]
On child care, Article 184 gives a breastfeeding mother one hour a day for a year and Article 186 requires a nursing room and crèche where a hundred women are employed; the company conceded it had the capacity. [Para 119][Para 120][Para 121]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer with a hundred or more women workers must meet the numerical latrine standard of Prakas 52/00 and provide the crèche and breastfeeding hour Articles 184 and 186 require; and no rule allows an employer to deduct seven days' wages because a worker resigns without notice, since Article 74 requires notice from either side and Article 91 requires proof of damage. [Para 63][Para 116][Para 119]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that converting annual leave into cash is void under Article 167, then declined to decide it for want of a live dispute, and remarked that dress is a worker's right subject to the employer's safety rules. [Para 70][Para 88]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel composition is scrambled: the label "Arbitrator chosen by the worker party" sits alone between two name lines, so the allocation of Tuon Siphann and Run Saray is inferred from their signatures at the end. [Para 11][Para 12] The reasons at the ninth issue state that the third and sixth issues, not the ninth, lacked Ministry approval of most representative status, contradicting the decision that rejects the third, sixth and ninth issues as interest disputes. [Para 107][Para 122] The employer's business licence is dated 23 December 2004, after the 7 December award. [Para 13] Passages are duplicated by OCR, including "The 1997 Labour Law and other obligation" twice, and the lateness threshold appears as both fifteen and five minutes. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 75][Para 77]
