# Award No. 111/04 — Sun Shiny Cambodian Garment (Arbitration Council, 12 January 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, An Nan, Ang Eng Thong presiding) · Doc ID 139364

## 1. Facts
Sun Shiny Cambodian Garment, a 600-worker factory at Dam Nak village, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, dismissed Mr B on 13 December 2004. He had worked under an undetermined-duration contract since 13 October 2003, held no union leadership post, and earned a basic wage of US$45 per month. [Para 17][Para 23][Para 24][Para 26] The disputed act was a single stained pair of trousers: he drew a picture on the ironing cover and the mark transferred when he pressed the garment. [Para 31] Of thirteen demands taken to conciliation on 16 December 2004, nine were settled and four were referred to the panel on 22 December 2004. [Para 20][Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the dismissal of Mr B was valid, and whether the company must reinstate him? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company must apply point 9 of the agreement of 13 January 2004, on attendance bonus where workers take three days of authorised leave? [Para 43]
3. Whether the company must pay back-pay for the period in August 2004 when it allowed workers to take annual leave because there was no work? [Para 47]
4. Whether the workers are entitled to wages and the US$5 attendance bonus for the strike days of 16–18 December 2004? [Para 48]
5. Whether the panel must examine, and order wages for, the company's refusal to admit workers on 20 and 21 December 2004? [Para 98]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr B committed no misconduct that justified termination and should be reinstated; the point 9 agreement was signed, stamped and never cancelled, yet the company refused to apply it for a long time; the company closed the door on 21 and 22 December and so owes wages for those days. [Para 2][Para 46][Para 51]
**Employer (respondent):** Mr B "makes the company's pants incorrectly", so the company would not reinstate him although it agreed to comply with the Labour Law; it did not know of the point 9 issue and had renegotiated it, but produced no new agreement; the workers had agreed to the August leave arrangement; the strike was unlawful, so no wages or bonus were owed. [Para 4][Para 43][Para 45][Para 48]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must pay Mr B 15 days' wages in lieu of notice (Articles 75 and 77), 15 days' dismissal indemnity (Article 89), 15 days' damages (Article 91, termination without valid reason under Article 74(2)) and unused annual leave (Article 167), a total of 45 days computed on his average daily wage over the last 12 months; it must respect the agreement of 13 January 2004; it must pay wages to all workers not permitted to work on 20 and 21 December 2004; the demands for annual-leave back-pay and for strike-day wages and bonus were rejected. [Para 72][Para 74][Para 107][Para 108]

On issue 1 the panel found the company held no warning or termination notice in writing, and its internal work rules (Clause 10) required an oral then a written warning before dismissal for minor misconduct and a written warning for medium misconduct. [Para 55][Para 33][Para 66] Staining a pair of trousers was not serious misconduct within Article 83(2), and under Article 27 a sanction must be proportional to the misconduct; dismissal was therefore disproportionate and invalid. [Para 32][Para 59][Para 63] The security chief's letter asserting many past mistakes did not show that written warnings had ever been given. [Para 57] Because the employer refused reinstatement and Mr B was an ordinary worker, the panel substituted the statutory entitlements rather than ordering him back to work. [Para 71]

On issue 2 the panel applied Article 307, under which a conciliated agreement signed by the parties and visaed by the conciliator has the force of a collective agreement. [Para 81] The employer had filed no notice of cancellation and no replacement agreement existed, so the parties remained bound. [Para 82]

On issue 3 the panel noted Article 170 and found that the workers had accepted the August arrangement without objection; it treated that agreement as disposing of the claim and rejected it. [Para 84][Para 86][Para 91]

On issue 4 the strike was called without the prior notice required by Article 324; under Article 332 a strike suspends the labour contract, so wages are not paid, and no agreement obliged the company to pay the US$5 attendance bonus above Notification 017. The demand was rejected. [Para 92][Para 93]

On issue 5 the panel held the matter a direct consequence of the dispute and within its competence under Article 312. [Para 102] The company's closure of the gate was a lockout exercised without the notification required by Articles 322 and 324, and Article 335 obliges an employer to pay workers for a lockout in breach of those provisions; wages were ordered for 20 and 21 December 2004. [Para 104][Para 105]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A single careless act — here staining one garment — that the internal work rules classify as minor or medium misconduct cannot support dismissal where the employer produced no written warning, since Article 83(2) serious misconduct is not made out and Article 27 requires the sanction to match the fault; termination on such a ground obliges the employer to pay notice, dismissal indemnity, damages and unused leave, and the panel will award compensation instead of reinstatement where the employer refuses to take the worker back. [Para 63][Para 66][Para 69][Para 71]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's general remarks that annual leave exists so workers may choose their own leave time, subject only to not disrupting the employer's operations, were not necessary to issue 3, which turned on the parties' agreement. [Para 89][Para 90]
