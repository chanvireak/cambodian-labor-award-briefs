# Award No. 49/04 — Ho Hing Garment Company (Arbitration Council, 27 July 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators: Kao Thach (employer party), Tuon Siphann (worker party), Sok Mathoeung (chair) · Doc ID 139322

## 1. Facts
Ho Hing Garment Company employed 533 workers paid by piecework. On 31 May 2004 the workers complained to the Labour Inspection Office in Khan Reussey Keo; conciliation from 2 to 12 June 2004 settled seven of ten demands, leaving special leave, maternity entitlements and a daily piecework record book. The company already gave 90 days' maternity leave with 50% of wage, 50% of the seniority bonus and USD15 for baby milk, but withheld the USD5 attendance bonus; it recorded piecework daily but reported it to workers only weekly through team leaders. [Para 6][Para 7][Para 8][Para 32][Para 27]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must grant special leave to general workers while preserving their daily wage and attendance bonus, the leave being deducted from annual leave? [Para 1][Para 10]
2. Whether the company must pay women who have worked one year and taken maternity leave 50% of wages, 100% of the attendance bonus (USD5) and 100% of the seniority bonus? [Para 1][Para 14]
3. Whether the company must keep a piecework record book every day? [Para 1][Para 21]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Special leave should preserve the USD5 attendance bonus for all workers including irregular ones, with the daily wage cut for irregulars and the leave charged against annual leave. On maternity, Article 183 and Notification No. 017 of the Ministry of Social Affairs and Labour give 90 days, half wage, 100% seniority bonus and 100% attendance bonus, as the Director of the Labour Inspection Department explained. A daily piecework book is needed because wages are never shown, machines break often and the workers cannot verify their pay. [Para 10][Para 15][Para 17][Para 21][Para 36]

**Employer (respondent):** Only regular workers may take special leave, and in practice not before six months; it pays the attendance bonus and cuts the daily wage when special leave is taken. On maternity it pays 50% of wage and 50% of seniority bonus, gives USD15 for baby milk, but not the attendance bonus, which requires a month worked without interruption. A daily piecework book is impossible with three accountants; workers may check weekly. [Para 11][Para 13][Para 19][Para 20][Para 25][Para 26]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Partly granted — on the first issue the parties' in-hearing agreement was recorded: special leave for any urgent case, with workers of one year or more keeping daily wage and attendance bonus and those under one year losing the daily wage but keeping the attendance bonus; on the second, the employer must give 90 days' leave and half wages plus all perquisites, computed on the 12-month average divided by 12 and then by two; on the third, the employer must report the piecework record weekly through team leaders, allow 15 minutes at week's end to check it, post a large notice of machine breakdowns and their duration, and pay 865 riels per hour for breakdowns exceeding one hour. [Para 82][Para 83]

On maternity the panel found the 90 days and the 50% wage already compliant with Articles 182 and 183, so those points were no longer in dispute. The seniority bonus is a perquisite within Article 183, whose purpose is that a woman on leave receive at least 50% of her usual benefits, and Notification No. 017 (18 July 2000) point 5 requires its payment at $2, $3, $4 and $5 per month for one to four years' service; at least 50% must therefore continue during leave. The USD15 for baby milk was praised as a good practice permitted by Article 13, since the Labour Law does not bar benefits better than the law. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 54]

The attendance bonus was also a perquisite: the third point of Notification No. 017 promises at least $5 to workers who attend every day of the month, which a woman on leave cannot do, but her leave is lawful and Article 103(4) treats bonuses as part of wages, so the bonus is included where she received it before leave. The letter of the Director of the Labour Inspection Department of 24 January 2003 was held to be an instructional document expressing his own opinion, not a juridical norm binding employer and employees, and not persuasive. Using the one-year service threshold in the last paragraph of Article 183, the panel measured entitlement by the 12 months before leave. [Para 63][Para 65][Para 67][Para 69][Para 59][Para 60][Para 68]

On the record book the panel held that changing a management system is the company's exclusive right and workers may not interfere save where a management decision affects their interests; the workers' fear of miscalculation was nonetheless founded, but they proved no loss, and both sides had agreed to keep the existing system with weekly written disclosure. On breakdowns, the company's failure to provide work obliged it either to keep paying or to suspend contracts under Article 71, which it had not done, so it must pay for stoppages over one hour at the agreed daily rate of USD1.73. [Para 72][Para 74][Para 75][Para 78][Para 79][Para 81]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a woman takes lawful maternity leave, the employer must continue not only half her wage but half of every perquisite she received in the 12 months before leave — attendance bonus and seniority bonus included — calculated by averaging those 12 months, dividing by twelve and then halving the result; an inspector's letter construing Article 183 is instructional only and does not bind the parties. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 67][Para 68]

**Obiter dicta:** That providing USD15 for baby milk is a good practice, the Labour Law not preventing benefits more favourable than itself, and that machine breakdowns leave the employer obliged either to pay or to suspend contracts under Article 71. [Para 54][Para 78]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award states that conciliation settled seven of ten issues "leaving two" unresolved, which accounts for nine, while three issues are in fact briefed [Para 2][Para 1]; the permissible breakdown period is given as two hours, then three hours, before the parties settled on one [Para 31][Para 37][Para 44]; the hourly rate is printed as USD0.31625 although USD1.73 divided by eight hours is USD0.21625, the 865 riel figure being consistent with the latter [Para 80]; OCR splicing leaves clauses broken and duplicated ("succeed", "athend", "less then one year") [Para 8][Para 82]; the first issue's terms appear only as the parties' agreement in the decision, not as an order, and its third limb is cut off mid-sentence [Para 82]; the seniority bonus figures are quoted from Notification No. 017 without reconciling them with the 50% already paid [Para 53][Para 51].
