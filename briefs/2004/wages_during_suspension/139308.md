# Award No. 25/04 — Standard Garment Factory (Arbitration Council, 25 May 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mr Hem Hour Narith, employer-appointed; Mr Venn Pov, worker-appointed; Mr Kong Phallack, chair) · Doc ID 139308

## 1. Facts
Standard Garment Factory, at Tuol Sanke in Phnom Penh, is a recently established company with no internal work rules of its own, applying instead the Ministry of Social Affairs announcement No. 17 of 18 July 2000. [Para 15] It pays a USD5 monthly bonus to workers who are punctual, withholding it from any worker late more than ten minutes in a month. [Para 15][Para 21] The workers struck on 10 April 2004 and again on 27 April 2004, and the employer refused to pay wages or the bonus for those days, saying it would apply the Labour Law. [Para 3][Para 12][Para 15] Both parties agreed that no seven-day notice of the strike had been given. [Para 16]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must maintain the wage and the USD5 bonus for the workers who went on strike on 10 April 2004 and on 27 April 2004? [Para 3][Para 12]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the strike was not planned — nine dismissed workers blocked their entry to work — and the union did not lead it; the employer recruited no new workers during the strike; and although they dropped the claim to wages for the strike days, they insisted on the regular work bonus. [Para 15][Para 17]
**Employer (respondent):** it could not afford to maintain the wage and bonus and chose to comply with the Labour Law instead, and it withheld the bonus because the workers had been late more than ten minutes in the month. [Para 3][Para 17]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the employees' demand for wages and for the bonus. [Para 31]

On wages, Article 332 of the Labour Law provides that a strike suspends the labour contract, so work is not performed and salary is not paid. [Para 18][Para 19] Article 334 obliges the employer to pay strikers' wages for the strike period only where it recruited new workers to replace them; none were recruited here, so no wages were owed. [Para 19] The workers had in any event ceased demanding strike wages at the hearing. [Para 20]

On the bonus, the panel treated the USD5 as a rule-based attendance bonus that the employer pays subject to the ten-minute condition, and held that the two strike days deprived the workers of it. [Para 21][Para 22] It then addressed Article 333, which forbids any sanction against workers for participating in a strike, and held that the prohibition is fully effective only where the strike was organised in compliance with the law, following the panel's decision in Lida Garment case No. 04/03: workers who do not comply cannot claim the law's full protection. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 26][Para 27] Withholding the bonus would be an improper sanction for a lawful strike, but the panel would not extend that protection to workers who ignored the Chapter XIII procedure, and both parties agreed no prior notice under Article 324 had been given. [Para 28] The strike being contrary to Chapter 13, Article 333 did not apply and all employees lost their right to the USD5 bonus for May. [Para 29]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Withholding an attendance bonus from workers who took part in a strike is not an unlawful sanction under Article 333 where the strike was called without the notice Chapter XIII requires: the immunity from sanction protects only strikes conducted in compliance with the law, and strike days carry no wage unless the employer hired replacements. [Para 19][Para 26][Para 28][Para 29]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's citation of Lida Garment No. 04/03 together with Wash Concept No. 08/04, Lucky Zone No. 15/04 and Yada Printing No. 16/04 is illustrative of a settled approach and adds nothing to the order in this case. [Para 30]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award records that conciliation succeeded on "one of three issues, with two unsuccessful issues" [Para 5], yet only one issue is set out [Para 3].
- The bonus instrument is dated "27 November 2004", after this award of 25 May 2004 [Para 10].
- The bonus demanded is USD5 in the issues list and in the decision [Para 3][Para 29] but USD2.50 in the workers' fallback position at the hearing [Para 17].
- The paragraph on the non-conciliation report is duplicated and interleaved: "A non-reconciliation report was submitted to the Arbitration ... Inspector on 27 April 2004 Council on 5 May 2004" [Para 13].
- The union held no registration certificate, only a list of seven newly elected committee members from the election of 24 April 2004 [Para 15]; its capacity to represent the workers is nowhere addressed.
- The operative decision is printed as an empty numbered item "." [Para 30], with the actual order appearing at the head of the following line [Para 31].
