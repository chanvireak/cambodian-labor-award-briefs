# Award No. 71/04 — Eternity Apparel (Cambodia) Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 13 September 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach, employer-appointed; An Nan, worker-appointed; Ang Eng Thong, chair) · non-binding award · Doc ID 139342

## 1. Facts
Eternity Apparel (Cambodia) Co Ltd, Street Trong Maen, Sangkat Tek Thala, Khan Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, employed 628 workers. The company terminated a probationary worker, and the workers of groups 1, 2 and the finishing section struck for one hour from 8:00 to 9:00 a.m. on 26 July 2004; they struck again for an hour on 11 August 2004, in neither case notifying the Ministry of Labour, and the company withheld the US$5 attendance bonus for the first strike day. Conciliation at the factory left three issues unresolved and the dispute reached the Secretariat; at the hearing of 10 September 2004 the parties settled the second and dropped the third issue, leaving the bonus claim to be decided. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 35][Para 37][Para 29][Para 31][Para 33]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the workers must receive the US$5 attendance bonus for 26 July 2004 despite the one hour they went on strike? [Para 5]
2. Whether the company must terminate a Chinese staff member, called "the red hair sister", for threatening the workers? [Para 6]
3. Whether the company must pay annual leave by adding it to the workers' basic wage? [Para 7]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** They claimed the right to keep the same US$5 attendance bonus notwithstanding the one-hour strike, and separately demanded the termination of the Chinese staff member said to have threatened workers, plus annual leave pay added to basic wage. [Para 28][Para 6][Para 7]
**Employer (respondent):** It would not pay the bonus because the workers did not follow the procedures the Labour Law defines for a strike; it would not terminate the Chinese staff member but would advise her on treating workers appropriately; and it could not add annual leave pay to basic wage because the workers already received more than the basic wage. [Para 6][Para 7][Para 8]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel rejected the workers' demand to maintain the US$5 attendance bonus with reference to 26 July 2004 when they struck for one hour; issues 2 and 3 were not decided, the company having agreed to issue a written warning to the Chinese employee called "the red hair sister" and both parties having agreed not to raise the annual leave issue. [Para 40][Para 32][Para 33][Para 34]

On issue 1 the panel took Notification 017/00 of the Ministry of Labour, under which the US$5 attendance bonus forms part of the minimum wage and must be paid to workers who come to work regularly, together with Article 332 of the Labour Law, under which a labour contract is suspended during a strike, allowance for work is not provided and wages are not paid. It found the strike unlawful: the employees did not perform the job for one hour on 26 July and again on 11 August 2004, went on strike with no prior notice to the employer or to the Ministry of Labour, and did not respect the other procedures for a strike. The bonus therefore was not owed and the employer's refusal to pay it was appropriate. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 37][Para 38][Para 39]

The panel disposed of the remaining demands by consent rather than decision: the written warning to the Chinese employee replaced the termination the workers sought, and the annual leave claim was withdrawn by agreement, so no award issued on either. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 34]

The panel cited its earlier awards in 04/03 (printed "Linda Garment"), 08/04 (Wash Concept), 15/04 (Lucky Zone), 16/04 (Yada Printing) and 25/04 (Standard Garment) as the line of authority for withholding the attendance bonus from workers who strike unlawfully. [Para 40]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The US$5 attendance bonus under Notification 017/00 is earned by regular attendance and forms part of the minimum wage, so a worker who strikes, even for a single hour, without prior notice to the employer and the Ministry of Labour and without observing the other strike procedures is not "working regularly" for that day: the contract is suspended under Article 332 and the employer may lawfully withhold the bonus. [Para 35][Para 37][Para 38]

**Obiter dicta:** That a written warning, rather than dismissal, was the appropriate response to a staff member accused of threatening workers, recorded as the parties' settlement rather than as a ruling. [Para 32]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The dates in this file contradict one another and are reported as printed, not reconciled: the non-conciliated report is said to be "dated May 6, 2004" and to have been sent to the Secretariat on 6 May 2004, while the evidence list gives a non-conciliated report "dated 17 August 2004 of MoLVT", the case summary gives transmission on 1 September 2004, and the factory conciliation is dated both 11 August 2004 and "11July 2004". The fact finding treats two separate one-hour strikes, on 26 July and 11 August 2004, as one course of conduct. The scan loses initial "th" throughout ("the employer" prints as "themployer", "shall" as "hall") and fuses sentences across line breaks; Award 04/03 is printed "Linda Garment" for Lida Garment, Article 332 is rendered "wages are offered" where the sense is that wages are not paid, and the arbitrator signs as "Kao Tach" but is composed as "Kao Thach".
