# Award No. 56/04 — Hui Ying Enterprises (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 6 August 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Kao Thach (employer-chosen), Liv Sovanna (worker-chosen), Men Nimmith (chair) · Doc ID 139328

## 1. Facts
Hui Ying Enterprises (Cambodia) Co., Ltd employed 400 workers at Canadia Park, Trorpangthleung village, Khan Daunkor, Phnom Penh. [Para 31] On 6 July 2004 the Daunkor labour inspectors received a telephone complaint that the workers were striking over ten points; conciliation on 10 July 2004 settled six, leaving four for the Council, which received them on 16 July 2004. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 34] The strike ran from 6 to 10 July 2004 without prior notice to the company or the Ministry, called after the company refused to negotiate the dismissal of the shop steward and union vice-president, Mr C. [Para 45][Para 46] Mr C resigned on 29 July 2004, and CLUF told the Secretariat on 4 August 2004 that it did not object to the resignation. [Para 37][Para 39]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must reinstate Mr C, a shop steward and union vice-president, and whether it must dismiss the chief of the sewing section and the Chinese supervisor for hitting Ms T and Mr C on 2 July 2004? [Para 8][Para 9]
2. Whether the company must build a proper shelter for bicycles and motorcycles? [Para 17]
3. Whether the company must build a day-care centre and allow mothers to breastfeed, or pay US$15 per month instead? [Para 18]
4. Whether the company must maintain wages and the US$5 attendance bonus during the strike of 6 to 10 July 2004? [Para 21]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr C had committed no misconduct and his termination did not follow Labour Law procedure, so he had to be reinstated; the sewing chief and the Chinese supervisor had hit Ms T and Mr C on 2 July 2004 and should be dismissed. The company had to provide a bicycle and motorcycle shelter, a day-care centre with breastfeeding time or US$15 a month, and had to keep wages and the US$5 attendance bonus for the strike days, because the strike was provoked by the dismissal of their two representatives without valid reason and the company's refusal to negotiate. [Para 8][Para 17][Para 18][Para 44][Para 45]

**Employer (respondent):** Mr C could not be reinstated because he had committed serious misconduct, and neither the sewing chief nor the Chinese supervisor had hit anyone. The premises were too small for a shelter and it could not meet the day-care demand, though it offered US$5 a month for nine months plus one hour a day for breastfeeding. It refused wages and bonuses for strike days, invoking Article 332 of the Labour Law and the illegality of a strike called without prior notice to company or Ministry. [Para 12][Para 17][Para 19][Para 20][Para 22]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand to dismiss the sewing chief and the Chinese supervisor was rejected; the company was ordered to provide the remaining cloth to cover workers' bicycles and motorcycles; the company was ordered to offer US$10 per month to women who have given birth for twelve months from their return to work, in place of a day-care centre and breastfeeding; and the demand to maintain wages and the US$5 attendance bonus during the strike of 6 to 10 July 2004 was rejected. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 73]

The reinstatement claim was not decided: after the hearing the Secretariat received Mr C' resignation of 29 July 2004, the company's letter of 30 July 2004 recording that he resigned because he was too busy with family affairs, and CLUF's telephone confirmation of 4 August 2004 that it did not object, so the panel issued no award on that section of the first issue. [Para 37][Para 39][Para 40]

On the dismissal demand, the panel held it had no power to order an employer to dismiss employees, following 04/03 Lida, 13/03 Chou Sing, 17/03 and 18/03 Ho Hing, 06/04 Chou Sing, 15/04 Lucky Zone and 16/04 Yada Printing: Article 65 makes the contract a matter for the contracting parties, and even Article 83 serious misconduct gives the employer a right to dismiss rather than a duty. An exception exists where an employee endangers others' occupational health and safety, but the alleged blow to Mr C' shoulder was unsupported by evidence such as a medical certificate, so the panel found the accusation unproven. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 53][Para 58][Para 61]

On the strike, Article 332 provides that a strike suspends the labour contract, so no salary is paid for strike days; Article 334 obliges the employer to pay strikers only where it recruits replacements, which this company did not; and Article 333's bar on sanctioning strikers applies only to a lawfully organised strike. Since the workers struck without prior notice, the panel held they could not take the benefit of the Law and the deduction of the US$5 attendance bonus was correct, following 04/03 Garment, 08/04 Wash Concept, 16/04 Yada Printing and 25/04 Sport Wear Garment. [Para 62][Para 65][Para 66][Para 68][Para 70]

The panel noted evidence suggesting the company had itself conducted a lock-out contrary to procedure but declined to apply Article 335, because that issue was not in the non-conciliation report and the workers had not asked the panel to decide it. The panel also stated, before the reasons, that the strike of 10 July 2004 was illegal under Chapter 13. [Para 41][Para 71]

Issues two and three were disposed of by the parties' agreement at the hearing: the company would give cloth to cover bicycles and motorcycles and US$10 each for twelve months from commencement of work. [Para 35]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a strike is called without the prior notice Chapter 13 requires, the panel will not extend Article 333's protection against sanctions to the strikers, and the employer may lawfully withhold wages and attendance bonuses for the strike days; payment under Article 334 is owed only where the employer hires replacements. Equally, the panel will not order an employer to dismiss a supervisor or a section chief at the union's request: dismissal is the contracting parties' affair under Article 65, and the occupational health and safety exception requires evidence of injury, not assertion. [Para 61][Para 65][Para 66][Para 68]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remark that it could not apply Article 335 to an apparently unlawful lock-out because the issue fell outside the non-conciliation report, and its observation that a party which does not follow the law "shall not receive benefits from the law", were not necessary to any of the orders. [Para 67][Para 71]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
OCR is heavily degraded ("theemployees", "athemployees", "withe", "torder"). The first issue is described as having two "sections" but the decision never numbers them, and the reasons open with a stray heading "1st issue-section II." The findings at paragraph 61 invert the parties: they speak of insufficient evidence that "Mr Ch [was injured]" when the allegation was that Mr Ch struck Mr C, so the passage cannot be relied on beyond its conclusion of insufficient proof. Paragraph 41 dates the strike to 10 July 2004, while the issue, the findings and the order all describe a strike from 6 to 10 July 2004; both appear as printed. The panel composition prints the employer-chosen arbitrator as "Koa Thach" and the signature block as "Kao Thach", and the chair as "Men Nimmith" in composition and "Men Nimith" when signing. The union's registration certificate for Hui Ying is dated 30 August 2004, after the award of 6 August 2004. Precedent numbers are garbled ("Chhhou Sing", "04/03-Lida", "08/04 - Wash Concept") and are reproduced without correction. [Para 26][Para 28][Para 41][Para 48][Para 61][Para 30]
