# Award No. 08/04 — Wash Concept Enterprise and FTUWKC member employees (Arbitration Council, 10 March 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, chosen by the employer; Tuon Siphann, chosen by the workers; Dr Sok Mathoeung, chair) · Doc ID 138843

## 1. Facts
Wash Concept Enterprise (also called the Laundry Company; 347 workers on two shifts until 29 February 2004) suspended the contracts of two union representatives, Mr C and Mr V, on 12 February 2004, the same day the workforce began a strike that continued to 27 February 2004. [Para 1][Para 16][Para 20] Conciliation failed and the non-conciliation report of 17 February 2004 reached the Council Secretariat on 25 February 2004; on 27 February the parties settled eight other issues but not the strike-pay demand. [Para 2][Para 12][Para 15] The disputed acts are the refusal to pay wages and bonuses for the strike period and the new third shift (Group C, 01h00–07h00) due to start 01 March 2004. [Para 1][Para 58]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the workers are entitled to wages and bonuses for the period of the strike (12–27 February 2004)? [Para 1][Para 12]
2. Whether the employer may require workers to work the new Shift 3 / Group C that started on 01 March 2004? [Para 1][Para 58]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer blamed and suspended Mr C and Mr V in retaliation for the earlier strike of 12 January 2004, which had been settled on terms of no ill will; the two were only stretching sore limbs while waiting for the van, not threatening anyone, and the employer brought in as many as 50 outsiders to replace strikers. [Para 20][Para 24][Para 24]
**Employer (respondent):** Prior notice of a strike is required, so non-compliance justifies cutting both the days not worked and the monthly bonus for regular work; it hired no outsiders, merely redeployed staff from quiet sections after merging the washing teams into one shift, and it suspended the two men for a month because camera footage showed them ordering others not to work overtime. [Para 13][Para 26][Para 21]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim for salaries and bonuses during the strike is rejected; the employer is ordered to pay wages and attendance bonuses to Mr C and Mr V for the period their contracts were suspended, payable by the end of April 2004, and to stop forcing employees into Group C (01h00–07h00). [Para 61][Para 62]
On issue 1 the panel split the question in two. Article 332 suspends the employment contract during a strike so that the salary is not paid, and Article 334 bars the employer from recruiting replacements except for minimum service, a breach of which obliges payment of the strikers' salaries. [Para 34][Para 37][Para 38] Following Lida Garment Award 04/03, the panel held that Article 334's protection applies in full only to a strike conducted according to the Labour Law, so it asked separately whether replacements were hired and whether the strike was lawful. [Para 39] It found recruitment did occur — five names were furnished (four on 22 February, one on 24 February) and the employer's own account of regrouping into two shifts betrayed it — which violated Article 334. [Para 31][Para 44][Para 46] The strike nonetheless failed Article 324's seven-working-day notice and Article 320(4)'s exhaustion of peaceful methods, having begun the day notice of the suspension was received and while conciliation was still running; the panel therefore denied strike wages and refused Article 333 protection against the USD5 bonus deduction. [Para 47][Para 49][Para 52] On the suspension, Article 74 read with Article 71(7) requires evidence of misconduct before a contract may be suspended; none was produced, so the suspension was null and void, the two had been kept from work by that act rather than by choice, and wages and bonuses for the suspension period follow. [Para 53][Para 55][Para 56][Para 58] On issue 2, the employer's verbal undertaking during the protest outside the Ministry of Commerce made Group C voluntary, so existing employees cannot be forced into it and must be assigned to Groups A or B at their own option. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Strike pay under Articles 332 and 334 is decided by two independent questions: the employer's breach of Article 334 by hiring replacements is not cured or offset, but it earns the strikers nothing unless their own strike complied with the notice and peaceful-settlement procedures of the Labour Law — so an unlawful strike forfeits strike wages even where the employer also broke the law. [Para 39][Para 46][Para 50] A suspension of contract without evidence of misconduct is void and must be paid as working time. [Para 55][Para 56]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's estimate that over 90 of approximately 200 workers struck and that the business could not have run without replacements was an assumption used to corroborate recruitment, not a measured finding. [Para 44][Para 45]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
OCR damage is heavy: the second arbitrator appears as "Tuon Siphhan" in the panel composition and "Tuon Siphann" in the signature block. [Para 3][Para 62] The article number introducing the right-to-strike sentence in the reasons is lost ("According to Article The right to strike..."), although Article 320(4) is cited two sentences later. [Para 49] "Non-conciliation" is printed "Inon-conciliation" and stray digits survive ("JURISDICTION1"). [Para 1] Numbers do not reconcile: the workers claimed 50 replacements, the documented list names five, and the workforce is given as 347 in the findings but "approximately 200" in the reasoning. [Para 40][Para 31][Para 16][Para 44] The award is internally contradictory on effect: the parties are recorded as agreeing at the hearing that the award "is not binding", while the closing paragraph makes it binding after 8 days of notification absent written opposition. [Para 16][Para 62] Only one amount is printed, the "USD5" bonus deduction; no wage figure is quantified. [Para 52] Individual workers and union officials are reduced to bare names in the original and are given as Mr C and Mr V here.
