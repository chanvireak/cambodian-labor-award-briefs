# Award No. 26/03 — Cambodia Sport Wear Garment Factory (Arbitration Council, 16 January 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mr O Ry, Mr V Vanna, chair Mr K Phallack · Doc ID 138835

## 1. Facts
The Cambodia Sport Wear Garment Factory at Building 1533, National Road Number 2, Chak Angrei Kraum quarter, Mean Chey district, Phnom Penh, employed 5,181 workers at the date of the award. [Para 1][Para 22] Its internal work rule, registered with MOSALVY on 19 December 1998, paid workers on hospital-certified sick leave 60% of wage for the first month and 40% for the second and third month, and said nothing about a bonus for that leave. [Para 15][Para 23][Para 24] Of nine demands raised in the collective dispute, eight were settled at conciliation; the outstanding demand was that the company maintain the regular wage and the regular attendance bonus for workers who are sick and hold certification from a state hospital. [Para 7][Para 18][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether workers absent on sick leave certified by a state hospital are entitled to the "regular work bonus" of at least US$5 per month under MOSALVY Notification No. 017, in addition to the sick-leave wage already paid under the company's internal work rule? [Para 7][Para 25]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The regular bonus of at least US$5 per month conforms with Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 and must continue to be paid for the months in which a worker is on certified sick leave. [Para 25]
**Employer (respondent):** The law does not stipulate such a bonus, the internal work rule does not mention any extra bonus for leave, and the company will pay sick-leave wage only in accordance with its registered internal work rule. [Para 8][Para 9][Para 24][Para 26]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must provide wage of 100% for the first month, 60% for the second month and 40% for the third month, under the agreement dated 2 January 2004 between the company and the shop steward, to workers on sick leave certified correctly by a state hospital; the demand for a "regular work bonus" for those workers is rejected. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39]

On the bonus, the panel read Notification No. 017 as granting at least USD5 per month to "workers who come to work regularly on regular working days of a month", and found that the notification does not address absence caused by serious illness that prevents attendance. [Para 27] It found no provision of the 1997 Labour Law conferring extra payment for sick leave, Article 71 paragraph 3 and Article 72 merely allowing the worker to suspend the employment contract without wage on a doctor's certification. [Para 29] The panel was not aware of any Ministry regulation on sick leave, and noted that Notification No. 9 of 2001 offers a sample internal work rule that includes sick leave without fixing the number of days. [Para 30][Para 31] Because the company had already agreed at the 2 January 2004 negotiation to pay 100% for the first month and 60% for the second and third, an offer exceeding both its internal work rule and the Labour Law, the panel held the additional bonus demand unreasonable and dismissed it. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 36]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The attendance bonus under MOSALVY Notification No. 017 is conditioned on regular attendance on regular working days; a month in which the worker is absent on certified sick leave is not a month of regular attendance, so the bonus is not owed for it — and where the employer's registered rule and a negotiated offer already exceed the statutory sick-leave entitlement, a claim for that bonus is not reasonable. [Para 27][Para 35][Para 36]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel recorded that it did not know whether any Ministry regulation deals with sick leave, and that in practice labour inspectors use their power to register internal work rules to ensure employers offer an appropriate number of sick-leave days, although Notification No. 9 of 2001 sets no number. [Para 30][Para 32]
