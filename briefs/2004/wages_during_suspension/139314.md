# Award No. 33/04 — Bou Ming Cambodia Garment Company (Arbitration Council, 14 June 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Koy Neam presiding; Ly Tay Seng appointed by the employer; An Nan appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 139314

## 1. Facts
Bou Ming Cambodia Garment Company employed about 700 workers, who struck from 26 to 29 April 2004 over working conditions. [Para 12] On 29 April the parties agreed that the company would pay USD3 for each strike day if the workers returned on 30 April 2004; some returned, others stayed away and asked for USD5, and the ordering company cancelled all its contracts on 2 May 2004. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 16][Para 32] The workers presented themselves on 4 May but the factory had no work for them until 19 May 2004, and the company never asked the Labour Inspector to approve a suspension of the contracts. [Para 22][Para 19][Para 33]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the period 4 to 18 May 2004 was a strike period, as the employer says, or a period in which the company offered no work and therefore owes wages? [Para 4][Para 34]
2. Whether Article 10 of the working conditions issued on 17 May 2004 must be amended so that workers accept work assignment "according to the production flow" and "without discrimination"? [Para 4][Para 27][Para 29]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** they came back on 4 May and found the company closed, so no jobs were offered and they were entitled to wages, which they claimed at 50 per cent for the period. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24]
**Employer (respondent):** the strike continued from 26 April and the period was therefore unpaid; at the hearing its representative did not contest the workers' account and stated that the company had no jobs for them. [Para 20][Para 31] On Article 10 both parties referred the added wording to the panel to settle. [Para 33]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must pay USD10.38 to each worker who returned on 30 April 2004 for 4 to 18 May 2004, in two instalments of USD5.19 added to the June and July wages; workers who did not return on 30 April 2004 may not claim it; Article 10 must provide that workers accept work assignment according to the production flow without any discrimination. [Para 46]
The panel found the strike was interrupted on 30 April, when some workers returned, and ended on 4 May, when all returned; thereafter the company simply had no work, as its own representative conceded and as the cancelled orders showed. [Para 40][Para 31][Para 41] Article 71(11) permits suspension only in serious financial difficulty, for no more than two months and subject to review by the Labour Inspector, and Article 72(1) keeps the wage obligation alive unless the contract is suspended. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39] Since the company notified no suspension, its obligation to pay continued. [Para 42][Para 44]
Exercising the power given by Article 34 of the Prakas on the Arbitration Council to reach a resolution that is "fair and correct", the panel weighed the workers' failure to honour the 30 April agreement, which led to the lost orders, and limited recovery to the 50 per cent demanded, calculated as (USD45 ÷ 26 days) × 12 days × 50 per cent. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45][Para 46] Workers absent on 30 April recover nothing because they caused the damage. [Para 45]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that has no work for its workers remains liable for their wages unless it formally suspends the contracts under Article 71(11) of the Labour Law, within the two-month limit and under Labour Inspector review; where the workers' breach of a return-to-work agreement caused the lost orders, recovery is confined to the reduced share they claimed. [Para 37][Para 42][Para 45]

**Obiter dicta:** unemployment of this kind "usually takes place at all kinds of business", and the Labour Law protects workers against contract suspension that is not in good faith. [Para 34][Para 35]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Issue 1 in the non-conciliation report dates the period "May 4 to 18, 2005"; the employer's demand reads "April 4 to 18, 2004"; the reasons and the first order speak of "May 4 to 8, 2004" while the calculation and order 2 use 4 to 18 May. [Para 4][Para 20][Para 34][Para 46] The computation excludes "Sunday 9 and 16 of April 2004", a month the period does not touch, and uses 12 days where 4 to 18 May less two Sundays gives 13. [Para 45] OCR drops the letter "t" throughout ("themployer" for "the employer", "non-conciliation issueubmitted"), "N/A" appears as "NIA", and the award is signed only with "Mr." before each arbitrator's name. [Para 6][Para 9][Para 10] The parties' representatives are named in the OCR but are not reproduced here. [Para 4]
