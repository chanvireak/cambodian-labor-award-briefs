# Award No. 30/04 — Honey Wear Garment Co., Ltd v National Union Federation of Honey Wear Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 15 June 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (You Sonty, employer-chosen, and Kong Phallack, chair, per the face of the award; Ly Tayseng and Sa Sovan per the signature page) · Doc ID 139312

## 1. Facts
Honey Wear Garment Co., Ltd employed 390 workers at National Road 4, Ang Keo village, Kontouk commune, Ang Snoul district, Kandal province. [Para 6] Its casual workers were engaged on daily contracts at 500 riels per day, with no clause granting them the benefits permanent workers received. [Para 16] The union's complaint of 6 May 2004 raised eight demands; six were settled in conciliation on 17 May 2004 and two were referred to the panel — equal treatment of casual workers and sick-leave pay. [Para 7][Para 8][Para 9] The employer offered 100% of wages for the first month of certified sick leave, 60% for the second and third months and nothing thereafter, matching clause 4(7) of its internal work rules; the workers wanted 100%, 80% and 50%. [Para 12][Para 15] The parties agreed the first issue at the hearing and chose a binding award. [Para 6][Para 14]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must provide casual workers with the same rights and benefits as permanent workers? [Para 2][Para 10]
2. Whether the company must pay sick-leave wages at 100% for the first month, 80% for the second and third months and 50% for the fourth to sixth months where illness is certified by a qualified doctor? [Para 2][Para 10]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** casual workers do the same work and should receive the same benefits; the sick-leave scale rests on Arts. 71(3) and 169 rather than the internal work rules, which the law outranks and which many workers had never seen. [Para 15][Para 25][Para 26]
**Employer (respondent):** it accepts Art. 10 and will give casual workers the same benefits, but for sick leave it can only follow its internal work rules — 100% for the first month, 60% for the next two, then job security without pay — and cannot meet the higher demand. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 21]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must provide casual workers with benefits and other rights the same as those of permanent workers; the demand for 100%/80%/50% sick-leave pay is rejected; and the company must continue paying sick-leave wages under clause 4(7) of its internal work rules, that is 100% for the first month, 60% for the second and third months, and job security but no pay for the fourth to sixth months. [Para 47]

On issue 1, Art. 10 subjects casual workers to the same rules and gives them the same rights as regular workers unless a clause stipulates separately, and no contract term here granted them anything beyond the 500 riels daily payment. [Para 16] That payment fell below the minimum wage in the textile field of US$45 per 26 days, and Art. 13 voids any rule, contract or convention inconsistent with the law, so casual workers were entitled to the same benefits as permanent workers. [Para 17][Para 18][Para 19] They must also receive the minimum wage under Notification No. 017/2000, including attendance bonuses and overtime at the rate of the previous day's work, and annual, special and public-holiday leave proportional to days worked, following Sport Wear Case No. 26/04. [Para 20][Para 21]

On issue 2, the panel held that Art. 71(3) only suspends the contract for up to six months of medically certified illness and says nothing about wages, and that Art. 72(1) relieves the employer of wage payment while the contract is suspended; Arts. 166 and 169 deal with paid annual leave, not sick leave. [Para 26][Para 28][Para 30][Para 31] No Prakas requires paid sick leave; the Ministry merely inserts the topic into its model internal work rules. [Para 31][Para 32] The panel therefore treated the demand as an interest dispute, since the law is silent, and decided it on equity under Art. 312(2), taking the internal work rules and actual practice as the yardstick. [Para 43][Para 46][Para 47] Practice in garment factories and hotels, and the panel's own earlier awards in Sport Wear No. 26/03, Top One No. 24/03 and Sport Wear No. 13/04, converge on 100% for the first month, 60% for the second and third months and no pay thereafter, so the workers' higher demand was not reasonable. [Para 37][Para 40][Para 41][Para 42]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Casual workers are entitled to the same rights and benefits as permanent workers under Art. 10, and a casual daily rate below the statutory minimum is void under Art. 13; but sick-leave pay is not regulated by the Labour Law, because Art. 71(3) merely suspends the contract and Art. 72(1) suspends wages, so a demand for a scale above the employer's internal work rules is an interest dispute to be decided on equity, and the panel will hold the parties to the prevailing practice of 100% for the first month, 60% for the next two and none thereafter. [Para 19][Para 30][Para 43]

**Obiter dicta:** internal work rules may protect workers better than the law does, and where the law is silent they and actual practice are the proper grounds of decision; the panel also warned the parties that an award on an interest dispute may operate as a collective bargaining agreement lasting one year. [Para 36][Para 45][Para 47]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel is named two ways: You Sonty as employer-chosen arbitrator and Kong Phallack as chair at lines 3 to 6, but Ly Tayseng as employer-chosen and Sa Sovan as chair on the signature page at lines 48 to 51; which composition sat cannot be resolved from the text. The casual rate is "500 riels per hour" at line 12 but "500 riels a day" at line 16. Documents from the employee party are dated 7 October 2004 at line 6, four months after the award, and the employer's letter of delegation is dated 31 April 2004, a date that does not exist. The internal work rules are dated 3 June 2004 though the hearing was 31 May 2004. The cited precedent appears as Sport Wear Case No. 26/04 at line 21 and No. 26/03 at line 39. The award is headed 15 June 2004 and its binding effect is immediate on notification.
