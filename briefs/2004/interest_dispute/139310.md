# Award No. 29/04 — Top World Garment Company v Free Trade Union of Workers of the Kingdom of Cambodia (Arbitration Council, 10 June 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tayseng, employer-chosen; Liv Sovanna, worker-chosen; Kong Phallack, chair) · Doc ID 139310

## 1. Facts
Top World Garment Cambodia Co., Ltd employed 380 workers at building #646, National Road No. 2, Sangkat Chak Angrekrom, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, under verbal contracts that were converted from specified to unspecified duration by agreement on 1 April 2004. [Para 8][Para 12] For economic and customer-standard reasons the company moved the whole operation some 13 kilometres to Kbal Koh village, Kbal Koh commune, Kean Svay district, Kandal province, and offered transport for four months or US$5 per month for four months; 168 workers, having asked for seven years and then three-and-a-half years, struck over the difference. [Para 10][Para 13][Para 15] Both parties agreed in the hearing that the award would not be binding. [Para 7]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must transport the employees from the old location to the new one for seven years — later reduced to three-and-a-half years — when the factory relocates 13 kilometres for economic reasons? [Para 1][Para 13]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** relocation makes daily travel impossible for those living near the old site; the new site has no market, no housing and is far from the city, so transport must be provided for the full period they had demanded, eventually three-and-a-half years. [Para 11][Para 13][Para 32]
**Employer (respondent):** it can afford only three months, later four months, of transport, or US$5 per month in lieu; the move was forced by the loss of its major customer and the expiry of the lease, and workers who cannot move will receive their last salary, one year's termination payment and a seniority payment. [Para 1][Para 13][Para 15]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must transport the employees from the old location to the new one for six months starting from the location change date, or pay US$5 per month for six months as extra money added to normal salary to those who do not join the company's transport; employees must choose one of the two within seven days of the award taking effect, failing which the employer chooses, and the company must pay dismissal indemnity under Arts. 89 and 90 to workers who do not move, provided they give notice of resignation within three months of the location change. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41][Para 42]

The panel first held that the 1997 Labour Law does not address relocation, that the verbal contract therefore governs, and that Decree No. 38 of 28 October 1988 applies because Art. 65 permits contracts in any agreed form and Art. 4 of the Decree allows oral contracts. [Para 16][Para 19][Para 21] Under Art. 29 of the Decree location is an essential term, so relocating changes the contract, and under Art. 22 the contract is the law of the parties and cannot be altered without their agreement; a relocation imposed without transport was therefore a breach, and if the employer's refusal causes workers to stop, that is a termination by the employer. [Para 22][Para 25][Para 26] Those who do not move are entitled to notice pay and dismissal indemnity under Arts. 75, 89 and 90, but not to damages, because the company's economic difficulties were found genuine. [Para 27]

The panel then held that no provision of the Labour Law requires an employer to pay daily transport on relocation, that any such grant is voluntary, and that the dispute is accordingly an interest dispute to be settled by negotiation. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 29] Proceeding under Art. 312 of the Labour Law and Art. 43 of the Prakas on the Arbitration Council, it decided on equity: 13 kilometres is a real hardship for workers living near the old site; the new site lacks a market and housing; workers need time to adapt; they have three or four years' seniority; the employer benefits from keeping experienced staff rather than training new ones; other factories — You Chheng, San and Grand Tech — provide transport or US$5 per month for an unspecified duration after relocating. [Para 30][Para 32][Para 33][Para 34][Para 35][Para 36] Weighing that against the cost of three-and-a-half years of transport as a heavy burden on an employer exercising its right to relocate for economic reasons, the panel found three-and-a-half years unreasonable and six months reasonable. [Para 37][Para 38]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer relocates for genuine economic reasons and the labour law imposes no transport obligation, the dispute is an interest dispute determinable on equity, not a statutory entitlement; the panel will award a limited bridging period of transport — here six months, or its cash equivalent — long enough for workers to adapt but affordable to the employer, rather than the multi-year period the workers claim. [Para 28][Para 29][Para 38]

**Obiter dicta:** location is an essential term of an oral contract under Art. 29 of Decree No. 38, so a relocation without transport breaches the contract and, if it drives workers out, converts into an employer termination carrying Arts. 75, 89 and 90 entitlements, though not damages where economic difficulty is shown. [Para 22][Para 27]
