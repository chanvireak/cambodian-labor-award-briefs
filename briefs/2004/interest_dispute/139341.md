# Award No. 73/04 — Yen U.N. Garment Cambodia Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 15 September 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambona, employer-appointed; Ven Pov, worker-appointed; Kong Phallack, chair) · heard as non-binding award by the parties' agreement · Doc ID 139341

## 1. Facts
Yen UN Garment (Cambodia) Co Ltd, Kul Village, Ang Snuol District, Kandal, employed 1,100 workers when 58 workers of group A13 struck at 2:00 p.m. on 18 August 2004 and about 900 followed at 7:00 a.m. on 19 August 2004 under the Free Worker Union of Cambodia, over a nine-point demand. Notice went to the Angsnuol district governor on 19 August 2004 but never to the company, tyres were burned, the company had its orders finished elsewhere, and the workers walked out of the 20 August 2004 conciliation when the company refused to dismiss the general manager; conciliation settled one of eight issues, and the parties agreed the award would not be immediately binding. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 34][Para 72][Para 73][Para 36][Para 37][Para 19][Para 32]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the Free Worker Union of the company, registered at MoSALVY on 24 April 2004 but not yet approved, is entitled to represent the workers in this collective dispute? [Para 74][Para 75]
2. Whether the company must pay the workers a meal allowance of 2,000 riels when they work overtime until 8:30 p.m.? [Para 14][Para 51]
3. Whether the workers are entitled to a regular attendance bonus of US$7 per month instead of US$5? [Para 15][Para 79]
4. Whether the company must dismiss Ms L, the general manager? [Para 16][Para 54]
5. Whether the company must dismiss Ms N, the team leader of group A13? [Para 17][Para 60]
6. Whether the company must maintain wages and bonuses during the strike? [Para 17][Para 70]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Group A13 demanded the dismissal of its team leader, Ms N, alleging she pressured and threatened workers into overtime, used obscene words, insulted the worker representative and threatened to eliminate them one by one if they failed to remove her; the workforce also demanded the general manager's dismissal over her handling of transfers and dismissals, a US$7 bonus, and pay and bonuses for the strike days. [Para 63][Para 65][Para 58][Para 80][Para 12]
**Employer (respondent):** It accepted overtime pay under Notification 17/00 by agreement, but could only maintain the US$5 bonus because it was making no profit, and its attendance policy already matched Notification 017/00; dismissal of its managers was not for the workers to demand, since only the parties to a contract may end it; it refused pay for an unnotified, unlawful strike, and its lawyer questioned whether an unregistered union could be the plaintiff. [Para 52][Para 82][Para 85][Para 91][Para 97][Para 45][Para 74]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel declared the Free Worker Union a legal representative of the Yen UN workers, ordered the company to apply overtime work in accordance with Notification 17/00 of the Ministry of Labour (by agreement), rejected the US$7 attendance bonus demand, rejected the demands to dismiss Ms L and Ms N, prohibited the company from firing the regular workers of group A13 and from dismissing any employee who joined the strike (both by agreement), set rules on proper conduct and on photographing workers, and rejected the demand for wages and bonuses during the strike. [Para 128][Para 129][Para 131][Para 132][Para 133][Para 134][Para 136]

On representation the panel held that Article 268 obliges the Ministry to answer a registration application within two months, failing which registration is granted automatically. The receipt was dated 24 April 2004 and the union had waited four months, so the delay was the Ministry's own administrative failing and the union held the rights of a registered union, entitling it to negotiate and sue for the workers. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 77][Para 78]

On the US$7 bonus, the panel held Notification 017 of MoLSAVY guarantees workers who work regularly at least US$5 a month, and that a demand above that figure, though not legally founded, could be entertained as an interests dispute decided on equity. Equity would be applied only if the workers had (1) made efforts to reach a collective bargaining agreement and (2) proved the increase reasonable. They did neither, so the demand was rejected; the panel also found that withholding the US$5 bonus for three days' absence without permission, as the company's policy allowed, conformed to Notification 017/00. [Para 80][Para 83][Para 84][Para 85]

On both dismissal demands the panel applied the rule that workers have no right to demand dismissal unless they prove the employee is dangerous and cannot be allowed in the workplace, citing Awards 04/03 (Full Value), 17/03 (Chou Sing), 15/04 (Lucky Zone), 16/04 (Yada), 32/04 (Sin Kam), 6/03 (Ho Hing) and 18/04. Article 65 and Decree-Law 38 make the contract the law of the parties, applicable only to them, so hiring and dismissal belong to the employer alone, and Article 83 confers a right to dismiss for serious misconduct rather than an obligation. [Para 88][Para 89][Para 90][Para 91][Para 96][Para 97][Para 99]

Ms L' case failed for want of evidence of danger beyond dislike of her management. Ms N' case was different: an A13 worker testified to her threat to eliminate everyone if they failed to remove her, and the panel found the dispute serious enough to harm the workplace, yet found no rational ground to order dismissal, holding instead that the employer should move her from team leader of A13 to another group. [Para 90][Para 102][Para 105][Para 107][Para 109][Para 111]

On strike pay the panel noted Article 332, which suspends the contract and salary, and Article 334, which obliges the employer to pay strikers where it recruits replacements; following Award 04/03 (Lida Garment) it treated having orders finished by other factories as equivalent to replacement hiring, so the demand was in principle open. It failed because the strike was unlawful: Article 324 requires seven days' prior notice to the company, and notice was sent to the governor on 19 August 2004, a day after the strike began and never to the employer, while Article 320(4) requires all peaceful methods to be exhausted and no Arbitration Council conciliation had occurred. Unlawful strikers lose the full protection of the law, including the Article 333 bar on sanctions, and participation in an unlawful strike is an abuse of Notification 017/00, so the US$5 bonus could be withheld. [Para 112][Para 113][Para 114][Para 116][Para 118][Para 119][Para 120][Para 121][Para 122][Para 126][Para 127]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the Ministry of Labour fails to answer a union registration application within the two months Article 268 allows, registration is granted automatically and the union may represent the workers in a collective dispute; and a strike that gives less than seven days' notice to the employer and is called before conciliation is exhausted is unlawful, so strikers forfeit both wages under Article 332 and the Article 333 protection against sanction, and may lose the Notification 017/00 attendance bonus. [Para 76][Para 78][Para 118][Para 120][Para 121][Para 126]

**Obiter dicta:** That a demand above the statutory bonus can be decided on equity if the workers show efforts towards a collective bargaining agreement and the reasonableness of the demand; and the panel's view that Ms N should be moved to another group although no order to that effect appears in the decision. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 111]
