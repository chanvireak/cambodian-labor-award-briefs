# Award No. 28/03 — Raffles Grand Hotel D'Angkor (Arbitration Council, 27 January 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mar Samborana (employer-nominated), An Nan (worker-nominated), Ang Eng Thong (chair) · Doc ID 139285

## 1. Facts
Raffles Grand Hotel D'Angkor, a five-star hotel in Siem Reap, employed about 310 staff; its employees' union was registered at MoSALVY by certificate No. 458 dated 23 May 2003. [Para 20][Para 18] The parties had negotiated a collective bargaining agreement for months and still had 32 articles in dispute when the union gave strike notice on 13 December 2003 for a strike on 22 December, and struck on 24 December 2003 after the Provincial Court temporarily prohibited it. [Para 21][Para 25][Para 28] The Council ordered the strike stopped and the workers back to their shifts without sanction, then decided to settle the whole CBA as a package rather than only the nine non-conciliation points. [Para 26][Para 29][Para 129]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the CBA should use the word "management (Neakkrobkrong)" or "non-labour" to describe those who recruit, terminate, discipline and warn employees? [Para 29][Para 30]
3. Whether the employer must refrain from deducting the wages and bonuses of union leaders who attend grievance meetings? [Para 33][Para 73]
4. Whether union delegates must be allowed two hours a week for union work without deduction of wages and bonuses? [Para 77][Para 81]
6. Whether the employer must let NGO representatives, the federation or lawyers enter the hotel to check implementation of the CBA? [Para 8][Para 81]
7. Whether the employer must provide a room, table, six chairs and a whiteboard for a union library? [Para 34][Para 84]
11. Whether temporary workers may be hired for long periods at wages below those of permanent staff? [Para 86][Para 91]
17. Whether a minimum wage should be fixed for the hotel's employees? [Para 113][Para 116]
19. Whether the employer must continue collecting and distributing in full the 10% service charge? [Para 117][Para 118]
21. Whether work at night, on public holidays and on weekly days off must be paid at the statutory multipliers? [Para 99][Para 102][Para 104]
23. Whether the employer must insure workers' vehicles parked on hotel premises against loss? [Para 127][Para 128]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The union needs the word "management" because the group it describes holds the power to hire and dismiss; union leaders must be protected from wage deductions while doing representational work, need two hours a week, and need a room to hold meetings and keep legal documents. [Para 29][Para 35][Para 8] Wages at the hotel start at US$30–35 for new staff and are unequal for equal work; the hotel must distribute all service charge except to the ten Executive Council members. [Para 48][Para 56]
**Employer (respondent):** "Non-labour" is the accurate term because the group includes salaried staff; hiring temporary workers follows the volume of work and their pay is by agreement. [Para 30][Para 40][Para 41] Wages should be set by agreement, since equal pay ignores differing skill and talent and a high floor would price out the less skilled. [Para 49][Para 52][Para 53] An exclusive library would be used for activity against the hotel, and the hotel already has a common library. [Para 36][Para 37]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel issued the whole CBA as Annex A, setting a minimum wage of US$50.00 per month, full distribution of service charge, two hours a week for union delegates, statutory multipliers for night and holiday work, and ordering each side to drop its claim against the other; the seniority and 13th-month bonuses were refused. [Para 129][Para 116][Para 118][Para 81][Para 130]

On Article 1-b the panel replaced "supervisor" with "any employee" who holds delegated management duties, and deleted Article 1-c as a duplicate of 2-a. [Para 70][Para 72][Para 73] On Article 2-a it held, under the Prakas of 22 November 2001 and Prakas No. 313, that negotiators and union leaders attending grievance meetings may not have wages, bonuses or benefits deducted. [Para 73][Para 75][Para 77]

On Article 2-c the panel found the hotel had no shop stewards elected according to law and no registered internal rules, so the registered union delegates must carry out shop steward work and are entitled to two hours a week without deduction until elections are held, applying Article 288 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 286. [Para 78][Para 80][Para 81]

On Article 2-e the panel held the demand unlawful: under Prakas No. 197 the labour inspector, not an outside body, inspects CBA implementation, though a lawyer may accompany the union in a dispute. [Para 81][Para 82][Para 83] On Article 2-g, however, Prakas No. 286 Article 5 requires the employer to give shop stewards an office, meeting room, supplies and a place to post information, so the hotel must provide a properly equipped room — not the exclusive library as framed. [Para 84][Para 85]

On Article 5 the panel held a temporary worker's contract must still be one of the three lawful categories, that one continued quietly beyond three months converts to fixed or indeterminate duration, and that agreed wages may never fall below the minimum wage. [Para 86][Para 89][Para 90][Para 91]

On Article 9-a the panel relied on Articles 104 and 106 of the Labour Law and on equity, compared contracts at Royal Angkor (US$50), Victoria Angkor (US$65) and Pan Sea Angkor (US$50), and fixed US$50.00 per month. [Para 113][Para 115][Para 116] On Articles 9-e, f and g it held Article 134 obliges the hotel to collect and distribute the service charge in full, that six years of collection had made it a binding custom, and that ceasing collection to cut prices was no answer. [Para 118][Para 119][Para 121][Para 122] Because 100% distribution raised wages substantially, it refused the US$11/US$10 seniority bonus and the 50/60/70% 13th-month bonus. [Para 116][Para 117]

On Article 7 it confirmed the eight-hour day with a one-hour meal break outside it, and set the multipliers: normal daily wage for public holidays, double for night work between 22:00 and 05:00, triple for night work on a holiday, and four times for night work on a weekly day off. [Para 96][Para 101][Para 104][Para 105] On Article 13-j it ordered workers to pay 2,000 riel a month for a car and 1,000 riel for a bicycle or motorbike so that the hotel could post a guard. [Para 127][Para 128]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An arbitral award settling an interest dispute replaces the CBA for one year, and where a hotel has collected a 10% service charge for years, Article 134 of the Labour Law plus that practice obliges it to keep collecting and to distribute the whole amount to all staff except the Executive Council. [Para 122][Para 128] Where no shop stewards have been lawfully elected, registered union delegates assume the shop stewards' statutory rights — two paid hours a week, an office and a meeting room — and an employer that fixes wages by agreement may not go below the minimum wage the panel sets on comparable local pay. [Para 81][Para 85][Para 116]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that some hotels kept their prices after ceasing to collect service charge by folding it into the basic price, and that a social fund financed by a US$10 monthly employer contribution had no legal basis but might be revisited if relations improved. [Para 119][Para 92][Para 93]

## 6. Source defects disclosed

[Para 77][Para 118] The award describes itself as non-binding by agreement yet prints the standard clause making it binding eight days after notification absent opposition. [Para 29][Para 130] The chair appears as "Ang Eng Thong" in the panel list and "Ang Eng Tong" at the signature block. [Para 17][Para 130] Article 9-a is pleaded at US$70 (Hotel Le Royal) in the demand but argued at US$50 in the reasons. [Para 13][Para 47] The 13th-month percentage is given as "50%, 60% and 70%" for 2003–2005 with 2003 also written as 50% of the basic wage in a different order. [Para 13][Para 117] Individual worker and representative names are reduced to initials. [Para 7]
