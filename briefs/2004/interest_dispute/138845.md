# Award No. 06/04 — Chou Sing Garment (Cambodia) Co. Ltd and Cambodian Workers' Rights Union and others (Arbitration Council, 10 March 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry, chosen by the employer; Tuon Siphann, chosen by the workers; Kong Phallack, chair) · Doc ID 138845

## 1. Facts
Chou Sing Garment, a factory on National Road 6A, Phnom Penh with 2,500 workers in 28 sewing groups and three unions on site, pays performance bonuses to sewing groups of 3000 riel per person when a group reaches 90–100% of a 76% industrial-engineering standard, while warehouse and piece-rate workers are paid individually. [Para 28][Para 29][Para 31][Para 53][Para 54] Its Internal Work Rules of 08 January 1999, recognised by the Labour Inspector on 12 January 1999, provide at Article 39 for an annual salary increase each December; the factory says it stopped applying Article 39 when Notification #017 of 18 July 2000 on seniority bonuses issued. [Para 21][Para 44][Para 56][Para 83] The District Labour Inspector conciliated 15 of 18 points on 26 January 2004; three points — bonus method, Article 39 increase, and dismissal of three supervisors — went to the Council on 06 February 2004. [Para 31][Para 32]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay incentive bonuses to workers individually rather than through the groups it currently uses? [Para 13][Para 53]
2. Whether the workers are entitled to an annual salary increase under Article 39 of the Internal Work Rules? [Para 13][Para 84]
3. Whether the employer must dismiss the Chinese supervisor Ms Y, the administrator Mr L, and the administrative assistant Mr N for misconduct toward workers? [Para 13][Para 46][Para 51][Para 52]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The 76% standard, less the 24% the factory deducts from it, is nearly impossible to reach, so the same few groups take the bonus every period; they ask for three tiers of 3000, 1500 and 1000 riel per person and for other sections to be treated alike, and for the three supervisors to be dismissed because Ms Y forced warnings on workers by thumbprint, cut their hours and withheld pay, contrary to the agreement of 06 August 2003 that a Chinese supervisor committing serious misconduct would be dismissed like a Cambodian worker. [Para 40][Para 42][Para 47][Para 50][Para 112]
**Employer (respondent):** The technical standard cannot be changed, though it already offers a 24% reduction, and it counters with tiers of 3000, 1000 and 500 riel; it raised salaries once or twice but was released from Article 39 by Notification #017; and the three supervisors have made no serious mistake, though the factory promises to move them to posts without direct contact with workers. [Para 59][Para 42][Para 88][Para 53]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must keep paying 3000 riels per person to groups reaching over 90% of the 76% standard until a collective bargaining agreement exists; the parties must negotiate a CBA before 31 August 2004, meeting weekly for at least 90 minutes; the employer must pay US$3.00 damages to each employee at the end of March 2004 for past failures under Article 39 and increase wages from December 2004 and each year thereafter, with the amount and procedure to be negotiated by 30 November 2004; and it must warn Ms Y in writing and move her, move Mr L to document work with no decision-making power over worker disputes, and change Mr N's work and notify him in writing of his past misconduct. [Para 144][Para 145][Para 146][Para 147][Para 148]
On issue 1 the panel treated the demand as both a rights and an interests dispute: the existing group bonus at 3000 riel has become established practice and a right the employer cannot reduce without agreement, so that part was maintained. [Para 70][Para 74][Para 75] The additional 85–90% and 80–85% tiers were new conditions, an interests dispute, and the panel declined to fix them because its award would then operate as a collective bargaining agreement binding the parties for one year, and neither side had been warned of that consequence. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 78] Instead, relying on Article 10 of Prakas #305 of 22 November 2001, it set a five-month negotiating window to 31 August 2004 with weekly meetings. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 81] On issue 2 the panel held Article 39 binding, since internal work rules must be applied under Articles 22 and 23 of the Labour Law and the labour contracts repeat the condition, and rejected the employer's confusion between two separate instruments: Notification #017 binds all garment factories on seniority bonus regardless of internal rules, while Article 39 applies only in this factory, so both apply. [Para 86][Para 87][Para 94][Para 95][Para 96] Because Article 39 fixes neither amount nor procedure, it awarded US$3.00 per worker for five years of non-implementation and left the future increase to negotiation. [Para 97][Para 98][Para 99] On issue 3 the panel preferred the workers' account of Ms Y, found serious misconduct, but held she should first receive a written warning and be moved, with dismissal following only if her behaviour does not change within three days. [Para 106][Para 108][Para 110][Para 111] Mr L must be moved at once per the successive agreements of 22 July 2003, 13 October 2003, 03 February 2004 and 11 February 2004, and Mr N investigated and warned; under Article 83 serious misconduct does not oblige termination. [Para 130][Para 131][Para 137][Para 139][Para 142]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where internal work rules recognised by the Labour Inspector promise an annual salary increase, a later ministerial notification on seniority bonuses does not displace them and the employer must apply both; failure to apply the rule is compensable even though the rule itself fixes no amount, the amount being left to negotiation. [Para 94][Para 95][Para 96][Para 97] A bonus practice consistently applied becomes an established right that cannot be reduced unilaterally, whereas new bonus tiers are interests to be settled by bargaining, not by award. [Para 74][Para 76][Para 77]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's discussion of Article 83 — that termination for misconduct is the employer's right, not its duty, and that the seven-day rule protects an employee not dismissed — justified accepting the transfer concession rather than ordering any dismissal. [Para 139][Para 140]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Hearing dates conflict: the notice gives Wednesday 18 February 2004, 08.00–12.00, while the case summary states the panel heard the parties on February 25, 2004 at 8:00 am. [Para 20][Para 34] The non-conciliation report is dated September 12, 2003 and submitted September 15, 2003, months before the January 2004 conciliation that produced the three unresolved points. [Para 15][Para 31] The number of past salary increases is printed two ways: the findings say "one time", the employer and the panel "once or twice". [Para 55][Para 88][Para 91] The two supervisors' near-identical names (Leang Narith, Leang Narin) are split across lines and rendered inconsistently, amid OCR duplication of whole clauses; they are given here as Mr L and Mr N. [Para 83][Para 129][Para 147] Bonus bands appear as "90% 100%" without the range word, and both offers are printed twice. [Para 42][Para 61] Riel is printed as "riel" and "riels" interchangeably. [Para 144]
