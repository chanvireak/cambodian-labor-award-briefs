# Award No. 31/03 — Cambodian Hung Wah Garment Company (Arbitration Council, February 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phallak presiding; Ouk Ry, employer nominee; Vong Vanna, worker nominee) · Doc ID 138840

## 1. Facts
Cambodian Hung Wah Garment Company, at Trapengkheung village, Khan Dongkor, Phnom Penh, employs 2,500 workers and has four unions in the factory, of which three are registered and the Khmer Youth Union of Hung Wah Company is not. [Para 12] Conciliation of the 12 issues raised by the Khmer Youth Union failed on 3 December 2003 because the other three unions would not negotiate with it; the union then added issues at each meeting until the list reached 50, while the three registered unions negotiated 19 issues of their own and settled them with the company by 19 December 2003. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 14] The company also terminated three workers on 30 December 2003. [Para 12] MOSALVY referred the 50 un-conciliated issues to the Council, which grouped them into disputes of law, of interest, and of law and interest, plus one complaint filed through the employer's lawyer. [Para 7]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the 50 non-conciliated issues referred by MOSALVY — divided by the panel into disputes of law, disputes of interest, and disputes of both, with one further complaint lodged by the employer's lawyer — can be settled by an award of the Council? [Para 7][Para 14]
2. Whether the Khmer Youth Union of Hung Wah Company has the legal status to represent the workers of the factory in an interests dispute and to make a collective bargaining agreement on their behalf? [Para 17][Para 18][Para 22]
3. Whether an award of the Council on an interests dispute would take the place of a collective bargaining agreement under Article 43 of the Prakas on the Arbitration Council (No. 338/2002)? [Para 16]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the Khmer Youth Union Federation complained on 10 December 2003 to the labour inspector of Dongkor district over 50 demands — wage recalculation for casual and piece-rate workers, full basic wage for periods of no work, reimbursement of health deductions and work-book fees, reinstatement of a dismissed worker, maternity and nursing entitlements, canteen, water, clothing, lockers and night-shift double pay — and asked the Federation to help settle the termination of three workers on 30 December 2003. [Para 12][Para 24][Para 25]
**Employer (respondent):** through its lawyer and lawyer's assistant, both assigned on 31 December 2003, the company had already negotiated and reached agreement on the 19 issues raised by the three registered unions by 19 December 2003, and objected to the 15 issues the Khmer Youth Union had tabled on 8 December 2003; it denied that the unregistered union spoke for the workforce. [Para 10][Para 11][Para 12][Para 14]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel denied the composition of the Khmer Youth Union in settling case No. 31/03 and refused all the demands of the employees it purported to represent. [Para 22]
The panel first observed that among the 50 points some are legal and some are rights disputes, but that they are closely connected and affect the common interest of all workers in the factory, so that settling them should lead to a collective bargaining agreement. [Para 14][Para 15] It reasoned that an award on those issues would itself take the place of a collective bargaining agreement under Article 43 of Prakas 338/2002. [Para 16]
That premise decided the case: the Council cannot issue an award on an interests dispute unless the party before it has the right to make a collective bargaining agreement, and that right requires representative status, which Article 277 of the Labour Law grants only to a union that is registered and meets the other statutory requirements. [Para 17][Para 18]
The panel found that the Khmer Youth Union of Hung Wah Cambodia Company was not registered according to law and had received no recognition of representative status in the company, so it had no legal right to conclude a collective bargaining agreement for the factory's workers. [Para 18][Para 19] That right belonged to the registered unions with the most members meeting the Article 277 criteria, but none of those unions was in dispute with the employer, so nobody with standing was before the panel. [Para 20][Para 21] It concluded the union lacked sufficient legal status to represent the employees and refused the demands. [Para 22]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that is neither registered under Article 277 of the Labour Law nor recognised as representative in the enterprise cannot bring an interests dispute to the Arbitration Council: because an award on such a dispute would replace a collective bargaining agreement, the Council has no power to make one at the request of a body that could not itself have signed that agreement. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 18][Para 19]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the right to negotiate would have belonged to the registered unions holding the most members and meeting the Article 277 criteria, and that none of those unions was in dispute with the employer — an observation that explains the vacuum rather than the decision. [Para 20][Para 21]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The date of award is printed only as ".02-2004", the month and day having been lost to the OCR, and the case number appears as "[31/03" on the face of the award but as "NO 31/03" in the orders. [Para 1][Para 22] The list of the four unions is garbled into a run of names with their registration status interleaved, so that the sentence reads "Democracy Development Union Worker Freedom Union registered registered Progress Democracy Union Khmer Youth Union not registered"; the names and statuses cannot be reliably paired from that line. [Para 12] The employer is called "Cambodian Hung Wah Garment Company" in the title, "Hung Wah Cambodia Garment Company" in the findings and "Hung Wah Company" elsewhere. [Para 1][Para 12][Para 10] Counsel appears as "Mr. L Vanna" and "Mrs. Y Sindy" in the evidence list but "Lim Vannas" and "Youn Sindy" in the assigning letters, and the ministry is spelled both "MSAVLY" and "MOSALVY". [Para 2][Para 3][Para 7][Para 10][Para 11] The 50 demands in the annex survive only in broken OCR, several truncated mid-clause ("Should take legal ac…", "50-" with nothing after it), so the list is summarised here rather than reproduced; the three terminated workers and the worker named in demand 10 are referred to in the source by full name and are not reproduced. [Para 12][Para 24][Para 25]
