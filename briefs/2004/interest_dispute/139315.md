# Award No. 34/04 — Full Value (Cambodia) Textile (Arbitration Council, 30 June 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phallack presiding; You Suonty appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 139315

## 1. Facts
Full Value (Cambodia) Textile employs 550 workers. [Para 12] On 8 May 2004 the workers filed a complaint raising eight collective disputes; the Labour Inspectors conciliated six, and the two remaining points were sent to the Council on 27 May 2004 under letter No. 632/MOSALVY of the same date. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 7] The hearing was held on 3 and 11 June 2004 and both parties agreed the award would be non-binding. [Para 10][Para 12] The complaint concerned an administration staff member's conduct and a wage demand for permanent workers. [Para 4][Para 5]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must dismiss Ms S, an administration staff member, for yelling abusively at workers, forcing them to work overtime under threat, and refusing leave for a marriage? [Para 4][Para 16]
2. Whether the company must raise permanent workers' wages by USD5 after three months, by a further USD5 seven months after they become permanent, and by a further USD5 one year after those seven months? [Para 5][Para 24]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Ms S shouted at workers and warned that "if you did not work overtime now, next time you cannot work overtime", and refused marriage leave; the company has grown, has two more premises and pays less than factories such as Sun Tex. [Para 16][Para 24][Para 25]
**Employer (respondent):** Ms S did use abusive language, but overtime is arranged by production group heads, not by her, and the marriage remark was a joke and the leave was granted; it lacks the ability to pay and could comply only if a law required an increase; the Canadia Park and Chamka Doung premises are leased clothing shops, not new branches, and 170 workers were hired only to meet existing production. [Para 18][Para 21][Para 23][Para 26][Para 27][Para 29]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both the demand that Ms S be dismissed and the demand for a wage increase above current practice are rejected. [Para 47][Para 48]
On issue 1 the panel applied Article 65 of the Labour Law, under which a labour contract establishes the employment relation, with Article 22 of Decree-law No. 38 on contracts: a contract binds the parties and may be amended only by their consent, so only the parties to it may terminate it, and hiring or dismissal is the employer's sole right. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 37][Para 38] Article 83 gives the employer the right to terminate for serious misconduct but nowhere obliges it to do so, so the panel cannot order dismissal on the workers' demand, as it has held in cases 04/03 Lida, 14/03 Chou Sing, 17/03 and 18/03 Hu Hing, 06/04 Chou Sing, 15/04 Lucky Zone, 16/04 Yada Printing and 32/04 Ecent. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 39][Para 40] The only exception is where an employee's conduct affects other workers' health and safety, as in 14/03 Chou Sing, which was not this case. [Para 34] The employer's admission of abusive language did not alter the absence of power to dismiss. [Para 31]
On issue 2 the panel treated the demand as an interest dispute because it exceeds what the law provides, to be decided on equity. [Para 41] The union produced no evidence for growth, export volume or better wages elsewhere, and did not contest the employer's account that the extra premises are warehouses. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43][Para 44] Productivity had not grown over six months and fell in June 2004, wages were paid in accordance with Notification No. 017/MOSALVY of 18 July 2000, and no provision or practice showed the textile industry paying more. [Para 31][Para 44][Para 45][Para 46]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The panel has no power to order an employer to dismiss a worker at the union's demand, because termination is a right reserved to the parties to the employment contract; a wage demand above the legal minimum is an interest dispute that fails where the union proves neither the employer's capacity to pay nor a better industry practice. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 41][Para 46]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel may order dismissal or transfer where an employee's conduct endangers the health and safety of other workers. [Para 34]
