# Award No. 17/04 — Cheer View Textiles Cambodia Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 8 April 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Kao Tharch (employer-chosen), Tuon Siphann (worker-chosen), Sok Mathoeung (chair) · Doc ID 139301

## 1. Facts
Cheer View Textiles Cambodia Co., Ltd, in Dangkor District, Phnom Penh, has operated since 1998 and employed 1,238 workers [Para 33]. In January 2004 employees working with CCAWDU notified the director of an election for a new union, attaching a list of candidates that included Mr L and Mr H [Para 109][Para 110]. The company dismissed Mr L on 18 February 2004 and Mr H on 19 February 2004, both candidates, without written warning and without seeking the Labour Inspector's approval [Para 115][Para 127][Para 148]. Conciliation on 10 March 2004 failed on all three claims [Para 35].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must re-employ Mr L and Mr H, candidates in the union elections? [Para 10]
2. Whether the employer must give those two workers back pay and other damages for the period of the labour dispute? [Para 10]
3. Whether the company must stop interfering with the newly established union? [Para 10]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissals were retaliation for union organising — the director had asked Mr L whether he was there "to work or to make trouble" and threatened to dismiss him, and Mr H was stripped of his ID badge for delivering "propaganda" [Para 44][Para 46][Para 76]. They sought reinstatement, salary and bonuses from dismissal to re-employment, and a further US$1,200 in compensation, though by the close of the hearing they dropped the damages claim and asked only for salary [Para 101][Para 102].
**Employer (respondent):** Mr L was dismissed for breaching the internal work rules — leaving his post, doing personal work and interfering with other workers' leave requests — after two oral warnings, and Mr H for soliciting signatures during working hours on a personal matter [Para 56][Para 57][Para 91][Para 96]. It denied all three claims, including any interference with union work [Para 102][Para 107].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the company must reinstate Mr L and Mr H to the same positions and pay them basic salary, overtime, piece rate and other bonuses calculated on their December 2003 earnings, from the date of dismissal to the date of re-employment; the claim for damages was rejected for want of proof; and the company was ordered to stop any act affecting the setting up of the new union [Para 151][Para 163][Para 164][Para 165].

On the first issue the panel framed the ordinary test under Articles 74, 27, 75, 293 and Prakas 305 — acceptable reason, proportionality, procedure, notice, and Labour Inspector approval — then narrowed the case to the last of these [Para 138]. Article 3 of Prakas 305 of 22 November 2001 protects union election candidates like staff delegates for 45 days before the election and 45 days after it if they lose, and Article 4.4 makes a dismissal without the Labour Inspector's approval void and finable under Article 373 [Para 139][Para 142][Para 143]. Both men were on the candidate list notified to the company, so they enjoyed that protection; they were dismissed with no notice and no approval [Para 145][Para 147][Para 148]. Independently, the dismissals breached Article 10 of the internal work rules, which requires a written warning first, and the employer conceded it had warned only orally [Para 62][Para 149][Para 150]. The panel therefore found the dismissals contrary to Article 293, Articles 3 and 4 of Prakas 305 and the internal rules, and ordered reinstatement [Para 150][Para 151].

On the second issue, because the contracts remained legally valid, the two were still employees and entitled to wages and bonuses measured by their December 2003 pay — the month in which the employer was informed of the election [Para 153][Para 154][Para 155]. Damages were refused: the workers produced no forensic accounting of their loss and withdrew the claim at the hearing [Para 156][Para 157][Para 161].

On the third issue the panel found, on the parties' competing presentations, that the workers' account was clearer and the employer gave no acceptable reason for its conduct [Para 134]. Dismissing two candidates, privileging the existing union led by Mr U over ordinary workers, discussing the employees' plan with that union's representatives, applying the rules unequally to candidates, and reassigning Ms C on 20 February 2004 at 07h25 so that she had to find work to fill her hours, amounted to discrimination contrary to Articles 12, 279 and 280 [Para 135][Para 136][Para 160]. Articles 279 and 280 forbid reliance on union affiliation in decisions on recruitment, assignment, promotion and discipline, and forbid acts of interference [Para 159].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union election candidate named on a list notified to the employer is protected from dismissal like a staff delegate — 45 days before the election and 45 days after it if unelected — and a dismissal effected without the Labour Inspector's prior approval is null and void, so the remedy is reinstatement with pay from dismissal to re-employment, measured by the earnings of the month in which the employer learned of the election [Para 139][Para 143][Para 155]. Failure to give the written warning the employer's own internal rules require independently voids the dismissal, whatever the substantive misconduct alleged [Para 149][Para 150].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the workers had a right to damages for discrimination but received none because they neither claimed nor evidenced an amount, and it noted that neither dismissal was reported to MoSALVY [Para 133][Para 161]. It also remarked that the employer had failed to specify which articles of the internal rules were breached [Para 120].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The letter notifying the election is dated 26 January 2004 on its face, while the employer said it was received on 6 January 2004 and the workers conceded the date in the letter could be wrong; the panel's findings accept 6 January and elsewhere still cite the 26 January letter [Para 40][Para 108][Para 145]. Mr H's employment contract is dated 12 May 2003 but his start date is given as 12 May 2002 [Para 20][Para 75]. The employer's file includes a dismissal letter to Mr H dated 11 February 2004 although the dismissal is found to have occurred on 19 February 2004 [Para 21][Para 127]. Mr L's contract is dated 25 May 2002 while he is said to have started in October 1999 [Para 24][Para 114]. The Prakas 305 quotation in Article 279/280 passage is garbled, with sentences duplicated and interleaved, and one reason paragraph breaks off mid-clause ("must stop their favourable bias towards") [Para 159][Para 161]. A witness is described as leader of the "Union for Solidarity and Development of Workers" and later as president of "Worker Development Solidarity" [Para 16][Para 137].
