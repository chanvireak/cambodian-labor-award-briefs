# Award No. 13/04 — Genuine Garment Factory Co., Ltd (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 30 March 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ly Tayseng (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Sar Sovan (chair) · Doc ID 139298

## 1. Facts

Genuine Garment Factory Co., Ltd (Cambodia) operates at National Road 4, Kul village, Kandal province, and the dispute began with a fight at the factory entryway on 26 January 2004 between Mr K, deputy chief of the Genuine Solidarity Union, and Ms C, chief of shop stewards and secretary of the Cambodian Industrial Garment and Textile Union. [Para 1] [Para 12] [Para 22]
After Mr K insulted her, Ms C yelled at him and threw a book and her shoes, which he dodged; the company sought suspension of both and on 20 February 2004 the Kandal social affairs department agreed to dismiss both. [Para 36] [Para 38] [Para 45] [Para 18]
The union filed a collective complaint on 1 March 2004 with eight points, discussion broke down, and the case reached the Secretariat on 5 March 2004; issues 2 to 8 were settled by mediation at the hearing. [Para 19] [Para 21] [Para 88]

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. Whether the company was entitled to dismiss Ms C for serious misconduct, or whether the dismissal was disproportionate and should be replaced by a lesser sanction? [Para 48] [Para 87]
2. Whether the company must pay 100% of wages in the first month, 60% in the second and third months, and preserve the position in the fourth to sixth months, where a worker is sick or injured from work? [Para 6]
3. Whether employment contracts must state wage rates of USD35 for apprentices, USD40 for casual workers and USD45 for permanent workers? [Para 6]
4. Whether the company must provide seats at the ironing stations and at the fabric distribution area? [Para 7]
5. Whether all overtime must be agreed at least one day in advance and negotiated with the union representative and workers' delegation? [Para 7]
6. Whether the company must pay 100% of wages for reasonable sick leave due to dizziness, headache, fainting, diarrhoea or fever? [Para 8]
7. Whether the company must discuss warnings and dismissals with the union representative or workers' delegation? [Para 8]
8. Whether the company must give union and delegation members time off during working hours to negotiate worker complaints? [Para 8]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** Ms C was provoked, and the company had planned the confrontation to remove a union leader; the dismissal was disproportionate, past yelling incidents were never sanctioned and cannot now be revived, and the remaining claims concern basic conditions the company had refused to fix. [Para 16] [Para 80] [Para 84]
**Employer (respondent):** The two fought and yelled at each other in the factory during working hours, which is serious misconduct justifying dismissal without notice, and Ms C had a history of yelling at colleagues. [Para 14] [Para 83]

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** partly granted — the dismissal was set aside and replaced by two months' suspension without pay from 20 February to 19 April 2004, with reinstatement on 19 April 2004 in the same position, wage and responsibilities; the company was ordered to restore all wages and benefits withheld during the suspension before the dismissal, within seven days of the award taking effect; issues 2 to 8 were recorded as agreed in mediation. [Para 90] [Para 92] [Para 94] [Para 95]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 82 of the Labour Law, which allows dismissal without notice for serious offences, and Article 83, which defines them as threats, abusive language or assaults on an employer or other workers. [Para 50] [Para 51] It found that Ms C's yelling at Mr K was a serious offence, while it was not clear that throwing the book and shoes, both of which he dodged, amounted to assault or a threat — a point it treated as irrelevant once the yelling was established. [Para 59] [Para 61] [Para 63]

The dismissal still had to satisfy Article 27, under which any disciplinary sanction must be proportional to the seriousness of the misconduct, so the panel examined the circumstances. [Para 64] [Para 66] It found that Mr K had insulted her by saying that if she acted like that she would never find another husband, a remark deeply offensive to a woman's reputation in Khmer traditional culture which he should not have shouted in a public area; a reasonable person so provoked would be likely to lose control, so she was provoked into the offence and did not engage in a fight. [Para 71] [Para 74] [Para 76] [Para 78] Dismissal was therefore disproportionate, but her conduct was not entirely excusable, so a two-month unpaid suspension was substituted. [Para 86] [Para 87] [Para 88]

The panel rejected two collateral arguments. The union's claim that the company had planned the confrontation was unsupported by evidence, and the company could not rely on earlier yelling incidents because it had not disciplined them within the fifteen-day period required by Article 26, so those sanctions were no longer available. [Para 82] [Para 85]

Issues 2 to 8 were resolved by agreement at the hearing, which the panel found lawful and consistent with clause 30 of the Prakas on the Arbitration Council, and it recorded the terms: sick and injury pay of 100% for one month and 60% for months two and three with position security to month six; apprentice pay of USD30 under Prakas 004/MOSALVY of 30 September 1999, casual pay of USD40 and permanent pay of USD45 after two months; three long sofas for the nine workers at the ironing station and three at the fabric distribution area; overtime by mutual agreement with notice before 13:00 and forced overtime reported to the company; union involvement in disciplinary decisions under clause 12 of the internal rules; and time off for representatives negotiating complaints, with the union undertaking not to disrupt production if those terms are met. [Para 88] [Para 89] [Para 95] [Para 96] [Para 98] [Para 100]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

Yelling at a fellow worker is a serious offence within Article 83 and may be sanctioned, but Article 27 requires the sanction to be proportional, and where the outburst was provoked by a grave public insult to the worker's dignity, dismissal is disproportionate: the panel will substitute a fixed-term suspension without pay and order reinstatement with back pay for wages withheld before the dismissal. [Para 61] [Para 76] [Para 87] [Para 92] An employer may not support a dismissal with earlier misconduct that it failed to sanction within the fifteen-day limit in Article 26. [Para 85]

**Obiter dicta:** Throwing a book and shoes that the target dodged may not amount to the assault or threat defined in Article 83, a question the panel left open as irrelevant to its finding [Para 59]; and a union's allegation that the employer engineered the confrontation requires evidence, which was absent here [Para 82].
