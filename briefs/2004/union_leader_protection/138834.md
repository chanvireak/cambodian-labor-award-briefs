# Award No. 27/03 — Standard Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, Tuesday, January 6, 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ms You Suonty, Mr Tuon Siphann, chair Dr Sok Mathoeung) · Doc ID 138834

## 1. Facts
Standard Garment Co., Ltd was registered on September 22, 2003 and began operating that month with about 300 employees on three-month temporary contracts at a monthly wage of US$35, and it had no internal work rules or written disciplinary procedure. [Para 20] [Para 21] [Para 31] [Para 33] [Para 34] Its workers struck on November 12 and again on November 15, 2003 over incorrect cash wage deductions, and a Khmer Union was created at the factory on November 23, 2003 with registration applied for at MOSALVY on December 11, 2003. [Para 40] [Para 42] [Para 43] [Para 67] On December 13, 2003 the employer dismissed seven employees — three union leaders (president, deputy and secretary) and four union members — without prior notice and without the Labour Inspector's permission, and then hung their pictures inside and outside the factory. [Para 67] [Para 68] [Para 69] [Para 70] A strike on December 15, 2003 over eight demands left only reinstatement unresolved, and the parties agreed in the hearing that the award would be non-binding. [Para 22] [Para 24] [Para 27]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate the seven dismissed employees, who were leaders and active members of the union? [Para 11]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that the dismissal was discrimination against the union, that the employer knew of the union through its managers, that the one-to-three-month temporary contracts had already run out for most of them, and that dismissal of union workers required compliance with Articles 268 and 279 of the Labour Law. [Para 71] [Para 73] [Para 77] [Para 81] [Para 82]
**Employer (respondent):** that the three-month temporary contracts had simply been terminated, that it did not know they were union leaders because it never received a leadership list from MOSALVY, that supervisors had warned them, and that it could not reinstate people who made trouble and caused the company loss, though it would pay severance under Articles 91, 92 and 99. [Para 72] [Para 79] [Para 83] [Para 84] [Para 85]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer must reinstate all seven dismissed employees from the date the award takes effect, pay them the same wage, compensation and bonus as other employees received during the dismissal period, and give each of them a written notice of misconduct; if any of them commits further misconduct within one year of reinstatement the employer may dismiss without warning. [Para 108] [Para 109]
On the contract status the panel held, under Article 68 of the 1997 Labour Law, that a temporary contract may run one month for normal workers, two months for skilled workers and three months for employees, and that sewing and cutting are manual tasks requiring training and skill, so these workers are "expert employees" whose temporary contracts were limited to two months. [Para 85] [Para 86] [Para 88] [Para 90] [Para 91] Six of the seven had served more than two months when dismissed, so their temporary period had lapsed and the employer had to give an appropriate reason, which it did not do; even for the seventh, still inside his contract, dismissal for union membership or union activity was forbidden. [Para 91] [Para 92] [Para 93] That dismissal failed to comply with Articles 12 and 279. [Para 94]
The panel also found the strikes unlawful — no prior notice as required by Article 324 and no attempt to settle peacefully as required by Article 320 paragraph 4 — and attributed that fault to the employees. [Para 94] [Para 95] [Para 96] Nonetheless, Article 26 bars any disciplinary sanction for misconduct the employer has known of for more than fifteen days: the employer knew of the strikes from November 12 and 15, 2003 and dismissed on December 13, 2003. [Para 97] [Para 98] Article 27 requires the sanction to be proportional, and the employer produced no document showing what the workers were warned about, or any specific misconduct inside the fifteen days before dismissal, so dismissal of the six was disproportionate. [Para 99] [Para 100] [Para 101] [Para 102] [Para 103]
Finally, Article 4 paragraph 1 of Prakas No. 305/MOSALVY dated November 22, 2001 gives founding members and those who join during the application period the same protection as shop stewards, from the filing of the application until 30 days after registration; the union applied on December 11, 2003 and the seven were founding members, so they could not be dismissed without the Labour Inspector's agreement. [Para 103] [Para 105] [Para 106] [Para 107]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker whose two-month skilled temporary contract has expired, or who is a founding union member during the protected window between filing and 30 days after registration, cannot be dismissed without the Labour Inspector's agreement and without a stated reason — a temporary contract does not license dismissal for union activity. Where the employer relies on misconduct, the fifteen-day bar in Article 26 and the proportionality requirement in Article 27 mean a sanction imposed more than fifteen days after the employer knew of the conduct, and unsupported by any written warning, is invalid and reinstatement follows. [Para 92] [Para 97] [Para 99] [Para 106]

**Obiter dicta:** that the employees' own unlawful strike was a fault on their side; and that, until the factory adopts internal rules, the parties must observe the code of conduct the panel laid down, including progressive verbal warnings and seven days' strike notice to MOSALVY, the Labour Inspector and the employer. [Para 94] [Para 111] [Para 112] [Para 113]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The dismissal is dated December 13, 2003 in the facts but December 12, 2003 in the reasoning. [Para 67] [Para 91] The narrative says the company agreed to keep three of the seven and dismissed five others, which conflicts with the finding that seven were dismissed. [Para 59] [Para 67] The company appears as Standard Garment Co., Ltd, Standards Co., Ltd and Standard Co., Ltd; the reasoning is numbered irregularly (1- to 7-, the fourth reason printed twice) and "th" is dropped throughout ("themployer"). [Para 1] [Para 28] [Para 85] [Para 97] Individual employees and union officers are reduced to initials per convention.
