# Award No. 67/04 — Jasca Garments (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 14 August 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Koy Neam presiding; Ms You Suonty appointed by the employer; Mr Tuon Siphann appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 139336

## 1. Facts
Jasca Garments (Cambodia) Ltd sits on National Road #4, Ang Snoul District, Kandal Province, and employs 2,500 workers. [Para 25][Para 26] Two federations, Solidarity and Khmer, asked for shop steward elections; the company consulted them on 22 July 2004, set no date, then held the ballot on 7 August 2004 without the CCAWDU Federation, which held members in the factory but had only a receipt of its registration application. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 34][Para 30] On 9 August 2004 more than 700 workers struck over those elections, and the dispute reached the panel on a non-conciliation report. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 7]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the CCAWDU Federation was deprived of its right to participate in the shop steward elections of 7 August 2004 because it was not yet registered, and if so how that interference should be remedied? [Para 6][Para 21][Para 80]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the federation held a receipt of application issued by the Ministry, which under Article 268 paragraph 2 of the Labour Law has the same value as registration once two months pass without a reply; the company refused its candidates, let the two rival federations veto it, and refused a one-day delay although the 45-day window ran to 5 September 2004. [Para 74][Para 78][Para 91]
**Employer (respondent):** it filed no claim of its own and would follow whatever the panel decided, while explaining that it rejected participation because the federation was unregistered, that the rival federations objected, and that the candidate list had already gone to the provincial labour office. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 81][Para 83]
**Third parties (Solidarity and Khmer Federations):** the results of 7 August 2004 should stand, since an application receipt has no validity for election purposes. [Para 76][Para 78]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — Jasca Garments (Cambodia) Ltd must organize new elections for shop stewards allowing the CCAWDU Federation to participate on equal footing with the other two unions, in accordance with the law and Prakas 286/01 of MoSALVY. [Para 101]
On jurisdiction the panel adopted its reasoning in award 66/04 (Winner Garment): Article 298 of the Labour Law refers election disputes to the Labour Court but does not reserve them exclusively, unlike Article 337, which gives the courts sole power over the legality of strikes; Article 12 of Prakas 99/04 requires the panel to resolve collective disputes under Article 309, and both parties accepted binding arbitration at the hearing. [Para 8][Para 10][Para 12][Para 14][Para 16][Para 18] The panel framed its task narrowly as whether the right to participate had been exercised, not whether the ballot was valid. [Para 19][Para 20]
On the merits the panel applied Article 268 of the Labour Law: where the Ministry does not answer a registration application within two months, the organization is deemed registered. [Para 84] The receipt issued on 27 May 2004 had run two months and eight days by 2 August 2004, and an administrative extension noted on the receipt could not cost the federation its rights. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 87] The company's ignorance of that rule was excused as such, but in doubt it should have taken advice or delayed the vote. [Para 88][Para 89]
The panel then held the refusal to postpone unjustified: Article 292 allows 45 days from the request of 22 July 2004, so elections could lawfully have been held until 5 September 2004, and no rule makes sending a candidate list to the provincial office a bar, since Article 4 of Prakas 286/01 requires only that minutes be sent to the Department of Labour Inspection. [Para 90][Para 91][Para 92] It also held the company could not delegate the decision to the rival federations, which were parties to the dispute: the employer alone organizes elections under Article 292, and where no union has representative status — as the Department of Labour Inspection confirmed for Jasca under Article 277 — Articles 288 and 290 and Article 2 of Prakas 286/01 entitle all unions with members to nominate candidates. [Para 94][Para 95][Para 97][Para 98][Para 99] No union may decide that another may not stand, and equity barred deciding the question with two interested rivals. [Para 99] The remedy was equal treatment in a fresh ballot. [Para 100]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that has filed a registration application and received a receipt is deemed registered once two months pass without a Ministry reply under Article 268 of the Labour Law, and an employer that excludes it from shop steward elections on the ground of non-registration interferes with a recognized right. [Para 84][Para 87][Para 99] Where no union at the enterprise holds representative status, every union with members may nominate candidates, and the employer may not let rival unions veto a participant; the remedy is a fresh election on equal footing. [Para 95][Para 98][Para 101]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel said it need not decide whether the 7 August elections were valid, only whether the right to participate had been exercised, and it declined to treat the company's lack of legal knowledge as blameworthy in itself, requiring only that it seek advice or delay. [Para 19][Para 88][Para 89]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The registration receipt is dated 27 May 2004 at para 85 but 24 May 2004 at para 74, and the receipt is said at para 41 to have been issued by the Department of Labour Inspection. [Para 85][Para 74][Para 41] The claimant is the "Coalition of Cambodian Democratic Apparel Workers Unions" on the face of the award but the "Federation of Cambodian Democratic Apparel Workers Unions" in the case summary. [Para 1][Para 26][Para 29] The case number prints as "67104" and the Prakas as "99104" for 67/04 and 99/04. [Para 1][Para 14] The election notice is posted 27 July 2004 at para 34, 29 July 2004 at para 62 and 22 July 2004 at para 59. [Para 34][Para 59][Para 62] The 45-day period is calculated from the 22 July 2004 consultations although the Solidarity request is dated 12 July 2004. [Para 32][Para 91] The human resources director appears as "Taan Bun Meng" and "Tan Bun Meng", a company representative as "Chun Yit Phong" and "Chun Yit Thung", and the union president as "Sem Vanna" and "Soem Vanna". [Para 3][Para 24][Para 5][Para 39] Paras 12 and 13, and paras 15 and 16, repeat their own text, and para 99 breaks off mid-sentence before resuming. [Para 12][Para 15][Para 99]
