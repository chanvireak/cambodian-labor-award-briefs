# Award No. 95/04 — A.S.D Co., Ltd (formerly Douson Cambodia Co., Ltd) (Arbitration Council, 21 December 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry, chosen by the employer party; An Nan, chosen by the worker party; Ang Eng Thong, chair) · Doc ID 139352

## 1. Facts
A.S.D Co., Ltd, at Ang Keo village, Kantok commune, Ang Snoul district, Kandal, employed 794 workers; it had bought the factory and taken over the name of Douson Cambodia Co., Ltd while keeping the same administrators, managers, workplace and seniority. [Para 31][Para 51][Para 74] From October 2004 the company suspended work section by section for want of purchase orders, each time asking the Kandal Labour Department for approval: 17 workers in back cutting, then 79 in ironing, then 25 in finishing, then the same 17 back-cutting workers again, whose suspension ran three consecutive months. [Para 41][Para 43][Para 45][Para 47][Para 48] In practice the company paid only 75 percent of basic wages for a half day worked, and US$5 monthly housing allowance during suspension. [Para 35][Para 36] A nine-issue complaint by the union was conciliated on 11 November 2004, five issues being settled. [Para 32][Para 33]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay 100 percent of wages for a day on which the worker has already worked a half day, and 50 percent of wages with other benefits when there is no work for more than one day? [Para 17][Para 18]
2. Whether the employer must reinstate the workers it suspended and pay them 100 percent of wages and benefits? [Para 19]
3. Whether the employer discriminated against the union in deciding whom to suspend? [Para 19][Para 20]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** half days worked must be paid in full, and when there is no work the company must pay 50 percent of wages plus benefits; the suspended workers must be reinstated on full wages, and the pattern of suspensions — most back-cutting workers being union leaders and members — shows anti-union discrimination. [Para 17][Para 18][Para 19][Para 89]
**Employer (respondent):** it may pay 75 percent for a half day and follows the Labour Law when work runs out; it never discriminated against any union, the cause being a serious economic crisis and lack of orders, and the back-cutting workers, unlike the front section, are skilled only in cutting. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20][Para 90]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered that a half day worked be paid at full wages; that during a lawful suspension wages and benefits follow the agreement of 13 October 2003, so the 79 ironing workers and 25 finishing workers receive 50 percent of basic wages for their suspension periods, payable on the first pay day after the award takes effect; that the 17 back-cutting workers be reinstated immediately; and that those 17 receive 50 percent of basic wages from 4 October 2004 to 4 December 2004 and full wages thereafter. [Para 94][Para 95][Para 96]

*Jurisdiction.* The Kandal Department approved each suspension under Article 71(11), which allows suspension for serious economic or material difficulty and caps it at two months under the Labour Inspector's control. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 55] The panel held that nothing in the Labour Law ousts its jurisdiction, that the Inspector's control is an additional protection for workers and cannot be read as excluding the panel, and that it may therefore decide whether a suspension was lawful, without reviewing the Inspector's decision as such. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 62][Para 63]

*Wages.* Performance of the contract is reciprocal: the worker owes the agreed hours and the employer owes wages under Article 117, and only a lawful suspension under Chapter 4 Section 2 relieves the employer, who must then provide accommodation unless a collective agreement or other agreement gives more. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 68] Absent a legal suspension the employer owes full wages for the period without work, and full wages for a day on which the worker has worked a half day. [Para 67][Para 69][Para 94]

*The agreement and the change of owner.* The second and third suspensions were lawful in form, so the only live question was the wage rate. The workers relied on point 19 of the conciliation minutes of 13 October 2003, and the employer answered that Douson, not A.S.D, had agreed it. [Para 70][Para 71][Para 73] Article 87 provides that a change in the employer's legal status, including sale, leaves all labour contracts in effect binding on the new employer and the workers [Para 76][Para 77]; Article 307 gives a conciliation agreement signed by the parties and visaed by the conciliator the force of a collective agreement [Para 78]; and Articles 65, 96, 13 and 98 allow such an agreement to be more favourable than the law and to void less favourable contract terms. [Para 80][Para 81][Para 83] The panel therefore held the minutes of 13 October 2003 still in effect after the change from Douson to A.S.D, binding the new owner. [Para 85][Para 87]

*The 17 back-cutting workers.* The panel found no sufficient basis to hold the suspensions discriminatory, since union leaders also worked in that section and the skill difference explained the selection. [Para 90][Para 91] But Article 71(11) permits no suspension longer than two months, and the agreement of 13 October 2003 says nothing about a stoppage of more than two months; the two consecutive suspensions of three months were therefore unlawful, and the panel ordered reinstatement with 50 percent of wages for the first two months and full wages thereafter. [Para 92][Para 93][Para 94][Para 96]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of the labour contract under Article 71(11) is valid only for up to two months and only for the period the Labour Inspector has approved; beyond that the employer must pay full wages, and if it wants to pay less it must point to a conciliation agreement or collective agreement that so provides. Such an agreement, visaed by the conciliator, survives a sale of the enterprise and binds the purchaser under Article 87, because a collective agreement has at least the force of the individual employment contracts it supplements. [Para 62][Para 87][Para 92][Para 96]

**Obiter dicta:** the remarks that the Inspector's control over a suspension is an extra protection for workers which does not exclude the Council's jurisdiction, and that a dispute over an Article 71(7) internal-rules suspension would plainly be within it [Para 58][Para 60], are propositions of competence, not steps needed to fix the amounts ordered.

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The non-conciliation report is dated 26 October 2004 and said to have been submitted on 27 October 2004 [Para 22], before the complaint and conciliation of 11 November 2004 [Para 32][Para 33]; a separate Kandal report dated 15 November 2004 also appears in the file [Para 31].
- Nine issues were complained of and four left unresolved [Para 33], yet three appear in the statement of issues, the third being dropped by agreement [Para 52].
- Point 19 of the 13 October 2003 minutes is quoted three ways: minimum wage plus benefits for a short stoppage and 50 percent of the minimum wage beyond one month [Para 37]; 50 percent of wages plus benefits under Notification 17/00 for a stoppage of less than one month [Para 71][Para 93].
- The length and dates of the first and fourth suspensions conflict: "two months from 4 November 2004 to 4 January 2005" alongside "three consecutive months" [Para 48]; "from 4 September 2004 to 4 January 2005" [Para 89]; and 4 October 2004 to 4 December 2004 in the order, which also ends "to the date of the suspension" rather than reinstatement [Para 96].
- The approval of the first suspension is a letter "without a date" in October 2004 [Para 42].
- The company appears as "A.S.D Co., Ltd.", "A.S.D Cambodia company" and "A.S.Director", the former owner as both "Douson Cambodia Co., Ltd." and "Douson Ltd." [Para 51]; the union side is variously the Cambodian Garment Textile Industry Union [Para 2], the National Independent Federation Textile Union of Cambodia [Para 31] and the Cambodian Garment Textile Industry Federation [Para 50], and one shop steward appears as "Suon Sokunthea" [Para 10] and "Suon Kunthea" [Para 41].
- The award bears only a file number ("Case: 95/04"), no award number; the file number is used here. Union and worker names appear as initials.
