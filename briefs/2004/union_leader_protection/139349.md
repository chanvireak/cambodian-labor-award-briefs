# Award No. 91/04 — Honey Wear Garment v Cambodian Garment Industry Union (Arbitration Council, 22 November 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambona, chosen by the employer party; Liv Sovanna, chosen by the worker party; Kong Phallack, chair) · Doc ID 139349

## 1. Facts
Honey Wear Garment, a factory of about 400 employees at Angkeo Village, Kantork quarter, Angsnuol district, Kandal, suspended Ms S — its acting union president and a workers' representative — by notification dated 22 October 2004, and on the same day asked the Kandal Department of Labour for permission to dismiss her for serious misconduct, namely insulting four co-workers under Article 83 of the Labour Law. [Para 25][Para 44][Para 45][Para 50] The company did not call her in to answer the accusations before acting. [Para 47] The workers struck on 25 October 2004; the Council ordered them back to work on 27 October 2004, and on 5 November 2004 the Labour Inspector issued decision no. 268 allowing the dismissal. [Para 32][Para 39][Para 72]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the Labour Inspector's decision no. 268 authorising the dismissal removed the case from the panel's jurisdiction? [Para 78][Para 79][Para 87]
2. Whether the dismissal of a single union leader was an individual or a collective dispute? [Para 102][Para 103]
3. Whether Ms S committed serious misconduct justifying dismissal? [Para 74][Para 75][Para 115]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the union demanded her reinstatement and ended conciliation when the employer refused that first point; she denied insulting or intimidating any worker on 6, 7, 8 and 15 October 2004, admitted only a joking "gate keeping dog!" on 18 October to a friend who took no offence, and said she was never told who accused her and so could not defend herself. [Para 13][Para 27][Para 52][Para 55][Para 69]
**Employer (respondent):** it had already suspended her and sought her dismissal for serious misconduct within Article 83, and refused to name the plaintiffs and witnesses, or produce their complaint, for the sake of those persons' security. [Para 31][Para 71][Para 72]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel held that it had jurisdiction over case 91/04 and ordered the company to accept Ms S back to work immediately after the order came into force; no compensation was awarded. [Para 132][Para 133]

*Jurisdiction.* Article 295 lets a company director suspend a shop steward or union leader immediately in a case of serious misconduct while awaiting the Labour Inspector's decision, and Article 293 makes termination of such a person conditional on the Inspector's permission, with a two-month right of appeal to the Minister. [Para 73][Para 80][Para 84] The panel reasoned that once the Inspector approves the dismissal the leader loses the statutory immunity and becomes an ordinary worker, so the panel keeps jurisdiction to weigh the facts and decide whether the employer had sufficient evidence to terminate — while declining to review the Inspector's decision or procedure, which belongs to the Ministry and, on appeal, to the court. [Para 92][Para 94][Para 96][Para 100][Para 101] Award 78/04 was distinguished because there the Inspector had not yet approved the dismissal. [Para 102]

*Collective or individual.* Testing the dispute against Article 302, the panel found (a) a dispute of some employees with one employer, since many employees struck to demand her reinstatement, and (b) a dispute over the exercise of recognised rights of professional organisations, since she was the union's acting president and the dispute affected the enterprise's operation. It therefore treated a one-worker dismissal as a collective dispute. [Para 105][Para 108][Para 110][Para 114]

*The misconduct.* The employer refused, despite the panel's repeated orders, to identify the four complainants, produce their complaint file or let them appear to prove the charge, so the accusation could not be tested. [Para 123][Para 126] The panel applied its approach in Awards 10/03 (Jacques Shintex) and 25/03 (Advance) that a party which fails to provide evidence risks an adverse decision, and found the accusation of serious misconduct unfounded, unreasonable and unjustified. [Para 127][Para 128][Para 130]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
When the Labour Inspector authorises the dismissal of a shop steward or union leader, the statutory immunity ends but the arbitration panel does not lose the case: the dismissed person is then an ordinary worker, and the panel will decide whether the employer actually held sufficient evidence of serious misconduct, leaving the Inspector's own decision to the Ministry and the courts. An employer that accuses a worker of misconduct yet withholds the complainants, the complaint and the witnesses cannot establish serious misconduct, and the panel will draw an adverse inference from the withheld evidence. [Para 92][Para 96][Para 127]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that Articles 293–295 exist to protect union leaders from discrimination and abuse, by an immunity comparable to that of deputies and senators, and its remark that letter no. 268 was inconsistent with Articles 293 and 295, are general observations: it expressly refused to review that decision. [Para 83][Para 89][Para 91][Para 100]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The OCR drops the letters "th" throughout — "themployer", "thearing", "withemployer" — so quoted statutory language had to be read through the corruption.
- The charge is cited as Article 83 (B) (4) at [Para 117] but as "point 4 of Article 83" at [Para 83]; both were reported as printed.
- The second hearing is given as 6 November 2004 at 8:30 a.m. at [Para 18] and as 8:00 a.m. at [Para 35] and [Para 36].
- A sentence recording what the parties agreed at the first hearing is truncated to "THIS AWARD IS NOT IMMEDIATELY" at [Para 25]; the content of the agreement is lost.
- Strike facts conflict: the panel finds at [Para 34] that the employees had not stopped striking, while the parties state at [Para 37] that they had stopped and returned to work, except Ms S.
- Names are broken across lines in the party lists and the signature block: "Tuy Rorng" at [Para 55] appears as "Tuy Rarng" at [Para 12], the union address is garbled as "lassess # 60A" at [Para 13], and the award carries only a file number ("Case: 91/04"), no award number, which is used here as the award number.
- The dismissed worker and the union officers are named on the face of the award; per the briefing convention they appear here as Ms S, with no name invented or expanded.
