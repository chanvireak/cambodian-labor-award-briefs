# Award No. 70/04 — Hanna Cambodia Factory (Arbitration Council, 13 September 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (You Suonty, employer-appointed; Tuon Siphann, worker-appointed; Men Nimmith, chair) · heard as non-binding award by the parties' agreement · Doc ID 139340

## 1. Facts
Hanna Cambodia Factory, a hat maker on National Road #4 in Ang Snuol District, Kandal Province, with about 700 workers and a litigious history since it began business on 26 June 2000, suspended two union representatives, Mr C (deputy president) and Ms N (secretary), on 14 August 2004 after they told other workers that the administration had refused a proper leave slip for a sick worker, Ms K. The workers struck from 14 to 19 August 2004 without prior notice, during five conciliation attempts between 29 June and 19 August 2004; the non-conciliation report #155 (MOLVT) of 19 August 2004 left two points unresolved, and at the hearing of 25 August 2004 both sides added issues and agreed the award would be non-binding. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 14][Para 15][Para 30]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must allow the suspended worker representatives, Mr C and Ms N, to return to work? [Para 1]
2. Whether the employer must dismiss Mr L, the general/administration manager? [Para 2]
3. Whether, if the manager is not dismissed, the employer must pay workers their wages and regular-attendance bonuses for the days of the strike, 14 to 18 August 2004? [Para 3][Para 15]
4. Whether the employer must cease discriminating against workers involved in the strike? [Para 3]
5. Whether the panel should permit the suspension of a further representative, Mr Y, order the dismissal of all three representatives, and award the employer US$230,000 in damages? [Para 4][Para 5][Para 6][Para 7]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The suspensions of 14 August 2004 were improper because neither representative committed any unlawful act; the strike was spontaneous, out of anger, and the two did not lead it; the manager's refusal to issue a leave slip workers could rely on caused it; the variation of strikers' work was a penalty; and the wages and US$5 bonus for 14 to 18 August were owed. [Para 17][Para 24][Para 27][Para 34][Para 35]
**Employer (respondent):** The two were troublemakers who spread information about the sick-leave request although the director had already signed and the leave was approved, provoking an illegal strike that contradicted earlier negotiations; the suspension rested on its internal rules and Article 83; the workers cannot force the dismissal of its own manager, since only the parties to a contract may terminate it; the work changes were discipline for slow or careless work after two or three oral warnings, not discrimination; and the unnotified strike forced air freight and cost it US$230,000 (two hundred and thirty thousands dollars). [Para 24][Para 26][Para 27][Para 37][Para 38][Para 40][Para 42]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to end the suspensions of Mr C and Ms N and accept them back to work immediately when the award came into force; the claim to dismiss the general manager/director of administration was rejected, the two representatives being considered to have received written notice of their misconduct in inciting other workers to strike; the issue-5 requests were rejected for want of jurisdiction; the discrimination complaint was rejected; and the claim for strike-day wages and attendance bonuses was rejected. [Para 76][Para 77]

On issues 1 and 2 the panel took Article 71(7), which allows suspension of the contract where an employee is temporarily laid off for a valid reason in accordance with internal rules. The employer's internal rules contain no provision for suspension at all, and Article 10(B-5) of those rules requires a written warning where a worker leads others into unlawful action, with termination only if there is no improvement. So even accepting serious misconduct, the response had to follow those rules, which require a written warning before dismissal. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46][Para 47]

The panel then tested the misconduct itself under Article 83 paragraph B(5), which makes "inciting other workers to commit acts of serious misconduct" serious misconduct. Taking "incite" from the Khmer dictionary of Samdach Chuon Nath as speaking provocatively so as to inflame anger, including spreading false information, and relying on the inquiry evidence of some 20 team leaders, it found the two representatives did incite the other workers to strike illegally. But the Labour Law nowhere states that a strike is serious misconduct, so this was not incitement to serious misconduct and could not ground termination. [Para 47][Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 54][Para 55]

On the dismissal demand the panel followed its Awards in cases 17/03, 04/03 and 14/03: Article 65 makes the employment contract a matter between employer and employee, so hiring and firing is the employer's and that employee's prerogative. The manager's carelessness over permission slips was not serious misconduct under Article 83, and the panel could order transfer or dismissal only where the employee presented a danger to others in the factory, which it did not find. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 59][Para 60][Para 61]

On issue 3 the panel held the strike unlawful: Article 324 requires prior notice of at least seven working days filed with the enterprise, and Article 320 paragraph 4 allows the right to strike only once all peaceful methods of settlement have been tried, including conciliation and arbitration; this strike met none of these. Under Article 332 a strike suspends the contract and salary is not paid, so no wages were owed even for a lawful strike. On the US$5 bonus, following six earlier awards including Lida Garment (04/03) and Eternity (71/04), the panel held the bonus under Notice 017/2000 (MOSALVY) of 18 July 2000 belongs to workers who work regularly the days required in a month; unlawful strikers have not, whereas for lawful strikers deduction is an unlawful punishment under Article 333 and the bonus is paid pro rata. [Para 62][Para 65][Para 66][Para 67][Para 68][Para 70]

The jurisdictional split turned on Article 312 paragraph 1, which confines the panel to issues in the non-conciliation report and issues directly related that arise after it. Issue 4 concerned the employer's conduct after the 19 August 2004 report and arose directly from the strike, so the panel had jurisdiction, but on the facts it found no penalty or discrimination, only discipline of workers who had worked slowly or against instructions after warnings. Issue 5 concerned the workers' conduct during the strike, which the employer knew of and should have raised at conciliation; not having done so, it fell outside Article 312. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 73][Para 75][Para 76]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of a worker representative cannot stand where the employer's internal rules provide no suspension mechanism and require a written warning before any termination, even if the underlying misconduct is proven; and incitement of other workers to strike, though it may be established as incitement, is not incitement to "serious misconduct" under Article 83 B(5) of the Labour Law because a strike is not classified there as serious misconduct, so it cannot justify dismissal. [Para 45][Para 47][Para 53][Para 54][Para 55]

**Obiter dicta:** That lawful strikers retain a pro-rata right to the US$5 attendance bonus and its deduction is an unlawful punishment under Article 333; and that an employer's variation of work after oral warnings is discipline rather than a strike penalty. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 38]
