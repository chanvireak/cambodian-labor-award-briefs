# Award No. 36/04 — Phnom Penh Garment City Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 June 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators: Mar Sambona (employer party), Toun Siphann (worker party), Kong Phallak (chair) · Doc ID 139319

## 1. Facts
Phnom Penh Garment City Ltd, Meanchey district, employed about 850 workers. On 16 May 2004 a fire broke out about 200 metres from the factory; some workers helped put it out without the management's prior permission and were refused re-entry until after lunch. On 17 and 18 May the workers struck, and from 7:00am on 18 May strikers switched off the electricity on the first, second and third floors. On 25 May 2004 the company dismissed six union committee members and suspended six union leaders and assistant shop stewards pending approval of their dismissal. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 14][Para 18][Para 145]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the dismissal of six union committee members and the suspension, in anticipation of dismissal, of six union leaders and assistant shop stewards — all for switching off the factory's electricity and inciting others to stop work on 18 May 2004 — was lawful, and whether the twelve must be reinstated? [Para 1][Para 19]
2. Whether the workers' demand that the employer's administrative chief (Mr D) be dismissed should be granted? [Para 1][Para 163]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The accusation and dismissal were unlawful: the conduct complained of on 17 and 18 May had already been settled by the agreement of 19 May, and no rule said to be broken was ever specified. Even if they were at fault, the employer had only suspended them on 25 May and so had lost the right to dismiss under Article 26 of the Labour Law. [Para 21][Para 50][Para 52]

**Employer (respondent):** Mr D, the administrative chief, maintained that the twelve switched off electricity repeatedly, blocked the cabin with cartons, pushed security aside, threatened and incited other workers to stop work, and that this was serious misconduct under Article 83(3), (4) and (5); no witness to the events appeared at the hearings. [Para 24][Para 53][Para 57]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Partly granted — the company must reinstate all twelve workers as soon as the award comes into force; pay full wages and other benefits to the five workers not shown to have committed misconduct from 25 May 2004 until reinstatement (no sum is quantified in the award); pay no wages for that period to the seven found to have switched off electricity or kicked the basket; and the award itself is to count as a second written warning under Article 10 of the Internal Work Rules. [Para 208][Para 209][Para 210][Para 211]

On the first issue the panel held that a strike must be peaceful under Article 330 and follow the Chapter 13 procedure, and that cutting the factory's electricity interrupts the whole production process and is a form of violence — serious misconduct open to dismissal. It found that six workers had in fact switched off the electricity, one of them (the union president, Mr V) at 7am, and that the employer therefore had a substantive ground. The dismissal nevertheless failed on timing: Article 26 gives seven calendar days from the day the employer learns of the facts, the seventh day falling on a Sunday being carried to Monday. The dismissal and suspension letters were dated Tuesday 25 May 2004, the eighth day after the employer knew, so the right to dismiss had lapsed; the workers returned, with a written warning substituted. [Para 172][Para 179][Para 180][Para 183][Para 184][Para 187]

On the second head, the assistant shop steward Mr H did not switch off electricity but deliberately kicked a cloth basket weighing 2–3 kilograms into Mr D's leg and swore at him; under Article 83(B)(4) that too was serious misconduct, yet the suspension letter of the same date attracted the same time-bar and he was reinstated on a written warning. [Para 189][Para 191][Para 193][Para 198]

On the third head, five workers were accused on the strength of verbal messages from security and fellow workers; the panel summoned the witnesses, the employer raised safety concerns and none attended, and the accusations were denied. The panel held the five had done no more than join the strike as hundreds of others had, which is not misconduct under Article 83(B), so no suspension or layoff could stand and their wages ran from 25 May 2004. [Para 201][Para 205][Para 206][Para 207][Para 209]

The demand concerning Mr D was dropped by the employee party at the hearing of 12 May 2004 and was not decided. [Para 163]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where serious misconduct is proved, the employer still forfeits the power to dismiss if the disciplinary decision is taken more than seven calendar days after he learned of the facts (fifteen days for measures short of dismissal), the count being of calendar days and a seventh day falling on Sunday carrying over to Monday; the dismissal then fails, the worker is reinstated, and the proved misconduct may be recorded only as a written warning under the internal rules. [Para 179][Para 180][Para 187]

**Obiter dicta:** That interference with a factory's electricity supply may in a serious case cause fire or turmoil throughout the factory, and that the right to strike subsists provided Chapter 13 notification and conciliation are observed and the strike stays peaceful. [Para 172][Para 177]
