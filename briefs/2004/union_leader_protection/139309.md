# Award No. 27/04 — MS International Garment Limited v CCAWDU (Arbitration Council, 16 June 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Hem Huor Naryth, employer-chosen; Vuong Vanna, worker-chosen; Men Nimmith, chair) · Doc ID 139309

## 1. Facts
MS International Garment Limited ("MS") ran a garment factory at #68 New Road, Tuol Sangkae, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, employing 380 workers and entered on the commercial register on 2 February 2000. [Para 17][Para 18] Falling orders led it to suspend production and terminate its entire workforce on 9 March 2004; it then changed its name to BC (Cambodia) on 24 April 2004 while keeping the MS statutes in force, and leased the premises to Cambo Fashion, which restarted manufacture without taking back some 200 former workers, among them the union leadership. [Para 21][Para 23][Para 24] Three points went un-conciliated: the wage base for termination indemnity, the rehiring of union leaders and old workers, and unpaid annual leave for 1999–2001. [Para 8][Para 10]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the Art. 89 dismissal indemnity must be calculated on the last month in which the workers had full work or on average earnings over 12 months, rather than on the basic salary of US$45? [Para 8][Para 33]
2. Whether the employer must reinstate the union leaders and former workers at the factory now operating on the old MS premises? [Para 9][Para 34]
3. Whether the employer must compensate annual leave accrued in 1999, 2000 and 2001, despite the three-year limits invoked under Arts. 167(4) and 120? [Para 10][Para 41]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the indemnity base should be the last month of full-time work, or the 12-month average; the operating factory must take back the union leaders and old workers; and leave never granted in 1999–2001 is still owed. [Para 8][Para 34][Para 41]
**Employer (respondent):** the basic salary of US$45 should be used because the company had not operated for three months, having closed from 1 January 2004, and the new company has nothing to do with MS; the 1999–2001 leave is time-barred, and on 2 May 2003 the parties agreed leave would run only from 2002. [Para 9][Para 11][Para 41]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered that any Art. 89 indemnity be computed as total gross wages including overtime and bonuses over the 12 months before dismissal, divided by 12, divided by 26, multiplied by the days of indemnity owed; rejected the demand for reinstatement of the union leaders; and ordered compensation for the full amount of unused annual leave at 1.5 days per month for the whole period of employment with MS, on the same 12-month earnings formula. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50]

On issue 1, Arts. 102 and 103 define wages as all remuneration for employment or service, including overtime and bonuses, so reading "wages and fringe benefits" in Art. 89 as the minimum wage was wrong; Art. 168, which fixes leave pay on average earnings over 12 months, supplied the method. [Para 33][Para 34]

On issue 2, Art. 95(5) gives workers dismissed for economic reasons a two-year priority of re-employment, but the claim had to be brought against the company then employing workers at the premises and had to show that company was a successor under Art. 87. The owner testified that neither he, MS nor BC employed anyone there and that he held no shareholding, directorship or management post in Cambo Fashion; documentary support was lacking, yet the panel found him a credible witness, so the claim failed. [Para 35][Para 38][Para 40][Para 41]

On issue 3, Art. 167(2) makes pay in lieu of untaken leave due only when the contract is terminated or expires, so the Art. 120 three-year limitation runs from the termination of 9 March 2004 and not from the year the leave accrued. Art. 167(4)'s cap on deferral presupposes an agreement to defer, and none arose here because annual leave was simply not practised in the enterprise. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 47][Para 48]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
"Wages and fringe benefits" in Art. 89 means actual gross earnings, overtime and bonuses included, averaged over the 12 months before dismissal under Art. 168 — never the statutory minimum wage; and because leave pay falls due on termination under Art. 167(2), the Art. 120 limitation runs from termination and the Art. 167(4) deferral cap does not bar leave that the employer never granted at all. [Para 33][Para 42][Para 48]

**Obiter dicta:** Art. 167 is protective legislation, as Art. 363's fine of 31 to 60 times the base daily wage for breach shows; and a re-employment claim under Art. 95(5) must be pressed against the current employer as an Art. 87 successor. [Para 44][Para 40]

Individual union officials and the worker-party address numeral are garbled beyond reliable reading.
