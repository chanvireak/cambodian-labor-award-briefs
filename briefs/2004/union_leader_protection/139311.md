# Award No. 28/04 — Raffles Grand Hotel D'Angkor v Union of Grand Hotel D'Angkor (Arbitration Council, 11 August 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, employer-chosen; An Nan, worker-chosen, dissenting in part; Run Saray, chair) · Doc ID 139311

## 1. Facts
About 200 of the hotel's workers struck from 5 April 2004; on 8 April 2004 the Siem Reap court issued judgment No. 20B declaring the strike illegal and ordering the strikers back to work within 48 hours. [Para 21][Para 22] The employer set up a registration point staffed by its security contractor behind a police cordon, then on 16 April 2004 dismissed 195 employees — 107 permanent and 88 temporary, including union leaders and workers on leave — for serious misconduct in failing to return within the 48 hours. [Para 46][Para 65][Para 66] Within two weeks of the dismissals the hotel held elections for a new shop steward committee and signed a collective bargaining agreement with it. [Para 161] The union party withdrew its demands to enforce an earlier award of 27 January 2004 and its claim over strike damage, and added a challenge to the validity of that agreement. [Para 6]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate the employees dismissed on 16 April 2004 — some 220, comprising 195 strikers and 25 workers who were on leave — to their former positions? [Para 5][Para 37]
2. Whether the employer must pay those workers their wages and other benefits, and from what date? [Para 146][Para 147]
3. Whether the election of new shop stewards on 30 April 2004 and the collective bargaining agreement concluded with them are valid, given the union's representative status in the hotel? [Para 6][Para 151]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the strike was a lawful response to unmet demands; large numbers tried to return but were stopped by police and the hotel's guards, and those on leave took no part in the strike at all; the new committee was elected and the agreement signed to strip the representative union of its bargaining role. [Para 26][Para 37][Para 154]
**Employer (respondent):** the dismissals were lawful under Art. 337 because the workers did not return within 48 hours and had no valid reason; registration was a reasonable management measure pending investigation of damage to hotel property; and the new shop stewards and agreement were properly made. [Para 69][Para 86][Para 94]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must reinstate, in the same positions and roles, the 19 employees who registered within 48 hours (Annex A) with full wages and benefits from the date of registration; the 156 employees who were prevented from registering (Annex B) with wages from 11 April 2004; the 10 employees on leave for the whole strike, paid as non-strikers; and the three who struck and then went on leave, paid from the date their leave ended. [Para 163][Para 164][Para 165][Para 167] The demands of the remaining workers were rejected; unpaid wages and benefits owed to workers lawfully dismissed for serious misconduct must be paid within 48 hours of the award taking effect; and the agreement with the new shop stewards was declared invalid, the employer being barred from negotiating with them. [Para 167][Para 169][Para 170]

On the reinstatement issue the panel held that the 48 hours ran from 8 April 2004 at 5:00pm, but the judgment was only posted to the workers at 8:30am on 9 April, so the 15 and a half hours of ignorance were a valid reason and the deadline became 8:30am on 11 April 2004. [Para 74][Para 77][Para 78] Registration was a lawful exercise of management authority under Art. 2, but the employer also had to facilitate it and impose no unreasonable conditions. [Para 84][Para 86] Because some 250 armed police formed a cordon around the hotel and the registration table sat inside it, the 156 workers who testified that they tried to register were held to have a valid reason and not to have committed serious misconduct under Art. 337. [Para 100][Para 109][Para 110] Suspending registered workers without wages to await an investigation was not among the eleven grounds in Art. 71, and after more than three months it was unjust. [Para 97][Para 98][Para 99] By contrast, workers who never attempted to register because they read the judgment as ordering work rather than registration had no valid reason and were lawfully dismissed, as were those who filed no statement of events. [Para 114][Para 121][Para 122] Employees on leave throughout the strike were not strikers under Art. 318, and the three who struck before leave had a valid reason because the hotel's yellow access cards were withheld from them. [Para 124][Para 125][Para 144][Para 145]

On the third issue the panel held it had jurisdiction under Art. 312 as a matter arising after the non-conciliation report and directly consequent on the dispute. [Para 152][Para 154] Art. 288 requires shop stewards to be chosen from candidates nominated by unions with representative status, and the hotel never consulted the union, so the election was illegal. [Para 155][Para 157] Under Art. 96(2) an agreement may be made with shop stewards only where no representative union exists, and one did exist. [Para 158][Para 159]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Failure to return within 48 hours of a judgment declaring a strike illegal is serious misconduct only where the worker had no valid reason; physical prevention by a police cordon, or the employer's withholding of access cards, supplies that reason, so a dismissal resting on it is unlawful and draws reinstatement with wages from the date the impediment began. An employer may not elect a fresh shop-steward committee or sign an agreement with it to displace a union holding representative status: Art. 288 consultation is mandatory, and an agreement made in its absence is invalid. [Para 109][Para 145][Para 157][Para 169]

**Obiter dicta:** had the hotel placed its registration desk 50 to 100 metres outside the police line, the obstruction would not have arisen; suspension pending investigation is lawful only if wages continue to be paid monthly; and silence in the face of an order to file event statements invites an adverse inference, as in cases 10/03 and 25/03. [Para 116][Para 98][Para 121]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is dated 11 August 2004 at the head and 11 August 2004 at the signature, and the hearings ran 27 May, 9 June and 19 June 2004. The non-conciliation report is dated 11 May 2004 and submitted on 17 May 2004 at line 7, while line 36 has MoSALVY referring the dispute on 17 May by letter dated 13 May. Registration figures conflict: 19 registered in time at line 87, but 31 registered employees (18, 8 and 5 on successive days) at line 57. The number of employees on leave is ten, yet the list at line 122 runs to eleven entries with "Sim Nang" repeated. The challenged agreement is dated 8 June 2004 at line 6 but signed 1 May 2004 and registered 11 June 2004 at line 67, and the shop-steward election is 30 April 2004 at line 66 against a report of elections dated 4 May 2004 at line 19. The worker-chosen arbitrator is "An Nan" on the face of the award but "ORNAN" and "ANAN" in Annex C, and his dissent is directed to "point 5 above", the award's numbered order 5. OCR damage is heavy throughout ("the" to "he", "employees" to "mployees"), several worker names are mangled, and telephone numbers at lines 3 and 5 are partly lost.
