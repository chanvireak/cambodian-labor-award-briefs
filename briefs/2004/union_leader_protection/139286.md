# Award No. 30/03 — Cambodian Apparel Industry Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 19 January 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Hem Hour Naryth (employer-nominated), Tuon Siphann (worker-nominated), Matthew Rendall (chair) · Doc ID 139286

## 1. Facts
Cambodian Apparel Industry Co., Ltd, on National Road No. 4 in Kompong Speu province, employed around 416 workers. [Para 17][Para 18] On 12 December 2003 the company terminated three workers who were respectively president, vice-president and secretary of the Free Democracy Union; the union says the terminations followed its request of 15 October 2003 to negotiate working conditions. [Para 19][Para 32][Para 20] The workers struck on 22 December 2003 demanding reinstatement, and also challenged the deduction of 5,000 riel from wages for replacement ID cards. [Para 20][Para 22] The employer did not attend the hearing and submitted none of the documents the panel requested, so the award is not immediately binding. [Para 1][Para 16][Para 28]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the termination of the three union leaders was valid, and whether the employer must reinstate them? [Para 25][Para 40]
2. Whether the employer may deduct 5,000 riel from a worker's wage to make a new ID card replacing a lost or broken one? [Para 26][Para 46]

The panel added a further question at the hearing: whether the payment of US$700.00 per worker, which the employer said it made as consideration for the workers not being reinstated and not continuing to protest, was valid. [Para 31]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The three leaders were dismissed because they began union activity and asked to negotiate; the union asked for reinstatement and for the company to stop charging for new ID cards. [Para 20][Para 21] They pressed that cards are lost mostly at work because the cardholder clip and the laminated paper are of poor quality after a year's use, and asked for a necklace holder and an annual card change. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54][Para 56]
**Employer (respondent):** The employer did not appear and offered no argument on the terminations. [Para 1][Para 31] On the cards it relied on its practice of charging 5,000 riel; it produced no evidence of the real cost and gave workers no prior notice of the deduction. [Para 22][Para 35][Para 48] It maintained that it had paid US$700.00 to each of the three workers to settle their reinstatement demand. [Para 31][Para 34]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the terminations were found procedurally unlawful, but no reinstatement was ordered because the parties' settlement of 31 December 2003 was upheld; the employer must change ID cards annually at its own expense and provide necklace holders, and may charge no more than 1,000 riel for a lost card and 500 riel for a broken one. [Para 57][Para 58]

On issue 1(a) the panel applied Article 293 of the Labour Law, under which an employee representative may be terminated only with the labour inspector's permission, and Article 4 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, which extends that protection to union founders, members and leaders. [Para 40][Para 41] No document showed that permission had been sought or that the Article 293 procedure had been followed, so the dismissal did not comply with the law. [Para 42][Para 43]

On issue 1(b) the panel noted that the employer's first letter reached it only on the hearing date and that no worker thumb-printed or witness confirmed the agreement at the hearing. [Para 43] It nevertheless had a copy of the 31 December 2003 agreement, concluded in the presence of the Kompong Speu provincial governor, under which the employer paid US$700.00 to each worker and they gave up reinstatement and any further claim. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 44] Because that agreement fully settled the reinstatement demand, the panel declined to make any order beyond it — even though it had already found the termination unlawful. [Para 45][Para 46]

On issue 2 the panel read Article 127 of the Labour Law as allowing a deduction to compensate tools and equipment not returned, and treated an ID card as company property and a necessary tool of identification and attendance. [Para 46] The deduction must however equal only the cost of the lost property, and no evidence proved the real cost. [Para 47][Para 48] From the evidence the panel put a normal card at 600 riel plus 400 riel of administrative cost, hence 1,000 riel as the reasonable price of a lost card, and accepted the union's figure of 500 riel for a broken one. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51] Since cards failed through poor holders and annual wear, the employer must supply a necklace holder and renew cards yearly at its own cost. [Para 56][Para 57]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The dismissal of a union leader or elected workers' representative is unlawful unless the labour inspector's permission has first been obtained under Article 293 of the Labour Law, a protection Prakas No. 305 extends to union founders and members; but where the parties have later signed a settlement in which the workers accept compensation and abandon reinstatement, the panel will enforce that settlement and order nothing further. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 44][Para 46] An employer may charge a worker for a lost or broken ID card only up to the card's actual cost, must prove that cost, and must itself bear the annual renewal and the cost of a proper holder. [Para 47][Para 50][Para 58]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that factories using necklace holders and renewing cards yearly have a low rate of loss, and that the modest cost of doing so would not disappoint an employer that requires workers to carry a card. [Para 39][Para 57]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's pleadings are missing entirely because it was absent at the hearing, so its position is reconstructed from the recitals and the settlement document. [Para 1][Para 31] The award text is badly broken by OCR: "themployer", "withe", "athearing" and duplicated clauses recur throughout, and sentences are truncated ("the termination crease their regular-attendance bonus"), which obscures a claimed US$5.00 monthly attendance bonus that appears nowhere in the decision. [Para 4][Para 5] The signed settlement is attributed to "Tob Sokkhang" in one place and "Tob Sokkhlang" in another. [Para 44][Para 28] The card cost is stated as 1,000 riel for a lost card and 500 for a broken one in the order, after 600 riel plus 400 riel in the reasoning. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 58] Individual workers and union officers are reduced to initials here; the panel names are as printed. [Para 1][Para 58]
