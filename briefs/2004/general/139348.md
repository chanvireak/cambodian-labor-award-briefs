# Award No. 87/04 — Nobel (Arbitration Council, 12 November 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambona, Liv Sovanna, Ang Eng Thong chair) · not immediately binding · Doc ID 139348

## 1. Facts
The Nobel factory at #141 Street Chom Chao, Steung Meanchey, Phnom Penh, employed 670 workers. On 22 September 2004 the workers complained to the Meanchey Labour Inspection Department; conciliation produced agreement on six issues on 2 October 2004, leaving two. The remaining dispute concerned the shift hours imposed by an announcement of 11 September 2004 and a demand that the company dismiss its Chinese quality-control supervisor, Ms P, for violence and contempt towards workers. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 24][Para 10][Para 11]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must maintain the existing work shifts and hours, with work from 6:00 a.m. until 11:00 p.m., a lunch break from 11:00 a.m. to 11:30 a.m., and work from 11:30 a.m. to 2:00 p.m.? [Para 10]
2. Whether the company must dismiss the Chinese supervisor Ms P because she looked down on workers and committed violence towards them? [Para 11]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The shift change should not be imposed; and Ms P had thrown clothes at workers, knocked their heads with pens, pulled their heads, ignored their explanations and taken revenge by having workers fired, so they feared she would find a pretext to dismiss them. [Para 10][Para 35][Para 50]

**Employer (respondent):** It had announced new hours by notice of 11 September 2004; Ms P denied the accusations, said she shouted only when workers made mistakes in quality control and had been reproached by the company president for it, that she tapped heads with a pen in a friendly manner and threw clothes back only for re-checking, and that her conduct followed her duties. [Para 11][Para 36][Para 44][Para 53]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the demand to dismiss Ms P was rejected; the hours question had already been settled by agreement at the hearing, work from 6:00 a.m. to 11:00 a.m., lunch from 11:00 a.m. to 12:00 p.m., and work from 12:00 p.m. to 2:30 p.m. [Para 77][Para 27][Para 57]

On issue 1 the parties reached agreement during the hearing, so the panel recorded the agreed hours rather than deciding them. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 65]

On issue 2 the panel stated the governing rule: workers are not entitled to order or demand the dismissal of an employee unless they can provide evidence that the person is dangerous and can no longer be allowed to work in the company, such that allowing them would harm or cause chaos in the workplace, citing awards 04/03 (Lida), 14/03, 17/03, 18/03 (Chou Sing/Hing), 16/04 (Chou Sing), 15/04 (Lucky Zone), 52/04 (Shin Kham) and 73/04 (Genuine). [Para 66]

On the facts, the panel found Ms P was team leader of 50 quality controllers, that she habitually spoke too loudly and reproached workers in Chinese, that her hands moved violently when she was angry, and that she threw garments back at workers to re-check, which looked inappropriate. But the employees themselves admitted that neither the throwing nor the hand movements had hurt any worker, and no clear evidence showed Ms P to be a dangerous employee rather than merely a manager whose style they disliked. [Para 58][Para 60][Para 62][Para 64][Para 66]

The panel also relied on Article 65 of the Labour Law, under which the labour contract is subject to common law and binds only its parties, and on Article 22 of Decree No. 38, that a contract is law between the parties and binds only them. Hiring and dismissal are therefore the employer's privilege, and even where an employee commits serious misconduct under Article 83, the Labour Law confers a right to dismiss rather than an obligation. The demand was not legally founded. [Para 66][Para 72][Para 73][Para 74][Para 75]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that an employer dismiss a supervisor or fellow employee will be rejected unless the workers prove that the person is dangerous and can no longer be permitted in the workplace; the labour contract binds only the employer and the worker, so hiring and dismissal remain the employer's privilege, and Article 83 permits but never compels dismissal even where misconduct is shown. [Para 66][Para 72][Para 73][Para 76]

**Obiter dicta:** That shouting at workers, moving the hands violently and throwing garments back at them is "wild" and inappropriate conduct, and that the company president had already reproached the supervisor for shouting — observations that did not alter the outcome because no injury was proved. [Para 38][Para 62][Para 64]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The hearing is dated "Tuesday, 26 August 2004" in the hearing record, a date before the complaint of 22 September 2004, while the case summary and reasons place it on 26 October 2004, with a further discussion on 28 October 2004. The employer appears as "Nobel" on the face and in the case name but the union is the "Noble Factory Union" and "Noble Company" elsewhere. The panel chairman is printed "Ang Eng Thong" and the employer-side arbitrator "Mar Sambona", a spelling that differs from the "Mar Samborana" of neighbouring awards; the worker-side arbitrator is "Liv Sovanna" on the face and "Liv Sovann" at signature. Paragraphs 63–64 are OCR-mutilated ("finds thathis wild behavior", the sentence breaking off), and the cited awards are named inconsistently ("Chou Sing" and "Chou Shing", "Hing" and "Hou Hing"). The award was declared not immediately binding, though the closing boilerplate says it binds after eight days. The supervisor is identified here by initial only. [Para 16][Para 25][Para 8][Para 56][Para 63][Para 66][Para 79]
