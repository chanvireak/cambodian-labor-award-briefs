# Award No. 62/04 — Ecent Garment Factory (Arbitration Council, 9 September 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — You Suonty (chosen by the employer party), Tuon Siphann (chosen by the worker party), Men Nymith (chair) · Doc ID 139331

## 1. Facts
Ecent Garment Factory employs 660 workers. On 30 July 2004 about 500 struck over 18 demands after the employer failed to attend a meeting; Kandal Labour Inspectors conciliated from 30 July to 2 August 2004, settling six of the 18, and the non-conciliation report of 3 August 2004 brought the rest to the panel, which heard the case on 9, 13, 16 and 17 August 2004. The workers withdrew points 7 and 11; the employer raised four strike-management questions but sought no award, and the workers added three demands outside the panel's jurisdiction. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 17][Para 19][Para 21][Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay the US$5 regular bonus and daily wages to workers on certified sick leave, and whether a certificate from a licensed private clinic suffices? [Para 89][Para 103][Para 121]
2. Whether the company must state the per-dozen sewing rate before taking a purchasing order, and within three days? [Para 2]
3. Whether the company must repay the 10,100 riel medical check-up fee each worker paid? [Para 2][Para 137]
4. Whether the company owes 100 percent of wages for workless periods over the past three years, having suspended contracts without informing the Labour Inspector? [Para 2][Para 147]
5. Whether the company must compensate in money the annual leave it deducted during no-work periods, and grant 19 and 20 days' leave after three and six years? [Para 3][Para 196]
6. Whether the company must refund union contributions of 1,000 riel per person since May? [Para 3][Para 74]
7. Whether casual, apprentice and probationary staff must receive the same benefits as regular workers? [Para 3][Para 207]
8. Whether night work from 10:00 p.m. to 5:00 a.m. must be paid at double rate for the past three years? [Para 5][Para 84]
9. Whether the panel may decide the three demands first raised at the hearing (apprenticeship centre, workplace temperature, "Made in Cambodia" labelling)? [Para 226]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, Khmer Youth Union of Ecent Garment Factory):** Notification 017 of 18 July 2000 entitles a worker absent with a doctor's certificate to the regular bonus; any legally recognised clinic certificate should count; suspension for lack of work was not a lawful suspension, so full wages are owed for three years; the seniority leave provision adds a day every three years cumulatively; night work was never paid at 200 percent. [Para 17][Para 40][Para 41][Para 84][Para 124]
**Employer (respondent):** the bonus is earned only by a worker present every day of the month; only a state hospital certificate justifies wages; the strikes of 2001–2004 cut orders and left it unable to pay; it needs seven days to price a new style; and it will deduct union dues only against a list signed by each worker. [Para 18][Para 26][Para 34][Para 43][Para 75]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must accept certificates from legally recognised public and private hospitals; pay the regular bonus to certified sick workers in proportion to days worked; pay full daily wages for a first month of sick leave and 60 percent for the second and third, back over three years for workers claiming within 30 days; notify the per-dozen rate within three days or 72 hours; repay 10,100 riel per worker for the medical check-up by 30 September 2004; pay 100 percent of wages for workless periods from September 2001 to August 2004; add a leave day per three years (18, 19 and 20 days) and compensate past seniority in days; and recalculate night-shift wages for the same three years. It rejected cash in lieu of annual leave, the medicine refund for six workers, the union-dues claim and demands 17–19. [Para 227][Para 228][Para 230][Para 231][Para 232][Para 233]
On sick leave the panel found Article 3 of Notification 017/00 silent on both sick leave and the days that make attendance regular; it preferred 63/04 (Sign Well), which holds a certified sick worker not absent without valid reason, deducting the whole US$5 unfair to the worker and paying it in full unfair to the employer, over 26/03 (Cambodia Sportswear), which refused the bonus. On wages it applied point 7 of Article 4 of the rules — full pay for the first month, 60 percent for the second and third — and Articles 169 and 120. Neither the Law nor the rules specified state hospitals, and practice accepts any certificate from a facility licensed by the competent authority. [Para 91][Para 99][Para 101][Para 102][Para 118][Para 119][Para 127]
On the sewing rate the panel applied Article 112, which requires the employer to inform workers of the salary terms applying to them and of every change; no period being fixed, it held that pricing a style from a small sample takes no more than three days, and imported unfinished goods no longer. On the medical check-up it held that a rule making the worker pay contravenes Articles 25 and 247(a) and (c)(4), and that joint Prakas 09 of 19 January 1994 fills the missing Prakas without offending Article 395. [Para 139][Para 140][Para 142]
On workless periods the panel applied Article 71(11), which permits suspension for up to two months but only under the Labour Inspector's supervision; the company suspended 30 to 40 percent of the workforce without notice to the Inspector, so the Article 72 exemption from wages did not apply. Article 120 fixed the recoverable window at three years. [Para 144][Para 145][Para 147][Para 149][Para 159]
On annual leave the panel applied Articles 166, 167 and 170: leave must be given, not bought, so cash compensation was refused, but the employer had to schedule leave and grant six paid days around Khmer New Year, Pchum Ben and the Water Festival; where Article 166 can be read two ways it adopted the reading that rewards seniority, 19 days for years four to six and 20 for years seven to nine. [Para 167][Para 189][Para 198][Para 199]
On union dues the panel held a bare list without signatures or fingerprints to be no consent, and a deduction taken without written consent to be unlawful interference under Article 281. On casual staff it held that apprenticeships require the conditions of Articles 51 to 64 and Prakas 004 of 4 January 2000, that Article 68 allows probation only at the start of a contract, and that demoting senior workers who took leave to apprentice status was inappropriate. On night work it applied Articles 139 and 144 and Prakas 80/99 at 200 percent, and held the conciliation point of 7 April 2004 waiving past claims void under Articles 13 and 119. It declined demands 17–19 under Article 33 of Prakas 099, as outside the non-conciliation report. [Para 202][Para 203][Para 217][Para 219][Para 220][Para 225][Para 226]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of contracts for lack of work under Article 71(11) excuses wages only where the employer has placed the suspension under the Labour Inspector's supervision; without that step the Article 72 exemption is unavailable and full wages are recoverable for three years under Article 120. Where a notification is silent, the regular bonus is reduced in proportion to certified sick days rather than forfeited, and any internal rule or conciliation waiver that removes a right the Labour Law guarantees is void under Articles 13 and 119. [Para 149][Para 102][Para 225][Para 226]

**Obiter dicta:** the employer's losses from repeated strikes, though real, cannot relieve it of a rights-based obligation, and requiring a receipt before refunding medicine a worker bought is a fair compromise. [Para 155][Para 168][Para 191]
