# Award in Case 69/04 — Common Way Fashion Company (Cambodia) / GMT MFG (Arbitration Council, 8 September 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambona, employer-appointed; Ven Pov, worker-appointed; Kong Phallack, chair) · heard as non-binding award by the parties' agreement · Doc ID 139339

## 1. Facts
Common Way Company, Phnom Penh, began business in January 2004 and employed 980 workers, of whom about 300 held probationary or apprenticeship contracts and about 80 were treated as "floating workers" paid US$1 per day while working 8 hours a day, 6 days a week like permanent staff. On 9 August 2004 the workers complained to the Dangkor labour inspectorate; conciliation on 12 August 2004 settled seven of nine issues, leaving the status and wages of the short-contract workforce and a reimbursement demand. The non-conciliation report 2240 MoSALVY/LI of 18 August 2004 went to the Secretariat on 19 August 2004, the hearing was held on 24 August 2004, and the parties agreed in the hearing that the award would not be binding. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 24][Para 31][Para 20][Para 21][Para 12][Para 15][Para 17]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must treat the workers who have worked more than two months as permanent workers and pay them wages in accordance with the Labour Law? [Para 10]
2. Whether the company must reimburse the workers 15,000 riels for medical check-up fees and US$3 for employment cards? [Para 10]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The floating workers had in fact worked full time since the factory opened and should be regular workers with the Labour Law's wages and benefits, a status they pressed rather than accept the company's offer of fresh two-month contracts; they filed evidence of individual hirings to show continuous full-time engagement. [Para 34][Para 32][Para 17][Para 46]
**Employer (respondent):** It classified its workforce in three groups — apprentices at US$35, probationary workers at US$40 and floating workers at US$1 per day — treated the floating group as a separate category outside regular employment, but offered to refund apprentices and probationers who had been underpaid and to recognise probationers of more than two months as regular if they signed new two-month fixed duration contracts for evaluation; it did not supply the floating workers' contracts when the Secretariat asked for them on 31 August 2004. [Para 26][Para 28][Para 30][Para 33][Para 42]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to treat the floating workers who had worked 8 hours per day, 21 days per month and two continuous months as regular workers with full rights immediately after the award came into force, on contracts of unspecified duration, and to refund and reimburse all workers to the dispute at US$30 per month (apprentice/first two months as casual), US$40 per month (first two months as regular; months three and four for apprentices) and US$45 per month thereafter, each inclusive of the US$5 attendance bonus and calculated from the date each worker started; on issue 2 the company was recorded as agreeing to pay 15,000 riels on 10 October 2004 and 9,500 riels (12,000 riels − 2,500 riels) for employment cards. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54][Para 23]

On issue 1 the panel held that "floating worker" is a label unknown to the Labour Law: Article 9 distinguishes only regular workers, who regularly perform a job on a permanent basis, from casual workers contracted for work of short duration, temporary, intermittent or seasonal. Most of the 80 floating workers had worked full time for more than seven months, and following its Awards No. 53/04 (Kong Hong) and No. 55/04 (You Chheng) the panel applied the test that a worker averaging more than 21 days per month over more than two continuous months cannot be working short-term, temporarily, intermittently or seasonally; they were therefore regular workers. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 37][Para 38][Para 39]

The panel then fixed the contract type. Under Articles 10, 66 and 67 a regular worker may hold a fixed or an unspecified duration contract, and Article 67(7) requires a fixed duration contract to be in writing, failing which the contract becomes one of unspecified duration. The company produced no contracts although asked on 31 August 2004, so the panel found no written contract existed and the 80 contracts were of unspecified duration. A change from unspecified to fixed duration needs the agreement of both parties; the workers refused the two-month contracts offered, so the change could not be made. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41][Para 43][Para 44][Para 45][Para 46][Para 47]

On the wage claim the panel used Article 10, under which casual workers enjoy the same rights as regular workers save for separately stipulated clauses, so Notifications 06/97 and 017/00 on garment-sector wages applied to all Common Way workers: US$30 for apprentices, US$40 for probationary workers and US$45 for regular workers, plus the US$5 monthly attendance bonus. It also held, on Notification 06/97, Prakas 04/00 and Articles 54 and 61, that apprenticeship in Phnom Penh garment factories may not exceed two months, and on Article 68 that probation for specialised garment workers is capped at two months, after which the worker is regular. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 51]

Issue 2 needed no adjudication: the Council declined to consider it because the employer had agreed to reimburse the medical check-up and employment card fees and the workers accepted that agreement, which was annexed to the award and signed for both sides. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 54]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker's status follows the work actually performed, not the employer's label: a worker who works full time, averaging more than 21 days per month over more than two continuous months, is a regular worker under Article 9 and cannot be treated as casual, whatever name the payroll gives the category; and where the employer cannot produce a written fixed duration contract, Article 67(7) makes the contract one of unspecified duration, which the employer cannot convert to fixed duration without the worker's agreement. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 44][Para 45][Para 47]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's general statements that the two-month ceilings on apprenticeship and probation derive from Notification 06/97, Prakas 04/00, Articles 54, 61 and 68 and apply to garment factories in Phnom Penh, and its finding that the workers had not yet received all benefits due under the Labour Law; also the parties' agreement to make the award non-binding. [Para 49][Para 51][Para 25][Para 17]
