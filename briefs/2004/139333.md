# Award No. 63/04 — Shine Well Industry Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 24 August 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mar Somborana (chosen by the employer party), Ven Pov (chosen by the worker party), Kong Phallack (chair) · Doc ID 139333

## 1. Facts
Shine Well Industry Co Ltd, at Chamkar Outloeuk, Khan Dangkor, Phnom Penh, has operated since November 2003 and employs 690 workers, about 600 of them women. The workers complained to the Dangkor Labour Inspection Department on 20 July 2004 over eight demands; conciliation on 27 July 2004 settled two, and the non-conciliation report of 30 July 2004 brought six to the panel. The employer told the Secretariat it could not attend but gave no valid reason, so the panel heard the case on 10 August 2004 without it under Article 21 of Prakas 099/04. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 18][Para 20][Para 21]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must reimburse the 100 riel medical check fee to every worker, including those already checked and those not yet checked? [Para 8][Para 22]
2. Whether the company must build a nursing room and day-care centre, or may instead pay US$10 a month for outside care plus US$12 for milk? [Para 8][Para 9][Para 29]
3. Whether the company must pay full basic wages when it has no work, or only 50 percent? [Para 9][Para 29]
4. Whether the company must pay round-trip transport for probationary workers when it terminates them? [Para 10][Para 30]
5. Whether the company must maintain the US$5 attendance bonus for workers on sick leave with a medical certificate? [Para 10][Para 33]
6. Whether the panel has jurisdiction to order the reinstatement of three workers dismissed on 1 July 2004? [Para 11][Para 34]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, Local Union of Shine Well and KYFTU):** the company should reimburse every worker's medical check and fund the checks still outstanding; it promised to comply with the Labour Law on child care but never fixed a date, hence the offer of US$10 and two cans of milk (US$12) for sixteen months after maternity leave; it should accept the conciliation terms on wages; travel costs are owed to probationary workers terminated in future; and the attendance bonus should survive certified sick leave. [Para 8][Para 29][Para 31][Para 33]
**Employer (respondent):** absent without valid reason and offering no position at the hearing; it had asked at conciliation for time to discuss the medical check, and had agreed at conciliation to comply with the Labour Law on the nursing room and day-care centre without setting a date. [Para 20][Para 26][Para 28][Para 33]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must reimburse 100 riel to each worker who paid for a medical check, within seven days of the award coming into force, and pay for those not yet checked; build a nursing room within two weeks; either build a day-care centre under Ministry of Labour control or pay outside day-care fees, by 10 September 2004; pay full basic wages to workers present eight hours a day when there is no work and 50 percent to those who do not come; and pay the attendance bonus to workers on properly certified sick leave in proportion to days worked. The transport claim was denied and the reinstatement claim fell outside jurisdiction. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 81][Para 82][Para 84][Para 85]
On the medical check the panel held Prakas 09/94 valid, though made under the 1992 Labour Law, because it contradicts nothing in the 1997 Law and is not void under Article 395, following 02/03 Chou Sing, 53/04 and 60/04 United Art; Article 274(c) independently obliges the employer to pay check fees, and Article 377 attaches a fine of 120 to 360 days' base wage and imprisonment of one to five years. A worker who pays first and hands over the receipt does not discharge the employer. [Para 39][Para 41][Para 44][Para 45][Para 46]
On child care the panel applied Articles 186 and 187: an enterprise with at least 100 women must have a nursing room and a day-care centre on site or nearby, and where no centre can be built the employer pays the charges for care elsewhere; the offer of US$10 and US$12 in milk for eighteen months in place of a nursing room contradicts the Labour Law and was not considered. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 49][Para 51]
On wages the panel simply ordered the parties to comply with their agreement recorded in the non-conciliation report: full basic wages for workers in the workplace eight hours a day when work is short, 50 percent for those who stay away. On transport it read Article 68 as covering two situations — work required far from the worker's habitual residence, and workers recruited from a remote province — and denied the claim because the union sought costs only for hypothetical future terminations and named no worker with a distant home. On the bonus it held Notification 017/00 silent on sick leave and no internal work rules were produced, so on equity the bonus is reduced in proportion to certified sick days rather than forfeited, since certified absence is not misconduct. [Para 52][Para 55][Para 57][Para 60][Para 63][Para 66][Para 68]
On reinstatement the panel held that terminating three named workers is an individual dispute under Article 300, not a collective one under Article 302, since it threatens neither the enterprise's operation nor social peace, and that the Labour Inspector and Minister decide which character a dispute bears; it therefore declined jurisdiction, following 41/04 Micasa. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 77]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer bears the cost of its workers' medical examinations, whether or not the worker paid first and produced the receipt, because Prakas 09/94 survives the 1997 Labour Law and Article 274(c) states the duty; and a cash alternative to the nursing room that Articles 186–187 require cannot be substituted by agreement — only the day-care centre may be replaced by paying outside fees. A demand to reinstate named dismissed workers is an individual dispute, outside a panel's collective jurisdiction, while a demand for travel costs must identify a probationary worker actually working far from home or recruited from a distant province. [Para 39][Para 44][Para 49][Para 57][Para 74][Para 77]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that Article 377 punishes a breach of Article 274 with a fine and imprisonment, and that the character of a dispute — individual or collective — is for the Labour Inspector and the Minister to settle before referral. [Para 45][Para 73]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The findings say the company "has these nursing rooms and day care centers approximately 600 women workers", which must be read as having neither, given ¶27. The panel holds Prakas 09/94 valid at ¶39 and then reasons "although the Prakas is void" at ¶40, and cites Article 274(c) where the parallel award 60/04 relies on Article 247(C-4). The fee is printed as 100 riel throughout, which is implausibly low beside the 10,100 riel charged in other awards. The conciliation count appears as "two issues ... six unsuccessful" and as agreement on "two out of eight". Worker names are printed inconsistently — Sao Siem and Sao Sim, Phine and Phin Chinda, and the translator as Chhive Vandeth and Chhiev — and are redacted here as Mr C, Mr S and Ms P, with the administrator as Mr L; one sentence about the dismissed workers is duplicated. [Para 27][Para 39][Para 40][Para 49][Para 11][Para 12][Para 18][Para 34][Para 41]
