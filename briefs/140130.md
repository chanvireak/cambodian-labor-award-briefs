# Award No. 98/12 — Hung Tak Garment Co (Arbitration Council, 19 June 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom, employer-selected; An Non, worker-selected; Ang Eng Thong, chair) · Doc ID 140130

## 1. Facts
Hung Tak Garment, a Phnom Penh cutting and sewing factory employing about 4,450 workers, faced 18 demands brought by two federations; six points were not settled at conciliation and reached the panel. [Para 45][Para 20] Neither federation had a local union inside the factory: 305 workers had signed written authorisations asking the two unions to represent them in the dispute. [Para 47][Para 75] The workers withdrew claim 2 (holiday pay at Sunday rates) during the hearing, leaving five claims, which the panel kept under its original numbering 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6. [Para 24][Para 26]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise the regular attendance bonus from USD 7 to USD 10 a month? [Para 78]
3. Whether the employer must raise the base wage from USD 61 to USD 70 a month? [Para 89]
4. Whether the employer must pay USD 15 a month as a transport or housing allowance? [Para 94]
5. Whether the employer must build a nursery (ទារកដ្ឋាន), or pay USD 10 a month as milk money in its place? [Para 100]
6. Whether the employer must pay 4,500 riel meal money for overtime worked until 20:30? [Para 116]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the attendance bonus should reach USD 10 because neighbouring factories pay USD 10 or USD 15 and the bonus encourages effort; the base wage should rise to USD 70 because the workers have served the company for years; transport or housing money is owed because most workers migrate from Kampong Speu and Takeo and must pay to travel and lodge; and meal money of 2,000 riel is due for each overtime block because a worker taking overtime from 16:00 to 18:00 receives 2,000 riel, i.e. 1,000 riel an hour. [Para 52][Para 57][Para 59][Para 72]
**Employer (respondent):** it pays the USD 7 minimum fixed by Ministry of Labour Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 and will continue to apply that notification; it did not recruit in the provinces, so it owes no travel or lodging money, although it paid USD 5 a month for that purpose in 2006–2008 until the economic crisis; it cannot build a nursery because it rents a cramped site; and holiday overtime is already compensated at 300 per cent. [Para 54][Para 61][Para 62][Para 67][Para 3]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to provide a nursery (and a nursing room) for its women workers, and, where it cannot, to let mothers place children over 18 months in an outside nursery with the employer paying the care costs against actual invoices; it refused the USD 10 milk-money claim and refused the bonus, wage, transport and meal-money claims. [Para 126][Para 127][Para 128][Para 129]

The panel first disposed of representation, because the answer decided four of the five claims. Under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 the parties may appear through a person holding written authorisation, and the panel found the two unions held such authorisation from 305 workers, which the employer did not contest. [Para 73][Para 75][Para 77] That sufficed for appearance, but the panel held that an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement for a year and binds every worker in the enterprise, so only the most representative union — one with more than half the workers of the enterprise — may bring such a claim. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 85] No local union existed at the factory, so none was most representative, and the unions had no standing to pursue interest claims. [Para 86][Para 87] The bonus, wage, transport and meal-money claims were each found to exceed what the law or any agreement required and were therefore interest disputes, and each was refused on that ground. [Para 81][Para 91][Para 94][Para 120]

Claim 5 alone was a rights dispute. Article 186 of the Labour Law obliges every enterprise employing at least 100 women to fit out a nursing room and a nursery; the factory employed far more than that, and its lack of space was no answer because the second paragraph of Article 186 provides the alternative. [Para 100][Para 102][Para 103] The panel read that paragraph as converting the building duty into a duty to pay the real cost of outside care for children over 18 months, not into a flat monthly payment, and refused the USD 10 milk money because neither the Labour Law nor any agreement between the parties provided it. [Para 106][Para 111][Para 112]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union holding written authorisations from a minority of an enterprise's workforce may represent those workers in an arbitration, but a claim that exceeds what statute, agreement or collective convention grants is an interest dispute, and only the most representative union at the enterprise — one organising more than half its workers — may bring such a claim; without it the panel will not decide the claim on the merits. [Para 75][Para 85][Para 87] Where an enterprise employs at least 100 women, Article 186 imposes a nursery and nursing room regardless of the employer's claim of insufficient space, and the statutory alternative is reimbursement of the actual cost of outside care, not a flat monthly allowance. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 106]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that an interest award displaces the collective agreement for one year and thereby removes other workers' right to strike over that issue is a general statement about Article 43 of Prakas No. 099, not a step in the reasoning on any of the five claims. [Para 84]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The number of workers who authorised representation is printed four different ways: 205 and 100 at [Para 25], 4,100 and 205 at [Para 49], 305 at [Para 75], and "4,305" at [Para 86]; the panel reasoned from 305. The header gives the case as 98/12, but the employer's two postponement letters are filed under case 97/12 at [Para 37] and [Para 38]. The hearing is dated 8 June 2012 at [Para 18], while the invitation in the procedural summary cites 8 May 2012 at [Para 22]. Claim 1 is pleaded as USD 10 at [Para 2] and as an extra USD 3 on top of USD 7 at [Para 78]. The panel's names appear in two spellings, "ឈីវ ភិរម្យ" at [Para 1] and "ឈីវ ភរិម្យ" at [Para 131]; the employer's representatives are named in the source but are reduced to roles here.
