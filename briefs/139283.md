# Award No. 116/19 — Phum Vorbathorm Cambodia (Arbitration Council, 29 January 2020)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun for the employer party, Tuon Si Phan for the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 139283

## 1. Facts
Phum Vorbathorm Cambodia is a Siem Reap business selling tickets to visit its cultural village, employing 324 workers; the union of its employees (សហជីព និយោជិត វិស័យ ទេសចរណ៍) was certified most representative on 15 March 2019. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61] From August 2018 the union sought a collective bargaining agreement on a three-phase timetable; on 23 August 2019 it delivered draft internal bargaining rules and a draft CBA. [Para 62][Para 66][Para 110] Conciliation failed on 9 December 2019 and the Council heard the case at a Siem Reap hotel on 10 January 2020; the award is **non-binding**. [Para 11][Para 16][Para 20]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to bargain the collective agreement in good faith and with the most-representative union, and to cease its delaying conduct? [Para 98]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Article 96 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 303 give a most-representative union the right to request bargaining and impose on the employer a duty to bargain; the employer has postponed since August 2019 without ever fixing a date, has never appointed a representative to negotiate, and so is obstructing the process, which costs the workers the benefits the agreement would bring. [Para 80][Para 81][Para 82][Para 83][Para 84]
**Employer (respondent):** it does want to bargain and has always received and answered the union's letters, explaining why it needed time; it needs two to four months to prepare documents and obtain a decision from senior management because the draft raises wages and other benefits and therefore cost; the company faces falling tourist numbers and revenue; and it has in fact fixed 23 March 2020 for the meeting on the internal bargaining rules. [Para 88][Para 89][Para 90][Para 91][Para 92][Para 93][Para 95]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted in part — the employer was ordered to join the bargaining meeting with the union and to ensure the collective agreement is concluded within **six months** from the date the award becomes final. [Para 120][Para 121]

The panel asked whether the employer must bargain in good faith with the most-representative union and stop delaying. [Para 98] It set out Article 54(1) of the Trade Union Law, which recognises most-representative status within the enterprise and gives that union the exclusive right to bargain and to settle collective disputes, and Article 53(2) and (3), which require good faith — expressly including meeting and convening promptly for the purpose of bargaining and considering solutions to complaints arising from the agreement, while not obliging an employer to agree to any particular proposal. [Para 98][Para 99][Para 100][Para 101][Para 102] Article 71 requires each side to hold a full mandate from its members, and Article 69(3) allows a CBA to be more favourable than the law and requires claims differing from the law to be resolved through a proper bargaining process. [Para 103][Para 105][Para 106]

On that basis the panel held that a most-representative union has the right to request bargaining, that the employer must join, and that both sides must observe good faith, which includes recognising the union's right and convening promptly so that bargaining concludes without unjustified delay and is conducted collectively. [Para 107][Para 108][Para 109]

It rejected the employer's claim that it could not decide because it sits under a parent company, Mega Asset Management: the company holds its own patent and statutes registered with the Ministry of Commerce and is a lawful legal person. [Para 87] On the facts, the union delivered its drafts on 23 August 2019 and by the hearing the parties had still not met to decide the bargaining rules or the content of the agreement. [Para 110] The panel accepted that the employer had shown willingness — it answered letters and met the union to explain its timing — and had now fixed 23 March 2020 to settle the internal rules, after which bargaining on substance would follow. [Para 111][Para 112]

Nevertheless, six months had passed since the request, the union did not accept the March date, and delay of that length was unreasonable; the panel found the repeated requests for time to be evasion of good-faith bargaining rather than necessity. [Para 86][Para 113][Para 114] Because the employer's own practice was to take only two to four months to decide additional costs, six months to complete the whole bargaining was a reasonable period for both sides. [Para 95][Para 118][Para 119] Relying on Article 34 of Prakas No. 099, which empowers the panel to order the parties to bargain and to set the conditions, it ordered the employer to attend and to bring the agreement to conclusion within six months of the award becoming final. [Para 117][Para 120][Para 121]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a union has been certified most representative in the enterprise, the employer is under a duty to bargain a collective agreement with it, and that duty is one of good faith: the employer must recognise the union's exclusive right to bargain, fix meeting dates promptly, mandate a representative with authority to decide, and avoid repeated requests for postponement. [Para 107][Para 108][Para 109] Delay that runs to roughly six months after delivery of draft rules and a draft agreement, on grounds of internal review and cost, is unjustified; a parent-company relationship is no answer, because a registered subsidiary is a separate legal person competent to bargain. [Para 87][Para 114] The panel will order completion within six months of the award becoming final, measuring that period against the employer's own practice of taking two to four months to approve additional expenditure. [Para 118][Para 119][Para 120]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's findings that the employer had in fact shown willingness to bargain [Para 111] and that the parties agreed some draft clauses were already being applied [Para 115] are observations on the state of the relationship; they do not qualify the order, which rests on the length of the delay. Its remark that the union refused to accept the employer's 23 March 2020 date [Para 113] is similarly contextual.
