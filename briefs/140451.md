# Award No. 153/09 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន រី វើរី ច តិច ស្តាយ អិលធីឌី and the C.CAWDU union at the factory (Arbitration Council, 20 November 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (chair គង់ ផល្លា, printed also as គង់ ផលៈ; employer-party nominee មាំរ សំបូរ ណា; worker-party nominee វ៉ែន ពៅ) · Doc ID 140451

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន រី វើរី ច តិច ស្តាយ អិលធីឌី is a garment factory at Prek Ta Pring, Sitbo commune, Saang district, Kandal province. [Para 1] Forty workers in its ironing section had been recruited by សិប្បកម្ម ហ្វាំង ស៊ាំង, a sole-proprietorship printing workshop holding patent No. 00463 of 05 May 2009, under a labour-supply agreement of 28 May 2009 between the two businesses; they were engaged on 20-day fixed-term contracts from 01 June 2009, renewed three times and expiring on 30 October 2009. [Para 22][Para 28][Para 37][Para 38][Para 70] The workers were selected in front of the factory, worked there, were sometimes paid by the factory and sometimes by the workshop, and some carry the workshop's staff cards. [Para 46][Para 47] When the supply stopped, the union demanded that the factory take the ironing workers back. [Para 2][Para 40] Of five demands, conciliation on 07 October 2009 settled two, and two more were settled at the hearing — the election of worker representatives, which the company said it would hold in December 2010, and the piece-rate demand, which the union withdrew — leaving only the reinstatement demand. [Para 5][Para 14][Para 18]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether ក្រុមហ៊ុន រី វើរី ច តិច ស្តាយ អិលធីឌី must take back the 33 ironing workers it stopped using and let them work again? [Para 2][Para 37][Para 93]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — the C.CAWDU federation and its factory union, represented by its officials and twelve activists):** the 33 named workers were not the workshop's own staff and had never worked at the workshop; the factory recruited them on its own premises and put them to work, so the factory is their employer and must reinstate them; the workers did not know where the workshop was located, and some of the supply contracts bore no clear company name and were only thumbprinted. [Para 45][Para 52][Para 54][Para 62]
**Employer (respondent — ក្រុមហ៊ុន រី វើរី ច តិច ស្តាយ អិលធីឌី, through its administrative chief):** the 33 are not its employees; it borrowed them from the workshop under a labour-supply agreement, and when the ironing line no longer had work it lawfully stopped borrowing. [Para 44][Para 65][Para 90] សិប្បកម្ម ហ្វាំង ស៊ាំង, appearing separately through its administrative chief, accepted that the 33 are its mobile workers and that it is responsible for them, but said it could not renew their contracts because the factory had stopped calling for them. [Para 86][Para 87]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the demand that the factory take back the 33 ironing workers. [Para 92][Para 93]

The panel first found that a labour-supply agreement really existed, since Article 45 of the Labour Law defines the labour contractor (ការិយភារី) as one who recruits workers on the entrepreneur's instructions for a lump sum and requires the contract to be in writing; the agreement of 28 May 2009 contained nothing contrary to the law on labour contracting, and the workshop was operating as a registered sole proprietorship. [Para 67][Para 70][Para 73][Para 74] It then held that the 33 had contracted with the workshop, not the factory: Article 65 defines the employment contract as the link between worker and employer, the workers admitted listening to the contract read out and thumbprinting it, and Article 47 makes a labour contractor answerable as an employer. [Para 75][Para 78][Para 80][Para 81][Para 83] Although Article 48 lets the entrepreneur step in where the contractor fails to perform, that provision was not engaged, because the workshop appeared at the hearing and accepted responsibility for the 33 and for any breach; the only obstacle was that the factory had stopped requesting workers. [Para 84][Para 85][Para 86][Para 87] The factory was therefore entitled to suspend borrowing under the agreement and by reference to its production cycle, and the demand had no legal basis. [Para 90][Para 91]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Workers recruited by a licensed labour contractor (ការិយភារី) under a written supply agreement remain employees of that contractor, not of the enterprise where they work: under Articles 45 and 65 of the Labour Law the contract of employment runs between the worker and the contractor, and the host enterprise cannot be ordered to reinstate them. The entrepreneur's subsidiary liability under Article 48 arises only where the contractor fails to perform its obligations, and it is not engaged where the contractor appears in the proceedings and accepts responsibility for the workers. [Para 67][Para 75][Para 81][Para 85][Para 90]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the factory sometimes paid the workers directly through the workshop's account, and that the workshop held a patent rather than a commercial register certificate because it is a small handicraft, are background findings that did not alter the employment relationship. [Para 49][Para 51]
