# Order No. 320/15 — Khon Brass Holding (Cambodia) Garment Factory Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 8 January 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry, chosen by the employer party; An Non, chosen by the worker party; Kong Phal, chair chosen by the two) · Doc ID 139487

## 1. Facts
Khon Brass Holding (Cambodia) Garment Factory Co., Ltd, a garment factory in Mean Chey district with about 612 workers, had for years hired on two-month probation and then on successive six-month fixed-duration contracts, renewing them even once the total passed two years, and paying 5% termination indemnity at each year-end together with unused annual leave. [Para 111][Para 112][Para 113][Para 115] Six union federations brought a five-point claim after a strike from 1 to 9 December 2015, held without notice and outside the legal procedure, which the Council ordered suspended by order 039/15 of 8 December 2015; the workers returned on 17 December. [Para 134][Para 17][Para 20][Para 103] Only three points survived to the hearing on 22 December 2015: the contracts, the meal allowance and wages during the strike. [Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay severance to worker No. 1387 under the Labour Law? [Para 3]
2. Whether the employer must convert to undetermined-duration contracts the contracts of workers with more than two years' service, and pay them severance on dismissal? [Para 111][Para 116]
3. Whether the employer must pay a meal allowance of 3,000 riel a day? [Para 6]
4. Whether the employer may deduct the attendance bonus when a worker is absent with proper permission? [Para 7]
5. Whether the employer must keep paying wages and bonuses during the strike of 1 to 9 December 2015? [Para 8][Para 192]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer renewed contracts silently, without notice, and would not recognise that workers with more than two years' service hold undetermined-duration contracts; it also failed to give lawful notice before dismissal; the claim rests on Articles 67(1) and (8) and 73(5). [Para 117][Para 118][Para 119] As to the strike, it was caused by the employer's own fault, which is why they stopped work. [Para 137]
**Employer (respondent):** each worker signed a contract accepting fixed duration precisely in order to obtain the 5% indemnity, and Article 67(2) permits renewal so long as no single renewal exceeds two years, so it has complied and has paid the 5% at every termination. [Para 121][Para 122][Para 123] It cannot raise the meal allowance because orders are scarce, and the striking workers performed no work so are owed no wage. [Para 132][Para 139]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to convert to undetermined-duration contracts the contracts of workers whose successive fixed-duration contracts total more than two years; it refused the severance claim, the meal allowance and the strike wages. [Para 208][Para 209][Para 210]

Before the merits the panel fixed who was claiming. Under Article 283 every enterprise with at least eight workers elects employee delegates who alone represent all the workers, and since 19 workers attended the hearing including two recognised delegates, the panel treated the delegates as the claimants. [Para 143][Para 144][Para 146]

On the contracts, the panel applied Article 67(2), which caps a fixed-duration contract at two years and allows renewal only while the maximum stays within two years, and Article 73(5), under which a contract renewed without notice continues or becomes undetermined where the total exceeds that limit; it followed its earlier order in case 10/03 that the two-year clock runs on the aggregate of all the contracts, not on each renewal. [Para 151][Para 152][Para 154][Para 157] It added that ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982 discourages fixed-term contracts for long-term work, as context for reading Article 67. [Para 158][Para 160] On the facts, the list filed by the workers held 224 names but only 207 had in fact completed two years by the hearing date, and the employer could not keep issuing fixed-duration contracts to them. [Para 126][Para 127][Para 163][Para 164]

The employer's answer — that each worker had agreed to fixed duration in clause 14 of the contract in order to draw the 5% indemnity — was rejected. Article 13 makes the Labour Law public order and voids any rule arising from a unilateral decision, a contract or a convention that does not comply with it, so the agreement, though real, was contrary to Articles 67(2) and 73(5) and void. [Para 166][Para 167][Para 168]

The second limb of that issue — payment of severance whenever such a worker is later dismissed — was refused: the workers themselves conceded it was a claim "kept for the future", and the panel does not resolve problems that have not yet arisen. [Para 170][Para 171][Para 174]

The meal allowance was treated as an interest dispute: no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, contract or past practice obliged the employer to pay 3,000 riel, while 1,000 riel a day is what it pays. [Para 175][Para 176][Para 178] Because an award on an interest dispute becomes a collective agreement binding the whole workforce for a year under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099, only a union with most-representative status may bring one, and the claimant here was a body of employee delegates, not such a union. [Para 183][Para 184][Para 189][Para 190]

On the strike, Article 332(1) suspends the employment contract on the days of a strike and provides that no work and no wages are given, and Article 72(1) makes suspension affect the employer's duty to pay; the panel followed its order in case 27/10 that an employer owes neither wages nor bonuses for strike days, whether the strike was lawful or not. [Para 192][Para 193][Para 194][Para 195][Para 202] It added that a worker who strikes without following the legal procedure does not attend regularly and so does not earn the attendance bonus. [Para 203][Para 206]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive fixed-duration contracts whose aggregate duration exceeds two years convert by operation of law into an undetermined-duration contract under Articles 67(2) and 73(5), and a clause in the contract by which the worker "chooses" fixed duration in order to draw the 5% indemnity is void under Article 13 because it defeats that rule. [Para 163][Para 167][Para 168] An employer owes no wage or attendance bonus for the days of a strike, lawful or not, the contract being suspended under Article 332(1); and only a union holding most-representative status may bring an interest dispute, since the award would bind the whole workforce for a year. [Para 184][Para 195][Para 202]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's use of ILO Recommendation No. 166 as "useful context" for interpreting Article 67, while noting it binds no one, is a general observation the outcome did not turn on. [Para 158][Para 160] Its remark at [Para 141] that it would first explain who the claimant is, and its account of delegate status, likewise go beyond what the order required.
