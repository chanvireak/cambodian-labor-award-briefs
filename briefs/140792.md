# Award No. 59/05 — Tack Fat Company (Arbitration Council, 27 October 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Panel: Kao Thach, An Nan, Kong Phallack (Chair) · Doc ID 140792

## 1. Facts
Tack Fat Company, at No. 1159, National Road 2, Sangkat Chak Angreh Leur, Khan Meanchey, Phnom Penh, employs approximately 6,000 workers and was founded in 1996 [Para 17][Para 18]. The Coalition of Cambodia Apparel W.D.U (C.CAWDU) of Tack Fat Factory and the Cambodian Labour Union Federation (CLUF) of Tack Fat Factory raised twelve demands on 12 September 2005; seven were conciliated and five remained [Para 9][Para 11]. The company had suspended labour contracts from 7 July to 1 September 2005, affecting 2,562 workers in the F2, F3 and F5 buildings, under Ministry letter No. 926 dated 6 July 2005 [Para 21][Para 23]. The remaining disputes concerned a short lay-off on 27 and 28 September 2005, the dismissal on 28 June 2005 of a translator, Mr S, equal health allowances, and reimbursement of a 10,100 riel medical examination fee [Para 65][Para 89][Para 46][Para 5]. Two of the five demands were withdrawn by letter of 19 October 2005 and letter No. 1399 dated 21 October 2005, so the panel did not consider them [Para 54][Para 55].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether piece-rate workers must be paid full wages of US$1.73 per day during a lay-off of not more than three days, including the lay-off of 27 and 28 September 2005? [Para 56]
2. (Issue 3) Whether Mr S must be reinstated, his dismissal not being for serious misconduct and he being a C.CAWDU activist? [Para 3]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the company must pay an equal health allowance to all workers whose health is affected by their work? [Para 46]
4. (Issue 5) Whether the company must reimburse the medical examination fee of 10,100 riel to each worker? [Para 5]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the two unions argued that the 27–28 September 2005 lay-off was made without Ministry authorisation and without payment, that Mr S broke a fan unintentionally and the sanction was disproportionate, that the health allowance is paid unequally, and that the medical fee was an employer cost [Para 65][Para 87][Para 47][Para 116].
**Employer (respondent):** Tack Fat Company said the short lay-off entailed 50 percent of the regular wage with the workers staying home, that Mr S committed repeated misconduct and vandalism against company property under Article 10 of the Internal Working Rules, that the health allowance is a discretionary bonus beyond the law, and that the medical examination is the worker's own obligation [Para 30][Para 45][Para 48][Para 116].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered US$1.73 for the one-day unlawful suspension and US$0.86 for the half-day, a total of US$2.595, to Mr V within one month; ordered compensation to Mr S under Articles 77, 89, 91, 116 and 166; ordered Mr S to buy or repair a fan; declined to consider the equal health allowance; and ordered reimbursement of the 10,100 riel medical fee [Para 127][Para 130].
On the lay-off, Article 71 permits suspension only for the reasons it states, and neither Article 71(7) nor the company's Internal Regulations covers this short lay-off [Para 59][Para 60]. Article 71(11) allows suspension for serious economic difficulty but only under the Labour Inspector's control, and it applies to piece-rate workers as much as to any other worker [Para 62][Para 63]. No authorisation was requested for 27 and 28 September 2005, and although an inspection occurred there was no Ministry approval, so the suspension breached the 1997 Labour Law [Para 65][Para 67]. Only Mr V's case was evidenced, so the panel confined itself to it and ordered his full daily wage [Para 65][Para 66].
On the dismissal, the panel accepted that Mr S broke the fan but found the employer's five witnesses inconsistent — some said a metal tube, some a PVC tube, and most did not see the incident — while the worker's witnesses were consistent and saw it [Para 70][Para 80][Para 86]. Following 13/04-Jeou Star the panel weighed motive, and held the misconduct minor, not serious [Para 79][Para 86]. Article 27 requires any sanction to be proportional to the misconduct, and this dismissal was not [Para 87][Para 90]. Following 06/04-Chu Sing, dismissal is a power, not a duty, so the employer should have considered all the facts [Para 73][Para 74]. Reinstatement was nonetheless refused because the relationship had broken down beyond repair [Para 92][Para 94]. Under Article 91 a dismissal needs a valid reason, which was absent, so full compensation was due under Article 89 [Para 95][Para 96].
On the health allowance, Article 230 and Prakas 1/97 require a safe workplace but not a health allowance, and the company supplies masks, gloves and clothing [Para 101][Para 102]. Paying it equally would therefore be an interest dispute, and both unions lack most representative status under Article 277, so neither may create a collective convention or bring such a dispute, following 31/03-Hong Wah, 60/04-United Art and 99/04-AIA and Article 43 of Prakas 99/04 [Para 105][Para 110][Para 111][Para 113].
On the medical fee, Article 247(c) obliges the employer to reimburse pre-employment medical examinations, and Prakas 9/94 Article 7 says the same, as applied in 60/04-United Art [Para 117][Para 120][Para 118]. The panel noted its own divergent views on the continued force of Prakas 9/94 but found Article 247 sufficient [Para 121][Para 122]. On the employer's attempt to abandon the binding award, the panel held that the company had authorised its representatives by letter No. 1251/2005, both parties chose and signed for a binding award before the hearing, and letter No. 1255/2005 could not undo it [Para 123][Para 125][Para 126].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A lay-off is a labour contract suspension and is unlawful unless it falls within Article 71 and has been authorised and controlled by the Labour Inspector, so a short lay-off made with an inspection but no Ministry authorisation obliges the employer to pay the suspended workers' full wages [Para 62][Para 67]. A sanction must be proportional to the misconduct under Article 27, and where the employer's witnesses are inconsistent and the damage unintentional the dismissal lacks the valid reason Article 91 requires, entitling the worker to full compensation under Article 89 even where reinstatement is refused [Para 87][Para 95][Para 96]. A demand for an equal health allowance is an interest dispute that a union without most representative status may not bring [Para 105][Para 110].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that an employer need not dismiss even where the Internal Working Rules permit it [Para 73]. It also recorded that it has differing views on whether Prakas 9/94 remains legally effective, while holding Article 247 sufficient on its own [Para 121][Para 122].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is dated 27 October 2005 but the dismissed worker's dismissal letter is dated 26 August 2005 while the reasons and facts state the dismissal occurred on 28 June 2005 [Para 15][Para 35][Para 89]. The non-conciliation issues are numbered from 1 but the same numeral "2" is printed for two different demands, and the facts re-open a numbering that the demands list had already used [Para 2][Para 21]. Prior awards are cited with corrupted names ("Best Kin Tai", "Eccent", "Hung Wah", "Kin Tai") in one run-on paragraph [Para 113]. The award type is printed as "Binding awards" in the plural although a single award is meant [Para 130]. Words are merged throughout ("themployer", "Traing", "withemployer"), and the evidence list is partly truncated mid-word ("Photof broken f") [Para 15]. The dismissed translator and the piece-rate worker are named in full on the face of the award and in its orders; they appear here as Mr S and Mr V [Para 35][Para 127].
