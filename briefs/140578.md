# Award No. 158/08 — Im & V No. 4 (អ៊ឹម និង វ៉េ ទី៤) (Arbitration Council, 20 January 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — employer-appointed កៅ ថា ច, worker-appointed អន ណន, president ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា · Doc ID 140578

## 1. Facts
The Labour Development Union at the Im & V No. 4 factory (សហជីព អភិវឌ្ឍន៍ ការងារ ប្រចាំ រោងចក្រ អ៊ឹម និង វ៉េ ទី៤) brought a collective dispute against Im & V No. 4 (អ៊ឹម និង វ៉េ ទី៤), a garment factory in Kampong Chhnang employing about 2,758 workers, eight of them in the laundry section [Para 41]. Two laundry workers who were also union officers — the deputy president and the secretary — held fixed-duration contracts stated to run from 10 August to 30 November 2008 [Para 20][Para 44]. On 22 November 2008 the company gave them seven days' notice that their contracts would not be renewed because there was no work in the coming month [Para 59]. Conciliation by the Kampong Chhnang labour department failed on this single point and the file reached the Council on 30 December 2008 [Para 7][Para 12].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company's decision not to renew the fixed-duration contracts of Mr E and Mr K was discrimination against union leaders? [Para 76]
2. (Issue 2) Whether that non-renewal was carried out in accordance with the Labour Law? [Para 76]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant union):** The two men were union officers named in the leadership notification of 24 November 2008, and the company dismissed them the moment it received that notice and replaced them with new hires; the union drew the inference of anti-union motive from that sequence [Para 54][Para 56]. It asked for their contracts to be renewed so that both could return to normal work [Para 46].

**Employer (respondent):** The contracts simply reached their term, and the company had no work for October to December, as in every year; seven days' notice was given on 22 November 2008, before the union's leadership list arrived, and both men thumbprinted the notice [Para 59][Para 65]. The company added that the men came to the laundry on 25 November but refused to work and asked to be paid their severance, then declined to collect it until 25 December 2008 [Para 67][Para 73].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the union's demand that the company renew the two fixed-duration contracts and take both men back to work [Para 96][Para 97].

On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 12 of the Labour Law, which forbids an employer from using union membership or union activity as a ground for discipline or termination, together with Article 279, which bars employers from taking union participation into account in hiring, assignment, promotion, wages or dismissal [Para 76][Para 77]. It followed its earlier awards, including No. 28/07 and No. 123/07, for the proposition that reinstatement is ordered where a dismissal is proved to be anti-union [Para 78][Para 79]. It also adopted the line of Awards No. 90/06, No. 112/06 and No. 01/07 that the worker side carries the burden of proving discrimination, and of No. 19/04 and No. 17/07 on the sufficiency of that evidence [Para 82][Para 83].

On the facts the panel found the burden unmet. The union's case rested on the coincidence that the non-renewal followed its leadership notification, and on the assertion that the undated notice letters were thumbprinted on 24 November 2008; the panel held that no clear and consistent evidence supported that assertion [Para 87]. It observed that the union had been registered since 28 October 2008 but notified the company of its factory leadership only on 24 November 2008, which it treated as an irregularity rather than as proof of motive [Para 88][Para 89]. It concluded that nothing in the record showed the non-renewal to be discrimination against union leaders [Para 90][Para 91].

On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 73 paragraph 1, under which a fixed-duration contract lapses by operation of its own term, and Article 73 paragraph 6, which requires severance of at least 5% of the wages paid over the contract period where the collective agreement sets no rate [Para 92][Para 93][Para 94]. It found the company had paid severance in accordance with Article 73 and that both men had collected it on 25 December 2008 [Para 95]. The claim therefore failed on both issues [Para 96].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A decision not to renew a fixed-duration contract at its expiry is not anti-union discrimination merely because the workers concerned are union officers and the decision follows the union's notification of its leadership; the worker side bears the burden of proving the discriminatory motive, and where the only evidence is the timing of the notice and an unproven assertion about when it was signed, the demand for renewal is refused, the contract having lapsed under Article 73 [Para 87][Para 90][Para 92].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel remarked that the gap between the union's registration on 28 October 2008 and its notification of the company on 24 November 2008 was in itself an irregularity, though it decided nothing on that ground [Para 89].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's name is printed inconsistently across the award, appearing as អ៊ឹម និង វ៉េ in some places and with a different initial vowel in others [Para 2][Para 41]. One worker's name is rendered with two different final consonants in the reasons, and a stray Latin character intrudes before one of the names in the issue statement [Para 87][Para 76]. The contracts are described as two months long but dated from 10 August to 30 November 2008 [Para 20]. The panel itself notes that 30 November 2008 fell on a Sunday and treats the contracts as expiring on 29 November 2008 [Para 60]. The signature block is heavily garbled OCR and no arbitrator signature can be read from it [Para 99].
