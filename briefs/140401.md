# Award No. 79/09-Yung Wah Laundry — Yung Wah Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Branch 2) (Arbitration Council, 15 July 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun for the employer, Tuon Siphann for the workers, chair Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 140401

## 1. Facts
Yung Wah Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. ("Yung Wah") runs a laundry service as Branch 2 at Thmei Village, Ta Khmao, Kandal, having begun in 2007 with about 138 workers; it washes for other factories as well as for its own branches. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 21] The claimant is the Local Union of C.CAWDU, which holds most-representative status certified on 24 July 2008. [Para 15] In January 2009 the employer discontinued the night shift and moved everyone to the noon shift, then from 15 June 2009 required 45 of them back onto the night shift after new orders arrived; the workers struck on 15 and 19 June 2009. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 23] Three of the original demands were not conciliated and came to the panel. [Para 7][Para 8]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must reassign the 45 laundry workers back to the noon shift? [Para 15][Para 56]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay the full 13th month's wages for 2008 to workers dismissed before 10 April 2009? [Para 33][Para 68]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must reinstate Mr S (ID No. 9430 QW), dismissed on 1 April 2009 for offensive language, or instead pay him the statutory termination entitlements? [Para 42][Para 74]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the Local Union of C.CAWDU of Yung Wah argued that the night shift had been abolished in January 2009, that branches 1 and 2 no longer run a night shift and so no transport is provided, that the night shift ends at midnight and the truck leaves workers on the main road to walk through dark alleys, that the 13th month's wages paid to dismissed workers reached only 60% of what was due, and that Mr S said "bullshit" after leaving the office to a friend and not to the administration staff. [Para 25][Para 28][Para 35][Para 54]
**Employer (respondent):** Yung Wah said the slump in purchase orders forced the change, that it had tried to find work for the laundry, that the 13th month's wage equals the US$ 50 main wage and was withheld in full only because Branches 1 and 2 did the same, and that Mr S insulted administration staff, which is serious misconduct barring reinstatement. [Para 19][Para 31][Para 34][Para 38][Para 76]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel rejected the demand to return the 45 workers to the noon shift, ordered the employer to pay the full 13th month's wages for 2008 to the nine workers dismissed before 10 April 2009 within two weeks of the award, and, refusing reinstatement, ordered one month's pay in lieu of notice, a dismissal indemnity of 30 days' wages, pay in lieu of remaining annual leave and unpaid final wages for Mr S. [Para 125][Para 126][Para 127][Para 128]

On Issue 1, the panel applied Article 2, paragraph 2 of the Labour Law and its own line of awards (62/06, 108/06, 33/07, 106/07, 84/08, 08/09, 49/09, 22/09) that an employer may direct the enterprise, including shift changes, where the decision is lawful and reasonable; the slump, the idle noon shift and the new outside orders made the purpose the stability of the enterprise, and the workers themselves conceded the need to reorganise. [Para 57][Para 60][Para 61][Para 62] On transport, clause 6 of Prakas No. 80 obliges an employer with night work to provide a rest place or safe transport home; the truck satisfied this except for alleys it cannot enter, so the panel urged the parties to discuss a solution rather than order reassignment. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67]

On Issue 2, the panel held under Article 116, paragraph 5 that wages must be paid within 48 hours of termination, and that 13th month's wages for 2008 accrued while the workers were still employed, so dismissal before 10 April 2009 did not extinguish them; Article 120 gives a three-year limitation. [Para 70][Para 72][Para 73] The employer did not dispute the nine-worker list received on 8 July 2009, and the panel found the debt owing. [Para 40][Para 73][Para 74]

On Issue 3, the panel held that an undetermined-duration contract may be terminated at will under Article 74 but only for a valid reason tied to aptitude or behaviour, and that the employer gave none. [Para 81][Para 82][Para 83] Reviewing the "bullshit" incident, it found three possible meanings and held that treating the remark as serious misconduct directed at staff was disproportionate, especially as Mr S had accepted the warning and had no prior misconduct. [Para 91][Para 92][Para 93] Exercising its remedial power under clause 34 of Prakas No. 099, it awarded one month's notice pay under Articles 75 and 77 for over two years' service, an Article 89 indemnity of 15 days per year, capped at 30 days for two years, but no Article 91 damages because a reason for termination existed, plus annual leave under Articles 166 and 167 and final wages under Article 116. [Para 95][Para 104][Para 109][Para 110][Para 117][Para 123]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may change shifts, including restarting a night shift, where an economic slump and new orders make the change lawful and reasonable, but it must then provide the night-work rest place or safe transport that clause 6 of Prakas No. 80 requires; 13th month's wages accrue during employment and survive dismissal, and a single offensive remark made on leaving a disciplinary meeting, without prior misconduct, is not serious misconduct justifying dismissal without notice. [Para 61][Para 65][Para 72][Para 92]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel encouraged the parties to negotiate a transport arrangement for workers whose houses sit in alleys the company truck cannot enter. [Para 67]
