# Award No. 39/05 — Wing Tai 2 Apparel (Cambodia) Limited (Arbitration Council, 20 July 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Ly Tayseng (employer-chosen), An Nan (worker-chosen), Ang Eng Thong (chair) · Doc ID 140781

## 1. Facts
Company Wing Tai 2 Apparel (Cambodia) Limited operates at #4, National Road 5, Sangkat Russey Keo, Khan Russey Keo, Phnom Penh [Para 4]. The worker party is the Dhama Thpaktay Federation Union [Para 6]. On 2 March 2005 the company notified the workers of an arrangement first made in 2004 for them to take their annual leave from 11 to 19 April 2005, and the workers took that leave without objection [Para 10]. On 6 June 2005 the workers complained to the Labour Inspector at Russey Keo district that the arrangement was unlawful, and the Inspector attempted settlement on 8 June and again on 24 June 2005 [Para 10]. Settlement succeeded on two of the three issues; the remaining demand is that the company reimburse five days of annual leave, the workers arguing that the company scheduled their 2004 leave at Khmer New Year when there was no work for them to do [Para 10]. Conciliation failed on 30 June 2005 and non-conciliation report number 907/MoLVT reached the Secretariat the same day [Para 9][Para 10]. The hearing was held on 8 July 2005 [Para 10].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must reimburse the five days of 2004 annual leave which it arranged for the workers to take in April 2005, on the ground that the leave fell in a period when the company had no work? [Para 10]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, Dhama Thpaktay Federation Union):** the company arranged for the workers to use their 2004 annual leave during Khmer New Year while there was no work, so it must compensate them for the five days used; the workers said they never agreed to the arrangement, which is why they filed with the Labour Inspector [Para 10][Para 11].
**Employer (Wing Tai 2 Apparel (Cambodia) Limited):** an agreement with the workers, and with four of the five unions in the factory, governs the timing of leave, and the company complied with Article 170 of the Labour Law; no worker had complained directly about the arrangement before [Para 11][Para 13].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the workers' demand to be reimbursed five days of annual leave which the company arranged for them to take in April 2005 [Para 21].
The panel treated Article 170 of the Labour Law as the governing rule and quoted it: in principle annual leave is normally given for the Khmer New Year unless there is a different agreement between the employer and the worker, and the employer must inform the Labour Inspector of any such arrangement [Para 14][Para 15]. It read that provision as creating a presumption in favour of Khmer New Year and as giving both sides a right to negotiate the timing, the employer needing to protect production and the needs of the business while employees may request leave when they wish [Para 16]. Where either side wants leave taken at another time there must be an agreement, a proposition the panel supported with 62/04-Ecent and 21/05-Sinomax [Para 17]. On the facts it found the company had complied with Article 170 by granting the leave at Khmer New Year; the employees did not object when the direction was given, did not propose another time, and in fact took the leave then [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20]. The demand for compensation for the five days was therefore rejected [Para 21]. The award is non-binding and becomes binding eight days after notification unless a party lodges written opposition with the Secretariat [Para 21].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Under Article 170 of the Labour Law annual leave is presumed to be taken at Khmer New Year, and an employer that gives notice of a leave arrangement for that period, informs the Labour Inspector, and whose workers take the leave without objecting has complied with the article; a later demand to be paid again for those days fails for want of any agreement fixing a different time [Para 14][Para 17][Para 18].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that an employer must protect production while employees may request leave when they wish is a statement of the two competing rights under Article 170 and did not itself decide the case [Para 16].
