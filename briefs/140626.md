# Award No. 70/07 — LA Garment (Arbitration Council, 21 August 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phyrum, Tuon Siphann, Kong Phallack) · Doc ID 140626

## 1. Facts
LA Garment employs about 4,500 workers in two buildings, LA 1 and LA 2, and six unions operate there, none with most representative status; the claimant, Khmer Youth Trade Union (KYTU) at LA Garment, has about 1,500 members [Para 31][Para 33][Para 34]. Of 16 demands, five were conciliated before the hearing and three more at it, leaving Issues 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8 and 9 [Para 24][Para 25][Para 26]. On 9 May 2007 the company dismissed 50 night-shift workers and by agreement of 21 May 2007 reinstated all 50, but offered the seven specialised tailors ordinary posts in a different unit [Para 52][Para 55].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether workers already paid above US$50 must also receive the US$5 increase of the minimum wage? [Para 34]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay an annual bonus of US$50 at each year end? [Para 36]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must add US$5 to the attendance bonus? [Para 37]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must not deduct wages and the attendance bonus for one day's leave without permission? [Para 39]
5. (Issue 6) Whether the company must allow workers to leave at 3:00 p.m. on Saturday? [Para 46]
6. (Issue 7) Whether the company must pay wages and termination payments within 48 hours of resignation? [Para 46]
7. (Issue 8) Whether the company must pay three months' wages to workers taking maternity leave? [Para 49]
8. (Issue 9) Whether the company owes further termination payments to the 50 dismissed workers and must reinstate the seven tailors to their former posts? [Para 52]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), Khmer Youth Trade Union (KYTU) at LA Garment:** the unit chiefs paid above US$50 were treated unfairly by exclusion from the increase, and the union pressed the US$50 annual bonus, a higher attendance bonus and relief from deductions for a day's unauthorised leave [Para 34][Para 36][Para 37][Para 39]. It also sought earlier Saturday release, 48-hour final payment, three months' maternity wage and full termination payments plus reinstatement of the seven tailors [Para 46][Para 49][Para 58].

**Employer (respondent), LA Garment:** it has complied with Notification 745/06 and cannot afford further increases, having lost heavily through frequent strikes [Para 35][Para 36]. Its Internal Work Rules and the collective Memorandum of Understanding of 10 March 2005 govern attendance and absence, and it says probationary workers are owed neither notice nor benefits [Para 38][Para 59].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel declined to consider Issues 1, 2, 3, 6 and the medical fee claim, rejected Issues 4, 9A and 9B, and ordered the company to pay final wages within 48 hours of resignation, to pay 15 workers their maternity allowance before leave begins, and to reinstate the seven tailors to their former posts [Para 152][Para 153][Para 159].

Issues 1, 2, 3 and 6 were all treated as interest demands. Notification 745/06 fixes a minimum of US$50 and does not require a further US$5 for those already above it, following Award 33/07 Gold Fame; the annual bonus and the extra attendance bonus appear in no law or agreement [Para 67][Para 69][Para 79]. Because an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement for a year under Article 43 of Prakas 099/04, the panel held only a most representative union under Article 277 may bring one, and declined [Para 71][Para 75][Para 78].

On Issue 4 the panel upheld proportional deduction of wages under Article 6 of the Internal Work Rules, and held that Notification 017/00 and Article 3 of the collective Memorandum of Understanding reward regular attendance rather than protect wilful absence, following Awards 44/07 Winner Knitting and 63/07 Phnom Penh Garment [Para 90][Para 92][Para 95][Para 100].

On Issue 7 the panel found the single-accountant excuse no answer to Article 116(5), which requires wage and all indemnity within 48 hours of termination, and ordered the practice stopped [Para 110][Para 112]. On Issue 8 it held Article 183 sets the maternity entitlement but not the timing, and that Article 115(3) and Awards 57/06 Evergreen and 97/06 New Max require payment before leave begins [Para 114][Para 119][Para 121].

On Issue 9 the notice claim failed because Article 82 excuses notice for probationary contracts, and the annual-leave and premature-termination claims failed for want of any contract, start date or wage evidence, the panel relying on Clause 25 of Prakas 099/04 [Para 126][Para 135][Para 137]. The medical fee was outside the non-conciliation report under Article 312 [Para 143]. But "reinstate" means re-employment in the same position, so the offer of ordinary posts did not satisfy the 21 May 2007 agreement and the seven tailors were ordered reinstated to their own posts [Para 147][Para 151].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand exceeding what the Labour Law or an agreement provides is an interest dispute, which only a union with most representative status under Article 277 may bring, since an award on it would displace the collective agreement for a year [Para 71][Para 75][Para 78]. Final wages and all termination indemnity fall due within 48 hours of termination under Article 116(5), whatever administrative difficulty the employer pleads, and maternity allowance must be paid before leave begins [Para 110][Para 119]. Where an agreement promises reinstatement, the employer must restore the same position, not a substitute post, even if no vacancy exists [Para 147][Para 150].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that unauthorised absence can disrupt a production line because replacements cannot be organised in time [Para 102].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The case number is printed "70107", missing its separator [Para 1]. The collective Memorandum of Understanding is dated 10 June 2005 in the exhibit list and 10 March 2005 throughout the reasons [Para 26][Para 42]. The decision writes "3:00 a.m. on Saturdays" for a demand of 3:00 p.m. [Para 153]. The panel cites Article 123 of the Code of Civil Procedure and then refers to Article 124(1) for the same rule [Para 128][Para 133]. Its reference to Award 66/07 gives that award the name of a different company [Para 143]. The order numbering breaks, Issue 4 being labelled "Issue 49" [Para 153].
