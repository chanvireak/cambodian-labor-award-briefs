# Award No. 31/13 — Sippakam Phsar Ta El (Arbitration Council, 20 February 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (You Soun Ty, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chea, chair) · Doc ID 139643

## 1. Facts
Sippakam Phsar Ta El is a garment-sewing workshop at Ta Nu village, Saang, Kandal province, employing 98 staff. [Para 52] The Federation of Union Hopeful Workers was the claimant, and the workshop union held its leadership election on 11 December 2012, at which Ms N was elected president, Ms M vice-president and Ms S secretary. [Para 37][Para 71] Notice of the leadership composition was handed to the employer on 13 December 2012, refused, and posted the same day by EMS. [Para 66][Para 67] The employer dismissed all three on 21 January 2013, hours after they filed a complaint at the Kandal provincial labour department. [Para 77][Para 103] The employer did not appear at the hearing and gave no reason. [Para 34]

## 2. Issues in dispute
19. Whether the employer must take back the three elected union leaders, president, vice-president and secretary, into employment? [Para 55][Para 86]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The three were never warned, were elected on 11 December 2012, and were dismissed only because of their union activity; the employer told them on 15 January 2013 to leave the union within a week. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 74] They invoke the protection of articles 266 to 279 of the Labour Law. [Para 84]
**Employer (respondent):** Absent from the hearing and from the conciliation; at conciliation the employer stated it had no position on the dispute. [Para 77][Para 85]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to take back Ms N, Ms M and Ms S into employment. [Para 127]

As a threshold matter, the panel held it could proceed in the employer's absence: article 21 of Prakas No. 099/04 provides that where a party duly invited fails to appear without valid reason, the panel may continue or may close the arbitration by issuing an award, and the panel followed Awards 53/04-Kuong Hong and 148/07-Bay Hi in hearing the case ex parte. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 35] Of the 19 claims referred, the workers withdrew 18 at the hearing, leaving only claim 19. [Para 30][Para 31]

On the merits, article 293 of the Labour Law requires the labour inspector's consent before a staff delegate or candidate is dismissed, and Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 extends that protection to candidates in a union leadership election, for 45 days before the ballot and, for losers, 45 days after it; the union must notify the employer of the candidates by any reliable means. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 90] The panel applied its three-condition test from Awards 71/09 and 09/10-Chung Hao: the worker must be of the protected class, the dismissal must fall within the protection period, and the union must have notified the employer. [Para 93]

All three conditions were met. Because the union had lodged its registration application and held the ministry receipt, its founders and elected leaders counted as protected, and the panel noted that under Prakas 305 article 4 protection runs from the date of application until 30 days after registration. [Para 94][Para 100] The election was on 11 December 2012 and the dismissals on 21 January 2013, inside the 45-day window. [Para 104] Notice was given in person and by post on 13 December 2012, both reliable means, and the employer's refusal to accept the letter did not defeat it. [Para 107][Para 108] Since the employer produced no evidence of having sought the labour inspector's authorisation, the dismissal was procedurally unlawful. [Para 112]

The panel went on to find anti-union discrimination under articles 12 and 279: the employer had told the three to leave the union within a week, then dismissed them the same day they complained to the labour department, which showed it did not want the union formed in its workshop. [Para 124][Para 125][Para 126]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that has applied for registration may protect its elected leaders, and an employer that dismisses them inside the 45-day post-election window without the labour inspector's prior authorisation must reinstate them, the employer's refusal to accept the notification letter being irrelevant once notice was given by a reliable means. [Para 108][Para 112][Para 127]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that a dismissal timed to coincide with the leaders' complaint to the labour department, following a demand that they quit the union, itself evidences anti-union motive. [Para 124][Para 125]
