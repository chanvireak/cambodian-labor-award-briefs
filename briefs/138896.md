# Award No. 007/17 — Arin Manufacture & Cherring (Khmer) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 10 February 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ri for the employer party, Sin Kim Sean for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 138896

## 1. Facts
Arin Manufacture & Cherring (Khmer) Co., Ltd weaves textiles in Prey Veng province and employs about 1,870 workers. [Para 44][Para 45] Ms H, the deputy president of the plant's Democratic Union of Khmer Garment Workers (a C.CAWDU affiliate registered with the Ministry on 20 January 2016), had worked there since 20 May 2013 on a chain of three-month fixed-duration contracts; the last expired on 19 October 2016. [Para 46][Para 50][Para 51] She refused to thumbprint a further three-month contract, relying on an earlier award holding her contract indefinite, and on 25 October 2016 the company's security guards barred her from entering the factory. [Para 53][Para 64][Para 116] Her claim was for reinstatement with seniority, wage and benefits preserved, back wages from dismissal to return, and an order that the contract be indefinite. [Para 49] No most representative union exists at the plant; the claim was pressed by the union's three recognised officers for Ms H alone. [Para 47][Para 93][Para 94][Para 95]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take Ms H back with her seniority, wage and other benefits preserved and pay her wages from the date of dismissal until she resumes work, and whether it must conclude an indefinite-duration contract with her? [Para 49][Para 96][Para 117]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** successive fixed-duration contracts totalling more than two years had already converted to an indefinite contract under Article 67 of the Labour Law, as the panel held in the earlier case concerning her, so barring her entry was a dismissal of a protected union officer carried out without the labour inspector's authorisation. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63][Para 97]
**Employer (respondent):** weaving work brings no full-year orders, so the plant never uses indefinite contracts; each three-month contract was settled in full, including the 5 per cent end-of-contract indemnity, wages and accrued leave, so there was no dismissal — she simply never signed the renewal. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57][Para 58][Para 66]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to take Ms H back with her seniority, wage and other benefits preserved and to pay her wages from the date of dismissal until the date she resumes work; the claim for an order that an indefinite-duration contract be signed was refused as already decided. [Para 156][Para 157]

The panel first settled who could bring the claim. Because the plant has no most representative union, it applied the earlier ruling that workers may represent themselves or delegate to a union registered under Article 11 of the Trade Union Law; the three officers' registration and the Ministry's recognition letters satisfied that, and since the union claimed only for Ms H the panel decided only her case. [Para 77][Para 89][Para 92][Para 93][Para 95] On the contract, the panel found the last contract had expired on 19 October 2016 but that the chain of renewals exceeded two years, which under Article 67 of the Labour Law converts it to an indefinite contract; it followed Award No. 192/16 between the same parties. [Para 51][Para 69][Para 99][Para 100] The employer's argument that the 5 per cent indemnity under Article 73 paid at each expiry kept the contract fixed-term was rejected: the indemnity falls due when the employment relationship ends, and paying it early cannot bar the statutory conversion. [Para 111][Para 112][Para 113] Barring her at the gate on 25 October 2016 was therefore a dismissal, not a non-renewal. [Para 116] Because she was a union officer, Articles 67 and 43 of the Trade Union Law applied, and the employer conceded it never sought the labour inspector's authorisation before dismissing her; the panel held the dismissal procedurally unlawful. [Para 124][Para 127][Para 131][Para 132] On remedy it relied on Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, which allows an order to reinstate a dismissed worker in the old post and to pay sums due, and reinstated her with back wages. [Para 133][Para 135][Para 136] The separate request for an indefinite contract order was refused on res judicata: the parties, the issue and a fully reasoned decision were all identical to Award No. 192/16, whose three conditions the panel found met. [Para 142][Para 146][Para 150][Para 153][Para 154]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A chain of fixed-duration contracts whose combined duration exceeds two years converts to an indefinite-duration contract under Article 67 of the Labour Law, and the employer's payment of the 5 per cent end-of-contract indemnity at each expiry does not prevent that conversion, because the indemnity is owed only when the relationship actually ends. [Para 99][Para 112][Para 113] Refusing a union officer entry to the workplace is a dismissal, and a dismissal of a person protected under Article 67 of the Trade Union Law is unlawful unless the labour inspector authorised it beforehand, whatever the employer's contractual intention; the remedy is reinstatement with seniority and back wages under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 116][Para 124][Para 136] Where the same parties bring the same issue already decided on the merits, the panel will decline to decide it again. [Para 142][Para 154]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the absence of a most representative union could obstruct workers' access to collective dispute resolution, and that workers may represent themselves or delegate to any registered union, were general observations on representation and did not determine the outcome, which turned on the union officer's own claim. [Para 80][Para 81]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award's face names the employer "Arin Manufacture & Cherring (Khmer) Co., Ltd", but the union's own filings and several findings call it "Orange Manufacturing (Khmer) Co., Ltd" (paragraphs 3, 30, 31, 32, 33, 38); the two names appear to be a carry-over from a template or a related company and the discrepancy is unresolved in the text.
- Paragraph 152 breaks off mid-sentence ("...កម្មករ និយោជិត ចំនួន ១៣") inside the res judicata reasoning, so the third condition is only partly printed.
- The worker's name is printed as ហ៊ិន ស្រី អាំ, ហ៊ុន ស្រី អាំ and ហ៊ុន.ស្រអាំ in different passages, including within one paragraph 92.
- Paragraph 129 cites "Articles 67 and 43 of the Labour Law" where the quoted provisions are Articles 67 and 43 of the Trade Union Law; the reasoning at 123–127 is consistent with the latter.
- OCR losses: paragraphs 124, 139 and 142 contain broken syntax and stray characters ("រាល់ ការបញ្ឈប់ នៅ កម្មករ", "Ximanosshia"); paragraphs 84 and 120 carry stray numerals ("ភាព តំណាង ៤ បំផុត", "មាន ៤ ប្រធាន"); paragraph 13 repeats the year as "២០ ២០១៧".
- The award date is 10 February 2017, but paragraph 20 records that the parties extended the deadline from 6 February 2017 to that date; the hearing is recorded as 24 January 2017 at 2:00 pm while paragraph 14 gives "ម៉ោង ០២ និង 00 នាទី រសៀល".
- Evidence items 8 (paragraph 24) and the foreign-language document at paragraph 28 are untranslated, so their content cannot be assessed.
