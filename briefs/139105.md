# Award No. 055/19 — Cambodian Tech Style World (Phnom Penh) Work Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 1 August 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Pen Bun Cham chair) · Doc ID 139105

## 1. Facts
Cambodian Tech Style World (Phnom Penh) Work Co Ltd, a garment factory at Kbal Damrei village, Kakab commune, Po Sen Chey district, Phnom Penh, employed about 1,400 workers. [Para 45] On 25 June 2019 twenty workers complained to the Labour Disputes Department on ten working conditions; conciliation on 8 July 2019 settled six, and the four remaining points were referred to the Arbitration Council. [Para 15][Para 17] At the hearing the parties agreed on point 4, so the panel considered only three claims: a lunch allowance, a travel and accommodation increase, and a higher attendance bonus. [Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must provide a lunch allowance of 4,000 riel, paid weekly, to the workers? [Para 49]
2. Whether the employer must raise the travel and accommodation allowance by USD 8, to a total of USD 15 a month? [Para 54]
3. Whether the employer must raise the regular attendance bonus (ប្រាក់រង្វាន់ធ្វើការទៀងទាត់, "prime") to USD 15 a month? [Para 58]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** other factories pay 1,000 riel and some 2,000 riel a day for lunch, and the allowance would encourage workers to work harder; transport and rent have risen; other factories pay an attendance bonus above the legal minimum. [Para 51][Para 60]
**Employer (respondent):** no law requires a lunch allowance, and none requires it here; the company already complies with Ministry of Labour Notification No. 230, which sets USD 7 for travel and lodging and USD 10 for the attendance bonus. [Para 53][Para 62]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider all three claims, without awarding any sum; the award is non-binding and would become final after 8 days without written objection. [Para 121][Para 124]

The panel treated all three claims as interest disputes (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍), finding no provision of the Labour Law, no collective agreement, no internal rule, no contract and no past practice obliging the employer to pay the lunch allowance, the extra USD 8 or the higher bonus, and recording that the employer was paying exactly what Notification No. 230 prescribes. [Para 83][Para 107] Having classified the claims as interests rather than rights, the panel turned to admissibility: an award on an interest dispute substitutes for a collective agreement and is effective for one year, so it would make all the workers lose their right to a collective agreement and their right to strike over that claim. [Para 97] It followed that the Council may resolve an interest dispute only where the union bringing it is the most-representative union, or one of several local unions entitled to conclude a collective agreement under Articles 54 and 72 of the Law on Trade Unions. [Para 98] Because no most-representative union existed at the factory and the claimant did not meet those conditions, the panel declined to consider the claims. [Para 102]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An interest dispute — a claim for a benefit above the legal minimum with no basis in a collective agreement, internal rule, contract or established practice — may be brought to the panel only by a most-representative union, or by a local union entitled to conclude a collective agreement under Articles 54 and 72 of the Law on Trade Unions; where the claimant lacks that status the panel will not consider the claim at all, because an award would replace the collective agreement for a year and strip every worker in the enterprise of the right to bargain and to strike on that subject. [Para 97][Para 98]

**Obiter dicta:** the findings that the three claims were "above the law" and that the employer was already complying with Notification No. 230 are merits findings that did not decide the case, since the claims were refused on standing. [Para 107]
