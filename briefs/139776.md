# Award No. 327/14 — Green Thine International (Cambodia) PLC (Arbitration Council, 30 December 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuch Hun, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · Doc ID 139776

## 1. Facts
The company is a garment factory in Chom Chao, Phnom Penh, with about 5,308 workers, some 95% of them women [Para 77][Para 78][Para 124]. Two federations brought the dispute — the Union Federation for Workers and the Cambodia Workers' Support Union — but neither factory union was registered with the Ministry; the most representative union in the enterprise is the Cambodian Garment Workers' Development Union [Para 79][Para 83]. Fifteen workers attended, including four staff delegates [Para 80][Para 82]. Of 22 demands, nine were settled at the hearing and four withdrawn, leaving issues 1, 2, 3, 7, 11, 13, 14, 16 and 17 [Para 35][Para 36]. A strike ran from 20 to 30 October 2014 with over 90% participation and ended after a court injunction of 27 October [Para 130][Para 138].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must preserve the USD 5 weekly share of the attendance bonus for the week in which a pregnant worker attends an antenatal check? [Para 147]
2. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of 4,000 riel a day? [Para 165]
3. Whether the employer must pay a USD 10 monthly incentive to workers who attend 26 days? [Para 172]
7. Whether the employer must raise the holiday and Sunday overtime meal allowance by 2,000 riel, to 4,000 riel in total? [Para 179]
11. Whether the employer must provide two sets of work clothing per worker each year? [Para 220]
13. Whether the employer must deduct the attendance bonus only in proportion to days of absence properly authorised by the employer? [Para 233]
14. Whether the employer must raise the travel and accommodation allowance by USD 3, from USD 7 to USD 10 a month? [Para 279]
16. Whether the employer must set up a nursery (ទារកដ្ឋាន) in the company? [Para 287]
17. Whether the employer must preserve wages and the attendance bonus during the strike of 20 to 30 October 2014? [Para 299]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** A pregnant worker who must travel for a check-up should not lose the USD 5 weekly bonus on top of the trip's cost [Para 87][Para 88]. ILO and Ministry findings of worker anaemia justify a 4,000 riel lunch allowance, and other factories pay 500 to 2,000 riel [Para 94][Para 96]. Nineteen of the workers are women of childbearing age, so a nursery is needed rather than the USD 5 childcare payment [Para 124][Para 126]. The employer knew of the strike in advance and must not strip wages for it [Para 132][Para 133].

**Employer (respondent):** It cannot meet the demands; it already pays above the law, including a USD 20 monthly attendance bonus paid in four weekly USD 5 parts, and 4,000 riel meal money for overtime [Para 85][Para 97][Para 102]. It offers USD 5 a month in place of a nursery, which some 200 women take up, and would build one only if the majority asked [Para 125][Para 127][Para 129]. The strike was not properly notified and the employer received no notice before it began [Para 139][Para 143].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issues 11, 13 and 16 were granted; issues 1, 2, 3, 7, 14 and 17 were dismissed [Para 326][Para 330][Para 331][Para 333][Para 335]

The panel treated the benefit claims as interest disputes. On issue 1 it found no law, collective agreement, internal rule or practice obliging the employer to keep the bonus during an antenatal visit, so the claim exceeded the legal floor and, the applicant not being the most representative union, was refused [Para 151][Para 152][Para 163]. Issues 2 and 3 followed identically: no instrument required a 4,000 riel lunch payment or a USD 10 attendance incentive, and the union lacked standing [Para 166][Para 167][Para 173][Para 174]. On issue 7 the panel held that holiday work is not overtime — it earns one extra day's wage, not the overtime rate — and that the 2,000 riel meal money already paid was more favourable than the law, so the claim failed on standing grounds [Para 184][Para 186][Para 191][Para 197]. Issue 14 likewise failed: USD 7 matched point 1 of Notification No. 230 and nothing required USD 10 [Para 280][Para 281][Para 286].

Issue 11 succeeded because point 6 of Prakas No. 307 of 14 December 2007 requires the employer to supply at least two sets of work clothing a year free of charge, and a Prakas binds the parties; the employer's argument that some workers decline to wear the clothing was no answer [Para 222][Para 230][Para 231][Para 232].

Issue 13 succeeded on the panel's settled reading of the attendance bonus: the bonus under Notification No. 230 is mandatory, Article 103 makes it a form of wage, and Articles 71(6) and 72(1) suspend the contract during authorised absence, so the employer may deduct only in proportion to the days of authorised absence, not the whole amount [Para 242][Para 268][Para 271][Para 274][Para 276].

Issue 16 succeeded on Article 186: an enterprise with at least 100 women workers must provide a breastfeeding room and a nursery, and paying USD 5 in lieu does not discharge that duty; where a child is over 18 months the mother may use an outside nursery at the employer's expense against receipts [Para 287][Para 289][Para 295][Para 296][Para 297].

Issue 17 failed. Under Article 332 the contract is suspended on strike days and no wage is payable; the panel also found the notice of 13 October 2014 fixed no date for the strike, contrary to Article 324, so the stoppage was unlawful and no bonus was owed for those days [Para 299][Para 300][Para 318][Para 324].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a Prakas imposes a specific obligation — two sets of work clothing a year, or a breastfeeding room and nursery in an enterprise with at least 100 women — the employer cannot escape it by showing that some workers decline the benefit or that it pays cash in lieu. [Para 230][Para 295]

The mandatory attendance bonus is a wage, and because authorised absence suspends the contract the employer may reduce it only in proportion to the days of authorised absence; but no wage or bonus is owed for days of an unlawful strike, the contract being suspended on those days. [Para 276][Para 300][Para 324]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that work on a public holiday is not overtime and attracts one extra day's wage rather than the overtime rate [Para 184][Para 186], and that a strike notice which names no date does not satisfy Article 324 [Para 318].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company name varies between "Green Thine International (Cambodia) PLC", "Regren Thin", "Agren Thin" and "Then Thine" [Para 1][Para 45][Para 68]. The worker-appointed arbitrator is "An Non" in the composition and "An Lean" in the signature block, and the employer-appointed signature is obliterated entirely [Para 1][Para 336]. The workforce figure is printed as 5,308, which is implausible for the facts recited but is reproduced as printed [Para 78][Para 292]. The record is inconsistent on the number of demands: 22 were conciliated, of which nine were settled and four withdrawn, yet only nine remained for decision [Para 30][Para 35][Para 36]. Paragraph 337 prints the Prakas date as "21 April 2019 2004", paragraph 157 likewise, and paragraph 13 of the earlier cited award carries a stray year [Para 157][Para 336]. The claim that "19" rather than 95% of workers are women appears at para 124 against para 292. Individual workers and representatives are redacted here as the project requires; the arbitrators are named as printed.
