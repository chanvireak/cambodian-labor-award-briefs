# Award No. 23/06 — Maxpearl Garment Factory (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 27 March 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach, Liv Sovanna, Kong Phallack as chair) · Doc ID 140692

## 1. Facts
Maxpearl Garment Factory (Cambodia) Ltd, at Prey Tear Village, Sangkat Choam Chao, Khan Dangkor, Phnom Penh, had operated since the beginning of 2004 and employed approximately 500 workers; out of those, the employer paid for the medical checks of 190, and the rest either paid for their own checks or had not yet been examined. [Para 4][Para 29][Para 30]
The employer party was represented by a lawyer, a chief administrator and two GMAC representatives; the worker party was the Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU) and the local union of KYFTU at Maxpearl Garment Factory (Cambodia). [Para 4][Para 9]
Nine demands were lodged on 6 February 2006, four were conciliated on 28 February 2006 and four more were settled at the hearings of 22 and 23 March 2006, leaving the medical examination fee as the only issue for decision. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the company must reimburse the 10,100 riel medical check fee to each worker who paid it personally and bear the cost of the check for workers not yet examined? [Para 31][Para 40]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The demand rested on Article 247 of the Labour Law and Prakas 09/94 on physical examinations: the company should refund the 10,100 riel to those who paid out of their own pocket and send the rest for examination at its own expense. [Para 32]
**Employer (respondent):** The company refused, arguing that the Labour Law gives no clear answer on who bears examination expenses and that no new Prakas has set a rule on the cost. [Para 33][Para 34]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to reimburse the medical expense of 10,100 riel to the workers who paid it themselves and to pay that expense for the workers who had not yet been examined. [Para 40]

The panel began from Article 247(c) of the Labour Law of 1997, which it read as requiring the employer to pay its workers' medical expenses and as contemplating a Prakas on the conditions of pre-employment, periodical and special examinations, at the employer's expense. [Para 35] It held that the absence of a new Prakas did not suspend the statutory duty: Article 247 itself supplied a sufficient basis for the obligation, including the examination required on recruitment of new staff, and Article 247(c) showed that any future Prakas would confirm the employer's liability rather than create it. [Para 35][Para 36]
The panel reviewed Joint Prakas 09/94, made under the 1992 Labour Law, whose clause 7 requires an enterprise to cover the cost of medical checks; it noted its earlier awards on the point, that the panel's opinions differ on whether Prakas 09/94 survives the 1997 Law, and that its own most recent position, supported by 60/04-United Art with a dissenting opinion, dispenses with the Prakas and relies on Article 247. [Para 37][Para 38]
It accordingly followed 60/04-United Art, 64/04 Mercury Garment, 98/04-Great Union, 106/04-Suit Way, 05/05-G.H.G. and 05/06-W&D, and concluded that Article 247 of the Labour Law of 1997 is sufficient legal basis to require the employer to pay the examination expense of workers before recruiting them. [Para 39][Para 40]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer's duty to bear the cost of its workers' medical examinations, including the pre-employment examination, arises directly from Article 247 of the Labour Law of 1997 and does not depend on a Prakas being issued; the absence of the Prakas the article contemplates leaves the statutory obligation intact and enforceable. [Para 35][Para 36]
A worker who has already paid for an examination the employer was bound to pay for is entitled to reimbursement of the sum advanced, while workers not yet examined are entitled to be examined at the employer's cost. [Para 40]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remarks on the unsettled question whether Joint Prakas 09/94 remains in force, and its reference to the dissent attached to 60/04-United Art, were not necessary to the order, which rested on Article 247 alone. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The demand is printed as "10,1000 riel" in the issues section but as 10,100 riel in the facts, the reasons and the order; the brief uses 10,100 riel, the figure repeated in the operative part. [Para 17][Para 30][Para 40] The panel never numbers the issue it decides: the worker party's list numbers the breastfeeding demand first and the medical fee second, and the award records only that Issues 1, 3, 4 and 5 were conciliated at the hearing, so the remaining issue is identified here as Issue 2 by elimination. [Para 15][Para 22] OCR drops word-initial letters throughout ("thecompany", "themployer", "athearing") and two separate entries repeat the chief administrator's name. [Para 6][Para 7]
