# Award No. 297/14 — Ken Sport Shoe (Arbitration Council, 24 November 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ieng Sothy, employer-appointed; An Nan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, presiding) · Doc ID 139748

## 1. Facts
Ken Sport Shoe, a footwear factory at Chamkar Svay village, Sethey commune, Samakki Mon Chey district, Kampong Chhnang, employs about 5,400 workers. The factory-level Free Workers' Union, registered in 2013, filed 18 demands in October 2014; 15 were settled in conciliation on 17 October 2014 and three remained. At the hearings of 31 October and 11 November 2014 the workers withdrew points 2 and 3, leaving only the demand about the timing of payslips. [Para 1][Para 12][Para 13][Para 45][Para 17][Para 18]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must issue the monthly payslip one week before payday, so that workers can check their wages before the tenth of the month? [Para 2][Para 48]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** workers need the payslip three days before payday, on the seventh of the month, to verify the amounts they will receive; other factories such as Chin Yine, M W and Long Lead already do this. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53]
**Employer (respondent):** payroll is computed by computer, then split into packets and handed to team leaders to pay out on the tenth; issuing slips a week early would add work to the administration and finance sections, and it is not possible. [Para 54][Para 55]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the claim; the award is non-binding. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 71]

The panel recorded the existing practice as agreed by both sides: on payday, the tenth of each month, the employer prepares an itemised payslip for each worker showing the wages received that month, and a worker who believes the calculation is wrong may raise it on that day, with the employer reviewing the complaint. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50]

It then applied Article 112 of the Labour Law, which requires the employer to ensure information about all of a worker's wages for each pay period is clear, certain and easy to understand whenever those wages may change. Following Award No. 05/06-DWND, the panel read that provision as an obligation of clear notification, not of advance notification, and held Article 112 does not require the employer to give wage information before payday. Since an itemised payslip was in fact issued on payday, the employer had complied. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 63][Para 64][Para 67][Para 68]

The panel added that itemised payslips serve transparency and dispel workers' suspicions about their pay, but that this is a merit of the practice, not a legal deadline. The claim was therefore refused in full. [Para 63][Para 69][Para 70]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Article 112 of the Labour Law obliges the employer to give each worker clear and comprehensible information about the wages for every pay period, but it fixes no deadline before payday: an itemised payslip handed over on the pay date, with a channel to challenge miscalculation then, satisfies the article, and a demand for earlier delivery is a benefit beyond the law rather than an enforceable right. [Para 57][Para 64][Para 68]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that transparency in payslips removes workers' doubts about their wages, and that other named factories had chosen to deliver slips three days early — a practice the panel noted without treating it as a legal standard. [Para 53][Para 63]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The conciliation figures do not reconcile: Para 12 records 28 points discussed with 15 settled out of 18 demands, while Para 13 speaks of three unresolved points. The factory operating permit is dated 11 August 2011 in the findings but 11 August 2014 in the evidence list. Para 20 is garbled, losing the document-submission deadline. Para 51 and Para 70 contain OCR-garbled Latin and Khmer fragments that obscure the end of the employer's practice description and repeat the dispositive twice. The union's name appears as "Ken Sport Shoe" and as "Ken Span Su" in consecutive paragraphs. Individual workers, union officers and the employer's representative (named in Latin script) are redacted here.
