# Award No. 152/11 — Rilay Able South Industries (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 30 December 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ri, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Pen Bunhea, chair) · Doc ID 140098

## 1. Facts
Rilay Able South Industries (Cambodia) is a garment manufacturer at Kbal Damrei village, Kakab commune, Dangkao district, Phnom Penh, employing about 2,000 workers. [Para 1][Para 37][Para 38] The Rilay Able Factory Workers Union — registered on 19 July 2011 under certificate No. 2202 and recognised by the Ministry of Labour as a professional organisation — filed the complaint, but the panel found it was not yet the most representative union in the factory. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 39][Para 40] Conciliation on 3 October 2011 failed on all eight demands; at the hearing two were settled and six remained. [Para 19][Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must allow pregnant workers one full day per month for antenatal health checks on full pay and benefits? [Para 41][Para 5]
2. Whether the employer's requirement of six workplace meetings a week, of about 15 minutes each, exceeds its lawful right to manage and organise the enterprise? [Para 49][Para 50]
3. Whether the employer must remove the cartons blocking the Group C entrance and widen that gate for the workers? [Para 60][Para 75]
4. Whether the employer must play music for workers during working hours? [Para 61][Para 103]
5. Whether the employer must give workers one day's notice before overtime from 6:00 pm to 7:40 pm? [Para 64][Para 109]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The half-day the employer grants for antenatal checks is too short, because pregnant women travel with difficulty and wait long at the hospital and cannot get back to work in time. [Para 43][Para 44] Six meetings a week feel coercive and repetitive. [Para 58] Music during working hours would relieve fatigue and lift morale. [Para 61] Notice of overtime a day ahead would let them arrange meals and transport home. [Para 68][Para 69]

**Employer (respondent):** It already grants half a day a month for workers living in or near Phnom Penh and a full day for those coming from the provinces, on production of a medical certificate, and no worker has ever asked for the full day. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47] The meetings are its management right, held in working hours, about quality and discipline, with no penalty for absence. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 59] No law requires it to play music. [Para 62] It cannot give a day's notice because it only knows whether overtime is needed after checking daily output, so it gives three to four hours' notice. [Para 70][Para 71][Para 72]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Partly granted — the panel dismissed the demands on antenatal leave, meetings, music and overtime notice, and recorded that the gate and carton demands had been resolved between the parties. [Para 92][Para 102][Para 108][Para 121][Para 122]

The panel first classified each demand as a rights dispute or an interests dispute. Following its earlier awards, a rights dispute is one grounded in a right set out in the Labour Law, a collective agreement or a collective convention. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 78] Issue 3, on the frequency of meetings, was a rights dispute because it concerned the employer's statutory right to manage and direct the enterprise. [Para 79] The antenatal-leave, music and overtime-notice demands were interests disputes because no law or agreement provided for them. [Para 80]

On the antenatal demand, the panel found nothing in the Labour Law or any agreement granting a full paid day a month, so the demand went beyond what the law gives and was an interests dispute. [Para 81] As a matter of principle the panel declines to consider interests disputes where the claimant union is not the most representative in the factory. [Para 82][Para 84] Article 277 of the 1997 Labour Law requires a union to be registered and to meet the other conditions, and Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 provides that an award resolving an interests dispute stands in place of a collective agreement for one year from the date it becomes final. [Para 86][Para 87][Para 88] The union held a registration certificate but no certificate of most-representative status for the factory, so the demand was dismissed. [Para 90][Para 91][Para 92]

On the meetings, the panel applied Article 2 of the Labour Law, which defines an enterprise as a group of people working together at a set place under the management and direction of an employer. [Para 93][Para 94] It found the six weekly meetings were held in working hours, lasted about 15 minutes, were conducted by team and section heads, and imposed no penalty for absence or lateness. [Para 50][Para 55][Para 57][Para 97][Para 99] The meetings served the employer's management right, were reasonable and did not affect workers' interests, so the demand to cut them to three a week was dismissed. [Para 100][Para 101][Para 102]

On the music demand, the panel found no law, prior practice or agreement requiring the employer to play music, so the demand was an interests dispute and was dismissed for the same want of most-representative status. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 105][Para 108] On overtime notice, the panel found no law requiring a day's notice and no prior agreement or practice, and that the employer never coerced or penalised workers who declined overtime, which rested on voluntary consent. [Para 115][Para 116][Para 117][Para 118][Para 121]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a union that is registered but not certified as most representative in the factory brings an interests dispute — a demand for a benefit beyond what the Labour Law, a collective agreement or a collective convention gives — the panel will decline to consider it, and will not order the employer to grant the benefit. [Para 80][Para 82][Para 91][Para 92]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that an award resolving an interests dispute operates as a collective agreement for one year, and that the right to strike cannot be used to reopen a collective agreement before it expires. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 89]
