# Award No. 125/09 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន វីន ខេម ខប ភើរេសិន and the Khmer Workers' Rights Promotion Union of the factory (Arbitration Council, 9 October 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of three: Ly Tayseng (employer-appointed), Sin Kim Sean (worker-appointed), Kong Phalla (president) · Doc ID 140436

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន វីន ខេម ខប ភើរេសិន, a garment factory on Street 70, Tuol Sangkae, Khan Russey Keo, Phnom Penh with about 600 workers, dismissed the 28 workers of sewing group 4 on 28 March 2009 after they stopped work for a morning to press for sick leave for a colleague. [Para 32][Para 35][Para 38]
The union **សហព័ន្ធ ហជីព-សហជីព លើកស្ទួយសិទ្ធិកម្មករ** (the Khmer Workers' Rights Promotion Union), registered on 14 December 2007 with about 380 members in the plant, brought the dismissed workers' claims for statutory compensation and, separately, for the pro-rating of the attendance bonus. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 4]
Of twelve demands, two remained unresolved after conciliation on 2 September 2009 and were heard on 16 September 2009 with both parties present. [Para 13][Para 16][Para 17]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay severance, notice pay and other statutory compensation to the 28 group 4 workers it dismissed on 28 March 2009, all of whom held open-ended contracts? [Para 35]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pro-rate the attendance bonus to the number of days a worker is absent on sick leave supported by a valid public hospital certificate? [Para 63]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), through the Khmer Workers' Rights Promotion Union:** the 28 workers received only their final wages and unused annual leave, although none of them committed serious misconduct, and they are owed notice pay, moral damages and severance; the dismissal was a response to their demand for leave for a colleague whose child was ill, not to any serious breach, and they had received no warning; on the bonus, the company deducts USD 2 for one day's absence and forfeits the whole USD 7 for a day and a half, even where the absence is authorised sick leave. [Para 37][Para 41][Para 64][Para 65][Para 68]
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន វីន ខេម ខប ភើរេសិន:** the 28 stopped work for a whole morning without notice and without following strike procedure, which halted a production line of 14 teams and prevented timely shipment, so this was serious misconduct under the internal rules and the labour contract; the bonus is paid only to workers who attend regularly for the full month, and pro-rating would unfairly reward those who are absent. [Para 43][Para 48][Para 58][Para 69][Para 72]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the company to pay severance, notice pay in lieu and moral damages to all 28 dismissed workers, and ordered the attendance bonus to be pro-rated to the days of authorised sick absence; the award is non-binding and becomes permanent eight days after notification. [Para 150][Para 151][Para 152]
On issue 1 the panel found that all 28 held open-ended contracts and that Article 74 permits termination only for a valid reason related to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the operation of the enterprise. [Para 74][Para 78]
It then characterised the one-morning stoppage as a strike within Article 318 and found that it did not follow the procedure the Labour Law requires. [Para 81][Para 84][Para 86]
But the panel held that a strike, whether or not it follows procedure, cannot by itself be treated as serious misconduct, because Article 337 reserves the determination of a strike's legality to the courts and no such determination existed; the dismissal therefore had no valid basis. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 91]
It also relied on Articles 332(2) and 333, which require workers to be reinstated at the end of a strike and forbid any sanction on the ground of participation in a strike, and followed Awards 76/04 and 70/04. [Para 92][Para 93][Para 96]
Severance was awarded under Article 89 on the basis of seniority, notice pay under Articles 75 and 77 because the dismissal was immediate, and moral damages under Article 91 because the termination was without valid reason; claims for annual leave and final wages were refused as already paid. [Para 99][Para 104][Para 108][Para 116][Para 122]
On issue 2 the panel applied Notice No. 745 of 23 October 2006, preserving the benefits of Notice No. 017 of 18 July 2000, which guarantees at least USD 5 a month, and noted that this employer pays USD 7 — better than the legal floor; since the company allows paid sick leave but then forfeits the whole bonus, and since Article 71(3) treats certified sick absence as a suspension of the contract, the deduction had to be pro-rated. [Para 127][Para 130][Para 135][Para 143][Para 148]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A strike that does not follow the statutory procedure is still not serious misconduct justifying dismissal unless a court has determined its illegality, so an employer that dismisses strikers owes severance, notice pay and moral damages; and a bonus that forms part of wages must be reduced in proportion to authorised sick absence rather than forfeited in full. [Para 91][Para 96][Para 143]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the employer's USD 7 bonus exceeds the USD 5 floor set by Notice No. 017 and that such an improvement over the legal minimum is commendable, though it does not license forfeiture. [Para 130]
