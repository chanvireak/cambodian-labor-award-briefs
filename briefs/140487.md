# Award No. 22/08 — Hytex Garment (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 5 March 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bunchhea chair; Kao Thach, employer nominee; Tuon Siphann, worker nominee) · Doc ID 140487

## 1. Facts
Hytex Garment (Cambodia) Ltd, on National Road 2, Sangkat Chak Angre Leu, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employs 718 workers, of whom 65 work in the cutting and printing sections and only three hold fixed-duration contracts. [Para 1][Para 23][Para 24] In the middle of October 2007 the company carried all the garments and equipment of those two sections to a new branch, leaving the workers with nothing to do at the old site, and asked them to work at the branch; the workers refused, and the company continued to pay their basic wage, seniority payments and attendance bonus of over 60 USD a month while they sat idle. [Para 26][Para 30][Para 31][Para 32] A joint notification of 6 August 2007, HYC 1408, signed by the company, the union federation and the union's representative, promised that any worker unable to move would be paid termination in accordance with the law, that one month's written notice and settlement would precede a move, and that voluntary principles would apply. [Para 25] An agreement of 25 January 2008 recorded in Award No. 08/08, issue 7, said the company would not change the workplace from the old location to the new one. [Para 33][Para 36] Conciliation on 11 February 2008 failed and the hearing was held on 15 February 2008. [Para 17][Para 19]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue) Whether the company must keep the cutting and printing sections working at the old location, and, if it requires those workers to work at another branch, whether it must first terminate the existing contracts and pay severance under the Labour Law? [Para 14][Para 40][Para 41]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers Democratic Union (C.CAWDU) Local Union of Hytex Company, through its deputy secretary, president and section workers, argued that the company had work for both sections but sent it to outsiders and quietly moved the equipment out without notice; all 65 workers refused the branch because travel was difficult, and they demanded that the company either keep the sections at the old location or terminate the old contracts first and pay severance. [Para 2][Para 14][Para 27][Para 30]
**Employer (respondent):** Hytex Garment (Cambodia) Ltd, through two administration staff, answered that the old location was narrow and needed repair and reinstallation to the buyer's standard, that the new place is merely a branch so the company's location has not changed, that seniority and benefits would be preserved and transport provided both ways with late return counted as overtime, and that it had not said when repairs would finish. [Para 15][Para 26][Para 28][Para 29][Para 38]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer must retain the cutting and printing workers at the same location, or, if it requires them to work at the new branch, must first terminate the old contracts and pay termination payments in accordance with the Labour Law. [Para 48][Para 50]
The panel began from Article 2 and its own earlier interpretation that the employer has the right to manage and supervise the enterprise, and so may change the company's location and move workers from one place to another. [Para 33][Para 34] That right is not unlimited: in Awards 17/03 and 18/03-Ho Hing the Council held that a transfer is lawful only if it brings no wage reduction, does not send workers to a far place, does not switch day and night shifts and does not require substantially different skills. [Para 42]
The panel then interpreted the two instruments under Article 312, paragraph 2, which gives it power to decide disputes over the interpretation and enforcement of laws and collective agreements. [Para 38][Para 40] The workers read the January agreement as a promise not to change the workplace, breached when the company moved the section's work and equipment; the company read the same words as an internal reorganisation within an unchanged location. [Para 33][Para 36][Para 38]
The panel preferred the workers' reading on the facts. It distinguished Ho Hing, where only two workers moved and production at the former site continued normally, from this case, where the whole of two sections, all 65 workers and all their equipment moved and the old site's production stopped. [Para 43][Para 44] It held that "changing location" means not only changing the factory building but changing the workplace of the section's workers and moving all the equipment they use daily, because that eliminates production at the old site. [Para 46][Para 47] The absence of any prior notification, against the notification's own promise of one month's notice, weighed against the company. [Para 44] The panel therefore found the company's insistence on the branch unreasonable and held it bound by its own notification and by the January agreement, so the choice it set out was retention at the old site or prior termination with statutory payments. [Para 45][Para 48]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer's managerial right to redeploy workers does not extend to moving an entire section's work and equipment to a branch in a way that closes production at the old site without notice: where the employer has promised in a joint notification and a collective agreement not to change the workplace, "changing location" is measured by the workers' workplace and their equipment, not by the factory building, and a transfer that crosses that line must be preceded by termination of the old contracts and payment of statutory severance. [Para 42][Para 46][Para 47][Para 48]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the company kept paying wages, seniority and attendance bonus to the idle workers from October 2007, a fact it treated as confirming that the workers had not abandoned their posts. [Para 31][Para 45]
