# Award No. 110/14 — Japan Rocks S.E.A (Phnom Penh) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 11 June 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Nan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chea, president) · Doc ID 139568

## 1. Facts
The Khmer Workers' Power Federation organised a union at this Phnom Penh garment factory, which employs about 535 workers, and held a leadership election on 20 April 2014 at which Mr S, Mr C and Mr D were elected president, vice-president and secretary. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 132] The union applied for registration but had no receipt from the Ministry. [Para 71] On 21 April the federation notified the company of the election and the names; the general manager accepted the letter but refused to sign it. [Para 134][Para 135][Para 137] On 24 April the three were told the company no longer needed them, were refused entry after lunch, and a strike followed from that afternoon to 28 April. [Para 115][Para 124][Para 128]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must take back the three elected union leaders and let them work normally again? [Para 75][Para 204]
2. Whether the company must maintain the travel and accommodation allowance of workers who use the company's own transport? [Para 243] (with issue 10, merged — [Para 27])
3. Whether the company may require a card and a fingerprint scan before a worker may enter the toilet? [Para 266]
4. Whether the company must deduct the attendance bonus only in proportion to days of absence that it has itself authorised? [Para 287]
5. Whether the company must add four fingerprint scanners? (not decided — the employer agreed to review it — [Para 193])
6. Whether the company must maintain wages and the attendance bonus during the strike of 24 to 28 April 2014? [Para 329]
Issues 2, 4 and 8 were settled in conciliation and issues 6 (the 10 USD increase) and 7 were withdrawn. [Para 24][Para 25]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the three were dismissed for union activity, since the company only tightened the uniform rule after learning they had been elected; and the toilet card system restricts a basic freedom and damages health. [Para 103][Para 107][Para 163][Para 164]
**Employer (respondent):** the three wore no uniform, were repeatedly warned, ignored instructions and incited an unlawful strike; the attendance bonus is an incentive that loses meaning if absentees receive it; the toilet card protects security and the production line. [Para 89][Para 93][Para 96][Para 171][Para 185]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to reinstate the three workers, to stop requiring a card and fingerprint scan for toilet entry, to deduct the attendance bonus only pro rata to authorised absence, and to run company transport at the normal finishing time without making non-overtime workers wait; the claims for the allowance to company-transport users, for extra cards and for strike wages were refused. [Para 342][Para 344][Para 346][Para 347]

On reinstatement the panel applied Article 293 of the Labour Law and Article 3(3) of Prakas 305: every candidate in a union leadership election is protected against dismissal in the same way as a staff delegate, for forty-five days before the election and forty-five days after, and the employer must be notified of the candidacy by any reliable means. [Para 204][Para 207][Para 208][Para 209] Following Awards 40/10, 239/13, 243/13 and 035/14 it held that protection continues after the election until the union applies for registration. [Para 213][Para 214][Para 225] All three conditions were met: they were elected candidates, the dismissal fell four days after the election, and the general manager admitted receiving the notice of 21 April. [Para 220][Para 227][Para 231] A dismissal of a protected worker requires the labour inspector's prior authorisation, which the company conceded it never sought, so the dismissal was void and reinstatement followed. [Para 234][Para 238][Para 240][Para 242]

On transport, Notification 230 requires 7 USD a month in travel and accommodation allowance, but releases the employer that supplies transport or lodging; this company paid 5 USD travel plus 5 USD accommodation, or supplied transport plus 5 USD, so its practice exceeded the standard, and the claim to keep the 5 USD while riding the company bus was refused. [Para 243][Para 246][Para 248][Para 252] The panel nevertheless held that transport must be provided at the end of ordinary hours, because overtime is voluntary under Prakas 80 and Article 137, and making a worker who does not volunteer wait until 18:30 amounts to compulsion. [Para 257][Para 259][Para 261][Para 263]

On the toilets, the panel held that managerial authority under Article 2 cannot override the worker's freedom to use the toilet; one card per group of twenty to twenty-five, with a fingerprint scan, forces workers to wait and risks kidney and other health harm, and the scan used to police frequency of visits is equally improper, so the system had to stop. [Para 272][Para 276][Para 280][Para 284][Para 286]

On the bonus, Notification 230 sets a minimum 10 USD a month for workers with no absence, and the panel read the word "absence" as covering only absence the law provides for, not authorised personal leave; authorised leave suspends the contract under Articles 71(6) and 72(1), so no wage is owed for the day, but the bonus may be cut only in proportion to those days, not forfeited entirely. [Para 292][Para 301][Para 323][Para 325][Para 327][Para 328] On strike wages the panel applied Articles 332(1) and 334 as in Awards 49/05 and 27/10 and refused the claim. [Para 330][Para 332][Para 340]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker who is a candidate in a union leadership election is protected from dismissal for forty-five days before and after the election, and thereafter until the union lodges its registration application; a dismissal made in that window without the labour inspector's prior authorisation is void and the worker must be reinstated, whatever the employer's stated reasons. [Para 208][Para 225][Para 240]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that a worker's authorised personal leave suspends the contract, and that an employer may still impose discipline through its internal rules where a worker abuses toilet breaks, were not necessary to the orders made. [Para 282][Para 325]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer-appointed arbitrator, Ing Sothy, filed a dissenting opinion on issue 6, appended at [Para 350] and arguing that a bonus is discretionary and conditional so that no pro-rata payment is owed; the award reports this separately from the majority's reasoning. [Para 352][Para 359] The number of scanners is four at [Para 187] and seven at [Para 190] and [Para 192]. The union's support is put at 177 transferred rights at [Para 72] while only sixteen workers attended the founding election at [Para 131]. Prakas 305 is dated "ឆ្នាំ ២០០១៩" at [Para 238] and stray years "២០១៥" and "២០១៦" interrupt [Para 243] and [Para 250]. The company's name is rendered "យេ ផេន រ៉ក់" in the case title but appears as "Japan Rocks S.E.A (Phnom Penh) Co.,Ltd" in its own notice quoted at [Para 126]. [Para 1][Para 126] The three workers are named in full throughout and are referred to here by initials, as are the federation officials.
