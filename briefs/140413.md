# Award No. 94/09 — Tak Faat Garmen (Cambodia) Ltd and the Khmer Youth Union Federation of Tak Faat factory (Arbitration Council of Cambodia, 14 August 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Eng Sothy អ៉ឹង សុទ្ធី (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna លីវ សុវណ្ណា (worker-appointed), Ing Phalla ឥង់ ផល្លៈ (president) · Doc ID 140413

## 1. Facts
Tak Faat Garmen (Cambodia) Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន តាក ហ្វាត ហ្គាមិន (ខាំបូឌា) អែល ធី ឌី) works at National Road 2, Chak Angre Leu, Mean Chey, Phnom Penh. [Para 1] The Khmer Youth Union Federation of the factory (សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ) filed a complaint on 22 June 2009 asking the company to improve working conditions, which the labour-dispute department received on 23 June 2009. [Para 10] Conciliation on 8 July 2009 settled three of the four demands, leaving one in dispute; the non-conciliation report No. 593 dated 15 July 2009 reached the secretariat on 16 July 2009. [Para 7][Para 11]

The single point referred was the wages of the 300 or so finishing-section workers who could not be given work at the company's building in Sub-penh. [Para 85] The company suspended work in two stretches — from 1 June to 15 June 2009 and from 15 June to 19 July 2009 — and offered 50% of the base wage for a short suspension, or, for a long one, a suspension placed under the labour inspector's supervision with only accommodation provided. [Para 3][Para 4] The workers refused and demanded 100% wages throughout. [Para 2] A strike took place on 11 June 2009 over the refusal to move from Building 2 to Building 5. [Para 88]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay 100% of wages to the finishing-section workers for the periods when it gave them no work? [Para 15]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**ភាគីកម្មករ និយោជិត — Khmer Youth Union Federation of Tak Faat factory (សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ, claimant):** while the company had no work for the finishing section it should pay full wages for that period and settle the arrears for the time already lost, not 50% of base salary, and not a suspension resting on accommodation alone. [Para 2][Para 3][Para 4]

**ភាគីនិយោជក — Tak Faat Garmen (Cambodia) Ltd (respondent):** it could not pay full wages because there was no work; for a short suspension it would pay 50% of base salary, and for a long one it would place the suspension under the labour inspector's supervision and provide only accommodation. [Para 3][Para 4]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay 100% wages to the 388 finishing-section workers who thumbprinted the demand, for 1 to 15 June 2009 and 15 June to 19 July 2009, except for the strike day of 11 June 2009. [Para 91][Para 92]

The panel started from Article 72(1) of the Labour Law, which allows an employer to suspend a contract when it faces a serious economic or material difficulty, or an unforeseeable event, and provided the suspension is made in proper form and placed under the labour inspector's supervision. [Para 82] It read the provision as the Council had in Award 72/09: where the suspension follows the statutory procedure and the inspector supervises it, the employer owes no wages for the suspended period. [Para 82]

The converse was the decisive point. Where the company has not carried out the suspension in accordance with the Labour Law, it must pay 100% of wages even though it has no work to give. The panel cited its own earlier reasoning to that effect in Awards 60/06, 74/07, 28/08, 53/08 and 71/09. [Para 83][Para 84]

On the facts, the panel found that from 1 June to 15 June 2009 and from 15 June to 19 July 2009 the company gave the finishing section no work at all, and the company itself admitted that about 300 workers could not be employed at its Sub-penh building and that it asked them to rest on 50% wages. [Para 85] Critically, the company had not carried out the suspension according to the legal procedure and had no authorisation from the labour inspector. [Para 86][Para 87] The statutory condition for wage-free suspension therefore failed, and the full-wage obligation arose.

The panel then excluded one day. It noted that on 11 June 2009 there was a strike over the refusal to move to Building 5, and that the company had in any event kept full wages for that day after conciliation by the Ministry of Labour and Vocational Training; since the company had conceded that benefit and the law does not forbid paying wages on a strike day, the panel declined to consider that day further. [Para 88][Para 89][Para 90]

The order was drawn to match the claim actually authorised: 100% wages for the 388 finishing-section workers who thumbprinted the demand, of whom ten were members of the Khmer Youth Union, for the two periods, excluding 11 June 2009. [Para 91][Para 92]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that suspends work without following the statutory procedure and without the labour inspector's supervision cannot rely on Article 72(1) to withhold wages; it must pay full wages for the period in which it gave no work, because the wage obligation is only displaced when the suspension itself is lawful. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 87]

**Obiter dicta:** that a day on which the employer has already paid full wages after ministerial conciliation, and which the law does not forbid paying, needs no further order from the panel. [Para 89][Para 90]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The masthead and running text are badly corrupted: the national motto is garbled, "Arbitration Council" appears as a mangled compound, and the statutory citation in the jurisdictional paragraph reads "ហត្រា ៣១៣" instead of "មាត្រា ៣១៣", while "អាជ្ញា" is repeatedly printed as "រាជ្ញា" or "អាជ្ញាក". The award date is printed once as 14 August 2009 but the period at issue is twice given as ending "19 July 2009" and once as "19 June 2009", and a stray "2017" is inserted into the sentence describing the two suspension periods. The workforce figure is about 300 in the findings and 388 in the order, and the ten union members among the 388 are identified only by count. The company's address and the union's address are each printed twice in succession. The signature block is almost entirely lost: the employer-appointed arbitrator's name is followed by Latin fragments, the worker-appointed arbitrator's line is separated from its label, and the president's signature is replaced by unrecognisable characters. The 388 individual names and thumbprints are in the file and are not reproduced here. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 85][Para 91][Para 94]
