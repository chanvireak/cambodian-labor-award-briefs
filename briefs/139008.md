# Award No. 068/16 — Cambodia Airport Management Services (CAMS), Siem Reap International Airport (Arbitration Council, 18 April 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mr Mar Samborana, appointed by the employer; Mr Tuon Siphan, appointed by the workers; Mr Pen Chhea, chair) · Doc ID 139008

## 1. Facts
Cambodia Airport Management Services (CAMS) operates Siem Reap International Airport under commercial registration Inv. 110 E/1995 of 06 December 1995 and employed about 479 workers. [Para 58] [Para 59] Mr S was deputy president of the Cambodia Tourism Industry Workers' Union at Siem Reap Airport, elected for term 5 and still within his term. [Para 60] [Para 67] The employer sought permission to dismiss him for serious misconduct; the Siem Reap Provincial Department of Labour and Vocational Training did not object on 29 June 2015, the company issued its termination letter on 02 July 2015, and the Ministry of Labour and Vocational Training did not object by letter No. 1979 of 07 October 2015. [Para 74] [Para 76] [Para 90] [Para 91] Conciliation of the collective dispute failed and the unresolved points were referred to the Council. [Para 9] [Para 14] The award is non-binding. [Para 18] [Para 100]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to recognise Mr S, a deputy union president whose term has not yet ended, as union representative in dealings with the company? [Para 85]
2. Whether the employer must supply the union with the list of new union members for the last month of 2016 so that it can be checked against the union's old list? [Para 80]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that Mr S's term as deputy president had not expired and that Article 294, Article 295 and section 5, paragraph 3 of Prakas No. 305 bar dismissal of a union leader unless serious misconduct is proved; that the Minister's letter confirmed he had committed no serious misconduct and had merely been treated as an ordinary employee under Article 74, so the dismissal was procedurally invalid; and that the employer refused to accept his signature on collective bargaining proposals. [Para 66] [Para 68] [Para 69] [Para 96]
**Employer (respondent):** that Mr S was dismissed in accordance with legal procedure after the Department and then the Ministry authorised it, so he is no longer a company employee and, under the final paragraph of article 3 of Prakas No. 305, no longer entitled to represent union members; it had issued the dismissal letter and prepared his final wages and severance, which he declined to collect. [Para 72] [Para 73]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the workers' claim that the employer must recognise Mr S as deputy union president whose term has not ended; on the second issue the employer agreed to provide the information sought and the panel recorded that agreement without further consideration. [Para 83] [Para 84] [Para 98] [Para 99]
On the recognition issue the panel read section 5 of Prakas No. 305 as giving all union leaders who are suspended or permanently dismissed for economic or other reasons the right to enter the enterprise to carry out the duties of their union mandate. [Para 79] [Para 85] [Para 87] It then read the closing words of that provision as limiting the right: a leader who has committed a fault and been lawfully dismissed must leave the union and has no right to continue as a union leader or representative. [Para 86] [Para 88] [Para 89]
Applying that reading, the panel found as fact that the employer had received the Department's authorisation on 29 June 2015 and that the Ministry had not objected by letter of 07 October 2015, so the dismissal was lawful and the permission procedure had been completed correctly. [Para 90] [Para 91] [Para 94] The claim therefore failed, because the employer is under no duty to keep recognising a leader it has lawfully dismissed. [Para 95] [Para 98]
The panel also addressed the workers' reliance on Articles 294 and 295 of the Labour Law, holding that those articles govern only the stage at which the employer is waiting for the labour inspector or the Minister to answer a request to dismiss a protected worker, and that they do not govern a case in which authorisation has already been granted. [Para 96] [Para 97]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer has obtained the authorisation of the labour inspectorate and, on appeal, of the Minister before dismissing a protected union leader for misconduct, the dismissal is lawful and the leader's right to be recognised as union representative ends with it; the access and representation rights in section 5 of Prakas No. 305 do not survive a dismissal that was carried out with that authorisation, and Articles 294 and 295 protect the worker only while the request is pending, not after it is granted. [Para 88] [Para 89] [Para 95] [Para 97]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's broader statement that Prakas No. 305 is to be read as allowing union leaders to exercise their rights "fully", including entering the enterprise, even where the employer has suspended them or dismissed them for economic or other reasons — an observation wider than the facts needed, since this leader was found to have been dismissed for fault. [Para 87]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
OCR damage is heavy throughout: the party and evidence sections are partly illegible, article numbers appear as "មាត្រា ៦៩៤" and similar digits, and several quoted letters survive only as fragments. [Para 21] [Para 68] The employer is printed variously as CAMS, Cambodia Airport Management Services, and Siem Reap International Airport Co. [Para 1] [Para 58] [Para 74] The termination letter is dated 02 July 2015 while the Department's authorisation is dated 29 June 2015, and the Ministry's letter No. 1979 is dated 07 October 2015 — three dates that the award never reconciles. [Para 74] [Para 90] [Para 91] Most significantly, the Ministry letter as quoted states that Mr S had committed many faults "but not serious misconduct" [Para 78], whereas the panel later says both authorisation letters rested on a finding that he had committed a dismissable fault [Para 92]; the award does not resolve that tension. The first page also carries unrelated letterhead text about a 2016 secretariat address. [Para 1] Individual workers and union officers are reduced to initials per convention.
