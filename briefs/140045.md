# Award No. 80/11 — T & K Garment Industry Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 27 July 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Oeng Sothy for the employer party, Liv Sovannara for the worker party, Run Saray chair) · Doc ID 140045

## 1. Facts
T & K Garment Industry Co., Ltd, a Phnom Penh garment factory, employed 1,588 workers and hosted three unions, of which the claimant union had roughly 300 members. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44] On 2 June 2011 the union federation filed a collective complaint seeking improved working conditions; conciliation on 16 June 2011 failed on four demands, and the case reached the Council on 5 July 2011. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 17] At the hearing of 12 July 2011 the parties settled the third demand — reinstatement of a named union vice-president — leaving maternity pay, music during working hours, and earphone confiscation. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the whole 90 days' maternity allowance in a single payment before the worker begins maternity leave, rather than one month at a time? [Para 46][Para 61]
2. Whether the employer must install and play music for workers to listen to during working hours? [Para 55][Para 84]
3. Whether the employer must stop taking away workers' earphones during working hours (former issue 4, merged with issue 2 by agreement of the parties)? [Para 21][Para 84][Para 98]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** maternity allowance must be paid in full in advance because workers on leave are poor and need money at delivery, and because the Labour Law requires wages to be paid before a rest day; music during work relieves fatigue and the earphone ban serves no purpose where the employer plays nothing. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 56][Para 57]
**Employer (respondent):** one month at a time is compelled by the monthly accounting system, and full advance payment is made only for caesarean births; music was abandoned because ten years of playing it at a previous factory produced hand and finger injuries and poor-quality sewing, and the buyer now forbids it. [Para 5][Para 52][Para 53][Para 54][Para 58][Para 60]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay the full 90 days' maternity allowance before the worker's maternity leave begins, and refused the demands concerning music and earphones. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 108]

On maternity pay the panel read Article 182 (90 days' leave) together with Article 183 (half wage including allowances, for women with one year of service), and noted that Article 183 fixes the rate but says nothing about when payment falls due. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65][Para 67] The employer's reliance on Article 116 — wages paid at least once a month — was rejected: that article governs ordinary monthly wage payment and cannot be extended to wages payable during maternity leave. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 72] Instead Article 115 paragraph 3 governs, forbidding payment on a day the worker is entitled to rest and requiring payment one day earlier where the due date falls on such a day; the panel reasoned that the start of maternity leave is exactly such a period, so the whole sum must be handed over before leave begins. [Para 74][Para 76][Para 77] It followed four prior awards on the same point — 70/09 GDB Y, 60/10 Kin Tai, 107/10 Juntics Thailand and 115/10 G-Fashion — and rejected the "accounting system" explanation as no answer to a statutory obligation. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 82][Para 83]

On music, the panel found no provision of law and no clause of the collective agreement granting the right, which made the demand an interest dispute rather than a rights dispute. [Para 85][Para 86] For interest disputes the panel habitually examines whether the filing union is the most representative in the factory, and declines to hear them where it is not; this union held no certificate of most-representative status and covered only some 300 of 1,588 workers. [Para 45][Para 87][Para 88][Para 95][Para 96]

On earphones, Article 2 was read as conferring on the employer the right to manage and direct the enterprise so long as that direction is reasonable. [Para 98][Para 101][Para 103] Confiscating earphones during working hours was found reasonable because it was aimed at preventing workplace accidents of the kind the employer described, so the demand was refused. [Para 104][Para 105]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Maternity allowance under Articles 182 and 183 of the Labour Law is payable in a single sum before the worker's maternity leave starts: Article 183 is silent on timing, Article 116's monthly rule does not reach maternity pay, and Article 115(3)'s prohibition on paying wages on a rest day requires the advance payment. An employer's internal monthly accounting cycle is not a defence. [Para 67][Para 72][Para 77][Para 82]
Separately, a demand for a benefit neither required by law nor contained in a collective agreement is an interest dispute, which the panel will not entertain where the filing union lacks most-representative status under Article 277; and an employer may confiscate earphones during working hours where the measure is a reasonable exercise of its Article 2 management right to prevent accidents. [Para 86][Para 88][Para 95][Para 104]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that most-representative status confers standing to bring interest disputes, and its agreement with prior awards on the scope of Article 2, were general statements unnecessary to the result, since this union admittedly lacked that status. [Para 90][Para 92]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The employer-party arbitrator is printed as "Oeng Sothy" in the header [Para 1] and differently in the signature block [Para 110]; the chair's name likewise differs slightly between the two. Both forms are reported, not reconciled.
- Article 277 of the Labour Law is printed as "Article 27725" [Para 95], an OCR run-together.
- Stray fragments interrupt the text: an isolated "2016" opens the employer's answer on music [Para 5], and "បញ្ហា សង្គម - 2022" closes the signature block [Para 110].
- The demand on music is rendered as "play the vaccine of songs" (ចាក់ វ៉ាក់សាំង ចម្រៀង) in the summary of decision [Para 97], plainly a mis-scan of "play songs".
- The reasons for the interest-dispute point contain repeated, broken sentences that restart mid-argument [Para 91][Para 92][Para 93][Para 94]; the operative finding was taken from the clear statements at [Para 94]–[Para 96].
- The union vice-president whose reinstatement was settled appears under two different spellings [Para 6][Para 7]; he is reduced to role here. Employer representatives and union officials are likewise given by role only.
