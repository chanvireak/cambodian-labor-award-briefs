# Award No. 105/04 — United Eternity (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 10 January 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry, Vong Vanna, Sok Mathoeung chair) · Doc ID 139358

## 1. Facts
United Eternity (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, at #650 National Road Number 2, Chak Angrek Kraum, Mean Chey district, Phnom Penh, employs about 689 workers, including roughly 210 sewing workers on the second floor. [Para 10][Para 11] The company suspended their labour contracts from 1 September to 18 October 2004 for want of purchase orders, and the C.CAWDU local union, which holds most representative status, signed an agreement on 6 October 2004 on wages and benefits during the suspension. [Para 13][Para 15][Para 16] The DTFU, a rival union with about 20 members registered on 19 October 2004, complained to the Meanchey Labour Inspector on 12 November 2004. [Para 12]

## 2. Issues in dispute
Two points were left non-conciliated after the 24 November 2004 negotiation. [Para 12]
1. Whether the company must pay 50 percent of wages during the suspension from 1 September to 18 October 2004? [Para 6][Para 19]
2. Whether the company must provide the US$5 attendance and seniority bonuses and one half day a month of paid annual leave for that period? [Para 6][Para 19]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the DTFU demanded half wages for the suspension and the attendance and seniority bonuses plus half a day of paid leave, relying on its earlier practice of claiming annual compensation of one and a half days, equal to US$2.6. [Para 6][Para 18]
**Employer (respondent):** the suspension was caused by a genuine economic difficulty, was notified to and recognised by the Labour Inspector, and the company had already paid what the agreement of 6 October 2004 with the representative union required. [Para 13][Para 15][Para 23]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both demands of the local DTFU were rejected, the agreement of 6 October 2004 with C.CAWDU being retained. [Para 39][Para 50]

The panel first asked whether the suspension was lawful under Article 71, point 11, which permits suspension where the enterprise faces a serious economic or material difficulty, for no more than two months and under the control of the Labour Inspector. [Para 19][Para 20] The employer produced no evidence of the difficulty, but the worker party did not contest the argument, the suspension was approved by the most representative local union, and the period of one month and eighteen days fell inside the two-month cap. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 22][Para 23]
On the control requirement the panel found the Law silent on timing and notification, and reasoned from the purpose of the provision: control lets the Inspector see whether difficulty is real, how many contracts were suspended and what benefits workers received. [Para 26][Para 33] Because nothing in the Law or the Prakas requires prior notification of a suspension — unlike the express notice rules in Prakas 80/99 for overtime and Articles 74 to 82 for termination — a letter of 6 October 2004 answered on 27 October 2004 sufficed, and the Inspector's recognition, even arriving as the suspension ended, did not stop it. [Para 31][Para 35] The panel added that it has no jurisdiction to examine the Inspector's letter itself, following 95/04 ASD. [Para 35]
On wages, Article 72 provides that workers receive no pay during a suspension absent a supplementary agreement, so no statutory entitlement arose. [Para 37] The agreement signed by C.CAWDU on 6 October 2004 nevertheless gave more than the Law — 50 percent of basic wages from 1 to 7 September, US$10 a month from 8 September to 16 October, US$5 a month for annual leave and seniority in September and October, and full wages for the first and second floors on 10 and 12 to 14 October — and the panel held Article 13 did not nullify an agreement more favourable than the Law. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 17][Para 38]
The minority union then failed on standing: Article 277 and Article 6 of Prakas 305 reserve representation of all workers to a union with an absolute majority, and a settlement of an interest dispute becomes a collective bargaining agreement binding the whole factory under Article 43 of Prakas 99, which a 20-member union cannot obtain. [Para 39][Para 41][Para 42] The panel therefore urged the parties to negotiate a collective bargaining agreement for the future rather than decide the claim. [Para 44][Para 50]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension ordered under Article 71, point 11 for genuine economic difficulty, within two months and recognised by the Labour Inspector even after the event, is lawful and generates no wage entitlement under Article 72 absent agreement; and a supplementary agreement on suspension benefits signed by the most representative union binds the whole workforce, so a minority union cannot reopen it through arbitration. [Para 23][Para 37][Para 39]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel remarked that had the Inspector found no genuine difficulty it could have stopped the suspension, and that both parties would benefit from fixing wage rules for future economic difficulty in a collective bargaining agreement. [Para 34][Para 44]

## 6. Source defects disclosed

[Para 14][Para 15] The panel's composition is ambiguous because the "Chair arbitrator" label precedes Vong Vanna's name while Sok Mathoeung signs as chair. [Para 9][Para 10][Para 50] Article 43 is cited from "Prakas 99" without the year, and the union is spelled both Dhamathipaktay and Dhama Thpaktay.
