# Award No. 071/19 — AM3 (Cambodia) Garment (Arbitration Council, 26 August 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — chair គង់ ផល្លៈ, employer-appointed អ៊ុក រី, worker-appointed អន ណន · Doc ID 139110

## 1. Facts
AM3 (Cambodia) Garment, a garment manufacturer at Trapeang Pou, Chom Chao, Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh, employed 320 workers, 74 of whom were party to this dispute. The workers filed 13 demands on 19 July 2019; conciliation on 31 July 2019 settled nine of them, and the four unresolved points were forwarded to the Council on 2 August 2019 under report No. 823 K.B/AK/VK. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 33][Para 34][Para 13]

The employer's representative was invited by letter No. 398 of 6 August 2019, did not answer the secretariat's telephone calls on the hearing day, and was absent from the hearing on 13 August 2019; the panel proceeded in its absence. [Para 19][Para 22][Para 23]

The panel found no evidence that the company had a union holding exclusive representative status, and accepted the four worker representatives certified by the labour disputes department in letter No. 014/19 of 24 July 2019. [Para 35][Para 83][Para 85]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must build a properly standardised nursery (ទារកដ្ឋាន), or pay USD 30 to workers with small children if it does not? [Para 2]
2. Whether the employer must pay annual leave in a lump sum calculated on average wages? [Para 4]
3. Whether the employer must pay a 2,000 riel meal allowance when workers work overtime on public holidays or Sundays? [Para 6]
4. Whether the employer must pay seniority indemnity for the period before 2019 and for 2019 in accordance with Prakas No. 443? [Para 8]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company employs about 120 women, provides only USD 5 a month towards childcare, while an outside nursery costs about USD 35 a month, so a further USD 30 should be paid; they had never been able to take annual leave and the employer instead paid it out every two months on base wage alone, contrary to Article 168; holiday and Sunday work is overtime and so attracts the 2,000 riel meal allowance under Notification No. 041/11; and pre-2019 seniority indemnity was never paid, while the 2019 payment was calculated on base wage only, contrary to Prakas 443. [Para 37][Para 39][Para 45][Para 47][Para 50][Para 53][Para 54][Para 55]

**Employer (respondent):** it cannot build a nursery because its premises are too small and already pays USD 5 to women with small children; it cannot pay annual leave as demanded and continues its old practice of computing from base salary divided by months worked; it cannot grant the meal allowance for holiday or Sunday work; and it had already paid seniority indemnity to workers on 15 August 2019. [Para 3][Para 5][Para 7][Para 9][Para 38]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the USD 30 childcare demand, the average-wage annual leave demand and the 2,000 riel public-holiday meal allowance were dismissed; the employer was ordered to stop paying cash in lieu of annual leave, to pay 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance per day under Notification No. 041/11, to pay pre-2019 seniority indemnity according to actual length of service under Article 3 of Prakas No. 443, and to pay 2019 seniority indemnity computed on average wage and allowances. [Para 168][Para 169][Para 170][Para 171][Para 172][Para 173][Para 174]

On issue 1, Article 186 of the Labour Law requires an enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a nursing room and a nursery, and where no nursery can be arranged the employer must bear the cost of an outside nursery at the actual rate charged. The panel found no clear number of women actually using outside childcare and no documentary proof of the monthly cost — no invoice or receipt — so the claimant had not discharged the burden of proof and the demand failed. [Para 88][Para 89][Para 95][Para 100][Para 101][Para 102]

On issue 2, Article 167(2) and (3) prohibit payment in lieu of annual leave while the employment relationship subsists, and any agreement waiving or forgoing annual leave is void. The panel therefore dismissed the demand for an average-wage buy-out, but held that the employer's practice of paying leave out in cash every two months was itself contrary to the spirit of Articles 166 and 167 and ordered it stopped. [Para 104][Para 106][Para 107][Para 113][Para 114][Para 115]

On issue 3, the panel distinguished holiday work from Sunday work. Working on a public holiday is not overtime — it earns one extra day's wage under Article 164 and Prakas No. 10 of 4 February 1999 — so no meal allowance is owed for it, and the demand was dismissed. Work on Sunday, by contrast, exceeds the normal 8 hours a day or 48 hours a week and is overtime; point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 K.B/SChN of 8 March 2011 entitles a worker who volunteers for overtime at the employer's request to 2,000 riel a day or a free meal. [Para 120][Para 123][Para 124][Para 127][Para 138][Para 140][Para 141][Para 143]

On issue 4, the employer's absence mattered. Under Article 118 the employer bears the burden of proving that wages have been paid, so the workers carried no evidential burden; the employer filed nothing. The panel read Article 89 (new) with Article 2 of Prakas No. 443: 7.5 days for a first year of service of one to six months and 15 days beyond six months, capped at six months' wages, and for service before 2019 the indemnity follows actual length of service under Article 3 of the Prakas. It ordered payment accordingly, and ordered the 2019 indemnity to be computed on average wage and allowances rather than base wage alone. [Para 145][Para 146][Para 147][Para 153][Para 157][Para 158][Para 161][Para 162][Para 166][Para 167]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for the cash equivalent of a statutory benefit — here the outside-nursery cost under Article 186 — fails unless the claimant identifies the workers concerned and proves the actual expenditure by documents; assertion of an average market rate is not enough. Separately, an employer may not discharge annual leave in cash while the contract subsists, and a worker's claim for a lump-sum buy-out cannot succeed even where the employer has been paying that way for years. [Para 90][Para 100][Para 106][Para 113]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's reasoning that a public holiday is a paid rest day rather than an overtime day, so that Article 164 compensation is exclusive of any meal allowance, was broader than needed once it had found no instrument creating the allowance; and its observations on the certification of worker representatives where no exclusive-representative union exists rest on an uncontested record. [Para 124][Para 127][Para 80][Para 85]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR is badly degraded and one paragraph carries foreign boilerplate: Para 86 names "ខ្វាន់ ទុម ខ្លួស៊ីង (ខេមបូឌា)លីមីធីត" as the respondent, though the case name, the header and every other reference give AM3 (Cambodia) Garment; Para 35 also repeats its own sentence twice. Amounts are garbled: Para 88 asks whether USD "២៣៥$ ១៩៩៦ (សាមសិប ប្រាំ ដុល្លារ)" is owed where the demand was USD 35, and Para 87 states the total as USD 35. Statute years appear as "ឆ្នាំ ២០២៣ ១៩៩៧" (Para 90). Dates carry stray digits: Notification 041/11 is dated "ថ្ងៃទី ៨២ ខែមីនា ឆ្នាំ អង្គ ២០១១" (Para 140), and Article 89 is paraphrased with "រៀងរាល់ ៦៨ ឆ្នាំ" (Para 149) where six months was meant. The dispositive paragraph 171 mangles the holiday meal allowance ("ប្រាក់ ថ្ងៃទី យ័ត្ន ឈប់ បុណ្យ"). The signature block omits the worker-appointed arbitrator's name and prints only "-១៦-" (Para 176), though the header names អន ណន. The face of the award lists individual worker representatives; those names are not reproduced here.
