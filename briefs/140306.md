# Order No. 90/10 — Sau Bay Interpri Co., Ltd v. Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers Democratic Union (Arbitration Council, 30 August 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, Sin Kim Sean, Kong Phalla) · Doc ID 140306

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន សៅ ធ៏ បាយ អ៉ិន ធើ ប្រាយ កូ អិលធីឌី — Sau Bay Interpri Co., Ltd — runs a garment factory on National Road 4 at Kantok commune, Ang Snuol, Kandal, employing 1,200 workers, all or nearly all of them members of one union affiliated to the Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers Democratic Union (សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា), which is most representative but has no collective agreement with the company. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48] Five cutting-section workers, hired between 17 March 1999 and 14 March 2000, were dismissed on 30 June 2010 before their contracts expired and without notice; the employer said the cutting section had little work and that the five had poor attitudes and disobeyed their supervisor. [Para 56][Para 59][Para 60][Para 61] Of six demands, the workers withdrew five (demands 2 to 6, on the rice allowance, seniority pay, the base wage, annual increases and short-term contracts) at the hearing, leaving only the demand over the five dismissals. [Para 19][Para 20]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must take the five dismissed cutting-section workers back, or instead pay them the wages and termination benefits owed under an indefinite-term contract? [Para 53][Para 81]
2. (Issue 1) Whether the employer applied its own disciplinary procedure before dismissing them, and if not, what that failure entitles them to? [Para 110][Para 114]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers Democratic Union and five cutting-section workers:** they were dismissed without notice before their contracts ended and ask to be reinstated, failing which they claim notice pay, dismissal damages of 15 days per year of service, moral damages equal to dismissal damages, unused annual leave and their final month's wage. [Para 59][Para 65][Para 66] Their contracts are indefinite-term because they entered service in 1999 and 2000 and were renewed continuously, so the company's conversion of all contracts to one-year fixed terms in 2005 cannot shorten that. [Para 68][Para 69] On the June 2010 incident, the pushing match with the supervisor was settled by mutual apology. [Para 64][Para 115]

**Employer (respondent), Sau Bay Interpri Co., Ltd:** the dismissals followed the cutting section having too little work, not poor performance, although it also says the five had bad attitudes and would not obey the supervisor, and that one of them pushed a supervisor in June 2010 and refused to sign the written warning. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 62][Para 63] It will not reinstate them and says it has already paid termination benefits calculated on fixed-term contracts: final wage, 5% severance, unused leave, 15 days' notice pay and moral damages, which the workers refused. [Para 67][Para 68] Since 2005 the whole factory has operated on fixed-term contracts. [Para 74]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel did not order reinstatement but granted Issue 1 in the alternative, ordering the company to pay the five workers dismissal damages, pay in lieu of notice, unused annual leave, final unpaid wages and moral damages equal to the dismissal damages. No total figure is stated in the order. [Para 144][Para 149]

The panel began with Article 67(2) of the Labour Law, which caps a fixed-term contract at two years and allows renewal only where each renewal does not exceed that ceiling, and Article 73(5), under which a fixed-term contract of more than six months carries a duty to give 10 or 15 days' notice of expiry and, absent notice, is renewed for an equal term or treated as indefinite where the total exceeds the Article 67 limit. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 88][Para 89] It noted that the Labour Law leans towards indefinite-term employment because that secures the job security workers need and rewards employers with committed staff, and that ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982 discourages fixed-term contracts for long-term work. [Para 92][Para 93][Para 96][Para 97] Following Awards 10/03 (Zacksintic), 36/06 and 57/06 (Evergreen), it held that the reference to "total duration" in Article 67(2) means the aggregate, not each renewal. [Para 99][Para 100]

Applying that, the five had served since 1999 and 2000, so their contracts are indefinite-term notwithstanding the 2005 conversion. [Para 101][Para 102] Turning to the dismissal, the panel read Article 10 of the internal rules, which grades penalties from oral education, to written warning, to dismissal on a third offence, and found the conduct alleged — disobedience and a quarrel — amounted only to a light fault. [Para 103][Para 106][Para 110] The employer produced no evidence of any oral education or written warning, so the dismissals breached its own procedure. [Para 113][Para 114] Since the company would not reinstate them, and dismissal was therefore wrongful, the panel ordered under Article 89 dismissal damages of 15 days' wages per year of service capped at six months, under Article 75 pay in lieu of 2 months' notice for 5–10 years' service and 3 months beyond 10 years, under Articles 167 and 116 compensation for unused annual leave, under Article 116(5) final wages within 45 hours, and under Article 91 moral damages equal to the dismissal damages. [Para 116][Para 121][Para 124][Para 130][Para 134][Para 136][Para 143][Para 149]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-term contract renewed so that the aggregate duration of the successive contracts exceeds the two-year ceiling in Article 67(2) becomes an indefinite-term contract, whatever form the parties last signed; and where such a worker is dismissed for conduct that grades only as a light fault under the employer's own rules without the oral education and written warning those rules require, the dismissal is wrongful and attracts damages, notice pay, leave compensation, final wages and moral damages. [Para 87][Para 100][Para 114] The panel will order compensation rather than reinstatement once the employer refuses to take the worker back. [Para 81][Para 143]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that, the contracts being indefinite-term, the employer was under no duty to pay the 5% severance for fixed-term contracts and might even claim back the 5% it had already paid. [Para 147][Para 148]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The order is heavily degraded: whole sentences repeat themselves verbatim, one paragraph prints the same clause twice in a row, and the recital of the workers' names and hire dates is interleaved with duplicated fragments, so the hire-date table can only be read by ignoring the repetition. [Para 56][Para 68] The employer's name is spelled at least four different ways, and one variant turns "Interpri" into an unrelated word. [Para 1][Para 28][Para 42] Two article citations are garbled into stray glyphs where Article 73(5) and Article 116 are meant, and one passage substitutes "company" for "collective agreement". [Para 88][Para 134] The five dismissed workers are named in full throughout, with their surnames printed in several variant spellings, including a nickname in parentheses for one of them; this brief uses roles instead. [Para 49][Para 54][Para 149] A numeric fragment intrudes on a page break in the reasoning. [Para 119]
