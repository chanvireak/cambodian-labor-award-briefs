# Award No. 112/12 — Horus Industrial Corp (Arbitration Council, 9 July 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chhea presiding; Ing Sothy, Tun Si Phan) · Doc ID 140139

## 1. Facts
Horus Industrial Corp has run a garment factory at Damnak Thom on Monireth Boulevard, Stung Mean Chey, Phnom Penh since 1992, employing 513 workers. [Para 56] The Federation of Unions for Cambodian Workers and its Horus local union brought 27 demands; four remained unsettled and one of those — a meal allowance for overtime between 16:00 and 20:00 — was agreed at the hearing, leaving three. [Para 2][Para 16][Para 19] The local union elected its leaders on 8 June 2012 and applied to the Ministry for registration on 14 June 2012, but had not been registered when the award issued. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 102] The federation held written authorisations from 352 workers, fourteen of whom appeared on the employer's list of workers who had retracted their thumbprints, leaving 338. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 69][Para 89][Para 91]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay each worker $12 a month as housing and travel allowance — the panel's issue 2? [Para 70][Para 93]
2. Whether the employer must raise the regular attendance bonus from $7 to $15 a month, an increase of $8 — the panel's issue 3? [Para 75][Para 105]
3. Whether the employer must raise the basic wage from $61 to $70 a month, an increase of $9 — the panel's issue 4? [Para 81][Para 111]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** prices, rent, water, electricity and transport have all risen, and other factories pay travel allowance, so $12 a month is needed to ease daily hardship; the attendance bonus should be raised to $15 to reward those who work hard and attend regularly, other factories such as Chang Luong paying $10; the basic wage should reach $70 to encourage effort, Chang Luong paying $80 to operators of second and third machines. [Para 72][Para 77][Para 78][Para 83]

**Employer (respondent):** the company cannot meet any of the three claims because it has no capacity to pay; it already pays $7 attendance bonus and $61 basic wage, and it recognises prior service for workers who produce cards from earlier employers. [Para 73][Para 79][Para 84][Para 85]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider all three remaining claims: the $12 housing and travel allowance, the $8 increase to the attendance bonus, and the $9 increase to the basic wage. [Para 104][Para 110][Para 116][Para 117][Para 118][Para 119]

The panel first tested the federation's standing, since it had to decide whether the federation could represent the 338 workers. Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 allows a party to appear through a person authorised in writing, and the panel found the authorisations valid, setting aside the fourteen workers named in the employer's retraction letters because the claimant raised no argument against that evidence. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 90][Para 91][Para 92]

On each of the three claims the panel applied the same analysis. It searched for a provision of the Labour Law, a collective agreement or an agreement between the parties fixing the sum claimed, and found none: no text requires $12 of housing and travel allowance, none requires an attendance bonus above the $7 actually paid, and none requires a basic wage above the $61 actually paid. [Para 93][Para 107][Para 113] Each demand was therefore an interests dispute rather than a rights dispute, a characterisation the panel made expressly for each. [Para 94][Para 95][Para 108][Para 114]

That characterisation decided the case. An interests dispute calls for an assessment of whether the union is the most representative in the enterprise, because such a union alone may negotiate a collective agreement and bring an interests dispute to the Council. [Para 96][Para 98] The panel relied on Article 43 of Prakas No. 099, under which an award on an interests dispute replaces the collective agreement for one year and applies to every worker in the factory, so that other, non-member workers lose the right to strike for those demands; that consequence requires a representative counterpart. [Para 99][Para 100][Para 101] The local union had not obtained registration from the Ministry, and the panel held that an unregistered union lacks the legal quality needed to represent workers in an interests dispute. [Para 102][Para 103] It therefore declined to consider the claims, following its earlier decisions in Cases No. 42/09 and No. 62/10. [Para 97][Para 109]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a demand is for a sum that no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement or agreement between the parties requires, it is an interests dispute, and the Council will decline to consider it unless the union bringing it is the most representative in the enterprise and is registered: only such a union may conclude the collective agreement that an interests award would replace for a year, and an unregistered union has no capacity to represent workers in an interests claim. [Para 93][Para 98][Para 99][Para 103]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's account of Article 43 — that an interests award binds all workers in the factory and removes non-members' right to strike on those demands — is background to the standing rule rather than a step in the order, and its observation that a union may strike to revise a collective agreement before it expires goes beyond what the outcome required. [Para 100][Para 101]
