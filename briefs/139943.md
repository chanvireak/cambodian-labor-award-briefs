# Award No. 12/12 — Kwai Yang (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 20 February 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Kong Phalla (president) · Doc ID 139943

## 1. Facts
Kwai Yang (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. is a garment factory at Damnak Ampil, Ang Snuol, Kandal, which began operating in early 2011 with about 500 workers; the Khmer Workers' Friendship Union was elected on 4 November 2011 and again on 6 January 2012, and its registration application was receipted on 16 January 2012 [Para 64][Para 95][Para 97][Para 128]. Of 13 claims filed on 11 January 2012, three were settled in conciliation and ten were referred; at the hearing of 27 January 2012 six more were resolved, leaving four — strike wages, a cooperation allowance, a skills allowance, and reinstatement of eight union leaders dismissed on 9 and 10 January 2012 [Para 24][Para 31][Para 32][Para 149]. A strike ran from 18 January 2012 until the panel ordered it stopped on 19 January 2012 [Para 26][Para 27].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay wages for the strike days of 31 December 2011 and 3 January 2012? [Para 67]
2. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must pay a USD 10 monthly cooperation allowance, having previously paid USD 5? [Para 71][Para 121]
3. (Issue 12) Whether the employer must pay a skills allowance of USD 3 for operating one machine and USD 6 for two? [Para 79][Para 136]
4. (Issue 13) Whether the employer must take back the eight union leaders, advisers and members it dismissed in January 2012? [Para 141][Para 81]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** about 300 workers struck on 31 December 2011 and 3 January 2012 and are owed wages for those days; the company used to pay a USD 5 cooperation allowance and should now pay USD 10; skilled operators should be paid for their experience; and the dismissals three or four days after the union notified its leadership were union discrimination [Para 67][Para 71][Para 74][Para 79][Para 103][Para 104].
**Employer (respondent):** it paid the regular bonus for those days but owes no wages during a strike; the 14 November 2011 agreement merged the USD 7 attendance bonus and the USD 5 cooperation allowance into a single USD 12 bonus, so the USD 5 no longer exists and cannot be doubled; it cannot afford a skills allowance; and the dismissals reflected declining work performance, though it accepts it made no prior evaluation and did not seek the Labour Inspector's authorisation [Para 68][Para 75][Para 80][Para 100][Para 102][Para 108][Para 109].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issue 13 ordered (reinstatement of seven women); Issues 2, 4 and 12 dismissed; no sum in riel or USD awarded [Para 181][Para 182][Para 183][Para 184][Para 185].

On Issue 2 the panel applied Art. 332: the labour contract is suspended during a strike and neither work nor wages are owed, and following Award 49/05 it held this applies whether the strike is lawful or unlawful [Para 112][Para 117][Para 118][Para 119]. It also found the strike unlawful for want of prior notice and because conciliation had not been exhausted under Arts. 324 and 320(k)(4), so the claim failed on either footing [Para 110][Para 111][Para 115][Para 116][Para 120].

Issue 4 turned on the 14 November 2011 agreement and the notice of 15 November 2011, which combined the USD 7 attendance bonus with the USD 5 cooperation allowance into a single USD 12 bonus and ended the separate allowance [Para 72][Para 73][Para 76][Para 122][Para 123]. A demand for a further USD 5 rested on no law and no agreement, was an interest dispute, and the union — with only 50 workers behind the complaint and no registered local union — was not the most representative in the factory under Art. 277 and Prakas 099 Art. 43, so the panel declined it [Para 124][Para 127][Para 128][Para 130][Para 131][Para 134][Para 135]. Issue 12 failed identically: no statute or collective agreement provided a skills allowance [Para 136][Para 137][Para 138][Para 139].

On Issue 13 the panel split the eight. For the president Ms M (name redacted), vice-president Ms T (name redacted) and secretary Ms T (name redacted), clause 3(3) of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 gave the protection of a shop steward from 45 days before the election, and after election for a reasonable period — following Award 40/10 (Meng Yan) until registration is applied for — provided the union informed the employer by any reliable means [Para 141][Para 142][Para 146][Para 151]. All three were elected on 6 January 2012 and dismissed on 9 and 10 January 2012, before their fixed-term contracts expired; the union delivered notice on 6 January, again by post on 10 January after the company refused it, and the panel found every reliable means used [Para 148][Para 149][Para 150][Para 152][Para 154][Para 155]. Under Art. 293 dismissal of such leaders requires the Labour Inspector's prior authorisation, and the employer sought none, so reinstatement was ordered [Para 156][Para 158][Para 161][Para 162][Para 163].

The four advisers and members stood differently: clause 7 of Prakas No. 313 limits protected leadership to president, first vice-president and first secretary, so advisers enjoy no special protection [Para 164][Para 165]. The panel nevertheless applied Arts. 12 and 279, which forbid using union membership or activity as a ground for discipline or termination, and its rule that the claimant bears the burden of proof [Para 168][Para 169][Para 170]. It found that burden discharged: all four were dismissed on 9 and 10 January 2012, three or four days after the union notified its office-holders, without any prior performance evaluation, without adequate stated reason, and with no evidence supporting the asserted decline in performance [Para 175][Para 176][Para 177][Para 178][Para 179]. Reinstatement of those four was ordered too [Para 180][Para 185]. The eighth, Ms R (name redacted), was excluded because no thumbprint transferring her claim to the federation was found [Para 93][Para 94].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Wages are not payable for days of strike, whether the strike is lawful or unlawful, because Art. 332 suspends the contract; and a demand for an allowance that neither statute nor agreement supports is an interest dispute the panel will decline where the union is not the most representative in the enterprise [Para 119][Para 134]. By contrast, dismissal of an elected union leader before the expiry of a fixed-duration contract, without the Labour Inspector's authorisation required by Art. 293, is unlawful whatever the stated reason, and reinstatement follows even where no prior warning was given [Para 158][Para 162][Para 163].

**Obiter dicta:** for advisers and ordinary members outside the special-protection class, the panel inferred union discrimination from the proximity of the dismissals to the union's notification combined with the absence of any evaluation or justification, and it observed that a union's notice counts as given where it uses every reliable means, including registered post refused by the employer [Para 154][Para 176][Para 178]. Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented on Issue 13, holding that non-renewal of three-month fixed-duration contracts at expiry was lawful under Art. 73 and that the seven had not attended the 4 November 2011 founding election [Para 191][Para 192][Para 193].

## 6. Source defects disclosed

Paragraph 77 dates the hearing of 27 January 2012 as "27 May 2012" [Para 77]. The non-conciliation report is dated "១៨១ ខែ ២០១២", a month outside the calendar [Para 25]. The operative paragraph prints the strike date as "31 December 2021 / 2011" [Para 181]. Ms P's contract is said to expire on 11 January 2012 but her dismissal on 9 January 2012 is treated as before expiry [Para 89][Para 90]. The worker-appointed arbitrator's name is truncated to "An N" in the signature block, and one head of the operative order lists the reinstated women in a different sequence from the reasoning [Para 187][Para 185].
