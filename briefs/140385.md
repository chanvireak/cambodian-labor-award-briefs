# Award No. 55/09 — Tack Fat Garment (Cambodia) Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 7 May 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry for the employer, Song Vannsin for the workers, Pen Bunchhea chairing) · Doc ID 140385

## 1. Facts
Tack Fat Garment (Cambodia) Ltd. employs workers at National Road 2, Chak Angre Leou Commune, Meanchey District, Phnom Penh, and was represented at the hearing by one administration staff member. [Para 1][Para 2] On 29 December 2008 the Khmer Youth Trade Union Federation (KYFTU) local union filed nine demands on working conditions; an expert officer conciliated on 12 March 2009 and seven were settled, leaving two. [Para 9][Para 10][Para 11] The Secretariat summoned the parties for 30 April 2009 at 2:00 p.m.; the employer attended, the union did not appear and told the Secretariat by telephone that it would not attend any further hearing either. [Para 13][Para 15][Para 16]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must reimburse the actual cost of workers' medical checks and pay the fees for newly recruited workers, as Article 247 of the Labour Law is said to require? [Para 2]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the US$ 5 monthly seniority bonus for workers with more than four years' service must be increased by US$ 1 because Notification No. 017 SKBY dated 18 July 2000 is unclear? [Para 4]

The panel reached neither question: it found itself unable to make any finding of fact because the claimant was absent, and closed the case. [Para 18][Para 42]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, Local Union of KYFTU):** medical checks should be reimbursed at the amount actually spent and paid for new recruits, since Article 247 of the Labour Law places the obligation on the employer; and the US$ 5 seniority bonus should rise by US$ 1 because Notification No. 017 SKBY of 18 July 2000 is unclear. The union filed nothing further and did not appear. [Para 2][Para 4][Para 8]

**Employer (respondent, Tack Fat Garment (Cambodia) Ltd.):** it cannot reimburse or pay medical-check fees because a worker must hold sufficient documents before recruitment; it has already implemented Notification No. 017 SKBY and refuses any increase; and it asked the panel to decide according to law. [Para 3][Para 5][Para 16]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** withdrawn — Case 55/09-Tack Fat Garment was closed, with no order on either demand. [Para 42]

The panel did not reach the merits. It applied Rule 4.7 of the Procedural Rules annexed to Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, which allows the panel either to proceed in a party's absence or to terminate the proceedings by award, provided it is satisfied the parties were properly notified, and Clause 21 of the same Prakas, which permits termination where a duly invited party fails to appear without good cause. [Para 21][Para 22]

Following Arbitral Awards 16/07-Lotus, 27/07-M & V (Branch 3), 95/08-Yung Wah, 132/08-GHG and 138/08-Marlyn, the panel tested three conditions. First, proper notification: the Secretariat dispatched a notice and telephoned before the hearing, and the union confirmed it knew the date, time and venue. Second, non-appearance: the panel read "appear" as being present and taking part in all four steps of the process — introduction and conflict disclosure, explanation of the process and confirmation of the issues, conciliation if agreed, and arbitration — and the union took part in none. Third, no proper reasons: the union gave none, merely restating that it would not come. [Para 24][Para 27][Para 28][Para 33][Para 34][Para 37]

The three conditions being met, the panel held it retained authority to issue an award, but concluded that the union had been given ample opportunity to argue its claim, had not discharged the claimant's obligation to supply reasons and evidence, and had therefore dropped its claim. It characterised the refusal to bring the dispute before it as a lack of good will in resolving the labour dispute, and closed the case. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 41][Para 42]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a duly notified claimant union neither appears nor gives proper reasons, the panel may close the case by award rather than decide the demands: absence removes the evidentiary basis for any finding of fact, and the claimant's failure to argue its claim is treated as abandonment of it. The three conditions — proper notification, non-appearance at every step, and absence of good cause — must each be satisfied before proceedings are terminated. [Para 21][Para 23][Para 41]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that the union's refusal to submit the dispute demonstrated a lack of good will in resolving the labour dispute — a comment on conduct, not a ground of the order. [Para 42]
