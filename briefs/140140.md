# Award No. 113/12 — All Super Inter Bright (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 4 July 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Aing Aeng Thong presiding; Ing Sothy, An Non), with a dissenting opinion from the employer-appointed arbitrator · Doc ID 140140

## 1. Facts
All Super Inter Bright (Cambodia) is a garment factory at Tuol Sangke, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, employing about 1,125 workers. [Para 35][Para 36] The Federation of Independent Khmer Workers' Unions brought eight demands; two were settled at the hearing (issues 3 and 5), the claimant withdrew five (issues 1, 2, 6, 7 and 8), and only issue 4 was left for decision. [Para 19][Para 22][Para 23][Para 24] The factory-level union was not yet registered, and the federation acted on written authorisations from five workers. [Para 38][Para 39] The employer pays a regular attendance bonus of $7 a month under Notice No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011, which gives the bonus to workers who attend every working day of the month. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 45][Para 46] The union asked that the bonus be reduced only in proportion to days of leave the employer has lawfully authorised, rather than forfeited entirely. [Para 40][Para 41]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reduce the regular attendance bonus in proportion to the number of days a worker is absent on leave lawfully authorised by the employer, instead of deducting the whole bonus for that month? [Para 40][Para 45][Para 64]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the demand is only that the attendance bonus be deducted pro rata to the days of leave properly granted by the employer, not that absence go unpunished. [Para 40][Para 41]

**Employer (respondent):** the $7 bonus is payable only to workers who came to work with no absence whatever during working hours in the month; Notice No. 041/11 requires regular attendance as the condition of the bonus, so it cannot pay it to a worker who was absent. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to deduct the regular attendance bonus pro rata to the number of days the worker was absent on leave lawfully authorised by the employer. [Para 63][Para 64]

The panel began from the character of the bonus. Article 103 of the Labour Law includes among wages sums paid as a bonus, and the attendance bonus set by Notice No. 041/11 is exactly that — a sum the employer pays to workers as a reward — so it is a form of wage. [Para 53][Para 54] It then applied Articles 71(6) and 72: the employment contract is suspended by the worker's absence with the employer's permission, given by law, by collective agreement or by mutual consent, and suspension affects the essential obligations of the contract, namely the worker's duty to work and the employer's duty to pay wages. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57] Leave lawfully granted therefore suspends the contract, and the employer owes no wage for those days — and so no attendance bonus either. [Para 58][Para 59]

The consequence the panel drew was proportionality: because the bonus is a wage earned day by day, the employer may not deduct all of it, but only the share corresponding to the days of authorised absence. [Para 60][Para 63] It noted that its earlier awards had ordered the same limited deduction, citing Cases No. 112/11, No. 03/12, No. 136/11, No. 154/11 and No. 28/12, and that in defining "the number of working days in each month" it had held the reference to be the days the employer requires work or the law obliges the worker to serve — 26 days, or 21 to 22, depending on the company. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 61][Para 62]

The arbitrator appointed by the employer dissented under Article 37 of Prakas No. 099, which allows an arbitrator who disagrees with the majority to record a dissenting opinion as an annex. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 68] His reasoning was that a bonus is by nature a conditional reward: the dictionary and the French legal lexicon define it as a sum paid over and above normal wages to encourage productivity or reward seniority, and the person who sets the condition is the owner of the bonus — here the employer — while the worker is the one who must satisfy it. [Para 69][Para 73][Para 76] He illustrated with a promise to pay $100 to anyone who counts to 1,000: a worker who stops at 900 because he is tired, even with permission to stop, cannot claim a share of the $100, because the owner decides whether to apportion. [Para 77][Para 78][Para 82][Para 83] On that view Notice No. 041/11 conditions the $7 on full and regular attendance, and a worker who does not meet the condition receives nothing. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 87]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A monthly attendance bonus is a wage, and where a worker is absent on leave the employer has lawfully authorised, the contract is suspended for those days: the employer may deduct the bonus only in proportion to the days of authorised absence and may not forfeit the whole bonus for the month, because the bonus is earned day by day rather than all-or-nothing. [Para 54][Para 57][Para 59][Para 60]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's adoption of the "working days in the month" formula from earlier awards (26 days, or 21 to 22, according to company practice) is background definition rather than a step in the order; the dissent's dictionary and French-lexicon excursus on the meaning of "bonus" is likewise argumentative colour. [Para 49][Para 73]
