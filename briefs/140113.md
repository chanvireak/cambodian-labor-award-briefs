# Award No. 172/11 — Vision Glory Knitting (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 26 January 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Kong Phalguok (president), Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Virak (worker-appointed) · Doc ID 140113

## 1. Facts
Vision Glory Knitting (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. is a garment cutting and sewing factory employing around 500 workers. [Para 43][Para 58] On 3 November 2011 the Cambodian Workers' Union Federation filed a collective claim for improved conditions listing eight demands; conciliation settled four and left issues 1, 3, 6 and 7 unresolved, which reached the Council on 14 December 2011. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 21] The union was not registered with the Ministry of Labour and had no written authorisation from the workers it claimed to represent, so after a hearing at which only four workers appeared the panel limited the case to those four. [Para 45][Para 96][Para 98] The parties had signed the 28 September 2010 memorandum of understanding on professional relations, under which rights-dispute awards bind them. [Para 22][Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company should be ordered to grant employment contracts of at least six months and one year (panel's issue 1)? [Para 51]
2. Whether the company must disclose lot prices in advance, which the workers framed as five days' notice (panel's issue 3)? [Para 59]
3. Whether the company must pro-rate the regular attendance bonus for days of legally authorised absence (panel's issue 6)? [Para 66]
4. Whether non-renewal of the contracts of pregnant workers amounts to discrimination contrary to the Constitution and the Labour Law (panel's issue 7)? [Para 72]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Fixed three-month contracts are renewed indefinitely because work is hard to find, the three-month term is really a probationary contract, and the employer terminates only union members, which is discrimination against the union. [Para 53] Lot prices are issued only after a full month's work although a style takes two to three months, so styles are hard, prices are low and workers lose the chance to claim; prices should be notified five days ahead. [Para 60][Para 61] The attendance bonus is part of wages under Article 103 of the Labour Law, the employer already deducts wages for the days absent, and Award No. 112/11-Yong Va I ordered pro-rating. [Para 69] Pregnant workers on three-month contracts are simply not renewed at expiry. [Para 72]

**Employer (respondent):** Three-month fixed contracts are company policy; November and December are dead months in weaving and work resumes only in March, so there was no work and therefore no discrimination against the union. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 56] Lot pricing needs study because the company is new and must establish an average; if a worker's wage falls below the base wage because of a low lot price the company will top it up. [Para 62][Para 63] The USD 7 attendance bonus is paid only to workers who attend every working day, so a worker lawfully absent has not met the condition. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 70] The company did not refuse to renew pregnant workers; it merely advised them not to resign before their contract expired. [Para 73][Para 76]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim for longer contracts was refused, lot prices must be notified in advance and issued within 3 to 7 days after a new series, the attendance bonus must be pro-rated for days of authorised leave, and the pregnancy claim was refused; the rights rulings take effect immediately. [Para 165][Para 166][Para 167][Para 168][Para 169]

On issue 1, the panel held the demand to be a rights dispute engaging Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 664 of the Civil Code, and reasoned through Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code that a contract arises only from a matching offer and acceptance. [Para 80][Para 104][Para 106][Para 108] Neither party may impose a term the other has not accepted: the workers cannot force a long contract and the employer cannot force a short one. [Para 110][Para 123][Para 124] The demand was therefore refused. [Para 126]

On issue 2, the panel treated the same demand as a rights dispute under Article 112 of the Labour Law, which requires clear notice of wage conditions whenever they change. [Para 81][Para 127][Para 129] The article fixes no number of days, but the obligation is real, and earlier awards settled on a practice of 3 to 7 days; the employer's argument that a new company needs time to study averages was held unreasonable because it defeats the purpose of the article. [Para 130][Para 132][Para 133][Para 136] The employer was ordered to issue lot prices before the work is done rather than after, within 3 to 7 days of a new series. [Para 138][Para 166]

On issue 3, the panel held the attendance bonus under Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 to be a bonus and therefore a form of wage under Article 103 of the Labour Law. [Para 147][Para 148][Para 149] Using the definition of "working days in the month" adopted in Award No. 112/11-Yong Va I, it distinguished full working days, days reduced by public holidays or annual leave, and days reduced by leave the employer authorises. [Para 142][Para 143][Para 145] Workers absent on public holidays or annual leave keep the full bonus; for other authorised leave the bonus is pro-rated; and the panel ordered pro-rating for the days of authorised absence. [Para 151][Para 152][Para 167]

On issue 4, the panel accepted that the Constitution and Article 73 of the Labour Law bar dismissing a woman because she is pregnant. [Para 82][Para 155][Para 157] But the four claimants were all men, none produced evidence of a pregnant worker affected, and the union held no written authorisation from one, so no concrete claim existed. [Para 160][Para 161][Para 162][Para 163][Para 164]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand to lengthen fixed-duration contracts is a rights dispute that fails on both sides, because contract terms arise only from matching offer and acceptance and neither party can impose a duration on the other. [Para 80][Para 110] Article 112 of the Labour Law obliges the employer to notify wage conditions clearly whenever they change; the absence of a stated deadline does not excuse delay, and lot prices must be issued before the work is performed, within 3 to 7 days of a new series. [Para 129][Para 138] A contractual bonus counts as wage, so it must be pro-rated rather than forfeited for days of leave the employer has authorised. [Para 149][Para 152] A discrimination claim requires at least one identified victim; a union that has neither registration nor written authorisation cannot litigate it on others' behalf. [Para 161][Para 162]

**Obiter dicta:** The employer-appointed arbitrator dissented on issue 3, arguing from dictionary and French labour-law definitions that a bonus is conditional and its giver decides whether the condition is met. [Para 173][Para 178][Para 181] The panel's remark that a new company's need to study averages cannot override Article 112 is guidance beyond the order. [Para 136]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The company name appears in at least five OCR variants across the face, exhibits and annex ("Vision Glory Knitting", "Vision Quarry Knitting", "Vision Glory", "Vision Kliry", "Vision Glory Knitting (Cambodia) Co., Ltd"); treated as one entity. [Para 1][Para 28][Para 43]
- The presiding arbitrator's name is truncated beside a stray digit on the signature page, so the panel composition above relies on the face of the award. [Para 1][Para 171]
- The award date is given as 6 January 2012 in the reasons and 26 January 2012 on the face, the difference explained by the parties' agreed extension. [Para 1][Para 27]
- Only issues 1, 3, 6 and 7 were heard; issues 2, 4, 5 and 8 were settled at conciliation, so the numbering in the source is discontinuous. [Para 21]
- Para 153 records an order of "2 days" of pro-rating while the operative paragraph 167 leaves the number of days unstated; both are reported as printed.
- The dissent refers to "all 4 days" where the notification speaks of all working days in the month. [Para 191]
