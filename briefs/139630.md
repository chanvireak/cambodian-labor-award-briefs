# Award No. 172/14 — Foot Forward (Cambodia) Socks Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 31 July 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Kong Phalha chair) · Doc ID 139630

## 1. Facts
Foot Forward (Cambodia) Socks Co., Ltd., a sock factory at Bos Ta village, Kaheng commune, Samrong Tong district, Kampong Speu, was registered on 16 March 2012 and employs about 210 workers. [Para 52][Para 53] Two union federations filed on 5 June 2014; conciliation on 12 June 2014 settled 1 of 8 demands and left 7, and the report reached the Council on 27 June 2014; the hearing was on 7 July 2014. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 17][Para 13] Only 11 workers were parties and 5 attended. [Para 58] The flashpoint was the employer's decision not to renew the six-month contracts of a union president and a union committee member, both expiring on 1 June 2014, after telling them around 20 May 2014 that there was no work, and its subsequent hiring of new workers. [Para 60][Para 70][Para 75][Para 87]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must renew the fixed-duration contracts of the two union leaders, which had already reached their term? [Para 60][Para 146]
2. Whether the employer must pay a transport and accommodation allowance of USD 15 a month, being USD 8 above the USD 7 now paid? [Para 101][Para 187]
3. Whether the employer must pay an attendance bonus of USD 15 a month, being USD 3 above the USD 12 now paid? [Para 108][Para 208]
4. Whether the employer must pay 5,000 riel a day as a midday meal allowance? [Para 112][Para 221]
5. Whether the employer must install a television in the canteen for the workers? [Para 122][Para 230]
6. Whether the employer must issue one-year contracts and pay 5% severance each time a fixed-duration contract expires while the worker stays on? [Para 130][Para 238]
7. Whether the employer must pay 2,000 riel an hour, rather than 2,000 riel a day, as meal money for overtime work? [Para 141][Para 285]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the two were the elected union president and a union committee member and assistant, the employer was told of the election result on 27 March 2014, and it hired replacements once their contracts lapsed — that is union discrimination; six-month contracts let the employer decide each worker's job security, and the 5% severance is due at every expiry. [Para 77][Para 79][Para 91][Para 134][Para 164][Para 165]
**Employer (respondent):** it never received the election notice and did not know the two held union posts; their contracts expired by their own terms because there was no work, and eight other workers' contracts lapsed the same day; it pays the USD 7 transport allowance that Notice No. 230 requires and a USD 12 bonus above the USD 10 minimum; six-month contracts are lawful. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 86][Para 94][Para 111][Para 139][Para 170]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — every demand was refused and nothing was ordered; the award is non-binding and becomes final 8 days after notification if no party objects to the Minister. [Para 297][Para 298][Para 305]

On issue 1 the panel began with Article 73(1) of the Labour Law: a fixed-duration contract expires of itself when its term arrives, and whether to renew is a matter for the parties' agreement, so no one can be compelled to continue a contract. [Para 146][Para 147][Para 148] It accepted that the notice of 27 March 2014, listing the president and signed for receipt by a company guard, showed the employer knew of her position. [Para 172] But Articles 12 and 279, which forbid an employer from using union membership or activity as the ground for discipline or termination, place the burden of proof on the party alleging discrimination, and the panel found that knowledge alone did not establish the cause; the workers produced nothing on the number, section or date of any replacement hires, and none of hostility to the union, while eight other workers' contracts expired on the same day. [Para 156][Para 158][Para 174][Para 178][Para 182][Para 185]

Issues 2 to 5 and 7 were classified together: no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice created the duties claimed, and Notice No. 230 of 25 July 2012 sets USD 7 for transport and USD 10 for the attendance bonus as minimums the employer already met or exceeded. [Para 188][Para 193][Para 194][Para 209][Para 211][Para 222][Para 223][Para 291] Each was therefore an interest dispute, which the panel will decide only where the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise; with 11 or 16 members among 210 workers it was not, so the claims were refused without examination on the merits. [Para 205][Para 206][Para 227][Para 229]

On issue 6 the panel held that a labour contract is formed by consent — Article 65 of the Labour Law and Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code — so neither side can force the other to accept a one-year term it does not want, and the demand for annual contracts failed on that ground alone. [Para 238][Para 243][Para 249][Para 263][Para 265] Article 73 requires the 5% severance when the employment relationship is cut off, not at each renewal while employment continues, so the claim to payment at every expiry also failed; the panel added that the employer's own practice of paying 5% annually was for the same reason incorrect. [Para 279][Para 280][Para 281][Para 283][Para 284]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-duration contract ends the employment relationship by its own terms, and a demand that it be renewed cannot succeed because neither party may be forced into a new contract; where the worker says the non-renewal was union discrimination under Articles 12 and 279, the worker carries the burden of proving both that the employer knew of the union role and that the role caused the decision, and proof of knowledge without evidence of replacement hiring, hostility or differential treatment does not discharge it. Severance of 5% under Article 73 falls due only when the employment relationship is actually cut off, not on each renewal. [Para 148][Para 185][Para 280]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's wider remarks that a party may refuse a proposed contract form and that a contract procured by coercion is void or voidable, and its observation that the employer's annual 5% payments were themselves wrong although no one claimed them, were not necessary to the refusals. [Para 250][Para 254][Para 283]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award is dated 31 July 2014 [Para 1], matching the extension from 21 to 31 July 2014 [Para 23], but the panel is said to have been constituted on "30 June 2020 2014" [Para 18], and the Prakas appointing the twelfth-term members is printed as 16 June 2013 [Para 9].
- The number of worker parties is inconsistent: 11 workers represented through the transfer letters [Para 58][Para 205], 16 workers elsewhere [Para 205][Para 218], and only 5 present at the hearing [Para 56].
- The chair's name is legible in the panel list but lost to OCR in the signature block, which reads "movemons 24 (2019)" [Para 1][Para 306]; individual workers appear only in a corrupted evidence table and are not reproduced here [Para 88].
- Amounts and references are corrupted in places: the bonus claim as "2 15 dollars" [Para 299], the meal claim as "20 5,000 riel" [Para 300], and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 as "Article 4300" [Para 234] and "Article 430" [Para 216].
- The company is registered under commercial number 1622 [Para 39] but its investment registration as "Inv. 0619 E/2012" [Para 52], and the name is printed in several Khmer variants, including "Snocks" in English [Para 38][Para 11].
