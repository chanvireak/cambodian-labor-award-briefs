# Award No. 74/07 — Global Apparels Limited (Arbitration Council, 30 August 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Tuon Siphann, Pen Bunchhea chairing) · Doc ID 140628

## 1. Facts
Global Apparels Limited, a garment factory at Tropaing Tuol Village, Kandal, faced a collective dispute filed by the Khmer Youth Trade Union (KYTU), the Cambodian Apparel Workers of Democratic Union (CAWDU) and the Cambodian Union (CU) at Global Apparel Limited Factory [Para 1][Para 2]. Of fourteen demands, ten were conciliated on 31 July 2007 and a further issue was settled at the hearing, leaving Issues 1, 2 and 4 for decision [Para 11][Para 12][Para 13]. From February to April 2007 the factory had no work and told workers to take leave of one week to two and a half months on 50 percent of wage, 50 percent of attendance bonus and 100 percent of seniority bonus, without any suspension of contracts authorised by the Labour Inspector [Para 14][Para 15][Para 39]. The remaining demands were compensation of 1,000 riels union dues KYTU says the company failed to deduct, and an extra US$3 on the US$5 attendance bonus [Para 7][Para 8][Para 45].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company owes the other 50 percent of wages for February to April 2007, having stopped work without suspending the labour contracts under the Labour Law? [Para 14][Para 41]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay KYTU the union contribution fees it did not deduct from the wages of workers who joined that union? [Para 28][Para 44]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the company must add US$3 to the existing US$5 monthly attendance bonus? [Para 8][Para 58]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — KYTU, CAWDU and CU):** that the company told them only of a suspension for renovation, not of the reduced wage and bonuses; that most workers did not understand the procedure and signed the leave lists fearing they would otherwise receive no wage; that a 5 June 2006 agreement promised 50 percent of wage when there was no work and 100 percent if workers were called in; that the resignation letters were valid and the dues deduction should have been made; and that the bonus increase was needed because prices were rising and the minimum wage had reached US$50 per month [Para 19][Para 23][Para 24][Para 25][Para 31][Para 32].
**Employer (respondent — Global Apparels Limited):** that it met the workers and explained the leave and the 50/50/100 payment, that the union's vice-president signed a leave list accepting those terms, that it could not deduct dues for the new union while resignation letters lacked the former union's signature and would draw protests, and that it had already been lenient over monthly and annual leave [Para 16][Para 19][Para 22][Para 30][Para 34][Para 35].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must pay a further 50 percent of wages for February to April 2007 within 15 days of the Award taking effect; the union-dues demand is declined and the US$3 bonus demand rejected [Para 62].
On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 71(11) of the Labour Law, under which suspension for serious economic difficulty may not exceed two months and must be under the control of the Labour Inspector, and Article 72, under which suspension relieves the duty to work and to pay unless provided otherwise [Para 35][Para 36][Para 37]. Because the company never notified the Inspector, the suspension was not lawful, and prior awards held that an employer who does not lawfully suspend owes 100 percent of wages, while one who does suspend owes nothing — Awards 21/03 Loyal New Point II, 46/04 M & A Cambodia, 60/04 United Art and 01/04 [Para 40][Para 41][Para 42]. The workers' signed acceptance of half pay was held void because it gave less than the law allows, Article 13(1) making derogations unenforceable [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44].
On Issue 2 the panel set Article 129 paragraph 2, which requires a written, revocable authorisation for dues deduction, against Article 281, which forbids employers to deduct dues, read in Award 62/05 Zhen Tai as protecting workers from employer interference [Para 44][Para 45]. Workers who move unions must in writing stop the old deduction; here they did not, the union produced neither resignation letters nor a list of demanding workers with request dates, so the evidence was insufficient and the demand failed, following Awards 63/04 Shine Well (Issue 4) and 99/06 South Bay (Issue 5) [Para 49][Para 52][Para 53][Para 55][Para 56][Para 57][Para 58].
On Issue 4 the panel read Clause 3 of Notification No. 745 dated 23 October 2006, preserving Clause 3 of Notification No. 017 dated 18 July 2000, as guaranteeing a US$5 attendance bonus and nothing more, so no US$3 was owed; the claim being an interest demand, it could be heard only from a union with most representative status under Article 277, and no union at the factory held that status, so the panel declined to consider it, citing Awards 57/06 Evergreen, 60/04 United Art (Issue 3) and 08/07 Siu Quinh (Issue 3) [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60][Para 61].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer stops production and sends workers on leave without the Labour Inspector's authorisation under Article 71(11), the contracts are not suspended and full wages remain due; the workers' signature on a list accepting half pay is a derogation void under Article 13(1) and cannot defeat the claim [Para 39][Para 41][Para 43]. An interest demand is entertained only from the union holding most representative status under Article 277 [Para 59][Para 60].

**Obiter dicta:** that a lawful suspension would leave workers with no wage at all, and the panel's observation that the company had been generous over leave [Para 27][Para 34].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel's Issue 3 (seniority bonus of an extra US$1 per year) was conciliated at the hearing and is therefore absent from §2 [Para 12]. The decision line reads "Reject to consider" for Issue 4, where the reasons say the panel declines to consider it [Para 61][Para 62]. OCR has dropped the letter "t" throughout and renders N/A as NIA [Para 1][Para 13]. The employer's exhibits include internal work rules of a different company, Jusca Garment (Cambodia) Ltd., and the union documents also refer to Jusca, so the identity of the employer of record is printed inconsistently [Para 13]. The representative list names a "Khmer Union (KU)" although the header names the Cambodian Union (CU) [Para 2][Para 3].
