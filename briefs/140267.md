# Award No. 46/10 — Yee Garment Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អ៉ី ហ្គាមេន) (Arbitration Council, 21 June 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy; Vann Pov; chair Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 140267

## 1. Facts
Yee Garment has operated since 2006 and employs 3,630 workers; two unions exist in the factory, the Khmer Youth Voice Union and the local of C.CAWDU (សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យកម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា), and neither holds most-representative status. [Para 42] [Para 43] [Para 44] [Para 45] The C.CAWDU local, the claimant, has about 500 members. [Para 46] [Para 47] Of five demands, two were settled in conciliation on 21 April 2010; at the hearing the union withdrew Issue 1, a demand to raise the lot price of P.O 0344 from US$0.28 to US$0.30 per piece, leaving Issues 2 and 3. [Para 2] [Para 14] [Para 15] [Para 18] [Para 19] Issue 2 was that the company must not ship garments out or recruit new workers to replace the workforce while a lot price is in dispute; Issue 3 that it must stop deducting dues for the old union from workers who had resigned from it and deduct only for C.CAWDU. [Para 4] [Para 6] [Para 48] [Para 62]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the company must refrain from shipping garments out and from recruiting replacement workers while the union is demanding an increase in the lot price? [Para 48] [Para 82]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the company must stop deducting union dues for the former union (Khmer Youth Voice Union) from workers who resigned from it and deduct dues for C.CAWDU only? [Para 62] [Para 104]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that on 16 March 2010 a Korean-registered truck carried P.O 0344 out to workshops in the La Thnam area and to a workshop opposite the MV company, and that 10 to 20 new workers were hired for night work, an ID card being found under a table in the ironing room; that shipping work out during a price negotiation frightens piece-rate workers into accepting, leaves them without work or overtime and pushes them onto annual leave; and that the company deducts 2,000 riel a month, 1,000 for each union, although 424 workers resigned from the old union. [Para 49] [Para 51] [Para 59] [Para 60] [Para 63] [Para 80]
**Employer (respondent):** that it never shipped goods out or recruited replacements; that sending work for washing, special treatment or printing to other factories is ordinary production management and not an anti-union act, and that the trade is organised that way in Cambodia; that where a price change is proposed workers are free to accept or refuse, and those who refuse keep their base wage; and that it deducts for both unions because both asked it to, and cannot stop until each worker files an official written request concentrated on that point. [Para 52] [Para 55] [Para 56] [Para 68] [Para 69] [Para 81]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both remaining demands are refused; the award is non-binding and becomes binding eight days after notification absent written opposition. [Para 127] [Para 128] [Para 129]

On Issue 2 the panel began from Article 2 of the Labour Law, which allows an enterprise several establishments under the employer's management and direction, and from its earlier awards 62/06-Quick Sew, 108/06, 33/07-Gold Fame and 119/09-SL, holding that the employer may organise and manage the company, including recruiting new workers and shipping goods out, so long as that right is exercised lawfully and reasonably. [Para 84] [Para 86] [Para 87] [Para 88] [Para 89] It found the workers' evidence insufficient to prove that goods had in fact been sent outside: a photograph of the two Korean trucks with plates ច ១៥៩៧ ភព២ and ៨៤៤០ ភព២ parked at the company does not show goods leaving, and would need a precise account of the date, time and recipient. [Para 91] [Para 92] Nor did the workers establish that the night workers were sewing the very garments under negotiation. [Para 93] [Para 95] Following awards 63/04, 99/06 and 33/07, the panel held that the party making an allegation carries the burden of proving it, and that vague assertions cannot be acted on. [Para 100] [Para 101] [Para 102] The demand was therefore refused for want of proof. [Para 103]

On Issue 3 the panel applied Article 129 paragraph 2, under which a worker may authorise deduction from wages to pay dues to the union of which he or she is a member, the authorisation being in writing and revocable at any time. [Para 107] [Para 108] It read the provision as making the choice personal to each worker, exercised directly by written request, and as protecting workers from deductions against their will while protecting the company from error, following awards 60/05-Evergreen and 13/09. [Para 109] [Para 110] [Para 111] [Para 113] On the facts, the resignation letters the workers produced were addressed to the leadership of the old union only and did not express any wish that the employer stop deducting; C.CAWDU had not filed an official written request to that effect and had only discussed the matter with the employer once. [Para 117] [Para 118] [Para 119] [Para 122] The panel accepted that once a worker files a written request the employer must stop the deduction, but held it could not order the company to stop in the absence of such requests. [Para 121] [Para 123] [Para 125] [Para 126]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer's right under Article 2 to organise production includes recruiting workers and sending work out, and it is not an anti-union act unless exercised unlawfully or unreasonably; but the union that alleges that goods were shipped out or replacements hired during a pay negotiation must prove it with evidence identifying the date, time and recipient, and photographs of trucks parked on the premises or hearsay about night workers will not do. Under Article 129 paragraph 2 the authorisation to deduct union dues belongs to each worker personally and must be given, and may be withdrawn, in writing; resignation letters addressed to the former union do not bind the employer, so the panel cannot order an end to the deduction until individual workers or the union file a written request with the employer. [Para 89] [Para 92] [Para 102] [Para 110] [Para 121] [Para 123]

**Obiter dicta:** that the panel has discretion to decline to hear a claim the evidence cannot support, and that where a worker refuses a reduced lot price the company still pays the base wage. [Para 54] [Para 97] [Para 102]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is a Khmer original; the Latin arbitrator names in the Forum line are transliterations, and the chair's name is followed at signature by a garbled Latin fragment. [Para 1] [Para 130] The non-conciliation report bears one number, 247/10, with two different dates, 4 April 2010 in the procedural section and 4 May 2010 in the reasons and evidence list, and the complaint is said to have arrived on 30 March 2010 for a hearing on 1 June 2010. [Para 10] [Para 15] [Para 38] The panel cites a Prakas appointing arbitrators of the eighth term dated 9 June 2010. [Para 8] The dues figures conflict: the union says 1,000 riel per worker for C.CAWDU, the company says 2,000 riel in total, and the passage recording the company's account prints "១១០០០ រៀល" for each union, which cannot be reconciled with either. [Para 62] [Para 68] The company is spelled several ways (អ៉ី ហ្គាមេន, អ៊ី ហ្គាមេន, អ៊ីន ហ្គារមន, រ៉ី ហ្កា មេន), and one document list is numbered out of sequence ("០០", then "១-", then "១៣-"). [Para 20] [Para 28] [Para 31] [Para 32] The evidence includes resignation letters naming seven individual workers, and a retirement letter naming one more; those names are not reproduced here and are given by role or collective description only. [Para 21] [Para 36] The operative order on Issue 3 repeats its own heading twice in the space of a line, and OCR corrupts the reasoning throughout ("ភាកី", "កាត្វកិច្ច", "ឆាស់"). [Para 114] [Para 123] [Para 128] Employer representatives are described by role only.
