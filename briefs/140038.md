# Award No. 74/11 — Forever Million Footwear Industry Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 18 August 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun, Tuon Siphann, Nhean So Munin) · Doc ID 140038

## 1. Facts
Forever Million Footwear Industry Co., Ltd has operated at Trapang Chouk, Tekthla, Sensok since 1997 and employs 1,700 workers; the employer also established Jocam Footwear Co., Ltd on the same premises, registered on 31 August 2010 and permitted to run its factory on 7 February 2011, employing 200 workers and sharing the administration system and employees [Para 17][Para 21][Para 23]. The Local Union of VKYUF, registered on 25 April 2011, is the only union in Forever Million and authorised VKYUF to collect its dues [Para 29][Para 30]. A strike ran from 2 to 11 June 2011 [Para 94]. Of ten demands, the workers withdrew issues 2, 5 and 7, leaving seven for decision [Para 14]. Mr S (Chun Setha), a knife organiser since 1 November 2008 on an undetermined-duration contract, and Ms D (Chan Sopharath), a fabric organiser, were both dismissed on 28 May 2011 [Para 33][Para 34][Para 46].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr S and Ms D? [Para 5][Para 112]
3. Whether the employer must provide a 1,000 riel meal allowance for overtime from 7:00 p.m. to 9:00 p.m.? [Para 5][Para 145]
4. Whether the employer must set up a day-care centre and nursing room in the factory? [Para 5][Para 154]
6. Whether the employer must provide a proper seniority bonus and back pay the underpayment of seniority bonus? [Para 5][Para 159]
8. Whether the employer must refrain from discriminating against the union, its committee and members? [Para 5][Para 165]
9. Whether the employer must dismiss David Qu immediately? [Para 6][Para 173]
10. Whether the employer must deduct wages from union members for the union contribution fee? [Para 7][Para 189]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The dismissals of Mr S and Ms D were union discrimination, as was the treatment of eight activists paid off at the security stand after the strike while non-activists returned [Para 39][Para 94][Para 95]. They sought a 1,000 riel meal allowance, a day-care centre and nursing room, seniority bonus arrears, reinstatement and back pay, dismissal of David Qu, and payroll deduction of union dues [Para 63][Para 145][Para 156][Para 191].
**Employer (respondent):** Mr S was dismissed for serious misconduct — handing out incorrect knives at least three times, producing at least 1,600 defective pairs — and Ms D for misconduct and absence; the eight were Jocam workers who abandoned their posts after the strike [Para 34][Para 35][Para 130][Para 101]. It refused the meal allowance for low profit, said the 2007 agreements on milk and day-care allowances remained enforceable, denied discrimination, refused to dismiss David Qu, and required proper deduction documents [Para 64][Para 96][Para 111][Para 157].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Reinstatement refused for Mr S but ordered for Ms D with May 2011 wages and back pay; the meal allowance was declined; a day-care centre and nursing room were ordered; seniority bonus was ordered for Ms S, Ms N and Mr L; the eight claimants were reinstated, with back pay only for Mr L; dismissal of David Qu was rejected; and the union was ordered to prepare deduction documents [Para 204][Para 205].

On Issue 1, the panel found that the employer's language and conduct showed an inclination to discriminate against the union, but that Mr S had not disputed the evidence that activists sought personal gain, and his repeated knife errors causing large loss were serious misconduct under the spirit of Article 83 [Para 115][Para 118][Para 128]. For Ms D, the employer never showed which internal rule she breached, her warnings were not substantiated by any explanation of how her work was evaluated, and her misconduct was not serious; the panel therefore held her dismissal was union discrimination [Para 137][Para 140][Para 143]. It ordered her reinstatement and back pay [Para 144].

On Issue 3, the panel found no contract or law requiring the extra allowance and classified the demand as an interests dispute; because the union did not show most representative status, it declined to consider the demand [Para 149][Para 150][Para 153]. On Issue 4, it found at least 100 women employed and no day-care centre, and held the 2007 agreements unenforceable because the Democratic Union lacked most representative status and had dissolved; Article 186 required the centre and nursing room [Para 155][Para 158][Para 159]. On Issue 6, it applied Notification No. 017 and Notification No. 041/11 with the three-year limit in Article 120 [Para 162][Para 165]. On Issue 8, it found the eight were Forever Million workers whose contracts were not lawfully terminated, and that allowing non-members to return while refusing Mr L established discrimination even though the strike was unlawful [Para 167][Para 169][Para 171]. On Issue 9, it held that only the contracting parties may terminate a contract and that a manager's wave to return to work was not a threat [Para 179][Para 183][Para 188]. On Issue 10, it ordered the union to supply the documents the employer required, after which deduction must follow [Para 201][Para 203].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
(a) A demand that no contract or law supports is an interests dispute, and a union without most representative status lacks standing to bring it. (b) An employer that lets non-union workers return after a strike while refusing union activists discriminates against the union, and the panel will order reinstatement even where the strike was unlawful. [Para 150][Para 153][Para 169][Para 171]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that it has no authority to order the dismissal or transfer of a general manager, country manager or board member. [Para 182]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The English version omits the evidence section entirely, stating only that it "has been omitted" [Para 14]. The award says Mr S was dismissed on 28 May 2011 but describes the misconduct as occurring "in the one month of June" [Para 34]. The employer's name and Jocam's are repeatedly fused or truncated in the text ("Forever Million Footwear Industry Co" without "Ltd."), and "possess" appears for "possesses" [Para 17][Para 21]. The decision names "Men Sarom" among the eight claimants, while the facts name "Men Phyrum" and "Men Phearom" [Para 87][Para 205]. The panel's Issue 1 decision rejects Mr S's reinstatement and orders Ms D's in one unnumbered paragraph [Para 204].
