# Award No. 20/05 — Fortune Co., Ltd v. C.CAWDU (Arbitration Council, 21 April 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, Sin Kim Sean, Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 140770

## 1. Facts
Fortune Co., Ltd is on National Road No. 21, Phum Prek Ksave, Khum Rakar, Saang district, Kandal province, and employs approximately 3,700 workers [Para 1][Para 40]. On 2 February 2005 an officer of the Kandal Department of Labour and Vocational Training received a C.CAWDU complaint raising four issues [Para 41]. Conciliation was postponed to 16 March 2004 and all four issues failed; non-conciliation report No. 304 L.V.T dated 17 March 2005 reached the Secretariat on 29 March 2005 [Para 15][Para 42][Para 43][Para 44]. At the hearing the parties merged issues 1, 2 and 3 into one issue on the termination of approximately 100 workers on undetermined-duration contracts, and kept the fourth, the termination of the local union president, separate [Para 45][Para 49]. The 100 workers were terminated in four separate phases; all but one accepted compensation [Para 50][Para 53]. The exception, Mr L, a C.CAWDU advisor hired on 28 October 2001 and terminated on 31 January 2005 on a basic wage of USD 45, refused compensation and demanded reinstatement [Para 52][Para 57][Para 58][Para 63]. The union president, Mr S, a Chinese translator elected on 8 August 2004, was suspended by letter dated 7 December 2004 for inciting an unlawful strike and making fake documents, and the Kandal department allowed his dismissal by letter No. 305 LVT dated 21 December 2004 under Article 83(2 and 5) [Para 66][Para 67][Para 68][Para 71][Para 73]. The Ministry refused his standing as union president on 13 January 2005 because he was under 25 under Article 286, his papers showing 1981 and 1979 birth dates [Para 78][Para 79][Para 80][Para 118].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the termination of approximately 100 workers on undetermined-duration contracts in four phases was a mass lay-off under the law or a termination by the employer's own will without valid reason, and whether compensation was correctly paid? [Para 8][Para 9][Para 10][Para 45][Para 46]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must reinstate Mr L, the C.CAWDU advisor who alone refused the termination and its compensation? [Para 12][Para 48][Para 93]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must reinstate Mr S, the local union president, whose dismissal the Labour Inspection Department authorised for incitement to an unlawful strike and use of fake documents? [Para 13][Para 49][Para 88]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (C.CAWDU):** The company should have arranged mass lay-offs under the statutory procedure rather than terminating contracts by its own will, and should pay mass-lay-off compensation correctly and post an announcement or agree on mass lay-offs and on rehiring when it hires again [Para 8][Para 9][Para 10]. The union demanded the reinstatement of Mr L and of its local president Mr S, and that the company stop interfering with the union [Para 12][Para 13][Para 88].
**Employer (Fortune Co., Ltd):** It never conducted a mass lay-off but terminated contracts by its own intention, and in any mass lay-off or rehiring it would follow the statutory procedure [Para 9][Para 11]. It terminated all 100 workers without any valid reason but agreed to pay damages under the law, and had already paid compensation for notice, dismissal damages and unused annual leave [Para 58][Para 60]. It maintained its position on terminating Mr L and Mr S and could not negotiate the union's demand, leaving it to the Minister [Para 14][Para 64][Para 65][Para 86].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider the workers' demand to reinstate Mr L and rejected C.CAWDU's demand to reinstate Mr S [Para 128][Para 129][Para 130].
On the 100 workers, the panel read Article 74 with Article 91 of the Labour Law and held that the four-phase termination of undetermined-duration contracts was a termination by one party without valid reason, entitling the workers to damages equal to the dismissal indemnity [Para 89][Para 90][Para 91]. As over 100 workers had accepted compensation, only Mr L's refusal remained [Para 91][Para 92]. The panel then examined jurisdiction, quoting Article 302 on collective disputes and noting that the Labour Inspector and the Minister decide whether a dispute is individual or collective before referral, a decision the Council generally follows absent compelling reasons [Para 97][Para 98][Para 99]. It found conditions (a) and (b) met but condition (c) not, since no evidence showed the dispute could jeopardise the effective operation of the enterprise, and so classified it as an individual dispute under Article 300 [Para 101][Para 102]. It pointed Mr L to the Kandal provincial court within two months of the non-conciliation report under Article 301, and declined to consider his complaint [Para 102][Para 103][Para 104][Para 105].
On Mr S, the panel quoted Article 293 on the dismissal of a shop steward, which requires Labour Inspector authorisation and gives two months to appeal to the Minister, after which an unanswered appeal is deemed rejected [Para 106]. It found the company suspended him on 7 December 2004 and sought permission the same day, the department approved by letter No. 305 LVT, and the union's appeals of 10 and 11 January 2005 went unanswered, so the objection was deemed rejected and the dismissal lawful in procedure [Para 107][Para 111][Para 113][Para 115][Para 116][Para 117]. It also found he had presented records with two different birth dates, 1981 and 1979, which is presenting false documents and serious misconduct under Article 83(B)(2), so the employer could dismiss him [Para 118][Para 119][Para 121][Para 122][Para 123][Para 124]. On the strike, it held Article 83(B)(5) does not cover inciting a strike even one that is procedurally improper, absent evidence of incitement to violence, so the strike was procedurally improper but not serious misconduct [Para 124][Para 125][Para 126].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the Labour Inspector or Minister has classified a dispute as individual, the Arbitration Council will decline jurisdiction and leave the worker to the courts, unless compelling reasons appear [Para 98][Para 100][Para 102]. A union leader's dismissal is procedurally lawful once the Labour Inspector authorises it and the union's ministerial appeal goes unanswered past the two-month period, the appeal being deemed rejected under Article 293 [Para 106][Para 115][Para 116]. Presenting identity documents bearing two different birth dates is the fraudulent presentation of false documents and serious misconduct under Article 83(B)(2) [Para 118][Para 121][Para 122].

**Obiter dicta:** Inciting a strike that is procedurally improper is not itself serious misconduct under Article 83(B)(5) unless the employer proves incitement to violence; the sanction is loss of the full protection of strike benefits [Para 124][Para 125][Para 126].
