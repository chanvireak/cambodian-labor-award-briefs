# Award No. 44/09-South Bay — South Bay Enterprise Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 9 April 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phyrum, Liv Sovanna, Ang Eng Thong chair) · Doc ID 140377

## 1. Facts
South Bay Enterprise Co., Ltd. employs about 600 workers at Vathanak Park, Chom Chao Commune, Dangkor District, Phnom Penh; the single union at the enterprise, the Cambodian Labour Union Federation (CLUF) Local Union of CLUF, holds most-representative status under certificate No. 065/08 of 17 September 2008. Buyers stopped placing orders in the global financial crisis. The employer suspended the workforce from 6 December 2008 to 5 February 2009 with Labour Inspector permission, then suspended 559 workers again from 6 February to 5 April 2009 after writing for permission on 4 February and receiving no reply. During the first suspension workers received a US$8 monthly accommodation allowance, a US$6 monthly living allowance and a seniority bonus, between US$14 and US$19 a month in total. [Para 9][Para 10][Para 12][Para 15]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the second work suspension of the 559 workers, from 6 February to 5 April 2009, was consistent with the law? [Para 17][Para 23]
2. (Issue 1) Whether the workers are entitled to full wages and other benefits, including the US$6 living allowance, for the duration of that suspension? [Para 24][Para 26]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, CLUF Local Union):** full wages and benefits must be paid for the second suspension because the Labour Inspector never authorised it, which makes it unlawful; the workers accept that there have recently been no purchase orders and no work. [Para 16][Para 14]

**Employer (respondent, South Bay Enterprise Co., Ltd.):** it cannot meet the demand during suspensions and will pay only the US$8 accommodation allowance, the US$6 living allowance and a seniority bonus proportionate to length of service, because the absence of buyer orders is caused by the global financial crisis. [Para 2][Para 17]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay full wages plus the monthly US$6 living allowance to the 559 workers suspended from 6 February 2009 to 5 April 2009. [Para 30]

On lawfulness, Article 71(11) of the Labour Law (1997) permits suspension where the enterprise faces serious economic or material difficulty, limits it to two months and places it under the control of the Labour Inspector. The first suspension was properly authorised; for the second the employer wrote on 4 February 2009 and heard nothing. The panel treated silence as the absence of authorisation, not as consent, and added that the two suspensions taken together exceeded the two-month ceiling. The second suspension was therefore unlawful. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 23]

On wages, the panel reasoned that an unlawful suspension leaves the contract unsuspended: the employer has simply failed to supply work, so the obligation to pay full wages survives. It followed Awards 21/03-Loyal Cambodia, 46/04-M & A Cambodia, 60/04-United Art and its own recent 43/09-Hugo. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 26]

On the living allowance, point 1 of Notification No. 032/08/SCN of 17 April 2008 requires employers in garment and footwear manufacture to pay US$6 a month, and the minutes of 5 May 2008 make it payable to all workers "without any conditions"; the allowance is not part of the main wage. Following Award 119/08-New Hung Wah, the panel held the allowance is owed whether the suspension is lawful or unlawful. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 29][Para 30]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A second suspension of the labour contract is unlawful where the Labour Inspector has not authorised it — an unanswered request for permission is not permission — and where the combined suspensions exceed two months; an unlawful suspension leaves the wage obligation intact, and the US$6 living allowance is payable unconditionally in any event. [Para 23][Para 25][Para 29]

**Obiter dicta:** the US$6 allowance is not a component of the main wage, and the employer's inability to pay during suspensions does not displace the statutory allowance. [Para 28][Para 2]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The text is an unofficial English translation with the article fused into the following word throughout ("themployer", "thevidence"), "crisis" truncated to "cris", and the evidence section omitted from the English version. The opening date of the first suspension is printed as "[6] December 2008" with a bracketed digit supplied by the translator. The finding on union status repeats its own opening words mid-sentence, and the excerpt of the minutes of 5 May 2008 is badly garbled, with fragments repeated three times, so the quoted words "without any conditions" are taken from the surrounding text rather than a clean quotation. Names of representatives are given in full in the source and are not reproduced here.
