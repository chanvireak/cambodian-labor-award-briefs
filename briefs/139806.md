# Award No. 116/13 — Tuki Sport (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 1 July 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Sok Matheung presiding; Ing Sothy, employer nominee; An Non, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139806

## 1. Facts
Tuki Sport (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a garment factory at Tuek Thla village, Sangkat Tuek Thla, Khan Sen Sok, Phnom Penh, employed about 287 workers. [Para 1][Para 41] Two of them, Ms Y and Ms E, brought this collective dispute with two officials of the Federation of Workers' Unions, demanding a USD 14 increase in the base wage to match the government's new minimum wage. [Para 3][Para 42] Conciliation on 31 May 2013 settled one of the two points in dispute, leaving this one, which reached the Secretariat on 07 June 2013. [Para 11][Para 12] Both parties attended the hearing of 19 June 2013. [Para 15]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must add USD 14 to each worker's former base wage, while also keeping the USD 5 monthly health allowance, so that wages conform to the government's minimum wage increase under Notification No. 103 of 09 April 2013? [Para 46][Para 70]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** before the increase the two claimants received a base wage of USD 80 plus a USD 5 health allowance; after the May 2013 increase they received USD 90 in total and lost the health allowance as a separate sum, so they wanted USD 14 added to the old base of USD 80 with the USD 5 allowance preserved, a total of USD 99. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54][Para 70] They also told the panel that an application to register the factory union had been filed with the ministry. [Para 43]
**Employer (respondent):** it could not meet the demand. [Para 55] It had already applied the increase — workers on an old base of USD 70 received USD 80 in total and those on USD 80 or more received USD 90 — and it treated the health allowance as absorbed into the new minimum wage, as the notification required. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider the demand for a further USD 14, leaving the employer's existing wage scale in place; the order is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Minister. [Para 101][Para 102][Para 103]

The panel dealt first with who was entitled to bring the claim. Under Article 268 of the Labour Law a professional organisation obtains the rights the law recognises only once its statutes and list of officers are deposited with the ministry and it is registered; a two-month silence by the ministry counts as registration. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58] The factory union produced no registration certificate, and the panel therefore presumed it was not yet a professional organisation and held that it enjoyed none of the rights the Labour Law confers, including the right to represent its members before the panel. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 64][Para 65]

Nor could the two claimants represent their fellow workers. Under Article 19 of Prakas 099 a party may appear in person or through another person holding that party's written authorisation; the two workers held no transfer of rights from the other workers of the company, and the two federation officials held none either, so only the claims of Ms Y and Ms E could be decided. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 66][Para 67][Para 68][Para 69]

On the merits the panel classified the demand as an interests dispute, because it found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, agreement between the parties, internal rules or past practice obliging the employer to add USD 14 to the old base wage while preserving the USD 5 health allowance. [Para 88][Para 89] It read Notification No. 103 of 09 April 2013 as folding the health allowance into the minimum wage: USD 75 for probationary workers (56 + 14 + 5) and USD 80 for full-status workers (61 + 14 + 5), effective 01 May 2013. [Para 70][Para 71][Para 72][Para 73] The purpose of the notification was therefore to set a floor of USD 80 and USD 75, not to add USD 14 to whatever each worker already earned. [Para 82][Para 83] On the facts, the employer paid the two claimants USD 90 (old base 80, plus 5 increase, plus 5 allowance), which exceeded the floor, and the health allowance had by then been absorbed. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 87]

Because an interests dispute is decided in equity and, once decided by a binding order, replaces the collective agreement for a year, the panel required the union bringing it to be the most representative in the enterprise, holding more than half the workforce as members. [Para 94][Para 95][Para 96][Para 97] It had refused interests disputes on that ground before, and here the two claimants were simply two workers of the company, so they lacked the standing the jurisdiction requires. [Para 98][Para 99][Para 100]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that adds the increment announced in a ministry minimum-wage notification on top of each worker's existing base wage, while also preserving a health allowance that the same notification folded into the minimum wage, double-counts the increment: the notification sets a floor, and an employer that pays above that floor owes nothing further, so the demand has no legal foundation and is an interests dispute the panel will refuse. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 87][Para 88]

A factory union that cannot produce a registration certificate is not yet a professional organisation and holds none of the rights the Labour Law confers, including the right to represent its members before the panel; and an interests dispute must be brought by the most representative union, holding more than half the workforce, or by workers who hold written authorisation from those they claim to represent. [Para 58][Para 64][Para 67][Para 97]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel explained that a binding order on an interests dispute takes the place of the collective agreement for a year and would extinguish the workforce's right to strike over interests in future, which is why it insists on the most-representative requirement — a point not needed to dispose of a claim that failed on its merits as well. [Para 96] It also noted that only the two claimants' demands were decided, so other workers of the factory remained free to bring their own. [Para 69]
