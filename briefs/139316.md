# Award No. 31/04 — Camintex Garment (Arbitration Council, 28 June 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Men Nimith presiding; Ouk Ry appointed by the employer; Vong Vanna appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 139316

## 1. Facts
Camintex Garment was established on 16 January 2004 and employed 700 workers. [Para 12][Para 23] Two strikes were held, on 14 February 2004 and on 24 and 25 March 2004; the company closed on 27 March 2004, terminated all 700 contracts and paid wages and severance benefits on 1 April 2004, which the workers accepted. [Para 13][Para 20][Para 25] It reopened on 22 April 2004 and took back 450 workers, agreeing to rehire the rest by seniority except 12 workers it called troublemakers; those 12 were the leaders of the union established on 13 February 2004. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 17]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the factory must reinstate the former union leaders and former workers, the factory having reopened after closing and dismissing the whole workforce? [Para 6][Para 7]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the 12 excluded workers are precisely those elected to the union leadership in February 2004, and their exclusion while others were rehired shows discrimination against the union; the other former workers should also return. [Para 17][Para 30]
**Employer (respondent):** it is willing to take back former workers without discrimination for union membership, and to rehire the remaining workers by seniority as its need grows, but not the 12, who caused trouble in the factory and provoked the closure. [Para 7][Para 29][Para 30] At the hearing it conceded that the 12 were the union leaders but denied discriminatory motive; it offered no evidence of their misconduct. [Para 30][Para 31]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must immediately reinstate 8 of the 12 union leaders, and must list the remaining 4 and the other former workers by seniority, giving them preference over new hires for two years from 1 April 2004. [Para 42][Para 43]
The panel held that the closure and termination of all 700 contracts was a collective dismissal under Article 95 of the Labour Law, which gives dismissed workers priority of rehire in the same position for two years where the business improves and needs workers. [Para 25][Para 27] Refusal to rehire is lawful only if the worker could have been dismissed for misconduct, in one of two categories: serious misconduct within 7 days of the termination, or misconduct for which dismissal was proportionate within 15 days. [Para 28] The employer proved neither. [Para 28][Para 31]
The employer gave one reason only — that the 12 caused problems — and produced no evidence of misconduct in the relevant period, repeating the allegation at both hearings. [Para 29][Para 31] A petition from fellow workers rejecting the 12 named no specific act, so the panel held it could not be treated as evidence of misconduct justifying dismissal. [Para 32][Para 36][Para 38] Article 12 then forbids an employer, when hiring, from taking union membership or union activity into account, so a rehiring employer may not weigh whether a worker was a union member, leader or activist. [Para 40] Since two-thirds of the workforce was already back, the panel ordered at least 8 of the 12 reinstated immediately and the remaining 4 placed first on the seniority list. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 43]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
After a collective dismissal under Article 95 of the Labour Law, an employer that refuses to rehire a former worker must prove misconduct of a kind that would have justified dismissal — serious misconduct within 7 days, or proportionate misconduct within 15 days — and where it rehired most of the workforce but excluded union leaders without such proof, the exclusion is treated as discrimination contrary to Article 12 and reinstatement follows. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 40]

**Obiter dicta:** where a worker's misconduct would have justified dismissal, the employer need not rehire him after a collective layoff. [Para 27]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The non-conciliation report is dated 20 May 2004 but is said to have been submitted to the Secretariat on "May 24, 2003", a year before the dispute arose. [Para 10] The chair is printed "Men Nimith" in the panel composition and "Men Nimmith" in the signature block. [Para 9][Para 43] The employer's representative is "Mr. L Heang, GMAC Officer" with the address truncated to "Bayab village, commune". [Para 5] The union president appears as both "Pa Soktha" and "Pa Sytha", and the list of 12 workers omits the union secretary. [Para 6][Para 8] Several sentences are duplicated by the OCR ("workers because the factory has reopened"; "The employer refuses to accept these union leaders"). [Para 6][Para 7] The number of workers who signed the petition rejecting the 12 is not stated. [Para 32]
