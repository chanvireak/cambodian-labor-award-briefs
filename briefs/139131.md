# Award No. 015/15 — Seduno (Cambo) Nit Thing Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 3 March 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Toun Si Phan (worker-appointed), Pen Bunchea (president) · Doc ID 139131

## 1. Facts
The respondent is a garment factory at Kandeung Touch, Bati, Takeo, employing about 500 workers. Three federations — Khmer Youth Voice, Khmer Workers' Solidarity and the Workers' Union Federation — none of them yet registered with the Ministry, took up 12 demands; two remained unresolved after conciliation, and 31 workers signed a letter of authority, of whom 8 later signed a letter disowning the Khmer Youth Voice Federation, leaving 23 complainants. On 5 January 2015 the employer suspended the contracts of three union representatives and, separately, of its administrative officer Mr L (Lay Samorn). [Para 10][Para 56][Para 60][Para 63]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must remove the administrative officer Mr L from the company, because he interfered in union affairs and caused problems in the factory? [Para 64]
2. Whether the employer must take back the three union representatives and pay their wages up to the date of their return? [Para 83]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the three representatives committed no fault; their suspension is unlawful because the law limits suspension as a disciplinary measure and the employer took no disciplinary step within the permitted period; Mr L had told the union president to collect thumbprints to unseat the head of administration and had told her not to deal with the federations outside the factory. [Para 67][Para 85][Para 87]

**Employer (respondent):** the dispute between the workers and the administrative officer is a private matter between them; the three representatives did go outside, re-entered the factory, shouted, stopped the sewing machines and told other workers not to work overtime, and it has suspended all of them — including Mr L — pending the panel's decision; it does not wish to take any of them back. [Para 70][Para 89]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted in full — the employer was ordered to remove Mr L from the company, and to take back the three representatives and pay their wages from the date of suspension until the date they resume work. [Para 201][Para 202]

On issue 1 the panel began from Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 311 of the 2007 Civil Code: a contract is dissolved only by the parties to it, so neither a union nor a third party may demand that an employee be removed, and the panel itself will not ordinarily interfere with management's staffing decisions, which are the employer's prerogative under Article 2 of the Labour Law. That prerogative is bounded, however. It may be exercised only lawfully and reasonably, and the panel will order removal where an employee's conduct endangers the health or safety of others. On the audio recordings submitted by both sides, Mr L had pressed the union president to collect workers' thumbprints, explained the procedure for unseating the head of administration, kept asking her about her contacts with outside federations, and asked her to report the names and staff numbers of new union members. That was interference in union affairs contrary to Article 280 and incitement to disorder, so his retention would disrupt the running of the company. [Para 112][Para 114][Para 122][Para 124][Para 143]

On issue 2 the panel examined whether the three had committed serious misconduct within Article 83 of the Labour Law, which alone would justify the measure. It found that the union president had in fact told workers to stop their machines, but only at about 4 p.m. as the working day ended, not at 2:30 p.m. as the employer alleged; that this was not the kind of serious misconduct listed in Article 83 or in Article 10 of the internal regulations; and that the employer produced no evidence at all for its allegations against the other two representatives, on whom the burden of proof lay. Article 71(7), which permits suspension as a disciplinary measure, therefore could not be applied. Because the suspensions from 5 January 2015 were unlawful, Article 72(1) required the employer to pay 100 per cent of wages for the period, and the panel ordered reinstatement with back pay. [Para 152][Para 162][Para 168][Para 187][Para 199]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer's managerial prerogative does not entitle it to remove or suspend a worker at a union's demand, nor to suspend a worker as a disciplinary measure without proved serious misconduct; but where the evidence shows that an employee has interfered in union affairs or incited disorder in a way that threatens the safety and order of the workplace, the panel may itself order that employee removed. A suspension lacking legal basis obliges the employer to pay full wages for the period of suspension. [Para 124][Para 199]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted, without deciding the point, that awards in earlier cases had held that participation in a strike is not in itself serious misconduct, a proposition it treated as settled background rather than as a ground of decision here. [Para 155]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's account of Mr L's status shifts: at the first hearing it asserted he had already been dismissed, at the second it repeated that but produced no document, and at the third it conceded the suspension was still in force. The award's reasons record his start date as "22 November 2023 2014", the years printed twice. The signature block leaves the worker-appointed arbitrator's name blank, so the name Toun Si Phan is taken from the face of the award only. Page breaks have also swallowed fragments of the list of demands and of one party submission. [Para 106][Para 203]
