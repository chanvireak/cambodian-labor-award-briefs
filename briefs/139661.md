# Award No. 201/14 — Im Vey Garment International (Branch 3) (Arbitration Council, 21 August 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; Venh Pov, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · Doc ID 139661

## 1. Facts
Im Vey Garment International (Branch 3) is a garment-cutting company at Prek Ta Long, Chak Angre Krom, Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, operating since 1997 with about 3,300 workers. [Para 51][Para 52] Of ten demands, three were settled in conciliation; the workers then withdrew issues 1 to 5, leaving only the two strike-wage claims. [Para 21][Para 27][Para 28] The union's third-term leadership had been elected and notified but was not yet recognised by the Ministry of Labour, and only eight workers attended the hearing. [Para 111][Para 128]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay wages and the attendance bonus to the ironing-section workers for 10 to 15 March 2014, when they stopped work to demand a higher lot price for PN 138367? [Para 142]
2. Whether the employer must pay wages and the attendance bonus for the nationwide strike of 26 December 2013 to 4 January 2014? [Para 192]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the March stoppage was not a strike because the workers demanded only the lot price of PN 138367 and were willing to work on other purchase orders, which the employer withheld; the December–January stoppage was a nationwide strike they joined out of safety fears rather than as instigators; and on earlier occasions the employer had never cut pay for such protests. [Para 66][Para 75][Para 102][Para 104]
**Employer (respondent):** the March stoppage was a strike conducted without the conciliation procedure in the 17 January 2014 agreement, and the employer's allocation of work is its management right; the December strike was procedurally unlawful; and both stoppages suspend the contract, so no wages are owed. [Para 68][Para 78][Para 83][Para 107]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both claims for wages and attendance bonus refused; the order applies to the parties immediately under the Memorandum of Understanding of 3 October 2012. [Para 200][Para 201][Para 203]

The panel raised standing of its own motion. Under article 268 of the Labour Law a union only acquires the rights the law recognises once its leadership list is registered with the Ministry, and Prakas 305 article 3(2) caps a leadership term at two years. [Para 119][Para 121][Para 112] The second-term leadership was recognised on 28 March 2011 and that recognition expired on 28 March 2013; the third-term leadership had not been recognised when the case was heard, so the new office-holders could not represent members before the panel. [Para 113][Para 114][Para 125] Prakas 099 article 19 does allow a party to be represented by another person holding written authorisation, but only eight workers attended and no authorisations from the others were filed by the 8 August 2014 deadline. [Para 126][Para 128][Para 63] The panel therefore ruled that only those eight workers were claimants. [Para 131][Para 132]

On issue 1 the panel applied article 318 of the Labour Law: a strike is the concerted cessation of work by a group of workers in an enterprise for the purpose of obtaining a settlement of their demands. It found all three elements — a group of ironing workers, a cessation (they scanned in and sat at their stations without working), and the purpose of forcing a higher lot price — and held the 10–15 March stoppage to be a strike notwithstanding the workers' wish to work on other orders. [Para 144][Para 148][Para 153][Para 155][Para 156] It followed awards 36/06, 125/09 and 81/13 on those elements. [Para 145] The employer's refusal to allocate other purchase orders during the stoppage was then upheld as an exercise of the management right in article 2 of the Labour Law, lawful because it followed the production line, and reasonable because the workers had bypassed the grievance procedure agreed on 17 January 2014, under which lot-price demands are negotiated through representatives without any work stoppage. [Para 161][Para 168][Para 173][Para 176]

On issue 2 the panel applied articles 332(1), 72(1) and 334: the employment contract is suspended during a strike, the obligation to pay wages falls away, and the employer is liable only where it hired replacements for the strikers. [Para 180][Para 181][Para 182][Para 183] Following awards 27/10 Hat In The Bay and 178/12 Dekoro (Cambodia), and having found no evidence that replacements were hired in either stoppage, it refused the claim. [Para 184][Para 187][Para 189][Para 190]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Wages are not payable for any period in which workers concertedly cease work to press a demand, whether the stoppage is lawful or not, and the only exception is proof that the employer hired replacements, which then obliges it to pay the strikers. A union whose current leadership has not been registered with the Ministry cannot represent its members before the panel, so only the workers who attend in person, or who have given written authorisation, are parties to the award. [Para 125][Para 185][Para 189]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that an agreed in-house procedure for negotiating lot prices, which keeps production running while representatives negotiate, is an appropriate method of resolving such disputes, and its remarks on the employer's freedom to sequence production, are general observations beyond the two refusals ordered. [Para 165][Para 175]
