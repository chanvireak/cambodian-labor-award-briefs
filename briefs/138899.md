# Award No. 010/17 — Jin Win In Das Stri (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 21 March 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Kuy Nam, president) · Doc ID 138899

## 1. Facts
Jin Win In Das Stri (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. is a garment manufacturer employing about 970 workers, where the Cambodian Workers' Heart Union held a leadership election on 3 December 2016: Mr S was elected president and Mr D secretary [Para 46][Para 47]. Both men had worked in the warehouse since 1 December 2014 and 21 July 2014 respectively on successive three-month fixed-duration contracts [Para 56][Para 58]. The company let their contracts lapse — Mr D on 21 December 2016 and Mr S on 23 January 2017, before the printed end dates of 20 December 2016 and 31 January 2017 — and refused to take them back [Para 57][Para 59][Para 5]. The union's demand for reinstatement with benefits preserved was the only issue the panel still had to decide [Para 3][Para 21].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to take back Mr S and Mr D and preserve their other benefits? [Para 101]
2. Whether the employer should be ordered to stop all acts of anti-union discrimination and obstruction of workers who engage in union activity? [Para 6][Para 87]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The two men were dismissed because they joined and led the union; their contracts had run more than two years in total and could no longer be treated as fixed-duration, so the employer must reinstate them on unchanged benefits [Para 51][Para 56].
**Employer (respondent):** It did not dismiss them — it simply declined to renew fixed-duration contracts because orders had fallen and there was no overtime, and it treated other warehouse, packing and cleaning workers the same way; their contracts happened to expire earlier than others' [Para 4][Para 52][Para 53][Para 55].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to take back Mr S and Mr D and to pay their wages from the date of dismissal until the date of reinstatement; the award is a non-binding award (សេចក្តីបង្គាប់មិនជាប់កាតព្វកិច្ច) that becomes final eight days after notification [Para 202][Para 203].

On the character of the termination the panel applied Article 67(2) of the Labour Law: a fixed-duration contract may not exceed two years, and renewal beyond that converts it into an indefinite-duration contract [Para 102][Para 104]. Following Award No. 10/03 (Zaksintek) and Article 73(5), it held that the two-year ceiling refers to the total accumulated duration, not to each renewal [Para 106][Para 110]. The employer conceded the month and year each man started but could not fix a day, offered no document contradicting the workers, and did not contest the claim at the hearing; the panel therefore accepted 1 December 2014 and 21 July 2014 [Para 60][Para 119][Para 121]. Both totals exceeded two years, so the contracts were already indefinite-duration and could only be ended by dismissal [Para 122][Para 124].

On protected status the panel applied Article 67 of the Law on Unions (2016) and Prakas No. 305: candidates and elected leaders are protected for 45 days before and 45 days after the election, and dismissal requires the labour inspector's prior consent under Article 43 [Para 126][Para 129][Para 143][Para 135]. Mr D was let go about 18 days after the ballot, inside the protected window [Para 168]. Mr S fell under the protection given to founders and members from the date of the registration application, which the union filed on 6 January 2017 and which was still pending when he was dismissed on 23 January 2017 [Para 131][Para 171][Para 172]. The panel held the notification condition satisfied: direct delivery failed when the employer's representative declined to come down, the postal copy was returned as undeliverable at the company's own address, and a copy went to GMAC with a signature — so no fault lay with the workers [Para 180][Para 182][Para 185]. No evidence showed the employer had sought the labour inspector's authorisation before dismissing either man, which Article 43 requires [Para 195][Para 197]. Having found the dismissals wrongful, the panel used its power under Prakas No. 099 to order reinstatement and immediate payment, as in Awards 169/16 and 170/16 [Para 198][Para 199].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive fixed-duration contracts whose accumulated duration passes two years convert to indefinite-duration contracts by operation of Article 67(2) read with Article 73(5); from that point the employer's failure to renew is a dismissal, and dismissing an elected union leader or a founder/member within the statutory protection window without the labour inspector's prior authorisation is unlawful and calls for reinstatement and back pay [Para 115][Para 124][Para 194].

**Obiter dicta:** Because no information showed any union to be the most representative in the enterprise, Article 54 of the Law on Unions did not bar the complainants from acting for themselves, and the panel confined its decision to the three workers present at the hearing [Para 93][Para 99][Para 100].
