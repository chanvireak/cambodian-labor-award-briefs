# Award No. 219/12 — Gladpeer Garments Factory (Cambodia) L.T.D. (Arbitration Council, 7 December 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambor Na, employer-appointed; Sin Kim Sean, worker-appointed; Taing Try, president) · Doc ID 140215

## 1. Facts
Gladpeer Garments Factory (Cambodia) L.T.D. — also printed as Klet Pi Garment Factory — is a garment manufacturer employing about 5,000 workers; the factory-level Democratic Garment Workers Union, registered 8 December 2010 with about 1,400 members, brought the complaint [Para 50][Para 51][Para 52][Para 53]. The union holds no certificate of most-representative status [Para 54]. Conciliation on 23 October 2012 failed on all four demands, which were referred to the panel on 30 October 2012 [Para 14][Para 15]. Because both parties had signed a Memorandum of Understanding of 3 October 2012 on improving industrial relations, the panel split its decision into rights disputes (binding) and interests disputes (non-binding) [Para 19][Para 20][Para 24]. Hearing was held on 13 November 2012 and the award date was extended by agreement from 23 November to 7 December 2012 [Para 12][Para 23].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay a 3,000 riel meal allowance to workers who voluntarily work overtime on Sundays or public holidays, when the employer pays nothing for Sunday work and the Holidays Prakas fixes the holiday rate differently? [Para 55][Para 100]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay a further 3,000 riel meal allowance, on top of the 2,000 riel already given, when workers work overtime from 6:30 pm to 9:00 pm? [Para 63][Para 143]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer may hold back (freeze) 20 USD of a worker's wages where the worker takes yearly leave in the first days of the month, before payday? [Para 73][Para 150]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must fold the whole seniority bonus into the base wage, rather than the first 5 USD only? [Para 86][Para 159]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Work on Sundays and holidays is overtime, pay is small, food near the factory is dearer on those days, and other factories give more than 3,000 riel; a meal allowance therefore merely helps living standards [Para 56][Para 57][Para 59][Para 69]. On the held-back wages, the retained 20 USD deprives workers of money already needed for rent and borrowing [Para 78][Para 74]. Seniority bonus grows with service, so any part above 5 USD should be counted in the base wage [Para 88].

**Employer (respondent):** Sunday and holiday work is paid at 200 per cent and 300 per cent respectively, which already exceeds the meal allowance, and the law requires only 2,000 riel [Para 62][Para 70]. The 2,000 riel for the 4:00–6:00 pm block and 1,500 riel for 6:30–9:00 pm are what the law prescribes [Para 64][Para 65]. The 20 USD retained is security against workers who leave without notice, recoverable under Article 75 of the Labour Law and repaid on return [Para 81][Para 82][Para 83]. No law requires folding seniority bonus into base wage; the 5 USD practice is the employer's own discretion and matches the collective agreement with the most-representative union [Para 89][Para 90][Para 91].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — on Issue 1 the panel refused the 3,000 riel claim but ordered 2,000 riel per day for voluntary Sunday overtime; Issue 2 and Issue 4 dismissed; on Issue 3 the panel ordered the employer to stop withholding wages [Para 170][Para 171][Para 173][Para 176].

The panel first defined the two categories: a rights dispute concerns a right set by law, a collective agreement or an agreement and is decided according to law (Article 312 (new) §2), while an interests dispute is decided by equity under Prakas 099 Article 43 [Para 93][Para 98][Para 99]. On Issue 1 it found Sunday work to be overtime because it exceeds the normal 8 hours a day / 48 hours a week under Article 137 [Para 101][Para 104]. Notice No. 041/11 point 2 gives 2,000 riel a day or a free meal to any worker doing overtime at the employer's request, without limiting the number of hours [Para 107][Para 108]. Since the employer paid nothing for Sunday overtime, the panel held that practice unlawful and ordered 2,000 riel a day [Para 109][Para 111]. Anything above 2,000 riel is an interests claim; because the union lacks most-representative status the panel declined to consider it, as in Awards 02/11 and 66/11 [Para 113][Para 114][Para 116][Para 124][Para 126]. Holiday work, by contrast, is governed by Article 139 (new) and Prakas No. 10 of 4 February 1999: a holiday worker earns one extra day's wage, capped at 8 hours, and holiday work is not overtime, so the 3,000 riel claim failed [Para 134][Para 135][Para 140][Para 175].

On Issue 2 the panel found the employer had paid the 2,000 riel for the 4:00–6:00 pm block and 1,500 riel beyond it, which complies with Notice 041; the extra 3,000 riel sought was above the legal rate and, as an interests claim, was refused [Para 143][Para 145][Para 147][Para 149].

On Issue 3 the panel classified the demand as a rights dispute because it concerns wages [Para 150]. Article 127 (new) §1 forbids any payment to the employer out of wages due, and the retention was designed to secure damages for departure without notice [Para 154][Para 155]. The panel held that a worker's failure to give notice is a separate question and could not justify the freeze, and ordered the practice stopped [Para 156][Para 157][Para 158].

On Issue 4 the panel noted Notice 041 point 3 grants a seniority bonus by years of service, while Notice 049/10 sets the minimum wage at 61 USD a month [Para 159][Para 164]. The seniority bonus is a separate allowance, not part of the minimum (base) wage, so the demand had no legal basis and was refused [Para 166][Para 167][Para 168].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that a non-binding interests award will still become a collective agreement for one year under Prakas 099 Article 43 if not contested, and that a rights award binds these parties through the 2012 Memorandum of Understanding [Para 122][Para 172][Para 177]. It added that any employer restraint on wage payment for its own benefit is impermissible [Para 154].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Sunday work exceeding the normal 8/48-hour week is overtime, so Notice 041 point 2 entitles every worker doing overtime at the employer's request to 2,000 riel a day or a free meal regardless of hours; a claim for more than that rate is an interests claim which an arbitrator will not hear from a union without most-representative status, and holiday work, being paid under Article 139 (new) and Prakas No. 10 rather than as overtime, earns one extra day's wage only [Para 108][Para 116][Para 135].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The scan is heavily degraded: stray Latin and truncated words break the reasons at [Para 60], [Para 142] and [Para 151], and the operative section is partly garbled at [Para 142]. The employer is named three ways — Gladpeer Garments Factory, Klet Pi Garment Factory, and a "Klet Pi Garment (Cambodia) Co." variant — and a Ministry letter is addressed to the company under yet another spelling [Para 37][Para 45][Para 50]. Worker names, attendance numbers and one team leader's name appear in full in the document list; they are withheld here and should be redacted at source [Para 29][Para 34][Para 35][Para 73]. The claim figures are printed inconsistently: the union asks 3,000 riel on Issue 2 but its own statement totals 4,500 riel including the 1,500 riel already paid [Para 66].
