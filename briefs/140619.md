# Award No. 60/07 — Suit Way Garment (Arbitration Council, 3 August 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy appointed by the employer, An Nan appointed by the workers, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 140619

## 1. Facts
Suit Way Garment employs about 1,000 workers at Tuol Sang Ke, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, of whom 716 were affected by this dispute. [Para 2][Para 34] The claimant is the Federation Union of Development Workers Right at Suit Way Garment (FUDWR), whose local UDWR at Suit Way Garment was registered with the Ministry on 9 October 2006 and claimed 638 members but held no certificate of most-representative status. [Para 3][Para 34] The union filed 25 demands on 7 June 2007; nine were conciliated, and of the 16 sent to the panel ten more settled at the hearing, leaving five for decision. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 33] The live issues were piece-rate targets under clause 8 of the agreement of 5 July 2006, a white board and a post box for worker delegates, payment of the 50 per cent maternity wage before leave, two hours a week for delegate meetings, and tables and chairs for meals. [Para 34][Para 41][Para 45][Para 47][Para 51]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the company must keep applying clause 8 of the agreement of 5 July 2006 on piece-rate targets? [Para 34][Para 52]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the company must provide worker delegates with a white board and a separate post box? [Para 41][Para 74]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the company must pay the whole 50 per cent maternity wage before the leave begins? [Para 45][Para 92]
4. (Issue 5) Whether worker delegates must be given two hours a week during working hours for their meetings? [Para 47][Para 101]
5. (Issue 8) Whether the company must provide tables and chairs for workers at meal times? [Para 51][Para 111]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** under the old policy a worker over-locking 250 pieces a day earned 40 riel a piece and 50 riel above that, whereas the new policy sets the same rates at a target of 350, and the union says the style has not changed. [Para 37][Para 39] They wanted a separate post box to keep complaints confidential, two hours a week inside working hours as the law allows, and tables and chairs because there is nowhere to eat. [Para 43][Para 50][Para 52]

**Employer (respondent):** the buyer's rate fell from 12 USD a piece to 8 USD, so the target had to rise and the old policy cannot continue; workers produced no evidence that the style was the same. [Para 38][Para 39] It offered a shared white board and a shared post box with a key and Saturday opening, and two hours on Saturday from 4 to 6 p.m. paid as overtime. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 47] It pays maternity wages monthly for fear that women will not return, and says there is no space for tables. [Para 45][Para 51]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must provide a separate post box and the shared white board for worker delegates, pay the full 50 per cent of three months' wages before maternity leave, and give delegates two hours a week within working hours; the piece-rate and tables-and-chairs demands were refused or not considered. [Para 124][Para 125][Para 126]

On Issue 2 the panel began from Article 1 of Decree No. 38 of 28 October 1988, under which parties must perform the obligations they have agreed. [Para 53][Para 54] The agreement's clause 8 required the old piece-rate policy to continue and barred blame where targets were missed, but it did not define the style then in use, so its meaning was unclear. [Para 60][Para 61] Article 23 of the same Decree directs that an unclear contract be read by the custom of the trade, and in Cambodian garment practice piece-rate targets change with the style. [Para 59][Para 62] The panel therefore framed the question as whether the style had changed, and since the burden lay on the claimants and the workers filed no evidence that it had not, the panel held the agreement could not be enforced in this case. [Para 65][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70] It nevertheless held, following Award 41/05-Violet Apparel, that the employer must not blame or discipline workers who miss the target without evidence of laziness or carelessness. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 73]

On Issue 3 the panel read Article 299 of the Labour Law with clause 5 of Prakas No. 286 on worker delegates of 5 November 2001, which requires a workplace, meeting room, working materials and a suitable place to post notices. [Para 75][Para 77] Since Article 284 gives delegates the task of receiving grievances and reporting to the labour inspector, they need independence and confidentiality, and a box opened jointly with the employer once a week would expose complainants and prevent urgent handling. [Para 80][Para 83][Para 87][Para 90] A separate post box was ordered. [Para 91][Para 126]

On Issue 4 the panel applied Article 183, which gives a woman half her wages during maternity leave, subject to one year of uninterrupted service, and Article 115(3), under which wages may not be paid on a rest day and must be paid a day earlier if the due date falls on one. [Para 92][Para 95][Para 96][Para 98] Following Awards 57/06-Evergreen and 97/06-New Max, it ordered the 50 per cent of three months' wages paid before the leave begins. [Para 98][Para 99][Para 126]

On Issue 5 the panel read Article 299 with clause 6 of Prakas No. 286, which gives each delegate two hours a week on normal wages but does not say when, and Article 284, whose duties fall during working hours. [Para 99][Para 102][Para 104] Because overtime is voluntary under clause 4 of Prakas No. 80/99 and Award 14/03-Chou Sing, the employer could not impose Saturday meetings. [Para 108][Para 110] The two hours were ordered within working hours. [Para 111][Para 126]

On Issue 8 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law requiring tables and chairs, no evidence on health or hygiene, and no agreement on the point. [Para 111][Para 112][Para 113] It treated the demand as an interest dispute, and since the union lacked most-representative status under Article 277 it declined to consider it, because an award on an interest dispute would stand as a collective agreement for a year under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 and would deprive other workers of their rights. [Para 114][Para 116][Para 120][Para 123][Para 126]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A collective agreement that fixes piece-rate targets by reference to "the old policy" without identifying the style is unclear, and is read by garment custom so that targets follow the style; a union claiming breach must prove the style is unchanged, and failing that the agreement is unenforceable, though the employer still may not blame workers who miss the target absent evidence of laziness. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 69][Para 73] Worker delegates must be given the means to keep grievances confidential, so a separate post box is required even where the employer offers a shared one, and the two statutory hours a week must fall inside working hours because overtime is voluntary. [Para 87][Para 91][Para 108][Para 111] The maternity half-wage is payable before the leave begins, under Articles 183 and 115(3). [Para 96][Para 98][Para 99]

**Obiter dicta:** the reference to Award 94/04-Eternity, where tables and chairs were ordered at a specific workplace, is explanatory only and was distinguished here. [Para 113]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The document is an unofficial English translation with no Khmer face text, so the company name appears only as "Suit Way Garment" and the unions as FUDWR and UDWR [Para 2][Para 3]. Paragraph [Para 33] contradicts itself, first saying only Issues 14, 15 and 16 remain and then that only Issues 2, 3, 4, 5 and 8 will be considered; the latter list matches the decision. [Para 33][Para 124] The conciliation count is inconsistent: 25 demands at [Para 30] against sixteen numbered demands reproduced in the text [Para 10][Para 26]. The registration date is given as 9 October 2006 at [Para 34] but the union certificate in evidence is dated 9 October 2007, after the award [Para 33]. The findings on Issue 2 repeat a sentence and drop words mid-paragraph [Para 34].
