# Award No. 38/05 — Fortune Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 18 July 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Ouk Ry (employer-chosen), Vong Vanna (worker-chosen), Pen Bunchhea (chair) · Doc ID 140780

## 1. Facts
Fortune Co., Ltd operates at National Road 21, Prey Svi village, Roka commune, Sa Ang district, Kandal province, and employs about 2,750 workers, of whom 280 work in the weaving section [Para 10]. The company transferred one worker who worked as a record-taker in the weaving section to the trimming section, and the worker party said the transferred worker received a lower basic wage and smaller bonuses than before [Para 21]. On 27 June 2005 approximately 280 weaving-section workers went on strike without consulting the local union representative, demanding that the company not withhold their attendance bonus [Para 21][Para 22]. The employer did not pay the striking workers their US$5 monthly attendance bonus [Para 24]. The Kandal provincial office of Labour and Vocational Training attempted conciliation at the factory, which failed; the non-conciliation report number 001/MoLVT dated 29 June 2005 reached the Secretariat on 29 June 2005, and the panel issued an interim order to halt the strike [Para 9][Para 11][Para 12]. At the hearing the company said the transfer dispute itself had been settled: the attendance bonus for the transferred worker was maintained at its previous level and the worker agreed to the trimming-section job [Para 25]. Both sides then confirmed that dispute resolved, leaving only the legal question the workers asked the panel to answer: whether withholding the bonus from striking workers is correct under the Labour Law [Para 25][Para 26].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the workers of the weaving section were entitled to their June 2005 US$5 monthly attendance bonus for the days they were on strike? [Para 26]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, unnamed worker party):** the company should not withhold the attendance bonus in June 2005 because the weaving-section workers went on strike, and the workers asked the panel to identify in the Labour Law whether the deduction of the bonus from striking workers was correct [Para 7][Para 25].
**Employer (Fortune Co., Ltd):** the employer rejected the demand and said the issue should be decided according to the law [Para 8]; it treated the underlying transfer grievance as settled by agreement with the transferred worker [Para 25].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the workers' demand for the attendance bonus for June 2005 during the strike conducted by the weaving section [Para 36].
On the notice requirement, the panel read Article 324 of the Labour Law as requiring a strike to be preceded by notice of at least seven working days, and found the weaving-section workers gave none: they walked out spontaneously on learning of the transfer and the reduced wage and bonus [Para 27]. On exhaustion of peaceful means, Article 320 paragraph 4 was read to permit the right to strike only once all peaceful methods with the employer have been exhausted, and the panel found the workers had neither given prior notice nor tried to negotiate a settlement [Para 28][Para 29]. From those two findings the panel stated the operative rule that workers are entitled to the attendance bonus where a strike follows all legal procedures and are not entitled to it where the strike does not [Para 30], supporting that position with a line of earlier awards including 04/03-Lida Garment, 08/04-Wash Concept, 15/04-Lucky Zone, 22/04-Raffles le Royal, 47/04-Dai Young, 111/04-Sun Shiny, 03/05-Flying Dragon and 12/05-P&E [Para 31]. It concluded that the strike was unlawful and the demand for the bonus during the strike could not be accepted [Para 31][Para 32]. Independently, the panel relied on Article 332 of the Labour Law, under which a strike suspends the labour contract, the allowance for work is not provided and salary is not paid, with reinstatement at the end of the strike [Para 33][Para 34]. That article, it held, means striking workers are not entitled to wages during a strike, and that is so even where the strike is lawful, citing 22/04-Raffles le Royal [Para 34][Para 35]. The award was declared immediately binding because the parties agreed to a binding award on 1 July 2005 [Para 36].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a strike suspends the labour contract under Article 332 of the Labour Law, the employer owes no wage and no work-related allowance, including the monthly attendance bonus, for the days of the strike; and where the strike was in any event unlawful for want of the seven-day notice required by Article 324 and for failure to exhaust peaceful means under Article 320 paragraph 4, the demand for that bonus cannot be granted [Para 30][Para 33][Para 35].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the bonus is payable where a strike does follow all legal procedures is not an operative holding here, since this strike failed both the notice and the negotiation requirements [Para 30].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR consistently drops the "t" from "the", producing forms such as "themployer", "thearing", "thisection" and "athe factory" throughout, and mangles "Training" as "Traing" [Para 11][Para 18]. The witness and representative lists in the header are broken across lines so that names and job titles run together, and the worker party is printed only as an unnamed party with no union name [Para 7]. One sentence is doubled within its paragraph, the repeated "This Article means that" [Para 34], and the list of worker representatives prints the same line leader twice [Para 7]. The hearing date is printed two ways: 1 June 2005 at 2:00 to 5:00 p.m. in the procedural summary and an invitation to appear on 1 July 2005 in the chronology [Para 9][Para 13].
