# Award No. 012/18 — YLT International Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 24 May 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Tang Tri presiding; Ouk Ry, employer nominee; An Virak, worker nominee) · Doc ID 138883

## 1. Facts
YLT International Co., Ltd manufactures wooden pallets at Sotthea village, Romchek commune, Preah Sdach district, Prey Veng, and employed 250 workers on the employer's own account at the hearing. [Para 31] On 5 March 2018 its workers lodged a complaint with the Prey Veng Provincial Department of Labour and Vocational Training; conciliation was attempted on 28 March 2018, the employer did not attend, and the non-conciliated points were referred to the Council on 18 April 2018. [Para 12] The panel was constituted on 20 April and heard the matter on 27 April 2018, leaving two live claims: 5% severance at the expiry of each fixed-duration contract, and annual leave withheld from 2015 to 2017. [Para 21][Para 35]

## 2. Issues in dispute
2. Whether the employer must pay severance of 5% at the expiry of each fixed-duration employment contract, and not only when the employment relationship is cut off? [Para 68]
3. Whether the employer must make good, in money or in days, the paid annual leave it did not grant the workers from 2015 to 2017? [Para 84]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the 5% should be paid each time a fixed-duration contract reaches its term, as other companies do, both to meet daily living costs without waiting for termination and as an incentive to work better; annual leave was owed at 18 days after one year of service and 19 days after three years. [Para 39][Para 47]
**Employer (respondent):** Article 11 of the collective agreement of 24 October 2016, duly registered with the provincial labour department, ties the 5% to the moment the contract is cut off, not to each expiry; as for leave, the employer cannot pay for 2015–2017 because that period has passed, and it began granting annual leave only from 2018. [Para 41]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 2 dismissed; on issue 3 the panel ordered the employer to grant annual leave for 2015 to 2017 in days according to each worker's actual seniority and to allow the workers to take that leave; no sum of money was awarded. [Para 113][Para 114]
Before the claims, the panel addressed who could bring them. Article 54(1) of the Law on Trade Unions of 17 May 2016 reserves the exclusive right to raise and represent a collective dispute to the most representative union, and no evidence showed YLT had one; [Para 55] following Award 180/16 (Cement Chakrey Ting), where no such union exists the workers may represent themselves or delegate, and clause 1 of Prakas 362 of 29 August 2016 empowers the provincial labour department to recognise worker representatives. [Para 61] Letter No. 001 of 7 March 2018 recognised the three workers who appeared, so the claim was properly before the panel. [Para 66]
On issue 2, Article 73(1) of the Labour Law provides that a fixed-duration contract ends normally at its term, and Article 73(6) requires severance on termination equal at least to 5% of the wages paid during the contract. [Para 76] The panel followed its earlier awards holding that the 5% falls due when the fixed-duration contract is terminated and the employment relationship is cut off. [Para 77] Here the six-month contracts were renewed successively at each expiry, so the relationship was never cut off; [Para 79] Article 11 of the collective agreement likewise provides for payment after the worker stops being employed, and the employer in fact paid only on that event. [Para 81] The claim was therefore dismissed. [Para 83]
On issue 3, Articles 166(1) and (4) give 1.5 working days of paid leave per month of continuous service, plus one day per three years of seniority, and Article 167(1) makes the right conditional on one year's service. [Para 84] The panel computed entitlement at 18 days in the early years, rising with seniority. [Para 89] But Article 167(2) allows payment in lieu only where the contract is cut off or ends before the leave is taken, and Article 167(3) voids any agreement to pay compensation in place of leave or to waive the right; the panel read this as barring cash payment while the worker is still serving. [Para 96][Para 97] It found the employer's refusal to grant leave from 2015 to 2017 contrary to Articles 166 and 167, but held that the remedy while employment continues is the grant of days, not money, and so ordered, invoking Article 34 of Prakas 099 as the source of its remedial power. [Para 108]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Both the 5% severance under Article 73(6) and cash in lieu of untaken annual leave under Article 167(2)–(3) of the Labour Law fall due only when the employment relationship is actually severed; successive renewal of fixed-duration contracts without a break does not sever it, so neither entitlement is triggered at each contract expiry, and leave withheld during a continuing relationship is made good in days, not in money. [Para 77][Para 79][Para 105]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the employer had only begun granting annual leave in early 2018 and that workers hired at the start of 2018 have no leave entitlement until a year's service — observations outside the 2015–2017 claim period. [Para 50]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The list of issues in dispute prints five items only, yet the procedural account and the panel speak of six non-conciliated points, of which issues 1, 4 and 6 were settled and issue 5 withdrawn, leaving issues 2 and 3; the sixth item appears nowhere in the source. [Para 2][Para 19][Para 20] The percentage claim is printed as "៥៦៣ (ភាគរយ)" in the statement of claim and as "៥២៦ ភាគរយ" in the panel's question, both corruptions of the 5% the same claim carries elsewhere. [Para 35][Para 72] The leave arithmetic is internally inconsistent: 19, 20 and 21 days for the fourth to twelfth years at one place, but 1.5 days a month or 18 days a year from 2015 at another, and no figure is fixed for any individual worker. [Para 89][Para 94] The conciliation report No. 043/18 is dated 10 April 2018 in one place and 9 April 2018 in another, and the employer appears as "YLT International Co., Ltd" and "YLT Internationa". [Para 15][Para 26] Names of individual workers and of the employer's representative are redacted here by policy; they survive in the source.
