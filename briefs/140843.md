# Award No. 030/21 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេ ផ្លាស់ អេ ស៊ូ (ខេមបូឌា) (Arbitration Council, 2 December 2021)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៊ីង សុទ្ធី for the employer, អន ណន for the workers, ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា presiding) · Doc ID 140843

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេ ផ្លាស់ អេ ស៊ូ (ខេមបូឌា) manufactures footwear and employed 1,572 workers when the award issued [Para 36]. On 14 September 2021 the workers' representatives filed nine demands to improve working conditions; conciliation on 13 October 2021 settled seven of them, and the two unresolved points were forwarded to the Council on 18 October 2021 under conciliation report No. 690 [Para 14][Para 15][Para 16]. The panel was constituted on 20 October 2021 and heard the parties online on 26 October 2021, both sides attending and neither objecting to procedure [Para 17][Para 19][Para 21]. The parties agreed to extend the award date from 11 November 2021 to 2 December 2021 [Para 25]. The award is expressly non-binding [Para 24].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer is obliged to install hot and cold water machines — two in the front building and two in the rear building — for workers to drink hot water? [Para 38][Para 63]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer's production quotas are set to match the workers' actual capacity? [Para 50][Para 78]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Workers needed hot drinking water because of COVID-19 prevention and to avoid catching cold, and the clinic was far from the production buildings so a dispenser in each building would be more convenient [Para 41][Para 44]. On quotas, they said Khmer supervisors set sewing and cutting targets every two hours, reprimanded workers who missed them and withheld the over-target bonus [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53].

**Employer (respondent):** The company already supplied drinking water for all workers and hot water was available in the clinic without any permit letter; the clinic was being moved to the centre of the buildings [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47][Para 75]. On quotas, targets rested on a tested formula, workers were only asked to report output every two hours so machine problems could be fixed, and no two-hour quota existed [Para 56][Para 59][Para 85].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both demands of the workers' representatives were rejected, with no sum or reinstatement ordered [Para 91][Para 93].

On Issue 1 the panel applied Prakas No. 054 SKBY of 10 February 2017 on the supply of hygienic drinking water, whose Article 1 requires only that employers provide sufficient hygienic drinking water to their workers [Para 63]. It followed Award No. 173/14 (Aison Cable Cambodia), which held that the Prakas nowhere specifies hot or cold water [Para 64][Para 69]. Both sides conceded that potable water was already provided, so the employer's obligation was met, and hot water was in any event obtainable in the clinic without a permission letter [Para 66][Para 70][Para 75][Para 76]. The panel therefore held there was no legal basis for dispensers in each building and dismissed the demand [Para 74][Para 77].

On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 2 of the Labour Law, treating the setting of production targets as part of the employer's right to organise and manage the enterprise, exercisable lawfully and reasonably [Para 78][Para 79][Para 81]. It followed prior awards 06/06 (Im Vao), 18/06 (GHG) and 108/06 (Trunggal Komara) on the same point [Para 80]. The demand nonetheless failed on the evidence: the workers never stated how many units the quota required, how many they could manage, or how many workers were disciplined for missing it [Para 86][Para 87]. Finding the allegations insufficiently precise to adjudicate, the panel declined to consider the claim [Para 88][Para 90].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Prakas No. 054 SKBY obliges an employer to supply sufficient hygienic drinking water, not water at any particular temperature; a demand for hot-water dispensers in each building therefore discloses no legal obligation, and the employer discharges the duty by making potable water and clinic hot water available [Para 65][Para 67][Para 73]. Setting production quotas is an employer management prerogative under Article 2 of the Labour Law, but a claim that a quota exceeds workers' capacity will not be adjudicated unless the claimant quantifies the quota, the feasible rate and the number of workers affected [Para 81][Para 86][Para 88].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the employer was studying relocating the clinic to the centre of the buildings and that no permission letter was required to draw hot water there [Para 47][Para 75]. It also recorded that the award becomes final eight days after notification absent a written objection to the Minister [Para 94].
