# Award No. 21/03 — Loyal Cambodia Limited (Arbitration Council, 08-12-2003)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, Liv Sovanna, chair Kong Phallak) · Doc ID 138833

## 1. Facts
Loyal Cambodia Limited employed 420 workers at #34, St.289, Sangkat Beng Kork, Khan Tuol Kok, Phnom Penh. [Para 12] On October 03, 2003 the labour inspector of Khan Tuol Kork received a complaint covering 11 demands; the company did not attend the first two conciliation meetings and nominated a representative only for the third, on November 28, 2003, where 3 of the 11 points were settled. [Para 13] [Para 15] [Para 16] [Para 17] The panel conciliated the rest and held hearings on November 25 and December 02, 2003, the parties agreeing to a binding award. [Para 19] [Para 20] What survived to decision was money deducted from wages — US$5 for a workbook and medical examination, US$15.00 to US$30.00 as a "deposit" — and the 50% wage paid on days with no work. [Para 22] [Para 31]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must employ a permanent nurse during working and overtime hours and keep the infirmary supplied with enough medicines? [Para 4]
2. Whether the employer must pay wages regularly? [Para 4]
3. Whether the employer must deduct 1000 riels per month from members' wages for the union's fee? [Para 4]
4. Whether the employer must maintain order at the time salaries are paid? [Para 4]
5. Whether the employer must reimburse unused annual leave from the year 1997? [Para 4]
6. Whether the employer must pay overtime wages and the food allowance every week or every two weeks? [Para 4]
7. Whether the employer may deduct US$5.00 for the employment card and US$15.00 to US$30.00 as a deposit from workers' wages, or must repay those sums? [Para 4] [Para 22] [Para 26]
8. Whether the employer must pay full salaries at times when it has no work for the workers to do? [Para 4] [Para 30]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that the cost of the medical examination and the workbook is the employer's, that the deposits were never charged to workers who began in 2002, and that waiting unpaid wastes time they could spend earning elsewhere. [Para 24] [Para 28] [Para 34]
**Employer (respondent):** that it deducted US$5 to cover the medical examination and the workbook, that it took US$15.00–US$30.00 as a deposit securing continued employment and repayable at the end of the contract (forfeited if the worker left within 3 months), and that 50% of salary is fair consideration for workers who attend and wait during a period without orders. [Para 23] [Para 27] [Para 28] [Para 33] [Para 35]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issues 1 to 6 were ordered as agreed: medicines and safety measures within 2 weeks with hospital costs borne by the employer, regular wage payment, a 1000 Riel union-fee deduction only on the member's written request through the union, order maintained at pay time, 1997 unused annual leave paid within three weeks, and overtime wages plus food allowance every two weeks. On issue 7 the employer must repay the equivalent of $4 plus 1500 Riel to each worker from whom the US$5 was taken, and repay the US$15.00 to US$30.00 deposits in full by 15 January 2004, including to former workers, with the list published and a neutral verifier if the union objects. On issue 8 it must pay full wages whether or not there is work, unless the contract is suspended under Article 71, when 50% may be paid for attendance. [Para 56] [Para 59] [Para 65]
On issue 7 the panel followed its award in Case No. 02/03 (Chou Sing Garment Co. Ltd, decided on 21 May 2003) together with Article 247(c) of the 1997 Labour Law and Joint Prakas No. 09 SALV of January 1994, whose Article 7 puts the pre-employment medical examination on the owner of the enterprise, and declined to depart from that interpretation. [Para 38] [Para 39] [Para 41] A workbook, by contrast, is the worker's own property under Article 32, so the worker bears its cost, which Notification # 13 of MOSALVY dated 29th May 1997 fixes at 1,500 Riel for the form and 1000 Riel for stamp duty, 2,500 Riel in total; the US$5 deduction therefore exceeded the lawful cost and the balance is repayable. [Para 43] [Para 45] [Para 46] [Para 47] The deposit agreements were signed, but their effect was to pay workers less than the US$40 minimum probationary wage in MOSALVY Notification #17 of 2000, so they are null and void under Article 13 as contrary to public policy, and Articles 126 and 129 raise a strong presumption against any wage deduction. [Para 48] [Para 49] [Para 50] [Para 51] Interest and damages were not ordered because that demand was not in the complaint. [Para 52]
On issue 8, Article 71(7) and (11) permit suspension only through internal rules or under labour-inspector supervision, for no more than two months; the company produced neither, so no proper suspension was shown and full wages are owed, Article 72 relieving the employer of wages only during a lawful suspension. [Para 53] [Para 55]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A deduction from wages is lawful only to the extent the cost is by law the worker's: the employer bears the pre-employment medical examination (Article 247(c), Joint Prakas No. 09 of January 1994) and the worker bears only the statutory workbook cost of 2,500 Riel, so any larger deduction must be repaid. A signed agreement to deduct a deposit from probationary wages is void under Article 13 where it brings pay below the minimum probationary wage. Work may be suspended at half pay only under Article 71(7) or (11); absent internal rules or inspector supervision the employer owes full wages. [Para 42] [Para 47] [Para 48] [Para 55]

**Obiter dicta:** that if a suspension is properly made under Article 71 and the employer nonetheless requires attendance, paying 50% of basic salary for it may be acceptable; and that interest or damages will not be awarded where never claimed in the complaint. [Para 52] [Para 55]

[Para 7] [Para 66] The non-conciliation report is dated November 05, 2003 and said to have reached the Secretariat on 11-11-2003, although the conciliation it reports is dated November 28, 2003. [Para 7] [Para 16] The conciliation is said to have settled 3 of 11 points, yet eight issues are listed and the panel records agreement on issues 1 to 6. [Para 4] [Para 17] [Para 20] The repayment is printed as 17,500 Riel in the reasons and as "$4 plus 1500 Riel" in the order. [Para 47] [Para 59] Paragraph 7 is badly corrupted by OCR with repeated fragments, the sangkat is Beng Kork 2 in the header and Beng Kork 1 in the summary, and "th" is dropped throughout. [Para 7] [Para 12] Individual names are initialised per convention.
