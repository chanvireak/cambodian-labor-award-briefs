# Award No. 50/06 — Genuine Garment (Cambodia) Co. Ltd. (Genuine) and FTUKC
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators · Doc ID 140707 · award dated 4 July 2006

## 1. Facts

Genuine Garment (Cambodia) Co. Ltd. (Genuine) operated a factory on National Road No. 4, Kul village, Kanthork commune, Ang Snuol district, Kandal, employing about 950 workers [Para 9]. The worker party was the Free Trade Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia (FTUKC), represented by its vice-president, Ms. S [Para 6]. The panel was Ly Tayseng, chosen by the employer; An Nan, chosen by the worker party; and Kong Phallack, chair chosen by the two arbitrators [Para 1].

The worker party demanded that the employer withdraw its lawsuit from the court and reinstate eight worker representatives [Para 7]. On 26 June 2006 the Arbitration Council Secretariat received case 50/06 and was told at the same time that the workers of Genuine were on strike [Para 9]. The workers refused to select an arbitrator, so one was chosen by lot under Prakas No. 99/04 [Paras 10–12]. The non-conciliation report No. 093/06 KBV/KN was dated 26 June 2006 [Para 9]. At the pre-hearing on 28 June 2006 the panel warned that arbitration could not proceed unless the strike stopped [Paras 15–16], but the workers continued their strike [Para 17].

The Council issued Interim Order No. 012 LKA on 28 June 2006 ordering the workers to stop the strike and return on 29 June 2006 [Para 18], and Interim Order No. 013 LKA on 30 June 2006 ordering them to return on 1 July 2006 and setting the hearing for 4 July 2006 [Para 19]. Clause 4 of the second order stated that if the workers continued the strike the Council would not continue the proceedings [Para 19]. The workers continued their strike [Para 19], and the hearing was held on 28 June 2006 [Para 9].

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. Whether the Arbitration Council should continue the arbitration while the worker party remained on strike in breach of the interim orders? [Para 27]

The merits demand — withdrawal of the employer's court action and reinstatement of eight worker representatives — was not reached [Para 7].

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** The FTUKC demanded that Genuine withdraw its lawsuit from the court and reinstate the eight worker representatives [Para 7]. The worker party refused to select an arbitrator and remained on strike throughout the proceedings [Paras 10, 19].

**Employer (respondent):** Genuine asked the Arbitration Council to intervene so that the workers would return to work, in a letter dated 26 June 2006 [Para 12]. It supplied a power of attorney from its director to its manager, dated 26 June 2006 [Para 13].

## 4. Award and reasoning

The panel applied Article 20 of Prakas No. 99/04, which requires the parties during arbitration to abstain from any strike or lockout as defined in Article 318 of the Labour Law, and from any action likely to aggravate the situation [Paras 20–21]. It read that article, consistently with its previous interim orders, as meaning that the Council will not continue an arbitration while the worker party is on strike [Para 21].

It followed a line of prior decisions: case 04/04-MSI, where a notification of 29 January 2004 stated that the Council could not settle a dispute referred to it while the parties were striking [Para 22]; case 18/04-Hotels, where Interim Order 2 of 22 April 2004 reserved the right to terminate proceedings if a party violated the order [Paras 24–25]; case 77/05-Franco Knitting Garment, Interim Order 2 of 24 December 2005 [Para 25]; and cases 28/06-G.D.M and 34/06-Gold Fame, where awards dismissed the cases because the workers continued to strike [Para 25]. It found the worker party showed no intention to bring the dispute to the Council for settlement [Para 26]. It therefore decided not to continue the proceedings [Para 27].

**Outcome:** dismissed — the arbitration process in 50/06-Genuine was discontinued [Para 28].

## 5. Ratio decidendi

A strike staged during Arbitration Council proceedings, in breach of an interim order to return to work, is incompatible with Article 20 of Prakas No. 99/04, and the Council will discontinue the arbitration rather than decide the merits; it may resume the case once the strike ends [Paras 21, 23, 27]. The Council's power to proceed depends on the parties abstaining from strikes and attending its meetings [Para 21].

**Obiter dicta:** the Council noted it may settle the dispute again once the workers stop the strike [Para 23].
