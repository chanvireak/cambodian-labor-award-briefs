# Award No. 123/12 — CIVCA Investment Development (Royal Cambodia Phnom Penh Golf Club) (Arbitration Council, 13 July 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tai Seng, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Pen Bunchhea, president) · Doc ID 140144

## 1. Facts
The respondent is CIVCA Investment Development, whose registered trade name is Royal Cambodia Phnom Penh Golf Club, a sports and golf centre in Phnom Penh [Para 1][Para 35]. The Independent Union of Cambodian Workers at the club, registered on 01 April 2004, complained to the Labour Disputes Department on 30 May 2012 seeking improvement of several working conditions [Para 14][Para 36]. Conciliation on 19 June 2012 settled two of four demands, and a further demand — food for the four workers in the hygiene section — was settled at the hearing of 05 July 2012, leaving one demand for arbitration [Para 15][Para 19]. That demand asked the company to dismiss Mr V, its head of administration, on five grounds: threatening workers who thumbprinted a petition for his removal, failing to report that petition to the director, using improper language, dismissing eight workers and two administrative staff who refused to move from open-ended to fixed-duration contracts, and general pressure and targeting [Para 37][Para 41].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the panel should order the employer to dismiss Mr V, the head of administration, from his post? [Para 37][Para 55]
2. Whether the employer must restore the food allowance to the four workers of the hygiene section? [Para 7][Para 19]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr V threatened workers who had thumbprinted the petition, telling them that if he could not be removed he would take action against those who signed; on 26 May 2012 a complaint against him was filed directly with him and he did not report it to the company director; he used improper language and dismissed eight workers and two administrative staff for refusing fixed-duration contracts [Para 38][Para 39][Para 41][Para 42]. At the hearing the workers could not give the date or place of the alleged words and filed no evidence by the deadline; they asserted only that workers in the Kandi section had all heard them [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47].

**Employer (respondent):** the employer refused the demand outright [Para 43]. Its counsel argued that the dismissed workers and staff had already received severance, and that the dismissals were the decision of the company director and not of Mr V; the workers did not contest that the employer, rather than Mr V, took the dismissal decisions [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 66][Para 67]. On the food claim it agreed to discuss the matter with the director, which produced the settlement at the hearing [Para 8][Para 19].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the demand that the employer dismiss Mr V, and made no order on the food claim because it was settled at the hearing [Para 69][Para 70].
The panel first narrowed the grounds: grounds 1, 3 and 5 were one and the same allegation, and because the workers gave no date or time for the words and produced no evidence within the deadline, it declined to consider them [Para 44][Para 45][Para 48]. On ground 2 it found that Mr V had not done what was alleged [Para 50]. Only ground 4 — the dismissal of ten people who refused fixed-duration contracts — remained for consideration [Para 54].
On that ground the panel applied Article 65 of the Labour Law, which defines the employment contract as one creating a working relationship between worker and employer, and Article 311 of the Civil Code, under which a contract is the meeting of wills of two or more persons creating, modifying or extinguishing obligations [Para 55][Para 57]. It concluded that only the parties to a contract may dissolve its obligations, so a third party has no right to dissolve the employment contract between employer and worker, following award 116/10-White Tech and a line of earlier awards [Para 58][Para 60][Para 63][Para 64]. In this case only the employer could decide to end Mr V's contract, not the workers; workers may seek the removal or transfer of another worker only if they prove that person is dangerous and cannot be kept in the company or would endanger others' safety, and no such evidence was produced [Para 61][Para 62][Para 68]. The award was issued as a non-binding decision, becoming final after eight days if neither party objects in writing to the Minister [Para 71].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Workers cannot obtain the dismissal of a fellow employee: under Articles 65 of the Labour Law and 311 of the Civil Code only the parties to the employment contract may dissolve it, so the demand fails unless the claimants prove the person is dangerous and cannot safely be retained in the enterprise [Para 58][Para 61][Para 62]. Allegations of threats or improper language that are left undated and unsupported by evidence filed within the deadline will not be examined, even where several workers say they heard the words [Para 45][Para 48].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that a third party has no right to dissolve another's employment contract, supported by the string of prior awards cited, goes beyond what was needed once the evidence of dangerousness was found absent [Para 60][Para 63].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's name is spelled inconsistently in the original: the case name and party details use one transliteration of CIVCA Investment Development while the facts section uses another, and the same company appears as Royal Cambodia Phnom Penh Golf Club throughout the documents [Para 1][Para 35][Para 21]. Internal rules dated 09 August 2001 in the file are issued in the trade name rather than the registered company name [Para 22]. Conciliation is recorded as settling 02 of 04 demands on 19 June 2012, with the remaining two forwarded, yet one of those two then settled at the hearing, so the arithmetic of the demands changes mid-award [Para 15][Para 16][Para 19]. The number of dismissals is given as eight workers and two administrative staff, but the award never reconciles that figure with the single demand concerning the head of administration [Para 41][Para 65]. OCR injects stray Latin fragments ("Sin", "Man", "SAN") at the start of sentences, and the head of administration's Khmer name is reduced here to a role and initial [Para 10][Para 17][Para 70].
