# Award No. 325/14 — Four Four Junior Thing (Arbitration Council, 13 January 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Tang Try, chair) · Doc ID 139775

## 1. Facts
The company is a garment factory at Sitbo, Saang district, Kandal, registered since 2007 and employing about 305 workers [Para 150][Para 151][Para 152]. Its factory union was registered on 12 November 2013 and elected a new leadership on 26 June 2014, notified to the employer on 27 June 2014 [Para 153][Para 157][Para 159]. On 30 September 2014 the employer ended the contracts of 35 workers, and 35 workers delegated their claims to the union federation by a letter of 20 September 2014 [Para 161][Para 165]. Of five unresolved demands, issues 3 and 4 were settled at the hearing, leaving issues 1, 2 and 5; the panel examined issue 1 only for the 28 workers named in the list filed on 16 December 2014 [Para 23][Para 24][Para 166][Para 262].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer discriminated against the Ciu Du union and must reinstate all dismissed members, activists and leaders and pay them back wages from the date of dismissal to the date of return? [Para 164]
2. Whether the employer discriminated against and dismissed pregnant women on the pretext of fixed-duration contracts? [Para 223][Para 303]
5. Whether the employer must allow pregnant workers one day a month to attend antenatal checks while preserving their wages and other benefits? [Para 245][Para 326]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The non-renewals hit the new union leadership elected on 26 June 2014; while the 28 were claiming reinstatement the employer recruited 72 new workers [Para 198][Para 371]. Workers with more than two years' service held indefinite-term contracts, so ending them required the labour inspector's authorisation, which was never sought [Para 201][Para 203]. Pregnant women were told to wait until they had given birth before reapplying, and one was dismissed after an unwell day [Para 227][Para 235].

**Employer (respondent):** The company merely let fixed-duration contracts expire because orders run out between August and early February, and it rehired union members — 32 of the new recruits belong to the union, including six of the claimants [Para 206][Para 207][Para 298][Para 300]. Notices were given and statutory payments made; the recruitment posters belonged to a neighbouring sister company [Para 212][Para 221][Para 222]. Absence without the team leader's permission is treated as unauthorised under the internal rules [Para 240][Para 244].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the claims under issues 1, 2 and 5 were all refused; no reinstatement, back pay or paid antenatal leave was ordered [Para 346][Para 347][Para 349]

On issue 1 the panel classified the claim as one of rights, since reinstatement is grounded in statute [Para 261]. It then asked whether the 28 held fixed- or indefinite-term contracts. Under Article 67(2) a fixed-duration contract may not exceed two years and renewals may not extend it beyond two years, failing which it becomes indefinite [Para 263][Para 264][Para 265]. The panel held, however, that where a worker accepts the 5% end-of-contract payment at each expiry and signs a fresh contract, Article 311 of the Civil Code treats the old contract as ended by agreement, so earlier periods are not aggregated [Para 274][Para 275][Para 277]. All 28 had done exactly that, and the company used terms of one, two, three and six months, none exceeding two years [Para 279][Para 280][Para 281]. Their contracts therefore remained fixed-term and simply expired, so Article 293 — which requires the labour inspector's consent only for dismissal — did not apply [Para 283][Para 285][Para 287].

On the discrimination allegation the panel applied Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law, which forbid using union membership or activity as a ground for hiring or terminating [Para 291][Para 292]. It weighed the documented resignations of September to November 2014, the rehiring of six claimants among 32 union-member recruits, and the fact that some recruits joined the union after recruitment, and found no established link between the non-renewals and union activity [Para 299][Para 300][Para 301][Para 302].

On issue 2 the panel held the question to be one of rights, because Article 46(2) of the Constitution forbids dismissing a woman on grounds of pregnancy [Para 303][Para 305][Para 306]. It accepted that three women on fixed-term contracts had taken paid maternity leave at 50%, which undercut any claim of a blanket policy [Para 308]. For the one worker whose dismissal was litigated, the employer had applied clause 6(2) of the internal rules of 16 February 2009, and the panel found she had been absent on 10 November and from 17 to 25 November 2014 without any request to the administration and without medical evidence, so the absence was unauthorised and the claim failed for want of proof [Para 313][Para 316][Para 322][Para 323].

On issue 5 the panel found no law, collective agreement, internal rule or practice obliging the employer to keep wages for antenatal checks; the claim was a benefit above the law, hence an interest dispute, and the applicant was not the most representative union in an enterprise of 305 workers [Para 329][Para 330][Para 332][Para 343][Para 344]. Arbitrator An Non dissented on issue 1, would have aggregated the successive terms into an indefinite contract and found union discrimination, ordering reinstatement and back pay [Para 353][Para 369][Para 378].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a worker accepts the 5% compensation payable at the expiry of each fixed-duration contract and signs a new one, the earlier term is treated as ended by mutual agreement and its duration is not added to the later term, so the contract does not convert into an indefinite-term contract and its expiry is not a dismissal requiring the labour inspector's prior authorisation. [Para 277][Para 285]

An allegation of anti-union discrimination must be proved; where the employer documents resignations and rehires union members, including some claimants, the link between non-renewal and union activity is not established. [Para 301]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that fixed-duration contracts must not exceed two years and that a contrary practice converts them to indefinite-term contracts, a rule it distinguished rather than applied here [Para 265]; it also noted that a woman on fixed-term contract who took 50% paid maternity leave is evidence against a discriminatory policy [Para 308].
