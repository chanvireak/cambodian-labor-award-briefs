# Award No. 067/19 — Khen Theren Apparel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 28 August 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ri, employer-appointed; Tuon Siphann, worker-appointed, dissenting in part; Pen Bun Chia, chair) · non-binding award · Doc ID 139117

## 1. Facts
The employer operates a garment factory at Trapeang Thleung village, Chom Chao, Phnom Penh, with 1,020 workers and no union holding most-representative status, as both parties acknowledged at the hearing. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46] The trimming team (ក្រុមកាត់ ព្រុយ), about 51 workers with its own team leader, was by notice of 22 July 2019 placed under the sewing team leaders from 23 July 2019 and its own leader withdrawn, with role, wage, seniority bonus and other bonuses preserved. [Para 47][Para 49][Para 50][Para 57] The workers struck over five demands; conciliation on 24 July 2019 failed and the file reached the Council on 30 July 2019. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20] At the hearing the workers merged issues 1, 2 and 3 into one, leaving that issue, issue 4 and issue 5, plus an additional claim arising from the morning of 31 July 2019. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 167]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer has the right to reorganise the trimming team under the sewing team leaders, keeping no separate trimming team leader? [Para 108]
4. Whether the employer must terminate the trimming workers' contracts and pay their wages and other benefits because it dissolved the trimming team? [Para 122]
5. Whether the employer must preserve wages and benefits for the half day of the afternoon of 23 July 2019 and the full day of 24 July 2019, during conciliation? [Para 134]
Additional. Whether the employer must preserve wages and benefits for the half day of the afternoon of 31 July 2019, when the workers came to the Council to select arbitrators? [Para 168]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The trimming team was separate with its own leader, and moving them under sewing team leaders puts them outside their skill, exposes them to orders to do other work and to reprimand or discipline if they refuse, and the change was not notified to them beforehand; placing them under sewing leaders is equivalent to dissolving the whole team, so the contracts should be terminated with statutory severance; and they clocked in on 23 and 24 July but were given no work, so the wage must be preserved, as it must for the 31 July selection of arbitrators. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 55][Para 56][Para 67][Para 72][Para 73][Para 82][Para 83]
**Employer (respondent):** It restructured management so the production line runs faster and sewers can help trim when the team falls behind, it will redeploy the former team leader elsewhere, one trimming leader cannot supervise members scattered across sewing teams 2 to 15, and the change affects neither skill, role, seniority nor wage; it has no wish to terminate anyone and the workers remain at work, so no severance is due; and on 23 and 24 July the workers entered but did no work, sitting together and talking, so wages were rightly withheld. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 61][Para 62][Para 63][Para 68][Para 75][Para 76][Para 87][Para 88]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay the trimming workers one half day's wage and other benefits for the afternoon of 24 July 2019; issues 1 to 3, issue 4, issue 5 as to the afternoon of 23 July and the morning of 24 July, and the additional issue were dismissed. [Para 177][Para 178][Para 179][Para 180][Para 181]

On issues 1 to 3 the panel applied Article 2 of the Labour Law, read as conferring a management right that must be exercised lawfully and reasonably, and the four limits it had derived in earlier awards for moving a worker: no wage cut, no move to a distant workplace, no reversal of day and night shifts, and no change too far from the worker's skill. It found the reorganisation breached none of the first three, and on the fourth that the work remained trimming — the same skill — so the only concern was possible reprimand by sewing leaders when helping with other work, which the workers did not support with any specific fact or evidence. The measure was therefore lawful and reasonable. [Para 108][Para 110][Para 112][Para 113][Para 114][Para 116][Para 117][Para 119][Para 120][Para 121]

On issue 4 the panel applied Article 311 of the Civil Code and Article 65(1) of the Labour Law: contractual obligations arise from the will of the two parties, and only those parties may extinguish them, so one party cannot compel the other to terminate a contract against its will. The employer had no intention to terminate and the workers were still working, so no severance was owed. [Para 122][Para 123][Para 126][Para 127][Para 130][Para 131][Para 132][Para 133]

On issue 5 the panel used Article 664 of the Civil Code and Article 102 of the Labour Law: wage is the price of work performed, so a worker who performs none is not owed it. The workers clocked in on the afternoon of 23 July and the morning of 24 July but did not work, the employer having assigned none because they had refused the reorganisation, so no wage was due for those periods. [Para 134][Para 136][Para 139][Para 143][Para 144] For the afternoon of 24 July, however, a conciliation meeting was held with the labour dispute officer present; Article 306 of the Labour Law and Articles 20 and 14 of Prakas No. 099 require parties and their representatives to attend every meeting and forbid any deduction of wage or bonus for attending, so the employer was ordered to pay that half day. [Para 145][Para 147][Para 150][Para 152][Para 154][Para 155][Para 156]

On the additional issue the panel held it admissible under Article 312 of the Labour Law and Article 33 of Prakas No. 099 because it arose after the non-conciliation record as a direct consequence of the dispute. On the merits, the seven workers selected arbitrators on the morning of 31 July and left at 10:40 a.m., not returning in the afternoon and not seeking permission, so no work was performed and no wage was due. [Para 159][Para 160][Para 162][Para 164][Para 165][Para 167][Para 174][Para 175][Para 176]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may reorganise the grouping and supervision of a team, without changing the work, wage, workplace or shift, and a worker's subjective fear of future reprimand does not make the measure unlawful where no fact or evidence supports it; and because wage is the price of work actually performed, a worker who clocks in but is given no work because he refused a lawful reorganisation is not owed wages for that time — except where the absence of work is attendance at a conciliation meeting, for which no deduction may be made. [Para 113][Para 117][Para 143][Para 152]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's statement that the additional claim was admissible as a direct consequence of the dispute, and its reading of Article 2 as requiring management acts to be both lawful and reasonable, were reasoned although the outcome turned on the narrower wage rule. [Para 112][Para 162] Arbitrator Tuon Siphann dissented in part, holding that the workers had clocked in and the employer's failure to assign work was not their fault, so one and a half days' wages were due. [Para 203][Para 207]
