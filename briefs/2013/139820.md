# Award No. 131/13 — Jiun Ye Garment (Cambodia) Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 17 July 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Virak, worker-appointed; Tang Tri, president) · Doc ID 139820

## 1. Facts
Jiun Ye Garment (Cambodia) Co Ltd runs a garment factory in Kampong Chhnang province and employed about 2,949 workers. Of twelve claims conciliated by the provincial labour department on 20 June 2013, eleven were settled and one came to the panel: a demand for a further USD 19 on every worker's base wage. The company had already raised the base wage from USD 61 to USD 72 by announcement of 17 March 2013, pending a Ministry sub-decree. The company-level union had applied for registration nearly two months before the hearing but produced no registration certificate, and only one worker attended. [Para 18][Para 25][Para 26][Para 34][Para 53]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to raise the base wage by a further USD 19 on top of the base wage already paid to all workers? [Para 31]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The federation and the Jiun Ye union officials who attended said the demand was made in the name of all workers in the factory, on the ground that the existing base wage was inadequate. [Para 31][Para 35]
**Employer (respondent):** The employer pointed to its announcement raising the base wage from USD 61 to USD 72 from 1 March 2013 as a temporary measure pending the Ministry's sub-decree on the base wage, and otherwise contested the claim; the record does not set out a fuller argument on the merits. [Para 18]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel declined to consider the demand for a further USD 19 on the base wage, on the ground that the dispute was not a collective labour dispute. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 64]

The panel first examined whether the union could be a party at all. Article 268 confers the rights and benefits the Labour Law recognises on a professional organisation only once it is registered with the Ministry responsible for labour, and those rights include representing members before the Arbitration Council. Prakas No. 099/04, Article 19, requires a representative to hold written authorisation from the party represented. No registration certificate was filed, so the panel held the Jiun Ye union did not yet enjoy the rights of a lawful professional organisation, including the right to appear as a party. [Para 40][Para 43][Para 44][Para 50]

Nor could the federation or the union officers act for the workforce. The federation confirmed it had received no letters of authorisation from any Jiun Ye worker other than the one who attended, and none was filed by the evidence deadline. Only Mr C Chhay therefore stood as claimant, and the panel would decide for him alone. [Para 30][Para 36][Para 51][Para 53]

That disposed of jurisdiction. Under Article 32 of Prakas No. 099/04 the panel may decide only collective labour disputes, and Article 302 defines a collective dispute as one between one or more employers and *a number of* their workers, subject to three cumulative conditions. The panel adopted the three-condition test it had stated in Award No. 10/03 (Zak Sintic): several workers against one or more employers; a subject-matter of working conditions, union rights or employer-worker relations; and a dispute capable of disrupting the enterprise or social peace. With a single claimant, the first condition failed, and the panel expressly held it had no jurisdiction to decide the point. [Para 54][Para 56][Para 57][Para 58][Para 61][Para 62]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Two rules follow. A union that has not obtained registration — and produces no certificate to the panel — cannot represent its members as a party before the Arbitration Council, and officials without written authorisation from the workers concerned cannot appear for them. And where only one worker is a claimant, the "number of workers" element of Article 302 is absent, so the dispute is not collective and the panel has no jurisdiction over the merits, however substantial the underlying wage demand. [Para 50][Para 52][Para 61]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel set out Article 268's deeming rule — that an organisation is treated as registered if the Ministry does not respond within two months of a complete application — without applying it, because no application documents or certificate were produced to show where the union stood. It also observed that the panel may decide only collective disputes, not individual ones. [Para 42][Para 55]
