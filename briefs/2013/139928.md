# Award No. 277/13 — Woorie Garment Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 20 February 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៊ីង សុទ្ធី appointed by the employer, who appended a dissenting opinion; ទួន ស៊ី ផាន់ appointed by the workers; ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា chair) · Doc ID 139928

## 1. Facts
The respondent is a garment factory employing about 1,350 workers, where the most representative union was the Union Movement of Workers of Woorie Garment Factory, registered only after the events in dispute. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 95] Of 20 demands, 11 were settled in conciliation, two were withdrawn and one was merged, leaving seven for the panel. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 37] The disputed acts were the dismissal on 14 November 2013 of four staff — Mr L, Mr K, Ms K and Ms I — for allegedly inserting three fictitious workers into the payroll, the non-renewal of Ms C's fixed-term contract, and deductions and leave practices the union challenged after its 3 November 2013 election. [Para 131][Para 133][Para 135][Para 176]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must supply two sets of work clothing a year to each worker? [Para 96]
2. Whether the employer must take back the four staff dismissed on 14 November 2013, and Ms C? [Para 119][Para 250]
3. Whether the employer must pay the overtime meal allowance once a week? [Para 183]
4. Whether the employer must dismiss Mr N, its administration head? [Para 196]
5. Whether the employer must stop deducting 1,000 riel in union dues without consent and refund what it took? [Para 210]
6. Whether the employer may dock wages and benefits for special leave where no annual leave remains? [Para 217]
7. Whether the employer must provide one special health check a year at its own cost? [Para 229]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissals were anti-union, coming days after the election; Mr N had insulted staff and offered Ms K and Ms I a choice between their union posts and demotion; dues were taken from non-members without consent; and special leave should not be charged against wages, the workers offering to repay one hour a day. [Para 142][Para 147][Para 149][Para 211][Para 224]
**Employer (respondent):** the four had committed serious misconduct under Article 83, confessing by thumbprint on 14 November 2013 to forging the names of dead workers and drawing their wages, while other implicated staff who confessed kept their jobs; Ms C's contract simply expired on 31 October 2013; and any wrongly deducted dues would be refunded after a file review. [Para 132][Para 152][Para 159][Para 215][Para 436]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to supply two shirts a year, to reinstate Mr L, Mr K, Ms K, Ms I and Ms C, to pay overtime meal money weekly, to stop deducting union dues and refund them, and to stop docking wages for special leave while arranging repayment; the demands to dismiss Mr N and to provide annual special health checks were refused. [Para 473][Para 474][Para 475][Para 477][Para 479][Para 481][Para 482]

On clothing, Article 6 of Prakas No. 307 requires at least two sets a year free of charge; a single shirt replaced only when torn falls below that standard, and the employer's argument that the new fabric washes and dries more easily did not make the practice more favourable than the law. [Para 237][Para 239][Para 247][Para 248] On the dismissals, Article 293 protects shop stewards and election candidates, and Articles 3 and 4 of Prakas No. 305 extend that protection from 45 days before an election and from the date registration is sought; but one condition is notice of the protected names to the employer, and the federation notified only GMAC, which has no duty to pass the notice on, so protection was not established. [Para 251][Para 253][Para 257][Para 284][Para 285][Para 286] The dismissals nonetheless failed on the merits: the employer bore the burden of proving forgery, produced only a thumbprinted confession contradicted by Ms R's statement, and offered no further evidence, so no valid ground was shown and reinstatement followed. [Para 328][Para 332][Para 334][Para 336][Para 337]

Ms C's fixed-term contract ran to 31 October 2013, and Article 73(5) required 10 days' notice of non-renewal, or 15 days for a contract of over a year; the employer proved neither an oral warning nor a signed letter. [Para 358][Para 361][Para 366] The contract was therefore renewed for an equal 10-month term, bringing the total to 2 years 10 months, above the two-year ceiling, so it became an indefinite-term contract; termination then needed a valid reason, none was given, and she was reinstated. [Para 368][Para 369][Para 372][Para 379]

On the meal allowance, point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 requires 2,000 riel or a free meal for each day of voluntary overtime and says nothing about timing; the panel ordered weekly payment so that workers can buy food as they work. [Para 388][Para 393][Para 395][Para 403] On dues, Articles 114 and 129(2) forbid restricting a worker's use of wages and allow deductions only on the written consent of a member; no consent was shown, so the deduction stopped and the money was to be refunded. [Para 430][Para 431][Para 433][Para 438] On special leave, Article 171 and Prakas No. 267 allow the days to be set against remaining annual leave, but where none remains the employer cannot deduct wages and benefits and must arrange repayment at the normal hourly rate. [Para 439][Para 440][Para 443][Para 456]

The demand to dismiss Mr N failed because only the parties to an employment contract may terminate it and because workers must prove the person dangerous to others; the allegations of insults and discrimination were supported by no evidence. [Para 407][Para 412][Para 421][Para 429] The annual health check failed as an interest demand: no law, agreement or practice required it beyond Article 247, and the claimants were not the most representative union. [Para 457][Para 458][Para 471][Para 472]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union leader or election candidate loses the statutory protection against dismissal unless the union actually notifies the employer of the candidacy; notice to the employers' association is not notice to the employer. [Para 284][Para 285] An employer alleging serious misconduct carries the burden and must corroborate a confession, and a bare thumbprinted confession contradicted by another witness will not justify dismissal. [Para 332][Para 334] Failure to give the Article 73(5) notice renews a fixed-term contract for an equal term, and where the total then exceeds two years the contract becomes indefinite-term, so that termination requires a valid reason and otherwise reinstatement follows. [Para 361][Para 368][Para 372] Deductions such as union dues need written consent, and special leave may not be charged against wages where no annual leave remains. [Para 433][Para 454]

**Obiter dicta:** the remarks that GMAC has no duty to forward notices to its members, and that a health-check demand is an interest claim requiring most-representative status, sit outside the orders actually made. [Para 285][Para 466]
