# Award No. 81/13 — M&V International Garment (Branch 1) (Arbitration Council, 31 May 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (An Nan for the workers, Pen Bunchhea chair; the employer-nominated arbitrator's name prints only as កៅ ថា ច) · Doc ID 139687

## 1. Facts
M&V International Garment (Branch 1), Phnom Penh, employs about 298 workers. On 1 April 2013 the company moved its packing section of 46 workers from Branch 1 to Branch 3, keeping wages, duties and benefits unchanged, because the Branch 1 packing space was too small and the buyer required a standard; 37 workers transferred. Nine packing workers refused and instead came to Branch 1 every day from 1 April to 6 May 2013, clocking in but not working, to press for a settlement. At the hearing the parties agreed the nine would stay at Branch 1 but no longer work in packing, since packing had ceased there, and the nine agreed to return to work as the employer directed. What remained was their claim for wages and five allowances for the period they did not work. [Para 3][Para 42][Para 45][Para 49][Para 50][Para 51]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the nine workers wages, seniority bonus, travel and accommodation allowance, attendance bonus, health-care allowance and living allowance for 1 April to 6 May 2013? [Para 53][Para 62]
2. Whether the workers' daily attendance at the factory without performing work amounts to a strike and, if so, what pay follows for that period? [Para 65][Para 71]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** They came to the factory every day and should be paid for the period; the transfer of their section was the grievance they were protesting, and their claims cover wages, seniority bonus, travel and accommodation allowance, attendance bonus, health allowance and living allowance. [Para 53][Para 70]
**Employer (respondent):** It refuses payment because the workers did not perform work and did not follow the employer's arrangement; the transfer kept wages, duties and benefits intact and followed a meeting with section heads and prior notice. [Para 48][Para 54]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claims for wages and living allowance during the strike period, and for seniority bonus, travel and accommodation allowance, attendance bonus and health-care allowance, are dismissed; the employer is ordered to pay wages for the paid public holidays of 14, 15 and 16 April and 1 May 2013 falling within the strike. The award is binding as a rights dispute under the parties' agreement of 6 May 2013. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 105][Para 109]

**Preliminary.** The nine workers were not members of the M&V1 factory union, so entrusting their case to the Cambodian Industry Union Federation could not make them MoU signatories; the parties nonetheless agreed at the hearing to apply the MoU, choosing binding awards for rights disputes and non-binding for interests disputes. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 23]

**Classification.** Applying Article 312 paragraph 2 and Clause 43 of Prakas No. 099, and following Awards 05/11, 13/11-Gold Kamvimex, 14/11-GHG, 31/11-Quint Major and 62/11-Ocean Garment, the panel held a rights dispute concerns an entitlement stated in law, an agreement or a collective agreement; this one did, because each head of claim rests on the Labour Law or a Ministry notification, so it was decided by law. [Para 56][Para 60][Para 64]

**Characterisation of the conduct.** Article 318 defines a strike as a concerted stoppage of work by a group of workers within an enterprise, aimed at obtaining a settlement of their demands and as a condition of returning to work. Following 36/06-Mong Duotich and 125/09-Vin Cham Khob, the panel identified three elements — a group of workers, a stoppage of work, and a demand — and found all three present: nine workers, daily clocking in without working, to press the transfer grievance. Their conduct was therefore a strike, and Article 324 required at least seven days' prior notice, of which the panel received no evidence, so the strike did not follow the procedure in the Labour Law. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 71][Para 95]

**Wages during the strike.** Article 332 paragraph 1 suspends the employment contract during a strike and provides that no work and no wages are given for that period; Article 72 paragraph 1 confines suspension to the main obligations. Following 49/05-OSC Angkor and 27/10-Hat Tieng, the panel held that a striker earns no wage whether the strike is lawful or not, so no wages were owed from 1 April to 6 May 2013. [Para 73][Para 75][Para 78]

**Paid public holidays.** Prakas No. 223 of 12 November 2012 lists the paid public holidays for 2013, including 14, 15 and 16 April for Khmer New Year and 1 May for International Labour Day. Because a paid holiday is a day on which workers are entitled to rest with pay, it is not a suspension of the contract, so wages for those four days were owed even though they fell inside the strike. [Para 79][Para 81][Para 82][Para 84]

**Remaining allowances.** The seniority bonus under Point 3 of Notification No. 041/11 is payable monthly to workers with a year's service who are working; the nine did not work in the period, so nothing was owed. The US$7 travel and accommodation allowance under Point 1 of Notification No. 230/12, the attendance bonus under Point 2 (at least US$10 for regular attendance without absence), and the US$5 health-care allowance under Notification No. 206 of 13 December 2011 are all tied to actually attending work, and following 03/05-Fung, 81/08-JBL Apparel and 27/10-Hat Tieng the panel held an unlawful striker is not attending regularly, so none was owed. The living allowance under Notification No. 049/10 had already been folded into the base wage and the additional allowance claimed was not that allowance, so it too was refused. [Para 86][Para 87][Para 88][Para 90][Para 97][Para 101][Para 102]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A concerted daily attendance at the factory without performing work, maintained to press a demand and as a condition of returning, is a strike even without a walkout, and strikers earn no wages or attendance-linked allowances for the period; but paid public holidays falling inside the strike remain payable, because a paid holiday is a statutory rest day with pay and not a suspension of the contract. [Para 71][Para 78][Para 82]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remark that the strike was procedurally unlawful under Article 324 did not change the wage outcome, since it held wages are lost whether the strike is lawful or not, and the same reasoning denied the attendance bonus. [Para 95][Para 75]
