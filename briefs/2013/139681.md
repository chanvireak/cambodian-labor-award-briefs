# Award No. 76/13 — Top Fame Garments Limited (Arbitration Council, 26 April 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 139681 · unofficial English translation of the Khmer original

## 1. Facts
Top Fame Garments Limited, a garment manufacturer in Dangkor district, Phnom Penh, employs 550 workers. [Para 21] The CLUF local union, registered 5 April 2012 with 260 members, filed demands on five points after conciliation failed; it does not hold most representative status (MRS) at the factory. [Para 22][Para 9] The employer did not appear at the hearing and gave no adequate reason, so the panel proceeded in its absence under Clause 21 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 11][Para 12][Para 15] Both parties are signatories to the Memorandum of Understanding of 3 October 2012, which makes awards on rights disputes binding but not those on interest disputes. [Para 17][Para 18]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the attendance bonus in proportion to the number of days of authorised leave taken by the workers? [Para 23]
2. Whether the employer must pay outstanding wages and termination compensation within 48 hours of resignation or termination of the contract? [Para 31]
3. Whether the employer must provide 4,000 riel per day in lieu of lunch? [Para 35]
4. Whether the employer must provide an additional USD 10 accommodation and transport allowance, raising the existing USD 7 to USD 17 per month? [Para 40]
5. Whether the employer must provide at least two sets of clothing per year? [Para 46]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer pays a USD 10 attendance bonus but withholds all of it for a single day of authorised leave, though Article 103 makes a bonus part of wages and wages follow days worked; final wages are paid only on the 10th of the following month, when workers have already moved away; food, rent and utility costs keep rising while neighbouring factories pay for lunch; and Prakas No. 307 requires two sets of clothing a year. [Para 24][Para 26][Para 34][Para 37][Para 39][Para 47]

**Employer (respondent, absent):** in the non-conciliation report it said it would maintain its current attendance practice, rejected the lunch payment and the additional allowance, and would discuss the 48-hour payment and the clothing demand within its management team. [Para 2][Para 5][Para 6]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — on the rights issues the panel ordered the employer to pay the attendance bonus in proportion to days of authorised leave, to pay final wages and termination compensation within 48 hours, and to provide two sets of clothing per year (binding award); on the interest issues it declined to consider the lunch payment and the extra allowance (non-binding award). [Para 97][Para 98][Para 99]

Issue 1 was a rights dispute because Point 2 of Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 governs it, and the panel held that the USD 10 attendance bonus is a statutory entitlement that all garment and footwear employers must pay, not a tool the employer may wield at will. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 53][Para 54] It could not interpret "without absence", which the Labour Advisory Committee left undefined, so it decided the case on its facts: authorised leave for personal commitment suspends the contract under Article 71(6) and Article 72(1), the employer owes no wage or bonus for those days, but it has no right to withhold the whole month's bonus and must reduce it in proportion to days absent. [Para 55][Para 58][Para 72][Para 73][Para 75][Para 76]

Issue 2 was a rights dispute under Article 116(5), which requires wages and any indemnity to be paid within 48 hours of termination; the employer paid only on the 10th of the following month and had never paid within 48 hours, so the order followed Awards 54/07, 70/07 and 54/10. [Para 77][Para 79][Para 81] Issue 5 was a rights dispute because Clause 6 of Prakas No. 307 of 14 December 2007 obliges the employer to supply at least two sets of clothing a year free of charge, and the employer had supplied none. [Para 92][Para 94][Para 95][Para 96]

Issues 3 and 4 were interest disputes: no law, agreement, collective agreement or practice required a lunch payment, and the employer already paid the USD 7 accommodation and transport allowance that Point 1 of Notification No. 230 requires, so the demand for USD 17 exceeded the law. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 90][Para 91] Since an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement for a year and binds all workers, only a union with MRS, or unions together representing over half the workforce, may bring one; this union had 260 of 550 members and no MRS, so the panel declined. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 87][Para 88]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the employer authorises a worker's leave for personal commitment, the contract is suspended under Articles 71(6) and 72(1): the employer owes no wage or attendance bonus for those days, but it may not withhold the whole month's bonus and must reduce it in proportion to the days absent. [Para 73][Para 75][Para 76]

Article 116(5) requires wages and termination compensation to be paid within 48 hours of termination; paying them on the next regular pay day instead is a breach, whatever the established practice. [Para 77][Para 81]

Clothing under Clause 6 of Prakas No. 307 is a statutory right, not a benefit to be negotiated: the employer must supply at least two sets a year free of charge, and a union without MRS can still enforce it because the claim is a rights dispute. [Para 92][Para 94][Para 87]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that bonuses vary in kind and amount and are for each enterprise owner to set as an incentive are general observations, since Point 2 of Notification No. 230 fixes the attendance bonus regardless. [Para 52][Para 53]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The hearing is dated 11 April 2012 at [Para 8] and 11 April 2013 at [Para 10]; only the latter is consistent with a case received on 5 March 2013 and an award of 26 April 2013.
- The issues list at [Para 1]–[Para 6] is scrambled by OCR: the employer's responses are spliced into the workers' demands, and the numbering restarts at "3." inside [Para 4], so the five issues appear as unnumbered fragments until the reasons begin.
- The final order refers to issue 4 as "Issue 43" [Para 99].
- The employer is "Top Frame Garment" at [Para 87] and Top Fame elsewhere; Prakas No. 305 is dated 22 November 2011 at [Para 84], while Prakas No. 099 is attributed to the Ministry of Social Affairs rather than Labour at [Para 85].
- The panel records that the evidence section was omitted from the English version and refers the reader to the Khmer original [Para 19]–[Para 20]; the employer was absent, so its position here rests only on the non-conciliation report.
- Union officers named in the award are given as Mr S, Mr V and Mr P in this brief, the source spelling them inconsistently.
