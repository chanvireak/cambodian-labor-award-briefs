# Award No. 194/13 — Huey Chuen (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 28 October 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Tuon Si Phan (worker-appointed), Pen Bunchhea (president) · Doc ID 139866

## 1. Facts
Huey Chuen (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a shoe manufacturer in Trapeang Thloeng, Chom Chao, Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh, with about 2,400 workers and GSP export registration from 2011. [Para 61][Para 62]
Ms Y Phally (employee No. S41-142) began work in the sewing section on 13 January 2012 on a two-month probation contract, then signed successive six-month fixed-duration contracts. Her last contract ran from 1 June 2013 to 30 November 2013. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67][Para 69][Para 70]
On 24 July 2013 the assistant to the general director ordered her and one other worker transferred from sewing to the warehouse, described as temporary and without loss of benefits, because two warehouse staff had resigned. She refused the transfer. [Para 91][Para 92]
The employer dismissed her on 27 July 2013, before the contract expired, for serious misconduct. [Para 74][Para 115]
The Federation of Khmer Workers' Spirit union brought the case after receiving a transfer of rights from her. Conciliation failed and the file reached the Council on 9 September 2013; the hearing was held on 14 October 2013. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 19][Para 63]

## 2. Issues in dispute
The conciliation report listed six unresolved demands: (1) reinstatement of Ms Y Phally with her post and benefits preserved, and pay from dismissal to reinstatement; (2) a 3,000 riel monthly lunch allowance for 40 workers; (3) a US$15 monthly travel or housing allowance; (4) a US$50 seniority bonus; (5) deduction of the attendance bonus for leave days; and (6) preservation of wages, attendance bonus and other benefits during certified sick leave. [Para 2][Para 5][Para 7][Para 9][Para 11][Para 13]
Only demand 1 was decided, and only after the claimant changed it at the hearing: the union withdrew the reinstatement claim and asked instead for statutory severance on the ground that Ms Y Phally had committed no serious misconduct. [Para 64][Para 109]
The questions the panel put to itself were:
1. Whether the employer lawfully terminated Ms Y Phally's fixed-duration contract before its expiry date? [Para 109]
2. Whether she committed serious misconduct under article 83 of the Labour Law, so that the early termination required no compensation? [Para 109][Para 111]
3. Whether the employer must pay her termination indemnity, moral damages, payment in lieu of notice, unused annual leave and final wages? [Para 143][Para 144][Para 148][Para 156][Para 159]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** She did no wrong and the dismissal was unlawful, so the employer must pay severance and the statutory termination payments. The transfer to the warehouse was a move to work outside her skill, imposed on her and one other worker only a week after she had spoken about forming a union. The supervisor's account of the warnings changed between conciliation and the hearing: at the Ministry the warnings were said to be oral, at the hearing they were said to be written. [Para 99][Para 101][Para 102][Para 103]
**Employer (respondent):** She repeatedly sewed to the wrong standard operating procedure and produced defective shoes. She was warned orally twice and in writing three times and refused to accept the written warnings. She then refused a lawful temporary transfer, which is serious misconduct under article 11.C(6) of the internal rules. The company had already prepared her final wages and severance, but she did not come to collect them. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 83][Para 93][Para 105][Para 158]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted on the remaining claim — the reinstatement demand having been withdrawn, the panel ordered the employer to pay Ms Y Phally a termination package of five heads: contract-end indemnity, moral damages, pay in lieu of prior notice, payment for unused annual leave and final wages. No figure is stated for any head. [Para 159][Para 160]
*The termination.* Article 73 of the Labour Law allows a fixed-duration contract to be ended before its term only with the agreement of both parties, or in case of serious misconduct or force majeure. The panel accepted that the employer had ended the contract early and that the burden of proving serious misconduct therefore lay on it. [Para 109][Para 110]
*The misconduct.* Article 83(B) lists the conduct that counts as serious: theft, fraud, violence, threats, incitement of others, and gross breach of discipline, safety or hygiene rules. The panel measured the employer's allegations against that list. The written warnings in the file all concerned sewing errors and quality, not the refusal of the transfer: the first was dated 3 June 2013, the second 21 June 2013 and the third 29 June 2013, each annotated "the person refused to accept the warning". [Para 111][Para 112][Para 113][Para 114][Para 85][Para 87][Para 89][Para 86][Para 88][Para 90]
The transfer order of 24 July 2013 was issued only after those warnings and moved her to a section requiring different skills. The panel noted that the employer moved only two workers, and that this happened one week after she had raised the idea of a union in the factory. It found the employer had not shown that she did anything to provoke her own dismissal, and had not shown that it did not dismiss her of its own will. [Para 91][Para 102][Para 103][Para 141][Para 142]
It therefore held that serious misconduct was not proved and that the early termination was unlawful. [Para 143]
*The money.* Because the dismissal was unlawful, the panel ordered moral damages under article 73 of at least the wages she would have earned to the end of the contract. It also ordered ten days' wages in lieu of the notice required by article 73 for a contract of more than six months, which the employer had not given. [Para 143][Para 144][Para 145][Para 146][Para 147]
It further ordered payment for unused annual leave under articles 166 and 167, computed on the average daily wage over the last twelve months under article 168, and final wages under article 116. The employer's offer to let her collect the final wages did not answer the claim, so the panel ordered payment rather than collection. [Para 148][Para 151][Para 154][Para 155][Para 156][Para 157][Para 158][Para 159]
The five other demands in the conciliation report were not ruled on at all. [Para 2][Para 160]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that ends a fixed-duration contract before its term carries the burden of proving serious misconduct as defined in article 83 of the Labour Law; quality-related warnings that the worker refused to sign, followed by a refusal of a transfer to a different skill ordered a week after union organising, do not meet that standard, and the remedy is damages of at least the wages due to the contract's end plus notice pay and accrued leave. [Para 109][Para 110][Para 143][Para 147]

**Obiter dicta:** A temporary transfer said to preserve all benefits is still a change of skill, and the panel treated the choice of the two workers moved as evidence bearing on motive. [Para 91][Para 102][Para 103]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR is degraded and the paragraph numbering restarts inside the reasons. The dismissal date is printed as 27 July 2013 while the resignation notice and the refusal of transfer are both dated 24 July 2013, and the text never reconciles the two. [Para 74][Para 91][Para 96][Para 115]
The parties' accounts of the contract dates are expressly recorded as inconsistent and the panel says neither side produced evidence to settle them. [Para 71][Para 72]
The operative order says the package comprises "3" items but then lists five. [Para 160]
Demand 4 asks for a seniority bonus for the year 2019 in an award issued in 2013. [Para 9]
The worker's name is written both "យ៉ែម ផល្លី" and "យ៉ែម ផល្លី" with the employee number given as both S41-142 and SA1-142. [Para 68][Para 81]
The panel's order is silent on demands 2 to 6 of the conciliation report, so the award disposes of one of six listed disputes without saying so. [Para 2][Para 160]
