# Award No. 169/13 — MH Bio Energy Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 27 September 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chhean presiding; Mar Samborna appointed by the employer; An Non appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 139851

## 1. Facts
MH Bio Energy Co., Ltd (អិម អេច បាយ អូ អេនើជី) produces alcohol and was registered on 6 February 2007, employing about 105 workers. [Para 56][Para 57] In June 2009, during a temporary closure, it moved a group of workers to two subsidiaries, CJ Cambodia and MH Copper Resin, without paying severance and without their earlier service being recognised. [Para 3][Para 26][Para 67] The Khmer Workers' Power Union Federation, holding transfer-of-rights letters from 28 workers dated 19 July 2013, complained on 24 July 2013. [Para 58][Para 102] Conciliation on 9 August 2013 settled one of four demands; a third was settled at the first hearing on 23 August 2013, leaving issues 1 and 2, reheard on 19 September 2013. [Para 15][Para 20][Para 24][Para 25] Of the 28, nine were held to own the complaint. [Para 62]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the two subsidiary companies must recognise the workers' seniority from the date they began work at MH Bio Energy in 2008, or MH Bio Energy must pay severance for the 2008–2009 contract period, the workers having been moved in June 2009 without any severance? [Para 26][Para 59]
2. Whether the employer must transfer the workers back from MH Copper Resin and CJ Cambodia to MH Bio Energy? [Para 26][Para 75]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** they were hired by MH Bio Energy in 2008 and hold its identity cards; the 2009 move was temporary because MH Bio Energy had shut its doors, and it has since reopened, so they ask to return; if the subsidiaries will not recognise their service, MH Bio Energy must pay severance for 2008–2009. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 75][Para 76][Para 77]
**Employer (respondent):** the workers agreed orally to work for the new companies and have taken wages and benefits from them; their MH Bio Energy cards were valid one year and expired in 2009; they are now employees of the subsidiaries; MH Bio Energy no longer needs drivers because it has no transport vehicles; MH Copper Resin does not recognise their service from 2008 and paid no severance. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 78][Para 79][Para 80]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — on issue 1 the demand that MH Copper Resin recognise seniority from 2008 is not considered and the demand that MH Bio Energy pay severance for 2008–2009 is refused; on issue 2 the demand for transfer back is refused. The award is non-binding and becomes final 8 (eight) days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Minister. [Para 116][Para 117][Para 118][Para 119]

On issue 1, of the nine claimants four were employed by CJ Cambodia, which had recognised their service from their first day at MH Bio Energy, so their claims were resolved and closed. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 65][Para 66] The remaining five worked for MH Copper Resin. [Para 67] The panel found that they were serving that company and not MH Bio Energy, and applied Article 664 of the 2007 Civil Code: a labour contract is formed by one party's promise to work and the other's promise to pay wages, and it ends when the worker stops working for the employer and the employer stops paying wages. [Para 83][Para 88][Para 89][Para 90][Para 91] The contract with MH Bio Energy therefore ended in June 2009, when the five stopped working there and MH Bio Energy stopped paying them, and a new contract with MH Copper Resin began from that month. [Para 92][Para 99] Severance thus fell due in June 2009: Article 116 of the Labour Law requires all wages and severance to be paid within 48 hours of the last working day, and Article 120 provides that the right to claim wages lapses three years after the payment date, expressly including severance payable on dismissal. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 96][Para 97] The workers filed only on 24 July 2013, more than three years after that deadline, so their right to severance and related benefits was extinguished. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 104][Para 105][Para 106] The demand addressed to MH Copper Resin was not considered at all, because that company is not a party to the dispute. [Para 86][Para 87]

On issue 2, Article 65 of the Labour Law provides that the labour contract creates the employment relationship between worker and employer. [Para 107][Para 108] The panel held that a change of employer can occur only with the agreement of three parties — the worker, the current employer and the new employer. [Para 109][Para 111] MH Bio Energy refused to take the nine back, and the two subsidiaries could not be consulted because they were not parties and did not appear. [Para 112][Para 113] The workers had no legal basis to demand or compel the transfer of their contracts to MH Bio Energy, nor to compel MH Bio Energy to accept them. [Para 114][Para 115]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Service with a former employer does not carry into a new employer's undertaking unless the new employer agrees to recognise it; where the worker stops working and the employer stops paying wages the contract is terminated at that point, severance falls due within 48 hours under Article 116, and a claim brought more than three years later is time-barred under Article 120. [Para 90][Para 92][Para 97][Para 103] A transfer of an employment contract requires the agreement of the worker, the existing employer and the new employer, and the panel cannot order a former employer to take workers back where it refuses, nor make an order against a company that is not a party. [Para 109][Para 114][Para 87]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the five workers, being in a current employment relationship with MH Copper Resin, should discuss with that company the recognition of the service they gave MH Bio Energy before the transfer. [Para 84][Para 85]
