# Award No. 148/13 — DTV Star Co., Ltd (DiGi) (Arbitration Council, 15 August 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (You Soun Ty for the employer, An Vira for the workers, chair Mae Nimmit) · Doc ID 139832

## 1. Facts
DTV Star Co., Ltd ("DiGi"), an internet service provider registered as Co. 4748 E/2008 on 01 April 2008 in Phnom Penh, employed about 240 staff. [Para 63] [Para 64] The company-level union of the Federation of Union for Khmer Workers' Conscience (សហជីព ស្មារតីកម្មករ ខ្មែរ) was registered on 31 May 2013 under No. 2899 with about 40 members and no dues deducted; the employer says it is not the most representative union there. [Para 66] [Para 69] Of nine demands, eight were left unsettled at conciliation on 03 July 2013 and referred by report No. 844 of 10 July 2013. [Para 27]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must allow staff to take the seven days of additional special leave in one unbroken block? [Para 180]
2. Whether the employer must grant, or pay for, annual leave left unused over the last three years? [Para 201]
3. Whether the employer must pay travel and accommodation allowance of US$7 per month and back-pay it? [Para 210]
4. Whether the employer must pay a seniority bonus to all staff and back-pay it? [Para 227]
5. Whether the employer must fix a clear timetable for wage increases and a rate of increase tied to seniority? [Para 233]
6. Whether the employer must provide one midday meal, or 5,000 riel per day in lieu? [Para 248]
7. Whether the employer must pay a thirteenth month of salary as a share of profits? [Para 254]
8. Whether the employer must replace the cotton-blend work shirts with woven fabric, and with collared shirts? [Para 264] [Para 270]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that Khmer festivals run for many days and staff need travel time, so the additional special days should be usable consecutively; that unused annual leave was cut rather than carried over, so it should be restored or paid; that travel and lodging money, a seniority bonus, a thirteenth month and a midday meal are required by law or Ministry notification; that wage increases are announced but never actually granted; and that the cotton-blend shirts are thick, dry slowly and lack a collar to shade technicians' necks outdoors. [Para 75] [Para 98] [Para 118] [Para 157]

**Employer (respondent):** that the internal rules give seven to ten days of special leave and it adds seven more (three for marriage, four for bereavement) usable at other times, so seven consecutive days is impossible; that it grants nine days of annual leave by roster and carries the rest, capped at three; that travel and lodging money, the seniority bonus and the overtime meal allowance are owed only by the textile, garment and footwear sectors, which it is not; that wage review is discretionary under clause 11 and depends on performance and profit; and that shirt design is a management decision serving advertising. [Para 71] [Para 76] [Para 103] [Para 122] [Para 160]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issues 1 to 7 were dismissed; on issue 8 the panel dismissed the demand for woven fabric but ordered the employer to supply work shirts giving comfort and hygiene within three months. The award is non-binding. [Para 282] [Para 290] [Para 291]

On standing, the federation held delegation letters from the union's leaders and 61 staff; under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, read with Awards 161/09-Prek Treng, 43/10-Meng Chian and 144/12-E-Garment, a representative needs written authorisation, which it had, so it could act for those 61; the award covers the 40 union members and the 61. [Para 177] [Para 178] [Para 179]

On issue 1, Article 171 of the Labour Law and Article 2 of Prakas No. 267 of 11 October 2001 give the employer the right, not a duty, to grant up to seven days of paid special leave a year, and the internal rules give seven to ten; the employer had complied and granted seven further days for marriage and bereavement, and how that extra benefit is scheduled lies in its managerial discretion. [Para 184] [Para 186] [Para 198]

On issue 2, the claimant bears the burden of proof, and the workers identified neither the individuals, the days remaining nor start dates; the employer's leave records were in English, and Article 23 of Prakas 099 makes Khmer the language of the Council, so the panel declined to receive them and, lacking a factual basis, declined to consider the claim. [Para 204] [Para 206] [Para 208]

On issues 3, 4 and 6, Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 (travel and lodging) and Notification No. 041/11 of 07 March 2011 (seniority bonus, overtime meal allowance) limit themselves to the textile, garment and footwear sectors; an internet service provider falls outside them, and no law, collective agreement, internal rule or practice created the duty, so each was an interests dispute. [Para 211] [Para 213] [Para 229] [Para 250]

On issue 5, clause 11.3.1 of the internal rules provides for an annual salary review in December and clause 11.4 makes any increase discretionary, depending on performance and the company's profit or loss; Article 2 of the Labour Law reserves management to the employer within law and reason, so fixing the date, the rate, and keying the rate to seniority did not accord with that article. [Para 234] [Para 239] [Para 246]

On issue 7, Article 103 lists a share of profits among the forms wages may take but imposes no duty to pay it, and no agreement or practice did either, so the thirteenth month was an interests dispute. [Para 255] [Para 258]

On issue 8(a), the fabric of the uniform is a managerial choice under Article 2, and the shirt being thick and slow to dry was not a reasonable ground to compel a change. [Para 265] [Para 268] On issue 8(b), Article 229 requires every establishment to maintain cleanliness and the conditions necessary for workers' health; technicians work outside in the sun and the collarless shirt leaves the neck exposed, a complaint the employer did not contest, so the employer had not met that duty and was ordered to provide suitable shirts within three months. [Para 273] [Para 280] [Para 281]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A Ministry notification fixing an allowance for workers "in the textile, garment and footwear sectors" creates no duty towards an employer in another sector, and a demand for such an allowance is an interests dispute the panel will not decide where the claimant union is not the most representative in the enterprise. An employer's choice of uniform fabric is a managerial prerogative, but Article 229 obliges it to supply clothing meeting the health and comfort needs of the work actually performed, so a uniform leaving outdoor staff exposed breaches that duty. [Para 211] [Para 225] [Para 268] [Para 280]

**Obiter dicta:** that seven further days of special leave for marriage and bereavement are a benefit beyond the statute whose scheduling lies in the employer's discretion; and that leave records submitted only in English will not be received, Khmer being the language of the Council. [Para 197] [Para 206]

## 6. Source defects disclosed

[Para 29] [Para 42] The union's registration number appears as 2899 and once as "4 2899", and its membership is given as about 40 while the award is said to cover 40 members and 61 delegating workers, that figure also appearing as the number of staff who delegated. [Para 173] [Para 179] [Para 65] Prakas 099 is dated 21 April "2019 2004" and the Labour Law is cited as "19967". [Para 219] [Para 185] OCR inserts stray characters into the quoted Article 19 ("អាច មក ១០០"), a bare "ល ។" line stands for Article 2, and single letters ("Li", "John", "L") appear mid-sentence. [Para 176] [Para 191] [Para 233] The employer arbitrator's name is legible in the header but destroyed in the signature block, which reads "Peonny ... kulanchy". [Para 292] Individual workers and union officials are reduced to initials under the briefing convention.
