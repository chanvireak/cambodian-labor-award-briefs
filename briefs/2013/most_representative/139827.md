# Award No. 145/13 — CS Gold Way Textile (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 5 August 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambor Na for the employer party, Sin Kim Sean for the worker party, Kong Pollak chair) · Doc ID 139827

## 1. Facts
CS Gold Way Textile (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a garment factory at Ang Ta Saom, Tram Kak, Takeo, employing about 900 workers. [Para 30] The Labour Union for Cambodia, which has 138 members at the factory and no certificate of most-representative status, filed 14 demands on 17 June 2013; six went un-conciliated, and at the hearing of 18 July 2013 the parties settled issues 2, 3 and 4 by agreement, leaving three. [Para 33][Para 18] The employer pays a minimum of 80 USD a month, and 3.07 USD for a day's work whether the day is ordinary or a public holiday.

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether work performed on a public holiday must be paid at the Sunday rate of 6.14 USD a day, on the footing that holiday work is overtime? [Para 34]
5. Whether the employer must deduct the 10 USD attendance bonus pro rata to the days worked, rather than forfeiting it, once a worker's lateness exceeds 30 minutes in a month? [Para 41]
6. Whether the employer must deduct the attendance bonus pro rata for days of authorised absence for personal reasons, whether or not leave was granted? [Para 48]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** work on a public holiday is overtime and should attract the same 6.14 USD a day the employer already pays for Sunday work, as workers at other Phnom Penh factories receive; the attendance bonus is part of wages under Article 103, so the employer may not withhold it for occasional lateness or for genuine personal leave. [Para 35]
**Employer (respondent):** it already applies the law on holiday pay; the attendance bonus exists to encourage punctual attendance, a worker absent without permission cannot receive it, and the daily wage is kept intact where the lateness is justified and notified in advance. [Para 54]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the holiday-pay claim, the pro-rata attendance-bonus claim and the leave-deduction claim. No sum is awarded and no sum is quantified. [Para 97]

On issue 1, the panel held that working on a public holiday is not overtime. Article 137 caps ordinary hours at eight a day and 48 a week; Article 139 bis gives a 50% uplift for overtime on ordinary days and 100% for night work or weekly rest days; Article 164 and Articles 2 and 4 of Prakas No. 10 of 4 February 1999 instead give a worker who works on a holiday, in addition to wages earned, a compensatory payment equal to one ordinary day's wage. [Para 59][Para 67] Holiday work is therefore like an ordinary day except that the worker may rest without loss of wage, and if he works he earns one extra day on top of his monthly wage; the 150% and 200% overtime rates do not apply, and the 6.14 USD claim found no basis in any law, collective agreement, practice or internal rule. [Para 72] Following Award No. 115/08 the panel classified the claim as an interest dispute, and because the union held no certificate of most-representative status it declined to decide it. [Para 80]

On issue 5, the panel read point 2 of Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 as requiring attendance on every working day at the hours the company sets, so that a worker who arrives late has not attended regularly and does not earn the 10 USD bonus. [Para 83] It found the employer's actual practice more lenient than the notification, since the daily wage is kept where the lateness is justified and notified in advance and the bonus is forfeited only once lateness passes 30 minutes in a month [Para 87]; the workers here had passed that threshold, so the employer was under no obligation to deduct pro rata and the claim failed. [Para 90]

On issue 6, the panel refused to consider the claim at all: the workers never identified a single worker whose bonus had been deducted for personal leave, so there was no claimant whose case could be decided. [Para 95]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Work on a public holiday is not overtime: under Article 164 and Article 4 of Prakas No. 10 the worker receives, in addition to the monthly wage, a compensatory payment equal to one ordinary day's wage, and a claim for the 150% or 200% overtime rate on holidays fails for want of any legal basis. [Para 67] The attendance bonus under point 2 of Notification No. 230 is earned only by a worker who attends on all working days at the scheduled hours; once lateness passes 30 minutes in a month the worker is not regular, the employer may withhold the whole bonus, and it is under no duty to deduct pro rata. [Para 90] A claim that identifies no specific worker affected cannot be decided, even where the practice complained of is described accurately. [Para 95] An interest dispute may be decided only where the union bringing it holds most-representative status. [Para 77]

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that an award on an interest dispute would become a collective agreement for one year, binding all workers and extinguishing their right to strike over the same demands [Para 76], was a general remark on Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 and was not needed once the union was found to lack most-representative status.

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The header lists six un-conciliated claims, but issues 2, 3 and 4 were settled by agreement at the hearing [Para 18], so the panel decided only issues 1, 5 and 6 and the numbering is discontinuous.
- The panel is said to have been constituted on "11 July 2019 2013" [Para 15].
- The lateness threshold is given as "over 300 minutes in a month" at [Para 81] but as 30 minutes in the agreement recorded at [Para 47] and in the decision at [Para 91]; 30 minutes is followed here.
- The summary at [Para 96] records a refusal of the pro-rata claim while in the same sentence ordering the employer to deduct pro rata for 25 days; the operative order at [Para 99] refuses, and that is followed.
- The award is expressly non-binding, becoming final eight days after notification if no party objects [Para 100].
- The union's name appears in three spellings in the header, and the employer as both "CS Gold Way Textile (Cambodia) Co., Ltd" and its Khmer transliteration; no individual worker is identified anywhere in the award, so all are redacted in this brief.
