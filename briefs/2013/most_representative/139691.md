# Award No. 91/13 — Jean Ping Garmen Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 4 June 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Nan, appointed by the workers; Kong Phallha, president appointed by both) · Doc ID 139691

## 1. Facts
Jean Ping Garmen Co., Ltd. is a garment factory at Stueng Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employing about 700 workers; the claimant is the Union for Workers of Cambodia (សហជីពដើម្បីកម្មករកម្ពុជា), holding registration No. 2880 K.B/V.K. of 03 May 2013 but no most-representative certificate. [Para 32][Para 34] On 12 March 2013 the Labour Disputes Department received the union's 13 demands; conciliation on 25 April 2013 failed on all 13 and they were referred to the panel on 06 May 2013. [Para 19][Para 20] At the hearing of 10 May 2013 the workers attended but the employer did not appear and gave no reason, so the panel proceeded in absentia under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 23][Para 81][Para 83] The award is non-binding and becomes final if no party objects within 08 days. [Para 24]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must stop forcing workers to use their annual leave when the power fails? [Para 2][Para 35]
2. Whether the company must pay a 2,000 riel meal allowance for Sunday work? [Para 3][Para 37]
3. Whether the company must pay a 4,000 riel meal allowance for work on public holidays? [Para 4][Para 41]
4. Whether the company must let workers take special leave for personal or family events, deducted from annual leave, without cutting the attendance bonus? [Para 5][Para 43]
5. Whether the company must allow QC workers to sit down during working hours when there is no work? [Para 6][Para 45]
6. Whether the company must pay a USD 5 monthly lunch allowance? [Para 7][Para 50]
7. Whether the company must provide an adequately stocked infirmary with a permanent doctor until all workers have left? [Para 8][Para 52]
8. Whether the company must pay an additional 500 riel per hour of meal allowance for overtime? [Para 9][Para 165]
9. Whether the company must keep the toilets clean and supplied with soap? [Para 10][Para 57]
10. Whether the company must stop deducting 1,000 riel from workers who are not members of the Free Workers Union? [Para 11][Para 60]
11. Whether the company must pay USD 10 per month for travel and accommodation instead of USD 7? [Para 12][Para 64]
12. Whether the company must set up a nursery (ទារកដ្ឋាន) or pay USD 10 per month in milk money? [Para 13][Para 66]
13. Whether the company must grant leave on request where the worker has a valid reason? [Para 14][Para 69]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** power cuts happen about twice a year and last half a day, and the company sends workers home on annual leave though the outage is no fault of theirs; it pays no meal allowance for Sunday or holiday work, no lunch allowance, and only USD 7 travel money; the QC section must stand from 07:00 to 18:00, there is no infirmary or doctor, the toilets smell, and supervisors tear up leave forms. [Para 36][Para 45][Para 59][Para 73]
**Employer (respondent):** absent; the award records that no response was filed on any issue because the employer did not attend. [Para 74][Para 141]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — eight of the thirteen claims were granted, four were dismissed and one dismissed for want of evidence. [Para 211][Para 222][Para 225]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 170: annual leave is in principle taken at the Khmer New Year, and taking it at another time requires agreement plus prior notice to the labour inspector. Following Award No. 21/05-SINOMEK and No. 53/04-KONG HONG, it held that an employer may not use annual leave to cover a period when the factory has no work, and ordered the company to stop coercing workers. [Para 87][Para 95][Para 99]

On issue 2 it held that Sunday work is overtime, ordinary hours being 8 per day and 48 per week from Monday to Saturday under Article 137; point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 K.B/S.C.N. of 07 March 2011 entitles a volunteer for overtime at the employer's request to 2,000 riel per day of meal allowance or a free meal, regardless of hours worked. [Para 100][Para 106][Para 108]

On issues 3, 6, 8 and 11 it found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement or other agreement obliging the employer to pay the sums claimed, so each was an interest dispute; applying the rule that only a most-representative union may bring one — following Award No. 169/11-FORTUNE and No. 02/11-PO YUEN — and noting the claimant held no certificate, it declined to consider them. [Para 111][Para 121][Para 183]

On issue 4 it applied Article 171 and Prakas No. 267 SKBY of 11 October 2001: special leave of up to seven paid days a year is available for personal or family events, and the employer may deduct it from annual leave not yet taken, or require repayment once annual leave is exhausted. [Para 122][Para 125][Para 131]

On issue 5, Article 2 gives the employer the right to organise work, including requiring QC staff to stand to check quality, but only where lawful and reasonable; the employer being absent, the panel accepted the workers' account and found no justification for keeping them standing with nothing to do. [Para 141][Para 143][Para 144]

On issue 7, Article 242 requires an enterprise with at least 50 workers to keep a permanent infirmary with a doctor and nurse; Prakas No. 08 SKA of 19 January 1994 and No. 330 SKBY of 06 December 2000 set staffing by headcount and place the cost on the employer. With 700 workers and no infirmary, the panel ordered one plus adequate medicine. [Para 148][Para 155][Para 164]

On issue 9 it applied Article 229 and Prakas No. 052 SKBY of 10 February 2000, requiring clean toilets with sufficient soap. [Para 168][Para 177] On issue 10 it dismissed the claim for lack of the lists and letters the union was to file by 16 May 2013. [Para 63][Para 220] On issue 12, Article 186 requires an enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a breastfeeding room and nursery, or pay for care elsewhere on receipts. [Para 184][Para 222] On issue 13 the supervisors' refusal to sign, and tearing up of, leave forms meant no lawful procedure was applied. [Para 206][Para 224]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer, properly summoned, fails to appear without good reason, the panel may proceed in absentia under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 and will accept the claimant's factual assertions — but that concession reaches only claims founded on the Labour Law, a Prakas or an existing agreement. A claim for a benefit not yet granted by any of those remains an interest dispute and cannot be decided at the suit of a union without most-representative status. [Para 83][Para 111][Para 120][Para 141]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the refused meal allowances and other benefits could still be agreed between the parties even though it could not order them. [Para 107][Para 121]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The union's membership is printed as "ប្រហែល ២ ២០០ នាក់" (about 2,200) at [Para 34] against a workforce of about 700 [Para 32]; the figure cannot be right.
- Issue 8's amount varies: 1,500 riel per hour from 17:00 in the demand [Para 9][Para 54], "១៩៥០០ រៀល" in the same line, and 500 riel per hour from 16:00 in the reasons and order [Para 165][Para 218].
- The number "19" appears where no count belongs — "allow special leave to 19 workers" [Para 131] and "គ្មាន ១៩ ភស្តុតាង" [Para 179] — but is absent from the operative order [Para 214]; treated as OCR noise.
- The panel's constitution is dated "ថ្ងៃទី ០៧ ខែឧសភា ឆ្នាំ ២០២៣ ២០១៣" with a duplicated year at [Para 21].
- [Para 133] and [Para 200] contain untranslated English OCR fragments mid-sentence; the reasoning there survives only in the Khmer.
