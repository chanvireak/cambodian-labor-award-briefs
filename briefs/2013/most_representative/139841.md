# Award No. 157/13 — Asia Dragon (Garment) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 21 August 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Tuon Si Phan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · dissenting opinion by Ing Sothy annexed · Doc ID 139841

## 1. Facts
Asia Dragon (Garment) Co., Ltd, a garment factory in Bati district, Takeo, employing about 2,000 workers, faced seventeen demands. [Para 36][Para 37] Fifteen were settled in conciliation on 25 July 2013 and the second of the two remaining was withdrawn at the hearing, leaving one. [Para 10][Para 16] The surviving claim is narrow: the employer pays a USD 10 monthly attendance bonus, and cuts USD 5 for any authorised personal leave taken in the first half of a month and USD 5 for any such leave in the second half; the workers want the cut made proportionate to the days actually taken. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reduce the attendance bonus in proportion to the number of days a worker is absent with the employer's authorisation, rather than by a flat USD 5 per half-month? [Para 46]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** A worker who has already obtained the employer's permission to be absent should not then lose a flat half of the bonus; other factories including SL 1, SL 2, Hour Thyun, Gold Flame and New Oriend calculate the cut pro rata, and the flat cut is unjust where monthly wages are already low. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53]
**Employer (respondent):** It applies an agreement of 28 January 2013 concluded with the Khmer Youth Union, the most representative union in the factory, whose Article 12 fixes a USD 5 cut for absences between the 1st and 15th and USD 5 between the 16th and month-end; it already preserves the full bonus for sick leave and pays wages for annual and special leave. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 73]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to reduce the attendance bonus in proportion to the days of authorised absence. The order is non-binding and becomes final after 8 days without objection. [Para 143][Para 145]

The panel first settled standing. The factory's C.CAWDU union held only a receipt dated 6 August 2013 acknowledging its registration application, not a registration certificate, so under Article 268 it could not represent members. [Para 41][Para 74][Para 77] But 560 workers had thumbprinted a power of attorney dated 24 July 2013 in favour of the confederation president, and two of them, Sa Chhay and Eou Siha, attended in person; the claimants are therefore those 560 plus those two. [Para 43][Para 87][Para 88]

On the merits the panel applied Notification 230 of 25 July 2012, point 2 of which gives a worker who comes to work regularly for the working days of each month "without absence" a bonus of at least USD 10. [Para 91][Para 94] It held that notification to be a general norm binding every employer in the textile, garment and footwear sector, not a discretionary reward the employer may set as it pleases. [Para 94] It then found the phrase "without absence" unresolved: the Labour Advisory Committee has not said whether it covers authorised as well as unauthorised absence, and the panel declined to presume either meaning. [Para 97][Para 98][Para 102] Absence permitted by law — annual, special, maternity, public holiday and weekly rest — does not entail any cut. [Para 99][Para 100] Absence authorised by the employer at the worker's request is a suspension of the contract under Article 71(6), which under Article 72(1) removes the wage obligation for those days and, the bonus being a form of wage under Article 103, removes the bonus for those days too. [Para 118][Para 121][Para 124]

The panel therefore concluded that the employer may not cut the bonus entirely, only by the amount corresponding to the days actually absent. [Para 126][Para 127] It measured the employer's practice against Article 13, under which Labour Law provisions are public order and any unilateral rule, contract or collective agreement less favourable is void, while more favourable terms remain valid. [Para 133][Para 136][Para 140] The 28 January 2013 agreement's flat USD 5 halves fell below that standard, so the pro rata order followed. [Para 142][Para 143]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a ministerial notification fixes a minimum attendance bonus and does not define "absence", the employer may not withhold the whole bonus for authorised absence: the deduction must be limited to the days of absence, because an authorised absence suspends the contract and removes the wage only for those days, and any agreement providing a harsher cut is void under Article 13. [Para 102][Para 124][Para 136]

**Obiter dicta:** That an unrecognised union may not represent its members even where a registration application is pending, and that the panel will not fill a gap left by the Labour Advisory Committee's silence, were stated in passing rather than as grounds of the order. [Para 80][Para 97]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is not unanimous: arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented, treating the bonus as a reward whose conditions belong to the employer, so that a worker who does not meet them receives nothing. [Para 148][Para 156][Para 167] The notification at the centre of the case is cited as No. 041 of 7 March 2011 setting USD 7 and as No. 230 of 25 July 2012 setting USD 10, the first having been repealed. [Para 89][Para 91] Notification 230 is dated 25 July 2013 in one passage. [Para 142] The panel's constitution date prints as "1 August 2023 2013". [Para 12] The bonus figure appears once as "4 10 dollars". [Para 128] The employer's name is rendered variously as Asia Dragon (Garmen), (Gamen) and (Cambodia). [Para 18][Para 36] Union membership is given as 134 by the workers and 1,800 by the employer. [Para 40][Para 45]
