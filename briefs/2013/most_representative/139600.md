# Award No. 08/13 — Chevron (Cambodia) Limited (Arbitration Council, 14 February 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ri, employer-appointed; An Nan, worker-appointed; Tang Tri, chair) · non-binding award · Doc ID 139600

## 1. Facts
Chevron (Cambodia) Limited, registered in 1995 and trading in petroleum products, employed 535 staff at retail fuel stations. The claimant was the Workers' Democratic Union of Chevron, affiliated to the Cambodian Food and Service Workers' Federation (CFSWF), with 249 members and no Most Representative Status (MRS). Three demands survived conciliation: conversion of repeated fixed duration contracts to undetermined duration, a US$100 increase in the annual health allowance, and a US$131 monthly basic wage. [Para 1][Para 87][Para 89][Para 90][Para 2]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must sign undetermined duration contracts for workers whose successive fixed duration contracts total two years or more? [Para 2][Para 91]
2. Whether the employer must add US$100 to the current annual health allowance of all workers? [Para 4][Para 115]
3. Whether the employer must pay a minimum basic wage of US$131 a month to all workers? [Para 6][Para 126]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Fixed duration contracts were renewed repeatedly without the employer giving notice at the end of each one, so their total duration ran past two years; the health allowance was inadequate for treatment and the fuel-station environment harmed workers' health, and workers received only US$100, US$200 or US$300 a year by category; and a 2011 Ministry of Planning study put the living cost of one person in Phnom Penh at US$131, which the basic wage of US$70 for cleaning staff and US$75 for filling staff did not meet. [Para 91][Para 115][Para 117][Para 126][Para 128][Para 131]
**Employer (respondent):** It had no policy of converting contracts, because hiring and renewal rested on the parties' will and it contracted in accordance with the Labour Law; it gave 15 to 20 days' notice through station managers, by telephone, posted board notices and meetings; it could not raise the health allowance because no law made that its duty, and seven Phnom Penh hospitals were partners through which staff obtained treatment billed to the company; and it could not pay US$131 because no rule fixed a minimum wage for the petroleum sector. [Para 92][Para 97][Para 100][Para 123][Para 158][Para 172]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to sign undetermined duration contracts with union members whose fixed duration contracts had been renewed more than once and whose total duration exceeded two years; the health allowance and US$131 wage demands were declined. The award was declared non-binding. [Para 177][Para 180]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 67(2) of the Labour Law, under which a fixed duration contract agreed by the parties may not last longer than two years, and Article 73(5), under which, absent prior notice, the contract is renewed for an equal term or is deemed to be of undetermined duration where the total duration of renewals exceeds the Article 67 limit. It read the Article 67(2) reference as going to the total maximum period, not to each renewal, and followed its own Award No. 10/03 on the same point. It also treated ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982, paragraph 13 — that fixed term contracts should not be used for long-term work and should become undetermined where renewals exceed two years — as a non-binding but useful aid to interpretation. [Para 138][Para 141][Para 142][Para 146][Para 147][Para 149]

The panel noted that the Labour Law leans toward undetermined duration contracts because they give workers job security, while long service also benefits the employer by encouraging commitment; it found as fact that some workers' contracts had been renewed more than once and totalled more than two years, and that the employer's notices did not alter the character of those contracts. The employer's argument that renewal rested on consent did not answer the statutory consequence, since consent to renew does not show consent to the fixed term itself. [Para 143][Para 144][Para 150][Para 152][Para 153]

On issue 2 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, no collective agreement and no contract fixing a health allowance, so the demand was an interests dispute to be decided in equity; because an interests award under clause 43 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 stands in place of a collective agreement for one year and binds all workers, only a union with MRS, or one with more than half the workforce in membership, may bring it (Awards No. 81/04, 98/04, 169/11, 02/11, 66/11). With 249 members in a workforce of 535 the union lacked MRS, so the panel declined to consider the demand. [Para 158][Para 160][Para 165][Para 167][Para 169][Para 171]

On issue 3 the panel likewise found no provision fixing a minimum wage for workers in the petroleum sector and no collective agreement or contract on the point, so the demand was an interests dispute and was declined for the same lack of MRS. [Para 172][Para 173][Para 174][Para 176]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed duration contract renewed more than once, so that the total duration of the successive contracts exceeds two years, becomes a contract of undetermined duration by operation of Articles 67(2) and 73(5) of the Labour Law, whatever the form of the renewal notices and whatever the worker's consent to each renewal; only a union with MRS, or one representing more than half the workforce, may bring an interests dispute, including one over an allowance or wage that no law, agreement or contract fixes. [Para 141][Para 146][Para 150][Para 167]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that job security under undetermined contracts serves the employer's interest by encouraging commitment, and that ILO Recommendation 166 is persuasive though not binding on Cambodian employers. [Para 144][Para 149]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The source is the Khmer original only, with no English version, and the party names are transliterated inconsistently: the employer appears as "ឈេហ្វរ៉ុន", "ឈហ្វរ៉ុន", "ឈេហូរ៉ុន" and "លេហ្វរ៉ុន", while exhibits are contracts with "CALTEX CAMBODIA LIMITED", an earlier name for the same station network. The demand is US$100 in the issue list but the operative paragraph 178 prints "១០០០ ដុល្លារ" (US$1,000), and paragraph 179 prints "១៥ ១៣១" against US$131 elsewhere. Paragraph 150 cites "មាត្រា ៦៧៨" for Article 67(2). Individual worker names in the contract exhibits are redacted here. [Para 1][Para 4][Para 150][Para 178][Para 179]
