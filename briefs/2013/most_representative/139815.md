# Award No. 122/13 — S.H. International Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 25 July 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 139815

## 1. Facts
S.H. International Co., Ltd. is a garment factory in Chom Chao, Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh, employing about 700 workers. [Para 54] The claimants are the factory's two local unions of the Cambodian Labour Union Federation, one holding most representative status there. [Para 55][Para 57] A collective bargaining agreement registered No. 410 of 13 November 2012 runs from 1 November 2012 to 1 November 2014 and covers all workers except administrative and senior managerial staff. [Para 58][Para 60] On 31 May 2013, the expiry date of their three-month fixed-term contracts, three union officers thumbprinted severance payments and were not re-engaged; ten demands followed. [Para 208]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the three union leaders, reinstate them and pay back wages from termination? [Para 178]
2. Whether the employer must raise the base wage by US$20 for all workers? [Para 213]
3. Whether the employer must add US$10 to the existing US$10 attendance bonus? [Para 229]
4. Whether the employer must pay 5,000 riel per day in lieu of lunch? [Para 237]
5. Whether the employer must add US$5 to the existing US$8 accommodation and transport allowance? [Para 249]
6. Whether the employer must add 2,000 riel per hour to the overtime meal allowance? [Para 256]
7. Whether the employer must pay a US$1 per day target bonus in every section? [Para 263]
9. Whether the employer must let workers leave at 4 p.m. and reach the clinic during working hours without a slip from the team, section and production chiefs? [Para 271]
10. Whether the employer must maintain wages and benefits during the strike of 11 June 2013? [Para 332]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the three were union president and assistants, so non-renewal was union discrimination and they should return with back pay [Para 62][Para 179]; the money demands rest on rising prices and on what factories such as Yak Jin, SL, YTC and Gladpeer pay [Para 111][Para 120]; the lunch payment and the US$1 target bonus are claimed for equity, the sewing section alone receiving the target bonus [Para 246][Para 268].
**Employer (respondent):** the contracts reached their term, the employer did not know the three were union officers, and a contract under six months needs no notice [Para 66][Para 181]; every money demand exceeds the law and the collective agreement, which cannot be varied during its term [Para 226]; the permission slip controls how many workers take overtime so production is not disrupted [Para 282].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issue 9 was allowed in part: the employer must stop applying Option B of Article 4 of the fixed-term contract and must let workers reach the clinic on the team chief's permission alone; Issues 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 4 and 7 were rejected. No money was ordered. [Para 350][Para 351][Para 355]

Issue 1 turned first on discrimination. Articles 12 and 279 forbid using union membership or activity as a ground for discipline or termination [Para 182][Para 183], but the claimant carries the burden of proof [Para 186], and the panel found no evidence the employer knew the union composition before 31 May 2013: the notification bore no receipt signature and the EMS receipt copy was too badly stained to read. [Para 194][Para 198][Para 200] On legality, Article 73(1) makes a fixed-term contract expire at its term and Article 73(5) requires notice only above six months, so the three-month non-renewal was lawful. [Para 201][Para 206][Para 212]

Issue 2 was a rights dispute because Article 14 of the agreement fixes the minimum wage. [Para 213] The employer paid US$85, above the US$80 required by Notification No. 103 of 9 April 2013 and far above the agreement's US$61 [Para 221][Para 223], and Article 30 bars either party from varying its terms mid-term without consent. [Para 226][Para 228] Issue 3 failed the same way: Article 16 and point 2 of Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 set a US$10 attendance bonus, which was paid. [Para 230][Para 236] Issue 5 failed on Article 18 and point 1 of the same Notification, requiring US$7 against the US$8 paid [Para 250][Para 255], and Issue 6 on Article 7 and point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011, setting a 2,000 riel daily meal payment for voluntary overtime. [Para 257][Para 262]

Issues 4 and 7 were interests disputes, reachable on equity because the factory union holds most representative status. [Para 239][Para 244][Para 245] The panel refused both: other factories' practice was the only reason for the lunch payment [Para 246][Para 248], and the target bonus claim rested only on matching the sewing section. [Para 268][Para 270]

Issue 9 was a rights dispute, but attendance control under Article 2 must still be reasonable. [Para 272][Para 317] Requiring an unwell worker to obtain slips from three chiefs before reaching the clinic was unreasonable; workers who could not wait went to the toilet instead, harming their welfare. [Para 325][Para 329] The standing two-hour daily overtime in Option B of Article 4 also contradicted Articles 137 and the voluntariness rule in Clause 4 of Prakas No. 80, since overtime must answer abnormal pressure of work, not a permanent schedule. [Para 290][Para 293][Para 306][Para 312]

Issue 10 failed on Article 332: the contract is suspended during a strike and wages are not paid, whether the strike is lawful or not. [Para 335][Para 341][Para 344]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-term contract of under six months expires at its term without notice, and non-renewal is lawful under Article 73 unless the worker proves the employer knew of the union role and acted on it; an unreadable postal receipt and an unsigned notification do not discharge that burden. [Para 186][Para 199][Para 211] While a collective agreement is in force neither party may obtain a wage or bonus increase by arbitration, because Article 30 requires mutual consent to vary its terms — what the agreement and the Ministry notification already secure answers the demand, not equity. [Para 226][Para 234] An employer's attendance rules are struck down as unreasonable where they obstruct urgent access to first aid, and standing daily overtime written into a contract is void for contradicting the voluntary principle in Prakas No. 80. [Para 293][Para 307][Para 329]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that three-chief permission serves the legitimate interest in attendance control and workplace order, made before it found the practice unreasonable for unwell workers; and its general statement that workers may not be forced or disciplined for declining overtime. [Para 324][Para 297]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- Khmer-language original, no English version in the library; arbitrator names are transliterated here from the Khmer script (ឈីវ ភិរម្យ, លីវ សុវណ្ណា, គង់ ផល្ហៈ), and the chair's name is printed again in damaged form in the signature block. [Para 357]
- The dispositive on Issue 9 both rejects the demand and orders relief — clinic access on the team chief's permission only — so the outcome is recorded as partly granted. [Para 331][Para 351]
- The attendance bonus finding says the employer pays "100 dollars" a month [Para 232] while the agreement and the reasons set US$10; the US$10 figure was used.
- Stray numerals corrupt the text: "2016" inside the finding on most representative status [Para 244] and in the overtime reasoning [Para 306]; "4" inserted in the rejection of Issue 1 [Para 212]; the award date is printed as deferred from "09 July 2019 2013" to 25 July 2013 [Para 28].
- The panel once calls itself the "National Arbitration Council" [Para 190], and the union appears both as "Union of Power of Cambodian Workers" and as a CLUF local union [Para 55][Para 56].
- Issue 8 (repair of the water pump) was settled in conciliation at the hearing and is absent from the reasons and the order. [Para 23]
