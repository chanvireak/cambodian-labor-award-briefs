# Award No. 246/13 — Kalington Cambodia Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 5 December 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tay Seng appointed by the employer party, Liv Sovanna appointed by the worker party, Pen Bunhea chair appointed by both) · Doc ID 139898

## 1. Facts
Kalington Cambodia Co., Ltd runs a shoe factory at Roung Veng, Khna Rorng Dam Bang, Cheung Prey, Kampong Cham, employing about 864 workers. [Para 31][Para 32] The Labour Union for Cambodia, registered with the Ministry on 12 November 2013 under No. 3057, filed fourteen demands; conciliation narrowed them to four, and the panel's own further conciliation on 27 November 2013 settled two of those, leaving two claims: a travel and lodging allowance of USD 12 a month against the USD 7 paid, and a base wage of USD 120 against the USD 80 paid. [Para 25][Para 13][Para 18][Para 19] The employer said it had no capacity to pay more and asked that the law be applied as written. [Para 40][Para 46]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a travel and lodging allowance of USD 12 a month instead of the USD 7 it pays? [Para 37]
2. Whether the employer must pay a base (minimum) wage of USD 120 a month instead of the USD 80 it pays? [Para 41][Para 63]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the USD 7 allowance does not meet the actual cost of transport, which averages USD 10 a month; the USD 80 base wage is too small to cover daily needs, market prices keep rising, and tax deducted from monthly wages reduces take-home pay further. [Para 39][Para 43][Para 44][Para 45]
**Employer (respondent):** it has no ability to pay anything above what it already pays, and asks the panel to apply the legal minimum only. [Para 40][Para 46]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider (បដិសេធ ពិចារណា) both claims and ordered no payment. [Para 70][Para 71]

On the travel and lodging claim the panel found that point 1 of Ministry Notification No. 230/12 of 25 July 2012 fixes the allowance at USD 7 (ប្រាំពីរ) a month and that the employer pays exactly that. [Para 46][Para 48] It found no provision of the labour law, no collective convention, no agreement, no past practice and no internal rule obliging the employer to add a further USD 5, and concluded that the demand was therefore an interest dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍) rather than a claim of right. [Para 49][Para 50]

That characterisation carried a jurisdictional consequence. Article 96 of the Labour Law defines the collective convention and those who may conclude it, and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 restricts the right to request one to the most representative union, with which the employer is then obliged to negotiate. [Para 51][Para 53] Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 provides that an award deciding an interest dispute replaces the collective convention and binds for one year, so that all workers lose the right to claim the same benefit in future. [Para 55][Para 56] The panel therefore held that it may decide an interest dispute only where the union bringing it is the most representative in the enterprise, or where several unions together represent more than half of the workers. [Para 57] It followed its earlier awards No. 81/04-Ever Green (issue 4) and No. 98/04-Great Union (issue 3), and the decisions in No. 169/11 (issue 5), No. 02/11-Pou Yuen (issue 2) and No. 66/11-Inhansung (issue 1), where interest claims were refused on the same ground. [Para 57][Para 58]

On the facts the panel found the claimant union was not the most representative union in the enterprise, so it lacked the legal capacity to bring an interest dispute. [Para 36][Para 60][Para 61] It rejected the travel and lodging claim on that basis. [Para 62][Para 69]

The wage claim failed the same way. Point 2 of Ministry Notification No. 103 of 09 April 2013 sets the minimum at USD 75 for workers in probation and USD 80 once probation ends, and the employer pays USD 80. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65] The panel found no law, collective convention, agreement, practice or internal rule requiring a further USD 40, held the demand to be an interest dispute, and declined to consider it for the same want of representative status. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 69]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that is not the most representative in the enterprise, and that does not combine with others to represent more than half of the workforce, cannot put an interest (benefit) dispute to the panel at all: an award on such a dispute displaces the collective convention for one year and extinguishes every worker's right to raise the same benefit, so the power is reserved to the union empowered to conclude a collective convention. A demand for more than the amount fixed by the applicable Prakas or Ministry notification, where the employer already pays that amount and no contract, practice or internal rule promises more, is an interest dispute and not a rights dispute; a non-representative union's claim therefore fails without any examination of the employer's capacity to pay. [Para 50][Para 56][Para 57][Para 66]

**Obiter dicta:** the observations that the union's registration under Ministry letter No. 3057 of 12 November 2013 was lawful and complete, and that lawful registration does not by itself confer most-representative status, are general remarks on standing rather than findings needed to reject either claim. [Para 35][Para 36]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The panel numbers its two surviving issues inconsistently: the procedural narrative says the unresolved points are issues 5 and 12 [Para 19], while the reasoning and the operative order call them issues 1 and 11 [Para 37][Para 41][Para 70][Para 71]. The subject matter is clear in each case; the numbers are not reconcilable.
- The summary paragraph states that the panel rejects the workers' claim **and** orders the employer to pay the USD 12 allowance [Para 62], contradicting the operative order at [Para 70] which declines it. The sentence appears to conflate two drafts and was read against the order.
- The date of the panel's constitution is printed twice: "15 November 2021 2013" [Para 15]; a stray "២០១៥" and "២០១៧" surface in the reasons at [Para 58] and [Para 63].
- The respondent appears as ខាលីង តុន, កាលីង តុង, ខាលីតុង and Kalington Cambodia Co., Ltd [Para 1][Para 31]; one certificate of registration in the file belongs to a different company, "ទ្រី លាន អេផិក លីមីធីត" [Para 21].
- Sentences are cut mid-thought: "Arbitration Council, 27 November 2013" stands alone at [Para 33]; [Para 44] opens with a dropped "០០"; [Para 45] opens with "of"; [Para 47] and [Para 52] contain only ".." and "..៕".
- The Council's letterhead and contact block are spliced into the middle of the workers' representatives list [Para 1][Para 3], and the employer-party arbitrator's name in the signature block is lost to "ប៊ុណ . ALL Caver" [Para 72].
- Notification No. 103 as quoted is internally inconsistent: it computes USD 75 from a base of 56 plus 14 plus 5, and USD 80 from a base of 61 plus 14 plus 5 [Para 63][Para 64].
