# Award No. 58/13 — Radio and Television Station of the Royal Cambodian Armed Forces, Channel 5 (TV5) (Arbitration Council, 10 May 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Seng Vouchhun (employer-appointed), Tuon Siphan (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (president) · Doc ID 139676

## 1. Facts
TV5, the radio and television station of the Royal Cambodian Armed Forces, employed 120 workers at the time of the award; the Solidarity Union of Channel 5, registered as No. 23 Kb/Vk on 28 November 2011 with about 90 members, was certified as the most representative union on 4 December 2012. [Para 40][Para 58][Para 60][Para 61] Of fourteen demands, twelve were settled in conciliation at the hearing, leaving the thirteenth-month payment and the status of six stage-building (ជាងរៀបឆាក) workers. [Para 22][Para 28][Para 29] Those six had worked for the station for years, the shortest four years, without any written contract. [Para 86][Para 130][Para 131]

## 2. Issues in dispute
2. Whether the employer must resume the thirteenth-month payment that it made from 1995 until the end of 2010? [Para 62]
5. Whether the six workers in the stage-building team must be recognised as permanent employees entitled to the same rights and benefits as other permanent staff? [Para 71][Para 116]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** All workers used to receive the thirteenth-month payment and it should continue; the six stage-building workers do the same hours as everyone else — four to five days a week, eight hours a day, with fingerprint scanning — yet receive less because they get no fuel allowance of 30 US dollars a month. [Para 62][Para 72][Para 76][Para 77]
**Employer (respondent):** The payment was never a thirteenth-month salary but a discretionary incentive varying with the company's profit and each worker's output, discontinued at the end of 2010 because the station made no profit; the six are hired as a group on contract only when stage-building work arises, so they are not permanent staff. [Para 66][Para 68][Para 78][Para 82]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the thirteenth-month claim was dismissed, and the employer was ordered to recognise the six stage-building workers as permanent employees with the duties, rights and benefits of other permanent staff; the award is not binding and becomes final eight days after notification. [Para 139][Para 140][Para 141][Para 143]

On the thirteenth-month claim the panel found, and both parties accepted, that the money paid between 1995 and 2010 was an incentive tied to the station's profits and to individual productivity rather than a thirteenth-month salary, and that the amounts differed from worker to worker. [Para 63][Para 65][Para 66][Para 89] It treated the long, consistent payment as a past practice under article 65 of the Labour Law, which recognises unwritten terms arising from established conduct. [Para 93][Para 98][Para 100] Following Awards 95/09 and 48/09, it held that a past practice may lawfully end where the parties so agree, where they adopt a new arrangement, or where the circumstances underlying it change. [Para 106][Para 107] Because the employer stopped the payment at the end of 2010 and the workers raised no objection at the time, the panel found tacit acceptance of the change and dismissed the claim. [Para 112][Para 114][Para 115]

On the stage-building team the panel applied article 9 of the Labour Law, which distinguishes permanent workers, who perform a permanent job regularly, from temporary workers, whose work is not stable and is intermittent or seasonal. [Para 123][Para 124][Para 126][Para 127] It found the six worked every week, four to five days a week and eight hours a day, sometimes seven days, for at least four years each. [Para 128][Para 132][Para 134] It also found that although the employer paid them as a lump-sum team and gave no fuel allowance of 30 US dollars a month, it paid them monthly alongside permanent staff and required the same fingerprint attendance. [Para 84][Para 87][Para 121] Since stage-building is a standing function of the station rather than occasional or seasonal work, the panel held the six to be permanent employees and ordered their recognition. [Para 136][Para 137][Para 138][Para 139]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A long-standing gratuitous payment that the employer discontinues, and that the workers accept without protest at the time, ceases to be an enforceable past practice; but where workers perform a permanent function regularly over years, they are permanent employees under article 9 of the Labour Law even without a written contract or a job title to that effect. [Para 114][Para 133][Para 136]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that past practice is a legal term of art allowing it to review what actually happens in an enterprise, and that some practices — those belonging to the employer's own direction of the production chain — do not become binding past practice at all. [Para 92][Para 103]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The file is heavily corrupted. Paragraph 24 records the panel as constituted "30" on 18 March 2013, and paragraph 30 postpones the award from 5 April to "10 September May 2013", a date that does not exist. [Para 24][Para 30] The number of claimants shifts between six and sixty, including in the operative order itself, and the workforce figure appears as 120 despite the six-person team being the subject of the claim. [Para 58][Para 71][Para 139] The table of the six workers' names and start dates is scrambled across columns, so individual start dates cannot be reliably matched to names. [Para 72] The station is named in several ways, including "ខ.ភ.ម" and a form with an inserted "ម៉ូស្ត៏", and one quoted letter is dated to "ថ្ងៃទី" with the day missing. [Para 1][Para 41] The claim count is given as fourteen unresolved points but fifteen workers' demands are enumerated in the source. [Para 22][Para 23]
