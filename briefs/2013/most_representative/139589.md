# Award No. 02/13 — G4S Security Services (Cambodia) Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 8 February 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Run Saray, chair) · Doc ID 139589

## 1. Facts
The company provides private security services and employed 730 staff, of whom 175 belonged to the Cambodian Service Workers' Union at the enterprise. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 60] Mr P S, a security guard engaged on 13 December 2011 under an open-ended written contract at a base wage of 75 USD plus a 10 USD allowance, was also deputy president of that union. [Para 62][Para 63] The company terminated him on 1 November 2012 without prior notice, citing three occasions of sleeping on duty. [Para 64][Para 65] Two unsettled demands reached the panel: his reinstatement as a victim of union discrimination, and four monthly benefits for all guards. [Para 8][Para 13][Para 62][Para 91]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the dismissal of Mr P S was connected to union discrimination, contrary to Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law? [Para 113]
2. Whether that dismissal was carried out in accordance with the employer's internal rules? [Para 113][Para 123]
3. Whether the union's demand for a 10 USD attendance bonus, 15 USD health payment, 5 USD seniority payment and 15 USD travel or accommodation payment per month is an interests dispute that the panel may hear? [Para 134][Para 137]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissal was union discrimination because Mr P S was the union's deputy president and the employer had reassigned six union members, including him, on 26 November 2012; union security guards deserve the same benefits as factory workers. [Para 75][Para 91][Para 92]
**Employer (respondent):** it did not know he held a union post; it dismissed him under Article 6 of the internal rules after three sleep incidents, and monthly post rotation is normal for guards; the requested benefits apply only to the garment sector and were never agreed. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 93][Para 94][Para 95]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to take Mr P S back into work, and the panel refused to consider the four benefit demands. [Para 143][Para 144]
On jurisdiction the panel applied Article 302 of the Labour Law, which defines a collective dispute, and held that where an employer contests the labour inspector's and Minister's classification, the employer bears the burden of proving the dispute is not collective. [Para 105][Para 108][Para 109] The employer asserted only that the case concerned Mr P S alone and filed nothing to show the enterprise would not be disrupted, so the dispute was collective and within jurisdiction. [Para 106][Para 110][Para 111]
On discrimination the panel applied Articles 12 and 279, which forbid an employer from using union membership or activity as a ground for discipline or termination, and noted its consistent practice of ordering reinstatement where dismissal is discriminatory. [Para 113][Para 114][Para 115] The workers produced no specific evidence of discrimination beyond the fact of his post and the reassignment, and produced nothing to prove he had been elected deputy president. [Para 119][Para 120] The reassignment was ordinary monthly rotation that he did not deny, so the panel found no discriminatory link. [Para 121][Para 122]
On the internal rules the panel applied Article 6, which lists sleeping on duty as a light fault calling for an oral warning and permits termination only after three such faults. [Para 123][Para 124] It compared the signatures on the 18 October and 27 October warning letters, found them different, and noted the employer's admission of the difference plus its undertaking to file a supervisor's confirming letter, which it never filed by the 23 January 2013 evidence deadline. [Para 70][Para 71][Para 72][Para 73] Only the 18 and 30 October incidents were therefore proved — two, not three — so the dismissal breached the internal rules and an oral warning should have come first. [Para 125][Para 127][Para 130] Relying on Article 34 of Prakas No. 099, which empowers the panel to order reinstatement, it ordered his return to work. [Para 131]
On the benefits the panel found no statute, agreement or collective convention obliging a security employer to pay those four sums, so the demand was an interests dispute. [Para 133][Para 134] Applying Article 43 of Prakas No. 099, an award on interests substitutes for a collective agreement for one year and strips other workers of the right to strike over the same subject, so only a most-representative union may bring such a claim. [Para 135][Para 136][Para 137] With 175 members out of 730 staff the union was not most representative, and the panel refused to consider the demand. [Para 140][Para 141][Para 142]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer's own disciplinary scale makes termination available only after three proved instances of a light fault, a dismissal resting on two proved instances is contrary to the internal rules even if the conduct occurred, and the panel will order reinstatement rather than compensation. [Para 124][Para 127][Para 131]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that an employer represented by counsel ought to know that Khmer is the language of the proceedings, and declined to read the English rotation schedule filed after the hearing. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 97][Para 98]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is dated 8 February 2013 in the header but the reasons state it was due on 24 January 2012 and postponed to 8 February 2012. [Para 18] The termination letter lists three incidents (18, 27 and 30 October 2012) while the evidence list carries the 27 October warning twice as items 11 and 12. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 66] The union's membership figure is given against a workforce of 730 but the union is described as holding 175 members without stating the enterprise total it was measured against. [Para 58][Para 60] The signature page carries stray years "2016" and "2024" beside the arbitrators' names, and the power of attorney names the employer as "Xin". [Para 20][Para 146]
