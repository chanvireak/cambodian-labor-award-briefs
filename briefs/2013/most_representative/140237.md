# Award No. 248/12 — M & V Garment International (Branch 3) (Arbitration Council, 18 January 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach, An Non; Pen Bun Chhea presiding) · Doc ID 140237

## 1. Facts
A Phnom Penh garment factory employing about 3,300 workers faced seven collective demands from the Independent Workers' Union of Cambodia at the plant, of which six were non-conciliated; the union then withdrew Issues 1, 2 and 3, leaving attendance-bonus pro-rating, a meal allowance for holiday and Sunday work, and a milk allowance. [Para 1][Para 20][Para 25][Para 26][Para 47][Para 48] The union held a registration certificate but no certificate of most-representative status. [Para 49][Para 50] The company paid a USD 10 monthly attendance bonus but cancelled it entirely in any month a worker took authorised personal leave. [Para 54][Para 76] The award is binding as to rights disputes and non-binding as to interest disputes under the 3 October 2012 MoU. [Para 27][Para 31][Para 177]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must pro-rate the USD 10 attendance bonus to the number of days of authorised personal leave instead of cancelling it entirely? [Para 54][Para 71]
2. (Issue 5) Whether holiday work counts as overtime so as to attract a 1,000 riel per hour meal allowance, and whether Sunday work attracts 8,000 riel a day? [Para 60][Para 123]
3. (Issue 6) Whether the employer must provide two cans of milk a month to women workers with children aged one to eighteen months? [Para 162][Para 167]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Workers obtain proper authorisation before taking personal leave, yet the employer cancels the whole attendance bonus over a half-day or single day; holiday and Sunday work should count as overtime under the 2,000 riel meal rule, meaning 1,000 riel an hour and 8,000 riel for an eight-hour day; and because the company has no nursery, mothers must pay others to mind their children and need the milk. [Para 56][Para 63][Para 65][Para 68]
**Employer (respondent):** Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 conditions the bonus on no absence at all, and authorised personal leave is the worker's own business rather than performance of the contract, so no bonus is due; the company already pays 2,000 riel for holiday work and 2,000 riel for Sunday work, which is more than the law requires; and it is building a nursery to comply with the Labour Law. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 61][Para 69][Para 70]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to reduce the attendance bonus in proportion to days of authorised leave (Issue 4), while the meal-allowance claim (Issue 5) and the milk claim (Issue 6) were refused. [Para 176][Para 178][Para 179]

On Issue 4 the panel treated the bonus as a rights dispute because point 2 of Notification No. 230 fixes a minimum USD 10 for all textile, garment and footwear employers. [Para 74][Para 75] It found that notification replaced Notification No. 041 of 7 March 2011 but left the phrase "without absence" undefined, and neither the Ministry nor the Labour Advisory Committee had explained whether it covered authorised absence. [Para 79][Para 84][Para 86][Para 87] Rather than construe it generally, the panel held that authorised leave suspends the contract under Article 71(6) and Article 72(1) so that only the wage obligation for those days falls away; the employer can therefore deduct the bonus only in proportion to the days taken. [Para 110][Para 113][Para 115][Para 116]

On Issue 5 the panel held that work on a public holiday is not overtime, because Article 164 and clauses 2 and 4 of Prakas No. 10 of 4 February 1999 give a flat compensatory day's wage instead of the premium rate. [Para 124][Para 128][Para 129][Para 130][Para 131] Since the employer already paid 2,000 riel for holiday work — more than the law requires — that part of the demand was an interest claim and was refused. [Para 135][Para 136][Para 137][Para 146] Sunday work, by contrast, is overtime because the normal week runs Monday to Saturday and Sunday is the weekly rest day, so Notification No. 041's 2,000 riel a day applies; the employer paid it, and the claim for 8,000 riel exceeded the legal entitlement, making it an interest demand. [Para 148][Para 149][Para 155][Para 156][Para 157][Para 158][Para 161]

On Issue 6 the panel found Article 186 requires a nursing room and nursery, not milk, so a demand for two cans substituted money for a duty and was an interest claim. [Para 162][Para 167][Para 171][Para 172] Because the claimant union held no most-representative certificate, the panel refused to consider the interest demands at all. [Para 143][Para 144][Para 145][Para 175][Para 179]

Arbitrator Kao Thach dissented on Issue 4, reading Notification No. 230 as unambiguously requiring full attendance for the whole USD 10 and finding no basis to pro-rate. [Para 184][Para 190][Para 192]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer has authorised leave, the worker is not absent for the purposes of the USD 10 attendance bonus: the contract is suspended, no wage is owed for those days, and the employer may deduct only in proportion to the days of leave rather than forfeiting the whole bonus. [Para 99][Para 113][Para 115]
An interest demand cannot be arbitrated at all unless the union bringing it holds most-representative status in the enterprise, even where the panel has already decided a related rights dispute on the merits. [Para 143][Para 145]

**Obiter dicta:** That holiday work is compensated by a flat day's wage rather than an overtime premium, and that paying above the statutory meal rate does not create a legal entitlement to more. [Para 131][Para 136]
