# Award No. 127/13 — Quint Major Industrial Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 18 July 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kol Vathana for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 139814

## 1. Facts
Quint Major Industrial Co., Ltd. (QMI) is a garment factory at Trayeung Village, Ang Snoul District, Kandal Province, employing 4,470 workers. [Para 21] The claimant is the local union of the Khmer Workers Power Federation Union (KWPFU). [Para 21] Seven demands went to conciliation, producing non-conciliation report No. 461/13 of 21 June 2013; a further conciliation at the hearing of 2 July 2013 resolved none. [Para 6][Para 9] Both parties elected non-binding arbitration for the interests disputes. [Para 15][Para 16]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must increase the overtime meal allowance from 2,000 to 4,000 riel per day for workers working overtime from 4 p.m. to 6 p.m.? [Para 23]
2. Whether the employer must provide a daily 3,000 riel payment in lieu of lunch? [Para 29]
3. Whether the employer must add US$10 to the existing US$7 accommodation and transportation allowance, bringing it to US$17 per month? [Para 30]
4. Whether the employer must specify the company name on workers' new ID cards? [Para 35]
5. Whether the employer must cut the number of punch-ins from four to two per day? [Para 44]
6. Whether the employer must refrain from deducting special leave from remaining annual leave, and from wages and bonuses where no annual leave remains? [Para 48]
7. Whether the employer must add two days of annual leave for each year of seniority? [Para 52]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** living costs have risen, a meal now costing 2,500 riel against 1,500 before, so the 2,000 riel overtime allowance is insufficient [Para 25][Para 27]; other factories such as Master & Frank, Yak Jean and Generation pay a lunch allowance [Para 30]; monthly transport of US$10–20, accommodation of US$10, water of US$3 and electricity of US$2 justify US$17 [Para 32][Para 33]; the same owner runs Q.M.I G, where annual leave rises by two days a year [Para 52][Para 53].
**Employer (respondent):** it pays what the law requires — 2,000 riel for overtime, US$7 for accommodation and transport [Para 27][Para 34]; no law obliges a lunch allowance [Para 30]; ID cards are internal documents whose format does not touch wage, seniority or benefits, and the "QM" trademark still identifies the factory [Para 40][Para 41][Para 92]; four punch-ins are needed to control attendance, each taking only 7–10 minutes [Para 46][Para 47]; Quint Major and Q.M.I G (Grand Twin) are separate registered enterprises [Para 53].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — Issues 4, 5, 6(A) and 6(B) were rejected by binding award; Issues 1, 2, 3 and 7 were declined as non-binding interests disputes for want of a claimant with most representative status. No money was ordered. [Para 128][Para 129][Para 130][Para 131]

The panel first tested who could claim. Article 268 conditions a union's rights on registration and Ministry recognition of its named officers [Para 56], and prior awards treat the right to represent members as one of those rights [Para 57]. The three workers present had been notified to the employer and put forward for recognition, but no Ministry recognition was produced, so they were not leaders in law [Para 54][Para 55][Para 58]. Nor did the undated, purposeless list of ID numbers and thumbprints amount to written authorisation under Clause 19 of Prakas No. 099 [Para 59]. They were therefore the only claimants. [Para 62]

Issues 1 to 3 were each measured against a Ministry notification. Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 point 2 sets a 2,000 riel meal allowance for voluntary overtime, which the employer paid, so a demand for 4,000 riel exceeded the law and was an interests dispute. [Para 68][Para 69] The lunch payment had no basis in law, agreement, internal rules or practice, so it was also an interests dispute. [Para 78][Para 79] Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 point 1 sets the US$7 accommodation and transport allowance, which was paid, so the extra US$10 was an interests dispute too. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 84] Because an interests award replaces a collective agreement, binds all workers and removes their right to strike for a year, only a union with most representative status, or a collective union with over half the workforce, may bring one [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74]; the three claimants held no such status, so the panel declined all three. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 77][Para 80][Para 85]

Issue 4 was a rights dispute. [Para 88] No law or internal rule governs ID card format, so under Article 2 the employer may set it provided the card still identifies its workers and does not harm their interests. [Para 90][Para 91] The card kept the "QM" trademark, which both parties accepted as the company's mark, and the workers neither objected nor showed any material consequence. [Para 92][Para 96]

Issue 5 was also a rights dispute, turning on the employer's right to arrange attendance recording for wage calculation and production records. [Para 98][Para 101] Two punch-ins a day would not let the employer manage attendance, so the arrangement was lawful and reasonable and the demand was rejected. [Para 103][Para 107]

Issue 6(A) failed on Article 171, which allows the employer to deduct special leave from untaken annual leave, and on Clause 2 of Prakas No. 267 of 11 October 2001. [Para 111][Para 113][Para 114] Issue 6(B) failed on Clause 4 of the same Prakas: where annual leave is exhausted the employer may reduce wages and benefits for the days taken, and the worker may make up the hours to restore them. [Para 116][Para 117][Para 119]

Issue 7 was measured against Article 166: one and a half days per month gives 18 days a year, increased by one day for every three years of service. [Para 122][Para 124] Two days a year exceeded the law: an interests dispute, declined on the same ground. [Para 126][Para 131]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the Labour Law or a Ministry notification already fixes an entitlement and the employer pays it, a demand for more is not a rights claim: it is an interests dispute, because it rests on no provision, agreement, internal rule or practice, and only a union with most representative status, or a collective union representing over half the enterprise's workers, may bring it — three individual workers cannot, and the panel declines. [Para 69][Para 74][Para 76][Para 84] Conversely, where a matter is committed to the employer's management under Article 2 — the format of an ID card, the number of daily punch-ins — the panel will uphold the arrangement if it is lawful and reasonable and the workers show no material prejudice to wage, seniority or benefits. [Para 91][Para 102][Para 104]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's general remark that an employer must arrange a proper punch-in process which does not affect workers' rights and freedoms, and that unjustifiable punch-in time should not cause serious effects — a standard stated without any evidence of the actual time taken. [Para 104][Para 105][Para 106]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- Unofficial English translation with the evidence section expressly omitted, so the record behind Issues 1–3 cannot be verified. [Para 17][Para 18]
- The employer's name carries the sister case number: "124/13-Quint Major Industrial Co., Ltd." [Para 1] — the number of Award 124/13, not this one (127/13).
- The secretary's name appears four ways: "Run Sokun Pidor" [Para 23][Para 61], "Run Kunthea Pidor" [Para 22], "Run Sophea Pidor" [Para 54] and "Run Sokunthea Pidor" [Para 23]. Names are redacted in this brief.
- The MoU is dated 3 October 2012 at [Para 12] but "28 September 2010" in the enforcement clause at [Para 129]; Prakas No. 305 is dated 22 November 2001 here [Para 70] and 22 November 2011 in the companion award.
- The Ministry is printed "Ministry of Labour and Vocational Traing" throughout and once "Ministry of Labour and Education Traing" [Para 55]; the award date was deferred by agreement from 12 July to 18 July 2013 [Para 17].
- Pervasive OCR word-joining ("themloyer", "athearing", "righto", "USS"), with duplicated fragments where a sentence restarts mid-line, e.g. Notification No. 230 at [Para 82] and Article 2 at [Para 90]. Meaning was recoverable by reconstruction.
