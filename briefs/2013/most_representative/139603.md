# Award No. 19/13 — Blue Sam Sen Cheury (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 20 February 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (You Soun Ty, employer-appointed; An Nan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · non-binding award, with a dissenting opinion by the employer-appointed arbitrator · Doc ID 139603

## 1. Facts
Blue Sam Sen Cheury (Cambodia) Ltd is a garment factory in Bati district, Takeo province, employing about 1,175 workers. The claimant was the Cambodian Economic Workers' Union Federation for the factory union, which had 27 thumbprints, no registration certificate and no Most Representative Status (MRS). Of nine demands, three (the US$80 wage, lighting, strike-period wages) were withdrawn, leaving six. Central was the dismissal on 3 January 2013 of the union's elected secretary, Mr O, hired on three successive "occasional" contracts from 1 August 2012. [Para 1][Para 46][Para 48][Para 22][Para 49]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr O, elected secretary of the factory union, with back pay from the date of dismissal? [Para 49]
2. Whether the employer must pay a 2,000 riel meal allowance to workers who volunteer to work on Sundays or public holidays? [Para 74][Para 184]
3. Whether the employer must pay 100% wages when it has no work for workers and makes them rest for two or three days? [Para 82][Para 224]
4. Whether the employer may deduct the attendance bonus pro rata, or at all, when a worker takes authorised personal leave? [Para 88][Para 241]
6. Whether the employer must post piece rates so workers know them within 3 to 7 days of a new style starting? [Para 96][Para 281]
7. Whether the employer must pay a US$10 monthly milk allowance to women workers with children? [Para 102][Para 288]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr O was the elected union secretary, had worked 7 to 8 months with no fault and no warning, and work remained in his section, so his termination was discrimination against a union leader; the contracts were not genuinely occasional. They also sought the 2,000 riel meal allowance on the footing that Sunday and holiday work is overtime, full wages during short lay-offs, pro rata only deduction of the attendance bonus, posted piece rates, and a milk allowance other factories paid. [Para 66][Para 68][Para 69][Para 75][Para 79][Para 106]
**Employer (respondent):** It denied discrimination, saying it never received notice of the union leadership, and that Mr O's occasional contracts ran at most two months and could end whenever it had no work; it had paid 4,000 riel for Sunday and holiday work (the workers said that was travel money), could not pay 100% wages, deducted the whole attendance bonus for personal leave, would not change its piece-rate practice and owed no milk allowance. [Para 70][Para 71][Para 72][Para 78][Para 86][Para 94][Para 101][Para 108]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement of Mr O with back pay, the 2,000 riel Sunday meal allowance, a direction to notify the labour inspector of any suspension and pay 100% wages absent authorisation, pro rata attendance-bonus deduction only, and posting of piece rates within 3 to 7 days; holiday and milk allowances were declined. The award is non-binding. [Para 292][Para 299]

On issue 1 the panel first characterised the contract. Each of the three documents was headed "occasional contract" but stated only a start date — "duration not fixed, depending on the work required, but not exceeding 2 months" — and no end date; Article 67(1) and (2) requires a fixed duration contract to state a precise termination date, failing which it becomes undetermined in duration. Its ending on 3 January 2013 was therefore a dismissal, not expiry. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 126][Para 146][Para 147]

Protection then turned on Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, article 4 chapter 1, which protects founders and voluntary members from the moment registration is applied for until 30 days after registration, read with Article 293 of the Labour Law requiring the labour inspector's prior authorisation. It held the protection covers dismissal only, not expiry (Awards 34/05, 53/06). Mr O was elected on 13 December 2012, the federation notified the employer on 14 December 2012 with a guard's signature acknowledging receipt, the union applied on 27 December 2012, the employer objected on 17 January 2013 and the department declined to forward the file on 21 January 2013 — so the 3 January 2013 dismissal fell inside the protected window. Its own guard's signature defeated any plea of ignorance. [Para 112][Para 114][Para 118][Para 154][Para 164][Para 173][Para 174][Para 176]

All three conditions were met and the employer had no inspector authorisation, so it had to reinstate Mr O with back pay from dismissal to return. The employer-appointed arbitrator dissented in an annexed opinion, reasoning that each contract stated a start and a term within two months, that Mr O knew when each ended, and that the third expired by time rather than being cut short, so Article 293 did not apply. [Para 181][Para 182][Para 183][Para 300][Para 343]

On issue 2 the panel held Sunday work to be overtime, because the normal week runs Monday to Saturday (Award 114/08), so point 2 of Notification 041/11 required 2,000 riel a day or a free meal; the employer paid 4,000 riel but the workers' unrebutted account was that this was transport money because the staff bus did not run at weekends, so the meal allowance remained owing. Holiday work is not overtime under Articles 137, 139 and 164 and Prakas No. 10 of 4 February 1999 (Award 115/08), and no rule required a meal allowance for it, so that part was an interests dispute declined for want of MRS. [Para 190][Para 192][Para 196][Para 208][Para 211][Para 223]

On issue 3, Article 170 allows annual leave to be fixed by agreement, and Article 71(11) permits suspension only for serious economic difficulty, for no more than two months, under inspector supervision. The employer notified no inspector and obtained no authorisation, so its short lay-offs were not a lawful suspension, the leave deduction was improper and 100% wages were owed. [Para 224][Para 231][Para 237][Para 240]

On issue 4, Notification 230 of 25 July 2012, replacing Notification 041/11, requires ten dollars for regular attendance "without absence". The panel found the term ambiguous and declined to read it as covering all absence; absences under annual, special, maternity, holiday and weekly rest permit no deduction, while authorised personal leave is a suspension under Articles 71(6) and 72(1), so only pro rata deduction is allowed. [Para 244][Para 251][Para 255][Para 274][Para 279][Para 280]

On issue 6, Article 112 requires the employer to inform workers clearly of wage conditions whenever they change; piece rates vary with each new style, and the employer had not shown it could not post them within 3 to 7 days, so it was ordered to do so. On issue 7 no law, collective agreement or contract provided a milk allowance, so the demand was an interests dispute declined for want of MRS. [Para 283][Para 285][Para 287][Para 288][Para 291]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A contract styled occasional or fixed duration that states no precise end date is converted by Article 67 into an undetermined duration contract, so ending it is a dismissal needing the labour inspector's authorisation under Article 293 where the worker is protected by Prakas No. 305 during registration; and short lay-offs are lawful only as an inspector-supervised suspension under Article 71(11), failing which full wages are due and annual leave may not be consumed. [Para 146][Para 176][Para 237]

**Obiter dicta:** the remark that knowledge of a union's leadership cannot be denied where the employer's own guard signed for the notice, and the refusal to read "without absence" in Notification 230 beyond the case. [Para 173][Para 255]

## 6. Source defects disclosed

The issue numbering skips 5, 8 and 9, withdrawn at the hearing, so the surviving issues are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 7. A separate dissenting opinion of the employer-appointed arbitrator is appended and carries OCR noise ("<unk>") and a stray name in paragraph 343; paragraph 26 is a bare fragment.
