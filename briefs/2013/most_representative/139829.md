# Award No. 141/13 — Sun Well Shoe Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 31 July 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Pen Bunhea chair), with a dissenting opinion by Ing Sothy annexed · Doc ID 139829

## 1. Facts
Sun Well Shoe Co., Ltd makes shoes at Trapeang Thleng, Chom Chao, Pur Senchey, Phnom Penh, and employed about 1,200 workers; it opened in 2000 under the name Jan Wai and took its present name in 2008. [Para 51][Para 71] The Khmer Youth Voice Union filed seven un-conciliated demands; its new leadership was never recognised by the Ministry, but 43 workers thumbprinted a transfer of rights, so the panel decided the case for those 43 only. [Para 144][Para 159]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must terminate the contracts of workers who served under the former name Jan Wai and pay them severance for that period? [Para 162]
2. Whether the employer must merge the 5 USD cooperation bonus into the 10 USD attendance bonus, making 15 USD, and deduct pro rata for authorised leave? [Para 182]
3. Whether the employer must provide a free midday meal or a 4,000 riel daily meal allowance? [Para 233]
4. Whether the employer must raise the base wage by 19 USD a month, from 71 USD to 90 USD? [Para 247]
5. Whether the employer must install heat-reduction equipment in the injection and closing buildings? [Para 268]
6. Whether the employer must provide a television and fans in the workers' eating area? [Para 291]
7. Whether the employer must supply clean water for use in the toilets? [Para 311]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the 2008 change of name was in substance a termination of the Jan Wai contracts, so severance is owed for that period [Para 162]; the 5 USD cooperation bonus is really part of the attendance bonus and the two should total 15 USD [Para 78]; the injection and closing buildings are unbearably hot and the toilets hold dark, algae-filled pond water in which fish are kept. [Para 117]
**Employer (respondent):** a name change is not a termination, the same owner and structure continued and seniority and benefits were preserved [Para 170]; the 5 USD is a discretionary incentive whose conditions the employer sets [Para 88]; fans are installed and cooling measures are being sought, and the eating area is shaded by mango trees [Para 126]; the pond water is fit for use. [Para 139]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to reduce the heat in the injection and closing buildings, to supply clean water in the toilets, and to deduct the 10 USD attendance bonus pro rata for authorised leave; it refused the severance, the 15 USD merger, the free meal, the 19 USD rise and the fans and television. No sum is quantified. [Para 335][Para 338][Para 341]

On issue 1, under Article 311 of the Civil Code and Article 65 of the Labour Law contract duties arise from the will of the parties, so one party cannot force the other to terminate. [Para 166] The panel found Jan Wai and Sun Well Shoe to be one company with the same owner and structure, the only change being the name in 2008, after which no new contracts were signed and seniority and benefits were kept. [Para 169][Para 170] There was no termination and no severance. [Para 181]

On issue 2, the 5 USD cooperation bonus is paid above the law for a stated condition of 26 full working days, so it is an exercise of the employer's managerial right under Article 2 that the panel would not merge into the statutory bonus. [Para 192][Para 193] But leave the employer has authorised suspends the contract under Articles 71(6) and 72, so the 10 USD attendance bonus may be deducted only in proportion to the days of such leave and no further. [Para 231][Para 232]

On issue 3, no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice requires a free meal or 4,000 riel a day; the claim was an interest dispute, and because the federation was not the most representative union the panel declined to consider it. [Para 246]

On issue 4, Notification No. 103 of 9 April 2013 fixes 80 USD for full workers (61 plus 14 plus 5 USD) and 75 USD for probationers (56 plus 14 plus 5 USD) from 1 May 2013, absorbing the health allowance into the minimum. [Para 248][Para 249] Adding 19 USD on top of the existing 71 USD misread that structure, and the employer had already raised wages to 80 USD, so the claim was an interest dispute and was refused. [Para 267]

On issue 5, Article 229 and Prakas No. 147 of 11 June 2002 require the employer to keep the working environment at a temperature compatible with health and to use every means — fans, extractors, coolers — to cool it. [Para 268][Para 270] Both parties agreed the injection building is hot; the buildings are low, zinc-roofed and without ventilation equipment, and the employer conceded the heat but could give no date for remedial work. [Para 277][Para 287] The panel ordered immediate measures. [Para 338]

On issue 6, the panel refused the fans: the eating area has no walls, is shaded by a dense row of mango trees and was conceded to be reasonably cool, so no obligation arose. [Para 301][Para 304] The television claim had no legal or practice basis and was an interest dispute. [Para 310]

On issue 7, Article 229 and Articles 1 and 6 of Prakas No. 052 of 10 February 2000 require toilet water to be sufficient, clean and clear. [Para 314][Para 316] The employer pumped pond water into an uncovered tank; it bore the burden of proving the water wholesome and produced only a photograph of the tank, which did not discharge it. [Para 329] The panel ordered clean, hygienic water. [Para 341]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A change of company name, where the owner, structure, seniority and benefits continue and no new contracts are signed, is not a termination of the employment contract and attracts no severance for the earlier period. [Para 169] A bonus paid above the law for a stated condition lies within the employer's managerial right and will not be merged into a statutory bonus; but authorised leave suspends the contract under Articles 71(6) and 72, so the statutory attendance bonus may be reduced only in proportion to the days of such leave. [Para 192][Para 231] Article 229 and Prakas No. 147 oblige the employer to hold workplace temperature at an acceptable level and to use every available means to cool it; conceding excessive heat while offering no timetable does not discharge that duty. [Para 287] Toilet water must be clean, clear and sufficient under Prakas No. 052, and an employer relying on pond water in an uncovered tank bears the burden of proving it wholesome. [Para 329]

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that the panel could not interpret "without absence" in Notification No. 230 because no competent body had done so, so each case must be decided on its facts [Para 202][Para 207], went beyond what the decision required once Articles 71 and 72 resolved the issue.

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award is dated 31 July 2013 in the header but 31 July 2012 at the foot [Para 367], and the panel is said to have been constituted on "08 July 2019 2013" [Para 18].
- The operative date of the new minimum wage is printed as "01 December May 2013" [Para 251].
- The union's new leadership was never recognised, so although the claim was framed for the whole workforce the panel decided only for the 43 workers who transferred rights [Para 144][Para 160].
- A dissent by the employer-party arbitrator is annexed against the pro-rata order in issue 2, on the ground that a bonus is discretionary and its conditions belong to the employer [Para 346].
- The company appears as both Jan Wai and Sun Well Shoe [Para 169]; individuals are redacted in this brief as required.
