# Award No. 32/13 — Indian Cooperation Project for the Restoration of Ta Prohm Temple (Arbitration Council, 5 April 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Hem Hou Narith, employer-appointed; An Vira, worker-appointed; Kong Phallna, chair) · Doc ID 139644

## 1. Facts
The Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) has restored Ta Prohm temple with APSARA since 2004, employing over 100 workers paid by the Government of India. [Para 57][Para 59] Seven skilled and unskilled workers, born between 1940 and 1952 and hired between 2007 and 2010 without written contracts, were told at a meeting on 30 December 2012 that they would be retired and were dismissed on 31 December 2012. [Para 69][Para 78] The embassy approved the retirement on the ASI's request, citing only the age of 60. [Para 90][Para 191] The union held a most-representative certificate dated 23 July 2012. [Para 62]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the Cambodian Labour Law governs the employment relationship between the seven workers and the Indian cooperation project? [Para 125]
2. Whether the employer dismissed the seven for a valid reason, and if not, whether they must be reinstated with back pay or paid termination compensation? [Para 155][Para 158]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The dismissal came four days after the union and the ASI signed an agreement to negotiate a collective agreement, and is therefore anti-union; alternatively the five statutory termination heads are owed — final wages, notice pay, unused annual leave, dismissal damages and moral damages. [Para 157][Para 156] They were never told of any retirement age when recruited. [Para 76]
**Employer (respondent):** The ASI says it has no power to rehire — only the Indian embassy can decide — and that Cambodian labour law does not reach its operations; the seven were retired at 60 under Indian civil-service law, four of five programmes are finished and the work has moved to the temple summit, which is unsafe for them. [Para 86][Para 104][Para 88][Para 99]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement was refused because the employer declined to take the workers back, and four heads of termination compensation were ordered instead: one month's wages and benefits in lieu of notice, unused annual leave, dismissal damages under article 89, and moral damages equal to those damages. [Para 198][Para 223]

On jurisdiction, article 1 of the Labour Law governs every employment relationship arising from a contract to be performed on Cambodian territory, whatever the parties' nationality, and applies to all staff not covered by the civil-service or diplomatic statute. [Para 126][Para 127][Para 135] The seven supplied labour as skilled and unskilled workers, were not civil servants or diplomats, and worked in Cambodia, so the law applied. [Para 136][Para 137][Para 139] The panel then identified the employer: the ASI is the executing agency for the Government of India, and the embassy decides on terminations on the ASI's request, so the Government of India, represented by the embassy, is the employer and the ASI its suitable representative in the proceedings. [Para 140][Para 152][Para 154]

On the merits, article 67(7) makes a fixed-duration contract void as to form unless written, and converts it into an indefinite-duration contract; the seven had no written contracts, so their engagement was indefinite. [Para 170][Para 173] Under article 74 such a contract may be terminated at will, but not without a valid reason related to the worker's capacity or conduct or to the running of the enterprise. [Para 174][Para 176] The panel rejected the retirement ground because Cambodian law, not Indian civil-service law, governed, and the draft collective agreement invoked as the source of the age-60 rule had never been concluded. [Para 181][Para 82] It rejected the two operational grounds because the 27 December 2012 request to the embassy cited only retirement, and no documentary or technical evidence supported either programme completion or the change in work. [Para 191][Para 193] The dismissal therefore breached article 74. [Para 194]

The anti-union allegation failed: the workers showed no link between the dismissal and the collective-agreement demand, were not shown to be leading or prominent members, and did not show that the union lost its voice. [Para 165][Para 166][Para 167] Because the employer refused reinstatement, the panel awarded notice pay under articles 75(4) and 77, annual leave under article 166, dismissal damages under article 89, and moral damages under article 91 equal to those damages. [Para 198][Para 203][Para 210][Para 222]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The Cambodian Labour Law governs work performed in Cambodia for a foreign State's project, so a foreign civil-service retirement age cannot justify dismissal, and where the employer refuses reinstatement after an unlawful dismissal the panel substitutes the statutory termination payments, including dismissal damages calculated on actual seniority. [Para 135][Para 181][Para 216]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that a draft collective agreement still under negotiation cannot be invoked as a source of binding terms. [Para 82]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The worker count is printed as 7 throughout, but as "700" in the recital of the discrimination claim and "76" in the finding that the dismissal breached article 74. [Para 157][Para 194]
- The dismissal date appears as "12 31 December 2012" in the notice-pay reasoning. [Para 204]
- The panel says it cannot fix article 89 damages because the first dates of employment were not proved, then orders them computed on actual seniority. [Para 215][Para 216]
- The retirement-age basis is described both as the draft collective agreement and as Indian law in adjacent passages. [Para 179][Para 180]
- The ASI is transliterated several ways, including "Archeological-Sunvey-of India" and three different Khmer renderings. [Para 20]
- The employer arbitrator is printed as ហែម ហូណារិទ្ធ on the face and ហែម ហួ ណារិទ្ធ at the signature.
