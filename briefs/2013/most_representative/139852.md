# Award No. 170/13 — CIIDG Erdos Hongjun Electric Power Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 20 September 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mae Nimmit presiding; Chhiv Phirom appointed by the employer; An Non appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 139852

## 1. Facts
CIIDG Erdos Hongjun Electric Power Co., Ltd (ស៊ី អាយ ឌី ជី អេដូស ហុង ជុន អេឡិកទ្រិក ផៅវ័រ) is building a 3 x 135 MW (405 MW) coal-fired power plant at Kampenh, Stung Hav, Preah Sihanouk, registered on 10 August 2011. [Para 54] It recruits trainees (សិក្ខាកាម) under training-scholarship agreements to study electrical trades in China and then work at the plant; about 173 were in training and none had passed the employer's final assessment or examination. [Para 68][Para 163][Para 169][Para 171] The Khmer Workers' Power Union Federation complained on 12 August 2013 on 13 demands; conciliation settled 7, and at the hearing of 28 August 2013 the claimants withdrew issue 2, leaving issues 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 23] The federation's union at the plant had not been registered by the Ministry, and rights were transferred to it by only 13 of the trainees. [Para 56][Para 61][Para 144]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must arrange for trainees of every intake to become members of the National Social Security Fund (បេឡា ជាតិ របប សន្តិសុខ សង្គម)? [Para 80][Para 145]
3. Whether the employer must organise the election of staff representatives before 20 September 2013? [Para 99][Para 176]
4. Whether the employer must pay a meal allowance of 4,000 riel a day to the trainees? [Para 108][Para 194]
5. Whether the employer must pay a regular attendance bonus of USD 10 a month to the trainees? [Para 119][Para 209]
6. Whether the employer must set up an enterprise infirmary for the trainees? [Para 123][Para 215]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the trainees are "vocational students" within Article 248 of the Labour Law and so entitled to its protection; the employer bought only a Forte Insurance accident policy, which does not show cover for work risks or benefits equal to the NSSF; they want staff representatives so that internal rules can be registered, a 4,000 riel meal allowance because meals at the isolated dormitory cost USD 3 a day, a USD 10 attendance bonus as in the garment sector, and an infirmary because medicines are sold at the dormitory. [Para 81][Para 108][Para 113][Para 119][Para 123][Para 127]
**Employer (respondent):** "vocational student" in Article 248 means a person who is already a worker and has been sent for training, which these trainees are not; the plant is still under construction, has not yet made a profit, and no law requires these payments for trainees; the training agreement gives a USD 100 monthly living allowance, above the USD 80 garment minimum wage, and trainees may buy food outside. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 114][Para 115][Para 121][Para 122][Para 127][Para 128]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — issue 1 refused; issue 3 refused; issue 4 not considered; issue 5 not considered; issue 6 refused. The award is non-binding and becomes final 8 (eight) days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Minister. [Para 225][Para 226][Para 227][Para 228][Para 229]

On representation, the panel held that under Article 268 of the Labour Law a professional organisation obtains the rights and benefits the law recognises only when it is registered with the Ministry; its union at the plant had not been registered, so it could not represent its members, and past awards were followed on that point. [Para 130][Para 132][Para 133][Para 135] Under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 a party may be represented by a person holding written authorisation, and on the evidence only 10 trainees had transferred rights, so the federation lawfully represented 13 trainees, that being the 10 plus 3 of the 6 who attended; the panel would decide the case for those 13 only. [Para 136][Para 141][Para 142][Para 143][Para 144]

On issue 1, the panel applied Articles 248, 249, 256 and 257 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 022/08 K.B/Prak of 11 February 2008, Prakas No. 021/08 and Prakas No. 108 K.B/Prak: compulsory occupational-risk insurance runs through the NSSF and applies in the initial phase only to enterprises with 8 or more workers. [Para 145][Para 150][Para 153][Para 158][Para 159] It then found that a worker is a person who has contracted, orally or in writing, to work for wages under another's direction, and that these trainees had signed only a training agreement, had not passed the employer's assessment, were still in training and had never served the employer, so they were not workers of CIIDG. [Para 164][Para 165][Para 170][Para 171][Para 173] The company was therefore not an enterprise with 8 or more workers for that purpose, and the demand was refused. [Para 174][Para 175]

On issue 3, Articles 283 and 292 require an employer with at least 8 workers to arrange the election of a staff representative, and Prakas No. 286 of 5 November 2001 requires a representative and deputy in industrial enterprises; but because the claimants were not workers, the duty was not engaged and the demand was refused. [Para 176][Para 177][Para 181][Para 186][Para 192][Para 193]

On issues 4 and 5, the panel found no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice requiring a 4,000 riel meal allowance or a USD 10 attendance bonus for trainees, so each was an interest dispute. [Para 194][Para 195][Para 196][Para 211][Para 212] Under Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 an award on an interest dispute stands in place of a collective agreement and binds all workers, so the panel will decide such a dispute only where the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise; this federation, unregistered and representing 13 trainees, was not, and both demands were therefore not considered. [Para 199][Para 202][Para 203][Para 204][Para 207][Para 208]

On issue 6, Article 238 and Article 242 of the Labour Law and Article 1 of Joint Prakas No. 330 SKBY of 6 December 2000 require an enterprise with 50 or more workers to maintain a permanent infirmary at the employer's cost; since the claimants were not workers, the demand was refused. [Para 216][Para 217][Para 220][Para 223][Para 224]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A trainee who has signed only a training-scholarship agreement, has not passed the employer's assessment or examination and has never worked under the employer's direction for wages is not a worker, and the duties the Labour Law owes to workers — NSSF registration, the election of staff representatives and the enterprise infirmary — do not arise in his favour. [Para 165][Para 173][Para 174][Para 192] A union not yet registered with the Ministry has no legal right to represent its members before the panel, and the panel will not decide an interest dispute unless the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise. [Para 132][Para 135][Para 204][Para 207]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that once the trainees become employees of the company, the employer must then organise the election of staff representatives in accordance with the Labour Law. [Para 107]
