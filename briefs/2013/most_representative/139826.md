# Award No. 135/13 — Bright Sun International Corporation (Arbitration Council, 30 July 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Li Tai Seng for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Pen Bunhea chair), with a dissenting opinion by Li Tai Seng annexed · Doc ID 139826

## 1. Facts
Bright Sun International Corporation runs a shoe factory on National Road 41 at Samrong Tong, Kompong Speu, employing about 800 workers; the union and several exhibits still call the plant Global Footwear, the workers saying the name changed on 1 March 2013. [Para 62] Workers struck from 16 May 2013, and on 6 June 2013 the employer dismissed three of them — the president and secretary of the Free Workers Union at the factory and one ordinary member — for inciting and threatening workers who did not strike and for violence and destruction of company property. [Para 75] The Federation of Trade Unions for Solidarity of Cambodia (CLSUF) brought the claim for 367 workers. [Para 118]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the three dismissed workers and pay them wages from 5 June 2013 to the date of return? [Para 67]
2. Whether the employer must pay back the 5 USD health-care allowance it did not pay for April 2013? [Para 80]
3. Whether the employer must add 5 USD to the 10 USD monthly transport and accommodation allowance? [Para 92]
4. Whether the employer must add 2,000 riel to the 2,500 riel overtime meal allowance? [Para 99]
5. Whether the employer must add 5 USD to the 10 USD monthly attendance bonus? [Para 104]
6. (Additional issue) Whether the employer must pay termination compensation of five kinds to each worker? [Para 109]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the three were union officers or members dismissed for their union activity, and the dismissal of a union leader requires the labour inspector's prior authorisation, which was never sought; the factory had cut the 5 USD health allowance out of the April 2013 wage. [Para 128]
**Employer (respondent):** the three incited and threatened workers who refused to strike, contrary to Article 331, and took part in violence and the destruction of factory property, which Article 330 makes serious misconduct; it produced photographs of the damage. [Para 137][Para 151] On the interest claims the employer said its existing payments were already appropriate or above the Prakas minimums.

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the reinstatement of all three workers with back pay from the date of dismissal to the date of return, and refused every other claim, including the April 2013 health allowance, the three allowance increases and the termination compensation. No sum is quantified in the award, which is non-binding.

On issue 1, the panel found Mr M and Ms K to be the president and secretary of the Free Workers Union at the factory, so Article 293 of the Labour Law and Article 4(2) of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 applied: a union leader may not be dismissed without the labour inspector's prior authorisation, and the employer sought none. [Para 129] Their reinstatement with back pay followed. [Para 133] Mr C was a member only, so that protection did not reach him; his dismissal turned on proof, and because the accuser bears the burden and the photographs of damaged property did not identify him as the worker dismissed, the employer had not established serious misconduct under Articles 83 or 330, and he was reinstated on the same terms. [Para 154]

On issue 2, the panel refused the April 2013 health allowance. Notification No. 103 of 9 April 2013 set the minimum at 70 USD plus 14 USD plus 5 USD, equal to 80 USD, from 1 May 2013, while the employer had already applied the 80 USD agreement from 1 April 2013 — a benefit above the law for that month; a worker cannot claim an allowance for a period before the law obliged the employer to pay it. [Para 156][Para 164]

On issues 3 to 5, each claim exceeded anything the law, a collective agreement or past practice required, so each was an interest dispute. Under Article 96 and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 the panel may decide an interest dispute only where the union bringing it is the most representative in the enterprise [Para 176]; the federation represented 367 of some 800 workers and was not that union, so the panel declined jurisdiction. [Para 182]

On the additional issue, the severance claim appeared in a second complaint filed on 6 June 2013 and not in the non-conciliation record, and the 18 February 2013 agreement had ended the old contracts against 75 USD for one year of seniority, which the workers accepted without coercion before re-signing, so no termination without valid reason was shown. [Para 203][Para 211]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union president or secretary may not be dismissed without the labour inspector's prior authorisation under Article 293 and Article 4(2) of Prakas No. 305, and the omission voids the dismissal whatever the alleged misconduct; the protection does not extend to an ordinary member, whose dismissal is judged on the employer's proof. [Para 136] Photographs of damaged factory property that do not identify the dismissed worker do not discharge the employer's burden of proving serious misconduct. [Para 138] The panel has no jurisdiction over an interest dispute unless the union bringing it is the most representative in the enterprise, nor over a claim absent from the non-conciliation record that is not a direct consequence of the dispute. [Para 242] A worker cannot recover an allowance for a period before the law required the employer to pay it, where the employer had already paid more than the law for that period. [Para 163]

**Obiter dicta:** the observations that the 10 USD transport allowance was more favourable than the 7 USD in Notification No. 230, and that the 10 USD attendance bonus was "in conformity" with that notification, were unnecessary once jurisdiction over those claims was declined. [Para 165][Para 167][Para 189][Para 190]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The worker count is given as 376 in the first complaint and 377 in the second [Para 26], but 367 in the reasoning.
- The strike is recorded as beginning on 16 May 2013, stopping on 5 June 2013 and stopping permanently on 17 May 2013 [Para 74] — the last date precedes the second and cannot be right.
- The panel is said to have been constituted on "03 July 2019 2013".
- The award is expressly non-binding, becoming final only eight days after notification if no party objects, and it carries an annexed dissent by the employer-party arbitrator against issues 2 and the additional issue, on jurisdictional grounds rather than on the merits [Para 219]; those two were thus decided by two of the three members.
- The health-allowance claim was amended at the hearing from "raise the base to 80 USD and keep the 5 USD" to "pay back the 5 USD for April 2013" [Para 220]; the majority decided the amended claim on the merits while the dissent held it outside Article 312 bis (1).
- The employer appears as both Bright Sun International Corporation and Global Footwear, with a name change asserted at 1 March 2013; individuals are redacted in this brief as required.
