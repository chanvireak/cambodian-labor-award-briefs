# Award No. 134/13 — M.H Bio Energy Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 23 July 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Mae Nimmit, president) · Doc ID 139821

## 1. Facts
M.H Bio Energy Co Ltd operates an ethanol and alcohol distillery in Kandal province and employed 97 workers. The Khmer Workers' Power Union, which had applied for registration and was still awaiting its certificate, brought two unresolved claims through its federation: a regular attendance bonus of USD 10 a month and a seniority bonus under Ministry Notification No. 041/11. Twenty-two workers signed letters transferring their rights to the federation and the union; four of six claims had been settled at conciliation. [Para 12][Para 34][Para 36][Para 37][Para 38]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to pay a regular attendance bonus of USD 10 a month to the workers? [Para 39][Para 50]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to pay a seniority bonus under Ministry Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011? [Para 43][Para 74]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Wages are low, so the employer should pay a USD 10 monthly attendance bonus and a seniority bonus, as workers in the textile, garment and footwear sector receive; the company has never paid either. [Para 40][Para 45][Para 46]
**Employer (respondent):** The company cannot afford the claims; it already provides benefits better than the law requires — USD 0.85 travel allowance and USD 1.50 meal allowance per day worked, plus an incentive of 1.5 to 3 per cent of base wage — and it suspended operations in 2009 and 2010 through serious financial difficulty. It is an alcohol producer, not a garment factory. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 48][Para 49]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel declined to consider both claims, holding each to be a dispute of interest that the claimant union was not entitled to bring. [Para 73][Para 87][Para 88][Para 89]

On the attendance bonus the panel turned to Ministry Notification No. 230 K.B/S.J.N of 25 July 2012, which requires that a worker who attends on all working days of a month without absence receive at least USD 10 a month. Following its own reasoning in Award No. 03/13 (Tai Wang Printing), a manufacturer of shoe boxes held outside the Notification, the panel read the instrument as confined by its terms to the textile, garment and footwear sector. M.H Bio Energy distils alcohol and is not in that sector, so the Notification does not apply to it. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 55][Para 58]

The panel then searched for any other source of obligation — the Labour Law, a collective agreement, an agreement between the parties, the internal rules, or past practice — and found none requiring a USD 10 monthly attendance bonus. That being so, the claim was not the enforcement of an existing right but a demand for a new benefit, and the panel characterised it as a dispute of interest. [Para 59][Para 60]

That characterisation decided the case. Article 96 defines a collective agreement and Prakas No. 305, Article 9, gives the right to request negotiation of one to the most representative union. Prakas No. 099/04, Article 43, provides that an award resolving an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement and takes effect for one year. Because such an award binds all workers in the enterprise and removes their right to strike over that subject, the panel held that only the most representative union — or several unions together representing more than half the workforce — may bring an interest dispute. [Para 61][Para 64][Para 67][Para 68][Para 69]

Here only 22 workers had transferred their rights, and the union was not the most representative organisation in the company. The panel therefore held the claimants lacked the legal standing to put an interest dispute to it, and declined to consider the claim. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 73]

The seniority bonus claim met the same fate. Notification No. 041/11 sets a seniority bonus scale, but the panel found its terms confined to workers in textile, garment and footwear establishments. Since the company produces alcohol, the Notification does not reach it, and no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice imposed a seniority bonus on this employer. The claim was again an interest dispute, and was again refused for want of a most representative claimant. [Para 75][Para 78][Para 82][Para 83][Para 84][Para 85][Para 87]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a Ministry notification on attendance or seniority bonuses is limited by its own terms to the textile, garment and footwear sector, it creates no entitlement for workers in another industry, and a claim for such a bonus there is a dispute of interest rather than a dispute of rights. An interest dispute may be brought to the panel only by the most representative union in the enterprise, or by unions together representing more than half the workers; a union that is not most representative, acting on transfers from a minority of the workforce, cannot invoke the panel's jurisdiction over it. [Para 55][Para 60][Para 69][Para 72]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel recorded the employer's evidence of allowances and incentives exceeding statutory minima and its 2009–2010 shutdown, but decided nothing on the employer's ability to pay, since the outcome turned on jurisdiction. [Para 42][Para 48]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The seniority bonus table in Notification No. 041/11 is destroyed — the years of service and the corresponding dollar amounts survive only as a jumbled string, so the scale cannot be reproduced. The operative order on the second issue is likewise corrupted, with "bonus" printed as a different word and stray numerals embedded. The conciliation report number is printed as 777 in one passage and 77 in another, and the panel's constitution date reads "3 July 2019 2013". The header carries OCR debris, the company name appears in several spellings, and the union's address is given in Phnom Penh although the company sits in Kandal. The employer's representative is named both Tuy Ho and Tuy Hou. [Para 1][Para 8][Para 13][Para 14][Para 76][Para 89]
