# Award No. 252/12 — Golden Gen Su Com Peni Ltd (Arbitration Council, 28 January 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom, An Non; Tang Tri presiding) · Doc ID 140232

## 1. Facts
A Phnom Penh shoe factory employing about 2,500 workers faced eight collective demands from a minority union — meal allowance, extra fans, milk allowance, overtime for cleaners, a mechanical lift, an end to anti-union discrimination, reinstatement of two women and extra uniform shirts. [Para 1][Para 22][Para 58][Para 59] Two local unions exist at the plant; the Cambodian Workers' Union there is the most representative, and the claimant union, with 997 members, accepted it was not. [Para 60][Para 67] A 3 October 2012 memorandum of understanding made awards binding on rights disputes and non-binding on interest disputes. [Para 29][Para 34] Issues 2 and 4 were settled or withdrawn before hearing; issue 7 survived only as to Ms T. [Para 26][Para 79][Para 116]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay a 4,000 riel daily midday meal allowance to every worker? [Para 162]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay USD 15 per month for 24 months as a milk allowance to women returning from maternity leave? [Para 174]
3. (Issue 5) Whether the employer must install a mechanical lift for loading in factory 2's carton section? [Para 177]
4. (Issue 6) Whether cutting or withholding the additional bonus from Mr S and Ms C, the claimant union's president and vice-president, was anti-union discrimination? [Para 185]
5. (Issue 7) Whether the employer must reinstate Ms T with back pay from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 211]
6. (Issue 8) Whether the employer must supply one further shirt per interpreter per year, and whether interpreters may stop wearing the company uniform? [Para 260]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Wages are low and prices rising, so the meal allowance is needed for health and retention and other factories already pay it; the same bonus was paid to Mr S and Ms C until they became union leaders in March 2012 while others kept receiving it, which proves discrimination. [Para 68][Para 71][Para 72][Para 94]
**Employer (respondent):** The company cannot afford the meal allowance; the collective agreement signed with the other union already sets milk allowance at USD 10 for 18 months, and no instrument provides USD 15 for 24 months; the ramp used in factory 2 has caused no accident; the bonus is discretionary and supervisors did not propose it for the two leaders. [Para 73][Para 75][Para 83][Para 105]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — all interest demands dismissed (Issues 1, 3, 5), the discrimination claim dismissed (Issue 6) and reinstatement refused (Issue 7), but the employer was ordered to pay Ms T's unused annual leave and to issue one additional shirt per interpreter per year while interpreters resume wearing the uniform. [Para 280][Para 282][Para 283][Para 284]

The panel first split rights from interest disputes: rights disputes rest on law, agreement or collective agreement, interest disputes on neither and are decided in equity. [Para 160][Para 161] On Issue 1 it found no statute or agreement guaranteeing 4,000 riel, classified the demand as one of interest, and refused to consider it because the claimant union was not the most representative in the enterprise. [Para 162][Para 163][Para 172][Para 173] Issue 3 failed on the same two grounds, the panel noting that the only provision on milk, clause 18(7) of the agreement with the other union, gives USD 10 for 18 months. [Para 76][Para 174][Para 176]

Issue 5 was decided on the merits in equity: Article 229 obliges employers to keep workplaces safe and to remedy hazardous arrangements promptly, but both sides accepted no worker had been injured on the ramp, so the union had not shown sufficient reason to compel a lift. [Para 179][Para 182][Para 183][Para 184]

On Issue 6 the panel held clause 21 of the collective agreement makes the incentive bonus discretionary, resting on the employer's management prerogative, and found the employer's supervisor request forms listed employee numbers without names or monthly amounts, so no comparison between paid and unpaid months was possible; the claim therefore failed. [Para 197][Para 198][Para 108][Para 204][Para 210] Arbitrator An Non dissented on this issue, reasoning that bonus is part of wages under Article 103 and that Article 118 places the burden of proof on the employer, who produced no evidence rebutting the drop after the two became leaders. [Para 301][Para 303][Para 313]

On Issue 7 the panel applied Article 74 (indefinite contracts, notice, no dismissal without proper grounds) and Article 83 (serious misconduct): Ms T was absent without notice from 16 to 25 July 2012, the medical certificate carried no diagnosis, and the time-card record showed no entry, so dismissal on 26 July 2012 was justified and no severance, damages or notice pay were owed. [Para 211][Para 218][Para 221][Para 229][Para 236][Para 241][Para 248] Unused annual leave, however, remained payable under Articles 166 and 168. [Para 255][Para 258][Para 259]

On Issue 8 the panel read clause 6 of Prakas 307/07 as requiring two sets of clothing a year, so one extra shirt was ordered; but requiring uniforms is a lawful exercise of the management right, so interpreters were ordered back into uniform rather than excused. [Para 263][Para 266][Para 267][Para 278][Para 279]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A panel will not arbitrate an interest demand on its merits at all where the union bringing it is not the most representative in the enterprise, however reasonable the demand; representativeness is the gateway to the remedy, not a factor in its measure. [Para 169][Para 172]
Separately, unauthorised absence of six consecutive days, in breach of registered internal rules requiring notice, amounts to abandonment of employment and serious misconduct, so dismissal attracts no severance, damages or notice indemnity. [Para 221][Para 238]

**Obiter dicta:** That requiring staff to wear factory uniforms is a lawful management act, and that an employee with a worn or torn shirt should report it rather than stop wearing the uniform unilaterally. [Para 275][Para 277]
