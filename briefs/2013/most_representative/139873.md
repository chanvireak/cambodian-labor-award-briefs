# Award No. 199/13 — Kovel E Peril (Cambodia) Inc (Arbitration Council, 12 November 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Kong Phalla chair) · non-binding award · Doc ID 139873

## 1. Facts
Kovel E Peril (Cambodia) Inc, a garment factory in Chom Chao that began operating on 1 May 2013 with more than 500 workers, held a workplace union election for the Cambodia Workers' Solidarity Union Federation on 1–2 September 2013, at which Mr S and Mr B were elected as officers. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 74] Both were still probationary sewers hired in mid-June 2013 on three-month contracts; the employer dismissed Mr B on 2 September 2013 and Mr S on 4 September 2013, two days before his probation ended. [Para 64][Para 68][Para 117] On 9 September 2013 workers struck behind 11 demands; conciliation failed, seven demands were withdrawn, and issues 01, 03, 08 and 09 remained for the panel. [Para 19][Para 26][Para 27]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take the union leaders, Mr S and Mr B, back into normal work and end all forms of anti-union discrimination and retaliation against workers who volunteered for the strike? [Para 53]
2. Whether the employer must refrain from deducting the regular attendance bonus, and keep the special support bonus paid in full, when a pregnant worker takes one day a month off for a health check? [Para 80][Para 199]
3. Whether the employer must add 1,000 riel per hour of meal allowance for overtime worked from 16:00 to 18:00? [Para 89][Para 236]
4. Whether the employer must grant male workers seven days' paid leave to visit a wife who works in the same company across the river? [Para 105][Para 246]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the two men were dismissed because the employer knew they had won union office; the union gave notice of its elected leadership to the employer on 2 September through the security desk, and again on 3 September by EMS courier. [Para 54][Para 75][Para 78] Attendance and special bonuses are docked when pregnant workers attend their monthly check, so they stop going, to the detriment of their health. [Para 84][Para 87]
**Employer (respondent):** both men were dismissed for not performing to capacity, as an evaluation sheet scoring Mr S at 37 per cent shows, and the company did not know either was a union leader; a probationer may be dismissed without reasons, severance and final wages were paid, and management's discretion over discretionary bonuses and overtime meals is lawful. [Para 55][Para 58][Para 60][Para 62][Para 104][Para 115]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to take Mr S back into normal work, and dismissed the claim for Mr B together with all three interest claims (issues 03, 08 and 09); no sum of money was ordered. [Para 258][Para 259][Para 261][Para 262]

On issue 01 the panel began with Article 293 of the 1997 Labour Law, under which a staff delegate or candidate may not be dismissed without the prior agreement of the labour inspector, and followed awards 27/03, 12/10 and 191/12 for the proposition that no reason need be given during probation yet the protection survives it. [Para 119][Para 124] Article 3 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 extends delegate protection to election candidates for 45 days before and after the poll, and following award 40/10 (Meng Yan) the panel held that an elected candidate's protection runs from the day of the election until the union obtains registration, and set three conditions: protected status, dismissal inside the protected period, and notice of the names to the employer by any reliable means. [Para 125][Para 136][Para 144] Mr S met all three — he was elected, he was dismissed on 4 September within the protected period, and the EMS slip marked "received but not signed", uncontested by the employer, proved notice — so his dismissal, made without the inspector's prior approval, was void. [Para 162][Para 171][Para 174] Mr B failed the third condition: notice reached the employer only on 3 September, after his 2 September dismissal, and the panel had held in awards 64/05 and 66/07 that notice given after the fact defeats the protection; his claim was therefore dismissed. [Para 190][Para 192][Para 197]

On issues 03, 08 and 09 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice requiring paid time off for an antenatal check, so the demand was a claim more favourable than the law, that is an interest dispute. [Para 200][Para 201][Para 202] Because Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 makes an award on an interest dispute a substitute collective agreement binding every worker in the enterprise, only the most representative union may bring such a claim; the federation here represented six workers and was not the most representative union at the company, so the panel declined to consider all three claims, as it had in award 204/13. [Para 212][Para 213][Para 216][Para 217] It added on the merits that the special support bonus was a discretionary bonus conditioned on no absence in the month, so a worker who took the day off could not claim it [Para 228][Para 233], and that Notification No. 041 of 7 March 2011 requires 2,000 riel or one free meal per overtime day, which the employer was already providing, so no further 1,000 riel per hour was owed [Para 237][Para 240].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker elected to union office at a workplace election holds Article 293 protection from the day of the election until the union is registered, although Prakas No. 305 fixes no period for elected candidates; the protection fails where the union notifies the employer of the protected names only after the dismissal, prior notice being a condition of it, and a dismissal carried out without the labour inspector's prior approval is void. [Para 144][Para 171][Para 174][Para 190] A claim for a benefit greater than the Labour Law secures is an interest dispute, which the panel will not consider unless the union bringing it is the most representative in the enterprise. [Para 213][Para 217]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's reference to Philippine law allowing paternity leave [Para 108], its remarks that an employer may by unilateral decision create bonuses outside the law provided they are applied lawfully and reasonably [Para 225], and arbitrator Mar Samborana's dissenting opinion that Article 293 protection should never apply during a probationary contract [Para 269][Para 272] are observations outside the majority's ground of decision.
