# Award No. 201/13 — Sabrina (Cambodia) Garment Manufacturing Corp (Arbitration Council, 28 October 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, employer-appointed; Ann Vireak, worker-appointed; Kong Phallack, chair) · Doc ID 139869

## 1. Facts
Sabrina (Cambodia) Garment Manufacturing Corp is a garment manufacturer employing 5,020 workers; the FTUWKC local union was registered on 21 November 2012, while C.CAWDU held most representative status under a collective agreement of 2 January 2013. [Para 10][Para 11] After strikes on 27 May and 3 June 2013 the employer dismissed 22 workers between 6 and 20 June 2013 for carrying sticks and stones during the strike. [Para 44][Para 46] Only 11 of the 22 attended the hearing, so the panel heard the case for those 11 only. [Para 15][Para 20] On the day of the hearing the parties signed an MoU of 3 October 2012 accepting binding arbitration for rights disputes and non-binding arbitration for interest disputes. [Para 6][Para 8]

## 2. Issues in dispute
The award states a single issue — reinstatement of 11 workers or, failing that, termination compensation — which the panel resolved in three parts:
1. Whether the dismissal of Ms BS, transferred from punch-in to fabric organising and dismissed for refusing the transfer, was an individual dispute outside the panel's jurisdiction? [Para 29][Para 31]
2. Whether the eight workers who admitted carrying sticks or stones during the strike committed serious misconduct justifying dismissal without compensation? [Para 77][Para 89]
3. Whether the employer proved that Ms CS and Ms SN carried sticks, so as to justify their dismissal? [Para 113][Para 115]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the 22 dismissed workers were not violent; some carried nothing, and for those who did the employer has no evidence of actual violence or damage. [Para 44][Para 50][Para 51] Ms BS's transfer came straight after the strike and punished her union activity, and the new tasks were too difficult. [Para 31][Para 35]

**Employer (respondent):** Ms BS refused a lawful transfer to a simple task on unchanged pay, which is serious misconduct under Article 83; [Para 36][Para 37][Para 44] the ten others carried sticks or stones at the plant, which is violence during a strike under Article 330 and a threat to security guards, so none will be reinstated. [Para 45][Para 57]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — jurisdiction over Ms BS was declined and reinstatement refused for all; the employer was ordered to pay the eight admittees only their outstanding wages and unused annual leave, and to pay Ms CS two months' wages in lieu of notice, 135 days' indemnity and equal damages, annual leave and outstanding wages, and Ms SN one month's wages in lieu of notice, 75 days' indemnity and equal damages, annual leave and outstanding wages. [Para 131][Para 132][Para 133][Para 134]

The panel first classified the dispute. Under Article 312 and Clause 43 of Prakas 099, disputes over the interpretation or enforcement of law or a collective agreement are rights disputes decided according to law, while everything else is decided in equity. [Para 59][Para 61][Para 63] Because the demand was for termination compensation fixed by the Labour Law, the whole matter was a rights dispute and the award binding. [Para 63][Para 135]

On Ms BS, the panel applied Article 302's three conditions for a collective dispute and the presumption that claims in a non-conciliation report are collective, which the objecting employer must rebut. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 67] The first two conditions were met, but neither party asserted that the dismissal of one worker jeopardised the enterprise or social peace, so the third failed and the dispute was individual. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 74] The panel therefore had no jurisdiction and declined to order either reinstatement or compensation for her. [Para 75][Para 76]

On the eight admittees, the panel held that holding sticks or stones during a strike is not peaceful strike action and itself amounts to violence under Article 330, whether or not injury or damage resulted. [Para 88][Para 91][Para 92][Para 93] It also found a breach of the duty to avoid insecurity at work under Article 83 (B3), the workers having used threats to security as a means of pressing demands. [Para 99][Para 101] Dismissal was lawful, so no notice, indemnity or damages were owed. [Para 102][Para 103] For Mr RT, on a fixed-duration contract, Article 73 allowed termination for serious misconduct before expiry without damages, and the 5 per cent severance is calculated on the contract period actually served, which on a dismissal for misconduct does not extend to the unexpired term. [Para 104][Para 107][Para 111] All eight nonetheless remained entitled to outstanding wages and unused annual leave under Articles 116, 166 and 167. [Para 112]

On Ms CS and Ms SN the panel applied the burden-of-proof rule from earlier awards. [Para 114] The photographs were too small and unclear, and the video too short and indistinct, to identify either worker, and the employer had no other evidence beyond its supervisors' identification. [Para 118][Para 120][Para 121] The dismissals were therefore unlawful and compensation followed: notice under Articles 75 and 77, indemnity under Article 89, equal damages under Article 91, plus annual leave and outstanding wages. [Para 124][Para 125][Para 128][Para 129]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Holding sticks or stones during a strike is itself violence within Article 330 and serious misconduct under Article 83, sufficient for dismissal without notice, indemnity or damages even where no injury or damage is proved, because a lawful strike must be conducted by peaceful means. [Para 88][Para 91][Para 95]

A dismissal is collective only if it could jeopardise the enterprise's operation or social peace; where neither party asserts that, the panel has no jurisdiction over the individual claim. [Para 74][Para 75]

Where photographic or video identification of a striker is unclear, the employer has not discharged its burden and the dismissal is unlawful. [Para 120][Para 122]

**Obiter dicta:** the five per cent severance for a fixed-duration contract is computed on the period actually served, not the unexpired term. [Para 106]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
This is an unofficial English translation and the evidence section is expressly omitted, so no exhibit can be cited. [Para 8][Para 9] Line 10 dates non-conciliation report 1029/13 to 14 August 2012, while lines 3 and 5 put it in 2013 and give two different referral dates (16 September and 14 August 2013). [Para 3][Para 5] Line 125 states Ms CS's seniority as "nine months, one month, and five days" where the 135-day indemnity implies nine years; the same line and line 126 garble "four years" as "our years". [Para 125] The employer's own pleadings cite Article 83 as Point B(4) at line 45 but B(3) at lines 77 and 101. [Para 45] Worker names are spelled inconsistently throughout (Ry Oeung/Ry Eang, Sao Nath/Saov Nath, Bun/Buth Sovanny), which the award itself acknowledges. [Para 13][Para 132] The decision's Part II on interest disputes is empty and marked N/A. [Para 136]
