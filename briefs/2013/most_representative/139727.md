# Award No. 102/13 — Cambo Khoutop (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 19 June 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-chosen and dissenting; An Non, worker-chosen; Kong Phalana, chair) · Doc ID 139727

## 1. Facts
The respondent is a garment factory employing about 2,400 workers, named in the award only in Khmer as ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូ ខូថប. [Para 64] The union, registered on 4 December 2012 under certificate No. 2699 K.B/V.K, is the claimant; the individual claimant Mr A had worked since 26 June 2012, first on a two-month probation and from 25 August 2012 on a contract of undetermined duration, as the accessory-store opener in the warehouse. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 70][Para 71][Para 72] A one-day strike occurred on 11 February 2013; the employer suspended Mr A without pay for a week from 12 to 20 February, then let him return on 21 February but moved him from the store to counting shirt tags, work he had never done. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 75][Para 76] The employer dismissed him on 23 March 2013 for refusing the section head's assignments and leaving the workplace during working hours. [Para 84][Para 85] Of seven non-conciliated demands, issues 2 and 3 were withdrawn and issues 4 and 5 were settled by agreement at the hearing, leaving issues 1, 6 and 7. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back Mr A, a union activist dismissed on 23 March 2013, because the dismissal was anti-union discrimination and he committed no serious misconduct? [Para 118][Para 120]
6. Whether the employer must pay a midday meal allowance of USD 13 a month, it having paid USD 3? [Para 110][Para 153]
7. Whether the employer must add 2,000 riel to the meal allowance for the two overtime hours worked from 4:00 to 6:00 p.m.? [Para 114][Para 167]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissal was connected with anti-union discrimination and the worker committed no serious misconduct; he was a popular activist who had brought 20 to 30 grievances to the administration since the union formed on 2 October 2012, and his transfer to tag-counting was a punishment he was untrained for. [Para 106][Para 108][Para 109] On the money claims, other factories pay USD 13 a month for lunch and wages are insufficient. [Para 111][Para 115]
**Employer (respondent):** it dismissed him because he refused the section head's assignments and left the workplace during working hours, conduct it treats as serious misconduct under the internal rules and Article 83 of the Labour Law; it also says the internal rules nowhere state expressly that refusing to work or walking out is a disciplinary breach, and that its own warning letters were issued for earlier absences. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 94][Para 102] It cannot afford the meal claims. [Para 113][Para 117]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer is ordered to take back Mr A; the demands for a USD 13 monthly lunch allowance and for an extra 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance are both refused consideration. [Para 170][Para 171][Para 172] The award is non-binding and becomes final 8 days after notification if no party objects. [Para 174] Arbitrator Ing Sothy appended a dissenting opinion against the reinstatement order. [Para 180][Para 182]

On issue 1 the panel split the question in two: whether the dismissal was anti-union, and whether the worker committed serious misconduct. [Para 120] On discrimination it applied Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law, which forbid an employer to use union membership or activity as a ground for discipline or termination, recalling Awards 148/07-Baray Hu and 93/06-Evergreen that the party alleging discrimination bears the burden of proof. [Para 120][Para 121][Para 122][Para 125] The workers produced no specific facts or documents beyond asserting his activist role and popularity, so the panel found no sufficient evidence of anti-union discrimination. [Para 129][Para 131]

On misconduct, Article 83(b)(3) of the Labour Law treats a serious breach of disciplinary, safety or hygiene rules as serious misconduct, and Article 10 of the company's registered internal rules sets a graduated scale: oral education for a first light fault, a written warning for a repeat, dismissal only on a third. [Para 132][Para 133][Para 135] The panel followed Award 51/08-ESD that an employer's right to dismiss must be exercised in accordance with the contract, the internal rules and the Labour Law, while noting the employer's concession that its rules do not expressly list refusal to work or walking out as breaches. [Para 140][Para 141][Para 94] It found no written warnings for the 15 and 18 March incidents, and found no specific evidence of serious misconduct, nor that the warnings issued complied with the internal rules and Article 83. [Para 102][Para 142][Para 143]

Because Mr A held a contract of undetermined duration, Article 74 applied: such a contract may be ended at either party's will but only by written notice, and no dismissal may occur without a valid reason relating to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the operational needs of the enterprise. [Para 144][Para 145][Para 146][Para 150] The employer had no such reason, so the panel ordered reinstatement, following Award 56/13-HBO Network. [Para 148][Para 151][Para 152]

On issues 6 and 7 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, agreement between the parties, internal rule or past practice obliging the employer to pay USD 13 a month for lunch or an extra 2,000 riel for overtime hours, so both were interest disputes. [Para 153][Para 167] Applying Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305, it held that it may decide an interest dispute only where the claiming union has most-representative status or the unions together represent more than half the workforce, since an interest award replaces the collective agreement and binds all workers, following Awards 81/04-Evergreen and 169/11-Gordon Thieu Trang. [Para 158][Para 161][Para 163][Para 164] This union is not most representative at the company, so both claims were refused consideration. [Para 165][Para 166][Para 167]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a worker holds a contract of undetermined duration, a dismissal is unlawful unless the employer proves a valid reason under Article 74 going to aptitude, conduct or the operational needs of the enterprise; an allegation of serious misconduct fails where the employer produced no written warnings and its own internal rules do not list the conduct as a breach, and the remedy is reinstatement. [Para 143][Para 146][Para 151][Para 152]

A claim for a meal or overtime allowance that no law, agreement or practice supports is an interest dispute, and the panel will decline to decide it where the claiming union is not the most representative at the enterprise. [Para 154][Para 163][Para 166]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that an interest award takes the place of a collective agreement and thereby removes the workers' right to strike over the same interest demands in future, which is why most-representative status is required before it will rule. [Para 162]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is a Khmer original with heavy OCR damage: the company name is printed only in Khmer and its Latin form here is a transliteration; the signature block at Paragraph 175 collapses into a run of Latin noise ("the mainter the track thathe land …"). [Para 1][Para 175] The worker's name is printed "អំ លាភ" in the Ministry's report and "អំ លៀប" at the hearing, and the panel expressly adopted the latter; he is rendered here as Mr A. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 69] His staff number appears as 2367 and 2357 in different paragraphs. [Para 28][Para 40][Para 84] The award date is 19 June 2013 in the header and at Paragraph 191, while Paragraph 23 records the parties' agreement to extend it "from 14 June 2013 to 19 December June 2013", a date that does not exist as printed. [Para 1][Para 23][Para 191] Paragraph 153 prints the claim as "USD 413 a month" and Paragraph 190 cites "Articles 74, 75, 84, 89, 9166 and 167" of the Labour Law. [Para 153][Para 190] The closing paragraphs of the majority decision and the start of the annex are merged at Paragraph 170. [Para 170]
