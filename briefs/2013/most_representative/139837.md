# Award No. 153/13 — Cambodia Beer Khmer Brew Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 August 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirem, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Kong Phallak, president) · Doc ID 139837

## 1. Facts
On 1 May 2013, 101 beer promoters (ស្ត្រីផ្សព្វផ្សាយស្រាបៀរ) of Cambodia Beer Khmer Brew Co., Ltd — a brewery employing about 2,200 staff — mandated the Cambodian Food and Service Workers' Federation (CFSWF) to represent them in a demand for improved conditions. [Para 83][Para 77] Conciliation failed on 8 of 10 points, and the file reached the Council on 16 July 2013. [Para 24][Para 25] The disputed acts were the employer's refusal to bargain with the federation and eight benefit claims pressed on the promoters' behalf. [Para 89][Para 102]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer discriminated against the union's leadership, worker representatives or federation members by refusing their requests to negotiate? [Para 89]
2. Whether the employer must let beer promoters switch at will, without limit, between commission and fixed-salary pay? [Para 102]
3. Whether the employer must pay a 6,000 riel daily meal allowance to promoters working far from base or outside set hours? [Para 115]
4. Whether the employer must subsidise medical service costs during maternity? [Para 127]
5. Whether the employer must pay USD 30 per month in breast-milk allowance until the child reaches 36 months? [Para 134]
6. Whether the employer must set a basic wage of USD 150 per month for promoters? [Para 143]
7. Whether the employer must fix an annual wage-increase rate of 10% to 20%? [Para 149]
8. Whether the employer must pay a USD 5 per year seniority bonus? [Para 157]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer never answered the federation directly and refused every request, replying only by letter — trade-union discrimination under Article 12 and Article 279 of the Labour Law and the collective-bargaining convention; promoters had lost the pay-type switch since 2007 and earned too little to live on. [Para 95][Para 96][Para 104]
**Employer (respondent):** It keeps the right to bargain with the most representative union, with which it had just concluded a collective agreement and raised basic wages; pay type is a contract term and a management prerogative; no law, agreement or practice requires the meal, medical, milk, seniority or fixed-wage benefits claimed. [Para 3][Para 114][Para 123][Para 160]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all eight claims refused; the order is non-binding (សេចក្តីបង្គាប់មិនជាប់កាតព្វកិច្ច) and becomes final after 8 days without objection. [Para 267][Para 275]

On issue 1, applying Article 12 and Article 279 of the 1997 Labour Law, the panel held that the party alleging discrimination bears the burden of proof, and that a means of communication may take any form, including a letter, so long as it conveys the information. [Para 189][Para 192] The employer did reply in writing; the promoters did not prove that the refusal to bargain amounted to discrimination. [Para 193][Para 194]

On issue 2, Article 311 of the 2007 Civil Code makes contract terms a matter of mutual will, and neither party may impose a change on the other. [Para 196][Para 199] Pay type is a condition of the employment contract, so a promoter who accepted one form cannot switch without the employer's consent. [Para 200][Para 201]

Issues 3 to 6 and 8 were held to be interest disputes (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍): no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice created the claimed entitlement. [Para 205][Para 227][Para 236][Para 242][Para 263] Under Article 96 and Article 9 of Prakas 305/01, an interest award replaces the collective agreement and binds every worker, so Article 43 of Prakas 099 confines the panel to disputes brought by the most representative union. [Para 208][Para 211] The Democratic Union of Angkor Beer Workers was not the most representative union at Khmer Brewery, so the federation lacked standing to bring these claims. [Para 217][Para 218] On maternity the employer already paid 100% wages for 90 days plus a USD 50 fund contribution — above the half-wage floor of Articles 182 and 183 — and the crèche duty in Article 186 did not translate into a milk allowance. [Para 225][Para 226][Para 233] Wage increases had already been granted through the collective agreement with the representative union. [Para 240][Para 241]

On issue 7, Article 2 reserves management of the enterprise to the employer, exercised lawfully and reasonably; the panel found no rule fixing the timing or rate of increases and held the employer's arrangement lawful and reasonable. [Para 249][Para 253][Para 256]

On issue 8, Notification 041/11 by its own terms applies only to garment, textile and footwear workers, not to breweries. [Para 260][Para 261][Para 262]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where granting a claim would produce a collective agreement binding all employees, the panel will only hear it from the union holding most-representative status in the enterprise; a federation acting for a minority union cannot litigate interest claims on its members' behalf. [Para 211][Para 212][Para 218]

**Obiter dicta:** That a written reply is a legally adequate channel of communication whether or not talks follow, and that wage-setting is lawful where exercised lawfully and reasonably, were stated generally rather than as the ground of decision. [Para 192][Para 249]
