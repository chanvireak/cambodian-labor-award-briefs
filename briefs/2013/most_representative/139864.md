# Award No. 188/13 — Dewhirst (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 September 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed, dissenting in part), Tuon Si Phan (worker-appointed), Pen Bunchhea (president) · Doc ID 139864

## 1. Facts
Dewhirst (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a garment factory with about 2,200 workers, dismissed Ms D (union president) and Ms S (vice-president) on 6 August 2013. [Para 67][Para 179] Both held fixed-duration contracts — Ms D twelve months from 13 August 2012, Ms S six months from 13 February 2013 — and their union, registered only on 25 July 2013 under No. 2966, did not hold most representative status. [Para 73][Para 76][Para 70] Eleven demands went to conciliation; four were settled, three withdrawn, and five reached the panel. [Para 25][Para 26]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate Ms D and Ms S, as elected union leaders, and pay back wages and benefits from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 71]
2. Whether the employer must dismiss the seven administrative and security staff said to have threatened the union leadership? [Para 108][Para 297]
3. Whether the employer must issue all workers one-year fixed-duration contracts and give each a copy? — the copy requirement was settled, the one-year term remained. [Para 25][Para 320]
4. Whether the employer must provide two dishes of food at midday? [Para 352]
5. Whether the employer must stop setting production quotas so high that workers labour beyond their strength? [Para 369]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the two were dismissed for union discrimination because they had just been elected leaders of the Khmer Youth Voice Union; the administrative staff and four guards escorted them out, took their identity cards and barred them from collecting their belongings, which shows the employer's hostility. [Para 72][Para 110][Para 308]
**Employer (respondent):** both women were on fixed-duration contracts terminated early on the basis of work evaluations that fell short of factory requirements; the employer did not know they were union leaders, and had it known it would have gone to the Ministry; it also points to having dismissed ten workers that day, not only these two. [Para 83][Para 94][Para 98]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement was refused for both women and the demands on staff dismissals, one-year contracts, two-dish meals and production quotas were all refused; the employer must pay Ms D a contract-end indemnity of five per cent of wages, moral damages of at least the wages she would have earned to the end of the contract, pay in lieu of notice of ten days' wages, final wages and unused annual leave, and must pay Ms S moral damages of at least the wages due to the end of her contract. [Para 391][Para 392][Para 393][Para 394][Para 395][Para 396]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 293 of the Labour Law with Article 4 of Prakas 305, and Awards 40/10 Meng Yang and 154/13 Bou Kheour: candidates elected before registration are protected from the ballot until registration. [Para 168][Para 203][Para 206] It set three conditions — that the worker be a person entitled to protection, that the dismissal fall inside the protected period, and that the union have notified the employer of the protected names by any practicable means. [Para 212] The first two were met: the two were elected on 25 May 2013 and dismissed on 6 August 2013, after registration. [Para 215][Para 224] The third failed: the 27 May 2013 notice carried only the union's own annotation that security had refused it on 28 May, the workers did not ask the post office whether the 30 May mailing arrived, and the panel treated that as careless indifference rather than notice. [Para 229][Para 234][Para 237]

On discrimination the panel held the claimant bears the burden and had not discharged it, so no union discrimination was found. [Para 248][Para 253] Since no protection attached, and because a fixed-duration contract may be ended early on payment of moral damages rather than reinstatement, the employer owed no reinstatement but did owe the statutory payments. [Para 257][Para 262]

On issue 2 the panel held that only the parties to a contract of employment may terminate it; a third party may not demand another worker's dismissal, and the workers proved no threat. [Para 302][Para 305][Para 317] On issues 3 and 5, freedom of contract and the employer's lawful right to manage under Article 2 meant neither a one-year term nor a lower quota could be imposed; the quota system was verified by industrial engineering and carried no sanction for under-performance. [Para 344][Para 378][Para 383] On issue 4 the demand was an interest dispute and the union lacked most representative status, so the panel declined to consider it. [Para 355][Para 367]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The protection extended to union election candidates and leaders depends on the union having notified the employer of the protected names before the dismissal; a letter the union merely annotated as refused, with no proof of delivery, leaves both the Article 293 protection and a union-discrimination claim unestablished. [Para 212][Para 237]

**Obiter dicta:** no party to an employment contract may ask the panel to compel the employer to dismiss a third worker unless it proves that worker is dangerous to the safety of others. [Para 305]

[Para 193][Para 241] Paragraph 27 states four remaining issues but lists five.
