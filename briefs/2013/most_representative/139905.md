# Award No. 252/13 — M & V International Manufacturing Ltd (Branch 3) (Arbitration Council, 16 December 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 139905

## 1. Facts
M & V International Manufacturing Ltd, Branch 3 (M & V3), is a garment manufacturer on National Road 2, Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employing 3,300 workers. [Para 1][Para 15][Para 16] Its local union of C.CAWDU held a registration certificate dated 13 September 2006 and a Ministry letter of 28 March 2011 recognising its second-term leaders: Mr Ak Chan (president), Mr K Choeung (vice-president) and Mr S Heng (secretary). [Para 16][Para 45] The union does not hold most representative status. [Para 17] Six demands on allowances, holiday bonuses and overtime meals were not conciliated; the workers withdrew Issue 6, leaving five. [Para 4][Para 6][Para 37]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must provide a US$5 living allowance to all piece rate workers? [Para 18][Para 77]
2. Whether the employer must provide a 4,000 riel payment in lieu of lunch to each worker? [Para 21][Para 81]
3. Whether the employer must provide a US$3 incentive bonus to workers working on holidays? [Para 24][Para 86]
4. Whether the employer must provide an additional US$5 accommodation and transportation allowance on top of an existing US$7 allowance? [Para 29][Para 92]
5. Whether the employer must provide a 4,000 riel overtime allowance for overtime from 4:30 p.m. to 6:30 p.m. and a 6,000 riel overtime allowance for overtime from 6:30 p.m. to 8:30 p.m.? [Para 32][Para 101]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer paid a US$5 living allowance to piece rate workers until May 2013 and then stopped it, while piece rates keep pay at or barely above the minimum wage and food prices rise; wages are too low for nutritious food, causing fainting; the US$2 holiday bonus dates from a US$61 base wage and should follow the rise to US$80; transport now costs US$12 a month against US$8 to US$9 before; and overtime meals cost 2,000 to 3,000 riel and are not clean. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 22][Para 23][Para 27][Para 28][Para 30][Para 34]
**Employer (respondent):** it cannot afford the demands and will comply with the Ministry notifications — No. 103 of 9 April 2013 on the living allowance, No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 on meals or meal allowance, and No. 230 of 25 July 2012 on accommodation and transport. [Para 20][Para 23][Para 31][Para 65] It already pays US$2 per month to holiday volunteers, provides instant noodles to workers staying until 8 p.m., and pays 2,500 and 3,000 riel overtime allowances under Point 4 of the collective agreement of 25 June 2013. [Para 24][Para 35][Para 36][Para 96]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the rights-dispute part is N/A and all five demands were declined for consideration as interests disputes; the award is non-binding and becomes binding eight days after notification unless a party lodges written opposition with the Minister. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 105]

The panel began with standing, not the merits. Although four workers attended, only Mr Ak Chan and Mr K Choeung claimed to be union leaders, and no evidence showed recognition in the third term. [Para 38][Para 39] Applying Article 268 of the Labour Law, the panel held that an organisation enjoys the rights and benefits of the law only once registered and recognised by the Ministry, and that these include the right to represent members before the Council, following Awards No. 62/06-Quick Sew, 31/08-South Bay and 94/09-Tack Fat. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 43] Under Paragraph 2, Clause 3 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 a leader's term may not exceed two years, so the second-term recognition of 28 March 2011 had expired on 28 March 2013, before the hearing, and no third-term election was proved. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46] The four workers were therefore not legitimate leaders and had no right to represent the membership; since no other worker had authorised them in writing as Clause 19 of Prakas No. 099 requires, the panel resolved the dispute for those four workers only. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51][Para 52][Para 53]

On classification, the panel restated that Paragraph 2 of Article 312 of the Labour Law gives it legal jurisdiction over disputes about the interpretation and enforcement of laws, regulations or a collective agreement, and equity jurisdiction over all others. [Para 53][Para 56][Para 57] Each demand was then tested for a legal source. Issue 1 failed because the workers did not prove when the US$5 allowance was paid or why it stopped, and the employer did not prove that Notification No. 103 mentions a living allowance; no law, agreement, internal rule or past practice obliged it, so the demand exceeded the legal minimum and was an interests dispute. [Para 63][Para 66][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70][Para 71][Para 72] Issues 2 to 5 were treated the same way, each with the finding that no instrument obliged the employer and that its existing practice was better than the notification: US$2 rather than the demanded US$3, US$7 accommodation and transport already paid, and 2,500 and 3,000 riel overtime allowances above the 2,000 riel meal allowance in Notification No. 041/11. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 83][Para 84][Para 87][Para 88][Para 93][Para 95][Para 96]

Because an interests award replaces the collective agreement for one year and binds all workers, stripping their right to strike, the panel held it can entertain such a demand only from a union with most representative status or one representing more than half the workforce, following Awards No. 81/04-Evergreen, 98/04-Great Union, 169/11-Fortune Teo, 02/11-Pou Yuen and 66/1-In Han Sung. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 75] The four claimants did not meet that requirement, so every interests demand was declined. [Para 76][Para 77]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The Council will decline to consider an interests dispute unless the claimant union holds most representative status in the enterprise or represents more than half its workers, because an interests award displaces the collective agreement for a year and binds non-members; and a union leader whose two-year term has expired without a proved re-election cannot represent members, so the case proceeds only for the individual workers present and authorised in writing. [Para 45][Para 49][Para 51][Para 75][Para 76]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's reading that Notification No. 032 of 2008 was superseded by No. 049 of 2010, and its remark that the employer's practice of paying more than the notification is "better than what is mandated by law", are general observations; the demands were declined on standing and status, not on the notifications' content. [Para 61][Para 84][Para 95]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The document is an unofficial English translation; the evidence section is expressly omitted and the reader is referred to the Khmer version. [Para 12][Para 13]
- OCR has fused articles and prepositions into following words throughout ("themployer", "thearing", "withe", "tworkers", "outling", "bargaing", "foregistration"), and dropped characters ("No." rendered "o.", "No. 103" as "Notificationo. 103").
- The union's secretary is "Mr S Hong" at [Para 16] but "Mr S Heng" at [Para 45]; the vice-president appears as "Kim Choeung", "Kim Cheung" and "Kim Choeun", and a worker as "Soen Chamroeun" and "Soeun Chamroeun". [Para 16][Para 17][Para 45][Para 47]
- The panel calls the US$5 living allowance "more than what is mandated by law" although the notification it discusses set the figure at US$6, and it holds that the allowance is additional to base wage while also recording that the 2010 notification folded it into base wage. [Para 59][Para 63][Para 71]
- Issue 5 is stated in the demand as 4,000 and 6,000 riel, while the employer's practice is 2,500 and 3,000 riel and the shortfall is described as 1,500 and 500 riel; the demand figure and the shortfall figure cannot both be right. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 105]
- The overtime window is given as "4:30 p.m. to 6:30 p.m." in the demand but "4:30 p.m. to 6 p.m." in Point 4 of the collective agreement. [Para 32][Para 36]
- The award date is 16 December 2013 in the header, while the hearing was 29 November 2013 and the non-conciliation report 22 November 2013. [Para 1][Para 3][Para 5]
