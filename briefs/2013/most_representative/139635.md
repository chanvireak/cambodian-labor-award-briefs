# Award No. 28/13 — Tek Fouk (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 20 February 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Nan (worker-appointed), Kong Pollak (president) · Doc ID 139635

## 1. Facts
Tek Fouk (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd is a cut-and-sew garment factory at Anlong Tnaot, Kampong Chhnang, employing about 5,700 workers. [Para 68][Para 69] On 11 January 2013 the Cambodian Workers' Spirit Federation lodged 17 collective demands; conciliation at the provincial labour department failed on 23 January 2013 and the file came to the Council. [Para 24][Para 21] The employer had already met eight of the 17 points (1, 2, 6, 9, 10, 13, 16, 17), leaving nine in dispute (3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 11, 12, 14, 15). [Para 29][Para 31] The award states that neither party agreed to be bound, so it is non-binding. [Para 32][Para 231]

## 2. Issues in dispute
3. Whether the employer must pay 5,000 riel a day for lunch, i.e. add 3,000 riel to the 2,000 riel now paid? [Para 79]
4. Whether the employer must raise the base wage to USD 70 a month, adding USD 9 to the USD 61 now paid? [Para 83]
5. Whether the employer must supply three cans of milk a month to female workers instead of nursery care, until the child is 18 months old? [Para 87]
7. Whether the employer must deduct the regular attendance bonus pro rata to the days of authorised absence? [Para 94]
8. Whether the employer must replace a lost swipe card free of charge? [Para 101]
11. Whether the employer must allow one day a month for antenatal checks with wage and bonus preserved? [Para 114]
12. Whether the employer must let pregnant workers leave 15 minutes before the shift ends? [Para 118]
14. Whether the employer must replace vests with shirts, supplying two shirts every three months? [Para 195]
15. Whether the employer must supply three additional scarves to each female worker? [Para 214]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** wages are too low and food has become dearer, so the meal allowance should rise by 3,000 riel and the base wage by USD 9; the factory nursery is impractical because of long, crowded commutes, so milk should be given instead; pregnant workers need a full day for hospital queues and 15 minutes to reach the gate. [Para 81][Para 85][Para 89][Para 116][Para 121]
**Employer (respondent):** it has no capacity to meet the demands; a nursery already exists in the factory, so Art 186 is satisfied; the 3,000 riel charge for a lost swipe card is a lawful exercise of management rights explained at induction; the vests supplied meet Prakas 307 and the workers produced no evidence of unfit size. [Para 82][Para 91][Para 108][Para 178][Para 206]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — on every one of the nine remaining points the panel refused to consider the claim; nothing was ordered in riel or USD, and the award is expressly non-binding. [Para 221][Para 231]

The panel began with a threshold of standing. An award on an interest dispute becomes a collective agreement binding all workers in the enterprise for one year, and so may only be made where the union bringing it is the most representative, holding more than half the workforce; awards 81/04 and 98/04 were followed, and 4169/11, 02/11 and 66/11 applied. [Para 142][Para 143][Para 144][Para 145] Here the federation represented a factory union that had not yet been registered and was not most representative, so the panel held it lacked the legal character to bring an interest dispute at all. [Para 147][Para 148]

On the meal allowance the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement or agreement between the parties fixing an extra 3,000 riel, and classified the claim as one of interest. [Para 137][Para 149] On the base wage it found that Prakas 049/10 of 9 July 2010 fixed the minimum at USD 61 for workers past probation, that the employer paid USD 61, and that nothing required USD 70 — a demand exceeding the law is again an interest claim. [Para 150][Para 151][Para 154][Para 155] On milk, Art 186 requires enterprises employing at least 100 women to provide a nursing room and nursery, allowing a care allowance only where no nursery can be arranged for children over 18 months; the factory had one, so the claim failed. [Para 158][Para 161][Para 165]

On the attendance bonus no claimant was identified among those who had thumbprinted the claim, so there was no one before the panel demanding it. [Para 168][Para 169][Para 170] On the swipe card Art 2 of the Labour Law was read as conferring management rights exercised lawfully and reasonably, internal rules were silent, the worker owes a duty to keep the card, and accidental loss cannot shift the cost to the employer. [Para 174][Para 179][Para 182][Para 183] The antenatal day and the 15-minute early departure were both refused for want of any legal or agreed basis. [Para 186][Para 188][Para 190][Para 194]

On clothing the panel held Prakas 307 Art 6 obliges the employer to give two sets a year, that a set may consist of trousers and shirt or simply a vest of fitting size, and that workers asserting unfitness bore the burden of proof, which they did not discharge. [Para 195][Para 198][Para 207][Para 208] On scarves Prakas 307 says nothing, and the employer's practice of issuing one headscarf and replacing it when damaged satisfied safety needs. [Para 214][Para 215][Para 217][Para 220]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that has not been registered and is not the most representative in the enterprise cannot bring an interest dispute to the panel; because an award on interests would bind the whole workforce as a collective agreement, the panel will decline to consider the claim whatever its merits. [Para 143][Para 144][Para 148]

**Obiter dicta:** that workers may ask to exchange a worn scarf and may discuss a payroll calculation with the company; that a set of work clothing need not be split into two parts. [Para 217][Para 238][Para 198]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The order on the wage claim is printed two ways: the summary at para 157 says the panel "rejects consideration" yet orders the employer to add USD 9 to the USD 61 base, while the operative order at para 222 refuses the claim for an additional USD 49. [Para 157][Para 222] The operative order on milk reads "three cans in one year month" (para 223), and on scarves reads "scarves, quantity 0" where the claim was three (para 230). [Para 223][Para 230] Prakas 307 is dated "14 December 2023 2007" in the reasons at para 216. [Para 216] The thumbprint count does not reconcile: 73 workers thumbprinted the claim, 52 thumbprinted withdrawals (two duplicated, six bearing no name on the claim), yet 28 claimants are said to remain. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 73] The internal rules appear as made 7 October 2008 and registered 275/08 on 27 November 2008 in the reasons, but as dated 27 November 2008 in the evidence list. [Para 111][Para 51] OCR losses in the header and party block (company name and address partly garbled, "GMAC" officers' titles run together).
