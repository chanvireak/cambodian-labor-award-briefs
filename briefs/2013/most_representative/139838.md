# Award No. 155/13 — Kart (KARTAT EMBROIDERY) (Arbitration Council, 14 August 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Kong Phallak, president) · Doc ID 139838

## 1. Facts
Kart (KARTAT EMBROIDERY), an embroidery factory in Phnom Penh employing 380 workers, faced eleven demands from the Khmer Youth Voice Union and its federation after conciliation failed on 15 July 2013. [Para 37][Para 20] The factory-level union had no registration certificate, but 14 workers signed a letter of 14 June 2013 authorising the federation president to act for them. [Para 38][Para 86] The employer did not appear at the hearing and gave no reason. [Para 24][Para 77]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay 100% of basic wage to night-shift workers for 9 October 2012, when it sent them home after two hours for lack of work? [Para 43]
2. Whether the employer must provide one free meal and one can of orange juice to night-shift workers? [Para 49]
3. Whether the employer must station a nurse and doctor on duty at all working hours and supply adequate medicine? [Para 50]
4. Whether the employer must preserve wages and the attendance bonus when a worker is absent sick with a valid medical certificate? [Para 52]
5. Whether the employer must restore the previous format of the monthly payslip? [Para 57]
6. Whether the employer must build a crèche and nursing room on site? [Para 59]
7. Whether the employer must install water tanks in the production building and a television in the canteen? [Para 64]
8. Whether the employer must let pregnant workers leave 15 minutes early? [Para 65]
9. Whether the employer must make the procedure for requesting leave more accommodating? [Para 68]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** On 9 October 2012 the night shift worked from 7 pm, was sent home at 9 pm for want of work, was marked absent and paid for two hours only, with no authorisation sought from the labour inspector; the factory had no nurse, no medicine and no crèche, and other factories in the group had them. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 51][Para 60]
**Employer (respondent):** The employer did not appear at the hearing and stated no reason for its absence, so it advanced no position on any of the nine claims; the panel therefore proceeded on the workers' evidence and the conciliation record alone. [Para 77][Para 78]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — claims 1 and 3 were ordered; claims 2 and 4 to 9 were refused. The order is non-binding and becomes final after 8 days without objection. [Para 198][Para 208]

On claim 1 the panel applied Article 118(1) of the Labour Law, which puts the burden of proving that wages were paid on the employer. [Para 97][Para 98] Because the employer was absent and offered nothing, the panel accepted the workers' account and then asked whether the suspension was lawful. [Para 100][Para 101] Article 72(1) removes the wage obligation during a suspension, but only where the suspension itself follows legal procedure; Article 71(XI) permits suspension for serious economic or material difficulty only under the labour inspector's supervision and for no more than two months. [Para 102][Para 104][Para 105] Following Awards 58/07-Star Sport Wear and 08/07-Siv Kyin, the panel read that as requiring prior authorisation from the labour inspector. [Para 107][Para 108] No authorisation was sought, so Article 72(1) did not apply and the employer owed 100% of wages for 9 October 2012, excluding the two hours actually worked. [Para 114][Para 116][Para 117]

On claim 3 the panel applied Articles 238 and 242 of the Labour Law, which require primary medical care on site and a permanent infirmary with a doctor and at least one nurse for enterprises of fifty or more, plus Prakas 330 of 6 December 2000, Article 3 of which scales staffing to headcount. [Para 135][Para 136][Para 141] With 380 workers the employer needed one nurse on duty and a doctor present at least two hours per working day, and Article 4 required adequate medicine. [Para 145][Para 146][Para 147] The employer had neither, so it was ordered to appoint them and to stock medicine per the Prakas annex. [Para 149][Para 150]

Claim 2 was held to be an interest dispute with no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice behind it. [Para 120][Para 121] Under Article 96 and Article 9 of Prakas 305/01, an interest award substitutes for the collective agreement and binds all workers, so under Article 43 of Prakas 099 only the most representative union may bring such a claim. [Para 124][Para 126][Para 127][Para 129] The 14 individual complainants were not a representative union, so the panel declined jurisdiction. [Para 131][Para 132][Para 133] Claim 7 was disposed of on the same ground. [Para 184][Para 186]

Claim 4 failed because the workers conceded the claim was for the future and could not name anyone whose wages had actually been cut. [Para 154][Para 155] Following Award 10/03-Zaksin, the panel held it cannot decide a dispute that has not yet arisen. [Para 155][Para 158]

Claim 5 failed on evidence: the workers asserted the payslip format had changed but never produced an earlier payslip, and the burden lay on them. [Para 163][Para 164][Para 168]

Claim 6 failed on Article 186, which obliges a crèche and nursing room only where the enterprise employs at least one hundred women. [Para 169][Para 174] The 380 figure covered the whole company, while the building in issue held about 80 workers of whom 50 to 60 were women, and the workers could not state the female headcount of the second building. [Para 175][Para 177][Para 178]

Claims 8 and 9 failed because no specific claimant was identified — the workers could not say how many of the complainants were pregnant or which of them had been refused leave. [Para 189][Para 190][Para 195][Para 196]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of the employment contract relieves the employer of wages under Article 72(1) only where the employer first obtained the labour inspector's authorisation and kept the suspension within two months; an unauthorised suspension leaves the full wage obligation intact. [Para 105][Para 111][Para 116]

**Obiter dicta:** That the panel cannot adjudicate a dispute that has not yet arisen, and that an unregistered factory union cannot represent its members, were stated as general limits on the panel's function rather than as the operative ground of any order. [Para 85][Para 158]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel records nine claims under adjudication but lists only eight numbers, omitting claim 3 from the enumeration, while deciding all nine. [Para 26] The date of the disputed suspension prints as 9 October 2012 in the reasons and as "9 September October 2012" in one passage. [Para 43][Para 112] The union's status is stated three ways: that it had no registration, that it received a registration receipt on 12 June 2013, and that it was not yet registered. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 79] The company is named "Kart" in the case title and "KARTAT EMBROIDERY" in the exhibits. [Para 27] The Prakas 330 staffing table is badly garbled by OCR, with column values running into one another. [Para 141]
