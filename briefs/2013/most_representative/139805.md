# Award No. 110/13 — Cambodia Hoi Fu Garment & Knitting Factory (Arbitration Council, 24 June 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kol Vuthana, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Kong Phalha, chair) · Doc ID 139805

## 1. Facts
Cambodia Hoi Fu is a weaving and garment factory that opened in 1999 and employs about 500 workers. [Para 47][Para 48] The Cambodia Workers' Union brought eighteen demands on maternity pay, travel and meal allowances, annual leave, medical supplies, overtime rates, wage arrears and union-dues deduction. [Para 2][Para 6][Para 11][Para 19] Conciliation left eighteen issues unresolved, and at the hearing fifteen were settled, leaving Issues 10, 17 and 18. [Para 26][Para 32] The union filed no registration certificate, no membership list and no written authorisation from the workers, so the panel held that only the four named workers who appeared could be claimants. [Para 51][Para 119][Para 124]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a 3,000 riel meal allowance to workers who work on public holidays and on Sundays? [Para 11][Para 71]
2. Whether the employer must pay a 3,000 riel daily lunch allowance to every worker? [Para 18][Para 190]
3. Whether the employer must deduct union dues from members' wages and pay them to the union? [Para 19][Para 194]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The Labour Law requires a meal allowance for holiday and Sunday work; a neighbouring factory pays 2,000 riel, and they ask 3,000; deductions for union dues were routine before the accounts staff changed. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 96][Para 100] At the hearing the workers also asserted the union held most-representative status. [Para 77]
**Employer (respondent):** It already pays double wages for Sunday and holiday work but no meal allowance, and it earns little profit, so it cannot meet the demands. [Para 74][Para 75][Para 76] It stopped deducting dues because only one wages clerk remains and splitting 1,000-riel notes risks error. [Para 101][Para 102][Para 103]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay a 2,000 riel meal allowance per day to workers who voluntarily work on Sundays, and declined every other demand; no other sum was ordered. [Para 181][Para 189][Para 211]

Jurisdiction and parties: Under Article 268 of the Labour Law and clause 19 of Prakas No. 099, a union acquires legal personality and the right to represent its members only on registration, and a representative needs written authority. [Para 110][Para 112][Para 117][Para 118] No such documents were filed, so the panel could not presume the union's status and limited the award to the four workers present, Mr C D, Mr A S V, Mr V S and Mr P R (names redacted). [Para 119][Para 123][Para 124]

Issue 10 was split in two. On public holidays, following Articles 137 and 164, Article 139 (2007 amendment) and clauses 2 and 4 of Prakas No. 10 of 1999, and Award No. 115/08-Thap Vann, the panel held that holiday work is not overtime: the worker earns a compensatory day's wage, not an overtime premium, so Notification No. 041/11 on meal allowances does not apply. [Para 141][Para 144][Para 147][Para 149] No law, agreement or practice requires 3,000 riel, so this limb is an interests dispute. [Para 151][Para 152] On Sundays, work beyond the 48-hour week is overtime under Article 137 and Award No. 114/08-Way Thich, so clause 2 of Notification No. 041/11 obliges the employer to pay a 2,000 riel meal allowance or a free meal whenever overtime is worked. [Para 172][Para 176][Para 177] The employer paid nothing, so its practice was unlawful and it was ordered to pay 2,000 riel per Sunday overtime day; the extra 1,000 riel claimed had no legal basis and was declined. [Para 179][Para 181][Para 184][Para 188]

Issue 17: No law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice requires a 3,000 riel daily lunch allowance, so the demand is an interests dispute. [Para 190][Para 191][Para 193]

Issue 18: Article 129(2) lets workers authorise in writing the deduction of union dues, revocably, and clause 50(5) of Prakas No. 305 requires the request at least 15 days before payday; following Award No. 62/04-ISEAN, the union must produce a signed list or thumbprinted consent. [Para 194][Para 201][Para 203] This is a rights dispute, but no registration certificate, membership list or consent list was filed, so the panel had no factual basis to decide and declined to consider it. [Para 200][Para 208][Para 209]

Interests issues: the union asserted most-representative status but filed no certificate, so under clause 43 of Prakas No. 099 and Awards 81/04-Evergreen, 98/04-Great Union, 169/11-Fortune Teo, 02/11-Pou Yuen and 66/11-In Han Sung the panel could not presume it and declined the interests demands. [Para 155][Para 158][Para 163][Para 164][Para 165] Part I is binding under the MoU of 3 October 2012; Part II becomes binding eight days after notification absent written opposition. [Para 213][Para 217]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Work performed on a public holiday is not overtime, so an allowance that attaches only to overtime does not apply to it; work on a Sunday beyond the normal 48-hour week is overtime, and the employer owes the meal allowance fixed by Notification No. 041/11 for each such day, whatever the workers demand above that figure. [Para 144][Para 149][Para 176][Para 177] A union that files neither its registration certificate nor written authority from the workers cannot represent them, and only the workers who appear in person are parties. [Para 118][Para 124]

**Obiter dicta:** A demand above the statutory minimum is an interests dispute even when a lesser version of it is a rights claim, which is why the same 3,000 riel demand was granted at 2,000 riel on Sundays and refused entirely on holidays. [Para 152][Para 184] The panel also observed that splitting 1,000-riel notes is not a lawful excuse for refusing a duly authorised dues deduction. [Para 102][Para 201]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The Khmer text is heavily degraded OCR with duplicated clauses and interleaved Latin fragments ("wontingerpritencents", "prodernombing") inside sentences 70, 92 and 166, and numbers corrupted into words ("ថ្ងៃទី ២២ អាទិត្យ"). [Para 70][Para 92][Para 132] The employer is named in Khmer as ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូឌា ហយ ហ្វ៊ូ and once in Latin as "Cambodia Hoi Fu Garment & Knitting Factory"; the Khmer is transliterated above and neither form is expanded. [Para 1][Para 41] The chair arbitrator prints as គង់ ផល្ហៈ in the header and as គង់ ផល្លៈ and គង់ ឥស្ទះ at the signatures. [Para 1][Para 218] The panel is said to have been constituted on "៣១ ខែឧសភា ឆ្នាំ ២០២៣ ២០១៣", year printed twice. [Para 28] Sentence 31 says fifteen of eighteen issues were settled but enumerates only fourteen (1–9, 12–16); Issue 11 appears in neither the settled nor the remaining list. [Para 31][Para 32] Issue 10 is listed under both the rights and the interests headings of the decision because it was split, so its two dispositions must be read together. [Para 211][Para 214] The union's four representatives and the individual claimants are named in the source and are redacted here.
