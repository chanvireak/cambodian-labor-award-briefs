# Award No. 229/13 — Isound Cable Cam Bo Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 14 November 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ma Sam Bora Na, employer-chosen; An Non, worker-chosen; Kong Palha, chair) · Doc ID 139889

## 1. Facts
Isound Cable Cam Bo Co., Ltd. assembles electrical and electronic equipment and currently makes telephone cases at Romdual village, Trapeang Kong commune, Samrong Tong district, Kampong Speu, employing about 1,500 workers. [Para 45][Para 46] Nine demands went to conciliation under report No. 1415/13 of 17 October 2013 after a strike, and the file reached the Council on 18 October 2013; the hearing was on 30 October 2013. [Para 14][Para 21][Para 16] At the hearing issues 2, 4 and part of issue 3 were settled, leaving issues 1, 3 (childbirth allowance), 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9 for decision. [Para 25][Para 26] The claimants were four male workers represented by the Free Workers' Union of Cambodia; the Independent Workers' Spirit Union held the certificate of most-representative status at the plant. [Para 52][Para 157][Para 165]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must continue paying a US$5 monthly health allowance on top of the base wage after the May 2013 minimum-wage increase? [Para 56]
3. Whether the employer must pay US$150 towards childbirth costs for pregnant workers delivering at a public maternity or private clinic? [Para 63]
5. Whether the employer must pay a US$15 monthly attendance bonus, i.e. US$5 more than the US$10 currently paid? [Para 68]
6. Whether the employer must deduct the attendance bonus pro rata to the days a worker takes lawfully authorised leave? [Para 179]
7. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr S B, a probationary worker and alleged deputy union president, to normal work? [Para 186]
8. Whether the employer must pay US$12 a month in travel and accommodation allowance, i.e. US$2 more than the US$10 paid? [Para 119]
9. Whether the employer must keep the full attendance bonus for workers absent on sick leave supported by a properly stamped medical certificate? [Para 125]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** other factories still pay the US$5 health allowance separately after the minimum-wage increase, and other factories pay more than US$10 in travel allowance and US$15 in attendance bonus; the employer should therefore match those practices and should not deduct the attendance bonus for sickness or lawful leave. [Para 57][Para 70][Para 121]
**Employer (respondent):** the US$5 health allowance was folded into the minimum wage by Notification No. 103 and the company is not in the garment or footwear sector anyway; it pays a US$10 attendance bonus and US$10 travel allowance, which already exceed the law; and Mr S B was a probationary worker whose contract simply ended. [Para 59][Para 61][Para 72][Para 89]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — every remaining claim was refused, and the award is a non-binding one. [Para 207][Para 208]
The panel first addressed standing. It held that only a union representing more than half the workforce may bring an interest dispute, and that the claimant union here represented only four workers, so the interest-dispute claims could not be entertained. [Para 152][Para 159][Para 160] It then construed the sectoral notifications: Notification No. 103 of 9 April 2013 merged the US$5 health allowance into the minimum wage for garment, textile and footwear workers, and Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 on attendance bonus, travel allowance and seniority applies only to those sectors. [Para 133][Para 137][Para 170][Para 172] Because the company assembles electrical and electronic goods rather than garments, the notifications do not bind it, but its own practice — US$10 attendance bonus, US$10 travel and accommodation allowance, US$2,000 riel lunch money and free meals — is more favourable than the law, so no further obligation arises. [Para 174][Para 181][Para 262][Para 263] On issue 3, all four claimants were men, so there was no pregnant claimant to support a childbirth-cost order. [Para 165][Para 166]
On issue 7 the panel applied articles 12, 279 and 293 of the Labour Law and articles 3 and 4 of Prakas No. 305. Probationary workers dismissed during probation need no reason; the protection for union leaders requires that the worker be a candidate or elected leader and that the union have notified the employer, and the claimant produced no evidence that Mr S B had been elected deputy president or that the employer knew of it before the 20 September 2013 termination. [Para 186][Para 189][Para 214][Para 215][Para 227][Para 231] It also found no proof of anti-union discrimination and noted that the only union leader dismissed was the claimant while the others continued working. [Para 241][Para 245][Para 254]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that represents only a small minority of the workforce cannot bring an interest dispute to the Arbitration Council, and the sectoral benefit notifications (Nos. 103 and 230) bind only garment, textile and footwear employers; where a factory outside those sectors already pays more than the statutory minimum, no further award will be made. A probationary worker claiming the special protection of a union leader must prove election and that the employer was notified of the candidacy before dismissal. [Para 152][Para 172][Para 231]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded the employer's instructions to two workers not to involve themselves with unions, and its concern that multiple unions would cause conflict, but held this insufficient to establish anti-union discrimination. [Para 236][Para 241]
