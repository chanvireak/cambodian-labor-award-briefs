# Award No. 117/13 — Tung Chao Enterprise (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 1 July 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Run Saray presiding; Ing Sothy, employer nominee; An Nan, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139807

## 1. Facts
Tung Chao Enterprise (Cambodia) is a garment factory at Beong Salang village, Toul Sangke commune, Reusey Keo district, Phnom Penh, registered under Inv. 2328 of 22 September 2011 and employing about 1,115 workers. [Para 2][Para 21] The workers filed eight demands; two were settled at conciliation on 4 June 2013 and four more were settled at the hearing of 20 June 2013, leaving two for decision. [Para 15][Para 17] Three unions claimed a role in the case — the local unions of CWEUF, CLIFU and CLKFU — and none held a certificate of most representative status in the enterprise. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 48]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must provide lunch, or a payment of 5,000 riel a day in lieu of lunch, to each worker? [Para 5][Para 40][Para 41]
2. Whether the employer must pay USD 25 a month in lieu of milk formula for babies younger than 18 months? [Para 6][Para 50][Para 53]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the demand for lunch money went to livelihood, since wages are low and house rents are rising, and the sum is needed to buy lunch; the demand for milk money went to the cost of formula at USD 15 to USD 20 a can, and to the danger of sending children to the day care centre on a crowded truck; the union also relied on its notification No. 123/13 of 10 June 2013, which recorded that newly elected candidates and committee members were under legal protection from discrimination. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 29][Para 53]
**Employer (respondent):** it could not afford either demand; it already paid a USD 9 monthly accommodation and transport allowance beyond what the law requires, had just raised the base wage, and provided a day care centre for children aged 18 months to 3 years, a nursing room for mothers returning from maternity leave, and one hour's breastfeeding break a day, none of which the workers were using. [Para 28][Para 29][Para 30][Para 52]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** declined to consider — both remaining demands were interests disputes and were not decided, the award on them becoming binding eight days after notification unless a party lodged written opposition with the Minister. [Para 50][Para 55][Para 57][Para 58]

The panel first fixed who could appear. Article 268 of the Labour Law gives a professional organisation the rights the law recognises only once its statutes and list of officers are filed with the ministry, silence for two months counting as registration; previous awards read that as including the right to represent members before the panel. [Para 31][Para 32] The CWEUF local never filed its registration certificate, and its notification of 10 June 2013 showed its new leaders were still awaiting recognition after the election of 9 June 2013, so the panel held it was not a professional organisation and its three elected candidates could not represent members. [Para 24][Para 33][Para 34][Para 35] Under Article 19 of Prakas 099 a representative needs express written authorisation, and no authorisation letter from other workers had been filed, so the two workers present, Ms M and Ms V, could speak only for themselves. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 37][Para 38][Para 39] The panel accordingly treated the dispute as brought by those two workers and by the CLIFU local, which by law could represent its own members. [Para 40]

On issue 1 it asked whether the demand arose from a right or an interest. It found nothing in the Labour Law, in agreements, collective agreements, internal rules or past practice obliging the employer to provide lunch or 5,000 riel a day, and classified the demand as an interests dispute. [Para 42][Para 43] On issue 2 it made the same finding on the same material: no provision or practice obliged the employer to pay USD 25 a month in lieu of formula for babies under 18 months, so that demand too was an interests dispute. [Para 52][Para 54][Para 55]

That classification decided the case, because an interests dispute is decided in equity and, under clause 43 of Prakas 099, an award on one takes the place of a collective agreement for a year and binds every worker in the enterprise, which also removes their right to strike over interests in future. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47] The panel therefore required the claimant union to hold most representative status, or the claiming unions together to represent more than half the workforce, and it had declined interests disputes on that ground in earlier cases. [Para 43][Para 48] The CLKFU local, named as claimant, held no such certificate, so neither it nor the two workers had the right to bring an interests dispute, and the panel declined to consider both demands. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 57]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a benefit that no law, agreement, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice requires is an interests dispute, and the panel will decline to consider it unless the union bringing it holds most representative status in the enterprise, or the unions together represent more than half the workforce; two individual workers, however present at the hearing, cannot supply that standing. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 48][Para 49]

The reason for that bar is that an award on an interests dispute replaces the collective agreement for a year and binds the whole workforce while extinguishing its right to strike over interests, so it may only be made at the instance of a body entitled to speak for that workforce. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that the employer already ran a day care centre for children aged 18 months to 3 years, a nursing room and a daily breastfeeding hour, and that the workers were not using them — facts it noted but did not weigh, since it declined jurisdiction on both demands. [Para 29][Para 52]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
This is an unofficial English translation, and the OCR has dropped a letter throughout: "themployer", "athearing", "tworkers", "threelected", "babyounger", "here" for "there", and "righto" for "right to", so quotations must be read with that substitution. [Para 5][Para 26][Para 34][Para 38][Para 40] The award is inconsistent about which unions are the claimants: one passage names the CLIFU and CLKFU locals, another names CWEUF and CLIFU, and the reasoning declines the claim because "the union of CLKFU" lacks most representative status while an earlier passage holds that the CLIFU local may represent its members. [Para 21][Para 30][Para 40][Para 48] The chair is printed "Run Saray" on the face of the award and "Ruy Saray" in the signature block. [Para 2][Para 58] The Prakas on the appointment of arbitrators is rendered "(Ten Term)" where the tenth term is meant. [Para 12] The evidence section is expressly omitted from this version, so the documents the panel reviewed cannot be checked. [Para 20][Para 21] The notification of 10 June 2013 is quoted twice in differently truncated form. [Para 26][Para 33] Individual workers and union officials are named in the translation and are redacted here as Ms M and Ms V; the dismissed worker named in the settled reinstatement demand is likewise not reproduced. [Para 11][Para 26]
