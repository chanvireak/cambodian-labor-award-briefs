# Award No. 205/13 — E Garmen Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 12 November 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana for the employer party, Tuon Siphan for the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 139875

## 1. Facts
E Garmen Co., Ltd, a garment factory registered as Inv. 1002 E/2005 and employing about 3,618 workers, faced a collective dispute over six demands; the local union of the Khmer Workers' Pride Union Federation, registered as No. 2220, held the most-representative certificate No. 063/13 of 15 August 2013, valid until 15 August 2015. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 56] Conciliation failed and the non-conciliation report No. 697/13 of 16 September 2013 sent six issues to the panel. [Para 16][Para 21] At the hearing the union withdrew issue 02, leaving issues 01, 03, 04, 05 and 06. [Para 27][Para 28]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay workers who work on public holidays on the same formula as Sunday overtime, a total of 300 per cent? [Para 57][Para 141]
2. Whether the employer must convert occasional (temporary) workers into permanent workers and give them the benefits of permanent staff? [Para 69][Para 149]
3. Whether the employer must add 2,000 riel per overtime session to the meal allowance, making 8,000 riel for two sessions in a day? [Para 98][Para 171]
4. Whether the employer must provide transport home for workers who finish overtime at 20:30? [Para 109][Para 198]
5. Whether the employer must enlarge the nursery and breastfeeding room, or failing that supply two cans of milk of 1.5 kg and 20 US dollars a month in childcare? [Para 122][Para 215]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** public holidays and Sundays are both rest days of equal length, so holiday work should earn the Sunday formula; occasional workers average 21 days a month under repeated fixed-term contracts, do the same work as permanent staff for 86 US dollars, and should be converted; food and transport prices near the factory have risen, justifying another 2,000 riel a session; it is dark at 20:30 and the road home is unsafe; and the nursery is too small and ill-ventilated. [Para 60][Para 91][Para 104][Para 112][Para 125]
**Employer (respondent):** holiday work is paid as the Labour Law requires and 4,000 riel of meal money is added on holidays; fixed-term contracts cover urgent heavy work and those workers already receive the same wages and benefits; the factory pays above the notification rate for overtime meals; and it built a nursery and breastfeeding room that opened on 16 September 2013. [Para 65][Para 93][Para 97][Para 120][Para 128][Para 223]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — every remaining claim was rejected; the rights-dispute award became final immediately under the MoU of 3 October 2012 and the interest-dispute award after eight days. [Para 242][Para 247][Para 250]

The panel first separated rights from interest disputes under Paragraph 2 of Article 312 of the Labour Law, which requires legal decision of disputes over the interpretation and enforcement of law or a collective agreement and equity for all others. [Para 135][Para 139][Para 140] On issue 01 it held the claim to be a rights dispute and applied Article 4 of Prakas No. 10 of 4 February 1999, which gives a worker who works on a public holiday compensation equal to one day's normal wage: read with the 100 per cent the worker already receives for the holiday, the employer owes a further 100 per cent only, which it was already paying, so the Sunday 300 per cent formula did not apply. [Para 142][Para 143][Para 145] On issue 03 it applied Articles 9 and 10 and followed the new jurisprudence of award 19/08-Eternity Apparel, that occasional and permanent workers hold the same statutory rights, so conversion of status is unnecessary where the employer honours those rights; awards 26/04 and 10/08 had treated 21 days a month over two consecutive months as permanent employment, and the panel found the employer already paid 86 US dollars, 5 per cent termination pay and seniority benefit. [Para 155][Para 157][Para 163][Para 168] Issue 04 was classified as an interest dispute; Article 2 of Notification No. 041 of 7 March 2011 requires 2,000 riel a day or a free meal for voluntary overtime, the employer paid 2,000 riel per session (4,000 for two), which is better than the law, and the union produced no evidence of the price increases it asserted. [Para 171][Para 175][Para 193][Para 195] Issue 05 was a rights dispute: Article 144 defines night work through an eleven-hour span that must include 22:00 to 05:00, and Article 6 of Prakas No. 80 requires rest facilities or safe transport after night work; following award 146/12-Wing Star the panel held transport is owed only where the worker leaves between 22:00 and 05:00, and overtime here ended at 20:30. [Para 198][Para 203][Para 207][Para 213] On issue 06 the panel held a claim about a state of affairs that has not arisen is not justiciable, since the employer had built and opened a nursery that no worker was yet using (award 10/03-Jacksantic); the milk-and-childcare alternative was an interest claim on the same unarisen matter and failed for that reason. [Para 219][Para 225][Para 232][Para 240]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Work on a public holiday earns 100 per cent on top of the wage already paid for the holiday under Article 4 of Prakas No. 10, not the 200 per cent additional payment that attaches to Sunday overtime; and an employer owes transport home only where the worker's shift ends inside the statutory night period of 22:00 to 05:00, not merely because the shift ends after dark. [Para 143][Para 207] A claim directed at a situation that does not yet exist is not a dispute the panel will decide. [Para 219]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the union held a most-representative certificate [Para 187] was not decisive, since the interest claims failed on the evidence and on the unarisen-issue ground; so too its remarks on the 50 square metre nursery and the single child-minder [Para 224], which were not needed once the claim was refused.

- The number of milk cans claimed is unstable: "០២៣" (023) at [Para 215], "០២២" at [Para 233] and [Para 249], and "០២" at [Para 239] — the brief follows the two-can figure used in the parties' submissions.
