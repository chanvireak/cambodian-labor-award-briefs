# Award No. 146/13 — CS Goldway Textile (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 05 August 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana for the employer, An Non for the workers, chair Kong Phalha) · Doc ID 139831

## 1. Facts
CS Goldway Textile (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a garment factory at Ang Tnaot East village, Ang Ta Som commune, Tram Kak district, Takeo, employing about 650 workers, faced a collective dispute brought by the Federation of Union for Promoting the Livelihood of Khmer Workers (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីពលើកតម្កើងជីវភាពកម្មករនិយោជិតខ្មែរ) over 20 demands. [Para 1] [Para 47] Conciliation at the Takeo provincial labour department on 02 July 2013 left 9 of the 20 points unsettled; the panel's own conciliation at the hearing of 18 July 2013 settled 5 of those (points 1, 3, 5, 6 and 8), leaving points 2, 4, 7 and 9. [Para 17] [Para 21] [Para 22] The award is non-binding: the memorandum of understanding of 03 October 2012 was signed by a company-level union that had not yet been registered as a professional organisation, so its signatories are not the parties now before the panel. [Para 23] [Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Panel's issue 2) Whether the employer must allow pregnant workers to leave 10 minutes before the end of the shift, that is, 5 minutes beyond the 5 minutes it already allows? [Para 55] [Para 91]
2. (Panel's issue 4) Whether the employer must pay a further 1,000 riel of meal allowance on top of the 1,000 riel it already pays for overtime worked from 18:00 to 20:00? [Para 63] [Para 106]
3. (Panel's issue 7) Whether the employer must install one more container of hot and cold water at the workers' workplace? [Para 113]
4. (Panel's issue 9) Whether the employer must pay the overtime meal allowance every Saturday? [Para 124]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that pregnant workers need the extra 5 minutes because they crowd when buying food outside the compound; that overtime past 18:00 exceeds what the law contemplates and the workers are helping the employer, so the employer should help them with 1,000 riel more; that they do not dare enter the clinic room for hot water; and that Saturday is a rest day on which they need money to buy food and gather. [Para 56] [Para 64] [Para 71] [Para 78]

**Employer (respondent):** that the building is a single floor about 30 metres from the gate, that from three months of pregnancy it already grants 5 minutes early on a doctor's certificate, and would consider more if that proved difficult; that it cannot pay more meal money because the company has only just begun operating; that hot and cold water is in the clinic room alongside two ordinary drinking-water containers at the workplace, with no one ever warned for using the clinic; and that Saturday payment is impossible because the accounts staff need a day, preparation is not ready, and the factory head is busy shipping from Thursday to Saturday. [Para 59] [Para 60] [Para 66] [Para 72] [Para 80]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issues 2, 4 and 7 were dismissed; on issue 9 the panel ordered the employer to pay the overtime meal allowance on Saturdays, once every week, to workers who worked overtime. The award is non-binding (សេចក្តីបង្គាប់មិនជាប់កាតព្វកិច្ច) and becomes final 8 days after notification if no party objects in writing to the Minister of Labour. [Para 142] [Para 145] [Para 146]

On standing, Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 lets a disputant appear in person or through another person holding written authorisation, an expression the panel read as requiring written authorisation, following Awards 161/09-Prek Treng and 43/10-Meng Chian. Twelve workers had transferred their rights to the federation president on 17 June 2013, so it could appear for them; but the panel had ordered proof of the numbers represented by 22 July 2013 and none was produced, so the award covers only those 12. [Para 82] [Para 83] [Para 51] [Para 86]

On issue 2, no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules or past practice obliges the employer to grant 10 minutes, so the demand was an interests dispute. [Para 91] [Para 92] For interests disputes the panel applied Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, which reserve the conclusion of a collective agreement to the most representative union, together with Article 43 of Prakas 099, under which an interests award stands in place of the collective agreement for one year. Because only 12 workers had authorised the federation, it did not have the representative character the panel requires — following Awards 81/04-Evergreen and 98/04-Fret Union and the refusal in Award 169/11-Fortune TV — and the demand was not considered. [Para 98] [Para 99] [Para 101] [Para 104] [Para 105]

On issue 4 the panel found no legal provision, agreement, internal rule or practice creating a duty to add 1,000 riel, so this too was an interests dispute, and it adopted its reasoning on issue 2. [Para 108] [Para 110] [Para 112]

On issue 7, Article 1 of Prakas No. 054 of 10 February 2000 requires the employer to supply sufficient hygienic drinking water to its workers; the panel read it as requiring sufficiency, not hot or cold water. The employer had hot and cold water in the clinic room and two ordinary drinking-water containers at the workplace, and the workers did not dispute the sufficiency of ordinary drinking water, so the obligation was met and no additional container was owed. [Para 113] [Para 119] [Para 120] [Para 122]

On issue 9, point 2 of Ministry Notification No. 041/11 of 07 March 2011 entitles a worker who volunteers for overtime at the employer's request to 2,000 riel per day or one free meal, and fixes no time for payment. [Para 124] [Para 125] Following Awards 76/11-Zung Thich and 244/12-Khmot, the panel held the money must be paid within a period that lets the worker buy food for the overtime worked, whether daily, weekly or monthly. [Para 129] [Para 136] On the facts, weekly payment each Saturday was more reasonable and necessary, because otherwise the workers could not buy food for the following week's overtime; the employer's accounting and shipping difficulties did not displace that. [Para 140] [Para 141]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union or federation may bring a collective dispute only for the workers who have authorised it in writing, and an interests dispute — a demand for a benefit that no law, collective agreement, internal rule or established practice yet grants — can be decided only where the claimant organisation is the most representative in the enterprise; a federation authorised by a small minority of the workforce therefore cannot obtain an interests award. [Para 83] [Para 101] [Para 104]

**Obiter dicta:** that the 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance need not be paid daily; payment within any suitable period — daily, weekly or monthly — satisfies Notification 041/11 provided the worker can buy food for the overtime worked, weekly Saturday payment being ordered here only because the facts required it. [Para 129] [Para 135]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is dated 05 August 2013 on its face, while the reasons record that it was to issue on 31 July 2013 and that the parties agreed to extend the time to 05 August 2013. [Para 1] [Para 26] Conciliation is dated 02 July 2013 in the summary, but the non-conciliation report No. 353 Kbv/Tk is dated 08 July 2013 and reached the secretariat on 09 July. [Para 13] [Para 17] The company appears as CS Goldway Textile, CS Gold Way Textile and once as "CS Gold Long Way Textile". [Para 32] OCR damage is heavy: the chapter reference at the jurisdiction paragraph reads "ជំពូក Lin ទី១២", a stray year "២០១៦" is inserted inside the quotation of Article 19 of Prakas 099, one line is a bare "..។", and the Article 96 quotation is broken across three lines. [Para 12] [Para 82] [Para 95] [Para 137] Individual workers, union officials and representatives have been reduced to initials under the briefing convention; arbitrator names are given as printed, and the header and signature block agree on all three.
