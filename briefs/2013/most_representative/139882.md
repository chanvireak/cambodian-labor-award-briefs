# Award No. 215/13 — RJ Footwear (Cambodia) Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 24 October 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Pen Bunchhea, chair) · Doc ID 139882

## 1. Facts
RJ Footwear (Cambodia) Co Ltd makes shoes at Ang Snuol, Kandal, and had been operating for about six months with roughly 400 workers. On 3 September 2013 the Khmer Workers' Rights Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព សិទ្ធិ កម្មករ ខ្មែរ) filed a collective dispute of nineteen claims; conciliation on 18 September settled thirteen and left six live. On 7 August 2013 the company had already signed a conciliation minute promising to deduct 1,000 riel a month in union dues from each member. The workplace union held only a receipt for its registration application, not a registration certificate. [Para 20][Para 56][Para 189][Para 30]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the overtime meal allowance weekly rather than monthly? [Para 136]
2. Whether the employer must pay a travel and accommodation allowance above the 10 USD a month it already pays? [Para 160]
3. Whether the employer must raise the base wage by 15 USD, from 80 USD to 95 USD a month? [Para 179]
4. Whether the employer must deduct 1,000 riel a month in union dues under clause 6 of the 7 August 2013 agreement? [Para 188]
5. Whether the employer must pay a 15 USD monthly milk allowance to women who have recently given birth? [Para 231]
6. Whether the employer must pay a 5 USD monthly health allowance to every worker? [Para 235]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** overtime meal money should come weekly so that a worker has cash to buy food, since by month's end they must borrow to eat; other factories paying a free lunch also pay the overtime meal allowance weekly. Travel costs have risen from 12 to 13 USD and rent from 15 to 30 USD, and other factories pay a 95 USD base. The company signed the 7 August agreement and must honour it. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 76][Para 80][Para 81][Para 90][Para 101]

**Employer (respondent):** the factory has traded six months only and cannot afford these increases; weekly payment of the meal allowance would require extra accounting staff. It already gives a free lunch, 10 USD travel and lodging (above the 7 USD required), 80 USD base wage and the statutory benefits, which together exceed the law. It will deduct dues once the union is registered. [Para 68][Para 72][Para 84][Para 86][Para 92][Para 98][Para 103]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must pay the overtime meal allowance weekly; the other five claims were refused consideration or dismissed. The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent written objection. [Para 261][Para 262][Para 267]

*Overtime meal allowance.* Point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 requires that a worker who voluntarily works overtime at the employer's request receive 2,000 riel a day or a free meal, without fixing when it must be paid. The panel held the payment must fall at a time that lets the worker actually buy food for the overtime shift, and that a free lunch is not a substitute for it — a benefit better than the law cannot excuse a legal duty. The employer's accounting difficulty was no answer to a statutory obligation, so payment was ordered weekly as claimed. [Para 137][Para 138][Para 141][Para 156][Para 157][Para 159]

*Union dues.* Clause 6 of the 7 August 2013 minute was a valid contract under Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code, made freely and lawfully. But Article 129 paragraph 2 and Article 5(5) of Prakas No. 305 presuppose a union that is registered: Article 268(1) makes registration the condition of a professional organisation's legal rights. Because this union held only a receipt of application, the condition precedent in the agreement was unmet and the employer had not yet breached it. [Para 190][Para 198][Para 209][Para 218][Para 224][Para 229]

*Travel, base wage, milk and health allowances.* Each was found to rest on no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice, and so to be a dispute of interest. Applying Article 96 of the Labour Law with Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 and Article 43 of Prakas No. 99/04, the panel held an interest award binds all workers for a year, so only a most-representative union, or unions together with more than half the workforce, may bring one. This union had no registration and no such status, so the panel declined to consider all four. [Para 163][Para 164][Para 173][Para 183][Para 187][Para 195][Para 202]

*Jurisdiction over the health allowance.* Article 312 confines the panel to issues recorded as non-conciliated. The health allowance appeared only in the "agreed" part of the 18 September minute, yet also in the original 3 September complaint; the panel found the record contained a deletion and an insertion, treated the placement as a technical error corrected by the labour department, and held it had jurisdiction. [Para 243][Para 245][Para 248][Para 256]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The overtime meal allowance under Notification No. 041/11 must be paid at an interval short enough for the worker to buy food for the overtime shift, and an employer's administrative cost is no defence; but a union dues deduction agreed in a conciliation minute remains unenforceable while the union holds no registration certificate, because Article 129 paragraph 2 presumes a lawfully registered union. [Para 141][Para 156][Para 209][Para 225]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that paying daily is the most obviously reasonable interval, because a free meal must be given on each overtime day, but accepted weekly payment as sufficient where the worker can still provision for each shift. [Para 144][Para 145]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The claim count varies: nineteen issues in the complaint, thirteen settled and six remaining, but the workers' letter is said to raise eighteen [Para 20][Para 48]. Attendance does not reconcile — six workers are named as attending, yet three are treated as the attending parties alongside 133 who transferred rights [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63]. The travel allowance is printed as "10 USD 4" in two places and the rice allowance as "15 persons dollars" in the operative order [Para 160][Para 161][Para 265]. The overtime order reads "in one day week once" at line 159 and again at line 153 [Para 153][Para 159]. Paragraph 267 repeats whole clauses of the finality sentence. Prakas No. 99/04 is dated 2004 in the body and 2023 nowhere, but the parent award citations are garbled throughout (lines 150, 173, 174). The chair's signature line at 268 is destroyed by OCR.
