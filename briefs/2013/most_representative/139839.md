# Award No. 154/13 — Bouv Kheir Garment Factory (Cambodia) Khom Pheni Ltd (Arbitration Council, 20 August 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Yu Soun Ty, employer-appointed; An Virak, worker-appointed; Mae Nimmit, president) · Doc ID 139839

## 1. Facts
Bouv Kheir Garment Factory (Cambodia) Khom Pheni Ltd, a garment factory in Ang Snuol district, Kandal, employed 690 workers. [Para 69][Para 70] Nine demands went to conciliation, which failed on 16 July 2013. [Para 19] Two were settled at the hearing (3 and 5) and two withdrawn (6 and 9), leaving five. [Para 24][Para 25] The core dispute was the removal of three union office-holders — president, vice-president and secretary — elected on 12 June 2013, told on 13 July 2013 they were dismissed, and later re-labelled as suspended. [Para 73][Para 90][Para 97]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate four union representatives and preserve their benefits from the date of removal? [Para 76]
2. Whether the employer must dismiss the manager Lao Tav for threatening a worker and attempting to strike her? [Para 129]
4. Whether the employer must pay the overtime meal allowance weekly, every Saturday? [Para 143]
7. Whether the employer must use one-year fixed-duration contracts instead of three-month contracts? [Para 152]
8. Whether the employer must pay a skills allowance of USD 10 for three-machine sewers and USD 7 for two-machine sewers? [Para 159]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The three women were removed because they were union leaders — the employer said as much at the time — and the later conversion into a "suspension" was a device to escape the labour inspector's authorisation; the manager Lao Tav raised his hand to strike a worker and insulted her; monthly payment of the meal allowance leaves nothing to buy food with. [Para 77][Para 94][Para 134][Para 303]
**Employer (respondent):** It did not discriminate: it did not regard them as union leaders because the union was not yet registered, and the measure was a suspension pending the labour inspector's decision, with 50% wages paid, taken because their work performance fell below the standard required of team leaders and because they incited a stoppage on 1 July 2013. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 102][Para 124]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement ordered for three workers, weekly meal allowance ordered, the other claims refused; the order is non-binding and becomes final after 8 days without objection. [Para 361][Para 367]

On issue 1 the panel first held that the fourth representative's claim was already decided in case 137/13 and refused to hear it again on res judicata grounds. [Para 246][Para 251][Para 261] For the other three it applied Article 293 of the Labour Law, under which dismissal of a staff delegate or a candidate requires the labour inspector's prior consent. [Para 199][Para 237] Prakas 305/01 Article 3(3) protects election candidates for 45 days before and, if unsuccessful, 45 days after the poll, and Article 4 protects founders and members during the registration request and for 30 days after registration. [Para 200][Para 205] Following Award 40/10-Meng Yan, the panel extended that protection to successful candidates from election day until the union obtains registration, since otherwise they would be exposed to discrimination in exactly that interval. [Para 217][Para 218][Para 221] The union notified the employer of the three successful candidates on 14 June 2013 and held a registration receipt from 17 June 2013, so the removals of 13 July 2013 fell inside the protected period. [Para 232][Para 229]

The panel then rejected the employer's re-characterisation. Article 2 gives the employer management authority only where exercised lawfully and reasonably; here the employer conceded at the hearing that it had told the three on 13 July 2013 that they were dismissed, and only on 15 or 16 July changed course, backdating the suspension letters and the request to the provincial labour department to 13 July. [Para 180][Para 185][Para 187][Para 188] That was not reasonable, so the legal act remained a dismissal; no prior authorisation had been obtained, and under Prakas 305/01 Article 4(4) a measure taken without it is void. [Para 192][Para 194][Para 240][Para 243] Reinstatement with benefits from the date of removal was ordered. [Para 245][Para 361]

On issue 2 the panel held that under Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 311 of the Civil Code only the parties to a contract may dissolve it, so a third party — here the workers — cannot compel the employer to terminate a manager's contract. [Para 265][Para 268][Para 270] A worker may demand such a removal only on proof that the person is dangerous and cannot be kept in the enterprise; no evidence at all was produced of the alleged assault. [Para 272][Para 281][Para 283]

On issue 4 the panel applied point 2 of Notification 041/11, which gives a worker doing voluntary overtime 2,000 riel a day or one free meal, without fixing the payment interval. [Para 289][Para 291] It held the purpose is to let the worker buy food when working overtime, so monthly payment bundled into wages defeats it; the employer's justification that the buyer requires monthly payment was not reasonable. [Para 294][Para 305] Weekly payment on Saturdays was ordered. [Para 307][Para 364]

On issue 7 the panel rejected the argument that Article 73(5) sets a six-month minimum term: that provision only conditions entitlement to prior notice, and says nothing about minimum duration. [Para 338][Para 339] Under Articles 65 and 311, and Civil Code Articles 336 and 337, a contract requires matching offer and acceptance; the parties' wishes here diverged, so no obligation to use one-year contracts arose. [Para 321][Para 329][Para 335]

On issue 8 the panel found no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or practice creating a skills allowance, held the claim to be an interest dispute, and refused jurisdiction because the federation represented only 60 of 690 workers and so was not the most representative union. [Para 346][Para 347][Para 358][Para 359]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union election candidate's statutory protection runs from the poll until the union is registered, and a dismissal during that window without the labour inspector's prior consent is void; an employer cannot cure the defect by later re-characterising the dismissal as a suspension and backdating the paperwork. [Para 218][Para 221][Para 243]

**Obiter dicta:** That a third party cannot compel the termination of another's employment contract, and that Article 73(5)'s six-month figure is a notice threshold rather than a minimum contract term, were stated as limits on the claims rather than as the operative ground of any order. [Para 270][Para 339]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award date is 20 August 2013 in the header, while the reasons state that the order in this case issued on 2 August 2013 — the date also given for the earlier case 137/13. [Para 1][Para 89] One passage puts the number of dismissed workers at "30" where three are meant. [Para 195] The base wage for three-machine sewers appears as USD 90 and as "2 90". [Para 162][Para 344] Prakas 305 is dated "22 November 20019" and its protection rule is attributed to "the year 2019". [Para 207][Para 242] The union is said both to hold a registration receipt of 17 June 2013 and not yet to be registered. [Para 74][Para 75] The 17 July 2013 agreement is cited as Article 3 in one place and Article 4 in another. [Para 97][Para 168] The worker authorisation letter's date is destroyed by OCR. [Para 56]
