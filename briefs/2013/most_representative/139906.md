# Award No. 256/13 — Dignity Knitter Limited (Arbitration Council, 24 December 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 139906

## 1. Facts
Dignity Knitter Limited runs a garment factory at Prek Ta Pring Village, Setbo Commune, Sa Ang District, Kandal Province, employing approximately 1,975 workers. [Para 1][Para 14] Its local union of C.CAWDU was registered under No. 1190 dated 30 July 2007 and held a certificate of most representative status dated 9 January 2008, which the workers admitted they had not asked the Ministry to renew. [Para 15] In October 2013 six product categories were put into production in the Trimming Section on piece rates the employer fixed and the workers rejected; the workers demanded higher rates per lot, the employer offered less, and the dispute was not conciliated. [Para 16][Para 1]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must increase the piece rate of the six product categories (IDs 8022, 11009, 9050, 10034, 10053 and 10046) to the rates the workers demand, measured by the workers' own time-test results? [Para 16][Para 35][Para 75]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** piece rates had for three years been set by negotiation on test results, with rates at least US$0.5 to US$0.6 above base wage or above the test result; the employer's rates for these six categories fall below the US$80 monthly minimum wage for October 2013. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 18][Para 35] They submitted thumbprints of 270 workers, 72 from the Trimming Section, to support the demand. [Para 20]
**Employer (respondent):** the test result is only an input, not a criterion: supervisors propose, management decides, and the official rate is approved by the Factory President and Company Director after comparison with other branches. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 25][Para 26] The test had not been conducted for three years, the Trimming Section test was abnormal because production time was unusually long, and no worker attending full time earns below the minimum wage. [Para 27][Para 33][Para 36]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the workers' demand that the employer increase the piece rate of the six product categories according to the workers' test results; the award is binding and enforceable as the parties chose binding arbitration at the hearing. [Para 75][Para 76]

The panel first examined who could bring the demand. Under Clause 19 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 a representative needs express written authorisation, and there was none from Dignity's workers to C.CAWDU, so the union officer Mr S Yot had no right to represent them, following Awards No. 161/09-Prek Treng, 43/10-Ming Jian and 144/12-E Garment. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43] Article 268 of the Labour Law was then applied to Mr P Sophea: rights under the law, including the right to represent members, arise only on registration and Ministry recognition, and no letter recognising the leaders for the new term was produced, so his claim to the presidency was unverified. [Para 44][Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50] The panel therefore decided the demand only for the three individuals who attended in person. [Para 51][Para 52]

The demand was classified as a rights dispute, because it concerned the method for determining piece rate, which is wages. [Para 57][Para 58] Article 2 of the Labour Law gives the employer the right to direct and supervise, and the panel accepted that right where exercised lawfully and reasonably, citing Awards No. 62/06-Quicksew, 108/06-Trinungal Komara, 33/07-Goldfame and 119/09-SL. [Para 58] Article 108 requires a piece rate that lets a worker of mediocre ability working normally earn at least the guaranteed minimum wage, and Point 3 of Notification No. 103 of 9 April 2013 requires supplementation where productivity falls short, at US$75 for probationary and US$80 for permanent workers. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 62]

On the evidence, the October 2013 wage schedule showed hourly piece-rate earnings equal to or above the hourly fixed base wage (US$80 divided by 26 days and 8 hours), and only one worker received US$67.57, below the minimum, but that worker had worked only six days. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40][Para 67][Para 68][Para 69] The panel found that the parties had never put the test-based method in writing, that the employer disputed any such agreement, and that no agreement therefore obliged the employer to follow the workers' test results. [Para 71][Para 72] It concluded that the employer's method did not depress wages below the minimum and was lawful and reasonable, so no obligation to raise the six rates existed. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a union's most representative status has lapsed and its leaders' recognition for the current term is unproved, the Council will decide a piece-rate demand only for the workers who attend in person and are authorised in writing; and on the merits, an employer's piece-rate method is lawful under Article 2 and Article 108 of the Labour Law where the resulting hourly wage equals or exceeds the hourly minimum, so the Council will not substitute the workers' time-test results for the employer's rate unless a written agreement requires it. [Para 42][Para 49][Para 72][Para 74]

**Obiter dicta:** the observations that the dispute arose because the method for fixing piece rates was never written down, and that the parties had agreed in Case No. 64/12 to fix rates within seven days before production began, are general remarks on the record; the demand failed on the absence of any binding test-based method, not on that earlier agreement. [Para 29][Para 69][Para 71]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The document is an unofficial English translation; the evidence section is omitted and the reader is referred to the Khmer version. [Para 11][Para 12]
- The non-conciliation report is dated 29 November 2013 at [Para 2] but 20 November 2013 at [Para 14], and is said to have been submitted to the Secretariat on 22 November 2013 — before the later of the two dates. [Para 2][Para 14]
- The panel records that 55% of workers could not earn the minimum wage at [Para 64], yet finds at [Para 38] and [Para 72] that hourly piece-rate earnings met or exceeded the fixed base wage; the two findings are irreconcilable and only the later one supports the order. [Para 38][Para 64][Para 72]
- The award opens by naming "Dignity Knitter Limited" as claimant in the case, though the demand is the workers'; the company is typed "Dinity" at [Para 42] and "Dignity Factory (River Rich)" at [Para 50], importing a name found nowhere else in the award. [Para 13][Para 42][Para 50]
- The piece-rate table at [Para 16] is mangled by OCR: the columns of serial number, employer rate, worker rate and increase run together, only some rates carry a "1 lot =" prefix, and the increases cannot be reconstructed for every category. [Para 16]
- The award date is 24 December 2013, deferred by agreement from 17 December 2013; the hearing was 4 December 2013 and the panel formed 26 November 2013. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 10]
- Two workers are each printed under several name variants, mixing given names with initials; the source's own text calls the six products "one non-conciliated issue". [Para 1][Para 15][Para 2]
