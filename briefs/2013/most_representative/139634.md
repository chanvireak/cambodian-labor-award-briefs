# Award No. 27/13 — Shun Wah Auto Embroidery Factory (Arbitration Council, 14 March 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Kong Phalha chair) · Doc ID 139634 · includes a dissenting opinion by Mr L Sovanna on issue 2

## 1. Facts
Shun Wah Auto Embroidery, at Trapeang Krasang, Ang Snuol, Kampong Speu, embroiders and presses labels and lettering onto shirt panels and employs about 300 workers. [Para 46] Its union was registered on 27 December 2012 and is not certified as most representative, and neither party signed the memorandum of 3 October 2012. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50] Of twelve demands, conciliation on 16 January 2013 left six; two were settled at the hearing and four remained — the attendance bonus, deduction of that bonus for authorised absence, back-payment of the travel allowance, and the timing of maternity pay. [Para 21][Para 24][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise the attendance bonus to USD 15 a month, from the USD 10 now paid? [Para 51][Para 52]
2. Whether the employer must deduct the attendance bonus pro rata to the days a worker takes authorised unpaid leave, rather than cancelling the whole bonus? [Para 58][Para 59]
3. Whether the employer must pay the travel and accommodation allowance of USD 7 for every worker who has not received the full amount, back to 1 September 2012? [Para 68]
4. Whether the employer must pay the 50% maternity allowance for 90 days as a single sum at least one day before the worker begins her maternity leave? [Para 77][Para 135]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** a neighbouring printing factory with the same owner pays a USD 12 attendance bonus; the company pays only USD 6 of the USD 7 travel allowance that the Minister's notice requires; cancelling the entire USD 10 bonus for a single day of authorised leave is unjust when the worker has permission and the leave is recorded; and paying maternity money month by month forces women who have just given birth to travel to collect it, at cost, when they have no income. [Para 53][Para 74][Para 60][Para 84]
**Employer (respondent):** the two factories have been separately registered and separately managed for nearly two years; there is no agreement on any bonus; USD 6 was paid from April 2012 and raised to USD 7 from January 2013, so nothing is owed for the earlier months; it cannot afford USD 15; and paying all the maternity money at once would mean women do not come back to work. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 70][Para 57][Para 85]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer is ordered to pay the maternity allowance for 90 days as a single sum at least one day before the worker's maternity leave begins; the bonus, deduction and travel-allowance claims were refused. [Para 149][Para 150][Para 151][Para 152][Para 153] The award is non-binding and becomes final 8 days after notification. [Para 154]

On issues 1, 2 and 3 the panel began with the scope of Notice No. 230 of 25 July 2012, which replaced Notice No. 041 of 7 March 2011 from 1 September 2012. [Para 172] It found the company's activity is embroidery and pressing of labels and lettering, not the cutting and sewing of garments, so the enterprise belongs to a supporting industry outside the notice's field of application, which is confined to textile, garment and footwear manufacture. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 95][Para 96] Because the notice does not bind this employer, the demand for a USD 15 bonus, and the demand that the bonus be reduced rather than cancelled, were claims for more than the law requires — interest disputes — which the panel will not decide unless the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise; this union is not. [Para 99][Para 100][Para 112][Para 113] The same reasoning defeated the travel claim: the employer's USD 6 and later USD 7 payments were a benefit more favourable than the law, not the discharge of a legal duty to back-pay USD 1 a month for four months. [Para 131][Para 132][Para 133]

The panel then considered, without deciding, what "absence" in the notice means, distinguishing authorised unpaid leave from unauthorised absence, and concluded that only unauthorised absence is meant. [Para 195][Para 196][Para 202]

On issue 4 the panel found a legal duty. Articles 182 and 183 give a woman 90 days' maternity leave and half her wage and allowances during it, but fix no time for payment. [Para 135][Para 137] Article 115 requires wages not to be paid on a rest day and, if the pay day falls on one, to be paid at least a day earlier; Article 103 lists the sum payable during maternity leave among the components of wage. [Para 138][Para 141] The panel therefore held that maternity pay must be handed over in one sum at least one day before the leave starts, and that paying it monthly was contrary to the law. [Para 142][Para 143][Para 149]

**Dissent.** Mr L Sovanna disagreed on issue 2. In his view the question was not the notice's scope but its interpretation, because the employer had applied Notice No. 041 for years and then Notice No. 230, and under Article 309 of the Civil Code a duty may arise from a party's own conduct as well as from contract; the employer thus remained bound by the practice even if the notice did not apply to it of its own force, and he would have ordered the pro rata deduction. [Para 156][Para 161][Para 162][Para 164]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The benefits fixed by Notice No. 230 — the attendance bonus and the travel and accommodation allowance — bind only textile, garment and footwear manufacturing enterprises, and an embroidery and pressing workshop in the supporting industry is outside it, so a demand for those benefits, or for a larger one, is an interest dispute the panel will not decide unless the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise. Maternity allowance is different: being a component of wage under Article 103, and wages not being payable on a rest day, the 50% maternity sum for 90 days must be paid in one amount at least one day before maternity leave begins. [Para 96][Para 113][Para 141][Para 143]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's survey of the categories of absence — paid and unpaid, authorised and unauthorised — and its view that "absence" in the notice means unauthorised absence, were expressed although it had already held the notice inapplicable, and were not needed for the refusals. [Para 193][Para 195][Para 202]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award carries a dissenting opinion on issue 2 signed by the worker-party arbitrator alone and dated the same day as the award, so the refusals on issues 1 and 3 are majority decisions while issue 2 is decided over a dissent. [Para 1][Para 155][Para 207]
- The award is dated 14 March 2013 [Para 1], consistent with the extension from 18 February 2013 [Para 27], but the hearings are given as 8 February and 11 March 2013 [Para 18][Para 19] while the procedural narrative puts the first hearing on 8 January 2013 [Para 23].
- The company is named "Shun Wah Auto Embroidery", "Sun Wah" and, at one point, "Shung Landa Wah Auto Embroidery" [Para 1][Para 118], and the union's registration is given as 2725 K/B of 27 December 2012 while the Minister's letter of the same date is No. 369 [Para 29][Para 30].
- The two ministerial notices are easily confused in the text: No. 041 of 7 March 2011, revoked, and No. 230 of 25 July 2012, in force from 1 September 2012, with the operative dates printed as "25 September 2012" and "1 September 2013" in places [Para 127][Para 172].
- The maternity claim is pleaded as three months and decided as 90 days [Para 77][Para 135]; individual workers and union officials are named in the record and are not reproduced here.
