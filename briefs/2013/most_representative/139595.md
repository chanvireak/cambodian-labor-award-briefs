# Award No. 07/13-Ocean Garment — Ocean Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 6 March 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phyrum, employer-chosen; Liv Sovanna, worker-chosen; Kong Phallack, chair) · Doc ID 139595

## 1. Facts
Ocean Garment Co., Ltd is a garment manufacturer at Prey Teer Village, Sangkat Chom Chao, Khan Po Sen Chey, Phnom Penh, employing 2,200 workers and hosting two local unions; the CLUF local union, registered as No. 1998 dated 24 November 2010 with 201 members, brought the claim. [Para 21][Para 23] A strike ran from 11 August 2012 to 30 August 2012; workers who stayed at work say strikers disrupted them on 16, 17 and 18 August 2012, and their wages and August attendance bonus were docked. [Para 25][Para 26] Four demands survived conciliation, and the parties chose non-binding arbitration for interests disputes. [Para 11][Para 19][Para 12]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must maintain the wages and benefits of the workers who did not take part in the strike, including wages for 16, 17 and 18 August 2012 and the August 2012 attendance bonus? [Para 1][Para 24]
2. Whether the employer must pay a US$5 bonus to single-machine operators, US$10 to double-machine operators and US$15 to triple-or-more machine operators? [Para 3]
3. Whether the employer must use the average of wages, overtime and all benefits over the last 12 months as the basis for payment in lieu of annual leave? [Para 5][Para 41]
4. Whether the employer must implement Issue 2 of Arbitral Award No. 06/12-Ocean Garment issued on 16 February 2012? [Para 7][Para 43]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** those claiming were not strikers; they came to work every day but could not perform eight hours because strikers disrupted them, so their wages and the US$10 August bonus should not have been docked. [Para 25][Para 26] Current leave practice, based only on base wage plus the latest month's attendance bonus, loses them overtime, and a previous award ordered the broader basis. [Para 42]
**Employer (respondent):** it never refused to pay; it would pay once workers produced payslips, supervisor acknowledgement and fingerprints, which they failed to do, and it had already paid US$20 to all workers in August 2012 as a gratuitous payment. [Para 29][Para 30] It rejected the machine bonus for want of any legal basis and financial difficulty, and said no law prescribes the leave calculation claimed. [Para 40][Para 42]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer ordered to pay 108 workers full wages for 16, 17 and 18 August 2012 and their full attendance bonus for those days, and to take average wages, overtime and other bonuses earned over the last 12 months into the payment-in-lieu-of-annual-leave calculation; Issue 4 declined for lack of jurisdiction and Issue 2 declined as an interests dispute. Part I is binding, Part II non-binding. [Para 97][Para 98][Para 99]

Issue 1 was a rights dispute, since wages are governed by the Labour Law and the attendance bonus by Notification No. 041 dated 7 March 2011. [Para 52] Under Article 102 the employer pays for work performed, so no wage is due for hours not worked; but the panel asked whether the employer is excused where the shortfall came from strikers' disruption. [Para 54][Para 56][Para 57] Reading Article 2 with Awards 62/06-Quicksew, 108/06-Trinunggal Komara, 33/07-Gold Fame and 119/09-SL Garment, it held the employer must supervise and arrange an organised, safe workplace and must anticipate disruption by strikers. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60] The disruption began six days into the strike, so it was foreseeable, and the police guard the employer arranged was inadequate. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 65] The employer therefore could not escape paying full wages, and its willingness to pay on production of documents did not defeat the claim. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 71]

On jurisdiction over the number of claimants, the panel worked from the evidence: 137 workers made the demand, 130 had proper thumbprints, 22 had resigned or not been scanned, leaving 108 properly thumbprinted workers whom it considered. [Para 38][Para 39]

For the bonus, Notification No. 230 dated 25 July 2012 applied only from 1 September 2012, so August 2012 fell under Notification No. 041 dated 7 March 2011, which requires at least US$7; the employer's US$10 exceeded that, and because the shortfall was not the workers' fault it could not be reduced. [Para 73][Para 74]

Issue 3 was a rights dispute under Article 168, which requires an allowance at least equal to average wage, bonuses, benefits and indemnities over the preceding twelve months. [Para 86][Para 87] Following Awards 27/04-MS International and 94/07-Fortune Garment, the panel ordered the 12-month average method, holding the company's latest-month practice non-compliant. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 91]

Issue 4 was declined: under Point 1 of Article 353 of the Code of Civil Procedure 2006 only a court may enforce an arbitration award. [Para 92][Para 94][Para 95] Issue 2 was an interests dispute, and because an interests award displaces the collective agreement and binds non-members, only an MRS union may bring it. [Para 76][Para 80][Para 81]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that fails to prevent foreseeable disruption of working hours by strikers — here six days into a strike, behind an inadequate police guard — cannot deduct wages or attendance bonus for the hours its non-striking workers lost. [Para 62][Para 66]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel has no jurisdiction to enforce its own earlier award; enforcement lies with the court under Article 353 of the Code of Civil Procedure. [Para 94][Para 95]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award contradicts itself on union status: the facts find that "the local union of CLUF holds the most representative status (MRS)", while the reasoning on Issue 2 finds that it "does not hold MRS". [Para 22][Para 83] The award date is 6 March 2013 in the header but the parties' deferral is recorded as moving issuance from 14 February 2013 to 4 March 2013. [Para 1][Para 20] The worker count shifts between 143, 137, 130 and 108 across the demand, the evidence and the order. [Para 1][Para 38][Para 72] The case number prints as "0713-Ocean Garment", without the slash used elsewhere. [Para 1] The evidence section is omitted from the English version. [Para 20]
