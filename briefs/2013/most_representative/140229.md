# Award No. 244/12 — Khomflit Onneur Footwear Industrial (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 7 February 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy; Oun Non; Pen Bunhea presiding) · Doc ID 140229

## 1. Facts
Khomflit Onneur Footwear Industrial (Cambodia) (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខម ភ្លីត អន នើរ ហ្វូត វែរ អ៉ីន ដាស ស្ទ្រៀល (ខេមបូឌា)) is a shoe factory employing 1,825 workers, of whom 1,190 are women [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58]. The claimant is the union federation Samleng Yuvachon Khmer (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព សម្លេង យុវជន ខ្មែរ) and its factory local; the local union was certified on 14 November 2011 and claims about 1,300 members, but holds no certificate of most-representative status [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61]. Nineteen demands went to conciliation at the Kandal provincial labour department, where all nineteen failed [Para 27][Para 28]. At the hearings eight were settled by agreement (issues 1, 7, 8, 11, 12, 13, 15 and 18) and one was withdrawn (issue 14), leaving ten for decision [Para 32][Para 33]. The award was issued on 7 February 2013 after the parties extended the deadline from 11 January [Para 34].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must issue payslips in Khmer three days before the pay date? [Para 117]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay the overtime meal allowance every two weeks? [Para 135]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must raise the daily lunch allowance from 2,000 to 4,000 riel? [Para 153]
4. (Issue 5) Whether the employer must raise the attendance bonus from USD 10 to USD 15, and whether it may deduct the bonus in full for authorised special leave rather than pro rata? [Para 163][Para 167]
5. (Issue 6) Whether the employer must raise the travel and accommodation allowance from USD 7 to USD 15 a month? [Para 208]
6. (Issue 9) Whether the employer must build a daycare centre and a nursing room? [Para 213]
7. (Issue 10) Whether the employer must supply two raincoats a month instead of one? [Para 222]
8. (Issue 16) Whether pregnant workers must be allowed to leave 15 minutes before the end of the shift? [Para 229]
9. (Issue 17) Whether pregnant workers must be given one paid day a month for antenatal checks in addition to annual leave? [Para 238]
10. (Issue 19) Whether union leaders must be allowed to leave their workstations during working hours to resolve members' problems? [Para 245]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), the Samleng Yuvachon Khmer federation and its factory local:** Each claim rested on need and comparison with other factories: the lunch allowance no longer buys a meal, the travel allowance does not cover fares, and two raincoats are needed so one can dry while the other is worn [Para 73][Para 89][Para 97]. On the attendance bonus, the employer should not forfeit the whole month's USD 10 for a single authorised day or half-day of leave [Para 82].
**Employer (respondent):** The factory has no capacity to meet the claims and asks to continue applying the law as it stands [Para 74][Para 90][Para 99][Para 155]. It issues payslips two days before payday, which is a reasonable period, and following the law produced a bonus of USD 10 a month under Notification No. 230 [Para 66][Para 81][Para 168].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay the overtime meal allowance every two weeks [Para 259], to provide a daycare centre and nursing room within a reasonable time with external daycare costs paid pending construction [Para 264][Para 265], and to deduct the attendance bonus pro rata to authorised leave days only [Para 262]; the remaining seven claims were refused [Para 258][Para 260][Para 261][Para 263][Para 266][Para 267][Para 268][Para 269].

The panel first set out the framework. A dispute over the interpretation or application of a law or collective agreement is a rights dispute, decided according to law; any other dispute is an interest dispute, decided on equity [Para 117][Para 125]. An interest award replaces the collective agreement for a year and binds all workers in the enterprise, so only a union with most-representative status may bring such a claim [Para 127][Para 129]. Following Awards 81/04, 98/04 and 169/11, the panel found this union not most representative, having no certificate to that effect, and therefore declined to decide the interest claims — issues 2, 4, 5 in its first limb, 6, 10, 16 and 17 [Para 61][Para 130][Para 132][Para 134][Para 160][Para 166][Para 212][Para 228][Para 237][Para 244].

Issue 3 succeeded on a rights footing: point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 requires 2,000 riel or a free meal for each day of voluntary overtime, and although it sets no payment date the money must reach workers in time to buy food, so payment every two weeks was ordered [Para 135][Para 136][Para 140][Para 152]. On issue 9, Article 186 requires any enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a nursing room and a daycare centre; with 1,190 women the obligation was engaged, and pending construction the employer must pay for outside daycare against actual invoices [Para 213][Para 217][Para 220][Para 221]. On the second limb of issue 5, the panel held the USD 10 attendance bonus fixed by Notification No. 230 to be a wage component under Article 103, not a discretionary gift; authorised leave suspends the contract under Articles 71 and 72, so no wage and no bonus accrues for those days, but the employer may deduct only the share matching the leave days [Para 198][Para 200][Para 201][Para 204][Para 206][Para 207]. On issue 19, Articles 2 and 266 were read together: the employer's right to manage must be exercised reasonably and lawfully, the union may protect its members, but no breach was shown, and union leaders remain bound to work diligently under Article 69 [Para 245][Para 248][Para 255][Para 256][Para 257].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the claimant union is not the most representative in the enterprise, the panel will decline to decide interest claims that would create new benefits binding all workers, but will still decide rights claims arising under the Labour Law or a ministerial notification. A bonus fixed by notification is a wage component, so authorised leave permits deduction only in proportion to the leave days taken. [Para 129][Para 206]

**Obiter dicta:** The award is non-binding and becomes final if neither party objects in writing within eight days [Para 270]. The employer arbitrator dissented on the pro-rata attendance bonus, arguing from dictionary definitions that a bonus is a conditional gift whose conditions the giver alone sets [Para 277][Para 279][Para 290].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The scan is heavily corrupted, with Latin fragments ("Live", "John", "CC", "KNA", "NNA") cutting into Khmer text and stray years ("2016", "2017") inserted mid-sentence [Para 56][Para 113][Para 132][Para 181][Para 224]. The payroll figure appears as USD 1,000 a month at line 161 where USD 10 is plainly meant by the operative order [Para 161][Para 262]. The worker-side arbitrator's name is printed as Oun Non on the face of the award but Oun Lean at the signature [Para 271]. The company name is transliterated in at least four different ways across the file [Para 21][Para 46][Para 132]. Employer representatives, union officials and a worker cited by way of example are all named in full in the source; they are described by role here. Several sentences are truncated by page breaks, and the dissent's dictionary citations survive only in fragments [Para 275][Para 276][Para 277].
