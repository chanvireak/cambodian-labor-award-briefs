# Award No. 85/13 — Hong Va Cambodia Garmen Manufacturing Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 5 June 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna, appointed by the workers; Sok Mothoeun, president appointed by both) · Doc ID 139689

## 1. Facts
Hong Va Cambodia Garmen Manufacturing Co., Ltd., a garment-cutting and sewing enterprise in Pou Senchey district, Phnom Penh, employed about 2,300 workers. [Para 40] On 04 March 2013 the Labour Disputes Department received a complaint from the Federation of Unions for Cambodia raising 10 demands to improve working conditions; conciliation on 01 April 2013 left 4 of the 10 unresolved, and those 4 were referred to the panel on 29 April 2013. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 15] At the hearing the parties settled issues 1 and 2 by agreement, leaving issues 3 and 4 — a productivity bonus and an increase in the attendance bonus. [Para 19] The claimant union held registration No. 2849 dated 04 April 2013 but no certificate of most-representative status in the enterprise. [Para 41][Para 42]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must build a nursery and a breastfeeding room, or instead pay USD 35 per month? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company must issue the "pong" book (សៀវភៅបុង) so that workers may inspect it twice a month? [Para 4]
3. Whether the company must pay a productivity bonus (ប្រាក់រង្វាន់ស្នាដៃ) of USD 35 per month to workers in the inspection, garment-counting, garment-mending and cutting sections? [Para 43]
4. Whether the company must pay an additional USD 5 per month of regular attendance bonus, i.e. USD 15 per month in total? [Para 53]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the four sections earn less than the sewing workers even though they work hard, averaging only about USD 105 to USD 120 per month, so a USD 35 monthly skill bonus is owed; neighbouring factories such as "6-storey company" already pay a USD 15 attendance bonus. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 56]
**Employer (respondent):** it has no capacity to pay the USD 35 bonus; the four sections have different skills and duties from sewing workers and their wages are correspondingly different, and it already pays a USD 7 monthly incentive plus average weekly bonuses of 15,000 riel, and a USD 10 attendance bonus as required by Notification No. 230 K.B/S.C.N. of 25 July 2012. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 55][Para 58]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider (បដិសេធ ពិចារណា) the claims for the USD 35 productivity bonus and for the additional USD 5 attendance bonus; issues 1 and 2 were disposed of by the parties' agreement. [Para 19][Para 78][Para 89][Para 90][Para 91]

On issue 3 the panel first characterised the claim: no provision of the Labour Law and no collective agreement or other agreement between the parties obliges the employer to pay a USD 35 skill bonus to these four sections, so the demand is an interest dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍), not a rights dispute. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63] It cited Article 96 of the 1997 Labour Law on the definition and parties to a collective agreement, and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001, under which only the most representative union may request and negotiate a collective agreement and the employer must bargain with it. [Para 64][Para 66] From this the panel derived a standing rule: because an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement for one year under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 and removes the workers' right to strike on that subject, the panel may decide an interest dispute only where the union bringing it is most representative — alone or with others holding more than half the enterprise's workers — following Award No. 81/04-AVIGREEN and No. 98/04-GREAT UNION. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70] It applied the same approach as in Award No. 169/11-FORTUNE, No. 02/11-PO YUEN, No. 66/11-IN HAN SUNG and No. 31/12-B&N. [Para 71][Para 73] Since the claimant union had registration No. 2849 of 04 April 2013 but no most-representative certificate, the panel held it lacked the legal standing to bring an interest dispute and declined to consider the claim, leaving the parties to find a suitable solution themselves. [Para 74][Para 75][Para 76][Para 78]

On issue 4 the panel applied the same characterisation by reference to its reasoning on issue 3. [Para 84][Para 85] Point 2 of Notification No. 230 K.B/S.C.N. of 25 July 2012 requires at least USD 10 per month for workers who attend every working day without absence, and the panel found the employer already paid USD 10, so the demand for USD 15 exceeds the notified minimum. [Para 81][Para 82][Para 83] The panel accepted that the workers have a right to claim USD 15, but held the employer is under no obligation to grant it under the Notification, and again declined to consider the claim. [Para 86][Para 89]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim for a benefit that no provision of the Labour Law and no collective agreement or other agreement yet grants is an interest dispute, and the panel will not decide it on the merits unless the union bringing it holds most-representative status in the enterprise, alone or jointly with others representing more than half the workforce. A union holding only a registration certificate has no standing to bring such a claim, however strong its members' underlying economic grievance. [Para 63][Para 70][Para 75]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that if the employer were able to pay the USD 35 skill bonus or the USD 15 attendance bonus, the labour law would not prohibit it, and that the parties should seek an appropriate solution — remarks that do not affect the dismissal. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 87][Para 88]
