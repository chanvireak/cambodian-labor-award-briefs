# Award No. 237/13 — Sae Han International (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 13 December 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Vira, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · Doc ID 139894

## 1. Facts
Sae Han International (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a garment-cutting factory at Trapeang Thleung, Chom Chao, Pur Senchey, Phnom Penh, registered on 6 February 2008 and employing about 1,100 workers. [Para 1][Para 48][Para 49]
On 25 October 2013 the Labour Dispute Department received a telephone complaint that the workers had struck to press for improved working conditions; conciliation on 28 October 2013 left 8 of 15 demands unsettled, and the report No. 1374 of 31 October 2013 sent those 8 points to the panel. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 25]
The hearing was set for 8 November 2013, postponed at the employer's request to 13 November, and held on 15 November 2013; at it one point (removal of the head of administration) was settled and the workers withdrew points 4, 5 and 8, leaving points 1, 2, 3 and 6. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 28][Para 30][Para 31][Para 32]
Twelve workers were the original complainants, but only two attended the hearing — Mr P and Mr S — and the panel considered only their claims. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 59][Para 60]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the union leaders Ms P, Ms S and Ms L, and the many other workers, into employment? [Para 2][Para 54]
2. Whether the employer must pay a midday meal allowance of 4,000 riel per day? [Para 94]
3. Whether the employer must pay a 13th month's salary every year? [Para 100]
6. Whether the employer must pay an overtime cooperation allowance of 10 US dollars per month? [Para 110]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the two dismissed workers had done nothing wrong and should be reinstated; the company gave one meal to workers at 12 buildings in the Canadia Park area and 4,000 riel elsewhere, the wage was too low to live on, and 13th month pay was paid by other factories such as Sun Tech, Blue Tem and Bright Sky. [Para 92][Para 95][Para 96][Para 105][Para 107]
**Employer (respondent):** it could not take the workers back because their fixed-term contracts had reached their term, it had no capacity to pay a meal allowance, a 13th month or an overtime cooperation allowance, and it was currently making losses. [Para 3][Para 5][Para 7][Para 13][Para 99][Para 109]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all four remaining points were rejected and no payment or reinstatement was ordered. [Para 179][Para 180][Para 181][Para 182]
On point 1 the panel first cut the claim down: the three named union leaders and 29 other workers had given no written authority to the federations and did not appear, so only Mr P and Mr S were considered. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 56][Para 59][Para 60]
For Mr P the workers asserted a 3-month fixed-term contract from 20 July 2013 and dismissal on 30 September 2013, but produced no contract; the employer produced a probation contract signed 20 August 2013 that fixed no end date and only said it would not exceed 2 months. The panel therefore could not find a 3-month contract, held he began on 20 August 2013 on probation, and — because the employer neither contested the dismissal nor discharged the burden of showing there was none — presumed he was dismissed on 30 September 2013 during probation. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 74][Para 75][Para 129][Para 130][Para 135][Para 136]
Article 68 of the Labour Law caps a probation contract at 3 months for specialised workers and 1 month for ordinary workers, and the panel followed its earlier awards (No. 2703-Span Det, No. 12/10-Blue Som Senjury, No. 191/12-Alim) holding that an employer need not give a reason for dismissing during probation. It rejected his reinstatement. [Para 137][Para 138][Para 139][Para 140][Para 141][Para 142][Para 143]
For Mr S the probation contract was signed 21 May 2013 and the fixed-term contract ran 21 July to 20 October 2013, with notice of termination on 19 October 2013. Because 20 October 2013 was a Sunday, the term was pulled back one day to 19 October 2013, which both parties accepted as the last day of employment. The panel held this was not a dismissal but the non-renewal of a fixed-term contract under Article 73(1) of the 1997 Labour Law, and that an employer cannot be compelled to renew a contract without agreement. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 87][Para 145][Para 146][Para 147][Para 148][Para 149][Para 150][Para 152][Para 153]
On points 2, 3 and 6 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules or past practice creating the entitlement, classified each as an interest dispute, and cited Articles 96 and 96(1) of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 on the most representative union, together with Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 on the one-year effect of an interest award. [Para 155][Para 156][Para 157][Para 160][Para 162][Para 163][Para 175][Para 176]
The order is expressly non-binding and becomes final 8 days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Minister of Labour through the Council's secretariat. [Para 183]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker who asserts a fixed-term contract but produces none, against an employer's written probation contract that fixes no end date but caps the trial period, is treated as having begun on the probation date; dismissal during probation needs no stated reason, and the panel will not order reinstatement. [Para 129][Para 130][Para 141][Para 142]
Where a fixed-term contract's term falls on a weekly rest day, the term expires the preceding working day, and its expiry is a non-renewal, not a dismissal, so no reinstatement can be ordered. [Para 147][Para 149][Para 150]
A demand for a meal allowance, a 13th month or an overtime cooperation allowance that no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice supports is an interest dispute, and the panel will not create the entitlement. [Para 155][Para 156][Para 175][Para 176]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the employer's evidence showed it had also declined to renew the contracts of 50 other workers for lack of work, but decided nothing on their position. [Para 72]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's evidence list is numbered corruptly, jumping from 8 to "19" and "10" with items 11 to 18 absent, so the contract and identity documents cannot be matched to their numbering. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 42][Para 43]
The worker's name is printed two ways: "ភារ សុចាន់" in the narrative and "ភា សុចាន់" in the resignation letter and probation contract, and the union president appears as both "នាង ប៉ុន ថេត" and "នាង ប៊ុន ថេត". [Para 2][Para 54][Para 76][Para 89]
The text of Article 68 at para 138 is garbled, reading "ពី ខែ ចំពោះ កម្មករ ឯកទេស និង មូយ ខែ ចំពោះ កម្មករ ធម្មតា", so the 3-month and 1-month limits are reconstructed from context. [Para 138]
Para 64 repeats a fragment ("មូលហេតុ នៃ អវត្តមាន អត់ ច្បាប់ នេះ នៃ អវត្តមាន អត់ ច្បាប់ នេះ"), para 33 doubles the word "ខែ", para 180 carries a stray "2016", and the signature block at para 184 is largely destroyed, leaving "2025" and "ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ណាស់2977247" beside the chair's name. [Para 33][Para 64][Para 180][Para 184]
The panel's own numbering skips: the surviving points are 1, 2, 3 and 6 although only four remain, because the withdrawn points keep their original numbers. [Para 32]
