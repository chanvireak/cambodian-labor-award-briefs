# Award No. 41/13 — Ta Prohm Restoration Project (Archaeological Survey of India) (Arbitration Council, 5 April 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Ruoch Hun, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Kong Phally, president) · Doc ID 139658

## 1. Facts
The Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) executed the Indian-Cambodian cooperation project restoring Ta Prohm temple at Siem Reap under the 2002 inter-governmental agreement and a 2002 MoU with APSARA, hiring skilled and unskilled workers directly, 31 of whom are the claimants. [Para 18][Para 21][Para 190][Para 192][Para 223] Thirty were dismissed on 31 January 2013 and one woman on 2 January 2013, after the union asked to negotiate a collective agreement and the employer refused. [Para 3][Para 104][Para 110][Para 129] The workers had no written contracts, worked 8 hours a day 5.5 days a week, were paid daily and paid for six days. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 108][Para 109] Of the 31, three were third-term union leaders and the rest union members. [Para 111][Para 112] Conciliation failed on 7 points (report No. 008 of 12 February 2013); at the hearing points 4 and 5 were settled and point 7 withdrawn, leaving points 1, 2, 3 and 6. [Para 20][Para 26][Para 27][Para 28]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate the 31 union leaders and activists in their original jobs and pay them wages from dismissal until reinstatement? [Para 2][Para 101]
2. Whether the employer must grant paid annual leave as required by the Labour Law? [Para 4][Para 303]
3. Whether the employer must grant maternity leave and maternity pay to pregnant women workers? [Para 5][Para 312]
6. Whether the employer must negotiate a collective agreement with the workers? [Para 9][Para 322]

The panel first had to decide two procedural objections raised by the project head: whether the Labour Law applies at all to ASI's employment relationships, and which body is the employer. [Para 99][Para 100][Para 188][Para 189]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissal of all 31 was union discrimination, since the union was registered and certified as most representative and had notified its third-term leadership to the employer in writing on 21 August 2012; the employer was short of labour afterwards and hired 25 APSARA workers at USD 1 a day on top of their APSARA wages, which shows the dismissals were not economic. [Para 101][Para 134][Para 136][Para 139][Para 144]
**Employer (respondent):** the Labour Law does not cover ASI's employment relationships under Article 1; only the Indian Embassy can decide these claims, not the project head; and no work remains for the dismissed workers because 4 of its 5 programmes ended, leaving only the dance hall, with the embassy's approval of the reduction. It also denied knowing which workers were union members, never having received a membership list. [Para 99][Para 102][Para 103][Para 147][Para 148][Para 153][Para 154]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered reinstatement of the three union leaders with back pay from dismissal to return, and reinstatement of the remaining 28 with back pay; paid annual leave for all workers with one year's service; 90 days' maternity leave plus half wages and allowances for women with one year's service; and negotiation of a collective agreement with the union. [Para 332][Para 333][Para 334][Para 335][Para 336]
On jurisdiction the panel held Article 1 of the Labour Law applicable because the workers were not under the public-service or diplomatic statutes and the relationship was formed and performed on Cambodian territory; it read "all staff" as covering employees party to a labour contract. [Para 207][Para 208][Para 216][Para 219][Para 220] On the employer question it held that the Government of India contracted orally through ASI as its project agent, that the contract was civil rather than sovereign in character, and that under Article 2 the Government of India, represented by its embassy, is the employer, with ASI the only appropriate representative for information and evidence. [Para 223][Para 232][Para 234][Para 235][Para 237]
On issue 1 the panel applied Article 12 and Article 279 (no anti-union discrimination) and held the burden of proof on the party alleging discrimination; it found the employer knew of the union and its three leaders but had never been given the full membership list, so discrimination as to the 28 was not established. [Para 240][Para 241][Para 253][Para 254][Para 255] On procedure, however, the three leaders were protected under Prakas No. 305 clause 4 and Prakas No. 313 clause 7, and Article 293 required the labour inspector's prior authorisation for their dismissal, which was never sought; following awards 71/09, 09/10 and 91/11 it ordered them reinstated with back pay. [Para 257][Para 262][Para 263][Para 273][Para 279][Para 283] For the other 28 it applied Article 95: a mass layoff must follow merit, seniority and family burden, with written notice to worker representatives, and the employer must prove the reduction, which it did not; the dismissal was therefore unlawful and they too were reinstated with back pay. [Para 285][Para 296][Para 301][Para 302]
On issue 2 the panel applied Articles 166(1) and 167(1) — paid annual leave of one and a half days per month, 18 days a year, after one year's service — and rejected the employer's argument that daily-paid workers are excluded. [Para 303][Para 306][Para 310][Para 311] On issue 3 it applied Articles 182 and 183 — 90 days' maternity leave and half wages including allowances after one year's service — and held the method of wage calculation irrelevant to the entitlement. [Para 312][Para 315][Para 317][Para 320][Para 321] On issue 6 it applied Article 96 and Prakas No. 305 clause 9: the most representative union may require negotiation of a collective agreement, and this union held a valid certificate to 23 July 2014. [Para 322][Para 325][Para 328][Para 330][Para 331]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A foreign State that hires workers on Cambodian territory for a civil project is an employer under Article 2 of the Labour Law and cannot claim sovereign immunity from the Arbitration Council, and its implementing agency acts only as its representative; a mass layoff that ignores Article 95, or the dismissal of a protected union leader without the labour inspector's prior authorisation under Article 293, is unlawful and requires reinstatement with back pay. [Para 234][Para 279][Para 296][Para 301]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the Labour Law leans toward protecting union leadership, and that the burden of proving anti-union discrimination rests on the party alleging it, so a union that never gives the employer its membership list weakens its own discrimination case. [Para 242][Para 253][Para 266]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer-appointed arbitrator is printed as "Seng Ruoch Hun" in the header and "Seng Touch Hun" in the signature block. [Para 1][Para 337] The number dismissed is inconsistent: 31 claimants, 30 dismissed on 31 January 2013, and one woman on 2 January 2013, while the employer's letter of 28 February refers to 30. [Para 3][Para 104][Para 105] The 2002 agreement is dated only "09 month ? 2002" and the MoU date is given as both 2002 and 06 November 2002. [Para 190][Para 194] Paragraph 232 reasons that India did not contract as a sovereign State, which sits awkwardly with the sovereign-immunity discussion at paragraph 225; paragraph 188 repeats paragraph 99 verbatim. [Para 225][Para 232][Para 188] The annex table of the 31 workers is heavily garbled, mixing Latin fragments, Thai digits and inconsistent wage entries, so individual names, functions and start dates cannot be relied on. [Para 337] One earlier award is cited as 148/08 in one place and 148/07 in another. [Para 265][Para 298]
