# Award No. 202/13 — Unixtar Technology (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 17 October 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothi (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Pen Bunchia (chair, appointed by the two arbitrators) · Doc ID 139872

## 1. Facts
Unixtar Technology (Cambodia) Co., Ltd produces and assembles electronic connectors and cable assemblies, registered as Inv. 3164 E/2012 on 18 December 2012, employing about 530 workers. [Para 47][Para 48] The Union of Cambodia Labour (CLC) and the company's Democratic Union of Workers claimed on behalf of 503 workers who transferred representation rights by letter of 12 September 2013. [Para 52] Conciliation at the Kandal provincial labour department on 13 September 2013 covered 13 points: 4 settled, 9 not. [Para 26] Workers struck from 12 September 2013 at about 12:00 to 17 September 2013 at about 11:30 without giving the employer prior notice. [Para 56][Para 61] At the hearing two further points (5 and 7) settled and five (1, 2, 3, 4 and 9) were withdrawn, leaving issues 6 and 8. [Para 31][Para 32][Para 33]

## 2. Issues in dispute
6. Whether the employer must maintain basic wages and the regular attendance bonus for workers throughout the strike from 12 September 2013 (about 12:00) to 17 September 2013 (about 11:30), and refrain from retaliating against any worker? [Para 12][Para 55]
8. Whether the employer must allow pregnant workers one day per month to attend antenatal health checks while maintaining their basic wage and other benefits? [Para 16][Para 66]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the strike followed the employer's failure to answer the successive six-point request of 30 August 2013; wages and bonuses should continue during the strike; and deducting pay for antenatal visits frightens women out of seeking care. [Para 56][Para 60][Para 77]
**Employer (respondent):** there is no duty to pay wages or the attendance bonus during a strike, and the Labour Law nowhere requires paid time off for antenatal checks. [Para 63][Para 79]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both remaining claims were rejected; the claim on issue 8 was dismissed without consideration (បដិសេធ ពិចារណា). [Para 144][Para 145]

On issue 6 the panel held that articles 332 and 72(1) of the Labour Law suspend the employment contract during a strike, so wages are not payable, and it followed its earlier awards holding that strikers receive no wage whether the strike is lawful or not. [Para 95][Para 98][Para 101] It found no breach of article 334's ban on hiring replacements, since no replacement hiring was alleged. [Para 104] The strike itself was unlawful: no seven-day notice as required by article 324 and no exhaustion of conciliation under article 320(4). [Para 118] The regular attendance bonus likewise failed, because strikers were absent from work. [Para 111][Para 120]

On issue 8 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice requiring the employer to keep basic wage and benefits during an antenatal check, so the claim was an interest dispute. [Para 129][Para 130] It then held that an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement and binds all workers for one year, extinguishing their right to strike on that issue, and that only a most-representative union may bring such a dispute. [Para 136][Para 137] The Democratic Union had no registration receipt from the Ministry, so it lacked the legal capacity to bring the claim. [Para 141][Para 142]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that has not received its registration receipt from the Ministry of Labour cannot bring an interest dispute, because an award on such a dispute binds the whole workforce and displaces its right to strike; only a union representing more than half the workers may do so. Workers who strike forfeit wages and the regular attendance bonus for the strike period, whether the strike is lawful or not.

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the employer did not forbid antenatal visits and that no replacement hiring was alleged; neither observation changed the outcome. [Para 104][Para 127]

[Para 67][Para 74] Withdrawn claims included 4,000 riel for lunch and 2,000 riel for overtime meals. [Para 4] The award date is 17 October 2013, the deadline having been extended from 11 October.
