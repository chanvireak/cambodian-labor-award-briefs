# Award No. 143/13 — Cambodia Brewery Ltd (Arbitration Council, 13 August 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Li Tai Seng for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Kong Pollak chair) · Doc ID 139830

## 1. Facts
Cambodia Brewery Ltd, a brewery on Street 315, Boeung Kak 1, Tuol Kork, Phnom Penh, employs about 345 staff and engages women as beer promoters in bars and shops on three shifts with different monthly wages. [Para 1][Para 64] The Democratic Union of Workers at Cambodia Brewery, with about 100 members at the plant, filed 13 demands; four were settled at the hearing and one withdrawn, leaving nine, and the union is not the most representative in the company. [Para 26][Para 67]

## 2. Issues in dispute
4. Whether the employer must recalculate overtime for promoters working the second and third shifts, counting hours after 21:30 as overtime at 150% of the 61 USD base wage? [Para 68]
6. Whether the employer must reimburse the actual medical costs of a promoter's maternity leave? [Para 83]
7. Whether the employer must pay a 30 USD monthly milk allowance until the child is 36 months old? [Para 90]
8. Whether the employer must grant 100 days' maternity leave on 100% wages? [Para 93]
9. Whether the employer must fix a single annual sales target that does not change month to month? [Para 97]
10. Whether the employer must raise the base wage to 150 USD a month? [Para 107]
11. Whether the employer must apply an annual wage increase of between 10% and 20%? [Para 112]
12. Whether the employer must pay a seniority bonus of 5 USD for each year of service? [Para 116]
13. Whether the employer must pay a 13th month's salary equal to the last month's wage? [Para 125]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the promoters' normal hours are 17:00 to 21:30, so work after 21:30 is overtime at 150% of the 61 USD base wage, as the rival brewery Cambrew Ltd pays its promoters [Para 70]; the wages are too low and the company should match Cambrew's 100 USD base, and the demands on maternity pay, milk and sales targets are necessary for their livelihood.
**Employer (respondent):** the three shifts are the promoters' normal hours, each carrying its own monthly wage of 61, 71 or 81 USD, and the promoters choose their shift before signing, so no overtime arises [Para 149]; the remaining claims exceed the law or are matters of managerial discretion, and the company has no capacity to meet them.

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay the 13th month's salary equal to the last month's wage and refused all eight other claims. No sum is quantified. [Para 273][Para 274][Para 276]

On issue 4, the panel classified the claim as a rights dispute under Articles 139 (new) and 144 of the Labour Law, then found that the working-hours clause in the contracts — hours to be fixed by the promotion coordinator according to the number of shops and the season — did not support the employer's account, because the hours it described at the hearing differed from those in the three contracts produced. [Para 159][Para 160] It nevertheless refused the claim: the parties agreed on the actual practice of three shifts and their wages, and the promoters accepted at hiring that a higher monthly wage corresponded to longer hours, which showed they had agreed those hours as their normal hours. [Para 164][Para 168]

On issue 6, no law, collective agreement, internal rule or practice requires reimbursement of maternity medical costs, so the claim was an interest dispute; and because the union was not the most representative in the enterprise the panel declined to consider it. [Para 174][Para 187][Para 188]

Issues 7, 10, 11 and 12 were refused on the same footing: each exceeded anything required by law or practice, each was an interest dispute, and the union lacked standing to bring it. [Para 194][Para 240][Para 246][Para 252]

On issue 8, Articles 182 and 183 give 90 days' maternity leave on half wages, and the employer already grants 90 days on 50%, so a claim for 100 days at 100% exceeded the law and was an interest dispute refused for want of standing. [Para 195][Para 199][Para 205]

On issue 9, the claim concerned the employer's right under Article 2 to organise and manage the company, so it was a rights dispute. [Para 206][Para 207] The employer sets each month's target from the average of the preceding three months, the promoters produced no evidence that this made the incentive bonus unattainable, and the panel found the practice lawful and reasonable. [Para 232][Para 234]

On issue 13, the claim was a rights dispute because it rested on the company's internal rules made under Article 23. [Para 254][Para 257] Article 5(d) of those rules grants one month's wage to any employee who has completed a year, and pro rata to those who have not, payable two weeks before Khmer New Year; the rules contain no exception for promoters, who are employees under Article 3, so the benefit was owed. [Para 266][Para 269][Para 270]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the parties agree on the actual shift structure and the monthly wage attached to each shift, and the workers knew at hiring that a higher wage meant longer hours, those hours are the workers' normal hours and work within them is not overtime — even where the contractual clause leaves the hours to the employer's coordinator and the contracts do not match the employer's account. [Para 160][Para 164] An internal rule made under Article 23 binds every employee of the enterprise unless it expressly excludes a category; a beer promoter is an employee under Article 3, so she is entitled to the 13th-month payment the rule grants. [Para 257][Para 270] A claim that exceeds a statutory entitlement — 90 days' maternity leave on half wages under Articles 182 and 183 — is an interest dispute, and the panel will not decide an interest dispute where the union is not the most representative in the enterprise. [Para 195][Para 187]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that the rival brewery Cambrew Ltd, though in the same Beer Selling Industry Cambodia association, is a direct competitor whose benefits differ, so its practice proves nothing about this employer [Para 74][Para 75], was not necessary once the claim failed on the parties' own agreed practice.

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The panel is said to have been constituted on "08 July 2022 2013" [Para 24].
- The third shift's end is printed as 24:30 in the table of working hours [Para 81] and as 00:30 in the contracts and reasons [Para 154]; the table also records "75 hours" where 7.5 hours is meant.
- The maternity entitlement in the company's practice appears as "15 90 days" [Para 199], a stray figure left in the text.
- The operative order on the 13th month inserts a stray "10" after "bonus" [Para 276]; the reasons at [Para 273] and the rule at [Para 266] give one month's wage.
- The award splits its effect: the part on rights disputes is immediately final under the agreement of 17 July 2013, while the part on interest disputes becomes final only after eight days [Para 277][Para 285]. The union's name is spelled inconsistently throughout; individuals are redacted in this brief as required.
