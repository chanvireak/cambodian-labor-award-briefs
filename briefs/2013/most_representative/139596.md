# Award No. 05/13-Cambo Kotop — Cambo Kotop Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 January 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-chosen; Liv Sovanna, worker-chosen; Ang Eng Tong, chair) · Doc ID 139596

## 1. Facts
Cambo Kotop Ltd is a garment factory at Kantork Cheung Village, Sangkat Kantork, Khan Po Sen Chey, Phnom Penh, employing about 2,000 workers; its Khmer Workers' Power Union local is the claimant and held only a request receipt for registration dated 13 November 2012. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 34][Para 35] Of five demands raised in the 11 December 2012 complaint, conciliation on 2 January 2013 resolved all but one: the demand that the company issue payslips stating the total monthly wage in advance of payday. [Para 10][Para 11][Para 12] The union does not hold most representative status in the factory. [Para 36]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must issue a payslip stating the total monthly wage to workers 05 days before payday, as demanded in the non-conciliation report? [Para 3][Para 38]
2. Whether that demand, restated at the hearing as 02 days before payday, is an enforceable right? [Para 38][Para 53]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** receiving the payslip two days before payday lets workers object in time where wages have been miscalculated, so that the whole correct wage is paid on payday itself. [Para 46][Para 47] The demand in the non-conciliation report was for five days. [Para 3]
**Employer (respondent):** it already gives notice two days ahead by issuing a monthly total-hours sheet showing base hours and overtime hours for each group, which workers sign, and the administration then computes wages from that signed sheet; a miscalculation is corrected within two days, the practice has been used for years and other branches follow it, and the payroll system cannot be recalculated on a single day. [Para 4][Para 39][Para 40][Para 43][Para 49][Para 51][Para 52]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider the demand for a payslip showing total monthly wage 02 days before payday; the award is non-binding. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 85]

The panel framed the question as whether the employer is under a duty to provide the payslip two days before payday, and read Article 112(b) of the Labour Law, which requires the employer to take measures to inform each worker clearly and understandably of all wage components for each pay period whenever the wage may change. [Para 53][Para 54] Following Award No. 05/06-DBLYN D, it noted that wages vary with absence, with voluntary overtime and with piece-rate contracts, so notice must be given whenever the wage changes. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57][Para 58][Para 62]

The panel accepted that a detailed payslip is a transparency measure that removes workers' suspicion and improves professional relations, but held that Article 112(b) imposes only a duty of clear notification and does not require the notice to precede payday. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65] On the facts, the employer did issue each worker a payslip on payday itemising base wage, overtime and other benefits, and so complied with Article 112(b). [Para 66][Para 67] The two-day correction period the employer allowed for miscalculation was found appropriate. [Para 69][Para 70] No article other than 112(b) requires a payslip before payday, so the demand was classified as an interests dispute. [Para 71][Para 72]

Having classified it as an interests dispute, the panel applied Article 96(2) of the Labour Law and Clause 9 of Prakas No. 305 dated 22 November 2001: because an interests award under Clause 43 of Prakas No. 099 replaces the collective agreement for one year and strips non-members of the right to strike on the subject, only a most representative union, or unions together holding more than half the workers, may bring such a claim. [Para 73][Para 75][Para 78][Para 79] Since this union lacked that status, the demand was declined, consistent with Awards 81/04-Evergreen, 98/04 and 4169/11-Fortune Teo, Issue 5. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 81][Para 82]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Article 112(b) obliges an employer to notify each worker clearly of every wage component for each pay period, but not before payday; a demand to move payslip delivery earlier than the day of payment is an interests dispute that only a most representative union may bring. [Para 65][Para 72][Para 79]

**Obiter dicta:** itemised payslips are a transparency measure that dispels workers' suspicions about their wages and improves professional relations. [Para 63][Para 64]
