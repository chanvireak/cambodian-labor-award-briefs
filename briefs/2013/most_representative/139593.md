# Award No. 04/13-CIK CAMBO — CIK CAMBO Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 13 February 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun, employer-chosen; Liv Sovanna, worker-chosen; Kong Phallack, chair) · Doc ID 139593

## 1. Facts
CIK CAMBO Co., Ltd. is a garment factory at Ang Village, Sangkat Chom Chao, Khan Po Senchey, Phnom Penh, employing 979 workers. [Para 1][Para 14] After a strike from 23 November 2012 to 30 November 2012, the Cambodian Labour Union Federation (CLUF) local union raised nine demands; conciliation resolved four of them and six went to arbitration, the parties choosing non-binding arbitration for their interests disputes. [Para 23][Para 6][Para 10] The union was registered as professional organisation No. 2634 dated 26 September 2012 and, after 57 of 70 fee-paying workers withdrew, held 13 members. [Para 16][Para 17]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (B) Whether the employer must permit each worker an advance payment of US$50 from wages for travel to and from their homeland? [Para 18]
2. Whether the employer must dismiss Kim Heng, the translator, for verbally abusing the workers? [Para 19]
5. Whether the employer must raise the minimum wage of packaging, ironing and carton workers to US$71, equal to the sewing section? [Para 20]
6. Whether the employer must refrain from replacing striking workers with newly recruited workers? [Para 23]
7. Whether the employer must pay an additional US$10 attendance bonus per month on top of the existing US$10? [Para 25]
8. Whether the employer must pay a monthly US$2 in lieu of lunch? [Para 27]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** workers needed the US$50 advance to travel to their homeland; the translator had verbally abused strikers, though no worker heard it firsthand; packaging, ironing and carton workers produced the same output, held the same skills and had the same contracts as sewing workers; the employer recruited new workers daily during the strike; other factories paid a US$20 attendance bonus and US$13 monthly for lunch. [Para 19][Para 21][Para 23][Para 26][Para 27]
**Employer (respondent):** it could not afford the advance; it had no knowledge of any verbal abuse and had rejected the demand; sewing workers were skilled and hard to recruit while packaging workers were not; it recruited no replacements and had no such policy; it rejected the bonus and lunch demands. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 22][Para 24][Para 28]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issues 2 and 5 rejected, Issues 6, 1(B), 7 and 8 declined for consideration; Part I (Issue 2, 5, 6) is a binding award and Part II (Issues 1/B, 7, 8) a non-binding award. [Para 80][Para 81][Para 82]

The panel first separated rights disputes from interests disputes under paragraph 2 of Article 312 of the Labour Law and Clause 43 of Prakas No. 099, treating interpretation and enforcement of law or a collective agreement as rights disputes decided by law and all else as interests disputes decided in equity. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 32][Para 33] On representation, it read Clause 19 of Prakas No. 099 to require written authorisation, and refused a request letter from 32 workers dated 19 January 2013 because it was submitted on 23 January, after the 18 January hearing; the disputing parties were therefore the 13 union members only. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 39][Para 41]

Issue 1(B) was an interests dispute: no provision of the Labour Law and no collective agreement or contract allowed advances from wages. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45] Because an interests award displaces the collective agreement for a year and removes non-members' right to strike on the subject, the panel held only a most representative union (MRS) or unions with more than half the workforce may bring such a claim; this union held neither, so the demand was declined. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 52][Para 53]

Issue 2 was a rights dispute about contracts of employment; under Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 311 of the Civil Code only the contracting parties may terminate, so a third party cannot demand a dismissal. [Para 54][Para 57][Para 59][Para 60] The panel added that workers may seek removal only with evidence that the person is dangerous and cannot be kept, and found the allegation of verbal abuse unsupported because no witness heard it. [Para 61][Para 64]

Issue 5 engaged Article 106's equal-wage rule, which requires equal working conditions, professional skill and output. [Para 66][Para 67] The workers produced no evidence of output or skill, so the claim that packaging work equalled sewing work failed. [Para 70]

Issue 6 was declined because no strike was current and the demand concerned a future event, following Award 10/03-Jacqsintex. [Para 72][Para 73] Issue 7 was an interests dispute because Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 sets a minimum US$10 attendance bonus that the employer already met. [Para 74][Para 75][Para 76] Issue 8 was an interests dispute because neither statute nor agreement provided for lunch payments. [Para 78][Para 79]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union may bring an interests dispute to the panel only if it holds most-representative status or represents more than half the enterprise's workers, because an interests award substitutes for the collective agreement and binds non-members. [Para 51]

**Obiter dicta:** a union appearing for workers must hold written authorisation, and a letter of authorisation produced after the hearing cannot cure the defect. [Para 37][Para 39]

[Para 30][Para 36] The translator's name appears as both "Kim Heng" and "King Heng".
