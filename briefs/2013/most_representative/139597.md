# Award No. 10/13-Gladpeer Garment — Gladpeer Garment Factory (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 February 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-chosen, dissenting in part; Ven Pov, worker-chosen; Pen Bunchhea, chair) · Doc ID 139597

## 1. Facts
Gladpeer Garment Factory (Cambodia) Ltd is a garment factory at Prey Pring Village, Sangkat Chom Chao, Khan Po Sen Chey, Phnom Penh, employing 5,500 workers; the C.CAWDU local union, registered as No. 2007 dated 08 December 2010, is the claimant and does not hold most representative status. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 35] Of fifteen demands, conciliation resolved three and a strike followed, ended by the panel's cease-strike order of 16 January 2013; five more were agreed before the hearing and two withdrawn, leaving Issues 5, 7, 8 and 11. [Para 15][Para 17][Para 20][Para 23] Issue 11 concerned the non-renewal of a union activist's fixed-duration contract at its expiry on 31 October 2010. [Para 61][Para 62]

## 2. Issues in dispute
5. Whether the employer must provide a 4,000 riel payment in lieu of lunch? [Para 36][Para 69]
7. Whether the employer must triple the bonuses paid to team leaders in the cutting section and double those paid to fabric organisers? [Para 42][Para 78]
8. Whether the employer must pay the attendance bonus in proportion to the days of authorised leave for personal commitment taken by workers? [Para 52][Para 81]
11. Whether the employer must reinstate Ms S and provide back pay from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 55][Para 118]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** lunch payment supports livelihoods as living costs rise and other factories provide it; the production bonuses are low for the section relative to others and exist to motivate; Article 103 and Awards 107/06 and 119/09 support pro-rata attendance bonus; the non-renewal was union discrimination, Ms S having been elected a union activist on 18 September 2010 and having joined the national strike of 13 to 16 September 2010. [Para 38][Para 49][Para 54][Para 62]
**Employer (respondent):** it cannot afford the lunch payment, and it has never provided one nor been asked for one; no law, Prakas or agreement obliges it to raise the production bonuses; it maintains the current practice on attendance bonus; it simply did not renew an expired fixed-duration contract, giving notice on 21 October 2010 and paying outstanding wage, payment in lieu of annual leave and five per cent severance, and it knew nothing of Ms S's union role and dismissed strikers and non-strikers alike. [Para 39][Para 51][Para 54][Para 61][Para 62][Para 63]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer ordered to pay the attendance bonus in proportion to days of authorised leave for personal commitment (Issue 8), reinstatement and back pay rejected (Issue 11), and Issues 5 and 7 declined; Part I binding, Part II non-binding. [Para 139][Para 140][Para 141]

Issue 5 was an interests dispute: neither the Labour Law, a collective agreement nor any agreement between the parties provides a lunch allowance, and under Article 96(2) and Clause 9 of Prakas No. 305 an interests claim may be brought only by a most representative union, because a Clause 43 award replaces the collective agreement and removes non-members' right to strike. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 73][Para 76][Para 77] The union lacked that status, so the demand was declined, as in Awards 81/04-Evergreen, 98/04-Great Union and 169/11-Fortune Teo. [Para 77] Issue 7 failed on the same ground, no provision requiring tripled or doubled bonuses being shown. [Para 80][Para 81]

Issue 8 was a rights dispute because the attendance bonus is set by Point 2 of Notification No. 230 dated 25 July 2012. [Para 83][Para 84] That notification superseded Notification No. 041 dated 7 March 2011 and raised the bonus to US$10 while adding the words "without absence"; the Labour Advisory Committee has never defined "absence", and the panel declined to assume it means authorised or unauthorised absence. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 92][Para 93][Para 96] Read with Article 103, the bonus is part of wages. [Para 111][Para 112] Under paragraph 6 of Article 71 and paragraph 1 of Article 72, authorised leave suspends the contract and the employer need not pay wages or bonus for those days, but it also may not withhold the entire bonus. [Para 113][Para 114][Para 115][Para 116][Para 117] The employer was therefore ordered to pay pro rata. [Para 118]

Issue 11 turned on two questions: whether the employer may decline to renew, and whether it discriminated. [Para 121] Under paragraph 1 of Article 73 a fixed-duration contract terminates automatically at its end date, so neither party can force renewal; following Awards 100/07-Hoyear Cambodia, 122/10-Meng Yan and 105/11-Goldfame, the panel found no duty to renew. [Para 126][Para 128][Para 132] On discrimination, Articles 12 and 279 forbid weighing union affiliation in decisions on recruitment, management, discipline and dismissal, and prior awards reinstate where discrimination is proved. [Para 133] The claimant bears the burden of proof, and the workers produced no statements or evidence that Ms S's activism caused the non-renewal, or that strikers were singled out; her own commencement date was given inconsistently. [Para 57][Para 59][Para 133][Para 135] The panel therefore found no discrimination and no basis for reinstatement or back pay. [Para 136][Para 137]

Arbitrator Ing Sothy recorded a dissent under Clause 37 of Prakas No. 099, arguing that a bonus is a conditional incentive whose conditions belong to the employer, so a worker who takes leave does not fulfil the condition and is not entitled to a proportionate share. [Para 141][Para 142][Para 149][Para 158]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Authorised leave for personal commitment suspends the contract, so the employer need not pay wages or the attendance bonus for those days but may not forfeit the whole month's bonus; it must pay the US$10 bonus in proportion to the days of authorised leave taken. [Para 114][Para 116][Para 117]

**Obiter dicta:** for an interests claim to be arbitrable the claimant union must hold most representative status or represent more than half the workforce, since the resulting award binds non-members who did not strike over it. [Para 76][Para 77]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award date is 22 February 2013 in the header, while the procedural section records a deferral from 7 February to 20 February 2013. [Para 1][Para 29] The Memorandum of Understanding is dated 3 October 2012 in the procedural section and 30 October 2012 in the order. [Para 25][Para 139] The dismissed worker's name is printed inconsistently as Thai Sreymao, Thai Sreypov and Thai Sreymaor, and her commencement date is given as 17 January 2008 in the hearing and 17 January 2009 in her written statement. [Para 57][Para 59][Para 23] The dissent annex itself refers to "issue 6 ... Issue 8", an internal misnumbering. [Para 143] The evidence section is omitted from the English version. [Para 30]
