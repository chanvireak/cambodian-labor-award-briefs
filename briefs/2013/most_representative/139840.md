# Award No. 156/13 — Pou Yuen (Cambodia) Enterprise (Bray) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 28 August 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Kong Phallak, president) · dissenting opinion by Ing Sothy annexed · Doc ID 139840

## 1. Facts
Pou Yuen (Cambodia) Enterprise (Bray) Ltd, a shoe factory in Phnom Penh employing about 3,700 workers, faced thirteen demands from two factory unions and their federation. [Para 67][Para 68] Three were settled at the hearing and ten went to adjudication. [Para 27] The disputes arose after the dismissal of a team leader, Khim Champa, elected president of the Khmer Workers' Spirit Union on 2 June 2013 and given notice on 28 June 2013. [Para 88][Para 87] The parties had signed a memorandum of understanding on 3 October 2012 making decisions on rights disputes binding. [Para 29][Para 31]

## 2. Issues in dispute
2. Whether the employer must reinstate Khim Champa, president of the factory union, without union discrimination? [Para 76]
4. Whether the employer must dismiss the staff member Sith for abusive language and anti-union conduct? [Para 95]
5. Whether the employer must continue the contracts of Samol Sopheap and Va Kim Sron? [Para 104]
6. Whether the employer must add USD 10 to the monthly attendance bonus and 1,000 riel to the overtime meal allowance? [Para 130]
7. Whether the employer must provide one free lunch per day to every worker? [Para 135]
9. Whether the employer must open an additional staff gate? [Para 138]
10. Whether workers in Building A must be allowed to leave five minutes early? [Para 151]
11. Whether the employer must pay a USD 10 monthly milk allowance for children aged 1 to 18 months, instead of the existing USD 5 for 18 months to 3 years? [Para 157]
12. Whether the employer must restore a properly equipped crèche with carers and toys? [Para 164]
13. Whether the employer must add USD 2 to the USD 8 monthly travel and accommodation allowance? [Para 172]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Khim Champa's successive fixed-term contracts ran past two years, converting his engagement into one of indefinite duration, so non-renewal was a dismissal; as an elected union leader he was protected under Article 293 and no inspector's consent was sought; the union's other demands were modest and long overdue. [Para 76][Para 79]
**Employer (respondent):** Khim Champa's contract simply expired and was not renewed because his team leader asked that it not be, he being uncooperative when goods had to be moved; every renewal had been compensated at 5% under Article 73(6); no law or agreement obliges the extra bonus, meal, gate, crèche or allowance. [Para 77][Para 84][Para 134]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement of one worker and restoration of the crèche were ordered; the remaining eight claims were refused. [Para 421][Para 427]

The panel first addressed standing. The federation's authorisation letter was not valid, so it could not represent anyone. [Para 180][Para 181] Yan Buntha and Nguo Ro, recognised union officers, could represent members; Khim Champa and Vong Nam, elected but not yet formally recognised, could act only for themselves. [Para 190][Para 195][Para 199] Decisions therefore bind only the factory union's members and those two individuals. [Para 200]

On issue 2 the panel held the claim to be a rights dispute and applied Article 67(2), under which a fixed-duration contract may not exceed two years and renewals may not carry the total beyond that. [Para 208][Para 210] Khim Champa's contracts ran from 13 July 2011 to 13 July 2013, so the engagement became one of indefinite duration. [Para 81][Para 224] Non-renewal was therefore a dismissal, and under Article 293 and Prakas 305/01 Articles 3(3) and 4 an elected union candidate is protected from the poll until recognition and may not be dismissed without the labour inspector's prior consent. [Para 226][Para 227][Para 244] The union had notified the employer and held a receipt dated 24 June 2013; no consent was sought, so the dismissal breached Article 293 and reinstatement was ordered. [Para 266][Para 275][Para 278]

On issue 4 the panel applied Articles 65 and 311 of the Civil Code: only the parties to a contract may dissolve it, and a worker may demand a manager's removal only on proof that he is dangerous. [Para 280][Para 283][Para 288] No evidence of the alleged assault was produced, so the claim failed. [Para 298][Para 299]

Issue 5 failed for want of jurisdiction: neither woman attended and neither had given a power of attorney, and the union could not prove their membership from a dues table lacking dates or union identity. [Para 304][Para 316][Para 322]

Issues 6, 7, 11(b) and 13 were held to be interest disputes. [Para 327][Para 347][Para 398][Para 417] The panel found the employer already paid the USD 10 attendance bonus under Notification 230/12, the 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance under Notification 041/11, and USD 8 travel allowance against the USD 7 required. [Para 324][Para 339][Para 415] Because an interest award replaces the collective agreement for all workers, only the most representative union may bring such a claim, and this union was not. [Para 330][Para 335]

Issue 9 was a rights dispute but turned on Article 2: the number of gates is a management prerogative, and the workers produced no evidence that fifteen minutes were needed to leave. [Para 356][Para 364] Issue 10 likewise failed: Article 137 caps the working day at eight hours, the employer's 07:00–11:00 and 12:00–16:00 schedule was lawful, and a six-minute walk within a one-hour break was reasonable. [Para 371][Para 374][Para 383]

Issue 11(a) failed because changing a contract term requires the consent of both parties, and the existing agreement already fixed USD 5 for children aged 18 months to 3 years. [Para 387][Para 395] Issue 12 succeeded: Article 186 requires a crèche where at least 100 women are employed, the employer had built one and then abandoned it for lack of users, and it was ordered restored. [Para 400][Para 407][Para 412]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive fixed-term contracts whose combined duration passes two years convert the engagement into one of indefinite duration under Article 67(2), so ending it is a dismissal; where the worker is a protected union candidate, that dismissal requires the labour inspector's prior consent and is otherwise unlawful. [Para 210][Para 221][Para 275]

**Obiter dicta:** That a third party cannot compel the termination of another's contract, and that the number of factory gates and the timing of shifts fall within lawful management discretion, were stated as limits on those claims rather than as grounds of the orders made. [Para 285][Para 356]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is not unanimous: arbitrator Ing Sothy annexed a dissent on issue 2, arguing that Article 73(6) compensation at each renewal kept the contracts fixed-term. [Para 436][Para 445] The contract's total length is printed as "2 years 01 day" in the findings and "2 years 019 days" in the reasons. [Para 83][Para 222] Prakas 099 is dated "21 April 2023 2004" and "21 April 2019 2004". [Para 203][Para 330] The travel allowance is described as USD 8 "per year month". [Para 414] The USD 10 milk claim prints as "2 10". [Para 431] The union's recognition letter is numbered 1522 and dated 31 December 2012 in the evidence list but 30 December 2012 in the reasons. [Para 55][Para 73]
