# Award No. 207/13 — Quantum Clothing (Cambodia) Limited (Arbitration Council, 18 October 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Huon Chundy for the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair) · binding award under the MoU of 3 October 2012 · Doc ID 139874

## 1. Facts
Quantum Clothing (Cambodia) Limited, a garment factory registered as Inv. 701E/2000 of 31 July 2000 and employing 3,200 workers, was renamed during 2013 from New Eastland Clothing; its United Kingdom parent, Brown Hope, was acquired by Etozo in July 2012, with the ownership change taking effect on 2 June 2013. [Para 15][Para 24][Para 18] The FTUWK local union at the factory, registered as No. 2965 and claiming 494 members, demanded that the employer pay each worker termination compensation because ownership had changed. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 19] The employer answered that only the parent company's owner had changed, that positions and benefits were untouched, and that the lease had been renewed. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 29]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay termination compensation to each worker under the Labour Law because of a change in corporate ownership? [Para 18][Para 36]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the renaming and the new owner have dissolved the factory's credibility with the workforce, and the previous owner must therefore pay each worker's termination compensation under the Labour Law. [Para 18][Para 19]
**Employer (respondent):** the factory was renamed only to avoid confusion, there has been no change to the workers' positions and benefits, and only the owner of the parent company in the United Kingdom changed; the company has operated in Cambodia for 13 years, has committed to continue, and has renewed its land lease for another 8 years from 2013. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 25][Para 27][Para 29]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the workers' demand that the employer pay each worker termination compensation under the Labour Law because of a change in corporate ownership; type of award: binding [Para 45][Para 47].

The panel first classified the claim. Paragraph 2 of Article 312 of the Labour Law gives it legal decision of disputes over the interpretation and enforcement of laws, regulations or a collective agreement, and equity for all other disputes, while Clause 43 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 makes an award on an interest dispute a substitute collective agreement [Para 30][Para 32]. Following awards 05/11-M & V (Branch 1), 13/11-Gold Kamvimex, 14/11-GXG, 31/13-Quint Major Industrial and 62/11-Ocean Garment, the panel held that a demand founded on a statutory right is a rights dispute, and since this demand concerned the statutory right to termination compensation it fell to be decided by law [Para 34][Para 36]. On the merits it applied Paragraph 1 of Article 87, under which a change in the legal status of the employer, particularly by succession, inheritance, sale, merger or transfer of funds, leaves all labour contracts in effect on the day of the change binding between the new employer and the workers of the former enterprise, and followed awards 98/07-Sky Sino, 83/08-Le Grand Cafe and 100/08-Gawon Apparel holding that such a change does not affect the contracts. [Para 36][Para 37] On the facts, only Brown Hope's owner had changed, corporate changes were minimal, management, posts and benefits were maintained, and no contract had been terminated — so Article 87 was not violated and no compensation was owed. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 43][Para 44] The panel accepted that the change of ownership had cost the factory credibility with the workers, but found the employer had given reasons for the renaming and evidenced its commitment to keep operating. [Para 42]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A change in the legal status of the employer — including the acquisition of a foreign parent company — does not terminate the employment contracts of the Cambodian workforce and does not entitle the workers to termination compensation, provided the contracts, posts and benefits continue with the new employer: Paragraph 1 of Article 87 of the Labour Law binds the successor employer to the contracts in effect on the day of the change. [Para 36][Para 43][Para 44]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's finding that the union, with 494 members, does not hold most representative status at the factory [Para 17] played no part in the decision, which rested on the claim being a rights dispute; so too the remarks on the factory's loss of credibility, which the panel resolved without making it a ground of decision [Para 42].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The panel's composition contradicts itself: Huon Chundy is named as the worker-party arbitrator in the header [Para 1] but "An Nan" signs that seat in the signature block [Para 49]. Both names are reproduced as printed; neither was corrected.
- The document is an unofficial English translation, and the "Evidence" section was omitted from it entirely, so the award lists no exhibits. [Para 13][Para 14]
- OCR has systematically dropped the "th" ligature, producing "hemployer", "thevidence", "istill", "to" for "the" throughout [Para 1][Para 3][Para 18]; the same fault leaves "whathis factory has been renamed" and "their claim. to" as fragments [Para 18][Para 23].
- The lease is described as renewed "for another 8 years starting from 2013" [Para 29] but the quoted Clause 2 states a term of 3 years from 10 January 2013 to 31 December 2015 with a possible 5-year renewal — the two figures do not reconcile.
- Worker representatives' names are printed without labels, e.g. "Under-Secretary General of FTUWK 1. Mr Y Roth Keo Pisey" and "Activist of the union 3. Mr K Chorda" [Para 1], so which title attaches to whom is not recoverable from the English text.
