# Award No. 159/13 — Samudra Plastic Packing (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 August 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Ang Eng Thong, president) · Doc ID 139842

## 1. Facts
Samudra Plastic Packing (Cambodia) Ltd, a plastic-bag factory in Phnom Penh employing about 110 workers, faced four demands from the Cambodian Labour Union and the factory's C.CAWDU union. [Para 51][Para 52] One was settled in conciliation on 24 July 2013; the three remaining went to the panel. [Para 14][Para 15] Seventy-eight of the 110 workers were the claimants, having transferred their representation to the union. [Para 53][Para 54] The factory union held no registration certificate, only a receipt for its application. [Para 55][Para 40]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise the travel and accommodation allowance from USD 7 to USD 15 per month? [Para 56]
2. Whether the employer must add USD 5 for health cover and USD 14 to basic wage, USD 19 in all, on top of existing pay? [Para 63]
3. Whether the employer must pay wages for the second half-hour of the night-shift break, from 12:00 to 1:00 am? [Para 71]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Other factories — Sabrina, Hong Da and Sin Chang Sin — pay USD 15 in travel and accommodation allowance, and the recent basic-wage increase was applied unevenly so that workers already on USD 80 received only USD 5 while those on USD 70 received USD 10; a one-hour night break is needed because they cannot eat and rest properly, and it should be paid. [Para 60][Para 64][Para 68][Para 76]
**Employer (respondent):** It cannot meet any of the claims: it pays what the Ministry's notifications require, it has already spent heavily on benefits above the statutory minimum, and it already grants a full hour's break while paying half of it. [Para 3][Para 69][Para 70][Para 77]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three claims were refused; the order is non-binding and becomes final after 8 days without objection. [Para 113][Para 116]

On issues 1 and 2 the panel found no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice creating the entitlements claimed. [Para 81][Para 95] It classified both as interest disputes and applied Article 96 of the Labour Law with Article 9 of Prakas 305/01, under which only the most representative union may bring such a claim because an interest award substitutes for the collective agreement and binds the whole workforce. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 88] The 78 individual complainants were not a representative union, so the panel declined to consider the claims at all. [Para 91][Para 92][Para 93]

On issue 3 the panel reached the merits. Article 102 defines wages as the price of work performed or to be performed, and following Award 107/13-Sanvel Su the panel held that wages are the consideration for work actually done. [Para 101][Para 102][Para 104] The employer had already extended the night break from thirty minutes to a full hour, from 12:00 to 1:00 am, while paying for half of it. [Para 73][Para 99] That arrangement is more favourable to the workers than the law requires. [Para 106] For the additional half-hour the workers perform no work for the employer, so under Article 102 they have no right to wages for it and the employer owes none. [Para 109][Para 110][Para 111]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Wages are the consideration for work actually performed: where an employer grants a rest break longer than the statutory minimum and pays for part of it, the workers have no right to wages for the unpaid remainder, because no work is done in that period. [Para 104][Para 110]

**Obiter dicta:** That a group of individual complainants, however numerous, cannot litigate an interest dispute in place of a representative union, was stated as a limit on the panel's jurisdiction rather than as the ground of the order on the break claim. [Para 88][Para 92]
