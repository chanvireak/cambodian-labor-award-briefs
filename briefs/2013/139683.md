# Award No. 78/13 — G-Foremost (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 8 May 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer, Liv Sovanna for the workers, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 139683

## 1. Facts
G-Foremost (Cambodia) is a footwear manufacturer at National Road No.4, Kambol, Phnom Penh, employing 950 workers, of whom 500 belong to a local union of the Cambodian Labour Union Federation (CLUF) that holds a registration certificate but no Most Representative Status. The union raised sixteen demands on working conditions; seven were settled in conciliation, six more at the hearing, and two were withdrawn, leaving Issue 1 and Issue 4 for the panel. Both concern money: whether seniority survives the employer's practice of paying a five per cent severance and imposing a one-week unpaid break before each three-month contract is renewed, and whether the skill bonus counts in the divisor for Sunday and holiday work. [Para 1][Para 13][Para 14][Para 31]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must maintain the workers' seniority after it pays a five per cent severance payment and arranges a one-week break before renewing a fixed-duration contract? [Para 30]
2. Whether the employer must calculate wages for work performed on Sundays and holidays by dividing the sum of the base wage and skill bonus by 26 and multiplying by 200 per cent? [Para 42]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Seniority accrues from continuous service and ends only when the worker stops working for the enterprise; the break-and-severance cycle is a device to strip seniority bonuses, maternity payments and annual-leave entitlement, and the skill bonus is a fixed monthly benefit that forms part of the wage base. [Para 34][Para 36][Para 91]
**Employer (respondent):** The five per cent severance paid at contract expiry discharges the contract, and the one-week gap means the worker has stopped working, so seniority restarts with each new contract; the skill bonus is separate from the base wage, and Sunday/holiday work is already paid at 200 per cent plus a lunch allowance of 3,000 riel per day. [Para 38][Para 45]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer is ordered to maintain the workers' seniority after paying the five per cent severance and arranging the one-week break, and to calculate Sunday and holiday wages by dividing base wage plus skill bonus by 26 and multiplying by 200 per cent. The rights-dispute award is binding under the MoU of 3 October 2012; Ing Sothy dissented on Issue 1. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 105]

**Issue 1 — seniority.** The panel first classified the demand as a rights dispute, adopting the definition from Awards 05/11-M & V1, 13/11-Gold Kamvimex and 14/11-GHG Cambodia that rights disputes concern the interpretation and enforcement of law or a collective agreement, because maintaining seniority turns on the employment contract and the Labour Law. [Para 45][Para 46]

On the merits the panel adopted the definition of seniority in Awards 68/05-Gold Lida, 75/05-Fortune Garment, 17/10-Zongtex, 60/10-Kin Tai and 101/10-Tripos as the length of successive service conferring rights by duration of service, ending only when the worker stops working for the enterprise. It then asked two questions. First, does paying five per cent severance mean the worker has stopped working? No: Article 73 paragraph 6 requires severance proportional to wages and contract length at expiry, with the exact amount fixed by collective agreement or, failing that, at least five per cent of wages paid over the term; following Award 67/10-New Hung Wah, 70/10-Manhattan, 17/11-JRB Action Textiles and 144/11-Manhattan Textile, payment of severance and of pay in lieu of annual leave does not terminate seniority, since neither party intended the relationship to end. Second, does the one-week break do so? No: both parties know renewal will follow, the break is agreed, and following Award 235/12-Manhattan Textile a two-day agreed break is not a cessation; the break is taken one month before the contracts would cumulatively reach two years. [Para 50][Para 53][Para 59][Para 61][Para 64][Para 68]

The panel added that the practice let the employer withhold severance, maternity payment under Article 183, and annual leave under Article 166 on the ground that a year's seniority had not accrued, which shows an intent to avoid legal obligations contrary to Article 13 paragraph 1 of the Labour Law (public-order provisions, with unilateral rules in breach being null) and the good-faith principle restated in Award 64/11-M & V3. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 69][Para 75]

**Issue 4 — Sunday and holiday wages.** Classified as a rights dispute under the Law amending Articles 139 and 144 (20 July 2007) and Clause 4 of Prakas No. 10 (4 February 1999). Applying Article 137 and Awards 114/08-Whitex, 52/12-8 Star Sportswear and 248/12-M & V3, the panel held the legal week runs Monday to Saturday at 48 hours, so voluntary Sunday work is overtime. New Article 139 sets 150 per cent for ordinary overtime and 100 per cent additional for night or weekly-rest work, but does not define the wage base; nor does Clause 5 of Prakas No. 80 (1 March 1999). The panel therefore construed "wages during normal working hours" and, following Award 74/08-Generation International, held the skill bonus part of the minimum wage: it is paid monthly irrespective of output, workers rely on it, and the skill is used during overtime. The employer's base-wage-only divisor was therefore wrong, and the same construction governs holiday pay under Clause 4 of Prakas No. 10. [Para 78][Para 82][Para 83][Para 85][Para 92][Para 94][Para 100]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Paying the five per cent severance required by Article 73 at the expiry of a fixed-duration contract, and an agreed unpaid break before renewal, do not amount to the worker ceasing employment, so continuous service and the seniority it carries survive renewal; and "wages during normal working hours" for overtime, Sunday and holiday pay include a skill bonus paid monthly irrespective of performance. [Para 76][Para 94]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's account of the MoU regime — that awards on rights disputes bind signatories even where the employer's attorney refuses to sign, while interests disputes are non-binding and may be objected to without affecting rights-dispute obligations — was not needed to decide either issue. [Para 16][Para 19][Para 23]
