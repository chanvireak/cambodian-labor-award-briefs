# Award No. 220/13 — Ban Chamat Khon Strak Sen Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 4 November 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Sok Matheung, chair) · Doc ID 139881

## 1. Facts
Ban Chamat Khon Strak Sen Co Ltd, a company in Daung Tung, Kampot, employing about 120 workers, refused to deduct 2,000 riel a month from the wages of members of the Construction and Forestry Workers' Union of Cambodia as union dues. The workplace union had been registered on 25 January 2013 (No. 2760 KB/VK) and claimed about 100 members; 42 of them asked the employer to make the deduction from 12 September 2013, by a federation letter of 13 August 2013 delivered on 24 August 2013. [Para 37][Para 40][Para 30][Para 42]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to deduct 2,000 riel a month from the wages of each union member who has voluntarily authorised the deduction in writing, in order to pay that member's union dues? [Para 48]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the claim rests on Article 129 of the Labour Law and Article 5 of Prakas No. 305; 42 members signed authorisations and the employer was given the list, so the deduction is a formality the employer must perform. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43][Para 45]

**Employer (respondent):** Article 281 of the Labour Law forbids any employer from deducting wages in order to pay union dues, so the company declined; at the hearing the employer nonetheless said it would act in accordance with the law on this question. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 63]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer must deduct 2,000 riel a month from the wage of each union member who voluntarily authorised the deduction in writing, and pay it to the union. The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent written objection to the Minister of Labour. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67]

The panel read Article 281 ("any employer is prohibited from deducting a worker's wage in order to pay union dues") against Article 129 paragraph 2, which lets a worker authorise deduction of his own wage to pay dues to a union he belongs to, provided the authorisation is in writing and may be withdrawn at any time. Article 5(5) of Prakas No. 305, 21 November 2001, requires the request to be in writing at least fifteen days before the month's payday and requires the employer to give effect to it. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 51]

The panel reconciled the two provisions by asking who initiates the deduction. Article 281 bars an employer from deducting on its own motion; it does not bar an employer from executing a worker's own written request, which is what Article 129 paragraph 2 expressly permits. Because a written request had been made and delivered, the employer was under a duty to make the deduction. The evidence of the 42 authorising members was filed and the employer did not contest it. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 43][Para 44][Para 64]

The panel added that the writing requirement serves to establish that the request genuinely reflects the worker's will, so that a worker is protected from deduction against his wishes and the employer is protected from making an unauthorised deduction. Consistent with its earlier awards, it treated the right to request or to stop a deduction as personal to each worker, exercised by signed letter. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Article 281's prohibition on deducting wages to pay union dues is directed at employer-initiated deduction; where an individual worker gives a written authorisation that is revocable at any time, delivered to the employer at least fifteen days before payday as Prakas No. 305 requires, the employer is obliged to make the deduction and remit the dues, and cannot rely on Article 281 to refuse. [Para 49][Para 51][Para 52][Para 64]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the written-authorisation rule exists to protect the employer as much as the worker, by shielding the company from liability for a deduction the worker never consented to. [Para 56]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The claim amount is printed as 2,000 riel throughout, but the question as framed at line 48 reads "20 riel", the OCR having lost a zero, and the order at line 65 reads "2,000 riel in one year month" [Para 48][Para 65]. Two different commercial registration numbers appear for the same company — "Co.2817/06E" of 3 March 2006 at line 36 and certificate No. 712 of 10 March 2006 at line 19 — alongside a second certificate, No. 6337, of 27 October 2011 [Para 19][Para 21][Para 36]. Member numbers do not reconcile: the union claims about 100 members, the petition is brought for 42, and the employer's list of consenting workers is described only generically [Para 31][Para 40][Para 42]. Case dates are corrupted at lines 12 and 13 ("ឆ្នាំ ២០២", "២០១៩"), and paragraph 60 repeats a garbled sentence. The signature block at line 68 is largely destroyed, with the employer- and worker-appointed arbitrators' names lost.
