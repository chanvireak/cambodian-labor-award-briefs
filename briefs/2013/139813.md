# Award No. 124/13 — Quint Major Industrial Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 18 July 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (You Suonty for the employer party, Ann Vireak for the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 139813

## 1. Facts
Quint Major Industrial Co., Ltd. (QMI) has run a factory at Trayeung Village, Ang Snoul District, Kandal Province since 2006 with 3,700 workers. [Para 26] The claimant is the local union of the Cambodian Labour Union Federation, 1,412 members, holding a Certificate of Most Representative Status issued 30 September 2011 and valid to 30 August 2013. [Para 27] Of seven demands referred after unsuccessful conciliation on 20 June 2013, four were settled in a further conciliation at the hearing and three remained for decision. [Para 15][Para 17] Both parties elected non-binding arbitration for the interests disputes. [Para 22][Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must provide 4,000 riel per day in lieu of lunch? [Para 64]
2. Whether the employer must maintain a US$5 health allowance separate from monthly wages? [Para 74]
4. Whether the employer must increase the wages of workers in the finishing section twice a year by at least US$5 each time? [Para 80]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** many factories pay 1,000–2,000 riel towards lunch, their wage is inadequate and the allowance motivates workers [Para 34][Para 67]; the US$5 health allowance must sit on top of the US$19 wage increase rather than be absorbed into the minimum wage, as at Gladpeer [Para 39][Para 41]; finishing-section workers receive only a US$2–3 bonus while cutting and production-line workers receive US$5–10 twice a year [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46].
**Employer (respondent):** it has never paid a lunch allowance and has just raised wages by US$19, so it cannot afford the demand [Para 36][Para 66]; its implementation complies with the Labour Law [Para 42]; bonuses turn on each worker's skill and are decided by section supervisors, so equal bonuses cannot be given to all [Para 49][Para 84].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the finishing-section claim (Issue 4) was rejected by binding award, and Issues 1 and 2 were declined as non-binding interests disputes for want of a properly authorised claimant; no sum was ordered. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 91]

The panel began with representation. [Para 52] Article 268 makes registration with the Ministry in Charge of Labour the condition of a professional organisation's rights, which prior awards read to include the right to represent members before the panel. [Para 54][Para 55] Paragraph 2, Clause 3 of Prakas No. 305 limits a leadership mandate to two years, so the second mandate recognised on 31 May 2011 expired on 31 May 2013, and the three workers present were not among the leaders recognised for it. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 31] No written authorisation from other workers was produced as Clause 19 of Prakas No. 099 requires, so the panel examined the dispute only for the three workers present. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63]

On Issue 1, nothing in the Labour Law, a collective agreement, the internal work rules or any past practice obliged the employer to fund lunch, which made the demand an interests dispute. [Para 68][Para 69] Because an award on an interests dispute replaces a collective bargaining agreement, binds all workers and removes their right to strike for a year, only a union with most representative status, or a collective union with more than half the workforce as members, may bring one. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 73] Three individual workers were the only claimants, so the panel declined to consider the demand. [Para 73][Para 74]

On Issue 2, Notification No. 103 of 9 April 2013 had already folded the US$5 health allowance into a minimum wage of US$75 during probation and US$80 afterwards, leaving no separate obligation, and the parties agreed the US$19 increase had been paid. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 77] A further US$5 on top of that increase rested on no legal provision, agreement, collective agreement, internal rule or practice and was a second interests dispute, declined on the same ground. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 80]

On Issue 4, the panel treated the claim as a rights dispute because it concerned a bonus actually paid. [Para 86] Article 2 and the earlier awards in 62/06 Quicksew, 33/07 Gold Fame and 24/05 Wearwel give the employer the right to manage the enterprise and to arrange bonus schedules outside those fixed by the Labour Law. [Para 82][Para 83] Skills and the value of each worker's performance differ, so assessment by section supervisors was a reasonable exercise of that right and the demand for a flat US$5 twice yearly was rejected. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 89]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that would stand in place of a collective bargaining agreement — a new allowance or pay increase with no basis in the Labour Law, an agreement, internal rules or past practice — is an interests dispute that only a union holding most representative status, or a collective union representing more than half the enterprise's workers, may bring before the panel; individual workers cannot confer that jurisdiction, however many they speak for, and the panel will decline to consider the demand. [Para 69][Para 71][Para 73] Within that boundary, an employer may set performance bonuses by unilateral decision under its management right in Article 2, and the panel will not convert a discretionary bonus into a fixed wage entitlement where the assessment is reasonable and tied to individual skill and performance. [Para 82][Para 88]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's account of the 3 October 2012 MoU — binding arbitration for rights disputes, non-binding and opposable arbitration for interests disputes — and its remark that an interests award strips all workers in the enterprise, members or not, of the right to strike over the covered issues for one year. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 72]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- This is an unofficial English translation and the evidence section is expressly omitted, so the panel's evidentiary basis for Issues 1 and 2 cannot be checked. [Para 24][Para 25]
- OCR word-joining throughout: "themployer" for "the employer", "athearing" for "at the hearing", "USS5" for "US$5", "righto"/"tothe" for "right to"/"to the". Meaning was recoverable but several sentences required reconstruction.
- The union president's name is printed "Bev Man Norin" at [Para 27] and [Para 52] but "Beer Man Norin" at [Para 31] and [Para 48]; the second-mandate vice-president appears as "Seng Sambath" at [Para 31] and [Para 57] but "Ul Sam Arth" is given as the newly elected vice-president at [Para 31]; "Sok Vichara" becomes "Sok Vicharare" at [Para 32]. Names are redacted in this brief.
- Contradiction left unresolved: the panel records the union's most representative certificate as valid to 30 August 2013 [Para 27], yet declines the interests issues because only three workers were claimants [Para 73], without saying why the union's own status could not cure that.
- The same Prakas No. 305 is dated 22 November 2001 at [Para 56] and 22 November 2011 at [Para 70]; the Ministry is printed "Ministry of Labour and Vocational Traing" throughout.
- At [Para 36] the sentence "The workers claim that there is no legal basis for the demand" is attributed to the workers but plainly belongs to the employer, which is the party denying the demand; it was read as an employer contention.
- The non-conciliation report lists seven issues and the panel's own numbering skips Issue 3 and Issue 4(B), which were settled at the hearing; the heading at [Para 42] reads "Issue 4" while the reasons at [Para 80] decide only 4(A). The award date was deferred by agreement from 11 July 2013 to 18 July 2013 [Para 24].
