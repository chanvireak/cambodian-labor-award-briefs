# Award No. 243/13 — Bodykids Fashion Wear Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 13 December 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Virak, worker-appointed, dissenting; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · Doc ID 139896

## 1. Facts
Bodykids Fashion Wear Co., Ltd is a garment factory at Tuol Pongro village, Chom Chao commune, Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh, registered commercially under No. Inv.0661 E/2010 of 25 March 2010, with patent No. DLT.038445.2013 of 27 February 2013 and about 750 workers. [Para 1][Para 70][Para 71]
On 30 October 2013 the Khmer Workers' Federation telephoned a complaint that the workers had struck for improved conditions; conciliation on 31 October 2013 left both of the two demands unsettled, and report No. 1389 K.B/AK/VK of 4 November 2013 reached the Council on 6 November. [Para 11][Para 12][Para 13][Para 63]
The panel was constituted on 7 November 2013 and heard the case on 20 November 2013; the award is expressly non-binding and the parties moved its date from 2 December to 13 December 2013. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 18][Para 19]
Both parties agreed the workers struck on 28 October 2013 from 7am to 4pm and on 30 October 2013 from 7am to noon. [Para 74][Para 154]
Four workers were the original complainants and attended: Chhoeun Thach, Oeun Maly, Sjang San and Soeun San. [Para 72]
The union held its leadership election on 25 October 2013; Sjang San was elected president, Oeun Nyat vice-president and Oeun Maly secretary. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 172]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the basic wage, the regular attendance bonus, and the travel and accommodation allowance for the hours of the strike? [Para 2][Para 73][Para 124]
2. Whether the employer must take back Sjang San, the elected union president, into employment? [Para 4][Para 81][Para 159]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** pay must continue during the strike; the employer hired new workers during the strike to replace the strikers; Sjang San was dismissed because of his union role and without any fault, and the notice of the leadership composition was given to the employer on 26 October 2013 about 9 or 10am and signed for by a security guard, though the letter was later lost during the strike. [Para 2][Para 75][Para 85][Para 95][Para 96]
**Employer (respondent):** it followed the Labour Law and owed nothing for strike hours; it hired no new workers during the strike, having recruited before it; it never received the leadership notice, since security guards do not accept letters from outside, which go directly to administration; and Sjang San was dismissed for serious misconduct after two written warnings and a third offence. [Para 3][Para 76][Para 77][Para 78][Para 97][Para 98][Para 99][Para 86][Para 89]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both demands were rejected, with no payment for the strike hours and no reinstatement. [Para 245][Para 246][Para 247]
On point 1 the panel held that Article 332(1) of the Labour Law suspends the contract during a strike and Article 72(1) removes the wage obligation for the suspended period, so strikers receive no wage whatever the strike's legality. [Para 124][Para 125][Para 126][Para 128][Para 129]
It also held the strike unlawful: Article 324 requires seven days' prior notice, and Article 320(4) requires the exhaustion of peaceful settlement, yet the workers struck without notice and before the Council process, in reliance only on the employer's refusal of their request. [Para 145][Para 146][Para 148]
For that reason the attendance bonus was also refused, since a striker does not come to work regularly within Notification No. 230 K.B/S.Ch.N of 25 July 2012, point 2. [Para 141][Para 142][Para 144][Para 149][Para 150]
The travel and accommodation allowance of $7 a month under point 1 of the same notification is an incentive owed only to workers who come to work, so nothing was owed for the strike period either. [Para 151][Para 156][Para 157]
The panel also rejected the replacement-worker complaint: the workers bore the burden of proving that new hires replaced strikers during the strike, were ordered to file evidence, filed none by the deadline, and so failed to discharge it; the allegation was not considered. [Para 135][Para 136][Para 137][Para 138][Para 139]
On point 2 the panel set out the three conditions for protection under Article 293 of the Labour Law and Articles 3 and 4 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001: the worker must be a protected person, the dismissal must fall within the protected period, and the union must have notified the employer of the protected names by any workable means, protection running from election day. [Para 159][Para 160][Para 161][Para 169][Para 168]
Sjang San satisfied the first two: he was elected president on 25 October 2013 and dismissed on 26 October 2013. [Para 172][Para 173][Para 174][Para 175][Para 178]
He failed the third. The panel found the workers gave no proof that the 26 October notice reached the employer, accepted that the letter had been lost, noted the employer's evidence that guards do not receive outside mail, and observed that the employer first learned of the composition from the Ministry at conciliation on 31 October 2013. [Para 179][Para 180][Para 181][Para 185][Para 189][Para 190][Para 191][Para 192]
Condition three therefore failed, and he had no Article 293 protection. [Para 193][Para 194][Para 196]
On anti-union discrimination the panel applied Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law and held the workers adduced no specific argument or evidence of discrimination, so the dismissal was not shown to be discriminatory. [Para 197][Para 198][Para 206][Para 207][Para 209]
On misconduct the panel found the employer had not followed its own internal rules, registered on 6 April 2011, whose Article 10 requires education, then a written warning, then dismissal for a repeated light offence; it had instead treated three accumulated offences as serious misconduct, which the panel held was not correct. [Para 210][Para 212][Para 213][Para 215][Para 222][Para 223]
Even so, it held that under Article 73(3) an employer may terminate a fixed-term contract at will on paying damages at least equal to the wages due to the contract's end, and found the employer had paid the 5 per cent severance, the damages to 18 December 2013, the unused annual leave and the final wage. [Para 224][Para 225][Para 227][Para 229][Para 233][Para 237][Para 239][Para 241][Para 243]
Reinstatement was therefore refused. [Para 244][Para 246]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
No wage, attendance bonus or travel allowance is owed for hours of strike, whether the strike is lawful or not, because the contract is suspended and the allowance is an incentive for attendance. [Para 126][Para 129][Para 150][Para 157]
A union leader's protection under Article 293 depends on the union having actually notified the employer of the protected names before the dismissal; a notice lost in a strike and unproven at the hearing leaves the leader unprotected. [Para 192][Para 193][Para 196]
Failing to follow internal disciplinary rules invalidates a dismissal for serious misconduct, but the employer may still terminate at will under Article 73(3) by paying damages to the contract's end, which defeats a reinstatement claim. [Para 223][Para 224][Para 225]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted the workers' replacement-labour allegation failed only for want of evidence, leaving the practice itself undecided. [Para 138][Para 139]

**Dissent (An Virak, worker-appointed):** he agreed on conditions one and two but would have found condition three satisfied: the employer's own objection of 4 November 2013 relied on the union's letter No. 264/013 of 25 October 2013, which proves it knew the leadership composition on 26 October, so Sjang San was protected and should be reinstated. [Para 262][Para 263][Para 264][Para 265][Para 268][Para 269]
