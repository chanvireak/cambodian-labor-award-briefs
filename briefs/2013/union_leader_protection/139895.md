# Award No. 238/13 — Scandinavian Textile (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 29 November 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Somborna, employer-appointed, dissenting; An Non, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · Doc ID 139895

## 1. Facts
Scandinavian Textile (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a garment-cutting enterprise holding patent (self-declaration regime) No. DLT.040186.2013 of 24 June 2013, at Tuol Sangke village, Tuol Sangke commune, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, employing about 326 workers. [Para 1][Para 61][Para 62]
On 26 October 2013 the Labour Dispute Department received a telephone complaint that the workers had struck to press for improved conditions; conciliation at the factory covered 15 points, settled 13 and left 2, and report No. 1354 K.B/AK/VK of 31 October 2013 sent the two open points to the Council the same day. [Para 11][Para 12][Para 13][Para 56]
The panel was constituted on 1 November 2013, sat on 8 November 2013, and both parties chose a binding award and agreed to move the award date from 26 November to 29 November 2013. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 24][Para 25][Para 60]
The company union of the Khmer Workers' Improvement Federation held its leadership election on 31 July 2013 and gave notice No. 099/13/SLSK of 6 August 2013 to the employer, received and signed for that day by the security guard Phon Seang Ly; the federation held transfers of rights from 118 workers by letter of 7 October 2013, of whom five attended the hearing. [Para 120][Para 121][Para 122][Para 124][Para 66][Para 67]
The employer objected to the union's registration on the ground that the four elected leaders had not held their occupation for at least one year as Article 269 of the Labour Law requires, and the Department notified the objection to Preap Srey on 30 August 2013. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 66]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must cut the regular attendance bonus in proportion to the days a worker is absent with the employer's lawful permission? [Para 2][Para 126][Para 127]
2. Whether the employer must take back Preap Srey and An Ratha, the union leaders it excluded, into employment? [Para 4][Para 168]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer's method of cutting the attendance bonus makes workers lose too much of it, whereas a proportional cut would not; the permitted absences relied on are moving house, repairing a broken vehicle, visiting one's home province, joining alms and other ceremonies, attending a relative's or friend's wedding and necessary harvest collection, and do not include annual, sick or special leave; the two leaders were excluded on 26 October 2013 because of their union roles. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 73][Para 87]
**Employer (respondent):** it cannot grant the demand because a proportional cut would encourage more absence and disrupt the production chain, and the lawful permission already disrupts it, so a further proportional cut would cost it more; it could not take the two back because their fixed-term contracts had already been ended, and says that was a non-renewal, not a dismissal, made with compensation under Article 73(3). [Para 5][Para 74][Para 75][Para 96][Para 112]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** the binding award upheld both points — the employer must cut the attendance bonus only in proportion to days of permitted absence, and must take back both workers. [Para 233][Para 234][Para 235]
On point 1 the panel started from Notification No. 230 K.B/S.Ch.N of 25 July 2012, point 2, which requires a minimum $10 monthly attendance bonus for workers who come to work regularly for the working days of each month without absence, and held that this is a general legal rule binding on all textile and footwear employers, not a bonus the employer may dispose of at will. [Para 127][Para 130][Para 131][Para 133]
It found the phrase "without absence" ambiguous: the Labour Advisory Committee has never interpreted whether it covers only unlawful absence or also absence lawfully permitted, and the panel declined to presume either meaning. [Para 136][Para 137][Para 141]
It held that absences the law itself provides for — annual leave, special leave, sick leave, maternity leave, public holidays and weekly rest — do not require any deduction, so the bonus is paid at 100 per cent, i.e. $10. [Para 138][Para 139]
For other absence with the employer's permission, Articles 71(3) and 71(6) of the Labour Law make the contract suspensory, and Article 72(1) removes the employer's duty to pay wages for the suspended period; the panel reasoned that permitted leave is a suspension agreed between the parties, so no wage and no attendance bonus is owed for those days. [Para 160][Para 161][Para 162][Para 163][Para 164][Para 165]
It followed that the employer may not cut the whole bonus but only the amount proportional to the days of permitted absence, and ordered accordingly. [Para 166][Para 167][Para 129]
On point 2 the panel noted the agreed term: probation 4 June to 4 August 2013, then a three-month fixed-term contract from 4 August to 4 November 2013, with the employer barring both from the factory from 26 October 2013. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 78][Para 171]
Because the employer ended the contract before its term, and paid compensation under Article 73(3), the panel held this was a dismissal, not a non-renewal. [Para 172][Para 173]
It then applied Article 293(1), under which a union leader or candidate may be dismissed only with the labour inspector's agreement, extended to unions by Articles 3 and 4 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001; Article 3(3) protects election candidates for 45 days before the poll and 45 days after, and Article 4 protects founders and voluntary members during registration for up to 30 days after registration. [Para 174][Para 175][Para 176][Para 177][Para 180][Para 181]
Following 40/10-Meng Yan, 103/12-Sophea and 191/12-Alim, the panel held the protection runs from the election day until the union is registered, because otherwise a candidate could be dismissed in the gap. [Para 186][Para 188][Para 195]
Preap Srey, elected president, satisfied condition one; An Ratha, though not a candidate, was elected adviser and was a voluntary member during registration, which brings him within Article 4; both were dismissed while protected; and the union had notified the employer of the four names on 6 August 2013, satisfying condition three. [Para 199][Para 200][Para 201][Para 203][Para 205][Para 216][Para 219][Para 220]
The employer never said it sought the labour inspector's permission, and produced no evidence of any request, so the dismissal breached Article 293 and reinstatement was ordered, following 71/09, 09/10-Jung Hao and 91/11-Blik Su. [Para 223][Para 224][Para 226][Para 227][Para 230][Para 231][Para 232]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The $10 attendance bonus is a statutory minimum, not a discretionary gift, so an employer may deduct it only in proportion to days of absence the employer lawfully permitted, and must pay it in full despite absences the law itself allows. [Para 133][Para 139][Para 166][Para 167]
Ending a fixed-term contract before its term is a dismissal, not a non-renewal, however the employer labels it or whatever compensation it pays under Article 73(3). [Para 172][Para 173]
A union leader or candidate dismissed without the labour inspector's prior agreement is dismissed unlawfully and must be reinstated, and protection begins on election day, not on registration. [Para 188][Para 195][Para 230][Para 232]

**Obiter dicta:** none.

**Dissent (Mar Somborna, employer-appointed):** on point 2 he would have rejected the claim: only 8 days of the contract remained, the labour inspector has one month to decide, so the contract would expire before any refusal, and the employer should be allowed to terminate on paying the 8 remaining days. [Para 244][Para 245][Para 246][Para 248][Para 249]
