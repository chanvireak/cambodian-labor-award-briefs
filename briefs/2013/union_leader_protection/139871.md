# Award No. 198/13 — Soo Apparel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 16 October 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Aing Eng Tong, chair) · Doc ID 139871

## 1. Facts
Soo Apparel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a garment factory in Phnom Penh, suspended and then sought to dismiss Mr S, the elected president of the factory's Labour Union for Cambodia, after he threw his mobile phone in front of the general affairs officer on 9 August 2013. The union had been elected on 8 June 2013 but had not yet been registered by the Ministry; the employer had objected to registration on the ground that Mr S lacked one year in the trade. The union also claimed strike wages for 7–8 August 2013 and 100% wages plus 50% back pay for periods when the employer had no work. Conciliation failed on 4 of 7 claims and 3 issues remained. [Para 1][Para 112][Para 125][Para 135][Para 149]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay base wages to workers who joined the strike on 7 and 8 August 2013? [Para 149]
2. Whether the employer must pay 100% wages when it has no work for the workers to do, and repay the 50% of base wages it withheld from 8 workers? [Para 184][Para 261]
4. Whether the employer must take back Mr S and restore his wages and benefits, with back pay from the date of suspension? [Para 263]

(The panel numbered the issues 1, 2 and 4; issue 3 was settled at the hearing and is not decided.) [Para 21][Para 22]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The union leaders demanded base wages for the 7–8 August 2013 strike, 100% of base wages when the company had no work, and reinstatement of Mr S as union president with unchanged wages and benefits. [Para 2][Para 8][Para 111]
**Employer (respondent):** The company said it could not pay for the strike because a strike earns no money; it paid 50% of base wages for 1–3 days of no work and would seek suspension from the labour inspectorate for longer stoppages; and it had suspended Mr S for serious misconduct under Article 83(B)(4) and was awaiting the labour inspector's decision. [Para 3][Para 5][Para 9][Para 116]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 1 dismissed; issue 2 granted in part, ordering 100% wages when no work is provided and repayment of the 50% withheld from 8 named workers; issue 4 granted, ordering reinstatement of Mr S with unchanged wages and benefits and back pay from the suspension date to the date of return. [Para 260][Para 261][Para 262][Para 263]

On issue 1, the panel applied Articles 332(1) and 72(1) of the Labour Law: a labour contract is suspended during a strike and no wages are owed for the strike period, whether the strike is lawful or not. It followed its earlier awards and dismissed the claim. [Para 150][Para 153][Para 156]

On issue 2, the panel found the employer's 50% arrangement — evidenced by two staff-representative letters of 2 and 30 September 2013 — was not consistent with Article 13 of the Labour Law and could not be applied further, so the full 100% wage was owed whenever the employer had no work and had not lawfully suspended the contracts. [Para 36][Para 182][Para 183]

On issue 4, the panel first dealt with representation. The union lacked registration and therefore legal capacity to represent members, but 143 workers had signed a letter of authorisation of 9 August 2013, of which 141 were valid; the two names sharing a single thumbprint were rejected. The panel then applied Prakas 305: an elected union leader is protected from the date of election until registration, and the three conditions for special protection were met — Mr S was a protected worker, the suspension fell within the protection period, and the employer had been notified of the election. Because the labour inspector did not respond to the dismissal request within one month, the request is deemed refused and the termination void under Article 295. [Para 136][Para 143][Para 145][Para 221][Para 226][Para 234][Para 245][Para 252][Para 257]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A candidate elected to union leadership acquires protection against dismissal from the day of the election, not from the day the union is registered; an employer that suspends and seeks to dismiss such a leader without the labour inspector's approval within one month acts on a void termination and must reinstate with back pay. [Para 226][Para 255][Para 257]

**Obiter dicta:** An unregistered union has no legal capacity to represent its members, though individual workers may authorise the federation to act for them; and a document bearing one thumbprint between two names cannot prove the consent of either. [Para 136][Para 143]
