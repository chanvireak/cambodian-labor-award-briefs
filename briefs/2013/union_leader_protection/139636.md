# Award No. 29/13 — Sang Woo (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 11 March 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Chiv Phirom (employer-appointed), An Nan (worker-appointed), Kong Pollak (president) · Doc ID 139636

## 1. Facts
Sang Woo (Cambodia) is a garment factory in Trapeang Veng, Samraong Tong, Kampong Speu, employing about 4,563 workers. [Para 42][Para 43] Mr S, an electrician engaged orally in February 2010, was moved on 13 October 2012 from maintenance inside buildings 1 and the cutting room to electrical work outside the buildings, and lost a technical allowance of USD 30 a month from his monthly package. [Para 49][Para 52][Para 78] He was elected president of the Cambodia Union at the factory; the employer says it learned of the union only in November 2012 through a labour-department complaint. [Para 72][Para 80] Conciliation on his two demands failed and the file came to the Council; the award was to issue on 20 February 2013 but both parties agreed to defer it to 11 March 2013. [Para 11][Para 26]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the transfer of Mr S and the deduction of his technical allowance were connected to union discrimination? [Para 104]
2. Whether transferring Mr S from an electrician working inside the buildings to an electrician working outside them was lawful? [Para 121]
3. Whether the employer may deduct the technical allowance in order to penalise Mr S? [Para 156]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the transfer and the USD 30 deduction were retaliation for union activity — the administration head met him four times and asked him to resign from the union, warning that otherwise the company would find every means to keep the Cambodia Union out of the factory, and promising that all deducted benefits would return if he left the union. [Para 58][Para 66][Para 70] He also asked for overtime as before and reimbursement of the allowance deducted over the previous three months. [Para 53]
**Employer (respondent):** there was no discrimination and it knew nothing of the union until the labour department wrote; the move was a response to frequent absence — two to seven unlawful days or two to six lawful days a month — which blocked production, and the USD 30 cut was a warning, to be restored after six months if his conduct improved. [Para 79][Para 89][Para 90][Para 93][Para 95]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the request to be returned to his original post was dismissed, but the employer was ordered to restore the technical or skill allowance of USD 30 a month and to pay back the amounts deducted in the past period; no aggregate sum is printed. [Para 165][Para 166]

On discrimination the panel applied Arts 412 and 279 of the Labour Law, which forbid an employer to use union membership or activity as a ground for decisions on assignment, grading, wages, benefits or discipline, and recalled that the party alleging discrimination carries the burden of proof (awards 148/07, 34/11, 155/11). [Para 104][Para 105][Para 106][Para 107] It found the transfer dated 13 October 2012 while the union leadership election was held on 4 November 2012, so the move preceded the election; Mr S's account of the four meetings was uncorroborated and denied, and the concession that he was often absent was common ground. [Para 117][Para 115][Para 120] Discrimination was therefore not established. [Para 119]

Overtime was treated as no longer in dispute because the employer undertook to offer it when work existed, including in February. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57]

On the transfer itself the panel read Art 2 of the Labour Law as conferring management rights that must be exercised lawfully and reasonably, following awards 06/06, 18/06, 108/06 and 17/03-18/03, which list the limits: no cut in wage, no move to a distant site, no day-to-night shift change, and no change too far outside the worker's skill. [Para 124][Para 129] Mr S kept the same shift, the same factory, the same base wage and the same electrical trade; the outside duties (steam-press boilers, ironing motors, ventilation) remained electrical work, and his claim of a heavier workload was unsupported. [Para 131][Para 143][Para 152][Para 154] The transfer was upheld.

On the penalty the panel cited Art 28, which forbids an employer to impose a monetary fine on a worker for misconduct and defines such a fine as any measure reducing wages normally earned, and Art 103, which lists wages to include bonuses, so a skill or technical allowance is a wage. [Para 156][Para 157][Para 161] Deducting USD 30 to punish absenteeism was therefore contrary to Art 28, and discipline should instead follow the internal rules. [Para 162][Para 163]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may exercise its management right to move a worker to other duties in the same factory, shift and trade without reducing base pay, but it may not enforce discipline by cutting a skill or technical allowance, because that allowance is part of wages and Art 28 forbids monetary penalties for misconduct. [Para 129][Para 161][Para 162]

**Obiter dicta:** that the union's participation in the hearing could not make an unregistered factory union a signatory to the 3 October 2012 memorandum of understanding, and that overtime ceases to be a dispute once the employer undertakes to offer it when available. [Para 20][Para 56]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The deduction is printed as USD 30 a month in the findings and as "300 dollars a month" in the reasoning at para 160. [Para 160] The union election date is given three ways: 5 October 2012 in the worker's account, 4 November 2012 in the panel's findings, and again 4 November at para 117. [Para 59][Para 72][Para 117] The award date is 11 March 2013 on the face and in the reasons, with the original issuing date of 20 February 2013 recorded as deferred by agreement. [Para 26] The parties' memorandum is dated 3 October 2012 at paras 16 and 18 but "20 October 2012" appears garbled in the first recital. [Para 16] The company name is printed both as Sang Woo (Cambodia) and SANGWOO (CAMBODIA) CO., LTD; OCR has reduced several individual names and titles to fragments, and the worker is named inconsistently across the text (variants including "Chhun Sovannarun"). [Para 30][Para 112] The award states both that the parties chose a non-binding award and that it becomes final after eight days without objection. [Para 22][Para 167]
