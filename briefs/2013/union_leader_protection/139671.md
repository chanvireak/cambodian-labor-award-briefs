# Award No. 53/13 — Phnom Penh International Airport (Cambodia Airports Management Services) (Arbitration Council, 10 April 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ly Tayseng (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (president) · Doc ID 139671

## 1. Facts
The Cambodia Labour Union confederation filed on behalf of employees of Cambodia Airports Management Services (CAMS), the concessionaire that manages Phnom Penh International Airport with some 750 workers, after conciliation failed on all five demands on 26 February 2013. [Para 73][Para 15] The workers later merged demands 1 and 5, leaving four disputed points for the panel: a wage increase and skills-based rank allowances, revision of the internal regulations, reinstatement of a dismissed union secretary, and holiday pay where a public holiday collides with the weekly rest days. [Para 20][Para 21] A collective agreement registered as No. 299 Kb/Ak/Vk on 12 July 2011, valid three years from 1 January 2011, governed the wage terms in issue. [Para 76]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise base wages by 10 per cent a year plus multi-skills allowance for all staff, and grant a rank allowance of 30 US dollars per skill, with no worker performing more than three skills? [Para 154]
2. Whether the panel has jurisdiction to order the employer to revise the company's daily internal regulations? [Para 196]
3. Whether the employer lawfully dismissed Mr N, the union secretary, and must therefore reinstate him in his former post and pay back wages from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 201]
4. Whether the employer must pay wages for, or grant, an extra day of rest when a paid public holiday falls on both of a worker's two consecutive weekly rest days? [Para 226]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The 2011 collective agreement guarantees rank progression A to L tied to skill; the employer's 2012 multi-skilling scheme pushed ranks to A–T without consent, froze skill pay and blocked wage progression, so $30 per skill and a 10 per cent annual rise are owed. [Para 78][Para 88] The internal regulations in daily use are unregistered photocopies with no ministry seal, and Mr N was dismissed over a union role, so the inspector's approval is contestable. [Para 103][Para 113]
**Employer (respondent):** Multi-skilling was a voluntary skills upgrade with no effect on wages or ranks, already addressed in Award 140/12; the regulations have been used officially since 1995 and were found valid by labour inspection. [Para 83][Para 106][Para 107] The dismissal followed the Article 293 procedure, and Sub-decree 156 allows only one substitute day. [Para 211][Para 235]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — every claim was refused, with no sum ordered and no reinstatement; the award is stated not to be binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent objection to the Minister. [Para 247][Para 252][Para 254]

On the wage demand, the panel read article 6 of the collective agreement as fixing a flat addition of 10 dollars to the 2012 base wage for 2013, scaled by rank A–L, rather than a 10 per cent increase; article 21 of the agreement makes a law or prakas prevail only where it is more favourable, and the panel found no such instrument granting a 10 per cent annual rise. [Para 164][Para 165] Because the agreement sets the entitlement and neither party may claim beyond it, the demand failed. [Para 167][Para 168] No provision in the agreement obliges the employer to pay 30 dollars per skill, so that claim was refused on the same footing. [Para 170][Para 172]

On the cap of three skills, the panel relied on article 2 of the Labour Law and article 5 of the agreement, which reserve to the employer the right to organise production and deploy its workforce lawfully and reasonably. [Para 173][Para 180] It found a cargo worker, Mr H, still received 16 dollars a month in skill pay after multi-skilling, and that the workers had produced no evidence that multi-skilling cost them wages, skill pay or rank. [Para 190][Para 191][Para 192]

On the internal regulations, the panel held that a demand to rewrite them is not a collective labour dispute over working conditions, use of professional-organisation rights or recognition of such organisations within article 302 of the Labour Law, and that it therefore had no jurisdiction; the claim was not considered. [Para 197][Para 199][Para 200]

On the dismissal, article 293 requires the labour inspector's agreement before a union leader may be dismissed. [Para 201] The employer applied on 25 April 2012 and the inspector agreed on 23 May 2012; the union's appeal to the Minister drew no reply within two months, which under article 293 means the appeal is deemed rejected and the inspector's decision stands. [Para 206][Para 207][Para 211] Following Award 180/11, the panel held that approval ends the union immunity and that the employer was entitled to dismiss. [Para 214][Para 221][Para 225]

On holiday collisions, article 162 of the Labour Law and article 2 of Sub-decree 156 give one substitute day where a paid holiday falls on weekly rest, and only one day even where it falls on both consecutive rest days. [Para 234][Para 235][Para 237] The employer's practice of granting a single day was therefore lawful and the claim for two days or payment was refused. [Para 239][Para 246]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that an employer rewrite its internal regulations is not a collective labour dispute over working conditions within article 302 of the Labour Law, so an arbitration panel has no jurisdiction to decide it and will not examine the registration defects alleged against the regulations. [Para 199][Para 200]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that its earlier Award 140/12 did not decide rank entitlements but did discuss multi-skilling, and observed that once the labour inspector approves a dismissal, a union leader becomes an ordinary worker for the purpose of the panel's review of the merits. [Para 97][Para 214]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The text is heavily degraded OCR: paragraph 19 prints the number of unresolved points as "05" and then "405", and the reasoning on issues 1 and 5 appears twice, once in the summary of positions and again under "ហេតុផល" with different paragraph numbering. [Para 19][Para 77][Para 154] The non-conciliation report is dated 07 March 2013 in the procedural section and 27 February 2013 in paragraph 16. [Para 10][Para 16] The award date is given as 1 April 2013 but both parties agreed to postpone it to 10 April 2013, the date on the face of the award. [Para 22] The cargo worker whose skill pay is discussed appears as "Heng" in one passage and "Heng Bun" in another, and the name is treated here as a bare initial. [Para 182][Para 190] Quoted provisions of the collective agreement (articles 6 and 21) survive only as fragmented table cells, so the 10-dollar figure is read from the surrounding prose. [Para 98][Para 99]
