# Award No. 33/13 — Siem Reap International Airport (Cambodia Airport Management Services) (Arbitration Council, 30 April 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kov Thach, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 139649

## 1. Facts
Cambodia Airport Management Services (CAMS) operates Siem Reap International Airport under a public-service mandate, with 453 staff, 94% of them on shifts. The Cambodia Tourism Industry Workers' Union at the airport — the C.CAWDU-affiliated plant union — was the claimant, and CAMS had signed a three-year collective agreement with it and with the Independent Airport Workers' Union from 1 January 2011, registered on 12 July 2011. An 11-point demand was filed on 4 January 2013; after conciliation failed on 21 January 2013, the union withdrew point 11 and merged points 3 and 8, leaving nine issues decided over three hearings. [Para 159][Para 161][Para 162][Para 23][Para 29][Para 30]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must sign the minutes of every meeting immediately after it ends, without leaving it to the next day? [Para 163][Para 321]
2. Whether the employer must withdraw its current internal regulations and negotiate new ones with the staff delegates? [Para 179][Para 339]
3. Whether the seven workers supplied by UGS (United Ground Services) must receive the benefits of the collective agreement on the same terms as CAMS staff? [Para 189][Para 346]
6. Whether the employer must negotiate with the union, and study the effect on benefits, before applying its multi-skill programme? [Para 224][Para 359]
7. Whether the employer must preserve the salary bands A to L set by the collective agreement? [Para 231][Para 373]
9. Whether the employer must abolish the 04:00–11:00 shift and create a 22:00–06:00 night shift? [Para 237][Para 384]
12. Whether the employer must stop including the meal allowance in taxable pay? [Para 248][Para 402]
13. Whether the employer must pay three Marshaller Headset staff the same wage as Mr T (USD 361), rather than USD 286? [Para 251][Para 416]
21. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr D and pay him back wages from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 264][Para 437]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the labour ministry requires minutes when a union files a complaint and members want the outcome at once; the internal regulations were drafted by the employer alone without staff delegates; UGS workers do the same work under the same supervision but get less, which is a device to deny them the collective agreement and union membership; the multi-skill programme blocks promotion from band A to L; the 04:00 shift caused a road accident; meal money is not income and is not taxable; the three Marshallers do Mr T's job; Mr D only handed a phone to security and was dismissed outside Article 26. [Para 164][Para 180][Para 192][Para 224][Para 238][Para 249][Para 252][Para 265]
**Employer (respondent):** immediate signing is impractical because CAMS is foreign-owned, minutes must be translated and checked against ISO standards, and signatures were immediate only once, in 2008; the internal regulations are registered with the ministry; UGS staff are managed, paid and disciplined by UGS; CAMS has a managerial right to run the multi-skill programme and no worker lost promotion; shifts follow airline schedules; it applies the tax law; the three Marshallers are T1 while Mr T is T3 with three years' required experience; Mr D was dismissed for attempting to steal a passenger's phone. [Para 168][Para 182][Para 203][Para 210][Para 225][Para 232][Para 245][Para 250][Para 260][Para 266]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — every claim was refused, several for want of jurisdiction; the award is non-binding and becomes final after 8 days absent written objection to the Minister. [Para 475][Para 484]
On point 1 the panel applied the "past practice" doctrine under Article 65: a practice must be long-standing, consistent and accepted by both sides, and immediate signing had occurred only three times, in 2008, so it never became binding; the union's assertion that it had protested was unsupported by evidence. [Para 328][Para 331][Para 335][Para 337]
Point 2 was held outside jurisdiction: a demand to withdraw the internal regulations is not a collective labour dispute within Article 302 of the Labour Law or Prakas 099, so the panel could not decide it. [Para 342][Para 343][Para 344]
Point 3 turned on Article 97: a collective agreement binds only workers under the management and direction of that employer. The seven were UGS employees, paid, supervised and disciplined by UGS, so they were outside the CAMS agreement, and the agreement itself did not extend to non-CAMS staff. [Para 352][Para 354][Para 355]
Points 6, 7, 9 and 13 were decided on the employer's managerial right under Article 2, exercisable where lawful and reasonable, and on the burden of proof borne by the party alleging loss: no evidence showed any worker was denied a band increase, that the multi-skill programme froze bands, or that the three Marshallers met the three conditions of Article 106 (equal conditions, equal professional skill, equal output) alongside a T3 employee with three years' service. [Para 364][Para 367][Para 371][Para 381][Para 422][Para 428][Para 433]
On point 9 the panel found the 04:00–11:00 shift followed airline schedules and was not daily, and that the road accident of 17 November 2012 came from the driver falling asleep, not from the shift itself; it nonetheless observed that the employer should raise its attention to the safety of workers on that shift. [Para 392][Para 394][Para 399][Para 400]
Point 12 failed on jurisdiction: tax withheld from wages is an obligation between worker and State, not a condition of employment, and none of the three conditions of a collective dispute in Article 302 was met. [Para 405][Para 407][Para 409][Para 413]
On point 21 the panel watched the CCTV footage, found Mr D had pushed the bag aside, felt it twice and delayed reporting, rejected his explanation that he was reacting to a burst zip, and held the employer had proved serious misconduct under Article 83(3) and Article 4 of the internal rules; the dismissal came within seven days of the employer learning of the misconduct, as Article 26 requires. [Para 447][Para 457][Para 461][Para 470][Para 473]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A practice becomes binding only when it has been repeated long enough to be consistent and is accepted by both parties; three instances over five years do not create a past practice that either side can enforce. A collective agreement's benefits reach only workers under the management and direction of the employer that signed it, so staff supplied by a contractor cannot claim them; and a wage-equality claim under Article 106 fails unless equal working conditions, equal professional skill and equal output are all shown. [Para 331][Para 335][Para 354][Para 422]

**Obiter dicta:** that the panel had no jurisdiction over a demand to withdraw the internal regulations or over the tax treatment of a meal allowance, and its advice that the employer should give greater attention to the safety of workers taking the 04:00 shift. [Para 343][Para 413][Para 400]
