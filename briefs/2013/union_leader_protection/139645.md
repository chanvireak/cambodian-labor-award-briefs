# Award No. 35/13 — Nex-t Apparel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 25 March 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed, dissenting; Tuon Siphann, worker-appointed; Pen Bunchhea, chair) · Doc ID 139645

## 1. Facts
Nex-t Apparel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a garment manufacturer employing 980 workers. [Para 19] The C.CAWDU local union held its leadership election on 4 October 2012 with 30 participants and received its registration receipt on 23 October 2012 but no certificate; 223 workers asked C.CAWDU to represent them. [Para 42][Para 20] Mr He T, the elected union president, was on a three-month contract and was told on 8 October 2012 that it would not be renewed; it expired on 30 October 2012. [Para 20][Para 106] Mr O, an elected union activist, was dismissed on 8 November 2012 for serious misconduct. [Para 33] Three of the seven claims were settled at the hearing, leaving four. [Para 14][Para 15]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr He T (union president) and Mr O (union activist) in their positions and benefits with back pay from termination to reinstatement? [Para 4]
2. Whether the employer must pay the attendance bonus in proportion to the days of authorised leave taken? [Para 4]
3. Whether the employer must pay the portion of the accommodation and transportation allowance withheld in November 2012? [Para 4]
4. Whether the employer must pay pregnant workers 50 per cent of ninety days' wages before their maternity leave begins? [Para 4]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr He T never had a dispute with the employer and was told twice to resign from the union, once with an offer of a wage increase and once with a threat to withhold his US$80 skill bonus, so his non-renewal was discrimination. [Para 21][Para 23][Para 25] Mr O only discussed rights with members after hours and merely asked to read a posted letter; his dismissal was discrimination too. [Para 39][Para 40] Wages are docked by the day, so the bonus should be docked by the day as well. [Para 68]
**Employer (respondent):** The contract simply expired, for performance reasons documented in five complaints about slow response and instructions not followed. [Para 28][Para 30] Mr O was dismissed under article 83 for inciting a strike on 3 November and threatening an administrative assistant on 7 November. [Para 35][Para 38] It never received notice of the election, learning of the leadership only at the 13 November strike. [Para 50]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement refused for both men; the employer was ordered to pay Mr O damages equal to wages to contract end and 5 per cent severance, to reduce the attendance bonus pro rata rather than forfeit it, and to pay maternity wages in advance; the November allowance claim was rejected. [Para 196][Para 197][Para 174][Para 195]

On issue 1, the panel applied articles 12 and 279 and its consistent rule that the party alleging discrimination bears the burden of proof. [Para 90][Para 91][Para 92] The claim failed on notification: the workers said the election result was handed to guards at 3 p.m. on 4 October, but the EMS receipt showed the letter was posted at 9:05 a.m. that day, an inconsistency the panel treated as destroying the allegation of notice. [Para 92][Para 97][Para 98][Para 99] It added that the employer had not dismissed the elected secretary, which told against a discriminatory motive. [Para 100][Para 101] Mr He T's contract expired on its stated date under article 73(1), he was notified on 8 October, he signed for US$483.65 on 6 December including 5 per cent severance, and reinstatement was refused. [Para 105][Para 107][Para 108]

Mr O's case succeeded. Article 83 lists incitement to serious misconduct as an offence, but the panel followed Awards 20/05-Fortune Garment and 01/06-Goldtex in holding that calling workers to strike is not serious misconduct even where the strike is procedurally irregular, absent evidence of incitement to violence. [Para 111][Para 112][Para 113] Articles 332 and 333 protect strikers and forbid sanctions for participation. [Para 114][Para 115] The threat allegation was unsupported by any specific evidence. [Para 124][Para 125] The dismissal was therefore unlawful; under article 73(3) Mr O received damages equal to the wages he would have earned to contract expiry, plus 5 per cent severance under article 73(6). [Para 129][Para 136]

On issue 2, Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 raised the attendance bonus to at least US$10 and took effect on 1 September 2012, so the demand was a rights dispute. [Para 142][Para 143] Authorised personal leave suspends the contract under articles 71(6) and 72, so the employer owes neither wages nor bonus for those days, but it may not withhold the whole bonus. [Para 169][Para 171][Para 172] The order was to reduce it in proportion to the days taken. [Para 174]

On issue 3, a strike suspends the contract under article 332, so no allowance was owed for 13 November to 6 December; the 6 December agreement's bracketed phrase "including transportation allowance" fixed no amount and was read as 50 per cent, i.e. US$3.50. [Para 177][Para 188][Para 190][Para 191] On issue 4, articles 182, 183, 103 and 115 required half of ninety days' wages and perquisites, payable before the leave begins. [Para 192][Para 195]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Calling or inciting workers to strike is not serious misconduct under article 83 even where the strike is procedurally irregular, so a dismissal on that ground is unlawful and entitles the worker to damages to contract end plus 5 per cent severance; and the statutory attendance bonus must be reduced pro rata for authorised personal leave, never forfeited in full. [Para 113][Para 129][Para 172]

**Obiter dicta:** A contractual phrase referring to an allowance without stating an amount is construed as the same proportional share already agreed for wages. [Para 189][Para 190]

- Mr He T's contract is dated 1 September to 30 October 2012 in the facts and 1 August to 30 October 2012 in the reasoning. [Para 20][Para 105]
- Termination compensation is printed as US$483 and as US$483.65; the two-day leave deduction is printed as US$5 and as US$3. [Para 29][Para 139]
- Non-conciliation report No. 165 is dated 5 February 2013 and 31 January 2013 in adjacent passages. [Para 8][Para 10]
- The dissent labels the attendance-bonus issue "issue 6" and "Issue 2" in the same sentence, and is dated 23 January 2013, two months before the award it dissents from. [Para 199][Para 217]
- The panel rejects reinstatement for Mr O without stating why, having found his dismissal unlawful. [Para 135]
