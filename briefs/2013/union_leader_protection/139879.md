# Award No. 213-RJ Footwear (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 4 November 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (You Soun Ty, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Ang Eng Tong, chair) · Doc ID 139879

## 1. Facts
RJ Footwear (Cambodia) Co Ltd manufactures shoes at Ang Snuol, Kandal, and employed about 450 workers at the hearing. On 19 September 2013 at 14:00 the company announced the dismissal of 308 workers for want of work; they were let go on 20 September and paid their severance entitlements on 21 September. Two federations, the Khmer Workers' Rights Union Federation and the Khmer Youth Voice Union Federation, brought a collective dispute for reinstatement and for full wages when there is no work. After the panel's screening, only 19 workers remained parties. [Para 55][Para 68][Para 69][Para 113]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the 308 dismissed workers and let them resume work as normal? [Para 16]
2. Whether the employer must pay 100 per cent wages to workers during periods when there is no work to do? [Para 65]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company gave no advance notice and no lawful ground for the dismissals, merely asserting that there was no work; it obtained no authorisation from the Ministry of Labour for dismissing 308 workers; and the selection was anti-union, since those let go were union members whose membership the employer knew. Mr H and Mr L were said to be more skilled and longer-serving than the workers retained. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 73][Para 74]

**Employer (respondent):** this was a genuine collective dismissal caused by a collapse in orders, not a targeted one. Some 95 per cent of output is winter and autumn footwear and only 5 per cent summer lines; October to December requires barely 100 workers against a payroll of 450. The Article 95 procedure was followed: five staff delegates were informed and consulted, the Kandal labour department was notified in writing on 16 September 2013, fifteen officials inspected the plant, and section heads dismissed by a ranking of professional skill. [Para 78][Para 82][Para 85][Para 87][Para 91][Para 160]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both claims were rejected; no reinstatement and no wage order. The award is expressly non-binding (សេចក្តីបង្គាប់មិនជាប់កាតព្វកិច្ច) and becomes final eight days after notification if neither party objects in writing to the Minister of Labour. [Para 167][Para 168][Para 169]

*Representation and the 17 workers.* Article 19 of Prakas No. 99/04 allows a party to be represented by another person only on written authority. The panel found 17 workers had validly transferred their representation to the two federations and a further two attended in person, giving 19 parties; the panel would decide only for those. On the claim of the 17, the panel applied its settled rule that the party making an allegation carries the burden of proof, and found the workers had produced no list, no union membership records, no hire dates, no evidence of skill and no seniority data, although the panel had asked for them by the evidence deadline. The claim was therefore refused. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 110][Para 113][Para 121][Para 123][Para 125]

*Mr H and Mr L — protected status.* Article 293 of the Labour Law requires the labour inspector's consent before a staff delegate or candidate is dismissed, and Article 4 of Prakas No. 305/01 extends that shield to three union leaders. The panel read the extension strictly: it protects union leaders only, and does not reach union committee members or ordinary members. Both men were committee members, so neither enjoyed the shield. [Para 128][Para 129][Para 130]

*Mr H and Mr L — anti-union discrimination.* Articles 12 and 279 forbid an employer to use union membership or activity as a ground for discipline or termination. The panel found that the section heads who compiled the dismissal lists did not know who was a union member, and that only the employer held the membership list supplied by the workers. It also noted the workers' own concession that the three union-leadership candidates were not dismissed at all. With no evidence of the number or identity of union members dismissed, the allegation of discrimination was unsubstantiated. [Para 131][Para 132][Para 139][Para 141][Para 144][Para 145]

*Article 95 compliance.* Article 95 requires a dismissal order based on professional qualifications, seniority and family burden, prior notice to staff representatives, and measures to soften the impact. The panel accepted the photographic and oral evidence of idled lines and lost customer contact, the consultation with five staff delegates, the 16 September letter to the labour department, and the inspection by fifteen officials. It held the dismissals stood on a genuine reduction of the establishment's activity, and that the workers had not shown the skill ranking was misapplied. [Para 147][Para 157][Para 158][Para 161][Para 163][Para 165]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A collective dismissal under Article 95 is lawful where the employer proves a real reduction in the establishment's activity, consults the staff delegates, notifies the labour inspectorate, and selects by professional skill; workers challenging it must bring evidence that the ranking was misapplied, that they held protected union office, or that the dismissal was anti-union — assertion alone does not shift the burden, and Article 293's shield covers union leaders only, not committee members or the membership at large. [Para 129][Para 157][Para 163][Para 165]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that a workplace union holding no registration certificate from the Ministry is not yet a lawful professional organisation, so federation officials appearing for its members cannot make the parties signatories to the 3 October 2012 Memorandum of Understanding on industrial relations — a point that framed the representation analysis but did not decide the claims. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 25]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The worker headcount is printed inconsistently: 308 dismissed throughout, but line 163 renders the figure as "២ ៣០៨", and 306 are said to have received severance with only two outstanding [Para 70][Para 104][Para 163]. The employer's workforce is given as about 450 at line 56 but as 850 at line 78 [Para 56][Para 78]. Party arithmetic does not reconcile — 17 transferees plus two attending in person is put at 19, yet line 110 speaks of 17 and line 115 of 17 excluding Mr L while adding another worker [Para 110][Para 113][Para 115]. The company appears variously as "RJ Footwear (Cambodia) Co Ltd", "RJ Footwear (Cambodia) Digital" and "Ri Jen Footwear" [Para 25][Para 55]. Hire dates are printed twice for both men, differently each time (11 March against 7 March 2013; 12 February against 6 March 2013) [Para 97][Para 100]. Dismissal is dated 19 September for the announcement, 20 September for the terminations and 21 September for payment, while the labour department letter is dated 16 September [Para 68][Para 69][Para 91]. The employer-appointed arbitrator's signature block is corrupted by OCR at line 170 [Para 170].
