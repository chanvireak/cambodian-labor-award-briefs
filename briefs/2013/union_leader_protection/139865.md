# Award No. 192/13 — CJ Cambodia & MH Corporation (Arbitration Council, 25 October 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Orn Non, Kong Polypha) · Doc ID 139865

## 1. Facts
Thirty-four drivers and members of the Khmer Workers Power Union Federation claimed reinstatement after the company stopped them working; sixteen returned during the proceedings and the panel recorded those claims as settled. The remaining eighteen — union president Chan Ny, deputy Chhe Sokhom, secretary Pov Sary and fifteen other drivers — were dismissed on 15 July and 23 September 2013. The union was elected on 7 July 2013 and notified to the employer by letter of 8 July 2013, but had not yet been registered when the dismissals took effect. [Para 1][Para 118][Para 121][Para 181][Para 187]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back Chan Ny, Chhe Sokhom and Pov Sary, who were elected as union president, deputy and secretary, or whether it may refuse? [Para 158]
2. Whether the employer must take back the fifteen other drivers listed in table K5, or pay them lawful severance in lieu? [Para 158][Para 214]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The three leaders were elected union officers and the dismissals were union discrimination; the employer never notified the Ministry's labour inspector before dismissing them. The fifteen had not intended to abandon their jobs — security guards were barring them from the gate — and the employer's reinstatement notices never reached them. [Para 122][Para 142][Para 143][Para 152]

**Employer (respondent):** The company knew nothing of the union election and so could not have discriminated. Chan Ny attended an outside meeting with a third party during working hours without permission and held himself out as the company's representative. The fifteen had been absent without leave from 21 July 2013 and the attendance records prove it; three separate notices calling them back were posted and telephoned. [Para 125][Para 126][Para 137][Para 139][Para 148]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to reinstate Chan Ny, Chhe Sokhom and Pov Sary, and to pay the fifteen workers in table K5 severance, notice pay in lieu and outstanding annual leave. [Para 256][Para 257]

*Issue 1.* The panel applied article 293 of the Labour Law, which bars dismissal of a staff delegate or a candidate without the labour inspector's consent, read with articles 3 and 4 of Prakas 305 of 22 November 2001. It accepted that the Prakas does not in terms cover candidates who win office before the union is registered, and that article 293's consent requirement had not been met: the employer never sought the inspector's authorisation for any of the three. [Para 158][Para 133][Para 155]

Because the text was silent, the panel followed its own earlier awards. In 40/10 — Meng Yan it had held that protection for an elected candidate must run at least 45 days past the ballot, or continue until the candidate applies for registration; in 154/13 — Bouyung Garment it had held the three elected candidates protected from the ballot until registration, and as office-holders thereafter. The panel adopted that reading, reasoning that without it an elected leader could lawfully be dismissed in the gap before registration, which would defeat articles 266, 12 and 279 on freedom of association and anti-union discrimination. [Para 168][Para 171][Para 174][Para 175]

It then tested the three conditions it had set in earlier cases — the worker must be a protected person, the dismissal must fall inside the protected period, and the employer must have been notified of the protected names. All three were satisfied: the election was on 7 July 2013, the dismissals on 15 July and 23 September 2013, and the letter of 8 July 2013 naming the elected officers was signed for at security. The employer's denial of knowledge was not evidence, and it did not contest the letter. [Para 178][Para 181][Para 188][Para 190][Para 191][Para 192]

*Issue 2.* For the fifteen the panel went the other way on liability but not on money. The attendance records showed real and repeated absence from 21 July 2013, and the workers conceded the fact while denying intent. The claim that security blocked the gate was contradicted by the sixteen who did return, and by the guards' and personnel officers' statements, which the workers did not challenge. The panel therefore found the absences were not caused by the employer and that article 74 gave the company a proper reason to terminate. [Para 220][Para 221][Para 232][Para 235][Para 236]

Having found the terminations justified on the facts but effected without prior notice, the panel still ordered the statutory money. Article 89 required severance of seven days' wages and allowances for five to twelve months' service and fifteen days per year beyond that, capped at six months; article 91 moral damages were refused because the employer had a valid reason; articles 75 and 77 required notice pay in lieu because the dismissals were announced at the hearing itself; article 166 required payment for unused annual leave. Final wages under article 116 had already been paid, so nothing was added there. [Para 227][Para 237][Para 240][Para 241][Para 245][Para 250][Para 253]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A candidate elected to union office before the union is registered is protected against dismissal from the date of the ballot until registration, and needs the labour inspector's consent under article 293 to be dismissed in that window; the employer's assertion that it did not know of the election does not defeat the protection once the union has notified the result in writing. [Para 167][Para 174][Para 178]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that the sixteen workers who returned to work after the notices were posted are themselves the answer to any claim that the company physically barred the gate. [Para 145][Para 235]
