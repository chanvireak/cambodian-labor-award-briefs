# Award No. 239/13 — Meridian Industrial Garment (Cambodia) Limited (Arbitration Council, 16 December 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed, dissenting; An Nan, worker-appointed; Pen Bunchhea, chair) · Doc ID 139892

## 1. Facts
Meridian Industrial Garment (Cambodia) Limited opened a weaving factory in Kandal on 2 February 2013 and employed 500 workers; C.CAWDU had no lawfully registered local union there. [Para 14][Para 15]
C.CAWDU notified the employer of a union election by letter of 17 October 2013 sent by EMS, and held the election on 26 October 2013, at which Mr S became president and Ms D was a candidate. [Para 31][Para 32][Para 42]
Both were dismissed on 28 October 2013 at 3:09 p.m., days after the notification, on contracts due to expire on 1 November 2013; the union demanded reinstatement with back pay and an end to discrimination. [Para 19][Para 22][Para 50]
Eleven demands were conciliated; eight were settled at the hearing, Issue 6 was withdrawn, and Issues 1 and 7 were merged into the single claim decided here. [Para 6][Para 10][Para 11]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer was obliged to reinstate the two dismissed union leaders, who claimed the protection attaching to candidates in a union election? [Para 38]
2. Whether the dismissals were an act of union discrimination contrary to Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law? [Para 44]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the two had committed no misconduct; the dismissals came days after the employer was notified of the union's formation and were aimed at the union, as shown by the administration head's remark to Mr S, "Are you serious, Mr M?"; no prior notice was given. [Para 25][Para 28][Para 45]
**Employer (respondent):** Mr S was lazy, wasteful and inattentive and Ms D caused "chaos in the factory", both having had verbal warnings; their fixed-duration contracts were simply not renewed at expiry with termination compensation paid, and other union members remained employed. [Para 20][Para 24][Para 27]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to reinstate Mr S and Ms D, pay back wages from the date of dismissal to the date of reinstatement, and refrain from discriminating against the union. [Para 49][Para 50]
On reinstatement, the panel applied Article 293 of the Labour Law, which bars dismissal of a shop steward or candidate without the Labour Inspector's authorisation, read with clause 3 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 extending that protection to candidates for union leader. [Para 38][Para 39]
That protection, however, runs only 45 days before the election and 45 days after it, and following Award 104/12-Hai Yon the panel treated "election" as the union election and formation itself, with a date fixed to count the 45 days. [Para 39][Para 40]
Because the employer learned of the results only on 4 November 2013 and was not aware that Mr S had been elected when it dismissed them on 28 October 2013, and Ms D was not elected at all, neither enjoyed the Article 293 protection. [Para 43]
On discrimination, the panel applied Articles 12 and 279 and the approach of Award 123/07-E-Garment: dismissal grounded in union affiliation requires reinstatement. [Para 44]
Weighing the oral evidence at the hearing, as Awards 148/07-Pay Her, 34/11-Lim Line International and 155/11-Ying Dong Shoes direct, the panel found the administration head's question showed he did not want Mr S to form the union, and the dismissals followed the notification; the employer produced no convincing evidence to counter the allegation. [Para 44][Para 46][Para 48][Para 49]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a union shows that the employer was notified of union formation and that dismissals followed shortly, and an employer representative's words betray hostility to the union, the employer bears the evidential burden of answering the discrimination allegation; a bare assertion of laziness or disruption, unsupported by any convincing evidence, does not discharge it, and reinstatement with back pay follows. [Para 46][Para 48][Para 49]

**Obiter dicta:** the point that the 45-day protection requires the election date to be specifically fixed for accurate counting was stated generally and did not determine either dismissal. [Para 40]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
This is an unofficial English translation whose OCR has systematically dropped the letter "t" ("themployer", "thearing", "tworkers"); the evidence section was omitted from the English version entirely and refers the reader to the Khmer original. [Para 12][Para 13]
The award contradicts itself on binding force: para 12 says the award "in this non-binding", while para 51 describes a binding award becoming so eight days after notification; both are reported as printed. [Para 12][Para 51]
The same paragraph gives the Memorandum of Understanding as dated 3 October 2013 and 3 October 2012, and receipt of the notification letter is dated 18 and 19 October 2013 in different places. [Para 12][Para 33][Para 36]
Individual names of the two workers appear in inconsistent forms across the text, including "Noth Jot"; they are rendered here as Mr S and Ms D. [Para 43][Para 49]
