# Award No. 37/13 — N.T. Apparel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 4 March 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 139648

## 1. Facts
N.T. Apparel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a garment factory at Mal village, Dangkao district, Phnom Penh, employing 720 workers. The C.CAWDU plant union was registered on 4 July 2012 and had 387 members, but held no certificate of most-representative status. On 24 January 2013 the C.CAWDU federation filed a 17-point demand to improve working conditions; conciliation on 31 January 2013 left five points unresolved, and at the hearing of 18 February 2013 the parties settled points 1 and 4, leaving points 2, 3 and 5 for the panel. [Para 17][Para 18][Para 22][Para 23][Para 39][Para 41][Para 42]

## 2. Issues in dispute
2. Whether the employer must pay 50% of wages and allowances for the whole 90 days before a woman worker's maternity leave begins, rather than one month at a time? [Para 43][Para 64]
3. Whether the employer must pay a midday meal allowance of 4,000 riel a day to each worker? [Para 50][Para 82]
5. Whether the employer must pay the overtime meal allowance every Saturday, on the day the overtime is worked, rather than on the Monday of the following week? [Para 56][Para 95]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** women on maternity leave need the money during the confinement period itself; they are paid too little, so a 4,000 riel meal allowance would help their daily living and health; the employer pays the overtime meal allowance only on the Monday of the next week although workers must eat every day, and Prakas 041/11 and the practice of the sister company Beno require daily payment. [Para 44][Para 51][Para 57][Para 59]
**Employer (respondent):** it pays the 50% maternity wage monthly so that the woman can manage her household month by month, but cannot pay 90 days at once; it never paid a midday meal allowance and cannot afford 4,000 riel; Saturday payment of the overtime meal allowance is impossible because its accounts team must obtain approval from the head office in Shanghai, which does not work Saturdays, and the bank holding its funds is also closed. [Para 46][Para 49][Para 55][Para 60][Para 63]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must pay 50% of wages and allowances for the 90 days before maternity leave begins, and must pay the overtime meal allowance every Saturday; the 4,000 riel midday meal claim was refused consideration. [Para 116][Para 117][Para 119]
Point 2 was a rights dispute because maternity payment is fixed by the Labour Law. Under Article 182 a woman is entitled to 90 days' maternity leave and under Article 183 to half her wage, allowances included, where she has one year's service; Articles 115(3) and 103 extend the rule on payment before rest days to maternity pay, since maternity pay counts as wages. The panel therefore rejected the employer's monthly instalment practice and ordered 50% of wage and allowances for the 90 days to be paid before the leave starts. [Para 68][Para 71][Para 76][Para 77][Para 81]
Point 3 was an interest dispute: no law, agreement, collective agreement or past practice obliged the employer to provide a midday meal allowance, and the panel found the comparator factories did not establish such an obligation. Because the union was not the most representative in the enterprise — 387 members of 720 workers, with no certificate — it had no standing to bring an interest dispute, and the claim was refused without reaching its merits. [Para 84][Para 85][Para 89][Para 92][Para 94]
Point 5 was classified as a rights dispute, the overtime meal being fixed by point 2 of Prakas 041/11: a worker who volunteers for overtime at the employer's request receives 2,000 riel a day or one free meal, with no hour threshold. The Prakas fixes no payment date, so the panel asked only that payment fall within a reasonable period allowing the worker to buy food for the overtime day. It held that weekly payment on the Saturday was reasonable and necessary, since workers cannot fund the following week's meals, and the employer's approval and banking constraints did not defeat the entitlement. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 102][Para 114][Para 115]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Maternity pay under Articles 182 and 183 is wages for a fixed 90-day period and must be paid before the leave begins; an employer's preference to pay it month by month does not postpone the obligation. Where a Prakas fixes an overtime meal entitlement but not its payment date, payment must fall within a reasonable period that lets the worker buy food for the overtime day, and the employer's internal approval or banking cycle does not justify deferring it to the next week. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 102][Para 114]

**Obiter dicta:** that an interest award becomes a collective agreement for one year and removes the strike right over the same demands, and that a union without a most-representative certificate cannot found an interest claim on comparators or on a sister company's more generous practice. [Para 88][Para 89][Para 59]
