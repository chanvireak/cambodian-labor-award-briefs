# Award No. 261/13 — Bright Sun International Hop Corporation (Arbitration Council, 31 December 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chhea presiding; Kao Tha Chamroeung, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed) · Doc ID 139909

## 1. Facts
Bright Sun International Hop Corporation makes shoes at Trapaing Kong commune, Samrong Tong district, Kampong Speu, and employed about 1,057 workers. [Para 46][Para 47] The registered union is the Federation of Trade Unions for Solidarity of Cambodia, registered as No. 3085 កប/វក on 15 November 2013, with Ms D as president and Ms S as secretary. [Para 49] The company announced an election of shop stewards (ប្រតិភូ បុគ្គលិក) for 29 November 2013 and invited workers to stand; the union nominated Ms D and Ms S, and the company refused to let either stand, saying both had less than six months' service and could not read or write Khmer. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53] Conciliation failed and a single issue was referred to the panel on 3 December 2013. [Para 6][Para 16]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must accept the two union leaders as candidates in the election of shop stewards? [Para 50][Para 61]
2. Whether the panel has jurisdiction to decide that claim at all, given Article 298 of the Labour Law? [Para 64]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the union would not allow the election to proceed while its two leaders were excluded, and demanded that the employer accept Ms D and Ms S as candidates for shop steward. [Para 2][Para 13][Para 50]
**Employer (respondent):** neither woman had completed six months' service and neither could read or write Khmer, so neither was eligible; the election had been properly announced and all workers had been invited to stand. [Para 3][Para 14][Para 51]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel declined to consider, and rejected, the workers' claim that the employer accept the two union leaders as candidates, holding that Article 298 of the Labour Law reserves such complaints to the Labour Court. [Para 70][Para 71][Para 72]

The panel first had to classify the dispute, because the parties' memorandum of understanding of 3 October 2012 made awards on rights disputes binding on them while awards on interest disputes remained non-binding. [Para 23][Para 25][Para 28] It read Article 312 of the Labour Law as distinguishing rights disputes — those turning on a right already set by law, agreement, collective agreement, internal rules or past practice — from interest disputes, which it decides ex aequo et bono. [Para 54][Para 59][Para 60] Applying that test, it held the claim to concern a right to stand as a candidate that is written in the Labour Law, and therefore a rights dispute. [Para 61][Para 62]

Having so classified it, the panel asked of its own motion whether it could decide the claim. [Para 64] Article 298 of the Labour Law provides that complaints relating to the election of shop stewards, including the right to stand, and the correctness of such an election, must be filed with the Labour Court, or where none exists with the ordinary courts, for immediate and final decision. [Para 64][Para 65] The panel concluded that Article 298 removes from it jurisdiction over every dispute of that character, and that the present claim is exactly such a dispute about the right to stand as a shop steward. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 69] It accordingly found it had no jurisdiction and dismissed the demand. [Para 70][Para 71]

The award recorded that, being an award on a rights dispute, it becomes final and is applied once only, by force of the memorandum of understanding. [Para 73]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A complaint that a worker was wrongly refused the right to stand as a candidate for shop steward, or that a shop-steward election was wrongly conducted, is a matter that Article 298 of the Labour Law assigns to the Labour Court — and, where no Labour Court exists, to the ordinary courts — for immediate and final decision; the Arbitration Council has no jurisdiction to decide it, even where the parties' collective agreement makes rights-dispute awards binding, because the classification of the dispute as one of rights does not itself confer jurisdiction. [Para 62][Para 65][Para 67][Para 70]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that an award on a rights dispute becomes final and applies once only under the parties' memorandum of understanding, and its exposition of the difference between rights and interest disputes, were not necessary once jurisdiction was declined, since no operative order on the merits followed. [Para 25][Para 59][Para 73]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel was constituted on "05 December 2022 2013", two years printed for one. [Para 17] Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council is dated 21 April 2004 at para 4 and 21 April 2019 at para 56. [Para 4][Para 56] A stray "2017" sits inside the list of employer exhibits, whose numbering also skips from 4 to 6 and then to 8. [Para 34][Para 35] The employer's stated ground is truncated mid-sentence — "can read Khmer only a little and do not know the reason for the decision" — and the sentence that follows it runs straight into the panel's reasoning, so the employer's position on reading and writing Khmer is reproduced from paras 3 and 14 rather than from the narrative. [Para 53] The company name appears variously as ប្រាយ សិន, ទ្រាយ សិន and ប្រាយ សិនិង, and the union's name is printed both សាមគ្គីភាព and សាមគ្គី ភាព; the face of the award yields no further identification of either. [Para 2][Para 41][Para 48] The signature block at para 73 is partly lost to OCR, and the individual workers and the employer's lawyer, Ms K, are rendered by initials only. [Para 1][Para 29][Para 73]
