# Award No. 96/13 — F.W.K. (Cambodia) Limited (Arbitration Council, 5 June 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach, Sin Kim Sean, chair Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 139717

## 1. Facts
F.W.K. (Cambodia) Limited, a swimwear manufacturer at Chom Chao, Po Sen Chey, Phnom Penh, employed 670 workers; the local union of the Khmer Workers League Federation, registered on 6 January 2013, had 63 members and brought the claim. [Para 14] [Para 15] [Para 16] [Para 17] Of seven demands, five were settled in conciliation on 2 May 2013 and the remaining two came to the panel, which settled the second at the hearing, leaving only the demand for longer contracts. [Para 6] [Para 7] [Para 8] [Para 23] The employer gives a two-month probationary contract and then a six-month contract, and refused to change that practice. [Para 18] [Para 19] The award is an unofficial English translation with the evidence section omitted. [Para 12] [Para 13]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must offer employment contracts of at least one year, instead of the two-month probationary and six-month contracts it now issues? [Para 17] [Para 18]
2. Whether the dispute over contract length is a rights dispute or an interest dispute? [Para 24] [Para 25]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that short contracts cost them maternity leave and give no job security, and that the employer should therefore offer at least one-year contracts. [Para 20]
**Employer (respondent):** that contracts are made by agreement and their terms explained on the first day; that the company makes swimwear, so work varies with seasonal purchase orders; and that it has never docked the percentages of wages claimed in the second demand. [Para 20] [Para 22]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the demand for one-year contracts was rejected in a binding rights-dispute award (Part I), with no interest-dispute order (Part II). [Para 43] [Para 44] [Para 45] [Para 46]

The panel classified the demand as a rights dispute because it concerned the implementation of the one-year employment contracts said to be stipulated in the Labour Law and Civil Code, following awards No. 5/11, 13/11 and 14/11. [Para 25] [Para 26] It then reasoned from the formation of contract rather than from any stated minimum term: Article 65 of the Labour Law makes the labour contract subject to ordinary law and to the parties' agreed form, Article 664 of the Civil Code defines it as an exchange of promises of work and wages, Article 311 requires matching intentions to create, change or extinguish an obligation, and Articles 336 and 337 require a conforming offer and acceptance. [Para 27] [Para 29] [Para 30] [Para 31] [Para 32] [Para 33] It followed award No. 56/06 (Boric) for the rule that neither party can force the other to sign a contract or accept conditions it would not otherwise accept, and that a contract made under duress may be voided. [Para 36] [Para 38] [Para 39]

Applying that, the panel held that the workers' proposal of one-year terms was lawful and open to them to make, and the employer's refusal equally lawful, since neither side can compel the other; a contract actually agreed would bind until expiry or variation. [Para 40] [Para 41] [Para 42] It therefore rejected the demand. [Para 43]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that the employer grant minimum one-year contracts is a rights dispute, but the panel will not order it: the duration of a labour contract is fixed by the parties' agreement, both a worker's proposal of longer terms and an employer's refusal are lawful, and neither party can be compelled to accept a term it did not agree to — while a contract actually concluded binds until it expires or the parties vary it. [Para 41] [Para 42]

**Obiter dicta:** that a contract obtained under duress may be voided by law or by the other contracting party, and that the panel's rejection leaves the workers free to bargain for longer terms rather than to litigate them. [Para 39] [Para 40]
