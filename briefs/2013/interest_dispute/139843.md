# Award No. 158/13 — SG International Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 28 August 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bunhea presiding; Mar Samborana, employer nominee; An Vira, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139843

## 1. Facts
SG International Co., Ltd, a garment factory at Ang Keo village, Chom Chao, Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh, employed about 700 workers. [Para 56] On 12 June 2013 it dismissed five women who held six-month fixed-duration contracts running from 1 March to 31 August 2013. [Para 81] The employer said the five had led a strike on 11 June 2013 in which motorbikes were used to block the gate and workers were beaten; three of the five were union leaders recognised by the Ministry, the other two only candidates in an election for a union still awaiting registration. [Para 90][Para 137] No labour inspector authorised the dismissal, and the Labour Disputes Department refused the employer's notification letter for want of a police record. [Para 148]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay 4,000 riel a day towards the workers' midday meals? [Para 2]
2. Whether the USD 5 monthly health-care allowance must be paid in addition to the base wage, and not absorbed into it? [Para 3]
3. Whether the employer must take back the five dismissed union leaders and election candidates? [Para 4]
4. Whether the travel allowance must be raised by USD 10 on top of the USD 8 already paid? [Para 5]
5. Whether the regular attendance bonus must be raised by USD 10 on top of the USD 10 already paid? [Para 6]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the health allowance belongs to them separately from the base wage, as Article 17 of the collective agreement provides and as other factories pay it even where wages exceed USD 100; the five were dismissed without prior warning, without the inspector's authorisation and without being told why; and the travel and attendance allowances should each be topped up by USD 10. [Para 72][Para 82][Para 98]
**Employer (respondent):** its base wage of USD 85 already includes the USD 5 health allowance, exactly as Notification No. 103 K.B/S.J.N of 9 April 2013 requires, and sits above the legal minimum; the five were dismissed for leading a violent strike; and it cannot meet the travel and bonus claims. [Para 70][Para 88][Para 103]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issue 1 was withdrawn, Issue 2 dismissed, and on Issue 3 the panel ordered the employer to take back all five workers; Issues 4 and 5 were dismissed. [Para 211]

The panel began by classifying each demand as a rights dispute (វិវាទអំពីសិទ្ធិ) or an interests dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍) under Article 312(2) of the Labour Law and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY: the interpretation or application of a law or collective agreement is decided by law, everything else by equity. [Para 106] Issue 1 was withdrawn at the hearing and not considered. [Para 67]

On Issue 2 the panel treated the demand as a rights dispute, since it turned on the construction of the collective agreement and of Notification No. 103. Point 1 of that notification directs that the USD 5 health allowance granted in 2011 be merged into the base wage, and point 2 sets the minimum at USD 75 for probationary and USD 80 for full-rights workers on that basis. [Para 115] The employer was paying USD 85, above both figures, and clause 2 of Article 3 of the agreement applies a new legal standard only where it is more favourable. [Para 126] Because the agreement nowhere forbids merging the allowance, and the workers still receive the USD 5, merely inside the USD 85, the demand was dismissed. [Para 135]

On Issue 3, Article 293 of the Labour Law bars dismissal of a staff delegate or candidate without the labour inspector's prior consent, and clause 2 of Article 4 of Prakas No. 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001 extends that to union leaders. [Para 138] No such consent had been given before the three recognised leaders were dismissed, so their dismissal was procedurally unlawful and they had to be reinstated. [Para 149] For the two candidates, clause 3 of Article 3 of the same Prakas protects election candidates for 45 days before the poll and, if unelected, 45 days after it, while Article 4 protects founders and members during registration and for 30 days after. Following Award No. 40/10 Meng Yan, the panel held the protection runs from the day of election until the union is registered, since otherwise a candidate could be dismissed with impunity in the interval. [Para 165] The union notified the employer on 10 May 2013 and again by post on 4 June 2013, the latter marked REFUSED; the dismissal of 12 June 2013 therefore fell inside the registration period, all three conditions were met, and the two candidates were reinstated as well. [Para 186]

On Issue 4, Article 18 of the agreement sets USD 8 and point 1 of Notification No. 230 K.B/S.J.N of 25 July 2012 sets USD 7; the employer paid USD 8, the more favourable figure. [Para 195] Article 30 of the agreement freezes its terms for its two-year life, 1 November 2012 to 1 November 2014, absent mutual consent, so the workers could not raise the figure unilaterally and the claim failed. [Para 201] Issue 5 was decided the same way: Article 16 sets a USD 10 attendance bonus, point 2 of Notification No. 230 sets at least USD 10, the employer paid USD 10, and Article 30 barred a mid-term increase. [Para 207]

Arbitrator An Vira dissented on Issue 2 alone, holding that Article 17 of the agreement gives the USD 5 separately, that a convention more favourable than the law must prevail for its whole term, and that merging the allowance contradicted Article 13 of the Labour Law. [Para 217]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union leader or elected candidate for union office who is dismissed without the labour inspector's prior authorisation has been dismissed contrary to Article 293 of the Labour Law and must be reinstated, and a candidate's protection begins on the day of the election and continues until the union is registered, so an employer cannot escape it by dismissing in the interval between the poll and registration. [Para 167]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's general scheme for separating rights from interests disputes, and its observation that a collective agreement's terms are frozen for their term so that either party's unilateral demand to improve them is inadmissible, were stated in the abstract and were not needed to dispose of any single issue. [Para 111][Para 198]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The operative paragraphs print figures that contradict the reasoning: para 213 asks for USD 100 of travel allowance and para 214 for a USD 100 bonus, where the demands at paras 5, 97 and 101 are USD 10, and para 206 records a bonus payment of "USD 2023" where USD 10 is meant; para 191 orders reinstatement of "5,000" workers where five are named. [Para 191][Para 206][Para 213] Prakas No. 099 is dated 21 April 2019 at para 108 and 21 April 2004 elsewhere. [Para 108] The names of two of the five workers appear in three spellings each across their contracts, the Ministry's union registration letter and the attendance list; the panel resolved this at para 79 by adopting one form, which is not reproduced here. [Para 79] The case is headed 158/13 and rendered "158/13-SG" throughout; the signature block is partly lost to the scan, the worker nominee appearing once as "An Vira" and once in a garbled Latin form. [Para 216]
