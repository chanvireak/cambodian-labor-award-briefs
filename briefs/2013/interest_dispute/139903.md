# Award No. 250/13 — Sabrina (Cambodia) Garment Manufacturing Corp (Arbitration Council, 20 December 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach for the employer party, Ann Vireak for the worker party, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 139903

## 1. Facts
Sabrina (Cambodia) Garment Manufacturing Corp runs a garment factory on National Road 4 in Samrong Toung District, Kompong Speu, employing approximately 5,043 workers. [Para 15][Para 16] A local union of FTUWKC held certificate of registration No. 2687 dated 21 November 2012, and by Letter No. 332 dated 21 November 2013 the Ministry of Labour recognised three union leaders: Mr S (president), Mr S (vice-president) and Mr C (secretary). [Para 17] After a strike in May 2013 the employer suspended the contracts of the three leaders on 5 September 2013; the union demanded that the suspensions end, that the three be reinstated and that they receive back pay from May 2013 to the date of reinstatement. [Para 17][Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the demand to end the suspension of the three union leaders' contracts, reinstate them and pay back pay from May 2013 to reinstatement is a rights dispute or an interests dispute? [Para 30][Para 31]
2. Whether the Arbitration Council has jurisdiction to decide that demand when the employer has lodged a civil complaint on the same subject with the Primary Court of Kompong Speu? [Para 31][Para 33][Para 34]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** under Article 295 of the Labour Law the employer must obtain approval from the Labour Inspector before suspending a union leader, and no approval was obtained, so the suspension is null and void. [Para 19][Para 20] The case had already passed through Ministry conciliation and was referred to the Council, and court proceedings are a different dispute-resolution mechanism, so the objection to jurisdiction should not be entertained. [Para 32][Para 33]
**Employer (respondent):** the subject of the case is already an administrative dispute before the Primary Court of Kompong Speu, so the Council has no jurisdiction and should reject the demand and let the court decide on the merits. [Para 17][Para 18] The three leaders committed serious misconduct: breach of Points 3 and 5 of Clause 12(B) of the internal work rules, a strike staged before the Council had processed the case, breach of Clauses 11 and 12 of the collective agreement of 22 February 2013, breach of Articles 323 and 324 of the Labour Law for striking without a confidential vote and without seven days' written notice, failure to obey Arbitral Order No. 021/113 and Injunction No. 68 of 23 May 2013, and inability to control strikers resorting to violence. [Para 19] It had sought the Labour Inspector's approval on 7 June 2013, appealed to the Minister on 2 September 2013, and treated the absence of a decision two months later as a rejection. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 22]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel declined to consider the workers' demand that the employer reinstate the three union leaders and provide back pay from May 2013 to the date of reinstatement; the award in Part I is binding and enforceable under the MoU of 3 October 2012, and the interests-dispute part of the award is N/A. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 37][Para 38]

The panel first classified the dispute, because the parties' Memorandum of Understanding on Improving Industrial Relations in the Garment Industry of 3 October 2012 creates binding arbitration only for rights disputes, while interests disputes may go to non-binding arbitration and be objected to. [Para 9][Para 10][Para 11] Applying paragraph 2 of Article 312 of the Labour Law, the panel held that disputes over the interpretation and enforcement of laws, regulations or a collective agreement are rights disputes decided according to law, and that all other disputes are interests disputes decided in equity; Clause 43 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 was cited for the effect of an interests award, which stands in place of a collective agreement for one year. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 26][Para 28][Para 29] Because the demand concerned the suspension of contracts of employment, a matter stipulated in the Labour Law, the panel treated it as a rights dispute. [Para 31]

On jurisdiction, the panel compared the workers' demand with the employer's Civil Complaint No. 293 dated 15 November 2013, whose two objectives were to object to the Minister's rejection of the dismissal request and to seek approval to dismiss the three leaders for serious misconduct. [Para 33] The panel found the substance of the second objective identical to the demand before it — an end to the suspensions, reinstatement and back pay from May 2013 — and concluded that the object of the case before the Council was the same as that of the complaint pending before the Primary Court of Kompong Speu. [Para 34] It therefore decided not to consider the demand at all. [Para 35][Para 36] The panel did not reach the Article 295 argument on Labour Inspector approval, did not weigh the misconduct allegations, and made no finding on the lawfulness of the 5 September 2013 suspension.

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a demand before the Council and a complaint already filed by the employer in the ordinary courts share the same object — here, whether suspended union leaders must be reinstated with back pay — the Council will decline to consider the demand, even though it is a rights dispute that falls within its Article 312 jurisdiction, leaving the merits to the court seised. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 36]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's account of how the MoU of 3 October 2012 makes rights-dispute awards binding while allowing an objection to an interests award, and its statement that such an objection does not affect the duty to implement a rights award, are general observations; the case ended without any interests dispute to decide. [Para 9][Para 11][Para 38]
