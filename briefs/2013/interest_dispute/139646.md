# Award No. 34/13 — Papillion Tech Style (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 25 March 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Rik, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Ang Eng Thong, chair) · Doc ID 139646

## 1. Facts
Papillion Tech Style (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a garment factory employing about 850 workers; Tun Yun (Cambodia) is a separate garment factory registered in 2005 that no longer operates. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 91] From 26 January 2011 to mid-February 2011 Tun Yun's workers were moved to Papillion, roughly 600 at first and 101 by the date of the hearing, under agreements of 31 January and 27 May 2011 made with C.CAWDU federation representatives. [Para 68][Para 70] The 27 May 2011 agreement changed the company name on identity cards, paid seniority-based incentives of USD 50, USD 35 or USD 20, and preserved seniority and other benefits. [Para 78] The union then demanded that Papillion terminate the contracts of 113 transferred workers and pay five heads of compensation. [Para 62][Para 63]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must terminate the employment contracts of the workers transferred from Tun Yun and pay dismissal damages, annual leave, notice pay, moral damages and final wages? [Para 63][Para 110]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Agreeing to change the company name on their identity cards did not mean agreeing to change their employment contracts to Papillion; they remain Tun Yun workers, and Tun Yun has not closed its business. [Para 87][Para 90] The change was only to let them enter the Papillion factory temporarily. [Para 88]
**Employer (respondent):** The transfer was agreed with the union federation and the name change signified agreement to the new contract; seniority and benefits were preserved, wages, skills, shifts and location were unaffected, and a shuttle is provided. [Para 89][Para 93][Para 67][Para 69] It cannot terminate anyone because it needs more workers. [Para 66][Para 133]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the claim was refused, both as a rights dispute and as an interest dispute. [Para 135][Para 136]

As a threshold matter the panel applied article 312(2) of the Labour Law and article 43 of Prakas No. 099: disputes over the interpretation or application of a law, agreement or collective agreement are rights disputes decided according to law, while all others are interest disputes decided by equity. [Para 101][Para 103][Para 106][Para 108] Because the demand turned on the effect of the transfer agreements and of the Labour Law, the panel classified it as a rights dispute. [Para 110]

On the merits, article 2 of the Labour Law allows one enterprise to have several establishments under a single employer's management. [Para 117] The panel followed Award 06/06-Im Vey, holding that the employer's right to manage staff and operations stands so long as it is exercised lawfully and reasonably, and Awards 17/03 and 18/03-Hou Hing, holding that a staff transfer does not require termination procedure where it involves no wage reduction, no relocation far from the original site, no day-to-night shift change and no effect on core professional skills. [Para 118][Para 120][Para 121] Here the two agreements were concluded with the union federation, seniority and benefits were expressly preserved, incentives were paid, and both parties accepted that wages, skills, shifts and location were untouched and that transport was supplied. [Para 122][Para 123][Para 125] The transfer and the card change were therefore lawful and reasonable. [Para 126]

The panel then held that the workers could not compel termination at all: under article 311 of the Civil Code and article 65 of the Labour Law a contract's obligations are created, modified and extinguished by the will of the parties, so only the two contracting parties may dissolve them, and a demand for termination against the employer's will cannot succeed. [Para 127][Para 130][Para 131][Para 134]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Moving workers to an affiliated factory under an agreement with their union, where seniority, wages, skills, shift and work location are preserved, is a lawful exercise of management power and creates no right to termination and severance; and because a labour contract is modified or dissolved only by the will of both parties, workers cannot compel an employer to terminate them. [Para 126][Para 131]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel treated the change of company name on identity cards, coupled with payment of seniority incentives and preservation of benefits, as evidence that the workers accepted the new employer. [Para 116][Para 122]
