# Award No. 209/13 — Khvan Tum Khlu Sing (Cambodia) Limited (Arbitration Council, 18 October 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair) · award chosen by the parties as non-binding · Doc ID 139877

## 1. Facts
The company, a garment factory registered as Inv. 701 E/2000 of 31 July 2000 and employing about 3,200 workers, changed its name twice before — from Viyla to New Eastland Khu Sing and then to Khvan Tum Khlu Sing — and in 2013 changed owner. [Para 31][Para 32][Para 36] The factory is part of a group with plants in Cambodia, India and Pakistan, all under Brown Hope in the United Kingdom, which sold the business to Etozo in July 2012; the change of employer took effect on 02 June 2013. [Para 42][Para 44][Para 45] The Workers' Movement Union Federation, acting on a letter of authority of 18 September 2013 from workers at the factory, demanded that the employer pay each worker termination compensation under the Labour Law because of the change of owner. [Para 33][Para 35] Conciliation on 19 September 2013 failed on the single demand, and the non-conciliation report No. 1206 K.B/AK/VK of 20 September 2013 reached the secretariat the same day. [Para 6][Para 10][Para 11]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay termination compensation under the Labour Law to each worker because the company has changed to a new owner? [Para 35][Para 59]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the factory had already changed its name once before and has now changed owner; the change has destroyed the workers' trust in the factory and they fear losing their other benefits, so each worker is owed statutory termination compensation. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38]
**Employer (respondent):** the renaming was only to avoid confusion over the name, and the change did not alter any worker's post or benefits; only the owner of the parent company in the United Kingdom changed, and the Cambodian factory, its management, workforce, posts and benefits were all kept as they were. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 46][Para 48][Para 49] The factory has operated in Cambodia for 13 years, intends to continue, and has renewed its land lease for another 8 years from 2013. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the workers' demand that the employer pay termination compensation under the Labour Law to each worker on the change of employer; the award was non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification if no party objects in writing to the Minister. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74]

The panel first classified the claim. Since the demand was for statutory termination compensation, the dispute was a rights dispute rather than an interest dispute, and fell to be decided by law. [Para 58] It then turned to Paragraph 1 of Article 87 of the 1997 Labour Law, under which a change in the legal status of the employer — in particular by succession, inheritance, sale, merger or transfer of funds — leaves the labour contracts in force on the day of the change standing between the new employer and the workers of the former enterprise. [Para 59] Earlier awards had read that provision to mean that a change in the employer's legal status does not affect the labour contracts, and the panel followed awards 98/07-Sky Sino, 83/08-Le Grand Cafe and 100/08-Gawon Apparel on that point. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 62] Applying it, the panel found the Cambodian factory retained its management, its workers and staff, and their posts and benefits, so that nothing at all had changed. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 65][Para 66] Article 87 was therefore not violated: the contracts continued without any change of terms and without any benefit being lost. [Para 69][Para 70]

The panel accepted that the workers had lost confidence in the factory's future, since it had been renamed before and had now changed owner, and that they feared for their benefits. [Para 38][Para 67] It found, however, that the employer had given reasons for the renaming and had shown its intention to keep operating, including by renewing the land lease. [Para 54][Para 68] Because no contract had been terminated, the employer owed no termination compensation to any worker. [Para 71][Para 72]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A change in the legal status of the employer — including the sale of a foreign parent company and the resulting change of owner of the Cambodian enterprise — does not terminate the labour contracts of the workforce and does not entitle the workers to statutory termination compensation, so long as the contracts, posts, management and benefits continue unchanged with the new employer under Paragraph 1 of Article 87 of the Labour Law. [Para 59][Para 69][Para 70][Para 71]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's finding that the workers had lost confidence in the factory's future, and its observation that the employer explained the renaming and proved its commitment to continue, were not grounds of the decision, which rested on the absence of any termination of contract. [Para 67][Para 68]
