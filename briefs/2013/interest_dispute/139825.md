# Award No. 133/13 — Sabrina (Cambodia) Garment Manufacturing Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 27 August 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambor Na for the employer party, An Virak for the worker party, Kong Pollak chair) · Doc ID 139825

## 1. Facts
Sabrina (Cambodia) Garment Manufacturing Co., Ltd, a garment factory at Samrong Tong, Kompong Speu, employed about 5,020 workers. [Para 78][Para 79] A strike over working conditions ran from 21 May 2013; on 3 June 2013 workers carrying sticks and fire extinguishers entered the compound, threw stones and beat doors and glass. [Para 121] The employer issued notices terminating 94 workers for abandoning their posts and a further group for serious misconduct, and the Free Trade Union of Workers of the Kingdom of Cambodia claimed for 20 dismissed workers, seeking reinstatement or, failing that, termination compensation. [Para 42][Para 92]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the 20 dismissed workers or pay them termination compensation under the Labour Law? [Para 92]
2. Whether the employer must pay wages for the strike period, 21 May to 15 July 2013, to the 4,000 workers said to have struck? [Para 132]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissals were union discrimination — the employer marked union members on photographs in Chinese and left non-members who carried sticks and stones in their jobs [Para 118]; the internal rules required a written, thumb-printed warning before dismissal for serious misconduct and only an oral warning was given [Para 103]. The strike was a last resort after the employer failed to honour the agreement of 30 January 2013. [Para 135]
**Employer (respondent):** the 18 workers ran into the factory armed with sticks and fire extinguishers, threw stones and destroyed doors and glass, which is violence during a strike under Article 330 [Para 122]; the worker later excluded never appeared in the original claim [Para 95]. Wages: Article 332 suspends the contract during a strike, so none is owed. [Para 141]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel refused reinstatement for all 20, upheld the dismissal of the 18 workers who used violence while ordering payment of their last month's wage and remaining annual leave, declined to consider the case of Ms E, found the dismissal of Mr L wrongful and ordered severance for him, and dismissed the strike-wage claim. No sum is quantified in the award. [Para 248]

The panel first classified the claims: under Article 312 bis (2) and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 a rights dispute is decided by law and binds the parties, while an interest dispute is decided in equity and may be objected to, and a claim about termination is a rights dispute. [Para 153][Para 155][Para 158][Para 160]

On issue 1, Ms E attended none of the three hearings, so the panel refused to consider her case. [Para 161] For the 18, both parties agreed the acts in the panel's table — running in with sticks, throwing stones, beating doors — and the panel held that such conduct is not a peaceful strike but violence, serious misconduct under Articles 330 and 83(b)(3), whether or not damage or injury was proved. [Para 176] Dismissal was therefore lawful; on Article 73 a fixed-duration contract ended for serious misconduct carries no damages, so the 18 recovered only their last month's wage and unused annual leave. [Para 189] Mr L stood differently: the accuser bears the burden of proof, the employer conceded the video and photographs were unclear [Para 199], and the panel found no evidence that he held or threw a stone, only that he joined the strike. Reinstatement was still refused because the employer would not take him back, so the panel awarded compensation in lieu of notice under Article 73(3), the 5% termination indemnity under Article 73(6), unused annual leave under Articles 166 and 167, and his last wage under Article 116. [Para 211]

On issue 2, the strike was unlawful: no secret ballot or majority consent under Article 323 and Circular No. 05 of 6 March 2000 [Para 143], no exhaustion of the conciliation and arbitration route under Articles 319 and 320, and violence contrary to Article 330 [Para 146]. Under Article 332 the contract is suspended during a strike and no wage is paid [Para 236], a rule the panel had already applied in Awards 49/05 and 27/10 [Para 239]. Article 334 obliges the employer to pay striking workers only where it hired replacements, and the union conceded there were none. [Para 243] The claim also failed because no authority from the 4,000 workers to the union was produced. The panel nevertheless held the parties bound by the employer's own more favourable payment: full wage for 21 and 22 May, 50% for 23, 25 and 27 May and 3 June, and full wage for the 29 to 31 May shutdown. [Para 245]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A strike conducted with sticks, stones and destruction of factory property is violence rather than a peaceful strike, and is serious misconduct under Articles 330 and 83(b)(3) even without proof of damage; dismissal for it is lawful and, on a fixed-duration contract, yields only the last month's wage and unused annual leave. [Para 175][Para 182] Where the employer's photographic evidence is unclear and it concedes as much, the burden on the accuser is not discharged and the dismissal is wrongful; reinstatement may still be refused where the employer declines to re-employ, and Article 73 compensation then replaces it. [Para 192] Wages are not payable for strike days, but a voluntary payment more favourable than the law binds the employer who made it. [Para 238]

**Obiter dicta:** the observations that gatherings inside or outside a factory over a labour dispute fall under the Labour Law and not the Law on Peaceful Demonstration, and that workers must pursue grievances peacefully and may not use threats to company security as a means of settlement, were not necessary to either order.

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The procedural section records three of the five original claims as settled and two remaining, "issue 1" and "issue 3"; the reasons and the operative order number the wage claim "issue 02". The reasons' numbering is followed in §2. [Para 19]
- The header's list of claims is cut mid-sentence: claim 1 is lost and the text resumes at claim 2.
- The strike's end is printed as "3 March 2013" [Para 134], which precedes its start; elsewhere the panel records the strike as ended on 3 June 2013.
- The panel is said to have been constituted on "03 July 2023 2013", and the award carries no signature of any of the three arbitrators [Para 252].
- The order at [Para 189] reads as "4 [months'] last wage"; the summary at [Para 190] and the operative order at [Para 248] give only the last month's wage, which is followed here.
- Worker names recur in two spellings across the tables and lists, and the list of names at [Para 232] runs past 35 without matching the 20 claimants. Individuals are redacted in this brief as required.
