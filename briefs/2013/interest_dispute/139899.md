# Award No. 247/13 — Juhui Footwear Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 13 December 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy appointed by the employer party and recording a dissenting opinion, Liv Sovanna appointed by the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair appointed by the two) · Doc ID 139899

## 1. Facts
Juhui Footwear Co., Ltd, a footwear manufacturer registered on 29 March 2012 at Roveang Village, Cheung Prey, Kompong Cham, employs 4,300 workers; the claimant, the Local Union of CLUF, has 819 members and was registered on 5 September 2013. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 18] Of twenty demands raised through the Ministry, fifteen were settled in conciliation and four of the five remaining were resolved or withdrawn at the hearing, leaving one: the attendance bonus. [Para 6][Para 7][Para 8] The employer pays a US$10 monthly attendance bonus, pays it pro rata where a worker takes two days of authorised leave, but withholds the whole bonus once a worker takes two and a half days. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 28] The parties agreed to defer the award from 9 December 2013 to 13 December 2013. [Para 13]

## 2. Issues in dispute
3. Whether the employer must pay the attendance bonus in proportion to the number of days of authorised leave that the workers take? [Para 18][Para 25]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** workers request leave authorisation from the employer, and previous arbitral awards have already ordered this employer to pay the attendance bonus in proportion to authorised leave taken. [Para 20][Para 30]
**Employer (respondent):** it will not meet the demand because it follows the policy of its parent company in China. [Para 20]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay a monthly US$10 attendance bonus in proportion to the number of days of authorised leave for personal commitments taken by the workers, as a binding award. [Para 59][Para 61]

The panel first classified the claim, because the parties are signatories to the Memorandum of Understanding on Improving Industrial Relations in the Garment Industry of 3 October 2012, which makes arbitration of rights disputes binding but leaves interest disputes to non-binding arbitration that a party may object to. [Para 9][Para 10][Para 11][Para 12] Under paragraph 2 of Article 312 of the Labour Law the Council has jurisdiction over disputes concerning the interpretation and enforcement of laws, regulations or a collective agreement, and under Clause 43 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 an award on an interest dispute takes the place of a collective agreement for one year; disputes not stipulated in an agreement are interest disputes decided in equity. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24][Para 25] Since the demand concerns the attendance bonus stipulated in Point 2 of Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012, the panel held it to be a rights dispute. [Para 26]

On the merits the panel noted that the earlier awards it had issued on this bonus — No. 112/11-Yung Wah 1, No. 136/11-Cambo Handsome (Branch 1) and No. 154/11-B & N — rested on Point 1 of Notification No. 041 of 7 March 2011, which set the bonus at US$7. [Para 30] That notification was replaced by Notification No. 230, whose Point 2 raised the bonus to US$10 and added the term "without absence". [Para 31][Para 36] The panel treated the US$10 as mandatory for all garment and footwear employers and not a matter within the employer's discretion to direct or vary. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 34]

The difficulty was the meaning of "absence". The Labour Advisory Committee had not interpreted it, and the panel refused to assume that it meant absence with or without authorisation without taking legal provisions into account. [Para 36][Para 37] It found that "absence" in the law covers annual leave, special leave, maternity leave, holidays and weekly time off, and that those do not reduce the bonus, so the worker keeps the full US$10. [Para 37][Para 38] Authorised leave for a personal commitment is not among them, so the panel could not interpret the notification as covering it and instead decided each case on its own facts. [Para 39][Para 40]

On those facts the panel reasoned that authorising leave is the employer's own discretion, exercised through internal rules and production requirements, and that an employer which authorises leave knows production will not be interrupted. [Para 42][Para 43] Internal rules distinguish authorised from unauthorised leave, and a worker on authorised leave faces no disciplinary action. [Para 44] Taking authorised leave for a personal commitment is therefore an agreement that the worker will not be paid for the days not worked, the employer agreeing to preserve the job and benefits on return, and the employer cannot treat such leave as an absence attracting discipline. [Para 45][Para 46] "Working days in each month" means the days the employer requires the worker to attend, whether 26 days, 21–22 days where holidays fall, or fewer where leave is authorised. [Para 47][Para 49][Para 50][Para 51] A worker who works the days obliged and takes authorised leave has attended regularly, and the days counted exclude holidays and authorised leave. [Para 51][Para 52]

Applying Article 103 of the Labour Law, which includes bonuses in "wage", the panel found the attendance bonus is part of the worker's wage. [Para 54][Para 55] Under paragraph 6 of Article 71 and paragraph 1 of Article 72, absence authorised by the employer suspends the contract, affecting only the main obligations, so the employer need not pay wages for the days of authorised leave. [Para 55][Para 56] It follows that the employer need not pay the bonus for those days either — but also that it has no right to withhold the entire bonus, only to pay it in proportion to the days taken. [Para 57][Para 58]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Authorised leave for a personal commitment suspends the labour contract under paragraph 6 of Article 71 and paragraph 1 of Article 72 of the Labour Law, so the employer owes neither wages nor the attendance bonus for the days taken, but it may not forfeit the whole monthly bonus: the US$10 attendance bonus required by Point 2 of Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 is part of the wage under Article 103 and must be paid in proportion to the days of authorised leave taken. Because that bonus is fixed by the notification, a demand about it is a rights dispute and not an interest dispute, whatever the employer's parent-company policy. [Para 26][Para 54][Para 55][Para 57][Para 58]

**Obiter dicta:** the remarks that the amount of a bonus generally varies with each enterprise's own direction and supervision, and that "working days in each month" may be 26 or 21–22 days depending on company policy, are general observations on Cambodian practice and were not needed to fix the proportionate payment. [Para 31][Para 49][Para 50]

## 6. Source defects disclosed

- The header records a dissenting opinion by arbitrator Ing Sothy [Para 1], but no dissenting text appears anywhere in the translated award and the decision is signed by all three arbitrators without qualification [Para 63].
- The issue list in the non-conciliation report is numbered (1), (3), (18), (9), (10) [Para 1], while the narrative says issues 8, 9 and 10 were withdrawn and issue 1 resolved, leaving issue 3 [Para 8]; the "(18)" is evidently a misread "8". The first line of the source is truncated mid-sentence at "(9) The workers demand that the employer provide a 2,000 riel payment in lieu of ...".
- Severe OCR loss throughout: "th" is dropped after "t" ("themployer", "thentire", "tipulated"), "Vocational Traing" for Training, "NIA" for N/A, "USS10" for US$10, "wasubstituted", "outling", "sue" for "issue" [Para 4][Para 26][Para 30][Para 31][Para 41].
