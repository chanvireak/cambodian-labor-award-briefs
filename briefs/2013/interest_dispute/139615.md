# Award No. 21/13 — Darong Printing & Embroidery Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 28 February 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Sin Kim Sean (worker-appointed), Pen Bunchhea (president) · Doc ID 139615

## 1. Facts
Darong Printing & Embroidery Co., Ltd is a printing and embroidery enterprise in Kandal province with about 590 workers, organised by the Democratic Union of Cambodian Garment Workers at the Darong factory. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54] Of six unsettled demands, the parties settled issues 2, 3 and 4 at the hearing and the workers withdrew issue 6, leaving issues 1 and 5. [Para 25][Para 30][Para 31][Para 32] Because both parties had signed the memorandum of understanding of 3 October 2012 on improving industrial relations in the garment sector, the panel split its award into rights disputes, which bind immediately, and interest disputes, which do not. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 35]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must repay the wages and regular attendance bonus of the union secretary and adviser for 12, 13 and 14 November 2012, when they attended an ASEAN youth workshop without the employer's permission? [Para 3][Para 56]
2. Whether the company must pay the full statutory severance to the cook, employee No. 253, who was dismissed without valid reason? [Para 12][Para 127]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Clause 1, point 6 of the collective agreement obliges the employer to give union representatives time off for union work and study with NGOs or federations on invitation, without deducting wages or bonus; all four officers had to attend because the workshop is held once every ten years. [Para 58][Para 60][Para 113] The cook was dismissed for no valid reason, without any written or oral warning, and is owed final wages, leave pay, notice compensation, dismissal indemnity and moral damages. [Para 95][Para 97][Para 130]
**Employer (respondent):** It authorised only the president and vice-president to attend, and the collective agreement had expired and was being renegotiated, so it could no longer found a claim. [Para 62][Para 66][Para 116] The cook's food was no longer appetising, he was warned repeatedly, and he lost a vote against a new cook by two votes. [Para 73][Para 75][Para 81][Para 82]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 1 was dismissed; on issue 5 the panel ordered the employer to pay dismissal indemnity equal to 120 days' wages, moral damages equal to that indemnity, notice compensation of one month's wages, 20 days' unused annual leave, and final wages of USD 134. [Para 183][Para 184][Para 185][Para 186][Para 187][Para 188]
On issue 1 the panel first classified the claim: because it rested on clause 1, point 6 of the collective agreement it was a rights dispute, decided according to law under Article 312(2) of the Labour Law, not by equity. [Para 109][Para 105] It then examined clause 18(1) of that agreement, which gave it a two-year term running one week from signature, and clause 18(2), under which the parties must renegotiate three months before expiry, failing which the agreement renews for a further term. [Para 117][Para 120] The agreement was signed on 27 June 2009, so by 14–16 November 2012 it had already expired; the parties were in fact renegotiating a new agreement and had reached none by the hearing, which showed no intention to continue the old one. [Para 64][Para 118][Para 121][Para 124] The claim therefore had no clear legal basis and was dismissed. [Para 119][Para 126]
On issue 5 the dismissal was a rights dispute under Article 91. [Para 128] Article 74 allows termination at the employer's will only with a valid reason relating to the worker's aptitude or conduct or the needs of the enterprise, and Article 91 gives moral damages where the termination is without valid reason. [Para 131][Para 133][Para 136] The employer asserted misconduct under its internal rules but, ordered to file those rules by 7 February 2013, filed nothing, so the party bearing the burden of proof failed to show a valid reason. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 91][Para 146][Para 147] The panel also noted that the cook was given only 3,000 riel a day to prepare three meals per worker, that the employer never contradicted this, and that he was not reinstated when invited back. [Para 78][Para 148] It concluded the contract was terminated without valid reason and without compliance with the employer's own rules. [Para 149][Para 150]
On quantum, the panel read Articles 102 and 103 together to hold that "wage" includes base wage plus allowances actually received, not the base wage alone, so the USD 134 monthly total was the basis. [Para 154][Para 155][Para 157][Para 158] With about eight years' service, Article 77 and Article 75 gave two months' notice compensation, of which one month was paid by the notice actually given. [Para 166][Para 167][Para 168] Article 89 gave dismissal indemnity of 15 days per year, that is 120 days. [Para 170][Para 173] Article 166 gave pay for the 20 days of leave both parties agreed were unused, and Article 116 required the final wage within 48 hours. [Para 178][Para 179][Para 180][Para 182]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer terminates an indefinite-term contract without a valid reason, a panel will award moral damages under Article 91 equal to the dismissal indemnity, cumulating with notice compensation, the Article 89 indemnity, unused annual leave and final wages; and a claim founded only on a collective agreement that has expired and is being renegotiated has no legal basis. [Para 118][Para 136][Para 150]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remarks that the cook was given only 3,000 riel a day for three meals and that he declined to return were context for the absence of a valid reason, not separate heads of relief. [Para 78][Para 148]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is dated 28 February 2013, but the text records that it was to issue on 15 February 2013 and was postponed by agreement. [Para 1][Para 37][Para 38] The collective agreement appears as signed 27 June 2009 in the reasoning and as registered No. 172/12 in March 2010 in the evidence list. [Para 43][Para 64] The cook's daily food budget is printed as 30 riel at para 78 and 3,000 riel at para 148. [Para 78][Para 148] Unused leave is stated as 20 days by agreement but 19 days in the reasoning. [Para 177][Para 178] Article 116 is quoted as 48 hours in the text and applied as 48 hours after being described as "forty five hours", and the final-wage order cites the same article for a different period. [Para 180][Para 182] The dismissal demand is pleaded as issue 2 at para 72 and decided as issue 5 at para 127. [Para 72][Para 127] The signature page carries an extra garbled chair name ("Thai Bun Na") alongside Pen Bunchhea. [Para 191]
