# Award No. 123/13 — Naga World Ltd (Arbitration Council, 26 June 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phalha presiding; Chiv Phirum, employer nominee; An Nan, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139812

## 1. Facts
Naga World Ltd operates at Sangkat Tonle Bassak, Khan Chamkarmon, Phnom Penh, and its staff are organised in the Khmer Employees' Rights Union at Naga World. [Para 1] The union filed two demands on 4 June 2013 by letter No. 34/13, giving notice of a peaceful strike to press the company to settle and improve working conditions. [Para 11] Conciliation failed on both demands and the non-conciliation report No. 713 of 17 June 2013 reached the Secretariat on 19 June 2013. [Para 8][Para 12][Para 13] The staff began striking on 13 June 2013 and were still striking when the case came before the panel. [Para 14][Para 40]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must comply with Arbitration Council orders No. 10/2010 of 16 February 2010, No. 78/2010 of 6 September 2010 and No. 184/2012 of 1 November 2012? [Para 2][Para 3]
2. Whether the company must set a minimum wage of USD 150 a month and raise the base wage by 20 percent for every member of staff earning USD 150 a month or more? [Para 4][Para 5]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the union's members maintained that the company should be held to the three earlier awards, and pressed for the USD 150 floor and the 20 percent increase on the base wage; at the pre-hearing meeting of 24 June 2013 their representatives put the number still on strike at about 800 employees. [Para 2][Para 4][Para 40]
**Employer (respondent):** on the earlier orders it said it could not apply them because it had already lodged an appeal through the legal procedure; on wages it asked for time to study the demand carefully, which the union refused. [Para 3][Para 5] Its representatives did not attend the pre-hearing meeting. [Para 1]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to continue the arbitration and closed case 123/13, deciding neither demand and making no order against the company. [Para 41][Para 42]

The panel put the only question it would answer as whether the arbitration should proceed. [Para 28] It applied Article 20 of Prakas 099, which requires the parties to suspend every dispute measure during the panel's proceedings — a strike or a lockout under Article 318 of the Labour Law, or any other measure that would aggravate the dispute — and to attend every meeting the panel convenes. [Para 28][Para 29][Para 30]

It read the provision, as it had in earlier awards, to mean that the panel will not continue an arbitration while the workers are striking or the employer has locked them out. [Para 31] It cited its own practice: in case 04/04 it notified the parties on 29 January 2004 that a party without the right to strike during proceedings could not have its dispute decided; in case 18/04 its second order of 22 April 2004 required strikers at six hotels to cease all strike, demonstration or disturbance on hotel premises from 23 April 2004, and reserved the right to terminate if a party breached the order; in case 77/05 it stated that a continuing strike would end the proceedings; and in cases 110/11 and 17/13 it discontinued on that ground. [Para 32][Para 34][Para 35][Para 36][Para 37]

On the facts, the court order No. 09 of 13 June 2013 recorded that the staff had begun striking on 13 June 2013, and at the pre-hearing meeting the union representatives confirmed that about 800 employees were still on strike. [Para 14][Para 40] The panel therefore declined to continue and made no findings on either demand, recording that it had made none because it did not proceed. [Para 27][Para 41]

Two consequences follow from the text. The panel never classified the first demand as a rights or an interests dispute, although it rested on three earlier awards the union said bound the company, so the employer's answer that an appeal was already lodged went unexamined. [Para 2][Para 3] And because the case ended on the strike, the earlier orders the union sought to enforce were not enforced in this award. [Para 2][Para 42]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a workforce continues its strike after the panel has begun proceedings, the panel will not continue the arbitration and closes the case without deciding the demands, whatever the merits of those demands; the strike alone disposes of the case. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 41]

The bar is not permanent: the panel may take the same dispute again once the strike has ended. [Para 33]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted the union's assertion that about 800 employees remained on strike, a figure it recorded without testing, since the employer's representatives were absent from the meeting at which it was given. [Para 1][Para 40] It also observed that the employer had asked for time to study the wage demand and that the union had refused the request — a fact that played no part in the decision to stop. [Para 5]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The text is heavily corrupted. The employer's name is printed three ways — "ណាហ្គា វើល ដ៏ លីមីធីត" in the parties' block, "ណាហ្គា វើលដ៍ (ខេមបូឌា)" in the evidence list and "ណាហ្គា វ៉ើល" in the order — so "Naga World Ltd" is a transliteration, no Latin-script name being printed. [Para 1][Para 24][Para 41] Stray matter interrupts the reasoning: "CN" before the Council's name, "Li" in the chapter reference, "Apple" before the panel's name, and "កម្ពុជា" in the court-order sentence. [Para 6][Para 7][Para 14][Para 38] Prakas 099 is described as issued by the Ministry of Social Affairs, Labour, Vocational Training "and 20 Youth Rehabilitation", the digit differing from the "40" printed in the companion award on the same Prakas. [Para 28] The complaint reference is garbled as "លេខ ៣៤/១៣ ឆ្នាំ សទសខ". [Para 11] A page marker ")-៥" and the fragment "ទៅ _" interrupt the quotation from case 18/04 and the finding on page 5. [Para 27][Para 35] The citation to case 17/13 is broken into "អ៊ិនធើ ណាស់ ) ហេសិន រ៉ូយ៉ាល់". [Para 37] The number of pre-hearing meetings carries a stray "៤". [Para 39] The signature block ends in the string "montrackson" in place of the presiding arbitrator's signature. [Para 43] The employer's representatives are recorded as absent from the pre-hearing meeting, so the employer's positions appear only as summarised from the non-conciliation report.
