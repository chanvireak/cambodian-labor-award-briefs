# Award No. 94/13 — Eternity Global Sporting (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 13 June 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy; Tuon Siphann, dissenting; chair Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 139716

## 1. Facts
Eternity Global Sporting (Cambodia) (EGS), a glove and ball manufacturer at Damnak Ampil, Angsnoul, Kandal, employed 545 workers. [Para 15] [Para 16] The FTUWKC local union held its leadership election on 17 February 2013 and was registered on 5 April 2013. [Para 29] [Para 17] Workers struck from 22 February to 9 March 2013 over six demands; the employer dismissed Mr V on 22 February, Mr T, Mr E, Mr D and Mr K on 25 February, and Mr P on 9 March, and the workers now demand reinstatement of the first six with wages and benefits until reinstatement. [Para 29] [Para 48] [Para 49] [Para 64] [Para 18] The Primary Court of Kandal issued an injunction on 27 February 2013 ordering strikers back to work within 48 hours under Article 337. [Para 22] This is an unofficial English translation; the evidence section is omitted and the Khmer original governs. [Para 13] [Para 14]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate the six dismissed union leaders and activists and maintain their wages and benefits until the date of reinstatement? [Para 17]
2. Whether the dismissals were related to the formation of the union, so that the three elected leaders enjoyed the special protection of Article 293 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 305? [Para 72] [Para 82]
3. Whether the dismissals were union discrimination contrary to Article 279 of the Labour Law? [Para 72] [Para 98]
4. Whether the six workers committed serious misconduct within Article 83 of the Labour Law? [Para 72] [Para 100]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that the employer dismissed the six because they formed the union and were its leaders and activists; that the union notified the employer of the elected leaders through the security guards on 22 February and by registered post on 26 February, and that the guards' refusal to accept the letter was not the union's fault; and that had they assaulted the executive the police would have arrested them at the time. [Para 65] [Para 90] [Para 108] [Para 112]
**Employer (respondent):** that it did not know of the union leadership when it dismissed them, since the guards received no letter and the postal notice came later; that Mr V incited workers to leave early and to strike unlawfully and then assaulted an executive; that the other four also beat the executive on 23 February 2013; and that Mr P breached the court injunction. [Para 34] [Para 48] [Para 50] [Para 71]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the demand to reinstate Mr V, Mr T, Mr E, Mr D, Mr K and Mr P and to maintain their wages and perquisites was rejected, in a binding rights-dispute award (Part I), with no interest-dispute order (Part II). [Para 121] [Para 123] [Para 124] Arbitrator Tuon Siphann dissented. [Para 127]

The panel classified the claim as a rights dispute because reinstatement and union discrimination are rights stated in the Labour Law, following awards No. 5/11, 13/11 and 14/11. [Para 66] [Para 67] On protection it held that Article 293 and Clause 3 of Prakas No. 305 require three conditions — that the worker truly holds protected status, that dismissal falls in the protection period, and that the union has notified the employer of the names by any reliable means. [Para 75] [Para 82] The first two were met: the election was on 17 February 2013 and the dismissals of 22 and 25 February fell inside the 45 days after it. [Para 84] [Para 86] [Para 87] [Para 88] The third was not: the workers could not name the guards who allegedly refused the letter, and the postal notice was sent on 26 February, after the three leaders had already been dismissed. [Para 93] [Para 94] [Para 95] [Para 96] On union discrimination the panel applied Article 279, which forbids an employer to weigh union affiliation in dismissal decisions, and held that the party alleging discrimination bears the burden; the workers showed no evidence that the employer knew of their positions when it dismissed them. [Para 97] [Para 98] [Para 99] [Para 100]

On serious misconduct the panel held that inciting a strike is not serious misconduct under Article 83 B(5), even where the strike procedure was unlawful, unless the employer proves incitement to violence; it followed awards No. 20/05 (Fortune) and No. 08/05 (Winner Knitting), and found no such proof. [Para 102] [Para 103] [Para 104] [Para 105] [Para 106] But the assault on the executive was proved by a police report of 23 February 2013, a prosecutor's investigation order of 4 March 2013, a Labour Department letter and a health-centre certificate, none of which the workers contested, so the five were held to have committed serious misconduct. [Para 110] [Para 111] [Para 113] Mr P's dismissal stood separately: he did not return within the 48 hours set by the injunction, which Article 337 treats as serious misconduct. [Para 117] [Para 118] [Para 120]

The dissent reasoned that the union did notify the employer in writing through the guards on 22 February and again by post, that the employer's failure to receive it was not the workers' fault, and that the employer's own request for Labour Department approval of the dismissals showed it knew the three were protected leaders; as the Department never answered, the dismissals were void. [Para 131] [Para 133] [Para 136] [Para 137]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Special protection for elected union leaders under Article 293 and Prakas No. 305 depends on the union having given the employer notice of their names by a reliable means before the dismissal; a letter said to have been refused at the gate by guards the union cannot identify does not discharge that duty, and a postal notice posted after the dismissals comes too late. Inciting a strike, even an unlawful one, is not serious misconduct, but a proven assault on an employer officer is, and so is a failure to return to work within 48 hours of a court injunction under Article 337. [Para 96] [Para 104] [Para 113] [Para 118]

**Obiter dicta:** that the legality of the strike procedure is irrelevant to whether inciting the strike is serious misconduct, since the panel judges only whether violence was incited; and that the party alleging union discrimination carries the burden of proving the employer's knowledge at the time of dismissal. [Para 104] [Para 98]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Dates conflict in four places and are reported as printed: the assault is dated 23 March 2013 in the finding but 23 February 2013 in the evidence; the injunction is described as issued on "27 December 2011" and also 27 February 2013; the MoU is dated 3 October 2012 in the procedure and 28 September 2010 in the order; and the non-conciliation report is numbered 121/13 and 321. [Para 50] [Para 113] [Para 119] [Para 22] [Para 9] [Para 123] [Para 4] [Para 5] Prakas No. 305 is dated 22 November 2001 and 22 November 2011 in different passages. [Para 76] [Para 129] Two contracts end before they begin: Mr E's runs "1 December 2012 to 1 March 2012" and Mr P's "1 October 2012 to 1 January 2012". [Para 45] [Para 47] The earlier strike dispute is registered as case No. 49/13 while this award is 94/13. [Para 20] The union's registration date, 5 April 2013, postdates every dismissal. [Para 17] The English translation is unofficial, omits the evidence section, drops "th" throughout ("themployer", "athearing") and spells the same men three ways (Thoeun Chek, Ouk Ty, Mr I Vuthy Ravuth). [Para 13] [Para 128] Individual workers and union officers are reduced to initials per the briefing convention.
