# Award No. 24/13 — Digital Data Cambodia (Arbitration Council, 28 February 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (You Soun Ty, selected by the employer, dissenting; Tuon Si Phan, selected by the workers; Pen Bun Chhean, chair) · Doc ID 139624

## 1. Facts
Digital Data Cambodia (អង្គការ ប្រព័ន្ធ ទិន្នន័យ កម្ពុជា) is a foreign non-governmental organisation in Chamkar Mon, Phnom Penh with 481 staff, split between "staff" and "operators", and no internal regulations registered with the Ministry of Labour. [Para 63][Para 64] Its union local has 220 members; the tourism and services federation, holding a transfer of authority of 18 May 2012, filed 13 demands on 30 November 2012, and all 10 that went to conciliation remained unsettled. [Para 67][Para 19] The dispute turns on the departure of Mr S, an operator team leader of about nine years' service, on 28 August 2012, and on benefits the organisation had stopped paying since 2008–2009. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 96]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back Mr S at his former post with his role, wage, seniority and benefits, or pay him termination compensation? [Para 68]
2. Whether the employer must raise staff wages to match market price growth and annual economic growth? [Para 125][Para 251]
3. Whether the employer must keep the additional benefits women received during maternity leave? [Para 128]
4. Whether the employer must keep the monthly baby allowance as previously paid? [Para 135][Para 259]
5. Whether the employer must keep five days' paid sick leave a year? [Para 151][Para 304]
6. Whether the employer must give special leave without deducting it from annual leave? [Para 159][Para 318]
7. Whether the employer must station a doctor at the workplace during working hours? [Para 164][Para 330]
8. Whether the employer must keep the incentive payment for operators as previously paid? [Para 169][Para 346]
9. Whether the employer must pay an annual bonus to all staff as previously paid? [Para 178][Para 366]
10. Whether the employer must guarantee union freedom at the workplace, allow union meetings and stop discrimination against union members? [Para 185][Para 380]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr S was forced to sign a resignation on 28 August 2012 after the custom-print incentive rate fell from US$1.75 to US$1.45 and then to US$1 a book without negotiation, and his team stopped work in protest; the baby allowance, paid sick leave, resident doctor and annual bonus were long-standing and could not be withdrawn. [Para 80][Para 153]
**Employer (respondent):** Mr S resigned of his own accord after refusing to meet the senior production director as instructed, which was serious misconduct; incentive rates vary by project and productivity, so none was guaranteed; the old benefits were replaced by medical insurance covering spouses and children, and donor-funded budgets limit what can be paid. [Para 116][Para 103][Para 129]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer ordered to pay Mr S notice pay, severance and moral damages, less the 5% already paid to him; to restore five days' paid sick leave; to engage one nurse on duty and a doctor present at least two hours during working hours; and to continue the annual bonus for staff meeting its quality standards. Claims 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10 dismissed; claim 3 was settled by agreement and not decided. [Para 395][Para 399][Para 401][Para 403][Para 134]

On jurisdiction the panel applied Article 302 and held this was a collective dispute — a group of workers against one employer, over working conditions, capable of disturbing the enterprise — rejecting the employer's objection that it was purely individual. [Para 192][Para 194][Para 200]

On claim 1 the panel found the resignation did not flow from Mr S's own will but from the employer's inducement: he was called in, handed a letter and told that refusing to sign would cost him his severance. [Para 206][Para 211] Since his successive one-year fixed-term contracts ran past two years, Article 67(2) and Article 73(5) converted them into an indefinite-term contract, so the full statutory package followed: two months' notice pay under Article 75, severance of 130 days (four and a half months) under Article 89, and moral damages of the same measure under Article 91. [Para 223][Para 232][Para 241][Para 246] The 5% already paid was deducted. [Para 249][Para 250] The panel also held, however, that the employer had grounds to complain of his conduct, since he refused the meeting and led his team to stop work; arbitrator You Soun Ty dissented on this issue, treating the refusal as serious misconduct under Articles 83 and 2. [Para 429][Para 439]

On claim 2 the workers asked the panel to research inflation data but gave no specific sum, number of claimants, contract types or seniority; without those facts the claim was refused. [Para 256][Para 258]

On claim 4 the baby allowance was a past practice under Article 65, but it stopped in 2008, the workers never protested in writing, and a new US$5 monthly policy now covers those earning under US$300; the practice had ended by tacit acceptance. [Para 278][Para 282][Para 301]

On claim 5 the practice had run four years and the employer offered no concrete basis for ending it, so it was restored. [Para 306][Para 312] On claim 6, Article 171 and Prakas No. 267 of 11 October 2001 expressly allow special leave to be deducted from annual leave where the worker has not used it up, so the claim failed. [Para 321][Para 327]

On claim 7, Articles 238 and 242 require an infirmary and an on-duty nurse above 50 workers, and Prakas No. 330 a doctor present two hours per shift for 301–600 workers; improved insurance did not discharge that duty. [Para 331][Para 339][Para 344] On claim 8 the incentive payment stopped in 2009 without protest, so it too lapsed. [Para 362][Para 364][Para 365] On claim 9 the employer failed to prove the criterion used to exclude Mr S, so the annual bonus continues for staff meeting its quality standards. [Para 372][Para 380] On claim 10, Article 275 does not oblige an employer to provide meeting space, and no evidence of discrimination under Article 279 was produced. [Para 384][Para 389]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A resignation procured by an employer's threat that refusing to sign will forfeit severance is not a resignation at all but a termination at the employer's initiative, so the statutory notice pay, severance and moral damages are owed; and where successive fixed-term contracts have run beyond two years they convert to an indefinite-term contract under Article 67(2), which enlarges rather than narrows those entitlements. [Para 211][Para 232]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that a past practice may be ended by agreement, by a replacement practice, or by change of circumstances, and that silent failure to protest signals acceptance, are general statements of doctrine rather than holdings. [Para 280]

- Service is "9 years 1 month 28 days" at [Para 72] but garbled as "៤ ៩ ឆ្នាំ" at [Para 236]; the start date is 30 June 2003 at [Para 71] and "២៤ ៣០ ខែមិថុនា" at [Para 218].
- The non-conciliation report No. 104 is dated 22 January 2013 at [Para 15] but 12 January 2013 at [Para 21]; the first hearing is 31 January at [Para 17] and 30 January at [Para 22].
- At [Para 196] the panel writes that condition (b) of the collective-dispute test "ត្រូវបាន បំពេញ ទេ" ("is not fulfilled") while the reasoning and the outcome at [Para 200] treat it as fulfilled; the negative particle is an OCR error.
- The decision section omits issue 3, consistent with the parties' agreement at [Para 134], leaving the numbering 1, 2, 4, 5.
- Issue 9 is mis-numbered as "ទី ៨" at [Para 169] before reverting to 9 at [Para 178].
- The 5% payment is called a severance-type payment at [Para 76] and "ប្រាក់ បំណាច់ កិច្ចសន្យា" at [Para 248]; the award deducts it without fixing a figure.
- Arbitrator You Soun Ty's signature block is corrupted to "memini / Suband" at [Para 408] and "Quannony" at [Para 449]; the name is taken from [Para 1]. Mr S's full name appears throughout the source and is redacted here.
