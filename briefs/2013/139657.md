# Award No. 39/13 — YSA Foul Liveal (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 18 March 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Yu Soun Thy, employer-appointed; Tuon Si Phan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 139657

## 1. Facts
YSA Foul Liveal (Cambodia) Garment, a Phnom Penh garment factory employing 479 workers, of whom 26 were men and 453 women, hired new production workers on a one-week probation, then a three-month fixed-duration contract, and after two years' service terminated the relationship, paid 5% severance, and re-hired the same people first as two-day probationers and then on fresh three-month fixed-term contracts. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 63][Para 124][Para 126] It had moved from undetermined-duration to three-month contracts around 2005. [Para 55][Para 56] The local union, established under the Cambodian Workers' Economic Union Confederation, was registered on 30 January 2013 with 276 members but was not the most representative union in the company. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 52] Conciliation failed on all seven demands on 14 February 2013 (report No. 182) and the hearing was held on 22 February 2013; point 7 was settled at the hearing and point 5 withdrawn. [Para 20][Para 22][Para 28][Para 95]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must conclude undetermined-duration contracts with new workers after their probation and with old workers whose successive fixed-term contracts total more than two years? [Para 53][Para 107]
2. Whether the employer must raise the seniority bonus by USD 2 per year of service, to USD 13 a month, for workers with more than ten years' seniority? [Para 74][Para 154]
3. Whether the employer must restore the large company signboard on the front fence as before? [Para 81][Para 178]
4. Whether the employer must pay severance to long-serving workers who wish to resign? [Para 85][Para 182]
6. Whether the employer must raise the milk allowance by USD 5 a month, and set up a nursery (ទារកដ្ឋាន) for women workers with children? [Para 89][Para 198][Para 202]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** workers lose seniority and maternity benefits each time a fixed-term contract ends, probation work is meant for occasional jobs yet they have served continuously, and they are already skilled so probation is unnecessary; the signboard was replaced by a smaller one hung inside the office after the owner changed; and seniority pay should rise because clause 5 of the internal rules rewards service. [Para 54][Para 72][Para 82][Para 86]
**Employer (respondent):** work in each section is irregular, so after two years it terminates the contract and pays severance including the 5%, then re-hires on two days' probation before a new three-month contract if labour is needed; it denies any policy of dismissing workers and says the owner and director are one person, Mr J Chan Hominh, though managers changed; it pays seniority bonus per Notification No. 041/11 and has no objection on the signboard demand. [Para 63][Para 66][Para 67][Para 87][Para 88][Para 164]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel dismissed the demand for undetermined contracts for new workers, dismissed the severance demand, and refused to consider the seniority-bonus, signboard and milk-allowance demands as interest disputes; it ordered the employer to sign undetermined-duration contracts with old workers whose fixed-term contracts total more than two years (counting the two-day probation), and ordered it to set up a nursery for women workers with children. [Para 215][Para 216][Para 217][Para 218][Para 220][Para 221][Para 222]
The panel first classified the demands, holding that a dispute over the interpretation or application of law or a collective agreement is a rights dispute decided according to law under Article 312(2), while anything not fixed by law or agreement is an interest dispute decided by equity, following awards 05/11, 13/11, 14/11, 31/11 and 62/11. [Para 97][Para 102][Para 105][Para 106]
On issue 1 the panel treated the claim as a rights dispute turning on Article 66. [Para 108] For new workers it applied Article 65(1) and Article 311 of the Civil Code to hold that contract type flows from the parties' agreement, so workers cannot force undetermined contracts where the employer does not agree; that part was dismissed. [Para 112][Para 113][Para 117][Para 118] For old workers it applied Article 67(2), which caps a fixed-term contract and any renewal at two years and deems longer arrangements to be undetermined-duration, and Article 73(5), and followed award 410/03 (Zaksintek) that renewal beyond two years converts the contract. [Para 139][Para 141][Para 143][Para 146][Para 150] It took ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982 as useful context though not binding, and held that the two-day probation did not break seniority because the workers kept performing work for the employer throughout. [Para 147][Para 149][Para 134][Para 135]
On issue 2 the panel found clause 5 of the internal rules concerns annual wage review, not seniority, and that the employer's practice matched Notification No. 041/11, so the claim was an interest dispute that the non-most-representative union could not bring. [Para 157][Para 158][Para 165][Para 175][Para 177] The signboard demand at issue 3 was held an interest dispute on the same basis and refused. [Para 178][Para 179][Para 181] On issue 4 the panel held that severance follows only from the employer's termination, and it had no policy of dismissing anyone, so the demand failed. [Para 185][Para 194][Para 196][Para 197]
On issue 6 the panel applied Article 186, which requires any enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a nursing room and a nursery, and award 81/09 (Von Rech), and ordered a nursery because the company had 453 women workers and none; it refused the extra USD 5 milk allowance as an interest claim. [Para 202][Para 206][Para 208][Para 212][Para 214]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive fixed-duration contracts that together exceed two years convert into an undetermined-duration contract under Article 67(2) and Article 73(5), and a short probation interval between them does not restart seniority where the worker in fact kept working for the same employer; but contract type remains a matter of agreement, so a worker cannot compel an undetermined contract in the first place. [Para 117][Para 150][Para 134]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that ILO Recommendation No. 166 is not binding but is a useful interpretive context for Article 67, and observed that the Labour Law leans toward undetermined-duration contracts because job security benefits workers and long service encourages commitment. [Para 144][Para 149]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award mixes numbering: the operative part addresses "issue 6(a)" and "6(b)" while the reasoning numbers the same demand as issue 6 and earlier as issue 7, and the settled demand is described as point 7 in one paragraph and point 5 withdrawn in another. [Para 28][Para 89][Para 218] Paragraph 134 prints the two-day break as "2,016 days", and paragraph 61 states severance at 5% where other paragraphs speak of severance plus 5%. [Para 134][Para 61] The employer is named both as "YSA Foul Liveal (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd" and as "YSA"; the owner's name appears only in the employer's own submission. [Para 48][Para 87] Issue 4 was declared a dispute of interest yet decided on the merits rather than refused, unlike issues 2, 3 and 6(b); the award does not explain the difference. [Para 182][Para 197] Union officers and the employer's representative are reduced to names redacted, and the signature block is partly lost to OCR noise. [Para 224]
