# Award No. 138/13 — Manhattan Textile and Garment Corp (Arbitration Council, 23 July 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Nan, worker-appointed; Men Nimmith, chair) · Doc ID 139823

## 1. Facts
Manhattan Textile and Garment Corp runs a garment factory in Kampong Cham employing 3,900 workers, of whom 210 belong to the claimant local union of the Free Trade Union of Workers of the Kingdom of Cambodia. More than 100 workers in the M.Q section have had their contracts suspended since 13 August 2012 and receive 50 per cent of the minimum wage; 18 older workers aged between 55 and 74 work in administration, M.Q and energy. The union demanded that the employer terminate the contracts of the suspended and older workers and pay them termination benefits. [Para 3][Para 18][Para 19][Para 21]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must terminate the undetermined-duration contracts of the suspended M.Q section workers and the elderly workers and pay them final minimum wage, compensation in lieu of notice, payment in lieu of remaining annual leave, severance pay and damages? [Para 19][Para 35]
2. Whether that demand is a rights dispute or an interests dispute, and so whether any award on it binds the parties? [Para 29][Para 36]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The suspensions have run over a year, the 50 per cent minimum wage does not buy food, and the suspensions were imposed without complying with Article 71 of the Labour Law; if the contracts were terminated the workers could find new jobs. They refuse transfer to the sewing sections because sewing is not their skillset and errors would expose them to blame and eventual resignation. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 25][Para 26]
**Employer (respondent):** It has no policy of terminating contracts and there are no more buyers for the M.Q section, so it moves those workers to sewing sections where work exists, preserving seniority, minimum wage and benefits, and it will train them and not blame them for mistakes made while training. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 27][Para 28]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the demand that the employer terminate the undetermined-duration contracts of the suspended M.Q section workers and the 118 elderly workers, and pay severance, damages, unused annual leave, outstanding wages and notice compensation, is rejected; the award is binding under the Memorandum of Understanding of 3 October 2012. [Para 46][Para 48]

The panel began with classification. The parties are signatories to the Memorandum of Understanding on Improving Industrial Relations in the Garment Industry of 3 October 2012, which commits them to binding arbitration for rights disputes but creates no binding obligation for interests disputes, which remain open to non-binding arbitration and to objection. Article 312, paragraph 2, of the Labour Law and clause 43 of Prakas No. 099 divide the field the same way: the panel decides by law disputes over the interpretation and enforcement of laws, regulations or a collective agreement, and in equity all others. [Para 9][Para 10][Para 11][Para 30][Para 32][Para 34]

Because the demand concerned termination compensation governed by Articles 77, 89, 91 and 167 of the Labour Law, the panel held it to be a rights dispute, decided according to law. [Para 36]

On the substance the panel did not reach the merits of the compensation schedule. It relied instead on Article 311 of the Civil Code, under which a contract is the matching of intentions of two or more parties to create, change or extinguish an obligation, and on Article 65, paragraph 1, of the Labour Law, under which a labour contract establishes the working relationship. From those two provisions it concluded that creating, changing or extinguishing a contractual obligation requires the matching intentions of worker and employer, so the right to terminate belongs to both parties alike. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40]

The workers' demand was therefore, in substance, a demand that the employer terminate their contracts. The employer stated it had no policy of terminating them and did not wish to. The panel held that workers cannot demand termination against the employer's will, and that because the employer neither wills nor effects termination, the workers have no right to termination compensation. It accordingly declined to consider the provision of termination compensation at all, and rejected the demand in full. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44][Para 45][Para 46]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker cannot obtain termination compensation by demanding that the employer terminate an undetermined-duration contract. Since a contract is extinguished only by the matching intentions of the parties and the right to terminate belongs to worker and employer equally, a demand to terminate against the employer's expressed will fails; and where no termination is willed or effected, the consequential claims for notice compensation, severance pay, unused leave and damages have no foundation. [Para 39][Para 43][Para 44]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel recorded the workers' contention that the suspensions breached Article 71 of the Labour Law and their evidence that the suspensions had lasted over a year at half the minimum wage, but decided nothing on the lawfulness of the suspensions themselves, nor on the employer's offer to transfer the workers to sewing sections. [Para 21][Para 22]
