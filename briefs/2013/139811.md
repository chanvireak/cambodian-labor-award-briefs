# Order No. 120/13 — M & V Garment International (Branch 3) (Arbitration Council, 21 June 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Mae Nimmit (chair) · Doc ID 139811

## 1. Facts
M & V Garment International (Branch 3) (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អឹម វ៉េ កាត់ដេរ អន្តរជាតិ (សាខា ទី៣)) runs a sewing plant at Building 1623, National Road 2, Sangkat Chak Angre Krom, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh; this dispute was brought by the Cambodian Democratic Garment Workers Union, not the two federations behind the companion cases. [Para 1] Of 11 demands conciliated on 14 June 2013, nine remained unresolved in report No. 710 dated 17 June 2013. [Para 26][Para 22][Para 37] The company asked for an order stopping the strike, and on 17 June 2013 the panel issued Order No. 025/13 suspending the strike and requiring all strikers back at their shifts from the morning of 19 June 2013. [Para 28][Para 29] The strike continued; the union sent no representative to the 20 June 2013 pre-hearing meeting, where only the employer appeared and confirmed the strike was still running. [Para 30][Para 32][Para 33]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must raise the travel allowance by USD 5, to USD 12 per worker? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company must keep a health bonus of USD 5 per month above the statutory base wage, because many workers faint from hardship? [Para 4]
3. Whether the company must pay an incentive of 88 dollars to workers earning above the base wage? [Para 6]
4. Whether the company must pay a midday meal allowance of 4,000 riel per day per worker? [Para 8]
5. Whether the company must pay a USD 3 incentive to workers who work on public holidays? [Para 10]
6. Whether the company must continue the contracts of pregnant workers and not terminate them when maternity leave approaches? [Para 12]
7. Whether the company must pay overtime meal money of 4,000 riel for 16:30 to 18:30 and 6,000 riel for 18:30 to 20:30? [Para 14]
8. Whether the company must provide a proper dining area with tables and proper covered parking for motorbikes and bicycles? [Para 16]
9. Whether the company must act against section and building heads who behave improperly towards workers? [Para 18]
10. Whether the panel should continue the arbitration while the strike continued? [Para 42]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the union pursued the nine unresolved demands — the additional USD 5 travel money, the USD 5 health bonus, the 88 dollar incentive, 4,000 riel daily meal money, the USD 3 holiday incentive, continued contracts for pregnant workers, overtime meal money at 4,000 and 6,000 riel, proper eating and parking facilities, and discipline for abusive supervisors — and did not appear at the pre-hearing meeting. [Para 2][Para 4][Para 6][Para 8][Para 10][Para 12][Para 14][Para 16][Para 18][Para 33]

**Employer (respondent):** it refused the travel increase, applying Ministry Notification No. 230 dated 25 July 2012; it applied Notification No. 103 dated 09 April 2013 on the health bonus; it refused the incentive, the meal allowance and the overtime meal money; on holiday pay, pregnancy contracts, facilities and supervisor discipline it applied its existing practice, and on discipline it asked the union to name the offenders because it could do nothing without evidence. [Para 3][Para 5][Para 7][Para 9][Para 11][Para 13][Para 15][Para 17][Para 19]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no award on the merits — the panel decided not to continue the arbitration in case No. 120/13-M & V Garment International (Branch 3). [Para 56]

The panel put the only question it decided as whether to continue the arbitration, and answered it from Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council: during its proceedings the parties must suspend every dispute measure such as a strike or lockout under Article 318 of the Labour Law, or any other measure that could aggravate the dispute, and must attend every meeting convened. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44]

It repeated its standing interpretation that it will not continue an arbitration while the workers are striking or the employer has locked out, and listed the orders it had followed — No. 5906-Sang Vou, No. 3908 Choung Youv, Nos. 13709 and 13809-Ploem Phanh, No. 110/11-Simanao — plus No. 04/04-IMSI, whose section 4 held that a party to a labour dispute has no right to strike during arbitration and that the Council cannot then settle or order on the dispute. [Para 45][Para 46] It added the second order of 22 April 2004 in the Siem Reap hotel case, No. 77/05 (point 4: no processing while the strike continues), and Nos. 110/11-Simanao and 17/13-International Fashion-Royal, where proceedings were discontinued for the same reason. [Para 48][Para 50][Para 51]

On the facts, only the employer attended; it confirmed the workers were still striking in breach of Order No. 025/13, and nothing from the union contradicted that. [Para 54][Para 55] As no hearing on the merits was held, the panel made no finding of fact on any of the nine demands and took no evidence beyond the company's letter, its delegation letter, the non-conciliation report and its own order. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 38][Para 41]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 and Article 318 of the Labour Law bar a party from striking or locking out while arbitration is pending, and a panel will discontinue the case where the strike is shown to be continuing — proof that the strike has ended must come from the party asserting it, and a union that stays away from the pre-hearing meeting while the employer testifies the strike continues leaves the presumption of continuance unrebutted. [Para 42][Para 44][Para 54][Para 56]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's reproduction of the older observation that the Council may take the dispute up again once the strike stops, and of the reservation of a right to end proceedings on any breach of its order, was not necessary to this discontinuance. [Para 47][Para 49]
