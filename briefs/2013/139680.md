# Award No. 62/13 — Horus S.N. Das Strihl Corp (Arbitration Council, 10 April 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Run Saray chair) · Doc ID 139680

## 1. Facts
Horus S.N. Das Strihl Corp is a garment factory at Stung Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employing about 92 workers. [Para 1][Para 38][Para 39] Three union leaders — the president, vice-president and secretary of the Khmer Democratic Workers' Union at the factory — were among 89 workers whose employment the company ended on 10 October 2012 after a strike that ran from 9 August 2012; Award 238/12 of 25 January 2013 had already held that this was a dismissal, not abandonment of the job, and ordered the statutory benefits paid. [Para 41][Para 58][Para 61][Para 55] In this case the same three claim back wages from 9 August 2012 to 25 January 2013 for the time they spent as union leaders, and the parties had merged the two non-conciliated points into one. [Para 3][Para 15][Para 41]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to pay back wages to the union leaders for the period 9 August 2012 to 19 October 2012? [Para 70]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to pay back wages to the union leaders for the period 19 October 2012 to 25 January 2013? [Para 83]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the whole period was the strike period, during which the leaders were engaged in conciliating and resolving the dispute with the employer at the Ministry of Labour, which prevented them from finding other work; they performed the duties of a legally recognised union leadership and should be paid as though they had worked. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 46][Para 84] Ms T was on an indefinite-duration contract earning a basic wage of USD 61, a function allowance of USD 69 and seniority bonus of USD 11, about USD 150–160 in all; Mr V earned USD 61 plus USD 45 and USD 11 and a USD 5 health allowance; Ms R earned USD 61 plus the USD 5 health allowance. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49][Para 51][Para 53]

**Employer (respondent):** Award 238/12 of 25 January 2013 already decided this claim for the same 89 workers, the three included, at its issue 1; that award rejected wages for the strike period 9 August to 19 October 2012 and treated the employment as severed from 10 October 2012. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 56][Para 61]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both periods of the back-wage claim were refused, and the award on this rights dispute becomes final immediately and binds the parties under the Memorandum of Understanding of 3 October 2012. [Para 94][Para 95][Para 96]

The panel first classified the claim. Because it concerned wages and the employment contract as regulated by the Labour Law, it was a rights dispute, not an interest dispute, so the panel had to decide it rather than decline it for want of representativeness. [Para 62][Para 67][Para 68] It then split the claim into two periods, 9 August to 19 October 2012 and 19 October 2012 to 25 January 2013. [Para 69]

For the first period the panel applied res judicata. Where the same parties bring the same issue back to a tribunal that has already decided it, the tribunal may refuse to hear it, and the panel found no statutory provision to the contrary in the Council's procedure. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 75] The three conditions were met: the parties had appeared on this issue before, the issue had been brought to the Council, and the Council had decided it fully — Award 238/12, issue 2, had expressly rejected the claim to wages during the strike from 9 August to 19 October 2012, for the same 89 workers including these three. [Para 76][Para 79][Para 80][Para 81][Para 82] The purpose, following Awards 42/07, 68/09 Sinomek 2 and 32/12 Double Apparel, was to avoid contradictory outcomes and to bring the dispute to a single final decision. [Para 77][Para 78]

For the second period the panel held that the employment relationship had been severed on 10 October 2012, as Award 238/12 had found, so from that date the leaders performed no work for the employer. [Para 61][Para 91] Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 664 of the Civil Code both define the employment contract as work exchanged for a wage, so where no labour is supplied no wage is owed; the claim for 19 October 2012 to 25 January 2013 therefore failed. [Para 85][Para 87][Para 90][Para 92][Para 93]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim already decided by the Council between the same parties on the same issue will not be heard again: res judicata applies where the parties litigated the issue before, it was brought to the Council, and the Council decided it fully, and the panel will decline it even where the claimant reframes the period claimed. [Para 73][Para 76][Para 81]

Wages are owed only for labour actually supplied: once the employment relationship is severed, a union leader who performs no work for the employer cannot claim a wage for that period under Article 65 of the Labour Law or Article 664 of the Civil Code. [Para 90][Para 92]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's statement of the purpose of res judicata — avoiding contradictory outcomes and securing one final decision — is a general justification rather than part of the operative ruling. [Para 77]

## 6. Source defects disclosed

- Workforce is 96 in the conciliation report but 92 according to the employer at the hearing; the panel preferred the later figure [Para 38][Para 39].
- The non-conciliation report No. 346 is dated 14 March 2013 at [Para 6] and at [Para 32], but 11 March 2013 at [Para 11].
- The panel's constitution date reads "18 March 2023 2013" [Para 12].
- "Two periods" is printed as "20 stages" at [Para 69], and a stray "40" precedes the second-period claim at [Para 83].
- The sentence at [Para 64] breaks off unfinished ("the Arbitration Council in this case ."), and [Para 96] repeats "will become final immediately" twice.
- The three leaders' names are rendered inconsistently ("Timara"/"Ti Mara", "Run Sothea Ren" split across lines); they are anonymised here as Ms T, Mr V and Ms R.
