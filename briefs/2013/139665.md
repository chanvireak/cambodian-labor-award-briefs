# Award No. 50/13 — Can Sport Shoes Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 10 April 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mr I Sothy (chosen by the employer), Mr T Si Phan (chosen by the workers), chair Mr P Bun Chea · Doc ID 139665

## 1. Facts
Can Sport Shoes Co., Ltd, a shoe-sewing factory at Chumkar Svay village, Sethey commune, Samaki Meanchey district, Kampong Chhnang, employed about 3,930 workers. [Para 1][Para 71] The Khmer Youth Union Federation at the factory, registered under certificate No. 2447 and with its new leadership recognised by Ministry letter No. 1243 of 23 October 2012, was the claimant but was not the most representative union in the enterprise. [Para 73][Para 74] Of 21 demands raised in the collective dispute, conciliation on 28 February 2013 settled 7 and left 14 unresolved; four were then withdrawn and two remained for hearing: the timing of the overtime meal allowance and the employer's refusal to take back the union president, Ms S, whose fixed-duration contracts had run 25 months in total. [Para 22][Para 27][Para 28][Para 102]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the overtime meal allowance once a week, every Monday, to workers who work overtime, rather than once a month on the 10th? [Para 75][Para 112]
2. Whether the employer must take back Ms S, president of the Khmer Youth Union Federation at the factory, and let her work normally again, her non-renewal being alleged to be union discrimination? [Para 93][Para 95]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Workers who work overtime voluntarily must be given the meal allowance for each day they work overtime, because eating is a daily necessity for health; if the employer will not pay weekly, it should pay daily. [Para 80][Para 81][Para 132] Ms S was removed because of her union role, which is discrimination. [Para 95]
**Employer (respondent):** Weekly or daily payment is impossible: the factory is remote, bank withdrawals are difficult, payroll runs on an ERP system with badge scanning, there are some 4,000 workers, and the established practice is monthly payment on the 10th; the company also provides a free midday meal. [Para 79][Para 82][Para 87][Para 90] Ms S was not dismissed but simply not renewed, on the results of her work evaluation, and other workers were likewise not renewed, so there was no anti-union motive; no labour inspector authorisation was needed. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 103]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer is ordered to pay the overtime meal allowance once a week, every Monday, to workers who work overtime, and to take Ms S back into work; the rights-dispute orders became final immediately and enforceable against the parties under the memorandum of understanding of 3 October 2012. [Para 165][Para 166][Para 167]

On issue 1 the panel first classified the claim as a rights dispute, because the overtime meal allowance is fixed by Ministry Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011, and under Article 312 of the Labour Law the Council decides such disputes according to law. [Para 113] Point 2 of the notification entitles a worker who works overtime at the employer's request to 2,000 riel per day or one free meal, without limiting the number of overtime hours; but the notification says nothing about when the money must be paid. [Para 118][Para 119][Para 120] The panel held that payment must therefore fall within a reasonable period that actually lets the worker buy food for the overtime shift, and that a monthly payment does not do so. [Para 123][Para 138] It followed its earlier awards No. 76/11 and No. 244/12 in treating the free meal as an input to further overtime and a daily necessity the employer must supply. [Para 121][Para 130] The workers' fallback demand for daily payment was treated as a negotiating condition offered to obtain weekly payment and was not decided. [Para 116][Para 117]

On issue 11 the panel applied Article 67 paragraph 2: a fixed-duration contract may not exceed two years and may be renewed so long as each renewal does not exceed two years, and a breach converts the contract into one of undetermined duration. [Para 140][Para 141][Para 142] Ms S had three contracts — a two-month probationary contract from 1 February 2011, a twelve-month contract from 31 March 2011 and an eleven-month contract from 1 April 2012 — totalling 25 months, so her contract had become undetermined duration and ending it was a dismissal, not an expiry. [Para 99][Para 100][Para 101][Para 154] Article 293 requires the labour inspector's consent before a staff delegate is dismissed, and Article 4 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 extends that protection to union leaders. [Para 156][Para 157][Para 158] The employer conceded it had not sought consent, so the dismissal was unlawful and reinstatement was ordered. [Para 162][Para 163][Para 164]

The arbitrator chosen by the employer, Mr I Sothy, appended a dissenting opinion on issue 11, reasoning that the words "dismissal" and "sacking" in Prakas No. 305 do not cover the mere non-renewal of a fixed-duration contract, so no authorisation was required and the employer needed only to notify the labour inspector. [Para 169][Para 173][Para 175][Para 176]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where successive fixed-duration contracts are renewed so that their total duration exceeds two years, the contract becomes one of undetermined duration and its termination is a dismissal; if the worker is a union leader, that dismissal is unlawful without the labour inspector's prior consent and the remedy is reinstatement. A statutory overtime meal allowance must be paid within a period that lets the worker actually buy food for the overtime shift, so monthly payment does not satisfy the entitlement and a weekly cycle is a reasonable substitute. [Para 153][Para 163][Para 138]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that a free meal supplied by the employer serves to sustain further overtime because food is an unavoidable daily need, and that arbitration practice distinguishes rights disputes, decided by law, from interest disputes, decided by equity, citing its awards No. 05/11, No. 13/11 and No. 14/11. [Para 110][Para 111][Para 121]

## 6. Source defects disclosed

All figures are reproduced as printed. [Para 22][Para 26][Para 27][Para 28] The panel itself recorded that the claimant's name is written six inconsistent ways across the file — in the complaint, the conciliation report, the fixed-duration contract, the annual evaluation form, the probationary contract, the expiry notice, the daily evaluation form and the Ministry registration letter — and adopted one spelling; the OCR also loses it in places, and the name is redacted here as Ms S. [Para 93][Para 94] The union registration certificate is dated 26 March 2012 at para 73 but 26 March 2013 at para 44. Ministry letter No. 312 is dated 15 December 2013, after the award date of 10 April 2013. [Para 44][Para 47] The operative paragraph on issue 1 inserts a stray figure ("4") before "once a week" at para 139 while the order at para 165 omits it; both are printed as they stand. [Para 139][Para 165] Page-break OCR debris ("@-@ 59 %", "2014", "CAZ", "CCC") survives in paras 36, 69 and 152, and the award number is printed 50/13 in the header and in the annex title.
