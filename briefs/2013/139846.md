# Award No. 164/13 — N.C.X Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 9 September 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phalha presiding; Ing Sothy, employer nominee; Liv Sovanna, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139846

## 1. Facts
N.C.X Co., Ltd assembles and exports Honda motorcycles at Trapeang Thleung, Chom Chao, Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh, registered in the commercial register on 11 August 1998. [Para 59] It employs about 670 workers, of whom roughly 73 are women. [Para 60] The Khmer Workers' Consciousness Union Federation filed on behalf of 164 workers who delegated authority to it, and its local union at the company, elected on 2 June 2013, had 204 members. [Para 61][Para 63][Para 65] Twelve demands went to conciliation, three were settled at the hearing, and nine remained. [Para 25][Para 26]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must issue fixed-duration contracts of at least one year to all workers? [Para 66]
2. Whether the employer must allow pregnant women one day a month for antenatal checks on full pay, bonus and benefits? [Para 73]
3. Whether the employer must arrange special health checks every three months for the paint-spray and welding sections? [Para 75]
4. Whether the employer must bear responsibility for workers' livelihood when they become incapable of work? [Para 87]
5. Whether the employer must raise every worker's base wage by a further USD 30? [Para 92]
8. Whether the employer must pay a USD 10 monthly skill allowance to the paint-spray and welding sections? [Para 97]
9. Whether the employer must prorate the attendance bonus by the days of authorised unpaid leave taken? [Para 102]
10. Whether the employer must pay arrears of seniority pay under Notification No. 041/11? [Para 110]
12. Whether the employer must build a nursery and breastfeeding room? [Para 115]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** contracts of three to six months are too short and all workers are skilled, so they should get one-year terms; wages of USD 91 a month do not cover living costs; pregnant women lose a day's wage and the whole attendance bonus when they attend antenatal checks; the paint-spray and welding sections are exposed to fumes without adequate eye protection, so they need three-monthly special checks and a separate skill allowance; and the company should pay seniority arrears and provide a nursery. [Para 67][Para 92][Para 97][Para 152][Para 208]
**Employer (respondent):** it uses probationary, fixed-duration and indefinite contracts, fixing the fixed term at three to six months according to the seasonal cycle, which peaks from October to April and falls from May to September, so it cannot offer one-year terms; the base wage was only raised on 23 March 2013 and is topped by a USD 10 attendance bonus, USD 10 travel allowance, USD 5 health allowance, a daily meal and annual profit share; it already pays the USD 10 skill allowance, gives annual health checks, and with only 73 women employees is under the Article 186 threshold for a nursery. [Para 68][Para 70][Para 96][Para 98][Para 116][Para 221]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed in full — every one of the nine remaining demands was refused. [Para 223][Para 231] The award is non-binding and would become final eight days after notification unless a party objected in writing to the Minister. [Para 232]

On Issue 1 the panel held that a labour contract rests on matching offer and acceptance: Article 65 of the Labour Law puts it under the general law of contract, and Articles 664, 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code require a proposal and an acceptance that correspond. [Para 120][Para 122][Para 125][Para 129] Here the employer proposed three- to six-month terms matched to its seasonal production cycle and the workers sought one-year terms, so the two wills did not meet and no obligation to contract for a year could arise. [Para 132][Para 133][Para 134][Para 135]

Issues 2, 5, 9 and 10 turned on the same threshold question. The panel found no provision of the Labour Law, no collective agreement, no internal regulation and no past practice creating the rights claimed, so each demand was an interests dispute rather than a rights dispute. [Para 137][Para 138][Para 182][Para 183][Para 202][Para 210] It then applied Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 SKBY, read with Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY: an award on an interests dispute stands in place of a collective agreement and binds every worker in the enterprise, so only a union with the most representative status — or several unions together with more than half the workforce — may bring one. [Para 139][Para 141][Para 143][Para 145][Para 146] The federation spoke for 164 of about 670 workers and its local union had 204 members, well short of half, so it lacked standing and the panel refused to consider those four demands. [Para 148][Para 150][Para 151]

On Issue 3, Article 247 of the Labour Law requires the ministry responsible for labour to issue a Prakas setting the conditions for special health examinations, and the panel found that no such Prakas has yet been issued; with no collective agreement, internal rule or practice providing for three-monthly checks either, the demand was an interests dispute and was refused. [Para 158][Para 161][Para 162][Para 166] On Issue 4, the workers could not say whether the incapacity they meant arose from a work accident or from retirement; the panel treated these as two distinct social security regimes, work-injury risk and pension, and held that a demand covering "every situation" disclosed no specific claim it could decide. [Para 171][Para 172][Para 173][Para 176][Para 177]

On Issue 8 the panel found the USD 10 already paid to the paint-spray and welding sections is the skill allowance and not, as the workers argued, a "hot-cold" health payment: the workers themselves conceded they were told at interview that the USD 10 was a skill allowance, and the employer had paid it as such for nearly five years. [Para 190][Para 191] Since the demand sought an entitlement already being provided, it was dismissed. [Para 192][Para 193] On Issue 12, Article 186 obliges an enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a breastfeeding room and a nursery, and N.C.X employs only about 73 women, so no such obligation arose. [Para 214][Para 220][Para 221][Para 222]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a demand is classified as an interests dispute because no law, collective agreement, internal regulation or past practice creates the right claimed, the panel will refuse to consider it unless the union bringing it is the most representative union in the enterprise or the unions together represent more than half the workforce, since an award on such a dispute would displace the collective agreement and bind every worker. [Para 143][Para 145][Para 146][Para 150]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that Notification No. 041/11 on seniority payments is confined to the textile, garment and footwear sectors, and that the ministry has not yet issued the Prakas on special health examinations contemplated by Article 247, were not necessary to the outcome, because those two demands had already failed for want of standing. [Para 161][Para 207]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company is named in four ways across the file — "N.C.X", "NCX", "N C X" and "Honda" — and the union is described both as the Khmer Workers' Consciousness Union Federation and as its local at the company, with the 164 claimant workers and the 204 union members used interchangeably in the standing analysis. [Para 1][Para 61][Para 65][Para 148] The wage figure is printed as USD 91 at paras 94 and 178 and as "10" in the reasons at para 179. [Para 94][Para 178][Para 179] The attendance bonus is printed as USD 10 at para 108 and as "USD 1000" at para 198. [Para 108][Para 198] Article 186 is quoted as requiring a breastfeeding room and nursery for enterprises employing "at least one hundred women", but the same provision is rendered elsewhere as barring nurseries for children over "eighteen months" and, in the next quotation, over "18 months". [Para 214][Para 215][Para 217] The president's name appears as "Kong Phalha" in the composition and "Kong Phal" in the signature block, and the operative order at para 231 truncates the nursery demand to "build a nursery and the company". [Para 1][Para 231][Para 233] Individual workers are named in the source; their names are not reproduced here.
