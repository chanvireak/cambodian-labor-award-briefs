# Award No. 26/13 — Hong Va 2 Garment (Arbitration Council, 1 March 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry for the employer party, An Virak for the worker party, Ang Eng Thong chair) · Doc ID 139632

## 1. Facts
Hong Va 2 Garment, a garment factory employing about 1,250 workers, was struck by a dispute over an administrative officer known as Mr D: on 19 January 2013 workers demanded his removal, and on 21 January 2013 leaflets appeared urging that he be kept, after which the employer dismissed him that same day. [Para 53][Para 61][Para 63][Para 65] On 22 January 2013 members of one union collected thumbprints to explain the leaflets, and a confrontation followed with three team leaders who also led the rival "Workers' Development Union" at the factory. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 67][Para 68] The complaining union then demanded that the employer dismiss those three for insulting its president and other workers. [Para 2][Para 56]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must dismiss the three shop stewards and sewing-line team leaders of the Workers' Development Union at Hong Va 2 — its president, vice-president and secretary — because they insulted the president of the rival union and other workers on 22 January 2013? [Para 56][Para 57][Para 140][Para 144]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the three team leaders abused the rival union's president and other workers during the thumbprint collection of 22 January 2013, which has made working together difficult; they had never done so before, and this was the first time. [Para 60][Para 70][Para 71]
**Employer (respondent):** the company has no policy of dismissing the three, and management did not know of any insult because it was working in the upstairs office that day. [Para 3][Para 72] The three themselves denied the accusation at the hearing. [Para 73][Para 165]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the demand that the employer dismiss the three from their jobs was refused, and because the parties chose a binding award on 7 February 2013 it became final and enforceable immediately. [Para 176][Para 177][Para 178]

The panel first classified the claim as a rights dispute, since it concerned the labour contracts of the workers involved and so fell to be decided by law rather than by equity. [Para 143][Para 140] It then held that a labour contract is created by the consent of the worker and the employer — Article 65 of the Labour Law, Article 664 of the Civil Code of 2007 and Article 311 — so only the two parties to a contract may modify or terminate it, and a third party outside the contract cannot. [Para 145][Para 149][Para 152][Para 153][Para 154] The three team leaders each had their own contract with the employer, and the complaining workers were third parties to those contracts. [Para 160][Para 161]

Following its earlier awards, the panel held that workers may demand the dismissal or transfer of another worker only where they can prove that the person is dangerous and cannot be kept in the enterprise without creating real insecurity at work. [Para 155][Para 158][Para 162] On the evidence it could not reach that finding. Four witnesses for the workers said the insults were uttered and four for the employer said they were not; the panel concluded only that a heated argument and confrontation took place, that the words "whoever says they are the company union should come forward" were in fact used, but that there was no sufficient basis to find that the three had insulted other workers. [Para 167][Para 168][Para 169][Para 170] It added that if improper language of that kind was used, the employer ought to take suitable measures to stop it and secure a peaceful workplace, but that this did not justify dismissal. [Para 171] No specific evidence showed the three to be dangerous or a source of insecurity beyond the workers' general difficulty in working with them, so the claim failed. [Para 172][Para 176]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Workers cannot obtain the dismissal of other workers: a labour contract binds only the employer and the worker concerned, so a third party has no standing to demand that it be varied or ended, and the panel will order a dismissal only where specific evidence shows the worker is dangerous and cannot be kept in the enterprise without creating real insecurity at work. Evidence that insults were exchanged during a heated confrontation, disputed between equal numbers of witnesses and not proved, does not meet that threshold; the remedy is for the employer to take suitable measures against improper language. [Para 154][Para 162][Para 169][Para 171]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that, should such improper language have been used, the employer ought to act to preserve peace at the workplace, and its remark that the three had never previously insulted anyone, were not necessary to the refusal. [Para 70][Para 171]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The claim as recorded in the conciliation report is garbled: the union whose three leaders were to be dismissed is printed as the union "of development and health workers", and the sentence breaks off mid-name, so only the fact that three named women were targeted can be recovered. [Para 2][Para 56]
- The award is dated 1 March 2013 [Para 1], consistent with the extension from 15 February to 1 March 2013 [Para 19], but the hearings are given as 7 and 14 February 2013 [Para 8] while the first hearing is also said to have been held on 7 February with the binding-award agreement signed the same day [Para 51][Para 178].
- The incident date is 22 January 2013 throughout the reasons [Para 60][Para 68][Para 103], yet the workers' own complaint, dated 24 January 2013, describes the insults as occurring on 24 January 2013 [Para 32], and one witness said she could not remember the date [Para 88].
- The registration numbers of the two unions are printed inconsistently, 2712 K/B and 2712 K/VK for the same certificate [Para 21][Para 36], and the rival union's certificate appears as 1473 of 16 July 2008 [Para 24][Para 42].
- Individual workers, team leaders and union officials are named in the record; they are given here by initial, and one quoted insult is left untranslated because the OCR renders it unintelligibly. [Para 69][Para 104]
