# Award No. 149/13 — CIIDG Erdos Hongjun Electric Power Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 01 August 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ma Samborana for the employer, An Non for the workers, chair Tang Tri) · Doc ID 139835

## 1. Facts
CIIDG Erdos Hongjun Electric Power Co., Ltd, which builds coal-fired power plants at Kampenh commune, Stung Hav district, Preah Sihanouk, employed about 326 staff, including two cohorts of trainees (សិក្ខាកាម), and had no union. [Para 37] [Para 38] [Para 41] Two trainees, Mr C and Mr Y, were the claimants; they had been sent to a university in China under training-scholarship agreements, with tuition and wages paid by the employer. [Para 42] [Para 45] [Para 46] Of three demands, one was settled at conciliation; at the hearing the trainees withdrew point 2 and Mr Y confirmed he pursued nothing further, leaving point 1, which concerned Mr C's exclusion from classes. [Para 10] [Para 15] [Para 43]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Panel's issue 1) Whether the company must stop confining Mr C to a room and let him attend training again, and, failing that, terminate his contract according to law without requiring him to repay past training costs? [Para 2] [Para 44]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that from 27 June to 22 July 2013 the company refused to let Mr C into class and kept him in a room, although he had freedom to come and go outside class hours; that he asked the section head three times (28 June, 29 June and 09 July) to be readmitted and was refused; that he had given up a mid-level electrical course at NTTI and an electrician's job to take the scholarship, cannot catch up with classmates, and the teaching is in Chinese; and that the company breached point 9 of the scholarship agreement, which requires disputes to be settled by consultation rather than by barring him from study. He asked to withdraw from the training from 22 July 2013 and to be excused from repaying the training costs, claiming no other compensation. [Para 47] [Para 54] [Para 56] [Para 57] [Para 58] [Para 59] [Para 60] [Para 62] [Para 66]

**Employer (respondent):** that it had disciplined him for talking on the telephone during class and knocking on classroom doors while others were studying, and had housed him in a separate room because there was nowhere else; that it now agrees to readmit him to training provided he respects the internal rules and management's instructions like other trainees; that a unilateral withdrawal entitles it to claim compensation for the real cost of his study, which it reserves, the decision to sue being for later; and that it does not waive that right lest other trainees follow suit in a sector only now beginning in Cambodia. It also objected that the word "confining" (បង្ខាំង) denotes a criminal offence and could damage its reputation. [Para 48] [Para 51] [Para 52] [Para 53] [Para 63] [Para 64] [Para 65]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider the remaining demand, holding it a dispute about the future; the award is non-binding. [Para 80] [Para 81] [Para 82]

The panel first recorded that Mr C had replaced the word "confining" with "penalty of not being allowed into class", having not understood the term's meaning, and that he accepted he was free to enter and leave the room outside class hours. [Para 49] [Para 50] It then found that the dispute over access to training no longer existed: the employer stated at the hearing that he might return subject to conditions, and he did not wish to return. [Para 69]

On the withdrawal, the panel held that leaving work or training requires an administrative step — a written resignation to the employer so that the administration can settle terminal payments and the fact of departure can be evidenced, as Mr Y had done — and that Mr C had not submitted any written resignation. [Para 71] [Para 72] The legal relationship created by the scholarship agreement of 30 March 2013 was therefore not terminated, and no formal withdrawal had occurred. [Para 70] [Para 72]

Because of that, the panel characterised the demand as a dispute about the future: while no claim by the employer exists, no dispute has arisen, and the panel, following Awards 10/03-Zak Sin Tech, 68/04-CTY Yew, 14/06-Seng Yong, 36/06-Mondopich and 58/07-8 Star Sport Wear, is constituted to resolve labour disputes and not problems that have not yet arisen. [Para 73] [Para 75] [Para 76] [Para 77] [Para 78] The same applied to the demand that the employer be barred from claiming training costs, which depended on a claim the company had not yet decided to bring. [Para 74] [Para 80]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The panel will not decide a dispute that has not yet arisen: where a trainee has announced at the hearing that he wishes to leave a training programme but has not submitted a written resignation to the employer, the scholarship agreement remains alive, no rights have crystallised, and a demand that the employer be restrained from claiming training costs is premature. A resignation from training, like a resignation from work, takes effect through a written notice to the employer. [Para 71] [Para 72] [Para 77]

**Obiter dicta:** that an employer which agrees to readmit a trainee on condition that he respect the internal rules has removed the subject matter of an exclusion complaint; and that the employer's right to claim the real cost of training on unilateral withdrawal is reserved to it and is not decided here. [Para 69] [Para 74]
