# Award No. 92/13 — SL Garment Processing (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 14 June 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (employer-appointed Seng Vouchhun; worker-appointed An Non; president Mae Nimmith) · Doc ID 139720

## 1. Facts
SL Garment Processing (Cambodia) Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេស អិល ហ្គាមេន ប្រូសេស ស៊ីង), a laundry-services enterprise in Phnom Penh with about 4,800 workers, had changed its registered name from PPS to TW and then to SL, and in 2013 required workers to sign fresh employment contracts. [Para 74][Para 75][Para 82][Para 83] The Khmer Workers' Spirit Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ស្មារតី កម្មករ ខ្មែរ) claimed to represent 367 workers, but its enterprise union held no registration certificate and only four named male workers attended the hearings in person. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 172][Para 178] Thirteen demands went to conciliation; eleven were unresolved, one was settled at the hearing, and ten remained for the panel. [Para 31][Para 36][Para 37]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must terminate the old contracts and pay severance before signing new contracts with the workers? [Para 79]
2. Whether the employer must keep wages, bonuses, special bonus and other benefits unchanged when signing the new contracts? [Para 91]
3. Whether the employer must apply an eight-hour working day under the Labour Law while maintaining wages and the special bonus? [Para 96]
5. Whether the employer must pay an overtime meal allowance of 2,000 riel per hour? [Para 121]
6. Whether the employer must arrange and pay for periodic special health checks? [Para 126]
7. Whether the employer must provide transport or travel costs for the initial health check? [Para 131]
8. Whether the employer must pay USD 25 per month as a milk allowance until the child reaches 18 months? [Para 140]
9. Whether the employer must raise the base wage to USD 120 per month? [Para 147]
10. Whether the employer must provide a pension for workers who can no longer work? [Para 151]
11. Whether the employer must allow women one paid day per month for antenatal checks? [Para 158]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the repeated renamings and re-contracting put their seniority and benefits at risk, so the old contracts should be closed out with severance and the old terms carried into the new contracts; hours, meal, health, milk and wage claims followed from the Labour Law and past practice. [Para 81][Para 84][Para 92][Para 99]
**Employer (respondent):** there was no termination at all — only a change of company name with the same owner and structure, and the new contracts preserved total wages, seniority and benefits; no legal provision or agreement supports the remaining demands. [Para 87][Para 90][Para 210]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — every remaining claim was refused; no payment was ordered. [Para 259]

Threshold: under Article 268 of the Labour Law an organisation acquires legal rights only once registered, and Prakas No. 099 Article 19 lets a party be represented only by a person holding written authority. [Para 166][Para 176] The enterprise union had no registration certificate and the Federation never filed the written transfers of authority it claimed for 367 workers, so only the four workers who attended — Mr C, Mr N, Mr M and Mr S — were claimants, and the award speaks for them alone. [Para 171][Para 177][Para 180]

Issues 1 and 2 were dismissed because the four claimants had already signed the new contracts, so no claimant remained who had not re-contracted, and the claim was in substance one for future termination. [Para 183][Para 188] On issue 3, Articles 137 and 139 (new) of the Labour Law cap ordinary hours at eight a day and 48 a week, but Prakas No. 80/99 makes overtime voluntary; the new contracts already specify eight hours and overtime is paid at the statutory rate, with no evidence of coercion. [Para 191][Para 196][Para 203] Special bonus was refused because Articles 65(1) and 311 of the Civil Code leave modification of contractual obligations to the parties' will, and the employer did not consent. [Para 210]

Issues 5, 6, 9 and 10 were classified as interest disputes, which the panel may decide only where the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise; four individual workers lack that standing, so the claims were refused unexamined. [Para 215][Para 224][Para 227] Issues 7, 8 and 11 failed for want of a claimant with the relevant characteristic — the four are men who had already had their initial health checks, and none was pregnant or returning from maternity leave. [Para 239][Para 243][Para 256]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that has not obtained its registration certificate cannot lawfully represent workers before the panel, and a federation that fails to file the written transfers of authority it invokes represents nobody; only the workers who appear and authorise representation are parties, and relief is confined to them. A claim that presupposes a termination the employer has not made, or that seeks to rewrite a contract the employer has not consented to vary, fails. [Para 177][Para 180][Para 210]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's view that interest disputes require a most-representative union, and that an award on such a dispute would replace the collective agreement for a year and remove the right to strike on that subject [Para 223]; the observation that overtime must remain voluntary [Para 199].

[Para 26][Para 32][Para 41] Worker counts are unstable: about 4,800 employees, 367 claimed represented, four claimants — and Para 267 speaks of "20 workers". [Para 75][Para 180][Para 267] Hours are printed as 8, 10, 10.5, 12 and once as "8 8 hours", and the new-contract clause is described as both 8 and 10 hours per day. [Para 200][Para 203] Paragraphs 250 and 267 are heavily duplicated by OCR.
