# Award No. 186/13 — LC Garments (Arbitration Council, 19 September 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom, chosen by the employer party; Liv Sovanna, chosen by the worker party; Kong Phalla, chair) · Doc ID 139858

## 1. Facts
LC Garments, a garment factory at Phnom Penh Thmey, Khan Sen Sok, employed 250 workers. [Para 1][Para 39] After a strike the Cambodian Workers' Union of the LC Garments factory — elected on 25 July 2013 but not yet registered by the Ministry of Labour — put 13 demands. [Para 21][Para 45][Para 46][Para 58] Ten were settled at the hearing of 9 September 2013 and two were withdrawn, leaving the attendance-bonus claim and the calculation of maternity allowance. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 28] The employer paid a USD 10 monthly attendance bonus but withheld it entirely in any month a worker took annual leave, special leave or certified sick leave [Para 49][Para 50], and calculated maternity pay on the base wage rather than the average wage. [Para 123][Para 131]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must deduct the attendance bonus only in proportion to the days a worker is absent on annual leave, special leave or certified sick leave, rather than withholding it entirely? [Para 78]
2. Whether the employer must calculate the maternity allowance on the average monthly wage rather than the base wage? [Para 122]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** annual leave, special leave charged against annual leave and sick leave on a proper medical certificate are all leave the law allows and the employer authorises, so the bonus should be reduced only in step with the days taken; maternity pay should be the average of the last 12 months' wages divided by twelve. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 51][Para 55]
**Employer (respondent):** in any month a worker takes annual, special or certified sick leave the bonus is not paid at all, and maternity pay is computed on the monthly base wage; it could not grant either demand. [Para 50][Para 52][Para 56][Para 57]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer must keep the full USD 10 attendance bonus while a worker takes annual leave or special leave charged against annual leave, must deduct it only in proportion to days of certified sick leave, and must stop computing maternity allowance on the base wage and use the average monthly wage instead. [Para 135][Para 136]

Issue 8: the panel first settled who was before it. Under Article 268 of the Labour Law a professional organisation acquires the rights the law recognises only when the Ministry registers it, and the LC Garments union had no registration; its list of thumbprints was undated and transferred no right of representation, so it was not a written authorisation under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 59][Para 61][Para 68][Para 70][Para 71][Para 72][Para 73] Only the three workers who had signed a letter of 16 August 2013 asking the confederation for help, and who attended the hearing, were claimants. [Para 74][Para 75][Para 76] On the merits, Article 103 makes a bonus part of wages, and the USD 10 bonus in point 2 of Notice No. 230 of 25 July 2012 is a general entitlement. [Para 81][Para 82] Article 166(1) gives paid annual leave and Article 171 lets special leave be charged against annual leave, so withholding the whole bonus for using those rights breaches Article 166. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 88][Para 90][Para 91] For certified sick leave the panel weighed the incentive purpose of the bonus against Article 72 of the Constitution and Article 12 of the ICESCR, and held that total forfeiture would press sick workers to attend and so defeat the bonus's own purpose; a pro-rata deduction was the balanced solution. [Para 105][Para 114][Para 116][Para 118][Para 120][Para 121]

Issue 9/b: Articles 182 and 183 give 90 days' maternity leave on half wage including ancillary pay. [Para 124][Para 125][Para 126] Following earlier awards, the panel held maternity pay must be computed on the average monthly wage of the 12 months before the leave — summed and divided by twelve — then halved for the 90 days. [Para 127][Para 130] Computing it on the base wage alone deprives the worker of a benefit, so the practice was ordered stopped. [Para 132][Para 133][Para 134]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union not yet registered under Article 268 has no legal capacity to represent its members before the panel, and an undated list of thumbprints that says nothing about transferring representation is not the written authorisation Prakas No. 099 Article 19 requires; the panel will decide only for the individual workers who authorised the claim in writing and appeared. [Para 61][Para 70][Para 73] Because an attendance bonus is wages, leave the law grants — annual leave and special leave charged against it — cannot cost the worker the bonus, and certified sick leave may reduce it only in proportion to the days lost. [Para 82][Para 88][Para 120] Maternity allowance is computed on the average monthly wage of the preceding 12 months, not the base wage. [Para 127][Para 134]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's discussion of Article 72 of the Constitution, the Universal Declaration and the ICESCR in support of a pro-rata rule for sick leave, and its remarks on the productivity cost of forcing sick workers to attend, are policy reasoning beyond what the order required. [Para 112][Para 114][Para 118]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The panel says it will decide for "300 workers" where the same paragraph and the evidence establish three individual claimants; 300 is inconsistent with the finding of 250 employees. [Para 77][Para 39][Para 76]
- The number represented is given as 120 workers at [Para 40] and as 12,000 at [Para 65], in otherwise identical passages.
- The bonus is described as USD 10 "per month" at [Para 49] and "per year month" at [Para 79]; an incomplete sentence stands alone at [Para 93].
- The panel is said to have been constituted "2015 on 30 August 2013" [Para 23], and the employer-side arbitrator is "ឈីវ ភិរម្យ" on the face but "ឈីវ ត៌រម្យ" in the signature block. [Para 138]
- Article 183 is quoted as referring to "Article 11" instead of Article 182, and a block of untranslatable English text interrupts the reasoning at [Para 62]. [Para 125]
- The company is printed as "ក្រុមហ៊ុន អិល ស៊ី ហ្គាមេន", "អ៊ិល ស៊ី ហ្គាមេន", "LC GARMENTS" and "LC GARMENT".
