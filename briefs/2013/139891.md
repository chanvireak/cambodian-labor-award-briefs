# Award No. 232/13 — Dik Neathy Nit Ther Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 13 December 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 139891

## 1. Facts
Dik Neathy Nit Ther Co., Ltd, a garment factory at Prek Ta Pring, Sitbo commune, Sa'ang district, Kandal, employing about 1,975 workers, filed the complaint itself; the respondent was the C.CAWDU union at the factory's Ri Verich branch. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47]
The dispute concerned the piece rate ("lot", ឡូ) paid for four production series: 5044 in the cuff-cutting section and 9052, 10014 and 10010 in the sewing section. [Para 48]
Conciliation at the Kandal provincial labour department failed on 24 October 2013, and the parties agreed in the hearing of 13 November 2013 that the award would be binding. [Para 4][Para 141]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise the rate for series 5044 (cuff-cutting) from USD 2 to USD 3 per lot, on the basis of the workers' own time tests? [Para 102][Para 103]
2. Whether the employer must raise the rates for series 9052, 10014 and 10010 (sewing) by applying an oral agreement the workers say they concluded with the former section head? [Para 117]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (respondent):** the section head promised USD 3.50 per lot for 5044 after a trial, then the employer paid only USD 2; for the sewing series, the old section head had agreed rates scaled by panel length and count, and the 48-panel rate was cut to 35 cents. [Para 56][Para 69][Para 88]
**Employer (claimant):** the 3.50 figure came from a newly appointed section head who had no authority and whose proposal was never signed; cross-factory verification showed 10 minutes per shirt, not the 37 minutes claimed. [Para 81][Para 82][Para 83]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both claims rejected; the award is binding and final as of issuance. [Para 139][Para 140][Para 141]
The panel first classified both claims as rights disputes (វិវាទអំពីសិទ្ធិ) rather than interest disputes, because they concerned the method of fixing piece rates and therefore the wages workers were entitled to receive; rights disputes are decided according to law, collective agreements, internal regulations and past practice. [Para 100][Para 102][Para 115][Para 116]
On series 5044, the panel accepted that rates are normally set by the section head after time-testing, but held that the party asserting a claim carries the burden of proof; it ordered the workers to produce evidence that the rate was so fixed, they produced none, and the claim was therefore rejected. [Para 103][Para 105][Para 108][Para 109][Para 114]
On the sewing series, the panel went further in the workers' favour on principle: even if an oral agreement with the former section head existed, the employer could not plead ignorance, since a supervisor's unauthorised dealing is a gap in the employer's own exercise of its management right under Article 2 of the Labour Law, and the employer must define and communicate the limits of a section head's authority. [Para 117][Para 120][Para 124][Para 126][Para 129]
That reasoning did not save the claim: the panel again placed the burden on the claimant, noted that the workers produced neither document nor witness to prove the agreement's content when ordered to, and dismissed. [Para 130][Para 134][Para 135][Para 138]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker claiming a higher piece rate on the strength of an unwritten arrangement with a production supervisor need not prove that the employer authorised the arrangement — the employer cannot escape its supervisor's commitments by denying knowledge, because that is a gap in its own management — but the worker must still prove that the arrangement was made and what rate it set; absent that proof the claim fails. [Para 129][Para 134]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that rates are ordinarily fixed by the section head following a time test, and its observation that it follows the approach taken in Awards 79/05, 101/08, 168/09, 115/10 and 148/11 on burden of proof, were not necessary to the dismissal. [Para 103][Para 106]
