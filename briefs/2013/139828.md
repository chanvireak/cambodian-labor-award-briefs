# Award No. 142/13 — D & L Ultimate (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 31 July 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Kong Pollak chair) · Doc ID 139828

## 1. Facts
D & L Ultimate (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a garment factory at Anlong Kong, Prey Sa, Dangkao, Phnom Penh, employing about 530 workers. [Para 52][Para 53] Mr P worked under a two-month probation contract from 14 January 2013 and then a two-month fixed-duration contract from 14 March to 14 May 2013 at a base wage of 68 USD a month, and was dismissed on 23 April 2013. [Para 56] Of 15 demands, 11 were settled in conciliation and four reached the panel; at the hearing the parties removed the claim for two other workers and a fourth issue, leaving three. [Para 22][Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr P, a candidate in the forthcoming union leadership election, and pay him wages from the date of dismissal to the date of return? [Para 56]
2. Whether the employer must pay monthly wages on the 5th of each month, or on the 4th where the 5th falls on a Sunday or public holiday? [Para 77]
3. Whether the employer must maintain wages, the 10 USD attendance bonus and the 15 USD incentive bonus during the strike of 24 April to 2 May 2013? [Para 83]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissal came after the federation notified the employer on 29 March 2013 of the union election and the candidates, and the employer refused to accept the notice; the termination was therefore anti-union and without any legal basis. [Para 72][Para 73]
**Employer (respondent):** on 9 and 10 April 2013 Mr P shouted to production workers that the director had no money for wages and had fled to Korea, which caused a stoppage and disorder, and this was serious misconduct under Article 83(b)(5) as incitement of other workers to serious misconduct. [Para 58][Para 61]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — reinstatement and back pay were refused, and the wage-date and strike-bonus claims were refused for want of an identified claimant. The panel nonetheless found the dismissal unjustified, and refused relief only because the employer had already paid the full statutory package. [Para 173][Para 178][Para 182]

On issue 1 the panel took three questions in turn. First, candidate protection: Article 293 and Articles 3 and 4 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 protect a union-election candidate from dismissal as if he were a staff delegate, for 45 days before and 45 days after the poll [Para 102][Para 106]; but the notice of 29 March 2013 named no election date and no election had been held by the hearing, so following Award No. 104/12 the protection never attached. [Para 111][Para 114]

Second, union discrimination: the accuser bears the burden, and although the dismissal came almost a month after the election notice, the workers advanced no evidence that union activity was the reason, so no discrimination was established. [Para 128]

Third, serious misconduct: the stoppage on 10 April 2013 met the Article 318 definition of a strike, so urging workers to stop work was urging a strike, and Article 83(b)(5) does not reach the urging or calling of a strike even where the strike is procedurally unlawful — it reaches only incitement to violence, which the employer did not prove. [Para 137][Para 139][Para 140] The dismissal was therefore not justified. [Para 145] The panel then found the employer had nevertheless paid the 5% termination indemnity, damages equal to wages to the end of the contract, unused annual leave, the last wage and a notice payment, and that on a two-month contract no notice was even required, so the payments were more favourable than the law. [Para 150][Para 171][Para 172] Reinstatement and back pay were refused on that footing. [Para 173]

On issues 2 and 3, only Mr P had attended the hearing and he had ceased to be an employee on 23 April 2013, so neither claim had a claimant in the case; both were refused without consideration. [Para 175][Para 177][Para 181]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Protection of a union-election candidate under Article 293 and Prakas No. 305 attaches only once a definite election date is fixed and notified; a notice announcing an election without a date, and an election never held, confer no protection, and the dismissal is then judged on ordinary grounds. [Para 113] Urging workers to stop work is urging a strike, not incitement to serious misconduct under Article 83(b)(5): even an unlawful strike is not serious misconduct but only a defect of form that costs the workers certain statutory benefits during the strike, and Article 83(b)(5) bites only on proof of incitement to violence. [Para 139][Para 140] Where the employer has already paid the full statutory termination package on a fixed-duration contract, the panel will refuse reinstatement and back pay even though the dismissal was unjustified. [Para 172]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that the employer's notice payment exceeded what the law required on a two-month contract [Para 171], and its observation that an unlawful strike merely deprives workers of some benefits during the strike, were not necessary to the refusal of relief, which rested on the payments already made.

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The panel is said to have been constituted on "05 July 2020 2013" [Para 19], the conciliation is dated "25 December June 2013" [Para 17], and Prakas No. 305 is dated "22 November 2023 2001" [Para 113].
- The delegation letter of 24 April 2013 is described as covering 57 workers [Para 98] but the panel held the delegation invalid and proceeded for one worker only [Para 100][Para 101], so the number of claimants is inconsistent across the award.
- Four issues reached the panel but the parties removed part of issue 1 and all of issue 4 at the hearing [Para 22], so the decided numbering runs 1, 2, 3 over what were originally different items.
- The operative order prints the wage date as "the 22nd, 05" [Para 184], an OCR corruption of the 5th of each month.
- The award is expressly non-binding, becoming final eight days after notification if no party objects [Para 186].
- The claimant's name is printed in two forms across the reasoning and the orders, sometimes truncated to a single element, and the company appears as both "D & L" and "D and L"; individuals are redacted in this brief as required.
