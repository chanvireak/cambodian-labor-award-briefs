# Award No. 57/13 — Khin Nitich International (Cambodia) Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 5 April 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tai Seng for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139677

## 1. Facts
Khin Nitich International (Cambodia) Co Ltd is a garment factory at Stung Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employing about 1,309 workers. [Para 39][Para 40] The Samrithi Khmer Workers' Union at the company, registered 13 December 2011 with about 400 members, filed 11 claims to improve working conditions; conciliation resolved none of 6 disputed points, and the parties settled claim 6 (work certificates) at the hearing, leaving five for the panel. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 26][Para 27][Para 43] At the date of the award no union at the factory was most representative. [Para 41]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to raise the basic wage of workers from USD 61 to between USD 61 and USD 150 per month? [Para 81]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to provide one midday meal, or its equivalent of 5,000 riel per meal, to each worker? [Para 103]
3. Whether the employer is obliged to deduct the regular attendance bonus (luy prim) pro rata to the days a worker was absent with the employer's lawful permission? [Para 107]
4. Whether the employer is obliged to raise the transport and accommodation allowance to a full USD 15 per month? [Para 147]
5. Whether the employer is obliged to raise the basic wage of every team leader by USD 30 above the wage of an ordinary worker? [Para 155]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the USD 61 basic wage is below the cost of living while other factories in Kampong Speu, Takeo and elsewhere pay USD 70–80; the wage is too low for workers to buy adequate food, and rents and fuel costs are rising. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 50][Para 68][Para 70] On the attendance bonus, they ask only that any deduction match the days of permitted absence, because the employer currently cancels the whole USD 10. [Para 54][Para 62]

**Employer (respondent):** the company is unable to meet any of the claims. [Para 48][Para 53][Para 73] It already pays the USD 61 minimum, a USD 33 monthly role allowance to team leaders and USD 7 transport and accommodation. [Para 12][Para 65][Para 74] On attendance, it wants workers present daily to sustain production, since 90 to 110 are absent each day with or without permission, and it deducts pro rata only past one and a half days. [Para 7][Para 8][Para 63]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — claims 1, 2, 4 and 5 were not considered; on claim 3 the panel ordered the employer to deduct the regular attendance bonus pro rata to the number of days a worker was absent with the employer's permission. [Para 164][Para 165][Para 166][Para 167][Para 168]

On the four wage-and-benefit claims the panel held them to be interest disputes, since each demand exceeded what the Labour Law or any collective agreement or notification granted. [Para 88][Para 104][Para 152][Para 161] Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 reserve the right to conclude a collective agreement to the most representative union, and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 makes an award on an interest dispute stand as a collective agreement binding all workers for one year. [Para 89][Para 91][Para 93][Para 94] Following Awards 81/04 Evergreen, 98/04 Great Union, 169/11 Horizon, 02/11 Puyean and 66/11 Inhansung, the panel could decide an interest dispute only if the claimant union were most representative or unions together held over half the workforce; this union held about 400 of 1,309 and was not, so the claims were declined. [Para 95][Para 96][Para 97][Para 100][Para 101]

On claim 3 the panel found Article 71(6) and Article 72(1) of the Labour Law made leave authorised by the employer a suspension of the contract, so no wage was owed for those days and no full attendance bonus either; Article 103 treats a bonus as wage. [Para 140][Para 141][Para 143][Para 137] The panel could not interpret "without absence" in point 2 of Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012, which the Labour Advisory Committee had left undefined, and therefore decided the case on its facts: the employer may deduct, but only in proportion to the days absent, not the whole USD 10. [Para 112][Para 115][Para 116][Para 120][Para 145][Para 146]

Arbitrator Ly Tai Seng dissented on claim 3, and would have dismissed it: a worker absent with permission has not attended regularly without absence, so the bonus condition in Notification No. 230 is unmet and the panel should refuse the claim. [Para 173][Para 175][Para 183][Para 195]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that is not the most representative in the enterprise, and does not with others represent more than half the workforce, cannot bring an interest dispute to the panel: because an award on such a dispute replaces the collective agreement for a year and binds every worker, the panel must decline to consider the claim rather than fix the term itself. [Para 94][Para 95][Para 100]

Where the employer has authorised a worker's absence, the employment contract is suspended under Articles 71(6) and 72(1); the employer owes no wage for those days and need not pay the attendance bonus in full, but may deduct it only in proportion to the days actually absent. [Para 143][Para 144][Para 145]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that bonuses take many forms and that their amount is for each enterprise owner to set as an incentive, and its refusal to construe "without absence" pending authoritative guidance, are general observations going beyond the order on claim 3. [Para 111][Para 115][Para 120]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The panel declines four interest claims for want of representativeness at [Para 100]–[Para 101], yet decides the fifth, equally an interest claim, on the merits at [Para 146]; the award does not reconcile the two courses.
- The company's registration is dated twice: certificate No. 1247 of 28 February 2012 at [Para 29], and No. Inv. 667 E/1999 of 21 December 1999 at [Para 39].
- The current wage is said to have been paid "in 2019" [Para 44], which cannot be right for a 2013 award; the year appears to have been mis-OCR'd.
- Figures are garbled: "USD 610" for USD 61 at [Para 81], "USD 150 month dollars" at [Para 102], and a stray "20" after USD 7 at [Para 149].
- The panel's constitution date reads "14 March 2-3-2013" [Para 23]; the company name appears as "Khin Ngen Sinnol" at [Para 84] and in several other spellings elsewhere.
- Claim 6, on work certificates, appears in the list of demands at [Para 13] but was settled at the hearing and is not decided [Para 26]–[Para 27].
