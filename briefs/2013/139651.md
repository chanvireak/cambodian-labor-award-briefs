# Award No. 38/13 — Kbal Koh Gamen Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 11 March 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer, dissenting; Tun Si Phan, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 139651

## 1. Facts
Kbal Koh Gamen Co., Ltd has sewn garments on National Road 1 at Kbal Koh, Mean Chey, Phnom Penh since 2001 and employs about 970 workers. Two plant unions filed the demand — the Cambodia National Workers' Federation union, claiming some 470 members and lapsed most-representative status, and the C.CAWDU union, claiming about 120 — but neither produced its registration certificate despite an order to file by 26 February 2013. A 13-point demand followed a strike; conciliation on 5 February 2013 left eight points, four of which the union withdrew, leaving points 1, 3, 4 and 7 for the hearing of 20 February 2013. [Para 22][Para 24][Para 28][Para 29][Para 35][Para 66][Para 133][Para 134]
Only nine workers attended, and none held written authority to represent the others, so the panel resolved the issues for those nine alone. [Para 43][Para 45][Para 46]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a midday meal allowance of 2,000 riel a day? [Para 1][Para 70]
3. Whether the employer must pro-rate the USD 10 attendance bonus by the days of authorised absence, instead of deducting USD 5 for one day and everything from two days? [Para 6][Para 78][Para 143]
4. Whether the employer must pay a USD 10 monthly incentive bonus to the workers who issue materials to each team? [Para 8][Para 91][Para 182]
7. Whether the employer must pay an incentive bonus to the workers of the cutting-table teams, as it does for the sewing teams? [Para 14][Para 104][Para 192]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the factory has operated since 2001, earns well and has opened a laundry subsidiary, so it should pay 2,000 riel for lunch; other factories such as Huy Jian pay 1,000 riel; prices have risen and wages barely cover living costs; the material-issuing workers act as team-leader deputies and carry a heavy load for a USD 61 basic wage while team leaders get USD 15–20; cutting-table workers should share the 500–2,000 riel incentive the sewing teams receive; the attendance bonus should be cut in proportion to authorised days off. [Para 71][Para 73][Para 96][Para 100][Para 108][Para 81]
**Employer (respondent):** it has no capacity to pay a lunch allowance and would do so only if a Prakas required it; there is no post of team-leader deputy in the company, only material-issuing workers whose role and benefits were fixed on hiring; the incentive is paid only to sewing teams and team leaders; a written agreement of 7 December 2012 with the national union fixed USD 5 for one authorised day and nothing beyond, and the panel should not rewrite it. [Para 76][Para 92][Para 103][Para 110][Para 85]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must pro-rate the attendance bonus by the days of authorised absence for the nine claimant workers; the meal allowance and both incentive claims were refused. The award is non-binding and becomes final after 8 days absent written objection to the Minister. [Para 201][Para 202][Para 203][Para 204][Para 205]
On point 1 the panel found no provision in the Labour Law, the internal regulations of 30 July 2011 registered on 11 October 2011, or any other instrument requiring a lunch allowance, and classified the claim as an interest dispute. Although the two unions' combined membership would exceed half of the 970 workers, neither proved registration or membership, so the nine claimants had no standing to bring an interest claim and the panel refused to consider it. [Para 122][Para 123][Para 125][Para 139][Para 141][Para 142]
Point 3 was a rights dispute. The panel treated the attendance bonus as wages under Article 103 and held that the Prakas 230/12 condition "without absence" cannot be read to cover every kind of absence, since lawful absences — annual, special, maternity, public-holiday and weekly rest — do not permit any deduction at all, while authorised personal leave suspends the contract under Articles 71(6) and 72(1), so the employer owes neither wage nor bonus for those days but may not cancel the whole bonus. It ordered pro-rating by days of authorised absence. [Para 148][Para 154][Para 156][Para 173][Para 179][Para 181]
Points 4 and 7 were interest disputes: nothing in the law, the collective agreement or past practice required an incentive for material-issuing or cutting-table workers, and the employer pays it only to sewing teams and team leaders. With no standing proved, both claims were refused without reaching their merits. [Para 188][Para 189][Para 196][Para 197][Para 199]
Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented on point 3, reasoning from the dictionary and French-law definitions of "prime" that a bonus is a voluntary extra granted on conditions set by its owner, so a worker who cannot meet the attendance condition has no enforceable right to a pro-rated share. [Para 207][Para 212][Para 217][Para 227]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus is wages, and the condition "without absence" in Prakas 230/12 does not let an employer cancel it for absences the law itself authorises; where leave is granted at the worker's request, the contract is suspended, so the employer deducts only in proportion to the days actually missed and may not forfeit the whole bonus. A claim for a new allowance or incentive that no law, agreement or practice supports is an interest dispute, which a union that has not proved its registration and majority cannot bring. [Para 173][Para 179][Para 181][Para 139]

**Obiter dicta:** that the panel would sit only for the nine workers present in person, since none held written authority to represent the others, as Prakas 099 Article 19 requires. [Para 42][Para 45][Para 46]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company's registration and the union's status are both garbled: the internal regulations are dated "30 July 2019 2011" [Para 122]. The operative order on point 3 carries a stray year inside the sentence fixing the pro-rata calculation ("the number of days ... 2014") [Para 202]. Several paragraphs are doubled, e.g. [Para 117] and [Para 118]. The panel's statement of the parties' combined membership (470 plus 120, i.e. 590 of 970) is set out at [Para 135] but contradicted by its later finding that membership was never proved [Para 139]. Signature lines are partly OCR noise [Para 206]. The union names appear in several spellings ("សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ជាតិ កម្មករ កម្ពុជា" and its variants), so only the generic descriptions are used here.
