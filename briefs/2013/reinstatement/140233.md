# Award No. 255/12 — Te Yong (Cambodia) Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 23 January 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Vong Vanna; Nyan Somoninth presiding) · Doc ID 140233

## 1. Facts
A shoe and garment factory in Ang Snuol, Kandal, with about 580 workers and seven local unions, refused to renew the fixed-duration contract of Ms K, who had been elected deputy president of the claimant union on 12 October 2012. [Para 2][Para 63][Para 113] Ms K worked two consecutive two-month probationary contracts, from 25 July 2012 at USD 56 a month and from 25 September 2012 at USD 61 a month; the employer gave notice of non-renewal on 15 November 2012 and her contract ended on 25 November 2012. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 71] Of seven demands, only Issue 1 survived — the union withdrew Issue 7 pending another forum — and the panel held the award non-binding because the local union was not yet registered. [Para 15][Para 19][Para 20][Para 21][Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer's decision not to renew Ms K's fixed-duration contract was anti-union discrimination, so that she must be reinstated? [Para 65][Para 99]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The union saw the non-renewal as discrimination because Ms K had committed no employment fault and the employer never disclosed the result of any work evaluation; the company knew of the new local leadership from the 22 October 2012 notification, and a federation official's phone call with the head of administration showed the company meant to end the contract early. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 73][Para 74][Para 76]
**Employer (respondent):** Ms K was not renewed because her evaluation graded her criteria as weak and she did not try to fulfil her duties during probation, breaching the internal rules; the second probationary contract was signed because the company remained unsure of her capacity, not because of her union role; the company never knew of the phone discussion. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 71][Para 77][Para 98]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the claim for reinstatement was refused and the award was declared non-binding under the 3 October 2012 memorandum of understanding. [Para 116][Para 117]

The panel began with jurisdiction and character: although the federation and the company had signed the 3 October 2012 memorandum, the factory-level union had not yet been registered as a professional organisation, so the federation's agreement to represent these workers did not make the parties signatories and the award was therefore non-binding. [Para 21][Para 22]

On the merits the panel put the burden of proving discrimination on the party making the allegation, following its earlier awards, and said it would test the allegation against the hearing explanations and the documents. [Para 103][Para 105][Para 107] It applied Article 12 and Article 279, which forbid an employer from using union membership or union activity as grounds for hiring, discipline or terminating a contract. [Para 99][Para 100][Para 101]

The panel accepted that the record was imperfect: documents were mis-titled, dated inconsistently, and the 17 November 2012 evaluation said only that her criteria were "weak". [Para 110] It also found genuine grounds for suspicion, because the evaluation was written after the 15 November 2012 termination notice and after the company had received the union's leadership notification. [Para 72][Para 111] But it held suspicion was not enough: the union produced no specific evidence beyond the phone conversation, and the other two elected leaders, Mr C and Mr N, were neither terminated nor worried about their own renewals. [Para 108][Para 109][Para 112] The panel inferred from seven unions among only 580 workers that union activity at the plant was fluid and not specially targeted. [Para 113]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union alleging anti-union discrimination bears the burden of producing specific evidence of it; proof that an evaluation was written after the termination notice, and that notice followed the union's leadership notification, raises suspicion but does not by itself establish discrimination where the other elected leaders at the same plant kept their contracts. [Para 108][Para 111][Para 112]

**Obiter dicta:** That the proliferation of unions in a small workforce shows pluralist rather than targeted union activity, and the panel's remark that documentary sloppiness by the employer alone cannot found a discrimination finding. [Para 110][Para 113]
