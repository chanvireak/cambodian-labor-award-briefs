# Award No. 56/13 — MH BIO-ENG Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 11 April 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Seng Vouchhun (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Nhan Somunninth (president) · Doc ID 139675

## 1. Facts
MH BIO-ENG is an alcohol producer at National Road 5 in Phnom Penh employing about 110 workers, with no local union at the plant. [Para 58][Para 60] Twenty-three workers delegated their claims to the Khmer Workers' Power Union Federation, and ten of them were dismissed on 17 December 2012 without notice or compensation after drinking beer during the lunch break the previous day. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 97] The employer treated the resulting half-day and one-day absences as serious misconduct under its internal regulations. [Para 107][Para 114] Two of the three demands — six-monthly health checks and a permanent company doctor — were settled by agreement at the second hearing, leaving reinstatement and back pay. [Para 23][Para 64][Para 68]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must arrange a health check for every worker every six months? [Para 64]
2. Whether the employer must station a doctor at the company throughout working hours, especially during overtime? [Para 68]
3. Whether the ten dismissed workers committed serious misconduct (កំហុសធ្ងន់) justifying dismissal without notice or compensation, or must be reinstated with back pay? [Para 105]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** They did not commit serious misconduct; they were merely absent without leave for half a day, and one for a full day, and the employer should either reinstate them or pay notice pay, dismissal indemnity, moral damages, unused annual leave and outstanding wages. [Para 106][Para 108]
**Employer (respondent):** Nine workers jointly failed to return for half a day and one missed a full night shift, disrupting the production line, breaching internal regulations 10.1 and 11 and causing losses it puts at 78,000 US dollars a day; it refuses reinstatement. [Para 106][Para 138][Para 150]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must reinstate all ten workers and pay back wages; if it refuses, it must pay, within 48 hours, notice pay, dismissal indemnity, moral damages equal to the dismissal indemnity, unused annual leave and outstanding wages, and it must complete the agreed health checks and doctor by 15 July 2013. The award is not binding and becomes final after eight days absent objection. [Para 173][Para 176][Para 177][Para 178]

On the health claims the panel simply recorded the parties' agreement: checks for all workers who had none since joining, by 15 July 2013, with wages and benefits preserved while attending, and a permanent doctor by the same date. [Para 64][Para 67][Para 69][Para 175]

On the dismissal, the panel applied articles 74 and 27 of the Labour Law and held that a dismissal is lawful only where the worker commits serious misconduct as defined in article 83(b), and that the sanction must be proportionate to the fault. [Para 109][Para 115][Para 131] It then examined internal regulations 10.1, 11 and 6.2, which classify unauthorised absence and breaches of duty as serious misconduct, and held that classification contrary to the spirit of the law: only the faults listed in article 83(b) can be treated as serious, whatever the internal regulations say. [Para 123][Para 132][Para 133]

On the facts, the panel found the workers had drunk beer during their lunch break on 13 December 2012, that nine could not return that afternoon and one missed his night shift, and that this was improper conduct amounting to a fault. [Para 84][Para 87][Para 146] But it was an ordinary fault warranting a warning, not dismissal, particularly as none of the ten had ever been warned for a similar fault. [Para 102][Para 148] The employer's claim of catastrophic loss was not made out: it admitted the 25 January 2013 order was met on time, produced no evidence of actual damage, and its own account showed the blackout was caused by a sleeping substitute rather than by the dismissed worker. [Para 100][Para 138][Para 141][Para 142]

Because the dismissals lacked proper grounds, the panel ordered reinstatement with back pay. [Para 149] Failing reinstatement, it calculated notice pay at one month for the eight workers with more than four years' service and 15 days for the two with under a year, dismissal indemnity of 75 days' wages for the eight long-serving workers and 7 days for the two recent hires, moral damages equal to the dismissal indemnity, unused annual leave, and final wages within 48 hours under article 116. [Para 154][Para 155][Para 159][Para 160][Para 170][Para 172]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Only conduct falling within article 83(b) of the Labour Law can be treated as serious misconduct justifying dismissal without notice; an internal regulation that labels other breaches — such as unauthorised absence or drinking during the lunch break — as serious misconduct is contrary to the law and cannot support a dismissal. [Para 132][Para 133]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that where the employer refuses reinstatement after an unlawful dismissal, moral damages are assessed at the full amount of the dismissal indemnity, not at a reduced figure. [Para 168][Para 170]
