# Award No. 249/13 — F.Y. Cambodia Fashion Limited (Arbitration Council, 20 December 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Arbitrators Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Ann Vireak (worker-appointed), Sok Mathoeung (chair) · Doc ID 139901

## 1. Facts
F.Y. Cambodia Fashion Limited is a garment manufacturer registered under Inv 293 E/1997 dated 17 January 1997 and employing approximately 120 workers. [Para 19][Para 20] FTUWKC, which has no local union at the factory, held an authorisation letter dated 5 November 2013 from 89 workers; the employer did not object to the name list or thumbprints, and the panel confined the case to those 89 workers. [Para 13][Para 21][Para 22][Para 23] The workers demanded that the employer pay the monthly attendance bonus in proportion to the days of authorised leave they took for personal commitments. [Para 3][Para 24] The employer paid a US$10 monthly attendance bonus, withheld it entirely for one day of such leave, but preserved it for annual leave and paid it pro rata for sick leave. [Para 25][Para 26]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the demand concerning the attendance bonus is a rights dispute within the panel's jurisdiction? [Para 37]
2. Whether the employer must pay the US$10 attendance bonus in proportion to the number of days of authorised leave taken by the workers? [Para 50]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the attendance bonus must be reduced only in proportion to authorised leave days, as decided in Arbitral Award No. 230/12-M & V; the 2003 internal work rule does not bind the company because its address differs from the factory's. [Para 27][Para 32]
**Employer (respondent):** it complies with the Labour Law and maintains current practice; the 2003 rule (a US$5 bonus for 100 per cent attendance) still applies, was approved by the Ministry and posted at the premises; it would pay pro rata if the law so required. [Para 4][Para 26][Para 29][Para 30][Para 32]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — a binding award ordering the employer to pay a monthly US$10 attendance bonus in proportion to the number of days of authorised leave taken by the workers. [Para 71][Para 72]
The panel first classified the demand. Point 2 of Notification No. 230 dated 25 July 2012 requires a monthly bonus of at least US$10 for workers who attend regularly without absence, so the demand concerns the enforcement of a legal provision and is a rights dispute. [Para 37][Para 42][Para 43] The Labour Advisory Committee had not defined "without absence", and the panel declined to read it as covering authorised or unauthorised absence. [Para 45][Para 48] "Absence stipulated in the law" — annual leave, special leave, maternity leave, holidays and weekly time off — does not permit docking the bonus. [Para 46][Para 47] Authorised leave for personal commitments is not stipulated in law, so the panel assessed it case by case. [Para 48][Para 49]
Under Articles 71(6) and 72(1) of the Labour Law, authorised leave is a suspension of the contract: the employer owes neither wages nor the bonus for those days. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 69] But the employer's discretion to authorise leave does not entitle it to withhold the entire bonus; it may only reduce it in proportion to the days taken. [Para 51][Para 70][Para 71] Workers with remaining annual leave should use it before requesting personal leave. [Para 71]
The panel also found the 2003 internal rule unclear: it lists a US$5 bonus while the employer pays US$10, and the employer does not follow it for annual or sick leave. [Para 56][Para 57]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer authorises unpaid leave for personal commitments, the contract is suspended for those days; the employer may reduce the statutory US$10 monthly attendance bonus in proportion to the days of authorised leave but may not withhold the bonus in full. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 71]

**Obiter dicta:** the terms attached to the attendance bonus in the 2003 internal work rules are unclear, and the employer does not in practice apply that rule to annual or sick leave. [Para 56][Para 57]

[Para 26][Para 56] The internal rule fixes a US$5 bonus while the employer pays US$10.
