# Award No. 20/13 — E Garmen Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 January 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Hem Hour Narith, employer-appointed; Tuon Siphann, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · arbitration not continued · Doc ID 139602

## 1. Facts
E Garmen Co., Ltd, a garment factory at Svay Rolum, S'ang district, Kandal province, faced twelve demands from the apparel workers' democratic union confederation (C.CAWDU) and its factory union: enforcement of a 02-05-2009 agreement to take back 61 workers with back pay, several meal allowances, undetermined duration contracts, a US$93 monthly wage, and reinstatement of 33, 8 and 1 named union activists. The workers struck from 24 January 2013 during conciliation, which failed on 21 January 2013, and the twelve issues reached the panel under non-conciliation report No. 032/13 dated 22 January 2013. On 25 January 2013 the panel issued order No. 005/13 requiring an immediate halt to the strike and a return to shifts that afternoon. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 17][Para 29][Para 32][Para 34][Para 35]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must honour the agreement of 02-05-2009 with C.CAWDU to rehire 61 workers with back pay from the date of dismissal? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company must reduce the regular attendance bonus in proportion to the number of days absent? [Para 4]
3. Whether the company must pay a 5,000 riel meal allowance to workers who volunteer to work on Sundays or public holidays? [Para 6]
4. Whether it must pay a 5,000 riel meal allowance to workers doing overtime from 6 p.m. to 8:30 p.m.? [Para 8]
5. Whether it must pay a meal allowance of 12,000 riel per day? [Para 10]
6. Whether it must use long-term contracts of undetermined duration? [Para 12]
7. Whether it must pay workers a wage of US$93 a month? [Para 15]
8. Whether it must take back 33 C.CAWDU activists with back pay? [Para 17]
9. Whether it must take back 8 C.CAWDU activists said to have been beaten by the Khmer Youth union, with back pay? [Para 19]
10. Whether it must take back the named C.CAWDU activist, employee No. 603327, with back pay? [Para 21]
11. Whether it must refrain from retaliation by transfer or dismissal and from complaints against workers who struck? [Para 23]
12. Whether it must preserve wages and the regular bonus for all workers during the strike? [Para 25]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The union pressed all twelve demands and, at the meeting of 29 January 2013, confirmed its final position that the workers would keep striking; it did not obey the order to return to work. [Para 40][Para 58][Para 59]
**Employer (respondent):** It did not agree to demands 1 to 10, saying it had already paid 2,000 riel a day as a meal allowance on Sundays and 4,000 riel in total for a full eight-hour holiday, and 2,000 riel for overtime between 6 p.m. and 8:30 p.m., and that it used undetermined duration contracts generally, reserving fixed terms for occasional small jobs of pressing need; on demands 11 and 12 it asked only that the legal procedure be followed, and it told the panel it would let the workers return to work. [Para 3][Para 7][Para 9][Para 14][Para 24][Para 26][Para 39]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel decided not to continue the arbitration in case 20/13; none of the twelve demands was decided and no relief was ordered. [Para 60][Para 61]

The panel framed the sole question as whether to continue the arbitration at all. Clause 20 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 on the Arbitration Council requires the parties, while the panel sits, to suspend every dispute measure such as a strike or lockout (as contemplated by Article 318 of the Labour Law) or any other measure capable of aggravating the dispute, and to attend every meeting to which the panel summons them. [Para 48][Para 49]

On that text the panel repeated the reading it had adopted in earlier cases: it will not continue an arbitration while the workers are on strike. In case 04/04 (IMI) its notice of 29 January 2004 recorded that a party to a labour dispute has no right to strike during the panel's proceedings, that the panel can neither settle the dispute nor issue an award while a strike lasts, and that it may resume once all the workers stop striking. In case 18/04 (the hotels: Cambodiana, Raffles Le Royal, Raffles Grand Angkor, Sunway, InterContinental and Sofitel Angkor) the second order of 22 April 2004 required all workers to stop striking, demonstrating or disturbing the hotel premises from 23 April 2004 and reserved the panel's right to terminate proceedings if a party breached the order. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 54]

It also followed case 77/06 (printed "ហ្វ្រង់កូ នីត ធីង"), where the second order signed on 24 December 2005 provided at point 4 that if the workers continued to strike the panel would not proceed with the collective dispute, and case 110/11 (Simano), where it declined to continue because the workers kept striking during the proceedings; the panel said it agreed with those decisions. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57]

The facts were not in doubt: the workers struck from 24 January 2013, the company's letters of 24 and 28 January 2013 asked the panel to order the strike stopped and then reported that order 005/13 was not being obeyed, the strike went on after that order, and at the 29 January 2013 meeting the workers' representatives maintained it. The panel recorded expressly that it made no findings of fact on the twelve demands because it held no hearing. It therefore declined to continue the arbitration. [Para 34][Para 41][Para 42][Para 47][Para 58][Para 59][Para 60]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The panel will not continue an arbitration, hear the demands or issue an award while the workers remain on strike in breach of its order to suspend it; clause 20 of Prakas No. 099 obliges the parties to suspend all dispute measures and attend the panel's meetings, and the case may be taken up again once the strike ends. [Para 48][Para 52][Para 60]

**Obiter dicta:** the employer's account of meal allowances actually paid — 2,000 riel on Sundays, 4,000 riel for a full holiday, 2,000 riel for evening overtime — and its statement that it used undetermined duration contracts except for occasional small jobs, none of which was tested because no hearing was held. [Para 7][Para 14][Para 47]
