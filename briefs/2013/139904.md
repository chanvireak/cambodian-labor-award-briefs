# Award No. 245/13 — Fuhs Garment E. Parel (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 23 December 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139904

## 1. Facts
Fuhs Garment E. Parel (Cambodia) is a garment manufacturer on National Road 2, Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employing approximately 430 workers. [Para 2][Para 77] Its local union of the Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers' Democratic Union (C.CAWDU) was elected on 11 May 2013 and applied for registration on 7 May 2013; registration was still pending at the hearing. [Para 78][Para 235] Of twenty-two demands raised in collective bargaining, thirteen were not conciliated on 31 October 2013 and came before the panel on 25 November 2013. [Para 35][Para 33] The dispute concerns the non-renewal of the union secretary and a union activist, and a series of allowance, bonus and severance demands. [Para 81]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the two named workers and pay them wages from the date of suspension until their return to work? [Para 81][Para 161]
5. Whether the employer must pay severance of 5% of wages each time a fixed-duration contract reaches its term? [Para 273]
9. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of 4,000 riel per day to each worker? [Para 290]
10. Whether the employer must pay an additional 2,000 riel meal allowance for overtime worked from 16:00 to 20:00? [Para 309]
11. Whether the employer must pay a health-care allowance of USD 5 per month? [Para 319]
12. Whether the employer must pay a 5,000 riel meal allowance to workers who volunteer to work on public holidays or Sundays? [Para 332]
13. Whether the employer must restore the production bonus to the rate it paid previously? [Para 370]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the union secretary's contract was not renewed without a valid reason and his departure was tainted by anti-union discrimination, since he had been elected union secretary; the activist was dismissed on 22 July 2013 at 16:05 without notice because the employer disliked his union activity. [Para 87][Para 108] The 5% severance is owed at the end of every fixed-duration contract, and the allowances and bonus are benefits the employer formerly granted, now withdrawn. [Para 126][Para 150]
**Employer (respondent):** the claims concerning the two workers are individual disputes, their contracts being separate from one another and from the collective demand. [Para 81][Para 166] The secretary's six-month contract simply reached its term and non-renewal is the employer's right; the activist's contract also expired and his attitude had drawn oral warnings and one written warning on 28 June 2013. [Para 84][Para 103] The allowances and bonus depend on market orders and the company's capacity, and the 5% severance is payable only when a worker leaves. [Para 129][Para 151][Para 288]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement and back pay refused for both workers; the employer was ordered to give the activist (1) moral damages, (2) 5% severance, (3) compensation for unused annual leave and (4) final wages, while demands 5, 9 to 12 and 13 were refused. [Para 386][Para 387][Para 388][Para 392] The order fixes no sum for the four heads, leaving quantum to the parties.

The panel first classified the dispute: Article 312 of the Labour Law gives it legal jurisdiction over rights disputes and equity jurisdiction over interests disputes, and under Clause 43 of Prakas No. 099 an interests award replaces the collective agreement for one year. [Para 155][Para 157] It held the case a collective labour dispute within Article 302, because a union representing 187 workers had taken over the claims. [Para 163][Para 172]

On the secretary, the panel applied Article 73: a fixed-duration contract expires at its term and whether to renew is the parties' choice, no one being able to compel renewal. [Para 173][Para 174] His contract ran to 30 September 2013 and notice of non-renewal was given on 23 September 2013, so the relationship ended by expiry. [Para 176] On the discrimination allegation the burden lay on the worker, and no evidence of the employer's displeasure at union activity was produced, so the claim failed. [Para 184][Para 189][Para 190]

On the activist, the panel found that after his six-month contract ended on 25 January 2013 without notice it was tacitly renewed for a further six months to 25 July 2013. [Para 207][Para 208] The notice of 22 July 2013 described a seven-month contract, which did not match the contract actually held, and gave no date of notification; it could not operate as notice of non-renewal, and as his last paid day was 22 July 2013 the panel held he was terminated early. [Para 214][Para 217][Para 219] Article 293 protection did not apply: although he was a union member dismissed within the protected period, the union had never notified the employer of his name as a protected person, the third cumulative condition being unmet. [Para 227][Para 241][Para 243] Anti-union discrimination was again unproven. [Para 247] Early termination under the third paragraph of Article 73 nonetheless entitled him to damages at least equal to wages to the end of the contract, plus 5% severance, unused annual leave under Article 166 and final wages under Article 116; his July payroll had been computed at USD 177.95. [Para 255][Para 259][Para 266][Para 268][Para 111]

On demand 5, the panel held the 5% severance falls due only when the employment relationship is severed, not at each contract term, so a renewed contract attracts nothing at renewal. [Para 282][Para 288][Para 289] Demands 9 to 12 were classified as interests disputes for which no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice established an obligation, and because the local union was unregistered and not the most representative union the panel declined to consider them at all. [Para 294][Para 301][Para 307][Para 316] Demand 13 was treated as a rights dispute but refused: the workers proved no prior rate, and Article 13 permits an employer's unilateral grant to stand, the bonus varying with orders being reasonable. [Para 370][Para 382][Para 384][Para 385]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-duration contract that reaches its term ends the employment relationship automatically, and the Council will not order reinstatement unless the worker proves that non-renewal was motivated by union discrimination, the burden lying on the party alleging it; where the employer's notice misstates the contract term or its date, it cannot serve as notice of non-renewal and the termination is a unilateral early termination attracting damages to the end of the contract. Union leaders claiming Article 293 protection must show that the union notified the employer of their names, and an interests dispute cannot be entertained where the claiming union is unregistered and lacks most-representative status. [Para 174][Para 214][Para 241][Para 301][Para 307]

**Obiter dicta:** the observations that a renewed contract carries over existing benefits, and that an interests award would bind all workers in the enterprise and bar future claims, were not necessary to any order made here. [Para 283][Para 300]
