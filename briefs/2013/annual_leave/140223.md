# Order No. 234/12 — Cambo Cotop Ltd (Arbitration Council, 4 January 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mr Ing Sothy for the employer party, Mr An Nan for the worker party, Mr Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 140223

## 1. Facts
Cambo Cotop Ltd, a garment factory at Chom Chao, Phnom Penh with about 2,400 workers, faced five demands from the Cambodian Workers' Movement union, registered on 4 December 2012; conciliation on 23 November 2012 left two of the five unresolved, reported under No. 1394 and referred on 3 December 2012. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 39][Para 40][Para 42] At the hearing of 18 December 2012 the parties settled Issue 2 — transport to hospital and treatment costs when a worker falls ill, with the company continuing to report to the National Social Security Fund — so the panel considered only Issue 1. [Para 11][Para 16][Para 17][Para 57][Para 58] Issue 1 asks the employer to prorate, not forfeit, the regular attendance bonus for days a worker is lawfully absent with the employer's permission for compelling reasons. [Para 2][Para 43] The employer pays a USD 13 monthly attendance bonus to workers with no absence, keeps USD 9 for one permitted day, USD 7 for two days, and cuts the whole bonus from three days upward. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must prorate the regular attendance bonus to the number of days a worker is absent with permission for compelling reasons? [Para 43][Para 61]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** compelling reasons include transplanting or harvesting rice, a friend's wedding, a village celebration such as Pchum Ben, and a relative's traffic accident, all requiring immediate absence; this is not annual leave, since new workers cannot yet use annual leave and the company requires a week's notice; other factories and Awards 136/11-Cambo Han Sa, 154/11-B&N and 204/12-Cambo Khotob had granted the same proration. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46]
**Employer (respondent):** it cannot grant the demand and will keep applying its standing rule; the bonus is only for workers who come to work without absence, and the declining scale of USD 9, USD 7 and nothing is its settled practice. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 56]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider the demand for prorated attendance bonus for future permitted absences, and this being a non-binding order it becomes final eight days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Minister of Labour through the Secretariat. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 69]

The panel found that the claim was made for days not yet taken: at the hearing the workers confirmed that no member of the union had yet been absent for a compelling reason with permission and had the attendance bonus cut, so the demand was reserved for the future. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 62] One worker named by the union as an example, in the QC section, had been docked the whole bonus for permitted sick leave, but had complained and been paid, and the workers did not dispute the employer's account of its scale. [Para 51][Para 54][Para 55]

On that basis the panel held the dispute had not yet arisen, and applied its standing position that the Council is established to resolve labour disputes, not hypothetical problems: the claim for future days means the dispute does not yet exist. [Para 63][Para 66] It followed Award No. 10/03-Zaksintic (Issue 2), No. 14/06-Seng Yong (Issue 2) and No. 141/08-Blue Tham (Issue 3), and said it agreed with those panels. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65] The order of 4 January 2013 therefore refuses the demand, the due date having been extended from 26 December 2012 by agreement at the hearing. [Para 18][Para 67][Para 68]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that the employer change a rule for absences that have not yet occurred raises no existing dispute: the Council decides live labour disputes, not hypothetical ones, so a claim for proration of an attendance bonus reserved for "future days" will be refused as premature even where the employer's current practice would cut the bonus, and it can be brought only once a worker has actually suffered the deduction. [Para 63][Para 66][Para 67]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the union's example worker had already been reimbursed after complaining, and its agreement with earlier panels, are illustrations of the prematurity point rather than separate holdings. [Para 55][Para 65]

- The chair arbitrator is printed as "គង់ ផល្នៈ" on the face of the order and as "គង់ ផល្ទុះ" in the signature block, followed by OCR debris. [Para 1][Para 70]
- The demand summary lists only two of the five demands (items 1 and 2) before the jurisdiction section, so the numbering of 05 demands cannot be reconciled with the two unresolved ones. [Para 2][Para 5][Para 13]
- The employer's bonus scale in the reasons loses the zero-absence figure, printed as "០០០" instead of a dollar amount, before resuming at USD 9 and USD 7. [Para 48]
- Stray Latin tokens ("Styp", "Lid", "Song", "Lea", "Shame", "Chan", "Ellex", "The All") are embedded in the Khmer text and two headings are duplicated. [Para 8][Para 12][Para 58][Para 63]
- The individual worker cited by the union as an example is recorded here by role; her name survives in the source but is not reproduced. [Para 54]
