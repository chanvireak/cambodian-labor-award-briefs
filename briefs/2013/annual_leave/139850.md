# Order in Case No. 167/13 — Quin Th Major Industrial (Arbitration Council, 29 August 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phalla presiding; Kol Vathana appointed by the employer; An Non appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 139850

## 1. Facts
The company (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឃ្វីន ធ៍ មេជើរ អ៊ិន ដាស ស្ទ្រៀល) manufactures at Trayeang village, Peuk commune, Ang Snuol district, Kandal, and its internal rules were registered on 10 May 2007. [Para 1][Para 29] A collective dispute over seven demands was conciliated by the Kandal Provincial Department of Labour and Vocational Training on 9 August 2013; none of the seven was settled and non-conciliation report No. 611/13 KB/KN of that date referred them to the Council. [Para 19][Para 22][Para 23] The panel was constituted on 14 August 2013 and fixed the hearing for 27 August 2013 at 08:30; the employer attended and the workers did not. [Para 24][Para 21][Para 26][Para 27] The workers telephoned the secretariat to say they would not come because the demands repeated case No. 127/13 against the same company, already decided, and because they had given strike notice. [Para 38]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay an additional meal allowance of 2,000 riel, making 4,000 riel in all, to workers who work overtime from 16:00 to 18:00? [Para 3]
2. Whether the company must pay a midday meal allowance of 3,000 riel a day to all workers? [Para 5]
3. Whether the company must increase the transport and accommodation allowance by USD 10, making USD 17 in all, because most workers pay at least USD 17 for a shared van? [Para 7]
4. Whether the company, when replacing or issuing identity cards, must issue cards that clearly state the company name? [Para 9]
5. Whether the company must allow workers to swipe the time card only twice a day, on entering in the morning and on leaving in the evening? [Para 11]
6. Whether the company must not deduct seniority indemnity or annual leave, and must maintain wages and other bonuses, when a worker takes special leave for marriage, serious illness or death of a parent, or illness or death of a spouse or child? [Para 13]
7. Whether the company must add two days a year to the 18 days of annual leave, as at the Q.M.I.G factory? [Para 15]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** they did not appear; by telephone they said the issues were identical to those in case No. 127/13 against the same employer, which the panel had already decided, that the federation had objected to that decision, and that it had notified the employer of a strike. [Para 38][Para 39]
**Employer (respondent):** it attended the hearing; on demands 1, 2, 3 and 7 it asked that the Labour Law be applied, on demand 4 it asked for time to consider and correct the cards, and on demands 5 and 6 it disagreed and asked that the existing practice be kept. [Para 4][Para 6][Para 8][Para 10][Para 12][Para 14][Para 16]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** withdrawn — the panel treated the claimant as having abandoned its seven demands and terminated the arbitration in case No. 167/13, closing the file without deciding any demand on the merits. [Para 69][Para 72][Para 73]

The panel applied Article 18 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY of 21 April 2004, which requires it to invite the parties to explain their claims in person and to submit the necessary documents and information, Article 20, which requires the parties to attend every meeting the panel summons, and Article 21, which provides that where a properly invited party fails to appear without a valid reason the panel may continue in that party's absence or terminate the arbitration by order; Rule 4.7 of the procedural rules annexed to the same Prakas says the same. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43][Para 44][Para 46][Para 47]

Three conditions had to be met, and the panel took them one by one. [Para 49][Para 50] First, proper invitation: letter No. 869 KB/AK/VK/LKA of 19 August 2013 was issued to the Khmer Workers' Power Union Federation and to the workers' representatives, stating the date, place and contact telephone and fax of the hearing, and the federation's secretary-general signed for it at 15:30 on 19 August 2013. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 52][Para 53] Second, non-appearance: the workers were absent from the hearing and took part in none of the four stages — introduction and explanation of conflicts of interest, explanation of procedure and identification of the issues, conciliation, and the hearing itself. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 56][Para 57] Third, no valid reason: the panel held that the three reasons given did not qualify — that the demands had already been decided in an earlier order, that the claimant had objected to that order, and that it had notified the employer for a strike. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60][Para 61]

The panel added that a claimant must first present its grounds and evidence to make out its claim, and the respondent's duty to answer arises only afterwards; here the claimant never appeared to discharge that duty and had no proper reason for staying away. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67][Para 68] It therefore held the demands abandoned, found no need to continue, and terminated the proceedings by order. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 71][Para 72]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a claimant has been properly summoned, does not appear at the hearing or at any stage of the proceedings, and offers no valid reason, the panel will treat the claims as abandoned and terminate the arbitration by order under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 and Rule 4.7, without ruling on the merits. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 69] A pending objection to an earlier order on the same demands, and the giving of strike notice, are not valid reasons for non-appearance. [Para 60]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed generally that the burden of presenting grounds and evidence lies first on the claimant, the respondent's duty to answer arising only once the claimant has done so. [Para 65][Para 66]
