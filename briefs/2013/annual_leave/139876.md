# Award No. 212/13 — Quin Tho Meser In Das Striel (Arbitration Council, 22 October 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Yoo Soun for the employer party, Tuon Siphan for the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair) · proceedings terminated without a decision on the merits · Doc ID 139876

## 1. Facts
The company, a garment factory at Village Trayeung, Commune Peuk, Ang Snuol district, Kandal province, faced a collective dispute brought by the Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers' Democratic Union and the local union at the factory. [Para 1] The workers' demand concerned the day off before the Pchum Ben festival: the employer allowed everyone to stop one day early, on 02 [October 2013], but required the day to be exchanged against annual leave, with a return to normal work on 07 October 2013. [Para 1][Para 3] The workers refused the exchange and demanded two further days off without any exchange and without deduction of wages or other benefits. [Para 4] Conciliation on 19 September 2013 failed on one of the two demands, and the non-conciliation report No. 711/13 of 19 September 2013 reached the secretariat on 23 September 2013. [Para 7][Para 11][Para 12]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must grant the workers two additional days off around Pchum Ben without requiring an exchange against annual leave and without deducting wages or other benefits? [Para 4]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer's arrangement forced them to swap the pre-festival day for annual leave, so they demanded two more days off free of any exchange and free of any deduction from wages or benefits. [Para 1][Para 4]
**Employer (respondent):** it had already allowed all workers to stop one day before Pchum Ben, on condition that the day be exchanged for annual leave, with work resuming normally on 07 October 2013. [Para 1][Para 3] Neither party appeared at the hearing; through the secretariat both stated that the demands in the case had been settled in full. [Para 40]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** withdrawn — the panel terminated the arbitration proceedings in case No. 212/13 without ruling on the merits, because both parties were absent from the hearing. [Para 45][Para 46]

The panel first established its jurisdiction under Chapter 12, Section 2 (b), Articles 309 to 317 of the 1997 Labour Law, Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council of 21 April 2004 with its annexed procedural rules, and Prakas No. 155 K.B/Pr.K of 17 June 2013 appointing the eleventh-term members. [Para 5] The panel was constituted on 26 September 2013 and the secretariat summoned both parties to a hearing on 01 October 2013 at 14:00. [Para 9][Para 13][Para 14] The employer's invitation went by letter No. 1050 and the workers' by letter No. 1051, both of 26 September 2013, and the workers received theirs at 10:30 that morning; the employer did not collect its letter and was reached by email at 09:43 and by telephone on the hearing day. [Para 28][Para 30][Para 31][Para 32] The panel therefore found both parties properly notified of the date, time and place of the hearing, satisfying the first condition under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 33][Para 34]

On the second condition the panel read the words "appear before the panel" in Article 21 to require both physical presence in the hearing room and participation in the proceedings from beginning to end. [Para 35] Neither party attended and neither appointed a lawful representative, so the second condition was met. [Para 38] On the third condition — no valid excuse for non-appearance — the panel noted that the parties had telephoned the secretariat to say the demands were settled in full and that this was why they were not attending, but that neither had produced evidence proving the settlement. [Para 40][Para 41] It held the excuse invalid and the third condition met. [Para 42][Para 43] With all three conditions satisfied, the panel terminated the proceedings under Article 21 rather than deciding the claim. [Para 44][Para 45]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where both parties are duly summoned, neither appears nor sends a representative, and neither offers a valid excuse for the absence, the three conditions of Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 are satisfied and the panel terminates the arbitration proceedings instead of deciding the dispute; a telephone assertion that the claim has been settled, unaccompanied by evidence, is not a valid excuse. [Para 35][Para 40][Para 42][Para 45]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the parties had not evidenced their settlement before the Council [Para 41] is not a finding that the dispute was in fact settled; the panel expressly declined to make any finding on the merits. [Para 46]
