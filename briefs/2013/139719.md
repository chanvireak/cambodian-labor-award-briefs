# Award No. 93/13 — Star Night Thing Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 5 June 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (employer-appointed Kao Tha Ch; worker-appointed An Non; president Kong Phalha) · Doc ID 139719

## 1. Facts
Star Night Thing Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស្តារ នីត ធីង), a garment-weaving enterprise at Prek Samrong, Ta Khmao, Kandal, employed 233 workers, of whom the Democratic Union of Khmer Workers (CCAWDU) represented 190. [Para 31][Para 32] The union alleged that from December 2012 the employer suspended the employment contracts and stopped paying wages, telling workers only that the factory was being repainted; no application was made to any competent authority and no written notice was given. [Para 25][Para 40][Para 44] The employer did not attend the 23 May 2013 hearing and filed no documents or arguments. [Para 17][Para 20][Para 62]

## 2. Issues in dispute
Five claims were referred; at the hearing the workers withdrew issues 2, 3 and 4, leaving two. [Para 18][Para 19]
1. Whether the employer must pay 100 per cent of wages to the workers for the period during which it suspended the employment contracts, from March to May 2013? [Para 34][Para 54]
5. Whether the workers may require the employer to pay severance and contract-termination compensation because the company closed its doors? [Para 43][Para 69]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the suspension was unlawful because the employer neither applied to the competent authority nor gave written notice of it, so full wages are owed for March–May 2013; long service plus the closure entitle all 190 claimants to severance, whether on fixed-duration or indefinite contracts. [Para 38][Para 40][Para 45][Para 48]
**Employer (respondent):** absent from the hearing; no submissions, evidence or explanation for the non-appearance was placed before the panel. [Para 52][Para 62]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay 100 per cent of wages to the workers for the suspension period March to May 2013; the claim for severance and termination compensation was dismissed. [Para 83][Para 84]

As a threshold matter the panel held it could proceed in the employer's absence under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 (21 April 2004), since the employer had been properly summoned and gave no valid reason, following Awards 53/04 Kuong Hong, 63/04 San Velu, 148/07 Bay Her, 99/09 King Lea and 173/11 Jin Ying. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 53]

On issue 1, Article 72(1) of the Labour Law removes the employer's duty to pay wages while a contract is suspended, but that provision applies only where the suspension itself was carried out lawfully. [Para 58][Para 60] The employer having offered nothing, the panel accepted the workers' uncontested account that no suspension request was lodged with the competent authority, and found the suspension procedurally unlawful. [Para 63][Para 64] Article 72(1) could therefore not be invoked, and 100 per cent wages were ordered for March–May 2013, consistently with Awards 33/04 Boumin, 110/07 Nouv Khob, 43/09 Hugo and 26/12 N.C.A. [Para 65][Para 66]

On issue 5, Articles 311 of the Civil Code and 65(1) of the Labour Law make the creation, modification and extinction of contractual obligations depend on the will of the contracting parties. [Para 70][Para 71][Para 73] No termination notice had been given and the contracts had not been terminated, so the workers could not force the employer to terminate against its will and claim severance on that footing. [Para 76][Para 77] The panel noted its usual practice of awarding severance where an employer terminates without valid reason or legal procedure, but found no evidence of such a termination here. [Para 80][Para 81]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of employment contracts that was not requested from the competent authority and not notified in writing to the workers is unlawful; Article 72(1) cannot then be used to withhold wages, and the employer owes full wages for the whole suspension period. Where, by contrast, no termination has occurred, workers cannot obtain severance merely because the factory has stopped operating. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 77]

**Obiter dicta:** the general practice of ordering severance where an employer terminates without valid reason or procedure [Para 80]; the observation that a properly summoned party's absence does not prevent the panel from hearing the case [Para 53].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The dispositive reasoning at Para 68 orders payment "to 20 workers" (២០) although the award records the union as representing 190 (១៩០) [Para 32][Para 68], and Para 83 restates the order with no number at all. The start of the suspension is printed as December 2013 in two passages but as December 2012 elsewhere and in the original claim [Para 34][Para 44][Para 54]. The company name appears as ស្តារ នីត ធីង, ស្តារ នីត ធីន and ស្គរ និត ធីង [Para 32]. Lines 36–42 and 48–49 are badly broken by OCR, leaving duplicated and severed clause fragments, and the employer-appointed arbitrator's name is printed "កៅ ថា ច", which may be an OCR loss.
