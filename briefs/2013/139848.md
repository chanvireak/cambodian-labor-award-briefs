# Award No. 165/13 — CS Goldway Textile (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 10 September 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bunhea presiding; Mar Samborana, employer nominee; Liv Sovanna, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139848

## 1. Facts
CS Goldway Textile (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a garment factory at Ang Ta Nout East, Ang Ta Som, Tram Kak, Takeo, registered in the commercial register on 29 January 2013, employs about 900 workers. [Para 30][Para 31] Its union, the Labour Union for Cambodia, was registered on 10 July 2013 under No. 2946 K.B/W.K. [Para 33] Workers put in six days a week, Monday to Saturday, from 07:00 to 12:00 and 13:00 to 16:00, with a one-hour midday break. [Para 35] Of ten demands taken to conciliation, four were settled and six were referred to the panel. [Para 13][Para 14]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must move the midday meal break from 12:00 to 11:00 to avoid health problems? [Para 34]
2. Whether the employer must pay 4,000 riel a day to workers who volunteer to work on public holidays and Sundays? [Para 43]
3. Whether the employer must add 1,000 riel for overtime from 16:00 to 18:00 and 2,000 riel for 18:00 to 20:00 on top of the 2,000 riel meal allowance? [Para 50]
4. Whether the employer must let pregnant women leave 15 minutes early? [Para 56]
5. Whether the employer must introduce a team bonus for groups meeting their sewing target? [Para 66]
6. Whether the employer must pay a travel allowance of USD 15 a month to each worker? [Para 72]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the five-hour morning stretch leaves no time for breakfast, so workers are faint with hunger by noon and several have fainted in recent months, and moving the break to 11:00 is the remedy; work on holidays and Sundays is overtime and deserves a 4,000 riel meal allowance because 2,000 riel no longer buys a meal; pregnant women need more than five minutes to leave the compound; and a team bonus and a USD 15 travel allowance are owed. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 46][Para 52][Para 57][Para 73]
**Employer (respondent):** it balances the morning and afternoon sessions, which is why the morning runs five hours, most workers do overtime, and the contracts set the hours; it pays a 2,000 riel meal allowance for overtime and another 1,000 riel after 18:00, but nothing for holiday work of eight hours or less; pregnant women already leave five minutes early on a medical certificate; and it pays the USD 7 travel allowance required by Notification No. 230. [Para 42][Para 47][Para 55][Para 61][Para 74]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the midday break moved to 11:00, and ordered a 2,000 riel meal allowance for overtime worked on Sundays; the claims for 4,000 riel on holidays and for issues 3, 4, 5 and 6 were refused. [Para 176][Para 177][Para 178][Para 179][Para 180][Para 181][Para 182][Para 183]

On Issue 1 the panel began with Article 2 of the Labour Law, which it read as giving the employer the right to organise and manage the enterprise so long as that right is exercised lawfully and reasonably. [Para 76][Para 77] Article 138(1) then puts the arrangement of working time, including the midday meal break, within the employer's power. [Para 80][Para 81] That power is bounded by Article 229(1), which requires every establishment to maintain conditions necessary for workers' health. [Para 88][Para 96] The panel found the general practice in state bodies, private institutions and factories to be a four-hour session morning and afternoon, so the five-hour morning stretch from 07:00 to 12:00 exceeded that norm; with more than five hours from breakfast to noon, workers faced real hunger and fainting, which the employer did not contest. [Para 92][Para 93][Para 95][Para 91] It held that the employer must ensure no ordinary working session exceeds four hours and ordered the break moved to 11:00. [Para 99][Para 100]

On Issue 2 the panel split the claim. For holiday work it found no provision of the Labour Law, no collective agreement and no agreement between the parties requiring a 4,000 riel allowance, so the demand was an interests dispute. [Para 117][Para 118] Applying Article 96(2) and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY, it held that an award on an interests dispute stands in place of the collective agreement and binds every worker, so only a union with most-representative status, or unions together representing more than half the workforce, may bring one — and this union did not have that status. [Para 124][Para 125][Para 128][Para 130] It therefore refused to consider the holiday claim. [Para 131] For Sunday work the panel reasoned differently: the ordinary week is 48 hours from Monday to Saturday, so voluntary Sunday work is overtime beyond ordinary hours. [Para 132][Para 133][Para 134][Para 140] Point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 requires 2,000 riel a day, or a free meal, to a worker who volunteers for overtime at the employer's request, without fixing any number of hours. [Para 102][Para 141] The employer paid nothing for Sunday work, so it had failed that duty and was ordered to pay 2,000 riel; the further 4,000 riel claimed was refused. [Para 142][Para 143][Para 144][Para 145][Para 148]

On Issue 3 the panel again found no law, collective agreement, internal regulation or past practice creating the tiered allowance claimed, classified the demand as an interests dispute, and refused it for want of standing. [Para 150][Para 151][Para 153] Issue 4 was disposed of the same way: no instrument obliged the employer to grant 15 minutes, the existing five minutes was the practice, and the demand was an interests dispute refused on the same ground. [Para 158][Para 159][Para 161] On Issue 6 the panel noted that the employer already paid the USD 7 travel and accommodation allowance that point 1 of Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 requires, found no other source of obligation, and refused the demand as an interests dispute. [Para 171][Para 172][Para 173][Para 174][Para 176]

Issue 5 was refused on a different ground: because the employer had no policy of paying a team bonus and had announced that one would be introduced in September 2013, the claim concerned a dispute that had not yet arisen, and the panel recalled that it exists to resolve labour disputes, not problems that have not yet occurred. [Para 164][Para 165][Para 166][Para 168][Para 169]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Working time is a condition affecting workers' health under Article 229 of the Labour Law, so although Article 138 lets the employer arrange hours, it must keep every ordinary working session to no more than four hours; a five-hour morning session that leaves more than five hours between breakfast and the midday break is unreasonable and must be shortened, whatever the employer's internal regulations say. [Para 88][Para 95][Para 99]

Separately, a demand for a benefit that no law, collective agreement, internal regulation or past practice provides is an interests dispute, and the panel will refuse to consider it unless the union bringing it is the most representative union in the enterprise or the unions together represent more than half the workforce. [Para 128][Para 130]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted in passing that the company's internal regulations were still pending approval, so its hours did not rest on a registered rule; this founded no order. [Para 41][Para 86]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The overtime meal allowance for 16:00 to 18:00 is printed as "30 riel" in the list of issues but as 3,000 riel in the demand itself and in the reasons. [Para 3][Para 4][Para 50] The travel allowance is described as "USD 7 per month in February" at para 170, the month spurious. [Para 170] Stray numerals intrude into several sentences — "20" before the 12:00 break at para 100, "29" before the operative order at para 176, and "2017" spliced into the Prakas No. 099 citation at para 125. [Para 100][Para 125][Para 176] The union's demand is put at 4,000 riel throughout although the panel's own order is for 2,000. [Para 148] The employer is named "CS Goldway Textile (Cambodia) Co., Ltd", "CS Gold Way Textile Co., Ltd" and "CS Goldway" in different documents. [Para 18][Para 26][Para 30] The union president and vice-president are named in the source; their names are not reproduced here.
