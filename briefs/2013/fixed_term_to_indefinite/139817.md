# Award No. 125/13 — Shun Wah Auto Embroidery Factory (Cambodia) Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 9 August 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Vireak for the worker party, Mae Nimith chair) · Doc ID 139817

## 1. Facts
Shun Wah Auto Embroidery Factory (Cambodia) Ltd. carries on embroidery at Trayeung Krasaing Village, Beik Chan Commune, Ang Snoul District, Kandal Province, with about 95 workers. [Para 31][Para 32] The claimant is the Cambodian Labour Union Federation and its factory union, registered on 27 December 2012. [Para 24][Para 33] On 10 April 2013 the employer dismissed two union committee members, Ms T Srey and Ms C Dany, who had worked there since 2003 without ever signing a written contract; both were simply called in and told they were dismissed. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43][Para 44][Para 58] Three demands went to conciliation on 16 May 2013 and none was settled; the non-conciliation report was sent to the Council on 21 June 2013. [Para 13][Para 14]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay severance to the two dismissed union committee members in accordance with the Labour Law? [Para 38][Para 101]
2. Whether the employer must provide a doctor on duty at all hours and sufficient medicine for workers who fall ill? [Para 67]
3. Whether the employer must keep wages unchanged during certified sick leave and deduct the attendance bonus only in proportion to the days absent? [Para 77]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the two committee members were dismissed without valid reason, having committed no serious misconduct, and are owed the statutory severance [Para 57][Para 140]; the employer should keep a doctor on duty and stock medicine, and should not cut wages during certified sick leave, deducting the attendance bonus only pro rata [Para 5][Para 67][Para 77]. Against the employer's objection that this was a mere individual dispute, the workers asked that the point be put to the panel to decide whether it was individual or collective. [Para 49][Para 50]
**Employer (respondent):** the two were dismissed for serious misconduct under the internal rules — inciting other workers to strike on 8 and 9 April 2013 and telling them to switch off the electricity — though the employer could not identify the rule relied on [Para 55][Para 56][Para 124]; on the doctor and sick-leave demands it pointed to a 8 February 2013 agreement with the union and said it had complied [Para 68][Para 69][Para 78].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issue 1 was allowed: the employer must pay each of the two women payment in lieu of notice of two months' wages and benefits, dismissal damages of 150 days for Ms C Dany and 135 days for Ms T Srey, unused annual leave, and moral damages equal to the dismissal damages; Issues 2 and 3 were declined. [Para 184][Para 185][Para 195][Para 196][Para 197][Para 198]

The panel first fixed who was before it. Article 268 makes Ministry registration and recognition of named officers the condition of a union's rights, and the registered leadership here — president, vice-president and secretary — had all resigned, leaving only two committee members who were not recognised leaders. [Para 84][Para 90][Para 91][Para 93][Para 94] Under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 representation requires written authorisation, and no worker had given any, so only the two women were claimants. [Para 95][Para 96][Para 97][Para 98][Para 99][Para 100]

Jurisdiction then had to be established under Article 302, which requires a dispute between workers and an employer, about working conditions or union rights, capable of disrupting the enterprise. [Para 101][Para 104][Para 105] The panel found all three conditions met, the third because workers stayed away on 18 April 2013 to protest the five dismissals and the employer produced nothing to contradict that. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 108][Para 110][Para 113][Para 114]

On Issue 1, Article 67(7) provides that a fixed-duration contract must be in writing and otherwise becomes an indefinite contract; neither woman had signed one, so both held indefinite contracts. [Para 115][Para 116][Para 117][Para 118] Article 74 allows termination of such a contract only with written notice and a reason related to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the needs of the enterprise. [Para 119][Para 120][Para 121][Para 122] The employer relied on serious misconduct, but could not name the internal rule breached, called no witnesses and filed no documents by the deadline, so it failed to discharge the burden of proof confirmed in Award 107/04-Zacintic. [Para 124][Para 126][Para 128][Para 130][Para 133] On Article 83(b)(5), the panel followed Award 08/05-Winner Nighting: inciting or calling a strike is not serious misconduct unless the employer proves the worker incited violence during it, which was not shown. [Para 134][Para 135][Para 138] The dismissals therefore had no legal basis. [Para 139]

Severance followed: under Articles 75 and 77, payment in lieu of notice of two months' wages and benefits for service of five to ten years [Para 141][Para 142][Para 143][Para 145][Para 146]; under Article 89, dismissal damages of 15 days per year capped at six months, giving 150 days for Ms C Dany and 135 days for Ms T Srey [Para 151][Para 154][Para 155]; unpaid annual leave under Article 166 [Para 162][Para 165][Para 167]; and under Article 91 moral damages equal to the dismissal damages [Para 174][Para 178][Para 180]. Last wages had already been paid. [Para 183]

Issues 2 and 3 were declined, not on the merits, but because both demands were framed for the factory workforce generally and neither claimant was still employed there after 10 April 2013, so no claimant with an interest remained. [Para 186][Para 188][Para 189][Para 190][Para 191][Para 193][Para 194]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker engaged without a written fixed-duration contract holds an indefinite contract under Article 67(7), and the employer may end it only by written notice stating a reason tied to aptitude, conduct or the needs of the enterprise; an allegation of serious misconduct fails where the employer cannot identify the internal rule breached or produce evidence, and inciting a strike is not serious misconduct under Article 83(b)(5) absent proof of incitement to violence. [Para 117][Para 122][Para 133][Para 138] Where the only claimants are workers already dismissed, demands framed for the workforce at large — a staff doctor, sick-leave pay for serving workers — are declined for want of a claimant with an interest, however sound they may be. [Para 188][Para 193]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the internal-rules defence failed partly because the employer's representative had only recently taken the file and could not recall the relevant clause, and its remark that the two women had not been warned before dismissal. [Para 56][Para 58]
