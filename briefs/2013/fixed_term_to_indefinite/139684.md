# Award No. 74/13 — International Fashion Royal Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 30 April 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer, On Non for the workers, Tang Tri chair) · Doc ID 139684

## 1. Facts
International Fashion Royal is a garment factory at Canadia Garden, Choam Chao, Phnom Penh, employing 1,450 workers. Five workers — all identified by the award as leaders of their factory union — stopped work during a strike in January 2013 and, on 25 January 2013, were not allowed back in; each had earlier put a thumbprint on a letter of resignation, and a second letter dated 26 March 2013 recorded payment of final wages and outstanding annual leave. They asked to be reinstated or compensated, plus January incentive and attendance bonuses and a US$15 monthly food allowance; the latter two demands were settled or withdrawn at the hearing, leaving the compensation claim and a demand that the employer withdraw its court complaint. [Para 3][Para 40][Para 54][Para 56][Para 67]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take the five named workers back to work, or instead pay compensation in lieu of notice, dismissal compensation and moral damages, under the Labour Law? [Para 46]
2. Whether the Arbitration Council has jurisdiction to order the employer to withdraw its complaint against the workers from the court? [Para 68]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** They had indefinite-duration contracts, were stopped on 25 January 2013 without notice, and their resignations were coerced — the president of their union telephoned on 24 January 2013 telling them to sign or be sued; they signed believing the company would withdraw its complaint, and the sums received were too small to live on. [Para 54][Para 63][Para 64][Para 82]
**Employer (respondent):** The five resigned voluntarily on 25 January 2013 and were paid; it says it also paid US$7,500 on 30 January 2013 to settle the dispute, and that the workers acted against the company's instructions, caused a US$1 million loss through the strike and threatened to shut the factory within six months. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 59][Para 60]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the claims for pay in lieu of notice, dismissal compensation and moral damages for all five workers, and declined to consider the demand that the employer withdraw its court complaint. The award is non-binding. [Para 100][Para 101][Para 102]

**Preliminary — binding effect.** The federation handling the case is a signatory of the Memorandum of Understanding of 3 October 2012, but the five workers are leaders of a different union and produced no union registration certificate, so their transfer of rights to the federation could not make them MoU signatories. The award was therefore issued as non-binding, becoming final eight days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Minister of Labour through the Secretariat. The parties also agreed to defer issuance from 23 April to 30 April 2013. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20][Para 21][Para 102]

**Issue 1 — resignation or dismissal.** The panel framed the question as whether the resignations were coerced. It applied Article 74 of the Labour Law (an indefinite-duration contract may be terminated at either party's will, on written notice) and Article 77 (unilateral termination by the employer without notice or without observing the notice period obliges payment equal to wages and benefits for the unobserved period), Article 89 (dismissal compensation save for serious misconduct) and Article 91 (moral damages for termination without valid reason). Following earlier awards — 101/08-GVO, 168/09-Tek Thla Plaza, 115/10-G-Foremost and 148/11-Dynamic — it held the burden of proof lay on the party asserting the fact. The panel found the workers admitted thumbprinting the resignation letters and produced no specific evidence that the union president's call coerced them; the letters recorded voluntary resignation without pressure and the second letter confirmed payment of final wages and outstanding annual leave. Both letters were dated — 25 January and 26 March 2013. [Para 75][Para 79][Para 81][Para 83][Para 84][Para 86]

Consequently the panel found the workers had genuinely resigned, not been dismissed by the employer at its sole will, so Articles 77, 89 and 91 were not engaged and no notice pay, dismissal compensation or moral damages were owed. It did not decide whether the US$7,500 the employer said it paid had reached the workers, since the workers denied receiving it and the claim failed on the resignation finding. [Para 90][Para 91][Para 92]

**Issue 2 — court complaint.** Article 302 of the Labour Law defines a collective labour dispute as one between an employer and its workers over working conditions, the exercise of rights conferred on professional organisations, recognition of such organisations, or relations between employer and workers capable of disrupting the enterprise or social peace. Applying Chapter 12 Section 2 and earlier awards 94/04-IA, 81/06-Hong Yi and 83/06, the panel held its jurisdiction covers only collective labour disputes on those subjects, and a demand that the employer withdraw a complaint against an individual worker before the court fell outside it. It therefore refused to consider the demand. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 96][Para 98][Para 99]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a worker has signed and thumbprinted a written resignation that records voluntariness and settlement of final entitlements, the worker bears the burden of proving coercion, and absent specific evidence of it the separation is a resignation rather than a unilateral dismissal, so no notice pay, dismissal compensation or moral damages arise; and a demand that an employer withdraw a court complaint against individual workers is not a collective labour dispute within Article 302 and is outside the panel's jurisdiction. [Para 83][Para 90][Para 98]

**Obiter dicta:** Because the five workers transferred their rights to a federation that signed the MoU but were themselves leaders of an unregistered-in-evidence union, the panel observed that transfer of rights cannot convert them into MoU signatories and that the award therefore could not bind — an observation on the award's status rather than on the merits. [Para 19][Para 20]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The source is a Khmer original with severe OCR corruption. Several passages are unrecoverable, including a garbled Latin string inserted into the Article 91 discussion ("The ningristonianing agovernaphoundgymmegations actionst was who album") and the description of the strike period, which prints "ថ្ងៃទី ១១២ នឹង ១៣-រហូតមកដល់ ត្រឹម ថ្ងៃទី ២៤ ខែមករា ឆ្នាំ ២០១៣", leaving the start date unclear. The fifth worker's name appears three ways — ភន រ៉ែន, ភន វ៉ែន and រាន វ៉ែន — so she is given a bare initial here. The award attributes the US$7,500 payment to the president of one federation in para 58 while paras 18–19 treat a different federation as the MoU signatory; both are reported as printed. Para 14 carries a duplicated year ("ឆ្នាំ ២០ ២០១៣"). English renderings of company, union and arbitrator names are transliterations of the Khmer only. [Para 14][Para 41][Para 55][Para 58][Para 87]
