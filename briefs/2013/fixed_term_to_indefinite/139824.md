# Award No. 137/13 — Bowker Garment Factory (Cambodia) Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 2 August 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Ang Eng Thong, president) · Doc ID 139824

## 1. Facts
Bowker Garment Factory (Cambodia) Co Ltd, a garment factory in Kandal province with about 700 workers, dismissed Ms P Sokvy, a sewing team leader on USD 150 a month, on 29 June 2013 without notice. She had started on 21 May 2013 under a two-month probationary contract. She was an adviser to the Khmer Workers' Spirit Union, which had not yet obtained its registration certificate. The union demanded her reinstatement; the workers struck from 15 to 17 July 2013 and returned on 18 July after the Council's order No. 032/13. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 41][Para 42][Para 44][Para 48][Para 71]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the termination of Ms P Sokvy's probationary contract was connected to union discrimination? [Para 77][Para 79]
2. Whether that termination was carried out lawfully under the Labour Law? [Para 77][Para 99]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The dismissal was union discrimination, because she was an adviser to the Khmer Workers' Spirit Union; she performed well during the probation, and the employer gave her no notice of the dismissal. The 600 wrongly sewn trousers were made to the pattern measured by the department head, so the fault was not hers. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 52][Para 53][Para 57][Para 59]
**Employer (respondent):** She sewed 600 pairs of trousers wrongly during her probation, and an employer may dismiss immediately for fault while a worker is on a probationary contract; it may also dismiss without giving reasons. It had transferred the union's vice-president and secretary to other sections because they performed poorly. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 62][Para 76][Para 80]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the demand that the employer take Ms P Sokvy back into work is rejected; the award is non-binding. [Para 108][Para 109][Para 110]

On discrimination the panel applied Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law, which forbid an employer from using union membership or union activity as a ground for discipline or termination, and recalled its consistent position that the party making an allegation bears the burden of proving it. [Para 77][Para 78][Para 81]

The factual dispute was narrow: whether the department head or the team leader was responsible for measuring the trouser pattern that the workers sewed to. Because the parties disagreed on this, the panel convened a second hearing on 17 July 2013 to question other team leaders and the department head, and set 16 July 2013 as the deadline for filing evidence. The workers filed nothing on the union information the panel had asked for, and did not appear at the second hearing, although the employer, the other team leaders and the department head did. The panel held that the workers had thereby abandoned the opportunity to defend their allegation and to examine the witnesses, and had not discharged the burden of proof. The discrimination claim was rejected. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 68][Para 69][Para 93][Para 95][Para 96][Para 98]

On lawfulness the panel turned to Article 73, paragraph 1, of the Labour Law, which dispenses with the notice requirement in three cases: where the contract is one of probation or apprenticeship, where either party commits serious misconduct, and where force majeure prevents performance. Following its awards in case No. 27/03 (Scandit) and No. 12/10 (Blue Sam Sen Jury), it held that during a probationary contract an employer need neither give notice nor state reasons for dismissal. Since Ms P Sokvy was on a two-month probationary contract when she was dismissed, the termination was lawful. [Para 100][Para 101][Para 103][Para 104][Para 107]

The panel noted that the strike had ended on 18 July 2013 in compliance with its order, so it was able to continue its proceedings to the date of the award, which the parties had agreed to postpone from 23 July to 2 August 2013. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 20]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may terminate a probationary contract without notice and without giving reasons, because Article 73, paragraph 1, of the Labour Law disapplies the notice requirement during probation; a demand for reinstatement therefore fails unless the worker also proves that the termination was tainted by union discrimination. On such an allegation the burden lies on the worker, and a worker who files no supporting evidence and does not attend the hearing convened to test the allegation has not discharged it. [Para 81][Para 95][Para 107]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel recorded that the employer had transferred the union's vice-president and secretary to other sections for poor performance, but made no finding on those transfers, the dispute before it being confined to Ms P Sokvy's dismissal. It also left undetermined, as a matter of fact, whether the department head or the team leader measured the pattern. [Para 47][Para 90]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is heavily corrupted. One paragraph repeats a full sentence twice, and the operative reasoning splits into fragments across several lines so that the finding on the burden of proof must be reassembled from pieces. The panel constitution date is printed as "3 July 2019 2013", and a stray year appears in the signature block. The worker's name is printed as Pov Sokvy and Pov Sokvyreak, and the union president's name as Sroeun Hao and Srein Hao; the company name appears in several spellings. Paragraph 45, recording that she was the only worker dismissed, is garbled beyond clean reading. [Para 13][Para 45][Para 97][Para 98][Para 111]
