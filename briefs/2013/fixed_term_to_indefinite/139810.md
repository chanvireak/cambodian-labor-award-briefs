# Order No. 121/13 — M & V Garment International (Branch 3) (Arbitration Council, 24 June 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Mae Nimmit (chair) · Doc ID 139810

## 1. Facts
M & V Garment International (Branch 3) (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អឹម វ៉េ កាត់ដេរ អន្តរជាតិ (សាខា ទី៣)) operates a sewing factory on National Road 2, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh. [Para 2] After a strike over working conditions the Ministry conciliated nine demands on 14 June 2013 and six remained unresolved in report No. 709 dated 17 June 2013. [Para 21][Para 32] The company asked the Council for an order to stop the strike, and on 17 June 2013 the panel issued Order No. 026/13 requiring the strike to be suspended at once and all strikers to return to their shifts from the morning of 19 June 2013. [Para 22][Para 23] The strike continued; at the 20 June 2013 meeting the union said most members had returned, but no list of names with fingerprints was ever filed, so the panel presumed the strike was still running. [Para 24][Para 27][Para 28][Para 51]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay a travel allowance of USD 10 per month? [Para 3]
2. Whether the company must keep a health bonus of USD 5 per month on top of the legal minimum wage, because many workers faint from hardship? [Para 5]
3. Whether the company must add seniority pay of USD 1 per year for workers with 11 years' service or more, increased every year? [Para 7]
4. Whether the company must pay a USD 6 allowance to fixed-duration workers so they receive the same as undetermined-duration workers? [Para 8]
5. Whether the company must pay meal money of 3,000 riel for two hours of overtime? [Para 10]
6. Whether the company must pay a holiday bonus of USD 3 per day? [Para 12]
7. Whether the panel should continue the arbitration when the workers' return to work was not proved? [Para 37]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the six unresolved demands were the travel allowance, the USD 5 health bonus, seniority pay, parity for fixed-duration workers, the 3,000 riel overtime meal money and the USD 3 holiday bonus; at the hearing the union maintained that most of its members had already gone back to work. [Para 3][Para 5][Para 7][Para 8][Para 10][Para 12][Para 27]

**Employer (respondent):** it could not grant the travel allowance, applying Ministry Notification No. 230 dated 25 July 2012, and could not grant the health bonus, applying Notification No. 103 dated 09 April 2013; on seniority it applied Notification No. 041/11 dated 07 March 2011; it could not pay the fixed-duration allowance and could not pay overtime meal money; on holidays it applied its existing practice. [Para 4][Para 6][Para 8][Para 9][Para 11][Para 13]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no award on the merits — the panel decided not to continue the arbitration in case No. 121/13-M & V (Branch 3). [Para 52][Para 53]

The panel asked only whether it should carry on with the arbitration, citing Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council: during the panel's proceedings the parties must suspend every dispute measure, such as a strike or lockout under Article 318 of the Labour Law or any other measure that could aggravate the dispute, and must attend all meetings convened. [Para 37][Para 38]

It repeated its settled interpretation that it will not continue an arbitration while the workers are striking or the employer has locked out, and cited the same line of orders it had followed in companion cases: No. 59/06-Sang Vou, No. 3908 Choung Youv, Nos. 13709 and 138/09-Ploum Phanh, No. 110/11-Simanao, No. 04/04-IMSI, the second order of 22 April 2004 in the Siem Reap hotel case, and No. 77/05, whose point 4 refused to process a collective dispute while the strike went on. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41][Para 43][Para 45] It also relied on No. 110/11-Simanao and No. 17/13-International Fashion-Royal, where proceedings were discontinued for the same reason. [Para 46]

On the facts, the panel directed the union to file a list of returning workers with their fingerprints by 21 June 2013; nothing was filed, so the panel held the claim unproved and presumed the strike was continuing in breach of Order No. 026/13. [Para 28][Para 50][Para 51] Because no hearing on the merits took place, no evidence, witness or expert was taken beyond the company's letter, its delegation letter of 17 June 2013, the non-conciliation report and the panel's own order. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 33][Para 36]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A party asserting that a strike has ended must prove it: where a panel orders a list of returning workers with fingerprints and none is produced, the panel presumes the strike continues, and under Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 and Article 318 of the Labour Law it will not proceed to decide the merits while a dispute measure is running. [Para 37][Para 50][Para 51][Para 52]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's citation of the earlier observation that the Council may take the dispute up again once the strike stops, and its note that it retains the right to end proceedings where a party breaches its order, did not affect the discontinuance. [Para 42][Para 44]
