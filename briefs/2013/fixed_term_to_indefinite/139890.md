# Award No. 234/13 — Ever Glory (Cambodia) Garment Manufacturing Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 2 December 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-chosen and dissenting; An Nan, worker-chosen; Men Nimmith, chair) · Doc ID 139890

## 1. Facts
Ever Glory (Cambodia) Garment Manufacturing Co., Ltd. makes garments in Russey Keo district, Phnom Penh, with about 512 workers. [Para 13][Para 14] Ms T began work on 28 December 2010 as an overlocking and sewing worker, served a three-month probation and was then placed on successive six-month contracts, the last running from 28 March 2013 to 28 September 2013. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20] On 3 September 2013 she was elected vice-president of the Cambodia Asian Confederation local union, which was registered with the Ministry of Labour on 25 September 2013 and whose leadership the employer was notified of by letter. [Para 16][Para 35][Para 62] On 20 September 2013 the company notified her that it would not renew her contract; it did not seek authorisation from the Labour Inspector. [Para 29][Para 32][Para 65]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether Ms T's successive fixed-duration contracts had become a contract of undetermined duration, so that the 20 September 2013 termination was a dismissal rather than a simple non-renewal? [Para 44][Para 55]
2. Whether, as vice-president of a registered union, she enjoyed the special protection requiring the employer to obtain prior authorisation from the Labour Inspector before dismissal? [Para 57][Para 62]
3. Whether the dismissal, made without that authorisation, is null and void and she must be reinstated? [Para 66][Para 68]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the non-renewal was union discrimination — the employer renewed other workers' contracts but not hers after she became a union leader on 3 September 2013, although it knew of her position; she had worked since December 2010 and had never been disciplined. [Para 22][Para 23]
**Employer (respondent):** the non-renewal was performance-based; she produced less than her partner, earned below the US$80 minimum wage (US$62.31 in June, US$60.71 in July and US$60.72 in August 2013) and held up deliveries; it notified her on 20 September 2013 within the eight days required by article 73 and paid severance at the end of each contract, and it did not seek Labour Inspector authorisation because it understood the case as a non-renewal. [Para 26][Para 29][Para 33][Para 34]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to reinstate Ms T, and declared the dismissal null and void; this part of the award is binding under the parties' Memorandum of Understanding. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 68][Para 69]
The panel classified the dispute as a rights dispute, since it turned on the interpretation and application of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 305, and applied article 312 and clause 43 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 36][Para 39][Para 43] On the contract question it read article 67(2) as converting a fixed-duration contract into one of undetermined duration once the total length of the successive renewals exceeds two years, and article 73(5) as requiring ten days' notice for contracts over six months. [Para 46][Para 49][Para 51] Applying that to five successive six-month contracts spanning more than two years, it held the relationship was one of undetermined duration and the termination a dismissal, not a non-renewal. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57]
On protection, article 293 of the Labour Law requires Labour Inspector authorisation before dismissing a shop steward or candidate, and articles 3 and 4 of Prakas No. 305 extend that protection to three union leaders once the union has notified the employer of their names. [Para 58][Para 60][Para 61] Ms T was vice-president of a registered union and the employer had been notified, so she was protected, and no authorisation had been obtained. [Para 62][Para 65] The panel followed earlier awards (Hytex 71/09, Chung Hao 09/10, Quicksew 91/11, Alim 191/12, Long Lead 190/13, Sumi 48/13 and Pou Yuen 156/13) and held the dismissal null and void under article 4 of Prakas No. 305. [Para 66] It rejected the employer's argument that article 293 applies only to shop stewards, and its reliance on article 73. [Para 59][Para 64]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where fixed-duration contracts are renewed successively so that their total duration exceeds two years, the contract becomes one of undetermined duration and any termination is a dismissal; a union leader whose union has notified the employer of the leadership is protected under article 293 and Prakas No. 305, and a dismissal without prior Labour Inspector authorisation is null and void and attracts reinstatement. [Para 55][Para 60][Para 66]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the Labour Law shows a bias in favour of undetermined-duration contracts because employment security benefits both worker and employer, and referred to ILO Recommendation No. 166 as a non-binding aid to interpretation. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is an unofficial English translation and its OCR drops the "th" digraph throughout ("themp loyer", "athearing", "withine", "fixeduration"), while "Training" is printed "Traing". [Para 1][Para 13] The contract chronology is internally inconsistent: the third contract is given as 29 March 2012 to 29 March 2013 and the fourth as 29 September 2012 to 29 March 2013, overlapping by six months. [Para 20] Paragraph 55 describes five renewals but lists only four dates. [Para 55] The header records that arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented, and his dissenting opinion is annexed, arguing that each fixed-duration contract stayed within two years and that severance had been paid, so no conversion occurred. [Para 1][Para 74][Para 80]
