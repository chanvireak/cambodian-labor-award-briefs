# Award No. 160/13 — PSI (Population Services International Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 9 September 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mae Nimmit presiding; Seng Vouchhun, employer nominee; An Non, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139845

## 1. Facts
PSI is an international NGO and PSK (Khmer Population Health Service) a separate domestic NGO registered with the Ministry of Interior. [Para 76][Para 78] Forty-four packaging-section staff worked at PSI from between 1994 and 1996 without written contracts until 1 July 2007, when they signed five-month "out-of-framework staff" agreements that were renewed continuously until 29 July 2011, on which date they signed termination agreements and each received a lump sum. [Para 86][Para 88][Para 90] They were then re-hired by PSI on one-year fixed contracts from 1 August 2011, extended to 31 December 2012, and finally by PSK on six-month contracts from 1 January to 30 June 2013. [Para 98][Para 100] The packaging section no longer exists at PSI, and the employer objected that the dispute was not a collective labour dispute because the claimants were no longer its staff. [Para 31][Para 155]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the 44 former staff, or pay severance calculated on seniority from 1994? [Para 162]
2. Whether the employer must send staff for health checks at the Ministry of Labour at its own expense? [Para 130]
3. Whether the employer must allow staff to obtain work books from the Ministry? [Para 132]
5. Whether the employer must pay public-holiday wages correctly and carry a holiday forward when it falls on a Sunday? [Para 134]
6. Whether the employer must provide a nursery and a breastfeeding room? [Para 136]
8. Whether the employer must provide a first-aid box and adequate medicines? [Para 138]
9. Whether the employer must have a permanent nurse on site? [Para 139]
11. Whether the employer must add annual-leave days for seniority and pay the arrears? [Para 141]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the 44 were permanent, not casual, staff who worked continuously in the packaging section and were never casual day labourers; they were not allowed to read the 2011 termination agreement before thumb-printing it and were told only the amount they would receive, so it should not bind them; and they claim the termination and leave entitlements owed on seniority running from 1994. [Para 81][Para 102][Para 164]
**Employer (respondent):** the claimants were casual daily-paid workers before 2007 with no contract and no entitlements, so a claim reaching back to 1994 is misconceived because the Labour Law was only promulgated in 1997; the packaging work was formalised only from 1 July 2007; three months' notice was given; and severance, notice pay and leave were all paid under the 2011 agreement. [Para 107][Para 109][Para 110][Para 120][Para 122]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement was refused, but the employer was ordered to recalculate annual-leave compensation from each worker's first day of service and to pay moral damages equal to six months' severance; the remaining issues were refused consideration. [Para 293][Para 294][Para 295]

The panel first upheld its jurisdiction. Under Article 302 of the Labour Law a collective labour dispute needs three elements — several workers against one or more employers, a subject concerning working conditions or the exercise of occupational-organisation rights, and a capacity to disrupt the enterprise or public peace — and all three were satisfied because the dispute concerned the termination of 44 packaging staff. [Para 143][Para 147][Para 159][Para 161] The employer bore the burden of proving its objection and filed no evidence, so the objection was rejected; the panel added that a worker's present status cannot strip former staff of standing to complain of rights they say were violated. [Para 157][Para 158][Para 160][Para 153]

On Issue 1 the panel asked three questions. First, whether the 44 were permanent or casual before 1 July 2007: the employer asserted casual status but filed no evidence, so the assertion failed and the workers were found permanent. [Para 163][Para 168][Para 169] Second, what contracts they held: Article 65 allows an oral contract, and Article 67(7) provides that a fixed-duration contract not reduced to writing becomes a contract of indefinite duration, so their pre-2007 oral engagement was indefinite. [Para 170][Para 173][Para 174][Para 178] Third, what the position was from 1 July 2007: Article 67(2) caps a fixed-duration contract and its renewals at two years in total, after which it becomes indefinite, and the five-month agreement was renewed until 29 July 2011, exceeding that limit. [Para 180][Para 182][Para 191][Para 197][Para 199] The panel rejected the employer's argument that pre-1997 service falls outside the Labour Law: Article 390 applies the Law to contracts in existence when it entered force in 1997, and non-coverage of earlier relations does not erase seniority accrued before it. [Para 204][Para 206][Para 210][Para 212]

Reinstatement was nonetheless refused. The workers alleged the termination agreement was procured by deception and pressure, but they carried the burden on that allegation and filed no evidence, so the panel declined to find the agreement void on that ground. [Para 224][Para 225][Para 226] It did, however, find that the employer had shown no valid reason for the termination as Article 74 requires, having given no specific account of the operational need it invoked. [Para 236][Para 238] Because Article 13 makes the Labour Law's rules rules of public order, the agreement's waiver of statutory entitlements was void, and under Article 119(2) acceptance of a payment without protest does not amount to waiving severance, leave or allowances. [Para 276][Para 277][Para 278][Para 286][Para 287]

On the money, the panel refused notice pay because the employer proved three months' notice and the workers filed nothing in reply. [Para 247][Para 249][Para 251][Para 252] It refused further severance because the six-month maximum under Article 89 had already been paid. [Para 253][Para 256][Para 258] It ordered annual-leave compensation recalculated from each worker's actual first day, between 1994 and 1996, to 29 July 2011, with credit for what had been paid, because the employer's calculation ran only from 1 July 2007. [Para 262][Para 269][Para 270] Finally it awarded moral damages under Article 91 equal to six months' severance, since the termination lacked a valid reason. [Para 271][Para 273][Para 274] Issues 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 11 were refused consideration because no claimant party remained able to pursue entitlements as PSI staff. [Para 291][Para 292]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer ends the employment of long-serving staff by a termination agreement but can show no valid reason under Article 74, the agreement's waiver of statutory entitlements is void as contrary to the public-order rules of the Labour Law, and a worker's acceptance of the lump sum without protest does not waive the right to annual-leave compensation or to moral damages, which must be recalculated from the worker's actual first day of service. [Para 238][Para 277][Para 287][Para 288]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's discussion of ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982, which it described as non-binding but useful context for reading Article 67, and its general remark that the Labour Law shows a deliberate preference for indefinite-duration contracts, did not affect any order it made. [Para 184][Para 188][Para 190]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The lump sum is printed as USD 884.25 in the quoted termination agreement and as USD 868 in the panel's findings and order. [Para 93][Para 97][Para 257] The list of issues left for arbitration is given as 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 10, but issue 10 had been withdrawn and the eighth surviving issue is numbered 11 in the reasons and the order. [Para 28][Para 29][Para 291] Length of service is printed as "15 to 17 years" at para 242 and "150 years" at para 265. [Para 242][Para 265] The 2013 PSK contracts are said to run to 30 June 2013 while the union's complaint was filed on 25 June 2013. [Para 100][Para 21] Paragraph 243 carries stray fragments ("27 years", "the 2015 change") spliced into the notice calculation, and the employer's name is given in three styles — PSI, PSK and "Khmer Population Health Service" — with the two organisations distinguished only from para 76. [Para 1][Para 36][Para 76][Para 243] The individual workers are named in the source; their names are not reproduced here.
