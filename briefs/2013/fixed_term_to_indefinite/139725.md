# Award No. 106/13 — Gladpeer Garment Factory (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 2 July 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun, employer-chosen; Sin Kim Sean, worker-chosen; Pen Bunchhea, chair) · Doc ID 139725

## 1. Facts
Gladpeer Garment Factory (Cambodia) Ltd is a garment manufacturer employing 5,200 workers; the claimant is the local union of C.CAWDU at the factory. [Para 14] The union brought three demands: compliance with an earlier award, repayment of union contribution fees deducted for the Cambodian Labour Union (CLU) after members had withdrawn, and reinstatement with back pay of eight dismissed C.CAWDU activists. [Para 1] At the hearing the workers withdrew Issue 1 and part 1 of Issue 2 was settled by the employer's statement that the deductions had stopped, leaving part 2 of Issue 2 and Issue 3. [Para 7][Para 18] The workers filed no evidence by the 17 June 2013 deadline, and no certificate of union registration or written authorisation for their representatives. [Para 39][Para 41]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reimburse 1,000 riel to 500 workers whose wages were deducted in February 2013 and 2,000 riel to 300–400 workers whose wages were deducted in March 2013 as CLU contribution fees? [Para 49]
2. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr S Sarat (ID S-26-851), Mr P Komphak (ID S-25-510) and Mr C Chhaily (ID S-23-564) with back pay from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 67]
3. As a threshold matter, whether the eight workers in attendance and the C.CAWDU officers had legal standing to represent the workforce in this dispute? [Para 44]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The right to authorise or revoke a union-fee deduction belongs exclusively to the worker by written letter, so an employer that keeps deducting after a withdrawal letter interferes in union affairs and must refund, following the 2012 Goldfame award; the three activists were dismissed, not expired, and Soy Sarat had over two years' service making his contract of undetermined duration. [Para 19][Para 21][Para 31]
**Employer (respondent):** The deduction figures are inflated and unverifiable — some workers thumbprinted withdrawal letters three times — and the employer merely acts as a conduit, receiving nothing itself, so any refund must be sought from CLU; it never dismissed the three workers, whose fixed-duration contracts simply expired. [Para 24][Para 26][Para 36]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider both remaining demands, declining to order reimbursement of the 1,000 riel and 2,000 riel deductions and declining to order reinstatement or back pay for the three named workers; the rights-dispute portion is a binding award under the 3 October 2012 MoU. [Para 91]

On standing, the panel applied Article 268 of the Labour Law, which makes registration with the Ministry in Charge of Labour the condition for a professional organisation to enjoy the rights and benefits recognised by the law, and read those benefits as including the right to represent members before the Arbitration Council, following Awards 62/06-Quicksew, 30/08-E Garment and 31/08-South. [Para 42] Clause 19 of Prakas 99/04 requires a representative to be "expressly authorized in writing"; the panel read that as permitting any person to represent a party if written authorisation exists, following Awards 161/09-Prek Treng Trading, 43/10-Ming Jian and 144/12-E Garment. [Para 43] Because neither the registration certificate nor any authorisation letter was filed by the deadline, the panel held the eight workers present had no legal right to act as union leaders and the C.CAWDU officers no right to represent other workers, and it confined the claim to those eight individuals. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 47]

On Issue 2, the panel classified the demand as a rights dispute because it turned on Article 129 of the Labour Law, which allows a worker to authorise wage deductions for the union to which he belongs, in writing, revocable at any time. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 53] Paragraph 5, Clause 5 of Prakas 305 of 22 November 2001 requires the written request at least 15 days before the pay date and obliges the employer to implement it, but the same provision lets a worker request in writing that deductions stop. [Para 54][Para 58] The panel held the right to grant or refuse deduction is the worker's exclusive right expressed in writing, so on receipt of a clear letter of revocation the employer must neither deduct nor retain any part of the fee, following Awards 46/10-E. Garment, 15/11-Golden Gain Shoe and 154/11-B & N. [Para 62][Para 63] The demand nonetheless failed for want of proof: no authorisation letter naming the workers seeking reimbursement was filed, so the panel lacked sufficient factual evidence and rejected the claim. [Para 65][Para 67]

On Issue 3, the panel treated the demand as a rights dispute because it rested on contract type and dismissal under the Labour Law. [Para 67] For Mr S Sarat, Paragraph 2 of Article 67 caps a fixed-duration contract, including renewals, at two years and converts any excess into a contract of undetermined duration, as interpreted in Award 10/03-Jacqsintex. [Para 68][Para 70][Para 71] The panel reasoned that the law's bias toward undetermined-duration contracts reflects employment security and that the two-year limit is a maximum total, not a limit on each renewal, drawing support from non-binding ILO Recommendation No. 166. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 75] The workers' own claim put his service beyond two years, but no contracts were produced and no reason given, so the panel could not tell whether he was dismissed or had reached his contract's end and rejected the demand. [Para 75][Para 78][Para 79] For Mr P Komphak and Mr C Chhaily, service under two years left fixed-duration contracts, and Paragraph 1 of Article 73 provides that such a contract terminates at its stated ending date; neither party can force renewal without agreement, following Awards 122/10-Meng Yan and 105/11-Goldfame Garment. [Para 80][Para 83] Again no contracts were filed, so the panel could not decide dismissal versus expiry and rejected reinstatement and back pay. [Para 86][Para 87][Para 89]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union-fee deduction and its revocation are both exercised by the individual worker through a written letter to the employer; once a worker gives a clear written withdrawal, any further deduction or retention of the fee is unlawful, and the employer must refund it. [Para 62][Para 63]

A claim of reinstatement on the footing that repeated fixed-term renewals passed the two-year ceiling of Article 67 fails if the claimant files no contracts, because the panel cannot then determine either total service length or whether employment ended by dismissal or by expiry. [Para 79][Para 87]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the law's preference for undetermined-duration contracts serves both workers and employers, since long-term employment increases commitment to the work, and it treated ILO Recommendation No. 166 as a useful but non-binding aid to construing Article 67. [Para 72][Para 74]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is an unofficial English translation of a Khmer original with consistent OCR loss of "th" ("themployer", "athearing", "wit h"), which occasionally obscures a word's sense. [Para 2][Para 6] The non-conciliation report is printed as No. 593 dated 22 May 2013 in the opening statement but as No. 553 dated 22 April 2013 at Paragraph 5; both are reported as printed. [Para 14][Para 5] Worker names vary between paragraphs: Soy Sarat / Soy Sarath, Phuong Komphak / Phoung Kompheak, Chen Chhaily / Chen Chaily; C.CAWDU appears once as "C.CAUDU". [Para 29][Para 68][Para 89] Paragraph 29 says five workers withdrew but lists five ID numbers while elsewhere eight are enumerated; two identifier formats are used (S-21-607 and "S-21 607"). [Para 29][Para 46] The decision is headed "Decline to consider" although the reasoning in fact rejects the demands on the evidence. [Para 91]
