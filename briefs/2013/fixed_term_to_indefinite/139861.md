# Award No. 185/13 — Golden Hing (Cambodia) Garment Manufacturing (Arbitration Council, 23 September 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Seng Vouch Hun (employer-appointed), An Vira (worker-appointed), Kong Phalha (president) · Doc ID 139861

## 1. Facts
Golden Hing (Cambodia) Garment Manufacturing, a Phnom Penh garment factory with about 304 workers, dismissed two workers — Ms D on 26 July 2013 and Ms S on 24 July 2013 — while both held elected posts in a factory union still awaiting registration. [Para 68][Para 159] A 13-point collective demand went to conciliation; twelve points were settled and only point 1, the reinstatement of the union leadership, reached the panel. [Para 22][Para 28]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the two dismissed union leaders, Ms D and Ms S? [Para 29][Para 75]
   Within that single remaining issue the panel examined three sub-questions: whether candidates elected before the union obtained registration enjoy protection from dismissal; whether the employer discriminated on union grounds; and whether reinstatement must be ordered. [Para 126][Para 170][Para 183]
   Points 2 to 13 of the demand — canteen money, overtime, probation, rest-room access, attendance bonuses, section-head wages, transfers, strike pay, anti-union harassment and the return of a mechanic — were resolved by party agreement and were not decided. [Para 28]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** both women were elected leaders of the factory union on 13 July 2013 and were dismissed for that reason; the employer must reinstate them and pay what is owed. [Para 76][Para 175]
**Employer (respondent):** both were probationary workers on written probation contracts terminable at will, and were let go because other workers refused by thumbprint to accept their return and because the two pressured colleagues over overtime; the employer did not know of their union posts. [Para 77][Para 96][Para 98]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement was refused for both women, and the employer was ordered to pay Ms D severance including moral damages (the sum to be fixed by the competent court), payment in lieu of notice equal to seven days' wages, final wages if still outstanding, and unused annual leave; and to pay Ms S unused annual leave and a contract-end indemnity of at least five per cent of the wages paid over the contract. [Para 286][Para 287]

On protection, the panel applied Article 293 of the Labour Law with Articles 3 and 4 of Prakas 305 (22 November 2001): election candidates are protected as staff delegates for 45 days before the poll and 45 days after it, and founders and members are protected from the date of the registration application until 30 days after registration. Following Awards 40/10 Meng Yang and 154/13 Bou Kheour, a candidate elected before registration is protected from the ballot date until registration. [Para 128][Para 145] The panel found the first two conditions met — the women were elected leaders and the dismissals fell inside the registration window — but the third failed: notice letter 04/013 bore no employer acknowledgement and the workers offered no evidence of any other practicable means of notice, so protection under Article 293 was not established. [Para 159][Para 164][Para 168]

On discrimination, the panel held the claimant carries the burden and, on the same evidentiary gap, found no union discrimination in the dismissals. [Para 176][Para 182]

For Ms D the employer produced a probation contract bearing no signature of hers, so no probation was proved; lacking any written contract, her engagement became one of undetermined duration and the dismissal needed a valid reason related to her aptitude or conduct or the needs of the enterprise. [Para 189][Para 193] No such reason was proved against the employer's allegations. Because the employer refused reinstatement, the panel awarded the statutory payments but denied Article 89 severance (service under six months), held that moral damages under Articles 91 and 94 fall to the court to quantify, and added pay in lieu of notice under Articles 75 and 77 plus final wages and annual leave. [Para 221][Para 232][Para 238]

For Ms S the probation contract was written, dated and thumbprinted, so it stood as a fixed-duration contract; a probationary worker needs no reason to be let go, but Article 73(6) still gives a contract-end indemnity of at least five per cent, and Article 166 gives unused annual leave. No moral damages were due, because a probation contract differs in nature from an ordinary fixed-duration one. [Para 269][Para 276][Para 284]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker elected as a union leader before the union is registered is protected from dismissal from the date of the election until registration, but the protection only operates where the union has notified the employer of the protected names by any practicable means; absent proof of that notification the dismissal is not unlawful and reinstatement will not be ordered. [Para 145][Para 167]

**Obiter dicta:** co-workers have no legal standing to decide whether a dismissed worker is taken back — only the employer is party to the contract of employment; and a probation contract, though fixed-duration, differs in nature from an ordinary fixed-duration contract. [Para 204][Para 281]
