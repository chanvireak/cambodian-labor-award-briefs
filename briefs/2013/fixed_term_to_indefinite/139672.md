# Award No. 43/13 and 51/13 — Asia Form Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 29 March 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mar Sambor Na (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Kong Phalla (president) · Doc ID 139672

## 1. Facts
Asia Form Co., Ltd, registered in 2001, designed websites, ran training and did clipping and masking (កាត់ត និងលាបពណ៌) work; it stopped the clipping and masking operation in 2012 and now employs about 35 people. [Para 75][Para 77][Para 78] The employer terminated 18 workers in that section on 31 December 2012 after notice dated 14 December 2012, citing a 30 per cent fall in work volume and a halving of orders; a further 17 workers were dismissed when the whole section was shut. [Para 86][Para 108][Para 177] At the second hearing the workers withdrew three of their four claims and one of the two in the second file, leaving a single claim for statutory termination compensation for all 18. [Para 27]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay termination compensation under the Labour Law to the 18 workers dismissed at its will, calculated according to the type of each employment contract? [Para 116]
2. Whether Mr H, whose successive fixed-duration contracts ran beyond two years, held an unlimited-duration contract and was dismissed rather than simply not renewed, so as to attract a dismissal indemnity, notice pay, unused annual leave and final wages? [Para 130]
3. Whether the 17 workers dismissed when the whole clipping and masking section closed are owed a dismissal indemnity, moral damages, notice pay and unused annual leave, given that the employer did not prove it notified the labour inspector of the collective layoff? [Para 179]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The terminations had no valid reason and the contracts had not expired; the employer never formally declared insolvency or redundancy, and contracts running two years or more should have been converted to unlimited duration. [Para 81][Para 100]
**Employer (respondent):** It did not dismiss anyone but declined to renew fixed-duration contracts because there was no work left in clipping and masking after the 2009 and 2012 shutdowns; it also treated the first two months as training rather than probation, so no seniority accrued from them. [Para 83][Para 85][Para 89] It did not appear at the second hearing. [Para 26]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must pay termination compensation to Mr H (dismissal indemnity of 30 days' wages and allowances, one month's notice pay, unused annual leave and 8 days' wages for October 2012) and to the 17 workers (5 per cent of wages for the three fixed-duration workers; 30 or 45 days' indemnity, half that amount again as moral damages, 12 days' notice pay and unused annual leave for the fourteen long-serving workers); moral damages were refused for Mr H. [Para 161][Para 193][Para 211][Para 229]

The panel found the two-month opening period was probation, not training, because the employer produced no evidence of a training programme, and on that basis Mr H's successive contracts totalled more than 24 months. [Para 92][Para 93][Para 128] Applying article 67(2) of the Labour Law, and following Award 10/03, it held that renewals taking the total beyond two years convert a fixed-duration contract into one of unlimited duration, so the ending of the relationship on 8 October 2012 without notice was a dismissal, not a non-renewal. [Para 118][Para 119][Para 129][Para 130] The fall in work volume was a proper reason under article 74, so moral damages under article 91 were refused, but the absence of notice still required one month's pay under articles 75 and 77, unused annual leave under article 166 and the outstanding 8 days of October wages under article 116. [Para 136][Para 148][Para 152][Para 160]

For the 17 workers the panel applied article 95 on collective layoffs. Because the entire section was closed, no comparison of professional ability or seniority was possible and that part of the procedure was excused; but the employer, absent from the hearing, proved no notification to the labour inspector, so the procedure was not followed. [Para 175][Para 178][Para 179] Three workers on contracts under 24 months received the 5 per cent termination indemnity and moral damages for early termination under article 73; the fourteen with more than 24 months were treated as unlimited-duration and received 30 or 45 days' indemnity under article 89. [Para 185][Para 190][Para 199] Because the article 95 procedure was breached, each of the fourteen also received moral damages equal to half the dismissal indemnity. [Para 213] Notice pay of 12 days and unused annual leave were added; final wages had already been paid. [Para 219][Para 223][Para 226]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer closes an entire section and dismisses every worker in it, the comparative-ability and seniority ranking required by article 95 of the Labour Law cannot be performed and is excused, but the duty to notify the labour inspector of the collective layoff survives, and breach of it entitles each affected worker to moral damages equal to half the dismissal indemnity. [Para 175][Para 213]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the Labour Law leans towards unlimited-duration contracts because job security benefits both sides, and that ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982, though not binding, is useful context for reading article 67. [Para 121][Para 126]

[Para 1][Para 88] Paragraph 206 contains an untranslatable insertion ("៧៦៦៦០ លាន ផោន") in place of a legal phrase. [Para 206] The compensation tables survive only as fragments: amounts in days are separated from the names they attach to, one worker appears twice under number 5, and the moral-damages column cannot be matched line by line. [Para 102][Para 108] Deposits and page numbers are interleaved mid-sentence ("-១៣-", "-២១-"), confirming page breaks lost in OCR.
