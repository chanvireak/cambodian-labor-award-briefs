# Award No. 161/13 — Sin Chang Sing Garment (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 22 August 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phal presiding; Ing Sothy, employer nominee; An Non, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139844

## 1. Facts
Sin Chang Sing Garment (Cambodia), a garment factory at Toul Sangke, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, employed about 800 workers. [Para 38] Mr C had worked there since 2011 on successive three-month fixed-duration contracts, and on 10 April 2012 signed a contract of indefinite duration; he worked in the sewing section on a base wage of USD 80, an attendance bonus of USD 10 and a travel and accommodation allowance of USD 7. [Para 42][Para 45] On 19 June 2013 the factory union held an election at which 37 workers voted and he was elected president with 17 votes; the union had still not received its registration certificate from the Ministry when he was dismissed. [Para 41][Para 53][Para 54] The employer dismissed him without prior notice, and later agreed to pay five heads of termination compensation instead of taking him back. [Para 46][Para 66]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back Mr C, president of the Sin Chang Sing free workers' union, and let him work normally again? [Para 2]
2. Whether the dismissal was connected with anti-union discrimination? [Para 70]
3. Whether the dismissal was based on misconduct? [Para 70]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissal was anti-union discrimination, because he was the union president, and he committed no misconduct at all, so he must be reinstated; the union notified the employer of the election result through the security guard on 23 June 2013, the guard carried the letter inside and brought it back saying the head of administration was absent, and the same notice went by EMS post on 24 June 2013. [Para 47][Para 56][Para 67]
**Employer (respondent):** he worked badly and repeatedly ignored his managers' instructions, and the dismissal had nothing to do with union discrimination because the employer did not know he was a union president; the employer admits it warned him only orally, never in writing; it refuses reinstatement and offers instead the five statutory termination payments. [Para 48][Para 50][Para 51][Para 66][Para 69]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to take back Mr C and let him resume work. [Para 111][Para 112] The award is non-binding and would become final eight days after notification unless a party objected in writing to the Minister of Labour. [Para 113]

On the discrimination question the panel applied Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law, which forbid an employer to use union membership or union activity as the ground for a disciplinary or termination decision, and recalled its consistent position that the party making the allegation carries the burden of proving it. [Para 70][Para 71][Para 74] That burden required the workers to show the employer knew of Mr C's union office before the dismissal date. [Para 80] The panel weighed the two channels of notice and found neither sufficient: the security guard was never identified and the workers could not say when the letter was handed over, while the EMS receipt of 24 June 2013 recorded dispatch but not any refusal by the employer. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58][Para 61] It therefore held the burden undischarged and rejected the allegation of anti-union discrimination. [Para 90][Para 91] It also declined to consider the employer's objection of 31 June 2013 to the union's registration, because that date fell after the dismissal. [Para 62][Para 65]

On the misconduct question the panel took Article 74 of the Labour Law, under which an indefinite-duration contract may be terminated at either party's will but no dismissal may rest on anything other than a valid reason relating to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the operational requirements of the enterprise. [Para 92][Para 94][Para 95] Following Award No. 51/08-SD, it accepted that dismissal is the employer's right but must be exercised in conformity with the contract, the internal regulations and the Labour Law. [Para 97][Para 98] Here the employer conceded it had never issued a written warning, produced no record of the oral warnings it claimed, identified no article of its internal regulations and no article of the Labour Law that the worker had breached, and filed no specific evidence of the poor performance alleged. [Para 99][Para 100][Para 102][Para 103][Para 104] The panel accordingly presumed the dismissal was not founded on misconduct, found no valid reason within Article 74, and ordered reinstatement. [Para 105][Para 106][Para 111]

Arbitrator Ing Sothy, the employer nominee, dissented from the order alone. [Para 115] He accepted the facts and the reasoning but would have awarded compensation rather than reinstatement: the employer had shown a fixed determination not to take the worker back, the contract was of indefinite duration, and the worker enjoyed no protection as a union organisation. [Para 116][Para 117][Para 119][Para 120] He would have ordered the five termination payments — severance, medical costs, payment in lieu of notice, accrued annual leave and final wages. [Para 121]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that dismisses a worker for poor performance or disobedience must prove it: where it never issued a written warning, can point to no article of its internal regulations or of the Labour Law that was breached, and files no specific evidence of the alleged fault, the panel will presume the dismissal was not based on misconduct, find no valid reason under Article 74 of the Labour Law, and order reinstatement. [Para 104][Para 105][Para 111]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that an allegation of anti-union discrimination fails unless the complainant shows the employer knew of the union role before the dismissal, and that a union's registration objection dated after the dismissal is irrelevant to it, were made in the course of rejecting that allegation and did not affect the order to reinstate. [Para 65][Para 80][Para 90]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The dismissal date is printed two ways: 26 July 2013 in the agreed facts and 27 June 2013 in the reasons and at the points where the panel fixes the cut-off for the employer's knowledge. [Para 46][Para 64][Para 80][Para 90] The employer's objection to the union's registration is dated "31 June 2013", a date that does not exist. [Para 62] The postal receipt date is given as 24 June 2013 in the body but the covering EMS slip is listed as issued 24 June 2013 while the information session is recorded as 8 August 2013, and the panel's own account of what the receipt proves is repeated verbatim at paras 58-61 and 86-89. [Para 28][Para 58][Para 86] The dissent's list of termination payments is headed "4" but enumerates five heads. [Para 121] The worker is named throughout the award; his name is redacted here, and the two unions' full styles appear in three spellings ("Sin Chang Sin", "Sin Chang Sing", "Sin Chang Xing") across the documents listed. [Para 1][Para 19][Para 25][Para 29] The arbitrator's name is printed as "Kong Phal" in the composition and as "Kong Phalha" in the signature block. [Para 1][Para 114]
