# Award No. 240/13 — CIIDG Erdos Hongjun Electric Power Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 5 December 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana appointed by the employer party, An Nan appointed by the worker party, Tang Tri chair appointed by the two) · Doc ID 139900

## 1. Facts
Six trainees were sent by CIIDG Erdos Hongjun Electric Power Co., Ltd, a coal-fired power plant at Kampenh, Stung Hav, Preah Sihanouk, to two years' training at a Chinese university under a training-scholarship agreement, which obliged them to work at the company's plant for at least five years after completing it and stated a salary of USD 350 to USD 1,200 a month. [Para 1][Para 99][Para 108][Para 146] All six failed the examination of 16 October 2013, and the employer therefore refused to take them on as full employees and refused the USD 350 they demanded. [Para 2][Para 3][Para 103] The award is expressly non-binding. [Para 15]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take the six trainees on as full employees (បុគ្គលិកពេញសិទ្ធិ)? [Para 110][Para 173]
2. Whether the employer must pay the six trainees a wage of USD 350 a month or more? [Para 157][Para 174]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** they are trainees governed by the Labour Law and sitting within the framework of an apprenticeship; the company's own scholarship notice and its website promised a minimum of USD 350 a month on return, and the cost of goods has risen while the plant sits far from town. [Para 101][Para 102][Para 110][Para 168]
**Employer (respondent):** the six have not become employees of the company and have done no work for it; under the scholarship agreement a trainee must complete all four training modules and pass the examination to qualify for a post, and none of them did. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 105][Para 109]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider (មិនពិចារណា) both claims and ordered nothing; the award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification if no party objects to the Minister of Labour. [Para 173][Para 174][Para 175]

On issue 1 the panel asked first whether the parties stood in an apprenticeship relationship (កិច្ចសន្យាហ្វឹកហ្វឺនកូនជាង) governed by the Labour Law. [Para 111] Article 51 defines that contract as one by which an enterprise head undertakes to give full-time vocational education, in a technical manner, to a person who agrees to work as an apprentice on agreed terms and for an agreed duration, which may not exceed two years. [Para 111][Para 112][Para 113] Articles 58, 59 and 60 set the master's duties of supervision, of informing the parents of serious faults, of teaching and of not exceeding the apprentice's strength, and the apprentice's duty to obey; Articles 52 and 53 require the contract to be in writing, to contain eight prescribed particulars, and to be inspected, signed and registered by the labour inspector. [Para 114][Para 116][Para 118][Para 120][Para 121][Para 122][Para 130][Para 131] Article 56 provides that an apprentice who completes the four-part education and masters the trade leaves apprenticeship and becomes a skilled worker. [Para 132]

The panel found the scholarship agreement did not satisfy the substance or the form of an apprenticeship contract: the trainees did not work for the employer as apprentices, and no labour inspector examined, signed or registered the agreement. [Para 137][Para 138] It therefore held there was no apprenticeship relationship in the sense of the Labour Law. [Para 139]

It then asked whether there was instead an employment contract. [Para 140] Article 664 of the Civil Code defines the employment contract as the exchange of work for wages, and Article 65 of the Labour Law as the contract creating the employment relationship between worker and employer, subject to the rules of contract law and in whatever form the parties agree. [Para 140][Para 141][Para 142][Para 143] The panel found that the trainees had not become employees and had performed no work for the company, so no employment contract existed. [Para 109][Para 144][Para 158] No provision of the Labour Law or any other instrument gave the Council a basis to decide the claim, and it declined to consider the demand that the six be made full employees. [Para 155][Para 156]

On issue 2 the panel noted that the claim presupposed employees who do not yet exist: the USD 350 was demanded for the six at the point when they become full employees. [Para 157][Para 161][Para 162] It followed its own decision in Case No. 10/03-Jacksontic, that the Council is created to settle labour disputes and not problems that have not yet arisen, and its awards No. 68/04-CPV, No. 36/06-Mondolkiri, No. 58/07-08 Star Sport Wear and No. 155/13-Cat, in which claims whose subject was a future dispute were refused. [Para 163][Para 164][Para 165] The claim rested on facts neither existing nor occurring, so the question of a wage for full employees had not yet arisen. [Para 167][Para 168][Para 169] Even so, the panel repeated the finding under issue 1 that no legal provision conferred jurisdiction on it to decide the claim, and held it had no jurisdiction over issue 2 either. [Para 170][Para 171] It declined to consider the demand for USD 350 or more a month for the six when they become full employees. [Para 172]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A trainee sent abroad under a training-scholarship agreement is neither an apprentice nor an employee where the agreement was never signed and registered by the labour inspector and the trainee never worked for the enterprise as an apprentice; the panel will not order the enterprise to hire the trainee, because no provision of the Labour Law gives it a basis to do so. A claim for the wage such a trainee would earn once employed is a claim about a dispute that has not yet arisen, and the panel will decline it for want of jurisdiction. [Para 138][Para 139][Para 155][Para 163][Para 170]

**Obiter dicta:** the recital of Articles 56 to 60 on the master's duties and the apprentice's obligations, and the observation that the company's website and scholarship notice promised USD 350 to USD 1,200, are general remarks on the apprenticeship regime and the employer's own advertising; they were not needed to decline either claim. [Para 101][Para 108][Para 116]
