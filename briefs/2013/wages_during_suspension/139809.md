# Order No. 119/13 — Pu Yien (Arbitration Council, 25 June 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Mae Nimmit (chair) · Doc ID 139809

## 1. Facts
Pu Yien company (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ពូ យៀន) employed the workers at its Phnom Penh premises; after a collective dispute over working conditions the Ministry's labour-dispute department conciliated 17 demands on 11 June 2013 and six remained unresolved in report No. 695 dated 12 June 2013. [Para 1][Para 21][Para 22] The workers struck, the company asked the Council for an order to stop the strike, and on 14 June 2013 the panel issued Order No. 024/13 requiring an immediate suspension of the strike and a return to work that afternoon. [Para 23][Para 24] The workers did not comply; the panel summoned both sides on 17 June 2013, and both confirmed the strike was still running. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 27]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must continue the employment contracts of Samol Sopheap and Va Kim Sron? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company must pay a midday meal allowance? [Para 4]
3. Whether the company must open an additional entry and exit gate quickly? [Para 6]
4. Whether workers in Building A must be allowed to leave five minutes before 11:00 because the site is far away? [Para 8]
5. Whether women who have given birth must receive USD 10 per month as a milk allowance from month 01 to month 18? [Para 10]
6. Whether the company must provide a nursery for children aged one and a half to three years, or else USD 10 per month? [Para 12][Para 13]
7. Whether the panel should continue the arbitration at all when the workers were still striking during the proceedings? [Para 34]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the six unresolved demands — contract continuation for two named workers, a midday meal allowance, a second gate, an early release for Building A, a USD 10 monthly milk allowance and a nursery or USD 10 in lieu — were the demands their strike was pursuing, and they remained on strike when summoned. [Para 2][Para 4][Para 6][Para 10][Para 12][Para 27]

**Employer (respondent):** on each of the six demands the company stated it could not grant it, and it asked the Council to issue an order directing the workers to end the strike. [Para 3][Para 5][Para 7][Para 9][Para 14][Para 23]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no award on the merits — the panel decided not to continue the arbitration in case No. 119/13-Pu Yien. [Para 47][Para 48]

The panel framed the only question it felt able to decide: whether to carry on with the arbitration. [Para 34] Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council requires the parties to suspend every dispute measure, such as a strike or lockout (as provided in Article 318 of the Labour Law) or any other measure that could aggravate the dispute, throughout the panel's proceedings, and requires both parties to attend all meetings the panel convenes. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 36]

The panel recalled its own earlier interpretation of that provision: it will not continue an arbitration whenever the worker party is striking or the employer has locked out. [Para 37] It cited a run of prior orders to the same effect — No. 5906-Sang Rou, No. 3908 Choung Youv, Nos. 13709 and 13809-Ploum Phaprum, No. 110/11-Simanao, No. 04/04-MSI, the second order of 22 April 2004 in No. 18/04 against the Siem Reap hotels, and No. 77/05, where point 4 stated that if the workers continued striking the Council would not process the collective dispute. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 41][Para 43] It also followed No. 110/11-Simanao and No. 17/13-International Fashion-Royal, where proceedings were discontinued for the same reason. [Para 44]

On the facts, the panel found that both parties agreed the workers were still on strike and had not obeyed the order of 11 June 2013 [as printed] calling for a return to work; it therefore declined to proceed. [Para 46][Para 47] No evidence, witness or expert was taken beyond the company's letter of 13 June 2013, because no hearing on the merits was ever held. [Para 28][Para 33]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
While an arbitration is pending, a strike by the workers or a lockout by the employer suspends the panel's process under Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 and Article 318 of the Labour Law, and a panel will not continue to decide the merits until the dispute measure actually stops — non-compliance with an order to end a strike is by itself a sufficient ground to discontinue the case. [Para 34][Para 36][Para 37][Para 47]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's statement that it keeps the right to end proceedings where a party breaches any term of its order, and the cited observation that the Council may take the dispute up again once the strike ends, were not necessary to the discontinuance ordered here. [Para 40][Para 42]
