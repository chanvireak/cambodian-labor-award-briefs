# Award No. 49/13 — Eternal Global Sporting (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 14 March 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · Doc ID 139664

## 1. Facts
Eternal Global Sporting (Cambodia) operates at Damnak Ampil, Ang Snuol, Kandal. [Para 1] The union filed six demands, conciliation failed on 23 February 2013, and the case reached the Secretariat on 26 February 2013. [Para 10][Para 14][Para 15] The workers had been on strike since 22 February 2013; the panel issued order 009/13 on 27 February 2013 requiring the strike to be suspended immediately and all strikers to return to their shifts from the afternoon of 28 February. [Para 16] They returned on 1 and 2 March but resumed the strike from 4 March and were still striking at the hearing on 7 March. [Para 17][Para 20]

## 2. Issues in dispute
The six unresolved demands were:
1. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of USD 13 to each worker? [Para 2]
2. Whether the employer must stop recording a card swipe when workers go to the toilet? [Para 3]
3. Whether the employer must stop compelling workers to work overtime? [Para 4]
4. Whether the employer must stop transferring workers at will? [Para 5]
5. Whether workers doing the same work must receive equal pay? [Para 6]
6. Whether the employer must install three additional fingerprint scanners? [Para 7]

The panel decided none of them, closing the case on the strike. [Para 41][Para 42]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the strike was called to press the six demands for better working conditions, and from 4 March 2013 the workers resumed it and kept it going to the day of the hearing. [Para 13][Para 20]
**Employer (respondent):** it asked the panel on 26 February 2013 to issue an order suspending the strike, and appeared through a GMAC representative and its own staff. [Para 1][Para 21]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to continue the arbitration and closed the case without any finding on the six demands or any order against either party. [Para 41][Para 42]

The only question the panel took up was whether it could proceed at all. [Para 30] Article 20 of Prakas 099 of 21 April 2004 requires the parties, throughout the panel's proceedings, to suspend every dispute measure, expressly including a strike or lockout under article 318 of the Labour Law, and to attend every meeting to which the panel summons them. [Para 30][Para 31] The panel read that article, as it has in earlier awards, to mean that it will not continue an arbitration while the workers are on strike. [Para 32]

It relied on a line of its own decisions: the notice of 29 January 2004 in case 04/04 Im S I, holding that a party has no right to strike while its case is before the panel and that the panel cannot decide or issue an award while the strike lasts, though it may take the case up again once all workers stop striking; the second order of 22 April 2004 in the hotel cases 18/04, ordering all workers to stop strike, demonstration or disturbance on hotel premises from 23 April 2004 and reserving the right to close the case if any party breached the order; the second order of 24 December 2005 in case 77/06 Franco Niet Thing, point 4, to the same effect; and the awards in 110/11 Simano and 17/13 Internal Fashion 2 Royal, where the panel likewise refused to continue because the strike persisted. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 35][Para 36][Para 37][Para 38]

On the facts the panel found that the workers were still on strike and had not complied with its order of 27 February 2013, having returned only briefly on 1 and 2 March before resuming from 4 March. [Para 40] It therefore refused to continue and closed the file, expressly adopting the reasoning of the earlier awards. [Para 39][Para 41]

Because it did not take evidence on the merits, the panel recorded that it made no finding of fact on any of the six issues in the non-conciliation report. [Para 29]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
While a collective dispute is before the panel, the parties must suspend any strike or lockout; if the workers resume or continue a strike after an order to suspend it, the panel will not continue the arbitration and will close the case without deciding the demands, leaving them unresolved until the strike ends and the case is taken up again. [Para 30][Para 32][Para 41]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's statement that it retains the right to close proceedings where a party breaches an order, and its reference to the 2004 hotel orders as authority for ordering strikers off the premises, are general observations not needed for the closure ordered here. [Para 35][Para 36]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award contradicts itself on whether a hearing took place: paragraph 19 records that both parties attended the hearing on 7 March 2013 as summoned, while paragraph 29 says no findings of fact were made because the panel "did not conduct a hearing"; the reasons that follow rest on the hearing at which the resumption of the strike was reported. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 29]
- The employer's name is printed in several forms — Eternal Global Sporting, "Eternal Global Sport Thing", "Eternal Global Sporting (Cambodia)" and "Eternal Global Sport Thing (Cambodia)" — and the company is once rendered "Eternal Global Club". [Para 1][Para 21][Para 42]
- The worker-appointed arbitrator appears as An Non on the face of the award [Para 1] but as "An Lan" in the signature block [Para 43].
- The award refers at [Para 39] to "the decision of the Arbitration Council 40 in the above cases", an OCR intrusion of a page number into the sentence.
- Dates of the two conciliation events are inconsistent: the conciliation minute is dated 23 February 2013 [Para 23] while the file was received from the Kandal department on 22 February 2013 and the strike began that day [Para 13][Para 16].
