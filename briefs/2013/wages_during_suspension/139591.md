# Award No. 03/13 — Tai Wang Printing Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 31 January 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chea, chair) · Doc ID 139591

## 1. Facts
The company makes shoe boxes at Trapeang Thloung, Phnom Penh, and employed about 150 workers, of whom 84 belonged to the Khmer Workers' Power Union at the factory. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 41] The employer pays a monthly "skill allowance" (ប្រាក់ជំនាញ) ranging from 1 to 150 USD, assessed twice a year on seniority and daily performance, and reduces it proportionally for days not worked. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 46][Para 50] Four of six unsettled demands survived conciliation: retention of the skill allowance during authorised personal leave, during periods when the company has no work for a section, during certified sick leave, and retention of wages and allowances when a worker arrives fifteen minutes late. [Para 22][Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must keep the full skill allowance when a worker takes authorised leave for necessary personal business? [Para 83][Para 85]
2. Whether the employer must keep the full skill allowance during periods when it has no work for a section, and whether it may use the worker's paid annual leave to cover those periods? [Para 99]
3. Whether the employer must keep the skill allowance and the 10 USD attendance bonus when a worker is absent with a valid state medical certificate? [Para 120][Para 122]
4. Whether the employer must keep wages and allowances in full when a worker arrives fifteen minutes late? [Para 146][Para 152]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** because the employer already withholds wages and the attendance bonus on leave days, it should preserve the skill allowance, and should show understanding for lateness caused by a broken motorbike, rain or traffic jams. [Para 51][Para 53][Para 77]
**Employer (respondent):** the skill allowance is an incentive paid on seniority and actual daily performance, reduced pro rata to protect production flow and deter casual leave requests; the company is a box maker outside the scope of Notification No. 230, and pays the 10 USD bonus voluntarily to attract labour. [Para 43][Para 56][Para 79][Para 80]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — all four pay claims were dismissed, but the employer was ordered to stop substituting paid annual leave for periods when it has no work for a section. [Para 157][Para 159][Para 160][Para 161][Para 163]
The panel grounded the analysis in Article 2 of the Labour Law, which it read, consistently with its earlier awards, as giving the employer the right to manage the enterprise so long as that right is exercised lawfully and reasonably. [Para 85][Para 88] It held that an employer may unilaterally design incentive payments above the statutory floor. [Para 90]
On issue 1 the panel found the skill allowance to be an incentive tied to actual work performed, so a worker does not lose the whole allowance during authorised personal leave but loses the part corresponding to days not worked; deducting pro rata was a reasonable exercise of management right because the allowance exceeds what the law requires. [Para 92][Para 94][Para 97][Para 98]
On issue 2 the panel reached the same result for days when the company has no work for a section: the worker loses the allowance only for the time not worked, and the employer's arrangement was reasonable as an above-statutory benefit. [Para 101][Para 103][Para 104] It separated the annual-leave question, applying Article 170, which fixes annual leave at the Khmer New Year unless otherwise agreed, with notice to the labour inspector, and permits the employer to schedule leftover days elsewhere only by agreement. [Para 105][Para 106][Para 107][Para 110] Following Cases 53/04 and 181/11, it held the employer may not consume a worker's paid annual leave to cover idle periods, and ordered the practice stopped. [Para 111][Para 114][Para 119]
On issue 3 the panel applied Article 71(3) and Article 72(1): certified sickness suspends the contract, so neither work nor wage is owed, and the skill allowance, being an incentive paid for work done, need not be preserved. [Para 123][Para 124][Para 125][Para 127][Para 129] It also found the company, as a box manufacturer, falls outside Notification No. 230, so the 10 USD attendance bonus is a voluntary benefit exceeding the law and its terms are the employer's to set. [Para 135][Para 137][Para 142][Para 145]
On issue 4 the panel likewise treated the attendance bonus as discretionary, and found it reasonable not to reward lateness; the workers gave no sufficient reason to change a practice applied consistently since the bonus began. [Para 150][Para 153][Para 154][Para 156]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an incentive allowance exceeds what the Labour Law requires and is tied to work actually performed, an employer may reduce it proportionally for days not worked — whether the absence is authorised personal leave, certified sickness or a lack of work — but it may not discharge its own lack of work by consuming the worker's paid annual leave without the worker's agreement and notice to the labour inspector. [Para 97][Para 103][Para 118][Para 119]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the 10 USD attendance bonus, though voluntary for a box manufacturer, was introduced to attract labour in competition with garment and footwear employers who pay it under Notification No. 230. [Para 148]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award carries two overlapping procedural narratives: a conciliation report No. 026 of 8 January 2013 with a hearing on 22 January, and a report No. 1510 of 31 December 2012 with a hearing on 15 January. [Para 8][Para 10][Para 14][Para 16] The Ministry report is dated 31 December 2013 in the evidence list though the file reached the Secretariat on 2 January 2013. [Para 33] The award was due on 25 January 2013 but the header and the extension paragraph give 31 January. [Para 24] The reasoning asks whether the employer must keep the allowances of "all 20" workers, a figure appearing nowhere else. [Para 85] The signature block shows the employer-side arbitrator as Seng Vouch Hun, though the face of the award names him as such while the panel list also prints the chair as Pen Bun Chea. [Para 165]
