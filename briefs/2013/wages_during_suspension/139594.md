# Award No. 06/13-Cambo Kotop — Cambo Kotop Ltd (Arbitration Council, 31 January 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-chosen; An Nan, worker-chosen; Tan Try, chair) · Doc ID 139594

## 1. Facts
Cambo Kotop Ltd is a garment factory at Kantork Cheung Village, Sangkat Kantork, Khan Po Sen Chey, Phnom Penh, employing 2,000 workers; its local union of the Federation Union for Cambodian Workers (FUCW) was registered on 14 December 2012. [Para 18][Para 19] Workers who had withdrawn from the Cambodian Union demanded that the company stop deducting the 1,000 riel union contribution fee from their wages, and the employer kept deducting it as payment to that union pending what it called proper withdrawal letters. [Para 21][Para 2] Conciliation left two overlapping issues, which the parties merged at the hearing into one. [Para 7][Para 20]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must stop deducting the union contribution fee from the wages of workers who have already withdrawn their membership from the Cambodian Union? [Para 1]
2. Whether, from January 2013, the employer must stop deducting the 1,000 riel union contribution fee from the wages of those withdrawn workers? [Para 21][Para 36]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** they had submitted membership withdrawal letters to the Cambodian Union and twice asked the employer to stop deducting; 74 workers signed the first request around 20 December 2012, which administrative staff rejected as a union matter. [Para 22][Para 23] They no longer authorised any deduction from their wages. [Para 39]
**Employer (respondent):** its administrative staff, unfamiliar with union matters, mistook the letters for withdrawal notices and did not read them in detail until conciliation; the Cambodian Union objected to the requests, so the company treated the matter as one between the union and its members and refused. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 27]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer ordered to stop deducting 1,000 riel union fee from the wages of 52 workers as payment to the Cambodian Union starting from January 2013 onward; a binding award. [Para 59][Para 60]

The panel classified the claim as a rights dispute, since it concerned the wage-deduction rule in Article 129 of the Labour Law rather than a demand for new benefits. [Para 37][Para 38] Reading Article 114, it held that workers have full freedom to use and manage their wages and that the employer may not restrict that freedom. [Para 41] Article 129 permits deduction of trade-union dues only where the worker has authorised it in writing, and that authorisation may be revoked at any time; the panel therefore found the employer obliged to deduct only while written authorisation subsists. [Para 46][Para 47]

The purpose of the writing requirement was to protect workers from unwilling deduction and employers from wrongful deduction, so authorisation must be clear and proper, following Awards 60/05-Evergreen Apparel and 154/11, and Case 13/09-June Textile. [Para 48][Para 49] Where a worker revokes, the panel held, the employer must neither deduct nor retain part of the deduction once it receives a letter showing that intention. [Para 51][Para 52]

On the facts, the letters filed bore 52 signatures and thumbprints with specific dates, and the panel found those 52 workers had clearly expressed their wish to stop the deduction. [Para 54][Para 55] The employer's claim of a legal ground for continuing failed, and the Cambodian Union's objection was irrelevant to the employer's statutory obligation. [Para 56][Para 58]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Once an employer receives a worker's written revocation of authorisation to deduct trade-union dues, it must stop deducting that worker's 1,000 riel contribution from the following month; an objection by the union receiving the dues does not suspend the obligation. [Para 51][Para 59]

**Obiter dicta:** the writing requirement exists to protect both sides — the worker from unwilling deduction and the employer from wrongful deduction claims. [Para 47][Para 48]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The number of affected workers is printed three ways: 74 workers signed the first request, the employer's 4 January 2013 letter records 54, and the withdrawal letters before the panel carried 52 signatures and thumbprints. [Para 32][Para 34][Para 35] The operative order uses the 52 figure. [Para 59] The evidence section is expressly omitted from the English version. [Para 15] The award refers to "Prakas on the Appointment of Arbitrators No. 121 dated 7 June 2012", a year later than the citation used in comparable awards. [Para 3] The employer's registration certificate is printed as "No. Inv. 0158E/2012 dated 17 January 2012". [Para 17]
