# Award No. 40/13 — Mfone Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 15 March 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Yu Soun Thy, employer-appointed; Tuon Si Phan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 139654

## 1. Facts
Mfone (អឹម ហ្វូន ចំកាត់), a telephone-service company employing roughly 590–700 staff, suspended the employment contracts of 25 marketing staff who contacted customers by telephone, telling them orally on 15 November 2012 that they were suspended for one month from 16 November 2012 because the ASEAN summit would stop the company's telephone marketing service. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 29][Para 39] Only that department was suspended; other departments kept working. [Para 40] The one month passed without recall, the workers waited nearly two months, and on 28 January 2013 they were called back until 18 February 2013. [Para 41][Para 45] The 25 workers, who had no written contracts, assigned the Free Trade Union of Workers of the Kingdom of Cambodia to represent them, and claimed 100% wages for 16 November 2012 to 28 January 2013. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 37] Conciliation failed on 14 February 2013 (report No. 184) and the hearing was held on 28 February 2013. [Para 6][Para 8]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay 100% of wages to the 25 marketing staff for the period their employment contracts were suspended, 16 November 2012 to 28 January 2013? [Para 31]
2. Whether the employer's suspension of those contracts was carried out in accordance with the legal procedure, so that the wage obligation was displaced? [Para 52][Para 55]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** wages are owed in full during a suspension that did not follow legal procedure, and alternatively the suspension was a mere suspension of the contract in which the employer found no replacement work and left them waiting to be recalled. [Para 31]
**Employer (respondent):** it did not refuse in principle to pay wages for the suspension period and claimed to be willing to obey the law, but said it had no money to pay, having already petitioned the court for a declaration of insolvency, with its assets under a court-appointed provisional administrator; it conceded it had not notified the Ministry of Labour or sought the labour inspector's authorisation because it did not know the procedure. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay 100% wages to the 25 telephone-marketing staff for the suspension period from 16 November 2012 to 27 January 2013; no lump sum is printed, average pay being USD 140 to USD 150 per month. [Para 68][Para 36][Para 69]
On issue 1 the panel read Article 72(1) of the 1997 Labour Law — suspension of the contract affects only the principal obligations, namely the worker's duty to work and the employer's duty to pay, "unless there is a contrary provision requiring the employer to pay" — and held that this excuses wages only where the employer has itself followed the legal procedure. [Para 52][Para 53] It followed awards 45/05 (B N N) at issue 2 and 26/12 (NC EC) at issue 4. [Para 54]
On issue 2 the panel applied Article 71(11), which allows suspension of enterprise activity for severe economic or material difficulty but for no more than two months and under labour-inspectorate supervision, and adopted the reading in award 58/07 (Star Sport Wear) and 08/07 (Siv Kinh) that supervision means the employer must obtain the labour inspector's prior authorisation; awards 72/05 and 22/05 were cited to the same effect. [Para 55][Para 57][Para 58][Para 60]
Applying that to the facts, the suspension ran from 16 November 2012 to 27 January 2013, longer than two months, and the employer admitted it had placed the suspension under no inspectorate control and obtained no prior authorisation; both conditions in Article 71(11) therefore failed, so the suspension was not lawful and the Article 72(1) exception did not apply. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 66][Para 67] The panel made no order on the insolvency proceedings, which it merely recorded.

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that suspends employment contracts for economic or material difficulty without the labour inspector's prior authorisation, or for longer than two months, cannot rely on Article 72(1) to escape wages: it must pay the suspended workers 100% of their wages for the period of unlawful suspension, and the enterprise's insolvency or court-appointed administrator does not discharge that obligation. [Para 53][Para 60][Para 67]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the 25 workers had no written employment contracts, which it recorded as fact without attaching any consequence to it; and that the suspension was announced orally rather than in writing. [Para 37][Para 38]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The claimed period ends 28 January 2013 but every finding and the order end it on 27 January 2013, a one-day difference left unexplained. [Para 31][Para 61][Para 67] Paragraph 50 prints the number of suspended staff as "4 25", and paragraph 61 states the suspension exceeded "24 years - 7 months" where two months is meant. [Para 50][Para 61] The staff count is given as about 590 by the panel and about 700 by the employer. [Para 28][Para 29] The award prolongation is printed as "12 March 2013 to 2 15 March 2013". [Para 15] Paragraph 1 mixes Council letterhead and a stray numeral string into the case header, and paragraphs 63–64 repeat the same finding twice. [Para 1][Para 63][Para 64] The employer is named both as Mfone Co., Ltd and as "អឹម ហ្វូន ចំកាត់". Union representatives are reduced to initials; arbitrator names in the signature block are partly overlaid by OCR noise. [Para 71]
