# Award No. 83/13 — JRB Action Textile & Clothing Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 23 May 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun for the employer, An Nan for the workers, Kong Phallack chair, dissenting) · Doc ID 139686

## 1. Facts
JRB Action Textile & Clothing is a garment manufacturer in Vattanak Industrial Park, Chom Chao, Phnom Penh, employing 900 workers; the local union of the Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers Democratic Unions (C.CAWDU) holds registration certificate No. 1945 of 8 September 2010 and has 300 members. Of six demands, conciliation settled three and the hearing settled Issue 1, while Issue 3 was withdrawn, leaving the attendance bonus. The employer pays US$15 a month, above the US$10 minimum, but docks it for authorised leave: US$5 for one day, US$10 for two days, and the whole US$15 at two and a half days. A worker who took two days for her mother-in-law's death lost US$10, and lost the entire bonus when she took a further two days in the same month for the seven-day ceremony. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24][Para 25][Para 28][Para 29]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the attendance bonus in proportion to the number of days of authorised leave taken by the workers? [Para 24]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The bonus should be paid in proportion to days of authorised leave taken for sickness or a relative's death; such leave has nothing to do with annual or special leave, and the employer's practice strips the bonus for absences it has itself approved. [Para 26][Para 27]
**Employer (respondent):** It will maintain the current practice because changing it in line with the demand would encourage more workers to take leave. [Para 30]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer is ordered to reduce the attendance bonus in proportion to the number of days of authorised leave for personal commitment taken, rather than withhold it; the award is a binding rights-dispute award. [Para 60][Para 61]

**Classification.** The panel took the demand as a rights dispute because it concerns the attendance bonus provided for in Point 2 of Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012, adopting the definition in Awards 05/11-M&V1, 13/11-Gold Kamvimex and 14/11-GHG. [Para 30]

**The instrument.** Point 2 of Notification No. 230 entitles workers who "attend work regularly in accordance with the number of working days in each month without absence" to at least US$10 monthly. The panel treated US$10 as a floor binding on all garment and footwear employers, not a matter of managerial discretion, so the employer's US$15 payment exceeds but does not displace it. It noted that Notification No. 230 replaced Notification No. 041 of 7 March 2011, raising the figure from US$7 and adding the words "without absence" from 1 September 2012, which is the new legal basis distinguishing earlier awards such as 112/11-Yung Wah 1, 136/11-Cambo Handsome and 154/11-B & N that had ordered pro rata payment under the old instrument. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 35]

**"Absence" construed.** Because the Labour Advisory Committee had not defined "without absence", the panel refused to assume which kind of absence was meant and construed the term by reference to law: absence in law includes annual leave, special leave, maternity leave, holidays and weekly time off, and none of these dock the bonus, which is paid at 100 per cent. Authorised leave for personal commitment is not dealt with by any legal provision, so the panel considered it case by case. [Para 37][Para 41][Para 42]

**Authorised leave as suspension.** The employer decides whether to authorise leave, on its administrative procedures, internal rules and production needs; once it authorises, it knows production will not be disrupted and the worker faces no disciplinary action, so the leave is taken in accordance with the enterprise's rules. Applying Article 71 paragraph 6 (the contract is suspended by absence authorised by the employer under law, collective agreement or individual agreement) and Article 72 paragraph 1 (suspension affects the main obligations, so the worker need not work and the employer need not pay, absent contrary provision), the panel held authorised leave suspends the contract and no wage is due for those days. Since Article 103 makes the bonus part of wages and the notification makes it payable, the employer may not withdraw it entirely; it may only reduce it in proportion to the days of authorised leave for personal commitment. [Para 50][Para 57][Para 58][Para 59][Para 55]

**Dissent.** The chair, Kong Phallack, recorded a dissent under Clause 37 of Prakas No. 099, arguing that the bonus has conditions attached — regular attendance without absence — and that authorising unpaid leave and paying the bonus are separate practices, so the employer may withhold it; he invoked Award 181/12-New Orean for the proposition that Notification No. 230 creates a conditional bonus, and said the panel had a new legal basis to depart from earlier awards but chose not to. [Para 64][Para 67][Para 73][Para 84]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer authorises unpaid leave for a worker's personal commitment, the contract is suspended under Articles 71 and 72 so no wage is earned for those days, but because the attendance bonus under Notification No. 230 is part of wages the employer may only reduce it in proportion to the days of authorised leave, not withhold it in full. [Para 58][Para 59]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's wider statement that annual leave, special leave, maternity leave, holidays and weekly time off are absences that do not dock the bonus at all was not required by the facts, which concerned personal-commitment leave only. [Para 39][Para 40]
