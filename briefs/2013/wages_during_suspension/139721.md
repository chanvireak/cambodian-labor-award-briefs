# Award No. 98/13 — Ming Fu Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 10 June 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (employer-appointed Seng Vuoch Hun; worker-appointed An Nan; chair Men Nimmith) · Doc ID 139721

## 1. Facts
Ming Fu Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a garment manufacturer in Dangkor, Phnom Penh, employed 130 workers according to the non-conciliation report, though the workers claimed about 200 at the hearing. [Para 17][Para 19] The local union of C.CAWDU, registered on 15 March 2013, brought the claim with an authorisation letter bearing the thumbprints of 149 workers. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 22] The dispute concerned the monthly attendance bonus of US$15 that the employer withheld whenever a worker took any kind of leave; the employer did not attend the hearing. [Para 22][Para 29]

## 2. Issues in dispute
Seventeen demands went to conciliation; fifteen were settled, and the two remaining were referred to the panel. [Para 6][Para 7]
1. Whether the employer may reduce the attendance bonus in proportion to days of authorised leave, and must maintain the bonus when workers take special leave? [Para 22][Para 83]
2. Whether the employer must pay a 4,000 riel overtime meal allowance for work on holidays and Sundays? [Para 3]

Issue 2 was withdrawn at the hearing, leaving only Issue 1. [Para 9][Para 26]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer withheld the US$15 attendance bonus for any leave, authorised or not, and should either pay it proportionally to authorised leave taken or preserve it in full; proportional payment and preservation during special leave accord with equity and Article 103 of the Labour Law. [Para 22][Para 23] They relied on Award 71/13-JRB, which the panel corrected to Award 83/13-JRB of 23 May 2013. [Para 24][Para 25]
**Employer (respondent):** absent; it raised no objection and filed no counter-evidence, so the panel accepted the workers' account of the facts. [Para 20][Para 31]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to reduce the attendance bonus in proportion to days of authorised leave taken for personal commitments, and to maintain the attendance bonus of workers taking special leave; the award on the rights dispute is binding. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 85]

The panel first held it could proceed in the employer's absence under Clause 21 of Prakas No. 099, following Awards 53/04 Kuong Hong, 63/04 Shine Well, 148/07 Pay Her, 99/09 Kingsland and 173/11 Zhen Yun. [Para 28][Para 30] It then classified the claim: under Article 312 of the Labour Law a dispute over the interpretation and enforcement of a law, regulation or collective agreement is a rights dispute, and the bonus claim turned on Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012, so it was a rights dispute. [Para 32][Para 38]

On the merits, Point 2 of Notification No. 230 (25 July 2012) requires a monthly attendance bonus of at least US$10 for workers attending regularly "without absence", replacing the US$7 figure in Notification No. 041 of 7 March 2011. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 44] The panel treated the bonus as part of wages under Article 103 and held that "absence" in the notification covers annual leave, special leave, maternity leave, holidays and weekly rest, none of which may be docked. [Para 46][Para 65] Leave for personal commitments is not regulated by law, so the panel assessed it case by case: where the employer authorises it, the contract is suspended under Article 71(6), wages are not owed for the day, and the bonus may be reduced proportionally but not withheld entirely. [Para 48][Para 66][Para 67][Para 69]

Special leave stands differently: Article 171 and Prakas No. 267 of 11 October 2001 allow it for events affecting the worker's family, deduct it from remaining annual leave, or require make-up work where annual leave is exhausted, so wages and the bonus must be preserved. [Para 71][Para 73][Para 78][Para 82]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
"Absence" in Notification No. 230 does not include leave the law protects — annual, special, maternity, holiday or weekly rest — so the attendance bonus may never be docked for those days. Where an employer authorises unpaid leave for a personal commitment, it may reduce the bonus in proportion to the days taken but may not withhold the whole month's bonus, because authorised leave suspends the contract rather than constituting absence. [Para 46][Para 67][Para 69]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's further view that the panel cannot assume "absence" means any particular kind of absence without legal reasoning, and that authorising leave means the employer has accepted the production consequence of the absence [Para 45][Para 52].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
This is an unofficial English translation of a Khmer original, and the evidence section was omitted from it, so no document list or exhibit numbering survives. [Para 8][Para 13][Para 14] The judgment first cites the precedent as Award 71/13-JRB and then corrects it in the following paragraph to 83/13-JRB dated 23 May 2013, both printed. [Para 24][Para 25] The workforce figure is printed as 130 in the non-conciliation report and as approximately 200 in the workers' claim, with 149 thumbprints on the authority letter. [Para 17][Para 19][Para 22] The bonus is described as US$15 paid per month in the workers' account but as US$10 in the notification applied. [Para 22][Para 42] Numerous words are joined or truncated by OCR ("themployer", "attendance", "no." for "No."), and the non-conciliation report is dated 9 May 2013 while the last conciliation session is also given as 2 May 2013. [Para 5][Para 6]
