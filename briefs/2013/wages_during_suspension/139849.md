# Order in Case No. 168/13 — SL Garmen Process Sing (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 20 August 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chhea presiding; Ing Sothy appointed by the employer; Ven Pov appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 139849

## 1. Facts
SL Garmen Process Sing (Cambodia) (អេស អិល ហ្គាមេន ប្រូសេស ស៊ីង) is a garment factory at Stung Mean Chey, Mean Chey, Phnom Penh. [Para 1] A collective dispute over nine demands was conciliated on 13 August 2013; two demands were settled and seven were not, and non-conciliation report No. 993 K.B/AK/VK of 14 August 2013 referred the seven to the Council. [Para 15][Para 16] The union local struck; the Council issued Order No. 033/13 K.B.A on 15 August 2013 ordering the strikers to suspend the strike and return to work from the morning of 16 August 2013, and they did not return. [Para 19][Para 20] Neither party attended the pre-hearing meeting of 19 August 2013. [Para 22][Para 35]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must remove Mr P from the company at the union's request? [Para 1]
2. Whether the company must pay wages on the 7th of each month, and one day earlier where the 7th falls on a Sunday or a public holiday? [Para 3]
3. Whether the company must implement the agreement of 10 May 2013? [Para 4]
4. Whether the company must pay a meal allowance of USD 3? [Para 5]
5. Whether the company must pay equal wages and other benefits to workers doing the same work in the same role? [Para 6]
6. Whether the company must renegotiate with everyone concerned the new hours contract, restoring 10 hours 30 minutes in place of 12 hours? [Para 7]
7. Whether the company must pay workers a base wage of USD 150? [Para 8]
The panel treated as preliminary the question whether it should continue the arbitration at all. [Para 37]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** absent from the pre-hearing meeting; their case is the seven demands recorded in the non-conciliation report, pressed by a strike from 13 August 2013 that continued after the panel ordered a return to work. [Para 22][Para 34]
**Employer (respondent):** by letter of 14 August 2013 it asked the Council to issue an order suspending the workers' strike, and by a second letter of the same date asked that case No. 168/13 be closed; it did not attend the pre-hearing meeting. [Para 18][Para 23][Para 24]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel decided not to continue the arbitration and closed case No. 168/13-SL Garmen, making no order on any of the seven demands. [Para 54][Para 55]

Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY of 21 April 2004 on the Arbitration Council requires the parties, during the panel's proceedings, to suspend every dispute measure — a strike or lockout under Article 318 of the Labour Law — and any other measure that could aggravate the dispute, and requires them to attend every meeting the panel summons. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39]

The panel read that article as meaning it will not proceed while workers are striking or the employer has locked out, and it followed its own orders in cases 5906 (Sang Vo), 3908 (Choung Yov), 13709 and 13809 (Prum Phany) and 110/11 (Simano). [Para 40][Para 41][Para 47] In case 04/04 (MSI) its notification of 29 January 2004 stated that where the parties have no right to strike during the proceedings the Council cannot resolve or issue an order on the collective dispute, and may act again once all unions stop striking. [Para 42][Para 43] In case 18/04 (Hotel Cambodiana) its second order of 22 April 2004 required all strikes, demonstrations and disruption in the hotel to stop from 23 April 2004 and reserved the right to terminate the proceedings if a party breached the order. [Para 44][Para 45] In case 77/05 (Franco Nitting) point 4 of the second order provided that where workers continued to strike the Council would not proceed. [Para 46]

On the facts, Order No. 033/13 was issued on 15 August 2013 requiring a return to work from the morning of 16 August 2013; the secretariat was told by telephone on 19 August 2013 that the strike at the SL factory was still continuing to that date; and neither party came to the pre-hearing meeting of 19 August 2013. [Para 34][Para 49][Para 50][Para 53] Because no hearing was held, the panel found no facts on the strike and no findings at all on the issues in dispute referred to it. [Para 35][Para 36] It therefore agreed with its earlier decisions and declined to continue. [Para 48][Para 54]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The duty to suspend strikes and lockouts and to attend every meeting the panel summons during the proceedings is a condition on which the panel will proceed at all: where workers keep striking after the panel has ordered the strike suspended and a return to work, the panel will decline to continue the arbitration and close the case without deciding the demands. [Para 39][Para 46][Para 54]

**Obiter dicta:** quoting its own 2004 notification in case 04/04 (MSI), the panel recorded that it may resolve the collective dispute again at any time once all unions stop striking, so that closure of the file is procedural rather than a ruling on the merits of the seven demands. [Para 42][Para 43]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The arbitrator appointed by the employer is named Ing Sothy in the panel composition but signs as a differently named arbitrator at the end of the order; the OCR also renders that signature block with stray Latin characters. [Para 1][Para 56] Dates are repeatedly garbled: the constitution date reads "15 August 201 2013", the order date in the operative part reads "ថ្ងៃទី ទី ១៥", and the invitation to the second meeting is dated "19 December August 2013". [Para 17][Para 49][Para 51] Letter dates within single sentences conflict, the employer's letters of 14 August 2013 being described alongside a secretariat letter of 19 August 2013. [Para 24] The Phnom Penh Court order number is partly lost, printed as No. 29 with a single surviving letter. [Para 26] The telephone complaint narrative names the company as "Jive -2- SL", which is OCR noise. [Para 14] The individual named in demand 1 is printed in full in the source; he is not reproduced here. [Para 1]
