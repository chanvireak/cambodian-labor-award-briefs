# Award No. 204/13 — Bowker Garment Factory (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 21 October 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, An Non, Pen Bun Chia presiding) · Doc ID 139878

## 1. Facts
Ms P Malay was hired by Bowker Garment Factory (Cambodia) Co., Ltd on 2 March 2013 as a sewing-machine operator on a two-month probationary contract, which ended on 1 May 2013; she then signed a three-month fixed-term contract running to 31 July 2013. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 71] The union elected her deputy president on 19 July 2013 and notified the employer on 24 July 2013; the employer refused the letter at the gate and by post, and let her contract expire on 31 July 2013. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 76][Para 72] She and the union claim the non-renewal was a disguised dismissal for union activity and seek reinstatement with back pay. [Para 2][Para 78]

## 2. Issues in dispute
The panel recorded 21 worker demands but adjudicated only those not settled at the hearing. [Para 1][Para 131]
1. Whether the employer's failure to renew Ms P Malay's three-month fixed-term contract was a dismissal requiring reinstatement and back pay? [Para 2][Para 203]
2. Whether the employer was entitled not to renew the fixed-term contract at its expiry? [Para 204]
3. Whether the non-renewal was based on union discrimination contrary to Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law? [Para 208]
4. Whether the employer must pay an overtime meal allowance of 4,000 riel for the 16:00–18:00 shift, weekly rather than monthly? [Para 149][Para 150]
5. Whether the employer must pay a technical/skill allowance of USD 10–30 and sign one-year contracts? [Para 134][Para 141]
6. Whether the employer must grant pregnant workers 15 minutes' early leave, raise travel/housing to USD 15, raise the attendance bonus to USD 15, and supply raincoats? [Para 158][Para 336][Para 337][Para 388]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** a three-month contract is too short and the law does not permit fixed-term contracts under six months; the non-renewal came days after the union notified its leadership, so it was union discrimination; overtime meal money should be 4,000 riel paid weekly because food prices rose; the skill, travel, bonus and raincoat claims are owed. [Para 145][Para 152][Para 134][Para 336][Para 388]
**Employer (respondent):** the three-month contract lets workers receive the 5% termination indemnity sooner and was requested by worker representatives; the 3,000-riel meal allowance, USD 11 attendance bonus and USD 10 travel allowance are already paid; the buyer (Adidas) requires monthly payment; the contract simply expired. [Para 146][Para 140][Para 149][Para 154][Para 338]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel dismissed the reinstatement and back-pay claim, the 4,000-riel meal claim, the skill allowance, the one-year contract, the 15-minute pregnancy leave, the travel/housing increase, the attendance-bonus increase, the raincoats and the discrimination claims; it ordered the employer to pay the overtime meal allowance once a week instead of once a month. [Para 379][Para 380][Para 381][Para 382][Para 383][Para 384][Para 385][Para 386][Para 387][Para 388][Para 389][Para 390]

On the first issue the attendance record showed Ms P Malay worked a full 8 hours 2 minutes on 31 July 2013, the last day of her contract, so the relationship ended by expiry and not by dismissal. [Para 201][Para 203] Under Article 73 a fixed-term contract lapses automatically at its term and neither party can force renewal without agreement; the employer therefore had the right to decline renewal, unless the motive was union discrimination. [Para 204][Para 206][Para 207] On discrimination the worker bears the burden; the only evidence was the timing of the union notification, and the union produced no proof of the reason for the non-renewal or of the alleged dismissal of its president. [Para 214][Para 216][Para 221][Para 222] Articles 12 and 279 prohibit using union membership or activity as a ground for termination or discipline, but the claim did not meet that standard. [Para 208][Para 209][Para 375][Para 376]

On the meal allowance the panel found the employer already paid 3,000 riel, above the 2,000-riel daily minimum in Notification 041/11, but that notification fixes no payment frequency. [Para 297][Para 298][Para 303][Para 305] Because workers buy meals daily when they work overtime, and the employer gave no reason why weekly payment was impossible, the panel ordered weekly payment as a reasonable resolution of an interest dispute. [Para 306][Para 319][Para 320][Para 321][Para 384]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-term contract that reaches its term ends automatically; an employer's decision not to renew is not a dismissal and carries no reinstatement or back-pay obligation, and may be challenged only where the worker proves the refusal was motivated by union membership or activity. [Para 206][Para 207][Para 208] An overtime meal allowance is an interest dispute; where the employer already pays above the statutory daily minimum, the panel may still order payment at a frequency that lets workers buy meals, without increasing the amount. [Para 303][Para 322][Para 384]

**Obiter dicta:** providing a free meal during overtime is "additional strength" for further overtime, and the panel expects payment frequency to follow the workers' daily need rather than the buyer's monthly audit cycle. [Para 306][Para 313][Para 154]
