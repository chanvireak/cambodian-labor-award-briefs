# Award No. 176/13 — EINS (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 16 September 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phalla presiding; Ing Sothy appointed by the employer, dissenting; An Non appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 139853

## 1. Facts
EINS (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (អ៊ី អ៉ីនស៍) is a garment factory at Prey Sala, Kap, Pur Senchey, Phnom Penh, registered on 18 September 2012 and employing about 1,300 workers. [Para 66][Para 67] Ms P was hired on 1 July 2011, before the company took its present name, as head of the sewing section supervising 16 teams and was a staff delegate (ប្រតិភូ បុគ្គលិក) earning USD 640 a month. [Para 74][Para 75][Para 76] From 1 September 2012 she signed successive three-month fixed-term contracts without any settlement of her earlier service, the last running from 1 June to 31 August 2013. [Para 80][Para 77] On 12 August 2013 the employer terminated her before that contract expired, paid her about USD 760 in severance and had her sign a pre-prepared letter resigning her delegate post; it did not seek the labour inspector's authorisation. [Para 78][Para 87][Para 89][Para 118] Twenty demands were conciliated on 16 August 2013 with none settled; 17 were settled at the hearing of 30 August 2013, two were withdrawn, and only issue 18 remained. [Para 29][Para 34][Para 35][Para 36]

## 2. Issues in dispute
18. Whether the employer must take Ms P back into normal work and pay her back wages from the date of dismissal until her return, having dismissed a staff delegate without the labour inspector's prior authorisation? [Para 72][Para 73][Para 119]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer gave no notice, no warning and no fault; she was a staff delegate and the employer dismissed her without the labour inspector's prior authorisation; she never asked to resign the delegate post and signed the letter without seeing it, the employer's representative covering the paper with his hand; the internal rules said to ground the dismissal were not validly registered. [Para 92][Para 93][Para 94][Para 96][Para 108][Para 109]
**Employer (respondent):** she repeatedly disobeyed her superiors and committed serious misconduct, though it never said what that misconduct was; she agreed to the termination, signed the letter and accepted USD 760; the representative explained the severance and did not forbid her to read the resignation letter, and did not force her to resign the delegate post, although it prepared and filled in that letter and did not show it to her. [Para 97][Para 99][Para 102][Para 103][Para 105][Para 106][Para 139]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer is ordered to take Ms P back into normal work and to pay her back wages from the date of dismissal until her return, and she is ordered to repay the severance she received, except the final wage, which she keeps. The award is non-binding and becomes final 8 (eight) days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Minister. [Para 155][Para 156][Para 157][Para 158]

On the type of contract, Article 67(2) of the Labour Law provides that a fixed-term contract may not exceed two years and may be renewed only so long as the total does not exceed two years, and Article 73(5) converts such a contract into an indeterminate-term one where there is no notice of termination and the total exceeds that limit. [Para 119][Para 120][Para 121][Para 125][Para 126] The panel took ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982 as context, noting that fixed-term contracts should not be used for long-term work. [Para 127][Para 128][Para 129] It found that the successive three-month contracts from 1 September 2012 to 31 August 2013 together ran one year, so the contract remained fixed-term. [Para 135][Para 136]

On the dismissal, the panel held that ending the fixed-term contract before its expiry was a termination at the employer's sole initiative, and that her signature on the termination letter and her acceptance of the severance amounted only to accepting a unilateral dismissal and did not change its character. [Para 140][Para 141] Article 293 of the Labour Law requires the labour inspector's prior agreement before a staff delegate or candidate is dismissed, so that the inspector may investigate and allow or refuse the request within one month. [Para 142][Para 144][Para 145] Ms P was a staff delegate and the employer dismissed her on 12 August 2013 without seeking that authorisation. [Para 147][Para 148][Para 150] The dismissal therefore did not follow the procedure Article 293 requires, and the employer must take her back. [Para 151] Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 empowers the panel to order civil compensation, including that a dismissed worker be taken back into work. [Para 152] Since she had already received about USD 760 on dismissal, she must repay it, keeping only her final wage. [Para 153][Para 154][Para 155]

**Dissent (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer):** she signed three documents — the termination letter, the confirmation of termination and the resignation from the delegate post — and confirmed at the hearing that she was not coerced; she did not come to work the next day; and the benefits she received were not shown to be below what the law requires. [Para 162][Para 163][Para 164][Para 165] In his view the agreement was made with genuine consent, is valid and effective, and can only be set aside on the strict grounds of nullity, by evidence, under Article 13 of the Labour Law; he therefore dissented from the order to reinstate her. [Para 166][Para 167][Para 168]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A staff delegate may be dismissed only with the labour inspector's prior authorisation under Article 293 of the Labour Law; a dismissal made without it is unlawful and the panel will order reinstatement with back wages from the date of dismissal to the date of return, the worker repaying the severance received and keeping only the final wage. [Para 145][Para 151][Para 152][Para 155] A worker's signature on a termination letter and acceptance of severance do not turn a unilateral dismissal into a consensual one. [Para 141]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the Labour Law leans towards indeterminate-term contracts because job security matters to workers and long service encourages commitment, and that ILO Recommendation No. 166, though not binding, is useful context for reading Article 67. [Para 123][Para 124][Para 129]
