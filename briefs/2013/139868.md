# Award No. 197/13 — Quint Major Industrial Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 17 October 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Yu Sounti, employer-appointed; Tun Siphan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 139868

## 1. Facts
Quint Major Industrial, a factory operating since 2006 with about 3,800 workers, faced a collective dispute brought by the Khmer Youth Voice Union, registered on 2 August 2013 with roughly 100 members. [Para 39][Para 41] Nine welfare and allowance claims went to conciliation, which failed on all nine; four were settled at the hearing itself, leaving six issues for the panel. [Para 17][Para 22][Para 23] Every remaining claim is a demand for money or facilities — travel and accommodation allowance, a free lunch, attendance bonus during sick leave, the USD 5 health allowance, drinking water and the number of daily thumbprint checks. [Para 42][Para 46][Para 49][Para 58][Para 62][Para 151]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer had to raise the travel and accommodation allowance by USD 5, to a total of USD 12 a month? [Para 42]
2. Whether the employer had to provide one free midday meal, or 4,000 riel a day in lieu? [Para 46]
3. Whether the employer had to keep the full regular-attendance bonus when a worker takes sick leave backed by a medical certificate? [Para 49]
4. Whether the employer had to maintain the USD 5 health allowance separately from the basic wage? [Para 58]
5. Whether the employer had to supply drinking water of the same quality and price as that given to office staff, together with hot and cold water dispensers? [Para 62]
8. Whether the employer had to reduce thumbprint checks from four to three a day? [Para 151]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** wages are low while food is expensive; other factories give a free lunch or USD 5–13 a month in food money, and some pay USD 12 in travel allowance. [Para 44][Para 47] The water supplied is dusty and smells of chemicals, and one water dispenser in the clinic cannot serve more than 1,000 workers. [Para 62][Para 66]

**Employer (respondent):** the company simply cannot afford the claims and applies the Ministry of Labour's prakas; it buys purified water from New Champion, flushes the tanks weekly and tests the water monthly, and the 7–10 minutes needed for thumbprinting is unavoidable if attendance is to be managed. [Para 45][Para 68][Para 72][Para 84][Para 85]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the allowance, lunch, attendance-bonus, health-allowance and thumbprint claims were all refused; the employer was ordered to guarantee that the drinking water it supplies is hygienic, free of smell and free of dust. [Para 169][Para 170][Para 171][Para 172][Para 174][Para 176]

On issues 1, 2, 3 and 4 the panel took a single jurisdictional line. Each claim asked for more than the law or any collective agreement, internal rule or past practice provided, which made it an interest dispute. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 106][Para 114] Under Article 96 of the Labour Law and Prakas 305, an interest dispute may be heard only where the referring union is the most representative in the enterprise, or where several unions together represent more than half the workforce. [Para 94][Para 100] The Khmer Youth Voice Union had just been registered and held about 100 members out of some 3,800 workers, so it was not the most representative, and the panel declined to consider those claims. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 104] On the health allowance the panel added that Prakas 103 had already merged the USD 5 into the minimum wage and the employer was paying USD 84 in basic wage, so nothing further was owed. [Para 120][Para 121][Para 122]

On issue 3 the panel also found no provision of law, collective agreement or practice obliging the employer to pay the attendance bonus in full during sick leave; the employer's practice of pro-rating it by the days absent was lawful, and the claim exceeded the law. [Para 112][Para 113][Para 114]

Issue 5 was the one the workers won, and only in part. Article 1 of Prakas 054 requires an employer to supply sufficient hygienic drinking water. [Para 129] Because the employer carried the burden of showing the water was clean — it alone controlled the supply — and had filed no test report or ministry inspection report by the evidence deadline, it had not discharged that burden. [Para 138][Para 139][Para 140] The panel therefore ordered it to ensure the water is hygienic, without smell or dust. [Para 143][Para 174] The claim to water of equal value to the office supply failed because Prakas 054 sets no price, only a standard of hygiene and sufficiency [Para 142]; the claim for hot and cold dispensers failed because the prakas does not require them and supply was adequate. [Para 146][Para 149][Para 150]

On issue 8 the panel held that the number and siting of gate checks falls within the employer's right to manage the enterprise under Article 2, provided it is exercised lawfully and reasonably. [Para 154][Para 158] Two checks a day would not let the employer verify attendance and compute wages, so the existing four-check system was lawful and reasonable, and the union's demand was refused. [Para 159][Para 160][Para 164] Neither side had proved how long a check actually took, so the panel could not find the existing practice abusive on that ground. [Para 165]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim that asks for more than statute, collective agreement, internal rule or established practice grants is an interest dispute, and an interest dispute may be entertained only where the union bringing it is the most representative in the enterprise, or where the unions acting together represent more than half the workforce. [Para 100][Para 104]

Where the law imposes a duty on the employer alone — as Prakas 054 does for drinking water — the employer bears the burden of proving compliance, and silence in the evidence proves non-compliance. [Para 139][Para 141]

**Obiter dicta:** the number and siting of gate checks belong to management's prerogative, though they should not be arranged so as to interfere with workers' personal freedoms. [Para 158][Para 166]
