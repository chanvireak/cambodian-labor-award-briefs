# Award No. 230/13 — Everay Textile Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 15 November 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Seng Vouch Hun (employer-appointed), Tuon Si Phan (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (chair, appointed by both) · Doc ID 139888

## 1. Facts
The company runs a garment, bag and soft-toy factory at the Vay Seven industrial park in Samrong Tong district, Kampong Speu, employing about 350 workers. [Para 72] In October 2013 roughly 300 workers struck over 14 demands, led by the Khmer Youth Voice Union, which had been registered with the Ministry of Labour since 4 April 2013. [Para 23][Para 73] Conciliation at the provincial labour office failed on all 14 points and the case reached the panel on 21 October 2013. [Para 30] At the hearing 13 points were settled or withdrawn and only demand 14 — deduction of 1,000 riel a month in union dues — remained for decision. [Para 34][Para 44]

## 2. Issues in dispute
14. Whether the employer must deduct 1,000 riel a month from the wages of each worker who is a member of the Khmer Youth Voice Union, to be paid as dues to the federation? [Para 77]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the union is a lawful organisation holding a registration certificate from the Ministry; joining is a freedom guaranteed by law, and the employer must deduct dues from any member who authorises the deduction in writing. [Para 79][Para 81]
**Employer (respondent):** it will not deduct dues for this union at all, even on a written request, because the union was not formed according to legal procedure — no membership list was given before the election, no notice of the elected leadership or of the registration was given, and the registration still lists an officer who resigned in February 2013. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 87]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer is ordered to perform its statutory duty to deduct 1,000 riel a month per member for the union, but only once the union has completed the formalities required by Article 129(2) of the Labour Law and paragraph 5 of Prakas No. 305. [Para 134][Para 135]
The panel read Article 129(2) of the 1997 Labour Law as requiring the employer to deduct wages for union dues whenever a member authorises the deduction in writing, and as following its own earlier awards on that text. [Para 101][Para 103] Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, paragraph 5, adds the procedural step: the member's written authorisation, with the list sent to the employer 15 days before the payday of the month. [Para 106][Para 108] The purpose of the writing requirement is to protect workers from deductions against their will and to protect the company from error. [Para 109]
On the evidence, the union's list of 305 members was not in proper form: it carried names, identity numbers, sex and section but no signatures or thumbprints in places, some sheets gave an identity number without a name, some gave a name without a number, some sheets lacked the required heading, and some headings omitted the company's name. [Para 96][Para 99][Para 100] The panel therefore found the union had not yet met the legal formalities and had not delivered the file to the employer. [Para 125][Para 126][Para 127]
The employer's own grounds were rejected. Registration is a matter for the Ministry: if the employer believed the union was improperly formed it could complain there, but it could not use that belief to refuse a statutory duty. [Para 132] Nor could it rely on the procedural irregularity of the strike to escape that duty. [Para 133]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer's duty to deduct union dues from a member's wages under Article 129(2) of the Labour Law is conditional on the member's individual written authorisation and on the union serving the employer with a proper membership list 15 days before payday; where the list is unsigned, incomplete or undated, the duty is not yet triggered, and the employer's objection to the union's legal status is not a defence but must be raised with the Ministry. [Para 108][Para 130][Para 132]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the union's October 2013 strike was unlawful for want of prior notice, and that its members blocked containers and entered the factory, were narrated from the employer's account and played no part in the order. [Para 90][Para 94]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The union's registration number is printed as 2847 at Para 66 and 2887 at Para 87. The panel's creation date reads "24 October 2019 2013" at Para 31. The operative paragraph orders deduction for "4" workers at Para 134 while the order at Para 135 omits the numeral and the claim covers 305 members; the "4" appears to be stray OCR before "each worker". The company name is spelled several ways (Everay Textile Garment / Everay Rai Tek Stay Garment) and the union's own name is rendered inconsistently.
