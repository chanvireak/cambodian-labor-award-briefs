# Award No. 193/13 — Whitex Garment (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 10 October 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Nan (worker-appointed), Pen Bunchhea (chair) · Doc ID 139863 · unofficial English translation of the Khmer original

## 1. Facts
Whitex Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a Phnom Penh garment manufacturer with 800 workers, paid permanent workers US$80 a month, probationary workers US$75 and heads of tailors US$99, with a US$10 accommodation and transport allowance and a US$10 attendance bonus. [Para 20][Para 36][Para 52][Para 58] The claimant union, the Local Union of WUF, held registration No. 2896 of 16 May 2013, had 78 members, and did not hold most representative status in the factory. [Para 21][Para 22] Four of thirteen demands survived conciliation. [Para 10]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must provide each worker with a free lunch per day? [Para 22]
2. Whether the employer must provide an additional US$5 accommodation and transportation allowance on top of the existing US$10 per month? [Para 50]
3. Whether the employer must provide an additional US$5 attendance bonus on top of the existing US$10 per month? [Para 57]
4. Whether the employer must increase permanent workers' base wages by US$15, from US$80 to US$95 per month? [Para 62]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** consumer prices were rising; neighbouring factories such as ABC, American, Sanchow and Quantum provided free lunch, and other factories paid US$12 to US$15 allowances and US$18 attendance bonuses, so Whitex struggled to recruit and should match them. [Para 24][Para 29][Para 34]
**Employer (respondent):** it had just raised base wages from US$61 to US$80 and could not afford further increases; comparison with other factories was misplaced because the businesses differed, and current allowances and bonuses already met the Ministry's notifications. [Para 26][Para 35][Para 30]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider all four demands: the free lunch, the additional US$5 accommodation and transportation allowance, the additional US$5 attendance bonus, and the US$15 base-wage increase from US$80. [Para 69][Para 70] No rights dispute was before it. [Para 68]

On issue 1 the panel held that no legal provision, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice obliged a free lunch, so the demand was an interests dispute. [Para 43][Para 44] Applying Article 96 of the Labour Law and clause 9 of Prakas 305, and clause 43 of Prakas 099 — under which an award on an interests dispute takes the place of a collective agreement for one year and binds all workers, including non-members, while stripping them of the right to strike over the matters covered — the panel held it could entertain an interests dispute only from a union with most representative status or one whose members are more than half the enterprise's workforce, following Awards 81/04 Evergreen, 98/04 Great Union, 02/11 Pou Yuen, 66/11 In Han Sung and 169/11 Fortune Teo. [Para 45][Para 47][Para 48] WUF held neither, so the workers did not meet the legal requirement to bring an interests dispute. [Para 49]

Issues 2 to 4 failed on the same gate and independently on the merits: Notification No. 230/12 of 25 July 2012 required only a US$7 allowance and a US$10 attendance bonus, and Notification No. 103 of 9 April 2013 set the minimum wage at US$75 on probation and US$80 thereafter, so the employer was compliant and each residual demand exceeded what the law mandated — an interests dispute in each case. [Para 52][Para 58][Para 64][Para 65] Each was therefore declined on the reasoning given for issue 1. [Para 56][Para 61][Para 67]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An interests dispute may be brought to the panel only by a union holding most representative status in the enterprise or one with more than half its workers as members; a union without that standing cannot obtain an award on demands that merely exceed statutory minima, and the panel will decline to consider them. [Para 48][Para 49]

**Obiter dicta:** an award settling an interests dispute stands in place of a collective agreement for one year, binds workers who are not union members, and removes their right to strike over the matters it covers. [Para 46][Para 47]

The evidence section is expressly omitted from this unofficial English translation, so the exhibits cannot be checked. [Para 18][Para 19] OCR has joined words throughout ("themployer", "ach worker", "bargaing", "Traing") and duplicated whole clauses mid-sentence in paragraphs 24, 25, 36 and 58. [Para 24][Para 58] Paragraph 45 opens "Concerning this rights dispute" although issue 1 had just been classified as an interests dispute. [Para 11][Para 13] Paragraph 68 records "NIA" where "nil" was meant.
