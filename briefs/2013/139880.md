# Award No. 214/13 — E Garment Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 31 October 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach, employer-appointed; Ven Pou, worker-appointed; Pen Bunchhea, chair) · Doc ID 139880

## 1. Facts
E Garment Co Ltd is an export garment factory in Svay Rolum, Sa'ang, Kandal, with about 3,550 workers. On 29 August 2013 the Khmer Workers' Rights Union for Cambodia (សហជីពការងារដើម្បីកម្ពុជា) brought a collective dispute of twelve claims; conciliation settled three and left nine, of which the union withdrew two at the hearing. The panel heard six claims, all economic. The enterprise also hosts the Khmer Workers' Pride Union, which holds the most-representative certificate (No. 063/13 of 15 August 2013); the claimant union does not. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 61][Para 24][Para 32]

## 2. Issues in dispute
*(Panel numbering retained: claims 1, 2 and 4 were settled in conciliation, and part of claim 7 was withdrawn, leaving the following.)*
1. (No. 3) Whether the employer must provide one midday meal, or 4,000 riel per meal in lieu? [Para 107]
2. (No. 5) Whether the employer must calculate the output bonus daily, paying the excess for a good day and topping up to the base wage on a short day? [Para 121]
3. (No. 6) Whether the employer must raise the piece rate by 0.02 USD on every PO in every section? [Para 156]
4. (No. 7) Whether the employer must add 5 USD to the existing 10 USD monthly attendance bonus? [Para 185]
5. (No. 8) Whether the employer must raise the base monthly wage from 86 USD to 150 USD? [Para 191]
6. (No. 9) Whether the employer must raise the travel and accommodation allowance from 7 USD to 20 USD a month? [Para 198]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** other factories give one or two meals or a cash equivalent, and wages are too low to meet family expenses, so the meal, the higher bonus, the 150 USD base and the 20 USD allowance are all needed. Piece rates are too low for the labour they consume and a day's work does not yield even the daily base wage. The employer's weekly netting of the output bonus destroys the bonus for days the worker could not fill, and that shortfall is the employer's own fault when it supplies no work. [Para 70][Para 86][Para 91][Para 98][Para 101][Para 104]

**Employer (respondent):** the company's capacity does not allow any of these increases. Wages are a fixed base of 86 USD varying by skill and seniority, plus a weekly output incentive paid above the base — a scheme more generous than the law requires. Piece rates must flex with style and production stage and are set by time studies, so a flat 0.02 USD rise is impossible. [Para 71][Para 74][Para 76][Para 93][Para 96][Para 139]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to stop using the weekly output bonus to top up the daily base wage; every other claim was refused consideration or rejected. The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent written objection to the Minister of Labour. [Para 204][Para 205][Para 210]

*Standing to bring interest claims.* Reading Article 96 of the Labour Law with Article 9 of Prakas No. 305/01 and Article 43 of Prakas No. 99/04, the panel held that only a most-representative union, or unions together representing more than half the workforce, may bring an interest dispute, because such an award replaces the collective agreement for a year and binds everyone. The claimant union is not most-representative at E Garment, so the panel declined to consider the interest claims at all. [Para 110][Para 112][Para 113][Para 115][Para 118]

*Meal (No. 3).* No statute, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice obliges the employer to give a midday meal or 4,000 riel in lieu; the claim is therefore one of interest, and was refused for want of standing. [Para 108][Para 109][Para 120]

*Output bonus (No. 5).* This one was a rights dispute. Notification No. 103 of 9 April 2013 fixes a minimum of 80 USD a month for full-rights workers and 75 USD for probationers, and Article 108 requires piece work to yield at least the guaranteed minimum for an average-skilled worker. The panel found the base-plus-incentive scheme lawful and even better than the law, but held that the employer may not claw the weekly bonus back to fill a short day, because doing so erodes the statutory base wage. It ordered that practice stopped, while refusing to force daily calculation, which adds administrative burden and is left to managerial discretion. [Para 129][Para 136][Para 139][Para 140][Para 141][Para 152][Para 153]

*Piece rate (No. 6).* Article 108 and Notification No. 103 permit piece rates that flex with style and stage, provided an average worker can earn the minimum. The union produced no evidence that the rates were too low, despite being asked and given a deadline, so the claim failed on the burden of proof. [Para 163][Para 165][Para 175][Para 181][Para 182]

*Attendance bonus (No. 7).* Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 requires an attendance bonus of at least 10 USD a month; the employer pays exactly that. No instrument requires 15 USD, and the claim is an interest claim, so it was refused. [Para 185][Para 186][Para 187][Para 190]

*Base wage (No. 8).* The employer pays 86 USD, above the 80 USD minimum in Notification No. 103; nothing requires 150 USD, and the claim is one of interest. [Para 192][Para 193][Para 194][Para 197]

*Travel and accommodation (No. 9).* The employer pays the 7 USD required by Notification No. 230; nothing requires 20 USD, and the claim is one of interest. [Para 198][Para 199][Para 202][Para 209]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An interest claim — one asking for a benefit no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or practice already grants — can be arbitrated only if brought by the most-representative union or by unions together representing more than half the enterprise's workforce, because the resulting award displaces the collective agreement for a year; a union without that status cannot put such a claim to the panel at all. A rights claim, by contrast, fails or succeeds on evidence, and an employer that pays at or above the statutory minimum still may not use a discretionary incentive to reduce the guaranteed monthly base wage. [Para 115][Para 140][Para 190]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that setting how often an output bonus is calculated — daily, weekly or monthly — is nowhere fixed by the Labour Law and lies within managerial discretion, subject only to legality and reasonableness, and that converting to daily calculation would add appreciable administrative burden. [Para 150][Para 151][Para 152]
