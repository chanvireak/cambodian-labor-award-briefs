# Award No. 152/12 — Cambodia Hoy Fwo Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 21 August 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Ly Tai Seng (employer-appointed), On Non (worker-appointed), Tang Tri (chair) · Doc ID 140162

## 1. Facts
Cambodia Hoy Fwo is a garment factory in Trapeang Krasang, Phnom Penh employing about 380 workers [Para 34][Para 35]. The Khmer Workers' Union at the company lodged seven demands on working conditions; conciliation on 20 July 2012 settled nothing because the employer did not attend, and the seven disputed points were referred to the panel [Para 16][Para 18][Para 19]. At the hearing of 9 August 2012 the parties agreed to merge Issues 2 and 3, and settled Issues 1, 2–3 and 4, leaving Issues 5, 6 and 7 for decision [Para 22][Para 24][Para 25]. The panel noted that the union had 24 members plus about 30 workers who thumb-printed an authorisation, roughly 54 of 380 workers, and was not the most representative union in the enterprise [Para 37][Para 67].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 5) Whether work on public holidays and Sundays is overtime, and whether workers who volunteer for it are entitled to a 5,000 riel meal allowance? [Para 39][Para 50]
2. (Issue 6) Whether the employer must pay a regular attendance bonus of USD 15 a month where it already pays the USD 7 the Ministry notification sets? [Para 44][Para 85]
3. (Issue 7) Whether the employer must pay USD 15 monthly travel allowance plus USD 15 monthly accommodation allowance? [Para 48][Para 91]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the law allows only two hours of overtime, so holiday and Sunday work exceeds it and warrants meal money; nearby factories pay 5,000 riel; living costs, rent and transport have all risen; the USD 7 bonus is too small and a USD 15 bonus would encourage attendance [Para 40][Para 41][Para 45][Para 48].

**Employer (respondent):** it is short of funds and cannot meet the demands; overtime is voluntary and holiday work is already paid at the statutory rate; it already pays the USD 7 attendance bonus required by Notification No. 041/11 [Para 42][Para 47][Para 49][Para 86].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider the claims for 5,000 riel holiday and Sunday meal money, a USD 15 attendance bonus and USD 15 + USD 15 travel and accommodation allowances; the award is non-binding on the interest issues [Para 95][Para 96][Para 97][Para 98].

On Issue 5 the panel split holiday work from Sunday work. Applying articles 137 and 139 (new) of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 10 of 4 February 1999, and following Award No. 115/08 (Thbavant), it held that work on a public holiday is not overtime: the worker already receives a day's wage for the holiday, so working it earns one further day's wage rather than a 50 per cent premium [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58]. Notification No. 041/11 point 2 therefore imposed no meal-money obligation for holidays, and the 5,000 riel demand was an interest dispute [Para 60][Para 61].

For Sundays the panel reasoned differently. The company works Monday to Saturday, eight hours a day, so Sunday is the weekly rest day and not a normal working day; following Award No. 114/08 (Whitex), voluntary Sunday work is overtime, entitling the worker to one free meal or 2,000 riel under the same notification [Para 72][Para 76][Para 78][Para 80]. Because the employer had never paid it, the panel confirmed that entitlement — but the 5,000 riel claimed exceeded the law and was refused as an interest demand [Para 79][Para 81][Para 83].

On Issue 6 the panel read point 1 of Notification No. 041/11 as setting a minimum attendance bonus of USD 7, found the employer already paid USD 7, and treated the demand for USD 15 as exceeding the legal minimum and therefore an interest dispute [Para 85][Para 86][Para 87][Para 90].

On Issue 7 the panel found no statute, collective agreement or contract fixing USD 15 travel and USD 15 accommodation, classified the demand as one of interest, and refused it [Para 91][Para 92][Para 94]. Across all three interest issues the panel applied its standing rule that an interest demand may only be brought by the most representative union, which this union was not [Para 62][Para 69].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim above a statutory minimum is an interest dispute even where the underlying entitlement exists: the panel will confirm the statutory floor (2,000 riel or one meal for voluntary Sunday overtime) but will not award the excess without a most-representative union to carry it [Para 78][Para 81][Para 69]. Work on a public holiday is paid as a further day's wage, not as overtime at a premium, and so attracts no overtime meal allowance [Para 57][Para 60].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that holiday work differs from ordinary work because the worker keeps the holiday wage and then earns one more day's wage for attending [Para 57].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award contradicts itself on the union's legal status: the evidence schedule lists a union registration certificate dated 15 March 2011, yet the reasons state the union "does not yet have" registration and use that to deny it standing [Para 28][Para 68]. The membership figure is printed two ways, first as 24 members and later as "4 … approximately 54" [Para 37][Para 67]. Line 98 carries a block of stray Latin-script fragments inside the final-type paragraph, and the invitation letters and letters of authority repeat each other with variant spellings of the factory name (Hoy Fwo / Huy Fwo / Hoi Fwo) [Para 28][Para 32][Para 98]. Individual workers, union officers and employer representatives are identified in the source by full name; the brief names none of them [Para 4].
