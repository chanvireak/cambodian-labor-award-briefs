# Award No. 79/12 — Generation International (Arbitration Council, 31 May 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, An Nan, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 140016

## 1. Facts
Generation International operates a footwear factory at Trapeang Ampil, Samraong Tong, Kampong Speu, and recognised the Democratic Union of Garment and Footwear Workers at the company as the workers' representative, affiliated to the Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers Democratic Unions. The union filed 16 demands covering start times, seniority and attendance bonuses, maternity pay, annual and sick leave, cleaning duties, transport, canteen facilities and the demotion of a team leader. Conciliation on 31 March 2012 settled some points; the rest came to the panel. [Para 1][Para 34]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must fix the start of work at 7:00 a.m. and stop requiring earlier attendance? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company may deduct the regular attendance bonus in proportion to days absent? [Para 3]
3. Whether maternity pay for 90 days must be calculated on the average of the previous 12 months' wages? [Para 4]
4. Whether the company must accept responsibility for workplace accidents by providing first aid? [Para 5]
5. Whether workers on one month's service must receive 1.5 days' paid annual leave per month? [Para 6]
6. Whether the company must instruct Chinese team leaders not to insult or shout at workers? [Para 7]
7. Whether the regular attendance bonus must be raised by US$3? [Para 8]
8. Whether the company must pay US$10 per worker per month for transport and accommodation? [Para 9]
9. Whether the company must stop all threats and pressure against union leaders? [Para 10]
10. Whether Ms C Srey Mom must be restored as B23 team leader with back pay? [Para 101]
11. Whether sick leave on a proper medical certificate, including from a private clinic, must be paid? [Para 116]
12. Whether the company must provide enough cleaners per team and section? [Para 121]
13. Whether the company must stop forcing overtime at the midday break? [Para 132]
14. Whether the toilets must be supplied with adequate water and soap? [Para 135]
15. Whether the company must send its own vehicle when the hired transport breaks down? [Para 137]
16. Whether workers leaving at 16:00 without overtime must still obtain an administration signature? [Para 141]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the demands were minimum standards owed under the Labour Law and the company's own internal rules; the demotion of Ms C Srey Mom followed her election as union vice-president and was discrimination, as she had no record of fault or warning. [Para 102][Para 103]
**Employer (respondent):** the demotion reflected weak management capacity, not union activity; the company has the right to allocate cleaning rotas and to manage its own transport, and any absence without permission justifies withholding the attendance bonus. [Para 109][Para 125][Para 139]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered maternity pay at 50% of the 12-month average, reinstatement of Ms C Srey Mom as B23 team leader with back pay of 40 a month, acceptance of duly certified medical certificates, and an end to compulsory cleaning rotas; it refused the attendance-bonus claim, the US$10 transport and accommodation payment, union use of the premises, extra cleaners and the standby vehicle. Non-binding award. [Para 266][Para 267][Para 270][Para 272][Para 275]

On Issue 2, the panel read the 7 March 2011 notification, which replaced the 2000 notification, as entitling a worker who attends on all working days to a regular bonus of at least US$7 a month, and held that absence without permission forfeits the bonus in proportion to the days absent. It followed its earlier awards in Yong Vanna I and VVIP. [Para 145][Para 148][Para 152][Para 155]

On Issue 3, Article 183 of the Labour Law gives a woman with one year's service half her wage during 90 days' maternity leave, and Article 103 defines wages to include overtime and bonuses. The panel therefore ordered the employer to average the 12 months before leave, including overtime and attendance bonus, divide by 12, halve the result and multiply by three, following its awards in CTU and Chhun Teaksmey. [Para 163][Para 166][Para 167][Para 171]

On Issue 8, the panel found the $10 claim was an interest dispute. Since the union was not the most representative in the factory, it lacked standing to bring interest demands, and the panel declined to consider the claim, noting that the $7 travel allowance already paid exceeded the statutory minimum. [Para 175][Para 178][Para 185][Para 187]

On Issue 9, the panel held that the right to assemble and distribute union information inside the factory outside working hours is not absolute, and that the employer's ban on gatherings was within its management rights where the union was not most representative. [Para 188][Para 194]

On Issue 10, the panel found the election was held on 20 December 2011 and Ms C Srey Mom was elected vice-president that day, while the employer produced no written evaluation to support the demotion in February 2012. It ordered her reinstated and paid the difference from demotion to reinstatement. [Para 108][Para 112][Para 270]

On Issue 11, the panel required the employer to accept certificates from officially recognised public or private doctors trained under the Ministry of Health framework, and refused to let the employer insist on state hospitals alone. [Para 116][Para 271]

On Issue 12, the panel ordered the company to stop requiring sewing workers to clean in rotation and to hire cleaners, while refusing the demand for additional cleaners per section because staffing is an employer prerogative. [Para 258][Para 272][Para 273]

On Issues 13, 14 and 16 the parties agreed at the hearing and the panel did not rule; Issues 4, 5, 6 and 7 were not addressed in the decision. [Para 132][Para 135][Para 143]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Maternity pay under Article 183 is computed on the average of the 12 months' wages preceding leave, and "wage" under Article 103 includes overtime and regular bonuses, so the 50% floor must be applied to that composite figure rather than to base pay alone. [Para 163][Para 166][Para 171]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that benefits an employer voluntarily provides above the statutory minimum do not convert an interest demand into an enforceable right, and that a union without most-representative status cannot compel the panel to arbitrate interest disputes. [Para 175][Para 178]
