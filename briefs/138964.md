# Award No. 008/16 — Hwa Long (Cambodia) Outsole Industry Ltd (Arbitration Council, 5 February 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Ang Eng Thong, chair) · Doc ID 138964

## 1. Facts
Hwa Long (Cambodia) Outsole Industry Ltd makes outsoles and assembles shoe parts in Cheng Prey district, Kampong Cham, and employed about 114 workers [Para 17][Para 18]. The Cambodian Labour Union Confederation (CLUF) held a local union election on 21 December 2015, electing Moeun Somreth, Pom Rum and Maly Sokhan as president, vice-president and secretary; the local union was not yet registered and the result was notified to the employer on 22 December 2015 [Para 18][Para 34]. All three had been hired in April–May 2015 on two-month probation, then given undetermined-duration contracts, and were working in outsole pressing; on 24 December 2015 the company transferred two of them to grass planting, and on 28 December 2015 terminated all three [Para 23][Para 24][Para 25].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay 3,000 riel per day in lieu of lunch? [Para 9]
2. Whether the employer must reinstate the three local union leaders to their original positions and pay them back pay from termination to reinstatement? [Para 10]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the three were elected local union leaders with special protection; the company terminated them because it was discriminating against the local union, having first asked two of them to resign from CLUF and join a rival local union formed by Chinese staff, and reassigning them to work outside their expertise without reasons [Para 25][Para 26][Para 33].
**Employer (respondent):** it would neither reinstate nor pay back pay because the production chain was unstable and the Chinese team leader was dissatisfied with performance — Moeun Somreth took four days' sick leave a month, Pom Rum pressed outsoles wrongly and came late, and Maly Sokhan's performance was unsatisfactory; the terminations had nothing to do with union discrimination [Para 26][Para 27][Para 28][Para 32].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to reinstate all three workers and pay them back pay from the date of termination to the date of reinstatement [Para 60]. Issue 9 having been withdrawn at the hearing, the panel did not consider it [Para 16][Para 21].

The panel applied Article 293, paragraph 1, of the Labour Law, under which a workers' delegate or candidate may be dismissed only with the labour inspector's authorisation, together with clause 3 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, which extends the same protection to candidates for union leadership [Para 38][Para 39]. Following Award 40/10-Meng Yan, it held that protection for elected candidates lasts at least 45 days after the ballot or until they apply for union registration, because Prakas 305 does not say when it ends for a candidate who wins [Para 41][Para 42].

It then applied the three conditions drawn from Awards 50/05, 64/05, 07/06, 09/06 and 148/07: worker status within Prakas 305, dismissal inside the protection period, and reliable notice to the employer of who is protected [Para 43]. All three were satisfied — the three were elected president, vice-president and secretary; termination came seven days after the 21 December ballot; and the security guard received and signed the 22 December notification, which the employer conceded it had received [Para 45][Para 48][Para 51][Para 52][Para 54].

On procedure, the employer admitted it had not sought the labour inspector's authorisation before terminating the contracts [Para 57]. Under clause 4 of Prakas 305 such a termination is null and void, and under clause 34 of Prakas 99 the panel may order reinstatement to the former or another appropriate position [Para 58]. The panel therefore found the terminations unlawful and ordered reinstatement with back pay, without needing to resolve the parties' factual dispute over performance or anti-union motive [Para 59][Para 60].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a worker is elected as a local union leader and the union is not yet registered, the special protection under Article 293 and Prakas 305 continues for at least 45 days after the election, and a termination within that window without prior labour inspector authorisation is null and void, entitling the worker to reinstatement and back pay regardless of the employer's performance reasons [Para 42][Para 49][Para 58][Para 60].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the employer terminated only these three and no other local union members was recorded as the employer's contention and was not weighed, the outcome turning wholly on the missing authorisation [Para 33][Para 59].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
This is an unofficial English translation bearing the stamp of the authoritative Khmer original, and the OCR drops the letter "t" throughout ("hemployer", "thearing", "lected", "duration"), which required reconstruction of many words; the evidence list was omitted from the English version by the translator [Para 16][Para 26]. The award date is printed as 05 February 2016 on the face but the body states the award date is 09 February 2016, and the hearing is recorded as 29 January 2016 at 02:00 P.M. in one place and 02:00 P.M. on that date in another [Para 1][Para 16][Para 11]. The conciliation report date (14 January 2016) and its submission date (18 January 2016) are consistently stated [Para 11]. The company's representative list is truncated mid-name at "Ms." before the names Peng Li and Phal Somnang, and the worker representatives' names are only recoverable from the hearing attendance list [Para 1][Para 2][Para 18].
