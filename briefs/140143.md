# Award No. 126/12 — Food Glory (Cambodia) Menu Food & Clearing Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 29 June 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Run Saray, president) · Doc ID 140143

## 1. Facts
Food Glory (Cambodia) Menu Food & Clearing Co., Ltd, a Phnom Penh enterprise, faced a collective dispute brought by the Federation of Unions for Labour Rights on behalf of four of its workers [Para 1][Para 12]. The non-conciliation report of 25 June 2012 listed four demands: six-monthly renewal of employment contracts, a $15 monthly lodging and travel allowance, retention of that allowance on sick and other leave days, and 200 per cent pay for work on public holidays as on Sundays [Para 2][Para 3][Para 4][Para 5]. The employer answered that no law provided for any of the four [Para 6]. The workers had struck since 21 June 2012 and continued after the case reached the Council [Para 15]. On the employer's request the panel issued order 016/12 of 25 June 2012 requiring the strike to be suspended at once and all strikers to return to their shifts from the morning of 26 June 2012 [Para 16]. They did not return [Para 17], the union did not attend the pre-hearing conference of 26 June 2012, and no hearing was ever held [Para 19][Para 22].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether, the workers having carried on their strike after order 016/12 required them to return to work, the panel should continue the arbitration at all? [Para 23][Para 30]
2. Whether the four demands carried over from the non-conciliation report should be decided on the merits — six-monthly renewal of employment contracts, a $15 monthly lodging and travel allowance, retention of that allowance on sick and other leave days, and 200 per cent pay for public-holiday work? [Para 2][Para 3][Para 4][Para 5]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the four demands were pressed through a strike that began on 21 June 2012 and continued after the case was referred, and the union neither attended the pre-hearing conference of 26 June 2012 nor filed any document; the panel recorded that it received no evidence from the worker side at all [Para 15][Para 19][Para 20]. Their substantive case therefore rests only on the demands as recorded in the non-conciliation report [Para 2].

**Employer (respondent):** the company stated flatly that it could not grant any of the four claims because the law does not provide for them [Para 6]. It attended the pre-hearing conference through its director and administrative staff and asked the panel to order the strike suspended so that arbitration could proceed, which the panel did [Para 1][Para 16].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** withdrawn — the panel ordered that the arbitration in case 126/12 not continue, deciding none of the four demands and awarding nothing [Para 31][Para 32].
The panel never reached the merits. It framed the operative question for itself as whether arbitration should continue at all [Para 23], and read Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 as requiring the parties to suspend every dispute action — strike or lock-out under Article 318 of the Labour Law — and any other measure that could aggravate the dispute for the duration of the proceedings, and as requiring them to attend every meeting to which the panel summons them [Para 23][Para 24].
It then followed its own prior interpretation that arbitration will not be continued while the worker party keeps striking [Para 25], citing notification 04/04 of 29 January 2004 and awards 28/06, 34/06, 110/11 and 59/12, in which files were closed or proceedings not continued for the same reason [Para 26][Para 27][Para 28][Para 29].
On the facts it found that the workers continued their strike throughout the proceedings, disobeyed order 016/12 of 25 June 2012, carried on striking while returning to work and failed to attend the pre-hearing conference of 26 June 2012 [Para 30][Para 31]. Because no hearing took place, the panel recorded that it had found no facts relating to the disputed issues and had received no documents from either party [Para 20][Para 22]. It therefore decided not to continue the arbitration, and issued no order for payment, contract renewal or holiday premium [Para 31][Para 32].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the worker party keeps striking, or otherwise keeps up dispute action, after the case has been referred and after the panel has ordered the strike suspended, the panel will not continue the arbitration: Article 20 of Prakas 099 obliges the parties to suspend strikes, lock-outs and any aggravating measure and to attend the panel's meetings, and non-compliance removes the basis on which the panel proceeds [Para 23][Para 25][Para 30]. The consequence is procedural closure of the file rather than adjudication, so the demands remain undecided and may be pursued afresh [Para 27][Para 31].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that a party must attend every meeting to which it is summoned, and its reliance on awards in which files were closed after two failed return-to-work orders, were not necessary to the outcome, which followed from the single finding that the strike continued [Para 24][Para 27].

## 6. Source defects disclosed

Conciliation is recorded as leaving 04 of 16 claims unsettled, but only four demands are ever listed, and the same 22 June 2012 date is given both for receipt of the complaint and for the conciliation attempt [Para 13][Para 14]. The panel states expressly that no facts were found and no hearing held, so section 4 rests on the procedural findings alone [Para 22]. Odd Latin fragments ("Maxin", "Kall", "Made", "The") are injected at the start of several sentences by OCR, and the employer's director is identified on the face of the award by a Latin name that is reduced here to a role [Para 1][Para 8][Para 28].
