# Award No. 241/14 — V Bi Bo Best Production (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 19 September 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chea, president) · Doc ID 139706

## 1. Facts
Workers at V Bi Bo Best Production (Cambodia), part of the V Bo Group, raised three unsettled claims: a lunch allowance of 4,000 riel, reinstatement of six named workers, and indefinite-duration contracts. They struck for about seven days from late August 2014; on 1 September 2014 the panel issued injunction No. 046/14 ordering the strike suspended and all strikers back from the afternoon of 2 September 2014. The workers did not return, and about 60 remained outside the factory gate, some saying they were not striking but were being refused entry. [Para 14][Para 17][Para 21]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of 4,000 riel per day? [Para 2]
2. Whether the employer must take back the six named workers and let them resume normal work? [Para 3]
3. Whether the employer must use indefinite-duration employment contracts? [Para 5]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The six named workers should be reinstated, the lunch allowance raised to 4,000 riel, and indefinite-duration contracts used; the workers outside the factory were not striking but were asking to be let in to work. [Para 3][Para 26]
**Employer (respondent):** It can afford only 1,000 riel of lunch allowance; it will not take back the six because they incited workers not to work and caused large losses, invoking Article 83; it keeps fixed-duration contracts as before, in accordance with the Labour Law; and it took a firm stand against 10 union activists and the six claimants, saying it would rather close the factory than let all 16 return. [Para 4][Para 30]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel terminated the arbitration proceedings in case No. 241/14 without deciding any of the three claims. [Para 61]
The panel began by asking whether it could continue at all, citing Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004: during the operation of a panel the parties must suspend every dispute measure — strike, lockout or any other measure capable of aggravating the dispute (as also provided in Article 318 of the Labour Law) — and must attend all meetings to which the panel summons them. It noted that in earlier cases it had declined to continue where a party took measures that aggravated the dispute, citing Award No. 49/10 (Wing Ying). [Para 49][Para 53]
It then found the parties' accounts irreconcilable: the union said the 60 workers were outside asking to work, while the company said they were still on strike and that it had called them in and terminated their contracts. The two telephone confirmations obtained on 4 September 2014 — from a union official and from the company's lawyer — showed the workers were not striking but that the company was refusing them entry. At the hearing the employer confirmed it would not admit the 10 union activists or the six claimants under any circumstances, and would rather shut the factory. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 57]
The panel held that the employer's dismissal of the 10 union activists, and its refusal to let workers back in after the injunction, were measures that aggravated the dispute, contrary to Article 20. On that basis it terminated the proceedings rather than rule on the lunch allowance, the reinstatement or the contract type. It recorded expressly that it made no findings of fact on the issues in the non-conciliation report, because it had not proceeded to a hearing on their substance. [Para 47][Para 59]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where, during an arbitration, a party takes measures that Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 requires it to suspend — continuing a strike after an injunction to return to work, or an employer dismissing union activists and refusing workers entry in a way that aggravates the dispute — the panel will terminate the proceedings instead of deciding the claims on their merits, leaving the disputed entitlements unresolved. [Para 53][Para 59]
A panel that terminates on that ground makes no findings of fact on the claims themselves. [Para 47]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted the employer's statement that it was preparing criminal complaints against the six workers, and that it had invoked Article 83 in refusing reinstatement, without ruling on either. [Para 32]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award contradicts itself on the hearing: it records a hearing on 16 September 2014 at which the panel questioned the parties, yet later states that no findings were made because the panel did not conduct a hearing. [Para 12][Para 47] The header dates the award 19 September 2014, the reasons date the hearing to "16 December", and one sentence places the events in 2017. [Para 14][Para 57] The number of workers excluded shifts between 60, 16 and 10 without reconciliation. [Para 30] The company appears as "វ៉ី បូ បេស ប្រូដាក សិន", "វីបូ បេស្ត ប្រូដាក សិន", "វីបូ ហ្គ្រុប" (V Bo Group, also covering "វីបូ ស៊ី អាយ ស៊ី") and "វ៉ីបូ អ៊ីអាស៊ី ហ្គាមេន"; the union's registration certificate belongs to the last of these, not to the respondent. [Para 1][Para 39] The signature block is badly corrupted by OCR. [Para 62]
