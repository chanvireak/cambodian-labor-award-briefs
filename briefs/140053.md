# Award No. 81/11 — GHC Cambodia (Arbitration Council, 6 October 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, An Vira, Nhan Sumnith) · Doc ID 140053

## 1. Facts
The Democratic Union of Garment Workers of Cambodia brought claims against GHC Cambodia, a garment factory at Stung Meanchey, Meanchey, Phnom Penh, on behalf of its members. The union alleged that the employer had never paid the seniority bonus (ប្រាក់រង្វាន់អតីតភាព), paid maternity leave only one month at a time, and refused to convert long-serving workers' fixed-duration contracts to indefinite-duration contracts. The employer ran successive fixed-duration contracts and terminated each at expiry. [Para 2][Para 3][Para 7]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a seniority bonus to workers who have worked for more than one year? [Para 2]
2. Whether the employer must pay 90 days' maternity leave wages, 50 per cent in advance, to female workers? [Para 5]
3. Whether the employer must convert the fixed-duration contracts of workers with more than two years' service into indefinite-duration contracts? [Para 7]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Seniority runs continuously from the start of actual work and the bonus has never been paid; the Labour Law requires 90 days' maternity leave at 50 per cent of wages paid before the leave; and workers on successive contracts exceeding two years are entitled to indefinite-duration contracts. [Para 2][Para 5][Para 7]

**Employer (respondent):** No worker has more than one year's seniority because every fixed-duration contract is terminated at its expiry and a new one signed; maternity wages are paid one month at a time and a representative may collect them; and the company lawfully continues fixed-duration contracts under Article 73 of the Labour Law. [Para 3][Para 6][Para 8]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay the seniority bonus to 111 workers listed in Annex B, applying Notification No. 017 (2000) for the period before March 2011 and Notification No. 041/11 from March 2011; to pay the full 90 days' maternity wages to two named female workers, at least one day before the leave began; and the contract-conversion claim was dismissed. [Para 208][Para 209][Para 207]

On issue 1, the panel first rejected the employer's res judicata objection. Although the same seniority claim had been decided in Case No. 143/10, the workers who brought the present claim were not the same, so the "arbitration exhausted" (Res Judicata) principle did not apply. [Para 101][Para 105][Para 106] The panel then construed "seniority" (អតីតភាពការងារ), which neither the Labour Law nor the notifications define. Reading Article 73 alongside Article 166, it held that seniority is measured by the actual period of work at the enterprise, not by the sequence of contracts. It found 111 workers who had more than one year's service and whose claims the employer had not effectively contested. [Para 129][Para 130][Para 113][Para 199]

On issue 2, the panel found that the employer's practice of paying maternity wages one month at a time did not satisfy the statutory requirement that 90 days be paid in advance. It ordered full payment to the two workers concerned before their leave commenced. [Para 85][Para 209]

On issue 3, the panel held that the claim sought to change the future legal character of the employment relationship without identifying any concrete present infringement of a right. Citing Article 74 of the Civil Procedure Code and its own earlier awards, it reasoned that the Council resolves actual disputes, not hypothetical future ones, and dismissed the claim. [Para 192][Para 193][Para 202][Para 206]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Seniority for the seniority bonus is measured by continuous actual employment at the enterprise, not by the type or sequence of contracts; renewal of successive fixed-duration contracts does not reset seniority, and the employer's obligation to pay the bonus arises once service exceeds one year. [Para 199][Para 200][Para 130]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that it was not exercising the constitutional power to interpret law, and that ILO recommendations are not binding instruments it must apply. [Para 128][Para 329]
