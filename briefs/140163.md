# Award No. 148/12 — Golden Gen Sxu Cambodia Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 28 August 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Chiv Phirom (employer-appointed), Tuon Si Phan (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (chair) · Doc ID 140163

## 1. Facts
Golden Gen Sxu Cambodia Co., Ltd has run a footwear factory at Chom Chao, Phnom Penh since 2006 with about 2,600 workers [Para 54][Para 55]. Two unions exist on site: the Cambodia Workers' Union, certified as most representative with 1,600 members, and the CAWDU-affiliated union registered on 28 March 2012 with 831 members, which brought this claim on behalf of 1,394 workers by thumb-printed authority [Para 56][Para 57]. Of sixteen demands, five remained unsettled after conciliation on 20 July 2012 and were referred to the panel; a strike over compulsory overtime had run from 18 to 24 July 2012 [Para 19][Para 20][Para 99]. Because both sides had signed the memorandum of understanding of 28 September 2010, the panel divided its decision into binding rights findings and non-binding interest findings [Para 24][Para 25].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether severance for unused annual leave must be calculated on the average wage of the last twelve months? [Para 58][Para 125]
2. (Issue 2) Whether maternity leave pay must be calculated on the average wage of the last twelve months rather than the last month's wage? [Para 64][Para 143]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must provide two meals a day, or USD 2 a month in lieu? [Para 70][Para 151]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must raise the milk allowance from USD 10 to USD 20 a month until the child is 18 months old? [Para 78][Para 162]
5. (Issue 5) Whether withholding the small bonus and issuing warnings for missed voluntary overtime amount to forcing workers to work overtime? [Para 173][Para 175][Para 182]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** severance and maternity pay should both use the twelve-month average because the law says so and the last month understates earnings; two meals or USD 2 is needed for health and productivity; USD 10 does not buy enough milk, since a tin costs about USD 12 and two to three tins are needed monthly; the small bonus and warning letters are pressure to work overtime [Para 60][Para 73][Para 80][Para 115].

**Employer (respondent):** the collective agreement and the additional agreement of 16 July 2012 fix the last month's wage as the basis for severance, and maternity pay computed on the last month is actually more generous; the company cannot afford meals, has never provided food, and gives only rest breaks; it pays the agreed USD 10 milk allowance; it acted under the 24 July 2012 overtime agreement [Para 58][Para 65][Para 75][Para 84][Para 116].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay maternity leave at 50 per cent of wages and allowances computed on the twelve-month average, and to stop issuing warnings to workers for failing to perform overtime under the 24 July 2012 agreement; the annual-leave, meal, milk and small-bonus claims were refused [Para 206][Para 207][Para 210][Para 209].

On Issue 1 the panel classified the demand as a rights dispute under article 166 but refused it on the evidence: to decide, it needed the list of claimants, their current employment status and their remaining leave days, and the workers had supplied none of that by the 14 August 2012 evidence deadline [Para 127][Para 136][Para 137][Para 140].

On Issue 2 the panel applied articles 182 and 183, read with Awards No. 33/07 (Gold Fame) and No. 06/08 (King Land), and held that maternity pay is half of wages including allowances averaged over the twelve months before the leave; the employer's last-month method was unlawful, and the award states the arithmetic (twelve-month total ÷ 12 ÷ 2 × 3) [Para 143][Para 147][Para 150].

Issues 3, 4 and 5(a) turned on classification. The meal claim had no basis in law, collective agreement or contract and was an interest demand refused for want of a majority-representative union [Para 151][Para 152][Para 160]. The milk claim was a rights dispute, but the collective agreement of 13 March 2012 fixes USD 10 and the panel noted that the agreed duration already implies payment to 20 months; it declined to award more than the agreement and refused the USD 20 claim [Para 164][Para 169][Para 172]. The small bonus was found to be a discretionary incentive conditional on under eight hours' absence from overtime a month, outside any statutory entitlement, so withholding it was not coercion [Para 175][Para 177][Para 180].

On Issue 5(b) the panel held the 24 July 2012 overtime agreement invalid: article 96 and Prakas No. 305 article 9 reserve collective bargaining to the most representative union, and a minority union cannot conclude a separate agreement covering only part of the workforce, which would fragment conditions already governed by the collective agreement [Para 190][Para 196][Para 198][Para 201]. Warnings founded on that agreement had no effect, and the employer was ordered to stop issuing them [Para 203][Para 205][Para 210].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A rights claim that depends on individualised facts will be refused if the claimant supplies no list of affected workers, their employment status or the amounts owing, even where the legal entitlement is clear [Para 136][Para 137]. An agreement on overtime signed by a union that is not the most representative cannot justify disciplinary warnings, because article 96 confines collective bargaining to the representative union [Para 196][Para 203].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that a separate agreement by a minority union fragments working conditions across the enterprise and undermines the collective agreement, even where the employer acted in good faith under it [Para 197][Para 198].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The signature block prints four names where the header identifies three arbitrators, the extra name being an unexplained addition beside the presiding arbitrator's line, and it is surrounded by stray Latin fragments [Para 214]. The company name is rendered at least five different ways across the file ("Golden Gen Sxu", "Golden Den Gen Su", "Gold Den Hen Si" and others), and the union there is variously described as a garment and a footwear union [Para 1][Para 35][Para 37][Para 56]. Two paragraphs repeat each other almost verbatim where OCR merged a running header into the text, and issue numbering skips where Issue 3 was redefined at the hearing from three meals to two [Para 47][Para 70][Para 212]. Individual workers, union officers and employer representatives are named in full in the source; the brief names none of them.
