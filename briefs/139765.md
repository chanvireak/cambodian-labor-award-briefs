# Award No. 318/14 — Nan Ku Garmen Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 December 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chea presiding; Ing Sothy, employer nominee; An Non, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139765

## 1. Facts
Nan Ku Garmen Co., Ltd runs a garment factory in the Sihanoukville Special Economic Zone, Prey Nob district, employing about 375 workers, some 90 percent of them women. [Para 31][Para 55] A collective dispute over allowances and maternity benefits went to conciliation on 13 November 2014, where three of nine claims were settled and six were referred to the Council. [Para 15] Ninety-one workers delegated their rights to four union federations by letters dated 30 October 2014, and four more women appeared at the hearing of 28 November 2014, giving 95 claimants. [Para 34][Para 87]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise the transport and accommodation allowance from the USD 9 it pays to USD 15 a month? [Para 41]
2. Whether the employer must pay the overtime meal allowance once a week, every Saturday, rather than on another day of the week? [Para 45]
3. Whether the employer may withhold the incentive bonus from workers who fail to work overtime at its request for two or three days in a month? [Para 51]
4. Whether a pregnant worker may take one day a month for a prenatal check while keeping her wage, bonus and other benefits? [Para 55]
5. Whether maternity pay must be paid in a lump sum before the 90-day leave begins rather than month by month? [Para 61]
6. Whether the employer must keep paying the USD 5 monthly "cooperation money" to workers with three months' service? [Para 65]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the base wage is USD 100 while food and transport costs have risen, so the USD 9 travel and lodging allowance should rise to USD 15; meal money should be handed out on Saturday so it can be spent on the Sunday rest day; the incentive bonus was unconditional until September 2014; and maternity pay in monthly instalments leaves women short when expenses peak. [Para 43][Para 51][Para 63]
**Employer (respondent):** USD 9 already exceeds the USD 7 fixed by Notification No. 230; meal money is paid weekly but on a weekday because banks close on Saturday; the overtime-without-absence condition on the incentive bonus has always existed and merely was never written down; and lump-sum maternity pay would encourage women not to return to work. [Para 44][Para 47][Para 52][Para 64]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issues 1, 3, 4 and 6 dismissed; the employer ordered to pay the overtime meal allowance every Saturday and to pay maternity leave at 50 percent of wages for 90 days at least one day before the leave starts. [Para 178][Para 182]

The panel first disposed of the employer's objection that the four federations lacked authority. It held the objection to the unsigned minutes of 3 November 2014 too vaguely argued to be examined, and, on article 19 of Prakas 99/04, held that a disputing party may be represented by anyone authorised in writing and need not attend conciliation or hearing in person; the objection that the 91 authorisation letters bore no company ID numbers was not specific enough, since the employer did not say whether it challenged all or only some workers. The panel therefore accepted the 91 delegations plus the four women present. [Para 75][Para 80][Para 83][Para 87]

On issue 1, Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 fixes USD 7 a month and the employer pays USD 9. The panel found no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice requiring a further USD 6, and classified the claim as an interest dispute. [Para 88][Para 90][Para 92]

On issue 2, Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 gives 2,000 riel a day or one free meal for voluntary overtime but says nothing about when it is paid. Reading that silence as leaving the timing to whatever lets a worker buy food for the overtime shift, the panel rejected the employer's bank-travel excuse — there are banks in the Sihanoukville zone — and ordered weekly payment on Saturday. [Para 103][Para 108][Para 121]

On issue 3, the panel treated the condition that a worker do overtime without absence, or with no more than two absences a month, as an exercise of the employer's management right under article 2 of the Labour Law, lawful and reasonable, and noted that the scheme pays more than the law requires. [Para 126][Para 134]

On issue 4, the panel found no legal provision, agreement or collective agreement granting a paid monthly prenatal day, and classified the claim as an interest dispute. [Para 148]

On issue 5, articles 182 and 183 require 90 days' maternity leave at half wage including secondary benefits for women with a year's service. Article 115(3) forbids paying wages on a rest day and requires payment a day early, and article 103 counts maternity payments as wages, so the panel held the monthly practice unlawful and ordered payment before the leave begins. [Para 154][Para 160][Para 166][Para 169]

On issue 6, the workers could not say when the USD 5 was paid, to whom, in which section, or when it stopped, and filed no evidence by the deadline; with no identified claimants the panel could not rule. [Para 68][Para 176]

Issues 1, 3 and 4 failed on a single jurisdictional ground: an award in an interest dispute becomes a collective agreement binding all workers for one year and removes their right to strike over the same demand, so the panel decides such claims only where the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise, holding over half the workforce. The 95 claimants, drawn from four federations not yet registered at the Ministry, did not meet that test. [Para 33][Para 99][Para 100][Para 145]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A panel will decide an interest dispute — a demand for more than the law or an existing agreement already gives — only where the union bringing it is the most representative in the enterprise, meaning more than half the workers are members, because its award becomes a collective agreement binding every worker for a year and extinguishes the right to strike on that demand; a minority union's claim is declined without examination of its merits. [Para 99][Para 100][Para 102]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that meal money need not be paid daily, weekly or monthly so long as the worker can buy food for the overtime shift, and that a free meal is an added inducement to work further overtime — remarks wider than the order for Saturday payment required. [Para 106][Para 113]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The operative paragraph on maternity pay prints "500 percent" of wages where the reasoning and the final order both say 50 percent. [Para 166][Para 170][Para 182] Issue 3 is pleaded as two or three missed overtime days but reasoned and ordered as three. [Para 51][Para 180] Issue 4's heading says only that annual leave is deducted, while the reasons add a deduction from the regular attendance bonus. [Para 57][Para 147] The employer appears as Nan Ku, Nan Kou and Nan Tou Garmen across the file, and one sentence of the employer's submissions is printed twice. [Para 26][Para 31][Para 54] Untranslated English fragments survive in the text, and the signature block lost the arbitrators' names to OCR garbage, so the panel is named here only from the header. [Para 62][Para 185]
