# Award No. 003/24 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខ្វាន់ ទុម ខ្លូស៊ីង (ខេមបូឌា) លីមីធីត (Arbitration Council, 20 May 2024)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៉ីង សុទ្ធី appointed by the employer party, អន ណន appointed by the worker party, ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា chair) · Doc ID 140883

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខ្វាន់ ទុម ខ្លូស៊ីង (ខេមបូឌា) លីមីធីត is a garment factory in Sangkat Chom Chao, Khan Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh, which began operating in 2000 and now employs 641 workers, 577 of them women. [Para 1][Para 87][Para 95] From 2008 to 2022 it paid every worker an addition on top of base wage; in 2023 it stopped paying it to workers whose base wage was already at or above the minimum wage. [Para 95][Para 166]

The 105 claimants, represented by សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ, demanded USD 6 more on base wage for those earning USD 200 or more, which they put at USD 630 a month and USD 7,560 for 2023. [Para 93][Para 140] Four demands went to conciliation on 21 March 2024 without agreement. [Para 20][Para 21] At the hearing the parties settled issues 2 and 4, the workers withdrew issue 3, and only issue 1 remained. [Para 26][Para 27]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must add USD 6 to the base wage of the 105 workers whose base wage was already at or above the 2023 minimum wage, as it had done in previous years? [Para 140][Para 181]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, តំណាង កម្មករ និយោជិត):** in 2023 the employer paid the extra USD 6 only to workers on a base wage of USD 194 to bring them up to the USD 200 minimum, and paid nothing to those already at USD 200 or more, although all work in the same factory. [Para 98][Para 100] Since the employer resumed payments in 2024 and the company's position has improved, past practice should continue under Article 13 of the Labour Law and Article 106 on equal pay for equal skill and hours. [Para 101][Para 103][Para 104] They also argued the union's most-representative certificate remains valid because no union has challenged it within the two years set by Article 60. [Para 105][Para 149]

**Employer (respondent):** it applied Prakas No. 247/22 of 21 September 2022 correctly by topping up only wages below the minimum, and it has no profit to distribute after COVID-19 losses, four suspension orders in 2023, and a flood that cost USD 200,000. [Para 3][Para 4] The workforce fell from over 2,000 to 641. [Para 106] The USD 6 was never a legal obligation; permanent staff are assessed each September instead, and the most-representative union's certificate has expired. [Para 108][Para 111]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the claim for an additional USD 6 on base wage for the 105 workers earning USD 200 or more, which the workers valued at USD 630 a month and USD 7,560 for 2023. [Para 200][Para 201]

Two threshold questions were decided first. The panel held that under Article 60 of the Law on Trade Unions a most-representative certificate cannot be challenged for two years after it is granted; the certificate here is No. 006/21 of 13 January 2021, and the employer did not contest the workers' assertion that no other union had challenged it, so the status stood. [Para 150][Para 153][Para 154][Para 155] Under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 a party may act through a representative holding written authority, which the 105 thumbprints supplied. [Para 158][Para 159][Para 164] Documents filed only in a foreign language were not considered, Article 23 of the same Prakas making Khmer the language of proceedings. [Para 134][Para 136][Para 139]

On the merits the panel applied the past-practice doctrine: a practice is established by regular, consistent, clear and stable conduct accepted by both sides, and Article 65(2) and (3) of the Labour Law lets unwritten terms follow it. [Para 170][Para 172][Para 175][Para 179] The USD 6 addition paid to all workers from 2008 to 2022 was such a practice. [Para 180] But a past practice may lawfully end where the circumstances that grounded it change or the parties agree otherwise. [Para 182] The panel found the 2023 suspension justified by the documented losses, the four suspension authorisations, the flood closure and the fall in the workforce, and noted that the workers themselves accepted the economic position and made no claim that year. [Para 190][Para 195][Para 196][Para 197] The employer had resumed a USD 4 addition for all workers in 2024. [Para 198] There was therefore no duty to keep paying the USD 6. [Para 199]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A benefit paid regularly and consistently to all workers over many years becomes a past practice that the employer must continue, but it may be terminated lawfully where the objective circumstances that supported it have genuinely changed — here proved by suspension orders, falling orders and a reduced workforce — or where the parties agree to replace it; the workers' own acceptance of the changed situation and failure to claim in the affected year supports that conclusion. [Para 175][Para 179][Para 182][Para 196] A most-representative certificate is immune from challenge for two years under Article 60 of the Law on Trade Unions, and an unchallenged assertion that no rival union has contested it suffices to establish the claimant's standing. [Para 150][Para 155]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's treatment of the language rule under Article 23 of Prakas No. 099, excluding the employer's foreign-language evidence, is procedural and did not affect the outcome; the passing observation that no worker is paid below the minimum wage is likewise background. [Para 97][Para 139]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The employer's name is printed in more than ten different Khmer spellings across the file, including variants where the second and third elements are reordered; one of them reads “ខ្វាន់ ទូរ អង្គុយ”. [Para 1][Para 7][Para 31]
- The employer's representatives at the hearing appear as bare Latin initials rather than names, and the union's four leaders in the attendance list are given as truncated forms; none was expanded. [Para 1][Para 156]
- The panel was constituted “on 28 March 2017” and “2024” in the same sentence. [Para 21]
- The operative paragraph prints the sum claimed as “USD 68” where every earlier reference is “USD 6”. [Para 201][Para 140]
- The number of claimants appears as 105, as 127 thumbprints of whom 116 still work, and the union's request letter describes “104 (four) points”; the panel worked from the 105 verified signatures. [Para 73][Para 130][Para 165]
- Months and years are lost in the employer's letters of 12 October (year printed as “20”) and in the claim letter of 16 February 2024, whose date is partly replaced by digits. [Para 61][Para 76]
