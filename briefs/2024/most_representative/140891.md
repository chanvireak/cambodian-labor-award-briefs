# Award No. 011/24 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន គ្លូប៊ល អេស៊ា អឹភែរឹល (ខេមបូឌា) ឯ.ក (Arbitration Council, 24 October 2024)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៉ីង សុទ្ធី appointed by the employer, អន ណន appointed by the workers, គង់ ផល្លៈ presiding) · Doc ID 140891

## 1. Facts
A collective labour dispute at ក្រុមហ៊ុន គ្លូប៊ល អេស៊ា អឹភែរឹល (ខេមបូឌា) ឯ.ក, a garment and sock factory employing 1,423 workers, was filed by the workers' representative of the company, organised through the union សហជីព ការងារ ដើម្បីកម្ពុជា. [Para 46][Para 15] The demand, thumbprinted by 61 workers on 13 July 2024, listed 13 points of working conditions. [Para 15] The Labour Dispute Department settled 8 of the 12 remaining points and referred the 4 unsettled points to the Council on 16 September 2024. [Para 17][Para 18] At the hearing the workers withdrew Issue 4 and both sides agreed Issue 3, leaving Issues 1 and 2 for decision. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay 50% of total wages, including supplementary allowances, for the 90 days of maternity leave in a single payment before the leave begins? [Para 51]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay a US$15 monthly skill allowance to workers able to operate three or more machines, and a US$10 monthly role allowance to workers serving as assistants? [Para 73]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the workers' representative argued that Article 115 of the Labour Law forbids paying wages on a day the worker is entitled to rest, so the whole 50% maternity wage for 90 days must be handed over before the leave starts; on skills, they said workers at the old building receive the allowance while equally skilled workers at the new building do not. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 76]
**Employer (respondent):** the company said it pays maternity wage monthly through a Wing bank account as it has since 2021, that the law fixes no payment date, and that it has no policy granting a skill allowance; the US$10 incentive is payable only to assistants appointed and signed off by the Chinese production head. [Para 57][Para 62][Para 77][Para 87]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay 50% of total wages and allowances for 90 days to the pregnant claimant, Ms P, before her maternity leave begins, and dismissed both the US$15 skill allowance claimed for 20 workers and the US$10 assistant incentive. [Para 164][Para 165][Para 166]
On Issue 1 the panel applied Articles 182 and 183 of the Labour Law, which grant 90 days' leave and half pay including allowances, but found that neither article fixes when payment must be made. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 108] Reading Article 115(3), which bars paying wages on a rest day and requires payment the day before, together with Article 103, which lists maternity pay among the components of wages, the panel held that the 50% maternity wage must be paid at least one day before the leave starts. [Para 109][Para 112][Para 113] It followed its earlier awards 139/08, 109/09, 27/12, 35/13, 091/15, 203/16 and 026/17. [Para 115] Only one claimant proved her pregnancy, thumbprint and employee number, so relief went to her alone although the union asserted four women. [Para 104][Para 96]
On Issue 2 the panel found that 20 workers had proved names and thumbprints in both the annexed list and the 13 July 2024 letter, but held the claim to be an interest dispute. [Para 124][Para 135] Because the claimant union was neither the most representative union under the new Article 54 of the Law on Unions nor qualified under Article 72, the panel declined to decide it, as Article 43(1) of Prakas 099 reserves awards on interest disputes to such unions. [Para 147][Para 150][Para 145] The US$10 incentive failed because it is an extra benefit found in no statute or company policy, and the employer's condition of appointment by the Chinese production head was a lawful exercise of management under Article 2. [Para 161][Para 163][Para 153]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the Labour Law fixes an amount of maternity pay but not its date, the panel reads Article 115 into the entitlement and requires the 50% maternity wage for the full 90 days to be paid before the leave begins, not monthly during it. [Para 118]
A demand for a benefit found in no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice is an interest dispute, and the panel will not decide it unless the claimant union holds most-representative status under the new Article 54 of the Law on Unions or qualifies under Article 72. [Para 147][Para 133]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the parties wrote the claimant's name differently and treated the variants as one worker because neither side alleged that two different workers were meant. [Para 71][Para 72]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The counts of demands conflict: the Department recorded 13 points, the settlement 12, and the union's annexed letter carries 16 numbered items. [Para 15][Para 17][Para 172] The claimant's name is spelled differently by the workers and the employer, and her employee number appears as "1" in one passage and as 0824 in another. [Para 71][Para 96][Para 104] Issue 2 amounts are printed as US$15 and US$10 per month in the claims but as "1500 dollars" and "1000 dollars" in the two framing paragraphs of the reasoning. [Para 126][Para 153] The award is dated 24 October 2024 on its face, yet paragraph 19 reads "18 September 2019 ... 2024" and the annexed form carries 2016, 2017 and a 2015-style case reference. [Para 1][Para 19][Para 169] Sentences in the union letter and the internal-rules exhibit are partly destroyed. [Para 30][Para 84]
