# Award No. 005/24 — Komo Apparel Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន កូម៉ូ អឹភែរឹល ឯ.ក) (Arbitration Council, 3 May 2024)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Uk Ri for the employer, An Non for the workers, Pen Bun Chhea presiding) · Doc ID 140880

## 1. Facts
Komo Apparel Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន កូម៉ូ អឹភែរឹល ឯ.ក) runs a garment-cutting and sewing factory at Ampil, Kamboul, Phnom Penh, with 1,605 workers [Para 1][Para 57]. On 18 March 2024 the workers' representatives filed 14 demands to improve working conditions [Para 14]. Conciliation on 2 April 2024 settled 11 of them, leaving three, which reached the Council by report No. 260 of 8 April 2024 [Para 15][Para 16]. At the hearing the parties agreed Issue 2 and the workers withdrew Issue 1, leaving Issue 3 alone [Para 20][Para 21][Para 22].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must supply one mid-day meal, or pay 4,000 riel for it, to each worker every day [Para 59][Para 69]?

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, through the union សហជីព ស្មារតី កម្មករ ខ្មែរ):** the 1,500 riel a day the company pays is too little to buy a mid-day meal, and workers must also cover their own breakfast and evening food out of wages, so the allowance should be paid daily rather than weekly [Para 62][Para 64].

**Employer (respondent):** orders have fallen sharply, so it cannot fund 4,000 riel a day; the 1,500 riel it has paid since the notice of 2 May 2022 already exceeds what the law requires [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider the workers' demand that the employer provide one mid-day meal or pay 4,000 riel a day, and made no order [Para 90][Para 91].

The panel recorded that both sides accept the company pays 1,500 riel a day, handed out weekly on Saturday, and that no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice requires 4,000 riel daily [Para 59][Para 71]. It characterised the demand as an additional benefit found in no legal instrument, and therefore as a dispute over interests rather than rights [Para 72][Para 73]. It then applied Article 96(2) of the Labour Law, which defines a collective agreement as a written agreement between an employer and a union representing the workers [Para 74][Para 76]. Because an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement under Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, the panel held it may decide such a dispute only where the claimant union holds most-representative status under the new Article 54 of the Union Law, or has sufficient characteristics under Article 72 [Para 83][Para 84][Para 85]. It noted that this company has no most-representative union [Para 61]. Following Awards No. 118/16, 203/16, 19, 060/20, 035/21 and 038/21, it found the claimant was neither the most-representative union nor one with sufficient characteristics, and so did not consider the claim [Para 87][Para 88][Para 89].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A panel may decide an interest dispute — including a demand for a benefit that no law, collective agreement or practice provides — only where the claimant union is the most representative union in the enterprise or has the characteristics required by Article 72 of the Union Law; otherwise the demand is not considered [Para 72][Para 85][Para 88].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remark that the 1,500 riel already exceeds the statutory minimum rests on the respondent's own submission rather than on any cited instrument [Para 66].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The demand is printed "១ ៤០០០ រៀល" at [Para 6] but "៤០០០ រៀល" at [Para 59] and [Para 91], so the figure could be read as 14,000 or 4,000; the brief follows the decision. The non-conciliation report date is given as 9 March 2024 at [Para 11] and 8 April 2024 at [Para 16]. The panel-constitution sentence duplicates the year, "ឆ្នាំ ឆ្នាំ ២០២៤", at [Para 17]. The company name is spelled at least six different ways across the award, including "កូម៉ូ អឹកែ រឹល" and "ភូម៉ូ អឹភែរឹល" [Para 11][Para 16][Para 25]. The five worker representatives are reduced to bare fragments [Para 58].
