# Award No. 002/24 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ត្រាកស៍ អឹភែរឹល (ខេមបូឌា) (Arbitration Council, 10 April 2024)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (យូ សួន ទី appointed by the employer party, លីវ សុវណ្ណា appointed by the worker party, កុយ នាម chair) · Doc ID 140878

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ត្រាកស៍ អឹភែរឹល (ខេមបូឌា) runs a garment factory in Sangkat Tuek Thla, Khan Sen Sok, Phnom Penh, with 2,806 workers. [Para 1][Para 52] On 31 January 2024 the representative of its workers filed 20 demands; conciliation on 23 February 2024 settled none of 14 of them, and six came to the panel. [Para 21][Para 22] At the hearing the parties agreed issues 1, 2 and 6, leaving issues 3, 4 and 5. [Para 26][Para 27]

Those three concerned paid leave for CATU leaders and activists to attend federation training, the production-target bonus paid to sewing workers but not to five other sections, and an extra USD 40 responsibility allowance for 13 table-cutting section leaders. [Para 139][Para 164][Para 204] The workers' post-hearing statement of 21 March 2024 added a seventh claim not found in the non-conciliation report; it was not argued at the hearing and the evidence supporting it arrived on 27 March 2024, after the deadline, so the panel left it undecided. [Para 66][Para 133][Para 135][Para 137]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must grant union leaders and activists additional paid leave beyond 4 days or 32 hours a year so they can attend training with their federation? [Para 139]
2. (Issue 4) Whether workers in the ironing, fabric-pulling, folding, tying and numbering sections are entitled to the same production-target bonus as sewing-section workers? [Para 164]
3. (Issue 5) Whether the employer must pay an additional USD 40 responsibility (position or skill) allowance to the 13 table-cutting chiefs? [Para 204]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, តំណាង កម្មករ និយោជិត):** from 2022 the company granted leave whenever the federation wrote asking leaders and activists to attend training, but from January 2023 it stopped and cut wages and benefits for the days attended; 4 days or 32 hours a year is not enough, particularly for women leaders. [Para 72][Para 77][Para 85] On the bonus, the same quotas are set for the five sections yet only sewing workers are paid, which is discrimination and forced labour. [Para 93][Para 166] The table-cutting chiefs are skilled, long-serving workers whose workload the employer itself concedes is heavy. [Para 116][Para 121]

**Employer (respondent):** it already gives each listed union leader 4 days or 32 hours a year with pay and benefits by agreement with all three unions at the factory, which is better than the law requires, and further days may be taken from annual leave. [Para 78][Para 81] It sets no quota it does not also apply to sewing, does not deduct pay or discipline anyone who misses a target, and its bonus scheme follows the parent company's standard in Thailand and Vietnam. [Para 97][Para 99][Para 100] The 13 chiefs already receive USD 20 of responsibility pay; adding USD 40 would upset other workers. [Para 118][Para 119]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider or rejected all three remaining demands: extra paid training leave beyond 4 days or 32 hours a year, the sewing-section target bonus for the ironing, fabric-pulling, folding, tying and numbering sections, and the additional USD 40 responsibility allowance for the 13 table-cutting chiefs. [Para 214][Para 215][Para 216]

On Issue 3 the panel held that Articles 266 and 267 of the Labour Law and Articles 9 and 20 of the Law on Trade Unions address the right to form and run a union, not time off for training. [Para 141] The union's own statutes bind only its members, not the employer. [Para 142] An agreement already gives 4 days or 32 hours with pay, and no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice requires more. [Para 143][Para 145] The demand was therefore a dispute over interests rather than rights, which the panel may decide only where the claimant union is the most representative union under Article 54 (new) of the Law on Trade Unions or holds negotiating status under Article 72. [Para 147][Para 155][Para 158] The claimant was neither. [Para 161][Para 163]

On Issue 4 the panel found no discrimination on any ground listed in Article 12 of the Labour Law. [Para 167][Para 169] Article 8 of the Law on Minimum Wage requires equal pay for work of equal conditions, equal professional skill and equal output; a target bonus is part of wage under Article 103, but the four elements of equal work — same task, same process, same environment, same equipment — are not met as between the sewing section and the other five. [Para 173][Para 175][Para 181][Para 185] Article 15 on forced labour was not engaged, and the employer's direction of bonuses under Article 2 was lawful and reasonable. [Para 187][Para 191][Para 199][Para 203]

On Issue 5 the panel read Articles 102, 103, 104, 106 and 107 of the Labour Law as governing wages and the conditions for earning them, with nothing providing for an extra USD 40 allowance. [Para 207] No collective agreement, internal rule or past practice created one either. [Para 208] The demand was again an interests dispute, and the claimant was not a most representative or otherwise qualified union, so the panel declined to consider it. [Para 210][Para 211][Para 213]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a demand seeks a benefit that no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or established practice provides, the panel treats it as a dispute over interests and will not decide it unless the claimant union is the most representative union under Article 54 (new) of the Law on Trade Unions or has the negotiating status conferred by Article 72; a union's internal statutes cannot bind the employer. [Para 146][Para 158][Para 142] A claim that a target bonus is discriminatory fails where the four elements of equal work are not satisfied, even though the bonus counts as wage under Article 103. [Para 173][Para 185]

**Obiter dicta:** the remarks that the panel's award on an interests dispute would bind all workers and would strip them of their right to bargain and strike are general observations on the effect of such awards, not steps in the order. [Para 157] The panel's reference to earlier awards on the same questions is explanatory only. [Para 132][Para 160]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The employer's name is printed in at least four different Khmer spellings across the file, and the case is headed both 002/24 and 002/24-ត្រាកស៍; the Latin form of the name appears nowhere. [Para 1][Para 23][Para 47]
- The parties' representatives at the hearing are printed as bare initials rather than names, and the worker representatives in the evidence list appear as initials only; these were left unexpanded. [Para 1][Para 53]
- The workers' post-hearing statement numbers seven claims but labels the fifth and sixth items as “dispute 10” and “dispute 20”, while the non-conciliation report contains only six issues numbered 1 to 6; the panel worked from the report's numbering. [Para 61][Para 63][Para 66]
- The number of table-cutting chiefs is printed as 13, 14, 10, and “16 or 19” in different places. [Para 112][Para 113][Para 122]
- Amounts and dates are garbled in places: a conciliation date reads “23 February 20248”, and the responsibility allowance is printed as both “USD 40” and “USD 20”. [Para 22][Para 204][Para 112]
