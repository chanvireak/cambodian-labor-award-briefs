# Award No. 004/24 — J.A.W (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ជេ អេ ដាប់ បែល យូ ឯ.ក) (Arbitration Council, 21 May 2024)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ung Sothy for the employer, An Virak for the workers, Kong Phalla presiding) · Doc ID 140882

## 1. Facts
J.A.W (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ជេ អេ ដាប់ បែល យូ ឯ.ក) makes footwear at Chumrov, Kouk Roka, Prek Pnov, Phnom Penh, with 821 workers, 733 of them women [Para 1][Para 48]. Citing the global economic downturn and a lack of orders, the company suspended the contracts of all 821 workers for 14 days, from 12 to 25 February 2024, and obtained authorisation letter No. 051/24 from the Labour Inspectorate dated 13 February 2024 [Para 4][Para 62]. During the suspension it paid a $35 allowance, a $10 attendance bonus, $9 for travel and accommodation, and pro-rated function and incentive bonuses [Para 4][Para 154]. Of the 821 workers, 817 collected the allowance; four did not [Para 5]. The workers' representatives disputed the suspension and demanded 100 per cent wages and benefits for the period [Para 66].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer's suspension of the employment contracts from 12 to 25 February 2024 was carried out in accordance with procedure, so that no full wage is owed [Para 115][Para 153]?

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, through the union សហជីព កម្ពុជា (CATU)):** they never agreed to the suspension or to the allowances offered, which are lower than the law requires and leave them unable to live; the company in fact had daily work available, and the suspension is a habitual device to capture profit and escape social-security contributions [Para 70][Para 71][Para 72]. They did not see the authorisation letter until the hearing and do not accept it, because it rests on one party alone [Para 73][Para 74].

**Employer (respondent):** the suspension was driven by the economic crisis and the absence of orders, without any bad intent, and followed a plan of meetings with the union and the Labour Inspectorate [Para 76][Para 77][Para 145]. Article 72 of the Labour Law relieves an employer holding a valid authorisation of any duty to pay wages [Para 90].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the demand for 100 per cent wages and other benefits during the suspension for the 355 claiming workers, and ordered nothing [Para 156][Para 157].

The panel framed the question as whether the suspension complied with Article 71 of the Labour Law [Para 115]. Reading point 6 of Article 71, it held that a suspension may rest on the joint or separate agreement of worker and employer arising from a worker request [Para 116]. Here the suspension was not requested by the workers but applied for by the employer to the labour inspector, so point 6 did not apply [Para 117]. It then turned to point 11 of Article 71, under which economic difficulty permits suspension for no more than two months, subject to the labour inspector's oversight, following Award No. 58/07 [Para 118][Para 120]. It read that oversight as requiring prior notification and authorisation [Para 121]. Article 72(1) relieves the employer of the wage obligation only where the suspension was itself lawful; otherwise 100 per cent wages and benefits remain due [Para 123][Para 124][Para 126].

On the facts the panel found that the company had asked for the suspension from January 2024, held repeated meetings with the representatives and the inspectorate, notified the workers on 8 February 2024 and then received authorisation on 13 February 2024 [Para 145]. Because the workers did not challenge the authorisation letter before the authority that issued it, as Award No. 088/16 requires, and because the suspension was in fact carried out under the inspector's oversight, the panel held it compliant with point 11 of Article 71 [Para 147][Para 152][Para 153]. It recorded the allowances actually paid and concluded that no further wage or benefit was owed [Para 154][Para 155].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension ordered by the employer on economic grounds is lawful under Article 71(11) of the Labour Law where the employer has notified the workers and obtained prior authorisation from the labour inspector, and only then does Article 72 relieve it of the duty to pay wages [Para 121][Para 126][Para 153]. A worker who disputes the authorisation letter must complain to the authority that issued it, not to the arbitration panel [Para 151].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that the company supplied no report on the fall in orders despite being ordered to do so [Para 92].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The authorisation letter is dated 13 February 2024 throughout but 13 February 2025 at [Para 86], and the award date in the procedural section is reduced to "ទី ២៥ ឆ្នាំ ២០២៤" at [Para 23]. The panel's finding at [Para 68] prints the same letter's year as "២០១". A worker is named in full at [Para 97], and the panel's discussion of her identity reproduces the dispute over the spelling of her name [Para 96]. The representative list is internally inconsistent, giving five names at [Para 49], four at [Para 105] and "both 4" at [Para 106]. The operative paragraph is largely destroyed, [Para 157] reading in part "ថ្ងៃទី ២០០០៩".
