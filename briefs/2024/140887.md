# Award No. 010/24 — Yak Jin (Cambodia) Inc. (ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉ាក ជីន (ខេមបូឌា) អ៊ីន) (Arbitration Council, 6 September 2024)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (សេង វួច ហ៊ុន, អន ណន, ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា) · Doc ID 140887

## 1. Facts
Yak Jin (Cambodia) Inc. (ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉ាក ជីន (ខេមបូឌា) អ៊ីន) is a garment factory in Kamboul district, Phnom Penh, employing 2,744 workers, 2,200 of them women [Para 56]. Fourteen local unions operate there; the claimant, the Workers' Movement Union at the factory (សហជីព ចលនា កម្មករ), is not the most-representative union [Para 58]. Of five demands filed on 16 July 2024, three were settled in conciliation and two were referred to the Arbitration Council [Para 12][Para 13][Para 14]. The disputes were holiday overtime pay at 300% and a roofed motorbike park [Para 59][Para 77].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay 300% wages — a 200% increment over the 100% base — for work performed on paid public holidays, as it did from 2006 until its 2023 policy change? [Para 59][Para 90]
2. (Issue 5) Whether the employer must build a roofed shelter so that all workers' motorbikes can be parked under cover? [Para 77][Para 99]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** since 2006 the employer paid an extra 200% over the 100% base wage for work on paid holidays, and that practice became the applicable term [Para 60][Para 63]. On 18 June 2024, a paid holiday, the employer paid only 100% plus 100%, contrary to that practice, and the change is unjustified because the company's position is now stable [Para 63][Para 64]. On parking, roughly 100 motorbikes must stand in the sun because covered space is short, risking damage to machines workers are still paying off, and the demand has gone unanswered for a year and two months [Para 79][Para 81][Para 83].

**Employer (respondent):** on 17 February 2023 it met all unions and announced that holiday work would follow the law, that is 100% plus 100% [Para 61][Para 74]. It gave notice of the new policy to workers, and on 18 June 2024 more than 600 workers voluntarily registered to work at 100% on top of the 100% base [Para 67][Para 68]. The 200% increment appears in no agreement or collective agreement [Para 71]. On parking it says covered space is genuinely insufficient and construction needs expert assessment, time and budget it does not have in the economic downturn [Para 84][Para 86][Para 100].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider both the 300% holiday pay claim and the roofed parking claim [Para 118][Para 119]

On the holiday pay claim the panel first addressed who was claiming. Under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 a party may be represented only by a person holding written authority [Para 90][Para 91]. The panel found no thumbprint, no letter of authority and no document identifying any worker who actually worked on 18 June 2024, and the claimants conceded the demand was made for the future [Para 93][Para 94][Para 95]. With no identified claimant, it held it could not determine the substance [Para 96][Para 97]. It also noted that the 200% increment appeared in no agreement or collective agreement [Para 71].

On parking the panel found no labour law provision, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice obliging the employer to provide a covered park, and treated the demand as one for a benefit above the law — an interest dispute [Para 101][Para 102]. It set out Article 96 §2 of the Labour Law defining a collective agreement [Para 103], Article 54-new of the amended Trade Union Law on most-representative status and its 30% thresholds [Para 105][Para 107], and Article 72 on bargaining councils where no union qualifies [Para 109]. It then applied Article 43 §1 of Prakas No. 099, under which an interest-dispute award substitutes for a collective agreement and runs for one year [Para 111][Para 112]. Because such an award displaces the collective agreement and the right to strike, only a most-representative or otherwise qualified union may bring the dispute [Para 113][Para 115]. The claimant was neither, so the panel declined to consider the claim [Para 116][Para 117].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A panel will not adjudicate an interest dispute brought by a union that is neither most representative under Article 54-new of the Trade Union Law nor qualified under Article 72, because an award on such a dispute would take the place of a collective agreement and extinguish the workforce's bargaining and strike rights for a year [Para 113][Para 112][Para 115]. A claim for holiday pay allegedly owed under past practice must identify the workers who performed the work; absent thumbprints or written authority, the panel has no claimant before it to decide for [Para 93][Para 96].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that the 200% increment was never written into any agreement or collective agreement, a point not strictly necessary once it had found no identified claimant [Para 71]. It observed that 14 unions operate at the factory, relevant context for any future representativeness contest [Para 58].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The worker-selected arbitrator's name differs between the header and the signature block, where OCR has also injected stray Latin text and the years 2017 and 2014 [Para 121]. Representative and delegate names are reduced to fragmented Khmer syllables, so they are described rather than reproduced [Para 58]. The conciliation report is dated 14 August 2024 in the body but also given as 15 August 2024, the date of transmission [Para 11][Para 14][Para 51]. One paragraph prints the disputed holiday rate as "២០១%" instead of 200% [Para 74]. The findings and the reasons repeat the same analysis almost verbatim in two blocks [Para 59][Para 90]. The signature page carries mangled dates and a garbled Latin rendering of a prior award name [Para 92].
