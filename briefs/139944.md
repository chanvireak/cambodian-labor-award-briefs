# Award No. 14/12 — Sivica Golf Club (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 29 February 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Seng Vouch Hun (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Ang Eng Thong (president) · Doc ID 139944

## 1. Facts
Sivica Golf Club, also printed as Royal Cambodia Phnom Penh Golf Club, is a tourism and golf-services company at Samrong Krom, Chom Chao, Dangkor, Phnom Penh, employing about 300 workers, of whom the Independent Union of Cambodian Workers at the company claimed 159 members [Para 31][Para 32][Para 33]. The union filed its claim on 5 November 2011 and, after conciliation failed on 16 January 2012, two issues reached the panel, which heard the case on 9 February 2012 and issued a non-binding award [Para 11][Para 12][Para 13][Para 10][Para 18]. They were a demand that caddies and other low-paid workers be raised to USD 61 a month, and a demand that the seniority bonus paid until 2010 be restored [Para 3][Para 5][Para 43].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must raise the base wage of caddies and other workers earning under USD 61 to USD 61 a month? [Para 35][Para 57]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must resume the seniority bonus it paid from 2001 to 2010, at USD 1 a month after one year's service and USD 2 after two? [Para 43][Para 82][Para 105]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** caddies, who walk with guests and retrieve golf balls, are paid a base of USD 1 a day, about USD 30–31 a month, and all workers below USD 61 should be brought up to that figure; the water-spray team already won USD 61 by agreement [Para 36][Para 57][Para 40]. The company paid a seniority bonus of USD 1 a month after one year and USD 2 after two from 2001 to 2010, and its abrupt withdrawal was unlawful; they have protested at the pay office every month since, and the figure still appears at the foot of their pay slips while the total paid excludes it [Para 44][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49].
**Employer (respondent):** the club is a tourism and golf business, not a garment or footwear factory, so the USD 61 minimum wage fixed by Notice No. 049/10 does not apply and it cannot raise the caddies' base wage; it follows its old practice [Para 38][Para 39][Para 4]. On the bonus it said only that no seniority bonus is owed because the company is not a garment factory, and it produced no pay records when ordered, and gave no reason for stopping the payment [Para 51][Para 52][Para 54][Para 104].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issue 1 dismissed; Issue 2 granted, the employer being ordered to resume the seniority bonus of USD 1 a month for workers with one year's service and USD 2 for two years or more, for the union members listed in the annex [Para 114][Para 115][Para 113]. The water-spray team's claim had already been settled at USD 61 for 26 days and 8 hours a day, without the former bonuses [Para 40][Para 42].

On Issue 1 the panel found no statute, agreement or collective agreement obliging the employer to raise the caddies' base wage, and held the claim to be an interest dispute exceeding what the law grants [Para 58][Para 59]. It then applied its settled approach that an interest dispute may be decided only where the claiming union is the representative union in the enterprise, or where unions together hold more than half the workforce, citing Awards 81/04 and 98/04 [Para 79][Para 80]. Although the union had 159 of about 300 workers — 53%, a clear majority — the panel held that most-representative status must be certified by the ministry responsible for labour under Art. 277 of the Labour Law and clause 6(2) of Prakas No. 305, and no certificate had been issued [Para 61][Para 62][Para 64][Para 71][Para 74][Para 75]. It therefore declined the claim even though the membership majority was absolute [Para 76][Para 81].

On Issue 2 the panel treated the question as one of past practice. It defined past practice as a customary, repeated and consistent course of conduct accepted by both parties, which need not be written, following Award 495/09 (Tak Fat) [Para 88][Para 90][Para 91]. The employer had paid USD 1 a month to workers with a year's service and USD 2 to those with two years from 2001 to 2010, the USD 2 being a flat ceiling however long the service, and the figure still printed on pay slips [Para 92][Para 93][Para 96]. That was continuous, identical conduct over nine years, so a past practice existed [Para 95][Para 97].

A past practice can end lawfully only where the parties agree, where one party gives notice and the other accepts the change without protest, or where the original circumstances change, following Awards 95/09 (Tak Fat) and 48/09 (Rusing) [Para 98][Para 99][Para 100]. Here the workers protested at every pay period, so they never accepted the change, and the employer could give no reason for stopping the payment [Para 103][Para 104]. The entitlement therefore had not been extinguished and the bonus had to be resumed, though at the rates actually practised (USD 1 and USD 2), not the USD 10 the workers claimed [Para 102][Para 105].

Because the workers' proof of length of service gave only years and not exact dates, the panel excluded eight workers who began in 2011 as not yet having a full year, and awarded on the annex list [Para 106][Para 107][Para 111][Para 112][Para 113].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A benefit paid regularly, identically and over many years becomes a past practice binding the employer, and may be withdrawn only with the agreement of the workers, by notice accepted without protest, or because the circumstances founding it have changed; a withdrawal that meets none of these conditions is ineffective and the benefit must be restored at the rate practised rather than the higher rate claimed [Para 97][Para 98][Para 105]. A union that holds an absolute majority of the workforce but has no ministerial certificate of most-representativeness cannot bring an interest dispute, and the panel will decline it [Para 75][Para 76].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that a panel award on an interest dispute stands in place of a collective agreement for one year and binds all workers in the enterprise, depriving even non-members of the right to strike over the same interest claim — the reason it insists on representative status before deciding such a case [Para 78].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is badly damaged from the annex onward: the list of entitled workers and their start years, which the operative order makes part of the award, is reduced to scattered digits and Latin fragments and cannot be reconstructed from this OCR [Para 117][Para 118][Para 124][Para 127]. The caddies' base wage is stated as USD 1 a day equal to USD 30 a month in one paragraph and USD 31 in another [Para 36][Para 57]. The seniority bonus is said to have been paid from 2001 to 2010 "after" Notice No. 049/10 of 9 July 2010 fixed the USD 61 minimum wage, which cannot be right on those dates [Para 46]. Union membership is given as 159, 158 and 148 in different paragraphs, and the claimants' demand is recorded as USD 10 a month where the practice described is USD 1 [Para 33][Para 55][Para 106][Para 82][Para 105]. The company is named both Sivica Golf Club and Royal Cambodia Phnom Penh Golf Club in the same document [Para 1][Para 19].
