# Award No. 265/15 — Smart Axiata Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 12 November 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Somborana for the employer party, Tuon Siphann for the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 139443

## 1. Facts
Smart Axiata Co., Ltd, a telephone communications company employing about 931 workers, declined to renew the fixed-duration contract of Mr C, an intern call-centre agent who was also secretary of the UNICAM local union at the enterprise, when his three-month contract expired on 31 August 2015; it had notified him of the non-renewal on 17 August 2015. [Para 29][Para 31][Para 42][Para 172] He began on 13 September 2013 as a daytime part-time intern, moved to night work from 10:00 P.M. to 06:00 A.M., and had successive fixed-duration contracts; on 14 August 2015 the employer offered renewal together with a move to day shift, an undetermined-duration contract and wages rising from US$ 156 to US$ 191, which he refused on 17 August 2015. [Para 42][Para 90][Para 194] The union claims the non-renewal was union discrimination and that he should have become permanent. [Para 15][Para 53]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr C, secretary of the UNICAM local union, and pay him back wages from the date of termination to the date of reinstatement? [Para 13][Para 18]
2. Whether the employer discriminated against the local union, as shown in the statements of the local union members? [Para 15]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer gave no proper reason for not renewing the contract and demonstrated local union discrimination; it failed to promote him to permanent call-centre agent although his 2014 appraisal was good and comparators with the same scores were promoted, and it forced the conversion of a six-month contract into a three-month one, so he should have been converted to permanent status. [Para 53][Para 57][Para 59][Para 68]
**Employer (respondent):** the contract ended naturally and it will not reinstate him; the 2014 appraisal was completed and released on 25 November 2014, before it received the union election result on 4 December 2014; the six-month contract of June 2015 was offered by mistake and lawfully rescinded under the Civil Code, and he volunteered to sign the three-month replacement; it renewed his contracts twice after his election and the union president and vice-president still work for it. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 44][Para 48][Para 61][Para 67][Para 96]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the demand for reinstatement and back pay, finding the non-renewal of the fixed-duration contract lawful. [Para 201][Para 202]

*Special protection.* Article 293 and clauses 3 and 4 of Prakas no. 305 of 22 November 2001 require the labour inspector's authorisation before dismissing a workers' delegate or a union leader, and union leaders keep that protection for 45 days before and after election, or 30 days after registration for founders and members. [Para 101][Para 102][Para 104][Para 105] The panel held that this protection governs termination by dismissal, and both parties accepted that this case concerned the non-renewal of a fixed-duration contract, so Mr C was not entitled to it and the employer was not obliged to seek authorisation. [Para 106][Para 108][Para 112][Para 113] It noted that the employer had nonetheless informed the labour inspector because of his position as secretary, which it called better than the law requires. [Para 114][Para 115]

*Union discrimination.* Articles 12 and 279 forbid an employer to weigh union membership or activity in hiring, discipline or dismissal, and earlier awards reinstated workers where it did so (28/07 Daw Kwang, 123/07 E-Garment, 148/07 Pay Her, 10/10 and 78/10 NAGA World). [Para 118][Para 119] But an employer may refuse to renew a contract that ends naturally save where the refusal rests on union discrimination, and the party alleging discrimination bears the burden of proof. [Para 119] On the 2014 non-promotion the panel found no evidence of discrimination: the appraisal result was released on 25 November 2014 and the election result reached the employer on 4 December 2014, ten days later; the workers accepted this and that the aligned supervisor only nominates candidates while the Human Resource Department decides; and no documents proved the asserted equal scores or prior warnings of the comparators. [Para 131][Para 133][Para 137][Para 140][Para 143] On the conversion of the six-month contract, Articles 345 and 346 of the Civil Code allow rescission where a party is mistaken on an important contractual term and the other party could have known of it; the workers accepted the employer never intended to offer six months, that the change was unforced and that he realised the confusion, so the rescission was valid and no discrimination was shown — the employer had in fact renewed his contracts twice after his election. [Para 146][Para 150][Para 156][Para 158][Para 165][Para 166]

*Compliance of the non-renewal.* Article 73(1) ends a fixed-duration contract at its stated date, and Article 73(5) requires 10 days' notice of non-renewal only for contracts longer than six months, 15 days for those longer than a year; his contract was for three months, so no notice was due, and the notice of 17 August 2015 for a 31 August 2015 expiry was again better than the law required. [Para 170][Para 172][Para 173]

*Formation of the new contract.* Under Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code a contract requires matching offer and acceptance, and no party may force another to accept terms it does not agree to. [Para 175][Para 177] The evidence showed the employer still wished to renew and promote him, offering a day shift and a UDC on 14 August 2015, and that he refused the shift change on 17 August 2015; the panel also found, on the voice recordings and photographs unopposed by the workers, that he had used improper language to customers and fallen asleep on duty in August 2015. [Para 185][Para 190][Para 191][Para 194] No new contract was formed because offer and acceptance did not meet. [Para 196]

*Compensation.* Severance of at least 5% of wages under Article 73(6), outstanding wages under Article 116 and payment in lieu of annual leave under Articles 166 and 167 were owed; the employer said it had paid all three and the workers did not object, so the non-renewal complied with the Labour Law. [Para 197][Para 199][Para 201]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The special protection of Article 293 and Prakas no. 305 against dismissal does not extend to the non-renewal of a fixed-duration contract that reaches its stated end, so the labour inspector's prior authorisation is not required and the employer may let the contract expire. [Para 106][Para 113]

Where a worker alleges that a non-renewal was union discrimination, the burden is on the worker; an appraisal completed and released before the employer learned of the union election, and a contract correction made with the worker's informed consent under Articles 345 and 346 of the Civil Code, do not establish discrimination. [Para 119][Para 158]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that notifying the labour inspector of a non-renewal and giving notice beyond the statutory requirement are "better than the law requires" are commendations, not obligations. [Para 115][Para 173]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The date the employer received the union election result appears as 4 December 2014 [Para 61][Para 130] and as 4 December 2015 [Para 92][Para 134]; the appraisal comparison only works on 2014.
- Mr C's contract is said to have ended on "31 September 2015" [Para 42], a date that does not exist, while the reasons use 31 August 2015 [Para 172][Para 201]; the surname also appears as "Chit Sary" [Para 35].
- Two comparators the workers relied on are printed as "Chean Karo and Sman Sles" [Para 75] and "Chean Rok and Sman Sles" [Para 160]; their names and those of the other comparators are reduced here to "the comparators".
- This is an unofficial English translation and the evidence list is expressly omitted [Para 28]; the text drops the "e" of "the" throughout ("themployer", "thearing", "witheim").
- The issue statement is duplicated mid-sentence at [Para 14], and paragraphs 22–23 repeat "Having received the claim" after a broken line.
