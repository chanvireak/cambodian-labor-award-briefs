# Award No. 02/11 — Pou Yuen (Cambodia) Enterprise Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 26 January 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, An Nan, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 139961

## 1. Facts
Pou Yuen (Cambodia) Enterprise Ltd. employed 2,500 workers, and the Local Union of the Workers' Union Federation (WUF) — one of five local unions at the factory, none of which held most representative status — was the claimant, representing 400 workers. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20][Para 21] Of eight demands taken to conciliation, five were settled and three were referred to the panel; the workers withdrew the first at the hearing, leaving the claim for a 10% increase in the skilled workers' main wage and the claim to wages and benefits during a day of certified sick leave. [Para 8][Para 9][Para 12] Skilled trades were paid a main wage of US$ 66 plus a US$ 8 attendance bonus, a US$ 4 living allowance and a US$ 3-8 skill bonus. [Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a 1,500 riel meal allowance for overtime of two hours and a 2,000 riel allowance for overtime over two hours? [Para 2] (withdrawn by the workers at the hearing [Para 12])
2. Whether the employer must increase the main wage of skilled workers by 10%? [Para 21]
3. Whether the employer must maintain wages and other benefits when a worker takes one day of sick leave certified by the factory doctor? [Para 27]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Skilled workers should earn more than unskilled workers, and their pay does not reflect the high-priced goods they produce; a day of sickness certified by the factory doctor should not cost them a day's wage. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 49]
**Employer (respondent):** It cannot afford the meal allowance and will follow existing practice on wages; workers agreed the main wage in their contracts and should not have signed if dissatisfied; it deducts only the daily wage, not the attendance bonus, from workers who bring no outside prescription or pharmacy receipt. [Para 3][Para 4][Para 26][Para 27][Para 28][Para 51]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel declined to consider the 10% wage increase and ordered the employer to maintain the wages and benefits of workers who take one day off sick with the factory doctor's certificate. [Para 59]
On the wage claim, the panel found that the parties had made no agreement raising the main wage and that no law or regulation requires a 10% increase for skilled workers, so the demand had no legal or contractual basis. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 42] That made it an interest dispute, and the panel followed Awards 81/04-Evergreen, 09/05-Kin Tai, 84/07-Yung Wah 2, 108/07-8 Star Sportswear, 135/07-Wilson Garment, 14/08-Quicksew and 101/08 in holding that only a union with most representative status has legal capacity to negotiate a collective agreement and standing to bring an interest dispute. [Para 45][Para 46] The Local Union of WUF held no certificate under Article 277 of the Labour Law, so the panel declined to consider the claim. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49]
On sick leave, Article 71(3) of the Labour Law suspends the labour contract for absence due to illness certified by a qualified doctor, for up to six months. [Para 53] The panel adopted the definition of a qualified doctor used in Awards 102/07-Terratex, 72/08-Yung Wah 2, 39/09 Quint Major, 45/10-Yung Wah 1 and 115/10-G Foremost — trained at a Ministry of Health-recognised school, holding a proper certificate and authorised by the Ministry to practise — and found the factory doctor satisfied it. [Para 54][Para 55] The employer's requirement that workers also produce an outside prescription or pharmacy receipt was therefore improper, and the daily wage and benefits had to be maintained. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a wage increase that rests on neither law nor agreement raises an interest dispute, which the panel will not consider at all unless the claimant union holds most representative status under Article 277 — absence of that status defeats standing, not merely the merits. By contrast, a certificate from a doctor employed by the factory who meets the statutory definition is certification by a qualified doctor under Article 71(3), so the employer may not add a requirement of an external prescription as a condition of maintaining wages and benefits for the certified day. [Para 47][Para 49][Para 54][Para 56]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's definition of a qualified doctor as one trained at a Ministry-recognised school, holding a certificate and authorised by the Ministry of Health, and the observation that none of the five local unions at the factory held most representative status. [Para 20][Para 54]
