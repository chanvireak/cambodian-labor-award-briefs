# Award No. 56/10 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន អូសេអង់ ហ្គាមិន (Arbitration Council, 6 July 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Eng Sothy, appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna, appointed by the workers; Kong Phallha, chair) · Doc ID 140276

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន អូសេអង់ ហ្គាមិន, a garment factory at Prey Tea, Chom Chao, Dangkao, Phnom Penh, employs about 600 workers. [Para 1][Para 52] The claimant is សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា and its factory union, which held a leadership election on 24 April 2010 and notified the company of the result through a security guard on 26 April 2010; the union had not yet received its registration certificate. [Para 2][Para 54][Para 58][Para 62] Twelve demands were filed on 30 April 2010; conciliation failed on all twelve on 27 May 2010, but at the hearing of 24 June 2010 the parties settled ten, leaving Issue 9 (a skills allowance) and Issue 12 (reinstatement of two union leaders dismissed on 30 April 2010). [Para 19][Para 20][Para 24][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 9) Whether the company must pay a technical allowance of USD 6 for workers who can operate three machines, USD 4 for two and USD 1 for one? [Para 55][Para 117]
2. (Issue 12) Whether the company must take back the two elected union leaders and pay their wages and other allowances from the date of dismissal until they return? [Para 58][Para 132]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា and the Oseang factory union:** the skills allowance is owed to sewing-section workers who handle several machines; the two leaders were dismissed without notice, without any prior warning and without the labour inspector's permission, purely because they had just been elected, and one of them received no payment at all on dismissal. [Para 55][Para 77][Para 82][Para 94][Para 97][Para 98]
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន អូសេអង់ ហ្គាមិន:** the two were dismissed for serious misconduct — lounging on machines with legs swinging, talking instead of working, disturbing the production line and showing no respect to management — and the company applied its internal rules; it never received the election notification, since a security guard has no authority to accept letters. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 99][Para 100][Para 111]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the reinstatement claim was granted: the company must take back both union leaders and pay their wages and other allowances from the date of dismissal until they return; the skills-allowance claim was dismissed. [Para 186][Para 187][Para 188]

On Issue 9 the panel began with Article 268, under which a professional organisation obtains the rights the Labour Law recognises only once it is registered with the ministry, and with an earlier award on the same point. [Para 118][Para 121][Para 122] Because this union had no registration certificate it could not represent its members, so the panel ordered the union to produce by 28 June 2010 the thumbprints of the sewing-section workers who had mandated it and a count of them; nothing arrived. [Para 125][Para 126][Para 57] The workers who did appear at the hearing were not in the sewing section, and the claim was for that section only, so the panel, applying the rule that a claimant must prove its case, dismissed it. [Para 130][Para 131][Para 129]

On Issue 12 the panel first had to decide whether the notification of 26 April 2010 reached the company. Prakas No. 305 requires the union to notify candidacies "by all means", the security guard with badge 9232 signed for the letter at about 3 p.m., and the company's own evidence placed that guard on the day shift that day; the company's late assertion that its internal procedure was not followed was inconsistent and unsupported in writing, so the notification was valid. [Para 135][Para 137][Para 148][Para 149][Para 153][Para 155][Para 158]

The consequence was decisive. Prakas No. 305 article 3, paragraph 3 protects every candidate in a union leadership election against dismissal for 45 days before and 45 days after the vote, and Article 293 of the Labour Law allows dismissal of a protected delegate or candidate only with the labour inspector's agreement. [Para 135][Para 136][Para 145][Para 159][Para 162] The panel found the company had never applied for that permission, so the dismissals were procedurally unlawful regardless of fault. [Para 165][Para 166] On the misconduct itself, Article 83(3) requires a serious breach of discipline, the internal rules make warnings the graduated sanction, and the company had given none; its two witnesses could not fix the date of what they saw, and their evidence did not establish a breach serious enough for dismissal. [Para 168][Para 170][Para 178][Para 182][Para 183] The dismissals were therefore unlawful and the two were reinstated with back pay. [Para 184][Para 186]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Notification of a union leadership election takes effect when it reaches the employer by any means the union chooses, including delivery to a security guard at the gate; an employer cannot defeat protected status by asserting an unwritten internal mail procedure. Once a union has formally notified candidacies, Prakas No. 305 protects those candidates for 45 days before and after the election, and Article 293 requires the labour inspector's agreement before they can be dismissed; dismissal without that agreement is unlawful even where misconduct is alleged, and the employer must reinstate with back wages. A union that has no registration certificate cannot, however, bring a claim on behalf of its members under Article 268. [Para 135][Para 149][Para 158][Para 159][Para 166]

**Obiter dicta:** the observations that Article 268 registration is what triggers a union's legal capacity, and that 45-day protection for a winning candidate may continue until the registration application is filed, were general statements not needed for the dismissal of Issue 9 or the grant of Issue 12. [Para 122][Para 143]
