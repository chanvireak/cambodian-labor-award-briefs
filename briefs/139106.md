# Award No. 058/19 — Hong Va Cambodia Garment Manufacture Factory Co Ltd (Branch 1) (Arbitration Council, 6 August 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party — dissenting on issue 1, Kong Phalla chair) · Doc ID 139106

## 1. Facts
Hong Va Cambodia Garment Manufacture Factory Co Ltd (Branch 1), a garment factory at Trapeang Chek village, Chom Chao 1 commune, Po Sen Chey district, Phnom Penh, employed 1,608 workers, of whom about 400 held indefinite-duration contracts and 1,100 fixed-duration contracts renewed every six months against a 5 per cent end-of-contract payment. [Para 49][Para 54] On 21 June 2019 twenty workers complained of twelve working conditions; conciliation left seven unresolved, and at the hearing the workers withdrew two, leaving five issues. [Para 22][Para 29]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must convert to indefinite-duration contracts the fixed-duration contracts of workers whose successive contracts total more than two years, with effect from now? [Para 53]
2. Whether the employer must add USD 13 a month to the lunch allowance, bringing it to USD 26 a month? [Para 74]
3. Whether the employer must pay a 2,000 riel meal allowance for each hour of voluntary overtime? [Para 78]
4. Whether the employer must grant 90 days of maternity leave counted in working days, excluding public holidays and Sundays? [Para 81]
5. Whether the employer must recalculate the payment in lieu of unused annual leave for 221 serving workers on the average of the last twelve months' total wages and back-pay three years of miscalculation? [Para 91]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Article 67(2) forbids a fixed-duration contract longer than two years and any contract whose successive terms exceed two years becomes indefinite, so the conversions are owed now; food prices have risen and 13 dollars more a month is needed; Article 182 sets 90 days without saying they are calendar days. [Para 58][Para 76][Para 83]
**Employer (respondent):** Article 67(2) is ambiguous, each renewal stays under two years and the employer may renew indefinitely, so no conversion is owed yet; it already pays wages and a lunch allowance above the legal minimum; it will convert contracts in early 2020 after consulting representatives; it pays annual leave in cash at the basic wage. [Para 64][Para 77]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — issues 1, 2 and 3 were refused consideration and issues 4 and 5 dismissed on the merits; nothing was awarded. [Para 213]

*Issue 1 (contract conversion).* The panel held the real question to be whether Instruction No. 050/19 of 17 May 2019, which allows successive fixed-duration contracts totalling up to four years, conflicts with Article 67(2) of the Labour Law; that this was a question of the legality of a ministerial normative act which the Arbitration Council has no power to decide; and that the parties should seek interpretation from the competent government body, a competent court or the Constitutional Council. It therefore refused to consider the claim, though it recorded that the employer had undertaken to convert qualifying contracts in early 2020. [Para 142][Para 149] Arbitrator An Nan dissented: Article 312 obliges the panel to decide a collective labour dispute referred to it, an unclear or contradictory instruction is no ground for refusal, and he would have ordered the conversions. [Para 223][Para 247]

*Issues 2 and 3 (allowances).* Both were treated as interest disputes because no law, collective agreement, internal rule, contract or practice obliged the employer to pay them; the panel found the employer already paid a lunch allowance above the legal minimum and the 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance required by Notification No. 041/11, and refused to consider them for want of a most-representative union claimant. [Para 152]

*Issue 4 (maternity leave).* Article 182 does not say whether 90 days means working days or calendar days; the panel read it in light of the purpose of protecting the health of mother and newborn, found no Prakas defining the point, and applied the drafting convention that periods longer than 15 days are counted in calendar days, so the employer's 90 calendar days complied. [Para 194][Para 195]

*Issue 5 (annual leave).* Article 167(3) makes void any agreement to forgo annual leave or take payment in lieu of it, and the panel held that compensation for unused leave may be paid only when the employment relationship ends, never while the contract subsists; the employer's practice of paying cash during employment was itself contrary to the law, so the claim for recalculation and three years' back pay had no legal basis. [Para 205][Para 207]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Two rules decide future cases. First, where a claim can only succeed by establishing that a ministerial instruction contradicts the Labour Law, the panel will not entertain it: the legality of a normative act is reserved to the competent authority, a competent court or the Constitutional Council, and the panel will not convert fixed-duration contracts on that ground. [Para 149] Second, payment in lieu of unused annual leave can be ordered only when the employment relationship has ended; a claim to recalculate or back-pay such compensation while the worker is still serving is without legal basis, even where the employer has been paying it unlawfully during employment. [Para 205]

**Obiter dicta:** the drafting convention that periods of 15 days or less are counted in working days and longer periods in calendar days is a general observation on statutory interpretation, applied here only after the panel found Article 182 silent on the point. [Para 194]
