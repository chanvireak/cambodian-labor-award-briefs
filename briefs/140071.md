# Award No. 111/11 — Long Lead (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 14 September 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Run Saray, chair) · Doc ID 140071

## 1. Facts
Long Lead (Cambodia) is a shoe manufacturer in Samaki Meanchey district, Kampong Chhnang province, operating for nearly two years with about 1,600 workers and a single union, the Khmer Youth Federation at Long Lead, with 469 members whose dues the employer deducts. [Para 1][Para 30][Para 31] A collective dispute covering 12 points went to conciliation at the provincial labour department; 10 points settled and 2 were referred to the Arbitration Council, which received the non-conciliation report on 26 August 2011. [Para 6][Para 9][Para 10] At the hearing on 6 September 2011 the parties settled the second referred point, leaving only the Sunday overtime meal claim for decision. [Para 8][Para 13][Para 14]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must provide one free meal or a 2,000 riel meal allowance to workers who voluntarily work overtime on Sundays? [Para 2][Para 40]
2. Whether the employer must remove the Chinese managers from Groups 1 and 2 for using abusive language and improper conduct toward workers? [Para 3]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Sunday overtime workers should receive either one free meal or 3,000 riel, and at the hearing they pressed for one free meal or a 2,000 riel meal allowance, relying on Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011. [Para 2][Para 32][Para 36]
**Employer (respondent):** It paid Sunday overtime wages as the Labour Law requires but never provided the free meal or the 2,000 riel allowance; it said it understood the notification but did not yet have the capacity to comply, and for ordinary-day overtime it paid 2,000 riel per two hours worked. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 37]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay 2,000 (two thousand) riel per day or provide one free meal to workers who voluntarily work overtime on Sundays. [Para 47][Para 48]
On issue 1, the panel found that Sunday is the company's weekly rest day, so Sunday work is overtime. [Para 33][Para 41][Para 42] Point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 requires that a worker who voluntarily works overtime at the employer's request receive 2,000 riel per day or one free meal, without any condition on the number of hours worked. [Para 40][Para 43] The employer admitted it never provided either benefit on Sundays, so the obligation was breached and the order followed. [Para 44][Para 46] The panel declined to consider evidence the employer filed on 8 September 2011, one day after the deadline it had set. [Para 38][Para 39] On issue 2, the parties reached agreement at the hearing and the panel recorded that settlement rather than deciding the removal request. [Para 13][Para 14] The award is a binding award (សេចក្តីបង្គាប់ជាប់កាតព្វកិច្ច) made final by the parties' agreement to accept it on 6 September 2011. [Para 49]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer requests workers to volunteer for overtime on the weekly rest day, it must pay each such worker a 2,000 riel meal allowance per day or provide one free meal, and the obligation is not reduced by the number of overtime hours actually worked. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 46]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that the employer acknowledged understanding the notification but claimed it lacked the capacity to comply; this explanation did not excuse the obligation. [Para 37]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The workers' claim as pleaded asks for 3,000 riel or a meal, while at the hearing and in the order the figure is 2,000 riel; both amounts are reported as printed. [Para 2][Para 32][Para 47] The union and company names appear in variant forms ("Long Lead" and "Long Leada"), and the case reference is printed as "111/11-Long Lead". [Para 1] The paragraph numbering is disturbed: line 15 refers to "ចំណុច វិវាទ ទី ១២" although only two points were referred. [Para 15] Several invitation letters and the company address are partly garbled by OCR. [Para 28][Para 29]
