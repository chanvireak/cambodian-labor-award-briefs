# Award No. 052/16 — Khem Vanna Fashion Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 17 March 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mr Ing Sothy (employer-selected), Mr Ven Pov (worker-selected), chair Mr Kong Phalha · Doc ID 138999

## 1. Facts
Khem Vanna Fashion Co., Ltd., a garment-cutting and sewing factory at Prey Chas, Vihear Suor, Khsach Kandal, Kandal, registered under Inv. 0627 E/2012, employed approximately 1,050 workers. [Para 52][Para 53] The Cambodian Workers' Heart Union Federation held a factory union election on 2 November 2015 but the local union was never registered with the Ministry of Labour; the federation nonetheless filed 354 names of claiming workers, of whom only 173 had thumbprinted the transfer of rights, and the panel accepted those 173 as the claimants. [Para 54][Para 56][Para 92][Para 99] Of the seven original demands, four were settled in conciliation and a fifth (issue 2) at the hearing of 3 March 2016, leaving the work-book overcharge and the strike-pay claims, decided by a non-binding award of 17 March 2016. [Para 14][Para 19][Para 20][Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reimburse the excess of 6,000 riel per worker that it charged above the legal fee for issuing work books? [Para 2][Para 100]
2. Whether the employer must maintain wages and the regular attendance bonus for the strike period of 12 to 21 January 2016? [Para 69][Para 114]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer charged each worker US$4, or 16,000 riel, for a work book when the Ministry's own receipt shows the book costs 10 riel, so the excess is an unlawful charge; and the strike was lawful because notice was given to the employer by letter of 29 December 2015. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 70][Para 132]
**Employer (respondent):** It cannot repay because in 2016 it canvassed workers to have work books made and no worker wished to go to the Kandal Labour Department herself; workers far from the province may use the service at the US$4 they have paid since 2014, and lawful absence is allowed but daily wages and the attendance bonus are then cut; alternatively workers may obtain books before starting work. On the strike, workers may not receive wages or bonuses during a strike, and the employer objected to the thumbprint sheets as illegible and apparently assembled from a copied name list. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 64][Para 66][Para 67][Para 73]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must pay 6,000 riel in work-book fees to each of the 173 named workers, and the claims for wages and the attendance bonus during the strike of 12 to 21 January 2016 were refused. [Para 137][Para 138]

On standing, the panel applied Article 268 of the Labour Law and earlier awards holding that a professional organisation acquires the rights the Labour Law recognises only once its statutes and the list of responsible officers are registered with the Ministry of Labour; the factory union was therefore not yet such an organisation. [Para 74][Para 75][Para 78][Para 82] It then applied Article 19 of the Prakas on the Arbitration Council allowing a party to appear through a representative authorised in writing, and because 173 of the 354 listed workers had thumbprinted a transfer of rights, it recognised those 173, plus the 9 who attended the hearing, as the claimants and disregarded the rest. [Para 83][Para 90][Para 92][Para 95][Para 99] The employer's objection to the thumbprint sheets was rejected: it never denied the named persons were its workers, only that the prints were unclear on photocopies, and it produced no evidence of fabrication. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 88][Para 89]

On the work books, Article 32 of the Labour Law requires every Khmer wage-earner to hold a work book, and joint Prakas No. 1009 of 28 December 2012 fixes the public-service fee for a work book at 10 riel; following Award No. 21/03-Loy All Cambodia, the panel held the cost of the book falls on the worker himself, but a charge of 16,000 riel exceeds what the law allows, so the employer must repay the excess. [Para 100][Para 101][Para 103][Para 106][Para 108][Para 112]

On wages during the strike, Article 332 of the Labour Law suspends the employment contract during a strike and Article 72 confirms suspension removes the duty to pay, and prior awards hold this applies whether or not the strike was lawful; the panel therefore refused the wage claim. [Para 114][Para 116][Para 117][Para 121][Para 122] On the attendance bonus, Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 point 2 grants at least US$10 a month to workers who attend every working day without absence, and because the strike breached Articles 323, 326 and 320 of the Labour Law — no secret ballot, no minimum service agreed, and the strike began before Labour Inspectorate conciliation and before any Arbitration Council procedure — the strikers were not in regular attendance and earned no bonus. [Para 123][Para 125][Para 128][Para 132][Para 133][Para 134][Para 135][Para 136]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An unregistered factory union cannot itself claim the rights the Labour Law gives professional organisations, but individual workers who have thumbprinted a written transfer of rights are parties in their own right and may be recognised in the number that signed. Where a public-service fee for a work book is fixed by joint Prakas, any larger sum the employer collects from the worker is an unlawful deduction repayable to each worker who signed; and because a strike suspends the contract, neither wages nor a regular-attendance bonus is owed for the strike days, the bonus being lost independently where the strike failed the ballot, minimum-service and prior-procedure requirements. [Para 82][Para 95][Para 112][Para 117][Para 135]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that where the employer allows a worker lawful leave to obtain a work book it may still deduct the daily wage and the attendance bonus, and that workers may instead obtain a book before entering employment. [Para 67]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The arithmetic does not close: the fee fixed by Prakas is 10 riel, the employer collected 16,000 riel, yet the panel describes and orders repayment of "the excess of 6,000 riel", the figure the workers claimed, and states the overcharge as 6,000 riel rather than 15,990. [Para 62][Para 108][Para 109][Para 112][Para 113][Para 137] The Prakas establishing the Arbitration Council is No. 199 in the jurisdiction paragraph but the annexed procedural rules are attributed to Prakas No. 099 of the same date, and later references print "០៩#០៤" and "០៩៩/០៤". [Para 8][Para 83][Para 95] The claimant group is counted four ways — 354 names filed, 173 with thumbprints, 9 present at the hearing, and "១ក" in one line — and the panel's own concluding sentence adds the 9 to the 173 as its caseload. [Para 54][Para 56][Para 58][Para 97][Para 99] The strike month is printed as "ខែមភ រា" in the finding of illegality, and the strike itself is described as both eight days deducted and a period of 12 to 21 January. [Para 69][Para 134] The annexed list of 173 names is largely unrecoverable OCR with names, employee numbers and sections run together, so no individual worker is reproduced here. [Para 139][Para 143][Para 146]
