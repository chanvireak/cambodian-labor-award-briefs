# Award No. 262/15 — ហ្វូខឹស មេត (ខេមបូឌា) លេឌើ ហ្គូដ មេនូហ្វេ ក ឆឺរីង លីមីធីត (Focus Met (Cambodia) Leader Good Manufacture & Cherring Ltd) (Arbitration Council, 26 October 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy chosen by the employer party, An Non chosen by the worker party, Kong Phalla chair) · Doc ID 139445

## 1. Facts
The Khmer Workers' Spirit Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ស្មារតី កម្មករ ខ្មែរ) organised workplace union elections on 27 March 2015 and notified the employer of the elected leadership on 30 March 2015; the employer signed to acknowledge the notice, but the union was never registered at the Ministry of Labour. [Para 50][Para 103][Para 106] Between 12 and 17 September 2015 the employer terminated seven of the workers it had elected or enrolled — five ordinary members or advisers and two elected leaders — before their three-month fixed-term contracts had run, citing falling orders, and dismissed thirteen workers in all. [Para 61][Para 84][Para 96][Para 160][Para 163]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the seven dismissed workers and maintain their wages and other benefits? [Para 145]
2. Whether the employer must dismiss its administration chief, Mr V Rithy, from the company? [Para 227]

The original issue 3 (no discrimination against the union) was merged with issue 1 by the parties, leaving two issues for decision. [Para 21][Para 22]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the seven were dismissed without valid reason and had never committed a fault or received a warning; the dismissals were union discrimination because all seven were leaders or members of the workplace union. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57]
**Employer (respondent):** orders have fallen and there is no work — the building owner now handles electrical maintenance, and there is no sewing, dyeing or warehouse work left; it will pay termination benefits as the law requires, and there is no discrimination because thirteen workers were dismissed, not only the seven, and the union was left free to operate. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 87][Para 92]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement and maintenance of wages were refused, but the panel ordered the employer to pay all seven workers: damages at least equal to the wages they would have received to the end of each fixed-term contract (Article 73); indemnity for unused annual leave (Articles 166 and 167); a termination indemnity of at least five per cent of the wages paid during the contract (Article 73(6)); and the final wages still unpaid (Article 116). The award prints no riel or USD figures. [Para 223][Para 261][Para 264] The claim to have Mr V Rithy dismissed was refused. [Para 265]

On the first issue the panel worked from Article 293 of the Labour Law, which forbids dismissing a staff delegate without the labour inspector's prior agreement, and from Article 3 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, which extends that protection to candidates in a union leadership election — for 45 days before the poll and 45 days after it where the candidate is not elected. [Para 146][Para 149] Because the Prakas fixes no period for candidates who win, the panel followed Award No. 40/10 (Meng Yan): protection lasts at least 45 days after the election, or may continue for a reasonable period, such as until the union files its registration application. [Para 155][Para 156][Para 157] It then applied three cumulative conditions drawn from earlier awards — that the worker be one whom Prakas 305 covers, that the dismissal fall inside the protection period, and that the employer have been notified of the protected names. [Para 158]

Five of the seven (Ms O Channy, Mr C Khea, Ms N Kanhnya, Ms Kh Sreymom and Ms J Sokhun) were found to have been only advisers and members, never candidates, so the first condition failed and conditions 2 and 3 were not examined for them. [Para 160][Para 161][Para 166] Mr K Li Det and Mr S Vanna had been elected leaders, satisfying conditions 1 and 3, but they were dismissed on 14 September 2015, more than six months after the 27 March 2015 election; the panel held that this fell outside any reasonable protection period, so condition 2 failed. [Para 163][Para 168][Para 173][Para 177][Para 178]

Turning to the merged discrimination allegation, the panel cited Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law and its settled rule that the party making an allegation carries the burden of proof. [Para 181][Para 184] The workers produced no evidence of anti-union motive by the deadline for evidence, and the objecting letter of 1 October 2015 gave no supporting reasons. [Para 189][Para 190][Para 195] Against that, the employer had dismissed thirteen workers in September 2015, not merely the seven, had signed the leadership notice, had deducted union dues for about four months with roughly 121 contributing members, and had not obstructed the union's presence. [Para 105][Para 192][Para 193] The panel therefore found no link between the dismissals and union discrimination. [Para 197]

On compensation, the panel noted that Article 73(1) lets a fixed-term contract expire at its term, but found the employer had ended these contracts early by its own will, which Article 73(3) answers with damages of at least the wages due to the contractual end date. [Para 199][Para 204][Para 205][Para 212] It added the annual-leave indemnity and the five per cent termination indemnity, and held the employer bound to pay final wages under Article 116. [Para 217][Para 219][Para 222]

On the second issue the panel reasoned from Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 311 of the Civil Code that only the parties to a contract may extinguish the obligations it creates, so a third party has no standing to dissolve someone else's employment contract. [Para 230][Para 231][Para 232] It added that a panel will order an employee removed only where the claimant proves that person is dangerous and cannot be kept in the enterprise without risk to others, which was not shown. [Para 236][Para 257] The employer's undertaking to investigate and discipline proportionately was accepted as a reasonable answer. [Para 258][Para 259]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Protection against dismissal for union office under Prakas No. 305 runs from candidacy, not from membership: a worker who was never a candidate in the leadership election — however active a member or adviser — has no protected status under Article 293, and an elected leader is protected only within a reasonable window after the poll, which the panel measured at least 45 days and at most until the union applies for registration, so a dismissal more than six months after the election needs no labour inspector's authorisation. [Para 155][Para 161][Para 168][Para 173] Separately, where an employer ends a fixed-term contract before its term for its own convenience, Article 73(3) damages are owed even if the dismissal itself breaches no protective rule. [Para 205][Para 212]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that Mr V Rithy's words ("come in, come and fight") were improper and could aggravate conflict in future, offered while refusing his dismissal, and its remark that dues deduction evidences recognition of an unregistered union, were not necessary to either order. [Para 192][Para 259]
