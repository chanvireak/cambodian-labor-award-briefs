# Award No. 272/13 — Starlight Apparel Manufacturing Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 8 January 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (You Son Ty appointed by the employer party, An Non appointed by the worker party, Kong Phalna chair appointed by the two wing arbitrators) · Doc ID 139919

## 1. Facts

Starlight Apparel Manufacturing Co., Ltd. ("ស្តារ ឡែ ថិ អី ភែរីល មេនូហ្វេក ឆឺរីង ខូអិលធីឌី") operates at house No. 18, new concrete road, Sedha village, Vihear Suor commune, Khsach Kandal district, Kandal province. [Para 2] The worker party is the Federation of Khmer Workers' Spirit Union (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ស្មារតី កម្មករ ខ្មែរ), whose office is at No. 358, street, Kap sangkat, Pou Senchey khan, Phnom Penh. [Para 2]

On 25 November 2013 the Kandal provincial Department of Labour and Vocational Training received the federation's complaint No. 108 S.K.Kh of the same date, asking it to help conciliate a collective labour dispute over 12 points that the company refused to settle. [Para 21] Conciliation was attempted on 6 December 2013: 00 points were settled and all 12 points remained unconciliated. [Para 22] The non-conciliation report No. 026/13 K.B/K.NA dated 18 December 2013 reached the Council's secretariat on 19 December 2013. [Para 18][Para 23] The panel was constituted on 23 December 2013. [Para 24]

The secretariat summoned both parties to a hearing and conciliation on the 12 unconciliated points at 08:30 on 31 December 2013. [Para 25][Para 20] The employer party attended through its administration chief Meng Socheat, compliance officer Long Ly Heang and GMAC senior official Cheat Khemara. [Para 2] The worker party did not attend. [Para 26] On the morning of the hearing the federation's deputy secretary-general Su Sokha telephoned the secretariat to say the workers could not come because they were staying at home over the minimum-wage strike and demonstrations then occurring daily, and that no federation official could attend either; Su Sokha asked the panel to proceed according to law. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 57]

The 12 demands were: (1) full wages and benefits plus back pay for the lay-off period; (2) lawful measures against the Chinese sewing chief and inspection chief, who forced workers to enter 30 minutes early after the midday break and made one group work 50% longer; (3) measures against general manager Kem Digital, alias A Jeskor Kranbop, for arrogance and pressure on Khmer team leaders; (4) an extra $5 attendance bonus, making $1,500 a month; (5) an extra $5 transport allowance, making $15 a month; (6) three cans of milk powder or $20 a month for women returning from maternity; (7) voluntary overtime, without coercion or threats; (8) wages and benefits paid three months before maternity leave; (9) attendance bonus deducted in proportion to authorised leave; (10) correct application of internal leave rules, under which 16 or more consecutive days of unlawful absence is abandonment of employment; (11) an infirmary and more beds; (12) rice price for 16-to-18-hour overtime raised to 3,000 riel. [Para 3][Para 4][Para 5][Para 6][Para 7][Para 8][Para 9][Para 10][Para 11][Para 12][Para 13][Para 14][Para 15]

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. Whether the panel should decide the 12 collective demands when the worker party, which is the claimant, failed to appear at the hearing? [Para 26][Para 45][Para 62]
2. Whether the worker party's telephone explanation — that the workers were at home because of the minimum-wage strike and demonstrations — is a valid reason for non-appearance? [Para 57][Para 59]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** the 12 demands listed above, pressed through the federation after conciliation failed on every point. [Para 3][Para 22] The workers did not attend the hearing and sent no representative; their deputy secretary-general said by telephone that they could not attend because of the minimum-wage strike and asked the panel to continue the procedure according to law. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 28]

**Employer (respondent):** attended through three representatives and, by its refusal to settle at conciliation, opposed the demands. [Para 2][Para 21] The award records no substantive answer to the 12 points, because the panel never reached the merits. [Para 21][Para 62]

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** withdrawn — the panel terminated (បញ្ចប់) the arbitration in case No. 272/13, treating the claimant's 12 demands as abandoned without deciding them. [Para 68][Para 70][Para 71]

The panel applied Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY of 21 April 2004, which allows arbitration to proceed when a party is absent. [Para 49][Para 50] It found condition 1 satisfied: the worker party had been properly invited and notified of the date, time and place of the hearing by the secretariat. [Para 51][Para 52] Condition 2 was satisfied: the worker party neither attended nor appointed a lawful representative, and "appear before the panel" under Article 21 means both presence at the hearing and participation in the whole proceeding, which has four stages — introduction and conflict-of-interest explanation, procedure and issue identification, renewed conciliation if the parties agree, and the hearing on the merits. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 55][Para 56] Condition 3 was satisfied: the reason given was not valid. The panel held that a strike and demonstrations over the minimum wage, with workers staying at home, does not justify the claimant's absence from the Council, and Su Sokha's telephone request that the panel proceed did not cure it. [Para 57][Para 59][Para 60][Para 61]

Having found all three conditions met, the panel recalled that the claimant in a collective dispute must appear to state its claims and supply the documents and information needed for the panel to consider and decide. [Para 63][Para 64] The claimant bears the burden of proof and argument in support of its own claim; the respondent's burden to rebut arises only after the claimant has discharged its own. [Para 65][Para 66] Because the claimant neither appeared nor gave a valid reason, the panel held that it had abandoned its claims and that the arbitration need not continue and could be closed by order. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 69]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

Where the claimant party in a collective labour dispute is properly summoned but neither attends nor appoints a lawful representative, and gives no valid reason for its absence, the panel will treat the claims as abandoned and terminate the arbitration rather than decide the merits, because Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY permits the panel to proceed on a party's absence and the claimant carries the burden of presenting its own case. [Para 53][Para 56][Para 62][Para 65][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70] A strike or demonstration over the minimum wage is not a valid reason for the claimant's non-appearance, and a union official's telephone request that the panel proceed does not substitute for appearance or authorised representation. [Para 57][Para 59][Para 60]

**Obiter dicta:** the four-stage description of the hearing is explanatory, since no merits hearing took place [Para 54]; the observation that the respondent's burden arises only after the claimant's case is discharged was not reached [Para 66]; and "abandoned" is a procedural consequence, not a finding that the demands were unfounded. [Para 68]
