# Award No. 159/14 — Khem Tekang Sport Tech (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 18 July 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Mar Samborna (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Pen Bunchhea (president) · Doc ID 139612

## 1. Facts
The employer, Khem Tekang Sport Tech (Cambodia) Co., Ltd., a garment factory in Kandal province employing about 900 workers, paid senior workers a consolidated lunch allowance of USD 15 per month. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 39] From February 2014 it paid USD 10 as lunch allowance and USD 5 as a production bonus instead, without prior notice to the senior workers. [Para 40][Para 45] The union demanded that the two sums be consolidated back into a single USD 15 lunch allowance. [Para 2][Para 38] Of 13 demands, 11 were settled in conciliation on 13 June 2014; of the 2 remaining, 1 was settled at the hearing, leaving only the lunch allowance. [Para 12][Para 16][Para 17]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must consolidate the USD 10 lunch allowance and the USD 5 production bonus into a single lunch allowance of USD 15 per month for senior workers, as it did before February 2014? [Para 38][Para 57]
2. Whether the company must provide a nursery and a breastfeeding room for workers? [Para 4]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Before 2014 the company paid senior workers USD 15 per month as a lunch allowance with no condition attached; splitting it into USD 10 plus a USD 5 bonus in February 2014 reduced a settled entitlement and was done without notice. [Para 39][Para 45]
**Employer (respondent):** The restructuring was real but harmed no one: total pay stayed at USD 15, the company's revenue had fallen, the minimum wage had risen by USD 20 per worker per month, and no law or legal norm requires an employer to pay a lunch allowance or a production bonus at all. [Para 46][Para 52][Para 75]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the demand that the company consolidate the USD 10 lunch allowance and USD 5 production bonus into a lunch allowance of USD 15 per month for senior workers. [Para 80][Para 81]
The panel first disposed of the second issue: the company had already built the nursery and breastfeeding room, and that point was settled at the hearing, so only issue 1 was decided. [Para 4][Para 16][Para 17]
On the merits, both sides accepted that the practice had changed from February 2014 and that the USD 15 had been split into USD 10 and USD 5. [Para 44][Para 57] The panel invoked Article 2 of the Labour Law, which places the enterprise and its workers under the management and direction of the employer, and read it, as it had in earlier cases (No. 62/06-Klick Su, issue 5; No. 108/06-Trung Gal Komara; No. 33/07-Golfem, issue 3; No. 119/09-SL, issues 4 and 5), as conferring a right of management and organisation so long as that right is exercised lawfully and reasonably. [Para 66][Para 69][Para 70][Para 79]
Applying that to the facts, the panel found the split applied to old and new workers alike, the total received by senior workers was unchanged at USD 15, and no condition was attached for senior workers; the only difference was that new workers' USD 5 bonus depends on actually performing well. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 78] It noted the payment of USD 10 plus USD 5 was more favourable than the statutory minimum, and that the company was up to date on the other statutory payments — minimum wage, regular attendance bonus, transport and accommodation allowances and seniority bonus. [Para 48][Para 77] The restructuring was therefore a reasonable exercise of the employer's management right, consistent with the Labour Law. [Para 79]
The panel also addressed the contract the employer produced for a worker named Po Lim Seng, dated 26 January 2013, clause 5 of which lists a USD 100 base wage, USD 10 food allowance, transport allowance, USD 10 regular attendance bonus and USD 5 production bonus. [Para 54][Para 55] It observed that this contradicted both parties' accounts at the hearing but held that a single contract for one worker could not displace the parties' own admissions, especially as the employer had produced no other contracts. [Para 62][Para 64][Para 65]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may reorganise a consolidated monthly allowance into a smaller allowance plus a separate bonus, and a panel will not order it restored, where the total sum paid to existing workers is unchanged, no new condition is attached for them and the arrangement remains above the statutory floor; the employer's management right under Article 2 of the Labour Law covers the form of the payment. [Para 69][Para 78][Para 79]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remarks that new workers' USD 5 bonus depends on their actual performance, and that the employer had met its other statutory payment obligations, were not necessary to rejecting the demand. [Para 43][Para 48][Para 76]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Two versions of the same contract clause are printed: clause 5(C) gives the transport allowance as USD 10 and clause 5(D) the regular attendance bonus as USD 10 in one recital, while the repeat of that clause gives the transport allowance as USD 100. [Para 55][Para 60] The award is dated 18 July 2014 but the panel is said to have been constituted on "19 June 2023 2014". [Para 1][Para 14] The conciliation report number carries a stray "247" in its date. [Para 13] Amounts are garbled in the reasoning ("USD 15 people" at para 71, "USD 20 10" at para 76). [Para 71][Para 76] The signature page drops the employer-appointed arbitrator's name and OCR has replaced the third arbitrator's name with "Thes poress weaver", so the composition is taken from the face of the award. [Para 1][Para 83] The worker whose contract was filed is named as printed; the union officials appear only as Mr S and Mr O.
