# Award No. 283/14 — Lian Fa (Cambodia) Garment Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 17 November 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Kong Phalha chair) · Doc ID 139742

## 1. Facts
Lian Fa (Cambodia) Garment Co Ltd cuts and sews garments at Prek Samrong village, Ta Khmau, Kandal, and employs about 1,200 workers. [Para 54][Para 55] The Free Workers' Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia was formed at the factory by an election held in the company canteen on 11 January 2014, at which Mr O was elected deputy president; the union was still unregistered with the Ministry of Labour when the case was heard. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58] Mr O worked in the ironing section on successive six-month fixed-duration contracts, the last running from 2 December 2013 to 1 June 2014, after an earlier absence of four to five months through illness. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 67][Para 90] The employer notified him on 26 May 2014 that the contract would not be renewed, and it expired on 1 June 2014. [Para 74][Para 67] Of eleven demands, the workers withdrew all but the reinstatement of Mr O at the hearing. [Para 26][Para 27]

## 2. Issues in dispute
10. Whether the employer must take Mr O, deputy president of the Free Workers' Union at Lian Fa, back into work, the refusal to renew his contract being anti-union discrimination? [Para 62][Para 87][Para 90]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr O was a union leader and the employer knew it, because the founding election was held inside the factory and notice of the elected leadership was posted to the employer on 20 February 2014; the employer renewed the contract of a co-worker recruited at the same time but not his, and replaced him with two new hires in the ironing section, so the lapse of his contract was discriminatory. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 72][Para 73]
**Employer (respondent):** there was no discrimination — the contract simply reached its term and was not renewed, seven days' notice having been given on 26 May 2014; renewal depends on whether the worker's performance is active, and it did not know of his union post until the demand was raised. [Para 74][Para 78][Para 82]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to continue Mr O's employment contract and take him back into work. [Para 111][Para 112]

The panel first settled the legal frame: Article 12 of the Labour Law forbids an employer from relying on union membership or union activity in decisions on hiring or terminating a contract, and Article 279 forbids using membership or activity as a ground for recruitment or dismissal. [Para 92][Para 93] It read those prohibitions as extending to a refusal to renew a fixed-duration contract, since that refusal is a decision about the employment relationship as much as a dismissal is. [Para 97] That legal point mattered because the employer argued that letting a fixed-term contract expire is not a dismissal at all. [Para 96]

On the facts the panel found that the employer knew of Mr O's position. The founding election took place in the factory canteen, and notice of the elected leadership was sent by post on 20 February 2014 and returned on 21 February 2014 as refused. [Para 68][Para 70] Applying Clause 3(3) of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, which allows a union to notify the employer of candidacies by any reliable means, and Article 246 of the Civil Procedure Code on service by postal officer, the panel held that notice by post was valid and that the employer was deemed to know of it once the postal service confirmed delivery had been refused. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 104][Para 105]

The decisive evidence was the pattern. The employer was ordered at the hearing to produce the performance evaluation that justified not renewing Mr O, and it produced nothing by the 4 November 2014 deadline; the documents it filed on 11 November 2014 were out of time and were disregarded. [Para 84][Para 85][Para 86][Para 108] Without an evaluation, the stated reason could not be tested. [Para 81] Against that, the employer had completed the contract of another worker recruited alongside Mr O, had recruited two new workers into the ironing section, and had let the contracts of all three elected union leaders lapse in succession — first the president, then the secretary, and finally Mr O. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 109] The panel held that allowing every elected leader's contract to lapse while renewing other workers, with no evidence that the decisions rested on performance, was discrimination on union grounds. [Para 110]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The prohibition in Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law on using union membership or union activity as a ground for decisions about a worker's employment covers a refusal to renew a fixed-duration contract, not only a dismissal, so an employer cannot let a fixed-term contract lapse because the worker holds union office. Where the employer fails to produce the performance evaluation the panel ordered, and other workers in the same section were renewed or newly hired, the non-renewal is treated as anti-union discrimination and the panel orders the contract continued and the worker reinstated. [Para 97][Para 108][Para 110][Para 111]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's statement that it will disregard documents filed after the deadline it has set for evidence, whatever their content, is a general procedural stance rather than a ground of the order. [Para 85][Para 86]
