# Award No. 135/14 — Duhust (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 30 June 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Chiv Phirom (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Kong Polla (president) · Doc ID 139584

## 1. Facts
Duhust (Cambodia) Co., Ltd., a garment factory at Chom Chao, Phnom Penh, employed about 2,200 workers. [Para 101][Para 102] A Cambodia-ASEAN Union election on 27 April 2014 elected five leaders; the federation notified the employer of the result by post on 28 April, and on 29 April the employer ended the fixed-duration contracts of those five, along with four others in the pressing section. [Para 154][Para 204][Para 114] Eleven demands went to conciliation, ten were unsettled, and the union withdrew demand 10 at the hearing. [Para 28][Para 33]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the dismissal of the five union leaders was linked to anti-union discrimination? [Para 197]
2. Whether the employer must build a car shelter for staff and an additional bicycle and motorcycle shed for workers? [Para 241]
3. Whether the employer must enlarge the canteen? [Para 270]
4. Whether the employer must stop Saturday overtime? [Para 280]
5. Whether the employer must provide two dishes plus dessert every day? [Para 298]
6. Whether the employer must add 4 US dollars to the existing 11-dollar attendance bonus, totalling 15? [Para 307]
7. Whether the employer must add 8 dollars travel and 8 dollars accommodation to the existing 15? [Para 317]
8. Whether the employer must add 2,000 riel to the existing 4,000 riel overtime meal allowance? [Para 325]
9. Whether the employer must raise milk money from 5 to 15 US dollars per month for 18 months after birth? [Para 335]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the five were dismissed the day after the federation notified their election, though three held no prior warning; overtime is imposed under threat that fixed-duration contracts will not be renewed; the canteen has too few seats and the sheds too little space. [Para 2][Para 8][Para 165]
**Employer (respondent):** the five were not dismissed for union activity but because they failed their supervisors' evaluations, scoring 59%, 42%, 39%, 31% and 49%; it never received the notification; overtime is voluntary and signed for; it pays more than Notification No. 230 requires. [Para 3][Para 128][Para 184]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to take back the five union leaders; the shed, canteen, Saturday-overtime and two-dish claims were refused, and the bonus, travel, overtime-meal and milk-money claims were refused without consideration. [Para 343][Para 344][Para 346][Para 347][Para 348][Para 349]

**Parties.** Of 46 workers said to have transferred their rights, the panel found no written transfer before it and recognised only the five who attended, following Prakas No. 099 Article 19 on written authorisation. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 106]

**Reinstatement.** Articles 12 and 279 forbid using union membership or activity as a ground for discipline or termination. [Para 197][Para 198] The panel applied the rule that the party alleging discrimination bears the burden, then considered whether the employer had discharged the burden of its own assertion that Maney was not its security guard. [Para 233][Para 235] It found the employer produced no contrary evidence, so the notice of 28 April was received. [Para 236][Para 237] On the evaluations, the panel noted the employer never explained what the ten criteria measured or how often evaluation occurred. [Para 211] Article 37.k(1) of the internal rules treats any damage to company property as serious misconduct, which the panel found too broad, and Article 83 does not list damage to property as serious misconduct. [Para 222][Para 223] Since the internal rules classify failure to follow a supervisor's instruction as a medium fault attracting a written warning, and no warning was produced, dismissal was disproportionate under Article 27, which requires punishment to match the fault. [Para 213][Para 214][Para 225] The dismissals came one day after the notice and did not follow the internal rules, so the panel inferred anti-union discrimination and ordered reinstatement. [Para 238][Para 239][Para 240]

**Sheds and canteen.** The panel found no law, collective agreement or practice requiring a car shelter, so that demand was an interest dispute it could not entertain. [Para 253][Para 255] On the bicycle shed, the employer had built one and the workers produced no evidence of how many could not park; the claim failed for want of proof. [Para 246][Para 249] On the canteen, the employer showed 960 seats in two rooms against roughly 800 workers eating in two shifts, and the workers proved neither the shortfall nor that they had used the assigned shift. [Para 271][Para 276][Para 277]

**Saturday overtime.** Article 137 caps ordinary hours at eight a day and forty-eight a week, and Article 1 of Prakas No. 80 permits overtime only for exceptional pressure of work, with prior ministry authorisation. [Para 280][Para 282][Para 286] Article 4 of the same Prakas requires voluntariness and forbids pressure on workers who decline. [Para 287][Para 288] The workers proved neither how many were forced nor what form the threat took, so the claim failed. [Para 294][Para 296]

**Two dishes.** The employer provided rice, one dish and Friday dessert; the panel found this beneficial but no legal basis for more, and refused the claim. [Para 298][Para 301][Para 305]

**Bonus, travel, meal and milk money.** Each demand sought more than Notification No. 230 (10-dollar attendance bonus, 7-dollar travel) or Notification No. 041/11 (2,000 riel overtime meal) required, and the employer already paid 11 dollars, 14 dollars and 2,000 riel respectively. [Para 308][Para 318][Para 326] Being interest disputes, they could be brought only by a most representative union, which this claimant was not, so the panel refused to consider them. [Para 312][Para 322][Para 331]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A dismissal effected one day after the employer receives notice of a union leadership election, without the warnings the internal rules prescribe for the fault alleged, supports an inference of anti-union discrimination under Articles 12 and 279, and reinstatement follows. [Para 237][Para 238][Para 239]

An internal-rule clause treating any damage to company property, however slight, as serious misconduct cannot justify summary dismissal, because Article 27 requires the penalty to be proportionate to the fault and Article 83 does not list property damage among serious misconducts. [Para 222][Para 223][Para 225]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that Article 1 of Prakas No. 80 requires prior ministry authorisation before overtime is worked, a point not needed to decide the Saturday-overtime claim since the evidence of coercion failed. [Para 286]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The corporate name appears in Khmer in at least five OCR variants (ឌូហឹស្ត, ឌូហីស្ត, ឌូ ហឺស្ត, ឌីហឺស្ត and ឌូហឹស) and no Latin-script form is printed anywhere, so the transliteration "Duhust" used in the title is provisional and not taken from the face of the award. [Para 1][Para 35][Para 90] The number of federations holding transfers is printed as "២ ០៣" (two, three) at one point and three elsewhere, and the report counts 46 transferring workers while the panel recognises only the five who appeared. [Para 103][Para 106] One worker is called "គង់ ថា រ៉ុង" throughout the reasoning but "កម្មករ គង់ សារ៉ន" in the employer's serious-misconduct submission. [Para 149] The award states that ten of eleven points were unsettled, that ten were then conciliated, and that point 10 was withdrawn, leaving no consistent account of the eleventh. [Para 28][Para 33] The company's statutes are dated 07 February 2013 while registration is dated 14 September 2011, and the internal rules were approved 09 July 2012, so the internal rules predate the statutes they are said to derive from. [Para 36][Para 44][Para 47] OCR has dropped text throughout the evaluation tables at paragraphs 128, 132, 136 and 140, and the milk-money figures are printed as 15, then 10, then 100 dollars in successive passages. [Para 335][Para 351]
