# Award No. 042/14 — Hong Va 2 and Hong Va 3 (Arbitration Council, 24 March 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun for the employer party, An Virak for the worker party, Ang Eng Thong chair) · Doc ID 139529

## 1. Facts
Hong Va (Cambodia) Garment Manufacturing Ltd runs two garment factories, Hong Va 2 and Hong Va 3, in Phnom Penh with about 2,500 workers and a union affiliated to the Cambodian Workers' Union Federation. [Para 53][Para 55] Conciliation on 18 February 2014 settled one of five demands, leaving four: drinking bottles with glass lids, a meal allowance for overtime, a 20% rise in piece rates to match the minimum-wage increase, and payment of piece-rate wages twice a month. [Para 3][Para 5][Para 15][Para 16] The employer conceded the first demand by letter on 11 March 2014, leaving three for decision. [Para 22][Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a meal allowance for overtime, and a lunch allowance, every two weeks? [Para 5][Para 57]
2. Whether the employer must raise piece rates by 20%, in step with the rise in the minimum wage? [Para 70]
3. Whether the employer must calculate piece-rate wages twice a month and top up to the minimum wage at each of the two calculations? [Para 86][Para 191]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer already pays the overtime meal allowance every two weeks for work between 6:00 pm and 8:30 pm and on Sundays, so the allowance for 4:00 pm to 6:00 pm should follow the same cycle, and workers could use the money to buy food; the minimum wage rose from $80 to $100, about 20%, so piece rates should rise 20%, one worker reporting only $60 of output in February 2014. [Para 65][Para 71][Para 73] Wages should be calculated twice a month as Article 116 requires, otherwise a strong fortnight is offset by a weak one. [Para 89][Para 91]
**Employer (respondent):** changing the accounting system is difficult and the company is contractually bound to its supplier, so a fortnightly cycle would overload the accounts staff. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 69] Current piece rates already exceed the minimum wage, only about 100 workers fall short and the company tops them up, and average earnings are $180 a month or more. [Para 74][Para 79][Para 76]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay the overtime meal allowance every two weeks for the 4:00 pm to 6:00 pm shift, and to calculate piece-rate wages twice a month at intervals of no more than 16 days; it dismissed the 20% piece-rate rise and the claim to top up the minimum wage at each calculation. [Para 229][Para 231][Para 232]

The panel first separated rights disputes, decided according to law, from interest disputes, decided in equity, citing Article 312(2) of the Labour Law and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 107][Para 112][Para 113]

On the meal allowance it held the claim a rights dispute because Point 2 of Notice No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 entitles a worker who voluntarily works overtime at the employer's request to 2,000 riel a day or a free meal, without limiting the allowance by number of hours. [Para 115][Para 116][Para 117] The Notice fixes no payment cycle, but the panel found the meal is what sustains further overtime, and that payment must come at an interval that lets the worker buy food when working overtime. [Para 118][Para 119][Para 121] It ordered a fortnightly cycle, accepting that this was a reasonable middle course between daily payment and the administrative burden of the employer's system. [Para 132][Para 134]

On the piece-rate rise the panel held the claim a rights dispute about the wage rate, since the law requires only that rates not leave a worker below the minimum wage. [Para 135][Para 137][Para 159] Article 108 requires piece rates to yield at least the minimum wage for a worker of average skill in ordinary hours, and Article 137 caps ordinary hours at eight a day and 48 a week. [Para 164][Para 165] The panel refused to consider the employer's calculation sheets because they were in Chinese, contrary to Article 23 of Prakas No. 099, which makes Khmer the language of proceedings. [Para 176][Para 177][Para 180] It nevertheless found, from the payroll tables for December 2013 to February 2014 covering some 270 to 287 workers and from the workers' own concession, that only a small minority fell below the minimum wage and that the employer topped them up, so no rate rise was owed. [Para 185][Para 186][Para 188][Para 190]

On the second calculation the panel held Article 116 of the Labour Law and Circular No. 1903 of 24 November 2008 require wages to be paid at least twice a month at intervals of no more than 16 days for workers, and found that sewing-line workers are "workers" rather than "employees" on the definitions in Articles 5 and 6. [Para 193][Para 194][Para 201] The workers had asked only for calculation, not for two payments, so the panel ordered calculation twice a month while leaving the single monthly payment in place. [Para 207][Para 208][Para 213] It refused to require a top-up at each calculation, because Prakas No. 317 fixes a monthly minimum wage and the top-up duty arises only once a month. [Para 222][Para 225][Para 228]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A monthly minimum wage is not a daily or fortnightly floor: where wages are paid at least twice a month under Article 116, the employer must compute each payment separately, but the duty to top up to the minimum wage arises only once, against total monthly output, so a strong fortnight may not be set against a weak one to defeat the floor. [Para 213][Para 225] An employer that fixes piece rates must set them so that a worker of average skill working ordinary hours earns at least the minimum wage; evidence offered only in a foreign language cannot be relied on, Article 23 of Prakas No. 099 making Khmer the language of the proceedings. [Para 173][Para 180]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the overtime meal allowance must be paid at an interval allowing the worker to buy food, and its preference for a fortnightly cycle as a compromise with the employer's accounting system, are equitable observations on an interest element rather than a rule of law. [Para 121][Para 132]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award is dated 24 March 2013 on its face [Para 1], but every procedural step is in 2014 and the parties extended the deadline to 24 March 2014 [Para 31]; the year in the header is wrong.
- The panel's creation date reads "26 February 20 2014" [Para 17] and the conciliation minutes are dated "18 February 2022 2014" [Para 34].
- The employer's name appears as "Hong Va (Cambodia) Garmen", "Hong Va Cambodia Gamin Manu Facturing" and "Hong Va (Cambodia) Garment Manufacturing" across the text [Para 32][Para 53]; the award number appears as 042/14 [Para 1] and, in the closing order, as no number at all [Para 229].
- The number of workers earning below the minimum wage is given as about 100 [Para 76] and as about 1,000 [Para 181], against a total of 2,500 [Para 181].
- The decision section recording the interest-dispute order is blank and the arbitrators' signature block is illegible [Para 234]; the first demand's settlement is recorded only by reference to the employer's letter, with no order text [Para 22].
