# Award No. 256/14 — Than Yan International (Cambodia) Fashion (Arbitration Council, 21 October 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouchhun, Liv Sovanna, chair Pen Bunhea) · Doc ID 139713

## 1. Facts
Than Yan International (Cambodia) Fashion, a cut-make-trim garment factory at Village Khva, Sangkat Dangkao, Khan Dangkao, Phnom Penh, registered under the GSP scheme, employed some 800 workers. [Para 44] [Para 45] On 8 September 2014 the Labour Disputes Department received a complaint about a strike over working conditions and conciliation failed on all 13 demands, which came to the panel by report No. 1049 dated 10 September 2014; the hearing was held on 30 September 2014. [Para 20] [Para 21] [Para 22] [Para 19] At the hearing 9 of the 13 demands were settled, demand 3 was withdrawn, and only demands 2, 7 and 9 remained for decision. [Para 26] [Para 27] [Para 28] By agreement of the parties the award is non-binding. [Para 31]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the workers may require the employer to remove Ms O, the company's general manager, from her post because she threatened workers and interfered in the affairs of the Khmer Workers' Spirit Union? [Para 50] [Para 84]
2. Whether the employer must install two further fingerprint scanners in the sewing section and one further scanner in the packing section? [Para 62] [Para 105]
3. Whether the employer must set the target bonus on a proper footing — without raising the trouser quota once the qualifying quota has been fixed — and allow workers to go home early once they have sewn enough to earn the target bonus? [Para 70] [Para 122]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that on 5 September 2014 the general manager stopped 7 union representatives from working and pressed them to accept severance, that on 6 September she pulled Ms S's card and had her ejected by security, that she told every section to have no contact with the union and to bring problems to her instead, calling the union unlawful and its officers paid US$5 to US$10 to run around "like stray dogs"; and that the new manager raised the bonus quota and stopped early release once the quota was met. [Para 51] [Para 53] [Para 56] [Para 58] [Para 74] [Para 75] [Para 77]
**Employer (respondent):** that the 7 contracts simply reached their term and were not terminated, that it did not witness the card incident, that the union held no registration certificate from the Ministry so the manager's remark meant only that, that machine spacing had already been widened and the steel barriers cut away, and that quotas were raised only after workers became proficient, with 70 to 80 per cent of workers still earning the target bonus. [Para 59] [Para 60] [Para 61] [Para 46] [Para 68] [Para 78] [Para 79] [Para 80]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three remaining demands rejected; no sum ordered. [Para 137] [Para 138] [Para 139]

On demand 2 the panel held that a labour contract is formed by the consent of the parties and that only the two parties to it may vary or terminate it; a third party outside the contract cannot. It read Article 65 of the 1997 Labour Law together with Articles 664 and 311 of the 2007 Civil Code to that effect and followed its own earlier awards No. 54/08 (Choung Youv), No. 124/10 (Choun Tich Tha Yol) and No. 06/12 (Oseaung). [Para 84] [Para 86] [Para 90] [Para 91] [Para 92] [Para 93] On the facts it also found that the workers produced no evidence or witness that the general manager had uttered the words alleged [Para 98], and that even if she had, the conduct described could not create danger or any concrete job insecurity for other workers. [Para 101] [Para 102] [Para 103]

On demand 7 the panel applied Article 2 of the Labour Law, which places the enterprise under the management and direction of the employer, and held that the number and arrangement of attendance scanners is a managerial prerogative to be exercised lawfully and reasonably; it followed awards No. 62/06 (Clik Su), No. 108/06, No. 33/07 (Golfem), No. 119/09 (SL) and No. 33/13 (Siem Reap International Airport). [Para 105] [Para 108] [Para 109] [Para 111] Six scanners served roughly 800 workers — four in sewing, one in packing, one by the office — and a queue of about 7 minutes at exit was held to be a reasonable period; the move from card to fingerprint scanning was accepted as a measure to stop workers scanning for absent colleagues. [Para 112] [Para 118] [Para 119] [Para 120]

On demand 9 the panel again relied on Article 2 and on award No. 24/05 (Ware Well), which held that an employer may by unilateral decision establish bonus plans beyond those the law requires in order to encourage higher output. The target bonus ran from 3,000 to 4,500 riel a day, was paid separately from base wage across 17 sewing teams, and 70 to 80 per cent of workers reached it; both the qualifying quota and any permission to leave early were held to fall within the employer's discretion, and no unlawful or disproportionate effect on the workers was shown. [Para 127] [Para 128] [Para 131] [Para 132] [Para 133] [Para 134] [Para 135] [Para 136]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Workers cannot obtain the removal of a manager who is not their employer, because a labour contract binds only its two parties and only they may vary or terminate it; and the number and placement of attendance scanners, together with the quota and early-release conditions of a productivity bonus, are exercises of the employer's managerial prerogative under Article 2 of the Labour Law which the panel will leave undisturbed where they are lawful and produce no disproportionate effect. [Para 90] [Para 91] [Para 111] [Para 132]

**Obiter dicta:** that the general manager's description of the union as "unlawful" was properly read as meaning only that it had not yet obtained a registration certificate from the Ministry, not that it was an illegal organisation; and that an exit queue of some 7 minutes for 800 workers compares acceptably with 2 or 3 minutes under card scanning. [Para 61] [Para 100] [Para 119]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel is recorded as constituted on "12 September 2021 2014". [Para 23] The claimant group is unstable: 358 workers thumbprinted the transfer of rights, 7 attended the hearing, but 3 of those 7 also appear on the transfer list, and the panel assumed 362 claimants — 358 plus 4 — without saying which four. [Para 47] [Para 48] [Para 49] The same 7 workers appear as "076 persons" in one passage. [Para 62] Worker numbers are printed as both "about 800" and "800 hundred". [Para 45] [Para 112] The exit-queue duration is garbled as "078 14 minutes". [Para 116] Article 2 is twice quoted from a "Law on the Protection of Labour" rather than the Labour Law. [Para 125] The company is printed variously as Than Yan International (Cambodia) Fashion, Than Yan, and Than Yian. Award No. 24/05 is attributed to the "National Arbitration Council". [Para 127] Individual workers and union officers are reduced to initials per the briefing convention; the general manager is named on the face of the award as Ms O for the same reason. [Para 50]
