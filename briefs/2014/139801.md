# Award No. 361/14 — Can Sport Shoes Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 5 February 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Nyan Somunnith presiding; Ing Sothy, employer nominee; Liv Sovanna, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139801

## 1. Facts
Can Sport Shoes Co., Ltd manufactures footwear at Chumkar Svay village, Sethey commune, Samaki Mean Chey district, Kampong Chhnang, with 5,076 workers. [Para 1][Para 61][Para 62] Its collective dispute with the union at the factory turned on two demands that the provincial labour department could not settle at conciliation on 29 December 2014, when four points were conciliated, two agreed and two not; the file reached the Secretariat on 31 December 2014. [Para 9][Para 10][Para 11] Both parties attended the hearing of 14 January 2015, and by agreement extended the award date from 26 January to 5 February 2015. [Para 14][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether point 3 of the agreement recorded in the minutes of 20 June 2014 obliges the company to pay a functional allowance (ប្រាក់មុខងារ) of USD 6 per month to every machinist and electrician who has worked in the company for six months or more? [Para 67][Para 78][Para 116]
2. Whether the company paid the November 2014 wages of the claimant worker, Ms M (staff no. 016066, section A2, group 9), USD 100 short at the pay counter on 09 December 2014, and must make up that sum? [Para 86][Para 132][Para 134]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** point 3 of the 20 June 2014 minutes fixes an entitlement of USD 6 a month for every machinist and electrician after six months' service, consistent with a practice from 2010 that paid USD 2, USD 4 or USD 6 by job type; the company stopped paying some machinists and electricians around 2013 and paid nothing to new ones. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 70][Para 71] On the money claim, Ms M counted her notes beside the counter, found USD 100 missing, asked to be searched and was refused. [Para 94][Para 99][Para 106]
**Employer (respondent):** point 3 required only that a clear policy be drawn up, and the company did so — biannual skills tests in June and December, with USD 9 added to the base wage of those who pass — so no machinist or electrician acquires the allowance automatically at six months. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 81][Para 122][Para 123] It added that Ms M signed and thumbprinted without checking the notes, so the shortfall is not its fault, and that paying would multiply such claims. [Para 105]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand for a USD 6 monthly functional allowance for all machinists and electricians with six months' service was dismissed, and the company was ordered to pay USD 100 as the shortfall in Ms M's November 2014 wages; as a rights dispute the order became final on issue immediately, under the parties' agreement of 14 January 2015. [Para 149][Para 150][Para 151]

The panel first classified both demands, applying Article 312 of the Labour Law and Article 43 of Prakas 099: a rights dispute is one grounded in a law, agreement, collective agreement, internal rules or past practice, decided according to law, while an interests dispute is decided in equity. [Para 108][Para 110][Para 113][Para 114] Issue 1 was a rights dispute because it rested on point 3 of the minutes, which was in force; issue 2 was a rights dispute because it concerned wages owed under the contract and the Labour Law. [Para 118][Para 133]

On issue 1 the panel read point 3 narrowly. Its text bound the company "to arrange a clear policy" on the functional allowance for workers with six months' service, to be completed by the end of June 2014; that is a promise of a procedure, not a promise to pay USD 6 to each machinist and electrician from that date. [Para 119][Para 125] The company had discharged the promise by adopting a testing system that adds USD 9 to the base wage of a machinist or electrician who passes, which also lifts the overtime rate, and the workers did not contest that system's existence or operation. [Para 81][Para 126] Machinists and electricians with six months' service therefore hold no automatic right to the allowance, whether separate from or folded into the base wage, and the union had in any event identified no individual who once received it and then lost it. [Para 127][Para 129][Para 130]

On issue 2 the panel applied Article 118 of the Labour Law, which puts the burden of proving wage payment on the employer, and Article 119, under which accepting a pay slip without protest does not waive unpaid wages. [Para 134][Para 136][Para 137] The formal condition of Article 118 was satisfied, since Ms M signed and thumbprinted the pay sheet in front of her team leader; but the panel held that this did not discharge the substantive duty to pay in full. [Para 138][Para 139] It found that the signature was taken before she received the notes, that the bell ending the shift had pushed the queue forward, and that she had moved less than a metre from the counter and had not left it when she counted and complained. [Para 92][Para 93][Para 140][Para 146] Her account of receiving USD 8 and 2,500 riel against USD 108 and 2,500 riel was precise as to denominations, no witness confirmed full payment, and the team leader's refusal to search her excluded concealment on her part. [Para 90][Para 94][Para 144][Para 145] The panel found the company had paid short unintentionally and ordered the USD 100 made up. [Para 147][Para 148]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the employer bears the burden of proving wage payment, a pay sheet signed and thumbprinted by the worker is evidence of the fact of payment but not proof of its amount: if the payment was made under time pressure that prevented the worker from counting, and no witness can confirm that the full sum was handed over, the employer must pay the shortfall even though no dishonesty is shown, because acceptance of a pay slip without protest does not waive unpaid wages. [Para 137][Para 139][Para 144][Para 147]

A collective agreement clause obliging the employer to "arrange a clear policy" on an allowance creates an obligation to adopt a policy, not an entitlement to a fixed monthly sum for every worker who reaches the stated length of service; the entitlement is measured by the policy actually adopted. [Para 125][Para 126][Para 127]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel reserved that a machinist or electrician who is specifically identified, and who proves that he once received the allowance and was later cut off, must be back-paid for the missed months and paid it thereafter — a reservation unnecessary to the dismissal of a claim the union had left unnamed. [Para 128][Para 131] It also recorded that the CCTV footage of 09 December 2014 had been automatically overwritten before the workers were allowed to view it, without drawing any inference from the loss. [Para 100][Para 102]

The document is headed an order (សេចក្តីបង្គាប់) rather than an award, and it carries no panel signatures: the closing line reads "no signature of the members of the panel" and a stray "Grazz" sits before the president's name. [Para 152] The constitution of the panel is dated "05 January 2022 2016 2015", three years run together. [Para 12] Prakas 099 is dated "21 April 2019 2004" in the reasons although the jurisdiction section gives 21 April 2004. [Para 4][Para 110] The minutes are dated 20 June 2014 in the body but "20 September June 2014" and "20 2017 June 2014" where the clause is quoted. [Para 78][Para 117][Para 125] A factory permit is dated "29 March 20103". [Para 31] The union's evidence list includes a representative certificate issued for "CHANG HILL (CAMBODIA) Co., Ltd.", a different factory. [Para 49] The demand is printed as "១ ០៦ ដុល្លារ" (1 06 dollars) in the issue statement. [Para 67] A stray "ឆ្នាំ" (year) intrudes into the missing-amount phrase. [Para 106] The company appears as "CAN SPORT SHOES CO.,LTD." and "CAN SPORTSHOES CO.,LTD."; the worker whose wages are in issue is named on the face of the award and redacted here as Ms M.
