# Award No. 308/14 — Huey Chaen (Cambodia) Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 11 December 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Tuon Si Phan for the worker party, Pen Bunhea chair) · Doc ID 139756

## 1. Facts
Huey Chaen (Cambodia) Co Ltd, a shoe factory at Chroy Ampil, Kbal Koh, Chbar Ampov, Phnom Penh, employing about 1,235 workers, faced nine demands from the Khmer Union Federation; conciliation failed on 28 October 2014 and report No. 1251 of 31 October 2014 reached the Secretariat that same day. [Para 12][Para 17][Para 75][Para 76] At the hearings of 18 and 19 November 2014 the parties settled issues 4, 6, 7 and 8 and the workers withdrew issue 5, leaving issues 1, 2, 3 and 9 for decision. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24] Six workers attended and were treated as the complainants. [Para 80]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay Mr S, the factory union president, his full wages and benefits for August 2014 and from 1 to 14 September 2014? [Para 81]
2. Whether the employer must reinstate Ms S, an administrative assistant, in the same post with back pay from 30 July 2014? [Para 101]
3. Whether the employer must pay severance to all workers before changing the company name from Huey Chaen to Chien Ly? [Para 126]
4. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr S in the same post in compliance with the court's protective injunction? [Para 137]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr S attended regularly and his attendance was marked by hand by the administration, so withholding his pay for August and the first half of September 2014 was unlawful; Ms S was a protected union member dismissed without the labour inspector's consent; the change of name was a unilateral termination owed severance; and the employer had to obey the injunction. [Para 85][Para 121][Para 131][Para 144]
**Employer (respondent):** Mr S ignored the written notice of 8 August 2014 to clock in, so his absence is unexplained; Ms S raised her own salary after her name had been struck from the list of five, which is serious misconduct; the name change preserved every post, seniority and benefit and terminated nothing; and Mr S abandoned his job by not returning within the 48 hours the court allowed. [Para 96][Para 119][Para 136][Para 149][Para 152]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay Mr S's wages and benefits in full for August 2014 and 1 to 14 September 2014 and to reinstate him in his post, while the claims of Ms S and of severance on the name change were dismissed. [Para 266][Para 267][Para 268][Para 269]

Issue 1: Article 102 defines wages as what the employer must pay under the contract for work done. The panel applied its standing rule that the party asserting a fact bears the burden of proof, then noted that attendance records are held by the employer alone; the employer was ordered to produce the handwritten list kept for Mr S and produced only the clock-card data, so it failed to prove absence and the workers' account was accepted. [Para 157][Para 173][Para 176][Para 179][Para 180] Article 28 prohibits punishing the same fault twice and defines a monetary penalty as any measure reducing wages otherwise due, so failing to clock in could be disciplined under the internal rules but not by withholding pay. [Para 182][Para 183][Para 186][Para 187]

Issue 2: Article 293 requires the labour inspector's consent to dismiss a shop steward, and article 4 of Prakas No. 305 extends protection to founding and volunteer members. The panel set three conditions — protected status, dismissal within the protected period, and notice of the protected names to the employer — and found the third unmet because the union never notified the employer that Ms S was protected; she therefore had no immunity. [Para 191][Para 197][Para 200][Para 208][Para 209] On the merits, a staff member who saw her name struck from the salary-increase list and then instructed accounting to raise her own pay, promising to answer for any problem, committed serious misconduct within Article 83, so the dismissal was lawful. [Para 213][Para 226][Para 232][Para 233]

Issue 3: Articles 311 of the Civil Code and 65 of the Labour Law make the creation, modification and extinction of contractual duties the act of the parties' will alone. The panel found one director behind both companies, no formal name-change instrument yet, the old sign still in place, and every post, workplace, seniority and benefit preserved; there was no unilateral termination and no severance was due. [Para 239][Para 242][Para 244][Para 246][Para 247]

Issue 4: the employer's own account was contradictory — Mr S could not have been absent from the factory and at the same time inside it urging others to strike between 26 and 30 September 2014 — and the handwritten list that would have settled the point was again not produced, so the employer failed to prove abandonment and reinstatement was ordered. [Para 258][Para 261][Para 263][Para 265]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the employer alone holds the attendance record and is ordered to produce the handwritten list but does not, it fails to discharge the burden of proving absence and the workers' account is accepted; and a worker's failure to clock in as instructed may be met with internal discipline but not with the withholding of wages, because Article 28 forbids double punishment and treats any reduction of wages due as a monetary penalty. A change of company name that leaves the contract's parties, posts, seniority and benefits intact is not a termination and attracts no severance. [Para 176][Para 186][Para 244][Para 246]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the union's second-term leadership recognition had expired on 2 August 2014, so those who appeared could not represent all members — a point that did not decide anything, since the six attending workers were treated as the complainants. [Para 79][Para 80]
