# Award No. 121/14 — Sin Chang Sing Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 10 July 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, appointed by the employer; An Vireak, appointed by the workers; Kong Phalha, chair appointed by both) · Doc ID 139576

## 1. Facts
Sin Chang Sing Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a garment factory in Sangkat Tuol Sangke, Khan Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, employing somewhat more than 800 workers. [Para 45] After conciliation failed on 09 May 2014, 14 demands came to the Council; 49 workers had assigned their rights to the Federation of Trade Unions of Workers, which is the claimant. [Para 17][Para 23][Para 46][Para 47] The employer's administrative representative attended the first hearing only; at the second the employer stayed away, saying it had business far from Phnom Penh. [Para 1][Para 29][Para 66] Eleven claims were withdrawn at the hearing, leaving claims 2, 3 and 10. [Para 30][Para 31]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must provide tables on which workers can eat their meals? [Para 68]
2. Whether the employer must provide chairs for the workers of the quality-control (QC) section? [Para 81]
3. Whether the employer must repair and clean the toilets so that they are hygienic? [Para 93]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company has no canteen and no tables, so workers eat on the factory's carpet or on the floor of a tent, which is unhygienic and harms their health; QC staff must stand for eight hours a day and some cannot stand that long; the toilets are old and unclean because nobody cleans them. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 53][Para 54][Para 58][Para 99]
**Employer (respondent):** absent from the second hearing and from the merits; it filed no documentary evidence at all, and did not answer the secretariat's letter No. 727 of 12 June 2014 asking for evidence on the eating, seating and toilet conditions. [Para 29][Para 35][Para 51][Para 57][Para 61]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three surviving claims were refused and no order was made against the employer. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 108]

The panel first asked whether it could sit without the employer. Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 allows it to continue, or to close the case by issuing an award, where a party was properly invited and failed to appear without a valid reason. [Para 63] The panel followed awards 53/04, 63/04, 148/07, 99/09, 173/11, 142/12, 109/13, 284/13 and 150/14 on the same point, and held that having business outside Phnom Penh was not a valid excuse. [Para 64][Para 66][Para 67]

On the dining tables the panel applied the rule that the party asserting or accusing bears the burden of proof, citing awards 79/05, 101/08, 168/09, 115/10 and 148/11. [Para 74][Para 75] The workers gave no evidence of the conditions in which they ate, so the facts were not sufficiently established and the claim could not be decided. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 78][Para 79]

On the QC chairs the panel found a real duty: Article 1 of Prakas No. 053 of 10 February 2000 requires a suitable chair at each workstation where the work suits a sitting posture, and Article 2 requires sufficient chairs near the workstation even where the work must be done standing. [Para 83][Para 84] It accepted that QC work is done standing for eight hours and that this affects the workers' health, and approved award 94/04 on the same duty. [Para 87][Para 88] The claim nevertheless failed, because the workers could not say how many of the 49 who assigned their rights were QC staff, so there was no definite claimant and no order the panel could frame. [Para 90][Para 91][Para 92]

On the toilets the panel again found a duty: Article 229 of the Labour Law requires every establishment and workplace to be kept clean and to show hygienic and healthy conditions, and toilets are part of the establishment; Article 1 of Prakas No. 052 of 10 February 2000 requires suitable, hygienic toilets and Article 6 requires sufficient clean water. [Para 93][Para 95][Para 96][Para 97][Para 98] The workers asserted the toilets were old and uncleaned but produced no evidence, so the claim failed on the same burden. [Para 99][Para 103][Para 104][Para 105]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim founded on a genuine statutory duty still fails where the claimant cannot prove the facts that trigger the duty or cannot identify the workers on whose behalf it is brought: the burden lies on the party asserting, and the panel will not make an order it cannot enforce against an ascertainable class of workers. [Para 74][Para 91] A party that was properly invited and stays away without a valid reason does not prevent the panel from hearing the case; under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 it may proceed in that party's absence and decide on the record before it. [Para 63][Para 67]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's reading of "establishment and workplace" in Article 229 as broad enough to cover buildings and other places within the company, and so to include toilets, is a general interpretive remark; the claim failed for want of evidence, not on that reading. [Para 94][Para 95]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The record contradicts itself on attendance: the header says the employer's representative joined the first hearing and was absent from the second [Para 1], but the reasons state the employer did not attend the hearing at all and gave no valid reason [Para 29][Para 62][Para 66].
- The list of withdrawn claims carries a stray numeral — "claims 1, 4, 23, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11, 12 and 14" — which still yields the eleven withdrawals the award relies on [Para 30].
- The employer-appointed arbitrator is named Seng Vouch Hun in the composition [Para 1], but the signature block leaves that slot blank and only An Vireak and Kong Phalha are legible [Para 110]; the chair is printed Kong Phalha in one place and Kong Phalha (គង់ ផល្លៈ) in the other [Para 1][Para 110].
- The company appears both in Khmer transliteration and as "Xing Chang Xin (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd" in a union document [Para 35]; a complaint letter is dated 31 March 2014 but received 02 April 2014 [Para 21].
- The findings record that neither party supplied evidence on the toilets [Para 61], which the reasons then attribute only to the workers [Para 103]; OCR loss also obscures the Prakas numbers and article headings in places [Para 83][Para 93].
