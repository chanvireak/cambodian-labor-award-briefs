# Award No. 257/14 — T & K Garment Industry (Arbitration Council, 16 October 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, An Non, chair Sokh Mathoeung) · Doc ID 139715

## 1. Facts
T & K Garment Industry, a cut-make-trim factory on Toul Pongro Road, Choam Chao, Phnom Penh, employed about 1,250 workers. [Para 47] [Para 48] At about 04:45 on 28 July 2014 the Chang Sing factory, sharing a fence line, caught fire; the authorities closed all roads within 100 metres and cut the electricity, so neither workers nor employer could enter and no neighbouring factory operated that day. [Para 4] [Para 56] [Para 58] [Para 59] On 29 July the plant reopened but residual smoke made some workers faint, and the employer sent everyone home on full pay. [Para 60] [Para 61] Conciliation on 29 August 2014 failed on the single demand for the day's wage of 28 July, and the panel heard the case on 1 October 2014. [Para 12] [Para 13] [Para 10] The parties are signatories of the Memorandum of Understanding of 03 October 2012 on improving professional relations in the garment industry. [Para 21]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the daily wage for 28 July 2014 to workers who came to the factory but could not enter because the authorities had closed the roads after the fire at the neighbouring Chang Sing factory? [Para 55] [Para 85]
2. Whether that demand is a rights dispute or an interest dispute, and therefore whether the award binds the parties under the 2012 Memorandum of Understanding? [Para 75] [Para 76]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that they presented themselves for work and were prevented only by the authorities' road closure while firefighting, that other companies in the zone — YW, American King, Don Hut — suspended operations that day but still paid the daily wage, and that the employer should therefore pay 28 July. [Para 62] [Para 63]
**Employer (respondent):** that the stoppage was a case of force majeure arising from nature or from another, that T & K could not control a fire in Chang Sing, that it had no power to order the authorities to reopen the road, and that it had already paid full wages and benefits for 29 July. [Para 64] [Para 65] [Para 66] [Para 69] [Para 61]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay the daily wage for 28 July 2014; the order is a binding rights-dispute award for the claimants who are members of the Free Workers' Union and the Khmer Union at T & K, and a non-binding award for the remaining claimants, with no order on any interest dispute. [Para 116] [Para 117] [Para 118] [Para 119]

The panel first settled who the claimants were, holding that the eight persons who attended came as staff delegates under Article 283 of the Labour Law, which makes the elected delegate the sole representative of all workers in an enterprise, and it followed award No. 212/14. [Para 72] [Para 73] [Para 74] It classified the demand as a rights dispute because it concerned wages during suspension of the contract, a matter governed by the Labour Law, and distinguished rights disputes, decided according to law, from interest disputes, decided by equity under Article 312 paragraph 2. [Para 77] [Para 81] [Para 82] [Para 85] [Para 86]

On the merits it set out Article 71 paragraph 10, which suspends the contract for force majeure for no more than three months, and Article 72 paragraph 1, under which suspension relieves the employer of the duty to pay wages. [Para 89] [Para 91] [Para 92] Adopting the definition of force majeure as an event outside the will, unforeseeable and insurmountable, it found the fire and road closure were not force majeure: the closure of access to T & K could not have occurred but for the Chang Sing fire, and that fire was a human act which should not have happened had proper technical precautions been taken. [Para 97] [Para 107] [Para 108] It followed award No. 54/11 (Choun Tich Tha Yol) on the two conditions of force majeure and award No. 225/14 (Chang Sing (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd), which had already held that this same fire was not force majeure. [Para 101] [Para 104] [Para 105] [Para 109] Since the contract was not suspended, Article 72 did not apply and the wages were owed. [Para 111] [Para 112] [Para 114]

Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented on the same issue, reasoning that all three conditions of force majeure were met — the fire and closure arose outside the employer's will, were unforeseeable, and could not be overcome, since an employer cannot order the authorities to reopen a road and any attempt to cross the cordon would have exposed workers and employer alike to arrest under Articles 502 to 507 of the Penal Code and to physical risk. [Para 143] [Para 145] [Para 147] [Para 152] [Para 153] [Para 157] [Para 158]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fire in a neighbouring factory, and the road closures the authorities impose to fight it, are a human act rather than force majeure, so the labour contract is not suspended under Articles 71 and 72 of the Labour Law and the employer owes the wage for the day the workers could not enter — the panel judging the origin of the hazard, not merely the employer's inability to control it. A demand for wages during an alleged suspension is a rights dispute, and where the parties' collective memorandum provides that rights-dispute decisions bind them, the award is binding on the workers covered by that memorandum and non-binding on the rest. [Para 108] [Para 86] [Para 117] [Para 119]

**Obiter dicta:** that the panel cannot establish which claimants belong to which union, and so issues two awards on membership lines; and that an employer's objection that the mandates of the two local union leaders had expired succeeds where the workers decline to file evidence of their status. [Para 27] [Para 52] [Para 53]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The fire is timed at 04:45 in the facts, at "about 4 to 10 in the morning" in the agreed statement, and once as "4 to 2 10 in the morning". [Para 4] [Para 55] [Para 94] The award describes the closed roads as those "within 100 metres of Chang Sing" and elsewhere as access to T & K at the same distance, the two being adjacent. [Para 56] [Para 95] Prakas No. 099 is dated "21 April 2019 2004". [Para 79] Prior award No. 54/11 is cited in the reasons and 225/14 for the same fire, the latter with a garbled case reference ("case 14"). [Para 101] [Para 104] The company name appears as T & K Garment Industry, T & K, and five Khmer spellings of "Industry"; the union names are printed as both the Free Workers' Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia and the Khmer Union. [Para 47] [Para 70] The claimant group is described only as the 1,250 workforce with eight delegates present, two of them union leaders, so the number of workers covered by the order is never stated. [Para 48] [Para 49] [Para 50] Individual workers and union officers are reduced to initials per the briefing convention.
