# Award No. 089/14 — Pu Yuen (Cambodia) Inter Braly Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 12 May 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Kong Phalla (president), Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Virak (worker-appointed) · Doc ID 139550

## 1. Facts
Pu Yuen (Cambodia) Inter Braly Ltd. ("Pu Yuen") manufactures shoes at Thlok Tla, Sen Sok, Phnom Penh, and the conciliation report records about 3,400 workers. [Para 1][Para 42][Para 43] Two factory unions under the Cambodian Workers' Federation gave notice of a strike on 31 March 2014 over nine demands; conciliation on 8 April 2014 failed on three of them, and those three reached the Council on 9 April 2014. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 15][Para 16] The hearing was held on 22 April 2014 with both sides present, and the parties agreed to treat decisions on rights disputes as binding under the Memorandum of Understanding of 3 October 2012. [Para 19][Para 21][Para 23][Para 41]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must relocate the daycare centre and bring it up to standard, the present one being beside a drain, a generator and a security toilet, cramped and hot? [Para 2][Para 47]
2. Whether the employer must arrange acceptable cooling in every work area and every building, and fix a clear timetable for doing so? [Para 4][Para 58]
3. Whether the employer must give effect to point 1 of Award No. 255/13 — Pu Yuen, which fixed the base for calculating overtime pay? [Para 70][Para 118]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The daycare sits next to a drain, the generator and a guard toilet, is cramped and hot, has no child-minder and no play equipment, so no mother will leave her child there; summer temperatures reach 35.5 to 37 °C and workers faint; the earlier award on overtime base pay has still not been applied. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 59][Para 73]
**Employer (respondent):** The drain is sealed, the generator runs only during outages and the guard toilet is negligible; a child-minder is in place, the room is being repainted and it will be finished by 15 June 2014 for the labour inspector to assess; peak heat is only 35 to 35.5 °C and technicians have been asked to service the air-conditioning. [Para 50][Para 52][Para 54][Para 64][Para 67]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer is ordered to take every measure, immediately and at the latest within one month of the award becoming final, so that workplace heat is tolerable without harm to health; claims 1 and 3 are refused. [Para 127][Para 129][Para 130]

On issue 1 the panel classified the claim as a rights dispute because Article 186 of the Labour Law requires an enterprise with at least 100 women to provide a nursing room and a daycare centre. [Para 82][Para 87][Para 88] It then found the claim premature: the workers could not show that the feared harm had occurred or was occurring, the mothers had in fact left the centre unused, and the panel followed its earlier ruling that it is constituted to settle labour disputes, not problems that have not yet arisen. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 95][Para 96] Because the employer had formally undertaken to complete the work by 15 June 2014 and to have the labour inspector assess it and correct anything non-compliant, the demand was refused. [Para 98][Para 99][Para 100]

On issue 2 the panel also found a rights dispute, resting on Article 229 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 147 on heat in workplaces, which requires the employer to use every means — fans, blowers, coolers — to make the workplace cool where heat is severe. [Para 101][Para 102][Para 103][Para 108] Both sides' accounts of peak temperature were inconsistent, but the panel concluded from them that the range ran from 35 to 35.5 °C, which it treated as a hot day's temperature; the employer had only contacted technicians without fixing any start or end date, so it had not yet discharged the duty. [Para 109][Para 111][Para 113] One month was allowed because the hot season was ending and the servicing itself needed that time. [Para 116][Para 117]

On issue 3 the panel held it had no competence at all. Article 353(1) of the 2006 Civil Procedure Code requires a court decision to enforce an arbitral award, domestic or foreign, in Cambodia; only a court may order a party to comply with the Council's award, and the panel cannot entertain challenges about the enforcement of its own decisions. [Para 118][Para 122][Para 123][Para 124]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An arbitration panel has no jurisdiction over a demand that a party comply with one of its earlier awards: enforcement of an award requires a court order under Article 353(1) of the Civil Procedure Code, so such a claim will be refused however clear the earlier ruling. [Para 122][Para 123]

**Obiter dicta:** A demand directed at a workplace condition that has caused no demonstrated harm, and that the employer has formally promised to remedy and to submit to labour-inspection assessment, is a claim about a problem that has not yet arisen and will not be decided. [Para 89][Para 96]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's name has no Latin-script form anywhere in the award and the Khmer spelling varies between references, so the romanisation above is provisional. [Para 1][Para 42] The panel's constitution date is garbled as "ឆ្នាំ ២០១៩ ២០១៤" and Prakas 099 is dated "ឆ្នាំ ២០១៩ ២០០៤". [Para 17][Para 77] The operative part closes with an empty section 2 headed interests disputes, followed by no signature block for any of the three arbitrators. [Para 133] The third point of the operative part is repeated verbatim in two consecutive lines. [Para 130][Para 131] The 3,400 workforce figure comes from the conciliation report, not from evidence at the hearing. [Para 43]
