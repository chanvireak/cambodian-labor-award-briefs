# Award No. 262/13 — Sheico (Phnom Penh) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 16 January 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · arbitrators' names not legible in the OCR · Doc ID 139913

## 1. Facts
Sheico (Phnom Penh) Co., Ltd. employed Ms O and Ms H on fixed-term contracts after two-month probation; both were elected to the leadership of the Sheico branch of the Khmer Workers' Spirit Federation at a 27 October 2013 vote, and the union applied for registration, receiving only a receipt on 19 December 2013. The employer dismissed both women on 1 November 2013 and wrote the same day to the labour inspector for authorisation, but did not wait for a reply. [Para 60][Para 65][Para 184][Para 194]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back Ms O and Ms H, who were elected union leaders, and pay their wages from the dismissal date until reinstatement? [Para 64][Para 150]
2. Whether the employer must raise the monthly transport and housing allowance to USD 15? [Para 80]
3. Whether the employer must raise the daily lunch allowance to 5,000 riel? [Para 109]
4. Whether the AB bonus must be paid equally to workers in the sanitation section? [Para 109]
5. Whether the employer must keep the full regular attendance bonus during authorised sick leave? [Para 103][Para 242]
6. Whether the employer must stop deducting the regular attendance bonus in proportion to lawful leave days? [Para 103]
7. Whether the employer must stop deducting the ABC bonus when workers take other lawful leave? [Para 109]
8. Whether the employer must pay a regular attendance bonus of USD 15 a month to every worker? [Para 103]
9. Whether the employer must conclude one-year fixed-term contracts after probation? [Para 344]
10. Whether the employer must allow pregnant workers to leave 15 minutes early? [Para 345]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The two dismissed women were elected union leaders and candidates, so their dismissal needed the labour inspector's prior authorisation; they should be reinstated with back pay. The remaining claims sought higher allowances, equal AB bonuses, and full attendance bonuses during lawful leave. [Para 64][Para 81][Para 106]

**Employer (respondent):** The two were dismissed for serious misconduct and would receive termination indemnity, but the company would not take them back; it had asked the labour inspector for authorisation and knew of their union candidacy. It asked the panel to keep its existing allowance and bonus practice. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 77][Para 108]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to reinstate Ms O and Ms H and to pay their wages from the date of dismissal until the date it takes them back; the claim for Ms S was dismissed, as were the allowance, bonus and contract claims. The order is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification if no objection is filed. [Para 350][Para 351][Para 361]

On issue 1, the panel applied Article 293 of the Labour Law, which protects union leaders and candidates and requires the labour inspector's authorisation before dismissal. It found the three conditions met: the women were protected persons, the dismissal fell within the protection period, and the union had notified the employer of the 19 names to be protected. The employer's own admission that it knew of the candidacies when it dismissed them was decisive. [Para 179][Para 181][Para 186][Para 188][Para 189]

Because the employer filed its request on 1 November 2013 and more than 30 days passed without a decision, the panel held the inspector to have refused authorisation as of 30 November 2013, leaving the employer two months to appeal to the Minister. Dismissing the women on 1 November without waiting for that authorisation breached Article 293. [Para 202][Para 203][Para 204][Para 205]

The claim for Ms S failed on procedure: she did not attend the hearing and gave no written authority to the representatives who did, so the panel could not decide her claim. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 148]

The remaining claims were treated as interest disputes rather than rights disputes. On the attendance bonus, the panel held the USD 10 monthly bonus under Notice No. 230 to be a general legal requirement binding on garment and footwear employers, not a discretionary reward, but found no rule requiring the employer to keep it whole during sick leave, so that claim exceeded the law. [Para 272][Para 244][Para 245] The wage, allowance, AB-bonus and one-year-contract claims were likewise refused as demands for conditions the employer had not accepted. [Para 341][Para 343]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A dismissal of a union leader or candidate without the labour inspector's prior authorisation is unlawful under Article 293 even where the employer has applied for authorisation, where the inspector has not decided within one month; the employer must reinstate the worker and pay wages from the dismissal date to reinstatement. [Para 192][Para 204][Para 350]

**Obiter dicta:** Union rights under Article 268 accrue on registration, yet the panel treated the elected leaders as protected while registration was still pending. [Para 138][Para 185]
