# Award No. 073/14 — Sing Chang Xin Garment Cambodia Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 5 May 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Tuon Si Phan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 139544

## 1. Facts
Sing Chang Xin Garment Cambodia Co., Ltd. sews garments with about 860 workers. [Para 54] Thirteen demands went to conciliation on 18 March 2014; three were settled at the hearing, two were withdrawn, and eight remained for decision. [Para 22][Para 25][Para 26][Para 27] The union's newly elected leadership had not yet been recognised by the Ministry, so the panel heard the case for three workers present at the hearing plus three who had transferred their rights to the federation. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58][Para 141]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must allow pregnant workers one paid day a month for antenatal checks, keeping wages and benefits intact? [Para 60][Para 142]
2. Whether the employer must pay wages and all allowances within 48 hours of a worker's resignation? [Para 67][Para 158]
3. Whether unused annual leave must be paid out each year on a twelve-month average wage, rather than the base wage? [Para 77][Para 167]
4. Whether the employer must comply with point 1 of the negotiation minutes of 23 January 2013 and pay severance because the owner changed? [Para 83][Para 190]
5. Whether the employer must raise piece rates (PO) in all sections following the minimum wage increase from USD 61 to USD 100 a month? [Para 94][Para 210]
6. Whether the employer must pay dismissal indemnity and moral damages to the three dismissed workers? [Para 100][Para 240]
7. Whether the employer must convert the inspection section from standing work to seated work? [Para 117][Para 261]
8. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of 4,000 riel a day and add 2,000 riel to the existing 4,500 riel overtime meal money? [Para 124][Para 288]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Pregnant women need a full day because the injections given at hospital leave them unwell; wages and severance should be paid within 48 hours of leaving; annual leave should be valued on the twelve-month average, not the base wage; the owner has changed hands, triggering severance; piece rates never rose when the minimum wage did; standing eight to twelve hours causes swollen legs and back pain; and base wages are too low to live on. [Para 62][Para 69][Para 78][Para 85][Para 95][Para 120][Para 129]
**Employer (respondent):** It already gives half a day for antenatal checks; it cannot pay within 48 hours because it is paid by buyers on the 7th to 9th and pays severance on the 12th; it denies any change of owner, calling the minutes an incomplete copy without a clear seal; piece rates are set by buyers; the inspection job requires standing and no worker has reported illness; and it cannot pay more lunch money. [Para 61][Para 64][Para 72][Para 92][Para 97][Para 121][Para 123]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay wages and allowances within 48 hours of resignation (issue 4) and to pay dismissal indemnity of thirty days' wages plus allowances and equal moral damages to each of the three dismissed workers (issue 10); every other claim was dismissed. [Para 166][Para 260][Para 304][Para 308]

On issue 4, Article 116(5) requires wages and all indemnities to be paid within forty-eight hours of the last working day; the panel followed Award 76/13 and earlier awards holding the same. [Para 158][Para 159][Para 160] The company's practice of paying on the 12th because buyers settle on the 7th to 9th was an internal cash-flow arrangement that could not displace the statutory deadline. [Para 163][Para 164][Para 165]

On issue 10, Article 89 fixes dismissal indemnity at fifteen days' wages and allowances per year of service, capped at six months, with a fraction of six months or more counting as a full year. [Para 240][Para 241][Para 242] Each of the three workers had between one year seven months and one year ten months of service, so each received thirty days; Article 91(1) and (3) then entitled them to moral damages equal to that indemnity. [Para 243][Para 244][Para 246][Para 255][Para 260]

The rest failed. On the antenatal claim, the company already gave half a day with pay, which was more favourable than the law, and no rule required a full day; the demand was an interest dispute brought by too few workers. [Para 142][Para 143][Para 145][Para 156] On annual leave, Article 167(3) forbids cash payment in lieu of leave while the contract is still running, and Article 167(4) requires leave to be carried forward, so the company's base-wage valuation stood. [Para 180][Para 181][Para 188][Para 189] On the minutes, the workers bore the burden of showing a change of owner and produced nothing beyond the minutes, so the claim failed. [Para 199][Para 204][Para 206][Para 209] On piece rates, no law or agreement linked PO rates to the minimum wage; the employer only owes a top-up to USD 100 where output falls short, which it does. [Para 231][Para 234][Para 235][Para 238][Para 239] On seating, the panel refused to convert the job but held that Prakas No. 053 requires adequate chairs near the workstation even for standing work, and that eight to twelve hours standing may affect health. [Para 262][Para 267][Para 285][Para 286] The meal-money claim was again an interest dispute and was declined. [Para 291][Para 299][Para 302]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Wages and all indemnities fall due within forty-eight hours of the last working day; an employer's own payroll cycle, driven by when buyers pay, is no answer to Article 116(5). [Para 158][Para 165] Annual leave may not be bought out while the employment contract subsists — Article 167(3) voids such payment and Article 167(4) requires leave to be carried forward — so an employer that values leave on the base wage commits no breach in doing so. [Para 180][Para 181][Para 188] And an employer must place adequate chairs near the workstation even where the job itself is performed standing. [Para 262][Para 267][Para 286]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's acceptance that eight to twelve hours of standing may in fact affect workers' health, while declining to order any change of posture, and its remarks that the company should encourage workers to carry leave forward rather than pay it out, were observations not reflected in the orders. [Para 187][Para 285]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award year is corrupted repeatedly: the conciliation minutes date reads "ថ្ងៃទី ២៣ ខែមករា ឆ្នាំ ២០១៩ ២០១៣" [Para 204] and Notification No. 041 is dated "ឆ្នាំ ២០១៦ ២០១១" [Para 295]. The company is named "ស៊ីង ឆាង ស៊ីន", "ស៊ីន ឆាង ស៊ីង" and "សឺង ឆាង សីន" in different places. [Para 1][Para 22][Para 50] The number of claimant workers is six at one point and three at another, and the union registration certificate is cited as No. 2224 in one place and as issued to a differently spelled entity elsewhere. [Para 37][Para 55][Para 138][Para 155]
