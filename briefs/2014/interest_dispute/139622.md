# Award No. 166/14 — Sin Fang (Cambodia) Garment Manufacturing Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 17 July 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed, dissenting in part; Tun Si Phan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 139622

## 1. Facts
Sin Fang (Cambodia) Garment Manufacturing Co., Ltd cuts and sews garments under patent DLT.0424.2014 and employs about 515 workers. [Para 76][Para 77] The CCAWDU confederation held an election at the factory on 26 April 2014 and its slate — Ms C as president, Ms M as vice-president, Ms H as secretary — was notified to the employer by letter received on 2 May 2014; the union was not yet registered. [Para 189][Para 190][Para 191][Para 193] Of twelve demands, two were settled in the arbitration stage, leaving five; 213 workers (208 by transfer-of-rights letters plus 5 present at the hearing) were claimants, well under half the workforce. [Para 26][Para 28][Para 29][Para 81] Ms C, a Chinese-language interpreter in the sewing section earning $340 a month, was suspended on 8 May and dismissed on 30 May 2014 without the labour inspector's authorisation. [Para 133][Para 134][Para 142][Para 195]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay 2,000 riel per day as lunch money? [Para 83]
2. Whether the employer must pay $15 per month for travel and accommodation, that is $5 above the $10 already paid? [Para 95][Para 213]
3. Whether the employer must deduct the attendance bonus pro rata to the days a worker is absent with the employer's permission, rather than withholding the whole $10? [Para 102][Para 222]
4. Whether the employer must pay $3 per day as a meal allowance to workers who work on public holidays? [Para 115][Para 262]
5. Whether the employer must take back Ms C and pay her back wages from dismissal to return? [Para 282]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Wages are too low to buy adequate food and prices have risen; other factories give a meal or 2,000 to 3,200 riel a day; rents rose from $25 to $40–50 and transport from $10 to $15; the attendance bonus is part of wages, so only the days actually taken should be deducted; working a holiday costs more for food because no vendors are outside; and Ms C was dismissed for union activity, with the company coercing workers to thumbprint resignations from the rival union. [Para 85][Para 89][Para 97][Para 106][Para 124][Para 174][Para 176][Para 181]

**Employer (respondent):** It cannot pay lunch money — 2,000 riel is small for a worker but large across all staff — nor $3 for holidays; it pays $10 travel allowance, more than the $7 Notice 230 requires and already raised from $7 in January 2014; it denies anti-union discrimination and, at the hearing, declined to argue Ms C's fault further while offering to pay statutory severance. [Para 92][Para 100][Para 131][Para 187][Para 188][Para 215]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — points 1, 2 and 4 dismissed; on point 3 the employer ordered to deduct the attendance bonus pro rata to permissioned days of absence; on point 5 ordered to reinstate Ms C and pay her back wages. Point 3 was decided 2–1, Ing Sothy dissenting. [Para 334][Para 335][Para 336][Para 337][Para 338]

**Point 1 (lunch).** No law, collective agreement or practice obliged the company to pay 2,000 riel a day, so the demand was an interest dispute; the panel decides those only where the union has more than half the workforce as members, and 213 of 515 is not that. [Para 197][Para 198][Para 210][Para 212]

**Point 2 (travel).** Point 1 of Notice No. 230 requires $7 a month and the employer pays $10, so it has already exceeded the legal duty; the extra $5 was again an interest dispute outside the union's standing. [Para 213][Para 215][Para 219][Para 221]

**Point 3 (attendance bonus).** The panel found point 2 of Notice No. 230 — $10 for workers who come regularly "without absence" — ambiguous, since neither the Labour Advisory Committee nor any competent body has said whether "absence" covers authorised absence, and it declined to assume that it does. [Para 224][Para 230][Para 231] Statutory absences (annual, special, maternity, public holiday, weekly rest) require no deduction. [Para 232][Para 233] For absence with the employer's permission, Articles 71(6) and 72 of the Labour Law suspend the contract, so no wage is owed and no bonus accrues for those days; but the employer may deduct only the pro-rata amount, not the whole $10. [Para 254][Para 255][Para 258][Para 259]

**Point 4 (holiday meal).** Article 164 and Prakas No. 10 of 1999 entitle a worker who works a public holiday to one day's ordinary wage as compensation, not to a meal allowance; holiday work is not overtime, and the employer already pays 2,000 riel a day, which is better than the law requires. [Para 267][Para 269][Para 271][Para 276] The extra $1 was an interest dispute the union could not bring. [Para 277][Para 281]

**Point 5 (reinstatement).** Article 293 requires the labour inspector's agreement before a staff delegate is dismissed, and Article 3 (chapter 3) of Prakas No. 305 protects candidates for union office for 45 days before and after an election. [Para 283][Para 286] Following case 40/10-Meng Yan and 154/13-Boeung Khieu, the panel held that protection of an elected leader runs from the election until the union is registered, since without it the leader can be dismissed before registration exists. [Para 296][Para 298][Para 305] Ms C was elected on 26 April, the employer knew of the slate from 2 May, and she was dismissed on 30 May within that window, without the inspector's authorisation. [Para 313][Para 317][Para 321][Para 324] Under Article 4 (chapter 4) of Prakas No. 305 the dismissal was void, and Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 allowed reinstatement with back pay. [Para 330][Para 331][Para 332][Para 333]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker elected as a union leader is protected from dismissal from the day of the election until the union obtains registration, not merely for 45 days; a dismissal inside that window without the labour inspector's prior authorisation is void and the panel will order reinstatement with back wages. [Para 298][Para 305][Para 331]

**Obiter dicta:** Ing Sothy's dissent on point 3 — that a bonus is earned only on fulfilling the conditions its owner sets, so a worker who does not work the full month forfeits it, and that pro-rating rewrites Notice No. 230. [Para 349][Para 357][Para 361]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is 2–1 on point 3 and the dissent is appended, so section 5's first ratio rests on a majority only; the panel composition in the header records the dissent. [Para 342] Stray years and figures interrupt the text ("២០១៥" at Paras 269 and 299, "២០១៉៩" at Para 329), and the Article 230 quotation at Para 222 is partly replaced by untranslated OCR noise. [Para 222] Ms C is printed as ឆេន សុវណ្ណា throughout and is rendered as Ms C; the demand list names her in full where the panel does, but a companion award in this batch gives her surname differently. [Para 133][Para 282]
