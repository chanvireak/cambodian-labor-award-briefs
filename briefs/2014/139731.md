# Case No. 261/14 — Center World Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 17 October 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Hem Hunarith for the employer, Liv Sovanna for the workers, Sok Mathoeung chair) · Doc ID 139731

## 1. Facts
Center World Garment Co., Ltd (សេនធ័រ វើលដ៍ ហ្គាម៉ិន អិលធីឌី) is a garment factory employing about 350 workers. [Para 34][Para 35] The Cambodian Workers' Spirit Federation, acting for 74 workers who transferred their rights to it by letter of 12 August 2014, raised 14 demands; conciliation on 09 September 2014 settled 12 and left two unresolved. [Para 12][Para 36] The two remaining claims are a rise in the monthly attendance bonus from USD 10 to USD 15 and a rise in the monthly travel and lodging allowance from USD 7 to USD 15. [Para 39][Para 45] The local union of that federation had not yet been registered with the Ministry of Labour. [Para 37]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to raise the regular attendance bonus by USD 5, on top of the existing USD 10, for a total of USD 15 per month? [Para 50]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to raise the travel and lodging allowance by USD 8, on top of the existing USD 7, for a total of USD 15 per month? [Para 72]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** market prices for water, electricity and rent have risen, and factories near Center World Garment pay attendance bonuses of USD 12 to USD 15 and travel allowances of USD 10 to USD 15 per month, so the employer should match them. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 47]
**Employer (respondent):** the company's finances are weak for lack of orders and it cannot meet the claims; it already applies Ministry of Labour Notification No. 230 and pays benefits better than the law requires — Riels 2,000 for lunch, Riels 3,000 for the overtime meal (where the law requires only Riels 2,000), USD 50 for maternity, USD 50 for a family bereavement and a USD 20 speed-sewing bonus. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 48][Para 49]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider both claims for want of representativity and ordered no payment. [Para 71][Para 80][Para 81][Para 82]

On issue 1 the panel took point 2 of Notification No. 230 K.B/S.J.N of the Ministry of Labour and Vocational Training, dated 25 July 2012, which guarantees workers who attend every working day in the month without absence an attendance bonus of at least USD 10 per month. [Para 50] It found the employer in fact paid USD 10, so the practice complied, and that no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, employment contract, agreement between the parties or past practice obliged the employer to pay USD 15. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53] The claim was therefore a demand for a benefit above what the law fixes, that is an interest dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍). [Para 54][Para 55]

For interest disputes the panel applied Article 96 of the 1997 Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001, under which the most representative union has the right to request a collective agreement and the employer is then obliged to bargain with it; the panel said it consistently examines the representativity of the union before it, because that status is what confers the legal right to bring an interest dispute. [Para 56][Para 59][Para 61] It also relied on Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY of 21 April 2004: an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement and has effect for one year, and the workers lose the right to strike over such interest claims in future — so only a union representing more than half the workers may invoke the procedure. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 64] It followed Awards 81/04 (Evergreen) and 98/04 (Great Union), and Awards 169/11, 228/13 and 172/14, which refused to hear interest disputes where the claimant was not the most representative union. [Para 65][Para 67][Para 68] Here the claimant represented 74 of some 350 workers and its local union was unregistered, so it was not the most representative union and lacked standing. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 69][Para 70]

On issue 2 the panel repeated the analysis: point 1 of the same Notification No. 230 fixes the travel and lodging allowance at USD 7 per month, the employer paid USD 7, and no law, collective agreement, internal rule or practice required USD 15. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74][Para 75] The claim was again an interest dispute and was dismissed for the same lack of representativity. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 78][Para 79][Para 80]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a claim seeks a benefit above what the Labour Law, a collective agreement, internal rules, the contract or established practice already provides, it is an interest dispute, and the panel will decline to consider it unless the claimant union is the most representative union in the enterprise — in practice representing more than half the workforce. The reason is that an award on an interest dispute takes the place of a collective agreement for one year and extinguishes the workers' right to strike on those claims, so the power to invoke it belongs only to the union that could conclude such an agreement. [Para 61][Para 63][Para 64][Para 69]

**Obiter dicta:** the findings that the USD 10 attendance bonus and USD 7 travel allowance match Notification No. 230, and the listing of the employer's above-statutory benefits, were not necessary to the outcome, which rested on standing. [Para 44][Para 52][Para 74]
