# Award No. 102/14 — Kin Tai Garment Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 5 June 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Liv Sovannara, worker-appointed; Ang Eng Thong, president) · Doc ID 139566

## 1. Facts
A garment factory in Phnom Penh employing about 800 workers, Kin Tai registered in 1997 and last paid a seniority bonus in 2005; from then until March 2014 it paid none. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 57] The Cambodia Union Confederation, one of three unions at the plant, applied for registration on 19 December 2013 and received no answer from the Ministry; 368 workers transferred their rights to it. [Para 46][Para 50][Para 51][Para 77] Conciliation on 21 April 2014 settled eight of ten demands, leaving the seniority bonus and an overtime meal allowance. [Para 13]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay back the seniority bonus (ប្រាក់រង្វាន់អតីតភាពការងារ) to workers with a year's service or more, from the completion of one year up to March 2014? [Para 54][Para 89]
2. Whether the company must raise the overtime meal allowance from 2,000 riel to 4,000 riel? [Para 66]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company has paid no seniority bonus since 2005 although Notification 041/11 requires it, and an overtime meal costing at least 4,000 riel is not covered by the present 2,000 riel. [Para 68][Para 70][Para 102]
**Employer (respondent):** the back-payment is a new demand not found in the conciliation record and no other union ever claimed it; the meal allowance already exceeds what the law requires, since the company also pays 1,200 riel towards lunch. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 72][Para 73]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to pay back seniority bonus to the union's members according to each worker's actual service, counted to March 2014 and limited to the preceding three years [Para 113][Para 132], and the meal-allowance claim was refused [Para 131][Para 135].

The panel first held the union competent to sue. Under Article 268(2) of the Labour Law, if the Ministry does not answer a registration application within two months the organisation is deemed registered; the application was filed on 19 December 2013 and the hearing was on 6 May 2014, well past that period, and the union's assertion that the Ministry had suspended registrations was unsupported by any official letter. [Para 76][Para 79][Para 81]

On jurisdiction, Article 312(1) confines the panel to the issues recorded in the non-settlement minutes, and the minutes do record the seniority-bonus demand, so the employer's objection that the claim was new failed. [Para 89][Para 93][Para 95] The demand was classified as a rights dispute, because Notification 041/11 of 7 March 2011 fixes the entitlement in a table rising from one to eleven US dollars a month with length of service. [Para 96][Para 98][Para 99]

On the merits, the company had breached that obligation from 2005 to March 2014. [Para 101][Para 102] Article 120 of the Labour Law, read with Article 103, bars a claim for wages — including sums paid as a bonus — more than three years after the pay date, so the workers could recover only for the three years preceding March 2014, and the panel worked a table showing how a worker with sixteen years' service recovers only thirty-six months at eleven dollars. [Para 104][Para 105][Para 108][Para 109][Para 111]

The meal allowance was treated differently: no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice obliged the company to pay 4,000 riel, so the demand was an interest dispute. [Para 117][Para 118] There Article 96 and Prakas 305 require the claimant union to hold most-representative status, and this union had no certificate to that effect, so the panel declined to consider the claim at all. [Para 123][Para 126][Para 129][Para 130]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union whose registration application has gone unanswered by the Ministry for more than two months is deemed registered and may bring a rights dispute in its own name, but a claim for unpaid monthly seniority bonus is time-barred for any period more than three years before the demand, and a union without most-representative status cannot put an interest dispute before the panel at all. [Para 81][Para 107][Para 130]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's discussion of how a most-representative award would displace the collective agreement for a year and would strip workers of the right to strike over the same interest demands is general reasoning, since the claim failed on standing before any such order could be made. [Para 125]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company is named Kin Tai Garment in the case title at [Para 1] but "Phin Tai Garment" in the party details two lines later, and both spellings recur throughout. [Para 1][Para 44] The panel records the company as registered in 1997 at [Para 44] but as having begun operations in 1998 at [Para 56]. [Para 44][Para 56] Article 120 is printed as "three 2 years" (បី ២ ឆ្នាំ), the numeral corrupted, while the reasoning applies a three-year limit throughout. [Para 104][Para 106] The date of the 2005 payment is printed as "ឆ្នាំ ២០១៥ ២០០៥" at [Para 100], leaving two years in the text. [Para 100] The award's own extension of its issue date is recorded inconsistently, from 26 May to 5 June 2014, with a stray "1" before the date. [Para 25] The union's membership appears as 368 transferred rights at [Para 51] and as "approximately 368 members" at [Para 129], while the consent list covers 328 and the membership applications 176. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 50] Pages 138 onwards are appendix fingerprint sheets reduced to unreadable OCR and were not relied on.
