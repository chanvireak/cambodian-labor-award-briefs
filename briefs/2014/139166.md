# Award No. 070/14 — Khen Theron E Pheril (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 2 April 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ingh Sothy (employer-appointed), An Vireak (worker-appointed), Run Saray (president) · Doc ID 139166

## 1. Facts
The Ken Theron factory union, affiliated to the Union Federation for Development of Workers, brought two demands for the roughly 1,300 workers of a garment company: a lunch allowance of USD 14 on top of the USD 6 already paid, making USD 20 a month, and an overtime meal allowance of 2,000 riel an hour, or 4,000 riel for two hours [Para 2][Para 4][Para 35]. The union held a registration certificate dated 13 October 2014 and claimed about 115 members [Para 37][Para 39]. Conciliation on 4 March 2015 failed on both points and report No. 264 Kb/Ak/Vk of 12 March 2015 came to the Council [Para 12][Para 13]. A hearing was held on 19 March 2015 at 8:30 am with both parties present [Para 10][Para 16], and the award issued on 2 April 2015 as a non-binding order [Para 84].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer was obliged to pay a lunch allowance totalling USD 20 a month, i.e. USD 14 above the USD 6 already paid? [Para 55]
2. Whether the employer was obliged to pay an overtime meal allowance of 2,000 riel an hour, or 4,000 riel for two hours of overtime? [Para 71]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** USD 6 does not buy a meal, prices are high, nearby factories pay USD 10–20 a month, and one neighbouring factory supplies a free lunch; 2,000 riel does not cover an evening meal, and neighbouring factories pay 1,500–2,000 riel an hour for overtime meals [Para 42][Para 50][Para 51].
**Employer (respondent):** The company has little work and cannot bear more cost; it has already added USD 28 above the base wage, and March output will not cover costs and wages [Para 43][Para 45]. On overtime meals it applies the law, and will pay whatever the law requires; it already gives a USD 132 base wage, USD 6 lunch money, USD 10 transport and accommodation, a USD 10 attendance bonus and milk for children [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider both demands, on each of which it found the claimant union not to be the most representative union in the enterprise [Para 70][Para 81][Para 82][Para 83].
On issue 1 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, no collective agreement, no internal rules and no past practice obliging the employer to pay a total of USD 20 a month for lunch; the USD 6 was the agreed practice [Para 56]. It treated the demand as exceeding what the law requires and therefore as an interest dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍) [Para 57][Para 58].
On issue 2 the panel applied point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 Kb/SJN of 7 March 2011: a worker who voluntarily works overtime at the employer's request receives 2,000 riel a day for meals or a free meal, without any hour threshold [Para 71][Para 72]. The employer paid 2,000 riel a day to the 20 workers who worked overtime at its request, which the panel found consistent with that notification [Para 73][Para 74]. No law, collective agreement, internal rule or practice obliged payment of 2,000 riel an hour or 4,000 riel for two hours, so this demand too exceeded the law and was an interest dispute [Para 75][Para 76][Para 77].
On both issues the panel then applied Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001: the most representative union may require the employer to negotiate a collective agreement, and the employer must negotiate with it [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61]. Because Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 makes an award on an interest dispute a collective agreement binding all workers for one year, the panel will only adjudicate an interest dispute where the claimant union is most representative — that is, where it or several unions together hold over half the enterprise's workers [Para 62][Para 63][Para 64]. It followed its prior awards in Case No. 169/11, 81/04, 98/04, 02/11 and 66/11 to the same effect [Para 64][Para 65][Para 66]. It found the claimant was not the most representative union [Para 68][Para 69][Para 79][Para 80].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The Council will decline to adjudicate an interest dispute where the union bringing it is not the most representative union in the enterprise, since an award on such a dispute would replace the collective agreement and bind every worker for a year [Para 63][Para 64][Para 69]. A demand for a benefit above what the Labour Law, a collective agreement, internal rules or established practice requires is an interest dispute, not a rights dispute [Para 57][Para 76].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel recorded the employer's other benefits — USD 132 base wage, USD 6 lunch, USD 10 transport and accommodation, USD 10 attendance bonus and milk for children — without relying on them [Para 54].
