# Award No. 355/14 — Chembo Chothab (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 29 December 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3 — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Vireak (worker-appointed), Pen Bunchea (chair) · Doc ID 139794

## 1. Facts
The company operates at Kantoek Khang Cheung, Dangkao, Phnom Penh. [Para 1] Workers struck from 17 December 2014, led by the Labour Movement Union, and the company asked the Council on 20 December for an order ending the strike. [Para 17][Para 21] The panel issued Order 068/14 on 23 December requiring immediate suspension of the strike and return to work by shifts that afternoon; the strike continued. [Para 22][Para 23] At a pre-hearing meeting on 25 December the workers undertook to stop and return on 26 December, then by telephone that morning said the strike was continuing, larger than before and marching on the National Assembly. [Para 26][Para 27] No hearing was ever held. [Para 34]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must take back five union leaders — Mr R Makara, Mr Am Leap, Mr P Pheara, Mr K Vey and Mr Dy Saluch — and let them work normally? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company must deduct union dues from members of the Labour Movement Union? [Para 4]
3. Whether the company must add USD 1 to the midday meal allowance? [Para 5]
4. Whether the company must add 2,000 riel to overtime pay? [Para 6]
5. Whether the company must calculate the attendance bonus by day? [Para 7]
6. Whether the company must pay a meal allowance on public holidays? [Para 8]
7. Whether the company must refrain from interfering in union affairs? [Para 9]
8. Whether the company must reduce production quotas? [Para 10]
9. Whether the company must resume paying the midday meal and overtime meal allowance weekly, as before? [Para 11]
And, as a threshold question, whether the arbitration should proceed at all. [Para 35]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The five union leaders should be reinstated to normal work, and the company should stop interfering in union affairs; the remaining claims are for higher meal and overtime allowances, daily calculation of the attendance bonus, holiday meal pay, lower quotas and weekly payment of meal money. [Para 2][Para 9][Para 11][Para 5][Para 10]
**Employer (respondent):** The five cannot be taken back because they acted against the spirit of Article 69, committed serious misconduct under Article 83, and the company filed documents with the Ministry of Labour on 16 December 2014 to proceed under the legal procedure. [Para 3]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** proceedings terminated — the panel closed the arbitration without adjudicating any of the nine claims. [Para 51][Para 52]

The panel recorded that it made no findings of fact on the disputed issues, because it never held a hearing. [Para 34] Its single question was whether the arbitration should continue. [Para 35] It read Article 20 of Prakas 099/04, under which the parties must suspend all dispute measures — strike or lockout, and any other measure that could aggravate the dispute — for the duration of the arbitration, and must attend every meeting to which the panel summons them. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 37] Earlier awards, it said, have applied that rule by refusing to continue whenever workers are striking or an employer has locked out. [Para 38][Para 39]

The panel relied in particular on Notification No. 2 of 29 January 2004 in case 04/04, which held that a party to a labour dispute has no right to strike while arbitration is running and that the panel cannot settle or issue an award on a collective dispute referred to it in those circumstances, but may take the case up again once the strike ends. [Para 40][Para 41] It cited similar termination orders in the hotel cases of 2004, in Francophonie Thieng, Simano, International Fashion 2015 Royal and Kallisto E Pheril. [Para 42][Para 44][Para 45][Para 46]

Applying that to the facts, the panel found that after Order 068/14 the workers neither stopped the strike nor returned to work, that they repeated the undertaking on 25 December and broke it the next morning, and that the strike had grown. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50] It therefore terminated the proceedings. [Para 51]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where workers continue a strike after the panel has ordered its suspension under Article 20 of Prakas 099/04, the panel will terminate the arbitration instead of hearing the case, leaving every claim undetermined; the same applies to an employer that maintains a lockout, and the panel may resume if the dispute measures end. [Para 37][Para 40][Para 51]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observation that the Union had undertaken on 25 December to return to work and then reversed itself the following morning, and its remark that it may take the case up again once the strike stops. [Para 41][Para 49]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award decides nothing on the merits: no hearing was held, no evidence was taken from the workers at all, and the nine claims are left unresolved, so it cannot be cited for any proposition about reinstatement, dues, allowances or quotas. [Para 29][Para 34] The employer's two pre-hearing representatives are listed as a legal officer and a CSR director, the second under a Latin-script name only. [Para 1] Termination dates in the cited precedents are inconsistent — 23 December 2014 in Order 068/14, and a 2005 order dated 24 December 2005 but referred to as 2016. [Para 22][Para 44] Paragraph 27 carries OCR debris that obscures part of the account of the 26 December telephone call.
