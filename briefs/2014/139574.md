# Award No. 119/14 — Lan Globol Tech Sty (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 21 May 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Aing Eng Tong chair) · Doc ID 139574

## 1. Facts
Lan Globol Tech Sty (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a garment factory at Prey Trala, Chom Chao, Porsenchey, Phnom Penh. [Para 1] A strike began on 08 May 2014 over nine demands; seven were settled at conciliation and two were referred to the panel, the union's remaining demand being that the employer preserve wages for the strike days together with the attendance bonus and other benefits. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 15][Para 2] On 12 May 2014, at the company's request, the panel issued Order No. 026/14 directing an immediate suspension of the strike and a return to work from the afternoon of 16 May 2014 until the arbitration was complete. [Para 17][Para 18] The strike continued, and at the pre-hearing meeting of 19 May 2014 both parties confirmed it was still running. [Para 19][Para 21]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must maintain wages for the two days of strike, the attendance bonus and the other benefits unchanged, as the union demanded? [Para 2][Para 3]
2. Whether the panel may continue the arbitration and decide that claim at all while the strike it ordered suspended is still being observed? [Para 30][Para 44]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company must pay wages for the two strike days; if it does not, the workers will not return to work and the seven points already agreed at conciliation are to be treated as unsettled. [Para 5]
**Employer (respondent):** it will preserve one day of strike wages and keep the attendance bonus and other benefits unchanged, but if workers do not report on 10 May 2014 it will apply Article 332 of the Labour Law; it asked the panel for an order that the workers return. [Para 3][Para 4][Para 17]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel terminated the arbitration without any award on the merits, so neither the strike-wage claim nor the second unsettled demand was decided. [Para 44][Para 45]

The panel framed the only question it would answer as whether the arbitration could proceed. [Para 30] Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 requires the parties, throughout the panel's proceedings, to suspend every dispute measure — strike, lockout under Article 318 of the Labour Law, or anything else that could aggravate the dispute — and to attend all meetings the panel convenes. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 32] The panel read that obligation as a condition of its own ability to act, and noted its settled practice of refusing to continue where workers are striking or an employer has locked out, citing 06/09, 39/08, 138/09, 110/11, 104/13 and 119/13. [Para 33][Para 34] It traced the same position through its notice of 29 January 2004 in 04/04-ISI, where it held it could neither resolve nor issue an award while a strike continued and could only resume once the strike stopped, and through the second orders in the hotel cases of 18/04 and in 77/05, which reserved the right to close the file if a party breached the order. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 37][Para 39] It also relied on 110/11-Simanor and 17/13-Internal Fashion Royal, where proceedings were discontinued for the same reason. [Para 40]

Applying that to the facts, the panel recorded that it had ordered the strike suspended on 12 May 2014, that the workers did not comply, and that both parties admitted at the 19 May meeting that the strike continued. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 21][Para 43] It therefore closed the file. [Para 44] Because no hearing was ever held, the award contains no findings of fact on the two referred disputes. [Para 29]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where workers continue a strike after the panel has ordered it suspended, the panel will terminate the arbitration and issue no award on the merits: under Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 the suspension of strikes, lockouts and other dispute measures during arbitration is a precondition of the panel's power to decide, not a procedural formality, so a party that keeps striking removes the basis on which its claim can be heard. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 44]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's survey of its earlier awards — 04/04-ISI, 18/04, 77/05, 110/11 and 17/13 — is illustrative of consistent practice rather than a source of the rule, which rests on Article 20 alone. [Para 35][Para 40]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award decides nothing on the merits: no hearing was held and the panel expressly recorded that it made no findings on the referred disputes [Para 29][Para 44]. Nothing here should be read as a ruling on the strike-wage claim.
- The conciliation report is said to refer two unsettled points, but only the strike-wage demand is described in the source; the second point is not identified anywhere in the file [Para 15][Para 2].
- The demand is printed as preserving "wages for 40 days" together with wages for the two strike days [Para 2], a figure that sits oddly with a strike that began on 08 May 2014; the intended period cannot be recovered, and the brief does not adopt the 40-day figure.
- The company name appears as "Lan Globol", "Lan Koubol", "Lan Knu Bol" and "Lan Globol Tech Sty" across the file [Para 1][Para 9][Para 17][Para 22]; the brief uses the form on the face of the award.
- A strike start date of 08 May 2014 [Para 17] sits against the employer's reference to workers not reporting on 10 May 2014 [Para 4] and the panel's order of 12 May [Para 18]; the sequence is not narrated.
- The signature block is largely destroyed by OCR — "monional . The states , and" before the employer-party arbitrator's name and a run of untranslatable fragments after it [Para 46].
- Folio markers and blank sentences interrupt the text at [Para 11], [Para 28] and [Para 45].
