# Award No. 352/14 — Y Olette E Pheril (Cambodia) Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 20 January 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3 — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Vireak (worker-appointed), Run Saray (chair) · Doc ID 139792

## 1. Facts
The company is a garment factory at Trapeang Chhuk, Sen Sok, Phnom Penh, employing about 1,400 workers. [Para 42] Of seven claims filed in November 2014, six remained unsettled; at the hearing the uniform claim (issue 4) settled, leaving five for adjudication. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 25] The five concern seniority pay for January to June 2014, the repayment of severance paid in December 2013, seniority pay for piece-rate sewers, an extra meal allowance on overtime, and maternity leave of 100 rather than 90 days. [Para 47][Para 58][Para 73][Para 84][Para 90] The first three all turn on compliance with Award 043/14 of 4 April 2014 between the same parties. [Para 48]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay seniority pay for January to June 2014 according to each worker's years of service? [Para 47]
2. Whether the workers may repay the severance they received in instalments, on the ground that the payment was not lawful? [Para 58]
3. Whether the employer must state seniority pay separately from the basic wage for piece-rate workers? [Para 73]
5. Whether the employer must add 1,000 riel to the overtime meal allowance, bringing the daily total to 4,000 riel? [Para 123]
6. Whether the employer must grant 100 days of maternity leave instead of 90? [Para 142]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Seniority pay was withheld from January to June 2014 and should be restored, and the severance taken in December 2013 should be repayable by instalments, as the employer allowed 49 other workers to repay at USD 25 a month, which is discriminatory. [Para 47][Para 59][Para 60][Para 61] Seniority pay should again be shown outside the basic wage for piece-rate sewers, who earn little, and the overtime meal allowance is only 3,000 riel. [Para 74][Para 76][Para 84] Maternity leave of 90 days is too short for women in poor health. [Para 91]
**Employer (respondent):** The workers have not completed the service the law requires before seniority pay accrues, and all prior contracts ended in December 2013 with seniority paid to everyone. [Para 55][Para 56] The 49 workers who repaid were those who refused six-month contracts, so there is no discrimination; the seven claimants raised nothing at the time of the change. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 113] On meals, the company already pays 3,000 riel on overtime days, above the legal 2,000, and 2,000 riel on normal days; on maternity it simply applies the law. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 93]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all five claims were refused, and the panel declined to consider them. [Para 149][Para 150][Para 151][Para 152][Para 153]

Issues 1 to 3 were treated together, because the parties agreed all three concerned implementation of Award 043/14. [Para 48][Para 58][Para 73] That award had ordered the employer to keep the workers on indefinite-duration contracts with their seniority preserved, and had ordered the workers to repay severance of USD 60 per year of service. [Para 48][Para 49] The panel found the workers had not repaid it. [Para 100] Reading Article 353 of the Civil Procedure Code, it held that only a court may order compulsory enforcement of an arbitral or mediation decision, so the panel itself has no procedural power to enforce its own award. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 104] Issues 1, 2 and 3 were therefore refused as outside its jurisdiction, not decided on the merits. [Para 105][Para 121]

On issue 5 the panel applied point 2 of Notification 041/11 of 7 March 2011, which requires 2,000 riel or a free meal on overtime days; the employer pays 3,000, which is more favourable. [Para 123][Para 124] No law, collective agreement, internal rule, practice or contract provided 4,000 riel, so the claim was an interest dispute. [Para 125][Para 126] Under Article 96 of the Labour Law and Prakas 305, only the most representative union may bring an interest dispute; 126 workers out of 1,400 was not a majority. [Para 130][Para 135][Para 139]

On issue 6 the panel noted Article 182 of the Labour Law gives 90 days, which the employer grants, and found no law or agreement providing 100 days. [Para 142][Para 143][Para 144] The claim was again an interest dispute and failed for the same lack of representativeness. [Para 145][Para 146]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The panel will not adjudicate a claim that in substance asks it to enforce an earlier award of its own: enforcement of an award lies with the court under Article 353 of the Civil Procedure Code, so a follow-on claim about the same contract conversion is refused for want of jurisdiction, however the merits are framed. [Para 102][Para 104] A claim for a benefit above the statutory floor is an interest dispute that only a union with more than half the workforce as members may bring. [Para 135][Para 139]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observation that the employer paying 3,000 riel rather than the 2,000 riel required by Notification 041/11 is "more favourable" than the rule, and its remark that the 49 workers who repaid were those who had refused six-month contracts. [Para 71][Para 124]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is headed 20 January 2015, and the parties had extended issuance from 13 to 20 January. [Para 28] The company name appears as Y Olette E Pheril, Y Olette E Phery, and Y Olette Ipheril across the face and the documents list. [Para 21][Para 42][Para 51] The number of claimants is built from inconsistent figures: 125 thumbprints on the letter, two who attended, and one attendee not named on it, giving 126. [Para 45][Para 46] Paragraph 25 records six unsettled claims and then says five will be considered, issue 4 having settled at the hearing; paragraph 90 states the company gives 90 days of maternity leave, while the workers pleaded 100. [Para 25][Para 90] Paragraph 99 is largely OCR noise.
