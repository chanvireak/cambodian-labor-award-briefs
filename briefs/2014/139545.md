# Award No. 071/14 — Gatha International Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 30 April 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Kong Phalla (president), You Soun Ty (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed) · Doc ID 139545

## 1. Facts
Gatha International Co., Ltd. ("Gatha") cuts and sews garments and tents in Kong Pisei district, Kampong Speu, and employed about 450 workers at the hearing. [Para 1][Para 60] Workers struck from 14 March 2014 behind eighteen demands; provincial conciliation failed and the file reached the Council on 20 March 2014, with a hearing on 7 April 2014. [Para 25][Para 29][Para 34] At the hearing twelve of the eighteen points settled, leaving six: travel and accommodation allowance (3), probationary minimum wage (4), pro-rating of the attendance bonus (5), milk allowance (10), rubbish bins (15) and a daycare centre (18). [Para 37][Para 38]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must add USD 5 to the existing USD 10 monthly travel and accommodation allowance, bringing it to USD 15? [Para 70]
2. Whether the employer must add USD 5 to the USD 95 monthly probationary wage, bringing it to USD 100? [Para 78]
3. Whether the employer must pro-rate the regular attendance bonus to the days of absence it has authorised, instead of deducting a flat sum? [Para 85]
4. Whether the employer must pay a USD 20 monthly milk allowance to mothers from birth to 18 months? [Para 91]
5. Whether the employer must install rubbish bins in every building? [Para 102]
6. Whether the employer must build a daycare centre (ទារកដ្ឋាន) for workers' children? [Para 110]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Rents and transport costs rose before the 2014 minimum wage increase, so the USD 10 allowance no longer meets real costs; a probationer cannot live on USD 95; deducting up to the whole USD 12 bonus for authorised leave is unjust; other factories supply bins; Article 186 obliges a daycare. [Para 72][Para 81][Para 88][Para 106][Para 116]
**Employer (respondent):** It already pays USD 10 where Ministry Notification 230 requires USD 7, and USD 95 to probationers exactly as Prakas 317 requires; a collective agreement with another registered union fixes milk money at USD 8; only one of its two buildings operates; it lacks the means to build a daycare. [Para 74][Para 83][Para 94][Para 107][Para 117]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer is ordered to pro-rate the regular attendance bonus to the days of authorised absence (binding immediately, by agreement of the parties on 7 April 2014) and to establish a daycare centre in the company or, failing that, to reimburse external daycare fees against actual invoices; claims 3, 4, 10 and 15 are refused. [Para 246][Para 247][Para 249][Para 252]

The panel first cleared standing. The Gatha workers' union held no registration certificate, so under Article 268 it could not yet represent its members. [Para 125][Para 131] But 234 workers had transferred representation to the Federation by a thumbprinted letter of 15 March 2014 naming no individuals, and the panel accepted that letter as proof of identity because the employer had not contested it; the two handwritten additions to it were rejected. [Para 146][Para 148] The panel then classified each surviving claim as a rights or an interests dispute: interests disputes are decided by equity under the second paragraph of Article 312 and Article 43 of Prakas 099, but only a most-representative union, holding more than half the workers, may bring one, and 234 of 450 fell short. [Para 122][Para 162][Para 165]

On issue 3 the panel found an interests dispute: Notification 230 requires USD 7 and the employer pays USD 10, a practice more favourable than the statutory floor, and no instrument obliged it to pay USD 15. [Para 151][Para 152] Issue 4 followed the same route: Prakas 317 and Instruction 001/14 set USD 95 for probationers from 1 February 2014, the employer paid USD 95, and the claim for USD 100 was refused. [Para 171][Para 173] Issue 5 was treated as a rights dispute, since the bonus is fixed by Notification 230. Because Article 103 counts bonuses as wages, and Articles 71(6) and 72(1) suspend the wage obligation during authorised absence, the employer may deduct the bonus but only in proportion to the days absent, not in full. [Para 212][Para 216][Para 218] Issue 10 was an interests dispute: the collective agreement of 15 March 2014 with the Khmer Workers' Rights Federation fixes USD 8, and nothing required USD 20 or displaced that agreement. [Para 94][Para 221] Issue 15 was an interests dispute with no statutory or contractual basis. [Para 226][Para 227] Issue 18 was a rights dispute: Article 186 requires any enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a nursing room and a daycare centre, the company had over 100 women workers, and none had been provided; the duty could be met by paying external daycare invoices. [Para 230][Para 241][Para 244]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a demand asks the employer to pay more than the floor fixed by a Ministry notification or Prakas, the panel classifies it as an interests dispute and will decide it by equity only if the union seizing it represents more than half the workforce; and because a regular attendance bonus is wages under Article 103, an employer may withhold it for authorised absence only in proportion to the days absent. [Para 162][Para 218]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel recorded that it ordinarily follows its earlier awards unless a new legal basis appears, that the added words "without absence" in Notification 230 supplied such a basis on the bonus question, and that it nonetheless declined to depart from those earlier awards. [Para 285][Para 286] President Kong Phalla appended a dissent reasoning that the company's own USD 4/8/12 deduction scale was more favourable than the notification and so lawful. [Para 254][Para 280]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
A nineteenth demand — administrative staff leaving the company — appears only as a handwritten addition to the transfer letter and not in the non-conciliation report, so the panel held it outside its jurisdiction; the award cites it as issue 19 in one place and describes it inconsistently elsewhere. [Para 67][Para 69][Para 144] The probationary wage is printed as "៩៥៥៦០ ដុល្លារ" where USD 95 is meant. [Para 172] The milk allowance is printed as USD 8 in the reasons and as USD 80 in the quoted contract clause. [Para 94][Para 96] The attendance bonus is USD 10 under the notification but USD 12 in the company practice described at the hearing. [Para 86][Para 181] The non-conciliation report number appears both as 339/14 and 339/QE 14. [Para 22][Para 54] Worker representatives are reduced to initials.
