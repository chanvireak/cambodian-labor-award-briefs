# Award No. 169/14 — Diamond (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 July 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, selected by the employer; An Non, selected by the workers; Kong Phallaph, chair) · Doc ID 139627

## 1. Facts
Diamond (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a garment factory in Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, employing 872 workers. [Para 48] The Democratic Union of Cambodian Garment Workers Federation filed 14 demands on 26 May 2014; conciliation settled 10, one more was settled at the hearing, and the panel decided the remaining three: 1, 3 and 4. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 20][Para 21] The factory-level union was formed by election on 14 March 2014 but had not been registered with, or issued a certificate by, the Ministry of Labour, and 32 workers transferred their authority to the federation on 21 May 2014. [Para 38][Para 49][Para 51][Para 87] The case turns on a Khmer New Year party the company stopped holding, a US$10 payment for minor workplace injuries, and the non-renewal of a union adviser's one-year contract. [Para 53][Para 66][Para 76]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must hold a Khmer New Year party for the workers, or pay each of them US$17 in place of it, as in 2013? [Para 53][Para 102]
2. Whether the employer must pay US$10 to each of the eight workers who suffered minor workplace injuries that did not require hospital treatment? [Para 66][Para 138]
3. Whether the employer must take back Mr O, worker number 3770, and pay his wages from the date of termination until reinstatement, and cease anti-union discrimination? [Para 76][Para 162]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company held a New Year party every year from 2003 to 2011, gave US$13 in 2012 and US$17 in 2013, and in early 2014 told the staff delegates it would hold a party around May; it paid US$10 to any worker pricked by a needle during working hours, on production of a slip signed by the head of administration; Mr O was never warned or disciplined and was not renewed because he had been elected a union adviser. [Para 57][Para 68][Para 80]
**Employer (respondent):** the party was always conditional on the company having a profit, orders have collapsed and there is a financial crisis, so no party can be held; the company is registered with the National Social Security Fund and pays the occupational-risk contribution, and the Fund settles treatment costs and allowances for injuries; it lawfully declined to renew a fixed-term contract that had run its term, gave notice on 2 May 2014 and paid the 5% severance, and it renewed another union adviser, Ms K, in the same section. [Para 60][Para 63][Para 72][Para 73][Para 79][Para 83]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three claims refused; no order was made against the employer. [Para 192][Para 193][Para 194]

Before the merits the panel dealt with standing. Because the factory union was never registered, it had no legal personality and could not represent anyone; under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 only a worker present in person, or a person holding written authority, may appear, so the panel confined itself to the 32 workers who had transferred authority and to Mr S, who attended. [Para 90][Para 95][Para 96][Para 97][Para 101]

On the party, the panel applied Article 2 of the Labour Law: the employer's right to manage the enterprise covers the grant of discretionary bonuses, confirmed in Award No. 24/05 Watt World. [Para 102][Para 105][Para 107] It found the US$17 or the party was an incentive tied to annual profit, given in 2012 and 2013 in place of the earlier party, and not a duty owed every year. [Para 108][Para 112][Para 113][Para 115] Having classified the claim as an interest dispute, the panel held under Article 96, Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 that only a most-representative union may bring such a claim, and 33 individual workers are not one; the claim was refused. [Para 123][Para 128][Para 130][Para 133][Para 136][Para 137]

On the US$10 injury payment the panel set out Articles 248, 249, 256 and 257 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 022/08 and No. 108 on social-security registration and occupational-risk contributions: the employer must answer for workplace accidents, but the compensation regime is that of the National Social Security Fund, and an enterprise with eight or more workers must register and contribute. [Para 138][Para 143][Para 146][Para 151] The employer had registered and contributed and referred injuries to the Fund, so it had discharged its duty; no law required an extra US$10 and no written practice existed, so the claim exceeded the law and was refused as an interest dispute. [Para 153][Para 156][Para 158][Para 161]

On Mr O the panel applied Article 73(1) of the Labour Law — a fixed-term contract ends by its own terms at the stated date — and Article 73(5), which requires 15 days' notice where the contract exceeds one year. [Para 162][Para 165][Para 166] The contract ran from 3 June 2013 to 3 June 2014, notice was given on 2 May 2014, and the 5% severance under Article 73(6) was paid, so the termination was lawful. [Para 168][Para 169][Para 172][Para 173][Para 174] On discrimination, Articles 12 and 279 forbid using union membership in decisions on discipline or termination; the party alleging it bears the burden of proof, and the panel found that Ms K, also an elected union adviser in the same section, had her contract renewed, that the non-renewal followed a general reduction in staff for lack of orders, and that no concrete evidence of discrimination was produced. [Para 175][Para 176][Para 181][Para 186][Para 187][Para 189][Para 190]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An interest dispute — a demand for a benefit beyond what statute, agreement or established practice already gives — cannot be brought by a group of individual workers however numerous; only a union with most-representative status in the enterprise, or several unions together holding over half the workforce as members, may put such a claim to the Council, and a factory union that has not been registered has no standing to represent anyone. [Para 95][Para 130][Para 133]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that an employer's discretionary bonus scheme does not breach the Labour Law and does not infringe any worker's rights, and that the employer must explain the basis of discretionary payments, are general observations rather than holdings, since the claims failed on standing and on the statutory scheme. [Para 115][Para 119]
