# Award No. 175/14 — Presi Den Food (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 23 July 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambor Na, selected by the employer; An Non, selected by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · Doc ID 139628

## 1. Facts
Presi Den Food (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a noodle factory in Dangkao, Phnom Penh, employing about 125 workers, registered in 2001 and re-registered in 2012. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 23] The Federation of Trade Unions for Workers filed eight demands; six were settled in conciliation on 24 June 2014 and two went to the Council: a midday meal allowance and the deduction of the attendance bonus during sick leave. [Para 13][Para 14] The factory union was formed by election on 23 March 2014 and announced to the employer, but was never registered with the Ministry of Labour; 99 workers transferred authority to the federation. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a midday meal allowance of 4,000 riel a day to the workers? [Para 44][Para 79]
2. Whether the employer must keep the entire attendance bonus intact when a worker takes sick leave supported by a proper medical certificate? [Para 56][Para 93]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** wages are too low to buy adequate food, and rent, water, electricity and transport have all risen; about 60 per cent of enterprises in the textile, garment and footwear sector give a midday meal or its cash equivalent daily, and the workers want the same; a worker cannot avoid falling ill, and the attendance bonus should be preserved where a medical certificate proves the illness. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47][Para 68][Para 70]
**Employer (respondent):** the company understands the hardship but cannot afford the allowance; it has just raised wages by US$20 a month to match the garment and footwear sector, and already gives a US$1 monthly dessert allowance, 3,000 riel for two hours' overtime and 4,000 riel beyond, a case of noodles at Pchum Ben, and a trip to Sihanoukville on 8 March; "regular" means attending all 26 working days, so absence forfeits part of the bonus, and full wages are still paid during sick leave. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 52][Para 53][Para 54][Para 71][Para 73]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both claims refused; no order was made against the employer. [Para 123][Para 124]

On claim 1 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal regulation, party agreement or past practice requiring a 4,000 riel daily meal allowance, and classified the demand as an interest dispute. [Para 80][Para 81] Applying Article 96, Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099, it held the Council may decide such a dispute only where the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise, or several unions together hold over half the workforce; the federation here represented 99 workers, its factory union was unregistered, and it was not most representative, so the claim was refused for want of standing. [Para 82][Para 85][Para 87][Para 90][Para 91][Para 92]

On claim 2 the panel first examined Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012, point 2 of which guarantees at least US$10 a month to workers who attend every working day without absence. [Para 93][Para 94] It found that the notification applies only to workers in the textile, garment and footwear sector and that this company, which makes noodles, falls outside it, so the notification gave no minimum. [Para 95][Para 96][Para 98] The attendance bonus was therefore a matter of the employer's managerial prerogative under Article 2 of the Labour Law, as explained in Awards No. 62/06 Khlick Su and No. 24/05 Watt World, and an employer may by unilateral decision establish bonus schemes beyond those the law requires. [Para 105][Para 108][Para 110][Para 113]

The panel accepted that the deduction scale — US$3 for one day's absence, US$6 for two, US$9 for three and the whole US$10 for four or more — turned on actual absence, and that a worker on sick leave is in fact absent, while the employer still pays wages for those days. [Para 100][Para 115][Para 116] It held the scheme lawful and reasonable, and that paying a full bonus to absent workers would discourage those who attend regularly; no worker may require an employer to change a practice that falls within its lawful managerial discretion. [Para 118][Para 119][Para 120][Para 121]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Ministry Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 fixes minimum meal and attendance-bonus entitlements only for workers in the textile, garment and footwear sector; an enterprise outside that sector — here a noodle factory — owes no such minimum, and the terms of any attendance bonus are left to the employer's lawful managerial discretion under Article 2 of the Labour Law, which the Council will not rewrite at a worker's demand. [Para 95][Para 98][Para 113]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that an employer ought to apply benefits to all its workers with fairness and equity, and that paying a full bonus to absent workers would demoralise those who attend, are observations on good practice rather than the ground of decision. [Para 118][Para 119]

- The number of settled points conflicts: six settled and two unresolved at [Para 13], but the employer's evidence states five agreed and three remaining out of eight at [Para 22].
- The evidence deadlines are set as 14 and 16 May 2014 at [Para 20], months before the 10 July 2014 hearing [Para 10], and before the conciliation of 24 June 2014 — the year is evidently wrong.
- The panel is constituted on "ថ្ងៃទី ០២ ខែកក្កដា ឆ្នាំ ២០១៩ ២០១៤" at [Para 15], the year printed twice and wrongly.
- Two different bonus scales appear: the company's actual deduction of US$3, US$6, US$9 and US$10 at [Para 58] and [Para 100]–[Para 103], and a graduated award of US$10, 7, 4, 1 and 0 quoted from a September 2012 notice at [Para 62]–[Para 66]. The panel reasoned only on the first.
- The bonus is printed as "១០០ ដុល្លារ" at [Para 99] and the wage rise as "២០" without a currency at [Para 50] and [Para 104]; the appeal period is given as "៣៨ ថ្ងៃ" instead of eight days at [Para 125].
- The conciliation report is numbered 701 consistently, but the complaint date is garbled at [Para 11], which repeats itself and names the federation twice under different descriptions.
