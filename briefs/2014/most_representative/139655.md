# Award No. 194/14 — Bou Khir Gameen Factory (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 20 August 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 139655

## 1. Facts
Bou Khir Gameen, a garment factory in Ang Snuol, Kandal, registered in 2012 and employing somewhat more than 2,000 workers, faced 14 demands raised by 403 workers who on 8 April 2014 assigned representation to the Cambodian Confederation of Unions (សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្ពុជា). [Para 62][Para 63][Para 65] Conciliation on 15 July 2014 settled 6 points, left 8 unsettled, and report No. 995/14 of 17 July 2014 sent those 8 to the panel. [Para 25][Para 26] At the hearing on 4 August 2014 two further points were settled and the workers withdrew points 5 and 7, leaving points 1, 2, 3 and 8. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 32] The panel found that the confederation had no local union registered with the Ministry of Labour at the factory. [Para 35][Para 64]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must abolish fixed-duration employment contracts and conclude undetermined-duration contracts with the workers? [Para 67][Para 101]
2. Whether the employer must raise the regular attendance bonus by USD 4, from the existing USD 11 to USD 15 per month? [Para 78][Para 131]
3. Whether the employer must raise the transport and housing allowance by USD 5, from the existing USD 10 to USD 15 per month? [Para 84][Para 147]
8. Whether the employer must pay the overtime meal allowance and the midday meal allowance every Monday, within working hours and no later than 4:00 pm? [Para 88][Para 158]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** three-month fixed-duration contracts are too short and expose workers to the risk of non-renewal in work that is continuous rather than seasonal, so undetermined-duration contracts are required; and the USD 15 attendance bonus and USD 15 transport allowance are justified because other factories such as Queen City Mansion pay USD 20 and Tai Yian USD 15 on the same eight-hour schedule, while the USD 10 transport allowance does not cover the distance some workers travel. [Para 72][Para 74][Para 80][Para 81][Para 86]
**Employer (respondent):** contracts run two months' probation then a three-month fixed term, with 5% severance paid at each expiry, and non-renewal affects only a very small number; it cannot raise the bonus or the allowance because its management and production differ from other factories, which have operated for many years, and it already pays above the legal minimum; on meal money it says too few accounts staff exist to pay before 4:00 pm. [Para 67][Para 69][Para 77][Para 82][Para 83][Para 87][Para 99]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 1 dismissed; issues 2 and 3 refused consideration; issue 8 granted, the employer ordered to pay the overtime meal allowance and the midday meal allowance every Monday within working hours and no later than 4:00 pm; no sum is printed for issues 1–3, the amounts in dispute being USD 11 versus USD 15 for the bonus and USD 10 versus USD 15 for transport. [Para 208][Para 209][Para 210][Para 211]
On issue 1 the panel relied on Article 65 of the Labour Law and Articles 664, 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code to hold that a contract is formed by matching offer and acceptance, so neither party can impose a contract type on the other; it followed award 56/06 (Borik) at issue 1 and held that the workers could not compel undetermined-duration contracts, nor the employer compel its own preferred type. [Para 101][Para 112][Para 114][Para 116][Para 128][Para 130]
On issues 2 and 3 the panel found the employer already paid USD 11 attendance bonus against the USD 10 minimum in Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 and USD 10 transport against the USD 7 minimum, so the claims exceeded what the law requires and were disputes of interest rather than rights. [Para 131][Para 132][Para 147][Para 148][Para 134][Para 150] Applying Article 96 and Prakas No. 305 clause 9, it held that only a union with most-representative status may bring an interest dispute, that the 403 claimants were not such a union, and that Prakas No. 099 clause 43 makes an award on interests operate as a collective agreement binding all workers — a consequence the panel would not attach to this claimant. [Para 139][Para 141][Para 144][Para 145][Para 146][Para 157] It followed awards 169/11, 02/11 (Pou Yien) and 66/11 (In Hansung) and 81/04, 98/04. [Para 142][Para 143]
On issue 8 the panel held that the meal allowances are not part of wages but a benefit under Article 103 enabling performance of the work, applied Article 115(3) (wages not payable on a rest day, and if paid on such a day then the day before) as read in award 143/08 (Cham Teak Ta Y), and Notification No. 041/11 clause 2 (2,000 riel or a free meal per overtime day) as read in award 110/09 (Buri I Ferrell). [Para 158][Para 160][Para 162][Para 163][Para 169] Because the employer paid only on the following Monday and after 6:00 pm, when workers who did no overtime had already finished at 4:00 pm under Article 137, the timing was not reasonable; the panel ordered payment during working hours by 4:00 pm. [Para 183][Para 200][Para 204][Para 206]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim that exceeds the minimum fixed by law or by a Ministry notification of minimum benefits is a dispute of interest, and the panel will refuse to consider it unless the claimant union holds most-representative status in the enterprise, because an award on interests displaces the collective agreement and binds every worker. [Para 135][Para 139][Para 141][Para 145]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that a party which agrees to a contract type must perform it until expiry unless both sides agree to amend, and that neither party may be forced into a contract form it did not accept. [Para 129][Para 112]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award states at one point that conciliation settled "6 points" of 14 and at another that 8 of 14 were settled, and paragraph 30 leaves the second settled point number blank ("point 4 and point"). [Para 25][Para 30] Issue 3's total is printed as "1500 dollars" in the claim summary and USD 15 in the reasoning and order. [Para 147][Para 149][Para 210] The claimant is called a confederation (សម្ព័ន្ធ) in some paragraphs and a union in others, and the employer appears as both "Bou Khir Gameen Factory" and "Bou Khir" without the registered suffix. [Para 35][Para 89][Para 62] Paragraphs 166 and 177 repeat the same sentence twice, and the signatures block prints arbitrator names twice over OCR noise. [Para 166][Para 213] The panel granted issue 8, an allowance above the legal minimum, although it had refused issues 2 and 3 on the ground that the claimant lacked most-representative status; the award does not reconcile the two. [Para 146][Para 206][Para 211]
