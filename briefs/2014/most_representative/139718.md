# Award No. 258/14 — Crystal Martin (Cambodia) Limited (Arbitration Council, 13 October 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom, Liv Sovanna, chair Ang Eng Thong) · Doc ID 139718

## 1. Facts
Crystal Martin (Cambodia) Limited, a manufacturer of women's trousers at Sre Chea, Samrong, Po Sen Chey, Phnom Penh, registered in 2011, employed about 312 workers on monthly wages of US$180 to US$190, or US$240 to US$270 with overtime. [Para 35] [Para 36] [Para 55] The Federation of Khmer Workers' Spirit Unions elected a local union leadership on 30 July 2014, but the local union had not yet obtained legal professional-organisation status and was still assembling its registration papers. [Para 37] Six demands went to conciliation on 4 September 2014; three were settled and three remained. [Para 14] [Para 15] The employer pays a lunch allowance of US$6 a month — about 920 riel a day — with the monthly wage, an attendance bonus of US$10 under Ministry of Labour Notification No. 230, and a travel and accommodation allowance of US$7. [Para 41] [Para 46] [Para 51] [Para 84] [Para 92] The award is non-binding. [Para 22] [Para 104]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of 4,000 riel a day instead of the US$6 a month now paid, and pay it weekly rather than monthly? [Para 41] [Para 56]
2. Whether the employer must raise the regular-attendance bonus by US$5, from US$10 to US$15 a month? [Para 46] [Para 84]
3. Whether the employer must raise the travel and accommodation allowance by US$8, from US$7 to US$15 a month? [Para 51] [Para 92]
4. Whether the claimant union is the most representative union in the enterprise, so that the panel may consider these interest demands at all? [Para 61] [Para 64]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that a lunch costing 3,000 to 4,000 riel a day cannot be bought on 920 riel, that other factories in the zone pay at least 2,000 riel and some 4,000 riel a day, naming Bery, Y-Olate, Smart Shirts, Hong Wa and Sing Fang; that weekly payment is needed to buy food; and that other factories pay attendance bonuses of US$15 to US$20 and higher travel allowances. [Para 43] [Para 44] [Para 48]
**Employer (respondent):** that it already pays more than the law requires — US$6 instead of the statutory lunch provision, US$10 as Notification No. 230 requires and US$7 for travel — that it cannot pay more, and that the timing of payment is its managerial decision. [Para 3] [Para 45] [Para 50] [Para 54] [Para 85] [Para 93]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider all three demands; no sum ordered, and the award is non-binding. [Para 100] [Para 101] [Para 102] [Para 103]

On each demand the panel first asked whether it was a rights or an interest dispute. It found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules or past practice obliging the employer to pay 4,000 riel a day, to raise the attendance bonus above the US$10 that Notification No. 230 fixes as a minimum, or to pay more than the US$7 travel allowance that the same notification sets, and it therefore classified all three as interest disputes. [Para 57] [Para 58] [Para 86] [Para 87] [Para 88] [Para 94] [Para 95] [Para 96]

That classification was decisive. Reading Article 96 of the Labour Law with Article 9 of Prakas No. 305, the panel held that only the most representative union may require an employer to conclude a collective agreement, and that under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 an interest award replaces a collective agreement and binds every worker in the enterprise for a year; it therefore follows that only a most representative union may bring an interest dispute, as it held in awards No. 169/11, 02/11 and 66/11. [Para 59] [Para 60] [Para 61] [Para 62] [Para 63] [Para 65] [Para 66] On the figures the claimant fell short: 64 workers thumbprinted the transfer of rights and two more attended without having signed, giving 66 claimants against a workforce of 312, well under half. [Para 38] [Para 39] [Para 40] [Para 68] [Para 69] The union's want of registration reinforced the conclusion. [Para 37]

The panel added, on the weekly-payment limb of demand 1, that Article 2 gives the employer the right to manage the enterprise, that no law requires a lunch allowance at all, and that paying it monthly with the wage was reasonable and lawful. [Para 74] [Para 78] [Para 79] [Para 81] [Para 82] It nevertheless recorded a refusal to consider that limb as well. [Para 101]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that the employer pay more than the statutory minimum, where no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice gives the workers that entitlement, is an interest dispute; and because an interest award substitutes a collective agreement binding the whole workforce, the panel will decline to consider such a demand unless the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise — a union that has transferred rights from well under half the workforce, and is not yet registered, cannot make the panel's equity jurisdiction available. [Para 63] [Para 64] [Para 69]

**Obiter dicta:** that Notification No. 230 sets floors rather than ceilings, so an employer paying exactly US$10 and US$7 complies; and that paying a lunch allowance monthly with the wage, rather than weekly, is a reasonable exercise of managerial prerogative. [Para 82] [Para 85] [Para 93]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel is recorded as constituted on "11 September 2021 2014". [Para 16] The non-conciliation report is numbered 1093 in the procedural narrative and 1053 in two later references. [Para 10] [Para 15] [Para 31] The claimant group is unstable: 64 thumbprints, three workers present of whom one already appears on the list, and a panel assumption of 66 against 312 employees. [Para 38] [Para 39] [Para 40] The operative order mixes two formulas, declining to consider demand 1 and its weekly-payment limb while "rejecting" the demands, although the panel had already reasoned that the monthly payment was lawful. [Para 100] [Para 101] Notification No. 230 is said to cover sewing, cutting and footwear although the company makes trousers, and its point 1 is printed as "7 (five two) dollars". [Para 84] [Para 92] Prakas No. 099 is dated "21 April 2019 2004". [Para 62] The company, union and federation names appear in several Khmer spellings, and the signature block is partly destroyed. [Para 1] [Para 105] Individual workers and union officers are reduced to initials per the briefing convention.
