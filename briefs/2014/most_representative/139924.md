# Award No. 275/13 — Kala Khem In Ves Men (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 6 February 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouchhun, Liv Sovanna, Pen Bunhea presiding) · Doc ID 139924

## 1. Facts
Kala Khem In Ves Men is a garment factory employing about 400 workers [Para 51]. On 13 December 2013 the Kampong Speu labour office received a 15-point demand from the Cambodian Workers' Union Federation; conciliation on 18 and 19 December failed on every point [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24]. At the hearing eight points were settled, the workers withdrew three (points 1, 12 and 13), leaving points 5, 6, 7 and 15 [Para 28][Para 29][Para 30]. Eleven workers gave the federation president a written authorisation dated 12 December 2013 to represent them, twelve workers and two federation officials attended, and the factory union itself held only a registration receipt of 28 November 2013 [Para 31][Para 52][Para 53].

## 2. Issues in dispute
5. Whether the employer must install a television in the canteen for workers to watch during the lunch break? [Para 54][Para 97]
6. Whether the employer must stop requiring workers to take a shared card in order to enter the toilets? [Para 60][Para 112]
7. Whether the employer must keep paying the full skill allowance when a worker takes authorised leave or declines to work overtime? [Para 74][Para 129]
15. Whether the employer must pay a 4,000 riel meal allowance to workers who volunteer to work on public holidays and Sundays? [Para 88][Para 139]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Workers want somewhere to relax after lunch as other factories provide, such as New Archid [Para 56]. The toilet card is unworkable: in team A, with 46 members and two cards at most, a worker must wait until a teammate comes out before entering, which harms health [Para 63][Para 65]. The skill allowance appears in their contracts and is attached to their role, so it should survive authorised leave [Para 83]. And because the employer pays 4,000 riel as meal money for overtime from 4 pm to 6 pm on ordinary days, it should pay the same for voluntary Sunday and holiday work [Para 89].
**Employer (respondent):** A television would keep workers glued to a serial and slow their return to work, and would provoke disputes when the channel is changed [Para 57][Para 59]. Each team of 15 to 25 workers gets one or two toilet cards to avoid disrupting the production line and to stop workers hiding in the toilets [Para 66][Para 67][Para 68]. The skill allowance is a discretionary incentive of USD 5 to USD 25 assessed by team leaders on daily performance and attendance, and is not written into any policy [Para 75][Para 78]. No law requires meal money for holiday or Sunday work [Para 90].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to stop using cards to enter the toilets (issue 6) and to pay 2,000 riel of meal allowance to workers who volunteer to work on Sundays (issue 15), and dismissed the claims on the television (issue 5) and the skill allowance (issue 7); the award is non-binding and became final eight days after notification absent a written objection to the Minister [Para 180][Para 181][Para 182][Para 184][Para 185].

On standing, the panel applied Prakas No. 099 Article 19 and found the eleven workers had validly authorised the federation president, and that the twelve workers present could act for themselves, so it would decide for the eleven who signed plus the four who attended without signing [Para 92][Para 93][Para 94][Para 96].

Issue 6 succeeded as a right dispute. Under Article 2 the employer may direct and manage the enterprise, but that right cannot override the workers' access to sanitation [Para 119][Para 123]. A card system that leaves 46 workers sharing two cards makes them wait, and delay in reaching a toilet harms health, including urinary and bowel problems; the panel held the limitation unreasonable and ordered the practice stopped, while noting the employer may still discipline workers who abuse toilet breaks [Para 125][Para 127][Para 128][Para 124].

Issue 7 was dismissed as falling within the employer's management right: the allowance is a payment above the statutory minimum, no law or policy fixes its conditions, and an employer may set conditions for an incentive it grants [Para 135][Para 136][Para 137]. Arbitrator Liv Sovanna dissented, holding that in practice the allowance attaches to a worker's role rather than to overtime, that only two of the thirteen workers present had ever received it, and that it should be reduced pro rata for authorised leave and not cut at all for refusing overtime [Para 194][Para 200][Para 201][Para 202][Para 204].

Issue 5 was an interest dispute and was refused: no law, agreement or practice requires a television in a canteen, and the claimants were not the most representative body able to bind the workforce [Para 98][Para 99][Para 110][Para 111]. Issue 15 was split. Because the normal week runs Monday to Saturday for 48 hours, Sunday is not a normal working day and voluntary Sunday work is overtime, so Notification No. 041/11 requires 2,000 riel of meal money, which the employer had not paid [Para 159][Para 160][Para 166][Para 167][Para 170][Para 171]. Work on a public holiday is not overtime: it earns the one day's wage compensation under Prakas No. 10, not meal money, so the claim for 4,000 riel on holidays was refused as an interest dispute [Para 147][Para 157][Para 178][Para 183].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer's power to manage under Article 2 does not extend to rationing access to toilets through shared cards where that makes workers wait, and such a practice must stop; and where the normal week is Monday to Saturday, voluntary Sunday work is overtime attracting the 2,000 riel meal allowance under Notification No. 041/11, whereas work on a public holiday is not overtime and earns only the statutory one-day wage compensation [Para 123][Para 128][Para 166][Para 170][Para 147].

**Obiter dicta:** A skill allowance paid above the statutory minimum is a discretionary incentive whose conditions the employer may set, so a worker who does not meet them is not entitled to it [Para 135][Para 136]. The dissenting arbitrator would have treated the allowance as role-based and cut only pro rata for authorised leave [Para 201][Para 204].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The conciliation report is numbered 1694/13 at line 19 but 1695/13 at line 24, in both cases dated 20 December 2013. The list of authorising workers is unstable: line 52 names eleven but repeats two names, line 94 names twelve, and line 96 adds four who attended without signing, so the claimant group cannot be reconstructed with certainty. The claim is for 4,000 riel (line 139) but line 172 prints "2 4,000" and the disposition orders 2,000 (line 184), which is consistent with the reasoning but not with the claim as filed. The chair's name is garbled on the signature block (line 186, "ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ណា" beside a partly legible stamp) though the header gives Pen Bunhea. Company registration details are absent and the employer's address is given only to village and commune level. Page furniture, duplicated clauses and stray glyphs interrupt the reasoning at lines 85, 109, 121, 139, 151 and 164. Individual workers' names appear in the award but are not reproduced here by policy.
