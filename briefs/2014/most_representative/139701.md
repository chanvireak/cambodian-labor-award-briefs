# Award No. 234/14 — Trou Tham Phnom Penh (Arbitration Council, 12 September 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Ang Eng Thong, president) · Doc ID 139701

## 1. Facts
The Cambodia Confederation of Trade Unions (សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្ពុជា) filed on behalf of workers at Trou Tham Phnom Penh, a company whose stated business is computer-based retouching of images of clothing and footwear, employing 240 staff, which began operations on 1 June 2011. Of nine claims raised, five were settled at conciliation and one was withdrawn, leaving a demand for a basic wage of USD 250 per month and a merged demand for a USD 10 monthly attendance bonus plus USD 7 monthly travel and accommodation allowance. [Para 15][Para 37]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to pay a basic wage of USD 250 per month to the workers? [Para 71]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to pay a regular attendance bonus of USD 10 per month and a travel and accommodation allowance of USD 7 per month to the workers? [Para 91]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Both sides accept that the company pays USD 100 per month during a three-month training period and at least USD 150 thereafter, rising 5% each year to about USD 170, but the workers say this cannot cover daily living costs and that the employer earns well and constantly recruits new staff; the bonus and allowance are sought to encourage staff who work hard for the company. [Para 45][Para 56]
**Employer (respondent):** The company has no capacity: it began operating only on 1 June 2011, the work is not difficult — staff merely work on computers — each employment contract states the monthly basic wage, and as the company is not in the textile, garment or footwear sector it owes neither the attendance bonus nor the travel allowance under Notification No. 230. [Para 47][Para 60]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider both claims, so no sum was ordered. [Para 101]
On issue 1 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, no collective agreement, no internal rules, no past practice and no contractual term obliging the employer to pay USD 250, which made the claim an interest dispute (វិវាទ អំពី ផលប្រយោជន៍) rather than a rights dispute. It set out Article 96 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, under which only the most representative union may compel an employer to conclude a collective agreement, and Prakas No. 099 Article 43, under which an award on an interest dispute substitutes for a collective agreement for one year. Having reviewed its own prior awards, the panel held it may decide an interest dispute only where the claiming union is the most representative in the enterprise, or where several unions together represent more than half the workforce. [Para 74][Para 84]
Applying that test, the panel found the 11 complainants were not a most representative union: the confederation had no local union at the company, the 179 thumbprints it produced were rejected as authorisations because they stated neither date nor purpose, and only five workers had signed written authorisations — one of whom, Mr La Chhun, appeared under a duplicated name and counted once. The panel therefore held the claimants lacked the legal standing to bring an interest dispute and refused to consider the wage claim. [Para 41][Para 67][Para 90]
On issues 2 and 3 the panel read Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 as applying only to workers in the textile, garment and footwear sector; since the company's business is image retouching, it falls outside that notification's scope. Finding also no law, collective agreement, internal rule or contract granting the USD 10 bonus and USD 7 allowance, the panel classified this claim too as an interest dispute and, on the same representativeness ground, refused to consider it. [Para 92][Para 94][Para 100]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An arbitration panel will refuse to decide an interest dispute — a claim to a benefit not already owed by law, collective agreement, internal rules, contract or established practice — unless the union bringing it is the most representative in the enterprise, or the unions together represent more than half the workers; a bare group of individual complainants, unsupported by a local union structure, cannot invoke the panel's interest-dispute jurisdiction. [Para 84][Para 89]
A Ministry notification fixing an attendance bonus and a travel and accommodation allowance for textile, garment and footwear workers applies only to employers in that sector, so a company outside it owes nothing under the notification. [Para 94]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel recorded that the company employed 240 staff, that the work consisted of computer retouching rather than production, and it noted the difference between a training wage of USD 100 and post-training wages of USD 150–170 without ruling on adequacy. [Para 44][Para 49]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Several figures and dates are printed two ways. The claim is stated as USD 250 per month in the conciliation summary but the issue statement prints "២៥០០០ ដុល្លារ" (25,000) before reverting to 250 in the decision. [Para 2][Para 71] The attendance bonus is described in the conciliation summary as "USD 15 per year month" but appears as USD 10 per month in the reasons. [Para 4][Para 53] The panel's constitution is dated "25 August 2022 2014", giving both years; the reasons elsewhere refer to the "Arbitration Council 2015"; the panel is created 25 August 2014 in the procedural narrative. [Para 18][Para 88] The company carries two different commercial registration numbers, Co.0667 E/2011 and No. 7491, both dated 27 December 2012. [Para 35] Paragraph 83 is badly corrupted by OCR, retaining untranslated English fragments ("Bentroponds Benternamly"), so the quotation of Prakas No. 099 Article 43 is partly reconstructed from the surrounding text. [Para 83] Individual worker names are retained in the source but are reduced here to roles; the arbitrator appointed by the workers appears as "An Non", which OCR may have distorted.
