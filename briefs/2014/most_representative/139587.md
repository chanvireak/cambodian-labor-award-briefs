# Award No. 139/14 — Global E Feril Tech Thai Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 2 July 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Mae Nimmit, chair) · Doc ID 139587

## 1. Facts
The company sews garments at Dangkao, Phnom Penh, with about 550 workers, 45 of whom mandated the Cambodian Workers' Union Federation. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47] Following a walkout over working conditions, the Ministry's labour-dispute office received 16 unsettled demands and referred them to the Secretariat on 28 May 2014. [Para 20][Para 25] Eleven points were settled in conciliation, the workers withdrew points 9 and 10, leaving points 7, 13 and 16 for the panel: holiday pay at 300 per cent, weekly payment of the overtime meal allowance, and retention of the attendance bonus during the strike. [Para 28][Para 29][Para 30] Eight workers attended the hearing as the original claimants. [Para 48][Para 49]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to pay 300 per cent of the ordinary daily wage for work performed on a public holiday, that is the holiday day's wage multiplied by two plus the wage for the day worked? [Para 81]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to pay the overtime meal allowance once a week rather than once a month, as the workers demand? [Para 108]
3. Whether the employer is obliged to maintain the regular attendance bonus for the period of the strike from the afternoon of 27 May 2014 to 30 May 2014? [Para 129]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** holiday work should attract 300 per cent, pointing to neighbouring factories such as Bright Sky that pay that rate; the monthly meal-allowance practice forced them to borrow at interest; and the attendance bonus should survive the strike. [Para 54][Para 60][Para 71]
**Employer (respondent):** it pays holiday work at 200 per cent as the law requires and cannot pay more because it only began operations on 11 December 2013; weekly meal-allowance payment is impossible for want of accounting staff and systems; and strikers who performed no work forfeit wages and bonuses. [Para 56][Para 67][Para 79]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim on issue 7 was refused consideration, the employer was ordered to pay the overtime meal allowance for 15:00 to 17:00 once a week, and the attendance-bonus claim on issue 16 was dismissed. [Para 148][Para 149][Para 150]
On issue 7 the panel read Article 164 of the Labour Law with Article 4 of Prakas No. 10 dated 4 February 1999, which entitles a worker who works on a holiday to one additional sum equal to the ordinary day's wage — that is 200 per cent in total. [Para 81][Para 83][Para 86] The employer paid 3.84 USD multiplied by two, or 7.68 USD, per holiday worked, which the panel found to discharge that obligation. [Para 88][Para 89][Para 90] No statute, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice required 300 per cent. [Para 91] The panel therefore classified the demand as an interests dispute and, applying Article 96 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 305, held that only a most-representative union may bring such a claim; eight claimants out of 550 workers did not qualify, so it declined to consider the demand. [Para 92][Para 98][Para 104][Para 105]
On issue 13 the panel applied point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011, which grants 2,000 riel per day of overtime or one free meal but fixes no payment interval. [Para 108][Para 110] Reading that silence, the panel held the allowance must reach workers in time for them to buy food on the day they work overtime, while accepting that weekly, fortnightly or monthly payment could all serve that purpose. [Para 113][Para 118] Because the workers asked only for weekly payment and gave reasons of indebtedness the panel found reasonable, it ordered the employer to pay the allowance weekly. [Para 125][Para 127][Para 128]
On issue 16 the panel held that Article 332(1) suspends the employment contract during a strike so that no work and no wage are owed, and Article 103 plus point 2 of Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 make the attendance bonus part of wages. [Para 129][Para 130][Para 139][Para 140] The strike from 27 to 30 May 2014 was called without the secret ballot and prior notice required by Articles 320, 324 and 332, and the strikers performed no work, so withholding the bonus breached neither the Labour Law nor the notification. [Para 137][Para 143][Para 144][Para 145]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus is a component of wages, but because a strike suspends the contract and extinguishes the wage for the strike days, an employer does not owe that bonus for a period in which the workers struck and performed no work, whether or not the strike was procedurally lawful. [Para 133][Para 141][Para 146]
A demand for a benefit that no statute, collective agreement or practice grants is an interests dispute, which the panel will refuse to hear unless the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise. [Para 101][Para 104]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that a weekly interval is a reasonable period for the employer to organise meal-allowance payment, and that earlier awards had ordered weekly payment. [Para 63][Para 65][Para 115]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel was constituted on "29 May 2023 2014", two years printed over one another. [Para 26] The conciliation paragraph states that 11 points were settled but lists only ten (1–6, 11, 12, 14, 15). [Para 28] The award records three arbitrators but the signature block carries only the employer-side arbitrator and the chair, the worker-side name being absent. [Para 152] The ordered hours appear as "15:00" in the reasoning, "15 0 and 00 minutes" in the order and "17 and 4 00 minutes" in the dispositive summary. [Para 128][Para 149] The company name is transliterated inconsistently (Global E Feril / Global E Feril Tech Thai), and the worker-side arbitrator's name is lost from the signature page. [Para 45][Para 152]
