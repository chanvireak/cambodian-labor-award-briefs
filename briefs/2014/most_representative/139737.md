# Award No. 271/14 — Kron 2010 Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 October 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kol Vathana for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Kong Phalha chair) · Doc ID 139737

## 1. Facts
Kron 2010 Co Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ក្រណ ២០១០) manufactures construction materials at Chhngieng village, Dang Tong district, Kampot, and employed about 62 workers at monthly wages of USD 110 to USD 300. [Para 31][Para 37] On 7 June 2013 the employer and the Cambodia Construction and Forestry Workers' Union signed a three-point conciliation minute at the Kampot Provincial Department of Labour: point 1 recorded that the company agreed to a USD 10 raise per year, with more for workers whose work was good, and point 3 that the raise applied from June 2013. [Para 39][Para 64] The employer in fact raised wages by only USD 2 to USD 7, and the workers who received the full USD 10 were non-union members. [Para 43] The union filed lists identifying 27 workers who received less than USD 10; the employer never attended the hearing. [Para 44][Para 46][Para 51]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to raise the monthly wage by USD 10 per year for the workers of Kron 2010, as agreed in the conciliation minute of 7 June 2013? [Para 36][Para 58]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer signed the 7 June 2013 minute agreeing to a USD 10 annual raise, more for strong performers, effective June 2013; it applied only USD 2 to USD 7, refused a further meeting on 10 May 2014, and the 27 named workers are owed the balance. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 41][Para 42]
**Employer (respondent):** no position on the merits — the employer was duly summoned, did not appear and gave no valid reason, so the panel treated it as having abandoned its right to respond. [Para 13][Para 62]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to apply the agreement dated 7 June 2013 to all 27 named workers. [Para 90]

On the preliminary question whether it could sit in the employer's absence, the panel held it could: Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council, Article 21, allows the panel to continue or to close the file with an award where a properly summoned party fails to appear without valid reason. [Para 49] The secretariat had reached the company by telephone and been told its representative had travelled to Thailand, and the employer had sought no adjournment, so the condition was met. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 55][Para 56] The panel aligned itself with earlier awards that heard cases in the absence of the employer. [Para 50][Para 57]

On the merits the panel started from Article 13(2) of the Labour Law: the public-order character of the Law does not prevent an employer, by unilateral decision, employment contract, collective convention or agreement, from granting workers benefits or rights more favourable than those the Law sets, and such agreements are valid. [Para 73][Para 74] It found the USD 10 annual raise, with more for good performers, to be exactly such a more favourable agreement and therefore legally valid. [Para 75]

The panel then grounded the obligation in contract: Article 311 of the 2007 Civil Code defines a contract as the meeting of wills of two or more persons to create, modify or extinguish obligations, and Articles 336 and 337 require a matching offer and acceptance. [Para 76][Para 78][Para 81][Para 83] It followed its reasoning in Award No. 31/12 (B&N), where a company that had not honoured a signed agreement was held bound by the juridical tie it had created. [Para 84][Para 85][Para 86]

On the evidence, the panel recalled that the party asserting a fact bears the burden of proof, and found the workers had discharged it: they filed the list of 27 workers by the 14 October 2014 deadline, the employer filed no objection by the 16 October deadline, and the employer was therefore taken to accept the list. [Para 67][Para 69][Para 70][Para 71] The order was confined to those 27 workers. [Para 72] The award is not binding and becomes final eight days after notification if no party objects in writing to the Minister of Labour. [Para 91]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A wage increase recorded in a conciliation minute and more favourable than the Labour Law is a valid and enforceable contract under Article 13(2) of the Labour Law and Articles 311 and 337 of the Civil Code; where the employer signed it but granted only part of the increase, the panel orders the full increase for the workers the claimant can name, and a properly summoned employer who stays away forfeits the right to contest that list. [Para 74][Para 75][Para 83][Para 71][Para 56]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's survey of earlier awards permitting hearings in the absence of a party, and its remark that the claimant union was not the most representative union in the company yet was accepted as the claimant, are general observations not needed for the order. [Para 34][Para 50]

- Double dates and years: the panel is said to have been constituted on "01 October 2019 2014" [Para 11], Prakas No. 099 dated "21 April 2019 2004" [Para 49], and the file referred to the Council on "24 29 September 2014" [Para 10].
- The quoted point 1 of the minute is worded differently in the facts and in the reasons ("អោយ ដំឡើង" / "ដោយ ដំឡើង"; "កម្មករ និយោជិត ដែល ការ បាន ល្អ" / "អ្នក ដែល ការបាន លួ"). [Para 39][Para 64]
- The employer is printed variously as ក្រណ ២០១០, ក្រន ២០១០ and "Kron 2010"; the union's own name is split across lines at [Para 18] and [Para 23]. Arbitrator names appear only in Khmer script, and the signature block is overlaid with stray digits ("22017 42", "492"). [Para 2][Para 92]
- The non-conciliation summary says 1 of 5 claims remained unresolved, while the file forwarded concerns only the wage increase; the number of workers is given as "about 62" twice with no roll. [Para 9][Para 10][Para 32][Para 37]
