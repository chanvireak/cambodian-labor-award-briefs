# Award No. 259/13 — Long Lead (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 3 January 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (You Suonty, employer-chosen; Liv Sovanna, worker-chosen; Pen Bunchhea, chair) · Doc ID 139908

## 1. Facts
Long Lead (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a footwear manufacturer in Chamkar Svay Village, Sethey Commune, Samaki Meanchey, Kompong Chhnang, employing 1,691 workers. [Para 19] The Khmer Youth Trade Union Federation (KYFTU) local union, registered on 12 July 2010, brought a collective dispute over lunch payments, a housing and transport allowance and union contribution fees. [Para 20] The employer paid 1,500 riel in lieu of lunch weekly, including to volunteers working Sundays and public holidays, from May 2013 to the third week of October 2013, then stopped. [Para 22][Para 25] Under the MoU of 3 October 2012 the parties accepted binding arbitration for rights disputes and non-binding arbitration for interests disputes. [Para 12][Para 15]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must maintain the 1,500 riel payment in lieu of lunch for work performed on Sundays and public holidays? [Para 2][Para 23]
2. Whether the employer must increase the US$9 accommodation and transportation allowance to US$10? [Para 32][Para 34]
4 & 5. Whether the employer must implement the agreement of 13 September 2013 and repay the 1,000 riel union contribution fees deducted from wages in September and October 2013 as payment to the Cambodian Labour Union (CLU)? [Para 35]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** agreements in May 2013 provided 1,000 riel plus an additional 500 riel, and neither limited the payment to Monday-to-Saturday work; the employer stopped it without notice or meeting. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 63] Transport costs were rising and neighbouring companies paid US$10. [Para 35] Deductions for CLU continued after 99 workers withdrew in August 2013 and after the 13 September 2013 agreement; the 27 November 2013 agreement was void. [Para 37][Para 40][Para 45]
**Employer (respondent):** the Sunday and public holiday payment was an accounting and technical error, the Executive Director being too busy to check payslips, and it was discovered in the fourth week of October 2013. [Para 28][Para 29][Para 30] It could not afford US$10, and the extra US$1 merely motivated Sunday volunteers. [Para 35] Issues 4 and 5 were settled by the 27 November 2013 agreement, and no original withdrawal letters were received. [Para 38][Para 46]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Part I (binding): Issue 1 ordered continuation of the 1,500 riel lunch payment, and Issues 4 and 5 ordered repayment of the 1,000 riel monthly union fees for September and October 2013 to the 99 workers; Part II (non-binding): Issue 2 declined to consider the US$10 demand. [Para 119][Para 120][Para 121]

On Issue 1 the panel classified the claim as a rights dispute because the payment was stipulated in agreements between the parties. [Para 62] The agreement of 19 April 2013 added 500 riel from May 2013 and did not restrict payment to regular working days. [Para 59][Para 64] Where an agreement is silent, the panel held that past practice fills the lacuna. [Para 65][Para 82] Applying Article 65(2)–(3) of the Labour Law and Case 95/09-Tak Fat, a consistent, regular and accepted practice binds the parties. [Para 67][Para 72][Para 73] The accounting-error defence failed for lack of evidence and because weekly signing made a six-month undetected error improbable. [Para 78][Para 79] An abrupt stop could not create a new practice to replace the old one. [Para 82]

On Issue 2, Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2013 requires US$7, while the employer pays US$9, so the statutory standard is exceeded. [Para 85][Para 87] No law, agreement or practice required US$10, making the demand an interests dispute. [Para 88] The union did not hold most representative status (MRS), so the panel declined to consider it. [Para 94][Para 95]

On Issues 4 and 5, Article 129(2) requires written authorisation, revocable at any time, for union dues. [Para 96][Para 97] Point 3 of the 27 November 2013 agreement barred workers from recovering unlawful deductions, contrary to Article 13 of the Labour Law, and was void. [Para 102][Para 108] Deductions continued after 99 workers' written withdrawals, so the employer had to repay the September and October 2013 amounts. [Para 117][Para 118]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an agreement is silent on the days for which a lunch payment is due, the parties' consistent practice fills the gap and the employer must maintain the payment; an unproven accounting-error claim does not excuse stopping it. [Para 65][Para 82] Union dues may be deducted only under each worker's written, revocable authorisation, and an agreement that blocks workers from recovering deductions made after withdrawal is void under Article 13. [Para 96][Para 108]

**Obiter dicta:** An interests dispute may be brought only by a union with most representative status or by a collective union representing more than half the enterprise's workers. [Para 94]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The issues list states the demand as a "3,500 riel meallowance", but the panel and the order treat it as 1,500 riel. [Para 2][Para 21][Para 119] "US$" is repeatedly printed "USS", and "1,5000 riel" appears once. [Para 25][Para 32] The employer relies on Notification No. 041/13 dated 7 March 2011, a 2011 date cited in a 2013 dispute. [Para 30] The agreement of 27 November 2013 refers to a meeting held on "22/1/2013". [Para 39] The signature block is partly garbled, with stray figures ("2016, 2000, 0, 000"). [Para 122] The evidence section is omitted and refers readers to the Khmer original. [Para 16][Para 17]
