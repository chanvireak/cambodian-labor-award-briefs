# Award No. 118/14 — Komin Khmer Chamkat (Arbitration Council, 19 June 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (You Soun, appointed by the employer; An Vireak, appointed by the workers; Mae Nimmit, chair appointed by both) · Doc ID 139575

## 1. Facts
Komin Khmer Chamkat (ក្រុមហ៊ុន កូមិន ខ្មែរ ចំកាត់) is a Phnom Penh company registered on 01 March 2013 whose business is the trading of electrical equipment (អាហរ័ណ នីរហ័ណ), employing about 1,347 staff. [Para 1][Para 43] After a strike led by the Federation of Trade Unions of Workers, 13 demands went to conciliation, which failed on 09 May 2014; 155 workers transferred their rights to the federation to pursue them. [Para 20][Para 45] At the hearing the workers withdrew claims 1, 3, 4, 6 and 8, leaving eight for decision. [Para 26][Para 27]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise monthly basic wages by 60% of the current basic wage? [Para 46]
2. Whether the employer must supply two sets of work clothing a year, and by when? [Para 55][Para 59]
3. Whether the employer must pay 5,000 riel a day in meal allowance when workers work on holidays or Sundays? [Para 64]
4. Whether holiday work must be paid at 300% of the normal wage? [Para 71]
5. Whether the employer must pay USD 10 a month for travel and accommodation? [Para 81]
6. Whether workers engaged beyond a two-month probation become full-rights employees? [Para 86]
7. Whether the employer must pay a USD 10 monthly attendance bonus? [Para 96]
8. Whether the employer must pay 1,500 riel an hour in meal allowance for overtime? [Para 102]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** most workers have university-level knowledge, the company is an international-standard firm yet pays low basic wages, and long-serving staff earn the same basic wage as new hires; project workers had no vote for the employee delegates who signed the 09 May 2014 agreement, so it lacks their consent. [Para 47][Para 50][Para 52][Para 53]
**Employer (respondent):** wages follow the agreement of 09 May 2014, which set new minimum basic wages by job (helper 100 to 120, technician 120 to 140, team leader 140 to 160 USD) applied uniformly from 01 May 2014; the company is in electrical equipment, not textiles, so the ministry notifications on meal, travel and attendance allowances do not reach it, and it lacks the means to pay. [Para 49][Para 68][Para 83]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to provide two sets of work clothing a year at a reasonable time matching the workers' needs; every other claim was dismissed. [Para 256]

On the 60% increase the panel held the claim was an interest dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍) and that it could decide such a dispute only if the union bringing it were the most representative in the enterprise — a union or unions with more than half the workforce as members. [Para 119] The federation had no lawful base union at the company, and the 154 workers who assigned their rights did not make it the most representative organisation there, so the claim was not legally admissible. [Para 44][Para 121][Para 122] It followed earlier awards 81/04, 98/04, 169/11, 02/11 and 66/11 on the same point. [Para 119][Para 120]

On clothing, both parties agreed the employer does give two sets a year and disagreed only on timing, so the panel fixed no sum but ordered delivery at a reasonable, regular point each year that answers the workers' needs. [Para 126][Para 130][Para 132]

The meal allowance of 5,000 riel for holiday and Sunday work failed: Notification No. 041/11 of 07 March 2011 covers only textile, garment and footwear workers, the company trades electrical equipment, and no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice created the entitlement, leaving an interest dispute the panel would not decide. [Para 139][Para 140][Para 141][Para 144]

On the 300% holiday rate the panel accepted that the company paid 300% from start-up until 2009, when it moved to 200%, and that the workers did not object until 02 May 2014. [Para 156][Para 166] That silence of about five years was long enough to be a fresh tacit agreement binding both sides. [Para 169][Para 170] Article 164 of the Labour Law and Article 4 of Prakas No. 10 of 04 February 1999 give a worker on a paid holiday 200% in total, and Article 6(2) of the registered internal rules says the same, so the claim exceeded both law and the rules. [Para 172][Para 173][Para 177][Para 184]

Travel and accommodation, the USD 10 attendance bonus and the 1,500 riel overtime meal allowance were each defeated by the same sector point: Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 covers textile, garment and footwear only, the company is outside it, and nothing else obliged the employer, so each was an interest dispute dismissed. [Para 190][Para 193][Para 197][Para 241][Para 243][Para 251][Para 254]

On probation the panel found project or "floating" workers had no written contract, so under Article 67(7) their fixed-duration contract became one of undetermined duration; the employer already treated them as such and paid severance at each project's end, and Article 10 gives occasional and permanent workers the same rights, so no further order was needed. [Para 204][Para 206][Para 222][Para 229][Para 230]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a claim is not fixed by the Labour Law, a Prakas, a collective agreement, the internal regulations or a clear and continuous past practice, it is an interest dispute; the panel will decide it only if the union carrying it is the most representative organisation in the enterprise, because its award would stand in place of a collective agreement for a year, and a union without majority membership cannot put such a claim. [Para 116][Para 119] Where an employer has paid a benefit above the legal minimum for years and then reduces it, prolonged silence by the workforce converts the reduced rate into a new tacit agreement binding both parties, and a claim to restore the old rate fails once that agreement is found. [Para 157][Para 170]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that an award on an interest dispute takes the place of a collective agreement and removes the workers' right to strike over that subject in future, and that an employer may unilaterally set bonuses above the legal minimum as an exercise of its managerial right under Article 2, were general observations not needed for the orders. [Para 118][Para 215] Arbitrator An Vireak dissented on the holiday rate, reading the internal rules as more favourable than the law and so requiring 300%; his opinion is an annex and did not alter the award. [Para 263][Para 275][Para 277]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The claim as filed asked for three sets of clothing a year (ឯកសណ្ឋាន ០៣ កំប្លេ) [Para 6], but issue 5 as framed and decided concerns two sets [Para 55][Para 256]; the award records only the two-set figure.
- The internal regulations are dated 10 May 2010 in the findings [Para 61][Para 74] but "10 December May 2010" in the dissent [Para 175]; the dissent also prints the rate it seeks as "3000 percent" [Para 277] against 300% elsewhere [Para 274].
- The panel's constitution date reads "12 May 2023 2014" [Para 23], and the period of the old holiday practice reads "until 2016 year 2019" [Para 145].
- Worker numbers appear as 155 [Para 45] and 154 [Para 121]; the employer's headcount is "about 1,347" [Para 43].
- The employer-party arbitrator is legible as You Soun in the composition [Para 1] but lost in the signature block [Para 263]; the worker-party arbitrator appears as An Vireak and An Vivuok across [Para 263], [Para 266] and [Para 278]. OCR noise ("Aman", "Amm", "AK") also masks article headings [Para 29][Para 47].
