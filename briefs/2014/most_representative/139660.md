# Award No. 202/14 — Min Koun Thich Ta Y (Arbitration Council, 26 August 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna, appointed by the workers; Ang Eng Thong, chair) · Doc ID 139660

## 1. Facts
Min Koun Thich Ta Y ("Min Koun") is a garment-cutting company at Prey Sala, Kab, Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh, employing about 367 workers. [Para 43][Para 44] After a strike the workers lodged seven demands to improve conditions; conciliation settled four and left three, and at the hearing the parties settled issues 1 and 3, so only issue 2 remained for decision. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 21][Para 22] The surviving claim is for a travel and accommodation allowance of USD 15 a month, that is USD 8 more than the USD 7 the company pays. [Para 47][Para 48]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must add USD 8 to the travel and accommodation allowance, raising it from USD 7 to USD 15 a month? [Para 56]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** rent, water and electricity have all risen, rent going from USD 30 to USD 35 a month from December 2013; nearby factories pay more, Cambodia Coat USD 10 and Li Ly USD 12 a month; and the company must be profitable because it keeps opening branches. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 52][Para 81]
**Employer (respondent):** it has no capacity to pay the increase, and it already pays the USD 7 that the law requires. [Para 51][Para 6]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the claim for an additional USD 8, bringing the allowance to USD 15 a month, was refused. The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Minister through the Secretariat. [Para 91][Para 92]

The panel first identified the claim as an interest dispute: article 1 of Notification No. 230 K.B/S.C.N. of 25 July 2012 fixes travel and accommodation allowance at USD 7 a month, the company pays USD 7, and no law, collective agreement, internal rule, contract or past practice creates any obligation to pay more. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 59][Para 60]

Because the claim was an interest dispute, the panel had to check the claimant's standing before reaching equity. The union held registration certificate 2379 of 16 January 2012 and a most-representative certificate 033/13 K.B/V.K of 16 May 2013 valid until 16 May 2015, so it was entitled to bring an interest dispute, following awards 81/04 and 98/04. [Para 34][Para 45][Para 46][Para 69][Para 73] The panel then decided the claim on equity, as Prakas 099 article 43 requires for interest disputes, citing awards 115/14 and 151/14. [Para 67][Para 74]

On the merits the panel weighed evidence on both sides. The employer produced nothing to show it could not afford the increase, having missed the 8 August 2014 filing deadline it had been given to prove incapacity. [Para 53][Para 79] The workers, for their part, produced no monthly invoices or receipts for rent, water and electricity, nor any evidence of the employer's capacity, although ordered to do so. [Para 54][Para 83] The panel applied its settled rule that the party making a claim carries the burden of proof, following awards 79/05, 101/08, 168/09, 115/10 and 148/11, and that a claim unsupported by specific evidence is refused, following 63/04, 99/06, 33/07, 51/07 and 24/14. [Para 84][Para 86] The workers having failed to discharge that burden, the claim was refused even though the employer's own evidence of incapacity was equally absent. [Para 88][Para 89]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the employer already pays the allowance fixed by the applicable Notification, a demand for more is an interest dispute to be decided on equity, not a right; and on equity the union bears the burden of proving both the increased cost it asserts and the employer's capacity to meet it, so a claim resting only on comparison with neighbouring factories and general price rises fails. [Para 59][Para 84][Para 88]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that an employer's failure to file evidence of incapacity does not by itself discharge the claimant's burden, and that a company's expansion into new branches says nothing about its ability to raise this allowance, are observations going beyond the single order made. [Para 79][Para 82]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award disposes of a single issue because the other six were settled, yet the fee-earning paragraphs recite that only three of seven claims were unresolved at conciliation [Para 17] and that two of those three were then settled at the hearing [Para 21]; the numbering of the demands (1 to 3 in the conciliation report) does not match the seven claims described in the procedural section.
- The panel composition is printed twice and inconsistently: the header names Seng Vouch Hun for the employer, Liv Sovanna for the workers and Ang Eng Thong as chair [Para 1], while the signature block leaves the employer-appointed seat blank and prints "chair" against Liv Sovanna's name [Para 93].
- The date the panel was constituted reads "25 July 2019 2014" [Para 18], an OCR duplication of the year.
- Award numbers in the cited authorities are corrupted: "9804" for 98/04 [Para 70], "3307" for 33/07 [Para 86].
- Substantial passages of the reasoning are unrecoverable machine noise, including the workers' argument on cost of living [Para 76] and part of the employer's reply [Para 78]; the panel's own findings at [Para 80] and [Para 83] were relied on instead.
- The company name appears as Min Koun Thich Ta Y, "Min Koun", "Min Kuon" and "Min Kun" across the face and body of the award [Para 1][Para 29][Para 43].
