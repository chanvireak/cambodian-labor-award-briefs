# Award No. 272/14 — Camshay Mar Trading (Cambodia) Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 3 November 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Run Saray chair) · Doc ID 139741

## 1. Facts
Camshay Mar Trading (Cambodia) Co Ltd knits sweaters on National Road 8 at Dan Phleng village, Kamchay Mear district, Prey Veng, and employed somewhat more than 2,000 workers, having begun operating about a year earlier. [Para 42][Para 56] Five federations and one confederation appeared for the workers; two registered local unions claimed about 2,080 members. [Para 43][Para 47][Para 99] The workers struck from 11 September 2014 (a half day) to 17 September 2014 without prior notice. [Para 67][Para 197] Of 25 demands conciliated on 12 September 2014, 16 were settled and 9 referred; two more settled at the hearing and two were withdrawn, leaving five. [Para 17][Para 23][Para 24][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
4. Whether the employer must pay a 2,000 riel midday meal allowance for each working day? [Para 50][Para 81]
5. Whether the employer must pay a 2,000 riel meal allowance to workers who work on public holidays or Sundays? [Para 57][Para 118]
6. Whether the employer must recalculate, month by month during employment, the compensation for unused annual leave on the average of the last 12 months' wages? [Para 61][Para 159]
7. Whether the employer must pay wages and the USD 10 attendance bonus for the period of the strike from 11 to 17 September 2014? [Para 67][Para 184]
9. Whether the employer must add 1,000 riel per hour to the 2,000 riel meal allowance once overtime exceeds two hours in a day? [Para 76][Para 201]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** wages are low and travel alone costs about 50 riel a day; a Better Factories Cambodia report shows Cambodian workers eat inadequately, with stunted growth and anaemia; other factories such as Bowker pay a meal allowance; market prices have risen; the employer provoked the strike, other factories do not dock strike days, and the annual-leave payment should be computed on a 12-month average. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54][Para 69][Para 70][Para 61]
**Employer (respondent):** the company has operated only about a year, is still loss-making with no orders, and cannot afford the demands; it pays the 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance already; the strike was called without notice; and it deducted wages and the attendance bonus because of the strike. [Para 56][Para 74][Para 75][Para 105]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay a 2,000 riel meal allowance to workers who volunteer to work on Sundays; the daily meal allowance, the public-holiday meal allowance, the annual-leave recalculation, the strike wages and attendance bonus, and the extra 1,000 riel per overtime hour were rejected. [Para 214][Para 215][Para 216][Para 217][Para 219]

On issue 4 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, a collective agreement, an agreement, the internal rules or past practice obliging a midday meal allowance, and classified the demand as an interest dispute. [Para 82][Para 83] The claimant unions held standing, since Article 96 of the Labour Law and Clause 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 allow the most representative union, or unions representing more than half the workforce, to bring such a dispute, and the two registered unions together had an absolute majority. [Para 87][Para 94][Para 101][Para 102] On equity, the Better Factories report said nothing about an obligation to pay 2,000 riel, and other factories' allowances depend on their own economics and profits, so the burden of proof was not discharged. [Para 106][Para 112][Para 113][Para 116]

On issue 5 the panel split the demand. Working on a public holiday for the normal eight hours is not overtime: Article 137 fixes 8 hours a day and 48 a week, Article 139 (new) attaches the 50% and 100% premiums to overtime, night work and weekly rest days, and Article 164 with Articles 2 and 4 of Prakas No. 10 of 4 February 1999 gives only one day's wage as compensation for holiday work. [Para 118][Para 123][Para 126] Because holiday work therefore earns the ordinary day's wage and no overtime, no overtime meal allowance is owed, and the demand was rejected for lack of any supporting source. [Para 137][Para 143] Sunday is different: it is the weekly rest day, so a volunteer working Sunday works beyond normal hours, and Clause 2 of Ministry notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 entitles that worker to 2,000 riel a day or a free meal, without any hour threshold. [Para 145][Para 151][Para 152][Para 153] The employer had paid nothing, so the allowance was ordered. [Para 154][Para 157][Para 158]

On issue 6 the panel held that Article 167(3) voids any cash payment for unused annual leave during the employment relationship, and Article 167(2) allows compensation only on termination; Article 167(4) instead permits carrying leave forward, up to three consecutive years and only beyond twelve days. [Para 170][Para 172][Para 174][Para 179] The employer's monthly cash settlement was therefore contrary to the law, but so was the workers' demand, and it was rejected while the panel noted the practice should change. [Para 182][Para 183]

On issue 7 the panel applied Article 332(1), which suspends the employment contract during a strike so that no work and no wage are owed, and Article 72(1), which removes the principal obligations on suspension. [Para 184][Para 186][Para 187] It confirmed its consistent line that strikers earn no wages whether the strike is lawful or not. [Para 188][Para 190] The attendance bonus failed twice over: Article 324 requires seven days' notice of a strike, the workers gave none, so the strike was unlawful, and Clause 2 of notification No. 230/12 conditions the bonus on regular attendance. [Para 196][Para 197][Para 199]

On issue 9 the employer already paid 2,000 riel a day under notification No. 041/11, which sets no hourly threshold; with no source for a further 1,000 riel an hour and no evidence of ability to pay, the interest claim failed on equity. [Para 203][Para 205][Para 212][Para 213]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Work on a public holiday for the ordinary eight hours is not overtime, so it earns one day's wage under Prakas No. 10 and no overtime meal allowance; work on the weekly rest day is overtime, and Clause 2 of notification No. 041/11 then entitles the volunteer to 2,000 riel a day whatever the hours worked. Unused annual leave may not be converted into cash while the contract subsists — Article 167(3) voids such payment and Article 167(2) allows compensation only on termination — and a worker on strike is owed neither wages under Article 332 nor an attendance bonus, the latter also because a strike without the seven days' notice required by Article 324 is unlawful. [Para 126][Para 153][Para 174][Para 187][Para 197]

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that paying cash for unused annual leave contradicts the spirit of Article 167(3) and that leave should instead be carried forward, though no order was made, is guidance beyond the outcome. [Para 181][Para 182]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The order states the attendance bonus as "100 dollars" [Para 218] against "10 dollars" in the demand and the reasons [Para 68][Para 200], and the award is said to become final after "18 days" instead of the usual eight [Para 220].
- A summons in the file invites the director of "Kron 2010" to select arbitrators [Para 39] — a different company and a different award, evidently carried over; the employer here is Camshay Mar, printed variously as កំចាយ មារ ត្រេឌីង, កំចាយ មារ ធីឌី and "Camshay Mar Trading (Cambodia)".
- The non-conciliation report is dated 15 September 2014 [Para 14][Para 18] but the conciliation it records is said to have been held, and the complaint received, on 12 September 2014 [Para 16][Para 17].
- The workforce is "more than 2,000" yet union membership is put at "about 2,080", an overlap the panel resolved as an absolute majority without comment [Para 42][Para 99][Para 100].
- Union membership is 80 to 100 for one union and about 2,000 for the other [Para 47]; stray years ("2016", "2015", "2017") are embedded in quoted notifications and amounts [Para 126][Para 158][Para 201], and the unresolved issues are listed 14, 15, 13, 19, 21, 23, 24, 25, out of order [Para 4]–[Para 11].
