# Award No. 039/14-E — E Garmen Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 21 April 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (You Soun Ty for the employer party, Tuon Si Phan for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhean chair) · Doc ID 139527

## 1. Facts
E Garmen Co Ltd, a garment factory on National Road 3 in Kandal province employing about 3,258 workers, faced a collective dispute brought by three factory unions and their federations on 20 demands covering severance, wage calculation, bonuses, the crèche, heat, drinking water, fingerprint scanners and union dues. [Para 84] Conciliation at the provincial Department of Labour on 3 February 2014 failed on all 20 points; the case reached the panel on 19 February 2014. At hearing 6 points were settled and 3 withdrawn, leaving 11 in dispute. [Para 29][Para 36]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must now pay severance to every worker, before any termination has occurred? [Para 88]
2. Whether the employer must compute wages day by day for piece-rate workers, counting the excess over the daily minimum as a daily output bonus and topping up each day's shortfall? [Para 99]
4. Whether the employer must instruct group leader Kim Solyda to stop discriminating against workers because of their union membership? [Para 125]
5. Whether the employer must enlarge the crèche, provide medicine for sick children, and reimburse $30 a month for 18 months ($450) spent on outside childminders? [Para 140]
12. Whether the employer must install one fingerprint scanner per group and remove the scanner standing in front of the sample room? [Para 156]
14. Whether the employer must pay 100% of base wage when there is no work to give, without prior notice and without the labour inspector's authorisation? [Para 422]
15. Whether the employer must raise the minimum wage by 5%, 6% and 7% according to seniority bands? [Para 194]
16. Whether the employer must pay a regular attendance bonus of $15 rather than $10 a month? [Para 201]
17. Whether the employer must pay a minimum wage of $150 a month from 1 January 2014? [Para 208]
18. Whether the employer must provide one free midday meal per worker per day, valued at 4,000 riel? [Para 213]
20. Whether the employer must reimburse union dues for members of Khmer Youth Voice Union from July 2011 to date? [Para 219]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** workers have long service and fear E Garmen will shut down and abandon them as Yong Va I and II did, so they should receive severance now to live on; wages should follow output and effort, and the weekly bonus system destroys the value of a hard day's work. [Para 90] The crèche is cramped so mothers leave children outside, and the claimant unions speak for the workforce. [Para 143]
**Employer (respondent):** the company is international with many branches, has not closed and will pay compensation according to law if it ever does, so suspicion cannot found a severance claim; base wage is a fixed $106 a month with a weekly output bonus on top, and daily bonus calculation is impossible without inviting fraud. [Para 95][Para 116]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel dismissed the severance claim, ordered the employer to stop using the weekly output bonus to top up the daily minimum wage, ordered 100% wage for the period of unpaid work, and ordered payment of actual invoiced child-care costs incurred before 1 November 2013; every other claim was dismissed or refused consideration. [Para 477][Para 479][Para 488]

On issue 1 the panel held that Article 74 of the Labour Law permits either party to terminate an indefinite-duration contract, but Article 311 of the Civil Code and Article 65 of the Labour Law make the creation, modification and extinction of contractual obligations the act of the parties, so severance is owed only where the employer has actually dismissed without valid reason; the workers could not compel their own dismissal. [Para 232][Para 241]

On issue 2 the panel split three ways. Article 2 and Article 13(2) of the Labour Law let an employer grant benefits better than the law by unilateral decision, and the law fixes neither the frequency nor the method of computing an output bonus, so a weekly calculation was a lawful and reasonable exercise of management right. [Para 249][Para 259] But Prakas No. 317 of 31 December 2013 raised the minimum wage from $80 to $100 a month from 1 February 2014, and a fixed minimum wage does not vary with output, so the employer could not draw down the weekly bonus to cover days when piece work fell short — doing so breached its statutory minimum-wage obligation. [Para 264][Para 277] The panel then refused, on res judicata, to revisit the daily top-up claim already decided in Award No. 214/13, and held the top-up duty arises once a month rather than at each daily calculation. [Para 322]

On issues 4, 12 and 20 the claims failed on evidence: no proof of discriminatory allocation of overtime or leave by the group leader, no proof that scanning queues took 10 to 20 minutes, and no written request by any worker that dues be deducted. [Para 330][Para 418][Para 475] On issue 5 the panel refused to consider the enlargement claim as a dispute about a state of affairs not yet arisen, since only about seven children were enrolled in a 50 m² crèche with two childminders; found no legal basis for supplying medicine and, in any event, that the claimant unions were not the most representative; but held Article 186 obliged the employer to meet outside child-care costs at actual invoice before the crèche opened on 1 November 2013. [Para 347][Para 372]

On issue 14 the panel granted the claim: Article 71 requires the labour inspector's authorisation to suspend a contract, the employer never sought it, and Article 72(1) therefore did not relieve it of the wage obligation, so 100% was owed for the stand-down. [Para 430][Para 432] Issues 15, 18 and 20 were classified as interest disputes beyond the law; the $10 attendance bonus complied with Notice No. 230; and $106 exceeded the $100 Prakas minimum. [Para 440][Para 450]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may fix the frequency and method of an output incentive scheme as an exercise of management right, but it may not apply that scheme so as to reduce a worker's pay below the statutory minimum wage: the minimum wage under Prakas No. 317 is a fixed monthly entitlement that does not vary with output, and any shortfall must be made up by the employer rather than drawn from bonuses already earned. [Para 268] A contract of employment may not be suspended for lack of work without the labour inspector's prior authorisation; absent authorisation the employer remains liable for the full 100% wage even where no work is given. [Para 427]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement and binds all workers in the enterprise for one year under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099, and that the panel will not resolve problems that have not yet arisen, are general observations on its jurisdiction rather than reasons for the orders made. [Para 357]
