# Case No. 151/14 — T & K Garment Industrie Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 10 July 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Liv Sovannara, worker-appointed; Ang Eng Thong, chair) · Doc ID 139609

## 1. Facts
The employer is a garment factory at Damnak Thom, Chak Angre Krom, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, registered in 2009 and employing about 834 workers. [Para 41][Para 42] Workers struck twice over the refusal to cooperate with overtime, in February 2014 and again from 29 to 31 May 2014. [Para 53] Of three demands put through conciliation on 6 June 2014, two were settled and only the claim for a 2,000 riel daily lunch allowance remained. [Para 10][Para 11] Two factory unions brought the complaint: the Solidarity union, with 560 members and a most-representative certificate valid to 20 December 2014, and the Khmer Youth union, with 210 members. [Para 43][Para 45][Para 47][Para 48]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of 2,000 riel a day to the workers? [Para 49][Para 67]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** rents and market prices have risen; about 80 per cent of garment factories in Cambodia already pay a lunch allowance, including the Kampuchea Hosing factory, Nan Tay and the GDM branch, each at 2,000 riel a day. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 52][Para 77]
**Employer (respondent):** it sews on commission for other companies without direct buyers, earns little, and loses money every month, aggravated by having no direct purchaser, by the two strikes and by low output. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 56][Para 79]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the claim for a 2,000 riel daily lunch allowance was refused, and the panel issued no order on the settled points. [Para 83][Para 84]
The panel first classified the claim. Applying Article 312(2) of the Labour Law and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099/04, it held that a demand found in no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice is a dispute over interests, to be decided on equity, and that this claim met that description. [Para 60][Para 62][Para 67][Para 68] It then addressed standing: because an interest award replaces the collective agreement for a year and removes the right to strike, only the most representative union, or unions together holding more than half the workforce, may bring such a claim. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 73] Here both complainant unions were lawfully registered, one of them held the most-representative certificate, and the panel therefore found they could represent the workers on this interest dispute. [Para 74][Para 75]
On the merits the panel weighed equity. The employer had never paid a lunch allowance, which the parties agreed. [Para 49][Para 76] The workers showed that other factories pay 2,000 riel but produced no evidence that this employer had the capacity to do so. [Para 77][Para 78][Para 80] The employer's account of monthly losses, absence of direct buyers, the two strikes and low output was not contested, and the panel found the current situation unfavourable to imposing the benefit. [Para 79][Para 81][Para 82]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a new benefit that appears in no law, collective agreement, internal rule or established practice is an interest dispute, and an interest dispute may be brought only by the most representative union or by unions together representing more than half the workers, since the resulting award displaces the collective agreement and suspends the right to strike. [Para 68][Para 73][Para 75]
In deciding an interest claim on equity, the union must show not only that comparable employers pay the benefit but that this employer has the capacity to pay it; where capacity is neither evidenced nor conceded and the employer's losses are uncontested, the claim fails. [Para 78][Para 80][Para 82]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that roughly 80 per cent of Cambodian garment factories pay a lunch allowance, with named examples, describes the market but does not create an obligation. [Para 51][Para 52]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's name is printed variously as T & K Garment Industrie Co Ltd, T & K Cambodia Branch and T & K Garment Industrie (Cambodia) Branch, and the registration numbers given conflict (Inv. 1461 E/2009 in the findings, 2561 on the commercial register extract). [Para 1][Para 25][Para 41] The Solidarity union's registration certificate is numbered 2461 in one recital and 2641 in the evidence list. [Para 33][Para 44] The procedural recital describes a telephone complaint received on 5 June 2014 concerning "03 years" of demands, an evident corruption of the number of claims. [Para 9] Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 is quoted twice, once as "Article" and once as "Paragraph", with the Prakas dated 2004 in one place and 2019 in the other. [Para 62][Para 71] Parts of the signature block are lost to OCR noise. [Para 86]
