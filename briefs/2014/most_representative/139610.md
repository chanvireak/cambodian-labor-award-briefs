# Case No. 150/14 — Lean Fa (Cambodia) Garment Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 3 July 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · Doc ID 139610

## 1. Facts
The employer is a garment factory at Prek Samrong, Ta Khmao, Kandal, employing more than 1,000 workers. [Para 39] Workers formed a factory union of the Free Workers Union of Cambodia on 11 January 2014 with 27 members, electing Mr P as president, Mr O as deputy and Ms K as secretary, and notified the employer by EMS on 20 February 2014. [Para 91][Para 92][Para 93] The employer did not renew the six-month contracts of Mr P (expiring 21 April 2014) and Ms K (expiring 8 April 2014). [Para 79][Para 82][Para 88][Para 89] Conciliation of 11 demands on 2 June 2014 failed, 5 points came to the panel, and the employer did not attend the hearing. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 24]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of 2,000 riel a day? [Para 43][Para 103]
2. Whether the employer must pay a 2,000 riel meal allowance for work on public holidays and Sundays? [Para 46][Para 119]
3. Whether the employer must stop discriminating against pregnant workers by letting their fixed-duration contracts expire? [Para 51][Para 156]
4. Whether the employer must grant leave where a worker has a proper reason? [Para 54][Para 161]
5. Whether the transport and accommodation allowance must rise from USD 7 to USD 13 a month? [Para 57][Para 167]
6. Whether the employer must respect the voluntary principle in overtime? [Para 61][Para 173]
7. Whether the employer must set up a nursery (a), and pay a USD 15 monthly milk allowance (b)? [Para 65][Para 183]
8. Whether the attendance bonus must rise from USD 10 to USD 15 a month? [Para 68][Para 201]
9. Whether the attendance bonus may be deducted pro rata, rather than wholly, for authorised absence? [Para 71][Para 208]
10. Whether the employer must take back the two union leaders whose contracts expired? [Para 75][Para 248]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the factory has never paid lunch money and workers cannot eat adequately; overtime runs from 4:00 p.m. to 7:30 or 8:00 p.m. and sometimes until dawn, and refusal is punished at contract renewal; 95 per cent of the workforce is women aged 20 to 30 and there is no nursery; rents rose to USD 25–30 and transport to USD 13–18; USD 10 attendance bonus is too little; and the two leaders' contracts were not renewed because they formed the union. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 59][Para 60][Para 62][Para 64][Para 67][Para 70][Para 248]
**Employer (respondent):** no position was taken; the employer did not attend the hearing and filed no evidence. [Para 24][Para 29][Para 94]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to provide a nursery (or pay the fees of an outside one), to deduct the attendance bonus only pro rata for authorised absence, to pay 2,000 riel meal allowance for voluntary Sunday overtime, and to renew the contracts of the two union leaders; the claims on lunch money, holiday meal allowance, transport allowance, milk allowance, higher attendance bonus, leave facilitation and coercion in overtime were dismissed or not considered. [Para 281][Para 285][Para 286][Para 276][Para 274][Para 275]
On procedure, the panel applied Paragraph 21 of Prakas No. 099/04: an employer properly summoned who fails to appear without good reason does not stop the panel, which may proceed in absentia. The lawyer's request for postponement arrived on Friday 20 June for a Monday hearing, too late, and no colleague was delegated. [Para 95][Para 98][Para 99][Para 100][Para 102] Because the factory union had only a receipt for its registration application of 18 December 2013, it had no legal personality, so the panel could not treat the parties as MoU signatories. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 40] It therefore treated the three workers present as the complainants. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 116][Para 117]
On classification, issues 1, 2(a) of the holiday claim, 5, 7(b), 8 and 2 were held to be claims found in no law, agreement, internal rule or practice. [Para 67][Para 104][Para 105][Para 171][Para 198][Para 205] Under Paragraph 43 of Prakas No. 099 an interest award replaces the collective agreement for a year and removes the right to strike, so only a most representative union or unions representing more than half the workforce may bring such a claim; three individual workers could not, and the panel declined to consider those issues. [Para 112][Para 113][Para 114][Para 117][Para 118]
On Sunday work the panel reasoned that the normal week runs Monday to Saturday, 48 hours, so Sunday work exceeds normal hours and is overtime; item 2 of Notification No. 041/11 therefore entitles the volunteer to 2,000 riel a day. [Para 142][Para 144][Para 149][Para 150][Para 154] Work on a public holiday is different: it is not overtime, the worker receives one extra day's wage under Article 164 and Prakas No. 10, so no meal allowance is owed. [Para 125][Para 127][Para 129][Para 131][Para 140]
On the nursery, Article 186 requires any enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a breastfeeding room and a nursery; where that is impossible the employer must pay the fees of an outside nursery on actual invoices, so the claim was granted. [Para 183][Para 184][Para 189][Para 191][Para 193] On the attendance bonus, the panel found Notification No. 230/12 ambiguous as to whether authorised absence counts, and held that authorised absence suspends the contract under Articles 71 and 72, so only a pro rata deduction is permissible, not the whole USD 10. [Para 216][Para 217][Para 241][Para 244][Para 246][Para 247]
On the two leaders, the panel applied Articles 12 and 279 and its consistent rule that the complainant bears the burden of proving discrimination; the employer never appeared and never contested the EMS notification of the elected leadership, so the panel was satisfied that the non-renewal was connected to forming the union and ordered the contracts renewed. [Para 260][Para 261][Para 263][Para 271][Para 272][Para 273]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Work performed on Sunday is overtime because the statutory normal week is Monday to Saturday, so the meal allowance for overtime is owed; work on a public holiday is not overtime and attracts one extra day's wage instead. [Para 144][Para 149][Para 131]
An interest claim may be brought only by a most representative union or unions representing more than half the workers; individual complainants cannot sustain one, however many grievances they describe. [Para 113][Para 114][Para 117]
Authorised absence suspends the employment contract, so an attendance bonus may be reduced in proportion to the days absent but not forfeited in full. [Para 244][Para 246]
An employer who stays away and leaves the workers' evidence unchallenged risks a finding that a non-renewal was connected to union formation. [Para 271][Para 272]

**Obiter dicta:** although no coercion in overtime was proved, the panel recommended that the employer set up a monitoring mechanism so that no worker who declines overtime suffers reprisal. [Para 179][Para 181]

**Dissenting opinion:** arbitrator Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer, dissented on issue 9, reasoning that a bonus is a discretionary reward whose conditions belong to the giver, so a worker who does not meet the attendance condition cannot claim a pro rata share. [Para 290][Para 297][Para 306][Para 310]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award carries an annexed dissent signed on the same date, and the header names Ing Sothy's dissent before reciting the award. [Para 1][Para 288][Para 311] The recital says conciliation covered 11 points and that 5 were unresolved, yet the hearing invitation speaks of all 11 points and the list at the head of the award omits one demand. [Para 19][Para 22][Para 28] The union's registration is described both as a receipt dated 18 December 2013 and as a certificate issued on 11 October 2012, which are different unions or a copying error. [Para 25][Para 44] The employer's name appears as Lean Fa (Cambodia) Garment Co Ltd and Lean Fa (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd, and the worker's name as both Pheng Sophoeun and Phoeung Sophoeun. [Para 1][Para 39][Para 76][Para 255] The hearing date is once given as Monday 23 June 2013 instead of 2014. [Para 99]
