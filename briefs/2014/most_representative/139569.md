# Award No. 112/14 — T & K Garment Industry Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 06 June 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Tun Si Phan for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139569

## 1. Facts
T & K Garment Industry Co., Ltd is a garment factory at Toul Pongro, Chom Chao, Porsenchey, Phnom Penh, employing about 1,325 workers and paying a lunch allowance of 1,500 riel per worker per day. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 51] A strike on 26 April 2014 pressed 16 demands; 14 were settled at conciliation and two were referred to the panel — a lunch allowance of 4,000 riel a day and the dismissal of the company director. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 14][Para 51] Only 13 workers attended the hearing, and the factory-level union had not yet applied to the Ministry for registration. [Para 44][Para 48][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to pay a lunch allowance of 4,000 riel per day per worker, that is an additional 2,500 riel on the 1,500 riel already paid? [Para 51][Para 87]
2. Whether the workers are entitled to require the employer to dismiss the company director from his post? [Para 57][Para 105]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** 1,500 riel does not buy a midday meal and workers need more to stay healthy enough to work; the director is unreasoning and quick-tempered, swore at workers in Chinese, threw trousers at them, abolished the incentive and "blue board" bonuses, cut piece rates and tightened the leave procedure. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 62][Para 69][Para 115]
**Employer (respondent):** the 1,500 riel was introduced recently after several rounds of negotiation and two registered local unions signed an agreement accepting it, so no more can be paid; the director was only explaining that workers should use the washroom during the lunch break; no worker who was allegedly struck was ever named, and the matter was not raised at conciliation. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 73][Para 82][Para 84]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed on both issues — the panel declined to consider the 4,000 riel lunch allowance and refused the demand that the employer dismiss director Yien Hong Ei; a non-binding award becoming final 8 days after notification. [Para 104][Para 140][Para 141][Para 143][Para 144]

On issue 1 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal regulation or past practice creating an obligation to pay 4,000 riel, and classified the demand as an interest dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍). [Para 88][Para 89] Article 96 of the Labour Law defines a collective agreement as one concluded between an employer and a union organisation representing workers, and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 reserves the right to request a collective agreement to the most representative union. [Para 90][Para 94][Para 96] Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 provides that an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement, applies for one year to all workers in the enterprise and removes their right to strike on that subject; the panel would therefore only entertain such a dispute where the claimant union represents more than half the workforce. [Para 97][Para 98][Para 99] Here the 13 complainants belonged to a newly formed union that had still not applied for registration, so they were not a legal professional organisation and had no standing to bring an interest dispute; the panel noted it had refused jurisdiction on the same ground in 169/11 and 02/11. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 100][Para 102][Para 103]

On issue 2 the panel held that under Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 311 of the Civil Code only the parties to a contract may extinguish the obligations it creates, so a third party cannot terminate the employment contract between employer and worker; the power to end the director's contract lay with the employer alone. [Para 105][Para 107][Para 108][Para 110][Para 112] A worker may demand the removal of another employee only on evidence that the person is dangerous and cannot be kept in the enterprise without endangering others' safety. [Para 113] On Article 2 the panel repeated that managerial authority covers incentive bonuses, piece rates and leave procedures provided it is exercised lawfully and reasonably, following 10/03, 119/09 and 171/13. [Para 126][Para 128][Para 130][Para 133][Para 136] Placing the burden of proof on the party making the allegation, the panel found the workers had produced no precise evidence of who was struck or how, and no showing that the director's conduct made him dangerous; the listed grievances were therefore not a proper ground for dismissal. [Para 116][Para 121][Para 136][Para 137][Para 138]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a benefit above existing practice is an interest dispute that the panel will consider only where it is brought by the most representative union in the enterprise — one registered with the Ministry and representing more than half the workforce — because an award on such a dispute would substitute for the collective agreement and extinguish the right to strike; individual complainants in an unregistered union have no standing and their claim is dismissed unexamined. A demand that the employer dismiss a director or manager fails because only the parties to the employment contract may end it, and decisions on incentive bonuses, piece rates and leave procedure are within the employer's prerogative under Article 2 absent evidence that the person is dangerous to others' safety. [Para 99][Para 102][Para 110][Para 113][Para 136]

**Obiter dicta:** even if the alleged insults and behaviour were proven, the employer — the chief executive, the central-office representative or a legal head senior to the director — ought to take appropriate measures to stop such conduct and secure proper language and behaviour towards workers, so as to keep peace in the workplace. The claim failed on evidence, so this direction was not necessary to the order. [Para 139]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The dispositive section contradicts itself on issue 1: [Para 141] orders that the claim be refused consideration, and [Para 142] immediately orders the employer to pay the 4,000 riel allowance (an extra 2,500 riel). The reasons at [Para 104] and the summary at [Para 140] dismiss the claim, and the brief follows them.
- The director's name is printed "Yien Hong Ei" at [Para 57] and [Para 140] but "Yien Hong Ei Internet" at [Para 143] — the trailing word is OCR debris.
- The award date carries a stray numeral: "ថ្ងៃទី ០៦ ខែមិថុនា ឆ្នាំ ១ ២០១៤" [Para 1], repeated as "ឆ្នាំ ១ ២០១៤" in the extension of time [Para 20].
- The piece-rate chronology is impossible as printed: rates are said to have fallen again "from November 2014" [Para 70], five months after the award and after the 21 May 2014 evidence deadline [Para 19]; the intended year cannot be recovered.
- The company name appears in at least four forms across the file ("T & K Garment Industry Co., Ltd", "T & K Garment In Dasstry", "T & K Sen & K Garment") [Para 14][Para 42][Para 2].
- Workforce figure is garbled — "សរុប/ L- -៥-" precedes "approximately 1,325 persons" [Para 43]; the registered-capital and company-number strings at [Para 42] are likewise corrupted.
- Stray fragments interrupt the reasons at [Para 93] ("..។") and [Para 124] ("ល ។"), and a year intrudes into the text at [Para 108] ("ក្រុមប្រឹក្សា អាជ្ញា 2016 កណ្តាល") and [Para 131] ("អាជ្ញា in 1996").
- The four testifying workers are identified by bare names in the reasons [Para 62][Para 64][Para 69][Para 71]; they are rendered here as Ms V, Ms S, Mr S and Mr S.
