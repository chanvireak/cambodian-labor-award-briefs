# Award No. 307/14 — Cambodian Get Way And Wear (Arbitration Council, 2 December 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Pen Bunhea chair) · Doc ID 139755

## 1. Facts
Cambodian Get Way And Wear, a garment factory of about 348 workers in the Sihanoukville Special Economic Zone, was struck from 22 to 31 October 2014 by workers led by the Khmer Workers' Spirit Trade Union Federation over nine demands; conciliation failed and report No. 0650/14 of 27 October 2014 reached the Secretariat on 30 October 2014. [Para 20][Para 22][Para 42][Para 70] Only two workers attended the hearing, and the panel confined itself to their claims; the union held no registration certificate and filed no written authorisation from the workers it claimed to represent. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44][Para 45] The ninth demand, payment of October wages on 4 November 2014, was withdrawn at the hearing. [Para 12][Para 26]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must dismiss Ms K Thida, a section supervisor, for repeatedly insulting workers? [Para 46]
2. Whether the employer must raise the travel and accommodation allowance by USD 8, to USD 15 a month? [Para 53]
3. Whether the employer must raise the regular attendance bonus by USD 5, to USD 15 a month? [Para 57]
4. Whether the attendance bonus may be reduced pro rata to the days a worker was absent with the employer's permission for personal business? [Para 62]
5. Whether the full attendance bonus must be kept when a worker is absent sick with a valid medical certificate? [Para 66]
6. Whether the employer must pay wages and other benefits for the strike days of 22 to 31 October 2014? [Para 70]
7. Whether the employer must pay a health allowance of USD 5 a month? [Para 75]
8. Whether the employer must pay a skill allowance to trimming, QC Final and single-needle sewing workers? [Para 79]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the supervisor insulted workers repeatedly with inappropriate words and a dismissal request was made in September 2014; travel costs run to USD 15–20 a month while other factories pay USD 10–15; the attendance bonus is part of wages and about 90% of factories keep it during certified sick leave. [Para 47][Para 54][Para 63][Para 67]
**Employer (respondent):** the supervisor was counselled after the September 2014 complaint and has corrected herself; the company has no capacity to raise allowances; the bonus is conditional on regular attendance; no law requires a health allowance; the strike was called without notice and cost export orders. [Para 49][Para 55][Para 60][Para 65][Para 74]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim in issue 1 was dismissed and the claims in issues 2, 3, 5, 6, 7 and 8 were refused; on issue 4 the panel ordered the employer to reduce the USD 10 attendance bonus pro rata to the days of leave it had authorised. [Para 213][Para 214][Para 216][Para 217][Para 219]

Issue 1: Articles 65 of the Labour Law and 664 and 311 of the Civil Code make the contract the product of the parties' consent and alterable only by them; the supervisor's contract is separate and the workers are third parties to it, so they cannot demand her dismissal unless she is shown to be a danger to the enterprise, and no misconduct after September 2014 was proved. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 93][Para 95][Para 98][Para 99]

Issues 2 and 3: Notification No. 230 K.B/S.C.N of 25 July 2012 fixes USD 7 travel and USD 10 attendance bonus as minima, and the employer paid exactly those; a claim above the statutory minimum is an interest dispute which, under Article 96 and article 9 of Prakas No. 305, may be brought only by the most representative union, and the two complainants were not such a union. [Para 100][Para 103][Para 111][Para 118][Para 121][Para 124][Para 130]

Issue 4: the words "regular attendance without absence" in Notification 230 are ambiguous and the panel declined to read them as covering every kind of absence; leave authorised by the employer suspends the contract under Article 71(6) and Article 72, so no wage is due for those days, but the employer may withhold the bonus only in proportion to the days absent, not the whole amount. [Para 137][Para 144][Para 166][Para 170][Para 171]

Issue 5: sick leave certified by a doctor suspends the contract under Article 71(3) and Article 72; following awards 86/06, 62/07 and 125/09 the panel refused the full bonus. [Para 178][Para 181][Para 184][Para 187]

Issue 6: Articles 332 and 334 provide that a strike suspends the contract and no wage is paid, and Article 72 confirms it; the employer hired no replacements, so the exception did not apply, and awards 27/10, 49/05 and 178/12 were followed. [Para 188][Para 192][Para 199][Para 200]

Issues 7 and 8: no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice created either allowance, so both claims exceeded the law and were interest disputes the complainants lacked standing to bring. [Para 201][Para 207][Para 211]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a benefit above the minimum fixed by a Prakas or a ministerial notification is an interest dispute, and the panel will decline to hear it unless it is brought by the union with the most representative status in the enterprise; individual complainants cannot carry it. Where the employer has itself authorised unpaid leave, the contract is suspended for those days and the attendance bonus may be reduced in proportion to the days absent, but not forfeited in full. [Para 114][Para 119][Para 154][Para 170]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that it could not itself supply a meaning for "without absence" in Notification 230 because the Labour Advisory Committee had not interpreted it, and that such claims must be decided case by case. [Para 140][Para 141][Para 145]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The summary at [Para 120] says the travel-allowance claim is refused and then orders the employer to pay USD 15 a month, contradicting the operative order at [Para 214]; the operative order was followed.
- Issue 7 appears in the order as both "USD 5" and "USD 45" in one sentence [Para 218].
- Recurring year corruption: "4 November 2015" for the wage date [Para 12], "28 October 2015 9 2014" [Para 13], the panel's constitution dated "30 October 2022 2016 2014" [Para 23], and "2015" inside the Notification 230 quotation [Para 176].
- The company is printed as "Cambodian Get Way And Wear" on the face and "Cambodia Get Way And Wear" in the body [Para 1][Para 22].
- Arbitrator Ing Sothy's dissenting opinion runs at [Para 221]–[Para 236]; it argues that a conditional bonus is not wage and cannot be apportioned.
- The non-conciliation report lists nine demands but the text numbers item 1 twice, once for a dismissal warning and once for the travel allowance [Para 3][Para 4][Para 5].
