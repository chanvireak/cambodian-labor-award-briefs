# Award No. 170/14 — Lin Wen Ji San Bo Enterprise (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 17 July 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, selected by the employer; Venh Pov, selected by the workers; Kong Phallaph, chair) · Doc ID 139625

## 1. Facts
Lin Wen Ji San Bo Enterprise (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a shoe factory in Samraong, Kandal province, employing 900 workers; it has no local union and no elected employee delegates. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49] The Khmer Youth Voice Trade Union Federation, holding transfers of authority from only 14 workers dated 19 May 2014, filed 17 demands on 28 May 2014; conciliation on 17 June 2014 settled none, and the non-conciliation report No. 891/14 of 24 June 2014 came to the Council. [Para 50][Para 25][Para 26][Para 22] At the hearing the parties agreed 12 of the 17 points, the workers withdrew point 10, and four remained for decision: 7, 11, 14 and 17. [Para 31][Para 32][Para 33]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must let pregnant workers leave 15 minutes early twice a day, at the midday break and at the end of the shift, for their safety? [Para 52][Para 54]
2. Whether the employer must keep a worker's wage and regular attendance bonus unchanged while the worker is on sick leave supported by a proper medical certificate? [Para 58][Para 76]
3. Whether the employer must raise the travel and accommodation allowance by US$6 above the existing US$15 and the regular attendance bonus by US$3 above the existing US$15? [Para 64][Para 83]
4. Whether the employer must organise an election of employee delegates by the first week of August 2014? [Para 67][Para 102]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** ministry guidance and public information on protecting pregnant workers justify 15 minutes twice a day, and 10 minutes is too short; transport and accommodation costs have risen, so the allowances must rise; the law requires employee delegates and the company has never held an election. [Para 55][Para 66][Para 69]
**Employer (respondent):** only 10 pregnant women work at the factory and the 10 minutes already given in two instalments — at the lunch break and at departure — is enough to let them leave without crowding; the company has only just opened and cannot afford higher allowances; it lacks the human resources to hold an election by early August but could manage by the fourth week of August. [Para 56][Para 67][Para 70]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — claim 17 granted, the employer ordered to organise an election of employee delegates under Article 292 of the Labour Law once the award takes effect; claims 7, 11 and 14 dismissed. [Para 113][Para 117][Para 114][Para 115][Para 116]

The panel first limited the scope of the case: because the federation held transfers of authority from just 14 of the 900 workers, any point it decided would bind only those 14. [Para 50][Para 51]

On claim 7 the panel did not reach the merits. Reading Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, it held a party may be represented only by a person holding a written authorisation, and found that no pregnant woman was present at the hearing and none had transferred authority to the federation for this demand. No identifiable worker was therefore making the claim, and it was refused consideration. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 74][Para 75]

On claim 11 the panel found the demand concerned the future: no worker had yet been refused wage or bonus while on certified sick leave, and the workers themselves conceded the claim was "for the future". [Para 76][Para 77] Following Award No. 10/03 Zacksintec and others, the panel held the Council exists to resolve labour disputes that have arisen, not problems that have not yet occurred, so it declined jurisdiction. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 81][Para 82]

On claim 14 the panel found the employer already pays US$9 travel and accommodation allowance and US$12 regular attendance bonus, both above the minimum in points 1 and 2 of Ministry Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 (US$7 and US$10). [Para 83][Para 84][Para 85][Para 86] Finding no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice requiring more, the panel classified the demand as an interest dispute; applying Article 96 and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305, it held such a dispute may only be brought by the most representative union, which a group of 14 workers in a 900-worker enterprise is not. [Para 87][Para 89][Para 92][Para 97][Para 99][Para 100]

On claim 17 the panel applied Article 292 of the Labour Law, which makes holding the election the employer's duty where no delegates exist, and Article 8 of Prakas No. 286 of 5 November 2001, which requires the poll within six months of the enterprise opening; the employer's staffing shortage was not a lawful excuse, and the duty arose once the award took effect. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 107][Para 110][Para 112][Para 113]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The Council will decide a demand only where an identified worker is before it or has authorised the union in writing to claim on their behalf; a union that pleads a benefit for a class of workers — here pregnant employees — none of whom is present or has transferred authority cannot put that claim in issue, and it fails for want of a claimant rather than on the merits. [Para 72][Para 74][Para 75]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations on the interest-dispute category and the most-representative-union precondition under Prakas No. 305 and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 are general statements of jurisdiction rather than holdings on claim 14, which was refused on the merits of the notification anyway. [Para 94][Para 96][Para 97]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The employer's name shifts across the file: លីន វេន ជី សាន់ បូ អេនធើ ប្រាយ ខូ អិលធីឌី on the face [Para 1], "សាន់ បូ អ៊ីនធើ ប្រាយ" in the conciliation documents [Para 22][Para 42], and "លី វេន ជី" in the patent [Para 35]; the trade name "San Bo" is used throughout the union's own papers.
- The conciliation note is dated 8 May 2014 in the employer's evidence list [Para 37] but the conciliation itself is recorded as 17 June 2014 [Para 26][Para 43]; the non-conciliation report number appears as 891/14 កប/កណ and 891/14 កប/ភណ [Para 22][Para 42].
- The number of unresolved points is printed as "១៧N ចំណុច" at [Para 26], with a stray character.
- The employer's evidence list ends at item 3 at [Para 37] while the workers' list is headed "ខែ-" at [Para 38], both indicating lost list entries.
- Paragraph [Para 67] runs the heading of issue 17 into the employer's answer on issue 14 without a break; the issue numbering is recoverable only from [Para 33].
- At [Para 71] the panel quotes Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 in a passage dealing with representation generally, then applies it to the pregnancy claim — a doctrinal fit the source does not explain.
- The decision at [Para 117] cites "មាត្រា ៤២៩២", the digits of Articles 42 and 292 fused by OCR.
