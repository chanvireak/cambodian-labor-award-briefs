# Award No. 217/14 — Unit (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 15 September 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, president appointed by both) · Doc ID 139693

## 1. Facts
Unit (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. is a garment factory at Prek Ho, Ta Khmau, Kandal, employing about 1,000 workers. [Para 73][Para 74] The Khmer Workers' Conscience Union Federation filed 14 demands on 15 July 2014 on behalf of 19 workers whose letters of authority it held; the enterprise union formed on 12 July 2014 was not yet registered with the Ministry. [Para 22][Para 75][Para 83] Conciliation on 01 August 2014 failed on all 14 points, and at the hearing of 19 August 2014 the parties recorded identical positions on issues 5, 6, 11, 12, 13 and 14, so only issues 1, 2, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9 and 10 remained contested. [Para 23][Para 28][Para 29][Para 331] The award was postponed from 01 September to 15 September 2014. [Para 32]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay a 4,000 riel daily lunch allowance (panel issue 1)? [Para 173]
2. Whether the company must pay a 1,500 riel per hour meal allowance for overtime (issue 2)? [Para 194]
3. Whether the company must raise the attendance bonus to USD 15 and deduct it pro rata for authorised personal leave (issue 3)? [Para 201]
4. Whether the company must raise travel and accommodation money to USD 15 per month (issue 4)? [Para 252]
5. Whether the company must keep the attendance bonus whole, or deduct it only pro rata, for sick leave covered by a valid medical certificate (issue 7)? [Para 260]
6. Whether the company must let pregnant workers leave 15 minutes early (issue 8)? [Para 282]
7. Whether the company must allow one full day per month for antenatal checks (issue 9)? [Para 290]
8. Whether the company must issue six-month fixed-term contracts (issue 10)? [Para 298]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** market prices have risen and neighbouring factories pay a lunch allowance of 1,000 to 2,000 riel, 1,500 riel per hour of overtime meal money, attendance bonuses above USD 10 and travel money above USD 10; the company forfeits the whole USD 10 attendance bonus for a single half day of authorised leave or sick leave, and short three-month contracts leave workers afraid of non-renewal. [Para 86][Para 94][Para 101][Para 129][Para 153]
**Employer (respondent):** it pays what Notification No. 230 K.B/S.C.N. of 25 July 2012 requires — USD 10 attendance bonus, 2,000 riel overtime meal money, USD 10 travel money — and has added benefits above the law; Article 5(C) of its internal rules, approved by the Ministry, forfeits the attendance bonus for any absence, and it may not vary contract terms. [Para 111][Para 202][Para 253][Para 268]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issues 3 (in part) and 7 were granted, each ordering pro-rata deduction only; issues 1, 2, 3 (the USD 15 increase), 4, 8, 9 and 10 were dismissed. [Para 338][Para 341][Para 343]

On issue 1 the panel found no law, collective agreement, internal rule, contract or past practice obliging a lunch allowance, so the claim was an interest dispute; because the claimant union held no most-representative certificate the panel declined to consider it, applying Article 96 of the Labour Law, Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 174][Para 176][Para 191][Para 193] The same disposition followed for the overtime meal money (issue 2), since the employer already paid the 2,000 riel required by point 2 of Notification No. 041/11; for the USD 15 travel money (issue 4); for the 15-minute early departure (issue 8); and for the full antenatal day (issue 9), the company already granting two hours per month, which exceeds the law. [Para 197][Para 200][Para 257][Para 289][Para 297]

On issue 3 the panel split the claim. The demand for USD 15 was an interest dispute and was refused. [Para 203][Para 206] The demand that the bonus be reduced only in proportion to days of authorised personal leave was a rights dispute: the attendance bonus is a wage under Article 103, absence with the employer's permission suspends the contract under Article 71(6), and Article 72(1) then relieves the employer of wages for those days only. The panel drew on Notification No. 230, which sets a floor of USD 10 for workers with no absence, and concluded the employer may deduct in proportion to the days absent but may not forfeit the whole bonus. [Para 232][Para 241][Para 248][Para 251]

On issue 7 the panel reached the same result for sick leave. Where a worker is absent through illness with a proper medical certificate, the absence is lawful and the employer may reduce the bonus pro rata only; a total forfeiture under internal rule Article 5(C) would defeat the purpose of the bonus, pressure sick workers to attend, and conflict with Article 72 of the Constitution and Article 12 of the ICESCR on the right to health. [Para 276][Para 280][Para 281]

On issue 10 the panel held that a contract requires concurring offers and acceptances under Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the 2007 Civil Code, that no party may be forced to accept terms it does not agree to, and that the workers' wish for six-month terms could not be imposed on the employer; the three-month practice therefore stood. [Para 310][Para 316][Para 322][Para 327]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus is a wage under Article 103, so where a worker's absence is lawful — authorised personal leave under Article 71(6) or sick leave supported by a valid medical certificate — the employer may reduce the bonus only in proportion to the days absent. An internal rule allowing total forfeiture is applied unlawfully in that situation, whatever ministerial approval it carries. A union's preference for longer fixed-term contracts cannot be ordered, because no party can be compelled to accept contract terms. [Para 232][Para 248][Para 280][Para 316]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that a total forfeiture would deter workers from resting when ill and so depress productivity went beyond what was needed to fix the pro-rata rule. [Para 277][Para 278]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- Arbitrator Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer, filed a dissenting opinion annexed to the award, arguing that the attendance bonus is a "gratification" that cannot be apportioned and that the Ministry-approved internal rule should govern; the annex runs from [Para 350] to [Para 371] and is not part of the majority's reasoning.
- The lunch allowance appears as 4,000 riel in the demand and the order but as "៤២០០ រៀល" at [Para 85] and "២០ ៤០០០ រៀល" at [Para 173].
- The operative order on sick leave prints "១០០ ដុល្លារ" (USD 100) at [Para 281] where USD 10 is meant; [Para 339] likewise misprints the overtime figure.
- The Union's own name is garbled in the header, and conciliation is dated 01 August 2014 at [Para 23] but the report is cited as of 07 August 2014 [Para 19][Para 24]; the panel's constitution carries a duplicated year, "ឆ្នាំ ២០២២ ឆ្នាំ ២០១៤" at [Para 25].
