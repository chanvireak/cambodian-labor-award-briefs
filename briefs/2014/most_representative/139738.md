# Award No. 277/14 — Hotel Cambodiana Phnom Penh (Arbitration Council, 4 December 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Run Saray chair) · Doc ID 139738

## 1. Facts
Cambodiana Hotel Investment (Phnom Penh) PTE. Ltd. runs a hotel, restaurant and tourism business on Sisowath Quay, Phnom Penh, employing approximately 450 workers. [Para 34][Para 37] The Cambodiana Worker Union (CWU) held most representative status under certificate No. 005/13 dated 15 January 2013, valid until 15 January 2015. [Para 41] The fourth collective bargaining agreement ran from 1 January 2011 to 31 December 2012 and, by agreement of both parties, continued to govern until a fifth CBA replaced it. [Para 44][Para 45] Negotiations for a fifth CBA produced agreement on only 5 of 11 issues, and the employer refused to sign the draft until the fourth CBA was reassessed. [Para 49][Para 50]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer negotiated the fifth CBA in good faith and whether it must sign that CBA? [Para 41][Para 86]
3. Whether the employer must pay US$300 to a pregnant worker for a normal delivery and US$600 where the delivery requires an operation? [Para 62][Para 112]
4. Whether the employer must pay a monthly wage of at least US$170? [Para 66][Para 128]
5. Whether the employer must comply with Clause 9(A) of the fourth CBA dated 22 December 2010 and increase wages each year? [Para 70][Para 138]
6. Whether the employer must provide an annual bonus equal to one month's salary (13th month) at the end of each year? [Para 73][Para 141]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer repeatedly deferred negotiation — the October 2012 request slipped to March and then to July 2013 — reached agreement on five issues on 5 March 2014 and then refused to sign the draft; wages are low and inflation erodes them; other hotels pay a delivery allowance; and the 13th month bonus paid in 2010 and 2011 must continue. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 49][Para 68][Para 73]
**Employer (respondent):** every delay was notified and explained by the ASEAN summit and the General Manager's absence, and the employer attended every session and made counter-offers; it will not sign before the fourth CBA is reassessed; it already pays at least US$100; and it has no legal basis, and no profit, to fund a delivery allowance or a 13th month bonus. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 69][Para 82][Para 99]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to comply with Clause 9(A) of the fourth CBA dated 22 December 2010; the demands to sign the fifth CBA, for the US$300/US$600 delivery payments, for a US$170 minimum wage and for a 13th month bonus were rejected. [Para 153][Para 156]

On issue 1 the panel tested the employer's conduct against Clause 11 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, which obliges the parties to follow an orderly process, to make considered offers and counter-offers, to give the union facilities and to supply relevant information. [Para 87][Para 88] It found each limb satisfied: delays were notified with reasons, the employer attended every session, five of eleven issues were agreed, and the employer twice wrote demanding written comments on its draft — so there was no sufficient evidence of bad faith. [Para 90][Para 91][Para 94][Para 102][Para 103] On the demand for signature, the panel applied Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code, as it had in Award No. 170/11 (M.V, Kampong Chhnang): a contract is the matching of intentions, formed when an offer and an acceptance conform, and no party can force another to accept terms it does not agree with. [Para 105][Para 106] Signing was therefore the employer's right to accept or refuse, and the demand was rejected. [Para 108][Para 112]

On issue 3 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, the CBA, internal rules, employment contracts, agreement or past practice obliging a delivery payment, and classified the claim as an interest dispute. [Para 113] It confirmed that only a union with most representative status, or unions representing more than half the workforce, may bring such a dispute, and that the CWU qualified. [Para 116][Para 122][Para 123] On equity the panel found the workers' reasons — encouragement, and the practice of the InterContinental and Sunway hotels — insufficient, and noted the employer's inability to pay. [Para 125][Para 126][Para 127]

On issue 4 the parties gave conflicting figures (US$75 against US$100) and neither produced a document; applying the rule that the party alleging bears the burden of proof, the panel found the minimum wage lay between US$75 and US$100 but had no basis to order US$170. [Para 129][Para 131][Para 133][Para 135][Para 136]

On issue 5 the panel held that although the fourth CBA expired on 31 December 2012, the parties' agreement to keep applying it bound them as a contract, so the employer was obliged to honour every clause, including Clause 9(A)'s annual increase tied to the consumer price index, which it had not done. [Para 138][Para 140][Para 141]

On issue 6 the panel read the letters of 24 December 2010 and 30 December 2011 as covering 2010 and 2011 only, found no source of obligation for a recurring bonus, treated the claim as an interest dispute and rejected it on equity for want of supporting evidence. [Para 148][Para 149][Para 150][Para 153]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A party cannot be ordered to sign a collective bargaining agreement it has not accepted: under Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code a contract requires matching offer and acceptance, so an employer's refusal to sign, even after partial agreement, is lawful. But where the parties have agreed to keep applying an expired CBA until a new one is concluded, that agreement is itself a contract and every clause of the old CBA remains enforceable, including a clause requiring an annual wage increase. A claim for a benefit found in no law, CBA, internal rule, contract or practice is an interest dispute, which the panel decides on equity and rejects where the claimant offers no evidence. [Para 106][Para 140][Para 150]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that an award settling an interest dispute takes the place of a CBA for one year and removes the right to strike over future benefits, and its reference to Awards 81/04 (Evergreen) and 98/04 (Great Union), are general statements on the panel's jurisdiction over interest disputes. [Para 121][Para 123]
