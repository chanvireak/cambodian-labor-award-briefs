# Award No. 273/14 — Global A Paralee Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 20 October 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Kong Phalha chair) · Doc ID 139739

## 1. Facts
Global A Paralee Co Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ក្លូប័ល អា ប៉ារែលីមីធីត) cuts garments at Trapeang Thleung, Chom Chao, Phnom Penh, with approximately 2,200 workers. [Para 53][Para 54] The Cambodian Union of the Global A Paralee garment factory was registered on 26 September 2012 and had held a leadership election that the Ministry of Labour had not yet recognised. [Para 55][Para 56] The Federation of Cambodian Unions received a transfer of rights from 342 workers of the company by letter dated 19 August 2014, and those workers are the claimants here. [Para 57][Para 59] Both parties had signed a memorandum of understanding dated 3 October 2012 on improving industrial relations in the garment industry, which makes panel decisions binding on the parties for rights disputes and non-binding for interest disputes. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 33]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a meal allowance of 4,000 riel per day to the workers? [Para 60][Para 95]
2. Whether the employer must raise the regular attendance bonus from USD 10 to USD 20 per month? [Para 68][Para 114]
3. Whether the employer must raise the travel and accommodation allowance from USD 7 to USD 15 per month? [Para 72][Para 124]
5. Whether the employer must stop deducting tax from the workers' monthly wages? [Para 80][Para 134]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** monthly wages are low and do not cover their expenses; other factories pay a meal allowance of 2,000 to 4,000 riel a day, a higher attendance bonus and 8 to 12 dollars of travel money; housing alone costs them 25 to 30 dollars a month, and 7 dollars under the Ministry's notification is not enough. [Para 62][Para 70][Para 74][Para 75]
**Employer (respondent):** the company already grants benefits better than the law — a base wage of 105 dollars, a 10 dollar monthly incentive, a skills allowance of 4 to 12 dollars and a productivity bonus of 10 to 20 dollars — and cannot afford more while orders are falling. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 66]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all four remaining demands were rejected, in separate awards for union members and for non-members, both non-binding. [Para 147][Para 148][Para 149][Para 150][Para 156]

The panel first separated the case in two, because the parties were signatories of the 3 October 2012 memorandum: decisions on rights disputes bind them, while decisions on interest disputes may be opposed. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 31] It then defined the categories — a rights dispute concerns a right set by law, internal rules, past practice, a collective agreement or an agreement; an interest dispute concerns a claim for something none of those sources provides, and is decided on equity. [Para 92][Para 93]

On issue 1 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, a collective agreement, an agreement between the parties, the internal rules or past practice obliging the employer to pay a 4,000 riel meal allowance, and classified the demand as an interest dispute. [Para 96][Para 97] It then addressed standing: under Article 96 of the Labour Law and Clause 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, only the union holding most representative status may negotiate a collective agreement for all workers, and under Clause 43 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 an award on an interest dispute takes the place of a collective agreement for one year and extinguishes the right to strike over future benefits. [Para 98][Para 101][Para 104][Para 105] Because that consequence reaches every worker in the enterprise, the panel held it cannot decide an interest dispute unless the claimant holds most representative status or the claimant unions together represent more than half the workforce. [Para 106] The Federation here was not the most representative union, so it lacked standing, and the demand was rejected. [Para 111][Para 112][Para 113]

On issue 2 the panel applied Clause 2 of Ministry notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012, which requires at least USD 10 of attendance bonus for a worker with no absences, and found the employer already paid USD 10. [Para 114][Para 115][Para 116] No source obliged the employer to pay USD 20, so the excess was an interest claim beyond the law, and it failed for the same lack of standing. [Para 117][Para 118][Para 121][Para 123]

On issue 3 the panel applied Clause 1 of the same notification, which fixes USD 7 of travel and accommodation money, and found the employer paid exactly USD 7. [Para 124][Para 125] It noted its own earlier award No. 155/12 (Global A Paralee, 5 September 2012) had already refused a USD 9 top-up on the same facts, found no source obliging USD 15, and rejected the demand as an interest claim by a union without standing. [Para 78][Para 126][Para 130][Para 133]

On issue 5 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, a collective agreement, an agreement or internal rules obliging the employer to stop withholding wage tax, the deduction being made under Cambodian law as set by the Ministry of Economy and Finance; it treated the demand as an interest claim and rejected it on the same standing ground. [Para 136][Para 138][Para 142][Para 144]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the employer already satisfies the statutory floor — the minimum attendance bonus and travel allowance fixed by Ministry notification No. 230 — a demand for a larger amount is not a rights dispute but an interest dispute. The panel will not decide an interest dispute at all unless the claimant is the most representative union or represents more than half the workforce, because such an award replaces the collective agreement for one year and removes the right to strike; a federation to which workers have transferred their rights does not thereby acquire that standing. [Para 93][Para 106][Para 116][Para 125][Para 112]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that the union had elected a new leadership not yet recognised by the Ministry is recorded but played no part in the outcome. [Para 56]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- Prakas No. 099 is dated "21 April 2019 2004" with a doubled year [Para 89], and the memorandum of understanding is cited in the operative part only as "dated ... 2012" with the day and month lost [Para 146].
- The chair arbitrator is printed គង់ ផល្ហៈ on the face of the award [Para 1] and គង់ ផល្លៈ in the signature block [Para 157]; the employer's name shifts between ក្លូប័ល អា ប៉ារែលីមីធីត, កូប័ល អា ប៉ារែលី មីធីត and the short form ក្រុមហ៊ុន ក្លូប័ល អា ប៉ារែល [Para 53][Para 55].
- Issue 1 is pleaded as 4,000 riel "in one time" in the non-conciliation report but "in one day" in the reasons [Para 2][Para 61], and the attendance bonus is described as payable "in one year" at [Para 115] against "one month" at [Para 68].
- Two worker-facing tables omit issue 4 and 6 because those were settled at the hearing, so the numbering jumps from 3 to 5 throughout the decision [Para 27][Para 28]; "ចំណុច វិវាទ មី ៥" and "មី ៣" are misprinted for "ទី" [Para 134][Para 149].
- Stray characters and duplicated clauses litter the text — a "2016" inside the union registration sentence [Para 55], a doubled "ដោយ លើក មូលហេតុ" at [Para 76], and copied fragments of English at [Para 86] and [Para 109]. The five named workers who transferred their rights are reduced here to initials.
