# Award No. 216/14 — Great On Nhor Tech Style Fek Thery Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 4 September 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Nan, appointed by the workers; Kong Phallha, president appointed by both) · Doc ID 139692

## 1. Facts
Great On Nhor Tech Style Fek Thery Co., Ltd. is a garment factory at Svay Rolum, Sa'ang district, Kandal, employing about 1,890 workers. [Para 85][Para 87] The claimant is the enterprise's Democratic Union of Garment Workers of Cambodia, which held most-representative certificate No. 129 of 17 August 2012, valid to 17 August 2014. [Para 89] The union filed 13 demands on 15 July 2014; conciliation on 07 August 2014 left 13 unresolved, and at the hearing of 14 August 2014 the parties settled five (issues 1, 8, 9, 10 and 12), leaving eight for decision. [Para 33][Para 39][Para 40] The dispute concerns the weaving section's loss of work from February to August 2014 and a series of benefit claims. [Para 90][Para 96]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay full wages to weaving workers for the months when it had no work for them (issue 2 in the panel's list)? [Para 179]
2. Whether the company must convert the fixed-term contracts of workers with 12 months' service to indefinite-term contracts (issue 3)? [Para 201]
3. Whether the company must pay a health-care allowance of USD 8 per month (issue 4)? [Para 215]
4. Whether the company must pay USD 1,000 per month into a workers' health-care fund (issue 5)? [Para 251]
5. Whether the company must pay 50% of wage and allowances for 90 days of maternity leave to women with less than one year's service (issue 6)? [Para 263]
6. Whether the company must pay milk money of USD 15 per month rather than USD 8 (issue 7)? [Para 292]
7. Whether the company must provide the worker delegates with a separate meeting room and equipment (issue 11)? [Para 310]
8. Whether the company must allow one full day per month for antenatal checks instead of half a day (issue 13)? [Para 334]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company suspended work intermittently for about 130 weaving workers from February to August 2014 without the labour inspector's permission and paid only 50% of base wage, though the 2011 agreement promised 100% where no notice was given; women with under a year's service received no maternity pay, and a worker named Phallin lost her maternity benefit when her contract was renewed. [Para 97][Para 99][Para 127]
**Employer (respondent):** it applied the 23 August 2011 agreement giving 50% of base wage plus seniority bonus, attendance bonus of USD 10 and USD 7 travel money whenever work stopped; it did not seek a suspension order because this was performance of an agreement, not a suspension; it also denies discriminating against union members and says it lacks the means to pay the other claims. [Para 92][Para 98][Para 112]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the wage-suspension claim was granted with an order to pay; the other seven were dismissed. [Para 359][Para 362]

On the wage-suspension issue the panel held the claim a rights dispute, since suspension of the employment contract is governed by the Labour Law. [Para 179][Para 180] Article 71(11) allows suspension for economic difficulty for no more than two months and places it under the labour inspector's supervision; Article 72(1) excuses the wage obligation only where a provision says so. [Para 183][Para 188] An employer that follows the statutory suspension procedure owes no wage, but one that does not must pay 100% even where there is no work, as held in Award No. 53/08-YUNG VA and No. 42/09-RIVER REACH TIP SPAY. [Para 189][Para 190] The 23 August 2011 agreement giving 50% is null under Article 13(1) of the Labour Law, because it is less favourable than the law and the employer cannot rely on it to escape the duty to seek the inspector's approval. [Para 192][Para 195][Para 199] The panel therefore ordered payment of the missing 50% of base wage to the 130 weaving workers according to the actual days without work. [Para 200]

On contract conversion, Article 67(2) caps a fixed-term contract, and its renewals, at two years, beyond which the contract becomes indefinite; the claimants had only 12 months' service, so they had no right to conversion. [Para 202][Para 212][Para 213]

On the USD 8 health allowance the panel found that the USD 5 health allowance had been absorbed into the base wage by Notification No. 103 of 09 April 2013, and that Prakas No. 317 raised the minimum to USD 100; no law, agreement or past practice obliged a separate USD 8 payment, so the claim was an interest dispute, and it was dismissed for want of evidence. [Para 215][Para 219][Para 223][Para 250]

On the USD 1,000 fund the same result followed: no rule obliged a USD 1,000 monthly fund, and the claim rested on assertion only. [Para 251][Para 261][Para 262] On maternity pay, Article 183 grants half wages during maternity leave only to women with at least one year's service; the claim was an interest dispute and was rejected for lack of specific evidence, although the panel observed that seniority is not broken by a 5% termination payment and a one-day break before renewal, so a proved case would have succeeded. [Para 265][Para 275][Para 289][Para 291]

On the delegates' meeting room, Article 5 of Prakas No. 286 requires the employer to provide delegates with a workplace, meeting place and suitable equipment; the company's hall seats 20 to 30 with tables and chairs, so the claim failed, but the panel added that the CCTV must not run during delegates' meetings because it would chill their freedom to meet. [Para 312][Para 313][Para 331][Para 333] On antenatal time, half a day per month already exceeds the law, and no evidence showed it to be insufficient; the claim was an interest dispute and was dismissed, with a recommendation that extra time be granted case by case. [Para 336][Para 354][Para 358]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that stops work without obtaining the labour inspector's approval cannot rely on a private agreement to pay half wages: Article 13(1) makes such an agreement null as less favourable than the Labour Law, and the employer must pay the full wage for every day of enforced idleness, whatever its own financial difficulty. Conversely, a fixed-term worker reaches indefinite status only when the original term and its renewals together exceed two years. [Para 189][Para 195][Para 199][Para 211]

**Obiter dicta:** the remarks that CCTV should be switched off during delegate meetings, that extra antenatal time should be granted on request, and that seniority survives a 5% payout and one-day break, did not alter any order. [Para 289][Para 329][Para 356]
