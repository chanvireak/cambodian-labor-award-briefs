# Award No. 158/14 — BTU Full Spring Footwear (Arbitration Council, 24 July 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Ing Sothy (employer-appointed, dissenting), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Pen Bunchhea (president) · Doc ID 139616

## 1. Facts
BTU Full Spring Footwear is a shoe factory in Takeo province with about 1,500 workers. [Para 93][Para 94] After a conciliation that settled two demands, the parties reached agreement at the hearing on seven more, the workers withdrew issues 1 and 18, and 11 demands remained — issues 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 17 and 19. [Para 28][Para 41][Para 42][Para 43] Workers struck from 13 to 24 June 2014; the Council ordered the strike suspended by order No. 035/14 of 17 June 2014 with a return to work from 19 June, the strike continued, and it ended on the afternoon of 24 June 2014. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 34][Para 37] The parties chose a binding award for rights disputes only. [Para 44]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must raise the attendance bonus from USD 10 to USD 15 per month (issue 2)? [Para 102]
2. Whether the company must pay a lunch allowance of 4,000 riel per day (issue 4)? [Para 112]
3. Whether the company must deduct the attendance bonus in proportion to the days of absence it has authorised, rather than forfeiting it entirely (issue 5)? [Para 118][Para 210]
4. Whether the company must issue fixed-term contracts of one year instead of three months (issue 6)? [Para 127][Para 254]
5. Whether the company must pay a health-care allowance of USD 5 per month (issue 7)? [Para 133]
6. Whether the company must pay the overtime meal allowance weekly rather than monthly (issue 8)? [Para 141][Para 286]
7. Whether the company must raise the overtime meal allowance from 2,000 to 4,000 riel per day (issue 9)? [Para 147][Para 307]
8. Whether the company must let pregnant workers leave 15 minutes before the end of the shift rather than 10 (issue 10)? [Para 153][Para 315]
9. Whether the company must raise the transport and accommodation allowance from USD 7 to USD 10 per month (issue 17)? [Para 161][Para 325]
10. Whether the company must pay daily wages and the attendance bonus for the eight days of strike (issue 19)? [Para 166][Para 335]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Wages are too low for rising food and transport costs, and other factories on National Road 3 pay more — a USD 15 attendance bonus, a 4,000 riel lunch allowance, a USD 5 health allowance and USD 10 in transport and lodging; the three-month contracts are covertly coercive because they can be ended at any time; ten minutes is not enough for a pregnant worker to reach the gate. [Para 104][Para 105][Para 113][Para 129][Para 156][Para 163] Wages should be kept for the strike days because the strike was caused by a section chief throwing a shoe at a pregnant worker and by the company's anti-union discrimination. [Para 169][Para 338]
**Employer (respondent):** The factory opened on 1 March 2014, has spent heavily on buildings, and its workers are still in training without exportable output; it applies Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 (USD 10 attendance bonus, USD 7 transport) and Notification No. 041/11 on overtime meals, and it has no legal duty to pay more. [Para 106][Para 114][Para 138][Para 165][Para 325] No new workers were hired during the strike and no goods were sent out for subcontracting. [Para 168][Para 176][Para 348]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the company to deduct the attendance bonus in proportion to authorised absences and to pay the overtime meal allowance weekly; it declined to consider the remaining eight demands. [Para 358][Para 360]
The panel began by classifying each demand. Claims turning on the interpretation or application of the Labour Law or a collective agreement are rights disputes decided according to law; all others are interest disputes decided by equity. [Para 105][Para 108] For interest disputes it applied Article 96 and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305: only the most representative union may bring such a claim, and here the six federations held authority from 471 of 1,500 workers with no lawful local union in existence, so the panel declined to consider them. [Para 194][Para 196][Para 199][Para 200] On that ground it refused the attendance bonus increase, the lunch allowance, the extra overtime meal money, the extra five minutes for pregnant workers and the transport increase. [Para 186][Para 203][Para 314][Para 327][Para 334] The health allowance demand was rejected on the same basis without a separate order. [Para 140]
On the attendance bonus deduction (issue 5) the panel held the bonus to be a wage under Article 103, so it could not be forfeited outright; an absence authorised by the employer suspends the contract under Article 71(6) and Article 72(1), which removes the wage for those days but not the whole bonus. The company was therefore ordered to deduct only in proportion to the days absent. [Para 245][Para 247][Para 250][Para 252][Para 253]
On the overtime meal allowance (issue 8), Article 2 of Notification No. 041/11 gives 2,000 riel per day of overtime or one free meal, and fixes no payment date; the panel read that as requiring payment within a reasonable period so the worker can actually buy food when working overtime, and ordered weekly payment. [Para 287][Para 289][Para 295][Para 306]
On the one-year contracts (issue 6) the panel applied Article 65 of the Labour Law and Articles 664, 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code: a contract arises from matching offer and acceptance, and neither party may impose a form or term the other has not accepted. The workers could propose a longer term when contracting, and the employer's refusal was equally lawful; the demand was dismissed. [Para 258][Para 270][Para 280][Para 285]
On strike pay (issue 19) the panel applied Article 332(1), under which the contract is suspended during a strike and wages are not paid, and held that this applies whether or not the strike was lawful. [Para 339][Para 342][Para 345] Article 334 creates an exception where the employer hires replacements for strikers, but the workers' evidence — photographs of a blocked container truck — could not show that the truck carried the company's goods or that goods went to a subcontractor, so the exception was not made out and the demand failed. [Para 335][Para 354][Para 355][Para 357] Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented on issue 5, arguing from dictionary definitions that a bonus is by nature conditional, that its owner sets the conditions, and that a worker who does not meet them cannot claim a proportional share. [Para 372][Para 378][Para 389]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus is a wage, so where the employer has itself authorised an absence the contract is merely suspended and the bonus may be reduced only in proportion to the days absent; by contrast, during a strike no wages are owed at all, lawful or not, unless the employer hired replacements. [Para 252][Para 342][Para 345]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remarks on how long a pregnant worker needs to walk from the production building to the gate, and its computation of the distances involved, did not affect the outcome because the demand failed for want of union standing. [Para 156][Para 157][Para 158]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Issue 7 (the USD 5 health allowance) is reasoned and refused at paras 133–140 but appears in neither the rights nor the interest section of the final order. [Para 140][Para 358][Para 363] The workforce is given as about 1,500 at para 94 and "more than 1,600" at para 144. [Para 94][Para 144] The strike from 13 to 24 June 2014 is described as lasting eight days, though that span is twelve. [Para 167] The suspension order is No. 035/14 at para 33 and No. 025/14 at para 35. [Para 33][Para 35] The order on issue 2 states the total as USD 20 where the demand was USD 15, and the order on issue 17 gives the existing allowance as USD 07 and USD 10 in the same sentence. [Para 201][Para 367] The list of federations shifts between seven and six, one having been absent from the hearing. [Para 95][Para 97] The signature block has lost the employer-appointed arbitrator's name to noise. [Para 369]
