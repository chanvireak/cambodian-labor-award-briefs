# Award No. 273/13 — Doung Duo Tek Stay (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 15 January 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, An Vira, Tang Tri presiding) · Doc ID 139922

## 1. Facts
Doung Duo Tek Stay (Cambodia) is a garment factory that began operating in July 2012 and employs about 2,144 workers [Para 50]. On 9 December 2013 the employer itself asked the Labour Disputes Department to help settle 14 points raised by the workforce; conciliation settled 10 and left 4, and the workers then withdrew point 3, leaving points 1, 2 and 4 [Para 15][Para 16][Para 22][Para 23]. The union behind the demands, the Khmer Union of Workers for Development at Doung Duo Tek Stay, had lodged a registration application on 2 December 2013 but held no registration certificate on the hearing date; only two workers, Mr S (elected president) and Mr B (elected secretary), attended, accompanied by federation officials [Para 27][Para 28][Para 52].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must provide transport home for workers who leave at 4 pm without working overtime, as it does for those who do work overtime? [Para 126]
2. Whether the employer must pay the skill bonus ("machine bonus", USD 20 a month) on a transparent basis tied to attendance, so that it is not cut when leave is lawfully taken? [Para 147]
4. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of USD 10 a month? [Para 179]

The workers withdrew their issue 3 at the hearing, so the panel decided the three points above only [Para 22][Para 23].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Workers who leave at 4 pm for pressing personal reasons must pay their own fare home while overtime workers are driven back, which indirectly pressures them to work overtime; the skill bonus is awarded without any clear or consistent method and is now cut more often than before when lawful leave is taken; and a USD 10 lunch allowance should be paid [Para 57][Para 60][Para 77][Para 78][Para 102].
**Employer (respondent):** The company runs 40 vehicles for the journey to work and for overtime workers going home, and cannot extend this to the 100-odd workers who leave at 4 pm because they live scattered up to 20 km away on different village roads [Para 61][Para 62][Para 66][Para 69]. The skill bonus is a discretionary incentive assessed by team leaders on skill, attendance, cooperation and productivity, which no document fixes [Para 79][Para 83][Para 84]; and no law requires a lunch allowance, the USD 5 already paid being better than the legal minimum [Para 104][Para 105].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — no order was made against the employer. The claim in issue 2 was dismissed as a right dispute, and that part of the award became final and binding immediately under the parties' agreement of 31 December 2013; the claims in issues 1 and 4 were refused consideration as interest disputes brought by claimants without representative capacity, that part becoming final eight days after notification if no objection was filed with the Minister [Para 183][Para 184][Para 185][Para 187].

The threshold finding was standing. The factory union held only a receipt for its registration application dated 2 December 2013 and no certificate, so under Article 268 of the Labour Law it was not yet a lawful professional organisation and could not represent members [Para 27][Para 29][Para 118]. Prakas No. 099 Article 19 lets a party be represented only by a person holding a written authorisation, and the two workers produced none for the other workers, so the panel held that only Mr S and Mr B were claimants and it would decide for those two alone [Para 119][Para 123][Para 125].

On issue 2 the panel found a right dispute, because the bonus turns on the employer's powers of management and direction under Article 2 of the Labour Law, exercised lawfully and reasonably [Para 150][Para 157][Para 158]. On the evidence the workers could not show that the team leaders' assessment actually deprived them of a bonus they were entitled to, and payroll sheets for September to November 2013 showed differences among workers with identical hours as well as full bonuses for some who had not worked full hours [Para 88][Para 94][Para 97][Para 169][Para 170]. The claim was therefore dismissed, with a recommendation that the employer adopt and publish a clear written method of calculation [Para 173][Para 178].

On issues 1 and 4 the panel classified both as interest disputes: no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice obliged the employer to carry every worker home after a 4 pm departure or to pay USD 10 for lunch, and the applicable notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 requires only USD 7 of travel and accommodation allowance, which was paid [Para 133][Para 134][Para 180][Para 181]. Because Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 makes an interest award a collective agreement binding on the whole workforce, only the most representative union, or unions together representing more than half the workers, may bring such a claim [Para 139][Para 141][Para 142]. Two workers were not enough, so the panel refused to consider those claims at all [Para 144][Para 145][Para 146][Para 182].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that has filed a registration application but holds no registration certificate is not a professional organisation recognised by Article 268 of the Labour Law and cannot represent workers before the panel; and where a claim would produce an award binding on the entire workforce, the panel will decline to consider it unless it is brought by the most representative union or by unions together representing more than half the workers in the enterprise [Para 29][Para 123][Para 142][Para 145].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the skill bonus benefits both sides as an incentive and that the employer ought to fix, publish and explain a clear method of computing it, though this was not ordered [Para 171][Para 173][Para 177]. It also noted that the USD 5 lunch payment and USD 7 travel and accommodation payment were already more favourable than the legal minimum [Para 105][Para 128][Para 132].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The lunch figure is printed three ways: USD 10 a month in the claim (line 101), "USD 5 a day" in the reasoning at line 179, and "USD 1000 a month" in the disposition at line 186. Line 147 quotes Article 2 with a stray "2015" inserted into the text, and lines 148 to 149 are reduced to single syllables ("ល ។"), leaving that quotation fragmentary. The company's registration number appears as "Inv. 0926 E/2012" at line 49 and as No. 2274 at line 40. Page furniture and stray glyphs intrude on the reasoning at lines 126, 130, 147 and 161, where duplicated clauses interrupt the sentence. The union's own certificate is absent from the record and the award rests on the application receipt alone. Individual workers' names appear in the award but are reduced here to Mr S and Mr B by policy.
