# Award No. 278/13 — Meng Da Footwear Industry Steel (Arbitration Council, 24 January 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouchhun, An Non, Ang Eng Thong presiding) · Doc ID 139926

## 1. Facts
Meng Da Footwear Industry Steel Co., Ltd has made footwear since 2010 and employs more than 4,000 workers [Para 54][Para 55]. The Federation of Unions of Workers brought five demands on 21 November 2013; conciliation on 13 December failed on all five, the file reached the Council on 26 December, and at the hearing one point (4) was settled and two (3 and 5) were withdrawn, leaving points 1 and 2 [Para 19][Para 20][Para 23][Para 24][Para 25]. The factory union, Meng Da EVC, had obtained ministry recognition of a new leadership on 19 September 2013, but no union in the company is the most representative [Para 56][Para 57].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a separate "technical skill allowance" in addition to the A, B, C, D and T bonuses it already pays? [Para 58][Para 114]
2. Whether the employer must keep paying the "meeting money" it formerly paid, now that meetings have been moved from outside working hours into working hours? [Para 73][Para 132]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Technically skilled workers should receive a technical allowance reflecting their actual skill, separate from the A, B, C, D and T grading bonuses; the monthly payslip carries "technical skill allowance" and "ABC bonus" in two separate boxes, and the company's own policy document speaks of a technical skill allowance, yet the box has always been printed as "00" [Para 63][Para 65][Para 66][Para 68][Para 70]. On meetings, moving them into working hours has cut their above-target bonus and lowered morale, because the meetings are frequent and include scolding and insults [Para 94][Para 95][Para 96].
**Employer (respondent):** The ABC bonus is the technical bonus: A pays USD 10, B USD 8, C USD 5, D USD 2 and the special T grade USD 13 a month, and the company cannot pay a second technical allowance on top [Para 4][Para 71][Para 72]. The earlier practice of holding meetings outside working hours broke the two-hour overtime limit under the Labour Law and was corrected by holding them inside working hours instead [Para 98][Para 99].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both remaining claims were refused. The claim for a separate technical allowance (issue 1) was refused consideration as an interest dispute and the claim to retain meeting money (issue 2) was dismissed as a right dispute; the award is binding, took effect immediately and is enforceable against the parties under their agreement of 9 January 2014 [Para 156][Para 157][Para 158].

The panel found the ABC bonus to be a technical payment graded by skill: the policy document sets A at USD 10, B at USD 8, C at USD 5, D at USD 2 and T at USD 13 a month, with conditions on attendance and pro-rata reduction for partial months [Para 59][Para 60][Para 115][Para 116]. No law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice obliged the employer to pay a second technical allowance on top of that, so the demand was classified as an interest dispute [Para 118][Para 119]. Because an interest award binds the whole workforce, only the most representative union may bring one, and the panel found that no union at the factory held that status [Para 124][Para 127][Para 129][Para 130]. The claim was therefore refused [Para 131].

On meetings, the panel accepted that fixing when meetings are held falls within the employer's power of management under Article 2, provided it is exercised lawfully and reasonably [Para 136][Para 139]. Meetings held inside the normal eight hours attract no extra payment, because the monthly wage already pays for those hours; the employer's decision to move them was lawful and reasonable, and the former practice of meeting outside hours had itself breached the overtime limit [Para 144][Para 148][Para 149][Para 98]. The claim to retain the meeting money was dismissed [Para 155][Para 157].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a payment that no law, collective agreement, internal rule or established practice provides for is an interest dispute, which the panel will not consider unless it is brought by the most representative union in the enterprise; and an employer may move meetings from outside working hours into normal working hours without paying extra, since the monthly wage already covers those hours [Para 118][Para 124][Para 129][Para 144][Para 149].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that meetings twice a day of five to ten minutes each were frequent and that the employer had given no reason why such frequency was necessary; it observed that excessive meetings can harm morale and productivity, and that if workers were in fact scolded or insulted at meetings the employer should use appropriate language and avoid applying pressure [Para 150][Para 151][Para 152][Para 154].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The claimed basic wage is printed as "USD 85 in one year month" at line 7 against the employer's USD 80 a month at line 8, and the same monthly/annual confusion recurs elsewhere, so the figures cannot be reconciled from the text. The corporate name appears in several forms ("ម៉េង ដា ហ្វូត វៀរ អ៊ីន ដាស់ ស្ទ្រីល", "Meng Da Footwear In Das Steel", "Meng Da EVC"), and the union is called both EVC and EVCA. The employer's authority letter is dated 9 January 2013 at line 35 although the hearing was on 9 January 2014. The technical bonus table at lines 58 to 59 is largely destroyed by OCR and cannot be reproduced as a schedule; only the A/B/C/D/T amounts survive. Lines 114 and 132 to 135 repeat fragments of the issue headings, and page furniture such as "-៦-", "-១២-" and "២០" interrupts the text. A single worker's payslip entries at lines 86 to 93 are the only evidence of meeting money, and the individual is not named here by policy.
