# Award No. 315/14 — Zhu Crown Manufacturing (Cambodia) Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 16 December 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, employer-appointed; On Non, worker-appointed; Kong Phalha, chair) · Doc ID 139762

## 1. Facts
Two federations — Voice of Workers of Cambodia and Pride of Khmer Workers — filed collective complaints on 13 October 2014 (eight demands) and 1 November 2014 (seven demands) against Zhu Crown Manufacturing (Cambodia) Ltd., a garment factory in Samrong Tong, Kampong Speu employing about 1,200 workers. [Para 1][Para 47] Conciliation on 1 November failed on all fifteen issues; six were then settled at the hearing and four withdrawn, leaving five. [Para 24][Para 29][Para 31] Neither local union had yet obtained registration from the Ministry. [Para 50]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Issue 6 of the 13 October complaint — Whether the employer had to keep paying the 6 USD monthly incentive bonus for regular attendance when a worker arrived late occasionally? [Para 79]
2. Issue 3 of the 1 November complaint — Whether the employer had to pay a lunch allowance of 26 USD per month? [Para 96]
3. Issue 5 of the 1 November complaint — Whether the employer had to merge the 6 USD incentive bonus with the 10 USD attendance bonus into a single 16 USD sum, to be pro-rated against authorised days off? [Para 118]
4. Issue 6 of the 1 November complaint — Whether the employer had to raise the overtime meal allowance from 2,000 riel to 3,000 riel per day? [Para 125]
5. Issue 7 of the 1 November complaint — Whether the employer had to raise the travel and accommodation allowance from 8 USD to 15 USD per month? [Para 135]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the 6 USD bonus rewarded effort, so an occasional late arrival should not cost it; the 2,000 riel lunch allowance did not cover the cost of a meal; and travel costs had risen. [Para 60][Para 64][Para 77] They also argued the incentive bonus should be merged with the attendance bonus and pro-rated against authorised leave. [Para 68]

**Employer (respondent):** the 6 USD payment was a special incentive granted above what the Labour Law requires, payable only to workers with no absence, no lateness and no early departure, and the company was entitled to apply it on those terms. [Para 61][Para 69][Para 70] It could not afford the lunch increase, and its 8 USD travel allowance already exceeded the 7 USD set by Notification 230. [Para 65][Para 78]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the 6 USD bonus claim and the 16 USD merger claim were refused, and the lunch, overtime-meal and travel claims were refused consideration for want of standing. [Para 144][Para 145][Para 146][Para 147][Para 148]

On the 6 USD incentive bonus, Article 2 of the Labour Law gives the employer the right to manage the enterprise lawfully and reasonably, and prior awards recognise that an employer may unilaterally design bonus schemes above the statutory minimum to encourage productivity. [Para 82][Para 84] No law required the payment; workers who met the conditions kept it and those who did not lost it, which the panel found lawful and reasonable because the bonus was better than the statutory floor. [Para 90][Para 94][Para 95]

On the 26 USD lunch claim, no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice obliged the employer to pay it, and the existing 2,000 riel per day was already better than the law required. [Para 97][Para 98] That made the demand an interest dispute, and because the claimant federations were not a locally most-representative union the panel declined jurisdiction. [Para 100][Para 110][Para 117]

On the 16 USD merger, Article 2 again applied: the incentive bonus was discretionary and not fixed by law, so the employer could keep it separate from the statutory attendance bonus and attach conditions to it. [Para 120][Para 121][Para 124]

On the overtime meal allowance, clause 2 of Notification 041/11 of 7 March 2011 requires 2,000 riel per day or a free meal, which the company paid, and no rule required 3,000 riel. [Para 125][Para 126] The demand was an interest dispute and failed on the same standing ground. [Para 129][Para 133][Para 134]

On the travel allowance, the company already paid 8 USD, above the 7 USD in Notification 230, and no instrument required 15 USD; the claim was likewise an interest dispute the panel would not consider. [Para 137][Para 138][Para 141][Para 143]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a benefit exceeding what the Labour Law, a Prakas, a collective agreement, the internal rules or established practice already provides is an interest dispute, and the panel will decline to consider it unless the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise — alone or together with others covering more than half the workforce. Where the claim is instead that the employer broke the terms of a discretionary bonus it created, the panel will apply Article 2 and uphold the conditions as written, provided they are lawful and reasonable. [Para 110][Para 84][Para 121]

**Obiter dicta:** none identified.
