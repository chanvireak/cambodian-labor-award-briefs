# Award No. 079/14 — U-Knits (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 5 May 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Pen Bun Chhea (president), Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed) · Doc ID 139548

## 1. Facts
U-Knits (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. ("U-Knits") sews garments at Prek Ho, Ta Khmau, Kandal, and employed about 700 workers. [Para 108][Para 109] The Khmer Youth Voice Federation raised fifteen demands; conciliation on 12 March 2014 failed and the file reached the Council on 27 March 2014. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24] At the hearing four points settled and six were withdrawn, leaving five: the attendance bonus during sick leave (1), a higher bonus (2), early release for pregnant workers (4), longer fixed-term contracts (6) and a covered walkway to the canteen (13). [Para 28][Para 29][Para 30]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must keep the attendance bonus intact, as well as the daily wage, when a worker takes sick leave supported by a medical certificate? [Para 114]
2. Whether the employer must raise the attendance bonus to USD 15 a month and pro-rate it for other authorised leave? [Para 130]
3. Whether pregnant workers must be allowed to leave fifteen minutes before the end of the shift? [Para 143]
4. Whether the employer must issue fixed-duration contracts of six months to one year? [Para 157]
5. Whether the employer must build a covered walkway from the production building to the canteen? [Para 175]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Sick leave is paid leave, so the bonus should survive it, and the bonus is part of wages; short contracts strip maternity benefits from pregnant workers; the canteen is 200 to 300 metres away and unprotected from rain and heat. [Para 119][Para 132][Para 159][Para 176]
**Employer (respondent):** It keeps the daily wage for certified sick leave but deducts the whole bonus because the vetted internal rules forbid splitting it; the claimant union holds no registration certificate; it issues three-month contracts while preserving seniority; a covered walkway would breach standards and obstruct container traffic. [Para 115][Para 125][Para 135][Para 157][Para 178]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer is ordered to pro-rate the USD 10 attendance bonus to the days of sick leave supported by a proper medical certificate; claims 2, 4, 6 and 13 are refused. [Para 314][Para 315][Para 316][Para 320]

The panel first fixed the parties. Eleven workers had signed delegation letters, but two denied their thumbprints and one was not on the payroll, leaving eight claimants; the union itself was unregistered. [Para 110][Para 111][Para 113] On issue 1 the panel held the bonus to be wages under Article 103, and certified sick leave to be authorised absence suspending the contract under Article 71(6) and Article 72(1). [Para 209][Para 269][Para 273] It reasoned that Notification 230 exists to encourage regular attendance, so forfeiting the entire bonus for a certified illness destroys that purpose, contradicts the spirit of Article 72 of the Constitution, Article 25 of the Universal Declaration and Article 12 of the ICESCR, and pressures sick workers to attend; pro-rating reconciles the incentive with health protection. [Para 221][Para 222][Para 227][Para 228] Issue 2 was refused: USD 15 exceeds the statutory floor, making it an interests dispute, and the claimant was not a most-representative union. [Para 233][Para 234][Para 235] Issue 4 failed on standing, since none of the eight claimants was pregnant. [Para 278][Para 279][Para 280] On issue 6 the panel held that the form and duration of a contract rest on the parties' consent under Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code; neither side may impose terms, so the three-month practice was lawful and the demand was refused. [Para 294][Para 302][Para 305][Para 307] Issue 13 was an interests dispute with no legal or contractual foundation. [Para 309][Para 311][Para 313]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a worker's absence is covered by a valid medical certificate, the panel will order the attendance bonus reduced in proportion to the days absent rather than forfeited, because the bonus is wages under Article 103 and total forfeiture defeats the attendance-incentive purpose of Notification 230. [Para 221][Para 228]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel treated a bonus as a discretionary reward whose conditions belong to the employer, citing dictionary definitions and French labour-law doctrine, and observed that the worker may ask for apportionment but the employer decides whether to grant it. [Para 328][Para 331][Para 338] It also noted that the demand for a USD 15 bonus, even if granted, would bind all workers and remove their right to strike over the same issue for a year. [Para 201]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award decides five of fifteen demands, the rest having settled or been withdrawn, so the surviving numbering is 1, 2, 4, 6 and 13 and the skipped points cannot be reconstructed from the text. [Para 28][Para 29][Para 30] The claimant group is described as eleven signatories and then as eight, after two denials and one payroll check. [Para 110][Para 113] The bonus is described as "USD 10 in one year month" at one point. [Para 189] The company name is spelled U-Knits only once in Latin script, otherwise in three Khmer variants. [Para 108] Prakas 305 is dated "ឆ្នាំ ២០០១៩" and the Universal Declaration "ឆ្នាំ ២០២៣ ១៩៤៨". [Para 197][Para 218] The hearing is recorded at 08:30 "រសៀល" (afternoon). [Para 21] Arbitrator Ing Sothy appended a dissent on issues 1 and 2. [Para 323]
