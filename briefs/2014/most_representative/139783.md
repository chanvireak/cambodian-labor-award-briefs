# Award No. 338/14 — Bless Houl Fashion (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 December 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chean, chair) · Doc ID 139783

## 1. Facts
Bless Houl Fashion, a factory in Sa'ang district, Kandal that sews bags for export and employed 83 workers, did not renew the fixed-term contract of Ms M when it expired on 30 September 2014, and paid her severance. [Para 40][Para 42][Para 48][Para 49] Ten workers transferred their rights to the Cambodia-ASEAN union confederation, which filed four demands on their behalf on 20 October 2014; conciliation left four points unresolved and the file reached the Council on 3 December 2014. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 43] Ms M had been elected president of the factory union and says the non-renewal punished her for it. [Para 51][Para 60]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must take Ms M back and pay her wages from the day she was stopped until the day she returns? [Para 2][Para 50]
2. Whether the company must pay 3,000 riel a day as a midday meal allowance? [Para 3]
3. Whether the company must raise the transport and accommodation allowance from 7 to 15 dollars a month? [Para 5][Para 75]
4. Whether the company must pay 100 per cent of wages in periods when it has no work for the workers? [Para 7]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the non-renewal was union discrimination — from the moment Ms M became factory union president the employer harassed her, and if she had done wrong the employer was bound to warn her in writing or orally before ending her employment; wages must be paid in full during work shortages because a shortage is the company's fault, not the workers'. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 82]

**Employer (respondent):** it cannot take Ms M back because she did not follow management's instructions, and it did not know of the union leadership notification until October 2014; it nevertheless admits it gave no warning, oral or written, and no notice before the contract ended, and offered 7 days' extra wages as compensation. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 55][Para 61] On the allowances it said it had no capacity to pay more, already gave 7 dollars' transport and 50 per cent wages in slack periods, and would follow the law or a buyer's instruction. [Para 71][Para 74][Para 86]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — demand 4 was allowed in part, the company being ordered to pay 100 per cent of wages in periods when it had no work and had not lawfully suspended the contracts; demands 1, 2 and 3 were refused, with no sum in riel or dollars awarded on them. [Para 161][Para 163][Para 165][Para 166]

On demand 1 the panel applied Article 73(1) of the Labour Law: a fixed-term contract expires when its term falls due, and the decision whether to renew belongs to the parties, since a contract is an agreement no one can be forced to continue. [Para 88][Para 90] It followed its earlier awards (No. 100/07 Hoya (Cambodia), No. 64/09 Sino Me Ka 2) and held the employer under no duty to take Ms M back. [Para 90][Para 93]

The discrimination argument turned on Article 293 and on Article 3(3) of Prakas No. 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001, which protects candidates in a union leadership election for 45 days before and 45 days after the ballot. [Para 95][Para 97][Para 98] Earlier awards (No. 34/05 Zaksin Tech, No. 53/06 Hong Mi) hold that this protection does not reach the expiry or invalidity of a contract, and the panel agreed: non-renewal of a fixed-term contract is not a dismissal, so Ms M enjoyed no protection. [Para 102][Para 104] On discrimination the burden lay with the party asserting it; the only evidence that the employer knew of her candidacy was an EMS delivery slip signed by a security guard on 4 September 2014, and no further proof was offered, so the panel found no basis to hold that the non-renewal was tainted by union discrimination. [Para 109][Para 116][Para 117][Para 118]

On demands 2 and 3 the panel found no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice obliging the company to pay a meal allowance or 15 dollars' transport, so each was an interest dispute rather than a rights dispute. [Para 121][Para 122][Para 140][Para 142] Under Article 96 and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305, read with Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 — an interest award replaces the collective agreement for one year and binds every worker — only the most representative union may bring such a claim. [Para 125][Para 128][Para 130] The claimant was not the most representative union in the factory, so the panel declined to consider both demands; it also noted that the 7 dollars already paid matches Ministry Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 and that the claim for 15 dollars exceeds what the law requires. [Para 139][Para 141][Para 135][Para 145]

On demand 4, Article 71 permits suspension of contracts only where the enterprise faces genuine economic or material difficulty, and only under the labour inspector's supervision; earlier awards require notification to the inspector and a written authorisation (No. 160/14 Osan). [Para 147][Para 149][Para 154][Para 156] Here the company cut wages to 50 per cent in slack periods without notifying the inspector or obtaining written approval, so the suspension was invalid and full wages are owed for those periods; where a suspension was properly made, nothing extra is owed. [Para 159][Para 160][Para 161][Para 162]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The expiry of a fixed-term contract ends the employment relationship by operation of law, so the panel will not order reinstatement or back pay for non-renewal, and the 45-day protection given to union election candidates does not extend to non-renewal. Separately, where an employer reduces wages for lack of work without notifying the labour inspector and obtaining written authorisation, the suspension is invalid and 100 per cent of wages is owed. [Para 90][Para 102][Para 104][Para 159][Para 160]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded the employer's admission that it gave no warning and no notice before the contract ended, and its offer of 7 days' extra wages — facts that did not alter the outcome because the contract had run its term. [Para 54][Para 55] It also noted that it decided the dispute only for the 10 workers who had transferred their rights, although only Ms M appeared. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46]
