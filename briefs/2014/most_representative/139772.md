# Award No. 326/14 — Tien Sung Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 16 December 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer, An Non for the workers, Tang Tri chair) · Doc ID 139772

## 1. Facts
Tien Sung Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd., a Phnom Penh garment factory registered in 2008 with about 830 workers, faced a strike in November 2014 over twenty demands on bonuses, allowances and working conditions. [Para 55][Para 57][Para 28] Conciliation at the Ministry of Labour failed on all twenty, and at the hearing of 2 December 2014 the parties settled issue 13 while the workers withdrew fifteen others, leaving issues 1, 3, 4 and 9. [Para 27][Para 29][Para 35][Para 36][Para 37] The claimant was the Khmer Workers' Spirit Union, which had not yet received its registration certificate; eleven workers were original claimants, eight signed transfers of rights and three attended. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60][Para 62]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise the regular attendance bonus from USD 10 to USD 15 a month? [Para 62]
2. Whether the employer must raise the transport and accommodation allowance from USD 7 to USD 10 a month? [Para 67]
3. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of 3,000 riel a day? [Para 73]
4. Whether the employer must raise the overtime meal allowance for 16:00 to 18:00 from 3,000 to 4,000 riel a shift? [Para 77]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the attendance bonus is easily cut, so a higher rate would encourage attendance; room rents have risen and neighbouring factories pay USD 10 to 12; food prices have risen and cooking at home costs more than the allowance. [Para 63][Para 68][Para 75][Para 78]
**Employer (respondent):** it already applies Prakas notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 — a USD 10 attendance bonus and USD 7 transport and accommodation — and adds a USD 10 incentive for a full month and a company bus worth USD 15; lunch is 1,000 riel on weekdays and 2,000 on Saturdays and overtime meals 3,000 riel, against the 2,000 riel minimum in notification No. 041/11; the basic wage rises to USD 128 next year. [Para 64][Para 70][Para 72][Para 101][Para 121]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — all four remaining claims were refused, the panel holding that it had no power to decide them because they are interest disputes. [Para 132][Para 133][Para 134][Para 135]

Article 96 defines the collective agreement, and Prakas No. 305 article 9 gives the most representative union the right to request one; Prakas No. 099 article 43 provides that an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement, applies to every worker in the enterprise for one year and removes the right to strike. [Para 87][Para 91][Para 94][Para 95] Because that is the effect of deciding such a claim, the panel held it may do so only where the union seizing the dispute is the most representative in the enterprise — members above half the workforce — or where several unions together reach that threshold, following its awards 81/04, 98/04, 169/11, 02/11 and 66/11. [Para 96][Para 97]

Here the eleven claimants were individual workers, not a union, and the panel found they had no most-representative status; the union in whose name they acted had not been registered. [Para 59][Para 98][Para 99] Jurisdiction therefore failed on each head.

The panel nonetheless examined every demand and found the employer's practice met or exceeded the applicable ministerial notification: USD 10 bonus, the same figure notification No. 230 requires, and USD 7 transport and accommodation, also the notified figure; 1,000 riel weekday and 2,000 riel Saturday lunch allowances, where no text fixed a minimum; and 3,000 riel overtime meal pay against the 2,000 riel required by notification No. 041/11. [Para 81][Para 101][Para 111][Para 114][Para 121][Para 124] No law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice obliged the employer to pay more, so each demand sought an improvement rather than the enforcement of a right. [Para 85][Para 104][Para 125]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The Arbitration Council has no jurisdiction over an interest dispute — a demand to improve conditions beyond what statute, a Prakas, a collective agreement or established practice already gives — unless the union seizing it is the most representative in the enterprise, or several unions together represent more than half the workforce. Individual workers cannot confer that standing on themselves, and neither can a union that is unregistered. Where the employer's practice already satisfies the applicable Prakas minimum, a demand for more is an interest dispute, not a rights dispute. [Para 96][Para 99][Para 114]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's alternative findings that each demand was "more favourable than the law" [Para 113][Para 126], and its account of how an interest award would extinguish the right to strike for a year [Para 95], were not necessary once jurisdiction was declined.
