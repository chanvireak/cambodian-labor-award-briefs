# Award No. 220/14 — Sino Garmen (Arbitration Council, 9 September 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna, appointed by the workers; Ang Eng Thong, president appointed by both) · Doc ID 139694

## 1. Facts
Sino Garmen is a garment factory at Prek Samrong, Ta Khmau, Kandal, employing more than 600 workers. [Para 29][Para 30] The claimant is the Khmer Youth Voice Union at the factory, registered under No. 2428 K.B/V.K. of 12 March 2012, whose new leadership was recognised by the Labour Disputes Department on 05 December 2013; the panel found it was not the most-representative union. [Para 31][Para 32][Para 60] On 16 July 2014 the union filed eight demands; conciliation on 31 July 2014 settled seven and left one unresolved, which was referred to the panel on 13 August 2014. [Para 8][Para 10][Para 11] The hearing, postponed from 21 August 2014 because the employer's representative was abroad, was held on 27 August 2014 with both parties present. [Para 14][Para 16][Para 17]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must raise the attendance bonus from USD 10 to USD 15 per month and deduct it only in proportion to the days a worker takes authorised personal leave? [Para 2][Para 33]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the cost of rent, water and electricity has risen, so the USD 10 attendance bonus should be raised by USD 5; the governing notification gives USD 10 to workers who attend regularly but nowhere states that the bonus is lost entirely for absence, so a partial deduction is the proper reading. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 38]
**Employer (respondent):** it cannot afford an increase because it now only takes in subcontracted sewing work and has no direct buyers, and its operation has shrunk to a small workshop; in general it already complies fully with the Labour Law and asks only to apply the notification as written. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 42]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand for USD 15 was refused; the company was ordered to reduce the USD 10 attendance bonus only in proportion to the days of authorised personal leave taken. [Para 62][Para 103][Para 104]

On the increase the panel applied point 2 of Notification No. 230 K.B/S.C.N. of 25 July 2012, which requires at least USD 10 per month for a worker who attends on every working day without absence; the employer already paid USD 10, so it was complying. [Para 44][Para 46] Because no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rule, contract or past practice obliged a higher payment, the claim was an interest dispute, and since the claimant held no most-representative certificate the panel declined to consider it under Article 96 of the Labour Law, Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 61]

On the deduction the panel treated the claim as a rights dispute. [Para 63] It held the attendance bonus is a wage under Article 103 because it is "money paid as a bonus", not a discretionary tool of management, and that Notification No. 230 is a general rule binding on all garment, textile and footwear employers. [Para 69][Para 93][Para 95] Authorised personal leave is a suspension of the contract under Article 71(6), and Article 72(1) relieves the employer of the wage only for the days of suspension; the same reasoning it had applied in Award No. 112/11-YUNG VA and No. 154/11-B&N. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 99] It therefore concluded that the employer may deduct in proportion to the days of authorised absence but has no right to forfeit the whole USD 10. [Para 101][Para 102]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus paid under Notification No. 230 is a wage under Article 103, so where a worker's absence is authorised by the employer the contract is suspended under Article 71(6) and the employer may reduce the bonus only in proportion to the days of absence. Total forfeiture is not permitted, because Article 72(1) touches the wage obligation only for those days. A claim to raise the notified minimum above USD 10 is an interest dispute that only a most-representative union may bring. [Para 95][Para 99][Para 101]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's statement that a bonus conditioned on attendance must still be read as a wage, not a management instrument, is general reasoning that was not needed to fix the pro-rata figure. [Para 69]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- Arbitrator Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer, filed a dissenting opinion annexed to the award; he argues that a bonus is a gratification whose conditions the employer sets, so a worker who does not meet the condition cannot claim it and it may not be apportioned. The dissent runs from [Para 106] to [Para 127] and is not part of the majority's reasoning.
- The award date is 09 September 2014 in the header [Para 1] and at [Para 127], but the reasons record a postponement from 03 September to 10 September 2014 [Para 19]; both are reported.
- The notification is cited inconsistently: No. 230 of 25 July 2012 in the reasons [Para 44] but "ឆ្នាំ ២០១៩" in the dissent [Para 125].
- Conciliation is described as producing seven settled points and "មិន សះ ជា ចំនួន ៤៩ ០១ ចំណុច" — a stray "49" before the one unresolved point — at [Para 10].
- The company is named variously as "ស៊ីណូ ហ្គាមេន", "ស៊ីណូ ហ្គាមេន អ៊ីន ដាស្ទីល ខូអិលធីឌី" and "សីណូ ហ្គាមេន" [Para 1][Para 20][Para 29]; the union's name appears garbled in the header and as "សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ស៊ុម្លេង យុវជន ខ្មែរ" at [Para 21].
