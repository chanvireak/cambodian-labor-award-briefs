# Award No. 052/14 — S L Garment Processing (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 21 April 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun for the employer party, Tun Si Phan for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139538

## 1. Facts
S L Garment Processing (Cambodia) Ltd, a cutting and sewing factory on Road 371 in Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employs about 4,000 workers and is organised by the Cambodia Labour Union Federation (CLUF) and its factory union. [Para 50][Para 52][Para 53] The federation lodged 15 demands on 21 January 2014; conciliation on 25 February 2014 settled 11 and left 4, which reached the Council on 3 March 2014 under report No. 248 Kb/Ak/Vk dated 28 February 2014. [Para 12][Para 15][Para 16][Para 17] The hearing was held on 21 March 2014, and the parties agreed to take a binding award on rights issues and a non-binding award on interests issues. [Para 14][Para 22][Para 26]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay cash for unused annual leave every year during the employment contract, calculated on the average of total wages over the last 12 months? [Para 55]
2. Whether the employer must provide at least two sets of work clothing a year, free of charge, to each worker? [Para 69][Para 154]
3. Whether the employer must pay a midday meal allowance of 2,000 riel on top of the 1,000 riel a day it already pays to workers in the sewing building? [Para 82][Para 162]
4. Whether the employer must provide a free midday meal, as it did before 2011, to workers in the sewing building? [Para 178][Para 195]
5. Whether the employer must pay wages on the 7th of each month? [Para 102][Para 196]
6. Whether the employer must pay wages twice a month as the Labour Law requires? (raised for the first time at the hearing) [Para 221][Para 223]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** annual leave pay should be computed on the average of the last 12 months' wages because that is how leave days are paid; two sets of clothing a year are a necessity on low wages; 1,000 riel a day cannot buy a meal costing 2,500 to 3,000 riel, and the meal provided before 2011 was better and free; payment on the 7th would let them buy goods, send money home and clear debts before the 10th; and if the 7th is refused, wages should be paid twice a month. [Para 65][Para 67][Para 72][Para 73][Para 96][Para 105][Para 106][Para 109]
**Employer (respondent):** it pays unused leave only when a contract ends and computes it on the last month's basic wage divided by 26; workers themselves prefer freedom of dress and did not wear the 2005 shirts and did not ask for a uniform; the 1,000 riel meal allowance already exceeds the law and the company is in financial difficulty after a four-month strike; the payroll cycle — attendance to administration on the 1st and 2nd, accounts by the 4th, approval in Singapore on the 5th and 6th, bank on the 7th and 8th — makes payment before the 10th impossible. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 68][Para 74][Para 77][Para 99][Para 100][Para 101][Para 111][Para 217]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to provide two sets of work clothing a year free of charge to each worker (issue 2); the other claims were refused, and the claim for twice-monthly payment was declined for want of jurisdiction. [Para 241][Para 242][Para 244]

On annual leave, the panel classified the claim as a rights dispute under Articles 166 and 168, then held that Article 167(2) allows payment in lieu only where the contract is cut short or ends, and Article 167(3) treats any agreement to forgo annual leave as void; the article therefore forbids cash payment while the worker is still serving, and the employer's own annual pay-out practice was itself contrary to that article. [Para 127][Para 131][Para 132][Para 139][Para 143][Para 152][Para 153]

On clothing, point 6 of Prakas No. 307 Kb/Brak of 14 December 2007 requires the employer to give each worker at least two sets a year free, each set having at least a skirt or trousers and a shirt in a size suited to the worker's body and task. [Para 154][Para 155][Para 158] The employer conceded it provides none, its reasons being worker preference and the difficulty of laundering two sets; the panel found the obligation unmet and ordered compliance. [Para 159][Para 160][Para 161]

On the meal allowance, the panel found no law, collective convention, agreement, internal rule or past practice fixing a 2,000 riel top-up, and since the existing 1,000 riel already exceeded the law the claim was an interests dispute; the CLUF factory union was not most representative, so under Article 54 of the Trade Union Law and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 the panel declined to consider it. [Para 163][Para 164][Para 165][Para 175][Para 176][Para 177]

The claim to restore the pre-2011 free meal failed for the same reason, and independently because the panel, applying the definition of past practice in Award No. 95/09-Tak Fat, found the evidence inconsistent as to when the meal stopped, whether it was steady, and whether both sides accepted it; silence since 2011 suggested acceptance of the new arrangement. [Para 183][Para 187][Para 188][Para 189][Para 192][Para 194]

On the day of payment, the panel held that Article 2 of the Labour Law gives the employer the right to organise the enterprise, that Article 116 fixes only the number of payments and the maximum interval and not the date, and that setting the day is therefore a managerial right exercised lawfully here; payment on the 10th, or the 9th when the 10th is a holiday, was regular. [Para 196][Para 199][Para 200][Para 205][Para 211][Para 215][Para 216][Para 219][Para 220] The twice-monthly claim was raised only at the hearing and appears in neither the conciliation report nor the original complaint, so Article 312(1) and Article 33 of Prakas No. 099 deprived the panel of jurisdiction; it also predated the minute, being a long-standing practice rather than a consequence of the dispute. [Para 223][Para 227][Para 229][Para 231][Para 235][Para 239]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may not buy out unused annual leave while the employment relationship subsists; Article 167 permits payment in lieu only on termination, and a practice of annual pay-out is itself unlawful. A statutory Prakas obligation — two free sets of work clothing a year under point 6 of Prakas No. 307 — is a rights entitlement that the panel will order performed, and worker preference for free dress is no answer. The date of wage payment, by contrast, is a matter of managerial prerogative under Article 2 where the law fixes only frequency, and a claim for a different pay date or for twice-monthly payment made for the first time at the hearing falls outside the panel's jurisdiction. [Para 143][Para 152][Para 158][Para 161][Para 205][Para 223][Para 235]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's suggestion that the employer should encourage workers to carry leave over to the following year, within the three-year limit in Article 167(4), rather than pay it out, is advice not required by the order. [Para 147][Para 148][Para 151]

- The operative paragraph on clothing reads "បន្ទាប់ ឱ្យ និយោជក" ("next, order the employer") instead of the expected "order the employer" [Para 242], and the jurisdiction order misprints the period as "twice a month in February" [Para 244].
- The file names the union's president, vice-president and secretary individually [Para 54]; they are redacted here.
- The demand list gives four unresolved points but five issues were argued, the fifth (twice-monthly pay) being added at the hearing [Para 16][Para 221].
