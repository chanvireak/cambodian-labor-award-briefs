# Award No. 155/14 — Gatha International Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 15 July 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Seng Vuthy Hun (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Pen Bunchhea (president) · Doc ID 139614

## 1. Facts
Gatha International Co., Ltd is a garment factory in Kampong Speu province employing about 450 workers, 42 of them men. [Para 46][Para 47] The workers, through the Federation of Cambodian Workers' Unions, filed 17 collective demands on 28 May 2014; conciliation settled 4, the workers withdrew issue 10, and 11 issues remained. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 28][Para 29] No lawful local union yet existed at the factory: the union had only a receipt for its registration application dated 13 June 2014, and 91 workers had authorised the federation to act. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50] Both parties elected to have the award binding. [Para 30]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay a lunch allowance of 5,000 riel per day (panel issue 2)? [Para 51]
2. Whether the company must pay a position bonus of USD 15 per month to workers with two years' service and to the carding and sewing machine operators (panel issue 3)? [Para 56]
3. Whether the company must hire at least one more cleaner for the production building (panel issue 4)? [Para 65][Para 147]
4. Whether the company must pay a lunch allowance of 6,000 riel per day to workers who work on Sundays or public holidays (panel issue 5)? [Para 166]
5. Whether the company may require male workers from the cutting table and sewing sections to unload goods from containers (panel issue 7)? [Para 173]
6. Whether the company must apply award No. 071/14 of 30 April 2014 on deducting the attendance bonus (panel issue 12)? [Para 190]
7. Whether the company must pay 100 per cent wages whenever it has no work for the workers without the Labour Department's agreement (panel issue 13)? [Para 198]
8. Whether the company must pay compensation for unused annual leave during the currency of the contract, calculated on the average of the last 12 months (panel issue 14)? [Para 204]
9. Whether overtime pay must be calculated by adding the seniority bonus and the position allowance to the base wage (panel issue 15)? [Para 231]
10. Whether the company must give each worker a plastic water bottle with a lid and a carry strap (panel issue 16)? [Para 271]
11. Whether the company must provide enough chairs near the workstations in QC, carding, sewing and the cutting table (panel issue 17)? [Para 280]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Market prices had risen, so lunch should be 5,000 riel a day and 6,000 riel on Sundays and holidays; machine operators and long-serving workers deserved a USD 15 position bonus because their work is heavy and done standing; seniority bonus and position allowance are part of wages and must enter the overtime rate. [Para 52][Para 58][Para 96] They alleged one cleaner could not clean the building and that three or four workers' legs had swollen from standing. [Para 154][Para 109]
**Employer (respondent):** It applies the agreement of 7 April 2014 giving 2,000 riel a day; it cannot pay more, revenue has fallen, the minimum wage has risen, and no law requires a lunch allowance, position bonus or personal water bottle. [Para 53][Para 75][Para 104] It denied using the men for container work and said it had produced no contracts because they say nothing on the point. [Para 179][Para 184]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the company to stop assigning male cutting and sewing workers to load and unload containers, to recalculate overtime on base wage plus seniority bonus plus position allowance, and to place enough chairs near the workstations; it dismissed the remaining eight demands. [Para 288][Para 292][Para 296][Para 298]
Most demands failed on one jurisdictional ground. The lunch allowances (issues 2 and 5) and the USD 15 position bonus (issue 3) were held to be interest disputes, because no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, past practice, internal rules or contract created the entitlement. [Para 116][Para 137][Para 168] Under Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, only the most representative union may bring an interest dispute, and here there was no lawful local union at all — merely a registration application — so the 91 authorising workers lacked standing and the panel declined to consider those claims. [Para 124][Para 128][Para 130][Para 131][Para 132]
On the cleaner (issue 4), the panel applied Article 2 and its consistent reading that management decisions stand if lawful and reasonable; the four existing cleaners did the work and had idle time, and the workers produced no specific evidence, so the party bearing the burden of proof failed. [Para 150][Para 156][Para 163][Para 164]
On issue 12 the panel held it had no power: under Article 353(1) of the 2006 Civil Procedure Code only a court may compel compliance with an award, so it could not rule on the refusal to apply award No. 071/14. [Para 191][Para 195][Para 196] Issue 13 was a future dispute — the situation had not yet arisen — and the panel does not decide problems that have not occurred. [Para 199][Para 200][Para 203]
On annual leave (issue 14), Article 167(3) prohibits paying compensation for unused leave while the contract is still running, and Article 168 fixes the 12-month average only for the case of termination; paying it every six months contradicted that prohibition, so the demand was refused. [Para 216][Para 221][Para 229][Para 230]
On overtime (issue 15) the panel distinguished Article 102, which means the wage strictly due for work, from Article 103, which covers all monthly income including bonuses. Seniority bonus is paid in exchange for experience and loyalty, and position allowance for carrying out assigned duties, so both are part of ordinary-time wages and must enter the overtime base, following awards No. 255/13 (Pou Yien) and No. 74/08 (Generation). [Para 240][Para 243][Para 247][Para 253][Para 254][Para 269]
On the water bottle (issue 16), Prakas No. 054 requires the employer to provide clean drinking water and clean cups or other means, not a personal bottle for each worker, so the demand failed. [Para 272][Para 276][Para 278] On chairs (issue 17), Prakas No. 053 requires suitable chairs at each workstation and, whatever the posture required, enough chairs near the post for use when necessary; the standing sections had none, so the panel ordered them provided. [Para 280][Para 281][Para 286][Para 287]
On container work (issue 7), the panel read the internal rules' duty to perform assigned work as covering the worker's main job and work connected with it; unloading containers was unrelated to cutting or sewing, heavier, and unpaid at any higher rate. The employer was ordered to produce the contracts, produced none, and so failed to prove the assignment was contractual. [Para 176][Para 178][Para 185][Para 186][Para 187][Para 188]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a demand rests on no law, collective agreement, contract, internal rule or established practice, it is an interest dispute that the panel will not decide unless the union bringing it is the most representative in the enterprise; a local union with only a pending registration application cannot supply that standing. [Para 116][Para 128][Para 131]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observations that it cannot enforce its own awards and cannot decide future or hypothetical disputes were not necessary to any order, since those claims would have failed on the merits in any event. [Para 196][Para 202]

[Para 1][Para 33] A fifth demand (Sunday lunch allowance) is numbered as issue 5 but the text speaks of 4,500 riel, 6,000 riel and 3,000 riel at different points, and the decision quotes both 5,000 and 6,000 riel. [Para 112][Para 166][Para 167][Para 288] Paragraph 190 cites the earlier award as No. 71/14 and para 192 as No. 271/14. [Para 190][Para 192] The reasoning repeats sentences verbatim and OCR has left noise strings ("Phogram", "Ling Austic") and a garbled run on the signature page. [Para 230][Para 300] Issues 6, 8–9 and 11 are missing from the numbering.
