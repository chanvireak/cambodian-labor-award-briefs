# Award No. 224/14 — Meng Da Footwear Industry Steel (Arbitration Council, 29 September 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed, dissenting), An Vira (worker-appointed), Kong Phalha (president) · Doc ID 139698

## 1. Facts
Meng Da Footwear Industry Steel sews footwear at Chom Chao, Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh, with about 4,050 workers, most of them women. Two local unions are recognised at the plant but neither holds most-representative status. The unions filed sixteen demands on 30 June 2014; six remained unresolved and reached the panel. Both parties chose a binding award, the first in this run. [Para 1][Para 43][Para 45][Para 21][Para 22][Para 29]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay wages for work done on a holiday at the end of that holiday's working hours? [Para 3]
2. Whether the company must give each worker doing Saturday overtime one can of orange juice or one packet of noodles? [Para 5]
3. Whether the company must include the seniority bonus in the base wage used to calculate overtime pay? [Para 7]
4. Whether the company must compute holiday pay by adding the average of the other allowances? [Para 9]
5. Whether the company must build a nursery to standard, with a child-minder and adequate equipment? [Para 11]
6. Whether the company must pay overtime meal money once a week? [Para 13]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** A holiday is a rest day, yet workers come in to earn money for food and goods, so paying them at the end of the holiday shift rewards them; the base wage is low and overtime should be computed on base wage plus seniority bonus; and the company has never built a nursery for mothers to leave their children. [Para 51][Para 66][Para 76]
**Employer (respondent):** Paying immediately after a holiday shift creates heavy paperwork and accounting staff are absent, so the company keeps its practice of paying holiday work with the monthly wage on the 10th; it already pays a 10 USD attendance bonus, 8 USD travel money and above-statutory seniority increments, and gives 5 USD in place of a nursery. [Para 54][Para 68][Para 12][Para 74]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must include the seniority bonus in the base for overtime pay, set up a nursery (ទារកដ្ឋាន) or pay for an outside one, and pay overtime meal money weekly; the claims for immediate holiday pay, juice and noodles, and allowance-loaded holiday pay were refused. Being binding, the award takes effect at once between the parties. [Para 186][Para 188][Para 190][Para 191]

Issue 1 failed on the text. Art 116 requires workers to be paid at least twice a month, but the parties did not dispute the monthly payment, and the panel declined to reopen it. Holiday work pay is part of wages under Art 103 and Art 4 of Prakas 10, and award 146/014 held that Sunday pay may be issued together with wages. No law requires payment at the end of the holiday shift, so the claim had no legal basis. [Para 82][Para 85][Para 88][Para 90][Para 96][Para 97]

Issues 2 and 4 were refused on jurisdiction. Neither juice and noodles nor allowance-loaded holiday pay is required by any law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice, so each is an interest dispute; under Art 96 and Prakas 099 Art 43 only a most-representative union may bring one, and these claimants were not. [Para 100][Para 101][Para 110][Para 113][Para 139][Para 143][Para 145]

Issue 3 succeeded. Art 139 (as amended) sets overtime at 150%, but neither it nor Art 5 of Prakas 80 defines the "wage at normal time" on which that rate is computed. Reading Arts 102 and 103, the panel held the base is the wage earned for the work itself, and a seniority bonus is paid for length of service, experience and loyalty — qualities the worker also deploys during overtime. Following award 74/08 (January Sun), which added skill and position allowances to the base, the panel ordered base wage plus seniority bonus. [Para 116][Para 117][Para 123][Para 127][Para 132][Para 134][Para 135]

Issue 5 succeeded. Art 186 requires any enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a breastfeeding room and a nursery; Art 187 leaves standards to a Prakas that has still not been issued, so the panel set a minimum of cleanliness and hygiene itself. Paying 5 USD in place of building is no answer: Art 13 makes labour law provisions public order, so a payment or agreement that undercuts them is void, and cash in lieu does not deliver the statutory purpose of keeping mother and child close. The company must build the nursery, and where it genuinely cannot, mothers of children over 18 months may use an outside nursery at the company's expense on actual invoices. [Para 147][Para 154][Para 156][Para 157][Para 164]

Issue 6 succeeded. Point 2 of Notification 041/11 fixes 2,000 riel a day or a free meal but says nothing about when it is paid; the panel held payment must fall within a period that still lets workers buy food, and accepted the weekly demand as a genuine necessity. [Para 168][Para 171][Para 181][Para 183]

Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented on issue 3 alone, holding that adding the seniority bonus to the overtime base has no legal foundation and offends fairness, and that if it is added for overtime it must equally be added when deducting wages. [Para 194][Para 198][Para 199]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Overtime pay under Art 139 is computed on the wage at normal time, and that base includes the seniority bonus, because a benefit paid for length of service, experience and loyalty rewards qualities the worker also applies during the overtime hours. A statutory duty to provide a nursery is public order: cash in lieu cannot discharge it, and an employer that cannot build on site must fund an outside nursery on actual invoices. [Para 134][Para 156][Para 157]

**Obiter dicta:** Where the parties have long paid wages monthly on the 10th and neither contests it, the panel will not use Art 116's twice-monthly minimum to reopen the payment cycle in order to reach a complaint about the timing of holiday pay. [Para 84][Para 85]
