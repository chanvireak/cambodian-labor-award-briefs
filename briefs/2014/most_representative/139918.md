# Award No. 266/13 — Le Meridien Angkor Hotel (Arbitration Council, 2 January 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tai Seng appointed by the employer party, An Non appointed by the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139918

## 1. Facts
The Siem Reap hotel dismissed Mr L, president of the hotel's employee union and of the Cambodia Tourism and Service Workers Federation, and the union took eight demands to conciliation. [Para 2][Para 34] Conciliation on 2 December 2013 failed on all eight points and non-conciliation report No. 023 of 3 December 2013 referred the dispute to the Council; the panel was constituted on 12 December 2013 and the hearing fixed for 20 December 2013. [Para 12][Para 16][Para 18][Para 19] The employer wrote on 16 December 2013 asking to move the hearing to 13 January 2014, then did not appear, so only the workers were heard and the employer's case rested on its written documents. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 22] At the hearing the workers merged demands 1 and 8 and withdrew demand 6, leaving six points for decision. [Para 23][Para 24]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take Mr L back and stop discriminating against the union? [Para 2][Para 9]
2. Whether the employer must take Mr Y back and pay his wages from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 3]
3. Whether the employer must terminate the employment contract of the chief of the engineering department? [Para 4]
4. Whether the employer must stop concluding short fixed-duration employment contracts? [Para 5]
5. Whether casual staff with more than two years' service must be made permanent employees on the same pay and benefits? [Para 6]
6. Whether the employer must pay USD 2 a day in travel allowance to staff working split shifts? [Para 8]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissal of the union president was discrimination against the union; the engineering chief was a dangerous person who had dismissed a worker who had just given birth without proper cause and used abusive language towards staff; split-shift staff travel four times a day and are owed the USD 2. [Para 8][Para 34][Para 171]
**Employer (respondent):** absent from the hearing and represented by no one; in its letters it sought an adjournment to 13 January 2014 and further time to file documents, and maintained that there had been no discrimination since the union was created. [Para 9][Para 20][Para 22][Para 26][Para 27]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to take Mr L back to work and dismissed the remaining five demands. [Para 201][Para 202][Para 203][Para 204][Para 205]

On demand 1 the panel found Mr L's dismissal established and ordered his reinstatement without qualification, treating the removal of the union president as the operative wrong. [Para 201] On demand 2 it declined to order Mr Y's reinstatement or the back pay that went with it, the employer having dismissed him for serious misconduct. [Para 3][Para 202] On demand 3 the panel held that the engineering chief had his own employment contract with the hotel, that the workers were therefore third parties outside that contract, and that only the employer could decide to terminate it; it added that the workers had not shown the chief to be a dangerous person whose retention threatened the safety of other staff. [Para 166][Para 167][Para 168][Para 169][Para 172][Para 203] On demands 4 and 5 the panel reasoned that the claims about short fixed-duration contracts and about converting casual staff to permanent status were interest disputes, not rights disputes. [Para 184][Para 185][Para 186] On demand 6 it found no provision of the Labour Law, no collective agreement, no internal rule and no past practice obliging the hotel to pay USD 2 a day to split-shift staff, so the claim exceeded what the law required and was likewise an interest dispute. [Para 184][Para 185][Para 186][Para 200][Para 205]

The panel then examined whether the claimant could bring an interest dispute at all. Under Article 96 of the 1997 Labour Law a collective agreement is a written agreement between employers and unions, and under Prakas No. 099 the panel may decide an interest dispute only where the union bringing it is the most representative in the enterprise. [Para 187][Para 188][Para 189] The hotel's union held registration No. 2198 of 14 July 2011, but its leadership-recognition letter of the same date had expired, and the panel found the claimant did not represent the hotel's staff as a whole. [Para 196][Para 197][Para 198] It therefore declined jurisdiction over the interest-dispute demands and rejected them. [Para 199][Para 200]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union whose registration stands but whose leadership-recognition letter has expired is not the most representative organisation in the enterprise, and the panel will decline to decide an interest dispute brought by it even where the underlying practice is established. [Para 196][Para 198][Para 199] Employees cannot demand the dismissal of another employee: that employee's contract is with the employer, the workers are third parties to it, and the demand fails unless they prove the person is dangerous and cannot be retained without endangering others. [Para 167][Para 168][Para 169]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's statement that it agrees with the Council's earlier interpretation of most-representative status is an endorsement of authority rather than a finding on these facts [Para 195]; its observation that the employer's absence did not prevent the panel from proceeding on the written record is procedural comment [Para 22][Para 26].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The constitution date of the panel is garbled as "ថ្ងៃទី ១២ ខែធ្នូ ឆ្នាំ ២០២២ ឆ្នាំ ២០២១ ២០១៣" [Para 18], and Prakas No. 099 is dated "ឆ្នាំ.២០១៩ ២០០៤" in the quotation of Article 43 [Para 190].
- The operative list runs 01, 02, 03, 04, 05 and 07 with no 06, and paragraph 204 merges demands 4 and 5 into one block [Para 204][Para 205].
- The employer's name is printed several ways — "ឡឺ មេរី ឌាន", "ឡី មេរី ឌាន", "ឡឹ មេរី ឌាន" and "ឡឹមេរី ឌាន" [Para 1][Para 26][Para 28][Para 196]; "Le Meridien Angkor Hotel" is used here as the face-of-award spelling.
- The employer's representative is recorded only as "អវត្តមាន" (absent), and the signature block is partly lost to OCR [Para 1][Para 207].
