# Award No. 012/14 — You Fie Tailor Shop Pesion Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 20 February 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3 — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Kong Phalha (president) · Doc ID 139512

## 1. Facts
You Fie Tailor Shop Pesion Co., Ltd. (យូហ្វី ថេលើ រ ខប ភើរេសិន), a garment factory at Ta Doung, Bati district, Takeo, employing about 785 workers, faced eight unresolved demands out of nineteen. Conciliation on 14 January 2014 settled eleven; the panel's own hearing settled four more (issues 4, 5, 6 and 8), leaving issues 1, 2, 3 and 7. The company pays 50 percent of wage and allowances during maternity leave and pays the three months out one month at a time, the first instalment only when the woman stops work. [Para 18][Para 23][Para 57][Para 65]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay maternity leave at 100 percent of wage and allowances, and pay it in advance before the leave begins? [Para 2][Para 95]
2. Whether the employer must provide a properly staffed nursery, or pay USD 20 a month instead? [Para 3][Para 126]
3. Whether workers who stand while working are entitled to a 15-minute rest every two hours? [Para 4][Para 131]
4. Whether the employer must be flexible about sick leave supported by a medical certificate? [Para 5] — settled: the company's practice continues.
5. Whether the employer must pay a USD 15 attendance bonus? [Para 6] — settled at USD 12.
6. Whether the employer must pay USD 15 in travel and accommodation allowance? [Para 7] — settled at USD 10.
7. Whether annual-leave compensation must be computed on the average of total wages over the last 12 months, including overtime and bonuses? [Para 8][Para 145]
8. Whether the employer must give each worker a drinking cup? [Para 9] — settled: one cup every six months.

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** maternity pay should be 100 percent, paid before the leave starts; the USD 7 nursery allowance is not enough; standing workers tire and need rest; annual leave should be valued on total earnings, not base wage. [Para 67][Para 73][Para 78][Para 91]
**Employer (respondent):** the law requires wages to be paid monthly, so advance payment is impossible; the nursery allowance was just raised from USD 5 to USD 7; it provides stainless-steel chairs, special chairs for pregnant workers and free access to the toilets; base wage is the correct basis. [Para 69][Para 74][Para 79][Para 92]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel dismissed the claim for 100 percent maternity pay but ordered the employer to pay the 90-day maternity allowance before the worker's leave begins; it dismissed the nursery, rest-break and drinking-cup claims; and it ordered annual-leave compensation at termination to be computed on the average of total wages over the last 12 months, including overtime and bonuses. No sum was fixed. [Para 155][Para 156][Para 157][Para 158][Para 159]

On standing, the factory-level Workers' Union had only a receipt for its registration application of 4 December 2013, not a certificate, so it was not yet a lawful professional organisation. [Para 29][Para 58][Para 59] Three workers had nonetheless transferred their rights to the Workers' Federation, whose deputy secretary-general appeared, so the panel heard the case for those three only. [Para 60][Para 62][Para 63]

On issue 1, Articles 182 and 183 give 90 days' maternity leave at half wage and allowances; the employer already pays 50 percent, so the statutory duty is met and the claim for 100 percent is a demand above the law, i.e. an interest dispute. [Para 95][Para 97][Para 99][Para 101][Para 102] Because the claimant federation is not the most representative union in the company — that status belongs to the Democratic Samnang Workers Union — the panel declined to decide the 100 percent claim under Article 96 and Prakas No. 305, Article 9. [Para 105][Para 112][Para 113] It did, however, apply Article 115, which forbids paying wages on the day the worker is entitled to rest and requires payment a day earlier, and Article 103, which counts maternity-leave pay as wages; the staged practice therefore breached Article 115, and payment must be made before the leave begins. [Para 114][Para 117][Para 121][Para 124][Para 125]

On issue 2, all three claimants who transferred their rights were men, so no woman was before the panel claiming a nursery; the panel declined to consider it. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 126][Para 128][Para 130]

On issue 3, Article 2 gives the employer the right to organise and direct the enterprise where it acts lawfully and reasonably; no article of the Labour Law requires a 15-minute break every two hours for standing or seated work. [Para 134][Para 137][Para 138] The panel found the employer's arrangements — chairs, pregnant workers' chairs, unrestricted toilet access and short rests — showed reasonable consideration, so the claim lacked legal basis. [Para 140][Para 142][Para 143]

On issue 7, Article 168 requires the average of wages, bonuses, allowances and benefits over the 12 months before leave; using base wage was inconsistent with it and with earlier awards, so total earnings including overtime and bonuses must be used, divided by 12 and then by 26 to reach a daily rate. [Para 145][Para 147][Para 153][Para 154]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Maternity pay is owed at the statutory half of wage and allowances and must reach the worker before her leave starts, not in instalments during it; and unused annual leave paid out at termination is valued on the average of all remuneration, overtime included, over the preceding 12 months. [Para 117][Para 153]

**Obiter dicta:** A claim that goes beyond what the Labour Law guarantees is an interest dispute that only the most representative union may bring; and a claim pleaded by workers of the wrong sex cannot be decided for the class it was meant to benefit. [Para 102][Para 126]
