# Award No. 016/14 — Sun Wel Sou Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 28 February 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3 — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (president) · Doc ID 139510

## 1. Facts
Sun Wel Sou Co., Ltd. (សាន់ វែល ស៊ូ), a footwear manufacturer at Trapeang Thloeng, Phnom Penh with about 1,100 workers, was formerly registered as Jan Wai (ជា ន វៃ) until 1 October 2008. The Khmer Youth Voice Union Federation filed a collective dispute over nine demands; conciliation on 17 January 2014 settled two and left seven, of which the panel heard five. The company had been paying out unused annual leave in cash while workers were still employed, computed on base wage. [Para 18][Para 22][Para 48][Para 65][Para 100]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether maternity-leave pay for women should be computed on the average wage of the last 12 months, at 50 percent for 90 days? [Para 2]
2. Whether annual-leave compensation should be computed on the average wage of the last 12 months? [Para 4]
3. Whether the company must pay a 13th-month salary equal to one month's base wage each year? [Para 6]
4. Whether the company must build a nursery for children from 18 months to school age, or pay USD 50 a month instead? [Para 8]
5. Whether the company must issue fixed-duration employment contracts of one year? [Para 10]
6. Whether the company must raise the base wage to USD 150 a month? [Para 12]
7. Whether the company must pay statutory severance to workers who had served at the closed Jan Wai company? [Para 14]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** annual leave must be valued on the 12-month average under Articles 110 and 168; the 13th month is a share of profit under Article 103; Jan Wai was closed and a new enterprise opened on the old site, so severance is owed; a new transfer-of-authority letter proved this. [Para 71][Para 76][Para 98][Para 102]
**Employer (respondent):** it has always computed on base wage and cannot change; there is no legal basis it knows of; it never closed Jan Wai, only changed its name, keeping seniority and benefits; the 13th month is impossible. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 106][Para 111]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to compute annual-leave compensation at termination on the average of the last 12 months' total wage, and to stop paying out unused annual leave during employment; all other claims were dismissed, with no sum fixed in riel or dollars. [Para 249][Para 250][Para 251]

On representation, the panel held that Ms D, the administration head, could represent the company: Prakas No. 099, Article 19, allows a party to be represented by any person holding written authority, and although the 6 February 2014 letter post-dated the 4 February hearing, a corrected letter of 4 February 2014 was filed in time. [Para 118][Para 126][Para 128]

On issue 2, Article 168 requires the average of wages, bonuses and benefits over the 12 months before leave; Article 167 K.2 requires payment of untaken leave on termination. The company's base-wage method did not follow that spirit, so at termination the 12-month average applies; earlier awards 24/04-MSI, 94/07-..., 07/13-Ocean Garment were followed. [Para 134][Para 139][Para 140] For leave paid out while the contract continues, however, Article 167 K.3 voids any agreement to pay compensation in lieu of annual leave; the panel therefore rejected the 12-month-average claim in that situation and ordered the practice stopped, following 98/04, 45/05-BIN, 88/12 and 73/13. [Para 145][Para 146][Para 149][Para 150] The panel also refused to hear the separate claim on wages for days of annual leave actually taken, because it was absent from the non-conciliation report and was not a direct consequence of the dispute. [Para 151][Para 153][Para 160][Para 162]

On issues 3 and 6, both were interest disputes. Under Article 96 and Prakas No. 305, Article 9, only the most representative union may conclude a collective agreement or bring such a dispute, and a Prakas 099 Article 43 award replaces a collective agreement for one year. The factory had three unions, none most representative, and the claimant was not it, so the panel declined to consider both claims; it also found no contract, internal rule or past practice creating a 13th-month obligation, and noted the employer already paid USD 105 against a statutory minimum of USD 100 under Prakas No. 317. [Para 163][Para 173][Para 175][Para 177][Para 211][Para 216][Para 217][Para 221]

On issue 5, the panel held that no party can force contract terms on another: Articles 65 and 664 and Civil Code Articles 311, 336 and 337 require offer and acceptance. The workers' wish for one-year contracts was lawful to propose and the employer's refusal lawful to make, so the claim was dismissed. [Para 182][Para 189][Para 200][Para 203][Para 206]

On issue 7, the same claim between the same parties had been decided on the merits in case 141/13-Sun Wel Sou on 31 July 2013 and dismissed. Applying res judicata (អាជ្ញា អស់ ជំនុំ), the panel found all three conditions met and held the new CDC letter did not prove a closure and reopening, so it declined to decide again. [Para 227][Para 240][Para 243][Para 245][Para 247][Para 248]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The cash value of unused annual leave is fixed by the average of the last 12 months' total remuneration only when the employment relationship ends; while it subsists, Article 167 K.3 forbids buying out annual leave, and a claim for 12-month-average valuation of leave taken during employment must fail. [Para 146][Para 150]

**Obiter dicta:** An interest dispute may be entertained only where the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise; a panel will also decline a claim absent from the non-conciliation report unless it flows directly from the dispute. [Para 173][Para 160]
