# Award No. 247/14 — Hing Leader Wear (Cambodia) Fek Thermry Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 3 October 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party — dissenting, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Ang Eng Thong chair) · Doc ID 139708

## 1. Facts
Hing Leader Wear (Cambodia) Fek Thermry Co., Ltd is a bag-sewing enterprise in Ang Snonh district, Kandal, employing roughly 430 workers. [Para 43][Para 45] The Khmer Workers' Spirit Union Federation filed a 14-point demand; conciliation on 21 August 2014 settled 10 points, leaving 4, of which point 3 was settled at the hearing, so the panel decided points 1, 2 and 4. [Para 13][Para 18][Para 19] The bargaining unit was 44 workers who thumb-printed a power of attorney dated 28 July 2014, and the union had not yet received its registration certificate from the Ministry. [Para 47][Para 48]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must add USD 5 per month to the USD 10 attendance bonus, bringing it to USD 15 per month, and must deduct the USD 10 bonus only in proportion to the number of days the worker is absent with the employer's permission for personal reasons? [Para 49][Para 69][Para 70][Para 91]
2. Whether the workers are entitled to a lunch allowance of Riels 2,500 per day on top of the Riels 1,500 already paid, making Riels 4,000 per day? [Para 58][Para 137]
3. Whether the workers are entitled to an overtime meal allowance of Riels 1,000 per day on top of the Riels 2,000 already paid, making Riels 3,000 per day for overtime from 4:00 to 6:00 p.m.? [Para 62][Para 144][Para 147]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the attendance bonus should rise to USD 15 because living costs have climbed and the company is prospering on overtime work, with other factories such as Smart Sheet paying up to USD 20; the flat deductions of USD 5, 7 and 10 for two, three and four days' permitted absence are excessive; the meal allowances should rise for the same reasons. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 56][Para 59][Para 60][Para 63][Para 64]
**Employer (respondent):** it cannot pay more now because it is waiting to see the annual increase in the minimum wage — if the base wage rises the bonus stays as it is, and only if it does not rise can the bonus increase; a pro-rata deduction would turn the attendance bonus into ordinary wages; it lacks the capacity to raise either meal allowance. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 57][Para 61][Para 65]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim to raise the attendance bonus to USD 15 was refused, and the employer was ordered to deduct the USD 10 attendance bonus in proportion to the number of days absent with the employer's permission, counted from two days' absence upwards; the lunch allowance claim (Riels 2,500) and the overtime meal allowance claim (Riels 1,000) were both refused. [Para 136][Para 143][Para 153][Para 154][Para 155][Para 156][Para 157]

On point 1 the panel first held that the company falls within Notification No. 230 K.B/S.J.N of 25 July 2012, since it sews bags, a branch of the garment sector that the notification covers, so the USD 10 minimum attendance bonus applies to it. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 68] The employer was in fact paying USD 10, which satisfies the notification, and no law, collective agreement, internal rule, contract or past practice required USD 15, so that part of the claim was "more than the law provides" and thus an interests dispute. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74][Para 75] Because the complaining union was not the most representative in the enterprise, the panel declined to decide that interest under Article 96 of the Labour Law, Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 SKBY and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY, following its awards Nos 81/04, 98/04, 202/14, 02/11, 66/11 and 206/12. [Para 76][Para 79][Para 81][Para 82][Para 84][Para 85][Para 87][Para 88][Para 89][Para 90]

On the deduction schedule the panel reasoned differently. Absences that the law itself allows — annual leave, special leave, public holidays — cannot justify any deduction, so the worker keeps the full USD 10. [Para 104][Para 105] Absence for personal reasons with the employer's permission is not leave defined by law, but it suspends the employment contract under Articles 71(6) and 72 of the Labour Law, so no wage is due for those days; since Article 103 treats a sum paid as a bonus as wages, the attendance bonus may be reduced only in proportion to the days absent, not by the fixed USD 5, 7 or 10 steps, which do not conform to Notification No. 230. [Para 123][Para 124][Para 126][Para 127][Para 130][Para 131][Para 135] The employer's practice of keeping the full bonus for a single day's permitted absence is more favourable than the notification and may continue. [Para 132][Para 133][Para 134]

Points 2 and 4 were interests disputes on the same footing: the employer paid the Riels 1,500 lunch allowance and the Riels 2,000 overtime meal allowance required by Notification No. 041/11 K.B/S.J.N of 7 March 2011, no instrument required more, and the non-representative complainant could not bring the claim. [Para 137][Para 138][Para 139][Para 140][Para 141][Para 147][Para 148][Para 149][Para 150][Para 151][Para 152]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer grants an attendance bonus at the statutory minimum, it may reduce that bonus only in proportion to the days a worker is absent with its permission for personal reasons, counting from the second day; a flat deduction schedule that takes the whole bonus after four days is unlawful, because permitted personal absence suspends the contract under Articles 71(6) and 72 and the bonus is wages under Article 103, so it is earned day by day. Absence on leave that the law itself allows — annual, special or public-holiday leave — never justifies any deduction. [Para 105][Para 126][Para 130][Para 131][Para 135]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's discussion of why it could not interpret the words "without absence" in Notification No. 230 for want of a clarification from the competent authority, and its decision to assess each case on its facts, are general observations; the employer's more favourable practice of keeping the full bonus for one day's absence was likewise noted only to confirm it may continue. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 107][Para 133][Para 134]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- Arbitrator Ing Sothy filed a dissenting opinion annexed to the award, arguing that a bonus conditioned on attendance is not wages and cannot be apportioned, so the order on the deduction schedule is wrong; the award prints the dissent at [Para 159]–[Para 174] and it is summarised here rather than reconciled.
- The award date is 3 October 2014 [Para 1], but the parties had agreed to postpone issuance from "26 September 2019 2014" to 3 October 2014 [Para 22]; the signature block also reads 3 October 2014 [Para 175].
- Workforce figures conflict: "9 approximately 430 workers" at [Para 45] and 44 claimant workers at [Para 48]; the 44 figure is used.
- The operative paragraph on point 1 is garbled ("USD 2 5"), and the lunch-allowance order at [Para 156] reads "1 2500 riel" against Riels 2,500 in the reasons [Para 137].
- Paragraph 97 is largely corrupt ("10 100 and 499"), the union's registration status appears only at [Para 47], and several union and company spellings vary within the document ("Hing Leader Wear", "Hing Ngai", "Hing").
- The employer's representative is named as Sok Kong at [Para 1] but the power of attorney is dated 12 September 2014, after the 21 August conciliation [Para 24].
