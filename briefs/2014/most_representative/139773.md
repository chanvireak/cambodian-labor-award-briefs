# Award No. 324/14 — Se Du No (Cambodia) Nit Thing (Arbitration Council, 23 December 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · Doc ID 139773

## 1. Facts
The company operates a garment factory at Kandeung Touch, Bati district, Takeo, which opened on 1 July 2014 and employs about 500 workers [Para 40][Para 41][Para 51]. A union election on 14 July 2014 returned a president, vice-president and secretary, and the Ministry of Labour issued a receipt for the union's registration application on 3 November 2014 [Para 43][Para 44][Para 75]. Conciliation of the collective dispute settled two of seven demands; of the five unresolved, the workers withdrew issues 3 and 4, leaving issues 1, 2 and 5 for the panel [Para 12][Para 18][Para 19]. The disputed acts are the employer's refusal to raise travel and health allowances and its practice of cancelling the whole monthly attendance bonus for any authorised absence [Para 57][Para 58].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to raise the monthly travel and accommodation allowance by USD 5, from the existing USD 7 to USD 12, for the workers? [Para 87]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to provide a health-care allowance of USD 5 per month to the workers? [Para 107]
5. Whether the employer is obliged to deduct the USD 15 monthly attendance bonus only in proportion to the number of days the workers were absent with lawful permission from the employer? [Para 113]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Bus and accommodation costs have risen from USD 8 to USD 10 a month and neighbouring factories pay more than USD 7, so the allowance should reach USD 12 [Para 49][Para 50]. Several of them received a health allowance in Phnom Penh and lost it on moving to Takekao, so it should be restored [Para 54]. An authorised absence of a day or two should cost only that fraction of the bonus, as other factories and prior awards do [Para 60][Para 62].

**Employer (respondent):** The factory opened only on 1 July 2014; it already pays the USD 7 travel and accommodation allowance fixed by Notification No. 230 and has added a USD 2,000 riel monthly lunch allowance from 1 December 2014 [Para 51][Para 55][Para 87]. Health money is already absorbed in the USD 100 basic wage [Para 55]. Notification No. 230 confines the attendance bonus to workers with no absence at all, so pro-rating would destroy its incentive purpose [Para 65][Para 66].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issues 1 and 2 were dismissed; on issue 5 the panel ordered the employer to deduct the attendance bonus only in proportion to the days of absence authorised by the employer [Para 156][Para 157][Para 158]

The panel first fixed who was properly before it. Under Article 268 of the Labour Law a professional organisation acquires legal personality and the rights the law confers only once it and its list of leaders are registered with the Ministry, and registration is deemed complete two months after a valid application [Para 68][Para 69][Para 71]. Counting from 3 November 2014 to the hearing of 2 December 2014, less than two months had elapsed, so the Se Du No union was not yet a lawful professional organisation and its three officers were not yet lawful leaders able to represent the membership [Para 76][Para 77][Para 79]. The 17 November 2014 letter bearing four workers' thumbprints also failed, because it contained no statement delegating those workers' rights to the federation [Para 81][Para 82]. Only the three workers who appeared in person were claimants, and the panel confined itself to their claims [Para 84][Para 86].

On issue 1 the panel found the employer already paid USD 7 in conformity with point 1 of Notification No. 230, and that no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, contract, agreement or past practice obliged it to add USD 5 [Para 87][Para 88][Para 89]. The claim was therefore one of interest rather than of right [Para 91]. Under Article 96 of the Labour Law and point 9 of Prakas No. 305, only the most representative union may compel an employer to conclude a collective agreement and bring an interest dispute to the Council, and Prakas No. 099 point 43 makes an award on an interest dispute stand in place of a collective agreement [Para 92][Para 95][Para 97][Para 98]. The applicant union was not the most representative in the enterprise, so the panel declined to consider the claim [Para 104][Para 106]. Issue 2 followed the same path: no instrument obliged a USD 5 health allowance, the claim was one of interest, and the applicant lacked standing, so it too was dismissed [Para 108][Para 110][Para 113].

On issue 5 the panel held that the attendance bonus under Notification No. 230 is mandatory and not a discretionary management tool, and that Article 103 makes a bonus a form of wage [Para 123][Para 146][Para 148]. Absence on annual, special, maternity, public-holiday or weekly rest does not touch the bonus, but an authorised personal leave does, since Articles 71(6) and 72(1) suspend the contract and release the employer from paying wages for those days [Para 128][Para 149][Para 150][Para 152]. The employer may therefore deduct, but only to the extent of the authorised days, not the whole USD 15 [Para 154][Para 155]. Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented on this point, arguing that a "bonus" conditioned on attendance is not wage and cannot be apportioned [Para 162][Para 175].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim to raise a benefit beyond what the Labour Law, a collective agreement, internal rules, a contract or established practice already secures is an interest dispute, and the panel will not entertain it unless the applicant union is the most representative in the enterprise — which, under Article 268, a union whose registration has not been deemed complete after two months cannot yet be. [Para 77][Para 100][Para 104]

Where an employer has authorised a worker's absence, the contract is suspended and no wage is earned for those days, so the employer may reduce a mandatory attendance bonus by that proportion but may not forfeit the whole bonus. [Para 138][Para 154]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement for one year and removes the workers' right to strike over the same interest claims, which is why standing is policed strictly [Para 98][Para 99]; it also noted that "without absence" in Notification No. 230 has never been authoritatively construed [Para 126][Para 127].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's name appears on the face of the award as "សេ ខូ ណូ (ខេមបូ)", "សេ ឌូ ណូ (ខេមបូ)" and "សេឌូណូ" within the same document [Para 1][Para 40]. Para 14 dates the panel's constitution "24 November 2023 2014" and para 22 prints the extended award date as "២ ២០១៤" before the corrected 23 December 2014 [Para 14][Para 22]. The record is internally inconsistent on the number of claims: para 2 lists five issues, para 12 reports seven demands with two settled, and the header of para 2 refers to a conciliation report the panel says covered five [Para 2][Para 12][Para 13]. The deduction scheme in paras 57–58 is garbled as to whether the partial deduction is USD 7 or the full USD 15 [Para 57][Para 58], and the dissenting opinion cites a USD 10 bonus rather than the USD 15 awarded, while carrying stray year fragments "2019" [Para 175]. Several sentences are lost to OCR around the union-registration reasoning [Para 69][Para 100]. Arbitrator names survive intact; individual workers and the employer's representative are given in initials per this project's redaction rule.
