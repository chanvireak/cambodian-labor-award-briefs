# Award No. 138/14 — Jin Yi Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 8 July 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Virak (worker-appointed), Run Saray (president) · Doc ID 139585

## 1. Facts
Jin Yi Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd., a garment factory at Samaky Meanchey, Kampong Chhnang, employed about 3,350 workers, of whom some 2,100 were paid by output. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 54] After conciliation, four demands remained: a piece-rate increase, a 3,000 riel meal allowance, a 13-dollar travel and accommodation allowance, and a thirteenth month's salary. [Para 13][Para 18] Only three workers attended the hearing; the local union's elected leaders had all resigned. [Para 51][Para 52]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise piece rates for cutting, packing and sewing so that a worker of average skill earns at least the minimum wage? [Para 94]
2. Whether the employer must pay a midday meal allowance of 3,000 riel per day? [Para 123]
3. Whether the employer must pay a travel and accommodation allowance of 13 US dollars per month? [Para 139]
4. Whether the employer must pay a thirteenth month's salary equal to one month's wage each year? [Para 145]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** about 2,000 output-paid workers cannot earn the minimum wage at current rates, some taking only 2 dollars a day; food prices have risen, the minimum wage has risen, and bus fares have risen by 3 dollars. [Para 54][Para 56][Para 72][Para 74]
**Employer (respondent):** piece rates are set by an "IE" system and the employer tops up any worker who falls below the legal minimum; it pays 2,000 riel meal money, 10.40 dollars travel and accommodation, and has never paid a thirteenth month. [Para 57][Para 59][Para 69][Para 70][Para 80]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to reset piece rates so that an average-skill worker in normal hours earns at least 100 US dollars per month; the meal, travel and thirteenth-month claims were refused without consideration. [Para 149][Para 151][Para 152][Para 153]

**Parties.** The panel noted that the local union held a registration certificate of 15 January 2013 but its leadership had resigned, so no union leader was advancing the claims; under Prakas No. 099 Article 19 it would hear only the three workers who appeared. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 93]

**Piece rates.** The panel first classified the demand as a rights dispute, because it concerns the wage for output work fixed by Article 108, which requires piece rates to be set so that a worker of average skill working the same hours receives at least the guaranteed minimum wage. [Para 97][Para 98][Para 99] It noted Article 137's eight-hour and forty-eight-hour limits and Prakas No. 317's raising of the minimum to 100 dollars for 2014. [Para 100][Para 101] Both parties accepted that the employer tops up to 100 dollars where output pay falls short. [Para 103]

On the facts, the panel found the employer could not explain how the "IE" system sets rates, though asked directly. [Para 58][Para 117] Its own April and May 2014 lists showed only 148 of about 2,100 output-paid workers earning by output in April and 306 in May, which the panel read as evidence that the rates were too low for most workers rather than as proof of a rising trend. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 118] It found no evidence that the employer trialled rates on workers before fixing them, the first of the two checks it applies in rate cases. [Para 107][Para 116] It therefore held that the employer had not discharged Article 108 and ordered the rates reset. [Para 119][Para 121][Para 122]

**Meal allowance.** The employer paid 2,000 riel a day to all workers plus a further 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance, which the panel found better than the law requires. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 69] No law, agreement, internal rule or practice obliged a 3,000 riel payment, so the demand exceeded the law and was an interest dispute. [Para 124][Para 125][Para 126]

**Travel allowance.** The employer paid 10.40 dollars against the 7 dollars required by point 1 of Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012; the extra 2.60 dollars had no legal source, so this too was an interest dispute. [Para 139][Para 141][Para 142][Para 143]

**Thirteenth month.** The employer had never paid one, and no instrument required it, so the demand was an interest dispute. [Para 145][Para 146][Para 147]

**Standing on the interest claims.** Reading Article 96 with Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099, the panel held that an interest award substitutes for a collective agreement and removes the right to press the same demands, so only a most representative union may bring such a case; with three claimants and no functioning local leadership, the claimants were not most representative and the three claims were refused. [Para 130][Para 131][Para 132][Para 136][Para 137]

**Effect.** Because the parties are signatories of the Memorandum of Understanding of 03 October 2012, the rights decision is binding and final at once, while the interest decision is non-binding and becomes final after eight days absent objection. [Para 19][Para 150][Para 154]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Article 108 obliges the employer to fix piece rates so that a worker of average skill working ordinary hours earns at least the minimum wage; where the employer cannot explain its rate-setting method, cannot show rates were trialled, and its own payroll lists show most output workers falling below the minimum, the burden is not discharged and the panel will order the rates reset. [Para 99][Para 107][Para 121]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that setting piece rates is the employer's right, but that the right carries the duty to guarantee the minimum wage for average-skill work. [Para 120]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The header date is 08 July 2014 and no conflicting date is printed; the extension runs from 30 June 2014 to that date. [Para 1][Para 25] The panel says five of nine demands were settled, then that four were unsettled, and four issues are listed, so the ninth demand is never identified. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 18] The claimant count shifts between "three workers" and a union with a registered local: the panel finds both that the local union's leaders had resigned and that the claimants were not a most representative union, without saying what membership the local retained. [Para 89][Para 136] The April and May figures are internally inconsistent — 148 workers in April and 306 in May are described as both "receiving wages by output" and as evidence that rates are low, while the headcount of output workers is given as 2,000, 2,100 and "about 100" in different passages. [Para 60][Para 62][Para 114] The employer's system is called "IE" everywhere but "អាយ អ៊ី" in the reasoning and is never defined. [Para 57][Para 117] OCR damage is heavy: "តម្លៃបុង" becomes "តុំម្លូត៍" and "តម្លៃឡូតិ៍", the meal allowance is printed as 2,3000 riel at one point and 30 riel in the decision, and the travel figure appears as 10.40 dollars existing against a 13-dollar demand while the shortfall is computed as 2.60 in one place and "4 2.60" in another. [Para 67][Para 71][Para 151][Para 152]
