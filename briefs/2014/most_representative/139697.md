# Award No. 227/14 — Sing Chang Sing (Arbitration Council, 10 September 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Hem Hour Narith (employer-appointed), Sin Kim Sean (worker-appointed), Pen Bunhea (president) · Doc ID 139697

## 1. Facts
Sing Chang Sing cuts and sews garments at Toul Sangke, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, with about 600 workers. From 28 June 2014 the company suspended work section by section, then suspended all workers from 1 July 2014 until 10 September 2014, paying 50% of wages to some on 20 August 2014. The local union held its congress on 13 March 2014, but its new leadership list was never deposited with the Ministry. The employer did not attend the hearing. [Para 1][Para 36][Para 45][Para 46][Para 49][Para 42][Para 71]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay 100% of wages while it suspended the workers' labour contracts? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company must pay all wages it owes the workers? [Para 3]
3. Whether the company must repair the factory's electrical system, including wiring and sockets? [Para 4]
4. Whether the company must remove armed guards from the factory? (withdrawn) [Para 5]
5. Whether the company must clean the factory surroundings? (withdrawn) [Para 6]
6. Whether the company must stock adequate medicine for emergencies during working hours? (withdrawn) [Para 7]

Issues 4, 5 and 6 were withdrawn at the hearing, leaving 1 to 3. [Para 21][Para 22]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** There has been no work since mid-June 2014; the suspension was never submitted to or authorised by the labour inspector, so the company owes full wages, plus the annual leave money it has been paying out in cash, and the wiring is so old that workers are shocked at almost every sewing line. [Para 44][Para 50][Para 58][Para 61]
**Employer (respondent):** Absent. It was invited by letter 1085 delivered at the factory gate, signed for by nobody, and offered no reason for non-appearance. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 71]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must pay 100% wages for the suspension, plus a further 50% to top up the 50% already paid for 1 July to 10 September 2014; must stop paying cash in lieu of unused annual leave while workers are still employed; and must repair the wiring and sockets. The claim for the May–August 2014 leave money was refused. The order is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent written objection. [Para 124][Para 125][Para 126][Para 127][Para 128][Para 129]

The panel first held it could proceed in the employer's absence: Art 21 of Prakas 099 permits a hearing where a party was properly invited and stays away without valid reason, and the invitation had reached the factory. [Para 64][Para 71][Para 72]

On representation, Ms K was the only worker present, and no other worker had transferred authority to her. Her congress elected her president, but the new leadership had not been deposited or recognised under Art 268 of the Labour Law and Art 3(2) of Prakas 305, and a union mandate may not exceed two years. She was therefore not yet a lawful union leader and could not represent the workforce; the panel decided the issues for her alone, as a party appearing in person under Art 19 of Prakas 099. [Para 43][Para 76][Para 81][Para 82][Para 85]

On issue 1, Art 71(11) allows suspension only where the enterprise faces serious economic or material difficulty, and only under the supervision of the labour inspector, who must be notified and must confirm the difficulty. The company neither sought nor obtained that authorisation. Because the suspension was unlawful, the employer owed 100% of wages even though no work was given, and prior awards were followed to that effect. [Para 86][Para 88][Para 99][Para 93][Para 96]

On issue 2, Art 167(2) allows payment in cash for unused annual leave only where the contract has been terminated, and Art 167(3) voids any agreement to forgo annual leave. Paying cash in lieu while the worker is still serving therefore contravenes the law. The panel ordered the practice stopped, but refused the claim for the May to August 2014 payments, since a claim to commute leave during employment has no legal basis. [Para 105][Para 108][Para 112][Para 114]

On issue 3, Arts 229(1) and 230 require every workplace to be kept in a condition necessary for workers' health and safety. The workers reported about 80% of sockets broken or cracked and shocks on almost every sewing line; the employer, having abandoned its right to answer, could not rebut this, so the panel accepted the account and ordered the system repaired. [Para 115][Para 118][Para 121][Para 123]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of the labour contract is lawful only where the enterprise's serious economic or material difficulty has been notified to and confirmed by the labour inspector under Art 71(11); failing that authorisation the employer must pay full wages for the entire period of suspension, whether or not work was offered. Separately, cash may be paid for unused annual leave only on termination of the contract — paying it to serving workers is prohibited by Art 167(3) and must cease. [Para 88][Para 93][Para 108][Para 112]

**Obiter dicta:** A union president whose congress has elected her but whose new leadership list has not been deposited with the Ministry has no authority to represent other workers, however many members the union has. [Para 81]
