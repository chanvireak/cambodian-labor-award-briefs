# Award No. 095/14 — Kolim Bess E Apparel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 May 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Liv Sovannara, worker-appointed; Pen Bunchea, chair, chosen by both) · non-binding award · Doc ID 139555

## 1. Facts
Kolim Bess E Apparel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd cuts and sews garments at Tram Dot, Pong Tuek, Dangkao, Phnom Penh, with about 1,500 workers. A strike over five demands was conciliated at the factory on 21 April 2014, one point settled and four referred. The first demand was that the monthly regular attendance bonus be raised from USD 10 to USD 15. At the hearing the worker side withdrew the other three — the USD 10 travel or accommodation allowance, the demand that the State stop taxing wages, and the demand that strike days be paid — leaving the attendance bonus alone. The union at the factory has about 760 members who have thumbprinted consent to the check-off of dues, and two federations hold transferred representation rights. [Para 16][Para 22][Para 38][Para 40][Para 46]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise the regular attendance bonus by USD 5, to USD 15 a month? [Para 55]
2. Whether the employer must raise the travel or accommodation allowance to USD 10 a month? [Para 4]
3. Whether the State must stop deducting tax from wages? [Para 6]
4. Whether the employer must keep wages and the attendance bonus unchanged for strike days? [Para 8]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** USD 10 a month is too little to live on at present prices; other factories pay USD 15 to USD 20, though the union could not name them; the request is not unreasonable because goods have become dearer. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 50]
**Employer (respondent):** It already complies with Notification No. 230 K.B/S.C.N of 25 July 2012 by paying USD 10 to every worker; each company sets its own conditions, and a worker who prefers another factory's benefits is free to go there; it could not agree to the other demands either, and on strike pay said only that it would discuss with the owner. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 9]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider the demand for a raise to USD 15, making no order for any payment. [Para 72][Para 73]

The panel first found the facts: the company pays USD 10 a month to all workers, which matches the minimum in Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012, and neither the Labour Law, a collective agreement, an agreement between the parties, the internal rules nor past practice obliges it to pay USD 15. The demand therefore sought a new benefit rather than the performance of an existing one, and was an interests dispute, to be decided by equity rather than law. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58][Para 59]

That classification then limited the panel's power. Under Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 only the most representative union may require the employer to negotiate a collective agreement. The panel explained why the point matters procedurally: an award on an interests dispute takes the place of the collective agreement for one year and, by doing so, removes the workers' right to strike for that benefit in future. It follows that a panel may arbitrate an interests demand only where the union bringing it is the most representative in the enterprise, or where several unions together represent more than half the workforce, following its earlier Awards No. 81/04 and No. 98/04. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 68][Para 69]

On the evidence the threshold was not met. The panel assumed the Khmer Workers Union at the factory, with about 760 members, to be the claimant, since the other federation's representative did not attend; but it recorded that no union at the company holds most-representative status, and held that this union accordingly lacked the legal character to bring an interests dispute for decision. The demand was refused on that ground, and the award notes that it is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Minister. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 70][Para 71][Para 74]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand to increase a benefit already fixed at the statutory minimum is an interests dispute, which a panel may decide only where the referring union is the most representative in the enterprise, or the referring unions together exceed half the workforce; a panel that finds no union holds that status must decline the demand rather than fix a figure, since its award would otherwise replace the collective agreement and extinguish the right to strike for a year. [Para 59][Para 68][Para 69][Para 71]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remark that each enterprise sets its own benefit conditions and that a worker dissatisfied with them may move to another factory, and its observation that a panel must in every interests case begin by testing the union's representative character. [Para 53][Para 66]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The date of the panel's constitution is printed as "28 April 2019 2014" (para 18), and the evidentiary deadlines are given as 6 and 8 April 2014 (para 25) although the hearing was held on 2 May 2014, so the timetable as printed is impossible. The company's name appears in at least five spellings across the file — Kolim Bess E Pheril, Kolim Bess Ei Pheril, Kolim Beis E Apparel, Kolim Bess and Kolim Beis — and one run of text is corrupted into repeated telephone digits (para 2). The award does not state how many members the union needed to reach half of 1,500 workers, nor the membership of the rival federation, so the finding that no union is most representative cannot be checked from the face of the document. The employer's attendance-bonus statement is dated 24 April 2014, after the referral (para 30). [Para 2][Para 18][Para 25][Para 30][Para 44]
