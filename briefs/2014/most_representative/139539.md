# Award No. 057/14 — You Da Garment In Das Stri (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 4 April 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kau Thach, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 139539

## 1. Facts
Some 38 named garment workers at You Da Garment In Das Stri (Cambodia), a sewing factory employing about 880, struck from 21 February to 3 March 2014 over working conditions and benefits, and transferred their rights to two federations to pursue 16 demands. [Para 1][Para 44][Para 46] Of the three demands that failed conciliation, the third was settled at the hearing, leaving two: a skills allowance of USD 20 per month and travel/accommodation money of USD 15 per month. [Para 3][Para 5][Para 7][Para 20] The company already paid USD 2, 3 and 5 for one, two and three machines, and USD 7.5 travel money. [Para 4][Para 6][Para 65][Para 71]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to increase the skills allowance, so that workers who sew one, two or three machines receive USD 5, 15 and 20 respectively? [Para 63][Para 85]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to raise travel and accommodation money from USD 7.5 to USD 8.5, as provided in the agreement of 14 February 2014? [Para 69][Para 101]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Long service has made them skilled sewers, so the allowance should rise to reward and encourage them; the 14 February 2014 agreement signed and sealed by the company fixed travel money at USD 8.5, and comparable factories (Handsome, Eight) pay USD 10. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 74][Para 76]
**Employer (respondent):** No law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice requires the increase, and the company will consider it only if profits allow; the USD 8.5 figure was signed by the administrative manager, who attended only as a translator with no authority, and the general director, then abroad, never ratified it. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 80][Para 82]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider both remaining claims and ordered no payment. [Para 100][Para 114][Para 115][Para 117]

On issue 1, the panel classified the skills allowance as an interest dispute (ទំនាស់ផលប្រយោជន៍) rather than a rights dispute: no article of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice created the obligation claimed. [Para 86][Para 87] Applying Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, it held that only the most representative union may bring an interest dispute, because such an award becomes a collective agreement binding all workers and removes their right to strike over the same demand. [Para 88][Para 95][Para 96] The two federations represented 38 of roughly 880 workers — far below half — so they lacked standing; the panel followed Awards 81/04, 98/04, 169/11, 02/11 and 66/11. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 99]

On issue 2, the panel treated the 14 February 2014 document as a contract and applied Articles 311, 385 and 396 of the Civil Code: only a party to a contract may compel performance. [Para 102][Para 106] The parties were the company and the Cambodia Labour Union Federation; the workers were third parties with no right to enforce it, and the agreement's scope reached only those two signatories. [Para 107][Para 108][Para 113]

Before reaching the merits, the panel rejected the fresh authorisation letter filed on 24 March 2014: it bore the same date as the original, listed 67 workers against the original's 39, and asserted only 3 of the original 16 demands. [Para 53][Para 55][Para 57][Para 58] The original letter, thumbprinted without names, was still accepted as valid. [Para 59]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union may bring an interest dispute to the panel only where it is the most representative organisation in the enterprise — alone or jointly commanding more than half the workforce; below that threshold the panel will not consider the demand at all. [Para 96][Para 99] Separately, workers who are not parties to a collective agreement cannot invoke it against the employer; enforcement belongs to the signatories. [Para 106][Para 108]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remarks that a post-hearing authorisation letter repeating an earlier date and shrinking sixteen demands to three is "irregular" and cannot be received, and that thumbprints plus employee numbers suffice to identify claimants, were not necessary to the dismissal. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 59] Its correction of the company's registered name from "You Da" to "You Da Garment In Das Stri" is likewise collateral. [Para 41][Para 42]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The header date (4 April 2014) and the hearing date (20 March 2014) are consistent, but the file carries several OCR corruptions: the travel claim appears as "២៥០ ១៥ ដុល្លារ" in the issue list [Para 5], the milk-money demand reads "២០២៣ ដុល្លារ" [Para 7], and the agreement clause is printed as "៨៥ ដុល្លារ" (USD 85) in the reasons while the parties' statements and the award give USD 8.5 [Para 69][Para 76][Para 77][Para 101][Para 117]. The workforce is described as 39 workers at one point and 38 elsewhere [Para 46][Para 53][Para 98]. Article 96 is quoted with the phrase "AKP លក្ខណៈ" inserted where "ជាលាយលក្ខណ៍អក្សរ" belongs [Para 88]. The president's name appears both as "ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឆា" and "ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា" [Para 119].
