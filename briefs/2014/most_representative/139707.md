# Award No. 246/14 — For Hik Cross Bright Wait Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 26 September 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139707

## 1. Facts
Three union federations — Khmer Youth Voice, Workers' Heart of Cambodia and Labour Rights Development — filed a 14-point demand on working conditions against the garment factory For Hik Cross Bright Wait Co., Ltd on 7 August 2014. [Para 20][Para 39] Conciliation on 27 August 2014 settled 6 of the 14 points, leaving 8 unresolved, which reached the Arbitration Council on 1 September 2014. [Para 21][Para 22] At the hearing the worker party withdrew points 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6, so the panel considered only points 2, 7 and 8, and only for the 10 workers who attended and held powers of attorney. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 51][Para 52]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a technical-skills allowance of USD 20 per month to workers who can sew on one machine, USD 40 to those who can sew on two, and USD 60 to those who can sew on three? [Para 53][Para 69]
2. Whether the employer must add USD 5 per month to the existing USD 10 attendance bonus, bringing it to USD 15 per month per worker? [Para 59][Para 87]
3. Whether the employer must add USD 8 per month to the existing USD 7 travel and accommodation allowance, bringing the total to USD 15 per month? [Para 64][Para 97]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the skills allowance is needed to ease daily living costs and other companies already pay it, though the workers could not name those companies; on the bonus and the travel allowance they argued that market prices had risen sharply and their monthly wage could not cover living costs. [Para 54][Para 61][Para 62][Para 66][Para 67]
**Employer (respondent):** it has no capacity to meet the demands, since average monthly wages are already USD 180 to USD 225; it tops up piece-rate sewers to USD 200, pays a USD 4–5 daily target bonus, and already applies the law by paying the USD 10 attendance bonus and USD 7 travel and accommodation allowance. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57][Para 88][Para 97]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider all three remaining claims, on the ground that the complainant was not the most representative union in the enterprise, so the claims were not properly before it; nothing was ordered in riel or USD. [Para 84][Para 86][Para 94][Para 96][Para 106][Para 107][Para 108]

On point 2 the panel found that neither the Labour Law, a collective agreement, the internal rules, a contract nor past practice obliged the employer to pay a technical-skills allowance at the claimed rates — the parties themselves accepted that the company had never paid one — so the claim was "more favourable than the law" (លក្ខណៈប្រសើរជាងច្បាប់) and therefore an interests dispute. [Para 53][Para 70][Para 71][Para 72] Point 7 was reasoned the same way: Notification No. 230 K.B/S.J.N of 25 July 2012 requires at least USD 10 per month as an attendance bonus for workers with no absences, the employer was paying exactly USD 10, and no instrument required a further USD 5, so the claim was again an interests dispute. [Para 87][Para 89][Para 90][Para 91][Para 92]

Because an interests dispute resolved by award becomes, under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY of 21 April 2004, a collective agreement binding on every worker in the enterprise for one year and removes their right to strike over such interests in future, the panel held it could decide such a dispute only where the union bringing it is the most representative in the enterprise, or where several unions together represent more than half the workforce. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 80] It relied on Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001 on union representativeness, and followed its own earlier awards Nos 81/04, 113/14, 169/11, 02/11, 66/11 and 154/13, which had refused interests claims brought by non-representative unions. [Para 73][Para 76][Para 77][Para 80][Para 81][Para 82] On the facts the 10 claimant workers came from three separate federations and the complainant was not the most representative union in the company, so the panel held it lacked the legal character to bring an interests dispute and declined to consider points 2, 7 and 8. [Para 51][Para 83][Para 84][Para 85][Para 103][Para 104]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The Arbitration Council will decide an interests dispute — a claim for a benefit more favourable than the law, unsupported by the Labour Law, a collective agreement, internal rules, a contract or established practice — only where the union or unions bringing it are the most representative in the enterprise, alone or jointly representing more than half of the employees; otherwise the claim is dismissed without consideration of its merits, because the resulting award would bind every worker and extinguish their right to strike on that interest. [Para 71][Para 79][Para 80]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's account of Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 and its statement that an interests award substitutes for the collective agreement and removes the right to strike are general observations on the effect of such awards, not steps needed to dispose of three claims it declined to hear. [Para 78][Para 79]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The full 14-point demand is not reproduced: the award lists only the 8 unresolved points at [Para 2]–[Para 16] and records that 6 were settled without saying which. [Para 21]
- Point 3 of the demands is transcribed inconsistently: the conciliation list asks for a one-year fixed-duration contract [Para 5], while the employer's answer refers to six-month contracts [Para 6]; the point was withdrawn in any event. [Para 26]
- The award date appears at [Para 1] as 26 September 2014, but the panel's constitution is printed as "03 September 2019 2014" at [Para 23].
- The skills allowance is USD 20 for one machine in the reasoning [Para 69] but "8 dollars" in the operative paragraph [Para 86]; the attendance bonus is printed as both "4 10 dollars" [Para 88] and USD 10 [Para 59]; the travel allowance total reads "1 dollar" at [Para 96] against USD 15 elsewhere [Para 97][Para 105].
- The utility figures at [Para 66]–[Para 67] are badly corrupted ("15 to 2017 January", water at 2,500 riel per jar, 18 kilowatts), so no cost calculation can be taken from them.
- Employer and union names are spelled several ways in the same document (e.g. "For Hik Cross Bright Wait" and "For Hik Kros Bright Wait"), and the worker representatives appear under bare or partial names at [Para 46]–[Para 49]; they are rendered here as the panel printed them only where legible.
