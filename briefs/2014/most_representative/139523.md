# Award No. 037/14 — Wai & Double U Garment Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 21 March 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, An Non, Tang Tri presiding) · Doc ID 139523

## 1. Facts
The employer runs a cutting and sewing factory in Phnom Penh that began operating in 2013 with about 1,400 workers. A Khmer Youth union federation, holding only a receipt for its registration application of 18 December 2013, filed for better conditions on behalf of 121 workers who had given it written authority. Ten claims went to conciliation, five were settled and five came to the panel; one claim was later withdrawn and one added at the hearing. [Para 18][Para 51][Para 52][Para 56]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay 2,000 riel a day as a lunch allowance? [Para 62]
2. Whether the employer must raise the transport and accommodation allowance from 10 to 15 US dollars a month? [Para 68]
3. Whether the employer must raise the attendance bonus from 10 to 15 US dollars a month? [Para 78]
4. Whether the employer must provide adequate canteen space and motorbike parking? [Para 82]
5. Whether the employer must maintain wages and the attendance bonus during the five-day national strike from 25 to 31 December 2013? [Para 95]
6. Whether the employer must restore the 5 US dollar incentive bonus withheld for those five days? [Para 195]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** living costs and transport fares have risen and comparable factories nearby pay 15 dollars' transport and a lunch allowance; the canteen holds barely 200 of the workforce, so workers eat under trees, on trucks and by the parking area amid dust from the national road; the employer kept only three days' wages for the five-day stoppage and cut the attendance bonus. [Para 63][Para 70][Para 84][Para 95]
**Employer (respondent):** the company is newly opened and still training workers; it already exceeds Ministry Notification 230 by paying 10 dollars' transport against the 7 dollars required, pays a 10 dollar attendance bonus plus a 5 dollar incentive, and cannot build a larger canteen because the site sits on filled paddy land; the five non-working days were not the fault of either side, so three days' wages were paid as a concession. [Para 65][Para 67][Para 77][Para 92][Para 102]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the canteen claim was granted with a six-month deadline; every other claim was dismissed, the interest claims for want of jurisdiction. [Para 213][Para 216]
On representation the panel first held that the union had become a lawful professional organisation: under Article 268(2) a union is deemed registered if the Ministry does not respond within two months, and more than two months had passed between the receipt of 18 December 2013 and the hearing. The union could therefore represent the 121 workers who had transferred authority to it, even though the employer's coloured list showed 36 names no longer working there. [Para 113][Para 115][Para 127][Para 60]
On issues 1, 2 and 3 the panel found no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice creating the higher entitlements, and no evidence that the employer's existing payments fell short of Notification 230. Each was therefore an interest dispute. [Para 138][Para 156][Para 163]
On issue 4 the panel held the canteen claim to be a rights dispute because the employer had undertaken since opening to provide eating space and because health and safety at work are regulated; both sides accepted that workers face real difficulty, about 50 cannot find space, and neither side filed photographs by the deadline. The panel found six months a reasonable period, taking account of the rainy season, and ordered an adequate canteen within that time. [Para 167][Para 177][Para 187][Para 193]
On issue 5 the workers withdrew the claim after accepting three days' wages and a pro-rated attendance bonus, so the panel recorded the withdrawal and made no order. [Para 101][Para 104]
On issue 6 the panel held the added claim outside its jurisdiction: Article 312(1) confines it to the issues recorded in the non-settlement minutes, and the 5 dollar incentive bonus appears neither there nor in the original complaint. The panel noted the deduction accompanied the wage deduction the workers had already accepted. [Para 201][Para 207][Para 211]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union whose registration application has gone unanswered for two months is a lawful professional organisation by operation of Article 268(2) and may represent the workers who authorised it. The panel may decide an interest dispute only where the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise; otherwise the claim is dismissed for want of jurisdiction. A claim first raised at the hearing, and not recorded in the non-settlement minutes, is outside jurisdiction under Article 312(1) and Prakas 099, Article 33. [Para 115][Para 151][Para 201]

**Obiter dicta:** an employer that has promised to improve eating facilities cannot leave the timing open-ended; the panel fixed six months by reference to the rainy season, even while accepting the site's physical constraints. [Para 187][Para 192]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The operative order prints the dismissed incentive-bonus claim as 45 dollars where the reasoning says 5 dollars, and the lunch allowance as "25 2,000 riel". [Para 214][Para 216] The company's start is given as 2012 in one passage and 1 March 2013 in another; the strike year appears as 2014 at one point; the extension of the award date reads "16 21 March 2014" and Prakas 099 is dated "21 April 2019 2004". [Para 51][Para 66][Para 97][Para 27][Para 132] The claimant union is named both as Khmer Youth Union Federation and Khmer Youth Voice Union Federation, and its representative appears as Mr T. [Para 113][Para 128]
