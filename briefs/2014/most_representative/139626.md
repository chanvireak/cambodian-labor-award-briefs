# Award No. 173/14 — Ai Son Cable Cambodia Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 July 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, selected by the employer; An Non, selected by the workers; Kong Phallaph, chair) · Doc ID 139626

## 1. Facts
Ai Son Cable Cambodia Co., Ltd makes cable in Trapeang Kong, Samraong Tong, Kampong Speu, and employs about 1,555 workers; its internal regulations were filed with the provincial labour department in 2012. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 32] The Independent Workers' Spirit Union at the factory holds most-representative certificate No. 072/13 of 30 September 2013, valid to 30 September 2015, and is the claimant. [Para 38][Para 45][Para 46] Of 17 demands filed in June 2014 seven were settled in conciliation, ten remained unresolved, two were withdrawn at the hearing, and the panel decided seven points: 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 12 and 15. [Para 19][Para 26]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a midday meal allowance of US$15 a month, and 2,000 riel meal money on public holidays worked? [Para 47][Para 97]
2. Whether the employer must raise the regular attendance bonus (ព្រីម) by US$4, from US$11 to US$15 a month? [Para 53][Para 138]
3. Whether the employer must raise the travel and accommodation allowance by US$5, from US$10 to US$15 a month? [Para 59][Para 151]
4. Whether the employer must install hot- and cold-water tanks for the workers? [Para 66][Para 160]
5. Whether the attendance bonus must be deducted pro rata, divided by 26 days, when a worker takes authorised leave? [Para 79][Para 171]
6. Whether the employer must pay a target bonus of US$1 a day to workers who meet the daily quota? [Para 85][Para 183]
7. Whether the employer must raise the overtime meal allowance by 1,000 riel, from 2,000 to 3,000 riel a day? [Para 93][Para 201]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the daily 2,000 riel meal money means workers lose money and receive no meal on public holidays they work; the attendance and travel allowances have not kept pace and 90 per cent of workers live far from the factory, where transport alone costs US$15 a month; the water tank is unroofed, so the water is warm and foul and causes stomach illness, and women need hot water; the target-bonus formula is unintelligible and other factories pay by the week; overtime meal money of 2,000 riel does not cover food bought while waiting for the evening bus. [Para 50][Para 61][Para 70][Para 91][Para 94]
**Employer (respondent):** the company gives 2,000 riel plus a dish at midday and a can of juice each Monday, which is better than the law and better than a cash sum workers would simply save; it pays US$11 attendance bonus and US$10 travel allowance; the tank is cleaned every two weeks and bottled water may be kept in lockers; it is not profitable while building a new block and cannot pay more. [Para 51][Para 56][Para 63][Para 73][Para 96]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — every one of the seven claims was refused; no order was made against the employer. [Para 214][Para 216][Para 221]

Because each surviving claim sought more than the law or the existing practice gave, the panel treated them as interest disputes and first confirmed the claimant union's standing under its most-representative certificate, Prakas No. 305 and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 103][Para 108][Para 146]

On the US$15 meal allowance the panel found no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice fixing such a sum; the actual practice — 2,000 riel and a dish daily plus weekly juice — was already more favourable than the law, and the workers produced no evidence that US$15 cash would serve them better, so the claim failed on equity. [Para 100][Para 115][Para 116] On meal money for public holidays the panel applied Articles 137, 139 and 164 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 10 of 4 February 1999: working on a holiday is not overtime but earns one extra day's wage, and no provision requires meal money; with no evidence of any worker actually losing out, the claim was refused. [Para 121][Para 125][Para 133]

On the US$4 attendance-bonus increase and the US$5 travel increase the panel found the employer pays US$11 and US$10, above the US$10 and US$7 minima in Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012, although that notification in any event covers only textiles, garments and footwear and not cable manufacture; no other source obliged the employer, and the claims were interest demands refused for want of evidence. [Para 142][Para 143][Para 159]

On the water tanks, Article 1 of Prakas No. 054 on sanitary drinking water obliges the employer to supply sufficient hygienic drinking water but does not specify hot or cold tanks; the employer supplied enough water, so the demand exceeded the law. [Para 166][Para 168][Para 170]

On the pro-rata deduction the panel applied the employer's existing scale — US$3 for half a day to one day, US$6 for one and a half to two days, US$11 beyond — and held under Article 13(2) that an arrangement more favourable than the statutory minimum is valid; the claim failed. [Para 176][Para 180]

On the US$1 daily target bonus the panel held the employer's unilateral incentive scheme, paying US$3, US$2 or US$1 by percentage of quota met, fell within the managerial prerogative recognised in Article 2 and confirmed in Award No. 24/05 Watt World; the claim failed, though the panel observed the employer should explain the formula to workers. [Para 188][Para 194][Para 200]

On the overtime meal allowance the panel read point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 as requiring 2,000 riel a day or a free meal for voluntary overtime, without reference to the number of hours; the employer pays 2,000 riel, and no law or agreement required 3,000. [Para 202][Para 205][Para 213]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer's actual practice already exceeds the statutory minimum — a meal allowance paid in cash and kind rather than a monthly sum, allowances above a ministerial notification, or a bonus scale more generous than the Labour Law — a union demand for a higher figure is an interest dispute that fails unless the union proves both a legal or contractual source for the higher amount and that the higher amount would genuinely benefit the workers; the Council will not substitute its own view of what is fair for a practice already above the floor. [Para 115][Para 143][Para 180]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the employer should explain its target-bonus calculation to workers, and that working on a public holiday is not overtime, are observations that did not affect any order. [Para 129][Para 199]
