# Case No. 262/14 — Cambodian Great Way and Wear Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 09 October 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer, An Non for the workers, Ang Eng Thong chair) · Doc ID 139733

## 1. Facts
Cambodian Great Way and Wear Co., Ltd (ខេមបូឌាន ហ្គេត វេ អាន់ ឌើវែរ ខូអិលធីឌី) is a garment factory in the Sihanoukville special economic zone with rather more than 311 workers. [Para 30][Para 31] The Khmer Workers' Spirit Federation held a leadership election at the factory on 06 June 2014, and that local union had not yet obtained legal professional-organisation status. [Para 32] Eighty workers transferred their rights to the federation by letter of 08 August 2014 to pursue this case. [Para 33] Of three demands, conciliation on 09 September 2014 settled one and left two: a monthly travel and lodging allowance of USD 15 instead of USD 7, and a daily lunch allowance of Riels 4,000 instead of Riels 2,000. [Para 2][Para 3][Para 10][Para 36][Para 40]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to pay a travel and lodging allowance of USD 15 per month, that is USD 8 more than the existing USD 7? [Para 44]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to pay a lunch allowance of Riels 4,000 per day, that is Riels 2,000 more than the existing Riels 2,000? [Para 62]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** transport fares, room rents and market prices have all risen, and other factories pay a lunch allowance of USD 13 per month plus rice every day. [Para 38][Para 42]
**Employer (respondent):** the company already applies what the law requires and cannot pay more; it pays Riels 2,000 for lunch every working day and also on public holidays. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 43]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider both claims for want of representativity and ordered nothing. [Para 61][Para 71][Para 72][Para 73]

On issue 1 the panel set out point 1 of Notification No. 230 K.B/S.J.N of the Ministry of Labour and Vocational Training of 25 July 2012, which fixes the travel and lodging allowance at USD 7 per month. [Para 44] It found the employer paying USD 7 as the notification requires, and no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules or past practice obliging USD 15. [Para 45][Para 46] The demand therefore exceeded the statutory floor, which made it an interest dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍). [Para 47][Para 48]

The panel then addressed who may bring such a dispute. Under Article 96 of the 1997 Labour Law a collective agreement is made between an employer or employers' organisation and one or more unions representing workers, and under Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001 the most representative union may require the employer to bargain collectively and the employer must then bargain with it. [Para 49][Para 51] Because that status is what gives a union the legal capacity both to conclude a collective agreement and to refer an interest dispute, the panel said it always examines the representativity of the claimant union. [Para 52] Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY of 21 April 2004 supplied the reason: an award on an interest dispute stands in place of the collective agreement for one year and removes the workers' right to press such interest claims in future, so the panel may decide an interest dispute only where the referring union, or several unions together, has more than half the workers of the enterprise as members. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 55] It followed Awards 81/04 (Evergreen) and 98/04 (Great Union), and Awards 169/11, 02?/11 (Pou Yen) and 66/11 (In Huan Sung), which declined interest disputes brought by unions lacking that status. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57]

Applying that to the file, the claimant was 80 workers who had transferred their rights plus one worker who appeared at the hearing, out of a workforce of over 311, and its union was unregistered; it was not the most representative union and had no standing. [Para 33][Para 35][Para 58][Para 59][Para 60]

On issue 2 the panel found that the employer paid Riels 2,000 per day, including on public holidays, which was more favourable than the law, that nothing in law, collective agreement, internal rules or practice required Riels 4,000, and that the claim was again an interest dispute. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65][Para 66] It cross-referred to its reasoning on issue 1 and dismissed for the same want of representativity. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 70][Para 71]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim for an allowance above the amount fixed by ministerial notification, a collective agreement, internal rules or established practice is an interest dispute, and the panel will not decide it on the merits unless the referring union is the most representative union in the enterprise — representing more than half the workers — because an award on such a dispute substitutes for the collective agreement for a year and extinguishes the right to press those claims again. [Para 55][Para 60][Para 66]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's findings that the USD 7 allowance matches Notification No. 230 and that the Riels 2,000 lunch payment is more favourable than the law were not necessary to a decision that turned on standing. [Para 45][Para 63]

- The non-conciliation report is numbered 0538/13 at [Para 6] and 0538/14 at [Para 11], [Para 27] and [Para 30]; the federation's letter of 09 August 2014 speaks of 08 demands while the conciliation concerned 03 points [Para 9][Para 23].
- The transfer of rights is dated 08 August 2014 at [Para 22] and [Para 33] but 04 September 2014 in the employer's own authority letter [Para 18].
