# Award No. 036/14 — Great Union (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 20 March 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tai Seng, Tun Si Phan, Pen Bun Chhea presiding) · Doc ID 139521

## 1. Facts
A federation of garment unions filed 17 claims for workers at the employer's Phnom Penh cutting and sewing factory, which employs about 1,050 workers. Conciliation on 6 February 2014 settled 12 of them and referred five unresolved claims to the Council, which heard them on 24 February 2014. The live disputes were the formula for paying out untaken annual leave, the employer's refusal to deduct union dues from wages, and three allowance or benefit claims. [Para 14][Para 18][Para 19][Para 56]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must calculate payment for untaken annual leave on the average of total wages over the last 12 months rather than on base wage alone? [Para 59]
2. Whether the employer must deduct 1,000 riel a month from the wages of 316 members who consented in writing and remit it to the union? [Para 66]
3. Whether the employer must raise the transport and accommodation allowance from 7 to 10 US dollars a month? [Para 75]
4. Whether the employer must pay 4,000 riel a day as a lunch allowance? [Para 79]
5. Whether the employer must provide two sets of work clothing a year? [Para 82]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Articles 103 and 110 bring bonuses within "wage", so all wages actually received in a month must be the base; deducting from base wage alone understates leave pay. Dues deduction follows Article 129 bis (2) once consent is written, and clothing follows Prakas 307, Article 6. [Para 63][Para 67][Para 84]
**Employer (respondent):** its payroll practice is lawful and it never based it on an arbitral award; it says Article 281 forbids deducting union dues from wages and that collecting dues is the union's own job; the 7 dollar allowance already matches Notification 230, there is no basis for lunch money, and dress is a matter of worker choice. [Para 64][Para 71][Para 78][Para 81][Para 86]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issues 1, 2 and 5 granted, issues 3 and 4 dismissed for want of jurisdiction over interest disputes. [Para 162]
On issue 1 the panel held the claim is a rights dispute because untaken annual leave is governed by Articles 166 and 168, and read Article 168 to require the average of total wages, bonuses, benefits and the value of payments in kind over the 12 months before leave, divided by 12 and then by 26. Because the employer used base wage only, the practice departed from Article 168 and the panel ordered recalculation for the two workers shown on the attendance sheets. [Para 95][Para 98][Para 101][Para 103][Para 104]
On issue 2 the panel held Article 129 bis (2) and Prakas 305 (2001), Article 5, clause 5, oblige the employer to deduct on written request, and that Article 281 bars only deductions no worker personally authorised; it does not protect an employer that refuses a consented request, otherwise the union could never collect dues. The employer was ordered to deduct per the named list in letter 4081/14 of 24 February 2014. [Para 107][Para 108][Para 113][Para 122][Para 123]
On issues 3 and 4 the panel found no law, agreement, collective agreement, past practice or internal rule creating the claimed entitlement, so the claims were interest disputes; because the claimant union was not the most representative union in the enterprise, the panel declined jurisdiction, following earlier awards that interest disputes may be arbitrated only by a representative union. [Para 127][Para 131][Para 137][Para 138][Para 142][Para 144]
On issue 5 the panel held Prakas 307, Article 6, is mandatory: two sets of work clothing a year, free of charge, each comprising at least trousers and a shirt; inability to pay and freedom of dress are no answer. [Para 150][Para 155][Para 159][Para 160][Para 161]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Leave pay under Article 168 is computed on the average of all wages, bonuses and benefits in kind over the 12 months before the leave, divided by 12 and by 26; a base-wage-only calculation is unlawful. An employer must execute a written, individually signed request to deduct union dues, and Article 281 does not excuse refusal. A union that is not the most representative in the enterprise cannot bring interest disputes to the panel. [Para 98][Para 122][Para 138]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that a decision on a rights dispute binds the parties through their memorandum of understanding of 3 October 2012, so neither side may object to it, while an interest decision may be contested. [Para 26][Para 27]
