# Award No. 328/14 — Soula Moda (Cambodia) Garment (Arbitration Council, 16 December 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Tang Try, chair) · Doc ID 139774

## 1. Facts
The company is a garment factory at Khleang Sampat, Bati district, Takeo, employing 478 workers [Para 30][Para 31]. Two federations — the Solidarity Cambodia Labour Union Federation and the Cambodia-ASEAN Union Federation — organised elections and set up factory-level unions, but neither union had been registered with the Ministry of Labour [Para 32][Para 33]. Ten workers signed a letter of delegation dated 20 October 2014, and three appeared at the hearing, giving thirteen claimants [Para 34][Para 36]. Of nine demands conciliated, six were settled and issue 7 was withdrawn, leaving issues 4 and 5 [Para 11][Para 12][Para 17][Para 18].

## 2. Issues in dispute
4. Whether the employer is obliged to add 3,000 riel to the existing 1,000 riel daily lunch allowance, making 4,000 riel a day, that is USD 26 a month, for each worker? [Para 37][Para 38][Para 47]
5. Whether the employer is obliged to raise the monthly travel and accommodation allowance by USD 10, from the existing USD 10 to USD 20, for each worker? [Para 41][Para 42][Para 67]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** 1,000 riel does not buy lunch, since they now spend 3,000 to 3,500 riel a day, and they want to live decently [Para 39]. Their homes are far from the factory, travel costs have risen, and the USD 10 paid does not cover them [Para 43][Para 44]. The two federations hold delegated authority from ten workers and speak for the workforce [Para 25][Para 34].

**Employer (respondent):** The company began operating only on 24 March 2014 and its means are limited, so it cannot meet the demands [Para 40][Para 45]. It already pays more than the law requires — 1,000 riel a day for lunch and USD 10 travel and accommodation, USD 3 above the Notification No. 230 figure of USD 7 [Para 45][Para 48][Para 68].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both claims were refused as interest disputes brought by a union without standing; nothing was ordered payable [Para 77][Para 78]

On issue 4 the panel accepted that the employer's 1,000 riel daily lunch payment exceeds what the Labour Law requires, and found no provision of the Law, collective agreement, internal rules, employment contract, agreement or past practice obliging a further 3,000 riel [Para 48][Para 49]. Characterising the demand as a benefit above the legal floor, the panel held the claim to be one of interest rather than of right [Para 50][Para 51].

That classification decided both issues, because the panel then applied Article 96 of the Labour Law and point 9 of Prakas No. 305: only the most representative union may require an employer to conclude a collective agreement, and only such a union may bring an interest dispute to the Council [Para 52][Para 55][Para 57]. It added that under point 43 of Prakas No. 099 an award on an interest dispute stands in place of the collective agreement for one year and removes the right to strike over the same demands, which is why standing is examined strictly [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60]. Following its earlier awards, the panel refused to hear an interest dispute where the applicant is not the most representative union in the enterprise [Para 62][Para 63].

On issue 5 the panel repeated the analysis: the employer paid USD 10 against the USD 7 fixed by point 1 of Notification No. 230, which is more favourable than the notification, and no instrument required a rise to USD 20 [Para 68][Para 70][Para 71]. The demand was again a benefit above the law, hence an interest dispute, and the applicant unions were not the most representative [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74]. Both claims were therefore declined without reaching the merits of the amounts [Para 76][Para 78]. No dissent was recorded.

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a lunch, travel or accommodation allowance exceeding what the Labour Law, a collective agreement, internal rules, a contract or established practice already provides is an interest dispute; the panel will not consider it unless the union bringing it is the most representative in the enterprise, which a union not yet registered with the Ministry cannot establish. [Para 50][Para 60][Para 74]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that an award settling an interest dispute substitutes for the collective agreement for one year and extinguishes the workers' right to strike on those demands — a consequence it treats as justifying its strict approach to standing [Para 59]. It also observed that a payment above the statutory minimum is not itself a breach and creates no obligation to increase it further [Para 48][Para 70].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The worker-appointed arbitrator is printed "An Non" in the panel composition and "An Lean" in the signature block, with the signature line partly obliterated [Para 1][Para 80]. The company's name shifts between "Soula Moda (Cambodia) Garmen", "Gamen", "Gabaen" and "Sala Moda" across the document [Para 1][Para 30]. The commencement date is given as 24 March 2014 in the employer's submissions, while the factory operating permit is dated 25 September 2014 and the company statute was deposited on 28 August 2014 [Para 22][Para 23][Para 40]. Para 13 prints the panel's constitution date as "25 November 2019 2014" [Para 13]. The record is inconsistent on the number of demands — nine conciliated, three unresolved, yet only two remain after the withdrawal of issue 7 — and paragraphs 74 and 77 carry garbled article references ("point 4300 of Prakas No. 099", "20 4000 riel") [Para 11][Para 12][Para 74][Para 77].
