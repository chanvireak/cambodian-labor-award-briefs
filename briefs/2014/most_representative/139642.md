# Award No. 188/14 — Kuva Hara Cambodia Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 14 August 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (You Soun Ty, employer-appointed; Sin Kim Sean, worker-appointed; Ang Eng Thong, chair) · Doc ID 139642

## 1. Facts
Kuva Hara Cambodia Co., Ltd is a quality-inspection service company employing about 190 staff, registered in commerce under No. Co. 1764 E/2012 of 9 July 2012. [Para 36] Its staff inspect goods at client factories, often travelling outside Phnom Penh in teams, and are paid a USD 10 monthly attendance bonus and a meal allowance for overtime. [Para 50][Para 53] Of 17 claims raised, 14 were settled in conciliation and one more at the hearing, leaving two for adjudication. [Para 14][Para 19] The Federation of Labour Unions received rights transfers from 65 staff, the union having claimed 109. [Para 41][Para 44]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reduce the USD 10 attendance bonus pro rata to the minutes by which a staff member arrives late — up to ten minutes — in the same way it deducts ordinary wages? [Para 63]
3. Whether the employer must pay a meal allowance of 6,000 riel per day to staff working overtime from 22:00 to 05:00? [Para 81]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Lateness of up to ten minutes is occasional and caused by traffic, road closures or family needs, so the bonus should be docked by the minute rather than forfeited in full. [Para 48][Para 49] Overtime after 22:00 is voluntary and infrequent, and a former manager used to provide a free meal; 6,000 riel per day is therefore owed. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58]
**Employer (respondent):** Work is performed in teams that travel by vehicle to destinations outside Phnom Penh, so one late member delays the whole team; the bonus is an incentive, not a statutory duty. [Para 50][Para 52] Meal money of 2,000 riel per hour is already paid from 17:00 to 22:00 and overtime is paid at 200 per cent, so the claim exceeds the law. [Para 59][Para 60]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both remaining claims were rejected, the second for want of jurisdiction because the claimant union was not the most representative in the enterprise. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 101]

On issue 1, the panel applied article 2 of the Labour Law, which gives the employer the right to organise and manage the enterprise, read with article 13, under which a unilateral employer decision conferring benefits better than the law is valid. [Para 66][Para 75][Para 76] Ministry Notification No. 230 K.B/S.J.N of 25 July 2012, which fixes a USD 10 attendance bonus, binds only textile, garment and footwear enterprises, and the panel found a quality-inspection service company falls outside it. [Para 63][Para 65] The bonus was therefore a voluntary incentive for punctuality, not a legal entitlement, and the panel held that forfeiting it for lateness — even of a few minutes — was a lawful and reasonable exercise of management power that infringed no guaranteed right. [Para 74][Para 77] It noted that the team nature of the work made individual lateness affect the whole group. [Para 78]

On issue 3, Notification No. 041/11 K.B/S.J.N of 7 March 2011 requires 2,000 riel per day or a free meal for voluntary overtime at the employer's request, without reference to the number of hours. [Para 81][Para 82] The company pays 2,000 riel per hour from 17:00 to 22:00, which is better than the notification, and the panel again found the notification inapplicable to this sector. [Para 83][Para 84] For the 22:00–05:00 window no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice imposes an obligation, so the claim was one for benefits above the law — an interest dispute. [Para 86][Para 87][Para 88] Under article 96 of the Labour Law and Prakas 305, and Prakas 099 article 43, an interest dispute may only be arbitrated where the referring union is the most representative in the enterprise or the unions together represent more than half the workforce. [Para 89][Para 92][Para 95][Para 97] The panel rejected page 4 of the rights-transfer letter as a duplicate of page 3, reduced the transferees from 109 to 65 of 190 staff, and held the claimant lacked standing to represent the others. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 100][Para 101]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that is not the most representative in the enterprise, and whose members do not exceed half the workforce, cannot bring an interest dispute to arbitration, however meritorious the claim; and a unilateral bonus that exceeds the law may be conditioned and withdrawn by the employer so long as the condition infringes no right guaranteed by the Labour Law. [Para 97][Para 76][Para 79]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the statutory attendance-bonus notification is confined to the textile, garment and footwear sectors, so service enterprises are governed only by the general management prerogative. [Para 65][Para 84]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The panel states that issues 2 and 3 remain for decision, then reasons and decides under the headings "issue 01" and "issue 03"; the numbered claim list in the conciliation report runs insurance (1), attendance bonus (2), meal allowance (3). [Para 20][Para 45][Para 81]
- The rights-transfer count is printed as 109 in the findings and as 65 after the duplicate page is discarded. [Para 38][Para 41]
- The meal allowance is printed as 6,000 riel per day in the claim and as "600 riel" in the operative order; the employer's rate is printed as "20 riel per hour" where 2,000 riel is meant. [Para 83][Para 104]
- The order extending the award deadline reads "from 1 August 2014 to 1 14 August 2014". [Para 23]
