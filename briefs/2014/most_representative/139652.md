# Award No. 192/14 — Xin Chang Xing (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 5 August 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 139652

## 1. Facts
Xin Chang Xing (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd sews garments at Tuol Sangkae, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh and employs 700 workers. Its C.CAWDU plant union, registered on 26 August 2011 with 403 members, held a most-representative certificate of 15 August 2013 valid to 15 August 2015. After a strike on 28–30 April 2014 the federation and the company signed an agreement on 30 April 2014 promising USD 70 per year of service to workers who volunteered to leave, payable on 10 June 2014. A second strike followed the late payment of May wages, and the employer dismissed the union activist Mr S on 12 June 2014. The employer did not attend the hearing of 25 July 2014 or the conciliation, so the panel sat in absentia. [Para 14][Para 26][Para 29][Para 30][Para 46][Para 52][Para 58][Para 63]
Three points went to arbitration; the union withdrew point 2, leaving points 1 and 3. [Para 15][Para 16]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must apply the agreement of 30 April 2014, which promised USD 70 per year of service to workers who volunteered to leave, payable on 10 June 2014? [Para 2][Para 29][Para 64]
3. Whether the employer must pay Mr S, employee no. 08, severance, pay in lieu of notice, moral damages and unused annual leave after dismissing him on 12 June 2014? [Para 4][Para 39][Para 79]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the strike of 28–30 April 2014 won the 30 April agreement, but the employer paid the USD 70 only to some workers, leaving about 25% unpaid, then suspended work from 28 June 2014 without the written authorisation the law requires, although the union honoured its side by not striking between 30 April and 10 June 2014; Mr S had worked as an electrician since 15 January 2011 on an indefinite contract at USD 119 basic and USD 165 average pay, was a prominent union activist, had never been warned, and was dismissed after the employer threatened him with police and prosecution if he did not resign. [Para 30][Para 36][Para 40][Para 41][Para 47][Para 49][Para 50][Para 70]
**Employer (respondent):** absent from both conciliation and hearing, so it offered no position on either point and no evidence at all. [Para 51][Para 57][Para 85]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — point 1 was refused; on point 3 the employer must pay Mr S one month's wages in lieu of notice, severance of 45 days' wages and allowances, pay for unused annual leave, and moral damages equal to the severance. The award is non-binding and becomes final after the objection period. [Para 117][Para 118]
On point 1 the panel accepted that the 30 April agreement was valid and that the union had performed it, but held that the claimants carried the burden of proof: at the hearing they could not state how many workers had volunteered to leave, how many had been paid, or how many remained unpaid, and filed nothing to establish it. The panel therefore could not determine the claim and refused to consider it. [Para 71][Para 74][Para 76][Para 77][Para 78]
On point 3 the panel first confirmed it could proceed without the employer under Article 21 of Prakas 099, since both sides had been properly summoned and the employer's request to postpone to 1 August 2014 would have collided with the statutory deadline for the award; it then applied Article 74, under which an indefinite contract may be terminated at will but never without a valid reason relating to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the needs of the enterprise. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 61][Para 62][Para 81]
Because the employer never appeared, it did not discharge the burden of showing a valid reason, and the panel accepted the union's account: the dismissal was without valid reason. It then computed the statutory consequences. Under Article 75, three years and five months of service required one month's notice, which was never given, so one month's wages are owed in lieu. Under Article 89, service beyond twelve months earns 15 days of wages and allowances per year, rounded up, giving 45 days. Under Articles 166, 167 and 91(3) the worker recovers pay for annual leave not taken, and because the termination was without valid reason Article 91(1) and (3) entitle him to moral damages equal to the severance. Final wages were found already paid. [Para 88][Para 89][Para 92][Para 97][Para 108][Para 110][Para 113][Para 116]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that stays away from the hearing does not merely lose the argument: it fails to discharge its burden of proving a valid reason for dismissal under Article 74, and the panel will accept the claimant's uncontradicted account and award the full statutory consequences — notice, severance at 15 days per year, unused leave and moral damages equal to the severance. By contrast, a claim that an employer breached a settlement agreement fails if the union cannot identify the workers affected and the amounts unpaid, because the burden of proof stays with the party alleging the breach. [Para 81][Para 85][Para 86][Para 71][Para 77]

**Obiter dicta:** that a postponement request which would push the hearing past the deadline for issuing the award cannot be granted, and that the employer's payment of an unexplained extra USD 20 with the final wage did not affect the computation. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 43]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is internally inconsistent on dates and periods. The panel was constituted on "16 July 2023 2014" [Para 12]. The most-representative certificate is dated "15 August 201" at [Para 17] and 15 August 2013 at [Para 29], with validity to 15 August 2015. The order refers to annual leave for "2015" although the employment ended in June 2014 [Para 108]. The objection period in the disposition reads "20" days where the standard formula is 8 [Para 118]. The company name is printed in at least three forms (ស៊ីន ឆាង ស៊ីង, ស៊ីង ឆាង ស៊ីន and XING CHANG XIN (CAMBODIA)), so the Latin form in the title is taken from the union's own letterhead [Para 1][Para 18][Para 32]. Paragraphs 65 to 67 repeat the agreement text in degraded OCR, and the signature block carries OCR noise in place of the president's signature [Para 118].
