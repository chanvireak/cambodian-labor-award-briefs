# Award No. 285/14 — Quint Major Industrial Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 25 November 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kul Vathana, employer-appointed; Sin Kim Sean, worker-appointed; Kong Phalja, presiding) · Doc ID 139745

## 1. Facts
Quint Major Industrial Co., Ltd runs a garment factory at Trapeang Chheu Neang village, Peuk commune, Ang Snuol district, Kandal, with roughly 3,000 workers. A strike by the Khmer Free Workers' Union ran from 22 April to 8 May 2014; on 30 April 2014 the fixed-duration contracts of four activists of the Democratic Union of Garment Workers (C.D.U) expired. A five-point demand list went through conciliation on 6 October 2014 without agreement, and the panel heard it on 24 October 2014. [Para 1][Para 84][Para 98][Para 9][Para 11]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must preserve wages and benefits — including the regular attendance bonus of 20 US dollars and the regular card bonus of 05 US dollars — for workers who came to the factory in May 2014 but could not enter because of the strike? [Para 2][Para 184]
2. Whether the employer must take back the four C.D.U activists in their former roles and pay back wages from the date of dismissal? [Para 3][Para 204]
3. Whether the employer must pay a bonus of 44,000 riel to workers in the hygiene section? [Para 4]
4. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of 2,000 riel per day? [Para 5][Para 309]
5. Whether the employer must stop discriminating against workers who joined the past strike? [Para 6]

Points 3 and 5 were settled by agreement at the hearing and the panel made no ruling on them. [Para 18][Para 161][Para 168]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the four activists were dismissed on 30 April 2014 because of their union activity; other workers were deterred from entering the factory by strikers' threats; and the employer should fund lunch because wages are low and there is no time to cook. [Para 136][Para 199][Para 166]
**Employer (respondent):** the three were not dismissed but simply not renewed at expiry, with ten days' notice, and Mr S was dismissed for serious misconduct in ignoring Injunction No. 284; it never knew of the four's union roles and hired replacements only because orders increased. [Para 137][Para 140][Para 293][Para 296]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three remaining claims were rejected and no sum was ordered. [Para 329][Para 330][Para 331]

The panel first disposed of a representation objection. The employer appeared through its administration head, Mr V, holding a transfer of authority signed by Mr L as general director; the workers argued the company statutes name Ms Y as director, so the transfer was void. Applying Article 19 of Prakas No. 099, and Awards 161/09 and 43/10, the panel held a party may be represented by any person with written authority, that a general director holds power to delegate, and that the objection failed. [Para 171][Para 172][Para 173][Para 174][Para 181][Para 183]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 331 of the Labour Law, which protects freedom of work against coercion or threats during a strike, and read it as putting the duty to secure the workplace on the employer. It found the employer had in fact taken measures — obtaining Injunction No. 284, broadcasting it by loudspeaker, explaining the wages awaiting returning workers, and stationing security, police and military police at the gate — and that over 1,000 workers returned. The workers, who bore the burden of proof, never identified how many were deterred or how, so the claim failed. [Para 185][Para 188][Para 190][Para 193][Para 196][Para 202][Para 203]

On issue 2 the panel held that converting an indefinite contract to a one-year fixed-duration contract in May 2013 was valid: both sides agreed it was voluntary, termination payments were made, and the workers did not contest that at the hearing. Article 13 does not void such an agreement, and Article 67(2) is not breached because the fixed term was one year, within the two-year ceiling. The three non-renewals were therefore lawful under Article 73(1) with the ten days' notice required by Article 73(5) for contracts over six months. Mr S's dismissal was lawful: he ignored Injunction No. 284 and so committed serious misconduct under Article 337. [Para 207][Para 241][Para 255][Para 262][Para 267][Para 283][Para 285]

On issue 4 the panel held the lunch allowance was payable by no statute, collective agreement, internal rule, contract or past practice, so the demand exceeded the Labour Law and was an interest dispute. Because the claimant union was not the most representative in the enterprise, Article 96 and Prakas No. 305 Article 9 denied it standing to bring an interest dispute, and the panel declined to consider it. [Para 311][Para 312][Para 313][Para 326][Para 328]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim for a benefit that no law, collective agreement, internal rule, contract or established practice provides is an interest dispute, and an interest dispute can be arbitrated only by the most representative union in the enterprise, or by unions together commanding more than half the workforce; a minority union's claim is refused consideration, not merely dismissed. [Para 313][Para 321][Para 327]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that an award on an interest dispute stands in place of a collective agreement for one year under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099, which is why standing is policed strictly; and that an employer's duty to protect non-striking workers is judged by the measures it actually took, not by the outcome alone. [Para 190][Para 320]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award name attributes the case to "Quint Major Industrial Co., Ltd" while the Khmer face varies between ឃ្វីន ធ៏ មេជើរ អ៊ិន ដាស ស្ទ្រៀល and other transcriptions. The Institution's registration number appears as Inv. 1077 E/2006 in the findings but as 2546 dated 30 April 2013 in the evidence list. Para 15 prints the panel's creation date as 13 October "2019 2014". Para 268 and Para 307 say the non-renewals were lawful for "both" parties where three workers are meant. Para 279 states the injunction was announced on 28 April 2015 instead of 2014. Mr S's dismissal is dated 30 April 2014 in one place and reasoned under Article 337 in another, and the 48-hour and 83-hour return deadlines are both quoted. The claimant union's membership is given as about 500 of 3,000 workers. Named workers and representatives are redacted here.
