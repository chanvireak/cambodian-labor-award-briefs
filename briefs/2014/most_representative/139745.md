# Award No. 285/14 — Quint Major Industrial Co., LTD (Arbitration Council, 25 November 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Kong Phalla (chair), Kol Vadana, Sin Kim San · Doc ID 139745

## 1. Facts
The employer is a garment-cutting factory in Kandal province, registered in 2013. [Para 84] [Para 25] The complainant union was registered on 14 June 2013, had about 500 members, and was not the most representative union in the factory. [Para 95] Another union struck from 22 April to 8 May 2014; some workers alleged they were blocked and threatened when trying to enter, and one worker was injured on 7 May 2014. [Para 98] [Para 104] The employer obtained a Kandal provincial court protective order requiring strikers to return within 48 hours, posted it on 28 April 2014, and over 1,000 of about 3,000 workers returned. [Para 105] [Para 109] Four union activists had their indefinite contracts converted to one-year fixed-term contracts in May 2013 after receiving termination payments. [Para 207] [Para 208] Three contracts ended on 30 April 2014 with 10 days' notice; the fourth was dismissed for failing to return under the court order. [Para 137] [Para 140] At the hearing the parties settled Issues 3 and 5, and the union withdrew the claim concerning one worker; the parties chose a non-binding award. [Para 18] [Para 19] [Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer had to maintain wages and benefits, including the USD 20 attendance bonus and USD 5 quota bonus, for workers who came but could not work in May 2014? [Para 96]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer had to reinstate four union activists and pay back wages from the stoppage date until return? [Para 117]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the employer had to pay a lunch allowance of KHR 2,000 per day? [Para 164]
4. (Preliminary) Whether the employer's representative held valid written authority under Prakas No. 099? [Para 173]

## 3. Positions of the parties
The union argued that workers who attempted to work in May 2014 were entitled to wages and the USD 20 and USD 5 bonuses because the employer failed to protect them from strike intimidation. [Para 97] It sought reinstatement of the four activists with back wages, alleging union discrimination because the employer knew their roles and targeted them alone while hiring replacements. [Para 142] [Para 144] It argued the contract conversions breached Article 13 and Article 67(2) of the Labour Law. [Para 135] It sought KHR 2,000 daily lunch money because wages were low and workers had no time to cook. [Para 166]
The employer said it obtained the court order, posted notices, used security, police and military police, and enabled more than 1,000 workers to return; the workers did not dispute this. [Para 109] [Para 111] For three activists it said the fixed-term contracts simply expired with lawful 10-day notice. [Para 137] [Para 138] The fourth was dismissed for serious misconduct in ignoring the return order. [Para 140] It denied knowing their union membership, said 25 workers were not renewed, and linked new hiring to increased orders after the strike. [Para 143] [Para 145] It said no law required lunch money. [Para 167]

## 4. Award and reasoning
The panel first held that the employer's representative was validly authorised: Prakas No. 099 requires written authority, the delegation letter of 21 October 2014 was signed and sealed, and official letters showed the signatory acted as general manager. [Para 173] [Para 177] [Para 182]
On Issue 1, Article 331 protects non-strikers from coercion, and the employer bears a duty to secure the workplace; but the claimant must prove the specific workers, dates and threats. [Para 185] [Para 188] [Para 200] The union gave no such evidence by the 31 October 2014 deadline, while the employer's precautions were undisputed, so the claim failed. [Para 201] [Para 202] [Para 203]
On Issue 2, the panel found the four activists had freely converted their indefinite contracts to one-year fixed terms after payment, with no proof of coercion, so the change violated neither Article 13 nor Article 67(2). [Para 243] [Para 256] The three non-renewals were lawful: under Article 73 the fixed term expired on 30 April 2014 and 10 days' notice was given. [Para 260] [Para 266] [Para 267] The dismissal of the fourth was serious misconduct under Article 337 for failing to return within 48 hours of the order's posting on 28 April 2014. [Para 270] [Para 280] [Para 285] The panel found no union discrimination under Article 12 or Article 279, because the union produced no proof that the employer knew their roles or acted on them. [Para 286] [Para 287] [Para 306]
On Issue 4, no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules or past practice required lunch money; the claim was an interests dispute, which only a most-representative union may bring, so it was refused. [Para 311] [Para 313] [Para 327]
**Outcome:** dismissed; the claims for wage and benefit maintenance (USD 20 and USD 5), reinstatement and back pay for the four activists, and the KHR 2,000 daily lunch allowance were all rejected, and no payment or reinstatement was ordered. [Para 329] [Para 330] [Para 331]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A wage-continuation claim based on strike intimidation requires proof of the particular workers, dates and threats; general assertions do not discharge the claimant's burden of proof. [Para 201] [Para 202]
A conversion from an indefinite to a fixed-term contract is valid where genuine and free consent is shown, and a party challenging it must identify the specific legal defect. [Para 243] [Para 255]
An interests claim for a new benefit may be brought only by a most-representative union, or unions together representing more than half the workforce. [Para 321]
**Obiter dicta:** The panel's citations of earlier awards were persuasive comparisons only; this award is non-binding and is not precedent. [Para 23] [Para 197]
