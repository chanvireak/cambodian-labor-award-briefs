# Award No. 167/14 — Te Yong Cambodia Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 23 July 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Tun Si Phan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 139620

## 1. Facts
Te Yong Cambodia Co., Ltd cuts and sews garments and employs 943 workers, about 80 per cent of them women. [Para 42][Para 57] Its union, Te Yong's Labour for Cambodia, held registration certificate No. 3071 of 12 November 2013 and Ministry recognition letter No. 340 naming Ms V as president, Ms M as vice-president and Mr O as secretary; it was not the most representative union in the company. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45] Of eleven demands, four were settled in conciliation, three were withdrawn, and four came to the panel — lunch, health allowance, nursery and attendance-bonus deduction. The company has a room called a nursery, but around 58 workers from other unions use it to rest at midday, so mothers cannot leave children there. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 26][Para 53]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Point 2) Whether the company must provide one free meal a day or 5,000 riel per day as lunch money? [Para 46]
2. (Point 3) Whether the company must pay a $5 per month health allowance? [Para 50]
3. (Point 4) Whether the company must build a nursery on its premises, or pay $50 per month in its place, for children aged 18 months and over? [Para 53][Para 101]
4. (Point 6) Whether the company must deduct the attendance bonus in proportion to the days a worker is actually absent without permission, rather than withholding the whole $10? [Para 59][Para 113]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Minimum wages are still too low, other factories give a free meal or $10 to $15 a month for lunch, and the $5 health allowance is an incentive other roadside factories still pay; the nursery exists in name only and 80 per cent of the workforce are women; the attendance bonus is part of wages, so one day's absence should cost one day's worth. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 51][Para 56][Para 69]

**Employer (respondent):** It has no capacity to pay lunch money or a health allowance; it will rebuild the nursery but cannot say when; and it withholds the whole $10 bonus from anyone absent without permission, as no law requires a pro-rata deduction. [Para 49][Para 52][Para 58][Para 70][Para 71]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — points 2, 3 and 6 dismissed; on point 4 the company was ordered to set up a nursery able to take children from 18 months within three months of the award taking effect, and meanwhile to pay the actual cost of outside daycare against receipts. [Para 125][Para 126][Para 127][Para 129]

**Point 2 (lunch).** No law, agreement, collective agreement, past practice or internal rule obliged the company to give a meal or 5,000 riel a day, so the demand was an interest dispute. [Para 72][Para 73] The panel recalled that it decides interest disputes only where the union bringing them is the most representative in the enterprise, with more than half the workers as members, and this union was not. [Para 82][Para 85][Para 86]

**Point 3 (health allowance).** Point 1 of Notice No. 103 of 9 April 2013 merged the $5 health allowance into the basic wage, with the minimum set at $75 for probationary and $80 for full workers, and Notice No. 206 of 2011 was expressly derogated. Since 1 May 2013 the $5 has been inside the minimum wage, so no separate obligation remains; the panel followed earlier awards 229/13 and 137/14. [Para 87][Para 89][Para 94][Para 95] It added that the company in fact pays nothing, and that the demand was in any event an interest dispute this union could not bring. [Para 96][Para 98][Para 100]

**Point 4 (nursery).** Article 186 of the Labour Law requires an enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a nursing room and a nursery in or near the premises, and, where it cannot take children over 18 months, to let mothers place them in an outside nursery at the employer's expense. [Para 101][Para 102] The panel read that as a real obligation, not one met by a room that cannot accept children: a nursery used by other unions' representatives for midday rest does not discharge it. [Para 104][Para 107] The company's promise to rebuild without any date was not enough, hence the three-month deadline, with reimbursement of outside daycare against actual invoices in the meantime. [Para 108][Para 111][Para 112]

**Point 6 (attendance bonus).** The two sides gave opposite accounts of the deduction practice and neither produced a payroll record or any other document; the workers promised evidence and filed none by the deadline. [Para 115][Para 119][Para 121] With no basis to prefer either account, the panel declined to rule on the point. [Para 122][Para 124]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Article 186 is satisfied only by a nursery that can actually take children from 18 months; a nominal nursery the employer cannot open to them leaves the obligation unperformed, and the employer must then meet the real cost of outside daycare against invoices. [Para 104][Para 112]

**Obiter dicta:** That an attendance-bonus practice will not be characterised at all where neither party produces a payroll record, each side's assertion cancelling the other's. [Para 122]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The lunch figure is printed three ways — 5,000 riel in the demand and order, "២៥០ រៀល" in the reasons, and "២ ៥០០០ រៀល" in the dispositive point. [Para 46][Para 71][Para 125] The demand list is numbered irregularly ("90-", "អ្នក99-") and names leaders (Ton Sopheak Nita and others) who do not appear in the recognition letter. [Para 11][Para 12][Para 44] Stray years ("២០២២" at Para 21, "២០១៥" at Para 18) interrupt dates otherwise fixed in 2014, and the Article 186 quotation is OCR-damaged ("ក្តីក្មេងក្តី" for women). [Para 101]
