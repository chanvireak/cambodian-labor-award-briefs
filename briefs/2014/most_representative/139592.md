# Award No. 104/14 — Phnom Penh Sorya Transportation (Arbitration Council, 4 July 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Sin Kim Sean, worker-appointed, dissenting in part; Tang Tri, chair) · Doc ID 139592

## 1. Facts
The company runs inter-provincial bus and taxi services with about 400 staff, and 17 drivers who attended all four hearings were the original claimants. [Para 173][Para 174][Para 175] A collective dispute over seven demands, filed after a strike on 3 April 2014, left six points unsettled, of which the workers merged points 4 and 5. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 29][Para 533] The employer dismissed or declared abandoned 17 drivers between 8 and 21 April 2014, after a union election on 6 April in which the claimants stood as candidates, and after strikes on 3, 10–12 and 17–28 April. [Para 529][Para 530][Para 767][Para 855] The union notified the employer of its candidates on 4 April and of the results on 9 April 2014. [Para 524][Para 528]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise wages by 60 USD per month above the current wage, which averages 120 USD? [Para 551]
2. Whether the employer must pay 200 per cent of the ordinary daily wage for work on public holidays and on weekly rest days? [Para 570]
3. Whether the employer must pay a 10 USD monthly health care allowance? [Para 600]
4. Whether the 17 drivers' fixed-duration contracts of two years or more became indefinite-term contracts? [Para 605]
5. Whether the employer must take the 17 drivers back into work? [Para 688]
6. Whether the employer must pay wages and other benefits during the strike? [Para 999]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** wages of about 120 USD are too low to feed a family and keep a driver fit to drive, especially now that the employer bans them from carrying parcels and picking up passengers for extra income; the dismissals targeted union candidates; holiday and Sunday work must be paid at 200 per cent. [Para 189][Para 191][Para 192][Para 238][Para 206]
**Employer (respondent):** no minimum wage is fixed for transport, and driver pay already exceeds the garment minimum plus per-trip, parcel and passenger allowances; the dismissals were redundancies or abandonments, not discrimination, and the union was told but its letter was never opened. [Para 193][Para 194][Para 383][Para 527]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the wage-increase and health-allowance claims were refused consideration, holiday and rest-day pay at 200 per cent was ordered, 15 of the 17 drivers were ordered reinstated, two were refused reinstatement but awarded severance, and strike-period wages were refused. [Para 1017][Para 1018][Para 1019][Para 1020][Para 1021][Para 1022][Para 1025]
On issues 1 and 3 the panel found no statute, collective agreement, contract, internal rule or practice fixing those sums, so both were interests disputes. [Para 552][Para 553][Para 600][Para 601] Applying Article 96 and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099, an interests award substitutes for a collective agreement and removes the right to strike over the same subject, so only a most-representative union may bring such a claim; the 17 drivers were not, and the panel declined to consider both demands. [Para 561][Para 563][Para 568][Para 569]
On issue 2 the panel applied Article 164 and Article 4 of Prakas No. 10 of 4 February 1999, holding that holiday work earns the day's wage twice over, and Article 139 as amended for rest-day work, which attracts a 100 per cent premium. [Para 571][Para 572][Para 574][Para 597] It ordered payment at 200 per cent for both holiday and weekly rest-day work. [Para 582][Para 599]
On issue 4 the panel applied Article 67(7), under which a fixed-duration contract not reduced to writing becomes indefinite, and Article 67(8), under which a contract of two years or less renewed silently becomes indefinite. [Para 605][Para 606][Para 626][Para 632] Seven drivers who held only a probationary contract had no written contract thereafter, and two who held one-year contracts were renewed silently, so all nine had indefinite contracts. [Para 623][Para 633][Para 640] Following Case 10/03, it held that successive renewals totalling more than two years convert the relationship to indefinite, and found the seventh driver over the line. [Para 645][Para 653][Para 657] For the seven drivers on two-year contracts it accepted, on Article 311 of the Civil Code, that parties may agree to change an indefinite contract to a fixed one, but held the employer could not thereby escape its obligation to pay severance for the old indefinite contract. [Para 658][Para 673][Para 677][Para 678]
On issue 5 the panel applied Article 293, which bars dismissal of a staff representative or candidate without the labour inspector's prior authorisation, extended to union candidates by points 3 and 4 of Prakas No. 305, which protect candidates 45 days before and after an election. [Para 740][Para 741][Para 742][Para 750] Six drivers were candidates or elected leaders, were dismissed inside the protected window, and the employer never sought authorisation — notifying the labour-dispute office after the fact does not count — so the dismissals were void under point 4 of the Prakas. [Para 767][Para 770][Para 792][Para 794][Para 799][Para 800] For the nine drivers the employer called abandoners, the panel found the absences were strike days, Khmer New Year holidays and weekly rest days, none of which can be counted as unlawful absence, so no abandonment occurred and the terminations were the employer's own acts without valid reason under Article 74. [Para 855][Para 884][Para 897][Para 905][Para 907][Para 913]
On issue 6 the panel applied Article 332(1), under which a strike suspends the contract so no wage is owed, and Article 333, which forbids sanctions for strike participation. [Para 1000][Para 1004][Para 864] Because the strike was called without the seven days' notice required by Article 324 and without exhausting conciliation under Article 320(4), the drivers forfeited pay for the strike days. [Para 871][Para 877][Para 1014]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union candidate or elected leader dismissed within the 45-day window around an election, without the labour inspector's prior authorisation, is dismissed unlawfully; the dismissal is void and reinstatement follows, and a notice filed with the labour-dispute office after the dismissal does not cure it. [Para 742][Para 793][Para 799][Para 800]

**Obiter dicta:** where an employer replaces an indefinite contract with a fixed-term one without paying severance, it remains liable for the termination benefits of the earlier contract even if the later contract is valid. [Para 677][Para 678]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award date is 4 July 2014 in the header and at the foot, but the extension paragraph moves the deadline from 22 May 2014 only. [Para 31][Para 1081] The panel was constituted on "28 April 2019 2014" and one hearing is dated "22 May 2019 2014". [Para 25][Para 26] The number of drivers is unstable: 17 attend and sue, 15 are reinstated, 6 are treated as protected, and the dismissal dates are given as 10, 12, 13 and 21 April, with the panel at one point recording termination "from 12 January 2014". [Para 175][Para 736][Para 772][Para 1021] The dissent of the worker-appointed arbitrator argues for reinstating the two refused drivers and is annexed after the signature page, which is partly corrupted. [Para 1027][Para 1029][Para 1080]
