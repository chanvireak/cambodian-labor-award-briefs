# Case No. 263/14 — A & J Carter (Cambodia) Limited (Arbitration Council, 22 October 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer, Liv Sovanna for the workers, Pen Bun Chea chair) · Doc ID 139736

## 1. Facts
A & J Carter (Cambodia) Limited (អេ & ជេ ខាត ធើរ (ខេមបូឌា) លីមីធីត) is a garment factory at Trapaing Kong, Samraong Tong, Kampong Speu, with about 982 workers. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 95] The Khmer Workers' Union at the factory was registered by the Ministry on 16 September 2013 under certificate No. 3039, but holds no certificate of most-representative status; a rival, the Khmer Youth Voice Union, held that certificate under No. 023/14 of 01 April 2014. [Para 37][Para 78][Para 79][Para 97][Para 98] Seventy-four workers fingerprinted a transfer of rights to the federation on 15 September 2014. [Para 99] Of thirteen demands, five survived conciliation — reinstatement of Mr M as day-shift ironing section head, three allowance claims, and pay during the strike of 11–17 September 2014. [Para 22][Para 100][Para 131][Para 132][Para 133]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take Mr M back as day-shift ironing section head and restore him to his position? [Para 100][Para 156]
2. Whether the employer must pay an attendance bonus of USD 15 per month instead of the USD 10 it pays? [Para 203]
3. Whether the employer must pay a travel and lodging allowance of USD 15 per month instead of the USD 10 it pays? [Para 224]
4. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of Riels 4,000 per day? [Para 233]
5. Whether the employer must maintain 100 per cent of wages and other benefits during the strike? [Para 237]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr M was dismissed for union activity and his dismissal was discriminatory, since he had performed well and his team had won bonuses while the night-shift head had not; market prices have risen and other factories pay an attendance bonus of USD 15, a travel allowance of USD 13–15 and more for lunch; other factories such as FWKK kept wages and benefits during a strike. [Para 112][Para 113][Para 119][Para 124][Para 128][Para 137][Para 180]
**Employer (respondent):** the night-shift ironing team was abolished, leaving two section heads for one job; both were evaluated and Mr M scored lower, so his fixed-duration contract was ended with payment to its expiry date of 06 September 2014; the company has already granted benefits by agreement with the most representative union for 2014, including incentives of USD 40 to 80 a month; the strike was unlawful and unpaid, and no replacements were hired to break it. [Para 109][Para 110][Para 111][Para 115][Para 120][Para 121][Para 145][Para 250]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed on all five issues — the panel refused reinstatement, declined to consider the three allowance claims for want of representativity, and held that no wages or benefits were owed for the strike period. [Para 202][Para 223][Para 232][Para 236][Para 253][Para 254][Para 255][Para 257][Para 258][Para 259]

On issue 1 the panel first classified the claim as a rights dispute, because reinstatement of a dismissed worker is governed by the Labour Law. [Para 156][Para 157] Article 293 requires the labour inspector's agreement before a staff delegate or candidate is dismissed, and Article 3(3) of Prakas No. 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001 extends that protection to every candidate in a union leadership election for 45 days before and after the poll; Article 4 extends it to founders and members while registration is pending, and thereafter to the three leaders — president, vice-president and secretary. [Para 159][Para 160][Para 161][Para 164][Para 165][Para 170] The panel applied its three-condition test from Awards 50/05, 64/05, 07/06, 148/07, 155/11 and 200/12: protected status, dismissal within the protected period, and notice to the employer. [Para 167][Para 168][Para 169] Mr M was a union activist (សកម្មជន), a category the Prakas does not list, so the first condition failed and the panel did not need to reach the others. [Para 171][Para 172][Para 173][Para 174]

On discrimination the panel recalled Articles 12 and 279, which forbid an employer to use union membership or activity as a ground for hiring, discipline or dismissal, and its earlier awards requiring the worker to prove the discrimination alleged. [Para 175][Para 176][Para 178][Para 184][Para 185] The employer had acknowledged that Mr M's August 2014 work was of good quality but relied on a wider evaluation covering attendance, motivation, skill, responsibility and targets, in which he scored lower than the night-shift head; the workers produced no specific evidence of union motive, so no discrimination was found. [Para 183][Para 185][Para 186] The contract was a six-month fixed-duration contract running from 07 March to 06 September 2014, ended three days early with payment to expiry, which the panel held lawful under Article 73. [Para 187][Para 196][Para 197][Para 198][Para 199][Para 200][Para 201]

On issues 2 to 4 the panel found the employer paying the USD 10 attendance bonus required by point 2 of Notification No. 230 K.B/S.J.N of 25 July 2012, a USD 10 travel allowance above the USD 7 the notification requires, and a lunch allowance it considered more favourable than the law; no law, collective agreement, internal rule or practice required USD 15, USD 15 or Riels 4,000. [Para 203][Para 205][Para 206][Para 224][Para 226][Para 227][Para 233] Each claim therefore exceeded the legal floor and was an interest dispute, which the panel may decide only for a union representing more than half the workforce, since such an award replaces the collective agreement for a year and removes the right to strike over those claims. [Para 207][Para 208][Para 217][Para 218][Para 219] The claimant union was not the most representative union, so all three claims were declined. [Para 221][Para 222][Para 230][Para 231][Para 235][Para 236]

On issue 5 the panel held the claim to be a rights dispute and applied Article 332(1): the contract is suspended during a strike, no work is provided and no wages are paid. [Para 237][Para 238][Para 239][Para 240] Article 334 forbids the employer to hire replacements during a strike and obliges it to pay the strikers if it does; here no replacements were hired for that purpose. [Para 241][Para 246][Para 250] Following Awards 27/10 (Hat Industry) and 178/12 (Dae Koro), and because this strike was called without prior notice and without the other procedural steps, the panel held no wages or benefits were owed. [Para 243][Para 246][Para 249][Para 252][Para 253]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union activist who is neither a staff delegate, an election candidate, an ex-delegate, a founder or member awaiting registration, nor one of the three union officers, does not enjoy the protection of Article 293 and Prakas No. 305, so the employer may end his fixed-duration contract on an evaluation without the labour inspector's agreement. [Para 170][Para 172][Para 173]

Where the employer provides no work and pays no wages during a strike because the contract is suspended under Article 332(1), no attendance bonus or allowance is owed for the strike days either, and a duty to pay arises only if the employer hires replacements in breach of Article 334. [Para 240][Para 246][Para 252]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the allowances the employer pays already exceed Notification No. 230, and its account of the wider evaluation criteria, were not necessary to orders resting on the union's lack of standing and on the absence of protected status. [Para 183][Para 226]
