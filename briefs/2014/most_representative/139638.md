# Award No. 180/14 — Dewhirst (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 1 August 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed, dissenting in part), An Nan (worker-appointed), Kong Pollak (president) · Doc ID 139638

## 1. Facts
Dewhirst (Cambodia) is a garment factory at Toul Pongro, Chom Chao, Phnom Penh, employing over 2,000 workers, with a collective agreement of 25 July 2013 negotiated with the Khmer Union Federation, certified most representative until 25 October 2015. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 90][Para 51] The Democratic Workers' Union Federation of Cambodia filed 18 demands in May 2014; two were settled at the hearing, two were withdrawn, and ten were decided. [Para 35][Para 40][Para 42] The union relied on 147 thumbprints, but only 125 names were legible, so the panel treated 126 workers (125 plus the officer present) as claimants. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 166] Both parties chose a binding award, the first issuing date of 25 July 2014 being deferred to 1 August 2014. [Para 44][Para 43]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the overtime meal allowance and the weekly bonus once a week instead of once a month? [Para 78]
3. Whether the employer must pay an extra 2,000 riel an hour of overtime meal money, making 4,000 riel an hour? [Para 95]
4. Whether the employer must provide two dishes with the midday meal? [Para 98]
5. Whether the employer must grant 100 days' maternity leave and pay 50 per cent of the average of the last 12 months' wages before the leave begins? [Para 107]
6. Whether the employer must raise the milk allowance from USD 5 to USD 20 a month for children aged 18 to 36 months? [Para 120]
7. Whether the employer must pay USD 3 a day for meals on public holidays and Sundays? [Para 124]
8. Whether the employer must raise the attendance bonus from USD 11 to USD 15 a month? [Para 128]
9. Whether the employer must let pregnant workers leave 25 minutes early twice a day, beyond the existing 5 minutes? [Para 133]
10. Whether the employer must raise the travel and accommodation allowance from USD 14 to USD 20 a month? [Para 140]
11. Whether the employer must convert fixed-term contracts into unfixed-term contracts for workers whose successive contracts exceed two years? [Para 146]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** monthly payment of the overtime meal money leaves them borrowing by the third week of each month, and the weekly bonus is too small when spread over a week; one dish of rice with pork fat does not feed Muslim workers; 90 days is too short to recover after childbirth and travelling to collect maternity pay is unsafe; wages and rents have risen. [Para 80][Para 82][Para 103][Para 115][Para 142]
**Employer (respondent):** the collective agreement at clause 8.3 fixes 2,000 riel of meal support payable with the monthly payroll, which most workers prefer because a weekly sum is quickly spent; the factory gives free rice and one dish plus dessert on Fridays, a free meal on holidays, 11 USD attendance bonus, 5 USD milk money and 14 USD travel and lodging, all above the legal floor. [Para 84][Para 91][Para 100][Para 128][Para 140]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must pay the overtime meal allowance weekly, recalculate maternity pay at 50 per cent for 90 days and pay it at least one day before leave starts, and convert 20 workers' fixed-term contracts to unfixed-term; all other claims were dismissed. No lump sum is printed. [Para 361][Para 366][Para 373]

On the meal allowance the panel held Notification 041/11 of 7 March 2011 to be a legal norm of general and mandatory application across the garment and footwear sector, requiring 2,000 riel a day or one free meal for voluntary overtime without specifying the payment date. [Para 167][Para 186][Para 187] The collective agreement's monthly payment is contrary to that instrument and cannot override it; because money must be available to buy food while working overtime, payment must be reasonably frequent and the employer was ordered to pay weekly. [Para 189][Para 172][Para 192] The weekly production bonus, by contrast, falls within management's discretion under Art 2, and monthly payment with wages was upheld. [Para 210][Para 214][Para 215]

The claim for a doubled hourly meal rate failed for want of any law, agreement or practice fixing 4,000 riel. [Para 218][Para 219] The two-dish claim failed because the law requires no midday meal at all, the employer already gives free rice, one dish and Friday dessert, and the roughly 10 per cent of Muslim workers may buy alternatives on site. [Para 253][Para 255][Para 256] The milk, holiday-meal, attendance-bonus, early-departure and travel claims each exceeded Notification 230 of 25 July 2012 or practice, and were dismissed as interest claims the non-representative claimant could not bring. [Para 307][Para 327][Para 330]

On maternity, Arts 182 and 183 give 90 days at 50 per cent of wages and allowances, and the employer's delay of two or three days after leave began breached Art 115, which requires wages to be paid before the rest day; the panel ordered recalculation on the 12-month average and payment at least one day before leave. [Para 258][Para 268][Para 270][Para 285][Para 286]

On contracts, Art 67(2) caps fixed-term contracts and their renewals at two years, and Art 73(5) converts them to unfixed term where renewal takes the total beyond that; the panel followed award 10/03 and ILO Recommendation 166, and ordered conversion for the 20 workers shown to exceed two years. [Para 340][Para 343][Para 353][Para 354][Para 360] Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented, holding that an employer which has paid the 5 per cent end-of-contract allowance each time has performed Art 73 and need not convert. [Para 429][Para 444]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A sector-wide notification fixing a daily overtime meal allowance prevails over a factory collective agreement that defers payment to month-end, because the legal norm is mandatory and the agreement cannot reduce it; and successive fixed-term contracts whose total exceeds two years convert by operation of law into an unfixed-term contract. [Para 186][Para 189][Para 343]

**Obiter dicta:** that an employer may set bonus schemes and their payment dates within its management discretion where no law or agreement fixes them. [Para 210][Para 213]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is printed as binding yet the file also carries a dissenting opinion by the employer-appointed arbitrator against issue 11, so the conversion order is a majority, not unanimous, holding. [Para 44][Para 427] The claimant group is given as 500 in the federation's assertion, 147 on the thumbprint list, 125 legible names and 126 claimants; the legibility finding is itself printed twice with different counts. [Para 72][Para 75][Para 164][Para 166] The maternity paragraph reads "50 persons per cent" where 50 per cent is meant. [Para 258] The milk claim is pleaded as USD 20 total but argued as a USD 15 increase on a USD 5 practice, and the operative order says "156 dollars". [Para 121][Para 288][Para 368] Notification 103 is dated "09 December April 2013" in one recital. [Para 124] Marginal OCR has replaced several passages with Latin fragments (paras 76, 352, 423), and the annexed name lists are largely unreadable. [Para 76][Para 423]
