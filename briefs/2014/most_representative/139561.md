# Award No. 098/14 — Wai Foul Garmen (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 June 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Vireak, appointed by the workers, dissenting on issue 1; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 139561

## 1. Facts
Wai Foul Garmen (Cambodia) Ltd is a garment manufacturer at Pong Teuk village, Ang Snuol district, Kandal province, with about 850 workers. Eight demands survived conciliation in April 2014 after a strike: reinstatement of seven workers whose fixed-duration contracts were not renewed, higher attendance, meal and travel allowances, a full-month overtime incentive, a team-leader adjustment allowance, performance of all agreements with the Free Trade Union, and preservation of wages during the 18–19 April 2014 strike. [Para 3][Para 4][Para 9][Para 16][Para 18][Para 131] Both sides had signed a memorandum of understanding of 3 October 2012 making rights decisions binding but leaving interest disputes to a non-binding award. [Para 31][Para 35]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the seven workers whose fixed-duration contracts expired and were not renewed? [Para 135][Para 299]
2. Whether the employer must raise the attendance bonus from USD 10 to USD 20 a month? [Para 216][Para 368]
3. Whether the employer must raise the midday meal allowance from USD 6.5 to USD 15? [Para 222][Para 387]
4. Whether the employer must raise the travel and accommodation allowance from USD 8 to USD 15? [Para 228][Para 394]
5. Whether the employer must restore the USD 10 monthly incentive for a full month of two-hour overtime? [Para 234][Para 401]
6. Whether the employer must pay team leaders a USD 10 adjustment allowance? [Para 253][Para 420]
7. Whether the employer must perform the agreement recorded in the conciliation minutes of 15 January 2014? [Para 262][Para 431]
8. Whether the employer must preserve wages and the attendance bonus during the strike of 18–19 April 2014? [Para 267][Para 436]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The seven contracts expired and were simply not renewed without the three warnings and union participation promised in the 15 January 2014 minutes, though none of them had ever been warned or disciplined; market prices had risen and neighbouring factories paid more, so the allowances should rise; the overtime incentive was an established practice stopped without notice; the strike days should be paid. [Para 204][Para 218][Para 225][Para 247][Para 272][Para 304]
**Employer (respondent):** The contracts expired by operation of law and there was no work to renew them into; the 15 January minutes bound it only if the workers returned on 16 January, and only 50 to 60 of about 800 did; it pays at or above the Prakas No. 230 minimums and other factories' policies are not its own; past practice on the incentive is unproven; striking workers earn no wages. [Para 210][Para 219][Para 262][Para 305][Para 312][Para 437]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all eight demands were refused or dismissed; nothing was ordered payable and no sum was awarded [Para 450][Para 453][Para 456]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 73(1) of the Labour Law, under which a fixed-duration contract expires when its term ends, holding that expiry ends the employment relationship automatically and that neither party can compel the other to renew an agreement without consent. It found the warning procedure in the 15 January 2014 minutes was bargained for against the workers' return on 16 January 2014, a condition that failed when only a small fraction returned, so the employer was not bound by it. It noted that some of the seven had in fact kept working into April, but held that did not create an obligation to renew. [Para 300][Para 302][Para 306][Para 313][Para 315][Para 321]

Issue 7 was refused as already decided within issue 1. [Para 433][Para 434]

On issues 2 to 6 the panel first classified each demand. Under Article 312 of the Labour Law it decides rights disputes according to law and collective agreements and other disputes in equity, and under Prakas No. 099 Article 43 an award on an interest dispute stands in place of a collective agreement for one year and removes the right to strike over that subject. Reading Article 96 of the Labour Law with Prakas No. 305 Article 9, it held that only a most-representative union, or unions together representing more than half the workforce, may bring an interest dispute to it. [Para 293][Para 296][Para 379][Para 381]

Applying that, it found the union here had applied for most-representative status but had neither obtained the certificate nor protested the delay, so it was not most representative. The attendance, meal and travel demands were interest claims: the USD 10 bonus, USD 6.5 meal and USD 8 travel allowances already met or exceeded the Prakas No. 230 minimums, and no law, agreement, internal rule or practice required more. Those three were refused. The overtime incentive and the team-leader allowance failed for want of proof — the panel could not find the practice sufficiently clear, continuous and long-standing, and no team leader was identified as a claimant. [Para 283][Para 289][Para 292][Para 370][Para 386][Para 415][Para 428]

On issue 8 the panel applied Article 332(1), which suspends the employment contract during a strike and bars wages for that period, and Article 334, which requires wages to be paid only where the employer hires replacements — which both sides agreed had not happened. The demand was dismissed. [Para 437][Para 444][Para 447][Para 448]

Arbitrator An Vireak dissented on issue 1, reasoning that the warning clause in the 15 January minutes stood independently of the return-to-work sentence, that the employer terminated without any warning or union participation, and that the expired contracts should therefore be renewed for an equal term. [Para 461][Para 466][Para 468]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The expiry of a fixed-duration contract ends the employment relationship by operation of law and creates no right to renewal, so the panel will not order reinstatement of workers whose contracts have run their term; and where a union lacks most-representative status it cannot bring an interest dispute, which leaves demands for benefits above the statutory minimum undecided. [Para 302][Para 381][Para 385]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observation that some of the seven kept working into April 2014, and its remarks on the effect of an interest award in displacing a collective agreement for a year, did not affect the outcome. [Para 321][Para 380]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The Khmer original is heavily degraded. It exists in a bifurcated form: the eight issues are argued and decided in the body, but the order at Para 450 to 456 lists only issues 1, 7 and 8 as rights matters and 2 to 6 as interest matters, without restating the reasoning, and the numbering of the annexed dissent restarts at one. [Para 450][Para 458] The dissent is signed by arbitrator An Vireak, yet the signature block of the main award carries only two names and no date, and the award is dated 30 June 2014 at the foot of the annex rather than in the body. [Para 458][Para 469] The company appears under several transliterations, including "Wai Foul Garmen", "Wai Foul", "Wai Ful" and "Wai Garment". [Para 21][Para 131] Figures conflict internally: the attendance demand is stated as USD 20 but the addition is described as USD 100 plus USD 10; the meal allowance is given as USD 6.5 in the reasoning and USD 10 in the order; and the workforce is given as 850, then "about 700". [Para 217][Para 224][Para 393][Para 27][Para 132] The date of the 2014 collective agreement appears once as 24 February and once as 24 March, and the probative value of the union's most-representative application is dated differently in two places. [Para 28][Para 50]
