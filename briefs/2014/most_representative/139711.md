# Award No. 248/14 — Chin Yi Garmen (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 10 October 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party — dissenting, Kong Phalla chair) · Doc ID 139711

## 1. Facts
Chin Yi Garmen (Cambodia) is a garment factory at Samaki Mean Chey, Kampong Chhnang, employing about 3,350 workers. [Para 1][Para 65][Para 66] Four federations filed 17 demands on 25 August 2014; conciliation settled 1, and the hearing settled 12 more, leaving 4 points for decision: the attendance bonus, the overtime meal allowance, paid time for antenatal checks and maternity pay. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 29][Para 30] 450 workers transferred their rights to the four federations by power of attorney of 25 August 2014, but none of the local unions had been registered with the Ministry of Labour. [Para 54][Para 67][Para 68]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must add USD 5 to the USD 10 attendance bonus to make USD 15 per month, and must reduce that bonus in proportion to the days a worker is absent with its permission for personal reasons? [Para 69][Para 70][Para 90][Para 111]
2. Whether the employer must pay an overtime meal allowance of Riels 2,000 per hour on top of the Riels 2,000 already paid, making Riels 4,000 for overtime from 4:00 to 6:00 p.m.? [Para 76][Para 77][Para 140]
3. Whether the employer must allow women workers one day per month to attend antenatal checks while keeping their wage and other benefits intact? [Para 80][Para 150]
4. Whether the employer must pay 50% of wages, including allowances, for 90 days of maternity leave, at least one day before the leave begins? [Para 85][Para 163][Para 170]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the attendance bonus does not cover their expenses and the flat deductions are too steep; previous awards required pro-rata deduction; rice and food have risen in price; half a day is not enough for an antenatal check because hospitals are crowded and require queueing; the Labour Law requires 50% of wage and allowances for three months before maternity leave. [Para 71][Para 78][Para 81][Para 87]
**Employer (respondent):** it already applies Notification No. 230 by paying USD 10, and refuses the increase and the pro-rata deduction because the notification states that no bonus is paid in case of absence; it already pays Riels 2,000 for overtime meals under Notification No. 041; it allows one day or half a day for antenatal checks case by case and keeps pay intact; it already pays 50% of wage and allowances for two months before leave and the third month on return to work. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 82][Para 83][Para 84][Para 85][Para 86][Para 88][Para 89]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issues 1, 2 and 3 were refused; on issue 4 the panel ordered the employer to pay 50% of wages including allowances for 90 days of maternity leave at least one day before the leave begins. [Para 178][Para 179][Para 180][Para 181][Para 182]

On issue 1(a) the employer was paying the USD 10 minimum under point 2 of Notification No. 230 K.B/S.J.N of 25 July 2012, and no law, collective agreement, agreement, internal rule or past practice required a further USD 5, so the claim was for a benefit above the law, an interests dispute; because the complainant was not the most representative union, the panel declined to consider it under Article 96 of the Labour Law, Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 SKBY and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY. [Para 90][Para 92][Para 93][Para 94][Para 95][Para 96][Para 98][Para 101][Para 110] On issue 1(b) the panel noted that it generally follows its earlier awards, but found it could not do so here because those awards applied Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011, which set a USD 7 minimum and contained no words "without absence", whereas Notification No. 230 replaced it from 1 September 2012 with a USD 10 minimum and added that phrase. [Para 111][Para 114][Para 116][Para 117][Para 118] Reading the notification as making attendance without absence the condition of the bonus, and noting Article 115's rule that wages are paid monthly, the panel held the employer's schedule — USD 2 off for one day, 4 for two, 6 for three, and the whole bonus for four — to be a reasonable practice that is more favourable than the law and may continue. [Para 119][Para 120][Para 121][Para 128][Para 136][Para 137][Para 139]

Issue 2 was an interests dispute: the employer paid the Riels 2,000 required by point 2 of Notification No. 041/11, no instrument required Riels 4,000, and the non-representative complainant could not bring it. [Para 141][Para 142][Para 143][Para 144][Para 145][Para 146][Para 147][Para 149] Issue 3 was likewise: the Labour Law creates no right to extra paid leave for antenatal checks, following award No. 17/07, and the employer's practice of granting one day or half a day case by case is already more favourable than the law. [Para 150][Para 151][Para 156][Para 157][Para 158][Para 162]

On issue 4 the panel found Articles 182 and 183 confer 90 days' maternity leave at half wage including allowances, and Article 103 lists the sum payable during maternity leave as wages; Article 115(3) forbids paying wages on a rest day, so payment must be made at least one day before. [Para 163][Para 164][Para 168][Para 169] The employer's split — two months before and one month after return — does not conform, and the full 90 days must be paid before the leave starts. [Para 173][Para 174][Para 175][Para 176][Para 177]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Maternity pay of 50% of wage and allowances for 90 days is wages under Article 103, and because Article 115(3) forbids payment on a rest day it must be paid in full at least one day before the leave begins; an employer's practice of paying part before and part after the worker returns does not comply. By contrast, where a notification makes the attendance bonus conditional on attendance "without absence", an employer's graduated deduction — part of the bonus for one, two or three days' permitted absence and all of it for four — is more favourable than the law and may continue, and earlier awards applying a superseded notification do not bind. [Para 118][Para 130][Para 137][Para 169][Para 176]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that it will follow its earlier decisions unless there is a new legal basis or good reason to depart, and its review of Notification No. 041/11's replacement, are general statements of method; the refusal of issues 1(a), 2 and 3 turned on the complainant's lack of representativeness, not on their merits. [Para 95][Para 117][Para 147][Para 160]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- Arbitrator An Nan filed a dissenting opinion annexed to the award, arguing that the two other arbitrators ignored consistent earlier awards (Nos 223/2012, 216/2012, 214/2012, 211/2012, 204/2012, 203/2012 and 180/2012) requiring pro-rata deduction; the award prints the dissent at [Para 184]–[Para 189]. It labels the point "issue 10" where the award calls it issue 1(b).
- The award is dated 10 October 2014 [Para 1], and the parties agreed to postpone issuance from 29 September to 10 October 2014 [Para 38].
- The demand list is inconsistent: 17 points were conciliated [Para 24], one was settled there, 16 came forward [Para 25], and 12 of those were settled at the hearing [Para 29], yet the list at [Para 3]–[Para 17] contains only 16 numbered points and the first of them is numbered 2.
- Notification references are garbled in several places — "4041/11" at [Para 143], "ប្រការ ៤៣០" for Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 at [Para 160] — and the quotation of Article 115(3) is truncated at [Para 166].
- The operative paragraph on issue 1(b) reads "សមាត្រ ទៅ ១៨" (to 18), which is meaningless; the reasoning at [Para 136] supplies the actual schedule.
- An English-language fragment intrudes into the reasoning at [Para 123] ("Primatourato invorational…"), and the chair is printed as "Kong Phalla" in the reasons and "Kong Phalha" in the signature block [Para 1][Para 184].
