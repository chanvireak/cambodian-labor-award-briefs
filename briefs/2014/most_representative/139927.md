# Award No. 281/13 — Sixplus Industry Co., Ltd. (រោងចក្រ ស៊ិច ផ្លាស់ អ៊ិន ដាស ស្ទ្រី) (Arbitration Council, 28 January 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (កៅ ថា ច appointed by the employer, វ៉ែន ពៅ appointed by the workers, ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា chair) · Doc ID 139927

## 1. Facts
The respondent is a garment factory employing 1,700 workers, with two unions on site, of which the Khmer Child Labour Union (សហជីព ការងារ កូន ខ្មែរ) held the most representative status. [Para 45][Para 47][Para 48] Thirty-four workers transferred their right to sue to union federations, and three federations carried four unresolved demands to the Council after conciliation had settled 10 of 14 points. [Para 16][Para 49] The demands were to raise the transport and accommodation allowance from USD 7 to USD 15 a month, to raise the lunch allowance from 1,500 to 4,000 riel a day, to stop deducting the "cooperation bonus" when a worker leaves at 4:30 pm, and to dismiss an electrician named Ban Boeut. [Para 57][Para 71][Para 76][Para 88]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must add USD 8 to the transport and accommodation allowance, taking it from USD 7 to USD 15 a month? [Para 57]
2. Whether the employer must add 2,500 riel to the lunch allowance, taking it from 1,500 to 4,000 riel a day? [Para 71]
3. Whether the employer must stop deducting the cooperation bonus in proportion to the days a worker is absent when the worker leaves at 4:30 pm? [Para 76]
4. Whether the workers may require the employer to dismiss the electrician Ban Boeut? [Para 88]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** rickshaw fares, once USD 7 a month, rose as soon as the minimum wage rose, reaching USD 15 in Kampong Cham and USD 18–20 in Kang Meas and Kaoh Kong, and some factories already pay USD 15; the bonus is not by its terms tied to overtime and no internal rule says it is; Ban Boeut, as union president, refused to support their protest, failed to pay a promised 100,000 riel to a worker who gave birth, took union dues from non-members, and insulted strikers over a microphone. [Para 58][Para 61][Para 82][Para 89][Para 91][Para 92]
**Employer (respondent):** frequent strikes and thin order books leave no margin; total monthly earnings already reach USD 120–130 through attendance and special allowances, plus 1,500 riel lunch money and morning bread; other factories pay no lunch money at all; the USD 5 cooperation bonus goes only to workers who did overtime for a full month; and it cannot dismiss Ban Boeut, the administration head inviting complaints to be brought to him instead. [Para 63][Para 65][Para 69][Para 75][Para 81][Para 95][Para 96]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all four claims refused, with no sum ordered. The ruling on issue 4, as a rights dispute, became final immediately under the parties' agreement of 10 January 2014; the rulings on issues 1–3, as interest disputes, became final 8 days after notification unless a party objected in writing to the Minister. [Para 165][Para 166][Para 170]

The panel opened by separating rights from interest disputes: under Article 312(2) of the Labour Law and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099, a rights dispute is decided according to law, collective agreement and practice, while an interest dispute is decided by equity. [Para 98][Para 101][Para 103] On issue 1 it found the transport allowance fixed at USD 7 by Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012, a figure the employer in fact paid; no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice required USD 15, so the demand was "better than the law" and an interest dispute. [Para 104][Para 106][Para 107][Para 108] Issue 2 failed for the same reason: no new law, agreement, internal rule or practice obliged the employer to pay more than the 1,500 riel it paid. [Para 123][Para 124][Para 125] Issue 3 likewise: the bonus, paid only where the worker worked well through a month of overtime, was a practice more favourable than the law, and nothing required pro-rata deduction, so the demand was also an interest dispute. [Para 134][Para 135][Para 137][Para 139]

Issue 4 was treated differently. Because the claim turned on termination of an employment contract, the panel classified it as a rights dispute. [Para 143] Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 311 of the 2007 Civil Code confine the power to extinguish contractual obligations to the parties to the contract, so a third party cannot dissolve the contract between employer and worker; only the employer could end Ban Boeut's employment. [Para 144][Para 145][Para 146][Para 150] Following its earlier awards, the panel added that workers may demand a dismissal only where they prove the person is dangerous and cannot be retained without endangering others; the four accusations were unsupported by any specific fact or evidence, and the broken promise of 100,000 riel to a worker who gave birth was not a collective labour dispute within Article 302 at all. [Para 151][Para 153][Para 159][Para 161]

The interest claims then failed on a second, independent ground. Since an interest award replaces the collective agreement for one year and deprives workers of the right to strike over the same subject, the panel held that only a most representative union, or several unions together representing more than half the workforce, may bring such a dispute, citing Awards 81/04, 98/04, 169/11, 402/11 and 66/11. [Para 117][Para 118][Para 119] Here 34 workers out of 1,700, acting through three federations, none of them most representative, lacked standing, and the panel declined to consider issues 1–3. [Para 120][Para 121][Para 122]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a benefit exceeding what statute, collective agreement or established practice already provides is an interest dispute, and the panel will decline to hear it unless the claimant is the most representative union at the enterprise or unions together representing more than half the workers — because an interest award substitutes for the collective agreement for a year and forecloses strike action on that subject. A demand that the employer dismiss a co-worker is a rights dispute, but only a party to the employment contract may terminate it, and workers must prove the person is dangerous to others before any such order can be made. [Para 118][Para 122][Para 146][Para 151]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the Khmer Child Labour Union was bound by the 3 October 2012 memorandum on rights disputes although not a signatory, and that a rights ruling binds the parties immediately while an interest ruling binds only after the objection period, were general observations on the effect of the memorandum and were not necessary to the dismissal of any claim. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 166][Para 170]

- The electrician's name is printed four ways — បាន បឺត, បាន ប៊ីន, បាន បើន and បាន បឹង — and the panel itself resolved the variants in the reasons; the brief uses the form it adopted. [Para 53][Para 56][Para 165]
- The number of federations to which the 34 workers transferred their rights is given as five in the facts and as three in the standing analysis; the award is inconsistent on its face. [Para 49][Para 120]
- The chair's name appears as ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា in the header but the OCR of the signature block garbles it to a digit and "ថៃ" ប៊ុន ណា". [Para 1][Para 171]
- Prakas No. 099 is dated "21 April 2019 2004", two years run together. [Para 100]
- The December payroll of Ms C Yita is said to have been paid on 03 January 2013 although the month is December 2013. [Para 87]
- The order on issue 2 inserts a stray year into the amount ("២០១៦ ២៥០០ រៀល"). [Para 168]
- The lists of participating federations are unstable: one federation is rendered "សហព័ន្ធ ហស ជីព" and the list of five collapses to three. [Para 27][Para 51]
