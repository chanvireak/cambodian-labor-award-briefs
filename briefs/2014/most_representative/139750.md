# Award No. 299/14 — Hong Va (Cambodia) Garment Manufacturing Limited (Arbitration Council, 25 November 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ouk Ri (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Run Saray (president) · Doc ID 139750

## 1. Facts
Hong Va (Cambodia) Garment Manufacturing Limited, a garment factory registered in 1997 and employing more than 3,300 workers, faced a strike demand over allowances; conciliation settled seven of nine points and left two. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 12] Four base unions held registration certificates from the Ministry of Labour with about 1,199 members between them — 370, 100, 449 and 280 — while the Khmer Workers' Movement Union held none. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47] The two remaining claims were a monthly transport-and-accommodation allowance of USD 15 (paid: USD 8) and a monthly attendance bonus of USD 20 (paid: USD 10). [Para 50][Para 55]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to pay a transport-and-accommodation allowance of USD 15 per month, that is USD 7 above the USD 8 it already pays? [Para 61]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to pay an attendance bonus of USD 20 per month, that is USD 10 above the USD 10 it already pays? [Para 81]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** transport and room rents have risen repeatedly and workers live far from the factory, so the existing USD 8 is inadequate; neighbouring factories pay a USD 20 attendance bonus, and the USD 10 paid is too little to encourage attendance. [Para 52][Para 57]
**Employer (respondent):** it already does better than the law — USD 8 against the USD 7 required by Notification No. 230, and the USD 10 bonus that notification prescribes — and it is waiting for the Government's 2015 minimum-wage decision before reviewing the allowances. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 59][Para 82]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider both claims, finding the claimants lacked standing to bring an interest dispute. [Para 80][Para 87][Para 88]

On each issue the panel first characterised the claim. Point 1 of Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 fixes a transport-and-accommodation allowance of USD 7 per month; the employer pays USD 8, which is more favourable. [Para 61][Para 62] No provision of the Labour Law, no collective agreement, no internal rule and no past practice obliges the employer to pay USD 15, so the demand is an interest dispute rather than a rights dispute. [Para 63][Para 64] Point 2 of the same notification requires an attendance bonus of at least USD 10, which the employer pays; a demand for USD 20 has no statutory, contractual or customary basis and is likewise an interest dispute, exceeding what the law requires. [Para 81][Para 83][Para 84][Para 85]

For interest disputes the panel applied Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001: only a most-representative union, or several unions acting together whose combined membership exceeds half the enterprise's workforce, may bring such a dispute. [Para 68][Para 71] Neither party disputed that no union in the factory yet held most-representative status. [Para 43] The four registered unions together had about 1,199 members among more than 3,300 workers — under half — so the group was not representative and could not seize the panel of an interest claim. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 77][Para 78] The panel followed Awards 169/11, 19/08 and 20/08, which declined interest disputes brought by unions without representative capacity. [Para 72][Para 74][Para 79]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a benefit above what the Labour Law, a collective agreement, internal rules or established practice already secures is an interest dispute, and the panel will not consider it unless the union bringing it is most representative in the enterprise or the unions acting together represent more than half the workforce; a group of registered unions commanding under half the workers has no standing, and the merits are never reached. [Para 71][Para 78][Para 79]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that three individual workers attended claiming membership of the unregistered Khmer Workers' Movement Union, and that this union had filed no mandate — immaterial once standing failed at the aggregate level. [Para 47][Para 48]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel-building date and the Prakas 099 citation both print a stray "2019" before the correct "2014". [Para 14][Para 69] The award lists five federations at the hearing but only four registered base unions, the fifth having no certificate. [Para 44][Para 47] Amounts are corrupted in the operative part, where the USD 20 bonus appears as "៤ ២០" and elsewhere "១ ២០". [Para 81][Para 89] The objection period is printed as 08 days, unusually short. [Para 90] No riel figures appear; all sums are in US dollars as printed.
