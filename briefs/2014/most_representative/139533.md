# Award No. 050/14 — Global A Parallel Limited (Arbitration Council, 4 April 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Tun Si Phan for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139533

## 1. Facts
Global A Parallel Limited, a garment factory in Trapaing Tuol village, Khan Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh, employs about 2,196 workers and hosts seven unions, none of which holds most-representative status. [Para 42][Para 46] On 25 December 2013 the Ministry's labour-dispute department received a complaint, lodged in the employer's name, over workers' demands to improve several working conditions. [Para 17][Para 43] Conciliation on 26 February 2014 settled one of six demands; the five unresolved points reached the Council on 3 March 2014 under report No. 251 Kb/Ak/Vk of 28 February 2014. [Para 18][Para 19] The claims were brought by workers who are members of the Union Development federation's factory union. [Para 44]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a midday meal allowance of 2,000 riel or provide one meal a day to each worker? [Para 50]
2. Whether the employer must raise the regular attendance bonus by 15 dollars a month? [Para 58]
3. Whether the employer must allow pregnant workers to leave 15 minutes before the end of the shift? [Para 64]
4. Whether the employer must pay an overtime meal allowance of 4,000 riel a day for overtime worked from 15:15 to 17:15? [Para 76]
5. Whether the employer must pay the overtime meal allowance once a week? [Para 82]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** other factories pay a midday meal allowance — Hong Wah 2,000 riel, Bery 3,000 riel, Prey Boeung 4,000 riel — or give a free meal, so Global should do the same; the neighbouring Samy Na factory pays a 15-dollar attendance bonus; a single exit gate and crowding expose pregnant workers to danger; and food prices have risen, so the overtime meal allowance should be paid weekly rather than monthly. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 61][Para 67][Para 79][Para 86]
**Employer (respondent):** the company cannot afford the demands, having just raised the minimum wage, with most wages already above 100 dollars; it pays the 10-dollar attendance bonus and 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance required by ministerial notification; the five-minute early exit is already given by badge to pregnant workers, with three separate exits; and monthly payment of the meal allowance suits its accounting staff. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 62][Para 71][Para 74][Para 88][Para 90]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay the overtime meal allowance once a week (issue 5); the claims on issues 1 to 4 were dismissed. [Para 173][Para 175][Para 178]

The panel first separated rights disputes from interests disputes: under Article 312 of the Labour Law it decides rights disputes by law and interests disputes in equity, and a right exists only where a law, agreement, collective convention, internal rule or past practice provides it. [Para 92][Para 97][Para 98] Applying that split, it held that issues 1 to 4 raised no legal or conventional entitlement at the figures claimed, so they were interests disputes. [Para 99][Para 101][Para 119][Para 125][Para 138] Following Prakas No. 099 and earlier awards, an interests dispute may be arbitrated only where the referring union holds most-representative status, because such an award replaces the collective convention for a year and binds all workers; the federation's factory union did not, so the panel declined to consider those claims at all. [Para 105][Para 107][Para 113][Para 114]

On the attendance bonus, point 2 of Notification No. 230 Kb/S.J.N of 25 July 2012 requires at least 10 dollars a month for workers with no absences, and the employer's payment of 10 dollars complied; the further 5 dollars was an interests claim. [Para 115][Para 117][Para 119] On the overtime meal allowance, point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 Kb/S.J.N of 7 March 2011 fixes 2,000 riel a day or a free meal, which the employer paid, so the extra 2,000 riel was likewise an interests claim. [Para 133][Para 134][Para 138]

Issue 5 was different: because the allowance is fixed by Notification 041/11, the timing of payment is a rights question. [Para 142] The panel read the notification as requiring the meal, or 2,000 riel in its place, on each day overtime is worked, and as not specifying when the money must be handed over. [Para 144][Para 146][Para 147] It weighed the employer's accounting burden — which it accepted was real — against the workers' need to have money to buy food on the day of overtime, and held that an administrative burden cannot cut down a legal right; weekly payment was a reasonable compromise that also eased the company's paperwork. [Para 164][Para 165][Para 169][Para 170]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An interests dispute — a demand for a benefit not fixed by law, agreement, collective convention, internal rule or past practice — may be arbitrated only where the union referring it holds most-representative status at the enterprise; absent that status the panel will not consider the demand on its merits, whatever its equity. Where a ministerial notification fixes a floor (a 10-dollar attendance bonus, a 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance), payment at the floor discharges the legal obligation and any demand above it is an interests claim; but where the notification fixes the entitlement and is silent on the interval of payment, the frequency of payment is a rights question the panel decides by law, and the employer's accounting convenience does not defeat it. [Para 107][Para 113][Para 119][Para 142][Para 170]

**Obiter dicta:** though dismissing the 15-minute claim, the panel observed that early exit does not prevent crowding if ordinary workers also leave early, that the employer should take measures to stop that, and that no pregnant worker had yet been injured. [Para 128][Para 130][Para 131]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award date is printed as 4 April 2014 on the face [Para 1], but the panel is said to have been constituted on "05 March 2023 2014" [Para 20], and Prakas No. 099 is dated "21 April 2023 2004" twice [Para 94][Para 106]; the 2023 figures are OCR intrusions.
- The company name is rendered inconsistently in Khmer (ក្លូប័ល / កូប័ល / ក្លូប៊ល) [Para 1][Para 33][Para 44] and no Latin-script form appears anywhere in the file; "Global A Parallel Limited" follows the Council's usage in other awards on the same employer.
- The employer is recorded both as the party filing the complaint [Para 17][Para 43] and as respondent to claims brought by its own workers [Para 44]; both statements are reported as printed.
- The midday-meal demand is written "2,000 riel" in the conciliation list [Para 2] and the figures in issue 4 alternate between 4,000 riel total and "2,000 riel more" on top of 2,000 riel [Para 76][Para 78][Para 133].
- Issue 4's order recites "from the year 2016, 15:15 to 17:15" [Para 178], a stray year absent from the claim [Para 76].
- Repeated phrases and duplicated sentences ("the panel in this case… the panel in this case") mark OCR doubling at [Para 62][Para 111][Para 157][Para 175].
