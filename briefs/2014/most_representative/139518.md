# Award No. 032/14 — Wai Shing Plastic Products (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 14 March 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Pen Bunchea (Chair), Sin Kim San (worker party), Ing Sothy (employer party — dissenting) · Doc ID 139518

## 1. Facts
Wai Shing Plastic Products (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, at Trapeang Sang Chiek, Samrong Tong district, Kampong Speu, makes garment hangers with about 80 workers, some 30 per shift across three eight-hour shifts that run 24 hours [Para 35][Para 47][Para 51]. Workers receive US$100 a month in wages, an attendance bonus of US$18 and US$8 for transport and accommodation [Para 54]. The company union held a registration receipt dated 18 December 2013, and the federation held authorisations from 29 workers when the panel sat on 19 February 2014 [Para 40][Para 91]. Conciliation settled 11 of 17 demands, leaving six, from which the workers then withdrew one [Para 15][Para 20].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1 and 2 (merged). Whether the employer must provide one free meal a day, or 4,000 riel a day, to workers on all three shifts, payable weekly? [Para 44][Para 45]
3. Whether the employer must reduce the US$18 attendance bonus in proportion to the days of absence rather than forfeiting it, where the absence was authorised by the employer? [Para 56][Para 129]
5. Whether the employer must pay a US$10 monthly incentive bonus to all workers who are not section or team heads? [Para 65][Para 202]
6. Whether the employer must provide a free meal or 4,000 riel to every shift working on public holidays or Sundays? [Para 73][Para 207]

Demand 4, on retention of wages and bonuses during certified sick leave, was withdrawn at the hearing and is not decided; the panel renumbered the six surviving demands 1 to 6 before analysis [Para 20][Para 43].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the factory is about six kilometres from any market and an hour and a half from cooked food, so the 15 workers released to eat each shift must buy meals outside; comparable factories (Sang Woo, Sabrina, Wil Sen) provide a meal or meal money [Para 47][Para 48][Para 50]. A worker absent with permission should lose only a proportionate part of the attendance bonus, as other nearby factories do [Para 61][Para 62]. An incentive bonus should reward the ordinary workers who supply the labour, not only team heads [Para 71]. Work on holidays and Sundays is overtime and should attract a meal or 4,000 riel [Para 74][Para 208]. **Employer (respondent):** the wage, bonus and allowances already exceed what the law requires, and it will pay whatever a law or prakas requires [Para 54][Para 55]. It has never paid incentive bonuses, and from February 2014 only about five team and section heads would receive US$10 on conditions set at management's discretion [Para 66][Para 67][Para 69]. Holiday work is paid at 200 per cent and Sunday work at 200 per cent, and a 2,000-riel meal allowance is paid only for overtime beyond eight hours [Para 74][Para 77].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — demands 1 and 2, 5 and the 4,000-riel limbs of 6 are refused; the employer must reduce the attendance bonus in proportion to authorised absences (3a), may forfeit it entirely for unauthorised absence (3b), and must give a free meal or 2,000 riel for voluntary Sunday overtime (6) [Para 264][Para 265].

*Union standing.* Under Article 268(2) two months' silence from the Ministry deems a union registered, and more than two months had run from 18 December 2013 to the hearing, so the union was a lawful professional organisation even though the Ministry had issued no certificate and the claim of a suspension of registrations was unsupported [Para 93][Para 88]. No letter recognised its leaders, however, and the two workers who claimed the posts of president and vice-president produced no evidence, so neither could represent the membership; only the 29 workers who gave written authority were claimants [Para 104][Para 106][Para 109].

*Demands 1 and 2.* No provision of the Labour Law, a collective agreement, the internal rules or past practice obliges the employer to give a free meal or 4,000 riel, so the demand exceeds the law and is a dispute over interests rather than rights [Para 112][Para 113]. Because a Prakas 099 Article 43 award on interests creates a collective agreement binding all workers for a year, only a most-representative union may bring such a claim; with 29 of 80 workers the claimant was not, so the panel declined to consider the demand [Para 120][Para 125][Para 126].

*Demand 3.* Absence authorised by the employer suspends the contract under Articles 71(6) and 72(1), so no wage is due for those days and no attendance bonus either; but the bonus is a form of wage under Article 103, and the internal rules, which only say the company will pay "according to the prakas", do not justify forfeiting all US$18 for a permitted absence [Para 176][Para 178][Para 182]. Fairness, and the practice of neighbouring factories, support a pro-rata reduction keyed to days absent; the employer may still withdraw the whole bonus where the absence was never authorised, since such absence obstructs the production line [Para 175][Para 184][Para 200]. Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented, holding that a bonus is a gift whose conditions its giver alone sets, so a worker who fails the condition earns nothing [Para 288][Para 291].

*Demand 5.* No law, agreement or practice obliges the employer to pay an incentive bonus to non-supervisory staff, so the demand likewise exceeds the law and is an interests dispute; it was refused [Para 203][Para 204][Para 206].

*Demand 6.* Work on a public holiday is not overtime: Article 164 and Praka No. 10 of 4 February 1999 give an extra day's wage in compensation instead, so the 4,000-riel claim fails [Para 232][Para 234][Para 241]. Sunday work is different, because Sunday is the weekly rest day and work beyond the normal weekly hours is overtime, so Notification No. 041/11 entitles those workers to a meal or 2,000 riel, which the employer had not paid [Para 252][Para 254][Para 262].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus is a form of wage, so where the employer itself authorised the absence the contract is merely suspended and the bonus must be reduced in proportion to the days missed, not forfeited: a rule that the company will pay "according to the prakas" supplies no basis for total forfeiture. Total forfeiture remains lawful for unauthorised absence. A demand for a benefit no law, agreement or practice provides is a dispute over interests, and a union holding only a minority of the workforce cannot bring one, because such an award would bind every worker for a year. Holiday work earns an extra day's wage rather than an overtime meal, while Sunday work is overtime and does attract the meal or 2,000 riel [Para 184][Para 121][Para 234][Para 252].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that no competent institution has yet settled whether the words "without absence" in Notification No. 230 cover authorised as well as unauthorised absence, and that it could not itself so interpret them without regard to legality and reasonableness; it decided the point case by case instead [Para 154][Para 159].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The federation's authorisations are counted as 29 workers in the findings, but as 297 in the reasoning on representation [Para 40][Para 108][Para 124].
- The company is spelled វ៉ៃ ស៊ីង ប្លាស្ទីក ប្រដាក់, ប្លាក ស្ទីក, ផ្លាស្ទីក and ធ្លា ស្ទីក across the text; the only Latin form, WAI SHING PLASTIC PRODUCTS (CAMBODIA) Co., LTD, appears once [Para 34].
- Six demands were referred and one then withdrawn, yet the panel numbered six issues 1 to 6, leaving a gap at 4 that the analysis silently skips [Para 20][Para 43].
- The award's closing paragraph on its type repeats and garbles the eight-day opposition rule, naming the Minister twice in one sentence [Para 271].
- The internal rules are cited both by point 5 of part B and by point 4 of part A,  [Para 57][Para 81].
