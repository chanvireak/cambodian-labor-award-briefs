# Award No. 146/14 — Sou Pheachhem Footwear (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 3 July 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, employer-appointed; Tuon Siphann, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · binding award by agreement of the parties · Doc ID 139604

## 1. Facts
Sou Pheachhem Footwear (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a shoe factory at Prey Veng, Dangkao, Phnom Penh, employed about 450 workers. The claimant was the Cambodian Federation of Unions for Workers' Rights (the federation), which had no local union at the factory and filed no authorisation letters from workers; only two workers attended the hearing, and the panel treated those two as the claimants. Of twenty demands, thirteen were settled in conciliation on 29 May 2014, two more (issues 3 and 5) at the hearing, and issue 4 was withdrawn, leaving four: the basic wage, the timing of Sunday pay, an extra overtime meal allowance, and a skills allowance. [Para 1][Para 48][Para 50][Para 51][Para 22][Para 28]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a basic wage of US$110 a month to workers who became full-rights workers after 1 February 2014? [Para 52][Para 85]
2. Whether the employer must pay Sunday work wages one day after the work, rather than one week later? [Para 62][Para 105]
6. Whether the employer must add 2,000 riel to the current 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance, making 4,000 riel, for overtime worked from 16:00 to 18:00? [Para 74][Para 120]
7. Whether the employer must provide an appropriate skills allowance to workers? [Para 79][Para 130]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Full-rights workers with the same skills should receive the same basic wage, yet those becoming full-rights before February 2014 received US$110 while those becoming full-rights afterwards received only the US$100 minimum; waiting a week for Sunday overtime pay was hard on workers' household budgets; food, soup and sugar-cane juice had all risen in price, justifying 4,000 riel; and skilled workers who can run two or three machines, some with years of service, deserved a skills allowance, as Bavet garment factories paid US$10 to workers who could sew on two machines. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 65][Para 67][Para 77][Para 81][Para 84]
**Employer (respondent):** It applied Prakas No. 317 of 31 December 2013, under which the minimum for full-rights workers was US$90 before the Prakas took effect and US$100 after, and it had raised pay by US$20 from 1 February 2014 so that workers then reaching full-rights status received US$110; it paid Sunday overtime at 200% of the ordinary daily wage within one week of the work, which was better than the law required; and it already paid the 2,000 riel meal allowance required by Notification 041/11 and had never paid a skills allowance. [Para 55][Para 57][Para 61][Para 63][Para 68][Para 78][Para 79]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all four remaining demands were declined, and the award, made binding by the parties' agreement of 16 June 2014, became final immediately. [Para 135][Para 139]

On the classification question the panel set out the interests-dispute doctrine: Article 96 of the Labour Law and clause 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 give the union with Most Representative Status (MRS) the right to negotiate a collective agreement; clause 43 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 makes an interests award a substitute collective agreement binding all workers for a year and removing their right to strike; so only a union with MRS, or unions together representing more than half the workforce, may bring an interests dispute (Awards 81/04, 98/04, 169/11, 02/11, 66/11, 278/13). [Para 92][Para 95][Para 98][Para 100][Para 101]

Applying that, the two claimant workers had no such standing. The federation had no local union at the factory and produced neither an authorisation letter nor a delegation of rights from any worker, so the two who attended were the only claimants and could not bring an interests dispute at all. [Para 50][Para 102][Para 103][Para 104]

On issue 1 the panel found the employer had done what Prakas No. 317 required: the basic wage stood at US$90 before 1 February 2014 and the employer added US$20 from that date, so workers reaching full-rights status afterwards received US$100 as the Prakas set, while those already full-rights received US$110; no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice fixed US$110 for the later group. The demand was therefore an interests dispute and was declined. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 87][Para 89][Para 90][Para 91]

On issue 2 the panel found Sunday overtime pay to be part of wages under Articles 103 and 116 and payable at least twice a month with no more than sixteen days between payments; the employer in fact paid 200% of the ordinary daily wage within one week, which was more favourable than the law, and no rule or practice required payment the next day. The demand exceeded the law and was declined as an interests dispute. [Para 108][Para 111][Para 112][Para 114][Para 115][Para 117][Para 119]

On issue 6 point 2 of Notification 041/11 requires 2,000 riel a day, or a free meal, for voluntary overtime without fixing the amount by hours worked; the employer paid 2,000 riel for the two hours from 16:00 to 18:00, which satisfied the notification, and nothing obliged it to pay 4,000 riel. The demand was an interests dispute and was declined. [Para 121][Para 122][Para 124][Para 126][Para 129]

On issue 7 nothing in the Labour Law, a collective agreement, an internal rule or past practice obliged the employer to pay a skills allowance, and it had never paid one, so the demand was again an interests dispute and was declined. [Para 130][Para 131][Para 132][Para 134]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the claimant union has no local union in the enterprise, files no authorisation from the workers it claims to represent and brings only two workers to the hearing, it lacks standing to bring an interests dispute, because an interests award substitutes for a collective agreement and binds the whole workforce; and a demand that exceeds a Prakas, a notification or the Labour Law, with no agreement or practice supporting it, is an interests dispute that will be declined on that ground alone. [Para 100][Para 103][Para 115][Para 126]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the employer's one-week payment of Sunday overtime at 200% was "more favourable than the law", and its account of the pre-February 2014 basic wage of US$90 raised by US$20 to US$110. [Para 114][Para 88]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is a Khmer original only. The company's name is printed four ways — "ស៊ូភើ-ឈម ហ្វ៊ូត វែរ", "ស៊ូ ភើ ឈែម ហ្វ៊ូត វែរ", "ស៊ុប ភើឈែម ... ខូអិលធីឌី" and "ស៊ូ ភើ-ឈែម" — and paragraph 46 records that the panel adopted the name appearing in the commercial register and company seal. Registration numbers conflict (Inv 1620 E/2013 in one place, 4650 in another), and paragraph 24 dates the panel's constitution to "05 June 2022 ... 2014", an OCR corruption. Paragraph 26 speaks of eleven non-conciliated issues and two settlements, while the procedural recitals say seven non-conciliated issues, so only the final count of four (issues 1, 2, 6 and 7) is reliable. Amounts are printed inconsistently: paragraph 123 gives the meal allowance as "20 riel" against 2,000 riel elsewhere, paragraph 135 prints "11000 dollars" for US$110, and paragraph 85 gives the cut-off as "019 February 2014".
