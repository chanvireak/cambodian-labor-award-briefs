# Award No. 287/14 — Seng Hong Yan Gamen (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 21 November 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ieng Sothy, employer-appointed; An Nan, worker-appointed, dissenting; Nyan Somunninth, presiding) · Doc ID 139746

## 1. Facts
Seng Hong Yan Gamen (Cambodia), a sportswear factory at Prek Pring Khang Tboung, Choam Chao, Por Senchey, Phnom Penh, faced a strike and a 13-point demand list of which 10 were settled in conciliation on 2 October 2014. Two points remained for the panel: how the attendance premium is cut when a worker takes authorised personal leave, and a demand to add five dollars to the existing travel and accommodation allowance. At the hearing the third remaining point, a workplace nursery, was settled. [Para 1][Para 15][Para 20][Para 21]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must cut the attendance premium pro rata, by the number of days of authorised personal leave taken, instead of the present stepped deductions? [Para 2][Para 44]
2. Whether the employer must add five dollars to the travel and accommodation allowance, on top of the ten dollars already paid each month? [Para 4][Para 146]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the 15-dollar attendance premium should be divided by the 26 working days of a month so that each authorised personal leave day costs only its daily share, as Awards 01/13, 283/13 and 247/14 required; and other factories already pay more. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 55][Para 58]
**Employer (respondent):** it applies a stepped practice — one dollar off for a first leave day, a second dollar for a second day, and the whole premium forfeited from three days — with no cut for annual, special or sick leave; and it already pays ten dollars against the seven the 2012 notification requires. [Para 3][Para 49][Para 50][Para 52][Para 148]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both claims were rejected, and the award is expressly non-binding. [Para 168][Para 169][Para 170]

On issue 1 the majority treated the attendance premium (ប្រាក់រង្វាន់ធ្វើការទៀងទាត់) as a conditional reward rather than base wage: Article 103 classifies bonus payments as wage, but the premium depends on fulfilling conditions, so it cannot be split into daily fractions unless the instrument creating it says so. Reading point 2 of Notification No. 230 K.B/S.J.N. of 25 July 2012, the panel held "absence" there means unlawful absence, and that a worker granted unpaid personal leave has agreed to forgo wages for those days — so paying a full premium as well would be unfair to the employer. The claim for pro-rata deduction was therefore refused. [Para 138][Para 141][Para 142][Para 103][Para 104][Para 145]

On issue 2 the panel found the employer already paid ten dollars a month, more than the seven dollars that point 1 of the same notification requires, so no legal duty to add five dollars existed. The claim was in substance a demand beyond the law, hence an interest dispute. Applying Article 96 and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305, the panel held an interest dispute may be arbitrated only by the most representative union, or by unions together representing more than half the workforce; here the factory union held no registration certificate at all, and the 282 transferring workers fell short of the total workforce, so the panel declined to consider the claim. [Para 146][Para 147][Para 148][Para 150][Para 151][Para 160][Para 165][Para 167]

Arbitrator An Nan dissented on issue 1 only, recording that eight earlier awards (Nos 223/12, 216/12, 214/12, 211/12, 204/12, 203/12, 180/12 and 181/12) had ordered exactly the pro-rata deduction the majority refused, and that departing from them without new legal grounds broke the Council's own consistency rule. [Para 172][Para 176][Para 178][Para 179]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance premium is a conditional reward, not base wage, so it is not apportioned day by day: where the instrument creating it fixes a stepped forfeiture, a worker on authorised unpaid personal leave keeps only what that instrument allows, and a claim to a pro-rata share must be brought as an interest dispute — which only the most representative union in the enterprise, or unions commanding more than half the workforce, may bring. [Para 141][Para 145][Para 160]

**Obiter dicta:** the majority acknowledged that panels had interpreted point 2 of Notification No. 230 inconsistently since 2012, and said the divergence itself justified a fresh reading; the dissent's contrary view on precedent is recorded in the annex. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 177]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The workforce figure is printed two ways: "more than 10,430" at Para 38 and "more than 4,300" at Para 164, against 282 transferring complainants. The complainant union is described both as an unregistered factory union and, elsewhere, as a registered one with a 2013 certificate. Para 20 records that one of the three unresolved points was settled, yet the panel's reasoning still opens three issues and the dispositive mentions only points 1 and 2. Para 17 prints the panel's creation date as "14 October 2019 2014". Para 146 states the notification requires 7 dollars while Para 120 speaks of 10 dollars for the same instrument. The award type is printed as non-binding in the body but the notification paragraph speaks of it becoming final. Named workers, union officials and employer representatives are redacted here.
