# Award No. 019/14 — Dong Du Tech Sty (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 6 February 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3 — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (president) · Doc ID 139509

## 1. Facts
Workers at Dong Du Tech Sty (Cambodia) went on strike from 25 January 2014 pressing 16 demands on conditions of work, union recognition and the reinstatement of union leaders. The employer applied to the Labour Disputes Department, conciliation failed on 29 January 2014, and the file reached the Council the same day. On 31 January 2014 the Council issued Order No. 005/14 K.A.K. ordering an immediate suspension of the strike and a return to work from the morning of 1 February 2014; the strike continued. [Para 1][Para 23][Para 27][Para 28]

## 2. Issues in dispute
The non-conciliation report listed 16 demands: [Para 2]
1. Whether the company must take back the union leaders and activists of the Cambodia Labour Union for Development at unchanged wages and benefits? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company must remove the administrator Rok Ki Chhay from the factory for union discrimination and threats? [Para 3]
3. Whether the company must recognise that union at the Dong Du Tech Sty factory? [Para 4]
4. Whether workers are owed a meal allowance of 4,000 riel for overtime from 16:00 to 18:00? [Para 5]
5. Whether the company must build a canteen in the new building (Building 17)? [Para 6]
6. Whether the company must stop staff who insult, coerce or look down on workers? [Para 7]
7. Whether the company must provide hot and cold drinking water? [Para 8]
8. Whether the company must build a nursery for children? [Para 9]
9. Whether the company must issue long-term contracts of at least 6 months? [Para 10]
10. Whether the company must provide a vehicle to take workers to hospital in time? [Para 11]
11. Whether the company must answer for bus No. 9, which failed to collect workers at the gate on 25 January 2014? [Para 12]
12. Whether the company must reduce production quotas set beyond workers' strength? [Para 13]
13. Whether the company must pay wages regularly, at the latest by 7 January 2014? [Para 14]
14. Whether the company must preserve full wages and benefits during the strike? [Para 15]
15. Whether the company must allow women who have given birth one hour a day to breastfeed? [Para 16]
16. Whether the company must let pregnant women leave 20 minutes before the end of the shift? [Para 17]
17. Whether the arbitration proceedings should continue at all, the strike having continued after Order 005/14? [Para 40]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the 16 demands stood as filed; the unions attended the 4 February 2014 pre-hearing meeting and acknowledged that the strike was still running. [Para 2][Para 30][Para 53]
**Employer (respondent):** by letter of 29 January 2014 the company asked the Council to issue an order suspending the strike that had begun on 25 January 2014; it acknowledged at the meeting that the strike continued. [Para 26][Para 30]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel terminated the arbitration proceedings in case No. 019/14, ordering "បញ្ចប់ ដំណើរការ អាជ្ញា កណ្តាល", with no ruling on any of the 16 demands and no award of money. [Para 54][Para 55]

The panel recorded at the outset that it held no hearing and made no finding of fact on the disputed issues, because the proceedings were not conducted. [Para 39] Its single question was whether to carry on. Prakas No. 099 SKBY of 21 April 2004, Article 20, requires the parties to a dispute to suspend all dispute measures — strike or lockout under Article 318 of the 1997 Labour Law — and any other measure that could aggravate the dispute during the panel's work, and to attend every meeting convened. [Para 40][Para 41]

Applying Article 20, the panel held that the parties must suspend such measures throughout the arbitration. [Para 42] It followed its own earlier orders, in which it had read the provision to mean that the Council will not continue arbitration while workers are striking or an employer has locked out — citing Awards 59/06-Sang Vo, 3908-Chung Yov, 137/09 and 138/09-Phnom Kanh, 110/11-Simano, 104/13-Sabrina (Cambodia) and 119/13-Pou Yien. [Para 43][Para 44] It drew the same rule from case 04/04-MSI, where a notice of 29 January 2004 stated that a party has no right to strike during arbitration and the Council cannot resolve the dispute until all unions stop striking, and from case 18/04-Hotel (Cambodiana, Raffles Le Royal, Raffles Grand Hotel d'Angkor, Sunway, InterContinental and Sofitel Angkor), whose second order of 22 April 2004 required all workers to stop strike, demonstration or disruption from 23 April 2004 and reserved the right to end the proceedings if a party breached the order. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47][Para 48] It also relied on case 77/05-Front Com Ni Thinh, where continued strike meant the Council would not resolve the collective dispute. [Para 49][Para 50]

The panel then found the facts: both parties attended on 4 February 2014 at 13:30 and both admitted that the workers were still striking in disregard of the 31 January 2014 order. [Para 52][Para 53] On that basis it terminated the proceedings. [Para 54]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the panel has ordered a strike suspended under Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 and the workers nonetheless continue striking, the panel will not hear the merits and will terminate the arbitration; the dispute may be taken up again only once the strike has ended. [Para 43][Para 54]

**Obiter dicta:** Earlier orders are cited to show that a party has no right to strike during arbitration and that a panel keeps the power to close proceedings on breach of its order. [Para 45][Para 48]
