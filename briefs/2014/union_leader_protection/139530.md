# Award No. 048/14 — Peace Glory (Cambodia) Manufacturing Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 21 March 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambor Na for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139530

## 1. Facts
Peace Glory (Cambodia) Manufacturing Co Ltd runs a garment factory in Chom Chao, Phnom Penh, with about 500 workers; the factory union of the Cambodian Workers' Union had held its leadership election on 1 February 2014 but had still not obtained its registration certificate when the case was heard. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41] A strike on 12 February 2014 produced 11 demands, of which 7 were settled in conciliation and 4 came to the panel: a 2,000 riel daily lunch allowance, maternity pay, an attendance-bonus deduction, and reinstatement of four union leaders. [Para 14][Para 15] At hearing the third was settled, the first and fourth were withdrawn, leaving only the maternity-pay claim. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay 50% of wage and allowances for the 90 days of maternity leave in advance, before the worker stops work, rather than monthly during the leave or in one sum on her return? [Para 45][Para 52]
2. Whether the four workers who attended, and the factory union that filed the complaint, are competent claimants able to represent the workforce? [Para 52][Para 66]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the 50% maternity payment is a statutory right and should be received before the leave begins, because the woman needs the money for delivery costs, milk and baby supplies, and because workers whose homes are far from the factory, in the provinces, must spend time and money travelling to collect a monthly payment; Award No. 43/10 of 2 June 2010 is relied on. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50]
**Employer (respondent):** the company cannot pay in advance because it has previously met cases where women took three months' maternity pay up front and never returned to work. [Para 51]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the claim as brought, because the four claimants who appeared were all men and the benefit claimed exists only for women workers; the operative order therefore records a dismissal even though the panel declared the employer's practice unlawful and required payment before leave in future. [Para 85][Para 87][Para 91]

The panel first settled who was properly before it. Article 268 of the Labour Law gives a professional organisation the rights the law recognises only once it is registered with the ministry, and the factory union here had no registration certificate, the receipt for its application never having been produced despite a deadline of 14 March 2014. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 43][Para 66] Under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 a party may appear through a representative only with written authority, which the federation officials lacked for workers other than the four present. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 68] The panel therefore held that only the four named workers were claimants, and that the union could not bring the case for the workforce. [Para 67][Para 71]

On the merits the panel applied Articles 182 and 183 of the Labour Law: a woman is entitled to 90 days' maternity leave and to half her wage, including allowances, paid by the employer, provided she has worked for the enterprise for at least one year. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 76] It read Article 115(3), which forbids paying wages on a day the worker is entitled to rest and requires payment the day before, together with Article 103, which lists maternity pay as a component of wages, and concluded that those provisions apply to the 90-day maternity payment. [Para 77][Para 79][Para 80] The employer's practice of paying 50% monthly during the leave, or in a lump sum on return, was accordingly unlawful, and the payment must be made before the leave begins. [Para 81][Para 84]

That reasoning could not, however, assist these claimants. The panel recorded that all four are men, that the benefit runs only to women workers, and that male workers have no right to the 50% maternity payment, so it made no order in their favour. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 87][Para 88]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Maternity pay is a wage for the purposes of Article 115(3) of the Labour Law: because Article 103 lists the sum payable during maternity leave among the components of wages, the employer must pay the 50% entitlement for the 90 days before the worker begins her leave, and a practice of paying it monthly in arrears, or in a lump sum after her return, is unlawful. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 84] A union that has not yet obtained its registration certificate cannot represent workers before the panel, and a claim for a benefit confined to women cannot be pursued by male claimants. [Para 66][Para 87]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that a union which has filed a registration application is deemed registered if the ministry does not respond within two months, and its remarks on the employer's fear that women who are paid in advance will not return, are observations on registration and on the employer's practice respectively, neither being necessary to the dismissal. [Para 58][Para 51]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The reasoning holds the employer's practice unlawful and states that it must pay 50% for 90 days before leave [Para 84][Para 90], but the operative order dismisses the claim and prints the rate as "500 per cent" [Para 91]; the order contradicts the reasoning and misstates the rate tenfold.
- The claim is described as covering "three months before" the leave [Para 4][Para 45] and as payment "for 90 days before" the leave [Para 52]; the two formulations are used interchangeably, though they are not the same thing.
- The employer's name appears as "Peace Glory", "Pis Glory" and "PEACE GLORY (CAMBODIA) CO., LTD" in Latin script across the award [Para 1][Para 33][Para 39]; the union is named both "Cambodian Workers' Union" and "Cambodian Workers' Union at Peace Glory".
- The commercial registration is given as No. Inv.1011 E/2005 dated 30 November 2005 [Para 39], while the registration certificate in evidence is dated 20 September 2011 [Para 30].
- The signature block prints a fifth name, "Thai Khun Na / Leav", between the worker-party arbitrator and the chair [Para 93], which corresponds to no member named in the panel composition [Para 1].
