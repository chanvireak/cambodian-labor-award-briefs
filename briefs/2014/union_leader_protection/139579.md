# Award No. 131/14 — Te Yong (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 13 June 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; Sin Kim Sean, appointed by the workers; Ben Bun Chheam, chair appointed by both) · Doc ID 139579

## 1. Facts
Te Yong (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is at Bory Kamkar village, Bek Chan commune, Ang Snuol district, Kandal province. [Para 1] Eight demands went to conciliation after a strike, including a 500 riel daily lunch allowance, a USD 30 milk allowance, more toilets, hot and cold water, sufficient fans, payment of past wages and meal money, and reinstatement of eleven named worker representatives. [Para 3][Para 5][Para 6][Para 7][Para 8][Para 9] The employer did not appear at the hearing but wrote on 28 May 2014 that it had dismissed those eleven on 16 May 2014 for inciting workers to leave early on 12 May; seven had since signed resignations dated 24 May 2014 and taken severance, two were reinstated, and two were still under consideration. [Para 20][Para 32][Para 34][Para 35]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of 500 riel a day? [Para 3]
2. Whether the employer must pay a milk allowance of USD 30? [Para 4]
3. Whether the employer must take back the eleven worker representatives it dismissed? [Para 5]
4. Whether the employer must provide additional toilets for the workers? [Para 5]
5. Whether the employer must release the death and marriage grants it had previously sponsored within one week of notice? [Para 6]
6. Whether the employer must pay past wages and meal money up to 3:30? [Para 7]
7. Whether the employer must install hot and cold water tanks? [Para 8]
8. Whether the employer must provide a sufficient number of fans? [Para 9]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the eight demands were carried by the Federation for the Promotion of the Living Standards of Khmer Workers, which held assignments of rights signed on 19 May 2014 by seven of the dismissed workers; its president and an official attended the hearing. [Para 1][Para 29][Para 30]
**Employer (respondent):** absent from the hearing, and relying instead on letter No. 016/2014 of 28 May 2014: the eleven were dismissed for misconduct, seven then resigned voluntarily and were paid all entitlements, two were taken back, and two remained under review. [Para 20][Para 32][Para 34][Para 35]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel terminated the arbitration proceedings in case 131/14 without deciding any of the eight demands, because no claimant remained. [Para 55][Para 58]

The panel did not reach the merits. Article 18 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 requires the parties to come before it in person to state their case and to file the documents and information needed, and the panel read that as placing the burden first on the claimant; the respondent's duty to answer arises only once the claimant has discharged its own. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 46][Para 47] It followed award 094/14 on the same point. [Para 45]

On the facts, the panel found that the assignment of rights to the federation was executed on 19 May 2014, before the seven workers resigned on 24 May 2014 and before they accepted the severance payments; by 03 June 2014, the date of the hearing, they were no longer employees of the company. [Para 53] The union president and official who attended did not object either to letter No. 016/2014 or to the seven resignation letters. [Para 37] The panel therefore could not determine whether any live demand survived, since the only workers who had assigned their rights had left and been paid. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 54]

Having found that there was no claimant in the case, the panel held it could not conduct a hearing, and that it was not obliged to continue the arbitration but could close it by issuing an award. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 56][Para 57] It also recorded that it could make no findings on any of the issues in the conciliation report. [Para 42]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the workers who assigned their rights to the union have resigned and accepted severance before the hearing, and the union present at the hearing does not contest those resignations, there is no claimant left and the panel will close the file without deciding the demands: the burden of establishing a live claim rests on the claimant under Article 18 of Prakas No. 099, and an arbitration cannot proceed on a demand that no identified worker still pursues. [Para 44][Para 53][Para 54][Para 56]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's general statement that a respondent's burden to answer arises only after the claimant has presented its case is a description of the order of proof rather than a finding, since the case ended before any answer was called for. [Para 47]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The conciliation report is numbered 766/14 in the procedural section and the ministry's list [Para 12][Para 17][Para 24] but "366/14" in the reasons [Para 42]; both are dated 23 May 2014.
- The hearing is listed for 14:00 [Para 14] but the invitation and the secretariat's notice give 14:30 [Para 19]; the invitation was signed for at 14:00 on 03 June 2014 [Para 28].
- The employer's letter appears as dated 28 May 2014 in the findings but "28 May 2023 2014" in the reasons [Para 48].
- Eleven workers are named in the claim and in the employer's letter, but the letter's own enumeration lists only seven, and their names are printed differently across the three lists — one seventh name appears as ចក សោភសន៍, ចក់ សោភន៍, ចក់ សោកន៍ and ចក់ សោភ័ណ្ឌ [Para 5][Para 29][Para 33][Para 49][Para 51].
- The panel's reasons are printed twice in near-identical blocks [Para 32]–[Para 37] and [Para 48]–[Para 54], and the chair's name appears as ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈាម in the composition and ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា in the signature block [Para 1][Para 59].
