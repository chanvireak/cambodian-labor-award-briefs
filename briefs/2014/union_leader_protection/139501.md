# Award No. 002/14 — Bodykids Fashion Wear Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 31 January 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Virak, worker-appointed; Tang Tri, president) · Doc ID 139501

## 1. Facts

Bodykids Fashion Wear Co., Ltd cuts and sews garments at Chom Chao, Po Sen Chey, Phnom Penh, employing about 750 workers. [Para 55][Para 57] On 24 October 2013 a group of workers transferred their claims to the Khmer Workers' Union Federation, covering 20 points, the twentieth being reinstatement of Mr S (Seang San). [Para 67] Mr S, a candidate for union president, was dismissed on 26 October 2013; a strike followed on 28 and 30 October 2013, turning violent on 30 October, and the same day it ended. [Para 30][Para 84][Para 85] Award No. 243/13 of 13 December 2013 had already refused his reinstatement. [Para 79]

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. Whether the employer must reinstate the union leaders Mr S, Ms O (Oeun Nheat) and Ms M (Oeun Maly)? [Para 75]
2. Whether the authorities must arrest and punish those behind the violence against strikers and pay compensation to the victims? [Para 107]
3. Whether the panel should order an investigation into the Po Sen Chey district police for protecting the owner's unlawful dismissal of a union leader and protecting the thugs who beat workers? [Para 112]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** The three named leaders should return to work; Mr S was dismissed on 26 October 2013 as a union presidential candidate; on 30 October 2013 the employer hired limping street thugs armed with sticks and stones who beat strikers, injuring one federation officer with a fractured skull and a broken left arm; and the district police protected both the owner and the thugs. [Para 112][Para 107][Para 84] Ms O was refused entry when she came to work on 28 October 2013 and so joined the strike. [Para 86]

**Employer (respondent):** Mr S's case was already decided by award No. 243/13 and the company complied with it; Ms O and Ms M were never dismissed — they simply stopped coming, and their final wages and seniority indemnity were paid and left for them to collect. [Para 4] The company denied hiring thugs and said strikers burned tyres and threw stones, causing about ten pregnant and frail workers to faint, and that workers then threw stones back. [Para 108][Para 110] On the criminal complaints it said the union should pursue the criminal procedure. [Para 6]

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** dismissed — all three claims were refused; points 2 and 3 were declined as outside the panel's jurisdiction, and the reinstatement claims failed on res judicata and on the facts. [Para 189][Para 190][Para 191][Para 192]

As a threshold, the panel found that the Khmer Workers' Union at Bodykids was not a professional organisation enjoying the rights the Labour Law recognises: it had applied for registration and received only an acknowledgement of receipt on 28 October 2013, the employer objected on 05 November 2013, and the labour dispute department replied by letter No. 1396 that it could not compile the file for registration. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63][Para 64] The two workers who appeared, Mr S and Ms O, were therefore not lawful union leaders and could not represent members; they could claim only for themselves, and the Federation could claim only on the points and for the workers covered by the 24 October 2013 transfer letters. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 69] Ms M neither appeared nor gave a power of attorney to anyone present, so her claim was not considered at all. [Para 72][Para 73]

On Mr S, the panel applied the principle of res judicata as it had stated it in award No. 42/07: a point already brought by the same parties and decided on the merits will not be reconsidered, to avoid contradictory outcomes and to ensure finality. [Para 114][Para 118][Para 119] All three conditions were met — the same claimant federation and the same respondent company, the identical claim for reinstatement, and a decision on the merits in award No. 243/13 refusing it — so the panel declined to decide the claim again. [Para 122][Para 125][Para 127][Para 128]

On Ms O, the panel applied Article 69 of the Labour Law, which requires a worker to carry out the hired work personally and with care, and Article 6 of the registered in-house rules, under which six days of unexcused absence in a month, or six consecutive days, amounts to abandonment of employment. [Para 129][Para 133] The strike ended on 30 October 2013 and all workers returned except six, four of whom resigned; Ms O did not return, gave no notice and sought no leave, though she had the company's telephone number, and her explanation that she feared being beaten was unsupported — she never even telephoned to ask whether the violence had stopped. [Para 134][Para 141][Para 142][Para 149] The panel found she had no intention of continuing the employment, treated the absence as abandonment, and held that reinstatement would in substance require a new contract, which the employer refuses and cannot be compelled to make. [Para 150][Para 151][Para 166][Para 169][Para 170]

On points 2 and 3, the panel applied Article 302 of the Labour Law, which defines a collective labour dispute as one between an employer and its workers over working conditions, the exercise of professional-organisation rights, recognition of such organisations, or relations between employer and workers. [Para 173][Para 174] Both claims are disputes between workers and the public authorities — the perpetrators of the violence and the Po Sen Chey district police — not between workers and their employer, so the first condition failed and the panel did not reach the other two. [Para 176][Para 177][Para 178][Para 183][Para 185][Para 187]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

The panel will not re-decide a claim already brought by the same parties against the same respondent and decided on the merits, even where the claimant is dissatisfied with the earlier result; and a claim against the police or other public authorities for failing to arrest and punish assailants is not a collective labour dispute within Article 302, because the respondent is not the employer. [Para 118][Para 177]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that a union whose registration file the Ministry has declined to compile cannot represent members before the Council, though individual workers present may still claim in person. [Para 64][Para 71]
