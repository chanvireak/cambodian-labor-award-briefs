# Award No. 225/14 — Chang Sing (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 9 September 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Chiv Phirom (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Kong Phalha (president) · Doc ID 139700

## 1. Facts
Chang Sing (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd sews garments at Toul Pongro, Chom Chao, Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh, and began operating in 2007. At about 3:30 on the morning of 28 July 2014 the factory caught fire for no established cause; the plant has not operated since, no wages have been paid, and the employer has neither contacted the workers nor given notice. Nine hundred and sixteen workers transferred their rights to the CUDW federation — 420 members and 496 non-members — claiming 541 had unfixed-duration contracts and 121 fixed-term. [Para 1][Para 52][Para 53][Para 58][Para 61][Para 64][Para 23][Para 97]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay damages in lieu of notice under Art 75? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company must pay dismissal damages under Art 89? [Para 2]
3. Whether the company must pay moral damages under Art 91? [Para 2]
4. Whether the company must pay for unused annual leave under Art 167? [Para 2]
5. Whether the company must pay final wages under Art 116? [Para 2]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The fire was not force majeure but the employer's serious negligence in managing its fire-prevention system; the factory has closed, there is no work, no wage and no word from the employer, so the contracts are at an end and every statutory termination benefit is owed. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 59][Para 142]
**Employer (respondent):** Absent in substance. The factory head and one administrative officer attended but said before the hearing that they had no power to decide and no written delegation, because the director was ill in Shanghai; they asked only to observe while the panel proceeded as if the employer were absent, and never supplied the delegation the panel allowed until 27 August 2014. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 16][Para 18][Para 128]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — for the 541 workers on unfixed-duration contracts the company must pay Art 75 notice damages, Art 89 dismissal damages, unused annual leave and July 2014 wages; for the 121 on fixed-term contracts it must pay the 5% contract-end indemnity, moral damages equal to wages to the contract's end, July wages and unused leave. Moral damages under Art 91 and, for fixed-term staff, notice and dismissal damages were refused. [Para 249][Para 250][Para 251][Para 252]

The panel first held the employer to be absent: Art 21 of Prakas 099 lets it proceed where a party was properly invited and fails to appear without valid reason, and representatives who disclaim authority and produce no written delegation cannot bind the company. It decided on the claimants' evidence alone. [Para 120][Para 128][Para 130][Para 131]

On representation, the union's recognised leadership under letter 121 of 9 May 2011 had exhausted its two-year mandate by 9 May 2013, so its three officers could not act as union leaders under Art 268 and Prakas 305 Art 3(2). The 916 workers had nonetheless signed a written transfer of authority to the federation, which therefore represented them as an authorised agent under Prakas 099 Art 19. Because only 420 were members, the panel split the file: rights disputes bind the members through the 3 October 2012 memorandum of understanding, while the 496 non-members receive a non-binding award. [Para 100][Para 101][Para 110][Para 114][Para 26][Para 32]

The fire was held not to be force majeure. Art 87(3) relieves an employer of its obligations only in a case of force majeure, which the Civil Code dictionary and the Labour Code define as an event that is unforeseeable and insurmountable. Fire in a building is a risk the public can foresee, and most companies guard against it through alarm systems, staff training and insurance; and no evidence showed a natural event of a scale no one could resist. Neither condition was met, so the employer could not escape liability. [Para 148][Para 151][Para 157][Para 159][Para 160][Para 164][Para 165]

Since the workplace was destroyed, no substitute site was offered and the employer showed no will to continue, the panel found the contracts terminated by the employer's own will, without notice. [Para 146][Para 147][Para 182]

For the 541 workers on unfixed-duration contracts, Art 74 required a proper reason related to the worker's capacity or conduct or to the needs of the enterprise, plus written notice; neither was given. Art 75 fixes notice periods by seniority, Art 89 gives dismissal damages, Art 166 and 167 give cash for unused leave, and Art 116 requires the July wage within 48 hours. All were ordered. Art 91 moral damages were refused because, although the fire was not force majeure, it was a proper reason for ending the contracts, so no wrongful termination was shown. [Para 175][Para 183][Para 188][Para 203][Para 205][Para 208][Para 241]

For the 121 fixed-term workers, Art 73(6) requires the 5% contract-end indemnity and Art 73(3) gives moral damages equal to wages to the contract's end where the employer ends the contract early; July wages and unused leave follow the same articles. Art 73(5) notice damages and Art 89 dismissal damages were refused, because Art 89 applies only to unfixed-duration contracts. [Para 217][Para 224][Para 234][Para 237][Para 240][Para 243]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fire that destroys a factory is not force majeure unless it was both unforeseeable and beyond anyone's power to resist; where fire was a foreseeable risk and no natural cause is proved, the employer remains liable for every statutory termination payment, yet because the fire is still a proper reason for ending the contracts, Art 91 moral damages are not owed. Dismissal damages under Art 89 attach only to unfixed-duration contracts; fixed-term staff take the Art 73 package instead. [Para 160][Para 165][Para 204][Para 237]

**Obiter dicta:** A company officer who appears but disclaims power to decide, and never produces a written delegation, is treated as absent, and the panel will decide the case on the claimants' evidence under Art 21 of Prakas 099. [Para 128][Para 130][Para 131]
