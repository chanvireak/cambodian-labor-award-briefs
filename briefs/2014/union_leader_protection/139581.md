# Award No. 132/14 — Soua Plastech (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 1 July 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Kong Polla (president) · Doc ID 139581

## 1. Facts
Soua Plastech (Cambodia) Co., Ltd., a toy manufacturer at Kong Pisei, Kampong Speu, employed 433 workers. [Para 57] On 12 May 2014 thirty-five workers petitioned the Cooperation Union Federation for Cambodia to press sixteen demands on working conditions, and a strike followed; conciliation at the Department of Labour failed on sixteen points, twelve were settled and point 11 was withdrawn, leaving three for the panel. [Para 58][Para 31][Para 32] The employer disputed the federation's standing to represent the workers. [Para 60]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to pay the overtime meal allowance once a week rather than once a month? [Para 119]
2. Whether the employer's requirement that workers use cards to enter the toilet is lawful and reasonable? [Para 142]
3. Whether the fixed-duration contracts of Ms V and Ms M were terminated because of union discrimination? [Para 164]
4. Whether the two workers committed serious misconduct under Article 83 of the Labour Law? [Para 188]
5. Whether the early termination of their fixed-duration contracts was lawful, and what compensation is owed? [Para 200]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the meal allowance must be paid weekly because workers need cash daily and other factories pay weekly or fortnightly; one shared toilet card per group of twenty is inadequate; the two women did nothing wrong and were dismissed for union activity. [Para 62][Para 68][Para 84]
**Employer (respondent):** it cannot pay weekly for lack of accounting staff; one card per group plus a second card for emergencies is enough for twenty workers; the two were not dismissed but had fixed-duration contracts ended with lawful payments, for serious misconduct, not union discrimination. [Para 63][Para 69][Para 87][Para 88]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay the overtime meal allowance weekly; the two-card demand was refused but the employer was ordered to stop using toilet cards at all; reinstatement was refused, and the employer was ordered to pay 5% contract-termination indemnity, damages (srohoy jomngai chet), final wages and outstanding annual leave for both women. [Para 222][Para 223][Para 224][Para 225][Para 226]

**Standing.** Citing Articles 268 and 277, the panel held that a union or federation acquires representational rights only once its statutes and leadership list are registered with the Ministry of Labour, and that the federation had no registered local union at the factory. [Para 101][Para 107] It therefore could not represent members before the Council. [Para 108] Under Prakas No. 099/04, however, a party may appear with an "accompanying person" (anak aom), who may argue but not decide; the federation had that status for the nine claimants present, who were themselves the disputing parties. [Para 114][Para 115][Para 117]

**Meal allowance.** Point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 of 07 March 2011 entitles a worker doing voluntary overtime at the employer's request to 2,000 riel per day or one free meal, but fixes no payment interval. [Para 121][Para 123] The panel reasoned that the allowance exists so a worker can buy food when working overtime, and that weekly, fortnightly or monthly payment all serve that purpose provided the money is available when needed. [Para 126][Para 130] It noted its own earlier awards requiring a free meal daily, but concluded the demand was reasonable and necessary in this case and ordered weekly payment. [Para 127][Para 137][Para 138]

**Toilet cards.** Article 2 of the Labour Law gives the employer the right to manage the enterprise, but only lawfully and reasonably, and that right cannot override the workers' right of access to toilets. [Para 145][Para 147] The panel found that rationing cards is equivalent to rationing time, that a worker waiting for a free card may suffer kidney or bladder harm, and that nobody can timetable bodily functions. [Para 149][Para 150] A card system for "special cases" such as diarrhoea was also unworkable, since the employer cannot know who is ill and workers will endure rather than ask. [Para 154] Article 229 requires every workplace to maintain hygiene and conditions necessary for workers' health, which a card queue defeats. [Para 155][Para 156] The panel refused the demand for two unconditional cards but ordered the practice stopped altogether. [Para 160]

**Discrimination.** Articles 12 and 279 forbid an employer from using union membership or activity as a ground for discipline or termination. [Para 164][Para 165] The claimant bears the burden of proving discrimination. [Para 167] Under Article 3(3) of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, candidates in a union leadership election enjoy the same protection against dismissal as staff representatives, for 45 days before and 45 days after the election, and the union must notify the employer of the candidacies. [Para 173][Para 174][Para 175] The federation held its election on 04 May 2014 but never told the employer, so the employer could not be taken to know the two women were candidates. [Para 179][Para 180] The discrimination claim therefore failed for want of evidence. [Para 181][Para 186]

**Misconduct and compensation.** The employer alleged serious misconduct — creating disorder and halting the production line — but pleaded no particulars and cited no limb of Article 83, offering only that Ms V had argued with the sewing supervisor, which the panel could not assume. [Para 187][Para 194][Para 197] The employer thus failed to prove serious misconduct. [Para 198] Because the employer ended the three-month contracts early by its own will, Article 73(3) entitled each worker to damages at least equal to wages to the contract's end, and Article 73(6) to a 5% termination indemnity, with final wages under Article 116 and unused annual leave under Article 166. [Para 201][Para 205][Para 209][Para 214][Para 218]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer cannot be found to have discriminated against union candidates it was never told about: the 45-day protection in Prakas No. 305 depends on the union notifying the employer of the candidacies, and absent that notice the claimant has not discharged the burden of proof. [Para 175][Para 180][Para 181]

Separately, the management right in Article 2 does not extend to rationing access to toilets by card, because that rations time and so jeopardises the health the employer must protect under Article 229. [Para 147][Para 153][Para 156]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that a worker who abuses toilet access to avoid work may still be disciplined under internal rules or the Labour Law; and that the meal allowance, though properly payable weekly here, always depends on the workers' actual need. [Para 148][Para 128]
