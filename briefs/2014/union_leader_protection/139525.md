# Award No. 040/14 — Puntuality Garment Factory Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 25 March 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, Liv Sovanna, Pen Bun Chhea presiding) · Doc ID 139525

## 1. Facts
A Phnom Penh garment factory with about 190 workers dismissed the vice-president of the Khmer Youth Voice Union on 14 January 2014. She had been hired on 6 August 2013 on a three-month probation and continued working without a written contract after it ended. She had notified the employer of the union's leadership in October 2013, and the union held only a receipt for its registration application, dated 25 October 2013. Of six claims, five were settled at the hearing, leaving reinstatement alone. [Para 43][Para 48][Para 49][Para 63]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate the union vice-president with back pay and benefits from the dismissal date? [Para 48]
2. Whether the employer must stop using armed military force to threaten workers? [Para 3]
3. Whether overtime must be on a voluntary basis? [Para 4]
4. Whether the employer must provide a doctor on duty during all working hours? [Para 5]
5. Whether workers employed for more than two months must be reclassified as permanent staff? [Para 6]
6. Whether the employer must allow pregnant workers half a day a month for medical check-ups? [Para 7]

Issues 2 to 6 were settled by agreement at the hearing and were not decided. [Para 19][Para 20]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** she committed no fault warranting dismissal; as a union leader she could only be dismissed through the procedure the law requires, and the employer never sought labour-inspector authorisation; her absences were leave she had requested orally, connected to a family ceremony and to her own health after a medication on 13 January 2014. [Para 51][Para 57]
**Employer (respondent):** she was absent without leave on six days in January 2014, refused to produce a medical certificate, used improper words and threatened the employer; the company admits it did not obtain labour-inspector permission before dismissing her, on the view that her conduct justified it. [Para 52][Para 56][Para 61][Para 89]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer must take her back and pay wages from the dismissal date until she returns, in an award that is not binding unless unchallenged for eight days. [Para 92][Para 93][Para 94]
On the claimant's standing the panel applied Article 268(2): a union is deemed registered where the Ministry does not respond within two months, and more than two months had elapsed between the receipt of 25 October 2013 and the hearing of 11 March 2014, with no refusal ever notified to either party. The union was therefore a lawful professional organisation able to represent its members, and she was its vice-president. [Para 65][Para 69][Para 70][Para 78]
On the dismissal the panel applied Article 293, read with Prakas 305 (2001), Article 4, clause 2: union leaders enjoy the same protection as staff representatives, so dismissal requires prior labour-inspector consent. The employer conceded it had not sought that consent, and the panel held the dismissal therefore procedurally unlawful, without needing to resolve the factual dispute about the six absences. [Para 79][Para 81][Para 89][Para 90]
On remedy the panel relied on Prakas 099, Article 34, which empowers it to order reinstatement of a dismissed worker, and ordered her return with wages accrued from dismissal to reinstatement. [Para 91][Para 92]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that files a registration application and hears nothing from the Ministry for two months is lawfully registered under Article 268(2) from the expiry of that period, and its officers are protected accordingly. A union leader may not be dismissed at all without the prior consent of the labour inspector under Article 293; a dismissal given without that consent is procedurally unlawful, whatever the misconduct alleged, and the remedy is reinstatement with back pay. [Para 70][Para 81][Para 91]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel did not decide whether the six days of absence were unlawful or whether the alleged threat occurred, leaving the substantive justification for any future lawful dismissal untouched. [Para 52][Para 90]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award names the employer throughout as Pang Chou Lee Thy Garment Factory, Panh Chouli Thy and Puntuality Garment Factory Co., Ltd. in different passages. [Para 1][Para 43] The signature block adds a name, Thai Tukda, that appears nowhere in the panel composition printed in the header. [Para 1][Para 95] The panel's constitution date is printed with the year twice (ឆ្នាំ ឆ្នាំ ២០១៤), and a stray figure "40" interrupts the finding on Prakas 305. [Para 16][Para 81] The amount of back pay is not quantified anywhere in the award. [Para 93]
