# Award No. 044/14 — Mav Sun (Cambodia) Garmen Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 1 April 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kav Thach for the employer party, Tuon Si Phan for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139528

## 1. Facts
Mav Sun (Cambodia) Garmen Co Ltd, a garment factory on National Road 3 in Phnom Penh, dismissed three women who were leaders of the CCAWDU factory union; they claimed reinstatement and back pay, and the employer replied that it had already paid them termination compensation under the Labour Law and could not take them back. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 3] Conciliation on 18 February 2014 settled 9 of the 10 demands and left this single point unresolved, which reached the panel on 24 February 2014. [Para 10][Para 11] On 14 March 2014, between the two hearings, the employer filed letters in which the three workers accepted severance and withdrew their complaints; they did not attend the second hearing, and the union official who took the panel's call said he knew nothing of the agreement and asked the panel to decide the case as usual. [Para 20][Para 22][Para 29]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take the three union leaders back into normal work and pay them back wages? [Para 2]
2. Whether the panel must continue the arbitration and decide that claim, once the parties have reached an agreement disposing of it? [Para 89]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the three union leaders maintained throughout that the company should accept them back to work as normal and pay their arrears. [Para 2] Their federation's legal officer told the secretariat that he had not been informed of the settlement letters and asked the panel to rule on the case in the ordinary way. [Para 22]
**Employer (respondent):** the company could not re-employ them because it had already paid termination compensation in accordance with the Labour Law; the three had asked on 14 March 2014 for that payment, wanted to end their claims, and signed and thumbprinted letters to that effect, saying they would tell the federation to withdraw the complaint. [Para 3][Para 83][Para 85]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel found that the sole unresolved issue had already been disposed of by agreement between the parties, closed the arbitration of case 044/14 and issued no order on reinstatement or back pay. [Para 104][Para 105][Para 107]

The panel began from its own competence. Article 312(1) of the Labour Law gives it no task beyond the issues recorded in the non-conciliation minute and issues arising after that minute as a direct consequence of the dispute, and Article 33 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 limits the panel's power of examination in the same terms. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 91]

It then examined the documents the employer filed on 14 March 2014: one joint letter bearing the names, thumbprints and signatures of all three workers, stating that they had asked for termination pay, accepted the sum the company paid and withdrew their complaints from that date; and three separate letters, one per worker, to the same effect. [Para 92][Para 74][Para 75] The panel noted that it had convened a second hearing precisely to let the workers contest this evidence and explain whether they had truly consented, and that the employer attended and affirmed the consent was genuine. [Para 93][Para 94] The workers did not attend; the panel found they had thus abandoned their opportunity to explain and to challenge the employer's evidence and assertions. [Para 99][Para 100]

On that record the panel decided to believe the employer's account and evidence, found that the employer and the three claimants had reached a complete settlement of the disputed point, and held that no unresolved issue remained for it to decide. [Para 101][Para 103][Para 105] It therefore closed the proceedings and made no award on the merits. [Para 106][Para 107]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the parties to a collective dispute settle the only issue recorded in the non-conciliation minute before the panel rules, the panel has no jurisdiction to decide it: Article 312(1) of the Labour Law confines the panel to the issues in that minute and to issues arising after it as a direct consequence of the dispute, so a claim extinguished by the parties' own agreement must be closed rather than adjudicated. A party that is given a second hearing to contest written evidence of its own consent and does not attend is taken to have abandoned that opportunity, and the panel may act on the evidence before it. [Para 89][Para 100][Para 101]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the employer prepared the consent letters "in order to guard against" a later challenge, and its remarks on the reasons a union official might be unable to attend because he was managing a strike at another company, are background to the credibility finding rather than rules of decision. [Para 87][Para 96]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award number is printed as 044/14 on the face of the award [Para 1] but as "1 044/14" in the closing order [Para 106]; the company appears both as "Mav Sun (Cambodia) Garmen Co Ltd" and as "Mav Sun (Cambodia) Cho/Lthid" variants, and the chair's name is rendered "Pen Bun Chhea" here against "Pen Bun Chhean" in companion awards.
- The consent letters are dated 14 March 2014 throughout, but the letter listed at [Para 92] is described as dated "14 October March 2014", fusing two months.
- The union election minutes are dated 18 December 2014 [Para 60] although the election they record took place in December 2013 and is referred to in 2013 correspondence [Para 54].
- The award states at [Para 88] that the panel could not rely on facts found at the first hearing, so the first hearing's findings are not reproduced anywhere; the underlying reason for the three dismissals is therefore absent from the file.
- The three claimant names are printed in full in the source and are redacted here as Ms N, Ms S and Ms Y; two of the three consent letters bear the same employee number, MS 2175, on their face [Para 78][Para 79][Para 80].
