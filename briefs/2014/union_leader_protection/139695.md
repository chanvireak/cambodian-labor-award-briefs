# Award No. 219/14 — Smart Shirts Garment Manufacturing (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 19 September 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Kong Phalha (president) · Doc ID 139695

## 1. Facts
Smart Shirts Garment Manufacturing (Cambodia) Co., Ltd runs a garment factory in Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh. On 7 July 2014 the Cambodian Garment Workers' Democratic Union Federation (សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា, "CUDW") notified the company of an election to create a local union, and four workers — Mr N, Mr I, Mr H and Ms K — were on the candidate slate. All four were removed from work on 9 July 2014. CUDW's complaint of 15 July 2014 raised five claims; conciliation failed on all five, but at the hearings the parties settled issues 2–5, leaving only the reinstatement claim. [Para 1][Para 57][Para 187][Para 21][Para 27][Para 28]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must take back the four election candidates, Mr N, Mr I, Mr H and Ms K? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company must stop discriminating against the CUDW union? [Para 7]
3. Whether the company must respect the voluntary principle for overtime? [Para 9]
4. Whether the company must stop deducting union dues from non-members without proper written authorisation? [Para 12]
5. Whether the company must refund union dues already deducted without authorisation? [Para 13]

Only issue 1 was decided; issues 2–5 were settled by agreement at the hearings. [Para 27]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The four were dismissed on 9 July 2014 solely because their names appeared on the CUDW candidate list for the union leadership election, and none of them had committed any fault. [Para 187]
**Employer (respondent):** Mr N resigned of his own accord and collected USD 283; Mr I abandoned his post from 12 to 19 July 2014; Mr H refused to cooperate with management; and Ms K's fixed-term contract simply ran to its end. [Para 91][Para 123][Para 146][Para 173]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to take back Mr N and Mr I; the claims for Mr H and Ms K were dismissed. The order is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification if no party objects in writing to the Minister. [Para 336][Para 343]

Mr N: the panel asked whether the resignation letter of 15 July 2014 sprang from genuine will, applying Art 65 of the Labour Law and Arts 664 and 311 of the 2007 Civil Code. It found he signed after a federation representative threatened him in the administration office, that the administration head sat silent, and that he feared the company would lodge his severance with the Ministry. The resignation therefore did not express genuine consent and amounted to termination by the employer's will alone. [Para 193][Para 204][Para 205][Para 206]

Discrimination: Arts 12 and 279 of the Labour Law forbid an employer from using union membership or union activity as a ground for discipline or termination. The panel held that the administration head's silence, coupled with his knowledge that both workers were election candidates, made the company a participant in the discrimination, and ordered Mr N reinstated. [Para 207][Para 219][Para 222][Para 223]

Mr I: here the panel placed the burden on the employer to show it did not dismiss him. The only person able to clarify the disputed telephone instruction was the table-cutting section chief; the employer produced neither him nor any explanation for his absence, though he had resigned and could have been reached by telephone or letter. The burden was not discharged, so a dismissal on 9 July 2014 was found, and the same discrimination reasoning applied. [Para 232][Para 233][Para 243][Para 244][Para 250]

Mr H: the panel found no evidence tying his dismissal to union discrimination, since he gave no facts on that point and the union notification was not proved to have reached the administration. It nonetheless held the dismissal was not based on his fault either, because the company skipped the oral education and written warning its internal rules require before termination. That did not save him: under Art 73(3) an employer may terminate a fixed-term contract unilaterally, and the company had paid the 5% contract-end indemnity under Art 73(6) plus damages equal to wages to the contract's end, so the termination was lawful and reinstatement was refused. [Para 259][Para 261][Para 273][Para 275][Para 284][Para 289][Para 300]

Ms K: notice of non-renewal was given on 5 July 2014, four days before the company learned of her candidacy on 9 July, so the timing defeated the discrimination claim. Under Art 73(1) a fixed-term contract expires at its term, and the notice given was more generous than the law requires; severance was paid. Her claim was dismissed. [Para 315][Para 319][Para 330][Para 335]

Arbitrator An Non dissented on Mr H only, and would have ordered his reinstatement with back pay from dismissal to return. [Para 345][Para 357]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A resignation is not an expression of genuine will under Art 65 of the Labour Law where a union federation representative threatens the worker with dismissal in the presence of the employer's administration head and that head stays silent; the silence makes the resulting separation a unilateral termination by the employer, and where the worker's name stood on a union election slate it is discrimination under Arts 12 and 279, for which reinstatement is the remedy. [Para 205][Para 219][Para 223]

**Obiter dicta:** An employer that controls the only witness able to explain a disputed instruction — here the section chief — and produces neither the witness nor a reason for his absence has not discharged its burden of proving that no dismissal occurred, even where that witness has left the company. [Para 241][Para 243]
