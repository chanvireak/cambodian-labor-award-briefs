# Award No. 034/14 — Harta Pak K Ching Industry (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 21 February 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Pen Bunchea (Chair), Liv Sovannara (worker party), Ing Sothy (employer party) · Doc ID 139519

## 1. Facts
Harta Pak K Ching Industry (Cambodia) Ltd operates at Ang village, Choam Chao commune, Posenchey district, Phnom Penh, and was represented by counsel on a power of attorney of 13 February 2014 [Para 26]. Workers struck from 11 February 2014 demanding that the company pay "other compensation" under the Labour Law, which the company refused on the ground that it had never dismissed anyone [Para 2][Para 3]. The Labour Department received the complaint by telephone on 12 February 2014, conciliation on the same day failed, and non-conciliation report No. 170 of 13 February 2014 reached the Secretariat that day [Para 8][Para 10][Para 11]. The strike continued after the case was referred [Para 12].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the workers the "other compensation" (ប្រាក់បំណាច់ផ្សេងៗ) claimed under the Labour Law, in a dispute arising from a strike? [Para 2]

The threshold question the panel actually decided was procedural: whether the arbitration could continue at all while the strike persisted [Para 34].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** they agreed to return to work as ordered, but the employer would not let two of them back — one the factory union president, the other a production section head — whose contracts had been suspended on 12 February 2014, the day after the strike began, on a charge of inciting the strike [Para 19]. They argued the suspension breached both the Phnom Penh Municipal Court's protective injunction and the panel's own order, neither of which authorised it, and said the strike would not end until the two were reinstated [Para 20][Para 21]. **Employer (respondent):** the two had committed serious misconduct under the internal work rules, so they could not be readmitted; their contracts were suspended pending the Labour Inspector's decision [Para 22][Para 23].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel terminated its proceedings in Award No. 034/14 without deciding the compensation claim [Para 57][Para 58].

*The order to stop striking.* Clause 20 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 requires the parties, while an arbitration panel is seized, to suspend every dispute measure — strike or lockout under Article 318, or any other measure that could aggravate the dispute — and to attend all meetings convened [Para 34][Para 35]. Earlier awards read that clause as barring the panel from continuing whenever the workers are striking or the employer has locked out, and the panel followed them [Para 36][Para 42][Para 43].

*The panel's own injunction.* Having received counsel's request of 13 February 2014, the panel issued Order No. 006/14 of 17 February 2014 directing an immediate halt to the strike and the return of all workers to their shifts from the morning of 18 February 2014 [Para 13][Para 14]. The workers did not comply [Para 15].

*The aggravating measure.* At the pre-hearing meeting of 19 February 2014 the panel heard both sides, urged the return of all workers, and allowed until 11:00 on 20 February for a settlement [Para 24]. The workers then said the employer was still refusing to admit the two suspended workers, and on 20 February the Secretariat was told by telephone that the strike continued [Para 25]. The panel held that suspending the two strikers' contracts after the strike began, and refusing them readmission after its order, was an "other measure" aggravating the dispute within Clause 20 — the same characterisation it had given to an employer's dismissal of all union leaders during proceedings in Award No. 49/10-Wing Ying [Para 45][Para 54][Para 55]. The proceedings were therefore terminated [Para 56][Para 57].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
While a panel is seized of a collective dispute, Clause 20 of Prakas No. 099 obliges both sides to freeze every dispute measure, and a panel will not arbitrate a dispute that either party keeps escalating: if the strike continues, or the employer locks out, or the employer suspends and refuses to readmit strikers after an order to return, the panel terminates its proceedings rather than issuing an award. Termination is procedural and leaves the underlying claim undecided; the panel may take the case again once the strike has actually stopped [Para 39][Para 56].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that it made no findings of fact on the disputed points, because the hearing could not proceed at all, so nothing it said about the two suspensions binds a later panel [Para 33].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award records that no factual findings were made [Para 33], yet it finds as fact that the employer suspended two strikers and refused them readmission [Para 54]; the two passages sit uneasily together.
- The case name is spelled ហារតា, ហារ តា, ហារ តា ផេក, ផាក and ផេក ខេ ជីង in different places, and the Latin rendering "Industry" appears only in Khmer transcription [Para 1][Para 13][Para 27].
- The chair arbitrator's colleague is named Liv Sovannara in the panel list and Liv Sovanna in the signature block [Para 1][Para 59].
- Passage 24 speaks of four workers returning where the context plainly means all of them [Para 24].
- The award is short and in places garbled: the strike's start date of 11 February 2014 appears only in counsel's letter, and the order to return runs "from the morning of 18 February" although the hearing record dates the panel's order to 17 February [Para 13][Para 14].
