# Award No. 238/14 — Sidab Bel You Kh EH Garment (Cambodia) Limited (Arbitration Council, 8 September 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chea, president) · Doc ID 139702

## 1. Facts
The Khmer Workers' Spirit Trade Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ស្មារតី កម្មករ ខ្មែរ) and its local at CWKH Garment (Cambodia) Limited demanded that the company remove Ms C Shuping, its general manager, from her post. The claim reached the Arbitration Council as a single unsettled point after conciliation failed on 22 August 2014. Workers struck from 14–15 August and again from 22–25 August; the panel issued injunction No. 045/14 on 27 August 2014 ordering the strike suspended and all workers back from the morning of 28 August 2014, which they obeyed. [Para 3][Para 13]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must remove Ms C Shuping, its general manager, from her post at the workers' union's demand? [Para 3]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The union leadership at the company demanded the removal of Ms C Shuping from her position as general manager, and pursued the demand by strike from 14 August 2014 and again from 22 August 2014. [Para 3]
**Employer (respondent):** The company cannot comply because Ms C Shuping has done nothing wrong towards the company, and it sought first the Council's injunction and then a court protective order to end the strikes. [Para 4][Para 21]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel terminated the arbitration proceedings in case No. 238/14 without deciding the removal demand on its merits. [Para 90]
At the hearing both parties confirmed that the strike had ended and all workers had returned to normal work, so the panel went on to examine the substance of the dispute and set 16 September 2014 for its award. [Para 19]
The panel then turned to Prakas No. 099 Article 20, under which, during the operation of an arbitration panel, the parties must suspend every dispute measure — strike, lockout or other measure capable of aggravating the dispute (as also provided in Article 318 of the Labour Law) — and must attend all meetings to which the panel summons them. [Para 73]
It found that the employer had asked the Phnom Penh Court of First Instance for a protective order on 29 August 2014; on 3 September 2014 the court issued order No. 195 (K).Ph.P.KA requiring workers to stop the sit-in strike and return to work by shift within 48 hours, while temporarily suspending 15 named workers pending the court's decision on the merits. A deputy prosecutor and court officials enforced that order at the factory on 8 September 2014. The panel held that the employer had thus taken a measure that aggravated the dispute, because the enforcement triggered the renewed strike. [Para 79][Para 83]
It found equally that the workers struck again from 8 to 10 September 2014, after the hearing and while the panel was deliberating towards its award of 16 September 2014, without waiting for that award — conduct contrary to Article 20. Both parties' letters of 10 September 2014 confirmed the picket continued at the factory gate over those days. [Para 84][Para 89]
Weighing both breaches, the panel concluded under Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 that the arbitration proceedings in this case should be brought to an end, and so decided, issuing no order on the removal demand. [Para 91]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where, during the pendency of an arbitration, the parties resort to dispute measures that Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 requires them to suspend — an employer obtaining and enforcing a court protective order against strikers, or workers striking again after the hearing and before the award — the panel will terminate the proceedings rather than rule on the merits, leaving the underlying demand undecided. [Para 83][Para 86][Para 90]
A demand that an employer dismiss or remove a named manager is not determined on its merits by a panel that has lost the procedural basis to continue. [Para 91]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the renewed strike arose from the employer's request for the protective order and its enforcement, and that the workers acted without waiting for the award; neither observation altered the single outcome of termination. [Para 87]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award date is printed three ways: 8 September 2014 in the header, 16 September 2014 in the procedural narrative and reasons, and "16 September 2013" at one point in the reasoning. [Para 1][Para 19][Para 78] The enforcement of the court order is dated 8 August 2014 at one place and 8 September 2014 elsewhere; the sequence of events requires September. [Para 25][Para 81] The union's letter No. 199 is dated 10 September 2014 in the document list but 10 September 2001 in the reasons. [Para 59][Para 85] The employer is named "Sidab Bel You Kh EH Garment (Cambodia) Limited" on the face of the award and "CWKH Garment (Cambodia) Limited" throughout the body, with further spelling variants including "ស៊ីដាប់ ប៊ែល យូ ខេ អេដស៍" and "ស៊ីដាប់ បែប យូ ខេ អេដ"; the two appear to be the same entity and are not reconciled in the text. [Para 1][Para 91] Stray OCR matter ("2017 3572", "<unk>", bare "៩" page marks) appears in the document list and signature block. [Para 66][Para 92] Individual workers are identified in the source but are reduced here to the company and the named manager.
