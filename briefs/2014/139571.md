# Award No. 115/14 — Tae Young (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 27 June 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139571

## 1. Facts
Tae Young (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a garment factory at Borei Kamkar, Bek Chan, Ang Snuol, Kandal with about 943 workers, where the union's most-representative certificate ran from 14 November 2012 to 14 November 2014. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 65] On 07 April 2014 the company asked the Kandal labour department for permission to suspend and terminate the contracts of six union leaders and members, alleging damage to property and incitement to strike; it dismissed them on 08 April 2014, before any reply. [Para 46][Para 228][Para 230][Para 231] The dispute also covered thirteen welfare demands, of which four were settled at the hearing and one withdrawn. [Para 28][Para 29][Para 30]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must dismiss Mr M, its administration head, for interfering in union affairs? [Para 66][Para 186]
2. Whether the employer must reinstate the six suspended union leaders and staff representatives with back pay from the date of suspension? [Para 73][Para 213]
3. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of 5,000 riel per day? [Para 136]
4. Whether the employer must restore a health-care allowance of 5 USD per month? [Para 141]
5. Whether the employer must supply four sets of work clothing per worker per year? [Para 145]
6. Whether the employer must add 1,750 riel per overtime hour to the existing meal payment? [Para 148][Para 302]
7. Whether the employer must pay the target bonus for each day the quota is met rather than only where six days a week are met? [Para 154][Para 316]
8. Whether the employer must raise the skills allowance to 15, 10 and 10 USD for three-, two- and one-machine operators? [Para 168][Para 343]
9. Whether the employer must add 5 USD to the existing 10 USD attendance bonus? [Para 175][Para 352]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the administration head suspended the deduction of union dues, pressurised workers to resign from the union and insulted its leaders; the six were dismissed a day after the request to the labour department, without authorisation; the welfare demands rest on what neighbouring factories pay. [Para 67][Para 138][Para 177]
**Employer (respondent):** the administration head never interfered with the union and dues were suspended for all unions pending review; it acted after Mr R, microphone in hand, urged a strike and Mr C smashed a window of the director's office; each welfare demand exceeds what the law or its practice requires. [Para 70][Para 100][Para 102][Para 140][Para 153]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to take back all six workers and pay back wages from the date of dismissal to the date of return, and dismissed every other claim; the rights-dispute order became final immediately and bound the parties only under the Memorandum of Understanding of 03 October 2012. [Para 234][Para 363][Para 365][Para 372]

On issue 1 the panel applied Articles 65 and 311 of the Civil Code: only the parties to a contract of employment may extinguish its obligations, so a third party cannot force the employer to dismiss the administration head, and a worker may obtain such an order only on evidence that the person is dangerous and cannot be retained without endangering others' safety. [Para 191][Para 193][Para 196] The workers gave no specific instance — no date, act or identified member — and the panel found the burden of proof unmet, noting that the employer had suspended dues deductions for every union. [Para 200][Para 207][Para 208]

On issue 2 the panel classified the claim as a rights dispute, turning on dismissal of workers with unlimited-duration contracts and on Articles 74 and 293 of the Labour Law. [Para 214][Para 215] It separated the six: Mr C and Mr R were union leaders, Mr K, Ms P and Ms C were staff representatives, and Ms H was an ordinary worker. [Para 216][Para 217][Para 218] Article 293 and Article 4(2) of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 require the labour inspector's prior agreement before a staff representative or union leader is dismissed, and the employer asked on 07 April but dismissed on 08 April without awaiting a reply. [Para 219][Para 228][Para 229][Para 232] The five protected dismissals were therefore procedurally unlawful and the panel ordered reinstatement with back pay. [Para 233][Para 234] For Ms H it applied Article 74: dismissal requires written notice and a proper reason, the internal regulation does not make a breach of Article 2 dismissable, and the employer had not graded her conduct as light, medium or serious, so dismissal was disproportionate. [Para 235][Para 245][Para 247][Para 248]

On the welfare claims the panel found the union had standing — its most-representative certificate was current — but treated each demand as an interest dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍) decided on equity, and dismissed them one by one: 5,000 riel lunch money rested only on what other factories paid, with no rule or practice creating the obligation [Para 250]; the 5 USD health allowance had already been absorbed into the basic wage under Notification No. 103 of 09 April 2013 [Para 283]; Prakas No. 307 of 14 December 2007 requires two sets of clothing, not four [Para 288][Para 295]; the 2,500 riel already paid for overtime meals was above the 2,000 riel in Notification No. 041/11 [Para 302][Para 307]; the target bonus fell within the employer's managerial prerogative under Article 2, and the workers filed no evidence of how many received it [Para 325][Para 341]; the skills allowance and attendance bonus likewise had no legal basis, the 10 USD attendance bonus already matching Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 [Para 350][Para 359].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that dismisses a union leader or staff representative without the labour inspector's prior agreement acts contrary to Article 293 of the Labour Law and Article 4(2) of Prakas No. 305, and the panel will order reinstatement with back pay from the date of dismissal even where the underlying conduct is arguable; a request posted one day before the dismissal cannot supply that agreement. An ordinary worker dismissed for a breach the internal regulation does not rank as light, medium or serious is dismissed disproportionately and must also be reinstated. A claim for a benefit above statute or existing practice fails where the union can show only that other employers pay more. [Para 220][Para 232][Para 248][Para 250]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the target-bonus conditions "may be difficult" for workers and could discourage them, and that market prices had risen while the minimum wage was also raised in 2014, were comments on fairness that did not alter those dismissals. [Para 311][Para 333]
