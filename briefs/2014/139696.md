# Award No. 222/14 — Vai Full Garment (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 12 September 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Pen Bunhea (president) · Doc ID 139696

## 1. Facts
Vai Full Garment (Cambodia) Ltd cuts and sews garments at Ang Snuol, Kandal, with 950 workers. Eighteen workers, backed by two union federations, filed seventeen demands on pay and conditions; conciliation settled four, leaving thirteen. At the hearing the parties settled two more (issues 8 and 9), so eleven were decided, for those eighteen workers only. Neither union held registration with the Ministry at the time. [Para 2][Para 51][Para 21][Para 26][Para 52][Para 56]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay 4,000 riel in meal money for overtime worked from 16:00 to 18:00? [Para 3]
2. Whether the company must pay 4,000 riel a day as lunch money? [Para 4]
3. Whether the company must pay Monday-to-Friday overtime meal money in one sum every Saturday? [Para 5]
4. Whether the company must remove the factory director? [Para 6]
5. Whether the company must pay monthly wages from 14:00? [Para 7]
6. Whether the company must pay a 15 USD monthly attendance bonus? [Para 8]
7. Whether the company must pay 15 USD a month for travel and lodging? [Para 9]
8. Whether the company must refrain from dismissing workers without clear cause? (settled) [Para 10]
9. Whether the company must build bicycle and motorcycle parking? (settled) [Para 11]
10. Whether the company must pay 15 USD a month in skill allowance to workers operating three or more machines? [Para 12]
11. Whether the company must pay 15 USD a month in milk money to women who have just given birth? [Para 13]
12. Whether the company must grant fixed-term contracts of six months to one year? [Para 14]
13. Whether the company must preserve wages and benefits during the strike of 11–16 August 2014? [Para 15]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Food, rent and transport costs have risen, and other factories pay 13 USD lunch money, 15–18 USD bonuses and travel money, and skill allowances; the company should match those rates and pay overtime meal money weekly so it is not lost. [Para 63][Para 86][Para 96][Para 156]
**Employer (respondent):** Overtime is voluntary; the company already pays the 2,000 riel meal money, 6.5 USD lunch money, 10 USD attendance bonus and 8 USD travel money that Notification 041/11 and Notification 230 require, and cannot pay more. [Para 60][Para 65][Para 88][Para 93]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — only issue 3 was allowed: the company must pay the overtime meal money for Monday to Friday in one sum every Saturday. The other twelve claims were refused. The order is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent written objection to the Minister. [Para 275][Para 284]

The panel first held that every money claim here is an interest dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍). Under Art 96 of the Labour Law, Art 9 of Prakas 305 and Art 43 of Prakas 099, an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement for a year and strips the workforce of the right to strike over it, so the panel will decide one only where the claimant is the most-representative union in the enterprise. These eighteen workers were not, and the panel refused to reach the merits of issues 1, 2, 6, 7, 10 and 11. [Para 121][Para 126][Para 129][Para 134]

On issue 1, point 2 of Notification 041/11 fixes overtime meal money at 2,000 riel a day or a free meal, and the company pays 2,000 riel; the claim for 4,000 riel exceeded what the instrument requires. On issue 2, the company pays 6.5 USD a month, about 1,000 riel a day, and no law, collective agreement or past practice supported 4,000 riel. Both were refused. [Para 115][Para 119][Para 137][Para 139]

Issue 3 succeeded because Notification 041/11 fixes the amount but not the moment of payment. The panel held payment must fall within a reasonable period that lets workers buy food for the days they work overtime, whether weekly, fortnightly or monthly, and that the workers' reasons — the Friday portion being pushed to the following Saturday — were reasonable and necessary. [Para 145][Para 148][Para 158][Para 159]

On issue 4, Arts 65 of the Labour Law and 664 and 311 of the Civil Code confine the power to alter a labour contract to its parties; a third party cannot remove an employee, and workers must show the person is dangerous. No specific evidence or date was produced, so the claim failed. [Para 166][Para 168][Para 180][Para 182]

On issue 5, the 14:00 payment practice had been replaced by 15:00 for about a year without objection, so it had ceased to be a past practice; Art 115(3) requires wages to be paid on a working day within working hours, which the company observed. [Para 188][Para 189][Para 194][Para 201]

Issues 6, 7, 10 and 11 failed on the same footing: Notification 230 sets a 10 USD attendance bonus and 7 USD travel and lodging money, the company pays 10 USD and 8 USD, and no instrument obliges more. [Para 203][Para 213][Para 223][Para 230]

On issue 12, the panel held that neither party may impose a contract type or duration on the other: the workers' request was a lawful proposal and the company's refusal a lawful response, and an award could not force either. [Para 254][Para 256][Para 259]

On issue 13, Arts 332(1), 334 and 72(1) suspend the contract during a strike, so no wages or bonuses are owed, the exception being where strike-breakers were hired — which both sides agreed had not happened. [Para 260][Para 262][Para 265][Para 270]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An award on an interest dispute becomes a collective agreement binding every worker in the enterprise and extinguishing their right to strike over the same subject, so the panel will not decide such a claim — however well founded on the merits — unless it is brought by the union holding most-representative status there; a group of workers who are not that union has no standing to seek higher wages or allowances. [Para 128][Para 129][Para 134]

**Obiter dicta:** Where an instrument fixes the amount of an allowance but not the date of payment, payment is lawful if made within a reasonable period that still lets the worker meet the expense, so daily or weekly payment cannot be demanded as of right. [Para 148]
