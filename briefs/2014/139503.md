# Award No. 006/14 — Kolly Tex Style Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 3 February 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (ម៉ារ សំបូរ ណាស់ appointed by the employer party, អន ណន appointed by the worker party, គង់ ផល្បៈ chair) · Doc ID 139503

## 1. Facts
Kolly Tex Style Co., Ltd. (ខូលី ធី តេក្ស ស្ទីល ខូ អែល ធី ឌី), a garment factory at Trapaing Thleung, Chom Chao, Porsenchey, Phnom Penh, employing about 1,700 workers, faced a strike by roughly 1,000 or more workers from the afternoon of 25 December 2013 to 31 December 2013 — 5.5 days — and again from 02 to 04 January 2014. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 49] The employer deducted the 5.5 days of wages for the December strike while keeping the regular attendance bonus in place. [Para 64] The workers pursued 100% wages for the strike days, and in the alternative some portion of wages according to what the employer could afford. [Para 2][Para 50]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to pay the striking workers part of their wages, according to what it is able to pay, for the strike from the afternoon of 25 December 2013 to 31 December 2013? [Para 49][Para 74]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to pay 100% of wages for the strike from 02 January 2014 to 04 January 2014? [Para 72]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** they accepted that a lawful strike carries no wages, but some factories paid 100% and others deducted only 50% of base wage, so this employer should pay whatever portion it can afford for the December days. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 53]
**Employer (respondent):** it opened the factory gates and the workers chose not to enter; the strike was neither peaceful nor preceded by a secret ballot or notice to the employer; some 200–300 workers who stayed were given paid rest, and no replacements were hired. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 62][Para 65][Para 66][Para 71]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the claim that the employer pay part of the wages of workers who struck from the afternoon of 25 December 2013 to 31 December 2013; no sum was ordered. [Para 85][Para 86]

On the first issue the panel applied Article 332(1) of the Labour Law: the employment contract is suspended during a strike, work is not provided and wages are not paid. [Para 74][Para 75] Article 72(1) confines that suspension to the principal obligations of the contract, so it did not disturb the rest of the relationship. [Para 76] The panel followed its earlier reasoning in Award No. 49/05 (Ou Seang Ngam) and Award No. 27/10 (Hat 2 Inter) that strikers cannot receive wages during the strike whether or not the strike followed the procedure of the Labour Law — the workers' own evidence that the December strike had no secret ballot and no prior notice therefore did not matter. [Para 60][Para 77][Para 78] Article 334 bars the employer from hiring replacements during a strike and makes a breach of that bar the trigger for paying strikers' wages; the employer asserted no replacements were hired and the workers did not deny it, so the exception was not engaged. [Para 80][Para 81][Para 82][Para 83] The comparison with the factories named in Svay Rieng, Kampong Speu, Takeo and Phnom Penh was set aside: those employers chose to send their workers home, whereas here the workers left of their own accord. [Para 65][Para 68] On the second issue the parties had already agreed that the employer would keep 100% of wages for 02–04 January 2014 while deducting equivalent annual leave, so the panel made no ruling on it. [Para 72][Para 73]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A strike suspends the employment contract, so the employer owes no wages at all for the strike days, and that remains so whether the strike was called in compliance with the Labour Law or not; an employer becomes liable for the strikers' wages only where it hired replacement workers in breach of Article 334. Wage settlements reached at other factories, or an employer's capacity to pay, create no obligation to pay part of the wages. [Para 75][Para 77][Para 81]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's survey of the differing deduction practices at named factories in four provinces, and its findings that both in-house unions were lawful organisations and that the seven elected worker delegates present were among the 17 chosen in April 2013, were not necessary to the dismissal. [Para 44][Para 47][Para 53]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The company name is printed two ways: "ខ្វលី ធី តេក្ស ស្ទីល" in the header and most references, and "ខូលី ធី តេក្ស ស្ទីល" in others; the English rendering "Kolly Tex Style" is a transliteration of the latter, not a name printed in Latin script anywhere in the document.
- The date on which the panel was constituted reads "ថ្ងៃទី ១៤ ខែមករា ឆ្នាំ ២០១៩ ២០១៤" — two years printed for one date; 2014 is consistent with the surrounding chronology. [Para 13]
- The claim is stated as 100% of wages in the statement of issues but as "some wages according to what the employer can pay" in the reasons and the order. [Para 49][Para 74][Para 86]
- Year figures are corrupted in places ("ឆ្នាំ ២ ២០១៤" at [Para 49]); "មាត្រា ៣៣២ ក ថា ខណ្ឌ ទី១" is a garbled rendering of Article 332(1), and the citation of Article 72(1) carries the same defect. [Para 74][Para 76]
- The employer-appointed arbitrator's name is truncated at the signature block as "ម៉ារ សំបូរ ណា<". [Para 88]
- The duration of the December strike is given as 5.5 days running from the afternoon of 25 December; the panel adopted that figure without reconciling it against a full working-day count. [Para 49][Para 64]
