# Case No. 260/14 — Keep Top Sporting Goods (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 October 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun for the employer, Sin Kim Sean for the workers, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139732

## 1. Facts
Keep Top Sporting Goods (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (ឃីប ថ ប ស្ព័ត ធីង ហ្គូដស៍) makes sporting equipment in Preah Sihanouk with 173 workers. [Para 42][Para 43] The Democratic Union Federation of Cambodian Garment Workers held a leadership election at the factory on 20 July 2014; Mr M was elected president and the employer was notified by letter No. 090/14 dated 23 July 2014, signed for on 25 July 2014. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 51] The employer suspended Mr M's fixed-duration contract for one week from 02 September 2014 and dismissed him on 05 September 2014 for serious misconduct, namely telling workers to stop their sewing machines for half an hour on 26 August 2014 and inciting a strike. [Para 2][Para 52][Para 104] Neither the local union nor Mr M's own union had yet obtained legal professional-organisation status. [Para 45]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take Mr M back to work and pay his wages from the date of dismissal until the date of reinstatement? [Para 70]

The panel recorded that the workers withdrew issue 2 — the employer's threatened deduction of wages for days not worked during the strike and of the attendance bonus — leaving only issue 1. [Para 3][Para 15][Para 16]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr M was elected union president and so enjoyed the special protection the Labour Law gives to union leaders, yet the employer dismissed him without the labour inspector's authorisation and without any serious misconduct on his part; the machine stoppage was a spontaneous reaction by other workers. [Para 53][Para 61][Para 65]
**Employer (respondent):** Mr M committed serious misconduct — moving from section to section during working hours, ordering the machines stopped for half an hour on 26 August 2014 and inciting a strike that some workers resisted and were threatened over; the employer wrote to the labour inspectorate on 02 August 2014 for an inspection; the inspector declined to decide, so the file came to the panel. [Para 2][Para 54][Para 66][Para 67]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to take Mr M back to work and to pay his wages from the date of dismissal until the date he returns. [Para 121][Para 122]

The panel began with Article 293 of the Labour Law: the dismissal of a staff delegate, or of any candidate for that office, may be made only with the agreement of the labour inspector. [Para 70] It read Article 3(3) of Prakas No. 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001 as extending to every candidate in a union leadership election the same protection against dismissal as a staff delegate, for 45 days before the election and, for candidates not elected, 45 days after it. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 76] Following Awards 40/10 (Meng Yang) and 154/13 (Bou Kher Garment Factory), the panel held that the protection of an elected union leader runs from the day of election until the union obtains registration, because otherwise the leader is exposed to dismissal in exactly the interval before registration; the absence of that protection would contradict Article 266 (freedom to form professional organisations) and Articles 12 and 279 (prohibition of anti-union discrimination). [Para 79][Para 81][Para 84][Para 85]

The panel then applied the three conditions it had set in earlier awards for special protection: the worker must be a protected worker, the dismissal must fall within the protected period, and the employer must have been notified of the protected person by any reliable means. [Para 87][Para 88] All three were met — Mr M was elected president on 20 July 2014 [Para 90][Para 91]; the dismissal on 05 September 2014 fell inside the protected period that ran from election to registration [Para 95][Para 96][Para 97]; and the federation notified the employer of the three elected leaders by letter No. 090/14 of 23 July 2014, received on 25 July 2014, which the employer acknowledged at the hearing [Para 99][Para 100][Para 101].

On the employer's argument that Article 295 allowed immediate dismissal for serious misconduct, the panel held that the employer may suspend the contract while awaiting the labour inspector's decision, but if the inspector does not agree the suspension is void. [Para 105][Para 106][Para 107] Here the employer's letter of 02 August 2014 asked only for an inspection of the machine-stoppage incident; it did not ask for authorisation to dismiss Mr M, and no document showed any authorisation. [Para 110][Para 111][Para 112] The dismissal was therefore made in breach of procedure and, under Article 4 of Prakas No. 305, is void. [Para 113][Para 117][Para 118][Para 119] Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY empowers the panel to order reinstatement and immediate payment, which it did. [Para 120][Para 121]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker elected to union leadership acquires the special protection against dismissal from the day of the election and keeps it until the union is registered, even though the union has no legal status in between; a dismissal during that period is void unless the labour inspector authorised it beforehand, and a request that the inspector merely investigate the alleged misconduct is not a request for authorisation. [Para 81][Para 84][Para 112][Para 119]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that withholding this protection would defeat Article 266 and Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law, and that the employer could lawfully suspend the contract while awaiting the inspector's decision, are explanatory rather than necessary to the order. [Para 85][Para 106]
