# Award No. 274/13 — Sixplus Industry Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 29 January 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kav Thach appointed by the employer party, An Non appointed by the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair chosen by the two wing arbitrators) · Doc ID 139920

## 1. Facts

Sixplus Industry Co., Ltd (ស៊ិច ផ្លាស អ៊ិន ដាស ស្ទ្រី) runs a garment-cutting factory at Kandal village, Prek Anchanh commune, Mukh Kampul district, Kandal province, and employs about 1,700 workers. [Para 52][Para 53] Two unions represent the workforce: the Federation of Construction and Forestry Workers' Unions of Cambodia and the Independent Union of Workers' Solidarity Force at Sixplus, registered with the Ministry of Labour on 2 January 2014. [Para 1][Para 54] On 21 December 2013 the Kandal provincial Department of Labour and Vocational Training attempted conciliation of ten collective demands; none was settled, and non-conciliation report No. 038/13 K.B/Kandal of 21 December 2013 reached the Council secretariat on 23 December 2013. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 46]

The panel was constituted on 25 December 2013 and summonsed both parties to a hearing at 08:30 on 14 January 2014; both attended. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 22] Before the hearing the parties had already agreed six points — points 4, 1, 2, 3, 5 and 10 — and the worker party withdrew points 6, 7 and 8, leaving only point 9 for decision. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 25] The order records that it is a non-binding order under Article 313 of the Labour Law and becomes final eight days after notification unless a party lodges a written objection with the Minister of Labour through the Council secretariat. [Para 26][Para 86]

Point 9 is that the company should pay wages directly in cash to workers at the factory. [Para 11][Para 61] For nearly two years wages have been paid monthly through ACLEDA Bank accounts, so workers must withdraw their pay from an ATM. [Para 62]

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. Whether the employer must change from paying wages through an ATM account to paying wages directly in cash at the company? [Para 61][Para 66]
2. Whether the panel may decide the claim for the 798 workers on the union's name list when the list does not show that those workers authorised the union to represent them? [Para 55][Para 57][Para 58]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** relying on Articles 113 and 115 of the Labour Law, they say some workers do not know how to use an ATM, and each withdrawal costs 4,000 riel in motodop fares from the factory; they want net pay handed to them personally by the employer. [Para 63] They submitted a list of 798 workers, of whom 33 demanded direct payment and 765 said they had lost money. [Para 55]

**Employer (respondent):** it has dealt with ACLEDA Bank to place an ATM in front of the company in about four months; the nearest ATM is about 200 metres away; payment by ATM is safer for both sides, since workers have previously been drugged and robbed; fewer than 10% cannot use the machine, and it will arrange training for them; the practice has run for nearly two years and it cannot meet the demand. [Para 64][Para 65]

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected point 9 and ordered that the employer need not change from ATM payment to direct cash payment. [Para 84][Para 85]

On the representation point, the employer objected that it could not understand the meaning or authenticity of the fingerprint list received from the Council. [Para 56] The panel found that the list did not state that the workers had transferred authority to the union to represent them in the dispute, and so it did not consider the list at all. [Para 57][Para 58] Only four workers attended the hearing, and the panel decided point 9 for those four only. [Para 59][Para 60]

On the merits, Article 113 of the Labour Law requires wages to be paid directly to the worker in person, unless the worker agrees to another method. [Para 66][Para 70] The panel held that "paid directly in person" cannot be read narrowly as meaning hand-to-hand cash payment; it means the employer must pay the worker personally, avoiding payment to a substitute, a creditor or a relative, unless the worker agrees otherwise, because substituted payment can destroy part or all of the worker's benefit. [Para 70][Para 72] Paying through an ATM is still direct payment: the employer transfers each worker's wage into that worker's own account, not to anyone else, and the money in the account belongs to the worker, who may use, manage and enjoy it freely and without restriction by the employer. [Para 73] Under Article 115 wages are to be paid at the workplace or a nearby office, and the panel found that the transfer in fact takes place at the company to each worker's account. [Para 74][Para 76]

The panel also noted that government policy encourages use of the banking system over cash, to reduce risk to the national and household economy, and that most civil servants and company staff are paid by ATM for convenience, time-saving and safety; users can withdraw anywhere, day or night. [Para 77][Para 78][Para 79] The 4,000 riel motodop cost was not necessary, since ATMs exist in many places and workers can withdraw near home. [Para 80] The employer's commitment to train workers who cannot use the machine answers that concern. [Para 81][Para 82] First-time users may find it difficult, but the problem should disappear with use. [Para 83]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

Payment of wages by transfer into each worker's own bank account, withdrawable by ATM, satisfies the requirement in Article 113 of the Labour Law that wages be paid directly to the worker in person, where the employer pays no third party and the worker retains free control of the funds; the employer is not obliged to pay cash hand-to-hand. [Para 70][Para 73][Para 76][Para 84] A union's list of workers said to be affected will not be considered where it does not show that each worker authorised the union to represent them, and the panel will decide only for the workers who appear. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 60]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations on government policy favouring banking over cash, on the convenience and safety of ATM payment and on the availability of ATMs near workers' homes are supporting reasons, not part of the operative rule [Para 77][Para 78][Para 79][Para 80]; its remark that new users will adapt with time is predictive [Para 83]; and the employer's undertaking to install an ATM at the factory within about four months and to train workers was noted but not ordered. [Para 64][Para 81][Para 82]
