# Case No. 147/14 — Success Index Group (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 7 July 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ma Somborana, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Mae Nimmith, chair) · Doc ID 139607

## 1. Facts
The employer is a garment factory in Trapeang Chheu Neang village, Peuk commune, Ang Snuol district, Kandal, employing about 600 workers. [Para 75][Para 76] Mr Y began work on 4 October 2011 without a written contract, was later put on a one-year fixed-duration contract running from 1 May 2013 to 30 April 2014, and was told on 11 April 2014 that it would not be renewed. [Para 84][Para 100][Para 112] He had been elected president of the Cambodia Union Confederation at the factory on 15 September 2013, alongside Mr V as deputy and Ms K as secretary; all three were among the only workers not renewed. [Para 79][Para 97][Para 98] Conciliation on 29 May 2014 left four demands unresolved. [Para 12][Para 13]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take Mr Y back as union president, pay his back wages from dismissal until return, and stop discriminating against the union? [Para 130]
2. Whether the employer must pay the wages and benefits of 10 February 2014 to the three union leaders who attended an ILO training that day? [Para 206]

The workers merged issues 1 and 3 into issue 1, so the panel treated issues 1, 2 and 3 as one dispute over rights and issue 4 as the second. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr Y's continuous service from 4 October 2011 made his contract indefinite, so a change to a one-year term was ineffective; only the three union leaders out of 600 workers were not renewed, which shows the non-renewal was a device to remove the union leadership; and the employer refused to accept the notice of elected leadership. [Para 97][Para 99][Para 104][Para 177]
**Employer (respondent):** there was no discrimination and no knowledge of the elected leadership, since the union was not yet registered and the notification letter was never acknowledged; Mr Y agreed voluntarily to the one-year contract after his indefinite contract was terminated and severance paid; and a fixed-duration contract simply expires. [Para 101][Para 185][Para 91][Para 94]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the claims for Mr Y's reinstatement, for back wages from dismissal to return, and for an order to stop union discrimination were rejected, as was the claim for the wages of 10 February 2014; no sum was ordered. [Para 218][Para 219]
On standing, the panel applied Article 268 of the Labour Law: a union that files its statute and leadership list is deemed registered if the Ministry does not respond within two months, and the receipt of 2 December 2013 plus silence past 20 May 2014 gave the factory union legal personality and the right to complain. [Para 117][Para 120][Para 129] Because both parties had signed the Memorandum of Understanding of 3 October 2012, the panel classified the reinstatement claim as a dispute over rights, so its decision bound the parties and carried no right of objection. [Para 21][Para 131][Para 134]
On the contract, the panel found that Mr Y's indefinite contract had been terminated with severance paid, and that he then freely agreed to a one-year fixed-duration contract; under Articles 65 and 311 of the Civil Code and Articles 336 and 337, a contract requires concurring offer and acceptance, so a party cannot be forced into a contract type it did not agree to. [Para 155][Para 156][Para 143][Para 148]
On the non-renewal procedure, Article 293 and Paragraph 4 of Prakas No. 305 require the labour inspector's consent only for dismissing a protected union leader, not for letting a fixed-duration contract run to its term, so no consent was needed. [Para 166][Para 167][Para 168][Para 172] On discrimination, the workers had to prove the employer knew of the elected leadership; the letter of 17 September 2013 carried no thumbprint or signature of receipt, service was never established, and no other evidence of discrimination was produced. [Para 179][Para 186][Para 187][Para 189] Article 73(1) ends a fixed-duration contract at its term, the notice of 11 April 2014 satisfied Article 73(5), and the 5 per cent severance had been paid, so the relationship was at an end. [Para 192][Para 199][Para 203][Para 204]
On the second issue the panel applied res judicata: the same parties had already litigated the 10 February 2014 wage claim in case No. 114/14, decided on 13 June 2014, so it declined to reconsider it. [Para 211][Para 216][Para 217]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an indefinite contract is terminated with severance paid and the worker then agrees, without coercion, to a fixed-duration contract, the new contract is valid, and its expiry needs statutory notice but not the labour inspector's consent, which Article 293 reserves for dismissal of a protected union leader. [Para 155][Para 172]
A union acquires legal personality by operation of Article 268(2) two months after filing, whether or not the Ministry replies, and may then bring the complaint in its own name. [Para 120]
A claim already decided between the same parties on the same issue will not be reheard, even by the Arbitration Council, once the earlier decision was complete. [Para 217]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that recognising an indefinite contract would not obstruct the factory's operation, since Article 74 permits termination with notice and for proper reason. [Para 230][Para 232]

**Dissenting opinion:** arbitrator An Non, appointed by the workers, dissented from the dismissal of issues 1 to 3. In his view the successive contracts totalled more than two years, which under Article 67(2) and ILO Recommendation No. 166 converts the relationship into an indefinite contract regardless of the parties' agreement; the employer then used the expiry as a pretext to dismiss a union president without cause and without the labour inspector's authorisation, so reinstatement should have been ordered. [Para 222][Para 224][Para 226][Para 234][Para 237]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The file is the award with the dissent annexed, both dated 7 July 2014, and the header labels the document a dissenting opinion before reciting the award itself. [Para 1][Para 221][Para 238] The notification letter is dated 17 September 2013 in the evidence list but 17 September 2014 in the panel's finding, and appears as both 070/13 and 070/13 with different signatory acronyms. [Para 47][Para 186] The contract start is printed "01 May 2013" and corrupted to "2 01 ខែឧសភា". [Para 100][Para 198] The complaint is recorded as received on 28 May 2014 while conciliation is dated 29 May 2014, and the panel is said to have been constituted on "05 June 2023 2014". [Para 11][Para 12][Para 14] The worker's name appears as both Yun Samoeun and Yon Samoeun, and the earlier award is cited as case 114/14 only. [Para 79][Para 81][Para 210]
