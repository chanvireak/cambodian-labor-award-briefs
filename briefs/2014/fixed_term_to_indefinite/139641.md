# Award No. 181/14 — Syung Yin (Cambodia) Bi Tek Stay (Arbitration Council, 11 August 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, employer-appointed; Sin Kim Sean, worker-appointed; Ang Eng Thong, chair) · Doc ID 139641

## 1. Facts
The Syung Yin (Cambodia) Bi Tek Stay garment factory in Kandal province employed about 1,700 workers. [Para 56] On 5 May 2014 the Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers' Democratic Union (C.CAWDU) held an election of factory union leaders, although the union had not yet obtained legal status as a professional organisation. [Para 57] On 25 and 26 June 2014 the employer ended the employment of seven leaders and activists whose fixed-duration contracts all ran to 30 June 2014. [Para 163] The workers raised twelve claims; ten were settled at the hearing, leaving two for adjudication. [Para 24]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise the piece rate for the checking and pressing sections by USD 0.25 on top of the existing USD 0.25, that is USD 0.50 per lot? [Para 62]
6. Whether the employer must take back the seven union leaders and pay them back wages from the date of dismissal to the date of return? [Para 80]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The USD 0.25 rate is too low to give a decent living as article 104 requires; about 80 per cent of workers struggle, pressing workers most of all because of the thickness of the fabric. [Para 64][Para 66] The seven were dismissed for union activity, and the employer replaced them with unskilled new hires. [Para 118][Para 121]
**Employer (respondent):** The rate is adequate and easy and hard pressing is a normal mix; wages already reach the legal minimum and any shortfall is topped up. [Para 69][Para 72] The seven were not dismissed at all — their fixed-duration contracts simply were not renewed, because production fell from July to December. [Para 122][Para 125]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the piece-rate claim was dismissed, and on the reinstatement claim the employer was ordered to take back Toun Nai Leng, Tren Choern and Than Sim Heng with back pay, and to pay the other four 5% termination severance, moral damages and outstanding annual leave. [Para 255][Para 256][Para 258]

On issue 1, article 108 requires piece rates to be set so that a worker of average skill working normal hours earns at least the guaranteed minimum wage, and Prakas 317 raised the garment minimum from USD 80 to USD 100 per month from 1 February 2014. [Para 127][Para 129] The panel accepted that the employer may fix and change piece rates, but only on terms that let an average worker reach the minimum. [Para 133] The claim nevertheless failed on proof: the workers could not say how many of the 135 who transferred rights worked in checking and pressing, and the three payslips filed were for ironing, measuring and laundry work. [Para 67][Para 79] With no identified claimant in the affected sections, the panel declined to rule on the substance. [Para 155]

On issue 6, the contracts ran to 30 June 2014 while the terminations fell on 25 and 26 June, so this was dismissal before expiry and not a non-renewal. [Para 162][Para 164] Under article 293 of the Labour Law and Prakas 305, candidates in a union leadership election are protected as staff delegates, protection running from the election until the union is registered; the panel followed Awards 40/10-Meng Yang and 154/13-Boeung Khmer Garment in extending that protection to elected leaders before registration. [Para 167][Para 178][Para 179] All three conditions were satisfied: the three were elected on 5 May 2014, were dismissed inside the protection period, and the employer had been notified on 12 May 2014 by letter 132/14 signed for by the factory security guard. [Para 193][Para 202][Para 206] Because the employer never sought the labour inspector's prior authorisation as article 293 requires, the dismissals were procedurally unlawful and reinstatement with back pay followed. [Para 212][Para 218]

The remaining four were not shown to have been singled out for union activity; the workers produced no evidence of discrimination, so the allegation was rejected. [Para 231][Para 234] Their contracts were nonetheless cut short, which under article 73(3) entitles a worker to damages at least equal to wages to the end of the contract, plus 5% severance under article 73(6) and unused annual leave under article 166. [Para 235][Para 244][Para 251]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker elected as a union leader is protected against dismissal from the date of the election until the union is registered, and an employer that dismisses such a leader without the labour inspector's prior authorisation must reinstate them with back pay, whatever the contract's end date. [Para 186][Para 217]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed generally that an employer's freedom to set piece rates is bounded by the requirement that an average worker reach the minimum wage, even though the claim before it failed for want of an identified claimant. [Para 139]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The panel heads its piece-rate reasoning "ចំណុច វិវាទ ទី 09" but the issues list and the analysis both number it issue 01. [Para 62][Para 127]
- The award records the panel as constituted on "08 July 2019 2014", printing two years. [Para 22]
- The June terminations are dated 26 June 2013 in two places where the surrounding reasoning requires 2014. [Para 160][Para 163]
- The employer's name is printed variously as ប៊ី ថិក ស្ថាយ៍, ប៊ី ថំក ស្ថា យ៏ and ប៊ី ថេក ស្ថាយ; worker names appear only in Khmer and inconsistently (Tren Choern / Tren Choeun).
- The extension of the award deadline reads "from 28 July 2014 to 25, 11 August 2014". [Para 30]
