# Award No. 300/14 — Clin Sychal Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 27 November 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Seng Vouch Hun (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Aing Eng Tong (president) · Doc ID 139753

## 1. Facts
Clin Sychal Co., Ltd, a shoe factory in the Phnom Penh Special Economic Zone with 340 workers, faced eight demands from the Cambodian Asian Union Federation; six were settled in conciliation on 16 October 2014 and two remained. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 11] The union was not yet registered with the Ministry of Labour and had filed no written transfer of rights from the workers, so three workers attended the hearing directly as claimants. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47] The two live demands were permission to use personal mobile phones during working hours for urgent family calls, and continuation of the contracts of two workers said to be union leaders — reduced at the hearing to Mr N alone. [Para 48][Para 61][Para 62]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must let workers keep their personal phones switched on during working hours and take urgent family calls with their team leader's permission? [Para 83]
2. Whether the ending of Mr N's last fixed-duration contract was a non-renewal of a fixed-term contract or a dismissal by the employer alone, and whether he must be taken back? [Para 101]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company's own telephone line often rings unanswered — a child of one official called while sick and nobody picked up — and families cannot remember or pronounce workers' codes, so direct phones are the only workable channel. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54] Mr N had worked since 21 August 2012 on successive six-month contracts, was elected union president on 14 September 2014 and notified to the employer on 17 September, and was let go on 21 October 2014 without his 5% severance or final wages. [Para 62][Para 67][Para 82]
**Employer (respondent):** the internal rules of 19 May 2014 forbid personal business and all radio communication, phones included, during working hours, lest friends' calls disrupt production; phones may be used at the 5-minute morning break, the 10-minute afternoon break and lunch. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 59][Para 60] Mr N's contract simply expired with falling orders, and the company will rehire him when it recruits again. [Para 73][Para 74]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 1 dismissed; on issue 2 the panel ordered the employer to take Mr N back into work, and ordered no payment of any sum. [Para 140][Para 141]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 2 of the Labour Law, under which the employer directs and manages the enterprise provided the arrangement is lawful and reasonable, and followed Awards 08/09, 61/09, 169/09 and 37/11 holding managerial prerogative over personal calls. [Para 83][Para 86][Para 87] The internal rules validly prohibit personal work and all radio communication during working hours, and a mobile phone is such a means of communication; Award 183/12 held that a worker has no right to telephone during working hours without the employer's permission. [Para 88][Para 89][Para 90] The panel nonetheless observed that working life cannot wholly sever private and social ties, and that the employer should show understanding where a genuine emergency arises; it found the company's call-relay arrangement lawful and reasonable but directed that the network be corrected so that families can always reach a worker in an emergency. [Para 91][Para 93][Para 96][Para 98] The operative order nonetheless dismisses the claim as framed. [Para 140]

On issue 2 the panel characterised the relationship before deciding the label. Mr N began on 21 August 2012 under a two-month probation, then four successive six-month fixed-duration contracts; the last ran from 21 April to 21 October 2014, so total service exceeded two years. [Para 62][Para 66][Para 101][Para 102] Article 67 ¶2 caps a fixed-duration contract, and its renewals, at two years, and any breach converts it into a contract without a determinate term. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 105] Following Awards 10/03, 57/06, 155/09, 70/10, 105/11 and 216/14, the panel held the contract had become indefinite, so ending it was a dismissal. [Para 110][Para 113][Para 114] Because Mr N was an elected union leader and had been nominated and notified by post on 17 September 2014 — all three conditions of Prakas No. 305 being met — Article 293 required the labour inspector's prior authorisation, which the employer never sought. [Para 116][Para 122][Para 129][Para 132][Para 137] The dismissal was therefore unlawful and reinstatement was ordered. [Para 138][Para 139]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive fixed-duration contracts that together run beyond two years convert into a contract without a determinate term, so their ending is a dismissal requiring the labour inspector's prior authorisation where the worker is a protected union leader; by contrast, a rule banning personal phones during working hours is a lawful exercise of managerial prerogative, and the panel will not rewrite it. [Para 105][Para 114][Para 116][Para 90]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel encouraged the employer, without ordering it, to allow calls through an improved company relay in genuine emergencies, since working life cannot wholly cut a worker off from family. [Para 91][Para 93][Para 98]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Prakas No. 305 is dated 22 November 2023 in one recital and 22 November 2001 elsewhere. [Para 122][Para 118] Mr N's start date appears as both "21 August 2012" and "20 2012", and the evidence is said to have been filed on 03 November and 13 November 2014. [Para 76][Para 81][Para 101] The company name appears as "គ្លីន សឹខល", "គ្គីន សឹខល" and "គ្លីន សឺខល". [Para 43][Para 44][Para 80] The director's name is partly OCR-lossy ("Hi Daka Tadashi"). [Para 20] The award prints no riel or dollar amount because no money was ordered.
