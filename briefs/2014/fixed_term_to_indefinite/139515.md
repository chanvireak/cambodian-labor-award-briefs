# Award No. 028/14 — NagaWorld Limited (Arbitration Council, 3 March 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Kong Phallack (Chair), An Nan (worker party), You Suonty (employer party — dissenting) · Doc ID 139515

## 1. Facts
NagaWorld Limited ("NagaWorld"), a casino and hotel in Phnom Penh registered as Co. 176 Br/2000, employed about 5,000 workers; the claimant union held a certificate of registration dated 20 April 2000 and a Letter no. 518 dated 29 April 2013 recognising its 7th-term leadership [Para 19][Para 20]. A strike was staged from 13 to 25 June 2013: the union put the number on strike at 2,000, but only 963 were claimants in this case [Para 137]. Between July and November 2013 the employer dismissed eight staff in separate incidents, and the union sought reinstatement of four and termination compensation for the other four [Para 24]. Conciliation resolved one of six issues; report No. 136 dated 5 February 2014 reached the Secretariat the next day, and hearings sat on 21 and 27 February 2014 [Para 11][Para 13].

The eight workers appear below by initial: Ms U (cards distributor), Ms C, Ms S and Mr A (Casino Department), Mr J (Filipino national), Mr V (security guard), Ms T (Premium Hall) and Mr K (leave record keeper).

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate Ms U, Ms C, Ms S and Mr A with back pay of wages and compensation from the date of dismissal to the date of reinstatement, and pay termination compensation under the Labour Law to Mr J, Mr V, Ms T and Mr K? [Para 24][Para 142]
2. Whether the employer must maintain the wages of the 963 staff members who took part in the strike from 13 to 25 June 2013? [Para 137][Para 393]

Original demands 1 and 5 (non-discrimination, and the 2012 13th-month bonus docked by 50 per cent) were withdrawn at the hearing, and demands 2 and 3 were merged into the single issue above [Para 15][Para 16].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissals were discrimination for union and strike activity dressed up as misconduct; wages are not confidential and the memoranda asserting otherwise were never shown to staff; the strike was lawful, preceded by a survey of 1,039 staff (1,029 in favour) and a 4 June 2013 notification, and 624 workers signed a petition for the three casino workers' reinstatement [Para 67][Para 220]. **Employer (respondent):** each dismissal rested on distinct misconduct under Clause 10 (6) of the internal work rules; monthly wages are confidential by memoranda of 2 May 2012 and 23 January 2010, and collecting 30 payslips caused chaos [Para 57][Para 65]. On Issue 4 it relied on Article 332 and on Injunction No. 09 and the Prosecutor's Letter No. 110 declaring the strike illegal [Para 140].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement of Ms U, Ms C, Ms S and Mr A with back pay of wages and benefits from dismissal to reinstatement; termination compensation for Mr J, Mr V, Ms T and Mr K as itemised; the Issue 4 wage demand rejected [Para 394][Para 403]. The award is non-binding until eight days after notification, absent written opposition [Para 403].

*Contract type.* All eight contracts were of undetermined duration. Applying Article 67(2) as interpreted in Award 10/03-Jacquintex, a renewal that carries total service past two years converts the contract, and for Ms U two years and one month sufficed [Para 148][Para 154].

*Ms U.* There was no clause converting a fourth minor absence into a medium misconduct, nor a fifth into serious misconduct, so the escalation offended the rules themselves; and treating a fourth minor absence as a third medium misconduct skipped the prescribed sequence, breaching Article 27's proportionality requirement [Para 170][Para 176][Para 178]. The 13 September 2013 letter also gave "job abandonment" as the ground, which Clause 6 (B) reserves for absences of six days or more, and which contradicted the hearing claim of five separate absences; reinstatement was ordered with a direction to punish her according to the internal rules [Para 183][Para 184].

*Ms C, Ms S, Mr A.* Article 239 makes only health records confidential; the panel found no provision making wages confidential: wage details are each worker's own personal information, so the two memoranda were invalid to that extent [Para 201][Para 202]. The employer proved no specific damage or chaos, bearing the burden of proof under Awards 79/05-Evergreen and 101/08-GDM, so Article 74 gave no proper reason to dismiss [Para 205][Para 207].

*Mr J.* The employer produced no evidence written in Khmer of the 40 warning letters, and Clause 23 of Prakas No. 099 makes Khmer the language of proceedings (Award 54/11-June Textile) [Para 248][Para 249]. Its start-date claim of 15 October 1997 was unsupported, so 2 April 1995 stood, giving 18 years 3 months: three months' notice in lieu, indemnity of 270 days capped at six months under Article 89, payment in lieu of unused annual leave, and damages equal to the indemnity under Article 91 [Para 281][Para 283][Para 286].

*Mr V.* Permitting a barred ex-employee to enter showed no intent to keep information damaging the company, and no damage was proved; the 29 July 2013 suspension was within the seven-day window of Article 26 but the sanction was disproportionate [Para 302][Para 306][Para 308]. With 5 years 4 months' service he received two months' notice in lieu and 75 days' indemnity [Para 313].

*Ms T.* Asking a customer for a tip fell squarely within Point 3 (6) serious misconduct, so the dismissal was lawful under Article 27 and no indemnity, notice or damages were owed — only unused annual leave and already-paid outstanding wages [Para 328][Para 329].

*Mr K.* The leave rescheduling was not "counterfeiting company documents": his section head told him no retrospective request was needed and required him to make up the time, which amounted to verbal approval [Para 352][Para 354]. The conduct was at most medium misconduct under Point 2 (23), punished within the seven-day window but disproportionately, so he received two months' notice in lieu, 75 days' indemnity, annual leave and damages [Para 361][Para 371].

*Issue 4.* Article 332 suspends the contract and withholds salary during a strike; recruiting 113 replacements breached Article 334, but that article bites only where the strike itself complied with Chapter 13 [Para 373][Para 383]. The workers never claimed they negotiated before striking and struck while the Arbitration Council process was running, so the strike was unlawful and no wages were due [Para 392][Para 394].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A disciplinary escalation is valid only where the internal rules themselves prescribe the step taken: an employer cannot treat a fourth minor misconduct as a third medium misconduct, or a fifth as serious misconduct, where no clause says so, because Article 27 requires each sanction to be proportional to the misconduct actually committed. Wage details are the worker's own personal information and a memorandum cannot convert them into confidential company information absent a statutory basis. And wages for a strike are owed only where the employer used replacement labour and the strike itself complied with Articles 319 to 324; an unlawful strike earns nothing even if Article 334 was breached [Para 376][Para 386].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted the law's bias toward undetermined-duration contracts, citing ILO Recommendation 166 of 1982 as useful though non-binding; and it treated 624 supporting thumbprints as meeting Article 302's third condition (jeopardy to the enterprise or social peace) — not a merits ruling [Para 152][Para 222].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- Unofficial English translation: the evidence section was omitted and OCR losses ("themployer", "athearing") run throughout [Para 16].
- Ms U's dismissal is dated 11 September 2013 in the letter, 13 September in the employer's notification, and 16 September in the workers' account of collection [Para 29].
- The confidentiality memorandum is dated 23 January 2010 in the reasons but 23 January 2012 in the workers' account [Para 67][Para 193].
- Mr J's service is found to start 1 April 1995 in one place and 2 April 1995 in another, against the employer's 15 October 1997 [Para 94][Para 232].
- The strike runs 13 to 25 June 2013 throughout but "13-15 June 2013" once [Para 392].
- Ms T is "Mr" in the facts, "Ms" in the demands; the dissent describes a disposition (reinstating Mr V) the majority did not order [Para 118][Para 406].
