# Award No. 028/14 — NagaWorld Limited (Arbitration Council, 3 March 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · You Suonty, An Nan, Kong Phallack · Doc ID 139515

## 1. Facts
NagaWorld Limited employed 5,000 workers and was represented by senior human resources and legal staff; the claimant was the Khmer Workers' Labour Right Supports Union at NagaWorld, whose leaders were recognised by the department [Para 19] [Para 20]. The dispute arose after conciliation failed on five issues, with Issues 1 and 5 later withdrawn and Issues 2 and 3 merged [Para 12] [Para 15]. The remaining matters concerned dismissals, reinstatement, compensation, and strike wages [Para 16]. Four workers sought reinstatement and back pay: Ms U.P., Ms C.C., Ms S.S., and Mr C.S. [Para 24]. Four others sought statutory termination compensation rather than reinstatement: Mr J.P., Mr S.V., Ms T.S., and Mr C.K. [Para 25] [Para 27]. The strike claim concerned 963 workers who struck from 13 to 25 June 2013 [Para 137] [Para 394].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2&3) Whether the employer was required to reinstate Ms U.P., Ms C.C., Ms S.S., and Mr C.S. with back pay and compensation [Para 142] [Para 144]?
2. (Issue 2&3) Whether the dismissals of Mr J.P., Mr S.V., Ms T.S., and Mr C.K. gave rise to statutory termination compensation [Para 142] [Para 143]?
3. (Issue 2&3) Whether those dismissal claims constituted a collective dispute within the Arbitration Council's jurisdiction [Para 208] [Para 209]?
4. (Issue 4) Whether the employer had to maintain wages for 963 workers who participated in the strike from 13 to 25 June 2013 [Para 373] [Para 394]?

## 3. Positions of the parties
The union argued that the dismissals were discriminatory, linked to union activity or strike participation, and not in accordance with the Labour Law or internal rules [Para 3] [Para 4]. F. M. U.P., the union relied on a memorandum of understanding allowing four absences a year without penalty and argued that misconduct could not be accumulated beyond one calendar year [Para 32] [Para 33]. The employer said Ms U.P. had five unauthorised absences, progressively punished under the internal rules, culminating in dismissal [Para 29] [Para 31]. F. M. C.C., Ms S.S., and Mr C.S., the employer alleged serious misconduct by disclosing payslips and confidential wage information [Para 54] [Para 57]; the workers said wages were not confidential and they had not known of the memoranda [Para 56] [Para 67]. The employer disputed collective jurisdiction over several individual dismissals [Para 83] [Para 84], while the union relied on union membership and a petition signed by 624 workers [Para 89] [Para 220]. On strike pay, the workers asserted procedural compliance and employer replacement hiring [Para 138] [Para 383]; the employer relied on Article 332 of the Labour Law and said no wages were payable during strike [Para 138] [Para 374].

## 4. Award and reasoning
The panel treated rolling fixed-term contracts exceeding two years as undetermined-duration contracts under Article 67(2) of the Labour Law, relying also on Article 73(5) and ILO Recommendation 166 [Para 146] [Para 151] [Para 153]. Ms U.P.'s employment exceeded two years, so her contract was undetermined in duration [Para 154]. Her dismissal was unlawful because the internal rules did not expressly convert a fourth minor absence into medium misconduct or a fifth into serious misconduct, and the punishment was disproportionate under Article 27 [Para 170] [Para 172] [Para 178]. The termination letter also said "job abandonment," inconsistent with the hearing case [Para 179] [Para 182]. Reinstatement, back pay, compensation, and lawful punishment were ordered [Para 183] [Para 184].

The three payslip workers also had undetermined-duration contracts [Para 186] [Para 188]. The panel held that wage details belonged to each worker and that memoranda prohibiting sharing wages were invalid because the Labour Law contained no such confidentiality rule [Para 201] [Para 202]. The employer failed to prove specific damage to company interests, so dismissal lacked proper reason under Article 74 [Para 205] [Para 207]. Reinstatement and back pay followed [Para 207].

The panel found jurisdiction because the dismissals affected relations and could jeopardise social peace, with 624 workers supporting reinstatement claims [Para 209] [Para 222]. Mr J.P.'s dismissal was unsupported by Khmer-language evidence, contrary to Clause 23 of Prakas No. 099 [Para 248] [Para 250]. He received three months' notice compensation, six months' dismissal indemnity, annual-leave pay, damages, and outstanding wages [Para 275] [Para 286] [Para 300]. Mr S.V.'s alleged serious misconduct was unsubstantiated and dismissal disproportionate [Para 303] [Para 308]; he received two months' notice compensation, 75 days' indemnity, leave pay, damages, and outstanding wages [Para 320]. Ms T.S.'s request for tips violated Point 3(6) of Clause 10(6), so only unused annual leave and outstanding wages were due [Para 327] [Para 329] [Para 400]. Mr C.K.'s leave-record change was not counterfeiting but medium misconduct, and dismissal was disproportionate [Para 351] [Para 361]; compensation included two months' notice, 75 days' indemnity, leave pay, damages, and outstanding wages [Para 373].

Strike wages were denied. Article 332 suspends salary during strike, and replacement hiring triggers wages only where the strike complies with legal procedures [Para 374] [Para 386]. Although 113 workers were recruited during the strike, the workers did not show that peaceful methods were exhausted before striking [Para 383] [Para 392] [Para 393].

**Outcome:** partly granted: reinstatement with back pay and benefits was ordered for Ms U.P., Ms C.C., Ms S.S., and Mr C.S.; termination compensation was ordered for Mr J.P., Mr S.V., Ms T.S., and Mr C.K.; the strike-wage claim for the 963 workers was rejected; no final riel or USD totals are printed [Para 394] [Para 403].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-term contract becomes undetermined in duration where renewals or rolling terms exceed two years under Article 67(2) of the Labour Law [Para 147] [Para 154]. Dismissal requires a valid reason, proof by the alleging party, and punishment proportionate to misconduct under Articles 26, 27, and 74 [Para 172] [Para 207] [Para 307]. An employer rule declaring workers' own wage details confidential and non-shareable is invalid absent legal basis [Para 201] [Para 202]. Replacement hiring during strike creates wage liability only if the strike complied with statutory procedures [Para 383] [Para 386].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel cited ILO Recommendation 166 as a non-binding aid to interpreting Article 67 [Para 153]. Arbitrator You Suonty dissented, contending that security breaches, unapproved leave-record changes, and wage-data collection justified dismissal [Para 408] [Para 426] [Para 436].
