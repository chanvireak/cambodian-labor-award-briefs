# Award No. 178/14 — Soun Industry Men Hat Ten (Arbitration Council, 13 August 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Ang Eng Thong chair) · Doc ID 139631

## 1. Facts
Soun Industry Men Hat Ten, a garment factory at Ampil Leu, Kampong Siem, Kampong Cham, registered in 1999 and employing about 3,300 workers, ended the six-month contracts of three women on 30 April 2014. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 63] Two of them had worked there since 2006 and 2008 on rolling six-month contracts, the first with no written contract at all; both had been full-rights staff delegates whose terms had expired on 25 November 2013, and both had been suspended in January 2014 over collecting worker cards and a long strike, which the employer had taken to court. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 77][Para 83][Para 93] The third had one year of service under two six-month terms. [Para 96][Para 98]

## 2. Issues in dispute
The non-conciliation report left three issues; issues 2 and 3 were settled at the hearing, so the panel reasoned only on issue 1. [Para 2][Para 4][Para 5][Para 20][Para 21]
1. Whether the employer must take back and let work normally again the three women whose six-month contracts had ended — Ms C (ID 10-04249), Ms K (ID 1012) and Ms S (ID 11-00906)? [Para 63][Para 112]
2. Whether the employer must stop interfering in the work of the union federation? [Para 4]
3. Whether the employer must carry out the agreement of 4 June 2014 on continuing to deduct union dues and on handing over members' original documents for verification before 30 June 2014? [Para 5]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the two long-serving women were dismissed on 30 April 2014 although years of successive six-month contracts had made their employment indefinite; they committed no fault, and Ms S, a widow with a dependent child, lost her job and the employer then recruited two new hygiene workers. [Para 66][Para 100][Para 101][Para 102]
**Employer (respondent):** these were not dismissals but the ordinary expiry of six-month contracts; written notice was given two weeks in advance through the union president, who refused to accept it; the two are being sued in court over the card collection and the long strike, so the company awaits the judgment; and it will re-employ Ms S if it again needs hygiene staff. [Para 67][Para 73][Para 74][Para 89][Para 90][Para 104]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer is ordered to take back Ms C and Ms K and let them work normally again, while the claim to renew Ms S's contract is refused; because the parties signed the memorandum of 3 October 2012, the rights-dispute order binds them at once. [Para 145][Para 163][Para 164][Para 165][Para 166][Para 167]

The panel treated the two long-serving cases as dismissals, not expiries, through Articles 67(2) and 73(5) of the Labour Law. A fixed-duration contract may not exceed two years, including renewals, and where renewals carry the total beyond that limit the contract becomes one of indefinite duration; absent notice of termination, a fixed-duration contract continues for an equal term, or is deemed indefinite if the total exceeds the Article 67 limit. [Para 115][Para 116][Para 118][Para 119] It read Article 67 as deliberately favouring indefinite employment, since security of tenure serves workers and long service serves employers, and used ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982 only as non-binding context. [Para 121][Para 124][Para 126]

On the facts, Ms C had worked from 23 November 2006 to 30 April 2014 and Ms K from 2008 to the same date, both on successive six-month terms, so each total exceeded two years and each contract had become indefinite. [Para 128][Para 129][Para 131][Para 132] An indefinite contract may still be terminated under Article 74, but only by written notice and only for a reason related to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the operational needs of the enterprise; the employer offered nothing beyond the fact that it was awaiting the court's ruling on its own complaints, and the panel held that no valid reason had been shown, so the dismissals were without lawful basis and reinstatement followed under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 134][Para 137][Para 140][Para 142][Para 143][Para 145]

Ms S stood differently: she began on 1 May 2013, served one year over two six-month terms, and her last contract simply ran out, which the panel accepted as a genuine non-renewal that ended the relationship by its own terms, with renewal a matter for the parties' agreement. [Para 146][Para 148][Para 152][Para 154] Her assertion that two new hygiene workers were hired after her departure was unsupported: the burden lay on the complainant to show how many were hired, in which section and when, and she produced nothing. [Para 158][Para 161][Para 162]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive renewal of fixed-duration contracts so that total service exceeds two years converts the contract into one of indefinite duration under Articles 67(2) and 73(5), and the employer's ending of it is then a dismissal that requires a valid reason under Article 74 connected with the worker's aptitude, conduct or the operational needs of the enterprise; the employer's statement that the term merely expired, or that it is waiting for judgment in its own suit against the workers, is not such a reason and reinstatement follows, whereas a worker whose total service is under two years and whose last term genuinely expired has no right to renewal. [Para 119][Para 142][Para 154]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that Article 67 shows a policy preference for indefinite contracts because security of tenure benefits workers and long service benefits employers, and its use of non-binding ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982 as interpretive context, were not necessary to the orders. [Para 121][Para 124][Para 126]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award is dated 13 August 2014 [Para 1], consistent with the extension from 25 July to 13 August 2014 [Para 29], but the hearing is recorded as 24 July 2014 [Para 10][Para 18] after the workers asked to move the 15 July date to 22 July [Para 17], and the panel was constituted on 4 July 2014 [Para 14].
- The company appears as "Soun Industry Men Hat Ten", "Met Thek (Cambodia) Co., Ltd" and several Khmer variants, with registration number 3851 of 7 July 2011 in the evidence list [Para 30] but "Inv. 656 E/1999" of 22 October 1999 in the findings [Para 57].
- Dates for Ms K conflict: her last contract is said to run to 30 May 2014 [Para 82], the dismissal to 30 April 2014 [Para 84][Para 85], and the employer's notice to 31 May 2014 [Para 93].
- Prakas No. 099 is cited as "Article 4300" and the Prakas itself as "No. 4099" in the same passage [Para 109], and the number of workers affected appears as two in the conciliation report [Para 2] and three in the reasons [Para 63].
- Individual workers' names are legible only in places and are given here by initial; the chair's signature line is partly lost to OCR [Para 168].
