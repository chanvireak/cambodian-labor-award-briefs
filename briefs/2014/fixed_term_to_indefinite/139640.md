# Award No. 183/14 — San Long Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 29 July 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Yu Soun Ty (employer-appointed, dissenting), An Nan (worker-appointed), Aing Eng Tong (president) · Doc ID 139640

## 1. Facts
San Long is a garment-cutting company at Tuek Thla, Sen Sok, Phnom Penh, employing about 270 workers. [Para 24][Para 25] The Cambodia-ASEAN Trade Union Federation had not yet established a factory union and held rights transfers from only three workers, so the panel decided the case for those three alone. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 29] Mr P, a sewing-machine mechanic since November 2013 with no written contract and average earnings above USD 400 with overtime, was dismissed in June 2014 after refusing to work overtime on a Sunday. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 37][Para 76] The employer attended neither conciliation nor the hearing and sent no explanation, so the panel sat in its absence. [Para 16][Para 60][Para 68][Para 70]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr P, or failing that pay notice, dismissal, moral damages and unused annual leave? [Para 30][Para 31]
2. Whether the employer must apply overtime on a voluntary basis rather than compelling workers to stay? [Para 39]
3. Whether the employer must pay 2,000 riel of overtime meal allowance for work from 16:00 to 18:00? [Para 46]
4. Whether the employer must station a nurse and a doctor in the factory? [Para 158]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr P was dismissed without notice and without being told what he had done wrong, apparently because he refused one Sunday of overtime; the company routinely compels overtime, sometimes to 22:00 on weekdays and to 19:00 or 20:00 on Saturdays, and summons workers who decline to a meeting where they are warned of dismissal; meal money is withheld from those who work only two hours of overtime, and there is no infirmary, no doctor and no medicine during working hours. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 42][Para 44][Para 49][Para 53]
**Employer (respondent):** absent; the only position of record is its silence, which the panel treated as a waiver of its right to reply to the workers' assertions. [Para 78][Para 152][Para 168]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel declined reinstatement and instead ordered notice pay of 15 days' wages and benefits, dismissal damages of 7 days, unused annual leave, and moral damages equal to the dismissal damages; it also ordered voluntary overtime, the 2,000 riel meal allowance for overtime from 16:00, and one nurse plus a doctor or mid-level doctor present at least two hours a day. [Para 175][Para 176][Para 177][Para 178][Para 181]

On the contract, Art 67(7) makes a fixed-term contract that is not in writing an unfixed-term contract, and Mr P had none, so his engagement was indefinite. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 80] Under Art 74 such a contract may be ended at will only for a reason concerning the worker's capacity or conduct or the needs of the enterprise, and refusing overtime cannot supply that reason because Art 4 of Prakas 80 makes overtime voluntary and forbids compulsion or discipline for refusal. [Para 81][Para 83][Para 90][Para 91][Para 92] The dismissal was therefore without valid cause, following awards 118/07, 107/08 and 86/11 and the Sumi and Yong Sing awards. [Para 135][Para 136][Para 137]

Because the employer had ignored both conciliation and the hearing, the working relationship was no longer harmonious, and the panel held the compensation option better for the worker than reinstatement. [Para 99][Para 100][Para 101] It then fixed the elements: 15 days of notice pay under Arts 75 and 77 for seven months' service, 7 days of dismissal damages under Art 89 for that same seniority, annual leave under Art 167 for leave not taken because he served under a year, and moral damages under Art 91 equal to the dismissal damages, following award 199/12. [Para 102][Para 106][Para 110][Para 117][Para 123]

On overtime the panel ordered the employer to stop summoning those who decline to a meeting at which dismissal is threatened, since that practice compels overtime by fear. [Para 145][Para 146][Para 147] On meal money, Art 2 of Notification 041/11 of 7 March 2011 requires 2,000 riel a day or one free meal for voluntary overtime without limiting the hours, so withholding it from workers who do only two hours was wrong. [Para 148][Para 149][Para 155][Para 156][Para 157]

On medical staffing, Art 239 requires a workplace medical service and Art 3 of Prakas 330 of 6 December 2000 fixes staffing by headcount: for 50 to 300 workers, one full-time nurse and a doctor present at least two hours a day. [Para 158][Para 159][Para 160][Para 173][Para 174]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Refusing overtime cannot be a lawful ground for dismissal, because overtime is voluntary by law and an employer may not compel it or discipline those who decline; and where the employer has abandoned conciliation and the hearing, the panel will award termination payments rather than order reinstatement into a relationship that is no longer workable. [Para 91][Para 92][Para 101]

**Obiter dicta:** the employer-appointed arbitrator's dissent, that a demand concerning a single worker fails the first condition of a collective dispute under Art 302 and so falls outside the panel's jurisdiction, which if accepted would have left issue 1 undecided. [Para 195][Para 198]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award carries a dissenting opinion by the employer-appointed arbitrator denying jurisdiction over issue 1, so the compensation order rests on two of three arbitrators. [Para 186][Para 198] The worker's name is printed as Phal Sarath in some passages and in variant forms elsewhere, and one line refers to 530 claimants where the panel had found three. [Para 27][Para 90] The meal-allowance demand is printed as "2,000 riel 400 riel" and again as "20,000 riel" in a garbled line. [Para 46] The award cites "Article 2398" of the Labour Law where Art 239 is meant, and the operative order on medical staff reads "01 9 doctors". [Para 170][Para 181] The demand list is numbered with paired figures (1, 2, 3, then "4(6)"), and the first demand asks for "20 riel a day" as a wage. [Para 5] The award date is 29 July 2014 in the reasons and on the face, while the registry letter inviting the panel is dated 7 July 2014. [Para 199][Para 20]
