# Award No. 014/14 — Manhattan Textile and Garment Corp (Arbitration Council, 18 February 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Hem Huor Naryth, appointed by the employer party; An Nan, appointed by the worker party; Men Nimmith, chair) · non-binding award between parties to the MoU of 3 October 2012 · Doc ID 139506

## 1. Facts
Manhattan Textile and Garment Corp, a garment factory at Ampil Leu, Kampong Siem, Kampong Cham, employs about 3,500 workers, and the local union of the Cambodian Alliance of Trade Union (CATU), registered by certificate dated 18 September 2012, is the claimant. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24] The workers struck from 24 December 2013 to 6 January 2014; from 2 January 2014 the employer deducted daily wages and a USD 10 attendance bonus, and on 6 January 2014 it suspended the contracts of four local union leaders — Mr C Theang, Mr P Chanthy, Ms K Oun and Mr Ph Phalla — paying 50% of wages while a criminal complaint it had filed on 13 January 2014 was pending. [Para 31][Para 46][Para 30][Para 29]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate the four local union leaders whose employment contracts it suspended on 6 January 2014? [Para 25][Para 57]
2. Whether the employer must withdraw the criminal petition it filed against those four leaders in the Kampong Cham court? [Para 46][Para 87]
3. Whether the employer must maintain wages for the strike period from 2 January 2014 to 6 January 2014? [Para 46][Para 100]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the suspension was imposed without the Labour Inspector's authorisation and without prior notice, on no valid ground; the four were singled out although about 90% of the workforce struck; the strike was nationwide and aimed at an adequate wage, other Phnom Penh enterprises did not deduct wages, and the employer hired replacements. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 46][Para 49]
**Employer (respondent):** the four were not dismissed but suspended because they ignored the Royal Government's notification to return on 2 January 2014, collected other workers' identity and time cards, threatened and incited non-strikers, and the suspension was needed to prevent a violent strike; it recruited workers daily, before the strike, not as replacements. [Para 30][Para 33][Para 36][Para 50]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand for reinstatement was rejected and the demand to withdraw the petition was declined for want of jurisdiction, but the employer was ordered to pay full wages to the four workers during the work suspension while the court investigation and judgment are awaited. The wages claim for the strike was rejected. [Para 108][Para 112][Para 113]

On issue 1 the panel classified the demand as a rights dispute because it concerns suspension as provided by the Labour Law. [Para 55][Para 56] Article 71 lists exhaustively the eleven grounds of suspension, and the panel, following Award No. 28/04 (Raffle Grand D'Angkor Hotel) and Award No. 18/04 (Sunway Hotel), held that suspension pending a criminal investigation is available even though not listed, being an exercise of the management right recognised under Article 2 in Awards 62/06, 108/06, 33/07 and 119/09 — but that wages must still be paid every month until the court decides. [Para 57][Para 66][Para 67][Para 69] Award No. 146/11 (Heuy Cheun), where reinstatement was ordered, was distinguished because that suspension was of undetermined duration and unsupported by any police report, whereas here the employer undertook to reinstate once the court ruled. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 77] Given Article 38 of the Constitution, the four are presumed innocent until final conviction, so their wages may not be deducted during the suspension. [Para 78][Para 81][Para 82]

On issue 2 the panel treated the demand as a rights dispute but outside its jurisdiction: Article 302 confines collective disputes to working conditions, the exercise of recognised union rights and recognition of professional organisations, whereas the demand concerns a party's right to petition a court, which it declined to consider. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 89][Para 90]

On issue 3 the panel applied Articles 332(1) and 72(1) and its consistent line in Awards 49/05, 27/10, 91/10, 118/10 and 133/13 that strikers are unpaid whether the strike is legal or not. [Para 96][Para 98] Article 334 obliges payment only where replacements were recruited, and following Award No. 243/13 (Bodykids Fashion Wear) the burden of proving recruitment lay on the workers, who filed no evidence at all. [Para 101][Para 102][Para 105][Para 107]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may suspend a contract while a criminal complaint against the worker is investigated, but the suspension does not suspend the duty to pay: full wages continue until the court rules, because the worker is presumed innocent under Article 38 of the Constitution and Article 71 does not authorise unpaid suspension on that ground. A demand that the employer withdraw a court petition is not a collective labour dispute under Article 302 and is outside the panel's jurisdiction. Strikers' wages are owed only where the workers prove that replacements were recruited, and the burden of that proof is theirs. [Para 69][Para 81][Para 90][Para 105]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that suspension of a fixed duration is inherently more appropriate than an open-ended one, and that the employer had conceded the grounds were absent from its internal work rules, were not necessary once the payment order was made. [Para 65][Para 77]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- This is an express unofficial English translation; the award states that the documents, exhibits and witness evidence considered were omitted from the English version, so the evidentiary basis cannot be checked. [Para 1][Para 21][Para 22]
- The panel is named "Hem Hour Naryth" on the face of the award and "Hem Huor Naryth" at the signature block. [Para 1][Para 113]
- Worker names vary: "Chorn Theang" and "Chorn Teang"; "Phan Channy" in the registration certificate but "Phan Chanthy" elsewhere; the certificate names Sok Nary as secretary while Ms K Oun is called secretary and deputy-secretary in different passages. [Para 24][Para 46]
- Dates are unreliable and sometimes impossible: the injunction request is dated "13 April 2014" although the events precede February 2014; the petition is dated "13 January 204"; the evidence submission and objection dates are given as 3 and 6 January 2014, before the 29 January 2014 hearing; the Prakas on the appointment of arbitrators is dated 17 June 2014 though the award is dated 18 February 2014. [Para 14][Para 21][Para 43][Para 46]
- The award states the strike ran from 24 December 2013 to 6 January 2014 while the disputed wage period is given as 2 to 6 January 2014, with no explanation of the intervening days. [Para 31][Para 46]
- The Government notification ordering strikers back to work on 2 January 2014 was never produced, and the employer admitted it had not submitted it; the panel relied on the assertion nonetheless. [Para 33]
- One representative's name is truncated to "Mr. V" and the party list runs across broken line breaks throughout. [Para 10]
