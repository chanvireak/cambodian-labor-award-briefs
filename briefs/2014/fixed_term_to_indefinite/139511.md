# Award No. 020/14 — Yen Yu In Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 6 March 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3 — You Soun Thy (employer-appointed), An Vireak (worker-appointed), Sok Mathoeung (president) · Doc ID 139511

## 1. Facts
Yen Yu In Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (យេន យូអ៊ិន ហ្គាមេន), a garment factory with about 435 workers, shut its doors from 26 December 2013 during a nationwide strike wave in the Phnom Penh industrial zone and sent workers home, telling them to return on 30 December 2013. It paid full wages for 26 December and 2 January, and 50 percent for 27, 28, 30 and 31 December 2013 and 3 January 2014. Workers demanded the missing 50 percent for those five days. [Para 2][Para 40][Para 64][Para 68][Para 95]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay an additional 50 percent on top of the 50 percent already paid for 27, 28, 31 December 2013 and 3 January 2014, when it told all workers not to come to work? [Para 122]
2. Whether the employer must pay an additional 50 percent for 30 December 2013, when some workers did come to the factory and were sent home? [Para 134]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** they did not strike; the employer alone decided to send them home and paid only 50 percent without agreement; neighbouring factories paid 100 percent on the same days. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 105][Para 106]
**Employer (respondent):** workers did not work on those days; the shutdown was forced by violent strikes nearby, not by any wish to harm them; it consulted group leaders and union representatives and paid 50 percent out of goodwill, above what the law requires. [Para 58][Para 109][Para 112][Para 119]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel dismissed the claim for the four days 27, 28 and 31 December 2013 and 3 January 2014, and ordered the employer to pay an additional 50 percent of wages on top of the 50 percent already paid for 30 December 2013, to the claimant workers who came to the factory that day and were sent home. No total sum was fixed. [Para 142][Para 143]

Before the merits, the panel examined who could claim. The Khmer Workers' Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ ខ្មែរ) did not appear and had filed no transfer of rights, so it was not a party. [Para 43][Para 44] The Khmer Free Workers' Union at the factory had not yet received its registration certificate from the Ministry, so it was not yet a lawful professional organisation and its three elected leaders had no standing to represent members. [Para 19][Para 50][Para 52] The panel also found the thumbprint "transfer of rights" letters undated and silent on who received the authority, so they were insufficient. [Para 46][Para 47] Only the eight workers physically present — three elected leaders and five members — were complainants, and the award runs to them alone. [Para 54][Para 55]

On the four days, the panel applied Articles 71(6) and 72 of the 1997 Labour Law: a contract is suspended by absences the employer lawfully permits, and suspension removes the employer's duty to pay wages. [Para 122][Para 123] It found the workers had gone home on 26 December on the employer's instruction, had stayed away each time they were told, had accepted the 50 percent actually paid, and had never objected or protested. [Para 127][Para 128] It concluded the suspensions were agreed and that 50 percent was already a payment above the statutory requirement, so no further sum was due. [Para 129][Para 130][Para 131][Para 132]

On 30 December the facts differed: a small number of workers did return, the employer had not shown that group leaders told every worker to stay away, and those who came had not agreed in advance to a 50 percent suspension. [Para 85][Para 135][Para 137][Para 138] The panel therefore held the suspension was not agreed for that day and full wages were owed, ordering the outstanding 50 percent paid to the claimants who had reported for work. [Para 139][Para 140][Para 141]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
When an employer closes the factory and pays half wages, the shortfall is recoverable only for a day on which workers actually presented themselves for work without prior notice that they should stay away; for days on which they accepted the instruction to remain absent and took the half wage without objection, Article 72 leaves no residual claim. [Para 128][Para 130][Para 139]

**Obiter dicta:** A factory-level union without a registration certificate is not yet a lawful professional organisation, and its elected leaders cannot represent members before the panel; nor can undated thumbprint letters establish a transfer of rights. [Para 19][Para 47]
