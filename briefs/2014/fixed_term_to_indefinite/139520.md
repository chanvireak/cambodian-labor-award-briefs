# Award No. 035/14 — Kin Tay Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 18 March 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Pen Bunchea (Chair), Liv Sovannara (worker party), Kav Thach (employer party) · Doc ID 139520

## 1. Facts
Kin Tay Garment Co., Ltd, a garment factory at Chrok Angre Krom, Meanchey district, Phnom Penh, employs about 600 workers [Para 45]. Its union held a registration receipt dated 19 December 2013 from the Ministry of Labour [Para 46]. A first-term leadership election was held on 1 December 2013, and the federation notified the employer of the three elected leaders by letter No. 118/13 of 3 December 2013, received by the company's security guard on 12 December 2013 [Para 95][Para 96]. The company ended the union president's employment on 10 January 2014, eleven days before the dispute was filed, and the union demanded her reinstatement with back pay [Para 83][Para 12]. Conciliation on 7 February 2014 failed on both demands, and the panel sat on 25 February 2014 [Para 13][Para 11].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back Ms C, the factory union president, into normal employment and pay her wages from the date of dismissal to the date of return? [Para 19][Para 51]

The second limb of that demand, concerning Ms U, and the whole of demand 2 — that the company cease threatening and insulting union leaders and activists — were settled by conciliation at the hearing and were not decided [Para 18][Para 19].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Ms C was dismissed because she took part in the strike of 24 December 2013 calling for a US$160 wage, and because she was the elected union president; the company insulted her before the workforce on 30 December 2013, called her in on 10 January 2014 and told her the contract had expired, and never renewed it [Para 86][Para 88][Para 90]. Her contract had in any event become one of undetermined duration, so the dismissal was unlawful [Para 92]. **Employer (respondent):** there was no dismissal at all — her two-month fixed-duration contract simply ran out on 10 January 2014, the company's practice being to issue successive two-month contracts and pay five per cent compensation plus unused annual leave at each expiry, and whether to renew depended on the worker's performance [Para 69][Para 70][Para 84]. It denied threatening or discriminating against union leaders and asked for proof [Para 6].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer must take Ms C back into normal employment and pay her wages from the date of dismissal to the date of her return; the award on this rights dispute is binding at once under the memorandum of understanding of 3 October 2012 [Para 178][Para 180].

*The union.* Under Article 268(2) the Ministry's silence for two months after receipt of the registration file deems the union registered; more than two months had run from 19 December 2013 to the hearing, and no official letter proved the alleged suspension of registrations, so the union was a lawful professional organisation [Para 102][Para 104].

*Contract type.* Article 67(1) and (7) require a fixed-duration contract to be in writing and to state a precise end date, failing which it becomes a contract of undetermined duration [Para 125][Para 127]. The employer produced only four "reports on leaving employment" and four two-month payslips; the panel held these were not written contracts at all, since they stated no precise termination date and no terms creating the employment relationship [Para 135][Para 136]. Her contract was therefore of undetermined duration, and the employer ended it while such a contract was running [Para 139][Para 140].

*Protected status.* Article 293 forbids dismissing a staff delegate or a candidate for that office without the Labour Inspector's agreement, and point 3 of Article 3 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 extends that protection to every candidate in a union leadership election for 45 days before and 45 days after the poll [Para 141][Para 145]. The panel applied its Award 40/10-Meng Yan, which holds the protection to run at least 45 days after the election [Para 151][Para 153]. Ms C was the elected president, the election was on 1 December 2013, the dismissal fell within the protected window, and the employer had been told of her candidacy through the security guard, which it admitted [Para 158][Para 160][Para 167].

*The consequence.* Because the employer never sought the Labour Inspector's prior authorisation, the dismissal is void under point 4 of Article 4 of the same Prakas, and Praka 099 Article 34 empowers the panel to order reinstatement and payment [Para 175][Para 176][Para 177].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union leadership candidate, and the office-holder elected from that candidacy, is protected from dismissal for 45 days before and after the election; a dismissal inside that window without the Labour Inspector's prior authorisation is void, not merely compensable, and the panel will order reinstatement with wages for the whole period out of work. Because the protection attaches to candidacy, notice of the candidacy given to the employer — even by delivery to a gate security guard that the employer admits receiving — is enough to trigger it. And an employer cannot establish a fixed-duration contract by producing payslips or departure reports: without a written instrument naming a precise end date, the contract is of undetermined duration, so ending it engages Article 74 rather than simple expiry [Para 127][Para 176][Para 168].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the 45-day post-election protection need not be the outer limit, and that protection may reasonably continue until the union's registration is filed, an extension it derived from Award 40/10-Meng Yan but did not need to apply here [Para 151].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The panel finds that the four departure reports "are not the employment contract" [Para 135], yet earlier records that both parties accepted that those same documents were the employment contract [Para 81]; the two findings contradict each other.
- The dismissal date is 10 January 2014 in the findings and in the reasoning [Para 83][Para 160] but 1 January 2014 in the protection analysis, and the interval after the poll is stated as 41 days, which matches neither date [Para 162][Para 163].
- Ms C's start date is 4 November 2011 according to the workers, 9 May 2013 according to the employer, and 5 January 2012 on the panel's own reading of the payslips — a third date neither party advanced [Para 54][Para 57][Para 61].
- The second worker's name appears as Uy Sok Leng in the demand and Uy Sok Eng in the employer's evidence list [Para 2][Para 27].
- The company's name is given as Kin Tay Garment Co., Ltd in one place and Kin Tay Gament in another, and the panel formation date reads "18 February 2019 2014" [Para 28][Para 15].
