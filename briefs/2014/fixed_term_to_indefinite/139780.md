# Award No. not printed (case No. 333/14) — Tru Tham Phnom Penh (Arbitration Council, 26 December 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ri for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Run Saray chair) · Doc ID 139780

## 1. Facts
Tru Tham Phnom Penh, an enterprise whose main activity is photo cropping and editing (កាត់តរូបភាព), employed 235 workers. [Para 33][Para 34] On 9 October 2014 about 120 workers struck to demand the removal of one company manager; of 19 demands taken to conciliation, 2 remained unsettled and were referred to the Council. [Para 11][Para 36] The strikers returned to work after the Council ordered them back on 17 October 2014, and were then left out of a USD 20 wage increase paid to workers who had not struck. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 46] The disputed acts are the withholding of wages and benefits for the strike period and the withholding of that increase. [Para 35][Para 65]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must provide wages and other benefits to the workers who struck between 9 October and 17 November 2014 on the same terms as workers who did not strike? [Para 35]
2. Whether the failure to add USD 20 to the wages of the workers who struck, by reason of their participation in the strike, was lawful? [Para 65]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the strike followed the employer's use of security guards to expel three workers without notice, warning letter or clear reason, and the employer then recruited new workers to replace strikers contrary to Article 224; the USD 20 increase was announced only orally, so it reached them as rumour and they did not trust it. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 51]
**Employer (respondent):** the Labour Law imposes no duty to pay wages during a strike — whoever works is paid and whoever does not work is not; the USD 20 was conditional on returning within three days of 10 October 2014, the strikers did not return in time, so they forfeited it. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 48][Para 49]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim for wages and benefits during the strike was dismissed, and on the second issue the panel ordered the employer to add USD 20 to the wages of the workers who struck, counted from the day each of them agreed to return to work. [Para 79][Para 80]

On the first issue the panel set out Article 324 (prior notice of a strike), Article 320(a) paragraph 4 (exhaustion of peaceful settlement) and Article 332 (the employment contract is suspended during a strike). [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54] It found the strike procedurally unlawful: the workers kept striking while case No. 288/14 was before the panel and after order No. 052/14 of 16 October 2014 required them back on 17 October 2014, gave no prior notice, and had not exhausted conciliation. [Para 59] Following award No. 110/07 and award No. 49/05, and expressly agreeing with them, it held that a contract is suspended during a strike and no wage is payable for those days, whether the strike conforms to the law or not. [Para 57][Para 61][Para 62][Para 63]

On the second issue the panel applied Article 333, which forbids an employer to impose any sanction on workers for taking part in a strike and makes such sanctions void. [Para 65][Para 66] It found the USD 20 had been granted to non-strikers and withheld from strikers, and that the increase attaches to the employment relationship for its duration, so withholding it keeps depressing the worker's income long after the return to work — a sanction operating after the strike, not a mere refusal to pay during it. [Para 73][Para 74] The employer's stated reason, participation in the strike, was not a proper ground: the employer may take whatever measures the Labour Law allows against an unlawful strike, but may not use the strike as its reason for refusing an increase. [Para 73][Para 77]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A general wage increase is a term of the employment relationship, not payment for work on a given day. An employer that gives the increase to workers who did not strike and withholds it from those who did imposes a sanction prohibited by Article 333, and owes the increase from the date each striker returned to work, even where the strike itself was procedurally unlawful and even where the employer could lawfully have disciplined the strikers. Conversely, Article 332 suspends the contract for the duration of a strike, so no wage or benefit is owed for the strike days themselves, and that holds whether the strike was lawful or not. [Para 63][Para 74][Para 77]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's finding that the new recruitment was unconnected to the strike — the new hires were for 30 additional computers and the announcement dated from September 2014 — answered the workers' replacement-labour complaint under Article 224, although replacement labour was not one of the two issues submitted. [Para 41][Para 44][Para 45]

- The end of the strike is printed three ways: 17 November 2014 in the issue and in the workers' claim [Para 35][Para 46], 16 October 2014 as the return date both parties accepted [Para 50], and 17 October 2014 as the return date under order 052/14 [Para 38][Para 59].
- The signature block is badly corrupted — a digit string precedes Ouk Ri's name and the chair's signature line reads "mansua manu @-@". [Para 83]
- The workers' Article 224 complaint about replacement labour is recited but never ruled on. [Para 41]
