# Award No. 082/14 — Sun Oun Industrial Men Hat Ten (Arbitration Council, 9 May 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Kong Phalha, chair, chosen by both) · rights dispute, binding award · Doc ID 139551

## 1. Facts
Sun Oun Industrial Men Hat Ten cuts and sews garments at Ampil Leu, Kampong Siem, Kampong Cham, employing about 3,000 workers, some 95% of them women. A collective dispute over five working conditions was conciliated on 26 March 2014 without settlement, and the four unresolved points came to the panel. Behind it lay an agreement of 16 October 2013 in which the company promised a canteen and storage space, safety footwear, payday on the 8th, rubbish bins and drinking water. The union says those points were never performed, that three union leaders were suspended from 6 January 2014 pending a court case and then not renewed, and that there is still no daycare centre. [Para 1][Para 12][Para 52][Para 54][Para 61][Para 154]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must carry out points 2, 3, 4, 10 and 12 of the agreement of 16 October 2013? [Para 61]
2. Whether the company must take back Ms P, Mr P and Ms T, whose fixed-term contracts it did not renew? [Para 89]
3. Whether the company must establish a daycare centre (ទារកដ្ឋាន) for the children of its workers? [Para 154]
4. Whether the company must allow the leaders of the Kampuchea union at the factory to enter the premises to represent the workers? [Para 169]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The agreement was signed and binding, and the employer showed neither the will nor the care to perform it; the three named workers had served beyond two years and so held undetermined-duration contracts, and the suspension pending a court case was a device to end their employment without a valid reason and because of their union activity. There is no daycare near the factory and a childminder costs up to USD 40 a month; suspended union leaders must still be let in, since no law bars them. [Para 72][Para 108][Para 129][Para 147][Para 161][Para 173]
**Employer (respondent):** Points of the agreement had already been carried out and the rest would be finished by the end of May 2014, payday from 8 June 2014; the delay was caused by the change of general director on 20 November 2013, by losses during the strike of 24 December 2013 to 8 January 2014, and by the rise in the minimum wage from USD 80 to USD 100. The contracts were six-month fixed-term ones that simply expired, and the provincial labour department had said no inspector authorisation was needed for a non-renewal; the USD 7 monthly milk allowance stood in place of a daycare centre. [Para 79][Para 82][Para 84][Para 141][Para 165]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted in full — the company must complete points 2, 3, 4, 10 and 12 of the 16 October 2013 agreement by 31 May 2014 at the latest; take back Ms P, Mr P and Ms T to work; set up a daycare centre, and where it cannot take children over 18 months, allow mothers to place them in an outside centre with the company paying the actual invoices; and admit Mr C, Ms P and Ms K as workers' representatives. The claim for Mr T had already been settled by the parties at the hearing. [Para 223][Para 282][Para 290][Para 308][Para 319][Para 321]

On issue 1 the panel first classified the claim: because its content is written in an agreement between the parties it is a rights dispute, decided according to law and not by equity. Civil Code Articles 311, 336 and 337 make the agreement legally binding on the party that made it, so the company's duty was to perform, and neither the new general director, the strike losses nor the minimum wage increase excused non-performance; the workers had to accept a reasonable period, which the panel fixed as 9 to 31 May 2014. [Para 199][Para 200][Para 206][Para 212][Para 222]

On issue 2 the panel applied Article 67(2): successive fixed-term contracts whose total duration exceeds two years convert into an undetermined-duration contract. Ms P's identity cards, uncontested, put her first day at 1 June 2009 and her last contract ran to 31 January 2014, so her contract was already of undetermined duration and its ending was a dismissal, not a non-renewal. Because she was deputy president of the union, Article 293 and Article 4(2) of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 required the labour inspector's prior authorisation, which was never sought; under Article 4(4) of that Prakas the dismissal is void, and she was reinstated. [Para 240][Para 245][Para 262][Para 274][Para 280]

Mr P (first day 2 May 2008) and Ms T (28 January 2012) also held undetermined-duration contracts on the same arithmetic. Article 74 allows such a contract to be ended unilaterally only on notice and for a reason relating to the worker's capacity or conduct, or to the needs of the enterprise. The company proved no reason and put in no evidence, merely asserting expiry, so the two were dismissed without a valid reason and were reinstated. [Para 250][Para 259][Para 286][Para 289][Para 290]

On issue 3 Article 186 requires every enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a nursing room and a daycare centre in or near the premises. The panel found the threshold plainly met and held that the USD 7 monthly milk allowance, though agreed with the union in 2006 and paid for years, was not equivalent to the statutory duty and could not release the employer from it; Article 13 makes the Labour Law's rules ones of public order, so a contrary agreement is void. [Para 297][Para 301][Para 304][Para 306]

On issue 4 Article 3(5) of Prakas No. 305 provides that union leaders who are suspended or dismissed for economic or other reasons keep the right to enter the enterprise to carry out their union mandate. Suspension was therefore no answer, and the three leaders were to be admitted. [Para 310][Para 314][Para 318]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive fixed-term contracts whose aggregate duration passes two years become an undetermined-duration contract whatever severance was paid at each renewal, so ending them is a dismissal requiring a valid reason under Article 74; the dismissal of a protected union leader without the labour inspector's prior authorisation is void and the leader must be reinstated; and a contractual cash allowance such as milk money does not discharge the Article 186 duty to provide a daycare centre. [Para 239][Para 274][Para 286][Para 306]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remark that ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982, though not binding, is useful context for reading Article 67; its observation that an employer may choose to pay the invoices of an outside daycare centre for children over 18 months; and its note that a rights dispute decided under the 3 October 2012 memorandum becomes final and enforceable at once. [Para 238][Para 307][Para 219]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel is said to have been constituted on "02 April 2019 2014" (para 18), and Prakas No. 099 is printed as "0993" and dated 2019 for 2004 in two places (paras 187, 281); the operative paragraph dates the agreement "16 October 2019 2013" (para 320). The second claimant's name appears as Than Seang Hour, Khun Sean Hur and Than Seang Hor, and the first as Phan Channy, Chan Channy and Phan Chanthy; the company as Sun Oun, So Oun and Son Oun. The USD 80 to USD 100 minimum wage figures come only from the employer's account. The file also carries a dissenting opinion by arbitrator Ing Sothy (paras 324–344), who would have refused reinstatement on issue 2 because the 5% severance had been paid at each renewal; the majority's reasoning stands against it. [Para 18][Para 122][Para 187][Para 320][Para 324][Para 328]
