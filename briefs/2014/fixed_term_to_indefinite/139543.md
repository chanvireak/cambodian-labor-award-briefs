# Award No. 067/14 — Har Ta Pak Khe Jing In Das Stri (Cambodia) Limited (Arbitration Council, 30 May 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed, dissenting in part; Tuon Si Phan, worker-appointed; Tang Tri, president) · Doc ID 139543

## 1. Facts
Har Ta Pak Khe Jing In Das Stri (Cambodia) Limited makes cartons and employs about 500 workers. [Para 106][Para 107] On 13 February 2014, after the company suspended the contracts of two union officials, 285 workers struck outside the factory; on 18 February the Phnom Penh Municipal Court issued injunction No. 02 "D" ordering a return to work within 48 hours. [Para 118][Para 120][Para 121] The workers came back on 19 February, but the company refused entry to two of their representatives, chaos followed, and the company announced that anyone not working should leave; the workers left the compound. [Para 124][Para 185][Para 188] On 25 February the company dismissed all 285 for serious misconduct — failure to obey the injunction and violence during the strike. [Para 142]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the 206 workers dismissed on 25 February 2014 with their contracts, wages and benefits intact, preserve the terms of the 54 to 59 workers re-hired on new contracts, and reinstate the three suspended representatives? [Para 109][Para 115][Para 180]
2. Whether the employer must pay back wages and benefits — attendance bonus, travel and accommodation money, seniority bonus and annual leave — for the period from 19 February 2014 until the award? [Para 172][Para 309]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** They returned to work on 19 February as the injunction required, but the company shut them out and drove them off the premises, so the dismissal was the company's doing; they do not accept it and are still protesting. [Para 173][Para 174][Para 175]
**Employer (respondent):** It cannot take back workers who used violence, closed the factory gates, disregarded non-strikers and refused to obey the court's injunction; the dismissals were lawful and final wages and leave pay have already been settled. [Para 3][Para 145][Para 146]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement refused, but the employer ordered to pay compensation: five per cent of wages as end-of-contract indemnity plus moral damages for the 206 fixed-duration workers, dismissal indemnity and notice compensation for those on indefinite contracts, and reinstatement with back pay for the three suspended representatives. The back-pay claim was declined. [Para 208][Para 247][Para 299][Para 308][Para 310][Para 311][Para 316]

The panel first established the facts from video evidence neither side contested: the workers re-entered the factory on 19 February; the company barred the two representatives; both shifts were called in at once, making work impossible; the company announced "if you don't work, leave"; the workers left and continued to demand reinstatement of all, including the two representatives. [Para 134][Para 135][Para 137][Para 185] Once the workers were inside, it was the employer's duty to organise their return in line with the injunction. [Para 187] The panel therefore rejected the employer's account that the workers had defied the injunction, and found that the employer contributed to the disorder. [Para 189][Para 191] Consequently the dismissals were unlawful and could not rest on serious misconduct under Article 83. [Para 142][Para 223]

Reinstatement was nonetheless refused, and the panel split the 206 by contract type. For fixed-duration contracts, Article 73 allows termination before expiry only for serious misconduct or by mutual consent, so the employer owed the statutory end-of-contract indemnity, which the panel fixed at the five per cent minimum under Article 73 where no collective agreement provides otherwise. [Para 180][Para 193][Para 195][Para 208] For indefinite contracts, Article 74 permits dismissal on reasonable grounds, but the panel found none, so Articles 89 and 77 required dismissal indemnity scaled to seniority and compensation in lieu of notice. [Para 209][Para 212][Para 220][Para 240][Para 247] Moral damages under Article 91 were refused on the ground that the workers had contributed to their own dismissal. [Para 230][Para 231][Para 232]

The 54 to 59 workers re-hired after 25 February had signed new fixed-duration contracts; under Articles 65 and 311 of the Civil Code a contract needs offer and acceptance, and no party may force another to accept terms it has not agreed. Their demand to restore the old terms was therefore refused. [Para 248][Para 258][Para 268][Para 275]

For the three suspended representatives — Mr S, Mr C and Mr Y — Article 293 requires the labour inspector's authorisation before dismissal, and Article 295 makes a suspension void if authorisation is refused. [Para 276][Para 287] The inspector had expressly refused to authorise Mr S's dismissal and ordered his reinstatement with back wages; for the other two, no response came within one month, which the panel treated as a deemed refusal. [Para 160][Para 297][Para 301][Para 306] Reinstatement with wages for the suspension period was ordered. [Para 299][Para 308]

On issue 2 the panel found the wage and benefit claim for 19 February onwards had already been dealt with in issue 1 and declined to consider it again. [Para 309][Para 310]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A dismissal is unlawful where the employer, having obstructed a court-ordered return to work and provoked the disorder that followed, relies on that same disorder as serious misconduct; the remedy is compensation measured by contract type — five per cent end-of-contract indemnity for fixed-duration workers, dismissal indemnity and notice pay for indefinite-contract workers — not reinstatement. [Para 189][Para 195][Para 240][Para 247] And a union leader may be dismissed only with the labour inspector's authorisation; if the inspector does not answer within one month the request is deemed refused, the suspension is void, and reinstatement with back pay follows. [Para 290][Para 301][Para 306][Para 308]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's refusal of moral damages under Article 91 because the workers shared responsibility, and its discussion of offer and acceptance in the Civil Code as the basis for refusing to rewrite the new contracts, were observations beyond the orders actually made. [Para 231][Para 258]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award orders moral damages for the 206 fixed-duration workers while its own reasoning holds that Article 91 damages are not owed because the workers contributed to the dismissal; both passages are reported as printed. [Para 208][Para 232][Para 311] The number of dismissed workers is 285 at the dismissal but 206 in the claims, with 79 said to have returned. [Para 110][Para 111][Para 142][Para 180] The start of the back-pay period appears as 19 February in the claim and 12 February in the decision. [Para 172][Para 309] The panel date of constitution reads "ថ្ងៃទី ១៩ ខែមីនា ឆ្នាំ ២០២២ ឆ្នាំ ២០១៩ ២០១៤" [Para 17], and the company is printed as "ហារុតា", "ហាវតា" and "ហារ តា" in different places [Para 85][Para 121][Para 106].
