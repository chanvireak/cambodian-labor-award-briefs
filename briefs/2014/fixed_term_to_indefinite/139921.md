# Award No. 271/13-D & L — D & L Alty Met (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 22 January 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Liv Sovanna, Pen Bunhea presiding) · Doc ID 139921

## 1. Facts
D & L Alty Met (Cambodia) Co., Ltd runs a garment factory that began operating on 1 December 2012 and employed about 248 workers [Para 45]. The Khmer Union Federation and the Khmer Union of D & L factory brought a seven-point demand on 25 November 2013; conciliation failed on all seven, and six (points 2 to 7) were settled at the hearing, leaving only point 1 [Para 20][Para 21]. That point concerned two union leaders: Mr S, an electrician and deputy president of the factory union, engaged from 5 February 2013 on a two-month probation followed by successive two-month fixed-duration contracts and dismissed on 22 November 2013 [Para 49][Para 53][Para 51]; and Mr B, a mechanic and union secretary, engaged from 22 January 2013 on the same pattern and treated by the employer as not renewed after 22 November 2013 [Para 59][Para 64][Para 63]. Both worked on after their last written contract expired without signing a new one [Para 56][Para 66].

## 2. Issues in dispute
The panel recorded one live issue, its issue 1, and decided it through two sub-questions:
1. Whether the successive fixed-duration contracts of the two union leaders were tacitly renewed and became indefinite-duration contracts after 5 August 2013 and 22 July 2013 respectively? [Para 109]
2. Whether the two men, as registered union leaders, enjoyed the protection from dismissal requiring prior consent of the labour inspector? [Para 160]
3. Whether the employer must therefore take both men back and pay them back wages from the date of dismissal to the date of their return? [Para 177]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer dismissed both men without valid reason, they committed no fault, and the employer failed to follow the procedure in Article 293 of the Labour Law; Mr S added that he never received any oral or written warning and had asked his section supervisor for leave [Para 69][Para 71][Para 81].
**Employer (respondent):** Mr S was dismissed for repeated unauthorised absences (3.5 days in March, 3 in April, half a day in June, 2.5 in July and 2 in August 2013) despite oral and written warnings [Para 72][Para 73][Para 76]; Mr B was not dismissed at all — his last two-month fixed-duration contract simply was not renewed — and he had also been absent repeatedly and refused a transfer to packing [Para 63][Para 82][Para 90]. The employer conceded it never sought the labour inspector's authorisation [Para 98][Para 99].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to take Mr S and Mr B back to work as normal and to pay back wages from the date of dismissal to the date of return, with no sum fixed in the award; the order was declared immediately final and binding on the parties under the Memorandum of Understanding of 3 October 2012 [Para 177][Para 178].

On contract type, the panel applied Article 67(8) of the Labour Law: a fixed-duration contract of two years or less that reaches its term with neither party expressing an intention to renew or not renew is renewed by silence, and the employment relationship continues [Para 109][Para 110]. Because Mr S kept working after 5 August 2013 and Mr B after 22 July 2013 without any new written contract, each contract was tacitly renewed and became an indefinite-duration contract from those dates [Para 149][Para 158][Para 143][Para 151]. The panel expressly declined to follow the reasoning in Award No. 245/13 (Foshan ... (Cambodia)), which had treated Article 73(a) paragraph 5 alone as settling the consequences of a failure to give notice; it held that provision governs notice for contracts longer than six months and does not displace Article 67(8), and that reading it alone would make the two articles contradict each other [Para 124][Para 127][Para 135][Para 130].

On protected status, the panel found both men to be leaders of a union registered with the Ministry of Labour on 14 March 2013, recognised by letter No. 081/13 K.B of the same date [Para 47][Para 161][Para 164]. Article 293 requires the labour inspector's prior consent before a shop steward is dismissed, and Article 4 paragraph 2 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 extends that protection to three union leaders [Para 161][Para 162]. Dismissal without that consent being void under Article 4 paragraph 4 of the Prakas, the panel held the dismissals of both men void and applied Prakas No. 099 Article 34 to order reinstatement and immediate payment [Para 173][Para 174][Para 175]. Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented on this issue: in his view the contracts remained fixed-duration because total duration did not exceed two years, the case was mere non-renewal, and no reinstatement should be ordered [Para 186][Para 192].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a fixed-duration labour contract of two years or less expires and the worker carries on working without a new written contract while neither side declares an intention to renew or not renew, the contract is renewed by silence and becomes an indefinite-duration contract under Article 67(8); the resulting dismissal of a registered union leader without the labour inspector's prior consent is void under Article 293 and Prakas No. 305, and the remedy is reinstatement with back wages to the date of return [Para 112][Para 168][Para 176].

**Obiter dicta:** Article 73(a) paragraph 5 is confined to contracts longer than six months and is not an exhaustive statement of the consequences of failing to give notice [Para 135]; an employer facing expiry has two lawful options — conclude a new written contract of at least the old duration, or allow the relationship to continue by silence [Para 136]. The panel noted that for contracts of six months or less it would follow Award No. 245/13 and renew for an equal term if no notice was given [Para 126].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The date of the panel's constitution is garbled at line 17 ("ឆ្នាំ ២០២ ២០១៣") and the year of dismissal is duplicated at line 56 as "២០ ២០១៣". Line 151 describes Mr B as having signed four two-month contracts while only two written contracts are set out (lines 64 to 66); line 143 counts two written contracts for Mr S, matching the record. The company's commercial registration number appears as "៤ Inv. ២០៤០ E/២០១២" (line 44) but as certificate No. 5136 of 22 August 2012 elsewhere (line 32), and the statute number is given as 2544/2012 in one place and the market-entry certificate in another. Khmer contract-type words are repeatedly mis-OCR'd ("ថិរ"/"ថរ"/"ថរៈ"), and stray glyphs ("CL", "CK-", "Z", "០០", "%") interrupt several lines including the issue headings. Individual workers' names appear in the award but are reduced to Mr S and Mr B here by policy; the employer's representative and the union president are likewise unexpanded.
