# Award No. 093/14 — Terratex Knitting and Garment (In) Factory Ltd (Arbitration Council, 28 May 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouchhun, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Pen Bunchea, chair, chosen by both) · non-binding award, final after 8 days · Doc ID 139553

## 1. Facts
Terratex Knitting and Garment (In) Factory Ltd, on National Road 2 in Chak Angre Krom, Meanchey, Phnom Penh, employs about 850 workers. Ms C began on 23 May 2011 as a fabric inspector on a two-month probation, then on successive six-month and three-month fixed-term contracts. Between 23 May and 1 June 2013 she kept working with no written contract, and from 3 June 2013 the company signed three further three-month fixed-term contracts with her. On 12 January 2014 she was elected secretary of the Kampuchea union at the factory. On 18 February 2014 the company gave notice that her last contract, expiring 28 February 2014, would not be renewed, and she stopped work on 28 February 2014. The union demands her reinstatement with back wages and an end to anti-union discrimination. [Para 83][Para 87][Para 90][Para 91][Para 104][Para 155]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether Ms C's contract was a fixed-term contract or one of undetermined duration? [Para 123]
2. Whether she enjoyed the protection given to an elected union leader when she was dismissed? [Para 163]
3. Whether ending her contract was anti-union discrimination? [Para 190]
4. Whether the company terminated her contract lawfully under Article 74? [Para 209]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Ms C worked continuously from 23 May 2011 to 28 February 2014 without a break; the 2013 gap was a paper gap, not an absence, as 20 workmates attested by thumbprint, and she was at work every day in that period. She was elected union secretary on 12 January 2014, was never at fault and never warned, and the non-renewal punished her candidacy — the union had told the company of the elected leaders on 17 January, 5 March and 17 March 2014. [Para 93][Para 101][Para 113][Para 124][Para 137]
**Employer (respondent):** The contracts were three-month fixed-term ones that simply ran out on 28 February 2014 and were not renewed as was its right; she worked less diligently than required and did not cooperate, and on the same day it also let 31 other workers' contracts expire, so there was no targeting of the union. It had never received notice of the elected leaders, and a union cannot claim protection for leaders whose union was not yet registered. [Para 96][Para 102][Para 110][Para 213]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the company must take Ms C back to work and pay her wages from the day of dismissal until the day she returns, with no sum fixed because the amount depends on that date. The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Minister. [Para 218][Para 219][Para 220]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 67(7): a fixed-term contract must be in writing, and if it is not, the contract becomes one of undetermined duration. It found as fact that she worked from 23 May to 1 June 2013 without any written contract — the 20 thumbprints were unopposed and she was shown to have been at work — so from 23 May 2013 her contract was of undetermined duration. The three-month written contracts signed from 3 June 2013 could not turn that relationship back into a fixed-term one; the panel treated the company's attempt to re-hire her as a "new worker" after interview as ineffective. Ending the relationship on 28 February 2014 was therefore a dismissal, not an expiry. [Para 139][Para 142][Para 144][Para 160][Para 162]

On issue 2 the panel set out Article 293 and Article 3 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001: candidates for union office get the same protection as staff delegates, running 45 days before the ballot and ending 45 days after it for candidates who lose, but with no fixed end for those elected. It then applied its three-condition test — the worker must fall under Prakas 305, the dismissal must fall within the protected period, and the union must have notified the employer of the protected names by any reliable means. The first two were met: she was elected on 12 January 2014 and no registration application had yet been filed, which the panel held keeps protection alive. The third failed: the letter of 17 January 2014 went to a security guard whose name, card number and description the union could not supply, and the postal notices of 5 and 17 March 2014 both post-dated the dismissal. She was thus not a protected worker and no labour inspector authorisation was needed. [Para 166][Para 167][Para 175][Para 178][Para 185][Para 187][Para 189]

On issue 3 the panel found no evidence of anti-union motive. The union's account of the notifications was unclear, and the company had let 31 other contracts lapse on the same day, which answered the suggestion that she alone was singled out. The discrimination claim failed on the burden of proof. [Para 204][Para 206][Para 207]

On issue 4, however, the dismissal still had to satisfy Article 74: an undetermined-duration contract may be ended unilaterally on notice, but only for a reason relating to the worker's capacity or conduct or to the needs of the enterprise. The company asserted only that she worked less hard and did not cooperate, and produced nothing to prove it. Lacking a valid reason, the dismissal was unlawful and reinstatement with back wages followed. [Para 10][Para 215][Para 216][Para 217][Para 218]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Continuous work carried on after a written fixed-term contract expires, with no new writing, converts the relationship to one of undetermined duration under Article 67(7), and a written fixed-term contract signed afterwards cannot convert it back or reset the worker's seniority; and an elected union leader's statutory protection depends on the union having given the employer notice of the protected names by a means that actually reaches the employer, so notice left with an unidentified guard, or posted only after the dismissal, does not engage Article 293. [Para 142][Para 144][Para 185][Para 189]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remarks that a losing candidate's protection expires 45 days after the ballot while an elected leader's has no fixed end, and that protection continues while the union has not yet applied for registration, were not necessary to the outcome, which turned on the failed notification. [Para 167][Para 169][Para 178]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel is recorded as constituted on "22 April 2019 2014" (para 16), and the award's own reasoning twice slips in month and year: "23 December May 2011" for her start date (para 132) and "28 December February 2014" for the end of the relationship (para 161). Her first day is printed as 23 May 2011 in the findings and 22 May 2013 as the gap date, one day apart from the 23 May 2013 used elsewhere. Her name appears as Chhay An, Chhay Ann and Chhay And; the company as Terratex, Tera Tic and Tera Tex Knitting and Garment; and the union at the factory as both the Kampuchea union and the Kampuchea Confederation of Trade Unions. The security guard who refused the January letter is unidentified in the record, which the panel itself noted, and that gap decided issue 2. The signature block is partly illegible and no arbitrator dissent is attached. [Para 16][Para 132][Para 161][Para 185][Para 220]
