# Order No. 267/13 — Asia Dong Run Im Port & Port (Arbitration Council, 10 January 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Yu Soun Ty appointed by the employer party, An Non appointed by the worker party, Aing Eng Tong chair) · Doc ID 139916

## 1. Facts
The company, a garment factory in Ang Snuol district, Kandal province, employing about 317 workers, engaged Mr S as a sewer from 20 April 2013 without any written contract; on 20 June 2013 it had him sign a document and on 20 September 2013 told him his three months were up. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 53][Para 64] He had been elected deputy president of the Cambodia Union Federation local at the factory on 21 July 2013, and the union had filed for registration on 6 August 2013. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 129] After conciliation failed, the workers withdrew their discrimination claim and pressed two demands: reinstatement and back wages from dismissal to reinstatement. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 53]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the contract signed on 20 June 2013 is evidence the panel may consider? [Para 84]
2. Whether Mr S held a fixed-duration or an indefinite-duration contract, and whether his separation was a non-renewal or a dismissal? [Para 113]
3. Whether the employer dismissed Mr S lawfully under the Labour Law? [Para 119]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr S began work on 20 April 2013 with no written contract; on 20 June 2013 he was called to sign a three-month probation contract ending 20 September 2013, and was stopped at 16:00 on 20 September 2013 without notice; the dismissal was retaliation for his election as deputy union president. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 56][Para 70]
**Employer (respondent):** it did not renew a fixed-duration three-month contract that expired on 20 September 2013; the ending was unrelated to union discrimination; it paid damages in lieu of notice and severance; the contract form used was the only one it had for all fixed-term contracts; and it agrees it did not seek the Labour Inspector's permission before terminating him. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 65][Para 109]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to take Mr S back into work and to pay his wages from the day of dismissal until the day he returns. [Para 137][Para 138][Para 139]

On issue 1, the panel held that the document produced is not a labour contract at all: it names a different enterprise as the contracting party and labels both signatories "និយោជិក" (employee), so on the definitions in Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 664 of the Civil Code there is neither a worker nor an employer party to it. [Para 103] It also records an indefinite duration with a three-month probation yet fixes no end date, contradicting the employer's oral account of a three-month fixed term. [Para 104][Para 106][Para 108] Reading Article 25 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY of 21 April 2004, which leaves admissibility, relevance and weight of evidence to the panel, it found the document of insufficient weight to prove a fixed-duration contract. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 112]

On issue 2, Article 67(7) of the Labour Law requires a fixed-duration contract to be in writing and provides that otherwise it becomes a contract of indefinite duration; since Mr S entered the factory on 20 April 2013 with nothing in writing, the panel characterised his contract as indefinite and the separation as a dismissal rather than a non-renewal. [Para 113][Para 114][Para 117][Para 118]

On issue 3, Article 293 requires the Labour Inspector's prior agreement before a staff delegate or candidate is dismissed. [Para 119] Prakas No. 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001 protects founders and members from the date the registration application is lodged until 30 days after registration, and the panel applied its three-condition test drawn from earlier orders 149/08 and 175/09-Apsara: the worker must fall within Prakas 305, the dismissal must fall within the protected period, and the employer must have been notified of the protected names. [Para 122][Para 125][Para 126][Para 128] Mr S, elected deputy union president, satisfied the first condition; his dismissal on 20 September 2013 fell inside the protection running from the 6 August 2013 registration receipt; and the union's notice of 23 July 2013 naming its leadership was signed for by the company's security guard on 1 August 2013, which the employer admitted receiving. [Para 128][Para 129][Para 131][Para 132][Para 133] The employer conceded it never sought the Inspector's authorisation, so the dismissal breached Article 293 and Prakas 305. [Para 135][Para 136]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-duration contract that is not put in writing becomes a contract of indefinite duration under Article 67(7) of the Labour Law, so ending it is a dismissal that the employer must justify. A worker elected to union leadership is protected from the date the union lodges its registration application, and his dismissal inside that window without the Labour Inspector's prior agreement under Article 293 is unlawful, entitling him to reinstatement and back wages. [Para 117][Para 119][Para 125][Para 137]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that an employer must draft a contract form matching the type of contract it actually intends, and that using the wrong form is unacceptable, is a general instruction on drafting rather than a condition of the order [Para 110][Para 111]; so is its observation that the written form it did produce was the only one used for all fixed-term contracts. [Para 109]
