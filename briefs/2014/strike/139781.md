# Award No. not printed (case No. 332/14) — T & K Garment (Arbitration Council, 11 December 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Toun Si Phan for the worker party, Pen Bunhea chair) · Doc ID 139781

## 1. Facts
T & K Garment, a garment manufacturer in Phnom Penh, faced a strike in November 2014 over two demands: that the company move the production manager out of his post as production chief, and that it raise the bonus (បុង) rate paid to the workers. [Para 2][Para 4][Para 11] Conciliation on 21 November 2014 settled neither point, and both were referred to the Council. [Para 12][Para 13] The claimant workers did not attend the hearing of 5 December 2014; their federation told the secretariat they had returned to work and dropped the demands. [Para 17][Para 19] The panel sat as Ing Sothy (employer party), Toun Si Phan (worker party) and Pen Bunhea (chair). [Para 1]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must move the production manager out of the post of production chief? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company must raise the bonus (បុង) rate paid to the workers? [Para 4]
3. Whether the panel should continue the arbitration in the absence of the claimant workers, who did not appear? [Para 33]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** as recorded in the non-conciliation report, the production manager had to be moved out of the production chief post, and the bonus rate had to be raised for the workers. [Para 2][Para 4] They did not appear at the hearing, and their federation reported that the workers had agreed to return to work and had stopped making demands. [Para 19][Para 48]
**Employer (respondent):** the company cannot move the production manager out of the post because he is a technician, and it cannot comply with the workers' demands. [Para 3][Para 5]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** withdrawn — the panel treated the claim as abandoned and terminated the arbitration of case No. 332/14, deciding neither of the two demands. [Para 53][Para 55][Para 56]

The panel framed its reasoning on Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council of 21 April 2004, which allows it either to continue the proceedings in the absence of a duly summoned party or to close them by award, and on Rule 4.7 of the annexed arbitration procedure, which repeats that choice and adds that the panel must in any event be satisfied the parties were properly notified of the date, time and place. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 35] Following awards No. 16/07, 27/07, 138/08, 11/11, 131/12 and 108/13, it identified three conditions for closing a file in a party's absence: the party was duly summoned, it did not appear before the panel, and it gave no valid reason. [Para 36][Para 37]

On the first condition the panel found invitation letter No. 1678 dated 28 November 2014, signed for by the federation's dispute officer on 1 December 2014 at 4 pm, and a reminder telephone call made at the scheduled hour; the summons was therefore valid. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40][Para 41] On the second it read "appearance before the panel" as requiring both presence at the hearing and participation in the proceedings to the end, and found the workers absent from all four stages — introduction, explanation of procedure, conciliation, and hearing. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44][Para 46] On the third it found no valid or specific reason offered: the federation had merely said the workers agreed to return and no longer demanded anything. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 51] With all three conditions met, the panel held the claimant had abandoned the claim and could close the arbitration by award. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54]

Because the workers never attended, the panel also recorded that it could find no facts relating to the two disputed demands and made no finding on either. [Para 32]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a claimant has been duly summoned and reminded, fails to appear at any stage of the arbitration without a valid reason, and its own federation confirms the demands have been dropped, the panel closes the file by award for abandonment under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 rather than proceeding to judgment. Such a closure is procedural only: it leaves the merits of the demands undecided and is neither a finding for the employer nor a rejection of the claims, so the same demands are not res judicata against the workers. [Para 45][Para 53][Para 54]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's account of the four stages an arbitration passes through — introduction and conflict-of-interest explanation, explanation of procedure and identification of the issues, conciliation if the parties agree, and the hearing — is a general description of Council practice, not a step necessary to the finding of absence. [Para 43]
