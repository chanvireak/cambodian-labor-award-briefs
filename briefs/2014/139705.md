# Award No. 240/14 — Ever Green In Dasrial Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 7 October 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; Tun Si Phan, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chea, president) · Doc ID 139705

## 1. Facts
Ever Green In Dasrial Co Ltd cuts and sews garments and employs 1,800 workers. Facing no buyer orders, it asked the Labour Inspectorate on 14 July 2014 to suspend the contracts of 1,500 workers for two months, from 18 July to 18 September 2014; by letter No. 110 dated 16 July 2014 the Inspectorate allowed the suspension and inspectors held an information meeting at the factory. In a minute of 15 July 2014 the company agreed to pay, during the suspension, 50% of basic wage, 100% of travel and accommodation allowance, 100% of attendance bonus, 100% of seniority bonus and 50% of the responsibility or skill allowance. It paid on that basis from 18 July to 16 August 2014, then stopped. Of 166 workers who transferred rights to the confederation, 38 resigned, leaving 128. [Para 63][Para 86][Para 116]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay 50% of basic wage and the other benefits agreed in the Labour Inspectorate's minute of 15 July 2014 for the period 17 August to 18 September 2014? [Para 101]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer bound itself by the minute of 15 July 2014, made before the labour inspector, and must honour it for the whole two-month suspension; it has already applied it for 18 July to 16 August 2014, so the balance is owed. [Para 76][Para 77]
**Employer (respondent):** The company did agree on 15 July 2014 to the 50% wage and other benefits, but afterwards had no capacity to keep paying, since it may have no work for a long period and faces insolvency. [Para 79][Para 85]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to pay, from 17 August to 18 September 2014, 50% of basic wage, 100% of travel and accommodation allowance, 100% of attendance bonus, 100% of seniority bonus on each worker's seniority, and 50% of responsibility or skill allowance, as agreed on 15 July 2014. [Para 136][Para 137]
The panel first classified the claim, since the parties are signatories of the 3 October 2012 Memorandum of Understanding and had chosen a non-binding award for interest disputes. It distinguished rights disputes — founded on law, a collective agreement, an internal rule or an existing practice — from interest disputes decided on equity, and held this claim to be a rights dispute because it rested on an agreement between the parties. [Para 16][Para 102]
On the lawfulness of the suspension it applied Article 71 of the Labour Law, read with its earlier awards: an employer may suspend activity for serious economic or material difficulty provided the suspension is supervised by the labour inspector and does not exceed two months, supervision meaning notification to the inspector and prior authorisation. It found letter No. 110 to be that written authorisation, with the inspector also attending the factory, so the suspension complied with Article 71 and, under Article 72, no wages were owed for the period as a matter of law. [Para 109][Para 110][Para 117]
That did not end the matter: the employer had freely agreed on 15 July 2014 to pay half the basic wage and the other benefits during the suspension. Applying Article 13, under which terms more favourable than the statutory minimum remain valid, the panel held the agreement binding for the whole two-month suspension. The company performed it until 16 August 2014 and could not rely on its own later incapacity to escape the balance, so it must pay for 17 August to 18 September 2014. [Para 124][Para 126][Para 134]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer, before the labour inspector, agrees to pay part of wages and other benefits during a lawful suspension of contracts, that agreement binds it for the whole suspension even though Article 72 would otherwise relieve it of any wage obligation; Article 13 makes the more favourable term enforceable, and later inability to pay is no defence. [Para 124][Para 134]
A suspension under Article 71 is lawful, and no statutory wages are owed, where the employer obtained the labour inspector's prior written authorisation and the suspension did not exceed two months. [Para 110][Para 117]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that the company might face insolvency, and that about 1,700 workers had already been paid under the company's notice of 18 August 2014 and had left, leaving roughly 100 unpaid. [Para 85]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The minute of 15 July 2014 is described in percentages in one place and later restated with figures — "USD 7" for travel and accommodation and "USD 12" for the attendance bonus — without explanation. [Para 74][Para 129] The parties agreed to postpone issuance from 19 September to 7 October 2014, and one sentence dates the unpaid period from "17 August" while another starts it at "18 August". [Para 23][Para 131] The company's name appears in more than ten Khmer transliterations ("អេវើ"/"អេវី"/"អេវើហ្គ្រី" + "ហ្គ្រីន" + "អ៊ិន ដាស្រៀល"/"ដាស្ទ្រៀល"/"ដាសៀន" + "ខូ អិលធីឌី"), and the signature block carries a stray third name, "ថៃ ដុល្លា", beside the three arbitrators. [Para 1][Para 139] The document list gives three different workforce totals (1,822, 1,625 and 1,800). [Para 64]
