# Award No. 346/14 — Kwan Tum E Garment (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 15 January 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer, Liv Sovanna for the workers, Pen Bun Chhea chair; Ing Sothy dissenting) · Doc ID 139789

## 1. Facts
Kwan Tum E Garment (Cambodia) Ltd, a garment factory in Phnom Penh employing about 1,530 workers of whom about 1,380 are women, faced a strike on 11 December 2014 over 14 demands. [Para 16][Para 45][Para 46][Para 69] Five points remained unresolved; one was settled at the hearing, one was withdrawn by the workers, leaving three: a further USD 28 rise for workers already earning USD 128 or more, pro-rata treatment of the USD 10 monthly attendance bonus, and a nursery. [Para 17][Para 22][Para 23][Para 24]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise wages by USD 28 a month from 01 January 2015 for workers already receiving USD 128 or more? [Para 77][Para 103]
2. Whether the employer may forfeit the whole USD 10 monthly attendance bonus, or only deduct it in proportion to days of absence authorised by the employer? [Para 104][Para 143]
3. Whether the employer must provide a nursery (ទារកដ្ឋាន) and play equipment for the women workers with small children? [Para 144][Para 163]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** last year the employer raised every worker by USD 20; this year only those below USD 128 received a rise, so team leaders, trainers, mechanics and other skilled workers got nothing while rent, transport and food rose and neighbouring factories paid USD 28 to all; the USD 10 milk allowance is inadequate because childcare costs USD 40 to 50 a month. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 73]
**Employer (respondent):** Prakas No. 283 requires only that the minimum wage reach USD 128, not a USD 28 rise for everyone, and the rise was assessed against the company's performance after the 2013 strike; the attendance bonus has always been forfeited once annual leave is exhausted; the USD 10 allowance replaced the nursery by agreement recorded in case No. 249/12. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 64][Para 70][Para 74]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 1 not considered for want of standing; on issue 2 the employer was ordered to deduct the attendance bonus only in proportion to the days of authorised absence; on issue 3 the employer was ordered to set up a nursery, and, where it cannot take children over 18 months, to pay the actual invoices of an outside nursery. [Para 165][Para 166][Para 167][Para 168]

On issue 1 the panel read Articles 1, 2 and 4 of Prakas No. 283 of 12 November 2014 as setting USD 123 for probationers and USD 128 for full-rights workers from 01 January 2015, that is a USD 28 increase on the former floor, and found the employer's top-up of everyone below USD 128 to that figure complied with it. [Para 84][Para 87] No statute, collective agreement, agreement, past practice or internal rule obliged a rise for those already at or above USD 128, so the demand was an interest dispute rather than a rights dispute. [Para 88][Para 89] Under Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305, only the most representative union may bring an interest dispute, because Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 makes such an award a substitute collective agreement binding all workers for one year and removes their right to strike on the point. [Para 93][Para 95][Para 96][Para 97][Para 98] The claimant was not the most representative union: 108 of about 1,530 workers had mandated it and the second federation was not yet registered with the Ministry. [Para 48][Para 50][Para 100][Para 101] The panel therefore declined to consider the wage claim at all. [Para 102][Para 103]

On issue 2 the panel found that Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 sets the attendance bonus at USD 10 a month for workers attending on the working days of the month without absence, and that this bonus is a "ប្រាក់ដែលបើកជារង្វាន់" (gratification), which Article 103 lists among the components of wages. [Para 107][Para 110][Para 134][Para 136] Because Article 71(6) and Article 72(1) treat absence authorised by the employer as a suspension of the contract, no wage is earned for those days, so the employer may reduce the bonus in proportion to the days of authorised absence; but forfeiting it entirely is a deduction from earned wages the law does not permit. [Para 137][Para 138][Para 140][Para 141][Para 142]

On issue 3 the panel held that Article 186 obliges an enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a nursing room and a nursery, and that Article 187 leaving the standards to a Prakas which the Ministry has still not issued does not suspend that duty. [Para 144][Para 158][Para 160] Paying USD 10 a month in lieu does not serve the statutory purpose of keeping mother and child together, so the employer must provide the nursery; where it cannot take children over 18 months it must pay the actual invoices of an outside nursery. [Para 155][Para 156][Para 164]

Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented on issue 2, holding that a gratification is paid in full once its conditions are met and cannot be apportioned unless the conditions themselves provide for apportionment, so the pro-rata order was wrong. [Para 175][Para 177][Para 182]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a wage increase above the statutory minimum, unsupported by any law, collective agreement, practice or internal rule, is an interest dispute which the panel will not hear unless the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise, since an award would replace the collective agreement for a year and bind every worker. A monthly attendance bonus is a component of wages, so absence authorised by the employer suspends the contract and the bonus may be reduced in proportion to the days absent but not forfeited in full. An employer's duty to provide a nursery is not discharged by paying a cash allowance in its place. [Para 98][Para 103][Para 136][Para 142][Para 155]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's survey of what "working days in the month" can mean (26 days, or 21 to 22) and its account of the earlier notifications of 2011 and 2012 are background to the pro-rata rule and were not themselves applied to any order. [Para 105][Para 128][Para 132]
