# Award No. 056/14 — Yi Rong (Cambodia) Nit Thing Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 13 March 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139537

## 1. Facts
Yi Rong (Cambodia) Nit Thing, a garment factory in Thleuk village, Peuk commune, Ang Snoul district, Kandal province, faced five demands from workers organised by two federations — Khmer Youth Voice and the Labour Rights Development Federation. [Para 1][Para 10] Conciliation by the provincial labour department on 3 March 2014 settled two points and left three, which reached the Council on 5 March 2014 under report No. 315 Kb/K.N. [Para 11][Para 12] The panel was constituted on 6 March 2014, learned that a strike was under way, and issued Order No. 010/14 requiring all strikers back at their shifts from the afternoon of 7 March 2014; the workers did not comply. [Para 13][Para 15] No hearing was held and the panel made no findings of fact on any demand. [Para 19]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay the benefits due under the Labour Law to workers who complete one year of service, before moving its operations to another site? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company must stop transporting materials out of the factory while the workers' benefits remain unpaid? [Para 4]
3. Whether the company must deduct the regular attendance bonus in proportion to the days a worker is absent? [Para 6]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company was relocating and moving stock out while statutory benefits for workers reaching one year of service were unsettled, and the attendance bonus should be reduced pro rata for days absent rather than forfeited in full. [Para 2][Para 4][Para 6][Para 33]
**Employer (respondent):** it did not agree to the first two demands; no position on the third is recorded, and at the panel's telephone check the GMAC representative said the company had not yet negotiated with the workers. [Para 3][Para 5][Para 36][Para 37]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel terminated the arbitration in case No. 056/14 without deciding any of the three demands and without making findings of fact. [Para 39][Para 40]

The panel put one question first: whether the arbitration should continue at all. [Para 20] Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 requires the parties to suspend every disputing measure, including a strike or lockout under Article 318 of the Labour Law, while the panel is seized of the case, and to attend all meetings it convenes. [Para 21][Para 22] Previous panels had read that article as barring them from continuing while a strike was on, and this panel followed Award No. 04/04-IM SI (2004), the second order in the 2004 hotel case, Award No. 77/05-Franco Nitting and Awards No. 110/11-Simanou, 22/13-Kbal Koh, 49/13-Eternity Double Sport Thing (Cambodia) and 182/13-Nichthy E. Kevil. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 26][Para 28][Para 30]

On the facts, Order No. 010/14 required a return to work from the afternoon of 7 March 2014. [Para 31] That afternoon the federation official told the secretariat that workers had entered the factory but refused to work, waiting for a settlement of the case about the company changing its name. [Para 32][Para 33] On 11 March 2014 he confirmed the strike was continuing with the workers present but not working, and the GMAC officer confirmed at 09:04 that the strike continued and had become more serious, with no negotiation yet begun. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 36][Para 37] The panel therefore found the workers in breach of its order and terminated the proceedings, recording no findings because no hearing took place. [Para 38][Para 39] It repeated the position of the earlier awards that the dispute may be taken up again once the strike ends. [Para 25]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A panel will not arbitrate a collective dispute while the workers are still striking in the face of an order made under Article 20 of Prakas No. 099; it terminates the proceedings without a hearing, and the entitlement to arbitration revives only when the strike actually stops. [Para 21][Para 30][Para 38][Para 39]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's account of why the workforce stayed out — that they were waiting for the company's change of name to be resolved before returning — is a report of the union's explanation, not a finding on the merits of the three demands. [Para 33]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The company's name is printed as យី រ៉ុង (ខេមបូឌា) នីត ធីង, and in the evidence list as យី រ៉ុង (ខេមបូឌា) នីត ធីង ខូអិលធីឌី [Para 1][Para 16]; the file itself records that a change of company name was pending [Para 33], which likely explains the variants. No Latin form is printed, so "Yi Rong (Cambodia) Nit Thing" is a transliteration.
- Order No. 010/14 is dated "06 March, year year 2014" with the year doubled [Para 15]; the report number appears as 315 Kb/K.N, 315/14 Kb/K.N and 315 Kb/K.N in successive references [Para 9][Para 12][Para 16].
- A long block of untranslatable English OCR noise interrupts the quotation of Award No. 77/05 at [Para 28].
- The precedent number for the Kbal Koh award is printed 22/13 here [Para 30], while a parallel award in the same series cites it as 24/13.
- The award lists only the three unresolved demands [Para 1]–[Para 6]; the two points settled in conciliation are not reproduced, so the 13 March 2014 award cannot be read as a record of the full dispute.
- One worker-side representative is named in the file [Para 1]; the individual is redacted here.
