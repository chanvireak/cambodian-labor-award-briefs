# Award No. 101/14 — Quin Major Industrial (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 2 May 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Yu Soun Ty, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 139562

## 1. Facts
Quin Major Industrial (Cambodia) Ltd, also styled Quint Major Industrial Co., Ltd and QMI, manufactures garments at Trapeang Chheu Neang village, Ang Snuol district, Kandal province, with more than 4,000 workers. Two federations — the Khmer Free Workers' Union Federation and the Cambodian Labour Solidarity Union Federation — brought six demands after a strike that began on 22 April 2014: withdrawal of all court complaints against worker and union representatives, reinstatement of dismissed representatives and workers, payment of severance short by two points, performance of the 14 March 2013 minutes on the change of name from QMIB to QMIP, a midday meal allowance of 3,500 riel a day, and no deduction of wages or bonuses during the strike. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 3][Para 5][Para 6][Para 7][Para 13] Conciliation failed on all six and the file reached the Council on 28 April 2014. [Para 10][Para 15]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the panel may continue the arbitration at all where the workers remained on strike throughout the proceedings, contrary to Article 20 of Prakas No. 099? [Para 29][Para 31]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The six demands stood as recorded in the non-conciliation report; the workers continued the strike after the provincial court's protection order, and their representatives attended the pre-hearing meeting and confirmed the strike was ongoing. [Para 2][Para 21][Para 42]
**Employer (respondent):** The company asked the Council on 28 April 2014 to issue an order suspending the strike that had run since 22 April 2014, and reported that the strike was still continuing in front of the factory. [Para 18][Para 20]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel terminated the arbitration of case No. 101/14 without hearing the six demands and made no order on their merits [Para 43][Para 44]

The panel framed a single preliminary question — whether it should proceed at all — and answered it from Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, which requires the parties to suspend every dispute measure, including strikes and lockouts under Article 318 of the Labour Law, for the duration of the arbitration, and to attend every meeting convened. It read that provision as barring it from continuing while a party is on strike or locked out, and noted that it had consistently refused to proceed in that situation in earlier awards. [Para 29][Para 31][Para 32][Para 40]

It reviewed its practice: in Award No. 04/04 (MSI) it had notified the parties that it could neither resolve nor issue an award on a collective dispute while the right to strike was being exercised, and that it could take the case up again once the strike ended; in Award No. 18/04 (hotels) it had ordered the strike stopped and reserved the right to close the proceedings if the order was breached; and in Awards No. 110/11 (Simano) and No. 17/13 (Intercontinental Five Star Royal) it had terminated for the same reason. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 36][Para 37][Para 39]

On the facts, the Kampot provincial court had already issued protection order No. 284 dated 24 April 2014 requiring more than 4,000 workers to stop the strike and return to work by shift within 48 hours. Because that order existed, the panel declined to issue an order of its own on the company's request of 28 April 2014. At the pre-hearing meeting of 29 April 2014 both parties confirmed that the strike was still continuing and that the workers had not complied with the court order. The panel therefore closed the arbitration. [Para 17][Para 19][Para 20][Para 42][Para 43]

Because it never held a hearing, the panel recorded that it made no findings of fact on the six demands and gave no reasons on their substance. [Para 28][Para 38]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
While a party is exercising the right to strike or a lockout, the panel must suspend its proceedings under Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 and may terminate the arbitration by award; it may take the same dispute up again only once the strike has ended. [Para 31][Para 35][Para 43]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's statement that it would not issue a strike-suspension order of its own where a court had already issued a protection order was not necessary to the outcome, which followed from the continuing strike alone. [Para 19]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The Khmer original is degraded and internally inconsistent. The company appears as "Quin Major Industrial", "Quint Major Industrial Co., Ltd", "QMI" and "Quinn Major", so the corporate name on the face of the award cannot be fixed with confidence. [Para 1][Para 22][Para 44] The protection order is attributed to the Kandal provincial court in the narrative and to the Phnom Penh court of first instance in the exhibit list. [Para 17][Para 23] The strike is said to have begun on 22 April 2014 in one place and, in the recital of the company's request, to have run from the same date with a different duration; the conciliation date is given as 29 January 2014 although the complaint was received on 23 April 2014. [Para 14][Para 18] The list of prior awards relied on is badly garbled, with case numbers and party names run together and some citations reduced to fragments. [Para 33] The signature block is heavily corrupted, and the award offers no reasons on any of the six demands. [Para 28][Para 45]
