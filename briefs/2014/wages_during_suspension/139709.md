# Award No. 249/14 — Ly Chang Seng (Arbitration Council, 9 September 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom for the employer party, Liv Sovannara for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139709

## 1. Facts
Ly Chang Seng is a factory at Kong Pisey, Kampong Speu, and the Khmer Workers' Union Federation led a strike there from 1 September 2014 over 11 demands on working conditions. [Para 2][Para 19][Para 23] Conciliation on 2 September 2014 failed on all 11 points, which reached the Council on 3 September 2014. [Para 20][Para 21] On 4 September 2014 the Council issued Order No. 047/14 K.B.A. requiring the strike to stop at once and all strikers to return to their shifts from the morning of 5 September 2014; the strike continued, and the parties confirmed at the pre-hearing meeting of 8 September 2014 that it was still running. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 26][Para 46]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of Riels 2,000 per day? [Para 3]
2. Whether the employer must remove one worker (name redacted) from the company? [Para 4]
3. Whether the employer must remove the team B leader, Ms S, from the company? [Para 5]
4. Whether every team assistant must punch the card for the women workers at both 4:00 p.m. and 6:00 p.m.? [Para 6]
5. Whether the employer must install 2 further dining tables so that there are enough? [Para 7]
6. Whether the employer must accept every medical certificate, whatever the number of days it covers? [Para 8]
7. Whether the employer must remove the ironing team leader, Mr U, from the company? [Para 9]
8. Whether the employer must stop burning all cloth waste containing toxic substances? [Para 10]
9. Whether every team leader must stop taking sewing workers away to other locations? [Para 11]
10. Whether the employer must open the gate at 6:00 a.m. every day? [Para 12]
11. Whether all unions must stop sitting in one place and instead walk around to observe all the workers? [Para 13]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the 11 demands were pursued through conciliation, which failed on every point, and the workers maintained the strike after the Council's suspension order; the federation's representatives attended the pre-hearing meeting and confirmed the strike continued. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 26][Para 46]
**Employer (respondent):** on 4 September 2014 it wrote to the Council asking for an order suspending the strike that had begun on 1 September 2014, and it produced that letter and nothing else in evidence. [Para 23][Para 27][Para 28]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction / proceedings terminated — the panel terminated the arbitration in case No. 249/14 without deciding any of the 11 demands, and made no monetary order. [Para 47][Para 48]

The panel framed the only question as whether it should continue the arbitration or apply Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY of 21 April 2004, which provides that during the arbitration the parties must suspend every dispute measure such as a strike or lockout, and must attend all meetings to which the panel invites them. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 36] It noted that in earlier awards it had held it will not continue an arbitration while the workers are striking or the employer is locking out, citing cases Nos 138/09, 110/11, 104/13 and 119/13 among others. [Para 37][Para 38] It also relied on the Council's notice of 29 January 2004 in case No. 04/04, which stated that a party has no right to strike during its own arbitration and that the Council cannot resolve or issue an award on the collective dispute, though it may take the case up again once all the unions stop striking; and on the second order of 22 April 2004 in case No. 18/04, whose points 9 and 11 required strikers to stop all protest and reserved the Council's right to end the proceedings on any breach. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41][Para 42] The panel recorded case No. 77/05, where it declined to proceed while the strike continued, and aligned itself with its decisions in Nos 110/11 and 17/13. [Para 43][Para 44]

Because the Council had no factual findings to make — it never held a hearing on the merits, precisely because the strike persisted — the panel found the statutory precondition for proceeding was absent and terminated the case under Article 20. [Para 33][Para 45][Para 46][Para 47]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where workers continue a strike after the Arbitration Council has ordered it suspended under Article 20 of Prakas No. 099, the panel terminates the arbitration without hearing or deciding the merits, and the dispute may be taken up again only once all the unions have stopped striking; the same applies where the employer maintains a lockout. [Para 36][Para 39][Para 40][Para 47]

**Obiter dicta:** the recital of the earlier awards — Nos 138/09, 110/11, 104/13, 119/13, 77/05 and 17/13 — and of the 2004 notice in case No. 04/04 is illustrative of consistent practice, not a separate holding. [Para 38][Para 43][Para 44]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The 11 demands are reproduced only in the conciliation list at [Para 3]–[Para 13]; no reasons, evidence or findings on their merits exist, because the panel never reached them [Para 33].
- The award is dated 9 September 2014 in the header [Para 1], but the panel's summons to the parties is printed as "8 September 2019 2014" [Para 45], and the same slip appears in the operative reasoning [Para 26].
- The worker-party arbitrator appears as "Liv Sovannara" at [Para 2] and "Liv Sovanna" in the signature block [Para 49]; the signature block also omits the employer-party arbitrator's name entirely.
- The Prakas No. 099 article numbers are inconsistent: Article 20 is cited at [Para 34] and [Para 47], but the quoted text is introduced with a stray "ប្រការ ២០" duplicated at [Para 34] and the 2004 notice is described as "point 4 of this notification" [Para 39].
- Case citations are badly corrupted ("លេខ ៥៩០៦-សាង រ៉ូ លេខ ៣៩០៨ ចុង យ៉ូវ…) at [Para 38], and the party names in the 2004 hotel case are a string of unrelated hotel names [Para 41]; the numbers given here are only those legible.
- The employer's two representatives are named in the header [Para 2], but the company's letter of 4 September 2014 is the sole employer document in evidence [Para 27][Para 28].
