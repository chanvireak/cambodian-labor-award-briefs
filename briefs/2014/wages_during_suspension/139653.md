# Award No. 193/14 — Sun Well Shu (Arbitration Council, 25 July 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Ven Pov, worker-appointed; Tang Try, president) · Doc ID 139653

## 1. Facts
About 200–300 workers of Sun Well Shu, represented by the Federation of Friendship Unions of Workers (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព មិត្តភាព កម្មករ), struck from 15 July 2014 over seven collective demands; Ministry conciliation under report No. 779 dated 16 July 2014 failed on all seven. [Para 3][Para 12][Para 18] On 17 July 2014 the panel issued order No. 038/14 requiring the strike to be suspended immediately and all workers to return to their shifts from the afternoon of 18 July 2014 pending the arbitration. [Para 19] The workers did not comply: some entered the plant briefly on 18 July, were told by the federation not to work, and resumed the strike on 21 and 22 July. [Para 28][Para 29] The panel then held a pre-hearing meeting on 22 July 2014, which the worker side did not attend, and terminated the proceedings on 25 July 2014. [Para 24][Para 61]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay termination (severance) pay to all long-serving workers and settle other benefits in full? [Para 3]
2. Whether the company must bear the tax on wages and must not fold the rice allowance and overtime into the taxable wage base, and must restore the rice allowance as before? [Para 4]
3. Whether the company must add USD 5 to the regular attendance bonus for lawful rest days? [Para 5]
4. Whether the company must provide housing allowance or transport allowance of USD 15 to the workers? [Para 6]
5. Whether the company must remove the management-section chief, Mr O, from his post? [Para 7]
6. Whether the company must supply sufficient safety equipment — masks, gloves, boots, helmets (ការ ពារ) and work clothing? [Para 8]
7. Whether the company must fold the skill allowance (ប្រាក់ ជំនាញ) into the basic wage of all workers? [Para 9]

The panel reached none of these: it decided only the threshold question whether the arbitration could proceed at all while the strike continued. [Para 41]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the seven demands set out in the failed conciliation report were all that the record shows of their case; they did not appear at the pre-hearing meeting, telling the secretariat by telephone that they could not attend because they and the workers were on strike, and so forfeited their right of reply to the employer's submissions. [Para 23][Para 30][Para 33]
**Employer (respondent):** attended the pre-hearing, confirmed the strike was led by the Federation of Friendship Unions of Workers, and submitted that it had posted order No. 038/14 at the factory gate on 18 July 2014 so that workers would know to return, and that although some came back briefly the federation told them not to work and to resume the strike on 21 July 2014. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 28]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel terminated the arbitration proceedings in case No. 193/14 (Sun Well Shu) without deciding any of the seven demands, no sum being awarded either way. [Para 61][Para 62]
On the threshold question, the panel applied Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 dated 21 April 2004, which requires the parties to suspend all dispute measures — strike or lockout, and any other measure that could aggravate the dispute — throughout the arbitration, and to attend every meeting the panel convenes. [Para 41][Para 42] Because the workers remained on strike after order No. 038/14 and stayed away from the 22 July pre-hearing, the panel held the condition for continuing was not met. [Para 43][Para 60]
The panel followed its own prior practice rather than deciding afresh: awards 59/06 (Sang Wu), 39/08 (Chung Yew), 038/09 (Phnom Penh), 110/11 (Simano), 104/13 (Brina Cambodia) and 126/14 (Pyuu Vis Indas Steel) were cited as decisions in which it declined to proceed while a strike was live. [Para 44][Para 45] It also relied on case 04/04 (IMSI), where notice No. 39 of 29 January 2004 held that a party striking during arbitration has no right to have the collective dispute resolved, but that the panel may take the case up again once the strike ends; and on case 18/04 (hotels), whose second order of 22 April 2004 required workers to stop all strike, demonstration and disruption from 23 April 2004 and reserved the panel's right to close proceedings if any party breached the order. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49] Case 77/05 (Franc Com Nitting) and cases 110/11 and 17/13 were cited to the same effect. [Para 50][Para 51]
On the evidence, the panel noted that there had been no hearing, so no findings of fact were made on the merits of the seven demands; the documentary record comprised the employer's letter of 16 July 2014, the conciliation report No. 779, the conciliation minutes, order No. 038/14, the arbitrator-selection invitation and lottery minutes, and the two pre-hearing invitations. Nothing was filed by the worker side. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 40]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where workers continue a strike, or an employer a lockout, after the panel has ordered suspension under Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 and while the arbitration is running, the panel will terminate the proceedings instead of deciding the collective dispute; the case may be taken up again only when the strike has ended. [Para 43][Para 47][Para 61]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that a party's failure to attend the pre-hearing meeting amounts to abandoning its right to answer the other side's submissions, and accepted the employer's account on that basis alone. [Para 30][Para 31]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
OCR damage is heavy. Order No. 038/14 is dated "17 July 2019, 2014" in one place and "17 July 2014" elsewhere; the conciliation minutes are dated 15 July 2019 in the evidence list although the conciliation report is 16 July 2014. [Para 19][Para 34] The employer's statement of the strike's start is garbled as "12 15 July 2014", while the employer's letter puts it at 15 July 2014. [Para 18][Para 26] The list of prior awards in the reasons is partly illegible, with numbers run together (e.g. "13709: 038 09"). [Para 45] Paragraph 1–2 mix the Council's letterhead and address into the case header. The management chief named in demand 5 and the worker representative who telephoned the secretariat are reduced to Mr O and Mr S. [Para 7][Para 23] The number of striking workers is given only as "about 200 to 300". [Para 18]
