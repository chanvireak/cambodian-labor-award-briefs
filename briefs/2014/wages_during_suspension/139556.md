# Award No. 096/14 — Pemier (Bai Mi) Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 2 May 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom, employer-appointed; Liv Sovannara, worker-appointed; Ang Eng Tong, chair, chosen by both) · arbitration terminated during a continuing strike · Doc ID 139556

## 1. Facts
Pemier (Bai Mi) Garment Co., Ltd cuts and sews garments on Veng Sreng Road, Trapeang Thleung, Chaom Chao, Pur Senchey, Phnom Penh. Workers struck from 22 April 2014 over 18 demands; conciliation on 23 April settled 17 and left one, the demand that daily wages and other benefits, including the attendance bonus, not be deducted for strike days. The worker representatives walked out of that meeting and refused to sign or thumbprint the record. The strike continued after the file reached the Council, and on 25 April the company asked for an order suspending it. On 28 April the panel issued Order No. 022/14 requiring the strike to stop at once and all strikers to return to their shifts from the morning of 29 April. About 270 of some 360 strikers did return that morning, but by midday about 100 had rejoined; the strike continued with about 70 workers, of whom 10 to 20 were said to be blocking others from entering. [Para 9][Para 10][Para 13][Para 14][Para 20][Para 21]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must keep daily wages and other benefits, including the attendance bonus, during the days of the strike? [Para 2]
2. Whether the panel may continue the arbitration while the strike persists after its order of 28 April 2014? [Para 29]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** They maintained the single unresolved demand for full wages and bonuses over the strike days, and did not attend the pre-hearing meeting of 30 April 2014 or the hearing, so the panel heard nothing further from them on the merits. [Para 2][Para 17][Para 43]
**Employer (respondent):** It could not keep paying daily wages and the attendance "prime" for strike days, and asked the Council to order the strike suspended because it was disrupting the production line; its administration representative, Mr T, attended the pre-hearing meeting and reported that the order had been posted at the factory gate and circulated through section heads on 29 April 2014. [Para 3][Para 19][Para 45]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no award on the merits, arbitration terminated — the panel ordered the arbitration of case No. 096/14 closed, leaving the wage-and-bonus demand undecided and making no order for payment. [Para 49][Para 50]

The panel framed the only live question as procedural: whether to continue at all. Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 requires the parties to suspend every dispute measure — strike, lock-out under Article 318, or anything else that would aggravate the dispute — for the duration of the arbitration, and to attend every meeting the panel calls. The panel read that as a condition of its own ability to proceed, citing its consistent practice in earlier awards that it will not continue an arbitration while the workers are striking or the employer has locked out. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 31][Para 32]

It then applied the practice to the facts. Order No. 022/14 of 28 April had required the strike to end and the workers to return from 29 April; the order was posted at the gate and circulated, most strikers did return that morning, but a substantial group rejoined by midday and a smaller group remained outside, some blocking others from entering. On the company's account, corroborated by the union federation president's telephone report of 30 April that about 100 workers were still standing in front of the factory, the strike was still running when the panel met. The panel therefore held there were no facts it could find on the demand, since it never held a hearing on the merits, and terminated the arbitration. [Para 28][Para 44][Para 48][Para 49]

The panel's earlier decisions reserve the point: an award in case No. 04/04 (IMI) of 29 January 2004 recorded that where a party has no right to strike during the proceedings the panel cannot resolve the dispute or issue an award, and that it may take the case up again once the strike stops. The same approach was followed in the hotel cases of 2004 and in Awards No. 110/11 and No. 17/13, and in Award No. 66/14 against a factory whose workers ignored a similar order. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 39][Para 40][Para 41]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where, after the panel has ordered a strike suspended under Article 20 of Prakas No. 099, a party keeps the strike running through the arbitration, the panel must close the arbitration instead of deciding the demands on the record before it, and may reopen the case only once the strike has ended. [Para 31][Para 35][Para 49]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's statement that it may resume a terminated case once all the workers stop striking, and its reliance on the union federation president's telephone report as corroboration of the employer's account, were observations rather than findings necessary to closing the file. [Para 35][Para 48]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The file is badly duplicated: paragraphs 16 to 21 are repeated almost verbatim at 42 to 48, with the same pre-hearing meeting given as 16:30 in one place and the telephone report timed at 08:02 in the other. The settled points are listed as 1 to 12, 15, 16 and 17 — fifteen items — yet the report is said to have settled 17 of 18 demands, so points 13 and 14 are unaccounted for. The company name appears as Pemier (Cambodia) Garment, Pemier (Bai Mi) and Bai Mi; the Council's street is printed as "ឆ្នាំ ២០២៧" for Street 327 (para 7); and the 2004 IMI notification carries a stray "2015" (para 34). No hearing was held, so there are no findings on the single remaining demand. [Para 7][Para 10][Para 34][Para 42][Para 28]
