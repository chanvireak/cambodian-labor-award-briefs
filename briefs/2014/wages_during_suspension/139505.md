# Award No. 009/14 — Sino Garment Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 3 February 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៊ីង សុទ្ធី appointed by the employer party, អន ណន appointed by the worker party, អាំង អេង ថុង chair) · Doc ID 139505

## 1. Facts
Sino Garment Co., Ltd., a garment factory at Prek Samrong, Ta Khmau, Kandal, employing about 700 workers and operating under that name after several changes of company name and a change of owner in 2012, faced a strike by its workers from the afternoon of 25 December 2013 to 31 December 2013, 5.5 days in total, during the nationwide strike over the minimum wage. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 25][Para 30] The employer deducted six days of wages and the whole regular attendance bonus from the workers who struck, and the four workers who appeared at the hearing claimed both back. [Para 31][Para 32] Their in-house union, the Cambodia-ASEAN union at Sino Garment, held no registration certificate from the Ministry of Labour at the time. [Para 26]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must maintain the wages and the regular attendance bonus of the workers who struck from the afternoon of 25 December 2013 to 31 December 2013? [Para 30][Para 55]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the strike was part of a nationwide movement, not a Sino Garment dispute; other factories kept wages, some by offsetting annual leave, some paying half wages, some keeping the full bonus, so this employer should show the same understanding and the deductions have already driven some workers to resign. [Para 33][Para 37][Para 38][Para 42]
**Employer (respondent):** the employment contract is suspended during a strike so neither wages nor bonuses are owed; the strike was called on impulse without a secret ballot or prior notice and the workers came back only when the Government announced the return date, not out of consideration for the company. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 50][Para 52]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the claim that the employer maintain wages and the regular attendance bonus for the four claimant workers for the strike from the afternoon of 25 December 2013 to 31 December 2013. [Para 66][Para 67]

The panel applied Article 332(1) of the Labour Law: the contract is suspended during a strike, working time is not provided and wages are not paid. [Para 55][Para 56] Article 72(1) limits the suspension to the principal obligations, which are the worker's duty to work and the employer's duty to pay. [Para 58] It followed its own reasoning in Award No. 27/10 (Hat 2 Inter) that an employer is not required to pay wages or bonuses for any day or month in which the workers struck, whether or not the strike was procedurally lawful, and the same reading of Articles 332(1) and 334 in Award No. 178/12 (Dechoro (Cambodia)). [Para 59][Para 61][Para 62] Article 334 makes the hiring of replacements the only trigger for paying strikers, and on the workers' own account no replacements were hired, so the exception did not arise. [Para 57][Para 64][Para 65] The panel also noted that the strike was not a lawful collective action by a registered union, the in-house union having no registration certificate, and that the nationwide character of the stoppage did not convert it into a dispute the company had to fund. [Para 26][Para 34][Para 63]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Wages and the regular attendance bonus are both suspended by a strike under Articles 332(1) and 72(1), and the employer owes neither for the strike days regardless of whether the strike was called in accordance with the Labour Law; the duty to pay arises only if the employer hired replacement workers contrary to Article 334. That the stoppage was nationwide, that other employers chose to pay, or that workers later resigned over the deduction, does not create the obligation. [Para 56][Para 59][Para 61][Para 65]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's findings that the in-house union was unregistered and that the 25 December stoppage was a spontaneous walkout rather than part of the nationwide strike of 28 December were not necessary, since the claim failed on Articles 332 and 334 alone. [Para 26][Para 48][Para 49]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The union's registration status is asserted at [Para 26] without reference to a document, while the employer's own evidence list includes a Ministry letter of 31 December 2013 on the registration of that union, addressed to a person named differently from any party representative. [Para 17][Para 26]
- The strike is described as lasting 5.5 days but the deduction is stated as six days of wages; the award never reconciles the two figures. [Para 30][Para 31]
- Documents are listed as "foreign language documents" without translation or description, so their content cannot be assessed. [Para 18]
- The conciliation report is dated 07 January 2014 in two places but the minute of conciliation is dated 06 January 2014, the date the provincial office received the complaint. [Para 6][Para 20][Para 9]
- Dates are corrupted in the recitals: the panel is said to have been constituted on "14 January 2019 2014", and the hearing date was first set for 17 January 2014 then postponed to 22 January 2014 at the employer's request. [Para 12][Para 13]
- The employer party is named "Sino Garment" on the face of the award while the union and company names in the evidence carry garbled strings ("ស៊ីណូ ហ្គាមេន អ៊ិន ដាស ស្ទ្រី ខូអិលធីឌី"), and Article 334 is quoted with the words "និយោជកម្ពុជា" inserted into the text. [Para 1][Para 57]
