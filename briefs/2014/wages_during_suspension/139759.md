# Award No. 312/14 — Ever Green Industrial Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 25 December 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; Sin Kim Sean, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 139759

## 1. Facts
The company runs a garment factory in the Phnom Penh Special Economic Zone and told the panel it employed about 1,886 workers. [Para 51][Para 52] It suspended the employment contracts for two months, from 18 July to 18 September 2014, with the labour inspector's authorisation; when the workers returned on 19 September 2014 the administration told them there was still no work. [Para 56][Para 59] Eighty-three workers, whose list the employer did not contest by the deadline for objections, claimed full wages, the regular-attendance bonus and a monthly travel and accommodation allowance for the whole period of idleness. [Para 57][Para 58]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay 100 per cent of wages, the regular-attendance bonus of 10 US dollars per month and travel and accommodation allowance of 07 US dollars per month for the period from 19 September 2014 until it again provides work, the authorised two-month suspension having expired? [Para 56][Para 73]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the contract of employment remains valid because no official document shows the company dissolved or closed; under Article 71(11) the employer could not suspend beyond two months, and its failure to find work is its own fault under Article 83, as earlier awards held. [Para 66]
**Employer (respondent):** it has received no orders from customers since the suspension and has no means to pay wages, having borrowed money to pay severance of 70 US dollars per year of service to workers who resigned voluntarily; the factory has ceased operating and the premises are leased to another company, Thai Fong. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 64][Para 67]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer is ordered to pay 100 per cent of wages, the regular-attendance bonus of 10 US dollars per month and travel and accommodation allowance of 07 US dollars per month from 19 September 2014 until it provides work again. [Para 81][Para 82]
The panel began from Article 72(1): while a contract is suspended the employer owes no wages, because the worker owes no work either. [Para 68][Para 69] It then held that this rule reaches only the suspensions enumerated in Article 71. [Para 70] Article 71(11) allows suspension where the enterprise meets serious economic or material difficulty, subject to two cumulative conditions — the suspension must be supervised by the labour inspector and must not exceed two months — a reading the panel adopted from Award No. 58/07-Star Sport Wear. [Para 73][Para 75][Para 76] The first suspension, from 18 July to 18 September 2014, satisfied those conditions and was lawful. [Para 71] But the panel held that a contract may be suspended only once and for no more than two months, so the employer had no right to keep the contracts suspended after 18 September 2014. [Para 77] It found that the employer gave no ground under Article 71 for the continued stoppage, and therefore characterised the period from 19 September 2014 until work resumes as not a suspension at all. [Para 79][Para 80] With no suspension in law, the wage obligation revived in full, together with the two monthly allowances claimed. [Para 81]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension under Article 71(11) may be used once only and for no longer than two months, and only on a ground listed in Article 71 and under labour inspector supervision; once the two months expire without work resuming, the contract is no longer suspended and full wages and the contractual monthly allowances accrue until work is provided, whatever the employer's financial position. [Para 70][Para 77][Para 80]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded the employer's offer to pay 70 US dollars per year of service as severance to workers who resigned voluntarily, and the workers' reliance on the absence of any official closure document, without deciding that either point altered the claim. [Para 66][Para 67]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The attendance bonus is printed as 12 dollars in the summary of the claim and as 10 dollars in the statement of issues, the reasons and the order; the brief follows the operative figure of 10. [Para 2][Para 56][Para 81] The conciliation report is numbered ZA 391 in one place and 391/14 in another, both dated 11 November 2014. [Para 6][Para 11] The date the panel was constituted is printed as "13 November 2019 2014", and the agreed extension of the award deadline runs "from 3 December 2015 to 25 December 2014". [Para 12][Para 17] The reasoning paragraph restating the claim is badly corrupted by OCR, with fragments of unrelated text spliced into the sentence; it was reconstructed from the statement of issues and the operative order. [Para 68][Para 82] The employer's name is rendered in several different transliterations across the award. [Para 1][Para 47]
