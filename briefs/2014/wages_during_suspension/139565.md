# Award No. 105/14 — Chu Huy Footwear Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 30 May 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Tuon Si Phan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chea, president) · Doc ID 139565

## 1. Facts
Three workers at Chu Huy Footwear, a shoe factory in Kampong Cham employing about 5,200, were absent without permission on the afternoon of 18 April 2014, the first working day after the Khmer New Year holiday; about ninety per cent of the workforce left with them. [Para 33][Para 42][Para 43] The company responded by charging half a day of annual leave against each absence instead of deducting wages, and the workers struck from 19 to 28 April to have those leave days restored. [Para 44][Para 46][Para 54] Claiming transfer of rights from 643 workers, only the three who appeared at the hearing were treated as parties. [Para 35][Para 38][Para 39]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must stop deducting annual leave in place of wages and other benefits for the workers' unauthorised absence on the afternoon of 18 April 2014 and from the afternoon of 19 April to 28 April 2014, and must maintain wages and benefits for those days? [Para 40][Para 67]
2. Whether the company must raise the travel allowance from 7 to 10 USD? (withdrawn — [Para 18])
3. Whether the company must raise the attendance premium from 10 to 15 USD? (withdrawn — [Para 18])
4. Whether the company must provide parking for tuk-tuk drivers or pay 5 USD a month towards parking? (withdrawn — [Para 18])

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** annual leave may not be deducted without their agreement; on earlier strikes, in August 2013, the company deducted neither wages nor annual leave, and they asked for the same treatment now. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60]
**Employer (respondent):** it deducted annual leave precisely so as not to cut wages and benefits, notified the workers in writing, and cannot grant the claim as framed. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel refused the claim to bar the deduction and to preserve wages, but ordered the company to restore to the three claimants the annual leave days it had deducted, and ordered the three claimants to repay the company the wages and benefits it had paid in lieu of those leave days. [Para 116][Para 118][Para 119]

On the afternoon of 18 April the panel applied Article 170 of the Labour Law: annual leave is in principle taken during the Khmer New Year period, and leave at any other time requires agreement between employer and worker, with prior notice to the labour inspector; following Awards 21/05, 81/07 and 123/09 it held that the employer cannot charge annual leave against wages and benefits without the worker's consent. [Para 67][Para 69][Para 70][Para 79] The deduction was therefore improper. [Para 79]

That did not, however, entitle the workers to wages. Reading Article 664 of the Civil Code with Articles 102 and 69 of the Labour Law, the panel held that wages are the price of work actually performed, and the three claimants performed none on that afternoon. [Para 80][Para 86][Para 87][Para 89] So the company could not take the leave, and the workers could not take the wages; each side had to give back what it had received. [Para 90]

For 19 to 28 April the panel first characterised the absence: a group of workers agreeing to stop work in order to obtain a settlement of their demand is a strike under Article 318, as interpreted in Awards 36/06, 125/09 and 81/13, and this was one. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 97] Under Article 332(1) the contract is suspended during a strike and under Article 72(1) wages are not owed, following Award 27/10 and case 178/12, so no wages were payable for those days either, and the deduction of leave for them was equally improper. [Para 102][Para 105][Para 106][Para 114] Article 334's duty to pay strikers where replacements are hired did not arise, since no replacements were proved. [Para 108][Para 112]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may not discharge a worker's annual leave to cover an unauthorised absence without the worker's agreement, and the remedy for an improper deduction is restoration of the leave days rather than payment of wages, because wages are earned only by work performed and are not owed for strike days whatever the strike's legality. [Para 79][Para 87][Para 106]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that annual leave taken outside the Khmer New Year period must be notified in advance to the labour inspector, and its approval of the earlier jurisprudence on Article 334, were stated as background and did not decide anything, since no replacements were shown to have been hired. [Para 71][Para 109][Para 112]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The decision section orders the company to restore "annual leave for 2015" although every operative date in the award is in 2014 and the leave at issue was deducted in April 2014; the year is plainly wrong and is reported as printed. [Para 116][Para 118] The panel's reasoning refers to the absence running "to 23 January 2014" where the claim and the evidence cover 19 to 28 April 2014. [Para 92][Para 96] The award records four demands at [Para 11], the parties' agreement to extend the award date records "4 cases" rather than one [Para 22], and the three withdrawn items (travel allowance, attendance premium, tuk-tuk parking) are numbered 2 to 4 in a list whose first item is the annual-leave claim, so the numbering is preserved but the record is inconsistent about how many files were before the panel. [Para 18][Para 22] The signature block prints the president's name as "Thai Bun" with the surname lost to OCR, while the header names Pen Bun Chea. [Para 1][Para 121] The employer's representative is named only as a member of the Garment Manufacturers Association. [Para 1]
