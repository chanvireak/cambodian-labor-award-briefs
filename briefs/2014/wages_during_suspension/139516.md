# Award No. 024/14 — Kala Cam Inn Wes Men Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 11 March 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Aing Eng Thong (Chair), Liv Sovanna (worker party), Seng Vouch Hun (employer party) · Doc ID 139516

## 1. Facts
Kala Cam Inn Wes Men Co., Ltd is a garment factory at Phnov village, Trapeang Kong commune, Samrong Tong district, Kampong Speu, employing about 350 workers [Para 32][Para 33]. Its company union held only a registration receipt dated 28 November 2013 from the Department of Labour Disputes, not yet a certificate [Para 34]. The dispute arose after the employer deducted wages and the monthly attendance bonus for 27, 28, 30 and 31 December 2013, when workers stayed away during a nationwide garment strike, and after a further strike on 2, 3, 4 and 6 January 2014; twelve workers gave a power of attorney dated 2 January 2014 and eight attended the hearing of 18 February 2014 [Para 35][Para 38].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must maintain wages and the regular attendance bonus (ប្រាក់រង្វាន់ធ្វើការទៀងទាត់) of US$20 a month for the strike days of 2, 3, 4 and 6 January 2014 and for 27, 28, 30 and 31 December 2013? [Para 38][Para 65]

The second non-conciliated issue — that the employer must not dismiss workers without clear reason — was settled by conciliation at the hearing, leaving issue 1 alone for decision [Para 15][Para 16].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the January strike was forced by the employer's refusal to negotiate and by its deduction of wages and bonus for the December absences; those December absences were not voluntary, because a government announcement set the return to work at 2 January 2014, nearby factories (Sabrina, Sang Woo, Core) kept both wages and bonus, no transport ran, and workers feared being beaten by unidentified groups on the road [Para 39][Para 41][Para 43]. **Employer (respondent):** the days claimed were strike days or unauthorised absences, so no wage or bonus is payable, and it cannot meet the demand [Para 42][Para 55]. It also denied that any unidentified group had beaten workers on those dates [Para 53].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both limbs of the demand were rejected, for the January 2014 bonus and for the December 2013 bonus [Para 131][Para 133]. The award on this rights dispute became binding immediately, in accordance with the parties' agreement of 18 February 2014 [Para 135].

*Characterisation.* The panel first held the claim to be a dispute over rights (វិវាទអំពីសិទ្ធិ), not interests, because entitlement to the attendance bonus is fixed by Notification No. 230 K.B/S.C.N. of 25 July 2012, a text of general application [Para 65][Para 68].

*Article 334 route closed.* The panel set out Article 334, under which recruiting replacements during a strike obliges the employer to pay the strikers' wages [Para 69]. Both parties accepted that no replacement workers were recruited and no goods were sent out for sewing elsewhere during the January strike, so the workers could not rely on that article [Para 79][Para 81].

*Article 332.* The contract of employment is suspended during a strike, the allowance for work is not provided and wages are not paid; following Case 27/10-Heart Enterprise, this holds whether or not the strike was lawful [Para 70][Para 74][Para 75].

*Article 333.* The bar on punishing workers for taking part in a strike is fully effective only where the strike observed the procedure of the Labour Law; workers who do not comply cannot hope to gain the protection of the law, so deducting the bonus in such a case is not a punishable sanction [Para 87][Para 89][Para 91].

*The January strike was unlawful.* Article 324 requires at least seven working days' prior notice filed in the enterprise and sent to the Ministry, and Article 320(4) allows a strike only once all peaceful means have been tried [Para 98]. The workers struck without notice and before the conciliation and arbitration process had run its course, so they had no right to the attendance bonus for those days [Para 101][Para 103].

*The December absences.* The bonus under point 2 of Notification No. 230 is paid to workers who attend on all working days of the month without absence; a worker absent without the employer's permission has not met that condition and forfeits it [Para 112][Para 115][Para 119]. The workers bore the burden of proof on their justification and filed no evidence by the deadline of 21 February 2014, nor did they particularise why they could not attend; other workers did attend those days and neighbouring factories operated normally [Para 121][Para 125][Para 126]. The panel also recorded that Notification No. 041 of 7 March 2011, which set a minimum of US$7, was repealed and replaced by Notification No. 230, applicable from 1 September 2012, under which at least US$10 is due and this employer in fact paid US$20 [Para 114].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A monthly attendance bonus conditioned on presence on every working day of the month is forfeited by an absence the employer did not authorise, and the worker must prove both the authorisation and any legal entitlement said to excuse the absence; a nationwide strike, a government announcement and transport difficulties do not by themselves displace that condition. Strike days carry neither wage nor bonus, since Article 332 suspends the contract, and Article 334's remedy of paying strikers arises only where the employer actually recruited replacements — and the protection of Article 333 against sanctions presupposes a strike that complied with Articles 319 to 324 [Para 75][Para 91][Para 119].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that although the union federation and the employer had signed a memorandum of understanding of 3 October 2012, the company union's registration receipt of 28 November 2013 meant it did not yet hold professional-organisation status, so the parties to this case could not be described as signatories of that memorandum [Para 17].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The non-conciliation report No. 0088/14 is dated 29 December 2013 at one point but 29 January 2014 at two others [Para 6][Para 11][Para 25].
- The award is dated 11 March 2014 in the header, while the parties agreed on 18 February 2014 to postpone issuance from 28 February to 10 March 2014 [Para 19].
- The January strike is described as running from 2 to 6 January 2014 in the parties' accounts but as four listed days, 2, 3, 4 and 6 January, in the demand [Para 52][Para 38].
- One sentence of the demand breaks into stray text — "ខែមករា ឆ្នាំ 2016" and the untranslated words "and the" — amid the January 2014 dates [Para 38].
- Arbitrator names appear only in Khmer script and are transliterated here; the employer's representative is named only in Khmer and is not reproduced.
