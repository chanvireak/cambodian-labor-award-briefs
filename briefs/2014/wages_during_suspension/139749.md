# Award No. 295/14 — Cidase Bel UKH Garment (Cambodia) Limited (Arbitration Council, 27 November 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Tang Try (president) · Doc ID 139749

## 1. Facts
Cidase Bel UKH Garment (Cambodia) Limited ("CWKH"), a garment factory employing about 482 workers, excluded 15 named workers from the plant from 08 September 2014, after a strike that ran from 14 to 27 August 2014 and resumed on 08 September 2014. [Para 71][Para 81][Para 96] Two federations held written mandates dated 06 October 2014 from 210 workers to act for them, and the three unsettled points reached the Council after conciliation failed. [Para 80][Para 9]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the 15 workers — later reduced to 12 — stopped from working on 08 September 2014 without fault, preserving their wages and benefits and paying back wages to the date of return? [Para 81][Para 82]
2. Whether the employer must bear the wages and benefits of all workers during the strike from 08 September 2014, on the ground that the employer itself provoked the strike? [Para 96]
3. Whether the employer must withdraw the complaints it filed with the courts and other institutions against the union's leaders and the workers' representatives? [Para 112]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the 12 remaining claimants were stopped without fault and have a right to suitable work; the protective order the employer sought wrongly bars them from earning a living, and the employer provoked the strike by seeking that order. [Para 84][Para 100]
**Employer (respondent):** it never dismissed them but is executing protective order No. 195 K of 03 September 2014, which suspends their work until the criminal case is decided; the strike was unlawful and involved blockading other workers; it will not withdraw its complaints. [Para 85][Para 116]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 1 not considered (within the court's competence), issue 2 dismissed, issue 3 not considered (not a labour dispute); no sum of money was ordered. [Para 174][Para 175]

The panel first settled who was properly before it. Under Article 268 of the Labour Law a professional organisation is deemed registered once two months pass after its papers are lodged without a response from the Ministry; the CWKH union held a receipt dated 18 December 2013 and, by the hearing on 27 October 2014, far more than two months had passed with no objection or order to correct, so it was a lawful organisation. [Para 118][Para 121][Para 123] The three leaders elected on 23 April 2014 had no ministry recognition and could not represent members in their own right [Para 133], but Article 19 of Prakas No. 099/04 permits appearance through a representative holding a written mandate, and the federations held mandates from 210 workers — so only those 210 were considered. [Para 135][Para 137][Para 138]

On issue 1 the panel declined to rule: the fate of the 12 workers was already before the Phnom Penh Municipal Court, which had ordered their work suspended pending judgment, and the employer was executing that order rather than dismissing them. [Para 142][Para 143] On issue 2 it applied Article 332, under which the contract is suspended during a strike so that neither work nor wages are owed, and Article 72 ¶1, which preserves only the essential obligations. [Para 145][Para 147] Following Awards 49/05 and 27/10, it held that strikers receive no wages for strike days whether or not the strike was lawful, and dismissed the claim. [Para 148][Para 149] It further found the strike unlawful for want of prior notice and of exhaustion of conciliation under Articles 320 ¶4 and 324, so the Article 334 duty to pay strikers where replacements were hired never arose. [Para 154][Para 161][Para 166] On issue 3, Article 309 limits the Council to labour disputes, and a demand to withdraw complaints from courts and other bodies is not one. [Para 169][Para 171][Para 172]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that has held a registration receipt for more than two months without any ministry objection is lawfully registered under Article 268, and although its newly elected local leaders cannot represent members without recognition, a federation holding written mandates from those members may bring their claims. Where a court has already suspended the workers' employment by protective order, the panel will not decide a reinstatement claim; and where a strike was called without the notice and conciliation required by Articles 320 and 324, the Article 334 obligation to pay strikers' wages despite replacement hiring does not arise. [Para 123][Para 143][Para 166]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that replacements were recruited only in October, not September, and that hiring them breached Article 334 — findings unnecessary once the strike was held unlawful. [Para 107][Para 153][Para 160]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The first union election is dated 03 December 2013 in one finding and 03 December 2014 in another, the latter impossible because the registration receipt precedes it. [Para 71][Para 121] The re-election date is printed as both 25 and 23 April 2014. [Para 125] Article 334 appears once as "មាត្រា ៩៣៣៤". [Para 103] The claimant count shifts between 15 and 12, and one list renders the twelfth name as "១២៩ ហ៊ីម សុវណ្ណ". [Para 81][Para 82][Para 139] The employer's name appears in OCR variants ("Cidase" / "ស៊ីដាប់") and the award prints no riel or dollar figures. Individual workers are given here as unnamed parties, though the award prints their names.
