# Order No. 351/14 — Jin Vin In Das (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 25 December 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, chosen by the employer party; An Non, chosen by the worker party; Tang Tri, chair chosen by the two) · Doc ID 139790

## 1. Facts
Some 20 workers of Jin Vin In Das (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, Special Economic Zone 7NG, Vihear Suor commune, Kandal province, struck from 11 December 2014 in support of ten demands; conciliation on 15 December 2014 settled eight and left two open, and the non-conciliation report No. 017 Kb/Kn of that date reached the Secretariat on 16 December 2014. [Para 9][Para 10][Para 11][Para 12] On 17 December 2014 the Council issued order No. 066/14 K.B.A. suspending the strike immediately and requiring every worker back at his or her shift from the morning of 18 December 2014; the workers did not comply and stayed out. [Para 13][Para 14] The employer attended the pre-hearing meeting of 23 December 2014 at 10:30 and reported that about 800 workers had returned while about 200 were still out; the workers' side did not appear and telephoned the Secretariat to say the strike continued and to ask that the file be closed. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 18][Para 40]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must add 1,500 riel to the meal allowance paid for evening overtime, raising it from 2,000 to 3,500 riel? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company must immediately remove the Chinese national employee Mr C (name redacted)? [Para 3]
3. Whether the arbitration proceedings should continue at all while the workers remain on strike? [Para 26]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the meal allowance for evening overtime should be raised by 1,500 riel to 3,500 riel in total, and the named Chinese national should be removed from the enterprise at once; on both points the parties remained in disagreement after conciliation. [Para 2][Para 3] Before the panel the workers' side did not attend, but stated by telephone that the strike was continuing and asked the Council to close the case. [Para 17][Para 40]

**Employer (respondent):** it rejected both demands and, by letter of 16 December 2014, asked the Council to issue an order stopping the strike that had run since 11 December 2014; it told the pre-hearing meeting that roughly 800 workers had gone back to work and that about 200 were still striking. [Para 3][Para 12][Para 18][Para 19]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no decision on the merits — the panel terminated the arbitration proceedings in case No. 351/14, leaving both outstanding demands unresolved and the file open to be taken up again only once the strike has ended. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 32]

The panel framed the only question it would answer as whether the arbitration should proceed, and answered it from Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY of 21 April 2004: during the arbitration the parties must suspend every dispute measure, including a strike or lockout under Article 318 of the Labour Law, and must attend all meetings to which the panel summons them. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 28] It read that rule as a condition on its own jurisdiction rather than a formality, holding on the authority of its earlier orders that it will not run an arbitration while workers are striking or an employer has locked out. [Para 29][Para 30]

That reading was then applied on the facts. Order No. 066/14 K.B.A. of 17 December 2014 had already required the strike to stop and the workers to return from 18 December 2014; the Secretariat was informed on 23 December that the strike went on, and the employer confirmed at the pre-hearing meeting that about 200 workers were still out. [Para 13][Para 17][Para 40] The panel recorded that the workers had disobeyed the order of 17 December and that it therefore could not continue the procedure or hold a hearing. [Para 41] It aligned itself with the earlier line of authority — the notice of 29 January 2004 in case No. 04/04, which said the Council could not resolve a dispute while the parties had no right to strike and could take the matter up again once all the unions stopped striking; the second order of 22 April 2004 in case No. 18/04; the second order of 24 December 2005 in case No. 77/05; and the terminations in case No. 110/11, case No. 17/13 and case No. 066/14 — and reached the same result. [Para 31][Para 32][Para 33][Para 35][Para 36][Para 37][Para 38]

Because no hearing was held, the panel made no findings of fact on the two unresolved demands and did not reach the meal allowance or the removal request. [Para 25] Its order was accordingly one of termination only, signed by the three members of the panel. [Para 43][Para 45]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where workers continue a strike after the panel has ordered its suspension under Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY, the panel will terminate the arbitration instead of hearing the merits, and the dispute may be revived only when the strike has actually ended; a party cannot obtain a ruling on its demands while maintaining the industrial action the law requires it to suspend. [Para 26][Para 28][Para 32][Para 41]

**Obiter dicta:** the recital of the eight earlier case numbers and the panel's remark that it made no factual findings because it never sat are observations on its own record and on precedent, not part of the operative rule. [Para 25][Para 30]
