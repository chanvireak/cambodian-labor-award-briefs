# Case No. 267/14 — Kolea Leader (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 20 October 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun for the employer, Tun Si Phan for the workers, Pen Bun Chea chair) · Doc ID 139735

## 1. Facts
Kolea Leader (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (ខូលៀ លេឌើរ (ខេមបូឌា) ខូអិលធីឌី) makes bags in the Sihanoukville special economic zone and employs about 450 workers. [Para 39][Para 40] The Khmer Workers' Spirit Federation raised 13 demands; conciliation on 12 September 2014 settled nine and left four, and at the hearing two of those four were settled, leaving issues 2 and 3. [Para 14][Para 18] Thirty workers had transferred their rights to the federation by letter of 05 September 2014 and two attended in person. [Para 42][Para 43] The local union had held its leadership election on 04 September 2014 but had not yet been registered by the Ministry of Labour. [Para 41][Para 54] The two surviving claims were that the employer must not deduct the whole USD 10 attendance bonus for days a worker was absent with the employer's permission, and that it must deduct union dues of Riels 1,000 per month from members who had consented. [Para 6][Para 45][Para 51]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reduce the USD 10 monthly attendance bonus pro rata to the days a worker was absent with the employer's permission for personal reasons, instead of cancelling the whole bonus? [Para 56] (the panel's issue 2)
2. Whether the employer is obliged to deduct union dues from the wages of members who have authorised the deduction in writing? [Para 98] (the panel's issue 3)

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** a worker absent with the employer's permission — a slight fever, a death in the family, going to obtain an identity document — has still come to work almost every day and should not lose the entire monthly bonus. [Para 46][Para 47] The union collected 118 fingerprints authorising the deduction of Riels 1,000 a month and notified the employer and the Ministry of its 04 September 2014 election. [Para 51][Para 54]
**Employer (respondent):** the attendance bonus is paid for a whole month, so deducting pro rata would stop it being a monthly bonus; Notification No. 230 makes it monthly, and no deduction is made where there is a proper medical certificate or statutory special leave. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50] No law requires deduction of union dues, and dues are the union's own benefit, which it should collect itself. [Para 52][Para 53]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to deduct the USD 10 attendance bonus pro rata to the number of days absent with its permission, and dismissed the claim for deduction of union dues. [Para 115][Para 116]

On issue 1 the panel started from Article 103 of the Labour Law, which includes sums paid as a bonus within wages, and treated the attendance bonus fixed by Notification No. 230 K.B/S.J.N of 25 July 2012 as a statutory obligation on all textile, garment and footwear employers, not a discretionary instrument of management. [Para 63][Para 88][Para 89][Para 90] Point 2 of that notification, which replaced Notification No. 041 of 07 March 2011, requires a bonus of at least USD 10 a month for workers who come to work regularly for the working days of the month "without absence". [Para 58][Para 60][Para 61] The panel found that neither the labour advisory committee nor any competent body had defined "without absence", and that it could not itself interpret the phrase to cover both authorised and unauthorised absence without ignoring legality and reasonableness. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 71]

It therefore separated the cases. Absences that the law itself provides for — annual leave, special leave, public holidays — do not entitle the employer to deduct anything, and the worker keeps the full USD 10. [Para 68][Para 69] Absence with the employer's permission for personal reasons is not provided for in the law, and the panel must decide each case on its facts. [Para 70][Para 71] It held that permission is an exercise of the employer's discretion under administrative procedure, internal rules and the production line, and that where permission is given the employer has accepted that the line runs without that worker. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 76] Such leave suspends the contract under Articles 71(6) and 72(1): the worker forgoes wages for those days and the employer forgoes the work, without any disciplinary penalty, and the employment and other benefits survive. [Para 77][Para 78][Para 91][Para 92][Para 93][Para 94] It followed that the employer may not cancel the whole bonus but may deduct only the share corresponding to the days of permitted absence. [Para 79][Para 95][Para 96][Para 97]

On issue 2 the panel accepted that Article 129(2) of the Labour Law lets a worker authorise in writing the deduction of union dues from wages and that Article 5 of Prakas No. 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001 requires 15 days' written notice to the employer, who must then comply; it followed Awards 03/03 (Tong Garment), 05/03, 16/05, 62/04 (I Shin) and 60/05 (Evergreen). [Para 98][Para 100][Para 101][Para 102][Para 103] But Article 268 provides that a professional organisation obtains the rights and benefits the Labour Law recognises only when its statutes and the list of its officers are deposited with, and registered by, the Ministry. [Para 108][Para 109] Following Award 133/07 (Dynasty Garment), the panel held that a union without registration has no legal personality and cannot claim the dues; the 118 fingerprints were therefore not enough and the claim was dismissed. [Para 110][Para 111][Para 112][Para 113][Para 114]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a worker is absent with the employer's permission for personal reasons not provided for in the law, the contract is suspended for those days under Articles 71(6) and 72(1), so the employer may reduce the statutory attendance bonus in proportion to the days absent but may not cancel it in full; absences the law itself allows — annual, special and holiday leave — entitle the employer to no deduction at all. [Para 69][Para 94][Para 97]

A union that has not been registered with the Ministry of Labour under Article 268 has no legal personality and cannot require the employer to deduct union dues from members' wages, even where the members have given written consent and the union has served notice under Article 5 of Prakas No. 305. [Para 109][Para 113]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the attendance bonus under Notification No. 230 is a statutory rule binding on all garment employers rather than a management tool, and that no competent authority has yet defined "without absence", are general observations that frame the order rather than decide it. [Para 63][Para 66]

- The number of demands differs across the file: 13 conciliation points at [Para 14], six points in the federation's request letter at [Para 30], and four unresolved points.
- The company's name appears as ខូលៀ លេឌើរ, ខូលៀ លេឌីរ, and once as ថ្ងៃ ខៀ លេឌើរ at [Para 15]; the seat is given as Sihanoukville special economic zone at [Para 1] and as National Road 4, Sangkat Ream at the same line.
- Point 2 of Notification No. 230 is cited as "point 224" at [Para 65]; Prakas No. 305 is dated 22 November 2001 but described as issued by the Ministry of Social Affairs, Labour and Youth Rehabilitation with OCR losses at [Para 100]; the signature block is corrupted to "បន្ទាយមាន : 19449 2017" [Para 118].
- The award was extended from 14 October to 20 October 2014 by agreement, and the panel's invitation letters are dated 01 October 2014 while the hearing is recorded as 10 October 2014. [Para 20][Para 34][Para 12]
