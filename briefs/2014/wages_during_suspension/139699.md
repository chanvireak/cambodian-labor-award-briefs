# Award No. 229/14 — Lang Ji Industry (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 28 August 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Kong Phalha (president) · Doc ID 139699

## 1. Facts
Lang Ji Industry (Cambodia) Co., Ltd operates at Prey Svay, Krang Mkak, Ang Snuol, Kandal. The Cambodia Labour Solidarity Union Federation brought nine demands on 19 August 2014, including reinstatement of four dismissed workers. Conciliation failed the same day. The workers struck, and on 21 August 2014 the panel issued order 043/14 requiring them to stop the strike and return to work while the employer readmitted them. They did not comply. [Para 1][Para 13][Para 14][Para 17][Para 18]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must reinstate the four workers it dismissed without serious fault (Ms M, Mr N, Mr P and Mr V) and pay them statutory compensation? [Para 1]
2. Whether the company must recruit additional sanitation workers, since one is not enough? [Para 2]
3. Whether the company must stop assigning work beyond the workers' strength and restore its previous working principles? [Para 4]
4. Whether the company must remove the Chinese management head who wears glasses? [Para 5]
5. Whether the company must stop deducting wages and bonuses during the strike? [Para 6]
6. Whether the company must accept any medical certificate signed by a qualified doctor, without insisting on its own clinic? [Para 7]
7. Whether the company must distribute overtime across every team and section without discrimination? [Para 8]
8. Whether the company must admit workers who arrive up to ten minutes late, and not warn them? [Para 9]

None of these was decided; the panel closed the file on procedural grounds. [Para 33][Para 48]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The four were dismissed without serious fault and should return with statutory payments; work is over-assigned, sanitation is understaffed, and the Chinese management head should go. After the panel's order the federation took the position that there was no basis to stop striking until the dismissed workers were reinstated, that arbitration was too slow, and that the panel should simply apply its procedure. [Para 1][Para 4][Para 20]
**Employer (respondent):** The company asked the panel on 20 August 2014 for an order suspending the strike, and appeared through counsel with a written authority. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 26]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel terminated the arbitration in case 229/14 without any finding on the nine demands, because the strike continued in defiance of its order. [Para 48][Para 49]

The panel noted at the outset that it made no factual findings on the demands, since no hearing was ever held. [Para 33]

The governing provision was Art 20 of Prakas 099: while an arbitration panel is seised, the parties must suspend every dispute measure, including strikes and lockouts under Art 318 of the Labour Law, and must attend all meetings the panel convenes. Prior awards read that article as barring the panel from proceeding while a strike or lockout is running. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 36]

The panel then reviewed its own practice. In award 04/04 (IMSI) it had held that a party has no right to strike during arbitration and that the panel cannot decide or issue an award on a dispute referred to it in those circumstances, though it may take the case up again once the strike ends. Awards 28/06 and 34/06, 97/07, 110/11 and 17/13 had each been closed for the same reason. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40][Para 41]

Applying that to the file: order 043/14 of 21 August 2014 directed the workers to stop striking and return from the afternoon of that day, and directed the employer to readmit them. The secretariat was then told the workers had not returned. It telephoned the federation president three times — on 22, 25 and 26 August 2014. On 22 August the workers said they would not stop until the dismissed workers were reinstated and that they would not attend the panel because it was too slow; on 25 August they asked for one more day to seek a solution at the Ministry; on 26 August they confirmed the strike continued and asked the panel to apply its procedure. [Para 17][Para 44][Para 20][Para 21][Para 22]

The panel concluded that the workers had no intention of bringing the dispute to it for resolution, since they kept striking without respecting its order, and that it could neither continue the procedure nor hold a hearing. The arbitration was therefore terminated. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a party keeps a strike or lockout running after the panel has ordered it suspended under Art 20 of Prakas 099, the panel will not hear the dispute at all: it closes the file without findings on the merits, leaving the demands undecided until the dispute measure ends and the case can be taken up again. [Para 36][Para 39][Para 48]

**Obiter dicta:** none identified.

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Order 043/14 is dated 21 August 2014 at [Para 29] and [Para 17], but 21 June 2014 at [Para 47], a date that would precede the complaint itself. The nine demands are numbered inconsistently, with demands 1 and 2 run together as "១ + ២/" at [Para 1]. The company name shifts between លាំង ជី អ៊ិន ដាស្ទ្រី, លាំង ជី អ៊ិន ដាស្រី and លាំង ជី. The secretariat's transmission note at [Para 16] is reduced to "លេខ ២៨ /" and its date is unreadable. The award never states the company's line of business, and the four dismissed workers' names survive only as initials.
