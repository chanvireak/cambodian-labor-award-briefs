# Award No. 195/14 — Te Yung Cambodia Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 31 July 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Tang Try, president) · Doc ID 139656

## 1. Facts
Te Yung Cambodia, a factory in Ang Snuol, Kandal, faced a strike from 15 July 2014 over four demands left unsettled by conciliation on 17 July 2014 (report No. 999/14, sent to the panel on 18 July 2014). [Para 12][Para 19][Para 16] The panel issued order No. 039/14 on 23 July 2014 requiring all strikers to suspend the strike immediately and return to their shifts from the morning of 24 July 2014. [Para 20] The strike continued after that date in defiance of the order. [Para 21] The panel then summoned both parties to a pre-hearing meeting on 25 July 2014; neither attended, the employer saying by telephone that it could not leave the factory because the strike was ongoing and that the workers had ignored the 23 July order, and the worker side saying only that it could not attend. [Para 23][Para 46][Para 47][Para 48] The panel terminated the proceedings on 31 July 2014. [Para 50]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must take back six named workers and pay them wages from the date each was stopped until the date each returns to work? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company must pay a midday meal allowance of USD 2 per worker per month? [Para 4]
3. Whether the company must add 3,000 riel to the existing overtime meal allowance for the hours from 4:00 pm to 6:00 pm? [Para 6]
4. Whether the company must remove two managers, Mr S and Mr M, from the company because they suppressed workers' freedoms and stirred conflict with the union and workers? [Para 8]

The panel never reached any of these; it decided only whether the arbitration could continue at all given the ongoing strike. [Para 32]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the record shows only their four demands as filed, including reinstatement with back pay for six named workers and the removal of two managers; they did not attend the pre-hearing and gave the secretariat no reason beyond being unable to come, while confirming that the strike at Te Yung Cambodia was continuing. [Para 2][Para 8][Para 48][Para 49]
**Employer (respondent):** also absent from the pre-hearing, it told the secretariat by telephone that the continuing strike prevented it from leaving the factory, and maintained that the workers had not complied with the panel's order of 23 July. [Para 46][Para 47]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel terminated the arbitration proceedings in case No. 195/14 (Te Yung Cambodia) without ruling on any of the four demands, and no money was ordered. [Para 50][Para 51]
On the threshold question the panel applied clause 20 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, which obliges the parties to suspend every dispute measure — strike, lockout, or any other step that could aggravate the dispute — during the arbitration and to attend all meetings the panel convenes. [Para 32][Para 33] It held that the panel will not continue arbitration while workers are striking or the employer is locking out, and followed its earlier decisions to the same effect. [Para 34][Para 35]
It relied on awards 59/06 (Sang Wu), 39/08 (Chung Yew), 038/09 (Phum Penh?), 110/11 (Simano) and 104/13 (Sabrina Cambodia). [Para 36] It further relied on case 04/04 (IMSI), where notice No. 39 of 29 January 2004 held that the panel cannot decide a collective dispute submitted to it while a strike continues but may take the case up again once the strike ends; and on case 18/04 (hotels), whose second order of 22 April 2004 required all strike, demonstration and disruption to stop from 23 April 2004 and reserved the panel's right to close the proceedings if any party breached the order. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39][Para 40] Case 77/05 (Franc Com Nitting), 110/11 and 17/13 were cited on the same point, and the panel agreed with them. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43]
No hearing was held, so no findings of fact were made on the four demands; the sole documentary evidence before the panel was the conciliation report and minutes and the panel's own invitations and order. [Para 24][Para 31][Para 50]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the workers keep striking, or the employer keeps a lockout, after the panel has ordered suspension under clause 20 of Prakas No. 099 and before the arbitration is complete, the panel will close the proceedings rather than decide the collective dispute, and will reopen only if the strike ends. [Para 34][Para 38][Para 50]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted, without deciding anything on it, that points 1 and 3 of the demands had already been the subject of an order. [Para 3][Para 7]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The termination date appears in the header as 31 July 2014 but the pre-hearing summons is dated "25 July 2019, 2014", and the employer's telephone call and the order are recorded as occurring on 25 and 23 July 2013. [Para 44][Para 46][Para 47] The conciliation report is numbered 999/14 in one paragraph and 998/14 in another. [Para 12][Para 17] Paragraph 15 is a duplicated, partly meaningless fragment about the Kandal labour office. [Para 15] The 23 July order is numbered 039/14 where described and 040/14 in the evidence list. [Para 20][Para 28] Paragraph 50 and 51 repeat the termination order, and the signature block loses the president's name to OCR noise. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 52] Six workers named in demand 1 and the two managers named in demand 4 are recorded here as names redacted. [Para 2][Para 8]
