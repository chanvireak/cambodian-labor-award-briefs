# Award No. 086/14 — J.K. Four Ever Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 April 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Tuon Siphan, worker-appointed; Pen Bunchea, chair, chosen by both) · rights dispute binding at once · Doc ID 139554

## 1. Facts
J.K. Four Ever Co., Ltd cuts and sews garments at Tuol Sangkae, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, with about 764 workers. Of the five demands in the union's complaint of 6 March 2014, conciliation on 26 March 2014 settled two and left three; at the hearing the union withdrew the demand that Mr Y be reinstated and the parties reported agreement on the maternity-wage demand, leaving one point. The company pays a "regular attendance bonus" (ប្រាក់រង្វាន់ធ្វើការទៀងទាត់) of USD 10 a month, and in practice forfeits the whole sum whenever a worker takes leave with the employer's permission without spending accrued annual leave. The union says the forfeiture should be proportionate to the days taken. [Para 14][Para 19][Para 20][Para 21][Para 57][Para 59][Para 61]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company may deduct the whole USD 10 regular attendance bonus for days on which a worker took properly authorised leave, or only a sum proportionate to those days? [Para 59][Para 80]
2. Whether the company must reinstate Mr Y? [Para 4]
3. Whether the company must pay women 50% of three months' wages before maternity leave? [Para 6]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The leave in question was authorised by the employer under the law, and the bonus is part of wages, so it must be reduced only in proportion to the days of absence and not cancelled outright; using up accrued annual leave to cover such leave is not a condition the company may impose. [Para 65][Para 67][Para 68]
**Employer (respondent):** Proportional deduction would encourage workers to stay away and stop them coming regularly; almost all workers still have annual leave left, so the rule bites rarely; and the bonus is the employer's own instrument of encouragement, which it may condition as it wishes. [Para 70][Para 71]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted as to the remaining point — the company must deduct the regular attendance bonus in proportion to the number of days a worker was on leave properly authorised by the employer, and may not require the worker to set accrued annual leave against those days. No order was made on the reinstatement or maternity points, which were withdrawn and agreed respectively. [Para 124][Para 125][Para 19][Para 20]

The panel first classified the claim as a rights dispute, since the bonus is provided for in law rather than left to the parties' bargaining, and so decided it by law and not by equity. [Para 80][Para 81]

On the substance it traced the bonus to Notification No. 230 K.B/S.C.N of 25 July 2012, which replaced Notification No. 041 of 7 March 2011: the earlier text gave at least USD 7 a month to workers who came regularly for the working days of the month, the later one raised the figure to at least USD 10 and added the words "without absence", effective 1 September 2012. Because the notification is a general legal rule binding all textile, garment and footwear employers, the panel held the USD 10 is not a gratuity the employer may design at will but a legal obligation, and — since Article 103 counts bonuses among wages — a wage. [Para 83][Para 85][Para 88][Para 89][Para 114][Para 116]

It then asked what "absence" means, and declined to guess: neither the Labour Law nor the Prakas defines it, and the Labour Advisory Committee's statement of 25 July 2011 did not say whether it covers authorised as well as unauthorised absence. Rather than read the word as covering both, the panel said the question must be decided on the facts of each case. On these facts, leave granted by the employer is a suspension of the contract under Article 71(6), and under Article 72(1) suspension removes the main obligations, including the employer's duty to pay wages; so no wage, and no attendance bonus, is due for those days. But that reasoning cuts only as far as the days actually taken: the employer may not treat the authorised absence as a failure of the whole month's condition. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 95][Para 97][Para 117][Para 121][Para 123]

**Dissent:** Arbitrator Ing Sothy, the employer's nominee, disagreed in an annexed opinion. Treating the bonus as a gratuity whose conditions belong to the giver, he reasoned that a worker who has not met the condition — full attendance — cannot claim any part of it, illustrating with a promise of USD 100 for counting to 1,000 and a sliding scale of USD 10, 8 and 6 for eight, six and four hours worked; on his view the appeal lay to the employer's discretion, not to the panel. [Para 127][Para 128][Para 135][Para 142][Para 147]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a sector-wide notification fixes an attendance bonus as a legal minimum, a worker's authorised leave suspends the contract for those days only, so the employer may reduce the bonus in proportion to the days of authorised leave but may not forfeit the whole month's bonus, and may not force the worker to spend accrued annual leave to protect it. [Para 89][Para 121][Para 123][Para 124]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's refusal to construe "without absence" in Notification No. 230 one way or the other, leaving the meaning of unauthorised absence to be worked out case by case, and its remark that a large bonus withheld for a single authorised hour would be unjust to the worker as a full forfeiture would be to the employer. [Para 93][Para 97][Para 113]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The date of the panel's constitution is printed as "02 April 20 2014" (para 16) and the extension of the award date as "from 25 April 2014 to 1 30 April 2014" (para 29); Prakas No. 099 is dated "201 2004" (para 75), the Labour Advisory Committee statement "25 11 July 2011" (para 90), and Notification No. 230 is preceded by a stray "2015" (para 115). The company appears as J.K. Four Ever, J.K. For Ever and J.K. Four Ever Digital, and the Council's address as Boeung Rak II where the rest of the file says Boeung Kak II. The award records the withdrawal and the settlement of issues 2 and 3 without any reasons, and the company's evidence list includes a warning letter and probation contract for a Ms C Sochean (paras 44–46) who is not mentioned in the findings, so those documents cannot be tied to any issue. [Para 16][Para 29][Para 44][Para 75][Para 90][Para 115]
