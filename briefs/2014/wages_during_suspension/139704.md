# Award No. 235/14 — HIS Gavon Ei Beryl (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 18 September 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer, dissenting; An Non, appointed by the workers; Run Saray, president) · Doc ID 139704

## 1. Facts
HIS Gavon Ei Beryl (Cambodia) Ltd cuts and sews garments and employs 480 workers. From 17 July 2014 the company had no buyer orders and no work to give; it invited workers to transfer voluntarily to a sister company, Gavon, in Takhmao, and told those who declined that they would receive USD 50 if they came in with their code card and USD 40 if they stayed at home. The union claimed full wages for the period without work; two of the 139 listed workers were excluded for want of an authorisation or attendance, leaving 136 for whom the panel decided the claim. [Para 38][Para 49]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to pay 100% of wages to the workers while it had no work for them to do between 17 July 2014 and 1 September 2014? [Para 81]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer suspended the employment contracts without first obtaining the proper authorisation of the Ministry of Labour and Vocational Training, contrary to the Labour Law, so the suspension was unlawful and full wages are owed for the idle period. [Para 67]
**Employer (respondent):** There was no work because no buyer placed orders; workers who declined the transfer were offered USD 40–50, which the union's claim ignores, and the company did notify the Labour Inspectorate and submit to an inspection meeting on 30 July 2014 before suspending contracts from 1 August to 1 September 2014. [Para 68][Para 70][Para 96]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to pay 100% of wages to 136 workers for the suspension periods: 80 workers from 17 July to 1 September 2014, 42 workers from 6 August to 1 September 2014, and the 15 quality-control and packing workers from 16 August to 1 September 2014. [Para 110][Para 111][Para 112]
The panel set out Article 71 of the Labour Law, under which an enterprise facing serious economic or material difficulty may suspend its activity for no more than two months, subject to the supervision of the labour inspector, and Article 72, under which a suspension affects the principal obligations of the contract — the worker's obligation to work and the employer's obligation to pay wages — unless a contrary provision requires the employer to keep paying. [Para 81][Para 86]
Following its earlier awards, the panel held that where an employer has properly carried out the suspension procedure, with the labour inspector supervising, it owes no wages; but where the procedure was not followed, the employer must pay 100% of wages even though no work was given. [Para 88]
On the facts it found the company gave no work to the 136 workers from 17 July to 1 September 2014, and that for the period before 1 August it never showed any request to the Labour Inspectorate at all. For the one-month suspension from 1 August to 1 September 2014 the employer did notify the inspectorate, and inspectors held an information meeting at the company on 30 July 2014, but the panel held that a meeting minute recording what was said is not a written authorisation: the inspector's signature meant only that the participants had said what the minute records. No sufficient evidence of a written agreement by the labour inspector therefore existed, so the suspension was unlawful and Article 72's wage obligation was not displaced. The panel added that paying USD 40 or USD 50 for the idle period could not release the employer from paying 100% of wages. [Para 97][Para 104][Para 105]
Arbitrator Ing Sothy recorded a dissenting opinion, annexed to the award under Article 37 of Prakas No. 099: he considered the inspection of 30 July 2014 and the one-month suspension to have been procedurally regular, since a minute existed and the company had done what the law required, and the Labour Law requires only that a suspension be supervised by the labour inspector, not that it be authorised in writing. [Para 123][Para 126]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of employment contracts under Article 71 of the Labour Law relieves the employer of the duty to pay wages only if the statutory procedure was properly completed; supervision by the labour inspector requires a written authorisation or agreement, and a record of an information meeting signed by the inspector is not such an authorisation, so an employer that produces only a meeting minute must pay 100% of wages for the idle period. [Para 87][Para 104][Para 105]
A payment of USD 40 or USD 50 offered in place of work does not discharge the obligation to pay full wages where the suspension was unlawful. [Para 98]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the company and the Gavon factory in Takhmao were treated as belonging to the same employer, and that voluntary transfers did not affect the position of workers who declined to move. [Para 78]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award carries a dissent annexed under Article 37 of Prakas No. 099, so the reasoning in §4 is that of the majority and the dissenting view is reported separately; the dissent states the suspension ran to "1 December September 2014". [Para 123][Para 124] The number of packing workers is given as 14 in the claim summary and as 12 in the order, though the total of 136 is consistent with the higher figure only when added to 80 and 42 workers. [Para 112][Para 116] The employer's name appears in at least eight Khmer spellings ("ហ៊ឹស", "ហ្វឹស", "ហ្វីស", "ហ្វឺស" + "ហ្គាវ័ន្ត"/"ហ្គាវ័ន្ទ" + "អី ភែរីល"/"អី ភែរឹល"), and the case name on the face of the award differs from the company name in the body. [Para 1][Para 38] The transcript is heavily corrupted by OCR in the annexes. [Para 120] The header gives 18 September 2014 as the award date, which matches the date of signature.
