# Award No. 310/14 — Aeros Ho Smith Footwear Cham Kat (Arbitration Council, 15 December 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Virak, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chheam, president) · Doc ID 139758

## 1. Facts
Eight workers of a footwear factory employing about 300 staff in Battambang referred 22 demands to the Arbitration Council after conciliation at the Provincial Department of Labour and Vocational Training failed on 30 October 2014. [Para 28][Para 48] By the hearing 13 demands had been settled, three were withdrawn by the workers, and six remained for decision. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 36] The parties elected a non-binding award. [Para 37]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 8) Whether the employer must reduce the regular-attendance bonus in proportion to the days a worker was absent with the employer's permission, instead of deducting it in full? [Para 55]
2. (Issue 10) Whether the employer must allow pregnant workers one day per month to attend antenatal health checks while preserving wages and other benefits? [Para 62]
3. (Issue 12) Whether the employer must grant 90 days' maternity leave and pay 50 per cent of wages and allowances before delivery? [Para 68]
4. (Issue 15) Whether pregnant workers must be allowed to leave work 15 minutes before the end of the shift? [Para 73]
5. (Issue 20) Whether the employer must deduct union dues from the wages of members of the four local unions? [Para 79]
6. (Issue 21) Whether the employer must pay wages for annual leave taken during the two-and-a-half-day strike of 27 to 28 October and the morning of 30 October 2014? [Para 86]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the regular-attendance bonus is a legal entitlement, so it may only be reduced in proportion to the days of permitted absence; the antenatal, maternity and early-departure benefits are already applied by other garment and footwear factories, and the 15-minute rule was applied by this company itself from 1 October 2014, which makes it a benefit more favourable than the law and therefore binding. [Para 56][Para 66][Para 75]
**Employer (respondent):** it applies the Labour Law; the 10 US dollar bonus is payable only to workers present on every working day of the month and is a discretionary incentive; it cannot grant the pregnancy claims; the 15-minute practice was never authorised in writing by the director; no local union is registered, so no dues may be deducted; and under Article 332 a strike suspends the contract, so no wages are owed. [Para 59][Para 67][Para 76][Para 84][Para 89]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issue 8 granted: the employer is ordered to reduce the regular-attendance bonus in proportion to the days a worker was absent with the employer's permission; Issues 10, 12, 15, 20 and 21 dismissed. [Para 163][Para 164][Para 167]
On Issue 8 the panel traced the bonus to Notification No. 041 K.B/S.Ch.N of 7 March 2011, which set a minimum of 7 US dollars per month, replaced by Notification No. 230 K.B/S.Ch.N of 25 July 2012, point 2, which raised the minimum to 10 US dollars per month from 1 September 2012. [Para 93][Para 95] It held that Notification 230 is a legal regulation of general application binding on every textile, garment and footwear employer, not a bonus the employer is free to design. [Para 98] Absences created by the law itself — annual leave, special leave, maternity leave, public holidays and weekly rest — do not reduce the bonus at all, so the worker receives the full 10 US dollars. [Para 103][Para 104] Absence with the employer's permission is not regulated by any text, and the panel expressly declined to interpret the unqualified word "absence" in Notification 230 in either direction. [Para 105][Para 106] Because leave granted by the employer suspends the contract under Articles 71(6) and 72(1), no wage is owed for those days, and Article 103 counts sums paid as a bonus within wages; the employer may therefore withhold only the proportionate part, never the whole bonus. [Para 122][Para 125][Para 126][Para 130] Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented on this issue, reasoning that a conditional bonus is not wages, whereas a gratification under Article 103 is wages that cannot be apportioned, so prorating was wrong. [Para 172][Para 182][Para 184]
On Issues 10, 12 and 15 the panel asked first whether it could decide at all: Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 requires a party to appear in person or through a person holding written authority. [Para 132] None of the eight claimants was pregnant, no pregnant worker attended the hearing, and no pregnant worker had transferred her rights to the eight claimants, so no identified worker was claiming these benefits. [Para 62][Para 133][Para 135][Para 141] The panel therefore declined to consider the three demands without reaching their merits. [Para 136][Para 140][Para 144]
On Issue 20 the panel applied Article 129(2), which permits deduction of union dues only on the member's written authorisation, reading it consistently with earlier awards as requiring a request made after the union has been registered with the Ministry. [Para 145][Para 148] The four local unions were unregistered and no authorisation letters reached the Council by the filing deadline of 24 November 2014, so the conditions were not met. [Para 151][Para 152]
On Issue 21 the panel applied Article 170, which places annual leave at Khmer New Year unless the parties agree otherwise and notify the labour inspector in advance; the parties had agreed nothing, so annual leave could not be exchanged for paid strike days. [Para 154][Para 157][Para 161]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A regular-attendance bonus fixed by ministerial notification is a legally mandated wage component of general application, not a discretionary incentive: where a worker is absent with the employer's permission the contract is suspended, and the employer may reduce that bonus only in proportion to the days absent, never forfeit it in full. [Para 98][Para 130]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that it could not interpret the unqualified word "absence" in Notification 230 and would assess each claim on its own facts; it also noted that several other garment and footwear factories grant one antenatal day per month. [Para 66][Para 106]
