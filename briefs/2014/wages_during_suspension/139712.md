# Award No. 251/14 — Huychian (Mean Chey Branch) (Arbitration Council, 26 September 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Ang Eng Thong chair) · Doc ID 139712

## 1. Facts
Huychian (Mean Chey Branch) is a factory at Kbal Koh, Chbar Ampov, Phnom Penh, which changed its registered name from Huychian to Chian Ly while keeping the workers' seniority. [Para 1][Para 3] The Khmer Union Federation filed two demands — payment of five statutory termination benefits, and reinstatement of Ms S — and conciliation on 1 September 2014 failed on both. [Para 2][Para 4][Para 12][Para 13] On 15 September 2014 about 1,120 workers struck inside the compound, demanding payment on termination of contract; that afternoon the panel declined to hold the hearing and convened a pre-hearing meeting instead, and on the same day issued Order No. 049/14 requiring the strike to stop and all strikers to return from the afternoon of 16 September 2014. [Para 15][Para 18][Para 21] The strike continued and no worker returned up to 18 September 2014. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay five statutory termination benefits — payment in lieu of annual leave, severance for dismissal, moral damages, the last month's wage and payment in lieu of notice — because it changed its name from Huychian to Chian Ly? [Para 2]
2. Whether the employer must take Ms S back into employment, since she did not commit the serious misconduct of which she is accused? [Para 4]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the name change from Huychian to Chian Ly entitles them to the five termination payments, and Ms S should be reinstated because the alleged serious misconduct is not made out; they signed thumb-printed petitions opposing the name change unless the payments were made first. [Para 2][Para 4][Para 33][Para 35]
**Employer (respondent):** the name change preserved all the workers' seniority and the company is waiting for the panel's decision; Ms S cannot be reinstated because she committed serious misconduct under the internal rules and Article 83 of the Labour Law (defrauding the company). [Para 3][Para 5]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction / proceedings terminated — the panel terminated the arbitration in case No. 251/14 without deciding either demand, and made no monetary order. [Para 53][Para 54]

The only question the panel took up was whether to continue the arbitration. [Para 42] It applied Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY of 21 April 2004, which requires the parties to suspend every dispute measure such as a strike or lockout during the arbitration and to attend all meetings to which the panel invites them. [Para 42][Para 43] It recorded that its earlier awards — Nos 59/06, 39/08, 137/09, 138/09, 97/07, 63/08, 110/11 and 17/13 — hold that it will not continue an arbitration while the workers are striking or the employer is locking out, and that it had terminated proceedings on that ground in cases Nos 28/06, 34/06, 66/14, 92/14, 96/14 and 111/14. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 47][Para 48][Para 50] It relied too on the notice of 29 January 2004 in case No. 04/04, which stated that a party has no right to strike during its own arbitration, that the panel cannot resolve the dispute while the strike lasts, and that the case may be taken up again once all the unions stop striking. [Para 45][Para 46]

Applying that to the facts, the panel found the strike never stopped: the employer's administrative assistant confirmed by telephone at 2:44 p.m. on 16 September that the workers had not returned, a federation official confirmed at 4:09 p.m. the same day that the strike continued, and the employer's letter of 18 September 2014 confirmed that no worker had come to work. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24][Para 51] It concluded that the workers had no intention of having the collective dispute resolved by the Council, and terminated the case. [Para 41][Para 52][Para 53]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where workers continue a strike — or an employer a lockout — after the Arbitration Council has ordered it suspended under Article 20 of Prakas No. 099, the panel terminates the arbitration without hearing the merits, and the dispute may be revived only once all the unions have stopped striking and the workers have returned to work. [Para 42][Para 46][Para 52][Para 53]

**Obiter dicta:** the recital of prior awards and of the 2004 notice in case No. 04/04 illustrates consistent practice rather than deciding anything separately, and the panel's observation that a name change preserving seniority does not by itself trigger termination payments was not reached, both demands having fallen away with the case. [Para 45][Para 48][Para 50]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The two demands appear only in the conciliation list at [Para 2] and [Para 4]; no findings or reasoning on their merits exist, because the panel never held a hearing on them [Para 41].
- The demand for five termination benefits is garbled: it lists "payment in lieu of annual leave, severance for dismissal, moral damages, the last month's wage and payment in lieu of notice" as if all five followed from a mere change of name [Para 2], which the employer contradicts at [Para 3].
- The company's name is printed three ways — "Huychian", "Huychian (Cambodia) Co., Ltd" and "Chian Ly" — and the respondent is called "Huychian (Cambodia)" in the operative part although the case name is the Mean Chey branch [Para 1][Para 3][Para 22][Para 54].
- The award is dated 26 September 2014 [Para 1], but the phone record of the secretariat is printed with a stray "2015" at [Para 22].
- The hearing date is inconsistent: the panel says it would not sit on the afternoon of 15 September 2014 and held a pre-hearing meeting instead [Para 18], yet the same date is given for both the pre-hearing meeting [Para 10] and the hearing [Para 14].
- Two paragraphs duplicate the same thumb-print petition verbatim, with OCR corruption in the second [Para 33][Para 34]; the arbitrator list in the signature block omits the employer-party arbitrator's name [Para 54].
