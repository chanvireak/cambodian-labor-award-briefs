# Award No. 160/14 — Ocean Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 9 July 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Ing Sothy (employer-appointed, dissenting), An Nan (worker-appointed), Kong Phallack (chair) · Doc ID 139613 · unofficial English translation of the Khmer original

## 1. Facts
Ocean Garment Co., Ltd ("Ocean") is a garment manufacturer in Phnom Penh employing about 1,460 workers. [Para 10][Para 11] On 21 May 2014 its director wrote to the Ministry of Labour requesting a one-month suspension of production from 26 May to 26 June 2014, citing monthly losses and disruption of buyer orders. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 21] On 24 May 2014 two Deputy Heads of the Labour Inspection Office interviewed the parties and minutes were taken recording the one-month suspension, but no written authorisation was issued. [Para 21][Para 31] The union demanded full wages and benefits for the suspended month, or termination with compensation. [Para 1]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay full wages and perquisites to the workers during the one-month contract suspension from 26 May 2014 to 26 June 2014? [Para 1][Para 25]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The suspension was neither made under labour inspection nor authorised by the Labour Inspector, and no prior notice was given, contrary to clause 17 of the 11 July 2013 agreement between Ocean and the union; the workers were entitled to their monthly US$104, the US$119 team-leader minimum, the US$10 attendance bonus, US$7 accommodation and transport allowance, seniority bonus and skill bonuses. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 14]
**Employer (respondent):** It notified the Ministry on 21 May 2014 and inspectors came on 24 May 2014; the suspension was forced by a financial crisis caused by strikes since January 2014 that had cost US$2 million in disrupted orders, and it was paying the US$7 accommodation allowance to 1,655 workers. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 21] It offered to seek the owner's approval to pay 50 per cent of wages if the workers would settle for that. [Para 2]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to provide full wages and benefits to the workers during the employment contract suspension for the one month from 26 May 2014 to 26 June 2014. [Para 33]
The panel began with Article 72(1) of the Labour Law, under which suspension relieves both main obligations: the worker need not work and the employer need not pay unless a provision requires otherwise; on that basis alone no wages would be owed. [Para 26][Para 27] It then held that Article 72(1) applies only to suspensions that comply with Article 71. [Para 28] Article 71(II) permits suspension where the enterprise faces serious economic or material difficulty or other particularly unusual difficulty, for no more than two months and under the control of the Labour Inspector. [Para 29]
Following case No. 58/07 (8 Star Sportwear) and case No. 08/07 (Siu Quinh Garment, issue 2), and the earlier awards in No. 72/05 (North Gaiety Apparel, issue 1), No. 22/05 (Ocean, issue 2) and No. 198/13 (Soo Apparel (Cambodia), issue 2), the panel read "under the control of the Labour Inspector" as requiring the employer to give notice and obtain prior written authorisation before suspending. [Para 30] It distinguished North Gaiety Apparel, where a written authorisation dated 13 December 2005 made the suspension lawful. [Para 31]
On the facts, both parties agreed inspectors attended on 24 May 2014 and announced the suspension, and the employer had given notice; but the inspectors issued no letter in writing and recorded no recommendation in the minutes of interview authorising the suspension. [Para 31][Para 32] The statutory sequence — notice, inspection interview, then written authorisation — was therefore incomplete, so the suspension was not made in accordance with the Labour Law, Article 72(1) was not engaged, and full wages and benefits were owed. [Para 33]
The panel refused to consider the employer's letter of 8 July 2014, submitted at 4:33 p.m. one day before the award, alleging that union officers had threatened a Bangladeshi factory leader and blocked vehicles: it treated the allegations as one-sided and not established as fact. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24] Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented, finding that the inspectors' attendance and minutes of 24 May 2014 amounted to inspection, that the law requires suspension to be "under inspection" rather than written authorisation, and that the employer had also complied with Article 72 by paying the US$7 allowance. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39][Para 41]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of operations under Article 71(II) of the Labour Law is lawful only where the Labour Inspector has authorised it in writing after inspecting the enterprise; attendance at the factory and minutes of interview are not enough, and without that authorisation Article 72(1) does not release the employer from paying full wages and benefits for the suspended period. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 33]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's refusal to treat the employer's late allegations of worker threats as facts, and its remark that the letter arrived one day before the award, were not necessary to the order. [Para 22][Para 23]
