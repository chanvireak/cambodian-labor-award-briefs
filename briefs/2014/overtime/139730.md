# Award No. 259/14 — Cambodia Air Port Management Services (Arbitration Council, 14 November 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, employer-chosen; An Non, worker-chosen; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · Doc ID 139730

## 1. Facts
Cambodia Air Port Management Services (CAMS), registered in commerce under certificate No. 2731 of 24 May 2011, provides airport management services and employs about 916 staff. [Para 25][Para 45] The claimant is the Independent Airport Workers Union (ឯករាជ្យ ព្រលានយន្តហោះ អន្តរជាតិ), whose sixth-term leadership was recognised by the Ministry in letter No. 1436 of 12 December 2012. [Para 2][Para 46][Para 47] On 31 July 2014 the union complained of three demands; one was settled at conciliation on 2 September 2014 and, after the hearing of 14 October 2014 settled issue 1, only issue 2 remained, referred under non-conciliation report No. 1055 of 10 September 2014. [Para 7][Para 10][Para 12][Para 17][Para 18] The company runs two kinds of training: courses it arranges itself, and courses notified to it by client airlines at short notice whose dates it cannot change. [Para 56] Staff attendance at training falling on their weekly rest day is the subject of the claim. [Para 48]

## 2. Issues in dispute
For the panel's single surviving issue 2, two questions:
1. Whether attendance at employer-organised training that falls on an employee's weekly rest day counts as overtime work? [Para 65]
2. Whether the employer must pay wages at double rate for that attendance, or may discharge the obligation by giving a replacement rest day later? [Para 78][Para 88]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the weekly rest day is a day on which staff have a right to rest, and coming in for the company's training programme is the same as coming to work, so the employer must pay double wages as it does for overtime. [Para 49][Para 50]
**Employer (respondent):** it cannot comply because it grants a replacement day off for the day on which staff had to attend training, which also serves their health and wellbeing; training is arranged to meet the demands of the airlines that use CAMS's airport services and also develops staff skills; and in case 57/12 at Siem Reap International Airport it settled the same demand with another union without paying overtime. [Para 51][Para 53][Para 54][Para 62][Para 85]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer is ordered, when it requires staff to attend training on their weekly rest day, to treat the attendance as overtime and to pay overtime wages for it in accordance with the Labour Law. [Para 94][Para 95][Para 96] The award is binding and becomes final immediately between the parties under their agreement of 14 October 2014. [Para 97]

On whether the training is work, the panel applied Article 137 of the Labour Law, under which normal working time may not exceed 8 hours a day or 48 hours a week, and Articles 11.6 and 11.7 of the collective agreement of 2 April 2014, under which weekly hours run between 40 and 48 with an average of 44 hours, or 2,288 hours a year. [Para 65][Para 57][Para 58][Para 59][Para 68] It found the company's scheduling lawful and within those limits, and held that whenever the employer has fixed the weekly hours, work beyond them is overtime. [Para 67][Para 69][Para 70]

The panel then characterised the training: it is arranged by the employer to meet the requirements of the airlines using the airport, and it also develops the staff's own skills. [Para 71][Para 72] On that basis it held the training to be a duty the employee must perform for the employer and part of the employee's work. [Para 73][Para 74] It drew the limit expressly: training not arranged by the employer and attended purely for the individual's own benefit, and not required by the employer, is not performance of work for the employer. [Para 75] Attendance of the kind in this case being performance of work, a worker who attends voluntarily on a weekly rest day is working beyond normal hours, which is overtime. [Para 76][Para 77]

On the rate, the panel applied new Article 139 of the Law on Amendment of Articles 139 and 144 of the Labour Law: overtime on ordinary days attracts a 50 per cent premium, while overtime at night between 10:00 p.m. and 5:00 a.m., or on a weekly rest day, attracts 100 per cent. [Para 78][Para 79] It followed Award 30/13 (printed only in damaged Khmer script), Issue 19, that a worker who works overtime on a weekly rest day is entitled to overtime wages at the equivalent percentage of ordinary wages. [Para 80][Para 81] Attendance at training on a rest day being overtime under limb (a), the double rate followed, and the employer was found obliged to pay it. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 87][Para 93]

The panel rejected both of the employer's answers. Substituting a rest day later is not lawful, because the worker has already performed the overtime and did not in fact rest in the week in which the employer required the work, whereas the law and policy of weekly rest exist to protect health and to balance work with personal life. [Para 88][Para 89][Para 90] The Siem Reap agreement in case 57/12 does not bind this claimant, since the union in this case, the Independent Airport Workers Union, was not a party to it, so the panel gave it no weight. [Para 91][Para 92]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Training that the employer organises and requires in order to meet its own operational needs is part of the employee's work; attendance at such training on a weekly rest day is overtime, payable at the 100 per cent rate under Article 139, and granting a replacement rest day afterwards does not discharge that obligation. [Para 74][Para 77][Para 79][Para 90]

**Obiter dicta:** training that the employer did not arrange and that an employee attends purely for his own benefit, and which the employer does not require, is not performance of work for the employer and attracts no overtime. [Para 75]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is a Khmer original with heavy OCR damage; the party list at Paragraph 1 loses several representative names to repetition, and several paragraphs break mid-sentence across page markers. [Para 1][Para 17][Para 82] The case is numbered 259/14 in the header and in the file itself, but the union's letter of 14 October 2014 refers to case No. 269/14, and both are printed. [Para 29][Para 34] The company's registration is given as certificate No. 2731 of 24 May 2011 in the evidence list and as "Inv.110 E/1995" dated 24 May 2011 in the findings, where "1995" cannot be reconciled with a 2011 certificate. [Para 25][Para 45] The extension of the award date is printed "from 6 October 2014 to 14 November 2014" with a stray digit ("ឆ្នាំ ២ ២០១៤"). [Para 21] The precedent cited for the double rate is named only in damaged Khmer script and is reported here as printed, without a reliable English title. [Para 80] Paragraph 96 repeats the order already given at Paragraph 94. [Para 94][Para 96]
