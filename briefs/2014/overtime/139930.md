# Award No. 283/13 — Yong Sing (Arbitration Council, 23 January 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៊ីង សុទ្ធី for the employer, លីវ សុណ្ណា for the workers, គង់ ផល្ហាំ chair) · Doc ID 139930

## 1. Facts
The respondent operates a garment factory making woven shirts and employs about 600 workers, some 90 per cent of them women. [Para 43][Para 47] The factory union had a Ministry receipt for its registration application dated 30 October 2013, and 14 workers transferred their right to sue to the Khmer Youth Voice Federation. [Para 34][Para 44][Para 45] Fifteen demands went to conciliation, at which the employer did not appear; the workers then withdrew ten of them, leaving five: a nursery and breastfeeding room, an infirmary with a duty nurse, a lawful basic wage, the USD 7 transport and accommodation allowance with arrears, and genuinely voluntary overtime. [Para 24][Para 29][Para 30]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must build a nursery and a breastfeeding room for the workers? [Para 2][Para 72]
2. Whether the employer must provide an infirmary and a nurse or doctor on duty throughout working hours? [Para 4][Para 83]
3. Whether the employer must pay the basic wage required by law, including to workers paid by output? [Para 52][Para 103]
4. Whether the employer must pay the USD 7 monthly transport and accommodation allowance and reimburse what was not paid in the past? [Para 55][Para 121]
5. Whether the employer must organise overtime on a voluntary basis without coercion? [Para 58][Para 137]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** about 90 per cent of a 600-strong workforce are women, yet the employer has neither built a nursery nor paid anything in lieu of one; there is no infirmary or doctor on duty; some monthly-paid workers receive less than USD 80 and output-paid workers are not topped up when their piece-rate earnings fall below the minimum; the USD 7 allowance has never been paid; and a section head warned Mr L for declining overtime. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 50][Para 52][Para 56][Para 63]
**Employer (respondent):** absent. It did not attend the conciliation of 16 December 2013 or the hearing of 16 January 2014, gave no valid reason, and filed no documents. [Para 24][Para 28][Para 70]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — all five remaining claims were allowed: a nursery and breastfeeding room, or payment for childcare elsewhere; an infirmary with one nurse on duty and a doctor present at least two hours a day; a basic wage of USD 80 a month for the 14 claimants; the USD 7 monthly transport and accommodation allowance with arrears; and voluntary overtime. The award is not binding and becomes final 8 days after notification absent written objection. [Para 150][Para 151][Para 152][Para 153][Para 154][Para 155]

The panel first held that it could proceed in the employer's absence: Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 allows the panel to continue, or to close the case by issuing an award, where a party duly invited fails to appear without valid reason, and earlier awards had conducted hearings in absentia on that basis. [Para 65][Para 67][Para 71]

On the nursery, Article 186(1) requires any enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a breastfeeding room and a nursery, on site or nearby, and Article 186(2) requires the employer to pay the cost of childcare elsewhere where the enterprise cannot take children over 18 months. The panel noted that the employer had neither built the facilities nor paid for outside care. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74][Para 81]

On health care, Article 238 requires primary treatment for workers and Article 242 requires an infirmary on the premises of any enterprise employing at least 50 workers, staffed by a doctor with one or more nurses and covered permanently in day and night shifts, at the employer's cost; Article 1 of joint Prakas No. 330 of 6 December 2000 repeats the 50-worker threshold and Article 3 sets staffing by headcount. For an enterprise of 600, the schedule requires a nurse on duty and a doctor present at least two hours, and the panel ordered exactly that. [Para 84][Para 88][Para 89][Para 91][Para 94][Para 102]

On wages, point 2 of Notification No. 103 of 9 April 2013 fixes USD 80 a month for workers past probation, and point 3 requires output-paid workers to receive their actual piece-rate earnings, with the employer topping up to USD 80 where those earnings fall short. The 14 claimants' pay records showed basic wages below USD 80, and the employer produced nothing to contradict them, so the shortfall was ordered paid. [Para 104][Para 111][Para 116][Para 120]

On the allowance, point 1 of Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 fixed USD 7 a month from 1 September 2012, and the employer had never paid it. Because the claimants could not say when they or the company had started, the panel fixed the arrears at the date the notification took effect rather than at an unknown start date, ordering reimbursement from 1 September 2012. [Para 122][Para 123][Para 134][Para 135][Para 136]

On overtime, Article 4 of Prakas No. 80 of 1 March 1999 makes overtime voluntary: the employer may ask, the worker decides, and no pressure or disciplinary measure may follow a refusal. A warning issued to Mr L for declining overtime breached that rule, and the panel ordered voluntary arrangements. [Para 137][Para 140][Para 148][Para 149]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The statutory welfare duties are unconditional and measured by headcount, not by the employer's means: at 100 women workers a nursery and breastfeeding room must exist or be paid for elsewhere, and at 50 workers an infirmary staffed to the Prakas No. 330 schedule must exist, at the employer's cost. [Para 74][Para 89][Para 91] Output-paid workers must be topped up to the statutory minimum wage whenever piece-rate earnings fall below it, and the allowance under Notification No. 230 accrues from 1 September 2012, so arrears are owed from that date even where the workers cannot prove their own start dates. [Para 111][Para 134] A warning for refusing overtime is unlawful, because Prakas No. 80 makes overtime voluntary and forbids any disciplinary consequence for refusal. [Para 140][Para 148]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's statement that it could equally have closed the file without an award under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099, and its remark that it could not establish when the company began operating, were observations that did not affect any order. [Para 65][Para 133]
