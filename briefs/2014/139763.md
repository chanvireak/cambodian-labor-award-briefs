# Award No. 317/14 — Huy Chhean (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 18 December 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Suthy, employer-appointed; On Non, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · Doc ID 139763

## 1. Facts
Workers at Huy Chhean (Cambodia) Co., Ltd., a shoe factory in Kbal Koh, Chbar Ampov, Phnom Penh with about 1,220 employees, filed a complaint on 28 October 2014 after the employer stopped letting them into the production building from 29 September 2014. [Para 12][Para 65][Para 66][Para 71] The employer treated roughly 1,100 of them as having abandoned their jobs for not complying with a court protective order of 19 September 2014. [Para 72] Four demands went to conciliation; one was settled at the hearing and the rest were considered together. [Para 18][Para 19]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Issue 1 — Whether the employer had to reopen the production building so that all workers could return to work normally? [Para 71]
2. Issue 2 — Whether, if it did not reopen, the employer had to pay seniority compensation to all workers in accordance with the Labour Law? [Para 3]
3. Issue 4 — Whether the employer had to maintain all wages and other benefits from 20 September 2014, and throughout the period from 29 September 2014 until the doors were reopened? [Para 5]
4. A further Issue 4 — Whether the employer had to take Mr H, a staff delegate, back into normal work? [Para 114]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** they came to work on 20 September 2014 in compliance with the protective order, but the employer opened only a small gate rather than the main entrance, set tables outside to lend 20 to 50 USD against the Pchum Ben holiday, and never opened the production building. [Para 73][Para 74] They asked for reinstatement with back pay, or severance if the employer refused. [Para 71]

**Employer (respondent):** the workers abandoned their jobs by disregarding protective order No. 244 "M" of 19 September 2014; the main gate was kept shut only to avoid disorder during the strike, and more than 100 workers did clock in, so the building was never closed. [Para 72][Para 75] As to the delegate, it had warned him repeatedly, suspended his contract on 9 September 2014 and applied to the labour dispute department on 12 September 2014 for permission to dismiss him. [Para 89][Para 93][Para 94]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider both the mass reinstatement and back-pay claim and the delegate's reinstatement claim, leaving them to the court and to the ministerial appeal procedure respectively. No money was ordered. [Para 129][Para 130]

On the 1,100 workers, the panel found that on 2 December 2014 the workers produced an appeal judgment No. 403 "M" of 1 December 2014 against the protective order, and that this appeal was still travelling through the procedure of the Phnom Penh Court of First Instance. [Para 101][Para 111] Because it could not be established whether the workers had breached the protective order, the panel held it could not decide the reinstatement, back-pay or severance claims and declined to consider them. [Para 112][Para 113]

On the delegate, Article 293 of the Labour Law requires the labour inspector's agreement before a staff delegate is dismissed, and Article 293(4) deems the employer's request rejected if no decision is notified within one month. [Para 114][Para 118][Para 119] The employer applied on 12 September 2014 and, by the hearing on 27 November 2014, more than a month had passed without a response, so the request was deemed refused. [Para 121][Para 123] The employer asserted it had appealed to the Minister but never filed the appeal documents despite the panel's order, and the panel found the two-month appeal window had not expired, so the matter was still within the administrative procedure and it declined to consider reinstatement. [Para 124][Para 126][Para 127][Para 128]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the factual premise of a claim — here, whether workers breached a court protective order — is itself pending before another tribunal, the panel will decline to consider the claim rather than decide it on an assumed premise. Likewise, where an employer's request to dismiss a staff delegate has been deemed refused under Article 293(4) but the period for appealing to the Minister has not run out, the panel will not order reinstatement, because the administrative route is still open. [Para 112][Para 126]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that under Article 293 every dismissal of a protected staff delegate requires the agreement of the labour inspector and of the Minister responsible for labour, not merely the employer's internal decision. [Para 119]
