# Award No. 011/14 — Sau Len (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 13 February 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (យូ សួន ទី appointed by the employer party, អន ណន appointed by the worker party, ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា chair) · non-binding award · Doc ID 139508

## 1. Facts
Sau Len (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. — the factory also appears as Southland — makes garments and hats at Trapaing Thleung, Chom Chao, Porsenchey, Phnom Penh, with about 1,800 workers. [Para 81][Para 82] Of ten demands filed after the collective dispute, two were settled in conciliation and, at the hearing, the workers withdrew all but one: the reinstatement of Ms L Sok and Mr N Touch, who signed letters of resignation dated 29 October 2013 bearing their names and thumbprints and acknowledging receipt of all terminal payments, receiving USD 224.01 and USD 144.53 respectively. [Para 18][Para 27][Para 28][Para 109][Para 110] The workers say the two were coerced into resigning and into leaving the union. [Para 97][Para 98]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer in fact coerced and threatened the two workers into resigning from their jobs? [Para 140]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** a group inside the factory threatened the two almost daily, telling them they would face trouble on the road home if they did not resign from the job and the union, and when they refused the employer forced them to thumbprint resignation letters; they would not be claiming reinstatement had there been no coercion. [Para 98][Para 101][Para 103][Para 153]
**Employer (respondent):** the two came to the administration office themselves asking for resignation forms, the director met them and urged them not to leave but they insisted they had work at another factory, and they signed and thumbprinted the letters and collected their final wages, accrued annual leave, overtime and good-work bonus. [Para 104][Para 105][Para 107][Para 109]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider the demand that the employer take Ms L Sok and Mr N Touch back into normal work. [Para 160][Para 161]

Before the merits the panel resolved a representation objection: under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 SACB of 21 April 2004 a party may be represented by any person holding its written authorisation, and the two workers had transferred their rights to the federation president, who in turn transferred them to the two federation officials appearing — a resignation does not strip a worker of the right to pursue a dispute that arose while employed, so the representation stood. [Para 129][Para 130][Para 131][Para 133][Para 138][Para 139]

On the merits the panel placed the burden of proving coercion on the workers, following Awards 108/08 (Hugo), 41/09 (SD), 101/08 (GD IM), 163/09, 168/09 and 115/10, and held that a claim unsupported by specific evidence must be rejected (Awards 63/04, 99/06, 33/07, 51/07, 154/13). [Para 148][Para 149][Para 150][Para 151] The resignation letters state in terms that the workers resigned voluntarily from 29 October 2013 for family reasons and had received wages and all allowances in full. [Para 142][Para 143][Para 146][Para 156] Neither worker came to the hearing to describe any concrete act or threat; the representatives could not identify the group said to have threatened them, nor say in what form or when, and produced no document. [Para 152][Para 154][Para 155][Para 157] The claim therefore failed.

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A resignation letter signed and thumbprinted by the worker, acknowledging receipt of final wages and all terminal benefits, is treated as evidence of voluntary resignation, and a worker who alleges that the resignation was forced bears the burden of proving the coercion by specific evidence — the assertions of a union representative who cannot name the persons, the words used or the occasion are not enough. A worker's prior written transfer of representation in a pending dispute survives that worker's later resignation. [Para 138][Para 146][Para 151][Para 157]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's account of how the employer normally pays on the day of resignation, or the next day where the letter is sent through another worker, is background practice rather than a ground of decision. [Para 112][Para 113]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The company is called "Sau Len (Cambodia)" on the face of the award and in the evidence, but "Southland" throughout the resignation letters and the union documents; the panel treats them as one enterprise without saying so. [Para 81][Para 115][Para 118]
- The panel is said to have been constituted on "15 January 2023 2014", two years printed for one date. [Para 20]
- Employer evidence is recorded as filed on "17 January 2013" at one point and 17 January 2014 at another, and the award's own procedural recital refers to the second hearing being fixed for 28 January 2014. [Para 29][Para 114][Para 142]
- The union's second leadership election is dated 16 September 2013 in the workers' account and 14 September 2013 in the panel's finding from the employer's letter; the first election is given as 16 July 2013. [Para 121][Para 127]
- The two claimants' names appear in several forms ("ឡាយ សុខ ន", "ឡាយ សុខ", "ង៉ែ តូច", "ង៉ែត តូច", "ង៉ែ តូ"); the union president is "ស៊ាប ស៊ាង ហ័រ" in the evidence and "ស៊ាប អាស៊ាង ហ័រ" in the election result. [Para 33][Para 128]
- Of the ten original demands only eight are listed in the extract and the numbering runs 1 to 8, so the two settled points cannot be identified from the text. [Para 2][Para 18]
- Signatures are substantially lost to OCR: the employer arbitrator's block ends in garbled characters and the chair's name is printed twice. [Para 163][Para 164]
