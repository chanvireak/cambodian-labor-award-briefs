# Award No. 033/14 — Soun Ousak Mean Hat Ten (Arbitration Council, 9 May 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, An Non, Mae Nimmith presiding) · Doc ID 139522

## 1. Facts
A garment factory in Kampong Cham employing about 3,000 workers faced a claim from the Cambodia Union federation on behalf of 103 workers said to have been dismissed for joining a strike in January 2014. Conciliation settled 87 of the claims; withdrawals and settlements reduced the claimants to ten by the fourth hearing, five of whom the employer accepted as union members. The disputed act was the ending of each claimant's employment during or just after the strike. [Para 18][Para 22][Para 26][Para 91]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate the nine remaining workers with back pay from dismissal, and pay severance to Ms S? [Para 346]
2. Whether the employer must stop discriminating against the Cambodia Union federation and refrain from dismissing workers who joined the strike? [Para 3]

The workers withdrew issue 2 from the case, leaving only issue 1 for decision. [Para 15][Para 23]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissals punished participation in the strike; Ms S had applied for leave and the employer's internal rule on seven days' absence was invoked only against strikers; the employer could not rely on its January dues-deduction list to decide who was a member. [Para 95][Para 168][Para 172]
**Employer (respondent):** only 45 of the 103 were recognised members; each claimant either abandoned the job by unlawful absence under clause 6 of the internal rules, or reached the end of a fixed-term contract or was suspended on 50 per cent pay; the section chief's letter of 19 April 2014 confirmed abandonment. [Para 94][Para 113][Para 141][Para 285]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement claims dismissed for all but the protected representative; severance ordered for Ms S, Mr V and Ms P, and claims of the remaining five dismissed. [Para 704][Para 708][Para 714]
On representation the panel held the union was lawfully registered and could represent members, and that five claimants were proved members — four from the employer's own dues list and Mr V from the list of terminated members — while the other five had given no membership evidence, though their written authority to the union president allowed him to act for them. [Para 328][Para 332][Para 339][Para 343][Para 345]
On Ms S the panel found the labour law silent on abandonment, so it tested the employer's case against the attendance record and the testimony. The record showed four and then five and a half consecutive days of absence, not seven, and she returned on 13 and 20 January; the section chief's letter was general and he never appeared. The panel therefore held she did not abandon her job and was dismissed on 22 January 2014. [Para 349][Para 605][Para 611][Para 633][Para 636]
Because she had signed successive six-month contracts from 30 December 1999, the panel held her contract indefinite; because she was a staff representative whose term ended on 25 November 2013, Article 293 still protected her for three months, and the employer had not sought labour-inspector authorisation. She was awarded three months' notice pay, six months' dismissal compensation (the statutory cap), unpaid annual leave, six months' moral damages under Article 91, and final wages. [Para 643][Para 648][Para 658][Para 669][Para 700]
On Mr V the panel rejected the claimed suspension: the employer produced only an unsigned section note, knew no reason for it, gave no notice and no 50 per cent payments, so it failed to discharge the burden of proof. He was dismissed on 28 January 2014 before his contract expired on 31 January 2014, and received the fixed-term package — five per cent termination indemnity, moral damages, final wages and leave. [Para 449][Para 452][Para 463][Para 474]
On Mr V and Ms P the panel applied Article 67(2): successive fixed-term contracts totalling more than two years become indefinite-term, but Ms P's totalled less than two years, so hers remained fixed-term. For Ms M, Ms Y, Ms D, Ms K, Ms N, Ms A and Ms Kh neither party produced consistent dates or evidence, so those claims were dismissed without prejudice to the merits. [Para 387][Para 389][Para 502][Para 708]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer alleging abandonment of employment must prove it with specific, consistent evidence — attendance records, the responsible supervisor, notice — and a generalised confirmatory letter from a supervisor who does not appear carries no weight. Successive fixed-term contracts whose total duration exceeds two years convert to an indefinite-term contract under Article 67(2). A staff representative, and for three months an ex-representative, may not be dismissed without prior labour-inspector authorisation under Article 293. [Para 633][Para 638][Para 387]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the Labour Law contains no provision on abandonment of employment at all, so the concept must be built from other rules and from the facts of each case. [Para 348][Para 349]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award orders reinstatement of Ms S in the reasoning but the operative order grants only severance payments, and no reinstatement order appears in the disposition. [Para 649][Para 704] Her representative term is printed as ending on 25 and on 26 November 2013; the number of recognised members is given as four and as five in successive paragraphs; the worker count moves from 103 to 14 to 10 to 9 without explanation. [Para 308][Para 331][Para 643] Dates appear as "5 March 2019 2014" and "2019 2014"; worker names are printed in several spellings, and are given here as Ms S, Mr V, Ms P and Ms M. [Para 40][Para 226][Para 578]
