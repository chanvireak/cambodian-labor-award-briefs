# Award No. 133/14 — Royal Cambodia Phnom Penh Golf Club (Arbitration Council, 18 July 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Seng Vouch Hun (employer-appointed), Sin Kim Sean (worker-appointed), Pen Bunhea (president) · Doc ID 139583

## 1. Facts
The employer operates a golf driving range at Sangkat Samrong Krom, Phnom Penh, employing about 200 workers, of whom some 120 are women. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 243] Forty-four workers, through the Cambodian Workers' Union Federation, pressed ten demands; two were settled in conciliation and the panel settled four more, leaving four for decision. [Para 24][Para 29][Para 30] The employer accepted the claimants' 20 authorisation letters only after they were produced at the second hearing. [Para 150][Para 156]

## 2. Issues in dispute
3. Whether the employer must raise probationary wages from 80 to 95 US dollars and full-rights wages from 91 to 100 US dollars per month? [Para 184]
5. Whether the employer must pay a regular attendance bonus of 10 US dollars per month? [Para 208]
6. Whether the employer must pay a travel and accommodation allowance of 10 US dollars per month? [Para 225]
7. Whether the employer must establish a nursery (ទារកដ្ឋាន) at the enterprise? [Para 237]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the government sets 95 and 100 dollars for probationary and full-rights workers in garment, textile and footwear, other sectors pay at least that, and spray-section work here is no different; 10 to 20 workers live 7 to 8 kilometres away and pay rent; the law requires a nursery and there is none. [Para 97][Para 99][Para 122][Para 133]
**Employer (respondent):** the club is not in the textile, garment or footwear sector, so Prakas No. 317 does not bind it; it raised full-rights pay from 61 to 91 dollars on 17 June 2014 and cannot raise again; road works have cut customer numbers; no worker was dismissed or discriminated against. [Para 104][Para 108][Para 16][Para 119]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the wage, attendance-bonus and travel-allowance claims were refused without consideration; the employer was ordered to open a nursery by February 2015 in accordance with its own plan of 15 July 2014, and, until then, to reimburse the actual invoices of women placing children aged 18 months or over in an outside nursery. [Para 252][Para 253][Para 254][Para 255][Para 256]

**Parties.** The panel held that the 20 workers whose authorisation letter was undated and produced only at the second hearing were nonetheless claimants: under Articles 35 and 57 of the Civil Procedure Code and Articles 369 and 370 of the Civil Code, an act done without authority takes effect from the date it was done once the principal ratifies it (សច្ចានុមតិ), and the letter supplied that ratification. [Para 161][Para 172][Para 179][Para 182] It accordingly recognised 44 claimants. [Para 183]

**Wages.** Prakas No. 317 K.B./Prak of 31 December 2013 raised the minimum from 80 to 100 dollars for textile, garment and footwear workers. [Para 187] The panel found that Prakas expressly limited to those sectors, that this employer's business object is a golf driving range, and therefore that it falls outside the Prakas; no law, collective agreement, contract, internal rule or past practice obliged the employer to pay 95 or 100 dollars. [Para 189][Para 190][Para 191][Para 193] The demand was thus in excess of the law and an interest dispute (ទិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍). [Para 194][Para 195]

**Attendance bonus and travel allowance.** The panel applied the same analysis to Notification No. 230 K.B./S.J.N. of 25 July 2012, which fixes a 10-dollar attendance bonus and a 7-dollar travel and accommodation payment, and found its scope confined to textile, garment and footwear enterprises. [Para 212][Para 213][Para 214][Para 228] Because the employer is a golf club, neither instrument applied and no other source created the obligation, so both demands were interest disputes in excess of the law. [Para 217][Para 219][Para 231][Para 233]

**Standing on the interest claims.** Reading Article 96 of the Labour Law with Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099, the panel held that an interest award substitutes for a collective agreement for one year, that only a most representative union may therefore bring such a dispute, and that this union and its 44 workers were not most representative; it refused to consider issues 3, 5 and 6. [Para 199][Para 202][Para 204][Para 206][Para 207]

**Nursery.** Article 186 requires any enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a breastfeeding room and a nursery in or near the premises, and, where it cannot take children over 18 months, to pay for an outside nursery. [Para 237][Para 238] The panel followed Award No. 81/09 Vorn Reach and found the duty triggered here: 200 workers and about 120 women, with no nursery on site. [Para 239][Para 242][Para 245] The employer then filed a plan on 16 July 2014 providing for registration of women workers from August to September 2014, statistics to October, siting and construction from November 2014 to January 2015, and trial operation from February 2015. [Para 142][Para 143][Para 144] The workers accepted that timetable by telephone, so the panel ordered the nursery on those terms rather than at large. [Para 247][Para 248][Para 250]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer outside the textile, garment and footwear sectors owes no minimum wage, attendance bonus or travel allowance under Prakas No. 317 or Notification No. 230, and a demand for those sums is an interest dispute that only a most representative union may bring; the panel will refuse it without reaching the merits. [Para 191][Para 214][Para 204]

Separately, the Article 186 nursery duty is triggered by the number of women employed — 100 or more — and not by sector, and it is satisfied by an agreed construction timetable plus reimbursement of outside nursery invoices in the interim. [Para 242][Para 250][Para 251]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that where the employer genuinely cannot take children over 18 months, Article 186 itself offers payment for an outside nursery as the alternative, so the obligation is one of cost rather than of construction at any price. [Para 249]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's corporate name is printed in Khmer in at least six divergent OCR forms and cannot be reliably transliterated; the award is therefore titled by the trade name that appears in Latin script throughout, "Royal Cambodia Phnom Penh Golf Club", which the panel itself treats as the trade name (នាមករណ៍ពាណិជ្ជកម្ម) of the registered company. [Para 72] The registration number is given as Inv. 166KH/1996 in the reasons but the certificate relied on is No. 3175 dated 09 June 2011, and the statutes are dated 07 February 2013 — three dates that cannot all describe one registration. [Para 72][Para 35][Para 36] The presiding arbitrator is "ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈាន" in the header and "ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា" in the signature block. [Para 1][Para 258] The panel records four further points settled but lists only three (2, 4 and 8), so point 1 is unaccounted for; it also says eight points were unsettled when only four were decided. [Para 29][Para 30] The complaint is described as concerning "Royal Cambodia Golf Club" while the parties are the company and the union, and the 20 authorisation letters are undated. [Para 23][Para 150] OCR has lost text in several places: paragraph 157 contains an unreadable Latin-script intrusion, paragraph 237 garbles the Article 186 threshold, and the decision text at paragraph 252 repeats itself. [Para 157][Para 237][Para 252]
