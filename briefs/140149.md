# Award No. 119/12 — New Ark Knit Garment Factory Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 17 July 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed, dissenting), Tuon Siphann (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (president) · Doc ID 140149

## 1. Facts
New Ark Knit Garment Factory Ltd., a garment manufacturer at Trapeang Village, Peuk Commune, Ang Snuol District, Kandal, employs about 3,400 workers [Para 88]. The Khmer Pride Workers' Federation filed for the union Progress of Khmer Workers, whose local committee was elected on 8 January 2012 and registered on 26 March 2012 [Para 91][Para 112]. The company did not renew the fixed-duration contracts of two local leaders, Ms P (deputy president) and Ms Ph (secretary), which expired on 30 March 2012, giving notice on 17 March 2012 and paying terminal benefits of USD 135 and USD 115.63 [Para 94][Para 105][Para 107]. Of the 17 unresolved demands, 6 were settled at the hearing and 10 withdrawn, leaving the reinstatement claim alone [Para 32][Para 33][Para 34].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the reinstatement claim is a collective labour dispute within the panel's jurisdiction, the employer objecting that it was already conciliated as an individual dispute? [Para 118][Para 126]
2. Whether the company must take back Ms P and Ms Ph and pay their wages from the date of termination until reinstatement? [Para 2][Para 92]
3. Whether Ms P's successive fixed-duration contracts became a single indefinite-duration contract? [Para 137][Para 147]
4. Whether the non-renewal of Ms P's contract was a dismissal requiring the labour inspector's prior authorisation under Article 293? [Para 149][Para 172]
5. Whether the non-renewal of Ms Ph's contract complied with the notice required by Article 73? [Para 187][Para 193]
6. Whether the non-renewal of Ms Ph's contract was anti-union discrimination under Articles 12 and 279? [Para 196][Para 208]
7. Whether the company must reinstate Ms Ph and pay her back wages? [Para 176][Para 209]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** both women were elected union leaders, and the company picked them out of a 32-worker team for non-renewal although neither had any disciplinary fault, which shows anti-union discrimination [Para 106][Para 108][Para 109]. The claim is a collective dispute because it was resubmitted together with the other 17 demands to the Ministry [Para 122].

**Employer (respondent):** the fixed-duration contracts simply reached their term and the company had the right not to renew them, having paid everything the Labour Law requires [Para 104][Para 3]. The matter is an individual dispute already conciliated at the Kandal labour department, so the panel should not hear it [Para 118].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to take back Ms P and pay her wages from the date of termination until reinstatement, while Ms Ph's claim for reinstatement and back pay was dismissed; no sum was fixed in the order [Para 210][Para 211].

On jurisdiction, the panel held that every demand in a Ministry non-conciliation report is a collective dispute, and that an employer who says otherwise carries the burden of proving it; it then applied the three conditions of Article 302 as read in case 10/03-Zaksin Tech and found all three met, since the two women were union leaders and the dispute could disturb the enterprise [Para 123][Para 127][Para 136].

For Ms P, the panel chained her contracts — a two-month probation from 1 February 2010, then six-month renewals expiring 30 March 2012, a total of about 2 years 1 month 29 days [Para 94][Para 96][Para 97]. Reading Article 67(2) with case 10/03-Zaksin Tech, it held that renewals whose combined duration exceeds two years convert the contract to indefinite duration, so the non-renewal was in law a dismissal, not an expiry [Para 139][Para 146][Para 148]. Because she was a protected union leader, Article 293 and Prakas No. 305 required the employer to obtain the labour inspector's permission first, which it never sought [Para 161][Para 164][Para 172]. The 5 per cent severance already paid did not cure that, and the panel ordered reinstatement with back pay [Para 174][Para 175].

For Ms Ph, the panel reached the opposite result on shorter service: about 7 months 28 days, a single expiring term [Para 101][Para 102]. It held that a genuine fixed-duration contract that reaches its term is not a "dismissal" at all, so Article 293 does not apply [Para 184][Para 186]. Notice given on 17 March for a 30 March expiry exceeded the ten days Article 73(5) requires [Para 188][Para 192]. On discrimination, the claimant produced no evidence beyond the assertion, while the company had decided and notified before it knew the union leadership, so the burden never shifted [Para 206][Para 207][Para 208].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where successive fixed-duration contracts are renewed so that their combined term exceeds two years, the contract becomes one of indefinite duration; its non-renewal is therefore a dismissal, and where the worker is a protected union leader it is unlawful without the labour inspector's prior authorisation, regardless of severance paid [Para 146][Para 148][Para 172]. A genuine fixed-duration contract that simply reaches its term is not a dismissal and needs no such authorisation [Para 186].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's statement that any demand listed in a Ministry non-conciliation report is presumptively collective, with the burden on the employer, was broader than the facts required [Para 123][Para 124].
