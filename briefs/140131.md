# Award No. 88/12 — Magnate Footwear Co (Arbitration Council, 22 June 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-selected; An Non, worker-selected; Kong Phalla, chair) · Doc ID 140131

## 1. Facts
Magnate Footwear, a Phnom Penh shoe factory employing about 3,000 workers, decided to close its glue-assembly section and told the 13 workers there to move to a sister plant on National Road 3, roughly 10 km and 10 minutes away, on the same shift, skill grade, wage and benefits. [Para 85][Para 106] All 13 refused to move; on 13 February 2012 the employer terminated them with statutory severance, and eight accepted payment. [Para 107][Para 108][Para 109] The four who did not — the union president, vice-president, secretary and one further leader — claimed they were dismissed for union activity, and their union also brought six collective claims. [Para 88][Para 86]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate the four union leaders and pay them back wages from the date of dismissal? [Para 187]
2. Whether the employer must continue to pay the wages of representatives and workers required to attend conciliation and arbitration meetings, and reimburse wages already deducted for such attendance? [Para 223]
3. Whether the employer may deduct the regular attendance bonus for days of absence authorised by the employer, rather than only pro rata? [Para 248]
4. Whether the employer must pay 50 per cent of wages and allowances for the 90 days of maternity leave before the leave begins? [Para 272]
5. Whether the employer must pay in cash, during the employment relationship, for annual leave not taken? [Para 286]
6. Whether the panel may decide again a transport and housing claim already decided in Award No. 41/12? [Para 312]
7. Whether the employer must raise the milk allowance from USD 12 to USD 15 a month? [Para 321]
8. Whether the employer must add USD 5 to the USD 10 attendance bonus? [Para 334]
9. Whether the employer must reinstate Ms T R, a union vice-president who did not appear? [Para 341]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the four were dismissed because they had been elected union leaders three weeks earlier, after the union notified the company of the election result on 25 January 2012 through the factory guard; the employer had never objected to the thumbprints on the authorisations, and the new plant was not really a Magnate Footwear plant. [Para 92][Para 198][Para 219]
**Employer (respondent):** the terminations were a genuine redundancy of the whole glue section, not discrimination; the workers were offered the same wage, shift and skill at the branch and refused; the company never received the election notice, since only the administrative office may sign for incoming mail under its 5 January 2010 rule, and a guard has no authority to accept letters. [Para 97][Para 99][Para 123][Para 127]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — on the workers' claims the panel refused reinstatement of the four leaders but ordered the employer to pay each of them the full severance package (final wages, unused annual leave, moral damages, dismissal damages and pay in lieu of notice); it ordered the employer to retain the wages of party representatives required to attend conciliation and arbitration meetings and refused reimbursement of past deductions; it ordered the attendance bonus to be deducted only pro rata to days of authorised absence; it ordered 50 per cent of wages and allowances for the 90 days of maternity leave to be paid at least one day before the leave begins; and it refused the annual-leave cash-out, the transport and housing claim, the milk allowance increase and the bonus increase. [Para 352][Para 353][Para 354][Para 355][Para 356][Para 357][Para 358][Para 360]

On the dismissals the panel worked through Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001. Protection for elected leaders lasts at least 45 days after the election, or until the union applies for registration, and it is conditional on the union notifying the employer of the candidacies by all available means. [Para 188][Para 193][Para 197] The factory had a written rule that only the administrative office, not guards, may sign for incoming mail, and the guard who took the letter admitted he never passed it on. [Para 204][Para 207][Para 208] The panel therefore held the union had not used all available means and the three leaders never acquired protection. [Para 209] Turning to the dismissal itself, Article 2 gives the employer the right to manage and redeploy staff, subject to four conditions — no wage cut, no transfer far away, no shift inversion and no change away from the worker's skill — and the move satisfied all four, while the workers offered nothing to show the branch was a different company. [Para 213][Para 214][Para 217][Para 219] The dismissal was therefore lawful, but severance was still owed. [Para 221][Para 222]

On issue 3 the panel held the attendance bonus is wages under Article 103, that authorised absence suspends the contract under Article 71(6), and that the employer may deduct only in proportion to the days actually absent. [Para 260][Para 262][Para 265][Para 268] On maternity, Articles 182 and 183 give 90 days at 50 per cent of wage and allowances, Article 103 counts maternity pay as wages, and Article 115 requires wages to be paid before rest days; paying month by month was therefore unlawful. [Para 272][Para 273][Para 278][Para 283] The annual-leave claim failed because Article 167(3) voids any payment in lieu of leave during the employment relationship. [Para 299][Para 300] The transport claim was barred because Award No. 41/12 had already decided the identical claim between the same parties. [Para 314][Para 317][Para 319] The milk and bonus claims failed as interest claims, the union not being the most representative at the plant. [Para 326][Para 331]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The statutory protection of elected union leaders depends on the union having notified the employer of the candidacies by all available means; a notification handed to a factory guard, contrary to a written rule that only the administration may accept incoming mail, does not satisfy that condition, and the leader is then dismissible on ordinary grounds. [Para 197][Para 208][Para 209] Even so, a lawful termination for redundancy carries the full severance and damages package, and an employer who redeploys staff must show the four Article 2 conditions are met. [Para 214][Para 222]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that reinstatement was refused because the enterprise had a genuine operational reason, and its observations on the union's registration status, are not necessary to the order to pay severance. [Para 217][Para 221]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
This award carries two appended dissents: the employer-selected arbitrator dissents on issue 4, and the worker-selected arbitrator dissents on issues 1 and 2; the signed dissent of the latter sets out a different view of the guard's evidence. [Para 366][Para 388][Para 398] The case is numbered 88/12 in the header but the reconvened hearing is dated 15 June 2012 while the non-conciliation report is dated 4 May 2012 and received 8 May 2012. [Para 15][Para 18] The claim is pleaded as 67 technical workers at [Para 9] and 76 at [Para 120]. Five individuals (four leaders and one absent vice-president) are named in the source; they are reduced to roles and initials here, and one name is printed both as a union secretary and as a "Mr" elsewhere.
