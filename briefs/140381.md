# Award No. 51/09-Yung Wah (Branches 1 and 2) — Yung Wah Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 30 April 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phyrum, Tuon Siphann, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 140381

## 1. Facts
Yung Wah Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. was established in 1998 and employs about 5,000 workers at Thmey Village, Ta Khmao District, Kandal Province. Seven local unions are the claimants — KYFTU, FTUWKC, CLUF, CUF, TUWFPD, C.CAWDU and FUKDW — and the C.CAWDU local holds most-representative status. A shortage of purchase orders led the company to ask the provincial Department of Labour on 17 March 2009 to inspect and allow suspension of 792 workers at Branch 1 and 2,180 at Branch 2 for two months from 1 April to 31 May 2009; the Department replied on 30 March 2009 and 2,972 workers were suspended. The single demand is for full wages across those two months. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 36]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the suspension of the employment contracts of 2,972 workers for the two months from 1 April to 31 May 2009 was implemented in accordance with the Labour Law, so that the employer need not pay wages for that period? [Para 30][Para 43]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, the seven local unions led by the C.CAWDU local):** full wages are owed for the whole suspension because the employer notified the Department but not the workers, gave no explanation of the reasons and suspended them suddenly, closed the factory and locked them out on 31 March 2009, still had work since it moved materials out to other factories, and the provincial Department's letter is not an authorisation from the Labour Inspector. [Para 28][Para 24]

**Employer (respondent, Yung Wah Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd.):** the suspension followed Articles 71 and 72 of the Labour Law on the strength of its request of 17 March 2009 and the Department's letters of 30 March 2009; it met the workers on 27 March 2009 without result; it has moved no material out since 1 April beyond unfinished orders it already held; and it therefore owes no wages for the period. [Para 12][Para 21][Para 25][Para 30]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the workers' demand that the employer pay full wages for the two-month suspension from 1 April to 31 May 2009. [Para 45]

The panel framed the question as compliance with Article 71(11) of the Labour Law (1997), which permits suspension where the enterprise faces serious economic or material difficulty, caps it at two months and places it under the control of the Labour Inspector, read with Article 72(1), under which suspension removes the obligations to work and to pay. [Para 31][Para 33]

It found the procedure followed: the company requested inspection and evaluation on 17 March 2009 for both branches, stating the order shortage, and the head of the provincial Department replied on 30 March 2009. The panel held that reply acknowledged genuine difficulty caused by a fall in purchase orders and thereby authorised suspension of workers in some sections of both branches. [Para 36][Para 43]

On the objection that a provincial Department is not the Labour Inspector, the panel noted that letters issued by provincial and municipal departments in past cases share the same format and do not expressly say permission is granted; that in the provinces the departmental letter is the vehicle of Inspector control, as Awards 105/04-United Eternity and 28/08-FineGis show; and that the control requirement exists to check that real economic difficulty exists. [Para 39][Para 41][Para 42]

On notice to the workers, the panel followed 46/05-Ocean Garment: the Labour Law sets no minimum period of prior notice, and the obligation is satisfied by posting the authorisation letter immediately or as soon as it is received from the Ministry. It rejected the demand. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 44]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension under Article 71(11) is under the control of the Labour Inspector where the employer requests inspection and the provincial Department of Labour replies acknowledging the economic difficulty and authorising suspension of workers in identified sections; that reply is the operative authorisation, and the employer need only post it to the workers, no minimum period of prior notice being required. [Para 41][Para 43][Para 38]

**Obiter dicta:** the control requirement exists to prevent suspensions from seriously affecting the national economy and workers' benefits. [Para 41]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The non-conciliation report is dated 1 April 2008 and said to have been submitted on 1 April 2008, although the case concerns a suspension beginning 1 April 2009 and the referral plainly belongs to 2009. The suspension letter is cited in the issue statement as No. 130-131/09 KB/KN but later as No. 131/09 for Branch 1, with No. 132/09 for Branch 2. Sentences are duplicated mid-paragraph, including the twice-stated 17 March 2009 request and the referral to the non-conciliation report, and the definite article is fused into the following word throughout ("themployer", "thevidence"). The evidence section was omitted from the English version. The KYFTU local is listed with its representative as "Absent", so its position cannot be identified from the face of the award.
