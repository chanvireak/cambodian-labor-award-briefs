# Award No. 155/11 — Ying Dong Su Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 December 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Pen Bunhea, chair) · Doc ID 140099

## 1. Facts
Ying Dong Su Co Ltd ("យីង ដុង ស៊ូ ខូ អិលធីឌី") makes shoes at Borei Sen Monorom village, Chom Chao commune, Dangkao district, Phnom Penh, and employs about 1,000 workers. [Para 1][Para 60] The Khmer Union Federation filed complaint No. 257/11 SSK on 20 October 2011 seeking improved working conditions; conciliation on 8 November 2011 failed on all ten demands, and report No. 1291 K.B/A.K/V.K of 18 November 2011 reached the Council's Secretariat on 22 November 2011. [Para 16][Para 19][Para 20][Para 21] Hearings were held on 2 and 19 December 2011, and the parties agreed to extend the award deadline to 30 December 2011. [Para 17][Para 18][Para 26]

The union held an election on 9 October 2011, choosing Nhann Thongma as president, Ek Sary as vice-president and Siem Sokly as secretary. [Para 84] It notified the employer on 10 October 2011; the administration refused to accept the letter, asked to photograph it, and the union re-sent the result by post on 14 October 2011. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 88] The union received a receipt for its registration application from the Ministry of Labour on 14 October 2011 and was not yet registered when the case was heard. [Para 62][Para 89] On 17 October 2011 the employer objected to registration on the ground that Nhann Thongma was only 24, below the required age. [Para 90] On 22 October 2011 at 9 a.m. the three leaders were summoned to a meeting room with two security guards and two police officers, and the production chief handed them dismissal notices. [Para 92][Para 93] The stated reasons were that their work was not progressing, they would not work overtime, and they recruited workers into their union during working hours and formed a union unlawfully. [Para 93][Para 98]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the three union leaders, pay their wages from dismissal to reinstatement, and stop union discrimination? [Para 63][Para 105]

The remaining nine demands — a nursery and breastfeeding room or US$20 a month in lieu, removal of the administration chief, correct severance pay, hygienic drinking water, an extra doctor and nurse plus a larger clinic with first-aid medicine, social-security registration for over 1,000 workers, voluntary overtime capped at two hours, no US$10/US$20 salary deductions, and a cap on Chinese workers — were withdrawn at the hearing. [Para 3][Para 4][Para 5][Para 6][Para 7][Para 8][Para 9][Para 10][Para 11][Para 12][Para 13][Para 24]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The three leaders were dismissed because they were union leaders, so the dismissal was discriminatory; they asked for reinstatement, back pay and an end to discrimination. [Para 63][Para 104][Para 129] They said they discussed union matters only at the midday meal, never during working hours, and that any talking at work drew a reprimand or warning from the section chief. [Para 101][Para 102][Para 103] Ek Sary and Nhann Thongma said they had never been warned. [Para 67][Para 78] They pointed out that the union had no registration certificate and no written authority from other workers, which is why they withdrew the other nine demands. [Para 24]

**Employer (respondent):** It dismissed the three under Article 83(5) of the Labour Law for misconduct — recruiting workers into the union and causing disorder at the workplace. [Para 99][Para 100] It admitted it did not seek the Labour Inspector's permission before dismissing them. [Para 96] It also said Nhann Thongma had resigned on 14 January 2011, though it did not dispute that her section chief called her back and re-employed her without a contract after a week's break. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74][Para 75]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Upheld in part — the panel ordered the employer to reinstate Nhann Thongma, Siem Sokly and Ek Sary, pay their wages from the date of dismissal to the date of return, and stop union discrimination. [Para 139][Para 140][Para 141]

The panel first classified the demand. A rights dispute is one grounded in a right set out in the Labour Law, a collective agreement or a collective convention. [Para 105][Para 106][Para 107] This demand concerned the dismissal of union leaders under Article 293 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 305, and discrimination under Articles 12 and 279, so it was a rights dispute. [Para 108]

On the merits, Prakas No. 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001 protects founders and voluntary members of a union from the date the registration application is filed until 30 days after registration. [Para 109][Para 110][Para 111] Article 293(1) requires the Labour Inspector's consent before a protected worker or candidate is dismissed. [Para 112][Para 113][Para 114] Following earlier awards, protection requires three conditions: the worker falls within Prakas 305, the dismissal occurred during the protection period, and the employer was notified of the protected names by any reliable means. [Para 116][Para 117]

The panel found all three met. The three women stood as candidates in the union election and were therefore founders. [Para 124] The employer knew their names: it had the 10 October notice, the 14 October postal notice, the Labour Department's letters of 19 and 22 November, and the union's registration application. [Para 121][Para 122][Para 123] The dismissals took place on 22 October 2011, within the protection period, and the employer never sought the Inspector's authorisation. [Para 126][Para 127] The dismissals therefore breached Article 293 and Prakas 305. [Para 127]

On discrimination, the panel noted that the employer dismissed all three on a single day, 22 October 2011, after being told on 10 October that they had won the election and knowing they were union leaders. [Para 137] That timing showed the dismissals were connected to union discrimination. [Para 138] The panel relied on the established rule that the party making an allegation bears the burden of proof. [Para 132][Para 133][Para 134]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union founder or candidate is protected from the date the registration application is filed until 30 days after registration, and an employer that dismisses such a worker without the Labour Inspector's prior consent acts unlawfully, whatever the stated grounds; the remedy is reinstatement, back pay from dismissal to return, and an order to stop union discrimination. [Para 111][Para 113][Para 116][Para 127][Para 140]

**Obiter dicta:** Dismissing every union leader on one day, shortly after the employer learns of their election, is itself evidence linking the dismissals to union discrimination. [Para 137][Para 138]
