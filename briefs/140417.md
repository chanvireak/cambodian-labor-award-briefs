# Award No. 100/09 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉ុង វ៉ា អ៊ិន ដាស ស្ទ្រៀល (ខេមបូឌា) ខូ អិលធីឌី ("Yong Wah 2") (Arbitration Council, 25 August 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (សេង វួច ហ៊ុន, employer party; នួន ស៊ី ផាន់, worker party; ប៊ែន ប៊ុន ឈាម chair) · Doc ID 140417

## 1. Facts
The employer, ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉ុង វ៉ា អ៊ិន ដាស ស្ទ្រៀល (ខេមបូឌា) ខូ អិលធីឌី, runs a laundry and garment operation employing about 5,000 workers. [Para 3][Para 50] The claimant is the C.CAWDU union at Yong Wah 2, សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ នៃ រោងចក្រ យ៉ុង វ៉ា ២. [Para 7][Para 51] On 19 June 2009, during a strike in front of the factory gate, a 2.5-tonne company truck driven by the owner's driver left the gate at speed and ran over the feet of two striking workers, one of whom was on crutches for over a month. [Para 52][Para 54][Para 63] Of seven unresolved demands, five were withdrawn and issue 6 was settled at the hearing, leaving only the claim for treatment costs and moral damages, reduced at the hearing from US$500 to US$300 per worker. [Para 32][Para 85]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the two injured workers are entitled to US$300 each in treatment costs and moral damages for an injury inflicted by a company truck while they were on strike? [Para 52][Para 87][Para 88]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the driver of the 2.5-tonne truck drove out of the gate at speed into the strikers, injuring both; the union paid about 5,000 riel for bandages and the workers received no treatment money from the company; the police referred the accident to the court. [Para 54][Para 56][Para 82]
**Employer (respondent):** the workers never asked the company to take them to hospital or produced receipts for their treatment, so the company knew nothing of the accident; it would pay any documented costs, and it denies agreeing to pay US$150 through the police. [Para 66][Para 68][Para 75][Para 81]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the claim for US$300 each in treatment costs and moral damages was rejected, without prejudice to any other lawful remedy the two workers or the employer may pursue. [Para 110][Para 111]

The panel began with Article 248 of the Labour Law, which treats as a work accident any accident arising from the performance of work or occurring during working hours, regardless of fault, and Article 249, which makes the head of the enterprise liable for such accidents whatever the worker's medical condition. [Para 88][Para 91] It then asked whether an injury suffered during a strike falls within that definition. [Para 99] Reading Article 332 — under which the employment contract is suspended during a strike, no work is provided and no wages are paid — the panel held that strikers are not performing work for the employer at the material time. [Para 101][Para 105][Para 106] It followed award 85/07 in the same sense. [Para 107] Because the two workers were not working when the truck struck them, the injury was not a work accident within Articles 248 and 332, and the Labour Law therefore imposed no obligation on the employer to compensate their treatment. [Para 108][Para 109] That the strike itself was lawful did not alter the analysis. [Para 108]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An injury suffered by a worker during a strike is not a work accident, because Article 332 suspends the employment contract for the duration of the strike so that no work is being performed for the employer; the employer's liability under Articles 248 and 249 for accidents arising from work or occurring in working hours does not therefore extend to injuries inflicted by the employer's vehicle on strikers at the factory gate, and the injured workers must pursue the driver or other lawful remedies instead. [Para 88][Para 105][Para 108][Para 111]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that the incident was a grave event affecting health and safety, and its observation that a refusal of the claim bars neither a court action nor the employer's defence to one, are not part of the rule. [Para 111][Para 112]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The first six paragraphs of the source are largely unrecoverable: the header, the composition of the panel and the employer's address at [Para 1] to [Para 6] are reduced to fragments of Khmer, Latin letters, digits and stray punctuation, so the employer's full registered name, its address and the arbitration reference cannot be read with confidence from those lines.
- The two injured workers are named repeatedly in the award; their names are not reproduced in this brief. The truck driver and the company's administrative staff are likewise named and are referred to here by role only.
- The amount claimed is stated as US$500 per worker in the conciliation report and as US$300 in the reasons and the order [Para 52][Para 85][Para 110]; the police-brokered figure of US$150 appears only in the workers' account and is denied by the employer [Para 80][Para 81].
- Article references are corrupted in the text: Article 332 appears as "ហត្រា ៣៣២" and "មាត្រា ៣២២" in the same passage [Para 101][Para 108], and the hearing date is printed 7 August 2009 while the award is dated 25 August 2009 [Para 27][Para 1].
