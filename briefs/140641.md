# Award No. 94/07 — Fortune Garment & Woolen Knitting Factory Ltd (Arbitration Council, 3 October 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Ouk Ry (employer-chosen), Sin Kim Sean (worker-chosen), Kong Phallack (chair) · Doc ID 140641

## 1. Facts
Fortune Garment & Woolen Knitting Factory Ltd, Prek Kseo Village, Sa-Ang District, Kandal Province, employed around 2,050 workers, of whom the claimant Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers of Democratic Union (C.CAWDU) claimed around 1,200 as members; neither it nor the Cambodian Apparel Workers of Democratic Union (CAWDU) held most representative status [Para 1][Para 2][Para 23]. Two complaints of 10 and 16 August 2007 went to conciliation, where four of twelve demands settled; eight remained and reached the panel through non-conciliation report No. 142 on 10 September 2007 [Para 13][Para 15][Para 16]. The hearing was held on 19 September 2007 and conciliation again failed [Para 13][Para 18]. Two further demands, Issue 5 and Issue 7, were settled by agreement of 24 September 2007 and withdrawn from the award at the parties' request [Para 45][Para 67].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must deduct the attendance bonus in proportion to the days a worker was absent, rather than cancel it entirely? [Para 25]
2. (Issue 2) Whether payment in lieu of annual leave must be calculated on the previous twelve months' total wages rather than the daily minimum wage plus bonuses? [Para 31]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must stop using fixed duration labour contracts and convert them to undetermined duration contracts? [Para 33]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must pay workers in the Wrapping and Checking Unit by piece rate? [Para 38]
5. (Issues 6 and 8) Whether the company must dismiss the labour dispute coordinator and the Chief of the Weaving Unit? [Para 46][Para 48]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, C.CAWDU):** the company used to strip the whole US$5 attendance bonus even for authorised leave, so deduction should be pro rata for simple personal obligations such as a religious ceremony or a friend's wedding [Para 26]; leave pay should follow Article 168 and average the twelve months' wages [Para 31]; fixed duration contracts of two, three and six months were not renewed when workers were sick, refused overtime, were pregnant or joined the union, costing seniority and maternity pay [Para 33][Para 34]; the Wrapping and Checking Unit should be paid by piece because the company already counts by piece [Para 38]; the two supervisors should be dismissed for refusing to meet worker delegates and for shouting at and intimidating workers [Para 51][Para 55].
**Employer (respondent, Fortune Garment & Woolen Knitting Factory Ltd):** it did not deduct for sick or wedding leave and only in months of 27 working days, and pro rata deduction would invite absenteeism that disrupts the production line [Para 27][Para 28]; it would keep its practice of daily minimum wage of US$1.92 times days plus bonuses [Para 32]; renewal depends on the production line, no worker was victimised, and over 300 workers hold fixed duration contracts against 1,000 undetermined [Para 34][Para 36]; buyers require monthly pay in the Wrapping and Checking Unit for quality [Para 42]; and dismissal of its staff is its exclusive right, with only a warning given [Para 60][Para 63].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to deduct the attendance bonus in proportion to days absent (Issue 1) and to compute leave pay in lieu on the twelve months' formula (Issue 2); the demand to convert fixed duration contracts was rejected (Issue 3); the piece rate demand was declined as an interests dispute (Issue 4); the dismissal demands were rejected (Issues 6 and 8) [Para 132][Para 133][Para 134].
Issue 1: Point 3 of Notification 017/00 of 18 July 2000 grants a bonus of at least US$5 for regular attendance but is silent on special leave and on how many days make a month regular [Para 68]. Following Awards 62/04 Ecent Issue 1, 63/07 Shine Well Issue 5 and 60/06 New Max Issue 2, the panel held the bonus is an incentive, not a punishment for permitted sick leave, so where leave is taken with permission the bonus is deducted pro rata — two days' leave costing US$0.385 and leaving US$4.615 [Para 69][Para 72][Para 79]. The Internal Work Rules Clause 5 (5), elsewhere cited as Clause 5 (4), only fixes 26 working days and does not address permitted leave [Para 30][Para 73].
Issue 2: Article 168 requires an allowance at least equal to the average wage, bonuses and indemnities over the twelve months before leave, excluding expense reimbursement [Para 79][Para 81]. The panel confirmed the method in Award 27/04 MS Issue 3 — total wages, overtime and attendance bonus over twelve months, divided by 12 and by 26, times the days of leave — and held the company's daily-minimum-wage method inconsistent with the article [Para 83][Para 85][Para 88].
Issue 3: Article 65 and Article 22 of Decree No. 38 make the contract the law of the parties and prevent one party imposing its form [Para 89][Para 90][Para 92]. Conversion is ordered only where fixed duration contracts exceed the two-year limit of Article 67, per Awards 10/03 Jacqsintex Issue 1 and 36/06 Mondotex Issue 2; here no evidence showed renewal beyond two years [Para 99][Para 101][Para 102].
Issue 4: Article 2 gives the employer the right to manage, which includes choosing piece rate or monthly wage, as held in Awards 62/06 Quick Shoes Issue 5 [Para 104][Para 105][Para 107]. The demand exceeded the law and, the union lacking most representative status under Article 69 (2B) and Clause 9 (1) of Prakas No. 305, was declined [Para 109][Para 111][Para 113][Para 114].
Issues 6 and 8: only a party to the contract may terminate it, so dismissal is the employer's exclusive right, and the panel will order it only on evidence that the person endangers workplace security [Para 122]. No such evidence was produced, so the demand was rejected [Para 127][Para 129].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus is an incentive for regular attendance, not a penalty: where a worker takes leave with the employer's permission, the bonus is reduced in proportion to the days of permitted leave rather than forfeited in full [Para 71][Para 77][Para 78]. Leave pay in lieu must be computed on the average of the previous twelve months' wages, overtime and attendance bonus, divided by 12 and by 26, and not on the daily minimum wage plus bonuses [Para 86][Para 88]. The panel will not order dismissal of a supervisor at the workers' demand absent evidence that the person endangers workplace security [Para 122][Para 129].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that a contract exists only once the parties agree on its content, so a party may propose a contract's form while the other may refuse it [Para 93][Para 94], and that it would decide an interests dispute brought by a most representative union [Para 110].
