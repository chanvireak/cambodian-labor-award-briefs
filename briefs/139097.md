# Award No. 203/16 — INKYUNG WONDANG APPAREL Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 12 September 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chhea presiding; Ly Tai Seng, employer nominee; An Non, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139097

## 1. Facts
INKYUNG WONDANG APPAREL Co., Ltd, a garment manufacturer at Phnom Penh Thmei, Sen Sok, employs about 874 workers. [Para 46][Para 47] The Khmer Youth Union Federation raised 18 demands; mediation left 5 unresolved, at the hearing the parties agreed point 5 and the workers withdrew point 3, so the panel considered points 1, 2 and 4. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 20][Para 21][Para 22] No most-representative union existed in the company, and 53 workers transferred their rights to the federation by letter of 9 July 2016. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 90]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must deduct the regular attendance bonus proportionately to the days a worker is absent for personal reasons, both where it gave permission and where it did not, instead of deducting the whole bonus once absence reaches half a day? [Para 2][Para 55][Para 97]
2. Whether the employer must pay 50 per cent of wages and allowances for 90 days to a woman worker *before* she begins maternity leave, rather than month by month during the leave? [Para 4][Para 62][Para 156]
3. Whether the employer must raise the midday meal allowance by 1,000 riel per day? [Para 8][Para 69][Para 175]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the attendance bonus exists to ease workers' daily living, yet deducting all of it for a half-day absence discourages regular attendance, because a worker who has already lost the bonus has nothing left to lose and keeps taking leave; paying the bonus in proportion to days actually worked would be fairer. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58] Maternity pay for 90 days should be advanced before delivery, since a woman is weak after childbirth, finds it hard to come for monthly payment, and other companies already do this. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65] The 1,000 riel meal allowance is not enough to buy nutritious food, and other employers pay 2,000 riel. [Para 71][Para 72]
**Employer (respondent):** the 13 USD attendance bonus already exceeds the legal minimum and is its own means of encouraging attendance, so it will not change; on maternity it pays 50 per cent monthly during the leave because it fears women will not return if paid in advance, and will follow the law if the law so requires; it cannot afford a higher meal allowance. [Para 59][Para 66][Para 67][Para 68][Para 73]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to deduct the attendance bonus only in proportion to days of absence it had authorised, allowed the full deduction for unauthorised absence, and ordered the 50 per cent wage and allowances for 90 days to be paid at least one day before maternity leave begins; the meal allowance claim was refused consideration. [Para 196][Para 197][Para 198][Para 199]

On issue 1 the panel noted that Article 2 of Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 sets a minimum of USD 10 per month and is a legal obligation, not a discretionary reward, while the 13 USD actually paid is higher. [Para 106][Para 132] It found the words "without absence" still unclarified by the Labour Advisory Committee and refused to construe them as covering both authorised and unauthorised absence. [Para 109][Para 110][Para 114] Absences that the law itself provides — annual leave, special leave, maternity leave, public holidays and weekly rest — do not allow any deduction, so the worker keeps the full 10 USD. [Para 111][Para 112] For absences on personal grounds that the employer has authorised, the leave is a suspension of the contract under Article 71(6) and the employer owes no wage for those days under Article 72(1), so it may deduct only the portion corresponding to the days of leave and not the whole bonus. [Para 133][Para 134][Para 136][Para 137][Para 138][Para 140] For absence without permission, by contrast, the bonus is an incentive for a full month worked, so the worker loses the entitlement and a full deduction is lawful. [Para 141][Para 143][Para 144][Para 155]

On issue 2 the panel applied Articles 182 and 183 of the Labour Law: a woman may take 90 days for childbirth and receives half her wage including allowances, though only after one year of service. [Para 156][Para 158][Para 159][Para 161] Neither article fixes when payment must be made, but Article 115(3) forbids paying wages on a day the worker is entitled to rest and requires payment one day earlier where the pay day falls on such a day; maternity pay is a wage under Article 103, which expressly lists sums payable during maternity leave. [Para 163][Para 164][Para 166][Para 167] The employer's practice of paying month by month during the leave therefore did not conform to Article 115, and payment for the 90 days had to be made at least one day before the leave began. [Para 171][Para 172][Para 173]

On issue 4 the panel held the demand for a higher meal allowance to be a dispute of interest above the statutory position. [Para 175][Para 176] Because an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement and binds all workers for a year under Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099, only a most-representative union under Articles 54 and 72 of the Trade Union Law may bring one, and the 53 claimants were neither a most-representative union nor a sufficiently qualified one. [Para 186][Para 187][Para 189][Para 190][Para 193] The claim was therefore not considered. [Para 194][Para 195]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer grants leave on personal grounds at the worker's request, the contract is suspended, so the employer may deduct the attendance bonus only in proportion to the days lost and may not forfeit the whole bonus; where the absence was never authorised, the incentive condition fails altogether and the whole bonus may be withheld. [Para 138][Para 141][Para 144] Maternity pay being a wage, it must be paid before the leave begins rather than during it, because Article 115(3) bars payment on a rest day and requires earlier payment instead. [Para 166][Para 167][Para 173] An interest dispute may be considered only if brought by a most-representative union, since the award would bind the whole workforce for a year. [Para 189][Para 190]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that it still had no authoritative interpretation of "without absence" from the Labour Advisory Committee and that Article 183 conditions the half-wage on one year of service — neither point being necessary to the orders made. [Para 109][Para 159]

[Para 102][Para 112][Para 174] Stray "2017" tokens interrupt the reasoning at the representation passage, and the signature block carries "unproment 2017". [Para 92][Para 200] The union's own name appears in the case title as the Khmer Youth Union Federation, but the employer's evidence list registers an unrelated federation (Cambodian Labour Union Federation); the award does not reconcile them. [Para 1][Para 32] The three union officials attending the hearing are named in full and are redacted here. [Para 49]
