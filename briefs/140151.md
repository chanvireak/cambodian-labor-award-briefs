# Award No. 132/12 — Great Honour Tech Style Factory Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 23 August 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Tuon Siphann (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (president) · Doc ID 140151

## 1. Facts
Great Honour Tech Style Factory Ltd. has operated since April 1998 making garments at Village 4, Svay Rolum, Sa'ang, Kandal, and employs about 1,806 workers, roughly 80 per cent of them women [Para 72][Para 73][Para 93]. The Cambodian Democratic Garment Workers' Union Confederation, whose local union has 697 members, filed on 31 May 2012; conciliation on 22 June 2012 settled 14 of 26 demands [Para 31][Para 32][Para 75]. Twelve points reached the Council, and at the hearing of 8 August 2012 three were agreed and three withdrawn, leaving six for decision [Para 37][Para 38]. Both parties are signatories of the Memorandum of Understanding of 28 September 2010, which lets them choose a binding award on rights disputes, so the panel split its order into a rights part and an interests part [Para 39][Para 195][Para 202].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 8) Whether the employer must keep paying wages to workers absent on sick leave certified by an official doctor? [Para 80][Para 123][Para 125]
2. (Issue 9) Whether the employer must keep paying the regular attendance bonus during certified sick leave, and add USD 5 to the USD 7 it already pays? [Para 87][Para 165][Para 168]
3. (Issue 15) Whether the employer must build a nursery for workers' children? [Para 93][Para 172][Para 181]
4. (Issue 16) Whether the employer must raise the infant-milk allowance from USD 8 to USD 20 a month? [Para 102][Para 182][Para 183]
5. (Issue 20) Whether the employer must pay a housing and travel allowance of USD 10 a month? [Para 107][Para 186][Para 187]
6. (Issue 21) Whether the employer must contribute 2,000 riel a day towards the midday meal? [Para 113][Para 190][Para 191]

The panel settled issues 2, 6 and 12, and the worker party withdrew issues 1, 5 and 9 of the original twelve [Para 37][Para 38].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** wages rose nowhere while rents went from USD 10 to 15 or 20 to 25 and transport from USD 6 to 10, so the company should pay 2,000 riel for lunch, USD 20 for infant milk, USD 10 for housing and travel, and USD 5 more attendance bonus, as other factories do [Para 109][Para 110][Para 113][Para 124]. On sick leave they asked that any state, district, communal, health-centre or licensed private certificate be accepted, not just the single hospital it recognises, seven kilometres away [Para 80][Para 86][Para 130].

**Employer (respondent):** it recognises only one private hospital, seven kilometres away, because its doctors will not issue false certificates and workers have previously produced forged ones; outside that hospital it deducts wages and the bonus [Para 81][Para 82][Para 83][Para 86]. It pays USD 8 a month in lieu of a nursery since 2003 and would build a nursery but then stop the USD 8 [Para 95][Para 101], and it refuses the other allowances as beyond the law, offering instead to apply Ministry Notice No. 230 of 25 July 2012 fixing USD 7 for housing and travel from 1 September 2012 [Para 92][Para 107].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to set up a nursery for workers' children and to keep paying the USD 8 a month in lieu until it is running; the other five demands were refused, four of them without examination [Para 194][Para 201].

The panel first set out the divide it would apply: under Article 312(2) of the Labour Law the Council decides rights disputes by law and all other disputes in equity, and under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 an award on interests replaces the collective agreement for a year and binds every worker in the enterprise [Para 117][Para 119][Para 122]. Because of that reach, an interest claim may only be brought by a union with majority representativeness, alone or together with others [Para 133][Para 136]. The union here had 697 of 1,806 members, admitted it was not the most representative, and its receipt for a representativeness request carried no date, signature or seal, so the panel disregarded it [Para 76][Para 78][Para 79][Para 140].

On sick leave the panel held that certified absence suspends the contract under Articles 71(3) and 72(1), so neither side owes performance and the employer owes no wages unless a provision says otherwise [Para 123][Para 125][Para 127]. It found the company's practice — full wages and bonus for certificates from that hospital — more favourable than the law, but the demand to accept every hospital went beyond the law and was therefore an interest claim that a non-representative union could not bring [Para 129][Para 131][Para 141].

On the attendance bonus it treated Ministry Notice No. 041 as making the bonus part of wages under Article 103, and held that workers earn the full bonus for days actually worked, with deductions only for absences the employer permitted [Para 152][Para 153][Para 157][Para 158]. Since the company already paid the statutory USD 7, the demand for USD 5 more exceeded the law and fell away as an interest claim [Para 167][Para 168][Para 169].

The nursery was the one point grounded in law: Article 186 requires any employer of at least 100 women to provide a nursing room and a nursery, or to pay the cost of care elsewhere, and the company had never provided one [Para 172][Para 173][Para 175]. The panel rejected the USD 8 payment as a permanent substitute because the employer itself conceded it could build a nursery, and ordered both the nursery and continuation of the USD 8 until it operates [Para 177][Para 180][Para 181].

The milk, housing and lunch demands failed for want of any law, collective agreement or agreement fixing those sums, and so were interest disputes the union could not bring [Para 182][Para 186][Para 190].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that goes beyond what the Labour Law, a collective agreement or an agreement between the parties provides is a dispute over interests; because an interest award replaces the collective agreement and binds the whole workforce, only a union holding more than half the enterprise's workers may bring it, and absent that the panel will not examine the claim [Para 122][Para 136][Para 141]. A payment made in lieu of a statutory nursery is no answer to Article 186 once the employer concedes it can build one [Para 180][Para 181].

**Obiter dicta:** the reading of Ministry Notice No. 041 — that the attendance bonus is wages and is earned for days worked, deductions being limited to permitted absences — was not needed once the claim was classified as an interest dispute [Para 153][Para 157].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel's list of surviving issues is inconsistent: paragraph 38 names issues 3, 4, 7, 8, 10 and 11, while the reasoning and the operative order decide issues 8, 9, 15, 16, 20 and 21 [Para 38][Para 194][Para 196]. The conciliation figures also shift, with 26 demands but 12 unresolved points transmitted as 18 elsewhere in the file [Para 32][Para 33]. The internal rules in evidence belong to "Winner Knitting Factory Ltd", evidently the plant's former name, and the union's certificate is issued to the same old name [Para 50][Para 58]. OCR corruption is extensive: lines 60, 67, 74, 120 and 198 contain duplicated or garbled passages, and the milk-allowance demand is printed as USD 20 in one place and USD 12 in another [Para 103][Para 182][Para 198]. The signature block is unreadable apart from the president's name [Para 203].
