# Award No. 14/06 — Zheng Yong Factory Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 March 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Chhiv Phyrum (employer-chosen), An Nan (worker-chosen), Tan Try (chair) · Doc ID 140686

## 1. Facts
Zheng Yong Factory Co., Ltd employs 1,800 workers at National Road 5, Toul Sangke commune, Russey Keo district, Phnom Penh [Para 34]. The Free Trade Union of Workers of Kingdom of Cambodia (FTUWKC) at Zheng Yong garment factory raised seven demands; five were conciliated on 7 February 2006, leaving two before the panel [Para 16]. The first concerns 12 piece-rate workers in the cutting section — four section chiefs and eight polishing and cutting workers — who since October 2005 have been required to lay out and cut one table of white pockets a day instead of one table every three days, with the extra hour excluded from the piece-rate count [Para 35][Para 51][Para 61]. Their monthly piece-rate earnings run from about US$60 to US$300 [Para 50]. The second demand is that the company not transfer workers from one section to another [Para 14].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must calculate additional piece-rate wages for the 12 cutting-section workers when the section chief orders them to cut more white pockets, over and above the one table every three days counted in the rate? [Para 13][Para 54]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must refrain from transferring workers from the workshop section to another section in the future? [Para 14][Para 68]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), FTUWKC at Zheng Yong factory:** the company should pay increased wages to piece-rate cutting-section workers when the chief orders them to cut more pockets, because from October 2005 they spend an extra hour a day on white pockets that earns them nothing and leaves less time for counted work [Para 13][Para 65]. Warehouse workers should also not be moved to other sections [Para 14].
**Employer (respondent), Zheng Yong Factory Co., Ltd:** cutting the pockets is simply part of the cutting-section workers' duty, so no additional wage need be calculated, and transferring workers between sections is the company's right [Para 14][Para 55].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the company to calculate additional wages for the hours the 12 named workers in List "A" spend cutting extra pockets, using average hourly wage multiplied by the additional hours per month, and rejected the demand on transfers [Para 70][Para 68].

On the first issue the panel applied Article 65 paragraph 3 of the Labour Law, treating a verbal contract as a tacit agreement on the conditions laid down by the labour regulations [Para 60]. The pre-October 2005 routine — one table of white pockets every three days, about an hour's work, outside the piece-rate count, with monthly-paid workers helping — had held for many years and was therefore a term of the agreement between the 12 workers and the company [Para 49][Para 60].

From October 2005 a new supervisor changed the policy, raising the requirement to one table a day (still about an hour), excluding it from the piece rate, and stopping monthly-paid workers from assisting [Para 61][Para 62]. The panel found the change did not push the workers below the minimum wage but made them work harder, tired them and took time from counted work, so they lost the chance to earn what the piece rate should yield [Para 64][Para 65]. It accepted the employer's power to manage for a profitable line but held that new policies cannot unilaterally alter the status of the labour contract, citing Notice 017 on pieceworkers and Article 22 of State-Decree 38/88, and following Award 29/04 — Top One [Para 66][Para 67]. The panel found the contract was changed unilaterally without the workers' agreement, and ordered extra pay for the extra hour, calculated as average monthly wage divided by monthly working hours, multiplied by the additional hours set by the employer [Para 67][Para 68].

On the second issue the panel found the transfer demand concerned only hypothetical future facts: the workers alleged a past transfer that cost them benefits but made no specific claim about it, and the panel could not predict when or in what circumstances a transfer would occur [Para 70]. It therefore declined to decide that issue [Para 70].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a piece-rate practice has fixed itself over years — here one table of white pockets every three days, uncounted — it becomes a term of the verbal contract under Article 65 paragraph 3, and an employer that unilaterally raises the uncounted workload must pay for the additional hours at the average hourly wage derived from the monthly wage and monthly working hours [Para 60][Para 67]. A demand that concerns only hypothetical future conduct, with no specific past claim, is not one the panel will decide [Para 70].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's acknowledgment of the employer's power to manage the enterprise for a profitable production line, which it held did not extend to unilateral alteration of contract terms [Para 66].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's name appears variously as "Zheng Yong Factory Co., Ltd", "Zheng Yong company" and "Zheng Young factory", and the union as "FTUWKC" and "FTYWKC" [Para 4][Para 31]. Witness names are given in a list that repeats an entry number and a name, and "Witnesses from the employer's side: N/A" is OCR'd as "NIA" [Para 29][Para 30]. Several sentences are duplicated or truncated, notably the passage on the verbal agreement, the before-and-after October 2005 comparison and the award citation "29/04-Top In" [Para 60][Para 63][Para 67]. The non-conciliation report is numbered 264/MoLVT while a related minute carries 263/MoLVT/LID [Para 33]. List "A" gives twelve worker names but the OCR interleaves them with stray "Mr." fragments [Para 36].
