# Award No. 002/21 — Vinton Group Chamkat Co., Ltd (វិនីតុន គ្រុប ចំកាត់) (Arbitration Council, 22 February 2021)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun appointed by the employer party, Tuon Si Phan appointed by the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 140822

## 1. Facts
Vinton Group Chamkat (វិនីតុន គ្រុប ចំកាត់), a cigarette manufacturer at Kbal Damrey village, Khan Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh, employed 578 workers when the award issued. [Para 44][Para 1] On 12 January 2021 the Labour Disputes Department received a collective complaint from the workers raising seven demands on working conditions, and conciliation before the Ministry failed, the non-conciliation report being forwarded on 25 January 2021. [Para 13][Para 10] At the hearing the workers' representatives withdrew issues 3 and 4 and reached agreement on issues 5 and 6, leaving only issues 1 and 2 for the panel. [Para 20][Para 21]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay a fuel allowance of USD 60 per month to all workers on equal terms, full-rights and non-full-rights alike, and reimburse the USD 6 monthly special allowance for 2019 and 2020? [Para 46][Para 81]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay an additional USD 26 per month in meal allowance to workers holding oral employment contracts? [Para 62][Para 118]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, ភាគី តំណាង កម្មករ និយោជិត):** workers doing the same work over the same distances received USD 60 in Phnom Penh but only USD 50 in the provinces, although provincial drivers travel further, which they say is discrimination under Article 12 of the Labour Law; oral-contract workers work the same eight hours as written-contract workers but receive USD 26 instead of USD 52 in meal allowance. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 75][Para 76]
**Employer (respondent, ក្រុមហ៊ុន វិនីតុន គ្រុប ចំកាត់):** the fuel allowance follows a clear company policy keyed to workload and distance in each zone and involves no discrimination; the USD 6 special allowance depended on company profit, which was not available in 2019 and 2020; the USD 52 meal allowance is tied to performance evaluation and to signing a written contract. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 78][Para 119]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — every claim was rejected and the award issued as a non-binding order (សេចក្តីបង្គាប់ មិន ជាប់ កាតព្វកិច្ច). [Para 129][Para 130][Para 132][Para 133]

On the fuel allowance the panel applied Article 2 of the Labour Law, which it read, following earlier awards, as conferring a management right on the employer exercisable lawfully and reasonably. [Para 81][Para 82][Para 83] It found no law, regulation or agreement requiring the employer to pay a fuel allowance at all, and treated the payment as a benefit above the statutory floor. [Para 85][Para 86] On the discrimination argument under Article 12, the panel held that the party alleging discrimination carries the burden of proof, and found that the 2017 payroll table showed no instance of workers of the same level, doing the same work over the same distance, receiving different fuel allowances. [Para 91][Para 92][Para 94] The workers produced no other evidence, so the panel upheld the management decision as lawful and reasonable and dismissed the USD 60 claim. [Para 95][Para 96][Para 97]

The USD 6 special allowance was refused on the same basis: no law or agreement obliged the employer to pay it, and its grant rested on the company's unilateral decision taken in light of profits, which had not been available in 2019 and 2020. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 105][Para 106]

On the meal allowance the panel again found no legal or contractual duty, held that USD 52 was conditional on good performance, discipline, compliance with instructions and signature of a written contract, and found the workers had not shown any right of theirs to be affected. [Para 122][Para 123][Para 125][Para 126] The USD 26 claim was accordingly dismissed. [Para 127][Para 132]

A late demand for a USD 10 monthly special allowance, raised only in the workers' post-hearing brief, was not considered at all: Article 312 of the Labour Law and Article 33 of Prakas No. 099 dated 21 April 2004 confine the panel to the issues in the non-conciliation report and their direct consequences. [Para 107][Para 109][Para 110][Para 111][Para 117]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer pays an allowance above the statutory minimum under a policy of its own, the payment remains within the management right conferred by Article 2 of the Labour Law and does not become an enforceable entitlement merely because it is paid unequally; a worker alleging discrimination under Article 12 must prove that like workers doing like work in like conditions were treated differently, and bears that burden. [Para 82][Para 86][Para 91][Para 96]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that an employer may by unilateral decision grant bonuses beyond those fixed by law in order to encourage output is a general statement on incentive schemes, not necessary to rejecting the USD 6 claim. [Para 99]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The date the panel was constituted is printed twice over as "27 January 2019 2021", two years in one phrase. [Para 17]
- The year 2020 is truncated to "20" wherever it appears, including in the framing of the 2019–2020 claims and in the operative order. [Para 46][Para 98][Para 130]
- The workers' own figures conflict: the special allowance is recorded in the non-conciliation report as USD 6 per month, while the post-hearing brief is summarised as claiming USD 10 per month, which the panel treated as a separate late demand. [Para 46][Para 60][Para 107]
- Demand 3 in the heading reads as annual-leave compensation, yet the panel records issues 3 and 4 as withdrawn without stating their content, so the withdrawn demands cannot be reconstructed. [Para 4][Para 20]
- Several names on the face of the award are broken by OCR into role labels and stray characters; individuals other than the arbitrators are therefore identified here by role only. [Para 2][Para 25]
