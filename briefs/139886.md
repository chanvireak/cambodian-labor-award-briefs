# Award No. 221/13-Ly Long — Ly Long (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 21 November 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chhea presiding; Ing Sothy, employer nominee; Tuon Si Phan, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139886

## 1. Facts
Ly Long (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, at Santouch village, Srea Thmey commune, Rolea B'ier district, Kampong Chhnang, employs some 330 workers. [Para 1][Para 73][Para 74] On 30 September 2013 the Cambodian Workers' Union Federation and workers of the factory held an election creating a factory union and elected nine leaders; the employer received the notification letter No. 3991/13 naming them that same day. [Para 160][Para 161][Para 164] A strike on 8 October 2013 over fourteen demands produced no settlement at conciliation and the file reached the Secretariat on 10 October 2013. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 23] Five named leaders were then dismissed — three on 1 October 2013 and one on 7 October 2013 — while the union's registration application was only receipted by the Ministry on 17 October 2013, so the union was still unregistered when the dismissals occurred. [Para 166][Para 228][Para 233][Para 262] Two of the five did not attend the hearing of 31 October 2013. [Para 93][Para 156]

## 2. Issues in dispute
Of the fourteen demands, thirteen were settled at the hearing of 31 October 2013, two were withdrawn by the workers and the panel considered only issue 1. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48]
1. Whether the employer must take back three dismissed workers, Ms A (elected union president), Ms B (elected vice-president) and Mr C (committee member), with pay and allowances preserved from dismissal until return — and, as a preliminary, whether Ms A resigned at all? [Para 181]
2. Whether the three workers enjoy the special protection against dismissal of Article 293 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 305, given that the union was not yet registered? [Para 197][Para 207]
3. Whether the employer dismissed the three because of union discrimination contrary to Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law? [Para 252]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissals were union discrimination — the administrative chief told Ms A and Ms B that no union would be allowed in the factory and that its founders would be dismissed, and asked Mr C to sign a contract leaving the union to keep working; no fault was ever put to them. [Para 102][Para 108][Para 117][Para 148][Para 264]
**Employer (respondent):** there was no discrimination; Ms A left of her own accord, as recorded in an unsigned "employee resignation" form dated 15 September 2013; Ms B was dismissed within her two-month probation for misconduct and for stirring other workers; and Mr C's dismissal rested on reports that some workers sat playing with machines instead of working. [Para 152][Para 183][Para 192][Para 267][Para 268]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted in part — the panel ordered the employer to take back Ms A, Ms B and Mr C at the same workplace and to preserve their wages and allowances from the day of dismissal until the day of return; the claims of the two absent workers were not decided. [Para 278][Para 279]
On standing, the panel applied Article 268 of the Labour Law, under which a professional organisation acquires the rights the law recognises only when it is registered with the Ministry; an unregistered union therefore had no lawful right to represent its members here. [Para 167][Para 169][Para 172] It noted its consistent earlier awards to the same effect. [Para 170] Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 lets a party be represented by a person holding its written authorisation; the two absent workers' authorisation was signed on 1 November 2013, after the hearing of 31 October 2013, so it had no effect and three claimants remained. [Para 173][Para 174][Para 177][Para 178][Para 179][Para 180]
On Ms A, the party alleging resignation bears the burden of proving it. [Para 185][Para 189] The resignation form recorded only her staff number A29, the date 15 September 2013 and the reason "request to stop", with the manager's signature but not hers; the panel found it insufficient to show a voluntary expression of will and concluded she did not resign, so the employer's refusal to let her work from 1 October 2013 was a dismissal. [Para 191][Para 193][Para 194][Para 195][Para 196]
On protection, Article 293 requires the labour inspector's prior agreement before a staff delegate or candidate is dismissed, and Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 extends that protection to union candidates for 45 days before and after an election, and to union leaders once elected. [Para 197][Para 198][Para 199] Following Awards 40/10, 103/12, 191/12 and 154/13, it held that an elected leader is protected from the day of the election until the union is registered, otherwise the right to form a union under Article 266 and the ban on anti-union discrimination in Articles 12 and 279 would be emptied of content. [Para 211][Para 214][Para 215][Para 217] Ms A, elected president, and Ms B, elected vice-president, were dismissed on 1 and 7 October 2013, inside that window, and the employer had been notified of their names on 30 September 2013. [Para 221][Para 222][Para 233][Para 234][Para 237][Para 238] Mr C was a committee member who had stood unsuccessfully and was not a leader; his dismissal on 1 October 2013 preceded the 17 October 2013 registration application, so the Article 293 protection did not reach him. [Para 223][Para 227][Para 228][Para 229][Para 241]
The employer never asserted that it had obtained the labour inspector's authorisation before dismissing Ms A and Ms B. [Para 246] Under Article 4(4) of Prakas No. 305 a dismissal of a protected worker without prior authorisation is void, so both dismissals were annulled and reinstatement with preserved wages followed. [Para 248][Para 249][Para 250][Para 251]
On discrimination the panel weighed the circumstances together: five workers were dismissed, four on a single day, one day and six days after the election; all five were named in the notification the employer received on 30 September 2013; and the employer gave no specific reason or evidence for any dismissal, failing to produce the photographs and other proof it said it had. [Para 262][Para 263][Para 267][Para 270][Para 271] It found these facts consistent and sufficient to conclude the dismissals were connected to union discrimination, and ordered reinstatement of all three on that ground as well. [Para 272][Para 275][Para 276][Para 278]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker elected to union leadership is protected against dismissal from the day of the election until the union obtains registration; a dismissal during that window without the labour inspector's prior authorisation is void, and the employer's stated reason does not save it. [Para 217][Para 247][Para 250] Where dismissals cluster within days of the employer's receipt of the list of elected leaders, hit only named leaders, and are supported by no particularised reason or evidence, the panel will infer anti-union discrimination and order reinstatement even for a worker who falls outside the Article 293 protection. [Para 271][Para 272][Para 276]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel held that the fact that four other named leaders were still working could not by itself disprove discrimination, since an employer need not dismiss every union member at once, and some had signed away their union membership to keep their jobs. [Para 273]


## 6. Source defects disclosed
The arithmetic of the demand list does not close: fourteen demands went to conciliation, thirteen are said to have been settled but only eleven numbers (2 to 9, 11, 12 and 14) are listed, two (10 and 13) were withdrawn and one (1) remained — thirteen plus two exceeds fourteen. [Para 22][Para 46][Para 47][Para 48] The workers' demand spoke of five leaders (line 2), while the election of 30 September 2013 returned nine. [Para 2][Para 161] The panel records "04" workers present at the hearing and then counts three; one dismissal is printed "ពី ១៩៩៩៨ ការងារ", a stray digit string where a date should be. [Para 180][Para 245] The award is dated 21 November 2013, the parties having agreed to postpone issue from 6 November 2013; the two hearing dates of 21 and 31 October 2013 are given as bare dates in the procedure section. [Para 1][Para 19][Para 20][Para 50] The employer's nominee signature block carries no printed name. [Para 280]
