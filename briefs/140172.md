# Award No. 163/12 — M&V Garment International Co., Ltd, Kampong Chhnang branch (Arbitration Council, 31 August 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phallaph presiding; Kaov Thach, employer nominee; Liv Sovanna, worker nominee) · Doc ID 140172

## 1. Facts
M&V Garment International Co., Ltd, Kampong Chhnang branch, employed about 4,871 workers, of whom some 840 were members of the enterprise union M&V 4, affiliated to the Cambodian Workers' Trade Union Federation. [Para 1][Para 28][Para 30] The Federation complained on 24 July 2012 of three demands to improve working conditions; the Kampong Chhnang provincial labour office conciliated on 14 August 2012 and settled one of the three, leaving two unresolved in non-conciliation report No. 522 Kb/KCh of the same date, forwarded to the Secretariat on 17 August 2012. [Para 9][Para 10][Para 11] Both parties attended the hearing of 24 August 2012, conciliation again failed, and they agreed at the hearing that the award would be binding as to rights and non-binding as to interests — the split the panel then applied. [Para 13][Para 15][Para 16][Para 20] The workers' demand that the bonus be cut beyond the days of authorised leave, and their claim of 8,000 riel rather than 2,000 riel of food allowance, were never reached as such: the first was absorbed by the pro-rata order and the second was declined as an interest demand. [Para 86][Para 87][Para 100]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether, where a worker takes authorised special leave for personal reasons, the employer must deduct the monthly regular attendance bonus (ប្រាក់រង្វាន់ធ្វើការទៀងទៀង) pro rata to the days of absence, or may withhold it altogether? [Para 31][Para 48]
2. (Issue 2) Whether voluntary work on a Sunday is overtime, so that the employer owes the statutory overtime food allowance of 2,000 riel per day rather than the 1,000 riel it pays, or whether Sunday work is outside overtime and attracts no allowance at all? [Para 40][Para 73][Para 75]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** a day of leave permitted by the employer should cost only that day's share of the bonus, and a run of prior Arbitration Council awards had so ordered; and Sunday work is no different from weekday overtime, so at 1,000 riel an hour over eight hours the allowance should be 8,000 riel, the employer paying only 1,000. [Para 35][Para 42][Para 45][Para 83]
**Employer (respondent):** Notification No. 041 of 7 March 2011 makes the attendance bonus payable only to workers who actually came to work, so no bonus is owed for a day of absence; and Sunday work is like holiday work, which the law does not treat as overtime, so no overtime food allowance is due at all. [Para 37][Para 39][Para 47][Para 74]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must deduct the regular attendance bonus pro rata to the days the workers took authorised leave, and must pay an overtime food allowance of 2,000 riel per day to workers who voluntarily work overtime on Sundays; the demand for 8,000 riel was refused as an interest dispute. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 100]
On Issue 1 the panel first classified the demand, following Awards 05/11-IM V (branch 1) and 13/11-Goul Kimvich, as a rights dispute because the entitlement is written in Notification No. 041/11. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 53] It held the bonus to be a wage under Article 103 of the Labour Law, since it is money paid as a reward, and treated authorised absence as a suspension of the contract under Article 71(6) which, by Article 72, relieves the employer of paying wages for the days not worked. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63][Para 64][Para 65] The employer therefore owes nothing for the leave days themselves, but the bonus is earned across the month and must be reduced only in proportion to those days, as the prior awards had ordered and as the panel ordered here. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 69][Para 72]
On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 137 of the Labour Law, which caps normal hours at eight a day and forty-eight a week, and Award 114/08-YT, which fixed the normal week as Monday to Saturday. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 77] Work on Sunday therefore falls outside the normal days and hours and is overtime, whatever the employer's practice of paying 1,000 riel. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 83][Para 84] Under point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 a worker who works overtime at the employer's request receives 2,000 riel a day or one free meal, with no reference to the number of hours, so the employer's 1,000 riel practice was unlawful. [Para 81][Para 82][Para 85] The further claim to 8,000 riel was above the statutory floor and so an interest dispute; the union, with 840 members out of 4,871 workers, was not the most representative in the enterprise, and the panel declined to consider it, as it had in Awards 02/11-Pou Yien and 66/11-In Han Sung. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 90][Para 92][Para 98][Para 99][Para 100]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Voluntary work on a Sunday, being outside the six-day normal week, is overtime, and the flat statutory food allowance for overtime is payable per day worked and not per hour, so neither the employer's lower practice nor a worker's higher demand displaces the fixed sum. [Para 77][Para 82][Para 85] Where the sum demanded exceeds that statutory floor the demand becomes one about interests, which the panel will not decide unless the claiming union is the most representative in the enterprise; the award on rights stands as binding on the parties from the outset. [Para 88][Para 90][Para 92][Para 99][Para 104]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that an award resolving an interest dispute substitutes for the collective agreement for one year from the date it becomes final, and that the right to strike cannot be used to reopen a collective agreement still in force — observations unnecessary to an outcome it reached on rights and declined on interests. [Para 95][Para 96][Para 97]

[Para 23][Para 24][Para 25][Para 27][Para 60] Paragraph 95 is corrupted by an untranslated run of English ("the second the state of the state …") inserted into Article 43 of Prakas 099, so the text of that provision is quoted only so far as it survives. [Para 95] The claim is stated as "2 8000 riel" in one place and as 8,000 riel elsewhere, the employer's name appears as both "M&V" and "MV", and the worker representatives are listed only by role and initial. [Para 2][Para 86][Para 28][Para 98] The number of demands is given as three with one settled, yet only two unresolved issues reached the panel, and the notice is cited both as No. 041 Kb/SChN and No. 041/11.
