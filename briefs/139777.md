# Award No. 329/14 — Indochina I Pheril (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 30 December 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Seng Vouch Hun (employer-appointed), An Virak (worker-appointed), Tang Tri (chair) · Doc ID 139777

## 1. Facts
Indochina I Pheril (Cambodia) is a garment factory of about 1,500 workers. After conciliation failed on nine collective claims (non-conciliation report No. 1710/14 of 24 November 2014), the Federation pressed the case; at the hearing the workers withdrew claims 1, 2, 5, 7, 8 and 9, leaving issues 3, 4 and 6. [Para 1][Para 13][Para 23][Para 102] Only 190 workers had transferred their rights to the Federation, so the panel decided the case for those 190 alone. [Para 108][Para 110]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Panel's issue 3) Whether the employer must pay full wages and the regular attendance bonus to the 1,017 workers who struck on 11 October 2014, without distinguishing those who scanned their fingerprints out from those who did not? [Para 111][Para 155]
2. (Panel's issue 4) Whether the employer must (a) extend male workers' fixed-duration contracts from 3 to 6 months, (b) renew the contracts of male workers who committed no fault, and (c) convert workers with two years' aggregate service to indefinite-duration contracts? [Para 171]
3. (Panel's issue 6) Whether the employer must deduct 2,000 riel in union dues from 1,017 workers for December 2014? [Para 234]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** All strikers struck alike, so fingerprint scanning should not split entitlement to full wages; roughly 80 per cent of male workers hold 3-month contracts while most women hold 6-month contracts, which is sex discrimination contrary to Article 12; workers past two years' aggregate service must be moved to indefinite-duration contracts under Article 67(2); and the change of union leadership is an internal union matter that cannot justify stopping dues deductions. [Para 117][Para 126][Para 142][Para 152]
**Employer (respondent):** The 11 October strike was called without prior notice, the workers did not work, and no wage is owed; the employer already showed leniency by keeping half a day's wage and the attendance bonus for those who scanned out; contract duration follows individual performance evaluation, not sex, and the company has men on 6-month and women on 3-month contracts too; dues will be deducted once the new leadership is recognised by the Ministry. [Para 119][Para 128][Para 153]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three remaining claims were rejected; no sum was ordered, and the award became binding by the parties' agreement of 5 December 2014. [Para 170][Para 233][Para 244][Para 250]

On issue 3 the panel read Article 332(1) of the Labour Law as suspending the employment contract on the day of a strike, and Article 72(1) as removing the employer's duty to pay wages for that period. Following Award No. 27/10 and a line of earlier awards (04/03, 03/05, 63/07, 110/07, 81/08), it held the employer owes neither wages nor the attendance bonus for strike days whether the strike was procedurally lawful or not, so the claim was refused. [Para 158][Para 165]

On issue 4(a) the panel set out Article 12, which bars an employer from using race, colour or sex as the basis for hiring, allocation of work or training, while allowing distinctions inherent in a particular job. Applying earlier awards, it held the party alleging discrimination carries the burden of proof. The workers' evidence was the four workers who testified; all four admitted their output had declined because of union work, and the panel found the company also had men on 6-month and women on 3-month contracts. Discrimination was therefore not proved. [Para 173][Para 175][Para 184][Para 186]

On issues 4(b) and 4(c) the panel treated contract formation as offer and acceptance under Article 65 of the Labour Law and Articles 311, 336, 337 and 664 of the Civil Code: no party can force another to accept terms it does not agree to, so the employer owed no duty to renew or to lengthen contracts. On conversion, no worker present had more than two years' aggregate fixed-duration service; the panel ordered the workers to file a list of any who did by 11 December 2014, none was filed, and the claim was dismissed without consideration. [Para 200][Para 230][Para 232][Para 233]

On issue 6 the panel found that dues for December 2014 would only be deducted in January 2015, so the dispute had not yet taken concrete shape; the Council resolves live labour disputes, not future ones, and the claim was dismissed without consideration. [Para 240][Para 241]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A day of strike suspends the employment contract under Articles 332(1) and 72(1) of the Labour Law, so the employer owes no wage and no attendance bonus for that day, whether or not the strike followed legal procedure; and an allegation that contract duration is sex-discriminatory fails unless the worker who asserts it proves the difference was sex-based rather than performance-based, the burden lying on the party making the allegation. [Para 158][Para 185]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that a worker's request to move from a 3-month to a 6-month contract is not contrary to the spirit of the law, and that the employer's refusal is equally lawful, each being an exercise of contractual freedom; it also noted that the union's new leadership had not yet been recognised by the Ministry and so lacked standing to represent members. [Para 107][Para 210][Para 211]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company is named "ឥណ្ឌូចិន អី ភែរីល (ខេមបូឌា)" on the face of the award, but the enterprise-level union is repeatedly printed as sitting at "ក្រុមហ៊ុន អ៊ីន ដូស៊ីន ហ្គាវ័ន្ត", a second company name the text never reconciles. [Para 1] The panel's constitution is dated "២៤ ខែវិច្ឆិកា ឆ្នាំ ២០១៩ ២០១៤", carrying both 2019 and 2014. [Para 19] The complaint is said to have reached the provincial department on 21 November 2014, yet conciliation is dated 4 September 2014, with the month itself corrupted to "កញ្ជ្ញូ". [Para 16][Para 17] Membership figures do not reconcile: 4,176 members said to have resigned, 190 workers who transferred rights, and 1,017 dues payers; the union's deduction request letter asks for 4,200 riel while the claim is for 2,000 riel. [Para 6][Para 93][Para 108] The employer-side arbitrator's name survives only in the header; the signature block is lost to OCR ("mountrosting AMAXI"). [Para 251] Paragraphs 235–236 are badly corrupted, with figures printed as "+ 009៧ @" and "២០១៤៩៩".
