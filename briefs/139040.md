# Award No. 118/16 — I-Cheng (Cambodia) Corporation (Arbitration Council, 23 June 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun for the employer party, Sin Kim Sean for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139040

## 1. Facts
I-Cheng (Cambodia) Corporation, a shoe factory in Tang Doung village, Ba Ti district, Takeo, employed about 2,732 workers and recognised a local union affiliated to the Democratic Confederation of Labour (C.CAWDU); 97 workers transferred their rights to the confederation to bring this case. [Para 1][Para 67][Para 68][Para 70][Para 75] Conciliation of the eight demands failed on 17 May 2016 and the hearing was held on 3 June 2016. [Para 27][Para 25] The workers then withdrew demands 1, 2, 5, 6 and 8, leaving three: repayment of amounts deducted from Ms P for three days' workshop attendance, reinstatement with back wages of Mr S and Ms C, and bank transfer of union dues. [Para 28][Para 32][Para 33][Para 34]

## 2. Issues in dispute
3. Whether the employer must repay three days' daily wage, the regular attendance bonus, the lunch allowance, the role allowance and the position allowance it deducted when Ms P attended a workshop from 29 to 31 March 2016? [Para 105]
4. Whether the panel should decide afresh the demand that the employer take back Mr S and Ms C and pay their wages from the date of suspension to the date of return? [Para 128]
7. Whether the employer must hand over union dues deducted from members' wages to the union by bank transfer? [Para 159]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Ms P requested special leave 15 days in advance and the employer granted it, so the deductions penalised an authorised absence; the workshop benefited the employer; the email of 12 May 2016 proves the union notified its election candidates; dues should go by bank transfer because a previous leader handling cash was prosecuted over counterfeit dollars. [Para 83][Para 85][Para 90][Para 91][Para 100][Para 101]
**Employer (respondent):** the Labour Law does not require wages or benefits during a workshop, which is the worker's private business; reinstatement was already decided in Award No. 128/15 on identical parties and facts, and the email is nothing new; the union leaders work at the factory and can collect dues there. [Para 86][Para 92][Para 93][Para 94][Para 102][Para 104]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three remaining demands were refused, and the award is printed as a non-binding order that becomes final eight days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Minister of Labour. [Para 180][Para 181][Para 182][Para 183]

*Issue 3.* The panel found Ms P took special leave with the employer's authorisation to attend a workshop at the invitation of the Community Legal Education Centre, using her "special leave" entitlement. [Para 105] Article 71(6) suspends the contract for absence the employer authorises, and Article 72(1) makes suspension affect the principal obligations, including wages, unless a contrary rule provides otherwise. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 109] The company's internal rules of 22 November 2013, clause 5.6.2, describe special leave as unpaid leave requiring one day's prior application, so the panel treated the leave as a contractual suspension under Article 71(6). [Para 110][Para 111] It accepted that the attendance bonus, role allowance, position allowance and lunch allowance are all part of "wage" under Article 103, following its earlier awards, [Para 114][Para 115][Para 116][Para 119][Para 121][Para 122] but held that because the days were unpaid special leave, the employer was entitled to deduct those items pro rata and applied the internal rules and the law correctly. [Para 112][Para 113][Para 123][Para 124]

*Issue 4.* The panel asked whether it should decide the same issue again and applied "អាជ្ញាអស់ជំនុំ" (res judicata), which has no express basis in arbitration procedure but applies generally, on three conditions: same parties, same issue brought to the panel, and a full decision. [Para 128][Para 129][Para 130][Para 132] Mr S and Ms C were the original claimants on issues 1 and 10 of Award No. 128/15 of 30 June 2015 against the same company, the issue was the same, and the panel there dismissed reinstatement and back wages. [Para 136][Para 142][Para 144][Para 146] On the claimed new evidence, the workers could have produced the email within the time allowed in the earlier case (to 9 June 2015); their failure was their own negligence, and Cambodian law treats only postal notification as official. [Para 152][Para 155][Para 94][Para 157][Para 158]

*Issue 7.* The panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, contract, agreement or past practice obliging the employer to transfer deducted dues to the union's bank account, so the demand went beyond the law and was an interests dispute. [Para 161][Para 162][Para 163] Under Article 96 of the Labour Law, Article 54 and Article 72 of the Law on Trade Unions of 17 May 2016, and Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099, only the most-representative union may bring an interests dispute, and a panel award on interests replaces the collective agreement for one year; the claimants here were not such a union, so the panel declined to consider the claim. [Para 166][Para 170][Para 172][Para 174][Para 177][Para 179]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Leave taken with the employer's permission under an internal rule providing for unpaid special leave suspends the contract under Article 71(6) and Article 72(1), so the employer may deduct the daily wage and the wage components of that day — attendance bonus, lunch, role and position allowances, all of which are "wage" under Article 103 — without further liability; the fact that the leave benefited the employer does not convert it into paid leave. [Para 106][Para 109][Para 112][Para 122] A claim already fully decided between the same parties on the same issue will not be reheard, and evidence the party could have produced within the time the earlier panel allowed is not the kind of new evidence that reopens it. [Para 130][Para 132][Para 155][Para 158] An interests dispute may only be arbitrated at the instance of a most-representative union. [Para 174][Para 177]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that email is not yet official notification in Cambodian law and that the workers' omission showed they had not prepared their case thoroughly are not necessary to the res judicata holding; nor is its account of the purpose of res judicata — avoiding contradictory outcomes and ensuring finality. [Para 94][Para 133][Para 155]
