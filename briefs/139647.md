# Award No. 190/14 — Juhui Footwear Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 12 August 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; Tun Si Phan, appointed by the workers; Tang Tri, president) · Doc ID 139647

## 1. Facts
Juhui Footwear Co., Ltd runs a garment and footwear factory at Roung Village, Khnor Dambang Commune, Cheung Prey District, Kampong Cham, employing about 5,200 workers. The C.CAWDU shoe union at the plant held a registration certificate of 2 August 2013, had 724 members who thumb-printed for dues deduction in June 2014, and 1,577 members in total; a further 2,124 non-member workers transferred their rights to the C.CAWDU federation from 23 June 2014. On 20 June 2014 the union filed a 12-point demand; conciliation on 1–3 July 2014 settled two points and left ten, of which the panel took nine after point 12 was withdrawn at the hearing of 17 July 2014. [Para 17][Para 24][Para 49][Para 50][Para 53][Para 164]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay 4,000 riel a day as a midday meal allowance to each worker? [Para 55]
2. Whether the employer must add 2,000 riel a day as an overtime meal allowance, on top of the 2,000 riel already paid? [Para 61][Para 168]
3. Whether the employer must raise the transport and accommodation allowance from USD 7 to USD 10 a month? [Para 70][Para 180]
4. Whether the employer must issue pay slips four days before payday? [Para 80][Para 190]
5. Whether the employer must adopt a daily target bonus policy (class "A" USD 10, "B" USD 7, "C" USD 5 a month)? [Para 93][Para 215]
6. Whether the employer must pay a skills allowance of USD 5 a month to all workers with three months' service and USD 20 a month to large-machine operators? [Para 99][Para 223]
7. Whether the employer must raise the attendance bonus from USD 10 to USD 15 a month and pro-rate the deduction for authorised leave? [Para 112][Para 230]
8. Whether the employer must pay a USD 20 a month milk allowance for children up to three years old? [Para 126][Para 271]
11. Whether the employer must pay USD 500 a month into a worker welfare fund? [Para 138][Para 291]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** market prices and rent have risen while wages stay low; comparable factories pay 2,000–3,200 riel for the midday meal, and some provide it free; fuel costs push travel to USD 5–20 a month; large-machine operators face the highest accident risk yet earn the basic USD 100 wage; the employer's bonus and skills payments lack transparency. [Para 57][Para 73][Para 106][Para 136]
**Employer (respondent):** it applies the Labour Law and the Ministry's notifications 041/11 and 230/12 in full; the factories cited by the union are under 5% of GMAC members, so no legal obligation arises; safety equipment, a safety officer, accident cover and NSSF contributions are already in place; a nursing room exists and a nursery is under construction. [Para 59][Para 109][Para 128][Para 144]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay the lost wages within 6 days; every other claim was dismissed or refused consideration, in two operative parts: part A for union members and part B for the 2,124 non-members who transferred rights. [Para 214][Para 301][Para 316][Para 323]
The panel first separated rights disputes from interest disputes under Article 312 of the Labour Law and Prakas 099 (Article 43), holding that the award binds for rights disputes under the 3 October 2012 Memorandum of Understanding but is non-binding and appealable for interest disputes. [Para 147][Para 149][Para 152][Para 303]
Meal, overtime-meal, transport, target-bonus, skills-allowance, attendance-bonus, milk-allowance and welfare-fund claims (points 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7(1), 8 and 11) were all categorised as interest disputes: no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice obliged the employer to pay them, and the employer's existing payments matched Prakas 041/11 and 230/12. Because the claiming union was not the most representative in the enterprise — 1,577 members against 5,200 workers — the panel held it lacked standing to bring interest disputes at all, and refused to consider each claim without reaching its merits. [Para 168][Para 171][Para 217][Para 220][Para 225]
Point 4 was treated differently: the missing pay slips were in substance unpaid wages, a right guaranteed by Articles 120 and 121 of the Labour Law, so the panel had jurisdiction. The employer admitted that five workers were underpaid on 10 June 2014 owing to a computer fault, that it held the matter three days before sending it to accounts, and that payment came thirteen days later, with the shortfall rolled into the next month's payroll and taxed again. The panel found the delay longer than the law allows and the fault the employer's own; under Article 118 the employer bears the burden of proving payment. It ordered the lost wages paid within 6 days. [Para 191][Para 194][Para 205][Para 209][Para 211]
On point 7(2) the panel refused to pro-rate the attendance bonus for authorised absences, holding that permission to take unpaid leave and entitlement to the bonus are separate matters and that the employer's rule of deducting USD 10 divided by 26 per day, with total loss beyond two and a half days, was more favourable than the notification, which requires no absence at all. [Para 253][Para 257][Para 267][Para 269]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that is not the most representative in the enterprise cannot put an interest dispute to the panel, and the panel will decline to consider such a claim on its merits however reasonable the demand; but a claim that the employer withheld wages already earned is a rights dispute under Articles 120 and 121 and is heard and decided on the evidence, with the burden of proof of payment on the employer. [Para 162][Para 177][Para 191][Para 208]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that the award becomes a collective agreement for one year, that workers thereby lose the right to strike over the same interest demands, that 13 days is too long for a monthly wage to reach workers living hand to mouth, and that permission to take leave does not by itself satisfy the attendance condition for the bonus. [Para 161][Para 206][Para 211][Para 259]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
OCR is heavy and several amounts and dates are printed two ways. The payment date of the June 2014 payroll reads "10 December June 2014" [Para 194]. The ordered deadline appears as "1 6 days" in the reasons, "/6 days" in part A of the disposition and "6 days" in part B [Para 214][Para 301][Para 316]. The overtime meal allowance paid by the employer is given as "20 riel" once and 2,000 riel elsewhere [Para 170]. The attendance bonus figure reads "2 10 dollars" [Para 232]. In part B, point 1 of the disposition is left without any decision verb, so it is unclear whether the 4,000 riel claim was there dismissed or simply copied [Para 313]. Stray year digits ("2015", "2016", "2017") are embedded in the reasoning text [Para 77][Para 169]. Paragraph 298 survives only as the word "sex" ("ភេទ"). Worker and union names at [Para 106] survive only as OCR strings and are not used here.
