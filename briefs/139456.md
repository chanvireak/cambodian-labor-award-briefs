# Award No. 277/15 — Vanna Mi (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 2 December 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139456

## 1. Facts
Vanna Mi (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd cuts and sews garments at Bati district, Takeo province, with about 900 workers; the C.CAWDU local union was registered on 15 September 2015 and its three leaders recognised by the Ministry the same day. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63][Para 64] Ms T, a packaging worker, was hired on 3 March 2015 on two months' probation and then successive three-month fixed-duration contracts, the last expiring 3 November 2015; she was the local union president. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 122] On 20 October 2015 the administration called her in, ended the contract and told her to stop work, with no severance paid and no application to the labour inspectorate. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 75]

## 2. Issues in dispute
Of thirteen claims, seven were settled (points 4, 6, 7, 8, 11, 12, 13) and point 5 was withdrawn, leaving five for decision. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 29]
1. Whether the employer must take the union leader Ms T back into work and pay her wages from the day of dismissal to the day of return? [Para 71][Para 109]
2. Whether the employer must remove Mr Y, the administrative chief, from the company? [Para 88][Para 130]
3. Whether the employer must remove the Chinese production manager from the company? [Para 94][Para 155]
9. Whether the employer must pay all of the 50% maternity wage and allowances before the day the worker stops work for maternity leave? [Para 97][Para 161]
10. Whether the employer must pay a midday meal allowance of 2,000 riel a day? [Para 103][Para 176]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the administration ended Ms T's contract on 20 October while it still had weeks to run and without the inspectorate's consent, because she led the union — she had no fault or warning on record, and the company knew of her position, having been given the election result and the registration receipt. The administrative chief habitually used demeaning language and lost workers' documents, and the Chinese manager shouted, threatened dismissal and filmed workers; the 1,000 riel meal allowance should rise to 2,000. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 82][Para 84][Para 88][Para 94][Para 104]
**Employer (respondent):** the contract was not renewed because her work was poor, not terminated; it did not know she was a union leader, and it paid wages to the contract's end plus severance. It cannot remove its own staff, who must consult their superiors, and the shouting was only instruction to work faster. It pays 50% of the maternity wage in two halves, fearing the worker would not return if paid in advance; a 1,000 riel meal allowance is all a one-year-old company can afford. [Para 83][Para 91][Para 95][Para 102][Para 105][Para 106]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to reinstate Ms T and pay her wages from the dismissal to the expiry of her last fixed-duration contract, and to pay 50% of wages and allowances for the 90 days of maternity leave at least one day before the leave begins; the claims to remove the two managers and for a 2,000 riel meal allowance were dismissed. [Para 197][Para 200][Para 198][Para 199][Para 201]

Issue 1: following Award No. 181/14 — Syung Yin (Cambodia) B Thick Style, the panel held that ending a fixed-duration contract before its expiry is not a non-renewal but a unilateral dismissal before term. [Para 110][Para 111][Para 112] Ms T was told to stop on 20 October, before the 3 November expiry, so the act was a dismissal. [Para 113][Para 114] Article 293 requires the labour inspectorate's consent for dismissing a staff representative, art 295 makes a dismissal without that consent void, and art 4(2) of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 extends the protection to union leaders, as the panel had held in Award No. 007/14 — Alim. [Para 115][Para 118][Para 119] The employer admitted it never applied for consent. [Para 124] Reinstatement with back wages to the contract's expiry was ordered, and art 34 of Prakas No. 099 was cited as the source of power to order re-engagement and immediate payment. [Para 125][Para 128][Para 129]

Issues 2 and 3: arts 65 and 311 of the Civil Code mean only the parties to a contract may dissolve it, so a third party cannot compel the removal of an employee; art 2 of the Labour Law gives the employer the right to organise and manage, exercised lawfully and reasonably. [Para 130][Para 132][Para 133][Para 135][Para 141] Prior awards allow an order removing a worker only where the person is shown to be dangerous and their retention would endanger others' safety. [Para 143][Para 144][Para 146] The allegations were not substantiated to that standard, so both claims were rejected. [Para 151][Para 157][Para 160]

Issue 9: arts 182 and 183 give 90 days' maternity leave on half wages including allowances, but fix no payment date; art 115(3) forbids paying wages on a rest day and requires payment the day before, and art 103 counts maternity pay as wages. [Para 161][Para 162][Para 163][Para 164][Para 167] Following Award No. 154/11 — B & N, art 115 applies to the 50% maternity wage, so the company's split practice of paying half before and half on return breached it. [Para 168][Para 171][Para 173][Para 174]

Issue 10: the agreement of 21 May 2015 set 1,000 riel from 1 May 2015 and only promised to review the extra 500 riel after three months in the light of real capacity, which created no obligation. [Para 107][Para 108][Para 180] No law, collective agreement, internal rule, contract or practice supports 2,000 riel, so the claim is an interests dispute; under art 96 and art 9 of Prakas No. 305 only the most representative union may bring one, and this complainant union was not, so the panel declined to consider it. [Para 183][Para 184][Para 188][Para 194][Para 195]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Ending a fixed-duration contract before its expiry date is a unilateral dismissal before term, not a refusal to renew, so the statutory protection of staff representatives and union leaders applies: the employer must obtain the labour inspectorate's prior consent under arts 293 and 295 of the Labour Law, read with art 4(2) of Prakas No. 305, and without it the dismissal is void and the worker must be reinstated with wages to the date the contract would have expired. [Para 111][Para 114][Para 118][Para 124][Para 129]
Maternity pay is wages for the purposes of art 115(3), so the 50% payable over the 90 days must be paid in full at least one day before the leave begins, whatever split practice the employer has adopted. [Para 164][Para 167][Para 171][Para 174]
An interests dispute over a benefit the law does not fix may be brought only by the union with most-representative status in the enterprise; a union without that status cannot put such a claim to the panel at all. [Para 188][Para 194][Para 195]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that meetings to instruct the administration and section heads on workers' grievances would improve labour relations, and its encouragement of the employer to hold them, were observations going beyond what either order required. [Para 153][Para 159]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The panel is said to have been constituted on "30 October 2019 2015" [Para 24].
- The dismissal date is 20 October 2015 throughout the findings and orders [Para 73][Para 123][Para 197], but the reasons print it as "20 October July 2015" [Para 113].
- The employer's evidence includes a letter dated 21 December 2015 refusing recognition of three named leaders [Para 39][Para 52], a date later than the award itself and impossible for material before the panel; the same refusal is also dated 18 September 2015 [Para 38].
- The company name varies as "Vanna Mi", "Vanna Mmi" and "Vanna Mim" across the file, including in the case name [Para 1][Para 23][Para 61].
- Severance is called "05 percent" in the employer's account [Para 83] where 50% is meant elsewhere [Para 175], and a stray digit precedes "50 percent" there.
- The administrative chief's name survives as a single syllable, "Yinh", with no given name [Para 88][Para 130].
