# Award No. 105/15 — ឃែន ធើរេន អី ភែរីល (ខេមបូឌា) (Arbitration Council, 15 June 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bunchea presiding; Chhiv Phirom, employer nominee; An Non, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139197

## 1. Facts
The respondent is a garment factory at Trapeang Thloeng village, Choam Chao commune, Pur Senchey district, Phnom Penh, employing about 1,520 workers. [Para 1][Para 48][Para 49] Its Kanteran Development Workers Union, certified on 13 October 2014, claims 115 members. [Para 50][Para 51] On 24 March 2015 the labour dispute department received the federation's complaint No. 299/15 over ten demands for better conditions; conciliation on 23 April 2015 settled one of the ten, and report No. 443 of 4 May 2015 reached the Secretariat on 5 May 2015. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20] The panel heard the case on 18 May 2015, and the parties then agreed on two further points — correction of a worker's identity details and the voluntary character of overtime — which the panel recorded without deciding. [Para 17][Para 25][Para 54][Para 110] Seven issues remained. [Para 26]

## 2. Issues in dispute
2. Whether the employer must pay termination compensation now, in case the company later closes or relocates? [Para 55][Para 130]
3. Whether table-cutters sent to unload containers are entitled to an incentive bonus of USD 15 or USD 20 a container? [Para 65][Para 142]
4. Whether the employer must contribute to medical costs, and pay full seniority and outstanding benefits to the family, where a worker falls gravely ill or dies? [Para 77][Para 160]
5. Whether the employer must pay a thirteenth month's wage each year? [Para 87][Para 172]
6. Whether the employer must pay out unused annual leave every year while the worker remains employed? [Para 91][Para 178]
7. Whether pregnant workers may leave 15 minutes before the end of the working day? [Para 97][Para 199]
9. Whether a pregnant worker may take one paid day a month to attend an antenatal check? [Para 111][Para 206]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company rents its premises on a lease expiring in June 2015 and has moved once before without paying termination compensation, so about 95 per cent of workers fear closure and want the money set aside in advance; table-cutters are not warehouse staff yet are made to unload containers and should be rewarded as other factories reward that work; other companies pay a thirteenth month, a seniority payment on death and a US-dollar incentive; unused leave should be cashed out yearly because workers never take it all; and the five minutes the company allows pregnant women is too short, half a day a month is too little for antenatal checks at distant clinics. [Para 58][Para 60][Para 70][Para 79][Para 93][Para 98][Para 112]

**Employer (respondent):** the premises are rented and the lease does not end the employment relationship; it has given no notice of closure or relocation and will pay statutory compensation if it ever closes; unloading containers is part of the table-cutters' general duties and is done within working hours, with no policy of bonus; sick workers get full wages for the first month and 60 per cent for the next two; no law or agreement provides a thirteenth month; leave is carried forward rather than paid out; and the five minutes and half day already granted are enough, with a clinic nearby. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 73][Para 75][Para 83][Para 90][Para 96][Para 102][Para 115]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — no issue was granted: the claims for closure compensation, the container-unloading bonus, medical and death benefits, a thirteenth month, yearly payout of unused leave, 15 minutes' early departure and one day a month for antenatal checks were all refused. [Para 213][Para 214][Para 215][Para 216][Para 217][Para 218][Para 219]

On the claimant the panel first held that a registered local union may represent its members, and that the Kanteran Development Workers Union, certified on 13 October 2014 with its leadership recognised, was the complainant. [Para 126][Para 128][Para 129]

On issue 2 the panel held the claim premature: the company is still operating, the employment relationship continues, and no closure or relocation has occurred, so the dispute concerns a future event; it cited its consistent position that it is established to resolve labour disputes and not problems that have not yet arisen. [Para 134][Para 136][Para 138][Para 140]

On issues 3, 4, 5, 7 and 9 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice creating the entitlement, so each was an interest dispute. [Para 144][Para 165][Para 173][Para 200][Para 207] It then applied Article 96 and Article 9 of Prakas 305 with Article 43 of Prakas 099: an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement and binds all workers, so the panel decides one only for the most representative union, which this union was not. [Para 148][Para 149][Para 152][Para 158]

On issue 6 the panel read Article 167(2) of the Labour Law as allowing payment in lieu of annual leave only where the contract is terminated, and Article 167(3) as voiding any agreement to forgo the right to leave. [Para 183][Para 186][Para 187][Para 188] Article 167(4) permits deferral of leave, but not for more than three consecutive years, and only for the part above twelve working days. [Para 192][Para 193] It followed that a worker still employed cannot demand yearly cash in place of leave, and the employer's practice of carrying leave forward was correct. [Para 196][Para 197][Para 198]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for compensation that presupposes a closure or relocation that has not yet happened is a future dispute, which the panel will not decide while the employment relationship subsists. [Para 134][Para 140] While a worker remains employed, unused annual leave may be deferred but not converted into money: Article 167(2) allows payment in lieu only on termination, and Article 167(3) voids any agreement to give the right up. [Para 187][Para 192][Para 196]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel remarked that the employer ought to encourage workers to carry leave into the following year rather than pay it out, a preference that did not decide the case because the claim failed on Article 167 in any event. [Para 195]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award and the Secretariat papers give no Latin-script name for the company, whose Khmer name appears as ឃែន ធើរេន អី ភែរីល, សែន ធើរេន and ឃែន ធើ វេន; it is cited here as printed on the face of the award. [Para 1][Para 37][Para 45][Para 50] The conciliation is dated 23 April 2015, but the report bearing the unresolved issues is No. 443 of 4 May 2015 and reached the Secretariat on 5 May 2015. [Para 19][Para 20] The panel says nine of ten points were unresolved, yet only seven issues were argued, two having been settled by agreement after the hearing. [Para 19][Para 26][Para 25] Issue 7 is recorded as five minutes in the company's practice, but the reasoning once prints "50 minutes". [Para 97][Para 199] The quoted Article 167(4) speaks of a limit of twelve working days of deferred leave, although Article 166 gives eighteen days a year, and the panel did not reconcile the two. [Para 181][Para 193] The company's registered statutes are dated 19 September 2013 under number 105/2003. [Para 33] The order skips issues 1 and 8, which were settled by agreement, and carries a stray numeral in the refusal of issue 9. [Para 212][Para 213][Para 219]
