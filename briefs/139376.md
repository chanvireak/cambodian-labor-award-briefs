# Award No. 224/16 — Casino Koh Meas and Hotel (Arbitration Council, 23 November 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mar Samborna (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (presiding) · Doc ID 139376

## 1. Facts
Casino Koh Meas and Hotel, a casino and hotel operator in Preah Sihanouk province employing 137 staff, dismissed 23 workers of its gaming section on 29 September 2016 without prior notice, giving financial difficulty and the leasing-out of the section as the reason. [Para 36][Para 42][Para 50] Twenty of them claim; they had received their final wages but not their annual leave or any termination compensation. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45] The enterprise had no most representative union, and two workers designated by the provincial labour department were authorised by the claimants to act. [Para 75][Para 78]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the dismissal of 23 gaming-section workers at one time, on the ground of financial difficulty, was a collective dismissal? [Para 89]
2. Whether the employer owed the 20 claimants compensation on termination of their contracts? [Para 82]

Both questions were argued as a single numbered issue: the workers' claims to notice pay, severance, moral damages and outstanding annual leave. [Para 40][Para 82]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer gave no valid reason for dismissing the 20 workers, had not followed the collective-dismissal procedure, and owed notice pay, severance, moral damages and unused annual leave. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 49]
**Employer (respondent):** The company was in financial difficulty and could not pay; the gaming section had been leased to a third party who chose which staff to retain; and seven of the dismissed workers had breached internal rules by socialising with guests. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 52]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to pay the 20 claimants notice pay under Articles 75 and 77, severance under Article 89, moral damages under Article 91 and outstanding annual leave under Articles 166 and 167, each according to actual seniority; no monetary total is computed, and three disputed start dates are left to the parties to reconcile. [Para 164][Para 165]

Representation. With no most representative union at the enterprise, the panel applied its reasoning in Case 180/16 (Cement Cambodia Chakrey Ting): workers in dispute may represent themselves or authorise a local union or any other person to act for them. [Para 70][Para 79] The two representatives had been certified by the Preah Sihanouk labour department, held transfers of right signed by the 20 claimants, and appeared at the hearing, so they were entitled to act. [Para 75][Para 78][Para 80]

Collective dismissal. Article 95 requires an employer reducing the activity of its establishment, or reorganising internally, to rank those to be dismissed by professional qualification, seniority and family burden, to notify the workers' representatives so as to soften the impact, and to give priority to dismissed workers for two years. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 97][Para 98] The panel held that leasing the whole gaming section to a third party, which then decided which staff to keep, was a reduction of activity within Article 95 and not the total closure of a section with no staff retained — some workers continued in the same section. [Para 103][Para 104] The employer therefore had to rank before dismissing, and did not. [Para 105][Para 114][Para 115]

Misconduct. The employer asserted that seven named workers had socialised with guests contrary to internal rules but produced no evidence, only argument; the panel rejected the allegation. [Para 110][Para 111][Para 112]

The dismissal. Article 74 allows dismissal at the employer's will but never without a suitable reason relating to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the operational needs of the enterprise. [Para 83][Para 85] Unlawful collective dismissal being established, the panel found the terminations unsupported and awarded the statutory heads of claim: payment in lieu of notice under Articles 75 and 77, since no notice was given [Para 126][Para 128]; severance under Article 89 [Para 133][Para 137]; moral damages equal to the severance under Article 91, a head distinct from notice pay and severance [Para 144][Para 145][Para 149]; and unused annual leave, the employer having granted none before 2016 [Para 150][Para 155][Para 156]; average daily wage to be computed on the 12 months before dismissal [Para 130][Para 132].

Start dates. For three claimants the parties' lists of entry dates conflicted and neither produced contracts or identity cards bearing the date, so the panel declined to prefer either version and directed the parties to settle the dates with the documents before the severance was paid. [Para 118][Para 119][Para 120]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer leases out an entire operating section to a third party which then selects the staff to be retained, the resulting dismissals are a reduction of activity under Article 95, not a total closure [Para 103][Para 104], so the employer must first rank the affected workers by professional qualification, seniority and family burden and notify their representatives [Para 97][Para 105]; a dismissal that skips that step, and for which no aptitude, conduct or operational reason is proved under Article 74 [Para 83][Para 85], is unlawful and attracts notice pay, severance, Article 91 damages and outstanding annual leave [Para 126][Para 137][Para 145][Para 155]. An unparticularised allegation of misconduct, unsupported by evidence, fails [Para 110][Para 112].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that where the parties each file only a self-made list of entry dates and no contract or staff card, it cannot resolve the conflict and the parties should re-examine the documents before calculating severance [Para 119][Para 120], and that absence of a most representative union can obstruct workers' representation and so touch the constitutional right to claim reparation under Article 39 [Para 67][Para 68].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The dismissal date is given as 29 September 2016 in the findings [Para 42][Para 127] but as 30 July 2016 in the framing of the issue [Para 82]. The award's own reasoning speaks of "23 workers (20 of them claimants)" [Para 116] and elsewhere of 20 claimants out of 23 dismissed [Para 40], without reconciling the three who did not claim. The tables of entry dates at [Para 59] are badly mangled by OCR, with names, numbers and dates interleaved, and the third table's three disputed dates appear as pairs of years that cannot be read reliably [Para 118]. No monetary figure appears anywhere; even the days of notice and severance are expressed only by reference to seniority. A long OCR corruption at [Para 59] also introduces English fragments ("The Manner", "And American Arman") into a Khmer table.
