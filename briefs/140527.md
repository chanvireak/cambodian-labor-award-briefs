# Award No. 77/08 — Sing Tai Garment Co Ltd (Arbitration Council of Cambodia, 21 July 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of three: ហែម ហូណារិទ្ធ (employer-appointed), ហូន ជុន ឌី (worker-appointed), ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា (president) · Doc ID 140527

## 1. Facts
The employer, ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ឹង តាយ ហ្គា មិន (Sing Tai Garment Co Ltd), a factory of about 800 workers at Trapeang Chhuk, Tuol Kork district, Phnom Penh, faced a collective dispute brought by សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព-សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ ប្រចាំ ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ីង តាយ, a union of 100 members without most-representative status [Para 51][Para 54]. Its complaint of 14 February 2008 raised 12 demands; conciliation on 29 May 2008 left 5 unresolved, of which the panel's own conciliation settled one and the union withdrew another, leaving issues 1, 2 and 3 for decision [Para 20][Para 26][Para 27]. The plant has changed owners several times since 1999 and has been run by the present owner since January 2007 [Para 55].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must reimburse the 12,100 riel health-check fee to every worker, old and new alike? [Para 55][Para 86]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the panel may rule on the company's liability for workplace accidents that have not yet happened, and order it to state that liability in writing? [Para 101][Para 105]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must build a day-care centre and breastfeeding room, or may instead pay US$20 a month and three kilograms of milk powder per child? [Para 78][Para 116]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the union demanded 12,100 riel per worker for the health check, covering both long-serving workers and those transferred in from the former IN TIN TIN factory [Para 2][Para 62]; full company responsibility for workplace accidents, including treatment costs, wages and bonuses kept unchanged [Para 4]; and a nursery plus breastfeeding room, or failing that US$20 a month and three one-kilogram tins of milk [Para 6][Para 78]. It argued that the existing US$5 monthly payment was inadequate because market prices had risen [Para 84][Para 85].

**Employer (respondent):** the company pays for the health checks it requires and paid a 2007 health-check bill from the occupational health department for 350 workers, but cannot trace records for long-serving workers whose former management never settled the fee [Para 58][Para 87]. It said it already carries insurance for accidents inside the factory and cannot answer for accidents outside it, though it would stand behind any accident wherever it occurred [Para 75][Para 76]. It relies on clause 3 of the 3 February 2007 collective agreement substituting US$5 a month for a nursery, a term more than half the workforce accepted [Para 82][Para 129].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 3 was granted in substance by ordering the company to build a nursery, with the US$5 monthly payment permitted meanwhile; issue 1 was dismissed; and the panel declined to consider issue 2 [Para 134][Para 136][Para 137].

On issue 1 the panel used its power under Prakas 099 Article 25 to call for evidence and gave the union ten days to file a signed and fingerprinted list of the workers claiming the fee, with the dates they had paid it [Para 91][Para 93]. The union filed nothing, and instead produced a list of workers volunteering a union-dues deduction [Para 66][Para 92]. Following Awards 79/05 and 99/06, the panel held an unsupported assertion cannot be weighed, so the claim failed for want of proof of who had paid, when, and in what amount [Para 94][Para 96][Para 100].

On issue 2 the panel followed a consistent line from Awards 10/03, 14/06, 41/07 and 74/07: the Council exists to resolve actual labour disputes, not problems that have not yet arisen, since nobody can predict when or where an accident will occur or how serious it will be [Para 105][Para 107][Para 108]. It therefore declined to consider the demand and refused to compel the company to record a responsibility it already accepted in principle [Para 111].

On issue 3 the panel applied Article 186 of the Labour Law, which requires any enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a breastfeeding room and a nursery, and Article 13, which makes the Labour Law's rules public order so that no agreement may derogate from them [Para 116][Para 125]. It found the purpose of the rule is to keep mother and child together, allow natural breastfeeding for the first six months and keep the child safe while the mother works [Para 130]. A cash substitute, even one accepted by more than half the workforce under clause 3 of the 3 February 2007 agreement, cannot satisfy that purpose or release the employer from the duty [Para 131]. The demand for US$20 and milk was therefore rejected as contrary to law, the nursery was ordered, and clause 3 may continue to operate only until construction is complete [Para 132][Para 133].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer with at least 100 women workers cannot discharge the Article 186 duty to provide a nursery by paying cash in its place, even under a collective agreement accepted by most of the workforce, because the Labour Law's requirements are public order; the cash term may operate only as an interim measure until the nursery is built [Para 116][Para 131].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that a union representing only a minority of the workforce may still bring a collective dispute, and that it will not rule on a liability that all parties already accept in principle [Para 54][Para 110].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer-appointed arbitrator is printed as ហែម ហូណារិទ្ធ on the face of the award and as ហែម ហួ ណារិទ្ធ in the signature block. The factory's name is spelled at least five different ways across the award, and the union is likewise printed in two forms, so the exact registered name cannot be settled from this copy. The health-check sum is printed as ១២.១០០ riel in the demand and as "១២នាក់ ១០០ រៀល" in the operative paragraph, where a headcount has clearly displaced part of the figure. One receipt is dated 16 August 2007 in the evidence list and 6 August 2007 in the reasoning. The same receipt's total is printed as a malformed digit string and again as words that do not agree with it. Two worker names appear in full in the evidence list where the redaction should have run. Several paragraphs are truncated mid-sentence or carry stray digits.
