# Award No. 005/18 — Pou Yuen (Cambodia) Enterprise Ltd (Arbitration Council, 26 February 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed, dissenting; Ly Sovanna, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · Doc ID 138881

## 1. Facts
The company, a garment manufacturer, announced on 22 December 2017 that it was closing its entire production chain and told all workers to stop work from 23 December 2017. It paid termination compensation at the factory over three days, 26–28 December 2017, and on 4 January 2018 deposited the amounts of those who had not collected. Of about 1,900 workers, 1,781 were paid and 150 had not received their final wage; 478 of the 481 names on the workers' list had served more than two years. The workers' complaint of 2 January 2018 claimed termination compensation on the footing of unlimited-duration contracts. [Para 2][Para 88][Para 89][Para 102][Para 103][Para 143]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay termination compensation to workers who served more than two years as for an unlimited-duration contract (កិច្ចសន្យាមានថិរវេលាមិនកំណត់) — payment in lieu of notice, dismissal damages, moral damages, compensation for unused annual leave and the last month's wage? [Para 2][Para 87]
2. Whether the workers' contracts had become unlimited-duration contracts, given that the employer paid the 5% end-of-contract indemnity at each renewal? [Para 129][Para 133]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** asserted that workers who had served more than two years held unlimited-duration contracts because of their length of service, so the closure had to be compensated on that basis, and that the sums paid were short of what the Labour Law requires. [Para 94][Para 127]
**Employer (respondent):** said it had only ever used fixed-duration contracts, had no unlimited-duration contract with any worker, and had paid the 5% end-of-contract indemnity each time a fixed-duration contract expired, so nothing further was owed. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 128]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay termination compensation as for unlimited-duration contracts to the 478 workers who had served more than two years: payment in lieu of notice equal to the wages and benefits due for the notice period, dismissal damages equal to wages and allowances by seniority, compensation for annual leave not yet taken, moral damages, and the last month's wage where it had not been paid. [Para 196][Para 197]

On representation the panel applied Article 54(1) of the Law on Trade Unions and, both parties admitting that no most-representative union existed at the company, followed awards No. 171/16 (Jewelin), 183/16, 184/16, 188/16 and 227/16, together with Article 1 of Prakas No. 362 of 29 August 2016: workers recognised by the Labour Dispute Department may represent the collective dispute, and the three recognised representatives who appeared had that right. [Para 84][Para 112][Para 119][Para 121]

On contract type the panel applied Article 67 of the Labour Law: successive fixed-duration contracts whose total duration exceeds two years become unlimited-duration contracts by operation of law. Because the workers' list showed 478 of 481 with more than two years' service, their contracts were of unlimited duration. It rejected the employer's argument that paying the 5% indemnity under Article 73(6) at each renewal preserved the fixed-duration character: Article 73 concerns the sum payable when a contract ends, and an early payment cannot prevent conversion. [Para 133][Para 142][Para 143][Para 144]

On the heads of compensation the panel applied Articles 75 and 77 (notice period by seniority, and payment in lieu where notice is not given, calculated on the average wage of the last 12 months), Article 89 (dismissal damages, including 15 days' wages per year of service), Article 166 and 167 (annual leave and its commutation) and Article 91 (moral damages, equal to the dismissal damages, distinct from notice and dismissal compensation). It held the closure to be the employer's own decision and no valid ground for terminating without these payments, and ordered payment only where the sums had not already been paid. [Para 155][Para 165][Para 175][Para 192][Para 195]

The employer-appointed arbitrator, Ing Sothy, appended a dissenting opinion (paras 199–217): the employer is entitled to choose the contract type, the 5% indemnity was paid at every renewal, and the panel should not order unlimited-duration termination compensation for the 478 workers. [Para 201][Para 216]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer renews fixed-duration contracts so that a worker's total service exceeds two years, the contract becomes an unlimited-duration contract by operation of Article 67 of the Labour Law, and the payment of the 5% indemnity under Article 73(6) at each renewal neither preserves the fixed term nor bars conversion. If the employer then closes the enterprise, those workers are entitled to the full unlimited-duration package — notice in lieu, dismissal damages, unused annual leave, moral damages and the last month's wage — each computed on the average wage of the preceding 12 months. [Para 142][Para 144][Para 155][Para 165][Para 192]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that a worker's daily average wage is obtained by dividing the 12-month average monthly wage by 26, and that where annual leave compensation was already paid lawfully the employer owes nothing further; the dissenting arbitrator's view that the employer bears the loss when it chooses fixed-duration contracts for long-term work, since it must pay 5% each time. [Para 168][Para 177][Para 212]
