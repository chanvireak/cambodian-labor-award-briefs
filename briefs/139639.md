# Award No. 30/13 — F Juna Plantation Sen Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 20 February 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Yu Soun Ty (employer-appointed), An Nan (worker-appointed), Kong Pollak (president) · Doc ID 139639

## 1. Facts
F Juna Plantation Sen is an agricultural company in Oral district, Kampong Speu, employing about 300 workers. [Para 47] The Cambodian Labour Union Federation filed 18 collective demands in December 2012; five were settled at conciliation, the union withdrew nine, and four remained for decision. [Para 20][Para 25][Para 26] The employer did not attend the hearing and gave no reason, so the panel proceeded in its absence under Art 21 of Prakas 099. [Para 24][Para 31][Para 32] Mr M, a section head earning USD 250 a month after probation, was told on 15 December 2012 that his post was cut and his employment ended on 31 December 2012, three days before the union obtained registration. [Para 58][Para 67][Para 68][Para 69]

## 2. Issues in dispute
3. Whether the employer must reinstate the union activist Mr M to his former post and pay wages from the date of dismissal to the date of return? [Para 53]
12. Whether the employer must grant seven days of special leave to workers? [Para 72]
14. Whether the employer must grant 18 days of annual leave a year? [Para 82]
19. Whether the employer must pay arrears of overtime for work performed on Sundays in the past? [Para 84]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissal was union discrimination — the administration had forbidden Mr M to form a factory union, the union notified its election and its results to the employer in writing, and he was dismissed shortly after; workers never received special leave, annual leave or Sunday overtime pay. [Para 53][Para 62][Para 65][Para 81][Para 83][Para 85]
**Employer (respondent):** absent from the hearing and silent on the record; the only document it filed was a letter of 28 January 2013, and its dismissal notice of 15 December 2012 gave staff reduction as the ground. [Para 33][Para 67]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel rejected the discrimination claim but ordered reinstatement as head of the rubber-plantation section with back wages, plus seven days of special leave, 18 days of annual leave, and Sunday overtime arrears at a 100 per cent uplift. [Para 152][Para 153][Para 154][Para 155]

On discrimination the panel held that the party alleging it bears the burden of proof, following awards 79/05, 101/08, 168/09, 115/10 and 148/11, and that an absent employer waives its right of reply but does not thereby prove the claimant's case. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 97][Para 98] Because the union's letters of 7 November 2012 were never produced by the deadline, the panel could not find that the employer knew Mr M had stood for union office, and it rejected the discrimination allegation. [Para 95][Para 99]

The dismissal nonetheless failed on two independent grounds. First, Art 67(7) makes a fixed-term contract that is not reduced to writing an unfixed-term contract, and Mr M had no written contract, so his engagement was indefinite. [Para 115][Para 118] Second — and this is what decided the case — an unfixed-term contract may be terminated at will under Art 74 but only with a reason relating to the worker's capacity or conduct or to the operational needs of the enterprise, and the employer, who bears the burden on retrenchment, never appeared to prove that any staff reduction occurred. [Para 119][Para 121][Para 123][Para 125] Reinstatement with back pay followed. [Para 127]

On special leave the panel applied Art 171 and Prakas 267 of 11 October 2001, which allow up to seven paid days a year for personal or family events and permit deduction from annual leave only where annual leave remains unused. [Para 133][Para 135][Para 138] On annual leave it applied Art 166, which grants 1.5 working days a month, hence 18 days a year. [Para 139][Para 141][Para 143]

On Sunday work Art 139 (new) requires a 100 per cent premium for overtime on the weekly rest day, so the worker receives 200 per cent of the normal rate; the panel added that Art 120 limits wage claims to three years before the payment date and ordered recalculation on the daily base wage multiplied by the Sundays worked in that period. [Para 145][Para 146][Para 147][Para 151]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer who neither appears nor proves the operational necessity it invokes cannot rely on staff reduction to justify dismissing a worker on an unfixed-term contract; the burden of proving the lawful ground lies on the employer and is not discharged by its absence. [Para 121][Para 123][Para 125]

**Obiter dicta:** that an employer's failure to attend waives its right to contest the workers' assertions without shifting the burden of proof on to it. [Para 97][Para 98]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company name appears in at least four spellings ("F Juna Plantation Sen", "Fu Juna", "F Jouna"), and the address is given as Oral district in Kampong Speu while the union's address is Phnom Penh. [Para 1][Para 41] The demand list is numbered irregularly, running 1, 2, 3, "4(6)", "5(7)" and so on, and the withdrawal letter lists ten points where the award records nine withdrawn. [Para 5][Para 157] Demand 1 asks for "20 riel a day", an amount that cannot be a daily wage. [Para 2] The special-leave order is printed twice in the operative part, once truncated mid-sentence. [Para 153] Para 86 dates the claimant's evidence submission 14 February 2012 where the case ran in 2013. [Para 86] The operative order on Sunday pay says "multiply by the rate of 200 per cent" while the reasoning describes a 100 per cent uplift on the normal rate. [Para 146][Para 155] The arbitration panel's composition is printed with the employer-appointed arbitrator's name only in the signature block. [Para 157]
