# Award No. 047/16 — Din Han Enterprise Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 17 March 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ouk Ri (employer-selected), An Non (worker-selected), Kong Phallha (president) · Doc ID 138995

## 1. Facts
Din Han Enterprise Co Ltd, a Phnom Penh garment factory of about 2,200 workers, pays monthly wages on the 10th of each month [Para 48][Para 53]. Following a union demand lodged on 8 January 2016, conciliation settled 2 of 7 demands, leaving 5 unresolved, which reached the Arbitration Council on 23 February 2016 [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20]. At the hearing the parties agreed issues 2, 4 and 5 and the workers withdrew issue 3, leaving only the demand that wages be paid on the 8th of the following month [Para 24][Para 25][Para 26]. The claimants were 694 workers who had transferred their representation to the union confederation [Para 50][Para 52].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay monthly wages on the 8th of the following month? [Para 2]
2. Whether the employer must give the union or workers seven days' notice whenever work will or will not be performed on a national holiday? [Para 4]
3. Whether the employer must reinstate the union activist Mr H and pay him back wages from dismissal to return? [Para 7]
4. Whether the employer must play music for the workers to listen to during working hours? [Para 9]
5. Whether the employer must pay machine-section workers the same base wage as at another enterprise? [Para 11]

Issues 2, 4 and 5 were settled by agreement, issue 3 was withdrawn, and the panel reasoned only on issue 1 [Para 25][Para 26].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** wages should fall due on the 8th so that they can pay rent, which is owed on the 10th, before leaving the premises at 16:00; the long gap since the 25th of the preceding month forces them to borrow or ask relatives for money, and neighbouring factories pay on the 5th or 7th [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57][Para 59].
**Employer (respondent):** it runs two companies at once, with about 4,400 workers in total, and staggers payroll into two rounds, paying Din Han on the 10th; seven accountants cannot settle both payrolls earlier, and pulling the date forward would require accounting staff to work until about 22:00, contrary to the law [Para 60][Para 62][Para 63][Para 65].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the demand that wages be paid on the 8th of each month and made no order [Para 92][Para 93].

The panel found no law, regulation, notice, circular or other legal instrument fixing the day on which wages must be paid [Para 73]. Article 116 of the Labour Law requires payment at least twice a month with no more than sixteen days between payments, and at least once a month for salaried staff, but says nothing about a specific date [Para 70][Para 71][Para 74]. Applying Article 2, which it read as vesting management and direction of the enterprise in the employer so long as that power is exercised lawfully and reasonably, the panel held the employer may set the monthly payday [Para 76][Para 79][Para 82]. It followed earlier awards holding that the employer has that right where the date chosen is reasonable and lawful [Para 81][Para 83][Para 84].

On the facts, both parties accepted that the employer pays on the 10th and, where the 10th is a public holiday or weekly rest day, pays one day early [Para 85][Para 86]. The individual employment contract stipulates the 10th [Para 87][Para 69]. The panel weighed the payroll cycle described by the employer — data collection on days 1–2, verification on 3–4, transfer to accounting on 5–6, entry on 7–8 and calculation on 9–10 — and accepted that 4,400 workers across two companies genuinely require that time [Para 66][Para 89]. It also recorded that the employer had never previously been late and had always advanced the date around holidays [Para 90]. The arrangement was therefore reasonable and lawful, consistent with the content and spirit of Article 2 [Para 91][Para 88].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The Labour Law fixes the frequency of wage payment, not its date: where Article 116's twice-monthly minimum and sixteen-day maximum are met, the employer may set the payday under its Article 2 power of management, and that choice will be upheld where it rests on an agreed contractual term, is applied without lateness, and is justified by the practical constraints of the payroll operation [Para 74][Para 82][Para 83].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the workers' hardship between the 25th and the payday, and the earlier paydays of neighbouring factories, did not create an obligation, since comparison with other employers is not a source of entitlement [Para 57][Para 59].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The conciliation date is printed as "11 February, year" with the year omitted, and the hearing time as "02 and 000" [Para 19][Para 22]. The paragraph opening the findings runs the heading "ហេតុផល នៃ សេចក្តីសម្រេច" into the text of the facts [Para 47]. The award cites "Article 24" of the Labour Law where the reasoning plainly requires Article 2, the management-right provision quoted in full two paragraphs earlier [Para 76][Para 79]. The dispositive section repeats the same refusal twice, the second time in damaged wording [Para 93], and one signature block is reduced to stray OCR characters [Para 95]. The respondent's name appears in at least four transliterations across the file — Din Han Enterprise Co., Ltd, ឌីន ហាន អេនធើ ប្រាយ, ឌីហាន អិន ធើ ប្រាយ and ឌីហាន អ៊ិនធឺ ប្រាយ — and the union's own initials shift between "ស៊ី ខៅ ឌូ" and "ស៊ី ខ្មៅ ឌូ" [Para 1][Para 7][Para 43][Para 15].
