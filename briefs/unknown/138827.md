# Award No. (04/03 [case number OCR-damaged] — Lida Garment Company (Arbitration Council, 19 June 2003)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mr Ma Samborana, employer-appointed; Mr Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Mr Sok Matheoung, chair) · Doc ID 138827

## 1. Facts
Lida Garment Company employed about 600 workers over two shifts; on 21 May 2003 the worker representatives served 11 demands and notice of a strike. At a 27 May meeting the parties settled the first ten points, leaving only the demand that the Company dismiss its accountant, who the workers said had repeatedly miscalculated wages and bonuses. Some 500 workers struck from 17.30 on 28 May until 5 June 2003, returning on 6 June under a consent order of the panel. [Para 28][Para 15][Para 19]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the workers are entitled to wages and bonuses for the strike period 28 May to 5 June 2003? [Para 8]
2. Whether the Company must dismiss the accountant at the workers' demand? [Para 8]
3. Whether the Company is responsible for the accountant's incorrect calculation of wages, which was a cause of the strike? [Para 8]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The accountant calculated wages and bonuses wrongly month after month and answered complaints by telling workers to wait until the next month; they asked for strike-period pay unless she was removed, treating her conduct as embezzlement. [Para 33][Para 16]
**Employer (respondent):** The errors were a technical problem, not intentional, and complaints were rechecked and fixed quickly; hiring and dismissing staff is the Company's own prerogative, and wages are not owed for strike days. [Para 34][Para 17][Para 18]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the strike-wage claim was rejected and the dismissal demand rejected, but the Company was ordered to accept responsibility for the losses of workers whose wages and bonuses were miscalculated, to review salaries and bonuses for February, March, April, May and June 2003 within 2 weeks, to pay any shortfall within three days of discovery, and to pay $10 in damages to each employee who went on strike. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 59][Para 62]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 332, under which the employment contract is suspended during a strike so that neither salary nor benefits need be paid, and Article 334, which requires payment to strikers only where the employer hires replacements. The panel found that shifting raw materials to another factory to keep production going could have the same effect as hiring replacements and could defeat the pressure a strike is meant to exert, so the workers might have been entitled to strike pay. That entitlement failed, however, because the strike was unlawful: Article 324 requires at least 7 working days' notice and the notice here was 7 days counting a Sunday, and Article 320 paragraph 5 permits a strike only once all peaceful means are exhausted, whereas these workers struck before coming to arbitration. Article 334 and Article 333's protection against sanctions, the panel held, operate only for strikes organised in compliance with Chapter XIII. [Para 35][Para 37][Para 39][Para 42][Para 44][Para 46][Para 47]

On issue 2 the panel applied Article 65, under which only the parties to the employment contract may terminate it, together with Articles 73 and 74 on lawful termination procedure. Ordering the dismissal would violate the employer's rights and coerce it into breaching its contract with the accountant and the termination rules. [Para 51][Para 53][Para 55]

On issue 3 the panel relied on Article 112, which requires the employer to give workers clear information about pay conditions and the details of variable wages, and to submit the wage and bonus ledger to the shop steward monthly before payday. It found 35 workers listed in a 10 May 2003 attachment with incorrect April and May calculations amounting to 175.18 USD, verified in the Company's own document. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 32]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A strike that does not comply with Chapter XIII — sufficient working-day notice under Article 324 and exhaustion of peaceful dispute resolution under Article 320 paragraph 5 — forfeits strike pay and the Article 333 protection against sanctions, even where the employer's own conduct (continuing production elsewhere, as under Article 334) would otherwise have entitled the workers to be paid; but where the employer's accounting failures substantially caused the strike, the panel may still order the employer to make good the miscalculated wages and to pay equitable damages under Article 34 of the Prakas on the Arbitration Council. [Para 48][Para 46][Para 50][Para 51]

**Obiter dicta:** The Company's statement that sending raw materials to other factories is ordinary practice for all factories with surplus orders; the parties' undertaking to conclude a collective bargaining agreement and adopt a grievance procedure, with the panel supplying a model form. [Para 30][Para 34][Para 35]
