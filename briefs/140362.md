# Award No. 21/09 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉ុង វ៉ា អ៉ិន ដាស្ទ្រៀល (ខេមបូឌា) ខូ អិលធីឌី ទី២ (Yong Wa Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd II) (Arbitration Council, 6 March 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phallack presiding; Chhiv Phyrum, employer nominee; An Nan, worker nominee) · Doc ID 140362

## 1. Facts
Yong Wa Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd II (ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉ុង វ៉ា អ៉ិន ដាស្ទ្រៀល (ខេមបូឌា) ខូ អិលធីឌី ទី២), a garment factory at Thmei village, Ta Khmau, Kandal province with about 5,000 workers, faced thirteen demands from the local union of the Cambodia Labour Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា) which has about 300 members and is not the most representative union in the factory. [Para 1][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49] Conciliation of 3 February 2009 settled five of the thirteen. [Para 24] At the hearing of 24 February 2009 the union withdrew issues 4, 5, 6 and 8 and the parties settled issues 1, 2 and 7, leaving only issue 3: a demand that the company build additional canteens for buildings 3 and 4, as it had allegedly promised. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 49] The company acknowledged the demand had been raised before but said it could not build, having one canteen already, and that it had only promised to discuss the request. [Para 52][Para 53]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 3) Whether the company must build an additional canteen for workers of buildings 3 and 4 as promised, and whether the panel may decide that demand at all where the claimant union is not the most representative union in the enterprise? [Para 49][Para 75][Para 80]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the local union of the Cambodian Labour Union Federation at Yong Wa Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd II held an agreement with the employer on building the extra canteen which the employer never honoured; the existing canteen is too far and too small, so workers of buildings 3 and 4 eat in corridors, in front of foul-smelling toilets, or outside the factory. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 55][Para 57][Para 59]
**Employer (respondent):** the demand had been discussed before but the company cannot afford to build, given the global financial crisis; one canteen already exists, 50 metres away, larger and better than neighbouring factories' and able to seat about 2,000. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 56][Para 60][Para 62][Para 71]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider the demand that the company build an additional canteen for buildings 3 and 4, and made no order to build. [Para 85][Para 86]
The panel first noted that no Labour Law or regulation requires an employer to build a canteen, following Awards 24/04-Sodhany Be, 60/07-Su Ve and 81/07-Sup Prim. [Para 66][Para 67] It then found that clause 8(C) of the company's own internal rules of 26 May 2006 does require workers to eat in the canteen the company provides, which implies an obligation to provide one — but the company has provided one. [Para 63][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70]
No evidence of the alleged promise to build a second canteen was produced by either side, and the panel found the demand went beyond what the law obliges. [Para 72][Para 73] It classified the demand as an interest dispute, and held that for interest disputes it must ask whether the union bringing the case is the most representative in the enterprise, because only such a union may conclude a collective agreement binding all workers. [Para 74][Para 75][Para 77][Para 79][Para 80]
The union here has some 300 members among 5,000 workers and is not most representative. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 78][Para 80] Under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement for one year, so an order to build would bind every worker and would strip non-members of the right to strike or to bring the same interest demand for a year. [Para 81][Para 82] That would be unfair to them, so the panel declined jurisdiction over the demand. [Para 83][Para 85][Para 86]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a demand is an interest dispute rather than a claim of legal entitlement, the panel will decline to decide it unless the union that brought it is the most representative union in the enterprise, because an award would operate as a collective agreement binding all workers for a year and would extinguish non-members' right to strike or to re-litigate the same interest demand. [Para 75][Para 81][Para 82][Para 85]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the company's internal rules oblige it to provide a canteen and that it had done so, and that the existing canteen lies 50 metres from buildings 3 and 4 and seats about 2,000 — findings unnecessary once the demand was held to be an interest dispute outside its reach. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 71]
