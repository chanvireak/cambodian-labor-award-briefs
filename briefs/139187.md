# Award No. 096/15 — Chang Kai Printing Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 29 May 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy appointed by the employer, Vong Vanna appointed by the workers, Tang Tri chair) · Doc ID 139187

## 1. Facts
Chang Kai Printing Co., Ltd is a printing company that employed about 86 staff. [Para 34][Para 35] From early November 2014 the employer suspended the employment contracts without any authorisation from the labour inspectorate, paying only 50 per cent wages from November 2014 to February 2015, then closed the company on 28 March 2015 without paying March wages. [Para 40][Para 49][Para 51][Para 57] Twenty-six staff — 21 employees plus their five representatives — are the claimants; the union that originally referred the case withdrew from representing them, and a lawyer took over. [Para 25][Para 36][Para 39] Both parties elected a non-binding award. [Para 21]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay 100 per cent wages for the period of the unlawful suspension from November 2014 to February 2015? [Para 40][Para 64]
2. Whether the employer must pay severance to the 26 staff on closure of the company — final March 2015 wages, unused annual leave, moral damages, the 5 per cent end-of-contract indemnity and notice-in-lieu? [Para 49][Para 81]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** without a letter authorising suspension from the labour inspector, the employer owes 100 per cent wages; even with authorisation it could suspend for only two months, so January and February 2015 must be paid in full; the fixed-duration contracts were cut short without valid reason, and closure does not release the employer, since insolvency and court-ordered liquidation are not force majeure. [Para 42][Para 47][Para 58][Para 59]
**Employer (respondent):** it has no authorisation letter but called staff to a meeting to explain the suspension because there was no work, and staff agreed to the 50 per cent rate and thumbprinted it; orders stopped because of falling quality caused by staff, so the company had to close; it can pay USD 40 for March 2015 and USD 30 per month for the remainder of each contract. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45][Para 52][Para 60]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must add the missing 50 per cent so that the 26 staff receive 100 per cent wages for November 2014 to February 2015, and must pay final March 2015 wages, unused annual leave, moral damages equal at least to wages due until the end of each contract, and the 5 per cent end-of-contract indemnity; the notice-in-lieu demand was dismissed. [Para 122][Para 123][Para 124][Para 125][Para 126][Para 127]

**Issue 1 (wages during suspension).** Article 71(1)(11) permits suspension of enterprise activity for economic or material difficulty for no more than two months, and requires the suspension to be placed under the supervision of the labour inspectorate. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 66] Following Awards 27/08, 136/08 and 51/09, the panel held that suspension is lawful only where the inspectorate is notified, investigates whether the difficulty is genuine, and issues a written authorisation. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 72][Para 73] Where the employer suspends on its own initiative without that step, it must pay 100 per cent wages even though there is no work to do, per Awards 60/06, 74/07, 28/08, 53/08 and 71/09. [Para 74][Para 76][Para 77] Since 50 per cent had already been paid, the panel ordered the remaining 50 per cent. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 80]

**Issue 2 (severance on closure).** Article 87(3) provides that cessation of enterprise activity does not free the employer from its obligations under that section except in force majeure, and insolvency or court-ordered liquidation is expressly not force majeure. [Para 81][Para 82][Para 84] The closure here was caused by lack of orders and alleged staff non-cooperation, neither of which qualifies. [Para 83] The contracts of all 26 staff were still valid and unexpired on 28 March 2015. [Para 86][Para 113] Accordingly: final March wages under Article 116 [Para 87][Para 89]; unused annual leave under Articles 166 and 167(2) [Para 93][Para 94]; moral damages under Article 73(3) for unilateral early termination, at least equal to wages to the end of the contract [Para 97][Para 99][Para 102][Para 103]; and the 5 per cent end-of-contract indemnity under Article 73(6) [Para 104][Para 105][Para 107]. The notice-in-lieu demand failed: Article 73(5) requires prior notice only of expiry or non-renewal, not of early termination, and failure to give notice merely extends or converts the contract rather than generating a payment. [Para 109][Para 110][Para 112][Para 115][Para 116]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that suspends its activity for economic difficulty without notifying the labour inspectorate and obtaining written authorisation suspends unlawfully and must pay full wages for the whole period, notwithstanding any thumbprinted agreement by staff to accept half pay. [Para 66][Para 73][Para 77]
Cessation of business does not discharge an employer's statutory severance obligations: only force majeure does, and insolvency or liquidation is not force majeure. [Para 82][Para 84]
Where a fixed-duration contract is terminated early at the employer's sole will, the worker is entitled to final wages, unused annual leave, the 5 per cent indemnity and moral damages of at least the wages due to the contract's end; but no payment in lieu of notice is owed, because Article 73(5) governs only expiry and non-renewal. [Para 103][Para 107][Para 116]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that falling product quality was a management failure rather than the staff's fault, and its remark that staff did in fact work overtime, were not necessary to the orders. [Para 48]

- The signature block prints the chair's name as "អាស៊ាន ទី" while the face of the award gives Tang Tri (តាំង ទ្រី) [Para 1][Para 129].
- Staff numbers appear as "10 86" [Para 35] and as "86" at [Para 61]; the figure was read as about 86.
- The union is named inconsistently as the Khmer Workers' Trade Union Federation and as the "Union Federation of Workers for Labour" (សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ ដើម្បី ការងារ) [Para 25][Para 36].
- Issue 3, 4 and 5 of the conciliation report were merged into issue 2 at the workers' request, so the award decides two issues while the report lists five. [Para 19][Para 20]
