# Award No. 171/14 — Apsara Holiday Hotel (Arbitration Council, 27 August 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Hem Hunarith for the employer party, Tuon Si Phan for the worker party, Pen Bunchea chair) · Doc ID 139633

## 1. Facts
Apsara Holiday Hotel in Siem Reap, employing about 63 staff, was leased to a new operator who took over management on 1 April 2014. [Para 73][Para 81][Para 268] On 29 April 2014 the new manager met all the staff and announced that their two-year contracts were at an end and that work would continue without written contracts — "stay if you want, leave if you don't". [Para 83][Para 84][Para 276] Fifteen staff claimed reinstatement and back wages; a union was formed on 11 May 2014 and notified to the employer on 16 May, and 18 staff transferred their rights to the federation. [Para 74][Para 77][Para 222][Para 223]

## 2. Issues in dispute
Of seven demands, issues 3 and 5 were settled at the hearing, issue 7 was withdrawn and issue 2 was withdrawn at the second hearing, leaving two. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 27][Para 28]
1. Whether the employer must take back all fifteen staff at their original posts until their contracts end, with back wages from the date of termination, or failing that pay severance according to law? [Para 3][Para 77][Para 79]
2. Whether the employer must pay all staff a monthly wage of at least USD 160? [Para 8][Para 227]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the mass termination was tied to union discrimination; the old and new managers agreed that the new operator would take on all staff in the same roles, and clause 11.3 of the lease obliged the lessee to honour the existing labour contracts; the staff had not been paid severance. [Para 225][Para 90][Para 94][Para 96][Para 97]
**Employer (respondent):** it did not discriminate, because management did not know the election results when the four were dismissed; those who left did so of their own choice; ending the two-year contracts and managing without written contracts was simply its staffing policy; and USD 80 a month is already better than neighbouring hotels, which pay USD 70 to 75. [Para 226][Para 239][Para 256][Para 279][Para 752]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must pay severance and related entitlements to the fifteen staff for the termination of 29 April 2014, and further compensation for the second dismissals of four of them, plus final wages owed, while the claim to a USD 160 minimum wage was refused; the award is non-binding and becomes final 8 days after notification. [Para 774][Para 845][Para 849][Para 850]

On discrimination, the panel noted that the election was held on 11 May 2014 and notified on 16 May, after the four dismissals, and that the remaining eleven left of their own accord; it found no evidence linking the terminations to union activity. [Para 249][Para 253][Para 254][Para 255][Para 257]

The decisive point was the lease. Under Article 87 of the Labour Law, where the employer's legal status changes — by succession, sale, merger or incorporation — the contracts in force on that date continue between the new employer and the workers of the old enterprise. [Para 258][Para 262] The panel found the new manager had leased the hotel and continued its operations, and clause 11.3 of the lease required the lessee to respect and perform the labour contracts made with the previous operator. [Para 265][Para 271][Para 272] It therefore held the new operator liable for the contracts and for severance when it ended them. [Para 273]

It then characterised the announcement of 29 April 2014 as a termination by the employer's unilateral will rather than a consensual change of terms, since staff who did not accept the new arrangement had simply to leave. [Para 280][Para 281][Para 283] For the eight long-serving staff whose successive fixed-duration contracts had run beyond two years, the contracts had become indefinite under Articles 67(2) and 73(5). [Para 284][Para 297][Para 298] Terminating an indefinite contract requires a valid reason under Article 74 connected with aptitude, conduct or the needs of the enterprise, and management offered only its general staffing policy, so the terminations were unlawful and attracted notice pay under Articles 75 and 77, severance of 15 days' wages per year under Article 89, payment for unused annual leave under Articles 166 and 168, moral damages under Article 91 equal to the severance, and outstanding wages. [Para 302][Para 308][Para 312][Para 338][Para 351]

The seven staff still within valid two-year contracts were treated differently: their contracts had not expired, so terminating them early entitled them to the 5% severance under Article 73(6), damages equal to wages to the contractual end date, 15 days' notice pay and unused annual leave. [Para 561][Para 564][Para 566][Para 570]

A second round followed. Most staff returned on 30 April 2014 and the four union leaders were dismissed again days later, while ten chose not to return; the panel held the four second dismissals unlawful for want of a valid Article 74 reason, but measured their service only from the re-engagement, so they received seven days' notice pay and wages for days worked, with no severance or moral damages because that service fell below six months. [Para 637][Para 654][Para 655][Para 682][Para 741] The ten who chose not to return received only their final wages for the days worked. [Para 754][Para 755][Para 757]

On the wage claim, the panel found no law, collective agreement, internal rule or practice requiring USD 160, and treated it as an interest dispute; because the eighteen complainants were not yet a lawfully registered union and were not the most representative body in the enterprise, it refused to decide the claim. [Para 759][Para 760][Para 771][Para 772][Para 773]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an enterprise is leased or otherwise transferred and the incoming operator continues its operations, Article 87 keeps the existing labour contracts alive between the new employer and the old workforce, so the new employer inherits the duty to perform them and to pay severance when it ends them; and a blanket announcement that all contracts are at an end and that staff may stay without written contracts is a unilateral termination, not an agreed variation, requiring a valid reason under Article 74 for each indefinite contract. [Para 262][Para 272][Para 283]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that the hotel had no internal regulations at all, and that requiring every employee to sign a new disciplinary code was an unreasonable exercise of managerial authority, were made in the context of one dismissal and do not by themselves found an award. [Para 675][Para 677]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award is dated 27 August 2014 [Para 1] and the parties extended it to that date [Para 29], but the panel is recorded as constituted on "1 July 2019 2014" [Para 20], and the second hearing is placed at Hotel Empress Angkor on 1 August 2014 [Para 15][Para 16].
- The claimant group shifts between 18 workers who transferred rights [Para 74], 15 named in the demand [Para 77][Para 78] and 14 in the later reasoning [Para 632][Para 636]; five attended the first hearing and eight the second [Para 75][Para 76].
- The minimum wage claim is USD 160 in the pleadings [Para 8][Para 227] but "1,600 dollars" in the operative order [Para 849].
- Notice pay is measured at two months for some staff and 15 days for others on similar seniority, and for the four re-employed leaders at seven days, without reconciling Article 75 [Para 317][Para 362][Para 654].
- Prakas No. 099 is cited as "No. 59" and then "No. 199" and its Article 43 as "Article 4300" in the same passage [Para 10][Para 109][Para 234]; the hotel's commercial registration is dated 24 November 2004 in the evidence and 23 November 2004 in the findings [Para 35][Para 72].
- Individual staff and union officials are named throughout; they are given here by number or initial only, and the signature block loses both the worker-party arbitrator and the chair to OCR [Para 851].
