# Award No. 159/11 — Gret Phlek Tham (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 23 December 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (I. Sothy, employer-appointed; L. Sovanna, worker-appointed; K. Phalha, chair) · non-binding award closing the file · Doc ID 140102

## 1. Facts
More than 150 workers at Gret Phlek Tham (Cambodia), a factory at Kambol that had no union of its own, struck on 25 November 2011 under the Khmer Workers' Spirit Federation, pressing sixteen demands on working conditions. Conciliation failed the same week and all sixteen points were referred to the Council on 30 November 2011. On 2 December 2011 the Council issued Order No. 015/11 requiring the strike to stop and the workers to return to their shifts, but the company refused to readmit the federation's factory union president, Mr M, and on 8 December 2011 it dismissed every worker who had struck. [Para 1][Para 24][Para 25][Para 29][Para 30][Para 41][Para 71]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the panel may continue the arbitration while the strike it ordered suspended is still in progress? [Para 52][Para 54]
2. Whether the employer's refusal to readmit the union president, and its dismissal of all the strikers, was a measure aggravating the dispute that bars the panel from proceeding? [Para 63][Para 65]
3. Whether any claimant with an interest in the outcome remained once every striker had been dismissed? [Para 72][Para 73]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The workers would return to normal work at once, but only if Mr M was readmitted, because the protective order of the Phnom Penh Municipal Court did not place him outside the factory; by excluding him the employer breached both that order and the Council's own order, and they asked the panel to proceed by law. [Para 31][Para 36][Para 37][Para 46][Para 69]
**Employer (respondent):** The workers had abandoned their employment by failing to return within 48 hours of the protective order, so it applied that order and dismissed them all; it did not accept that everyone could return, even with Mr M excluded, and maintained that position at both pre-hearing meetings. [Para 38][Para 40][Para 45][Para 70]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to continue the arbitration in case No. 159/11 and closed the file without ruling on any of the sixteen demands. [Para 75][Para 76]

The panel began from Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, under which the parties must suspend every dispute measure — strike, lock-out or any other step capable of aggravating the dispute — for the duration of the proceedings, and must attend every meeting convened. It read that provision with its earlier decisions, in which the Council refused to arbitrate while a strike continued and closed files where the employer answered a strike with a lock-out or with the dismissal of union leaders; Award No. 49/10 (Wing Ying) treats the dismissal of all union leaders during proceedings as exactly such an aggravating measure. [Para 52][Para 54][Para 55][Para 59][Para 63][Para 64]

On the facts it found that the employer had taken its own aggravating measures: it refused to let the union president back into the factory notwithstanding the protective order and the Council's order, and it then dismissed all the strikers, which hardened the dispute instead of settling it. Because those dismissals left no claimant, and no one who could benefit from any award, the panel concluded that continuing the hearing would serve no purpose, and it terminated the proceedings rather than adjudicating the sixteen demands. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 68][Para 71][Para 72][Para 73][Para 75]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where, during the pendency of an arbitration, a union keeps its strike going or an employer takes measures that aggravate the dispute — refusing to readmit a union leader whom a protective order does not exclude, or dismissing every worker who struck — the panel will not continue the arbitration; and once all the claimants have been dismissed so that no party remains to benefit from an award, the file is closed on the merits being left undetermined. [Para 63][Para 65][Para 73][Para 75]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that it may take up the dispute afresh once the strike has entirely ceased, which implies that the closure is procedural rather than a final determination of the demands. [Para 56]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is an order closing the file, not a decision on the merits, so none of the sixteen demands is analysed. The employer's name is badly OCR-damaged on the face of the award and appears in shortened form in the style of the case; it is reproduced here as best the text allows. The number of strikers is given only as "more than 150". Dates conflict: the second pre-hearing meeting is dated 9 December 2011, while the dismissals were reported by telephone on 8 December 2011, and one precedent order is dated "25 24 December 2005". The signature block and annex line are partly garbled. The union president is an individual and is reduced to an initial here. [Para 1][Para 24][Para 41][Para 42][Para 60][Para 77]
