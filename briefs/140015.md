# Award No. 36/11 — Cambo Advertising Co., Ltd. v Cambodian Labour Confederation Local Union (Arbitration Council, 20 April 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, Ann Vireak, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 140015

## 1. Facts
Cambo Advertising Co., Ltd. employs about 130 workers in advertising and exhibition work; the Local Union of CLC represents 55 of them and is the only union. Workers are on verbal contracts, earn US$ 70–250 a month, and are paid every two to three months because the employer waits for client payments. A seven-point demand went to conciliation on 9 March 2011; three issues were withdrawn at the hearing, leaving issues 1, 2, 4 and 5. [Para 1][Para 12][Para 19][Para 30][Para 33]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay wages regularly each month? [Para 32]
2. Whether the employer must pay 200% of wages for work on Sundays and public holidays? [Para 41]
3. Whether the employer must refrain from deducting wages when workers take leave for personal commitments? [Para 46]
4. Whether the employer must pay 200% of wages for overtime worked late at night? [Para 53]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Wages were withheld and paid two or three months late, making it hard to support their families. Sunday and public-holiday work attracted no extra pay, one day of authorised leave was punished by deducting four days' wages, and exhibition overtime ran to 5:00 a.m. for a flat US$ 2.50. [Para 35][Para 43][Para 48][Para 54]
**Employer (respondent):** Its income depends on piece-work payments from clients, so it cannot pay monthly; it already provides accommodation, three meals and other benefits superior to the law; and it allowed long breaks in place of overtime pay. [Para 38][Para 40][Para 46][Para 60]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted in part — the panel ordered regular monthly wage payments, 200% for work on Sundays and public holidays, 200% for overtime from 10:00 p.m. to 5:00 a.m. (and 150% from 5:00 a.m. to 6:00 a.m.), refused to require wages during authorised personal leave but barred deductions exceeding the days taken and required a proper leave procedure. Non-binding award. [Para 114][Para 115][Para 116]

On Issue 1, Article 116 requires labourers to be paid twice monthly at intervals of no more than 16 days and employees at least monthly. The panel found the skilled auto-colour, mechanical and exhibition workers were employees rather than labourers under Articles 5–6, so monthly payment was required. Paying every two to three months breached Article 116, and superior benefits did not entitle the employer to defer wages. [Para 61][Para 67][Para 71][Para 73]

On Issue 2, Article 137 caps normal hours at 48 a week; because the workers worked six and a half days, Sunday work was overtime and Article 139 required 200%. For public holidays, Article 164 and Prakas No. 10 entitled workers to an additional 100%, so 200% in total, and accommodation and meals could not substitute. [Para 75][Para 81][Para 84][Para 92]

On Issue 4, Articles 71–72 suspend the contract during authorised absence, so the employer need not pay wages for those days; but it may not deduct more than the days taken. The absence of a consistent leave procedure required the employer to adopt and communicate one. [Para 97][Para 100][Para 104]

On Issue 5, Article 139 and Prakas No. 80 require 200% for night overtime between 10:00 p.m. and 5:00 a.m. The exhibition workers worked until 5:00 a.m. for a flat US$ 2.50 and received nothing after 10:00 p.m., which the panel found unlawful. [Para 106][Para 112][Para 113]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may not defer wages beyond the statutory payment interval on the ground that it provides superior non-wage benefits, and work on the weekly rest day or a public holiday is overtime payable at 200% regardless of accommodation or meal benefits. [Para 73][Para 81][Para 92]

**Obiter dicta:** Chinese New Year is not a public holiday under Prakas No. 196, although the employer granted paid days off for it. [Para 90]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is dated 20 April 2011 and the panel is named in the header as Mar Samborana, Ann Vireak and Kong Phallack (chair), but the signature block names Pen Bunchhea as chair — a direct contradiction. The document says 18 issues went to conciliation, then that the workers revised their claim to seven, and the issue list is numbered inconsistently. OCR losses throughout ("thearing", "remaing", "chosenon-binding"). [Para 1][Para 12][Para 13][Para 24]
