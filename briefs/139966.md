# Award No. 07/11 — June Textile Garment Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 28 January 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Suong Sophal, Pen Bunchhea chairing) · Doc ID 139966

## 1. Facts
June Textile Garment Co., Ltd. of Russian Federation Boulevard, Sen Sok District, Phnom Penh, faced a single non-conciliated demand from the workers of Group 8, brought through the Local Union of FTUWKC: that seven piece rates be raised, for example piece 1 (seam sewing) from US$ 0.03 to US$ 0.04 and piece 17 (sleeve insertion) from US$ 0.10 to US$ 0.12 [Para 2]. The employer answered that it could not meet the demand and would follow its existing practice [Para 2]. While the case was pending the Group 8 workers struck at their workstations and did not obey the panel's interim order No. 001/1 KBR of 13 January 2011 to cease striking; they later returned to work, but the union attended neither the 17 January 2011 pre-hearing session nor the 19 January 2011 hearing [Para 8][Para 9][Para 11][Para 13][Para 14].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must increase the piece rates for piece numbers 1, 6, 13, 17, 32, 33 and 37 as the workers demand? [Para 2]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The union demanded the seven specified increases and struck when they were refused; it then declined to attend, telling the Secretariat by telephone that it would not appear and leaving the issue for the Council to determine [Para 2][Para 33]. No written argument or evidence was filed.
**Employer (respondent):** It cannot provide what the workers demand and will follow its existing practice; its representatives attended both the pre-hearing session and the hearing as summoned [Para 2][Para 34].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** withdrawn — the panel closed case 07/11-June Textile without deciding the piece-rate demand [Para 42].
The panel did not reach the merits. Rule 4.7 of the Procedural Rules annexed to Prakas No. 099 SKBY (21 April 2004) and Clause 21 of the same Prakas allow an arbitration panel, where a party duly invited fails to appear without good cause, either to proceed in its absence or to terminate the proceedings by an award [Para 19]. The panel adopted the three conditions for closing a case stated in Arbitral Awards 16/07-Lotus and 27/07-M & V (Branch 3): proper notification, non-appearance, and no reason given for the absence [Para 20][Para 21].
On the first condition, the Secretariat contacted the worker party and informed it of the hearing, and the party replied that it would not attend, so notification was proper [Para 23][Para 24]. On the second, the panel read "appear before the arbitration panel" as requiring both presence and participation in the whole process, which comprises introduction and conflict disclosure, explanation of the process and confirmation of the issue, conciliation if agreed, and arbitration; the union attended none of these stages [Para 25][Para 31]. On the third, no reason was offered — at 2:00 p.m. on the hearing date the union repeated that it would not attend [Para 33][Para 35].
The panel held that although the worker party was absent it remained empowered to issue an award, that the union had been given ample opportunity to argue its claim, and that a claimant is obliged to set out reasons and evidence; having lost that opportunity, the worker party had dropped its claim, a decision reflecting unwillingness to bring the dispute to the Council [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39][Para 41][Para 42].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the claimant union is properly notified, states in advance that it will not attend, and appears at none of the four stages of the arbitration process without giving any reason, the panel will treat the claim as dropped and close the case by award instead of deciding the merits in the claimant's absence [Para 31][Para 39][Para 42].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel recorded that the Group 8 workers struck during the arbitral process and disobeyed interim order No. 001/1 KBR of 13 January 2011 to cease striking, but imposed no consequence for that disobedience [Para 8][Para 9].
