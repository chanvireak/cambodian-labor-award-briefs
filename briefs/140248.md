# Award No. 16/10 — Seths Garment (Cambodia) Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 9 March 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouchhun, An Non, Aing Eng Thong presiding) · Doc ID 140248

## 1. Facts
Seths Garment (Cambodia) Co Ltd was set up in August 2009 and employs about 950 workers; two unions operate there, and the Democratic Union of Workers at the factory — registered on 9 December 2009 — brought three demands. The case concerns one machine-section worker, Mr Y, who began on a two-month probation contract on 3 August 2009, was given a further fixed-term contract and was not renewed at the end of December 2009, having been elected eighth on the union's list of activists in October 2009. At the hearing the parties agreed to settle the second demand on union freedom themselves, so the panel decided only demands 1 and 3. [Para 34][Para 37][Para 53][Para 20][Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must take the dismissed union activist back into normal work and pay him back wages from dismissal until reinstatement, the dismissal having been made without notice? [Para 37]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay wages correctly and in full, and repay any shortfall within four days? [Para 57][Para 92]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissal was union discrimination, because the worker was an activist and had never been warned or disciplined; on 31 December 2009 the factory administration and the machine-team leader called him in and told him the contract would not be renewed unless he resigned from the union, which he refused; the company then replaced him with another worker in the machine section. On wages, the company had paid short every month — November and December 2009 had not been paid at all at the time of the claim — and the union wanted immediate repayment, fearing workers would lose small sums and be too afraid to claim. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 46][Para 58][Para 64]

**Employer (respondent):** the fixed-term contract had simply reached its end, which is why it was not renewed; a notice dated 23 December 2009 was given through the team leader and posted on the information board, and no warning was needed for a fixed-term contract; the company did not know he was a union activist, and the non-renewal was not discriminatory but based on poor performance. On wages, the company admitted shortfalls of about USD 1 or 2 affecting roughly 100 workers in November and December 2009, caused by an accounting system fault, and offered to repay within two weeks on a list submitted by the workers. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 59][Para 61]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to take the worker back and pay him back wages from the date of dismissal until reinstatement, and to repay the wage shortfalls within no more than fifteen days after payday; the demand for repayment within four days was not granted as such, and the settlement of Issue 2 was recorded. The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification. [Para 116][Para 117][Para 118]

On Issue 1 the panel split the question in two. First, was the non-renewal lawful? Under Article 73 of the Labour Law a fixed-term contract ends by its own terms at the agreed date, and both parties are free to renew or not. The panel accepted that the contract expired on 1 January 2010, that the employer had taken a decision not to renew, and that this was procedurally open to it. [Para 65][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70]

Second, was the non-renewal discriminatory? The panel noted that the party alleging discrimination bears the burden of proof, following Awards 108/08 and 41/09, and that it weighs the parties' evidence to see whether discrimination in fact occurred. The employer had produced no particulars of the poor performance it alleged and no written or oral warning during the contract, and admitted it could not confirm that the notice of 23 December 2009 ever reached the worker. Against that, the union's notification of its election result, listing the worker as activist number eight, had been signed for by a company security guard on 23 October 2009, so the employer could not plead ignorance of his union role. The panel also recorded that union leaders had previously been dismissed ahead of their contract dates and paid large sums to forestall protest. On that evidence it found the non-renewal was union discrimination contrary to Articles 12 and 279, and ordered reinstatement with back pay. [Para 75][Para 85][Para 86][Para 87][Para 88][Para 90][Para 91]

On Issue 3 the panel held that Article 112 requires the employer to notify wage terms clearly and Article 102 obliges it to pay the agreed wage; short payment is a failure of the employer's obligation. The employer had admitted the shortfall, affecting about 100 workers in November and December 2009, but attributed it to an accounting system fault rather than intent. The panel therefore ordered the company to repair and reorganise its payroll system, and to repay within at most fifteen days after payday, a period it considered reasonable because it allows a week for workers to verify and complain and a week for the company to check and pay. It found no provision fixing a specific repayment deadline, and noted that if shortfalls continued workers could invoke Articles 83 and 117. [Para 97][Para 103][Para 109][Para 110][Para 112][Para 113][Para 115]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The expiry of a fixed-term contract does not insulate a non-renewal from review: where the employer cannot substantiate the poor performance it cites, cannot show that notice reached the worker, and knew from a union notification received at its gate that the worker was an elected activist, the non-renewal is discriminatory under Articles 12 and 279 and reinstatement with back pay follows. A payroll error that causes admitted short payment must be corrected and repaid, but where no law fixes a deadline the panel will allow a reasonable period rather than the four days demanded. [Para 70][Para 87][Para 90][Para 113]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that no warning is required before letting a fixed-term contract expire, and that a worker who proves nothing more than a shortfall caused by an accounting fault may still have to pursue the labour inspector, were not necessary to the orders made. [Para 49][Para 110]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company's name is transliterated in at least four different forms across the header, the findings and the file list, and the union's name is likewise printed inconsistently. The length of the extension granted after probation is given as seven months in one passage and three months in another, and the termination date appears as both 31 December 2009 and, by implication, the contract expiry of 1 January 2010, while the notice is dated 23 December 2009. The paragraph that opens the reasoning for Issue 1 is badly corrupted by OCR, so the panel's framing of the two sub-questions survives only in fragments. The identity of the worker and of the officials he says spoke to him is recoverable only partially, and the individual's name appears under several spellings. Wage figures are given as approximations of USD 1 or 2 per worker, with no total. [Para 34][Para 37][Para 65][Para 79][Para 59][Para 119]
