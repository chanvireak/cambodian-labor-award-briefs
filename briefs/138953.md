# Award No. 048/17 — Nan Tai Garment Factory (Cambodia) Limited (Arbitration Council, 16 January 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (chair) · Doc ID 138953

## 1. Facts
Nan Tai Garment Factory (Cambodia) Limited is a Phnom Penh garment manufacturer employing about 316 workers, with a Khmer Workers' Spirit union branch and a Workers-for-Cambodia union branch on site. [Para 68][Para 69] On 4 December 2017 the workers filed a complaint seeking improved working conditions; three points failed conciliation at the Labour Dispute Department on 12 December 2017 and were referred to the Council on 18 December 2017. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 16] The workers struck from 1 to 20 December 2017, and the panel issued Order No. 005/17 on 20 December 2017 suspending the strike and ordering a return to normal shifts from 21 December 2017, which the parties reported as complied with. [Para 43][Para 19][Para 20]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to pay severance (ប្រាក់បំណាច់ផ្តាច់កិច្ចសន្យា) to the workers in the event that the company closes down? [Para 73][Para 136]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to provide the workers with sufficient work to do? [Para 86][Para 146]
3. Supplementary issue: whether the employer's non-renewal of the fixed-duration contract of worker representative Un Sovann Da is a dispute that is a direct consequence of this case and thus within the panel's jurisdiction? [Para 96][Para 162][Para 172]

The workers withdrew a third original issue — that wages and benefits be maintained during the strike — at the hearing, leaving issues 1 and 2 for consideration. [Para 8][Para 23][Para 24]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The company had cut staff since September 2017 and a Labour Ministry letter of 30 August 2017 stated the factory had closed, so the workers lost confidence that it would stay open and wanted severance secured now; they also wanted enough work, and overtime, to earn above the base wage. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 91] They argued the non-renewal of Un Sovann Da, a worker delegate and their representative in this case, was discrimination and a penalty under Article 333 of the Labour Law, made without the Labour Inspector request required by Article 43 of the Law on Trade Unions. [Para 105][Para 106][Para 107]

**Employer (respondent):** The company never closed and issued a written guarantee to that effect; a 6 September 2017 record confirmed there was no change of owner and no new company name. [Para 77][Para 85] It had little work to give since 25 August 2017 but continued paying full monthly base wages, was actively seeking new work, and had no plan to suspend operations in January 2018. [Para 87][Para 94][Para 95] On Un Sovann Da, it applied a general policy of not renewing fixed-duration contracts on expiry and had given lawful notice. [Para 109][Para 111]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed in full — all claims refused; the award is a non-binding order (សេចក្តីបង្គាប់មិនជាប់កាតព្វកិច្ច) that becomes final 8 days after notification absent written objection to the Ministry of Labour. [Para 185][Para 186][Para 187][Para 188]

On issue 1, the panel reasoned that a severance claim contingent on a future closure is a claim that has not yet arisen: both sides admitted the factory was still operating normally on the hearing date. [Para 73][Para 142] Following its own reasoning in Award No. 10/03 (Zac Sintic) that the Council exists to resolve labour disputes and not problems that have not yet occurred, and prior awards 36/06 and 155/13 declining to consider future disputes, the panel refused to consider the claim. [Para 138][Para 139][Para 140][Para 145]

On issue 2, the panel read Article 664 of the Civil Code (labour contract as exchange of labour for wage) together with Article 102 of the Labour Law (wages owed under the contract for work done), and found that although full work had been scarce since 25 August 2017, the employer had kept paying the full monthly base wage. [Para 146][Para 151][Para 156] The employer had therefore already performed its contractual obligations, and the request for more work was in substance a request for more overtime earnings. [Para 157][Para 160][Para 161]

On the supplementary issue, Article 312(1) of the Labour Law and Article 33 of Prakas No. 99 place the panel's jurisdiction within the non-conciliation record plus problems arising after it that are direct consequences of the dispute. [Para 163][Para 165] The notice of non-renewal came on 26 December 2017 and the contract expired on 3 January 2018 — after the 12 December 2017 conciliation — but the panel found the non-renewal was not a direct consequence of this case, since only 1 of the 6 named worker representatives was affected and the other 5 kept working normally. [Para 169][Para 177][Para 182] It also found the non-renewal matched a general staff-reduction policy for fixed-duration contracts under two years, his contract running 1 year 8 months, and was not retaliation. [Para 179][Para 180][Para 181]

As a threshold matter, the panel found no most-representative union at the company and recognised the six workers named in Labour Department letter No. 018/17 of 13 December 2017 as representatives under Article 1 of Prakas No. 362. [Para 72][Para 132][Para 134]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim whose operative fact has not yet occurred — such as severance payable only if the employer later closes — is not a live dispute the panel will decide, and a supplementary claim arising after conciliation is outside jurisdiction unless it is a direct consequence of the conciliated dispute rather than an application of a general policy affecting one worker among several. Where an employer continues paying the full contractual base wage during a period of reduced work, it has performed its obligation under Article 664 of the Civil Code and Article 102 of the Labour Law, and no order lies to compel the provision of more work or overtime. [Para 144][Para 160][Para 182]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that the employer still allowed Un Sovann Da to enter the company to carry out his role as worker delegate, which reinforced its finding of no discrimination. [Para 115][Para 178] It also observed that a Labour Inspector had advised the employer that no Article 43 permission was needed for non-renewal of a fixed-duration contract. [Para 110]
