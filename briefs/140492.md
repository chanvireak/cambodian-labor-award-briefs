# សេចក្តីបង្គាប់ លេខ ២៥/០៨ — អេស ឌី ខេមបូឌា ខូអិលធីឌី (SD Cambodia Co., Ltd) v សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព ឧស្សាហកម្ម កម្ពុជា (Arbitration Council, 14 March 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៉ីង សុទ្ធី / Ing Sothy for the employer party, អន ណន / An Nan for the worker party, គង់ ផល្លៈ / Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 140492

## 1. Facts
អេស ឌី ខេមបូឌា ខូអិលធីឌី (SD Cambodia Co., Ltd) runs a factory at Kandaok village, Ang Snuol district, Kandal, employing about 800 workers. [Para 35] The claimant is សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព ឧស្សាហកម្ម កម្ពុជា (the Cambodian industrial union federation), which claims about 160 members and does not hold most-representative status. [Para 36][Para 37] A strike ran from 11 to 18 August 2007, led by a different federation; the claimant's members say they tried to enter the factory but were sent home. [Para 38][Para 40][Para 41] Six non-conciliation points were referred and point 6 was settled at the hearing, leaving points 1 to 5 for decision. [Para 18][Para 21][Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must preserve the bonus and wages of the claimant union's members for the suspension period of 11 to 18 August 2007, on the ground that they did not join the strike? [Para 80][Para 86]
2. (Issue 2) Whether annual leave pay must be calculated on the twelve-month average under Article 168 of the Labour Law? [Para 95][Para 97]
3. (Issue 3) Whether maternity leave of 90 days means working days, and whether the half wage with perquisites must be paid before the leave begins? [Para 102][Para 116]
4. (Issue 4) Whether workers with one year of service are entitled to a US$5 annual wage increase? [Para 137][Para 138]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the company must pay a daily incentive of 1,000 riels per group reaching its production target, instead of US$3 at month end? [Para 162][Para 166]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព ឧស្សាហកម្ម កម្ពុជា):** their members stayed out of the strike and presented themselves for work, so wages and the attendance bonus should be preserved; annual leave and maternity pay should follow the twelve-month average; 90 days must count as working days and be paid in advance; and long-serving workers deserve a US$5 annual rise. [Para 38][Para 41][Para 55][Para 59][Para 69]
**Employer (respondent, អេស ឌី ខេមបូឌា ខូអិលធីឌី):** it never suspended the claimant's members, who simply refused to work while most of the workforce struck and the factory could not operate; it already applies Article 166 for leave, pays maternity monthly, and cannot raise wages because productivity is flat and unit prices have fallen since 2000. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 56][Para 71][Para 78]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel rejected the claim for wages and bonus during 11–18 August 2007, ordered annual leave pay to be computed on the twelve-month average of the months actually worked, rejected the working-day count of maternity leave but ordered the 50 percent wage with perquisites to be paid before the leave begins, and refused to consider the US$5 rise and the 1,000 riel incentive as interest disputes. [Para 171][Para 172][Para 173][Para 174][Para 175][Para 176]

On Issue 1, the panel applied Article 332 of the Labour Law: the labour contract is suspended during a strike, work time is not credited and wages are not paid. [Para 80][Para 81] It followed its earlier awards (403-Lida, 25/04-Saen Dithga, 47/04-Diamond, 58/06-Wil Sen, 63/07-Phnom Penh Garment) in refusing to preserve wages across a strike, noting that a strike benefits neither side since the employer loses production and the workers their pay. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 85] It found the claimant's members had not proved they attempted to work and were turned away. [Para 87][Para 92][Para 93]

On Issue 2, Article 168 requires the average of wages, bonuses, benefits and indemnities over the twelve months before leave, and the panel followed its award 23/08-M&V 1 in dividing total wages by 26 after finding the monthly average. [Para 95][Para 97][Para 99][Para 100][Para 101] On Issue 3, it read Article 182 as fixing 90 calendar days and rejected the working-day claim, but held under Article 115(3) and award 57/06-Evergreen that maternity pay must be advanced, and remitted the calculation to the 12-month average over months actually worked, consistent with 23/08-M&V 1. [Para 113][Para 115][Para 128][Para 131][Para 136] On Issues 4 and 5, both demands were interest disputes; citing Articles 96 and 277 and Prakas No. 305 Article 9, the panel found the union not most representative and so without standing to conclude a collective agreement, and declined to consider them. [Para 148][Para 150][Para 152][Para 154][Para 155][Para 166][Para 168]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Wages are not owed for days of a lawful strike: Article 332 suspends the contract, so a minority union's members cannot claim pay for a period in which the factory was shut by a strike, and the loss falls on both sides. [Para 81][Para 83][Para 85] An interest demand — a wage increase or a new incentive — will not be considered where the union bringing it lacks most-representative status, because only such a union may conclude a collective agreement binding the whole workforce. [Para 152][Para 155][Para 168] Maternity pay, once computed on the twelve-month average of months actually worked, must be paid before the leave begins under Article 115(3). [Para 128][Para 131][Para 136]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the Labour Law does not encourage strikes and that a strike leaves the employer without product and the worker without wages are general observations, not the ground of the rejection. [Para 84][Para 85]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The maternity reasoning repeats the contradiction seen in award 23/08: at [Para 109] the general presumption is said to be working days, and at [Para 112] the same sentence concludes that the number means normal calendar days, which is then applied to reject the demand at [Para 113] and [Para 115].
- The decision paragraph on maternity is corrupt: a stray year "2019" and stray digits appear at [Para 174], and the opening bracket of the rejection order at [Para 173] is never closed.
- Paragraph 22 lists the issues to be decided as 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5, but the text there is garbled with bracketed insertions and a duplicated "issue 3", so the list cannot be read at face value.
- Several award numbers cited in the reasons are OCR-damaged ("លេខ ៦៣០៧-ភ្នំពេញ ហ្គា មិន" at [Para 82], "១០៨០៧- ៨ ស្តារ ស្ពត វែរ" at [Para 153]) and the Prakas date appears as both 21 April and 21 July 2004 at [Para 12] and [Para 157].
- The union's membership figure is printed as "៤ ប្រហែល ១៦០ នាក់" with a stray digit at [Para 37].
- The employer's representatives and the worker representatives are named in full at [Para 1] and [Para 2]; they are given by role only here.
