# Award No. 19/08 & 20/08 — Quint Major Industrial Co. Ltd (Arbitration Council, 14 March 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phallack chair; Ing Sothy, employer nominee; Ann Vireak, worker nominee) · Doc ID 140486

## 1. Facts
Quint Major Industrial Co. Ltd, at Trayeung village, Peuk commune, Ang Snoul district, Kandal, employs about 2,100 workers, and three federations are claimants: the Cambodian Union Federation (CUF), the Cambodian Workers Labour Federation of Trade Union (CWLFU) and the Cambodian Labour Union Federation (CLUF), claiming 550, 640 and 530 members respectively, or 1,720 workers and 81.9 per cent of the workforce, none of them individually representative. [Para 1][Para 35][Para 36] Conciliation on 1 February 2008 failed on all 16 issues because the union and worker representatives walked out, the workers struck from 1 February, and 11 further demands raised at the pre-hearing meeting produced a second report with six un-conciliated issues. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 21][Para 24] At the hearing two of the six were conciliated, issue 16 of the first report was withdrawn, and the remaining fifteen were grouped into three: wage increases, skill and position bonuses, and attendance and weekly bonuses. [Para 27][Para 28]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the company must stop deducting annual leave when workers take special leave? [Para 36][Para 52]
2. (Issue 4) Whether the company must give pregnant workers one day of paid leave a month for a medical check and vaccination? [Para 38][Para 73]
3. (Issue 5) Whether the company must pay workers who are eight months pregnant maternity pay of half their wages even where they have not completed one year of service? [Para 42][Para 87]
4. (Issue 6) Whether the company must pay wages and the attendance bonus for the strike period of 1 to 16 February 2008? [Para 45][Para 95]
5. (Issue 1-new) Whether the company must raise the wages of team leaders, assistants, mechanics, tailors, electricians, blacksmiths and team members? [Para 46][Para 101]
6. (Issue 2-new) Whether the company must pay a skill and position bonus of $10, $15 and $20 to those trades? [Para 48][Para 110]
7. (Issue 3-new) Whether the company must increase the monthly attendance bonus by $5, $10 and $15 and pay the weekly bonus regularly? [Para 51][Para 113]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the Cambodian Union Federation, the Cambodian Workers Labour Federation of Trade Union and the Cambodian Labour Union Federation jointly demanded that special leave not be charged against annual leave, that pregnant workers receive one paid day a month for check-ups, that women eight months pregnant receive half wages, that strike-period wages and bonuses be paid, and that wages rise to $80 for team leaders, $60 for assistants, $70 for mechanics, $70–$80 for tailors, $90 for electricians and blacksmiths and $70 for team members, with skill, position and attendance bonuses on top. [Para 7][Para 12][Para 45][Para 46][Para 48][Para 51]
**Employer (respondent):** Quint Major Industrial Co. Ltd, through its manager, supervisors, interpreter and compliance staff, answered that special leave is deducted from annual leave under its Internal Work Rules and Article 171, that pregnant workers may take the day but unpaid, that half wages are paid only after twelve months' service, that the strike was unlawful and wages were withheld in proportion to days struck, and that it could not afford any increase because it had made no recent profit. [Para 36][Para 41][Para 44][Para 45][Para 48]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — every monetary demand was rejected; the only relief ordered was that the employer establish a clear policy for the weekly attendance bonus and pay it regularly, as it had undertaken at the hearing. [Para 118][Para 119][Para 120]
The panel first settled jurisdiction. Clause 43 of Prakas No. 099 makes an award on an interests dispute stand in place of a collective bargaining agreement for one year, so standing depends on who could sign such an agreement; under Article 277 and Prakas No. 305 none of the three unions alone qualified, but Clause 9(2) lets unions with members in the enterprise submit a joint draft where together they represent the majority, and with 81.9 per cent the three federations could, so the panel heard the interests claims. [Para 57][Para 60][Para 62][Para 69]
On Issue 2, Article 171 permits the employer to deduct special leave from annual leave not yet taken, so the demand was above the law and an interests demand; on the merits it failed because Article 167 forbids payment in lieu of annual leave except at the end of the contract and no evidence of heavy expense was produced. [Para 52][Para 54][Para 70][Para 72] On Issue 4, Articles 71(5)–(6) and 72(1) suspend the contract during authorised absence, so wages are not owed, and the union produced no evidence of the need; the demand was rejected. [Para 75][Para 77][Para 85][Para 86] On Issue 5, Article 183 confines half wages to women with one year of uninterrupted service, and neither the Internal Work Rules nor any agreement extended it, so the demand was rejected. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 92]
On Issue 6, Article 332 provides that a strike suspends the contract and that neither wages nor allowances are paid during it; the panel followed Awards 04/03-Lida and 63/07-Phnom Penh Garment, and since the company hired no replacements under Article 331 it rejected the claim. [Para 95][Para 98][Para 100][Para 101]
On the three new issues, wages, bonuses and their levels appear nowhere in the law, the Internal Work Rules or any agreement, so each was an interests demand; the panel required evidence of capacity to pay, industry standards, inflation, past practice, law and policy and productivity, as in Awards 14/04-June Textile and 07/06-Dai Young, and the unions tendered none. [Para 104][Para 110][Para 114][Para 117] Only the weekly bonus was left: the company having undertaken to fix an amount and pay it regularly, the panel declined to rule on that point and ordered the undertaking carried out. [Para 113][Para 120]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where no single union holds most representative status, unions with members in the enterprise may jointly bring an interests dispute if their combined membership is a majority of the workforce, because Clause 9(2) of Prakas No. 305 lets them jointly conclude the collective bargaining agreement that an award on such a dispute would replace. [Para 60][Para 62][Para 69]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the factory's neighbouring employers' practice of granting paid check-up days and the humanitarian case for pregnant workers were attractive but could not substitute for evidence, which the union had not produced. [Para 40][Para 83][Para 85]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The translation is a badly damaged OCR that repeats clauses mid-sentence ("The two remaing issues were The two remaing issues were", "Aftereceiving", "Traing" for Training), so several passages had to be read from context. [Para 14][Para 26] The case is numbered "19/08 & 20108" on its face, the second number having lost its slash, although the reasons refer to report 066/08 as a separate case heard by the same panel. [Para 1][Para 25] The evidence list dates both non-conciliation reports "1 February 2006" and "11 February 2006" where the procedure dates them 2008. [Para 16][Para 29] The first report is described as carrying 16 issues, yet the demands are listed up to item 22 and the grouping cites issues 1, 5, 10, 12 and 14 of that report, so the numbering cannot be reconciled. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 28] The decision section merges headings into text ("Issue 43 Reject...", "Issue 5: Reject..." inside the preceding sentence). [Para 118] Employer representatives and the federations' officials are named in full in the source and are described here by role and federation only. [Para 2][Para 7]
