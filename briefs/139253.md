# Award No. 182/15 — Wai Ful Gamen (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 21 July 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Sin Kim Sean for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139253

## 1. Facts
Wai Ful Gamen (Cambodia) Ltd is a garment factory at Pong Teuk, Damnak Ampil, Ang Snuol district, Kandal province, represented in the proceedings by a senior GMAC officer. [Para 1] On 6 July 2015 the Kandal Labour Department received six demands from the workers through the Cambodian Union Federation: 4,000 riel a day for lunch, a USD 20 monthly attendance bonus, a USD 20 monthly travel allowance, a skill allowance of USD 10 for workers able to run two machines and USD 15 for three machines, long-term employment contracts, and payment to two named women workers in accordance with an earlier arbitration award. [Para 2][Para 4][Para 6][Para 8][Para 10][Para 11][Para 18] Conciliation on 13 July settled none of them and all six were referred on 14 July 2015. [Para 19][Para 20]
The workers struck after the referral. [Para 21] The company wrote on 14 July 2015 asking for an order returning the workforce to work, while the federation wrote on 16 July 2015 asking that no arbitrator be appointed and that no return-to-work order be issued. [Para 22][Para 23] A pre-hearing meeting was held on 17 July 2015, at which the employer reported that the Phnom Penh Municipal Court had issued a protective injunction effective from 17 July 2015 and that it was monitoring compliance, and the workers' representative could not say whether the strikers had returned under it. [Para 26][Para 27] On 20 July 2015 both sides confirmed by telephone that the strike continued. [Para 28] No hearing was held, so no facts were found on any demand. [Para 37][Para 38]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the arbitration must continue, or be terminated, where the strike has continued after referral and a court injunction is in force? [Para 39]

The six substantive demands were never examined and no order was made on them. [Para 37][Para 2]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the federation pursued the six demands and, separately, asked the Council to refuse to appoint an arbitrator in the case and not to order the strikers back to work. [Para 23][Para 30]

**Employer (respondent):** the company asked for an immediate order returning the workers to work, and reported that it was tracking compliance with the Municipal Court's protective injunction of 17 July 2015. [Para 22][Para 26]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel terminated the arbitration in case No. 182/15 without a hearing, so none of the six demands was granted or refused on its merits. [Para 42][Para 43]

The panel confined itself to whether to continue, and cited Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, which obliges the parties to suspend every dispute measure during the panel's work — strike or lockout under Article 318 of the Labour Law, or anything else that could aggravate the dispute — and to attend all meetings convened. [Para 39] It recorded its agreement with the interpretation given in its earlier awards, and applied that practice to the facts before it. [Para 40]

Those facts were that the strike had begun after the file reached the Council and had not stopped: at the pre-hearing meeting of 17 July 2015 the employer spoke only of the court's injunction and the workers' side could not confirm a return, and on 20 July 2015 both the employer and the federation confirmed by telephone that the strike was still on. [Para 21][Para 27][Para 28][Para 41] The panel therefore terminated the proceedings under Article 20, without issuing the return-to-work order the company had sought and without ruling on the federation's request that no arbitrator be appointed. [Para 42][Para 43]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a strike continues after the dispute has been referred, and the parties confirm on enquiry that it is still running, the Council will terminate the arbitration under Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 rather than hear the demands — and it will do so on its own motion, without first issuing a return-to-work order, where a court injunction is already in force and the panel cannot obtain a reliable account of compliance from the workers' side. [Para 39][Para 41][Para 42]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's note that the employer was monitoring compliance with the Municipal Court's injunction, and its silence on the federation's request that no arbitrator be appointed, are observations that did not drive the termination. [Para 26][Para 27]
