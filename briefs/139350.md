# Award No. 85/04 — Kang Ning Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 28 October 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry, chosen by the employer party; Tuon Siphann, chosen by the employee party; Kong Phallack, chair) · Doc ID 139350

## 1. Facts
Kang Ning Co., Ltd, which began business in Cambodia two years earlier, makes clothes hangers for export and for sale in Cambodia, running three shifts with about 150 workers. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 17][Para 21] Of those, some 50 were regular workers and the rest casual workers, whom the employer called "disposable": roughly 100 were paid 6000 riels to 6500 riels per day at the end of each shift and worked four to seven days a week, alongside regular workers on the same 15 machines and often on the seventh day. [Para 24][Para 27][Para 28][Para 29] Every worker was on an oral contract; none had a written contract. [Para 25] On 2 October 2004 the employees complained to the Labour Inspector of Russey Keo district, struck, and after the Inspector intervened at the factory the parties settled one of ten issues. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether casual workers who had in fact worked regular full-time hours for months were temporary workers within Article 9 of the Labour Law? [Para 9][Para 33][Para 34]
2. Whether the employer must convert those casual workers into regular workers, and from which date their seniority and benefits run? [Para 10][Para 26][Para 47]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** casual workers employed for at least three months, doing the same work as regular workers on the same machines, must be made regular with seniority from the date they started; the employee party rejected the employer's offer to convert ten workers on 1 November 2004 and to evaluate the rest over two or three months. [Para 26][Para 31][Para 33]
**Employer (respondent):** casual workers are disposable or temporary labour, and it needed two to three months to evaluate their behaviour and work before deciding whether they were good enough to become regular. [Para 24][Para 31][Para 43]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to accept as full-time regular workers all casual workers who work for the company at least 21 days per month and longer than two months, enjoying the same benefits as full-time regular workers, from the date the award comes into effect; seniority running from the original date of hire, which the employees demanded, was not ordered. [Para 10][Para 26][Para 47]

The panel read Article 9 as distinguishing regular workers from casual workers by the stability of the employment: casual workers are those contracted for a specific, unstable job normally completed within a short period, or work that is temporary, intermittent or seasonal. [Para 33][Para 34] On the list of names, identification numbers and start dates submitted at the hearing, 48 casual workers had worked five to nine months, eight hours a day, four to seven days a week, next to regular workers; the employer offered no contradictory evidence, and the panel found they were not temporary workers within Article 9, so that article could not be applied to them. [Para 30][Para 34][Para 35][Para 36]

It then followed its earlier awards: 03/03 (Tonga Garment), where it ordered an employer to stop classifying workers as casual and to treat anyone working more than three months as on a fixed-duration or undetermined-duration contract with equal pay and benefits; 26/04 and 30/04 (Honey Wear); and the Sport Wear award, where regular use of casual or temporary workers over a long period was held contrary to the law and workers putting in at least 21 days a month for two months were treated as probationary or full-time. [Para 37][Para 39][Para 41][Para 42]

On the employer's request for further evaluation, the panel noted that Articles 68 and 82 and Notifications 06/97 and 017/00 permit probationary contracts of no more than three months, that in case 27/03 it had held all garment workers to be skilled, so probation may not exceed two months, and that five to nine months was already ample time to assess these workers. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45][Para 46]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker's label does not decide the worker's status: where casual or "disposable" workers in fact work full hours, four to seven days a week, alongside regular workers for more than two months, they are not temporary workers under Article 9 but regular workers entitled to the same pay and benefits, and any probation used to delay that conversion may not exceed three months — two for skilled garment work. The operative threshold the panel applied is 21 working days per month over at least two continuous months. [Para 34][Para 36][Para 41][Para 44][Para 47]

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that the company employs approximately 150 workers while the non-conciliation report of the Ministry of Labour stated only 70 [Para 15], and the general remark that regularly using casual workers for a long period is contrary to the law [Para 40], are not necessary to the order, which rests on the 48 identified workers.

- Sentences are duplicated or cut in the OCR: "The workers went on strike to The workers went on strike to demand..." [Para 19], "Regular workers work only" [Para 30], and the repeated fragment "If these casual workers are good they will months to evaluate their behavior and work" [Para 31]–[Para 32].
- Prior awards are cited with lost or run-together numbers: "cases 03/03 Tonga Garment, 26/04 30/04 - Honey Wear" [Para 37], an unnumbered "Sport Wear" award [Para 40], and "case 27103" for what is evidently 27/03 [Para 44].

- The award bears only a file number ("Case No. 85/04"), no award number; the file number is used here. Worker and union representatives are named on the face of the award and appear here as Ms S and Mr K, with no name invented or expanded.
