# Award No. 103/19 — សិប្បកម្ម បោះពុម្ព ចុង ហួរ (Chung Hour printing workshop) (Arbitration Council, 16 December 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Nyan Somunninth presiding; Ieng Sothy, An Vireak) · Doc ID 139274

## 1. Facts
The employer runs a printing workshop at Phsar Touch village, Toul Sangke, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, employing 63 workers. [Para 1][Para 53][Para 54] On 17 October 2019 twenty workers asked the Labour Disputes Department to settle three points on working conditions; conciliation on 30 October 2019 settled one, leaving two. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 15] The employer then dismissed 35 workers in three phases of 5, 10 and 20, having placed them on 50% paid leave in the meantime and charged their annual leave against those days, and notified the Labour Inspectorate that it would close at the end of February. [Para 26][Para 28][Para 57][Para 71]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the sums due on dismissal — compensation in lieu of notice, seniority indemnity, and moral damages — to the 35 workers dismissed in three phases? [Para 55][Para 77]
2. Whether the employer must stop using workers' annual leave, without their agreement, to cover days on which it had no work and paid only 50% wages, and pay the annual-leave indemnity in full? [Para 70][Para 141]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** notice given through team leaders never reached them, because workers and team leaders were rotated onto different days; the employer made no ranking by professional skill, seniority and family burden as Article 95 requires; and annual leave was deducted for 50% leave days — two workers from 11 to 14 October 2019 and a third from 16 to 19 October 2019 — without their consent. [Para 63][Para 72][Para 73][Para 85]

**Employer (respondent):** notice was given correctly — individually in phase 2, and by posting on the company notice board where workers refused to accept it, and again in phase 3; severance was paid lawfully; the 50% arrangement answered the workers' own earlier demand for paid annual leave, and team leaders were instructed to pass the change on as before. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 68][Para 75][Para 76]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay compensation in lieu of notice to the workers dismissed in phase 3; seniority indemnity for pre-2019 service plus indemnity for 2019 service, calculated on 15 days of wages and allowances per year; and moral damages equal to each worker's seniority indemnity; and on issue 2 to stop substituting annual leave without agreement and pay the annual-leave indemnity without deducting days already taken. No sum is quantified in the award; the order fixes the basis of calculation only. [Para 161][Para 162][Para 163][Para 164]

On issue 1 the panel read Article 95 as requiring the employer to rank dismissals by professional skill, then seniority, then family burden. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 80] The dismissal lists showed workers with 18, 16 and 14 years dismissed, and the employer did not contest the workers' assertion that no ranking had been made, so the procedure was not followed and the dismissals for reduced activity were unlawful. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 87][Para 88][Para 89] It held that a termination for financial difficulty and falling orders falls to be assessed under Article 95, not as an Article 74 dismissal for the worker's conduct or character, which the employer had not invoked. [Para 90][Para 94][Para 95][Para 96] On notice, it accepted phase 2 because letters were handed to each worker and only posted after refusal, but rejected phase 3 because the letters were posted only, not delivered, so Article 75 and 77 compensation was owed to that phase. [Para 105][Para 106][Para 107][Para 108][Para 109]

Seniority indemnity was computed under new Article 89 and Prakas No. 443 of 21 September 2018: pre-2019 service is back-paid at 15 days per year in two half-yearly instalments, with 2019 service paid on top, because the amendment protects workers and does not erase past service. [Para 110][Para 116][Para 119][Para 128][Para 129] Moral damages under new Article 91 were allowed, equal to the seniority indemnity, because termination without valid reason opens that claim, and the panel noted the head is distinct from notice and seniority payments. [Para 133][Para 134][Para 138]

On issue 2, Articles 166 and 170 allow annual leave at Khmer New Year or at another time by agreement, notified to the labour inspector. [Para 143][Para 146] The employer admitted using leave days in place of the 50% days and relied on agreement with team leaders, but the panel held a team leader has no authority to dispose of workers' rights under Article 369 of the Civil Code and the workers had not ratified the arrangement, so the practice was contrary to the Labour Law. [Para 152][Para 155][Para 157][Para 159][Para 160] Arbitrator Ieng Sothy dissented on issue 1, recording that no order should be made because the dispute was future or had not yet arisen. [Para 168][Para 176][Para 179]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A dismissal on grounds of reduced activity is lawful only where the employer actually ranks workers by professional skill, then seniority, then family burden, and can show that it did so; evidence that longer-serving workers went while shorter-serving ones stayed defeats the procedure, and financial difficulty does not convert such a dismissal into one for conduct. [Para 78][Para 87][Para 88] An employer may not charge annual leave against days on which it has no work and pays 50% wages without the worker's agreement, and a team leader's assent does not bind the worker. [Para 157][Para 159][Para 160]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel held that the claims of the 10 workers still within their notice period had not yet arisen and might become relevant if dismissal occurred. [Para 97][Para 99]
