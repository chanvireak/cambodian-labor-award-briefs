# Award No. 020/19 — ធី នើ ហ្វេ សិន (ខេមបូឌា) ខូអិលធីឌី (Arbitration Council, 28 March 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bunchea presiding; Chiv Phirom, An Non) · Doc ID 138858

## 1. Facts
The respondent is a garment manufacturer on Road 371, Sangkat Dangkao, Khan Dangkao, Phnom Penh, employing about 470 workers. [Para 70][Para 71] The union filed four demands on 7 February 2019; conciliation on 25 February 2019 left two unresolved, one of which the parties settled at the hearing, leaving only the demand concerning the clinic nurse, Ms S. [Para 10][Para 11][Para 16][Para 17] She had worked since 11 August 2016 on successive fixed-term contracts and then on an indefinite-term contract from August 2018, and the employer dismissed her on 9 February 2019 after written notice of 10 January 2019. [Para 76][Para 78][Para 79][Para 85] Both parties attended the hearing of 12 March 2019, and no union was shown to be the most representative. [Para 15][Para 74]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take Ms S back into her post as clinic nurse, she having cared for sick workers and committed no serious fault? [Para 2][Para 75]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Ms S committed no serious fault and holds an indefinite-term contract; the employer never gave written notice before dismissal — it told her orally on 21 January 2019 that she would be dismissed on 31 January 2019, then dismissed her on 9 February 2019; 223 workers fingerprinted a demand that she be reinstated. [Para 80][Para 82][Para 136] At the hearing a small group of workers supported the claim in person. [Para 138][Para 145]
**Employer (respondent):** this is an individual dispute, not a collective labour dispute, because one nurse was dismissed and no other worker claims on the point; 232 workers fingerprinted a letter refusing to support the demand. [Para 83][Para 100][Para 131] On the merits, dismissal followed three repeated minor breaches of Article 10 of the internal rules, written warnings were issued and she refused to fingerprint them, and her terminal benefits were paid. [Para 86][Para 91][Para 94]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel refused to consider the reinstatement demand, holding that it was not a collective labour dispute; the award is non-binding and becomes final 8 (eight) days after notification unless a party objects to the Minister. [Para 150][Para 152][Para 153]
The panel treated jurisdiction as the threshold question, because Article 32 of Prakas No. 099 empowers it to decide only collective labour disputes. [Para 120][Para 121] It applied Article 302 of the Labour Law and its own earlier ruling in award 10/03, which requires three cumulative conditions: a dispute between some workers and one or more employers; a subject-matter concerning working conditions or the exercise of rights of a professional organisation; and a dispute capable of preventing the enterprise from operating normally or damaging social peace. [Para 133][Para 134][Para 135]
On the first condition the panel weighed the competing fingerprint lists: 223 workers demanded reinstatement and 232 refused to support it, so the 232 were not claimants; the claimants were the five workers who attended and who did not appear on the employer's refusal list, and the dispute was therefore between some workers and the employer. [Para 136][Para 137][Para 139][Para 140] On the second, the demand concerned the employment contract of a worker and so related to working conditions. [Para 142][Para 143] On the third, the panel found that only four workers actually supported the demand and that nothing showed the dispute would obstruct production or make the company unworkable or disturb social peace; that condition therefore failed. [Para 145][Para 146][Para 147][Para 148]
Because one of the three conditions was unmet, the panel held the dispute was not collective and that it had no jurisdiction, and it declined to rule on whether the dismissal was lawful. [Para 149][Para 150]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that one named worker be reinstated reaches the panel only if each of the three Article 302 conditions is proved, including that the dispute may prevent the enterprise from operating normally or damage social peace; that third condition is a factual one, and a handful of supporters with no evidence of impact on production or social peace leaves the panel without jurisdiction, however strong the individual merits. [Para 135][Para 145][Para 147][Para 150]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted the employer's account that Ms S had been warned three times and had refused to fingerprint the warnings, and that her terminal benefits were paid, but expressly declined to decide those merits. [Para 91][Para 94][Para 150]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award says the panel was constituted on "4 March 2023", which cannot stand with a 2019 award. [Para 13] The nurse's name is printed inconsistently (ស៊ីប លីនដា and ស៊ី ប លីនដា) and is not reproduced here; individual workers are given as Ms S. [Para 75][Para 99] The two fingerprint lists are hard to reconcile: 223 signatures demanding reinstatement and 232 refusing support, from a workforce of about 470, with no statement whether they overlap. [Para 100][Para 136] The employer-nominated arbitrator's name is legible in the panel list as ឈីវ ភិរម្យ but lost to OCR in the signature block. [Para 1][Para 154] The employer is named only in Khmer, as ធី នើ ហ្វេ សិន (ខេមបូឌា) ខូអិលធីឌី, so no Latin name is asserted. [Para 1]
