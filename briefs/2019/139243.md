# Award No. 091/19 — Siem Reap International Airport (Arbitration Council, 31 October 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — ម៉ារ សំបូរ ណា (employer-appointed, dissenting), អន ណន (worker-appointed), ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា (chair) · Doc ID 139243

## 1. Facts
The respondent runs airport and flight services at Siem Reap and employs 563 workers. [Para 56] Because operations run daily, the single weekly rest day is rotated rather than fixed on Saturday or Sunday. [Para 58] From 2013 the employer paid the weekly rest day over to the following day for union leaders whose rest day coincided with a meeting or seminar; on 1 July 2019 it announced that it would stop doing so for attendance at labour dispute resolution. [Para 60] The 2014–2016 collective agreement had by then expired. [Para 67]

## 2. Issues in dispute
Conciliation produced two demands; demand 2 was withdrawn at the hearing, leaving demand 1, which itself carries a second limb on meeting minutes: [Para 17]
1. Whether the employer must carry over a union leader's weekly rest day to the following day where that rest day coincides with a meeting with a relevant institution or an invited seminar, as practised since 2013? [Para 57]
2. Whether the employer must implement clause 5 of the 4 December 2014 agreement ending the dispute and strike, read with point 2 of the 25 November 2014 meeting? [Para 4]
3. Whether the employer must record minutes of every in-company meeting on the day of the meeting, signed by all participants? [Para 2]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the practice since 2013 created an entitlement, and abolishing it on 1 July 2019 cost workers a benefit without proper reason; union participation in dispute resolution and seminars serves the collective interest of the workforce. [Para 61]
**Employer (respondent):** there is no general or automatic policy of paying weekly rest days to union leaders, past payments being case-by-case where the employer itself called them to a meeting; the expired collective agreement cannot be applied, the internal rules for negotiating a new one cover only that negotiation, and clause 4.4 requires merely that no wage be deducted for meeting days, not that rest days be carried over. [Para 63][Para 69]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to give Mr R a substitute day, at a date of the employer's choosing, for the weekly rest day of 10 October 2019 consumed by his attendance; the claim for payment in lieu for leaders attending Ministry conciliation was rejected. No monetary sum was awarded. [Para 93][Para 94]

Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 requires the parties to attend every meeting the panel convenes, and Article 14 requires an employer, where a worker is summoned to a hearing during working hours, to allow attendance without any negative effect on that worker's labour rights. [Para 77][Para 79] Following Awards No. 091/15, 005/16, 098/16, 003/17 and 012/17, the employer must therefore allow attendance without deducting wages or benefits. [Para 82]

Although Article 14 does not expressly cover attendance on a rest day, the panel held its purpose is to encourage attendance and protect those who attend, so a worker who attends on a weekly rest day must have that right preserved as well. [Para 83][Para 84] On the facts, no union leader had yet been summoned to the Department on a day they were entitled to rest, so that limb was not considered; only Mr R's case was live, because his rest day fell on the 10 October 2019 hearing. [Para 73][Para 75] The employer had given him three paid days for travel and attendance — more favourable than the law — but the travel still consumed his 10 October rest day. [Para 86]

The panel treated weekly rest as a labour right in its own right, to be preserved in the same way as wages for workers attending dispute resolution. [Para 87][Para 88] The employer was therefore obliged to move the rest day to another date, but because shifting a rest day can disturb airport operations, the new date was left to the employer's arrangement having regard to operational necessity. [Para 89][Para 91]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Preserving a worker's labour rights when they attend a hearing, as Article 14 of Prakas No. 099 requires, includes preserving the weekly rest day that the hearing consumed: granting paid leave for the trip does not discharge that obligation, and a substitute rest day must be given, its date being for the employer to set by reference to operational needs. [Para 84][Para 91]

**Obiter dicta:** the dissenting arbitrator would have dismissed the claim outright, reasoning that three paid days already exceeded the statutory minimum, that the worker chose to attend and so gave up the rest day himself, and that an award granting more than the law on an interests dispute becomes a collective agreement binding both parties for one year. [Para 101][Para 107]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The minute-taking limb of demand 1 is set out at line 2 but is neither analysed nor disposed of in the reasons or the order. The worker-appointed arbitrator appears as អន ណន in the panel list but as សន ណន in the signature block. [Para 96] The panel constitution date reads "26 September 2023 2019" at line 13. The award refers to itself as case No. 092/19 at line 27 and to "សំណុំរឿង លេខ39" at line 33. The employer is named variously as Cambodia Airport Management Services, CAMBODIA AIRPORT (SCA/CAMS) and, in the commercial register extract, a company registered under MOC-22109205 on 06 December 1995, so the precise contracting entity is unclear from the Khmer text.
