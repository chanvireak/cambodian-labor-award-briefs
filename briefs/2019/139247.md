# Award No. 100/19 — NagaWorld Limited (Arbitration Council, 15 November 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3 — Yu Soun Ty (employer-appointed), Venh Pov (worker-appointed), Pen Bunchhea (president, chosen by the two) · Doc ID 139247

## 1. Facts
NagaWorld Limited runs a 24-hour hospitality and casino business in Tonle Bassac, Chamkar Mon, Phnom Penh, whose revenue depends on holiday trading. [Para 1][Para 3] On 23 September 2019 workers filed four claims on working conditions and benefits, covering holiday leave, the removal of a senior casino manager, a wage increase, and the reinstatement of the local union president, Ms C. [Para 20][Para 23][Para 81] The Labour Disputes Department conciliated twice, on 7 and 18 October 2019, without agreement, and sent three unresolved points to the Council on 25 October 2019. [Para 22][Para 25] At the hearing of 7 November 2019 the workers' representatives appeared, then stated they could not continue because the reinstatement claim had not been referred to the panel, and refused to take part. [Para 29][Para 110][Para 111]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must apply national-holiday leave under the Labour Law, on a voluntary basis? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company must dismiss Mr F, head of casino operations, for grave misconduct consisting of insulting and harassing staff? [Para 6]
3. Whether the company must pay at least USD 500 a month to gaming staff and USD 300 to hotel staff on first employment? [Para 13]
4. Whether the panel may continue the arbitration where the claimant party attends but declines to participate? [Para 106]

The conciliator had classified the union-president reinstatement claim as an individual dispute to be handled under Prakas No. 318, and only the remaining three points were referred to the panel. [Para 23][Para 24]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** holiday leave should follow the Labour Law on a voluntary basis; Mr F committed grave misconduct by insulting, harassing and humiliating staff, and should be dismissed; entry wages of USD 500 for gaming and USD 300 for hotel staff are required. [Para 2][Para 6][Para 13]
**Employer (respondent):** the business operates 24 hours and depends on holidays, staff contract to work them and receive 300% pay; Mr F is a senior vice-president on the management board who breached no internal rule, so there is no ground to dismiss him; the wage claim is refused absolutely because pay and benefits are already good and an employee share scheme (ESOS) awaits approval. [Para 4][Para 5][Para 7][Para 14]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** withdrawn — the panel terminated the arbitration and closed case No. 100/19 without ruling on any of the three claims. [Para 117][Para 118]

The panel framed the only question it answered as whether it should continue in the absence of the claimant. [Para 106] Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 requires all parties to attend every meeting to which the Council invites them, and Article 21 provides that where a duly invited party fails to appear without valid reason the panel may either proceed in that party's absence or close the arbitration by issuing an award. [Para 106][Para 107]

The panel found the workers had been properly invited and did attend on the hearing date, but expressly refused to join the hearing. [Para 113] It treated that refusal as conduct contrary to Article 20 and as equivalent to a failure to appear under Article 21, which entitled it to terminate. [Para 114][Para 115][Para 116] Because the claimants had abandoned the substance of their own complaint, the panel recorded that it could not make findings on the disputed points in the conciliation report. [Para 105]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claimant party that appears at the hearing but then declines to participate is treated as having failed to appear without valid reason, and the panel will close the arbitration by award rather than adjudicate the claims in that party's absence. A panel will not decide issues that the conciliator did not refer to it, and a claim routed to the individual-dispute procedure cannot be argued in the collective proceedings.

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted, without deciding, that the conciliator had classified the union president's reinstatement claim as an individual dispute under Prakas No. 318, which the workers disputed as the reason for their withdrawal. [Para 23][Para 111]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is short and heavily corrupted. The employer-appointed arbitrator's signature is reduced to OCR noise ("monmuntogland") at [Para 119], though his name survives in the header. The panel's constitution date is printed twice as "30 October 2019 2019" at [Para 26], and the case number appears as "100/ and 19" at [Para 117]. Prakas No. 399 on holiday pay is dated "20 October 12019" at [Para 45]. Mr F is described as head of casino operations at [Para 6] but as senior vice-president on the management board at [Para 7]. Four claims were filed but only three reached the panel, with no document recording what became of the fourth beyond the conciliator's classification at [Para 23]. The company name is spelled NagaWorld, Nagaworld and Naga in different places.
