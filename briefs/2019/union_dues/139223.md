# Award No. 084/19 — Ken Sport Shoe (Arbitration Council, 11 October 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Aing Eng Thong, worker-appointed; Tuon Si Phan, chair) · Doc ID 139223

## 1. Facts
The respondent manufactures footwear at Samaki Mean Chey district, Kampong Chhnang province, and reports about 9,812 workers. [Para 39][Para 40] On 6 August 2019 the provincial labour department received seven demands from the workers; conciliation on 26 August 2019 settled five, and the two unresolved points reached the Council on 28 August 2019 under report No. 1025. [Para 11][Para 12][Para 13] The hearing was held on 6 September 2019 with both parties present. [Para 10][Para 16] Both surviving claims concern union dues deducted from wages — how they are recorded on the payslip, and whether one worker was paid for seven days of special leave. [Para 41][Para 51]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must print on each worker's payslip the name of the union to which the dues deducted from that worker's wage were paid, so that the deduction is clear? [Para 3][Para 41]
2. Whether the employer must pay wages to one worker (Mr V) for the seven days of special leave she took when her child was ill? [Para 5][Para 51]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** some workers left a union but the employer went on deducting dues for two or three months afterwards; some are members of one union yet are charged dues for two or three — one worker (Ms S) for two unions and another (Ms P) for three — and when they queried this, the deductions stopped for a while and then resumed; checking with the building assistants is difficult because of work pressure and fear of being scolded by team leaders, and a Chinese supervisor has rebuked workers as troublemakers and withheld overtime. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46][Para 47]
**Employer (respondent):** the company has as many as eight unions and adequate records; a worker who wants to know which union he joined can ask the administration; the deductions follow written consents and thumbprints, the payslip states "union dues" and the amount, and any worker may check the records with the building assistant; the seven days were unpaid because only a medical certificate was produced, about 20 days after the return to work, and no special leave request was ever received. [Para 4][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 56][Para 81]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected both claims, refusing to order the union name on payslips or payment for the seven days. [Para 71][Para 90][Para 91][Para 92]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 61 of the Law on Trade Unions 2016, which governs the records an employer must keep whenever it deducts a worker's wage to pay union dues or agency fees: the date on which the worker authorised the deduction, the date and amount of dues deducted for each worker, and the date and amount transferred to the relevant union. [Para 57][Para 59][Para 61][Para 62] It found the employer kept all of these records each time it made a deduction, and that it allowed workers to inspect them with the building assistant where they suspected an over-deduction or an irregularity. [Para 63][Para 64] The employer had therefore discharged its duty under the spirit of Article 61. [Para 65] On the specific demand, the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, the Law on Trade Unions, a collective agreement, internal rules, the employment contract, an agreement between the parties or past practice requiring the employer to name the union on the payslip, and refused the claim. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 71]

The panel nonetheless recorded the underlying problem and addressed it to the workers rather than the employer: some workers had been charged dues for two or three unions, and deductions had resumed two or three months after a complaint. To prevent this recurring, a worker leaving a union should file a written resignation of membership with the employer so that the administration can keep it on file and avoid deducting again; an oral notice may be forgotten or unsupported, since the employer is obliged to deduct when the union submits the documentation. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 68]

On issue 2 the panel found the parties agreed that the worker did use seven days of special leave because her child was ill, and that the dispute was purely procedural. [Para 52][Para 72][Para 73] Clause 1.10 of the company's internal rules, approved under No. 511 of June 2016, requires every worker to follow the company's leave procedure, to complete a leave request and send it to the payroll and attendance section, and to complete the formality within three days of returning to the factory. [Para 74][Para 75][Para 76][Para 77] The panel followed Award No. 091/15 Double U & (Cambodia) on the importance of that paperwork, since the forms are what the company relies on to compute wages, monitor attendance and enforce discipline. [Para 78][Para 79] The worker said her husband (Mr S) had filed the request on her behalf and sent it to the team leader, but she was not at the hearing, admitted she did not know the facts clearly, and produced no evidence that the request reached the team leader or the administration. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 80][Para 85] The employer's account that it received only a medical certificate went unrebutted. [Para 86][Para 87] Applying its consistent approach that the party asserting a fact bears the burden of proving it, the panel held that no valid authorisation had been sought and that no wages were owed for the seven days. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 88][Para 89]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Article 61 of the Law on Trade Unions obliges the employer to keep, for every deduction of union dues, the date of the worker's authorisation, the amount deducted for each worker and the amount transferred to each union; an employer that keeps those records and opens them to inspection has complied, and the panel will not add a further requirement — such as naming the union on the payslip — that no law, agreement, internal rule or practice imposes. Where internal rules require a leave request within three days of returning to work, a worker who takes special leave without completing that formality and proves no valid substitute authorisation is not entitled to wages for the days absent. [Para 62][Para 65][Para 69][Para 77][Para 88]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's guidance that a worker resigning from a union should give the employer written notice so that the administration can stop the deduction, oral notice being unreliable; and its observation that the employer is bound to deduct once the union submits the documentation. [Para 67][Para 68]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The internal rules are cited three ways: clause 1.10 approved under No. 511 of "10 June 2016" (printed as "១០០ ខែមិថុនា"), clause 10.1 in the reasoning at paragraph 88, and the same clause quoted twice. [Para 74][Para 88] The recitals at paragraphs 12 and 13 are badly garbled, so the count of five settled and two unsettled demands rests on the clearer statements at paragraphs 12 and 18. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 18] Paragraph 25 mangles the transfer-of-rights letter and the internal rules reference into a single run of text, and paragraphs 57 to 60 break the text of Article 61 mid-sentence. [Para 25][Para 57][Para 58][Para 60] The workforce figure of 9,812 is given only in the summary of the conciliation report and is not otherwise verified in the award. [Para 40] The chair is named as Tuon Si Phan in the header, but the signature block omits the worker-appointed arbitrator's name and repeats the chair's; individual workers, the husband and the two workers cited as examples of double deduction are referred to by initial. [Para 1][Para 94] The file also records a competing labour-relations body at paragraph 1 ("ប្រធាន សូហជីព កម្ពុជា (មូលដ្ឋាន)") whose status the award does not explain.
