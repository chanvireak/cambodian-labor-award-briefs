# Award No. 090/19 — Chong Huor Printing Workshop (Arbitration Council, 14 October 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; Kong Pollak, appointed by the workers; An Vireak, president appointed by both) · Doc ID 139230 · non-binding award

## 1. Facts
Chong Huor Printing Workshop carries on printing as its principal activity, with 63 workers on the conciliation report's count. [Para 36][Para 37] Its workers' union was registered as a professional organisation on 11 June 2019 under No. 234/19, and the Ministry of Labour confirmed its legal personality by letter of the same date. [Para 23][Para 24]
The workers filed a collective dispute on 03 September 2019 raising three working-condition demands; one was settled in conciliation, and two unresolved points reached the panel, of which the parties settled point 1 at the hearing, leaving point 2 on annual leave. [Para 10][Para 11][Para 17][Para 18] In that remaining point 62 named workers, with thumbprints, demanded retroactive annual-leave compensation for the last three years, calculated on base wage plus allowances. [Para 38][Para 50]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the five categories of compensation demanded for workers already given notice of dismissal and those it planned to dismiss further? [Para 2]
2. Whether the employer must pay retroactive compensation in lieu of annual leave for the last three years, calculated on base wage and allowances, to all workers still employed? [Para 38][Para 52]
3. Whether the employer may pay compensation in lieu of annual leave while the employment relationship continues? [Para 59]

Issue 1 was settled by agreement at the hearing and was not decided. [Para 17]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The workers never took their annual leave, and the employer instead calculated an annual-leave payment using base wage only; because the employer requires them to keep working, the calculation should take base wage and allowances together. The workers conceded that the Labour Law does not permit paying annual-leave compensation to workers who are still employed. [Para 39][Para 41][Para 44][Para 45]
**Employer (respondent):** The company pays only what the law requires and cannot meet the demand: the 62 workers agreed orally to come to work and to receive an annual-leave payment computed on base wage, and since 2003 annual-leave compensation has been calculated on base wage alone; Article 167 does not authorise such a calculation, and if the workers refuse the payment the company can let them take ordinary annual leave instead. The company also asked the panel to reject the list of 62 names on the ground that no such payment had ever been claimed before and no evidence had been filed. [Para 4][Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49][Para 22]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the demand that the employer pay retroactive annual-leave compensation for the last three years, calculated on base wage and allowances, to all workers was refused, and no sum was ordered. [Para 73][Para 74]

The panel first disposed of the employer's procedural objection. Following its own earlier awards, the party asserting a fact or making an allegation bears the burden of proof, and a claim unsupported by adequate or specific evidence is rejected. Here the employer filed no sufficient evidence that the 62 workers had never claimed the annual-leave payment, so the objection was disregarded. [Para 53][Para 55][Para 57][Para 58]

On the substance the panel read Article 167(2) as allowing payment in lieu of untaken annual leave only where the contract is terminated or ends before the worker has acquired the right to take that leave, and Article 167(3) as voiding any agreement to waive or forgo annual leave. It concluded that payment in lieu may be made only when the employment relationship between worker and employer comes to an end, and that the Law forbids such payment while a legally binding contractual relationship still subsists. The company's practice of paying annual-leave compensation year after year while continuing the contracts was therefore contrary to Article 167. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 62][Para 63][Para 64][Para 66]

The consequence was not an order to pay but an order to stop paying: the employer must not make annual-leave payments in lieu while the contracts remain in force and should instead allow the workers to take the leave. The claim accordingly had no legal basis, consistently with Awards No. 94/04, 45/05, 88/12 and 73/13. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 69][Para 72][Para 73]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Compensation in lieu of untaken annual leave under Article 167(2) falls due only on termination of the employment relationship; an employer that pays it annually while the contract continues acts contrary to Article 167, and a demand for retroactive payment in lieu made by workers who remain employed is without legal foundation — the remedy is to let them take the leave, not to pay them for it. [Para 63][Para 67][Para 73]

**Obiter dicta:** A party alleging that an earlier payment was never claimed bears the burden of proving it, and an unsupported objection will be disregarded [Para 53][Para 58]; the panel observed that workers who refuse the payment offered may instead be allowed ordinary annual leave [Para 49].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The case header carries a garbled OCR digit string before the case number [Para 1]; the paragraph creating the panel repeats the year ("២០១៩ ២០១៩") [Para 13] and the paragraph on the extended award date repeats "ឆ្នាំ" twice [Para 21]. The award gives the workforce as 63 in one paragraph and the claimant list as 62 in others, without reconciling the difference [Para 37][Para 50]. The paragraph that should state the workshop's workforce is syntactically broken ("តើ សិប្បក្សិ កម្មករ និយោជិត សរុប ចំនួន ៦៣ នាក់") [Para 37], and the signature block prints the president's name both as "គង់ ផល្លៈ" and as "គង់ ផល្លី", with a stray figure beside one arbitrator's name [Para 76]. The period for the award to become final is printed as 18 days, where the Council's usual formula is 8 [Para 75]. The terms of the point-1 settlement reached at the hearing are not recorded anywhere in the award [Para 17].
