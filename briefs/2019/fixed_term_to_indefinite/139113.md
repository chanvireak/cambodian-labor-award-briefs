# Award No. 068/19 — Mita Bay Omed (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 5 September 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — chair អាំង អេង ថុង, employer-appointed យូ សួន ទី (dissenting in part), worker-appointed លីវ សុវណ្ណា · Doc ID 139113

## 1. Facts
Mita Bay Omed (Cambodia), which manufactures dental medical equipment at Jey Oudom, Samrong Thom, Kien Svay, Kandal, employed 90 workers; 53 of them were complainants here. The company had lost money for several years and saw orders fall sharply, so management decided to close production from 1 July 2019, notifying the Kandal labour department on 25 May 2019 and the workers on 15 June 2019. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 43][Para 53]

The employer had used successive fixed-term contracts, each of three months, paying 5% end-of-contract compensation plus outstanding annual leave at the end of every term and renewing without a break. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 81][Para 82]

At closure the employer paid four heads — final month's wage to 30 June 2019, outstanding annual leave, 5% end-of-contract compensation and notice pay — and the workers accepted the first three. Forty-two workers took payment; the remaining 48 workers' money was deposited with the Kandal labour department. [Para 42][Para 44][Para 45][Para 46]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether, on the closure of the enterprise, the employer must pay the five entitlements claimed — final month's wage, annual leave pay, notice pay, seniority indemnity and moral damages (សោហ៊ុយជំងឺចិត្ត)? [Para 2][Para 42]

The workers put their case on the footing that the closure was a unilateral termination by the employer and that their contracts were indefinite-duration, because the aggregate of the successive fixed terms exceeded two years. [Para 49][Para 65]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the closure is a dissolution of the contract at the employer's will, and no opportunity was given to the employer to escape the duty to pay moral damages; the aggregate of their fixed-term contracts exceeded two years, so they held indefinite-duration contracts; and the contracts were never allowed to lapse, being renewed continuously. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 65]

**Employer (respondent):** the workers accepted the 5% end-of-contract compensation and accepted renewal of fixed-term contracts without interruption, so they hold fixed-term contracts and cannot claim more; the company's order book had collapsed and it could not find work for the workers as before. [Para 41][Para 51][Para 52]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay seniority indemnity to each complainant according to actual length of service, and to pay moral damages equal to that seniority indemnity; the claims for final wages, annual leave and notice pay were not in dispute and had been settled by the payments already made. [Para 129][Para 130][Para 90][Para 44]

On the character of the contracts, the panel applied Article 67(2) of the Labour Law — a fixed-term contract may not exceed two years and may be renewed provided each renewal keeps within that maximum — and Article 73(5), under which a fixed-term contract becomes indefinite-duration where there is no notice of termination and the aggregate duration exceeds the Article 67 limit. It followed award 410/03 and award 10/03 in holding that the reference is to the total maximum, not to each renewal separately, and relied on ILO Recommendation No. 166 as context. On the facts it found 60 workers with more than two years' service, so all complainants held indefinite-duration contracts. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 71][Para 76][Para 79][Para 83][Para 84]

On closure, the panel read the new Article 87 (printed also as Article 91 new): cessation of the enterprise does not release the employer from the obligations in Part 3, save in a case of force majeure, and liquidation or striking off through the courts is not force majeure. The employer was therefore still bound to give notice, pay seniority indemnity, final wages and outstanding annual leave. [Para 86][Para 87][Para 89]

On seniority indemnity, the panel applied Article 89 (new) and Articles 2 and 3 of Prakas No. 443 of 21 September 2018: 7.5 days for a first year of service of one to six months and 15 days beyond six months, capped at six months' wages, computed on average wage and allowances over the preceding 12 months under Article 110 (new). It ordered payment according to each worker's actual length of service. It also held that the 5% end-of-contract compensation already paid could be set off against the indemnity, because that 5% had been paid at the expiry of each fixed term. [Para 91][Para 98][Para 105][Para 107][Para 114][Para 116][Para 117]

On moral damages, the panel noted that Article 87(4) allows an employer to be relieved of moral damages and notice pay where an enterprise closes on conditions to be fixed by ministerial Prakas — but no such Prakas yet exists, so the exemption could not be invoked. Article 91 (new) permits moral damages where a contract is dissolved without a valid reason, and moral damages are distinct from notice pay and seniority indemnity. Finding that the employer produced no evidence to support the asserted closure and so did not establish a proper reason under Article 74, the panel ordered moral damages equal to the seniority indemnity. [Para 120][Para 121][Para 122][Para 126][Para 127][Para 128]

The employer-appointed arbitrator dissented on that last point alone, considering that Article 87(4) exempted the employer, that no Prakas was needed for the exemption to operate, and that the employer had a proper reason for closing and had notified the labour department on 25 May 2019. [Para 135][Para 136][Para 141][Para 142][Para 143]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive fixed-term contracts whose aggregate duration exceeds two years convert to indefinite-duration contracts, whatever the parties label each renewal, and the employer's liability on termination is measured by the whole period of service. On closure of an enterprise the termination entitlements survive, because the statutory exemption for moral damages and notice pay is conditional on a ministerial Prakas that does not yet exist, so an employer that closes without proving the reason for closure owes moral damages as well as seniority indemnity. [Para 76][Para 87][Para 125][Para 127]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's recourse to ILO Recommendation No. 166, which it acknowledged creates no binding obligation, and its observation that fixed-term contracts are disfavoured because indefinite-duration contracts promote job security, were context rather than grounds of decision. [Para 77][Para 74][Para 79]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is severely degraded from paragraph 144 onward, where the annexes of worker thumbprints and identity numbers collapse into unusable OCR; those names are not reproduced. The statutory provision on closure is cited inconsistently throughout: the same text is introduced as "Article 87 (new)" and then renamed "Article 91 (new)" within a single paragraph (Paras 86, 118, 121), so the numbering cannot be relied on from the face of the award. Dates carry stray years: the panel's constitution is dated "ឆ្នាំ ២០២២ ឆ្នាំ ២០១៩" (Para 10) and the reasons refer to "ឆ្នាំ ២០២៣" (Para 105). Worker counts are internally inconsistent: 90 workers at Para 40, 53 complainants at Para 53, and "កម្មករ និយោជិត ២០" at Para 64. The service-length table at Para 54 is garbled ("លើក ពី3000", "១៣នាក់ មាន អតីតភាពទាំ ឆ្នាំ") and cannot be reconciled with the 60 workers the panel relies on. Paragraph 99 repeats the year 2019 twice. The employer's name appears both as មីតា បាយ អូមេដ and មី តា បាយ អូមេដ.
