# Award No. 097/19 — Gold Den Earel (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 2 December 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy appointed by the employer party, Liv Sovanna appointed by the worker party, Kong Phalla chair; Liv Sovanna dissented in an annexed opinion) · Doc ID 140825

## 1. Facts
Gold Den Earel (Cambodia) Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្គោល ដិន អឹភែរឹល (ខេមបូឌា) អិលធីឌី), a garment factory at Longvek, Kampong Tralach district, Kampong Chhnang, employed 2,730 workers. [Para 86] On 17 September 2019 the provincial Labour Department received a union complaint raising nine demands, and conciliation failed on 16 October 2019. [Para 18][Para 19] At the hearing the workers merged issues 1, 3 and 9 into one and settled issues 2, 5, 6 and 7, leaving three groups for decision. [Para 24][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issues 1, 3 and 9) Whether the employer must take back five union leaders and activists it dismissed or suspended in September 2019? [Para 87][Para 88]
2. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must stop requiring workers to fingerprint in support of the Khmer Workers' Spirit Union (សហជីព ស្មារតី កម្មករ ខ្មែរ) as a condition of the Cambodia Union's leaders being allowed back to work? [Para 107][Para 184]
3. (Issue 8) Whether fixed-duration contracts must be converted into unlimited-duration contracts once their cumulative duration passes two years, and whether Instruction No. 050/19 governs instead? [Para 109][Para 190]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, ភាគី តំណាង កម្មករ និយោជិត):** the union president was not renewed after he won the union election, the two other leaders had their contracts suspended, and two members were terminated, all for union activity, contrary to Articles 62 and 63 of the Trade Union Law and Article 12 of the Labour Law; the employer also had police test workers' urine to harass the leadership. [Para 99][Para 100][Para 101][Para 102] The employer forced workers to fingerprint in support of its own union, and contracts held over two years should become unlimited-duration. [Para 107][Para 110]
**Employer (respondent, ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្គោល ដិន អឹភែរឹល (ខេមបូឌា) អិលធីឌី):** non-renewal is lawful under Articles 67 and 73 of the Labour Law; the president breached security rules and the internal regulations by leaving the premises without permission and quarrelling with security; the urine tests were voluntary; it did not create or support the rival union and did not force fingerprinting; and Instruction No. 050/19 allows conversion only after two years and six months. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 105][Para 108][Para 112][Para 113]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel refused reinstatement but ordered the employer to pay two dismissed workers their 5 per cent severance, remaining annual-leave compensation, final unpaid wages and damages of at least the wages due to the end of their contracts; the rest of the claims were rejected or not considered. [Para 182][Para 183][Para 217][Para 218]

On the three union leaders the panel did not reach the merits. For the union president, the labour inspector had already decided on 7 October 2019 that his contract had expired rather than that he was dismissed, and Article 43 of the Trade Union Law gives the parties two months to appeal that decision to the Minister, so the matter lay within the Minister's competence and the panel could not consider it. [Para 128][Para 130][Para 131][Para 132][Para 133] The two suspended leaders stood on the same footing: the inspector refused permission to dismiss them on 8 November 2019, and the same two-month appeal period applied. [Para 137][Para 138][Para 139][Para 140][Para 141]

For the two members dismissed on 14 September 2019 the panel found they had not shown they were union founders, and the election membership list did not name them, so the protection of Articles 43 and 67 of the Trade Union Law did not extend to them. [Para 148][Para 149][Para 150] The urine test of 11 September 2019 had found drug traces in six of 58 workers tested, four of them union people, and clause 11(g) of the contract allows termination for drug use on company premises. [Para 105][Para 106][Para 159][Para 160] The panel found however that the two had not used drugs inside the company, so the unilateral termination was not justified, and it ordered the statutory payments in lieu. [Para 161][Para 162][Para 163][Para 167][Para 172][Para 175][Para 180]

On issue 4 the panel applied Article 63 of the Trade Union Law, which forbids employer interference in, or financing of, a union, and found the workers had offered no evidence beyond their own assertions that fingerprinting was forced; the claim was dismissed. [Para 184][Para 188][Para 189]

On issue 8 the panel set Article 67(2) and Article 73(5) of the Labour Law, under which a fixed-duration contract cannot exceed two years in total and converts automatically beyond that, against Instruction No. 050/19 of 17 May 2019, which contemplates a total of up to four years; it held that the Instruction remains open to conflicting readings and that Article 47 of the Law on the Organisation of the Council of Ministers bars a ministerial instruction from contradicting a law. [Para 190][Para 193][Para 199][Para 201][Para 209][Para 211] Because it has no power to rule the Instruction contrary to the Labour Law, the panel declined to decide the claim and left the parties to seek an authoritative interpretation from the competent body. [Para 213][Para 215][Para 216]

**Dissent:** arbitrator Liv Sovanna recorded that he would have ordered the employer to reinstate the union president and to convert his contract, on the ground that the panel should apply the law itself rather than defer to the Minister on a question the parties had brought to it. [Para 275][Para 278][Para 305][Para 311][Para 314]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a labour inspector has already ruled on the character of a termination and the two-month period for appealing that ruling to the Minister under Article 43 of the Trade Union Law is still running, the Arbitration Council has no competence to decide the same question; and where a ministerial instruction is said to contradict the Labour Law, the panel will decline to apply it rather than rule on its legality. [Para 131][Para 132][Para 215]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the Labour Law favours unlimited-duration contracts because they secure employment are general observations taken from earlier awards, not the ground of decision. [Para 236]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award is dated 2 December 2019 in the header and again at the foot, but the paragraph recording the panel's constitution prints the year twice, and the paragraph extending the deadline does the same, so the procedural chronology is unreliable at those points. [Para 21][Para 27][Para 315]
- The ministerial instruction at the centre of issue 8 is printed variously as No. 050/19, No. 5/19 and No. 1 050/19, and its date is given once as 17 May 2019 and once as 2019 only. [Para 198][Para 212][Para 290][Para 300]
- The signature block names the employer-party and worker-party arbitrators but leaves the chair's name blank, although the chair is named on the face of the award. [Para 222]
- The annexed dissent repeats the majority's quotations at length, and the OCR has merged and duplicated whole clauses of it, so the dissent's exact wording cannot be reconstructed at several points. [Para 226][Para 279][Para 287]
- Workers, union officials and representatives are named in full throughout the record; they are identified here by role and issue only. [Para 44][Para 87]
