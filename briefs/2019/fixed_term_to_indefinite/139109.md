# Award No. 060/19 — Quint Major Industrial Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 15 August 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — chair គង់ ផល្លៈ, employer-appointed សេង វួច ហ៊ុន, worker-appointed ស៊ីន គីម ស៊ាន · Doc ID 139109

## 1. Facts
Quint Major Industrial Co., Ltd. (ឃ្វីន ធ៍ មេជើរ អ៊ិន ដាស ស្ទ្រៀល ខូអិលធីឌី), a garment manufacturer at Trapeang Chheu Neang, Ang Snuol, Kandal, employed some 3,707 workers represented by the Democratic Union of Workers at the company. The workers raised four demands on working conditions; conciliation at the Kandal provincial labour department failed on 15 July 2019 and report No. 714/19 K.B/Kandal reached the Council on 16 July 2019. [Para 18][Para 62][Para 63][Para 20]

The workers struck, and on 17 July 2019 the Council issued order No. 008/19 K.AB.A ordering immediate suspension of the strike and return to work in shifts from the afternoon of 18 July 2019; the workers complied. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24]

At the hearing the workers withdrew issues 1 and 3, and the parties reached agreement on issue 4, which the panel recorded in the award; only issue 2 remained for adjudication. [Para 27][Para 28]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay notice pay to the workers under Article 75 of the Labour Law? [Para 4]
2. Whether the employer must pay seniority indemnity (ប្រាក់បំណាច់អតីតភាពការងារ) for the period 2008 to before 2019? [Para 6]
3. Whether the employer must pay a midday meal allowance of 4,000 riel? [Para 8]
4. Whether the employer must maintain wages and other benefits during the strike? [Para 10]

Issue 2 was itself argued in two limbs: (a) reimbursement of indemnity for 2008–2012 and (b) 7.5 days' indemnity for 1 May to 30 June 2019 for workers whose fixed-term contracts expired on 30 April 2019. [Para 68]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the 2013 severance payments did not satisfy the conditions the law requires, because no indemnity for length of service was paid at all; any payment or agreement below the statutory standard is void under Article 13 of the Labour Law. They also argued that from 1 May 2019 their contracts were indefinite-duration, since the employer issued no written renewal and they kept working. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 81][Para 135]

**Employer (respondent):** in 2013 the workers thumbprinted acceptance of the severance for 2008–2012, which was paid only after a strike demanding it and a switch to one-year fixed-term contracts; contracts from 2013 were one-year fixed-term with a 5% end-of-contract payment. On issue 4 it argued that strikers earn no wage or bonus and that only statutory benefits — seniority bonus, travel and accommodation allowance — would be preserved. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 72][Para 96][Para 98]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim for seniority indemnity for 2008–2012 was dismissed, and the employer was ordered to pay seniority indemnity equal to 7.5 days of average wage and allowances for the first semester of 2019 (1 May to 30 June 2019), payable in June 2019. Issue 4 was closed by the parties' agreement on statutory benefits; issues 1 and 3 were withdrawn. [Para 165][Para 166][Para 99]

On limb (a), the panel read the 7 May 2013 agreement and the individual 1 May 2013 settlement letters and found both expressly listed "severance pay" under Article 91 — which the parties renamed "compassion money" — alongside Article 75 notice pay and final month's wage. It followed its own awards 271/14 and 271/13-D&L that a contract may be varied or dissolved by the parties' mutual will (Civil Code Articles 311, 337), and concluded that where severance under Article 89 has actually been paid and accepted, no law or legal standard requires it to be paid a second time; the claim was therefore dismissed. [Para 126][Para 129][Para 130][Para 132][Para 133]

On limb (b), the panel applied Article 67(7) and (8) of the Labour Law: a fixed-term contract must be in writing or it becomes indefinite-duration, and a contract of two years or less that expires while the parties stay silent is continued silently and treated as having no fixed term. Because the one-year contracts lapsed on 30 April 2019 without written renewal while the workers kept working, the panel held their contracts were indefinite-duration from 1 May 2019, rejecting the employer's contention that silent continuation preserved the original one-year term. [Para 146][Para 148][Para 152][Para 136]

The entitlement then followed Article 89 (new) of the Labour Law, Article 2 of Prakas No. 443 K.B/Pr.K.Kh.L of 21 September 2018 and Instruction No. 058/19 of 10 June 2019: a worker who serves at least one month in a semester and works to that semester's last month (June or December) earns 7.5 days of average wage and allowances. The workers served May and June 2019, the first semester, so 7.5 days were payable in June 2019. [Para 153][Para 157][Para 160][Para 162][Para 163][Para 164]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a fixed-term contract of two years or less expires and the employer issues no written renewal while the worker continues to work, the relationship becomes an indefinite-duration contract from the day after expiry, and the employer owes the semester's 7.5 days of seniority indemnity on the strength of the semester actually worked, whatever the lapsed contract was called. Conversely, seniority indemnity for an earlier period that was paid and accepted under a written settlement cannot be claimed again for the same period. [Para 132][Para 152][Para 164]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's account of Article 54 of the Trade Union Law, holding that only the union with exclusive representative status may bargain and litigate collective disputes, and its citations to awards 180/16, 171/16 and 092/15, were not necessary to the outcome, since the union's standing was uncontested. It also noted the award is non-binding and becomes final after eight days absent written objection to the Minister. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 109][Para 167]
