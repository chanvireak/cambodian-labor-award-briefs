# Award No. 005/19 — Credit Union Foundation Australia (CUFA), Cambodia office (Arbitration Council, 21 February 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Uk Ri (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Pen Bunchhea (chair) · Doc ID 138870

## 1. Facts
CUFA, a foreign non-governmental organisation doing humanitarian and welfare work in Cambodia, employed about 30 staff on one-year fixed-duration contracts tied to donor-funded projects. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 67] It terminated Mr N and Mr Y on 31 October 2018 when the project they worked on ended, and other staff on fixed-duration contracts also left at project end or resigned. [Para 54][Para 82] The workers demanded contract-severance payments, damages, notice pay, accrued annual leave, holiday pay, seniority indemnity and the organisation's Retention Fund; their second demand, on terms for a new project, was withdrawn at the hearing, leaving one issue. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 56]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the contract-severance benefits claimed — damages, pay in lieu of notice, accrued annual leave, public-holiday pay, seniority indemnity and the Retention Fund — to the workers whose contracts ended? [Para 121][Para 290]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr N and Mr Y had served more than two years, so their successive fixed-duration contracts became undetermined-duration contracts and the ending of the project was a dismissal, not a non-renewal; notice was short or absent, leave and holiday entitlements were below the Labour Law, and every head of severance claimed is owed. [Para 57][Para 59][Para 60]

**Employer (respondent):** the organisation is an international NGO working to annual donor budgets in five countries, so it can only hire project by project and cannot pay these allowances; it already gives benefits above the law, offers only 15 days' annual leave and 10 paid holidays as a matter of group policy, and pays the Retention Fund only where no new post is offered — Mr Y refused a new project post paying USD 50 less. [Para 63][Para 65][Para 69][Para 78]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must pay notice pay under Articles 75 and 77, pay in lieu of accrued annual leave, public-holiday pay for 2016–2018, seniority indemnity under new Article 89 and Prakas No. 443, the 5% contract-termination indemnity to the four fixed-duration workers, and the Retention Fund; the claims for damages (sokheu jamngue chit) are dismissed. [Para 265][Para 270][Para 330][Para 339]

On the contract type, the panel applied Article 67(2) and Article 73(5): renewals that push the aggregate beyond two years convert the contract to undetermined duration. [Para 124][Para 129][Para 139] The employer did not dispute that both men had served over two years, so their contracts had converted and their departure was a dismissal, not a mere non-renewal. [Para 142][Para 145]

On the reason for dismissal, Article 74 permits termination only for a reason related to the worker's capacity or conduct or to the necessities of the enterprise; the panel accepted that the project had ended with no further donor funding, so the dismissal was based on a valid reason and no damages were owed. [Para 146][Para 148][Para 159][Para 160] Notice pay still followed: Article 75 required two months for Mr N (5 years 7 months' service) and three months for Mr Y (10 years 8 months), and the employer showed no written notice for Mr Y and only one month for Mr N. [Para 167][Para 168][Para 172][Para 173]

On annual leave, Article 166 gives 18 days after a year plus one day per three years, so the employer's flat 15 days fell short and the balance was payable under Article 167(2). [Para 202][Para 206] On holiday pay, Articles 161 and 164 and Prakas No. 10 require pay for work done on paid public holidays; the panel awarded the actual days worked in 2016, 2017 and 2018, the three-year limit in new Article 120 barring older claims. [Para 243][Para 248][Para 251] Seniority indemnity was calculated under new Article 89 and Prakas No. 443, the dismissal falling after those rules took effect. [Para 223][Para 224] On the Retention Fund, the parties accepted that a project-end termination entitles the worker to it, so it was ordered for Mr Y and for the four fixed-duration workers. [Para 252][Para 263][Para 329]

The panel also refused English-only company policies, because Article 23 of Prakas No. 099 makes Khmer the language of proceedings, and it noted the memorandum with the Royal Government obliging the organisation to obey Cambodian law. [Para 195][Para 197][Para 200]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer cannot rely on its own internal group policy, offered in a foreign language, to give less than the Labour Law: annual leave, paid public holidays, seniority indemnity and notice pay are statutory minimums, and a fixed-duration contract renewed beyond two years converts to an undetermined-duration contract, so its ending is a dismissal requiring statutory notice — though a dismissal caused by the genuine end of a donor-funded project is founded on a valid reason under Article 74 and attracts no damages. [Para 160][Para 197][Para 202][Para 224]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the Labour Law leans towards undetermined-duration contracts because job security serves both parties, and its remarks on the employer's obligation to align its internal rules for the future, were not necessary to the orders. [Para 133][Para 193]
