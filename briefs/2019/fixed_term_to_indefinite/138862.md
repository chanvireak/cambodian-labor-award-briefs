# Award No. 016/19 — Clet Pi Garmen Factory (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 6 March 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Buncha presiding; Yu Soun Ty nominated by the employer, Toun Si Phan nominated by the workers) · Doc ID 138862

## 1. Facts
Clet Pi Garmen Factory (Cambodia) Ltd, at Prey Pring North, Chom Chao, Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh, cuts and sews garments. [Para 1][Para 24] The workers' complaint reached the labour dispute department on 2 January 2019; conciliation on 11 February 2019 left eight demands unresolved, forwarded in report No. 134 of 15 February 2019, and the hearing was fixed for 28 February 2019 at 14:00. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 26][Para 50] Neither party appeared: the workers' representative said by telephone that the workers had resigned and been paid and had withdrawn, and the employer said it would not attend for the same reason. [Para 28][Para 30][Para 31] On 25 February 2019 the employer filed two resignation letters its workers had addressed to the factory director. [Para 29][Para 32][Para 33] The award is non-binding. [Para 74]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the panel must continue the arbitration when the complainant workers, who were properly summoned, do not appear and give no valid reason for their absence? [Para 41][Para 43]
The eight demands left unresolved at conciliation — probation periods, indefinite-term contracts after two years, contract renewal notice, protection against dismissal without cause, a USD 26 monthly lunch allowance, 2,000 riel an hour for overtime meals, saving annual leave to swap for Pchum Ben and Khmer New Year days, and the reinstatement of three named workers — were never reached. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 3][Para 7][Para 9][Para 11][Para 13][Para 14][Para 26]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** absent. Their representative told the secretariat that the workers had resigned, accepted the employer's settlement and withdrawn the complaint, so they would not attend. [Para 30][Para 59] No document withdrawing the complaint or the case was ever filed with the panel. [Para 62][Para 63]
**Employer (respondent):** absent. It telephoned on the morning of the hearing to say it would not attend because the workers had withdrawn, and it filed only the two resignation letters of 22 February 2019, in which the workers asked to leave and to withdraw every complaint made to the labour department and the Council. [Para 29][Para 31][Para 32][Para 33]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** withdrawn — the panel closed the arbitration and issued an award, treating the complainant as having abandoned its claims; nothing was ordered on the merits. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 75]
The panel put the only question it decided as whether it must continue in the absence of the complainant. [Para 41] Article 18 of Prakas No. 099 requires the parties to appear in person and to produce documents, Article 20 requires them to attend every meeting, and Article 21 allows the panel either to continue or to close the arbitration and issue an award where a properly summoned party fails to appear without a valid reason; Rule 4.7 of the procedural rules repeats that choice and requires proof of proper notice. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43][Para 44][Para 45] The panel drew from these three conditions for closing the file: proper notice, non-appearance, and no valid reason. [Para 46][Para 47]
On the first condition it found the invitation of 21 February 2019, No. 110, gave the date, place and contact details and was communicated to the workers' representative, so notice was proper. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 52] On the second it held that appearing means both being present and taking part in the proceedings until they end, and that the complainant had attended neither the pre-hearing meeting nor the hearing. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 56][Para 57][Para 58] On the third it found the explanation — that the workers had resigned, been paid and withdrawn — unproved: the letters were addressed to the employer, not to the panel, expressed an intention to end the employment relationship, and said nothing that constituted a withdrawal of the complaint, and no withdrawal document ever reached the panel. [Para 59][Para 61][Para 62][Para 63][Para 64][Para 65]
All three conditions being met, the panel held that a complainant who does not appear to state its case and produce its evidence abandons its claims, and closed the arbitration rather than deciding the eight demands. [Para 66][Para 70][Para 71][Para 72][Para 73]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a party has been properly summoned, fails to appear, and offers no valid reason, the panel may exercise its discretion under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 and Rule 4.7 to close the arbitration by issuing an award instead of continuing in absentia. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 46] A complainant who does not appear to present its arguments and evidence is taken to have abandoned its claims, and the panel will not examine the merits on the file alone. [Para 70][Para 72] A withdrawal must be communicated to the panel in writing; resignation letters addressed to the employer and filed by the employer are not a withdrawal of the complaint, and an unproved assertion of settlement is no valid reason for absence. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 64]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel set out the parties' respective burdens — the complainant to give reasons and evidence for its claim, the respondent to discharge itself of it, the latter arising only once the former has done so. [Para 68][Para 69]
