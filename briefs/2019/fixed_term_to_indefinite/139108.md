# Award No. 062/19 — វ៉ាយ ធី ស៊ី ខប ប៉ូរេសិន (Arbitration Council, 15 August 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Pen Bun Cham chair) · Doc ID 139108

## 1. Facts
The employer, a garment factory at Chambok village, Vallis commune, Samraong Tong district, Kampong Speu, employed 2,136 workers. [Para 51] The workers raised three claims: repayment of the 5 per cent end-of-contract indemnity together with 7 days' seniority payment, correct calculation of five termination heads without deducting that 5 per cent, and conversion of workers with more than two years' service to indefinite-duration contracts. [Para 2] At the hearing the parties settled the conversion claim and merged the first two, leaving one issue. [Para 16] The claim concerns 73 workers whose contracts the employer ended for staff reduction. [Para 57]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must repay the 5 per cent end-of-contract indemnity, already paid to the 73 workers during their successive fixed-duration contracts, now that it has paid them the five termination benefits due under their indefinite-duration contracts and set that 5 per cent off against them? [Para 55][Para 96]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer has no right to take back the 5 per cent it paid in the past, because paying it was a practice more favourable than Article 13 of the Labour Law requires, no law authorises recovery, the workers were never told in advance, and the deduction was the employer's unilateral will without their agreement. [Para 60][Para 62]
**Employer (respondent):** it paid all five termination heads correctly under the indefinite-duration contract, then recovered the 5 per cent previously paid, as Award No. 330/15 and Instructions No. 057 and 058 permit; it gave each worker the payment sheet two days in advance, and each signed an agreement accepting the payment and the deduction. [Para 63][Para 69]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the claim for repayment; the award is non-binding and becomes final after 8 days without written objection. [Para 116][Para 118]

The panel began from Article 73(6) of the Labour Law: on termination of a fixed-duration contract the employer must pay an end-of-contract indemnity at least equal to 5 per cent of the wages paid during the contract. [Para 96] Following a line of earlier awards, it held that this obligation falls due when the fixed-duration contract comes to an end and the employment relationship with it — so that where such a contract is converted by law into an indefinite-duration contract, there is no severance and the 5 per cent is not owed at all. [Para 99] On that basis, once the employer had correctly paid the five termination heads attaching to an indefinite-duration contract (dismissal indemnity, unused annual leave, payment in lieu of notice, moral damages and final wages), it was entitled to recover the 5 per cent it had paid earlier, since retaining both would pay the same benefit twice. [Para 103][Para 114] The panel also relied on Part IV of Instruction No. 057/19 of 10 June 2019, which provides that a worker who has already received the 5 per cent end-of-contract indemnity for a given period cannot also claim seniority indemnity for that period. [Para 104] Having reached that conclusion on the text, it declined to rule on whether the instruction conflicts with the Labour Law, holding that it had no power to review or interpret the legality of a ministerial instruction and that a party who considers it unlawful should complain to the ministry or the competent authority. [Para 112][Para 113]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The 5 per cent end-of-contract indemnity under Article 73(6) compensates only the ending of a fixed-duration contract; it is therefore not payable where such a contract is converted into an indefinite-duration one, and where the employer has in fact paid it during successive fixed-duration contracts it may set that amount off against the termination benefits it later owes on lawfully ending the indefinite-duration contract, because the same benefit cannot be paid twice. [Para 99][Para 114]

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that the panel cannot examine whether Instruction No. 057/19 contradicts the Labour Law, and that the parties should take that question to the ministry or a competent authority, is a statement about the panel's own competence and did not decide the claim. [Para 113]
