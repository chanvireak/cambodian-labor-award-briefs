# Award No. 074/19 — Cambodia Airports Services (Phnom Penh International Airport) (Arbitration Council, 16 September 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambor Na, appointed by the employer; Tuon Si Phan, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 139177

## 1. Facts
Cambodia Airports Services, which manages Phnom Penh International Airport and employs about 1,000 workers, faced eight demands raised by two local unions through the Cambodian Labour Union. [Para 20][Para 82][Para 83] Two points settled in conciliation on 31 July 2019, and four more were withdrawn at the hearing of 19 August 2019, leaving issue 2 (immediate signing of negotiation minutes) and issue 6 (compensation for two dismissed baggage handlers). [Para 21][Para 22][Para 26][Para 27] One of the two, Mr Y, accepted at the hearing the compensation the employer had offered; the other, Mr K, had worked from 1 October 2013 and was dismissed with immediate effect on 30 April 2019 for serious misconduct. [Para 94][Para 97]

## 2. Issues in dispute
2. Whether the employer must record and sign the minutes of every negotiation meeting immediately on the meeting ending? [Para 3][Para 84]
6. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr K and Mr Y in their former posts with back pay from dismissal to reinstatement, and pay them termination compensation? [Para 12][Para 94][Para 95]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Delegates need the minutes immediately to report results to members, and cannot spend working time reviewing and tracking later revisions; the minutes as translated and reported to the foreign managers were altered from the original content. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 89]
**Employer (respondent):** Minutes must be drafted, translated and referred to the foreign managers, which takes 3 to 4 days; signing at once creates future problems, the union has been given office space and equipment, and delegates have never come to discuss errors in the minutes but return them late. [Para 90][Para 91][Para 92][Para 93]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim for immediate signing of minutes was dismissed, and the claim for reinstatement was withdrawn, but the employer was ordered to pay Mr K two months' pay in lieu of notice, unused annual leave, seniority indemnity calculated on 6 years of service, and the withheld final month's wage; moral damages were refused. [Para 202][Para 203][Para 204]

**Issue 2.** The panel applied articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code: a contract arises from mutual consent, and neither party may impose on the other a form, type or content to which it has not agreed. [Para 115][Para 118][Para 123][Para 124][Para 127] Minutes of a meeting are themselves a form of contract requiring the parties' agreement, and the employer had clearly refused this term, so the workers could not compel it. [Para 130][Para 132][Para 133][Para 134][Para 135]

**Issue 6.** The employer relied on article 83 of the Labour Law and clause 19.3(ង) of its internal regulations, which treats "repeated misconduct" (កំហុសច្រំដែល) as serious. [Para 136][Para 137][Para 138] The panel found that phrase undefined — neither the regulation nor the employer explained what level or how many repetitions were required — so the employer had not established serious misconduct and could not rely on that clause. [Para 141][Para 142][Para 143][Para 154] What the record did establish was absence without leave and without notice, a form of light misconduct under clause 19.3.1, which permits suspension without pay or termination after three such occurrences. [Para 145][Para 146][Para 151][Para 155] The employer had issued reminders, warnings and a one-week unpaid suspension in December 2018, and dismissed Mr K after a further absence on 23 April 2019, so the panel held the dismissal rested on a valid reason under article 74 of the Labour Law. [Para 152][Para 153][Para 161]

Because the employer terminated with immediate effect and gave no written notice, article 75 and article 77 entitled Mr K — with 5 years 7 months' service, in the 5-to-10-year band — to two months' pay in lieu of notice. [Para 162][Para 167][Para 168] Articles 166 and 167 gave him payment for unused annual leave. [Para 174][Para 176][Para 178] Article 89 (as amended) and Prakas No. 443 K.B/Br.K.Kh.L of 21 September 2018 gave seniority indemnity of 15 days per year, applied to 6 years of service, i.e. 90 days, with tips and bonuses included in the calculation base under article 110. [Para 181][Para 185][Para 190][Para 191][Para 193][Para 196] Article 116 required the withheld final month's wage to be paid. [Para 179][Para 180][Para 201] Moral damages under article 91 were refused because the dismissal had a valid reason. [Para 197][Para 199][Para 200][Para 204]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A dismissal is unfair in its manner, and attracts pay in lieu of notice under articles 75 and 77, where the employer ends an indefinite-duration contract with immediate effect without written notice, even though the misconduct established is only the light misconduct that its own internal regulations make actionable after three repetitions — and an employer that invokes a "repeated misconduct" clause without defining the threshold fails to prove serious misconduct under article 83.

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observation that minutes of a negotiation meeting are a species of contract, so that neither side may dictate their form, was broader than the outcome required, which turned on the employer's refusal of that particular term. [Para 130][Para 134]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The caption at [Para 1] is badly corrupted, the company name printed as "មែនី ជ្ជមែន" and the venue garbled. [Para 1] The record of withdrawals at [Para 26] is fragmentary and does not name the four withdrawn points. [Para 26] The claim heading at [Para 136] reads "សោហ៊ុយ ជំងឺ កូវីដ ចិត្ត" (Covid), an artefact not present in the operative text. [Para 136] Service is 5 years 7 months at [Para 99] but "6 years" in the calculation and orders. [Para 99][Para 190][Para 196][Para 203] The seniority calculation at [Para 190] runs 7.5 days for the first year then 15 days per year, yet arrives at 90 days for 6 years. [Para 187][Para 188][Para 190] The final order at [Para 204] is syntactically broken, placing the refusal of moral damages beside an order to pay it. [Para 204]
