# Award No. 099/19 — Arin Menuve K Chering (Khmer) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 18 December 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kol Vuthana appointed by the employer party, An Nan appointed by the worker party, Run Saray chair) · Doc ID 140823

## 1. Facts
Arin Menuve K Chering (Khmer) Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អរិន មេនូហ្វេ ក ឆឺរីង (ខ្មែរ) ខូអិលធីឌី), a garment factory at Dan Phleng village, Kamchay Mear district, Prey Veng, employed 1,899 workers. [Para 53] On 14 October 2019 the provincial Labour Department received a collective complaint raising 16 demands on working conditions, and conciliation failed on 24 October 2019 because the employer did not attend. [Para 23][Para 24] At the hearing the workers withdrew issues 2, 9, 14 and 15, agreed issues 4, 5, 7, 8 and 12, leaving issues 1, 3, 6, 10, 13 and 16 for decision. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 34]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay four hours' wages to the workers who came to work on 23 September 2019 before they struck? [Para 54][Para 84]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay a shortfall of 5 per cent in severance because annual-leave compensation was excluded from the calculation base? [Para 61][Para 103]
3. (Issue 6) Whether the 90 days of maternity leave must be counted so as to exclude public holidays and weekly rest days? [Para 66][Para 113]
4. (Issue 10) Whether fixed-duration contracts whose cumulative duration reaches two years must be converted into unlimited-duration contracts? [Para 70][Para 119]
5. (Issue 16) Whether the employer must sign an unlimited-duration contract with each worker after the probation contract ends? [Para 82][Para 173]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, ភាគី តំណាង កម្មករ និយោជិត):** about 500 workers came in from 7 to 9 a.m. on 23 September 2019, struck because the employer refused to swap the Pchum Ben holiday, and say a manager promised orally that four hours' wages would be paid if they signed the monthly fingerprint sheet, which they did. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 58][Para 59] Severance was short by 5 per cent because annual-leave compensation was left out of the base, and maternity leave should run 90 days clear of holidays and weekly rest. [Para 64][Para 68] Contracts held for more than two years should become unlimited-duration ones. [Para 70]
**Employer (respondent, ក្រុមហ៊ុន អរិន មេនូហ្វេ ក ឆឺរីង (ខ្មែរ) ខូអិលធីឌី):** the workers worked only two hours and only two hours' wages are owed; paying annual-leave compensation at every renewal already exceeds the law; maternity leave is counted on the calendar as company practice; and Prakas No. 050/19 was not yet being applied, the company waiting until the end of 2019. [Para 60][Para 65][Para 67][Para 73]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to convert the fixed-duration contracts of workers whose cumulative contract period has reached two years into unlimited-duration contracts, and dismissed every other claim, the order being non-binding. [Para 179][Para 176][Para 177][Para 182]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 332 of the Labour Law, under which the employment contract is suspended during a strike and wages are not paid, following award No. 49/05; the workers admitted they had not observed the procedure required by Article 324, which demands seven days' notice. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 90][Para 92][Para 93] They had worked only from 7 to 9 a.m., so no right to wages for the hours from 9 to 11 a.m. arose, and the alleged oral promise was unsupported by any evidence beyond their own statements. [Para 96][Para 101] The claim was dismissed. [Para 102]

On issue 3 the panel read Article 167(2) and (3) as making annual-leave compensation payable when the employment relationship ends, and as voiding any agreement to forgo it; an employer therefore cannot be required to fold that compensation into the severance base while employment continues. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 108][Para 110] The 5 per cent claim was dismissed. [Para 111][Para 112]

On issue 6 the panel noted that Article 182 grants 90 days of maternity leave without saying whether they are calendar or working days, and followed award No. 23/08 (IM & V1) holding that where the law is silent the count is calendar days; the employer's practice was therefore lawful and the claim dismissed. [Para 113][Para 114][Para 115][Para 117][Para 118]

On issue 10 the panel applied Article 67(2), which caps a fixed-duration contract at two years while allowing renewal, and Article 73(5), under which a contract becomes unlimited-duration once total duration exceeds the Article 67 limit; it followed award No. 10/03 (Zacsintek) and ordered conversion. [Para 119][Para 120][Para 121][Para 122][Para 123][Para 133]

Issue 13, a claim for an incentive bonus of at least USD 10 per month for the colour-dyeing section, was not considered at all: an award on an interest dispute replaces a collective agreement and binds every worker in the enterprise, so only the most representative union may bring such a claim, and this claimant was not that union. [Para 145][Para 147][Para 149][Para 151][Para 152]

On issue 16 the panel held that a contract arises from consent and that neither the other party nor the panel may impose a contract form on an unwilling party, citing Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code and awards No. 170/11 and No. 115/10. [Para 155][Para 168][Para 170][Para 175]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-duration contract that is renewed so that the total duration of the successive contracts exceeds two years becomes an unlimited-duration contract by operation of Article 67(2) and Article 73(5) of the Labour Law, and the panel will order the employer to re-classify it accordingly. [Para 121][Para 122][Para 133]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the Labour Law favours unlimited-duration contracts because they give workers job security, and that this also benefits employers by encouraging commitment, are general observations on legislative policy. [Para 124][Para 125]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The panel records that the workers withdrew "5" demands but then names only four, issues 2, 9, 14 and 15, so the identity of the fifth is unrecoverable. [Para 32]
- The figure for the severance shortfall is corrupted in the summary of the employer's evidence, printed as "5000 per cent" where 5 per cent is meant. [Para 62]
- Issue numbering is broken by OCR at several points: issue 13 is printed as "1 3" and issue 16 as "16" with a stray figure 335 inserted mid-sentence. [Para 134][Para 154]
- The conciliation paragraph is badly garbled, with an interleaved date reading "24 month of Ashadh" and a parenthetical about 35 days, so the second conciliation date cannot be read with confidence. [Para 24]
- A worker's name appears in full in the record at two points; that individual is identified here by role only. [Para 72][Para 132]
