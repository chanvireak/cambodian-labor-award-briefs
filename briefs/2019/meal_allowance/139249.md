# Award No. 092/19 — Bery Er Herel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 7 November 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Liv Sovannara for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 139249

## 1. Facts
Bery Er Herel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. is a garment-cutting and sewing enterprise at Chom Chao, Phnom Penh, employing about 3,000 workers. [Para 62][Para 63] On 10 September 2019 twenty workers petitioned the Labour Disputes Department over eight working-condition demands; conciliation settled one point and the seven remaining points were referred to the Council on 1 October 2019. [Para 23][Para 25] At the hearing the parties settled points 5 and 6, leaving points 2, 3, 4 and 7 for the panel. [Para 29][Para 30] The panel fixed the claimant group at 406 workers after the employer challenged the thumbprints. [Para 113][Para 115][Para 116]

## 2. Issues in dispute
2. Whether the employer must pay the lunch meal allowance once a week to the workers? [Para 117]
3. Whether the employer must add the position allowance (ប្រាក់រង្វាន់តួនាទី) to the base wage as the basis for calculating overtime pay for section heads, team leaders and assistants? [Para 142]
4. Whether the employer must place one hot-water tank in each building for the workers? [Para 161]
7. Whether the employer must pay piece-rate workers who earn exactly the base wage an amount above that base? [Para 171]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** wages are paid twice a month, so the lunch and overtime meal allowances should also be paid weekly, because workers depend on that money for daily food and send their wages home to the provinces; the position allowance is a fixed monthly entitlement and must enter the overtime base; the only hot-water tank is in the infirmary, too far for pregnant and sick workers who cannot leave the line; reassignment to an unfamiliar station cuts their piece-rate output. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 82][Para 90][Para 92][Para 100]

**Employer (respondent):** a 2018 agreement with ten unions fixed wage payment at twice a month, and the meal allowances follow the same cycle; the position allowance varies month to month and cannot serve as a stable overtime base; a hot-water tank on the floor would be used to cook noodles during working hours; supervisors reassign workers only to cover absences and keep the line moving, a right conferred by clause 1.5 of the internal rules and clause 8 of the employment contract. [Para 76][Para 85][Para 94][Para 104][Para 106]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim for weekly lunch allowance was dismissed, the hot-water and piece-rate top-up claims were dismissed, and the employer was ordered to pay the overtime meal allowance once a week and to add the position allowance to the base wage when calculating overtime for section heads, team leaders and assistants. [Para 183][Para 184][Para 185][Para 186][Para 187]

On point 2 the panel read Article 2 of the Labour Law as conferring a managerial right that must be exercised lawfully and reasonably, and found no article or legal norm requiring the lunch allowance to be paid weekly, so the two-week cycle stayed within that right and the claim was dismissed. [Para 118][Para 121][Para 123][Para 124] The overtime meal allowance was treated differently: Notice No. 041/11 entitles a worker who volunteers for overtime to 2,000 riel a day or one free meal, without limiting the number of overtime hours, and the panel held the payment must fall in a period that actually lets the worker buy food for the overtime shift — hence weekly. [Para 126][Para 129][Para 130][Para 141]

On point 3 the panel noted that Article 139 (new) of the Labour Law and Article 5 of Prakas No. 80 of 1 March 1999 fix the 150% and 200% rates but never define the wage on which they are computed. [Para 143][Para 146][Para 149] Following Award No. 74/08 (Generation) it held "wage during normal working time" means the wage the worker regularly and foreseeably receives each month, not a sum contingent on output or on the company's monthly target; since supervisors still exercise their skill and responsibility during overtime, the position allowance forms part of that wage and must be included. [Para 151][Para 153][Para 155][Para 156][Para 159]

On point 4 the panel applied Article 1 of Prakas No. 054, which requires the employer to supply sufficient hygienic drinking water but says nothing of hot water, and followed Award No. 173/14 (Ison Kable Cambodia) in finding no legal basis for the demand; the existing tank at the infirmary, available to all workers, reinforced the conclusion. [Para 162][Para 164][Para 167][Para 168]

On point 7 the panel applied Article 2 of Prakas No. 465 of 5 October 2018: a piece-rate worker is paid by actual output, and the employer must top up only where output falls below the minimum wage (USD 177 for probationary and USD 182 for full-rights workers); the employer was found to be applying that rule already and no provision requires more than the base where output equals it. [Para 171][Para 173][Para 180][Para 181]

The employer-appointed arbitrator, Ing Sothy, dissented on point 3 only, holding that including the position allowance has no legal basis and rests on equity, and that equity would equally require deducting the allowance when the worker is absent. [Para 190][Para 194][Para 195]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an earlier award of the Council has interpreted "wage during normal working time" to mean the wage regularly and foreseeably received each month, a position or skill allowance paid every month enters the base for overtime calculation for supervisory staff, notwithstanding the silence of Article 139 (new) and Prakas No. 80; by contrast, the interval at which a meal allowance is disbursed is an exercise of the employer's managerial right under Article 2 unless a legal norm or the worker's ability to buy food for the overtime shift requires a shorter cycle. [Para 118][Para 130][Para 149][Para 153][Para 159]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the infirmary hot-water tank is available to workers, and the dissenting arbitrator's remarks on symmetry between adding the allowance for overtime and deducting it for absence, were not necessary to the orders. [Para 168][Para 195]
