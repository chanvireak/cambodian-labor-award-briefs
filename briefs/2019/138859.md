# Award No. 019/19 — Khvan Tum Apparel (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 2 April 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phalla presiding; In Sothy nominated by the employer, An Non nominated by the workers; In Sothy's dissenting opinion annexed) · Doc ID 138859

## 1. Facts
Khvan Tum Apparel (Cambodia) Ltd, at Toul Pongro, Chom Chao, Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh, cuts and sews garments and employs about 1,700 workers. [Para 1][Para 36] Four workers filed a complaint on 16 January 2019, received on 21 January 2019, making five demands; conciliation on 27 February 2019 settled three and left two, which the panel took up at the hearing of 18 March 2019. [Para 11][Para 12][Para 19][Para 10] The parties elected a non-binding award. [Para 21] Both remaining demands concern the wage base used for statutory payments, not the amounts actually paid. [Para 41][Para 50]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must compute the wage for days taken as paid annual leave on the average wage (មធ្យមភាគ), instead of base wage divided by the number of working days in each month? [Para 41][Para 74]
2. Whether the employer must add the seniority bonus (ប្រាក់រង្វាន់អតីតភាពការងារ) to the base wage as the basis for calculating overtime pay? [Para 50][Para 82]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** annual leave is earned by a full year's work, overtime included, so paying leave days on base wage divided by working days is unfair; the average gives balance between days worked and days rested. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 42] The seniority bonus rewards a year's effort and loyalty, so it belongs in the base used for overtime. [Para 53][Para 54]
**Employer (respondent):** it cannot grant the demands; its practice has always been to use base wage alone for annual leave pay, for the payout of untaken leave and on termination. [Para 45][Para 46] Overtime is calculated under inspectorate letter No. 202 permitting overtime, whose annexed table uses base wage as the basis. [Para 55][Para 56]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer is ordered to divide the total wage the worker received in the last 12 months by 12 to obtain the average monthly wage and then by 26 to obtain the average daily wage, and to use that figure as the basis of annual leave pay; and to add the seniority bonus to base wage as the basis for computing overtime pay. [Para 105][Para 106] The award is non-binding and becomes final 8 (eight) days after notification if no party objects to the Minister of Labour. [Para 107]
On issue 1 the panel read Article 166 of the Labour Law of 1997 as giving every worker paid annual leave, and Article 168 as requiring pay for leave days of at least the average of the actual wage and benefits, in cash and in kind, over the twelve months before the leave. [Para 74][Para 76] It followed its earlier awards 27/04, 23/08, 25/08, 94/07, 57/10, 07/13 and 226/13 in holding that the average is found by dividing the total of the last twelve months by 12 and then by 26. [Para 78][Para 79] Dividing base wage by the working days of each month, as the employer does, was held not to match the spirit of Article 168. [Para 80]
On issue 2 the panel found that Article 139 (new) of the Labour Law, as amended on 20 July 2007, fixes the 50% and 100% overtime rates but not the wage on which they are computed, and that Article 5 of Prakas No. 80 of 1 March 1999 is equally silent. [Para 82][Para 84][Para 85][Para 87] Adopting the reading in award 74/08 (Generation), it treated "wage in normal working time" as the wage a worker regularly receives on ordinary working days, part of the package expected each month. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 92] Because the seniority bonus is paid for service, experience and loyalty, and because 74/08 already required the skill or position allowance to be added to base wage, the panel held the seniority bonus must likewise be added, following 255/13 (Poo Yien). [Para 95][Para 97][Para 101] The employer had used base wage alone. [Para 102]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Pay for days of annual leave is measured by the average of the wages actually received in the twelve months before the leave — total divided by 12, then by 26 — and not by the base wage divided by the working days of the month, even where the employer has long applied the latter to leave pay, untaken-leave payout and termination. [Para 78][Para 80][Para 81] For overtime under Article 139 (new), "wage in normal working time" includes the seniority bonus, because that bonus forms part of the wage a worker regularly receives, so overtime must be computed on base wage plus seniority bonus. [Para 101][Para 103][Para 106]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel conceded that the 74/08 ruling concerned the skill or position allowance rather than the seniority bonus, and extended it by analogy of content and purpose. [Para 100] Arbitrator In Sothy, dissenting under Article 37 of Prakas No. 099, accepted the averaging formula only for payments under Article 167 (second paragraph) and Article 168, and said that adding the seniority bonus to the overtime base has no legal foundation and rests on equity alone. [Para 113][Para 119][Para 120]
