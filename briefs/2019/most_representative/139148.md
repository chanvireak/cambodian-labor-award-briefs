# Award No. 075/19 — Kuwahara (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 12 September 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Yu Soun Ty, appointed by the employer, dissenting in part; An Non, appointed by the workers; Aing Eng Thong, chair) · Doc ID 139148

## 1. Facts
The company carries on goods quality inspection and employs 170 workers. [Para 52][Para 53] There is no most-representative union at the company, so four workers were designated as the workers' representatives by labour dispute department letter No. 010/2019 of 24 July 2019. [Para 79][Para 81][Para 84] Of three demands, one was settled at conciliation on 06 August 2019, leaving two: a USD 10 regular attendance bonus payable without any overtime condition, and full wages when the employer has no work and sends workers home. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 54][Para 69]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the USD 10 monthly regular attendance (work) bonus without tying it to an overtime condition, and must not deduct it pro rata for days off? [Para 54][Para 69]
2. Whether the employer must pay 100% of wages when it has no work for the workers to do? [Para 130]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** They ask for a USD 10 monthly incentive on top of wages, a USD 10 regular attendance bonus not conditional on overtime, and pro-rata deduction of the bonus according to days off; they say they cannot see the USD 10 incentive inside their base wage, and ask the employer to prove it was included. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 60][Para 61] On the second issue, even where the employer lets them rest at home, full wages should still be paid. [Para 138]

**Employer (respondent):** The USD 10 incentive is paid to workers who work 8 hours a day or 26 days a month without absence and has been folded into the monthly wage since January 2013; a further USD 10 is paid only to those who voluntarily help with overtime, and those who do not help at least twice a month do not get it. [Para 4][Para 5][Para 62] Where work is short, workers rest at home on 70% pay and receive 100% if they return when called. [Para 7][Para 70][Para 73]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — all three limbs of the bonus claim were refused; the employer was ordered to pay 100% of wages whenever it has no work for the workers. [Para 142][Para 143][Para 144][Para 145]

On standing, the panel found no most-representative union at the company and applied Article 16 of Prakas No. 303 (02 July 2018), under which a collective dispute is handled by workers' representatives where no most-representative union exists; the four designated representatives accordingly had standing. [Para 79][Para 81][Para 82][Para 86]

Before the merits of issue 1 the panel addressed res judicata, following Award No. 42/07 — Samdach Be and setting three conditions: the same parties previously took part on the point, the same issue was brought to the panel, and the panel decided it fully. [Para 89][Para 92][Para 93] It found the first condition unmet because the claimant union here differs from the claimant union in case No. 336/15 — Kuva Hara (Cambodia) Ltd on its third issue, so it was not bound and went on to decide the claim. [Para 97][Para 98][Para 99][Para 101]

On the incentive, the payroll records for April and May 2013 showed base wages rising from USD 95 to USD 105, so the USD 10 had been incorporated; that limb was refused. [Para 110][Para 111][Para 112][Para 114][Para 115] On the attendance bonus, Notice No. 230 (25 July 2012) requiring at least USD 10 a month for full attendance applies only to the garment, textile and footwear sector, and this company falls outside it, as held in case No. 188/14 — Kuva Hara; under Article 2 of the Labour Law the employer may manage the company as long as it acts lawfully and reasonably, so the overtime condition was lawful; the pro-rata deduction claim fell with it. [Para 119][Para 120][Para 123][Para 124][Para 125][Para 129]

On issue 2 the panel applied Article 664 of the Civil Code 2007, under which the worker owes labour and the employer owes wages as the price of that labour, and followed case No. 065/15 — Kip Thap Sport Thing Goods, which ordered back pay where the employer's own failure made work impossible. [Para 130][Para 133][Para 135] Since the employer itself authorised the workers to stay home and wait for work, the failure to work was attributable to the employer and 100% wages were due. [Para 139][Para 140][Para 141]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the employer itself directs workers to stay at home because it has no work, the resulting absence is attributable to the employer and full wages remain payable; the employer cannot shift that cost onto the workers by paying a reduced percentage. [Para 139][Para 141] A statutory notice fixing a minimum attendance bonus for the garment, textile and footwear sector does not bind a company outside that sector, and such a company may attach conditions to its bonus under its lawful management right. [Para 120][Para 124]

**Obiter dicta:** The employer-appointed arbitrator, Yu Soun Ty, filed a dissent on issue 1, reasoning that the first res judicata condition was satisfied because the respondent company was the same party as in case No. 336/15, and that the incentive claim should therefore have been refused as already decided. [Para 151][Para 152][Para 157][Para 158]

[Para 116][Para 143] Para 108 contains unrelated OCR text about attracting the Khmer citizen party, and Para 45 and Para 43 repeat lines verbatim. [Para 43][Para 45][Para 108] Two workers named in the payroll extract at Paras 111 and 113 are treated as individuals and are not named here.
