# Award No. 002/19 — Prestige Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 12 February 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Kul Vaddhana (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Pen Bunhea (chair) · Doc ID 138871

## 1. Facts
Prestige Garment Co., Ltd (ប្រេស្ទីច ហ្គាម៉ិន ឯ.ក) is a garment-cutting and sewing enterprise in Kandal province employing 443 workers [Para 74]. On 2 January 2019 the Kandal provincial department of labour received a 14-point claim from its workers on working conditions; five points were conciliated, and the five that remained were referred to the Council on 3 January 2019 [Para 18][Para 20]. The only point the panel still had to decide concerned Mr S, a sewing worker engaged on 24 May 2018 on a one-month probation followed by rolling three-month fixed-duration contracts, who was elected president of the factory's workers' movement union on 4 December 2018 [Para 77][Para 98]. The employer ended his last contract, which was due to expire on 24 December 2018, on 22 December 2018 [Para 130][Para 138].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take Mr S, the factory's union president, back into work? [Para 2]
2. Whether the employer must stop deducting pay when workers take a half-day of leave, and stop deducting the meal allowance? [Para 4]
3. Whether the employer must grant necessary leave and keep wages and other benefits unchanged during it? [Para 6]
4. Whether the employer must change the contract term from 2 months to 6 months? [Para 8]
5. Whether the employer must remove the administrative staff from their posts immediately? [Para 10]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr S was an elected union leader and so enjoyed protection from dismissal; the employer watched him and built a case against him, told him he would have to leave the union before his contract would be renewed, and he had committed no fault [Para 85][Para 86].
**Employer (respondent):** this was the non-renewal of a fixed-duration contract, notice had been given and wages were paid through to 24 December 2018, and the contract was ended early only to avoid trouble on his last day [Para 89][Para 90]. It said he was not re-engaged because he disobeyed his supervisor, sewed garments with missing labels and was rated monthly, and that it did not know he was a union leader [Para 92].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted as to the one contested issue — the employer was ordered to take Mr S back into work at his original workplace; the other four issues were recorded as settled by agreement between the parties. The award is non-binding (សេចក្តីបង្គាប់ មិនជាប់កាតព្វកិច្ច) [Para 179][Para 33].

**Standing.** Article 54(1) of the Trade Union Law 2016 reserves exclusive representational rights to the most-representative union, and the panel found none existed in this enterprise; under Article 16 of Prakas 303 and Article 1 of Prakas 362, workers' representatives certified by the provincial labour department may conduct collective dispute resolution where no such union exists. Five of the six certified representatives appeared, so the claim was properly before the panel [Para 117][Para 127].

**Dismissal or non-renewal.** Article 73(1) of the Labour Law provides that a fixed-duration contract expires at its term, and earlier awards treat renewal as a matter for the parties' consent. Following Award 096/16 (Tak Sam Anter Bright), the panel held that ending such a contract before its expiry date is a unilateral dismissal, not a non-renewal [Para 131][Para 134]. Although notice was given and wages were calculated to 24 December 2018, the employment was actually ended on 22 December 2018, before the term — so this was a dismissal [Para 139].

**Protected status.** Articles 43 and 67 of the Trade Union Law protect union leaders and candidates from dismissal without the labour inspector's prior consent [Para 141][Para 143]. Applying the three conditions set out in Award 169/16 (Moning Glory Garment), the panel found: Mr S was elected union president on 4 December 2018; the notice of the leadership composition was handed to the administration on 8 December 2018 and refused, a fact the employer's own administration chief conceded at the hearing; and the termination of 22 December 2018 fell inside the protection window [Para 148][Para 157][Para 160].

**Authorisation.** No prior authorisation from the labour inspector was shown, contrary to Articles 43 and 67 [Para 174]. Article 34 of Prakas 099 empowers the panel to order reinstatement and immediate payment, and prior awards have ordered reinstatement where the employer failed to seek the inspector's permission [Para 175].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer ends a fixed-duration contract before its expiry date, the act is a dismissal rather than a non-renewal, even though notice was given and wages were paid through to the expiry date; and a worker who has been elected a union leader and is dismissed inside the statutory 45-day protection window without the labour inspector's prior authorisation must be reinstated in the original position [Para 139][Para 148][Para 174].

**Obiter dicta:** workers' representatives certified by the provincial labour department may bring a collective dispute where no most-representative union exists, and the requirement that the union's notice be copied to the ministry does not add a further hurdle where notice has already been given to the employer by any reliable means [Para 122][Para 150].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The notice terminating Mr S's contract is dated 17 December 2018 in the employer's evidence list, but the panel's reasoning gives 18 December 2018 as the date notice was given; both are reported as printed [Para 44][Para 130]. The number of union committee members announced in the notice appears as 4, as 11 and as 20 in different passages [Para 98][Para 157][Para 161]. The employer's commerce registration number is printed as 00034969 in the findings and as MOC-12140719 in the evidence list, both dated 1 August 2018 [Para 42][Para 73]. The chair's name appears as ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា on the face of the award and ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈាន at the signature block [Para 1][Para 181]. Several figures are corrupted: one hearing invitation is dated "97 January 2019", the eight-day period for objection is garbled, and a stray "133" sits mid-sentence in the third-condition finding [Para 72][Para 161][Para 180].
