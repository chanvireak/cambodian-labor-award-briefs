# Award No. 050/19 — Quint Major Industrial Co., Ltd (QMI) (Arbitration Council, 26 July 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Seng Vouch Hun (employer-appointed), An Virak (worker-appointed), Nhan Somunninth (chair) · Doc ID 139100

## 1. Facts
Quint Major Industrial Co., Ltd (QMI) is a garment factory at Trapeang Chheu Neang, Ang Snuol, Kandal, which put its workforce at 378 and accepted that no union there is most representative [Para 35][Para 36][Para 39]. Six workers were certified by the Kandal provincial labour department as the workers' representatives in the collective dispute (letter No. 016/19 of 25 June 2019) [Para 32][Para 83]. From 2006 until 22 June 2019 the company deducted from the mechanics' good-work bonus one dollar per day of absence; on 22 June 2019 it issued a notice setting much heavier deductions, effective 24 June 2019, and added the sewing-team leaders to the committee that scores mechanics for that bonus [Para 41][Para 55][Para 56][Para 57]. Three points remained unsettled after conciliation [Para 11][Para 18].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must remove the sewing-team leader (ប្រធាន ក្រុម ដេរ) from the process of scoring and awarding the good-work bonus to mechanics? [Para 88]
2. Whether the employer must continue to apply the old rules on deducting that bonus, as practised in the past? [Para 101]
3. Whether the employer must pay a technical skill allowance (ប្រាក់ ជំនាញ បច្ចេកទេស) to the mechanics? [Para 124]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the team leaders have no mechanic's skill and do not understand machine repair; they had quarrels with the mechanics' team in 2013–2014, so the mechanics fear biased scoring [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47]. The new deduction scale was imposed by the employer's will alone, without their agreement, and cuts the bonus they used to receive; at a meeting the head of the mechanics' section had agreed to keep the old rule [Para 58][Para 59].
**Employer (respondent):** the bonus is not regulated by law, so how it is scored and paid is the employer's prerogative; the change followed a restructuring after consultation, because the old scheme was irregular and some mechanics took too much leave, disrupting production [Para 52][Para 54][Para 60][Para 61]. On the allowance it says base wages have already been raised each year under the Labour Law and the claim has no legal basis [Para 68][Para 69].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — point 1 was dismissed, the employer was ordered to continue applying the old good-work bonus deduction rules, and point 3 was dismissed without examination as an interest dispute [Para 149][Para 150][Para 151].

On point 1 the panel read Article 2 of the Labour Law as conferring on the employer the right to organise and manage the enterprise, provided the act is lawful and reasonable [Para 88][Para 89][Para 94]. It accepted that the employer had a scoring table with proper criteria, that the sewing teams are the direct users of the machines and can tell whether a repair worked, and that the employer consulted the workers before the change [Para 95][Para 54]. Against that, the workers showed only a general worry that scoring by team leaders might be unfair, without any concrete fact showing the arrangement was unlawful or unreasonable [Para 97][Para 98]. The panel therefore upheld the employer's right to include the team leaders [Para 99].

On point 2 the panel applied the doctrine of past practice (ការអនុវត្ត កន្លងមក), which it defined as a practice long, consistently and clearly followed and accepted by both parties [Para 106][Para 108][Para 109]. It found the facts admitted: from 2006 to 22 June 2019 the employer deducted one dollar per absent day up to five days and the whole bonus at six [Para 115], a period of about thirteen years, regular and acknowledged by both sides [Para 116][Para 117]. A past practice can end only by agreement of the parties, by a lawful act to terminate it, or because the circumstances that founded it have changed [Para 112]. Here the employer acted by its unilateral will, and the new scale deducted more than the practice it replaced [Para 119][Para 120][Para 122]. The panel therefore ordered the old rules to continue [Para 123][Para 150].

On point 3 the panel found no statute, collective agreement, internal regulation or past practice creating a duty to pay a skill allowance, so the claim was "more favourable than the law", i.e. an interest dispute [Para 125][Para 126][Para 127]. Because an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement and binds all workers for one year [Para 140][Para 142], only a union that is most representative, or able to conclude a collective agreement under Articles 54 and 72 of the Law on Trade Unions, may bring one [Para 139][Para 143]. The claimants were neither, so the claim was not considered [Para 146][Para 147].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may not unilaterally worsen a bonus-deduction scheme that has been applied regularly, clearly and with the acceptance of both parties for many years, even where the bonus itself is discretionary and no statute regulates it: a past practice of that kind can be ended only by agreement, by a lawful terminating act, or because its founding circumstances have changed, and an award will order the old scheme restored. Separately, a challenge to the composition of a scoring committee for a discretionary bonus fails where the employer shows proper criteria and the workers show only a general apprehension of bias, since Article 2 of the Labour Law leaves the organisation of the enterprise to the employer so long as the act is lawful and reasonable. [Para 89][Para 108][Para 112][Para 122]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's general remark that an employer may create bonus schemes outside those the Labour Law requires, in order to encourage productivity, is broader than the order needed. [Para 91]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The number of mechanics claimed is 76 in one place and 77 in another [Para 38][Para 40], and the panel's summary states the dispute was considered for "20" mechanics where the record elsewhere gives 76 or 77 — the figure is unusable as printed [Para 87][Para 86].
- The commercial registration certificate is numbered MOC-71613557 at [Para 26] and 00014938 at [Para 35], both dated 11 May 2006.
- The new deduction notice is dated 22 June 2019 at [Para 3] and [Para 55] but appears with no day at all at [Para 57]; the admitted practice period ends "22 June 2023 2019" at [Para 115].
- The company's name is transliterated at least five different ways across the face of the award, including "ឃ្វីន ធ៏ មេ ដើរ", "ឃ្វិន ធំ មេ ដើរ" and "ឃ្វីន ធ៍ មេជើរ" [Para 1][Para 24][Para 30]; the catalogue title "Quint Major Industrial Co., Ltd (QMI)" is used in this brief.
- Paragraph [Para 24] prints the internal regulations twice in one sentence, with visa No. 003/07 dated 10 May 2007, which is a decade before the events in issue.
- The award is dated 26 July 2019 [Para 1] although the parties extended the deadline to 29 July 2019 [Para 21].
