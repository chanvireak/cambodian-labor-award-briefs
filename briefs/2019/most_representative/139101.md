# Award No. 051/19 — W&D (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 26 July 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Sin Kim Sean (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chea (chair) · Doc ID 139101

## 1. Facts
W&D (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a garment manufacturer on Street 217, Stung Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, with 1,862 workers and no union that is most representative [Para 37][Para 38][Para 41]. On 9 May 2019, 59 workers attended an Arbitration Council hearing in case No. 037/19 after a valid invitation; the employer deducted half a day's wage (USD 3.5) and the regular attendance bonus (USD 5) from each, USD 8.5 in all [Para 42][Para 87]. Six of them were the workers' representatives certified by the Labour Dispute Department [Para 39][Para 43]. The parties agreed at the hearing to merge the two claims into one — repayment for that day, and preservation of wages for representatives attending hearings in future [Para 16][Para 17]. The parties chose a binding award [Para 18].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must repay the half-day wage (USD 3.5) and regular attendance bonus (USD 5), USD 8.5 in total per worker, to the 59 workers who attended the Arbitration Council hearing of 9 May 2019, and must in future refrain from deducting wages, bonus and other benefits from worker representatives attending conciliation or arbitration on a valid invitation? [Para 74][Para 83]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** they attended only to explain the grounds of their claim to the panel; the representatives had gone to the administration office at 11:00 on 9 May 2019 to ask permission, were told to clock in and wait until 13:00 or 13:30 to complete the form, and did not do so for fear of arriving late at the hearing [Para 45][Para 47][Para 48]. Workers outside the office were told the company would not let them leave for the hearing [Para 49].
**Employer (respondent):** the workers breached the internal regulations by leaving during the lunch break without completing administrative formalities or obtaining permission; in principle a worker needs a valid invitation and the employer's authorisation, and the employer may also consider whether attendance disrupts the production line [Para 50][Para 51][Para 52].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay USD 8.5 per worker to the six workers' representatives who attended the hearing of 9 May 2019; the claim for the remaining 53 workers was dismissed [Para 104][Para 105][Para 106][Para 107].

The panel first confirmed that the six representatives were properly recognised: where an enterprise has no most-representative union, Prakas No. 303 Article 16 leaves collective disputes to worker representatives, and Article 1 of Prakas No. 362 gives the Labour Dispute Department and provincial labour offices power to recognise them, including for proceedings before the Council [Para 61][Para 63][Para 65][Para 72]. It then applied Article 306 of the Labour Law and Articles 14 and 20 of Prakas No. 099: parties must attend every meeting to which the conciliator or arbitrator invites them, and during conciliation and hearing the employer must not deduct the representatives' wages, bonus or other benefits [Para 74][Para 78][Para 83][Para 86]. Both sides admitted the 59 workers attended on a genuine invitation and that the employer deducted USD 3.5 in wages and USD 5 in bonus from each [Para 87]. The panel held that deduction, as applied to the representatives, contrary to the Labour Law and the Ministry's Prakas, and ordered repayment [Para 90][Para 91].

For the other 53 the panel reasoned differently. Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 lets a party appear in person or through a representative holding written authority [Para 92][Para 93], and where workers have transferred authority to six representatives there is no need for each affected worker to establish the facts personally [Para 98][Para 101]. It also found that attendance of the whole of team 4 would disrupt the production chain [Para 102]. The claim of the 53 was therefore dismissed [Para 105][Para 107].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may not deduct wages, the regular attendance bonus or other benefits from recognised workers' representatives for time spent attending conciliation or an Arbitration Council hearing to which they were validly invited: Article 306 of the Labour Law and Articles 14 and 20 of Prakas No. 099 require attendance and forbid the deduction, and the amounts are repayable. The protection attaches to the recognised representatives only. Workers who have transferred authority to those representatives are not required to appear in person, so a deduction from non-representative workers who attended in numbers is not recoverable. [Para 83][Para 86][Para 91][Para 101]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that attendance of a whole production team may disturb the production chain is an ancillary remark supporting the refusal of the wider claim, not a rule about the right to attend. [Para 102]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The panel's finding on the representatives reads "កម្មករ និយោជិត ចំនួន ៦០០ នាក់" (600 workers) where six representatives are plainly meant [Para 88]; elsewhere the six are correct [Para 91].
- The 53 non-representatives are described as "10 members of team 4" in the same sentence [Para 95], and the team's size is given as 61 while the claimants are 59 [Para 46][Para 42].
- The representatives' recognition letter is dated 17 June 2019 in the evidence list but 17 July 2019 in the findings [Para 33][Para 39][Para 70].
- The commercial registration certificate is numbered MOC 5854396 at [Para 22] and 00013028 at [Para 37], both dated 19 November 1997.
- The procedural recital at [Para 10] is badly corrupted ("and @-@ 525 … ចុះ ថ្ងៃទី ៤ ខែមិថុនា ២០១៩"), so the filing date of the complaint cannot be read from it; the conciliation report date survives at [Para 7].
- The company name is transliterated inconsistently across the face of the award ("ដាប់ ប៊ីល យូ អែន ដ៏ ឌី", "ដាប់ ប៊ិល យូ អ៊ែនដ៍ ឌី", "ដាប់ ប៊ីល យូ អែន ដ៍ ឌី"); the catalogue title "W&D (Cambodia) Co., Ltd" is used here [Para 1][Para 26][Para 37].
