# Award No. 047/19 — Hoeng Jin Textile Factory (Arbitration Council, 18 July 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ri, employer-appointed; Tuon Si Phan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, presiding) · Doc ID 139064

## 1. Facts
Twenty workers of Hoeng Jin Textile Factory (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ៊ឹង ជីង ថេក ស្ថាយ ហ្វេក ធើរី), a garment employer of about 100 staff, lodged a collective claim on 29 May 2019 asking the company to improve nine conditions of work. Ministry conciliation failed and the unresolved points were referred to the Council on 17 June 2019; the hearing was held on 27 June 2019. [Para 17][Para 14][Para 49]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must let workers carry unused annual leave into the following year and pay it out in cash where leave remains? [Para 53]
2. Whether the employer must pay wages twice a month as Prakas requires and stop withholding USD 50 from the first payment when a worker takes one or two days' leave? [Para 63]
3. Whether the employer must stop setting output quotas beyond workers' capacity and stop reprimanding workers who miss the quota? [Para 71]
4. Whether the employer must complete wage payment within working hours, by 16:00? [Para 77]
5. Whether the employer must disclose the position of SLJ factory workers who have been working inside the Hoeng Jin premises since 19 April 2019? [Para 84]
6. Whether the employer must reinstate a doctor on site? [Para 86]
7. Whether the employer must install fans in every group and section? [Para 91]
8. Whether the employer must pay the outstanding 50 per cent so that 13 workers receive 100 per cent of wages for an improperly suspended period? [Para 94]

Point 5 of the original nine — that leave requests need only a group leader's approval — was withdrawn by the workers at the hearing. [Para 23]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company forfeits leave that is not taken by 31 December, cutting Mr C's five remaining 2018 days; the USD 50 withheld from the first monthly payment deprives them of daily living costs and contradicts the two-payment Prakas; quotas are set above what a worker can sew and administration summons and reprimands those who fall short; wages are paid as late as 18:00 and the delay is a device to extract extra hours; the company suspended work without notifying the labour inspector, contrary to Articles 71 and 72. [Para 57][Para 67][Para 73][Para 80][Para 95]
**Employer (respondent):** leave must be used up by 31 December, a practice applied for three years, with borrowing allowed in special cases; the USD 50 is withheld because leave requests are hard to administer and is credited back at the second payment; there is no reprimand, only guidance to the few workers who listen to music, use phones or chat; wages are in principle paid by 16:00 and the 10 March delay was a cash-flow problem notified in advance; the 50 per cent payment followed a meeting with workers and group leaders because there were no orders. [Para 58][Para 69][Para 75][Para 82][Para 96]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to give Mr C five days of 2018 annual leave to use in 2019; to stop the USD 50 deduction at the first payment and deduct only actual leave days taken; to pay wages by 16:00; to engage one nurse on site plus one doctor present at least two hours per eight-hour shift; and to pay the additional 50 per cent so that the 13 named workers receive 100 per cent of wages for 23 March 2019 to 1 April 2019 as set out in the annex. The claims on reprimands, SLJ workers and fans were dismissed. [Para 206][Para 207][Para 208][Para 209][Para 211][Para 212][Para 213]

On standing, the panel first held that only a most-representative union has exclusive rights in collective dispute resolution under Article 54(1) of the Trade Union Law 2016, found no most-representative union at the company, and held that the three workers certified by the Labour Disputes Department under Prakas No. 362 were therefore entitled to represent the claimants. [Para 99][Para 104][Para 115]

Annual leave: Article 167(4) makes leave non-waivable, permits postponement only of days exceeding twelve working days a year, and caps the carry-over at three consecutive years. Following Awards 27/04 and 14/10, the panel found the company's blanket forfeiture contrary to Article 167 and ordered the five days carried forward. [Para 117][Para 123][Para 126][Para 129]

Wage deduction: Articles 126 to 128 prohibit deductions save in the listed cases, and Award 21/03 reads them as barring any deduction that gives the worker no specific benefit. The panel found the USD 50 withholding unlawful, but held that leave authorised by the employer suspends the contract under Article 71(6) and Article 72(1), so wages may be withheld for days actually taken. [Para 131][Para 140][Para 151][Para 152]

Reprimands: the workers offered no evidence of the words used or of any effect on their dignity, so the claim failed. [Para 155][Para 157]

Payment timing: Article 115 forbids paying on a rest day and Article 137 caps the working day; payment on 10 March 2019 ran to 18:00, so the practice was unlawful, and the panel ordered payment completed by 16:00. [Para 158][Para 165][Para 166]

SLJ workers: no loss or damage was claimed or shown, and the SLJ workers sewed on separate lines under separate administration, so the claim had no legal basis. [Para 167][Para 169]

Medical staff: Article 239 and Prakas No. 330 of 6 December 2000 set staffing by headcount; with about 100 workers and no doctor on site since the nurse's unpaid absence, the panel ordered one nurse and one doctor for two hours per shift. [Para 171][Para 177][Para 179]

Fans: Article 229 and Prakas No. 147 of 2002 require an acceptable thermal environment by any means; nine fans, water-coolers and exhaust fans were in place, the workers did not dispute the employer's account or quantify the heat, and labour inspection had never required more. [Para 183][Para 187][Para 190]

Suspension: Article 71(11) requires the suspension to be under labour-inspector supervision, which the panel read as prior notification and authorisation; the company suspended 13 workers from 23 March to 1 April 2019 on 50 per cent pay without any request to the inspector, so the full wage was owed. [Para 194][Para 197][Para 201][Para 204]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Annual leave that exceeds twelve working days in a year is not forfeited at year-end: the employer must let the worker carry the excess into the next year, for up to three consecutive years, and a practice of cancelling all unused leave is contrary to Article 167(4) of the Labour Law. [Para 125][Para 127]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel accepted the employer's cooling measures as adequate without fixing any temperature threshold, because the workers neither quantified the heat nor showed a health effect. [Para 189]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel's constitution date is printed twice on one line as "20 June 2021 2019" (២០ ខែមិថុនា ឆ្នាំ ២០២១ ២០១៩) and cannot be resolved from the text. [Para 20] The procedural summary says eight issues remain and then lists only seven (1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9), omitting issue 7, yet issue 7 is reasoned and granted later in the award. [Para 24][Para 171] Article 167(4) appears as "មាត្រា ១៦៧២" in two places. [Para 206] The discussion of issue 4 runs into the heading of issue 6 on a single line, and "15:80" appears where 18:00 is plainly meant. [Para 84] The annex listing the 13 workers is destroyed by OCR — names, identity numbers and day counts are unreadable — so the per-worker amounts cannot be recovered from this text. [Para 215][Para 219] Employee names of the respondent's representatives survive only partly (Ms O, Mr T).
