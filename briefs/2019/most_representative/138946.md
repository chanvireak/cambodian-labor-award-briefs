# Award No. 049/18 — Khembo Ying Hneng Garment Investment (Arbitration Council, 8 January 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chhea presiding; Seng Vouch Hun, employer nominee; An Non, worker nominee) · Doc ID 138946

## 1. Facts
Khembo Ying Hneng Garment Investment is a garment-cutting and sewing factory at Ang Keo, Chom Chao III, Porsenchey, Phnom Penh, which told the panel it employed 380 workers and which both parties accepted has no most-representative union. [Para 1][Para 30][Para 31][Para 34] From their first engagement the workers were put on six-month fixed-duration contracts, renewed each expiry without any 5% severance being paid; within the last year the employer converted all of them to undetermined-duration contracts and counted seniority from their first day. [Para 35][Para 37][Para 38][Para 44][Para 84] On 16 October 2018 the employer dismissed the section 7 sewing team leader (name redacted), who had 14 months' service, after he refused a transfer to ordinary sewing at the lower team-leader wage; no prior notice was given. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 49][Para 52][Para 109] Eight claims went to conciliation; two remained unsettled and were referred to the Council. [Para 10][Para 11][Para 12]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the 5% severance for each past six-month fixed-duration contract, from the workers' first day of service, where it was never paid on expiry? [Para 2][Para 35]
2. Whether the employer must reinstate the section 7 sewing team leader in that role, with the wages and benefits he previously received? [Para 3][Para 46][Para 50]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** each six-month contract expired and was replaced without the 5% severance ever being paid, so the arrears run from the first day of employment; the team leader was dismissed with no notice, told his team was being dissolved for lack of work — which never happened — and punished for refusing a downgrade that cut his team-leader wage and benefits. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 51][Para 52]
**Employer (respondent):** it stopped using six-month contracts years ago and converted every worker to an undetermined-duration contract, crediting seniority from their first day and preserving all benefits, and will pay the correct terminal benefits calculated on that basis; the team leader led his team poorly with lower output than other teams and was assessed as such by the section chief, and the employer will pay him all statutory severance. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43][Para 54][Para 55][Para 56]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim for 5% severance on the past six-month contracts was dismissed (issue 1); reinstatement was also refused, but the employer was ordered to pay the team leader 15 days' notice compensation, 15 days' dismissal damages, the untaken annual leave, moral damages under the new Article 91, and his final wages (issue 2). [Para 154][Para 155][Para 156]

*Representation.* No most-representative union existing, the panel recognised the six workers certified by the Department of Labour Disputes under Prakas 362, applying Article 54(1) of the Trade Union Law together with Prakas 303 of 2018. [Para 59][Para 62][Para 67][Para 72]

*Issue 1 (5% severance).* Article 73(6) and (7) require severance on termination, and where an undetermined-duration contract replaces a fixed-duration one, seniority is the sum of both periods. [Para 77][Para 78][Para 82] The panel found the employer had converted every contract and had already credited seniority from the first day, so the workers lost nothing and would receive benefits calculated on the undetermined-duration basis; no separate 5% was therefore owed for the earlier terms. [Para 83][Para 85][Para 86][Para 87]

*Issue 2 (dismissal).* Article 74 allows either party to terminate an undetermined-duration contract by written notice, but never without reasonable grounds relating to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the needs of the enterprise. [Para 88][Para 89][Para 90][Para 91] The employer asserted poor leadership and low output, yet filed no evaluation letter or other evidence by the evidence deadline, and the party making an assertion bears the burden of proving it. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 99][Para 100] The dismissal was thus made at the employer's sole will, without reasonable grounds and without prior notice, contrary to Article 74. [Para 101] Because the employer refused to take him back and the working relationship was no longer viable, the panel declined reinstatement and awarded compensation under Article 34 of Prakas 099. [Para 102][Para 104][Para 106][Para 107]

*Quantum.* His 14 months' service gave 15 days' notice compensation under Articles 75 and 77, and 15 days' dismissal damages under the old Article 89; the panel held that the 2018 amendment to Article 89, in force from 26 June 2018, could not be applied because Prakas 443 makes the new seniority indemnity run only from 2019, so a 2018 dismissal is governed by the 1997 text. [Para 110][Para 119][Para 121][Para 124][Para 125][Para 130] The new Article 91, by contrast, was in force when he was dismissed and grounded an award of moral damages. [Para 147][Para 149][Para 150] Untaken annual leave under Article 166 and final wages under Article 116 were also owed. [Para 141][Para 151][Para 153]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer has replaced fixed-duration contracts with undetermined-duration contracts and counted seniority continuously from the first day of service, no separate 5% severance is owed for the earlier fixed-duration terms, because the worker loses no benefit. [Para 82][Para 85][Para 87] Terminating an undetermined-duration contract requires written notice and reasonable grounds; an employer that alleges poor performance but files no supporting evidence fails to discharge its burden, and the dismissal is unlawful, entitling the worker to notice compensation, Article 89 damages, leave pay, final wages and moral damages under Article 91. [Para 91][Para 97][Para 101][Para 150] The 2018 seniority-indemnity amendment applies only from 2019 under Prakas 443, so dismissals effected in 2018 are compensated under the 1997 Article 89. [Para 119][Para 124]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel accepted the employer's position that it would not reinstate the worker, treating the breakdown of the relationship rather than the employer's preference as the reason to award compensation instead. [Para 102][Para 104]
