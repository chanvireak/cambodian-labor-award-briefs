# Award No. 041/19 — Bou Heur Garment Factory (Cambodia) Com Peni Ltd (Arbitration Council, 19 June 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry, chosen by the employer; An Vira, chosen by the workers; Kong Phallak, chair) · Doc ID 138846

## 1. Facts
Bou Heur Garment Factory (បូវ ឃើរ ហ្គាមេន ហ្វេក ធើរី), a garment plant at Ang Snuol, Kandal with 4,600 workers, has no union holding most-representative status; four workers were recognised by the Kandal provincial Labour Department on 21 May 2019 as the employee representatives, three of whom appeared at the hearing. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 49][Para 81] The employer organised one-day annual excursions for office staff, supervisors, section heads and group leaders in May of 2014, 2016, 2017 and 2018 when the workers asked for one, but not in 2015, citing financial difficulty. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 96] The workers requested a 2019 trip by letter of 24 April 2019; the employer refused and proposed August instead. [Para 53][Para 54] The disputed act is that refusal; conciliation failed on 16 May 2019 and the non-conciliation report of 20 May 2019 reached the Secretariat on 21 May 2019. [Para 8][Para 12][Para 11]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must organise an annual excursion for 2019 and continue it every year for office staff, supervisors (supervisor), section heads and group leaders? [Para 50][Para 83]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The trip has been an almost unbroken habit since October 2014, missing only 2015, and it is not tied to the company's profit or loss; it builds solidarity and good relations between employer and workers, and the 2015 gap came from the company's own request to hold it every second year for financial reasons. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58]
**Employer (respondent):** It cannot afford a 2019 trip because costs have risen — it already pays above the minimum wage — and the company is making a loss and spending heavily in 2019; a trip is possible only when the company is profitable, and in any case the benefit is unequal because production-line workers do not receive it. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61][Para 62]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the workers' claim that the employer organise an annual excursion for 2019 for office staff, supervisors, section heads and group leaders is rejected. [Para 107][Para 108]
Before the merits the panel addressed standing, since no most-representative union exists at the factory: Article 54(1) of the Law on Trade Unions of 17 May 2016 reserves the exclusive right to conclude a collective agreement or resolve a collective dispute to the most representative union, and Article 16 of Prakas No. 303 of 02 July 2018 provides that where no such union exists the dispute is handled by worker representatives. [Para 66][Para 72] Following its earlier awards (180/16, 171/16, 223/16, 224/16, 008/17, 237/16) and Article 1 of Prakas No. 362 of 29 August 2016, it held that workers recognised by the Labour Department may represent the workforce, and that the three who appeared validly did so. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 78][Para 81] On the merits the panel found the annual trip is fixed in no legal instrument — not the Labour Law, internal rules, collective agreement, employment contract or any agreement. [Para 83] It then applied the past-practice doctrine (ការអនុវត្ត កន្លងមក), grounded in Article 65(2) and (3) of the Labour Law, which requires conduct repeated regularly over time, clear and stable, and recognised and accepted by both sides. [Para 86][Para 90][Para 92] The 2014, 2016, 2017 and 2018 trips were arranged each May only after the representatives asked, and in 2015 the employer refused and deferred; the panel therefore held the trips were not a past practice binding on the employer but a fresh contract or agreement made year by year. [Para 96][Para 98][Para 99] Applying Articles 336 and 337 of the Civil Code, which require an offer and a matching acceptance, it found no offer and acceptance coincided here, so no obligation arose for 2019. [Para 100][Para 104][Para 105] It added that workers remain free to request a trip in later years, on which the employer will decide according to actual circumstances. [Para 106]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A benefit granted year by year only on request, and refused or deferred in at least one of those years, is not a "past practice" binding the employer: past practice requires regular, clear and stable conduct accepted by both parties, and in its absence each year's grant is a separate agreement needing a matching offer and acceptance, so refusal in the current year breaches nothing. [Para 92][Para 99][Para 104]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remark that the benefit should also reach production-line workers, and that the trip is an incentive not written into the internal rules, played no part in the dismissal. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 64]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award survives only in Khmer and is heavily corrupted. The signature block loses the worker-chosen arbitrator and the chair entirely and renders the employer-chosen arbitrator as "ហ្វេសអ៊ុក រី", so the composition is reconstructed from the header. [Para 1][Para 110] The company name is spelled three ways (បូវ ឃើរ, ប៊ូវ ឃើរ, ខម ភេនី / ខម កេនី) and its Latin rendering here is a transliteration, not a printed form. [Para 8][Para 46][Para 82] One exhibit is impossible on its face: the union's letter of 06 June 2019 objects to company documents dated 30 June 2019, three weeks after the letter. [Para 38] Threshold dates are duplicated in the original ("២០១៩ ២០១៩", "នៅ ថ្ងៃទី ២៤ ខែមេសា ឆ្នាំ ២០១៩" twice) and the hearing date is given in both the Buddhist era (ថ្ងៃព្រហស្បតិ៍ ១២រោច ខែពិសាខ ឆ្នាំកុរ ឯកស័ក ព.ស ២៥៦៣) and the Gregorian (30 May 2019). [Para 10][Para 13][Para 53] The letter inviting the employer is cut off mid-word ("ចូ"). [Para 45] No monetary amount is at stake; the only figures printed are production targets of 62,000 PCS per day and 15.5 million units for 2019. [Para 4][Para 5]
