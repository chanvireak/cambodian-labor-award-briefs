# Award No. 011/19 — A Plas A Su (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 28 February 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Chhiv Phirom (chosen by the employer), An Non (chosen by the workers), Roun Saray (chair) · Doc ID 138864

## 1. Facts
A Plas A Su (Cambodia) Ltd manufactures footwear and shoe components at Choam Chao, Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh, with about 1,450 workers. On 26 December 2018 the workers lodged four claims on working conditions; conciliation on 30 January 2019 left two unresolved, and the panel was constituted on 7 February 2019. The dispute concerns the wage base used to compute overtime and a demand for a childcare subsidy in place of a daycare centre. Three unions are registered in the enterprise but none holds most-representative status. The parties chose a binding award (សេចក្តីបង្គាប់ជាប់កាតព្វកិច្ច). [Para 12][Para 13][Para 20][Para 46][Para 47][Para 50]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must use base wage plus seniority bonus and position allowance as the basis for computing overtime pay? [Para 91]
2. Whether the employer must pay a childcare allowance of USD 30 per month in place of building a daycare centre? [Para 130]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The law never defines "basic monthly wage" (ប្រាក់មូលដ្ឋានប្រចាំខែ) clearly, and the term should be read as base wage plus seniority bonus plus position allowance, so that all three are pooled for overtime; computing overtime on base wage alone gives workers too little. Seniority bonus is USD 2 per month after one year with USD 1 added each following year, and position allowance runs from USD 3 to USD 20 for team leaders. The existing USD 15 milk allowance is no substitute for the USD 30 claimed, since no daycare centre exists. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54][Para 55][Para 67]

**Employer (respondent):** The Ministry of Labour's method for computing overtime does not add seniority bonus or position allowance, and the employer follows the annex table attached to its overtime authorisation from the labour inspectorate, which requires the basic monthly wage as the basis. It cannot build a daycare centre: the factory opened in June 2016, has no adequate space, and already pays USD 15 per month to women with children aged 18 months to 3 years under a notice of 1 February 2018. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 66][Para 70][Para 73]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer is ordered to use base wage plus seniority bonus plus position allowance as the basis for computing overtime pay; the claim for a USD 30 per month childcare subsidy is declined. The award became final immediately under the parties' agreement of 13 February 2019. [Para 155][Para 156][Para 157]

**Issue 1.** Article 139 (new) of the Labour Law sets the 50% and 100% overtime rates but does not define the wage on which they are computed, and Prakas No. 80 of 1 March 1999 article 5 leaves "wage at normal time" undefined. Reading Article 102 (wage as consideration for work) against Article 103 (which lists the components of wage, including bonuses), the panel held that "wage at normal time" means the pure wage under Article 102, excluding other benefits. It then asked whether seniority bonus and position allowance nonetheless form part of that normal-time wage. Seniority bonus, under point 3 of Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011, rewards experience, effort and loyalty and is paid monthly; position or skill allowance is paid in exchange for carrying out an assigned role, following Award No. 74/08 Generation and Awards No. 78/13 and No. 107/13. Both are therefore additions to the normal-time wage, and the employer's practice of using base wage alone was wrong. [Para 92][Para 94][Para 97][Para 104][Para 106][Para 108][Para 121][Para 128]

**Issue 2.** Article 186 requires an enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a nursing room and a daycare centre, and where it cannot, the employer bears the cost of a place elsewhere. The panel found no daycare centre on site and a USD 15 per month milk allowance paid instead. It found no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice obliging USD 30 per month, and classified the claim as an interest dispute. Because Article 54 and Article 72 of the Law on Trade Unions and article 43 of Prakas No. 099 confine interest-dispute standing to a most-representative union — an award on such a claim replacing the collective agreement for all workers — and the claimants were not such a union, the panel declined to consider the claim, following Awards No. 118/16 and No. 016/18. [Para 130][Para 132][Para 134][Para 135][Para 149][Para 150][Para 152][Para 153]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
For the purpose of computing overtime under Article 139 of the Labour Law, "wage at normal time" includes the seniority bonus and the position or skill allowance, not the base wage alone, because both are paid monthly as consideration for the worker's accumulated experience and for carrying out an assigned role. Conversely, a claim for a new benefit in substitution for a statutory duty is an interest dispute that the panel will not decide unless brought by a most-representative union. [Para 128][Para 150][Para 152]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the term "wage" in Article 103 is broader than in Article 102, covering the worker's whole monthly income including benefits other than pure wage; and that the Article 186 daycare duty is triggered only where the enterprise employs at least 100 women. [Para 103][Para 130]
