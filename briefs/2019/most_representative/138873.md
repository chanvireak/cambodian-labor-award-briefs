# Award No. 040/19 — Hayashida Ye Pan Agri Call Chair (Arbitration Council, 20 June 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Yu Sounty (employer-appointed, dissenting), Tuon Si Phan (worker-appointed), Pen Bunhea (chair) · Doc ID 138873

## 1. Facts
The respondent is a Japanese-owned agricultural enterprise in Chamkarmon, Phnom Penh, employing 7 workers [Para 57]. On 18 March 2019 five workers signed a complaint to the labour dispute department; four of them had already resigned during 2018, leaving Ms N as the only worker still employed, and she was dismissed that same day [Para 65][Para 97][Para 141]. Ms N had been engaged orally on 1 August 2018 with no written contract and no probation period, and was dismissed on 18 March 2019 without prior notice and without her final month's wage [Para 94][Para 95][Para 96].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay overtime wages to the five named workers for the period from their engagement to the end of their contracts? [Para 4]
2. Whether the employer must pay Ms N termination compensation — moral damages, final wage, notice pay, seniority indemnity and unused annual leave — and pay unused annual leave to the four workers who resigned? [Para 6][Para 93]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer loaded them with work that required them to stay beyond normal hours, requiring attendance from 4:00 or 5:00 in the morning and, for Ms N, ten to twelve hours a day, and never paid overtime [Para 72][Para 73][Para 79].
**Employer (respondent):** it never required overtime; Ms N had agreed a flat USD 750 a month that already included any overtime, and the workers who stayed late did so voluntarily to finish tasks [Para 80][Para 82][Para 89]. It also argued the dispute was an individual dispute, not a collective one, and defended the dismissal on grounds of serious misconduct — insolent gestures and strong language toward the Japanese director, insubordination and poor relations with colleagues [Para 61][Para 108].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the overtime claim was dismissed outright; on the second issue the employer was ordered to pay Ms N 15 days' pay in lieu of notice, unused annual leave, seniority indemnity of 14.5 days (ដប់ បួន ថ្ងៃ កន្លះ), moral damages under Article 91 (new) equal to the seniority indemnity, and her unpaid final wage, and to pay unused annual leave to the four former workers [Para 279][Para 280][Para 281].

**Jurisdiction.** Article 302 of the Labour Law defines a collective dispute, and following Award 10/03 (Zacksintic) the panel tested its three conditions. Five workers had signed the original complaint, the subject matter concerned working conditions and employer-worker relations including dismissals and resignations, and unresolved it could disrupt the enterprise; all three conditions were met, so the dispute was collective and within jurisdiction [Para 143][Para 147][Para 151].

**Standing.** No most-representative union existed in the enterprise, and the labour dispute department's letter 005/2019 recognised Ms N as the workers' representative, which sufficed under Article 16 of Prakas 303 and Article 1 of Prakas 362 [Para 58][Para 60][Para 166].

**Overtime.** Normal hours were 8:00 to 17:00, six days a week, a fact both parties accepted [Para 70]. Article 137 caps normal hours at eight a day and forty-eight a week, and Article 139 (new) requires a 50% premium for overtime [Para 168][Para 169]. The party asserting bears the burden of proof; the workers produced no evidence that overtime was required or that refusal would be disciplined, and the employer's two witnesses said the extra hours were voluntary and occasional [Para 177][Para 182][Para 184]. The panel therefore found the late hours were workers finishing their own remaining tasks, not overtime demanded by the company, and dismissed the claim [Para 186][Para 188].

**Contract type and dismissal.** Under Article 67(7) an unwritten fixed-duration contract becomes one of indefinite duration, so Ms N's oral engagement produced an indefinite-duration contract [Para 189][Para 193]. Article 74 permits termination at either party's will but never without a valid reason, and Article 83 lists serious misconduct [Para 196][Para 199]. The panel found the conduct described in the dismissal notice did not reach that threshold: the employer's own witness conceded Ms N used no abusive words and had no conflict with colleagues, so the dismissal lacked a valid reason under Articles 74 and 83 [Para 208][Para 210][Para 211].

**Entitlements.** With seniority of 7 months and 18 days, notice pay was 15 days under Articles 75 and 77 [Para 215][Para 217]. Seniority indemnity was split as Prakas 443 requires: 7.5 days for 2018 service and 7 days for 2019 service, totalling 14.5 days [Para 249][Para 251][Para 255]. Moral damages under Article 91 (new) were fixed at the amount of the seniority indemnity [Para 261]. As the last month's wage had not been paid, Article 116 required payment within 48 hours of the cessation of work [Para 262][Para 264]. For the four former workers, Articles 166, 167 and 120 preserved the right to unpaid annual leave even on resignation and within a three-year limitation [Para 272][Para 277].

**Dissent.** Arbitrator Yu Sounty, appointed by the employer, agreed with the annual leave, the 14.5 days of seniority indemnity and the final wage, but dissented from the orders for 15 days' notice pay, severance and moral damages, holding that Ms N's conduct amounted to serious misconduct under Article 83 and clause 50 of the internal rules so that those entitlements did not arise [Para 287][Para 288][Para 298].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer terminates an employee for serious misconduct, it must prove conduct actually falling within Article 83 or the internal rules, and general complaints of insolence, insubordination and difficult relations that its own witnesses do not support cannot justify dismissal; absent such proof the dismissal is one without valid reason and the notice pay, seniority indemnity and moral damages follow [Para 210][Para 211][Para 261]. Separately, a worker who resigns remains entitled to payment for unused annual leave within the three-year limitation of Article 120 [Para 272][Para 277].

**Obiter dicta:** hours worked beyond the normal schedule that the employer neither required nor enforced by discipline, and that the workers chose to work in order to finish their own tasks, are not overtime giving rise to a statutory premium [Para 184][Para 186].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Ms N's start date is printed as 1 August 2018 in the findings and as 12 August 2018 where the panel recites what both parties acknowledged; the seniority computation appears to follow the former [Para 94][Para 214][Para 215]. The four former workers' names are unstable across the document — "រុន ភារ៉ា" also appears as "រ៉ុន ការ៉ា" and "ស្រ៊ុន ដារ៉ា" as "សៀន ដារ៉ា", and the operative order prints an additional garbled name [Para 97][Para 102][Para 279]. The signature block is heavily corrupted, with English OCR debris in place of the employer-appointed arbitrator's name and the chair printed as ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈាម against ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា on the face of the award [Para 283]. Stray numerals interrupt the text at the notice-pay order and in the seniority findings [Para 218][Para 248].
