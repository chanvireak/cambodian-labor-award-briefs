# Award No. 049/19 — Ken Park (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 July 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phallak presiding; Ing Sothy, employer nominee; An Non, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139095

## 1. Facts
Ken Park (Cambodia) Co., Ltd manufactures garments for export in Ang Snuol district, Kandal province. [Para 59] Workers raised 16 demands; mediation left 5 unresolved, and the workers merged points 2 and 4 into one, so the panel considered 4. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 25][Para 26] The Khmer Workers' Rights Protection Union at the company was registered only on 31 May 2019, and the panel found no most-representative union in the enterprise; two workers were recognised as worker representatives by provincial labour department letter No. 015/19 of 25 June 2019. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 63] The employer had reassigned both representatives to the packing section on 3 June 2019, suspended their contracts on 20 June 2019 and asked the labour inspector to rule on their alleged serious misconduct. [Para 74][Para 76][Para 177]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must conclude one-year fixed-duration employment contracts instead of the three-month contracts it now uses? [Para 2][Para 64][Para 114]
2. Whether the employer must take back Mr P and Ms T, the two worker representatives, into employment? [Para 9][Para 11][Para 162]
3. Whether the employer must pay an overtime meal allowance of 5,000 riel for overtime worked from 16:00 to 20:00, paid weekly every Saturday? [Para 6][Para 69][Para 138]
4. Whether the employer must pay an additional USD 65 in wages that were short-paid on 22 June 2019? [Para 96][Para 186]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** three-month contracts expose workers to dismissal and discrimination, so one-year terms are needed; the daily meal allowance should be 5,000 riel and paid every Saturday because workers must buy food each overtime day; the two representatives did no wrong, the internal rules were not yet in place, and their transfer to packing was union discrimination after the union applied for registration. [Para 66][Para 71][Para 72][Para 84][Para 85][Para 86]
**Employer (respondent):** contract terms are freely agreed and the law is being followed; it has already applied the legal minimum on meal money and cannot afford 5,000 riel; the two workers were suspended, not dismissed, pending the labour inspector's decision, and they committed serious misconduct under Article 83(b)(3) by refusing a lawful reassignment. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 73][Para 87][Para 88]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim for a one-year contract, the reinstatement claim, the 5,000 riel meal rate and the USD 65 wage claim were dismissed or refused consideration; the panel ordered the employer to pay the overtime meal allowance once a week, every Saturday. [Para 215][Para 216][Para 217][Para 218]

On issue 1 the panel held that the form and duration of a contract rest on the parties' consent: Article 65 of the Labour Law and Articles 664, 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code mean neither party can force a contract term on the other, and the practice in the company is three-month fixed-duration contracts. [Para 114][Para 116][Para 126][Para 134][Para 136] It dismissed the claim, leaving both sides free to agree otherwise. [Para 137][Para 138]

On issues 2 and 4 the panel declined jurisdiction rather than deciding. Article 43 of the Trade Union Law requires the labour inspector's authorisation before a union leader or candidate may be dismissed or transferred, gives the inspector one month to decide, and allows two months to appeal to the Minister. [Para 162][Para 163][Para 164] The employer had merely suspended the two contracts on 20 June 2019 and the inspector had not yet decided, nor had the one-month period expired, so the matter lay within the inspector's competence and was not final. [Para 177][Para 178][Para 179][Para 182][Para 183][Para 184] The panel therefore refused to consider the reinstatement claim. [Para 185]

On issue 3 the panel split the demand. The 5,000 riel rate had no legal basis: Article 2 of Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 requires 2,000 riel per day of overtime or a free meal, the employer actually paid 4,000 riel per day, and the practice of four hours of overtime itself contradicted point 1 of Notification No. 030/15 of 27 February 2015, which caps overtime at two hours a day and the working day at ten hours. [Para 138][Para 139][Para 140][Para 142][Para 143][Para 144] The timing claim succeeded: because the meal allowance is meant to let workers buy food on each overtime day, the employer must pay it at an interval that makes that possible, and the panel ordered weekly payment every Saturday. [Para 148][Para 156][Para 157][Para 159][Para 160]

On issue 5 the panel held that wages follow work. Under Article 664 of the Civil Code the employer owes wages in return for labour, so where workers did not work the employer owes nothing; the exception is where the failure to work flows from the employer's own unlawful act. [Para 186][Para 188][Para 189][Para 194] The reassignment to packing was lawful: it involved no wage cut, no day-to-night shift change and no move to a distant site, packing requires no special skill, and the workers produced no evidence that their contracts specified a section. [Para 198][Para 200][Para 203][Para 209][Para 211] The workers therefore refused a lawful order and were not entitled to the USD 65 claimed. [Para 212][Para 213][Para 214]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a demand concerns the form or duration of an employment contract, the panel will not order it: consent is the basis of contract, so neither the employer's three-month term nor the workers' one-year term can be imposed on the other, and only a term the parties agree binds them. [Para 136][Para 137] Where union leaders or candidates are suspended, transferred or dismissed, the panel will not decide the merits while the labour inspector's mandatory authorisation procedure under Article 43 of the Trade Union Law is still running, because that procedure has not produced a final decision. [Para 179][Para 182][Para 184] Where a meal allowance is payable for overtime, it must be paid at an interval short enough for the worker to buy food for the overtime days actually worked. [Para 156][Para 159]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that a four-hour daily overtime practice contradicted the two-hour cap in Notification No. 030/15 without making any order on it, and that packing work demands no particular skill so the transfer did not breach the third reassignment condition. [Para 140][Para 142][Para 203]
