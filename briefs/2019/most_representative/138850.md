# Award No. 026/19 — Na Jung Garmin Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 23 May 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chea presiding; Kol Vathana appointed by the employer; Tuon Si Phan appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 138850

## 1. Facts
Na Jung Garmin Co., Ltd (ណា ជុង ហ្គាម៉ិន) is a garment manufacturer at Trapeang Chrey, Kambol, Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh, registered on 4 January 2018 and employing about 240 workers at the date of the award. [Para 1][Para 50][Para 36] On 21 March 2019 the Labour Disputes Department received a complaint with 14 demands; conciliation on 22 March 2019 settled none, and ten were settled at the hearing of 11 April 2019, leaving issues 4, 10, 13 and 14. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 28][Para 29] No union with most-representative status existed at the company; six workers recognised by the Department represented the claimants. [Para 75][Para 51][Para 86]

## 2. Issues in dispute
4. Whether the employer must pay a midday meal allowance of 2,000 riel a day to the workers, the company having paid none? [Para 53][Para 88]
10. Whether the employer must send the workers for a medical examination (កាយសម្បទា) at the employer's expense? [Para 57][Para 111]
13. Whether the employer must reduce the regular attendance bonus in proportion to the days a worker has taken leave with the employer's permission? [Para 61][Para 125]
14. Whether the employer must pay full wages and other benefits for the days on which it has no work to give the workers? [Para 65][Para 172]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** goods have become dearer and workers cannot eat adequately, and other factories in the area do pay a lunch allowance; no worker has ever had a medical examination since starting work, though the company pays a USD 15 attendance bonus that it removes whenever annual, sick or special leave is taken. [Para 55][Para 58][Para 62] They also asked for full pay during work stoppages, saying the employer paid nothing during two- or three-day stoppages. [Para 66]
**Employer (respondent):** it cannot afford a lunch allowance and no law requires one; it requires a health certificate from new recruits before they start, but not from workers already employed; it could not answer the attendance-bonus and stoppage claims at the hearing and asked to consult its documents. [Para 56][Para 60][Para 63][Para 68]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 4 refused consideration; issue 10 the employer is ordered to allow workers who have not had a medical examination to attend one, with the employer bearing the fee; issue 13 the demand for a pro-rata reduction of the attendance bonus is refused, and the employer is instead ordered to pay the attendance bonus at 100 per cent whenever a worker is present on every working day, including days of statutory leave, contractual leave and legally compelled absence, unless it has a different policy or separate agreement; issue 14 refused consideration. [Para 178][Para 179][Para 180][Para 181] The award is non-binding and becomes final 8 (eight) days after notification if no party objects. [Para 182]
On issue 4 the panel found no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice requiring the allowance, so the demand was an interest dispute; following award 118/16 (E-Cheng) it declined to decide it because the claimants were individual workers and no most-representative union existed, since an award on such a dispute becomes a collective agreement binding all workers. [Para 88][Para 89][Para 105][Para 106][Para 108]
On issue 10 the panel applied Article 247(a) and (c) of the Labour Law with Articles 1, 3, 5 and 7 of Joint Prakas No. 09 SKA/94 of 19 January 1994: a worker must undergo a medical examination on first entering employment and the employer must bear that cost. [Para 111][Para 114][Para 117][Para 118] Both sides accepted that some serving workers had never been examined; the panel held a worker may attend the Department of Labour Medicine himself and need not be transported by the employer, but the fee remains the employer's. [Para 121][Para 123][Para 124]
On issue 13 the panel followed the Full Bench decision of 3 October 2017 on Notification No. 230 K.B/S.J.N of 25 July 2012, which distinguishes statutory leave, contractual leave and legally compelled absence: an attendance bonus must not be used to penalise a worker for exercising a legal right, and such leave counts as presence. [Para 125][Para 129][Para 132][Para 139][Para 143] The employer did not deny that it removed the bonus for all such leave, so the practice was held wrong; but the workers' own demand, that the bonus be cut pro rata, had no legal basis, so it was refused and the 100 per cent order substituted. [Para 167][Para 168][Para 169][Para 171]
On issue 14 the panel found no identified claimant and no specific dates on which wages were withheld, and, following award 181/14 (Syoung Yin), held it could not rule without them. [Para 172][Para 173]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer must bear the cost of the medical examination required on a worker's entry into employment under Article 247 of the Labour Law and Joint Prakas No. 09 SKA/94, and the worker may attend on his own without being transported by the employer. [Para 118][Para 123] A regular attendance bonus must be paid in full where the worker's absence was statutory leave, contractual leave or absence compelled by law, because a bonus may not be used to penalise the exercise of a legal right; a demand that the bonus instead be reduced pro rata for authorised leave has no legal basis. [Para 143][Para 169][Para 171] The panel will not decide a claim for unpaid wages where the claimants and the dates are not identified. [Para 173]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the workers may take at least 18 days a year of annual and special leave to deal with personal matters, and that leave beyond the legal, contractual and compelled categories may be granted at the employer's discretion without any bonus. [Para 162][Para 164]
