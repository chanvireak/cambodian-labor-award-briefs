# Award No. 054/19 — Nouv Khob (Arbitration Council, 30 July 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ri for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Kong Phalla chair) · Doc ID 139104

## 1. Facts
Nouv Khob, a garment factory at Cheung Ek village, Ang Snuol district, Kandal province, suspended work for two months from 26 April to 26 June 2019 without paying wages, giving only USD 12 a month for travel and lodging. [Para 45] On 4 July 2019 it closed its gates and then paid lump sums of between USD 320 and USD 90 per worker to 1,363 of its 1,379 workers; 16 workers refused the sum and brought this claim. [Para 46][Para 47] The employer did not attend the hearing but filed documents. [Para 56]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay termination compensation to the 16 workers — payment in lieu of notice, seniority indemnity and back seniority for years before 2019, moral damages, payment in lieu of unused annual leave, and final wages — after suspending work without wages and then closing the factory on 4 July 2019? [Para 95]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the 16 hold indefinite-duration contracts; the employer terminated them by its own will on 4 July 2019, the day it shut the gates, without notice and without valid reason, and has paid neither 2019 seniority, nor back seniority for earlier years, nor leave, nor wages for 26 June to 4 July 2019. [Para 50][Para 52]
**Employer (respondent):** absent and offered no explanation; it filed a 7 July 2019 agreement on ending the contracts and paying compensation, but that document bears neither the names nor the thumbprints of the 16 claimants. [Para 57]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay all five heads of termination compensation to the 16 workers on each worker's actual seniority, plus final wages for 26 June to 4 July 2019. [Para 154]

*Procedure.* The panel held it could proceed in the employer's absence: the invitation was properly served and delivered by telephone and Telegram, the secretariat telephoned again on the hearing day, and no valid reason was given, so Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 permitted a hearing in absentia. [Para 65][Para 73] The four worker representatives recognised by the Kandal provincial labour department could represent the claimants, since no most-representative union existed at the factory. [Para 92]

*Termination.* Article 87 (new) of the Labour Law provides that cessation of an enterprise's activity does not release the employer from the obligations in section 3 save in force majeure, and that moral damages and payment in lieu of notice do not apply to a closure effected under the conditions set by ministerial Prakas. [Para 100] The panel treated the conduct here — two suspensions, gates shut from 4 July 2019, and lump-sum settlements with every worker but the 16 — as a cessation of enterprise activity. [Para 105] It nonetheless held the employer not released, because there was no evidence the closure met the Prakas conditions; and since the act was made without notice or reasonable grounds, Article 74 made it a unilateral termination at the employer's will. [Para 106][Para 113]

*Quantum.* Payment in lieu of notice was ordered under Articles 75 and 77 by actual seniority, on an average monthly wage taken from total wages including overtime and bonuses over the last 12 months. [Para 121] Seniority indemnity was ordered under Article 89 (new) at 15 days per year, with back seniority for pre-2019 service under Prakas No. 443 of 21 September 2018 — 7.5 days for a first year of one to six months, 15 days where service exceeded six months, capped at six months of net wages. [Para 143] Unused annual leave was compensated under Articles 166 and 167 at 1.5 working days per month of service. [Para 161] Moral damages were awarded under Article 91 (new) equal to the seniority indemnity, capped at six months' wages by Instruction No. 057/19 of 10 June 2019. [Para 170] Final wages were ordered under Article 116. [Para 175]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer suspends work without wages and then shuts the factory without notice, the panel treats the act as a unilateral termination at the employer's will under Article 74 even while also characterising the conduct as cessation of enterprise activity under Article 87 (new): the employer is not released from termination obligations unless it proves the closure satisfied the ministerial Prakas conditions, and that burden rests on the employer, not the workers. [Para 106][Para 113]

**Obiter dicta:** the discussion of representative standing under Article 54 of the Law on Trade Unions, Article 16 of Prakas No. 303 and Prakas No. 362 is a procedural threshold that does not touch the merits, and the remark that Prakas No. 443 leaves open how a 2019 termination is treated is not something the order depended on. [Para 92]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The employer is printed throughout as "ក្រុមហ៊ុនូវ ខប" (an OCR corruption of Nouv Khob), but the agreement quoted in the reasons names the company "NOW CORP". [Para 1]
- Workforce figures conflict: the conciliation report gives 632 workers, while the 9 July 2019 minute records lump sums paid to 1,363 of 1,379 workers. [Para 58]
- The worker-chosen arbitrator is "អន ណន" in the header but "អន លាន" in the signature block, and the chair appears as "គង់ ផល្លៈ" in the header and "គង់ ផល្ល" when signing. [Para 179]
- Letter No. 018/19 recognising the worker representatives is dated 5 July 2019 in the findings but "ថ្ងៃទី ៩ ២០១៩" — day 9 with the month missing — in the representation reasoning. [Para 90]
- Article 87 (new) is quoted as barring moral damages and notice pay on a closure, yet both were awarded because the closure did not meet the Prakas conditions; the panel does not reconcile the two passages. [Para 100][Para 106]
- The list of the 16 claimants is badly scrambled, with names, staff numbers, job titles and start dates interleaved; no total riel or dollar figure for the award is printed anywhere. [Para 64]
