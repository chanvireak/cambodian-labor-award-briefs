# Award No. 010/19 — Long Vich Tori International (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 18 February 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Eng Sothy (chosen by the employer), An Virak (chosen by the workers), Ang Eng Thong (chair) · Doc ID 138865

## 1. Facts
Long Vich Tori International (Cambodia) Ltd cuts and sews garments at Bak Touk, Tuol Sangkae, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, with 318 workers; the Khmer Youth Voice union is registered there but no union holds most-representative status. On 23 January 2019 the labour dispute department received a complaint from workers who had struck over four working-condition claims; conciliation on 24 January 2019 left one issue unresolved, and the panel was constituted on 29 January 2019. Both parties accept that the strike ran from 21 to 24 January 2019. The employer agreed at the hearing to pay daily wages for one strike day, 21 January 2019. The parties chose a non-binding award. [Para 8][Para 15][Para 31][Para 36][Para 38][Para 39]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must maintain daily wages and the regular attendance bonus for the strike days of 22 to 24 January 2019? [Para 66]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The strike was not the free choice of the workers. The employer failed to implement an agreement of 14 January 2019 and kept shipping goods and materials out of the factory, and on 21 January 2019 it called team and department heads to submit resignation letters, which made workers fear the company would shut down. Those acts were intimidation and the direct cause of the strike. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 42][Para 43]

**Employer (respondent):** It cannot pay daily wages or the regular attendance bonus for 22 to 24 January 2019 because it is applying the Labour Law; workers with grievances must use the legal procedure rather than simply strike. The company is still operating and has not closed. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the claim for daily wages and the regular attendance bonus for 22 to 24 January 2019 is refused. The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Minister of Labour. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 87]

**Wages.** Article 332(1) of the Labour Law suspends the employment contract during a strike, and no work is given and no wage paid; Article 72(1) confines suspension to the principal obligations of the contract. Following Award No. 27/10 Hat Interprise, the panel held that on any day a worker strikes, whether the strike is procedurally lawful or not, the employer need not pay wages or bonus. Article 334 forbids the employer from recruiting replacement workers during a strike and makes any breach of that rule oblige the employer to pay the strikers' wages. Here the panel found no evidence that the workers gave notice of the strike to the employer and no evidence that the employer hired anyone to replace them, so no duty to pay wages for 22 to 24 January 2019 arose. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 69][Para 72][Para 73][Para 76][Para 77]

**Attendance bonus.** Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 point 2 grants at least USD 10 per month to a worker present on every working day of the month without absence. The panel followed earlier awards (No. 04/03 Lida, No. 154/04 Lucky Fun, No. 07/05 Coca-Cola, No. 54/08 Chung Yow, No. 81/08 Kbal A Parrel, No. 204/12 Cambodia Post) holding that strikers are absent and cannot claim it. It then tested the strike against Article 324 (at least seven days' prior notice) and Article 320(4) (exhaustion of peaceful means of settlement) and found the strike was called at once, without notice and without awaiting the panel's conciliation, so participants had no right to the bonus. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 80][Para 83][Para 84]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Wages and the statutory attendance bonus are not payable for days on which workers strike, because the employment contract is suspended for those days whether or not the strike followed procedure; the employer's obligation to pay strikers arises only where it breached Article 334 by recruiting replacements. A strike launched without the seven days' notice in Article 324 and without exhausting conciliation under Article 320(4) is procedurally unlawful and forfeits the attendance bonus. [Para 69][Para 73][Para 84]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel recorded the employer's statement that the factory continues to operate and has not closed, and noted the workers' complaint that resignation letters were being collected from team heads, without deciding whether that amounted to intimidation. [Para 41][Para 46]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The single issue is printed only as "ចំណុច វិវាទ" with its number lost, so the numbering "1" in section 2 is supplied. The strike is described as 21 to 24 January 2019 in the facts but as "22 to 20 ... 24" January 2019 in one reasoning paragraph, and the claim covers only 22 to 24 January. The representative accreditation letter No. 002/19 is dated 23 January 2019 in one place and 23 December 2019 in another. Eleven representatives are named in the accreditation letter while eight attended the hearing, and their names are transliterated inconsistently (Phon/Phén Savon, Khim/Khym Sreymom). The worker-appointed arbitrator's signature is replaced by OCR noise, though the header prints An Virak. The employer's name appears variously as Long Vich, Long Vich Tori and Long Vich Tori International. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 26][Para 34][Para 35][Para 38][Para 62][Para 63][Para 75][Para 88]
