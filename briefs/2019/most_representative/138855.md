# Award No. 030/19 — GFK Garment Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 29 April 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phalla presiding; Ing Sothy; An Non) · Doc ID 138855

## 1. Facts
GFK Garment Co., Ltd. cuts and sews clothing in Phnom Penh with about 208 workers, 200 of them women. [Para 39][Para 40] On 23 February 2019 the workers filed 13 demands; conciliation on 19 March 2019 settled six and left seven. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 23] The employer did not appear at the hearing of 11 April 2019, having asked for it to be moved to 7 May 2019. [Para 20][Para 28] No union was shown to be the most representative, and the workers were represented by two of the four representatives recognised by the Labour Disputes Department. [Para 44][Para 96]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether annual leave pay, and payment in lieu of unused leave, must be calculated on the average of total wages over the preceding 12 months rather than on the basic wage divided by the month's working days? [Para 45][Para 98]
2. (Issues 2 and 5, merged) Whether the employer must supply chairs and a 15-minute rest break to workers and pregnant women whose work requires standing continuously? [Para 29][Para 107]
3. Whether the employer must set up a standard nursery with child-minders and play equipment and, failing that, pay a monthly allowance to workers with children? [Para 52][Para 123]
4. Whether the employer must provide a doctor and sufficient medicine for workers who fall ill during working hours? [Para 54][Para 132]
6. Whether the employer must issue identity cards and give each worker a copy of the employment contract? [Para 57][Para 155][Para 164]
7. Whether overtime must be calculated on basic wage plus seniority bonus and function or position allowance? [Para 63][Para 180]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer takes only the USD 182 basic wage, divided by the month's working days, to price annual leave, which is not lawful; the base should be the 12-month average. [Para 45][Para 47] QC and large-ironing workers stand all day without a chair; there is no nursery although some 200 workers are women, no doctor or medicine on site, no identity card for later hires, no contract copy, and overtime priced on the basic wage alone. [Para 48][Para 52][Para 54][Para 58][Para 63]
**Employer (respondent):** it did not attend; its only document was its director's letter of 8 April 2019 asking that the dispute be postponed. [Para 28][Para 33] At conciliation it said it could grant none of the seven points, and on the medical claim that it already paid NSSF contributions. [Para 3][Para 10]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — annual leave priced at total wages for the last 12 months ÷ 12 ÷ 26; chairs in the QC and large-ironing sections for occasional sitting; a nursery, and where none can be set up, women with children from 18 months may use an outside nursery at the employer's expense against actual invoices; one full-time nurse and a doctor present at least 2 hours in working hours plus sufficient medicine under Prakas No. 330; a copy of the employment contract to each worker; seniority bonus and function allowance included in the overtime base. Identity cards and the 15-minute break were refused. [Para 204][Para 207][Para 209][Para 211][Para 213] The award is non-binding and becomes final after 8 (eight) calendar days unless a party objects to the Minister. [Para 213]
The panel held that the employer had been properly summoned, that postponement to 7 May 2019 was too long and outside its procedure, and that under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 it could proceed in the employer's absence; having stayed away, the employer waived its right to answer, and the workers' assertions were accepted. [Para 70][Para 76][Para 81][Para 129]
On issue 1 the panel read Articles 166 and 168 of the Labour Law as requiring the average of total wages received in the 12 months before leave, and followed its earlier awards in fixing 12 months' total wages ÷ 12 ÷ 26. [Para 99][Para 103] On issues 2 and 5 it applied Article 1 of Prakas No. 053 of 10 February 2000, which requires suitable chairs where work calls for sitting or occasional sitting, found that QC and large-ironing workers stand continuously, and ordered chairs; it refused the 15-minute break because no law or regulation provides for it. [Para 108][Para 117][Para 118][Para 120] On issue 3 it applied Article 186 and its earlier nursery awards: a nursery must be provided, and an outside nursery at the employer's cost is only the fallback. [Para 123][Para 126][Para 131] On issue 4 it applied Articles 238 and 242 and Article 3 of Prakas No. 330, whose table for 50 to 300 workers requires one nurse on duty and a doctor for two hours. [Para 133][Para 138][Para 143] On issue 6 it found no law requiring an identity card, but held that Articles 65 and 67 of the Labour Law and Article 5 of the Civil Code require each party to hold a copy of the contract. [Para 160][Para 178][Para 179] On issue 7 it read "wages for normal working time" in Article 139 and Article 5 of Prakas No. 080 as the pay the worker regularly receives, and followed award 74/08 on skill allowance. [Para 183][Para 199][Para 202]
The employer-nominated arbitrator, Ing Sothy, dissented on issues 1 and 7: the formula applied only to Articles 167(2) and 168, and adding the seniority bonus to the overtime base has no legal foundation and rests on equity alone. [Para 216][Para 224]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the Labour Law and Prakas No. 080 leave "wages for normal working time" undefined, the base for leave and overtime is what the worker regularly receives and expects each month, not the minimum basic wage: annual leave at total wages for the preceding 12 months ÷ 12 ÷ 26, overtime at basic wage plus seniority bonus and function allowance. [Para 106][Para 187][Para 202] An employer properly summoned that stays away without valid reason waives its right to answer, and the panel may decide on the workers' evidence alone. [Para 81][Para 129]

**Obiter dicta:** a worker without an identity card cannot use NSSF medical services or the free municipal bus, yet this created no legal duty to issue one. [Para 61][Para 162]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Issue 3 is printed as USD 300 a month in the list of demands and USD 30 a month in the reasons; both are reproduced, unreconciled. [Para 6][Para 52] The hearing is dated 11 April 2018 at para 25 but 11 April 2019 at paras 20 and 77. [Para 20][Para 25] The company appears only in Khmer, as ជី អេហ្វ ខេ អេច ហ្គាម៉ិន ឯ.ក, with no Latin form on the face of the award. [Para 1] The worker-nominated arbitrator is printed "អន ណន" in the panel list and "អន លាន" in the signature block. [Para 1][Para 214] Para 54 is heavily corrupted, losing the number of women with children and the outside nursery fee; the workforce is given only as "about 208". [Para 40][Para 54]
