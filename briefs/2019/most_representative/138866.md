# Award No. 012/19 — Ri Jen Footwear (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 20 March 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Eng Sothy (chosen by the employer), An Non (chosen by the workers), Kong Phalla (chair) · Doc ID 138866

## 1. Facts
Ri Jen Footwear (Cambodia) Co., Ltd makes shoes at Svay Chrum, Baek Chan, Ang Snuol, Kandal, with about 1,600 workers. On 3 January 2019 the Free Independent Union filed 15 claims with the Kandal labour department and later added 4 more; conciliation on 7 February 2019 settled 13 points, leaving six for decision after the hearing of 20 February 2019. The dispute concerns the non-renewal of a workers' delegate's contract, the pro-rating of an employer-paid attendance bonus, a needle-skill allowance, a transport increment, annual leave lost to health checks, and maternity pay. A Khmer union's most-representative certificate had expired on 14 November 2015, so no union held that status. The award is non-binding. [Para 25][Para 32][Para 33][Para 81][Para 138][Para 139]

## 2. Issues in dispute
5. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr C and pay him wages from the day of dismissal to the day of reinstatement? [Para 152]
8. Whether the employer must pro-rate its own USD 15 per month attendance bonus by the days a worker took leave? [Para 191]
10. Whether the employer must pay a needle-skill allowance of USD 5 per month for one needle and USD 10 for two needles? [Para 205]
11. Whether the employer must add USD 2 to transport and lodging so that the total reaches USD 12 per month? [Para 229]
13. Whether the employer must restore the annual leave days used when it required workers to attend a health check? [Para 234]
2 (additional). Whether the employer must pay three months' full wages to a worker who takes maternity leave? [Para 245]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr C, an elected workers' delegate and deputy president of the union, was dismissed without the labour inspector's authorisation, contrary to Article 43 of the Law on Trade Unions; because his successive contracts ran from 13 January 2016 to 28 February 2018 the contract had become indefinite-duration, so what the employer calls non-renewal is a dismissal. The employer deducted annual leave for compulsory health checks. Wages for the three months of maternity leave should be paid in full. [Para 84][Para 87][Para 88][Para 128][Para 130]

**Employer (respondent):** There was no dismissal but simply a decision not to renew a fixed-duration contract, because Mr C did not follow his team leader's instructions and never requested leave from him; the labour department was notified of the non-renewal of two contracts. The USD 15 bonus is the employer's own incentive scheme, better than the law, and the needle allowance never existed; transport of USD 7 plus USD 3 lodging already totals USD 10. It also pays maternity wages monthly rather than in a lump sum. [Para 94][Para 95][Para 97][Para 109][Para 115][Para 118][Para 131]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 5: the employer is ordered to reinstate Mr C and pay his wages from the dismissal to the reinstatement; issue 8 dismissed; issues 10 and 11 declined; issue 13: the employer must restore the annual leave used for health checks; additional claim 2: the employer must pay half wages including allowances for 90 days, before the maternity leave begins. The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent a written objection. [Para 264][Para 265][Para 266][Para 267][Para 268][Para 269][Para 270]

**Issue 5.** Article 67(2) caps a fixed-duration contract at two years; the panel read the French original to confirm that any renewal carrying the total beyond two years converts the contract, and it treated ILO Recommendation No. 166 as useful non-binding context. Mr C's contracts ran from 13 January 2016 to 28 February 2018, so his contract had become indefinite-duration and the non-renewal was in truth a dismissal. Article 43 of the Law on Trade Unions forbids dismissing a workers' delegate without the labour inspector's prior authorisation; none was obtained, so the dismissal was procedurally unlawful. Under article 34 of Prakas No. 099 the panel has power to order reinstatement, and it did so with back pay. [Para 154][Para 166][Para 169][Para 175][Para 177][Para 184][Para 189][Para 190]

**Issue 8.** The USD 15 attendance bonus is a scheme the employer created unilaterally above the statutory USD 10, following Award No. 24/05 Well Well; the employer may set the conditions of its own incentive bonus, and no provision of the Labour Law requires pro-rating it by days of leave, so the claim failed. [Para 197][Para 198][Para 203]

**Issues 10 and 11.** Both were new benefits with no basis in statute, collective agreement, internal rules or past practice, so the panel classified them as interest disputes. Because the claimant was not a most-representative union — Article 54 and Article 72 of the Law on Trade Unions and article 43 of Prakas No. 099 — the panel declined to consider them. [Para 205][Para 206][Para 223][Para 226][Para 229][Para 233]

**Issue 13.** Article 247(a) and (c)(4) put the cost of workers' health checks on the employer. The employer required workers to draw on annual leave to attend compulsory checks, which the panel held wrong; the employer must therefore restore the leave days taken. [Para 236][Para 238][Para 241][Para 243][Para 244]

**Additional claim 2.** Articles 182 and 183 give 90 days' maternity leave at half wages including secondary allowances; Article 103 lists maternity payments as wage, and Article 115(3) requires wages to be paid before a rest day, and the day before where the payday falls on one. The panel held Article 115 applies to maternity pay and that prior awards require it at least one day before the leave starts, so monthly payment was unlawful. [Para 246][Para 252][Para 259][Para 260][Para 262][Para 263]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where successive fixed-duration contracts carry the total engagement beyond two years the contract converts to indefinite duration, so refusing to renew it is a dismissal; and dismissing a workers' delegate without the labour inspector's prior authorisation under Article 43 of the Law on Trade Unions is unlawful, warranting reinstatement with wages from dismissal to reinstatement. Maternity pay is wage under Article 103 and must be paid in a lump sum at least one day before the leave begins, not month by month. [Para 176][Para 189][Para 259][Para 262]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the Labour Law leans toward indefinite-duration contracts for the job security of both parties, that ILO Recommendation No. 166, though non-binding, is a useful interpretive context, and that an employer may fix its own bonus schemes beyond statutory minimums to encourage output. It also noted that the Article 67(2) Khmer text is unclear and resorted to the French drafting. [Para 161][Para 166][Para 168][Para 202]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel records that 13 of 19 claims were settled but enumerates only twelve (nine original and three additional). The delegate's name appears as Chim Sopheak, Chim Sopheak-A and Chim Sophal across the reasoning, and the same man is described variously as workers' delegate and as union deputy president. The final page prints the award date as 20 December 2019 where the header and procedural paragraphs give 20 March 2019. The transport claim is printed as "USD 202" in one place and USD 2 elsewhere. The claim list describes issue 13 as repayment of a seniority indemnity while the reasoning and order treat it as annual leave. The worker-appointed arbitrator's name is replaced by OCR noise in the signature block although the header prints An Non. Arbitrator Eng Sothy filed a dissenting opinion opposing the issue 5 order.
