# Award No. 054/18 — Rousing Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 31 January 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed, dissenting), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (chair) · Doc ID 138957

## 1. Facts
Rousing Garment Co., Ltd is a Phnom Penh garment manufacturer at Toul Sangke, Russey Keo, registered in 1997, employing about 3,000 workers. [Para 1][Para 62][Para 63] On 6 November 2018 the workers petitioned on three working conditions; two points failed conciliation on 24 December 2018 and reached the panel on 26 December 2018. [Para 10][Para 11][Para 12] The company has for years issued fixed-duration contracts of three months at a time to workers whose service already exceeded two years, paying 5 per cent end-of-contract compensation and outstanding annual leave at each expiry. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 77][Para 78]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to convert the fixed-duration contracts of 535 workers with more than two years' service into indefinite-duration contracts? [Para 67][Para 132]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to deduct 2,000 riel per month from the wages of 74 members of the Cambodia Union to pay their union dues, having failed to do so? [Para 89][Para 172]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Article 67 of the Labour Law forbids a fixed-duration contract exceeding two years, and repeated three-month renewals leave them with no job security; they filed a fingerprinted list of 413 claimants. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 82] On dues, the union submitted three written requests in 2018, each attaching members' fingerprinted consent to a 2,000 riel monthly deduction, and the employer had no right to demand resignation letters from other unions first. [Para 95][Para 97][Para 100][Para 101]

**Employer (respondent):** It had previously used indefinite-duration contracts but moved to three-month contracts at the workers' own request from 2014, gives seven days' notice at each expiry, and pays 5 per cent plus leave; it cannot accept the demand and continues three-month contracts. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 80] On dues, it never received consent letters, only a long list of names, and required resignation letters from other unions only to avoid disputes with them. [Para 103][Para 106]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to convert to indefinite-duration contracts the contracts of 408 workers whose successive fixed-duration contracts total more than two years, excluding 3 named individuals circled in the annex, and to deduct 2,000 riel per month from each of 74 Cambodia Union members for union dues; the order is non-binding and becomes final 8 days after notification absent written objection to the Ministry of Labour. [Para 207][Para 208][Para 209]

On issue 1, the panel applied Article 67(2) of the Labour Law, which caps a fixed-duration contract at two years and allows renewal only while the maximum total stays within two years, and Article 73(5), under which a contract renewed without notice beyond that total is treated as indefinite. [Para 132][Para 133][Para 135] It followed its reasoning in Award No. 10/03 (Zac Sintic) and Award No. 91/10 (New Way) that the two-year reference is to the cumulative total, not to each renewal. [Para 136][Para 140][Para 157] It held that paying 5 per cent at each expiry does not sever the employment relationship, because the workers simply signed new contracts and kept working, so the 5 per cent payment cannot block conversion. [Para 163][Para 164][Para 157] Article 13 of the Labour Law makes any agreement contrary to the Law void, so the workers' own consent to successive fixed-duration contracts could not legalise what Article 67 forbids. [Para 149][Para 150][Para 152][Para 165] ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982 was cited as interpretive context only, not as binding. [Para 141][Para 143] From the claimants' list the panel counted 411 eligible names, struck 3 for a missing fingerprint, a missing start date and service under two years, and ordered conversion for the remaining 408. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 85][Para 168]

On issue 2, the panel applied Article 129 of the Labour Law, which permits wage deduction for union dues only with the worker's written, revocable consent, and Article 26 of the Law on Trade Unions, plus Articles 1 and 4(2) of Prakas No. 300 of 2 July 2018 requiring ten days' written notice and attached dated consent letters bearing each member's signature or thumbprint. [Para 172][Para 173][Para 174][Para 175][Para 180][Para 184][Para 185] It found the union had given the three notices with fingerprinted consents and that the employer had in fact deducted for some members and not others, which corroborated receipt; it therefore believed the workers. [Para 187][Para 192][Para 193][Para 195] It further held that Article 49(4) of the Law on Trade Unions requires only that the later union notify the employer, not that the worker resign from an earlier union, so the employer's precondition was unlawful. [Para 199][Para 202][Para 204]

**Dissent (Arbitrator Ing Sothy):** The employer should be free to choose the contract type; since it paid 5 per cent at each expiry, conversion would expose it to paying both that and Article 89 dismissal compensation on the whole seniority, contrary to the parties' will under Article 65. [Para 212][Para 213][Para 226][Para 229][Para 233] He also dissented on issue 2, because the workers conceded they held no copies of the consent letters and the employer denied receiving them, so deduction required proper documents. [Para 236][Para 237][Para 238]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive renewal of fixed-duration contracts is measured by the cumulative total of all renewals, not by each term; once that total passes two years the contract becomes indefinite by operation of Articles 67(2) and 73(5) of the Labour Law, and neither the employer's payment of 5 per cent at each expiry nor the worker's consent to renew can prevent the conversion, because Article 13 voids agreements contrary to the Law. [Para 133][Para 140][Para 152][Para 165] Union dues may be deducted only on the union's prior written request with each member's dated, fingerprinted consent, and an employer may not condition that deduction on the worker resigning from another union. [Para 185][Para 204]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the Labour Law shows a deliberate preference for indefinite-duration contracts because job security benefits workers and long service benefits employers, and that an employer choosing fixed-duration contracts for genuinely long-term work bears the cost. [Para 137][Para 138][Para 229][Para 230] It also noted no most-representative union existed at the company and recognised the four named worker representatives under Article 1 of Prakas No. 362. [Para 119][Para 130]
