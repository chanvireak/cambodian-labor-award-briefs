# Award No. 028/19 — Crystal Martin (Cambodia) Limited (Arbitration Council, 23 May 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chea presiding; Seng Vouch Hun appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna appointed by the workers) · Doc ID 138848

## 1. Facts
Crystal Martin (Cambodia) Limited, a garment manufacturer at Vihear Suor, Khsach Kandal, Kandal, registered in 2011, faced a complaint of 5 March 2019 setting out ten demands on working conditions, filed with the Kandal provincial labour department. [Para 23][Para 40][Para 53] Conciliation on 28 March 2019 left seven demands unresolved; at the hearing of 10 April 2019 the workers withdrew issues 4, 5 and 6 and the parties agreed issue 7, leaving issues 1, 2 and 3. [Para 24][Para 29][Para 30] There was no union with most-representative status at the company, and three workers recognised by the province represented the claimants. [Para 56][Para 54][Para 55] The award issued on 23 May 2019 after the parties agreed to postpone it from 25 April 2019. [Para 33]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise the midday meal allowance to 2,500 riel a day, as paid by the company's other branches elsewhere in Cambodia, instead of the USD 10 (ten American dollars) a month now paid? [Para 57][Para 97]
2. Whether the employer must make good the wages and other benefits it deducted during the strike of 1 to 15 February 2019, contrary to the settlement of 26 February 2019? [Para 61][Para 120]
3. Whether the employer must grant one full day of sick leave on pay to a worker holding a proper certificate from a public hospital doctor? [Para 68][Para 131]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the USD 10 a month meal allowance is too little, food has risen in price, and other companies in the area pay more; and the employer must honour the agreement of 26 February 2019 on wages during the strike, because the employer explained it unclearly and workers were confused when they agreed to deductions. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 66][Para 62] On sick leave they asked for one full day, since dizziness and similar illnesses need rest at home. [Para 69][Para 70]
**Employer (respondent):** labour costs, severance and the losses from the strike make the raise impossible; after the agreement the employer polled the workforce and about 85 per cent chose to have wages deducted for the strike days rather than work the eight make-up Saturdays at 100 per cent, so it applied the deduction once. [Para 60][Para 63][Para 64][Para 65] It granted sick leave of half a day upwards against the public hospital certificate, as it had agreed many times before, and its own doctor verified the illness. [Para 68][Para 72]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — on issue 1 the panel declined to consider the demand; on issue 2 it ordered the employer to apply the agreement of 26 February 2019 to those workers who did not agree to have their wages deducted for the strike; on issue 3 it ordered the employer to grant sick leave in the time actually requested, in accordance with the internal rules and the conciliation agreement of 1 February 2019, according to the worker's actual illness. [Para 146][Para 147][Para 148] The award is non-binding and becomes final 8 (eight) days after notification if no party objects. [Para 149]
On issue 1, the panel found the USD 10 a month already more favourable than the law requires and found no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice fixing 2,500 riel a day, so the demand was an interest dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍). [Para 98][Para 99] Reading Article 96(2) of the Labour Law with Articles 54 and 72 of the Law on Trade Unions, and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099, it held that an award on an interest dispute becomes a collective agreement binding on all workers and extinguishes their right to bargain and strike over it, so it will decide such a dispute only where the claimant is a most-representative union; these claimants were individual workers and no such union existed. [Para 100][Para 111][Para 112][Para 115][Para 118]
On issue 2, the panel applied Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code to hold that the agreement of 26 February 2019 bound the parties, and that a later staff poll could not release the employer from it towards the workers who had not consented to the deduction. [Para 120][Para 123][Para 129][Para 130]
On issue 3, article 4(g) of the internal rules of 25 December 2015 provides paid sick leave on a proper clinic certificate but fixes no number of days, and the conciliation minutes of 1 February 2019 record an agreement to allow one full day where the illness requires it. [Para 131][Para 136][Para 139] The panel therefore held the employer's practice of granting only half a day or hourly leave contrary to both instruments. [Para 141][Para 142][Para 144]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand to raise a benefit already above the statutory minimum, unsupported by any law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice, is an interest dispute; the panel will decline to decide it unless it is brought by a most-representative union, because its award would become a collective agreement binding every worker in the enterprise and would strip them of the right to bargain or strike over that subject. [Para 99][Para 112][Para 115] A written agreement between the employer and worker representatives binds the employer, and a subsequent poll of the workforce cannot vary it as against workers who did not consent to the variation. [Para 129][Para 130]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the employer's payment of USD 10 a month is more favourable than the law requires, and that 85 per cent of workers had voted for the deduction. [Para 98][Para 126]
