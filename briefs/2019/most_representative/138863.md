# Award No. 015/19 — Wai Full Garment (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 19 March 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Eng Sothy (chosen by the employer), Liv Sovanna (chosen by the workers), Pen Bun Chhea (chair, chosen by both) · Doc ID 138863

## 1. Facts
Wai Full Garment (Cambodia) Ltd runs a garment factory at Pong Teuk, Ang Snuol, Kandal, employing 926 workers. On 27 January 2019 the Kandal provincial labour department received 13 claims on working conditions from its workers; conciliation on 8 February 2019 failed to settle 8 of the 13, and the report reached the Arbitration Council, which constituted the panel on 15 February 2019. At the hearing the parties reached agreement on issues 4 and 6 and the workers withdrew issue 7, leaving five issues for the panel. No union with most-representative status existed in the enterprise; the four worker representatives were accredited by provincial letter No. 005/19 of 13 February 2019. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 32][Para 66][Para 70][Para 131]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must release workers one hour before the end of work on each payday so that they can withdraw their wages? [Para 135]
2. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of USD 1 per day to each worker? [Para 156]
3. Whether the employer must pay a meal allowance of USD 1 for overtime worked from 4:00 to 6:00 in the evening? [Para 171]
5. Whether the employer must convert fixed-duration contracts into indefinite-duration contracts for workers whose contracts total more than two years? [Para 181]
8. Whether the employer must raise base wages by USD 2 above the minimum wage of USD 182? [Para 211]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Before 2019 wages were paid in cash and the employer let them stop work at 2:00 pm to collect from accounting; ATM withdrawal now takes hours because other factories queue at the few machines nearby. The lunch allowance of USD 8 per month, equal to only 1,200 riel a day, no longer buys a meal as prices rise. Their successive fixed-duration contracts exceed two years in total, so under Article 67 and Award No. 10/03 they became indefinite-duration contracts. The USD 2 above the minimum wage was a benefit they already enjoyed and must be preserved under Prakas No. 465. [Para 79][Para 88][Para 96][Para 108]

**Employer (respondent):** Since January 2019 wages are transferred through ACLEDA bank into each worker's own account on the 23rd or 24th, before the 25th and 10th paydays, following discussion with the representatives and with their agreement, so nobody needs to leave early. It has no capacity to raise the lunch allowance because wages and benefits already rise every year. It pays the 5% end-of-contract indemnity at the expiry of every fixed-duration contract, as Article 73 requires, so the contracts remain fixed-duration. The USD 2 is base wage paid above the minimum, not a separate benefit. [Para 84][Para 89][Para 98][Para 110]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 1 dismissed; on issue 2 the operative order directs the employer to provide a lunch allowance of USD 1 per day to the workers, although the reasons had declined to consider that claim; issue 3 declined without decision; on issue 5 the employer is ordered to convert fixed-duration contracts to indefinite-duration contracts for workers whose contracts total more than two years; issue 8 dismissed. [Para 223][Para 224][Para 225][Para 226]

**Issue 1.** Article 115(3) of the Labour Law forbids paying wages on a day the worker is entitled to rest, and a payment falling on a rest day must be made the day before; earlier awards (No. 143/08 Cham Thich Thay, No. 72/10 Hy Yun) read that as requiring payment within working hours. The panel found the employer transfers wages into the worker's own account on the 23rd or 24th, inside working hours, so withdrawal outside hours is the worker's own choice and cannot be charged to the employer as an out-of-hours payment; Article 2 also gives the employer the right to organise the enterprise lawfully and reasonably. No law or agreement entitles workers to leave an hour early, so the claim was refused. [Para 139][Para 141][Para 150][Para 151][Para 154]

**Issue 2.** The panel found the USD 8 per month lunch allowance more favourable than the law requires, and that no statute, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice obliged the employer to pay USD 1 per day. It classified the claim as an interest dispute; because the claimants were not a most-representative union — Article 54 of the Law on Trade Unions and Prakas No. 303 reserve collective bargaining and interest-dispute standing to such a union — it held they lacked standing to bring it (following Awards No. 81/04 Avigreen, No. 98/04 Great Union, No. 169/11 Gortune Tee). The operative order nonetheless grants USD 1 per day, contradicting that reasoning. [Para 157][Para 159][Para 166][Para 168][Para 170][Para 224]

**Issue 3.** The same standing defect applied, and the panel expressly cross-referred to its interest-dispute analysis under issue 2 rather than repeating it, so it declined to consider the overtime meal allowance. It also found the employer already paid 2,000 riel per day of overtime, matching Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011. [Para 172][Para 174][Para 176][Para 180]

**Issue 5.** Under Article 67(2) a fixed-duration contract may not exceed two years, and a renewal that carries the total beyond two years turns it into an indefinite-duration contract; Article 73(5) adds that absent notice of termination the contract is treated as indefinite once the total exceeds the Article 67 limit. The panel followed Award No. 10/03 (Zaksinbek) that the reference is to the maximum total, not to each renewal. It held that paying the 5% indemnity does not sever the relationship where the worker signs a new contract and keeps working for the same employer, so the employment relationship was never cut and the contracts converted; the 282 workers listed with more than two years' service were covered. [Para 183][Para 191][Para 196][Para 207][Para 209][Para 210]

**Issue 8.** Prakas No. 465 set the 2019 minimum at USD 182 per month, and the panel accepted the employer's evidence that it pays USD 182 to full-status workers and USD 177 to probationary workers. Comparing three payslips for 2016, 2017 and 2018 (USD 142, 155 and 172), it found the USD 2 excess was base wage paid above the statutory minimum to attract workers, not a separate benefit under Prakas article 3, so there was no duty to preserve it. [Para 211][Para 215][Para 218][Para 220][Para 222]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive fixed-duration contracts convert by operation of law into an indefinite-duration contract once their combined duration exceeds two years, and payment of the 5% end-of-contract indemnity at each expiry does not defeat the conversion, because the employment relationship is not severed where the worker signs a new contract and continues working for the same employer. Conversely, a wage paid above the statutory minimum is base wage, not a distinct benefit that Prakas No. 465 obliges the employer to preserve. [Para 207][Para 209][Para 219]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the Labour Law leans toward indefinite-duration contracts for reasons of job security that benefit employer and worker alike, and noted that the ministry had issued no Prakas on converting contracts that total more than two years. It also remarked that a worker paid by bank transfer during working hours may withdraw the money whenever the worker needs it. [Para 100][Para 151][Para 189]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award contradicts itself twice. First, the panel records that five issues remained but enumerates only issues 1, 2, 5 and 8, omitting issue 3, which it nevertheless reasoned on and included in the order. Second, issue 2 is dismissed in the reasons for want of a most-representative union but granted in the operative order. The question framing issue 2 also prints the claim as USD 10 per day where the claim and order read USD 1. The 2017 minimum-wage comparison prints the employer's payment as 1,556 USD at one point and 155 USD at another. The employer's name is OCR-mangled into "Euro Hul Garmen" and "Wi Ful Garmen" in the document list. Arbitrator Eng Sothy filed a dissenting opinion as an annex, opposing the issue 5 conversion order. [Para 33][Para 106][Para 115][Para 156][Para 170][Para 224][Para 230]
