# Award No. 018/19 — Ever Green Apparel (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 4 April 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Buncha presiding; In Sothy nominated by the employer, Liv Sovanna nominated by the workers) · Doc ID 138861

## 1. Facts
Ever Green Apparel (Cambodia), at Chumteav Chas, Tuek Thla, Sen Sok, Phnom Penh, cuts and sews garments and employed 131 workers by the employer's own count. [Para 44][Para 45] The workers' complaint of 8 February 2019, received on 12 February 2019, made three demands; conciliation on 20 February 2019 failed and the panel heard the case on 19 March 2019. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 18] The employer paid half wages for parts of January 2019, paid nothing in February, and shut the gates on 20 February 2019 without informing or seeking authorisation from the labour inspectorate. [Para 108][Para 110][Para 111] The award is non-binding. [Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must top up the 50% wages paid in January 2019 to 100% and pay 100% for 1 to 20 February 2019 to the 122 workers, because the suspension of their contracts was unlawful? [Para 92][Para 112]
2. Whether the employer must pay half wages, including fringe benefits, to the eight women who took maternity leave? [Para 114][Para 119]
3. Whether the employer must pay termination compensation to 122 workers — notice pay under Articles 75 and 77, dismissal damages under Article 89, seniority indemnity under the new Article 89, unused annual leave under Article 166, mental damages under the new Article 91, and final wages under Article 116? [Para 126][Para 144]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer suspended their contracts and paid half wages without following legal procedure, then closed the factory on 20 February 2019 without notice, so they are owed the missing 50% for January and full wages to that date. [Para 49][Para 55][Para 69][Para 70] Eight women who gave birth received no half wage or benefits on returning. [Para 58][Para 59] Closing the doors was a termination at the employer's initiative without valid reason or notice, entitling all 122 to the full set of statutory payments. [Para 67][Para 68]
**Employer (respondent):** it has had no buyers since July 2017 and the company no longer operates, so it cannot meet the demands. [Para 56][Para 57] It nonetheless agreed at the hearing to pay four heads of termination compensation: notice pay, dismissal damages, unused annual leave and final wages. [Para 131][Para 71]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer must pay the further 50% to reach 100% of wages for January 2019 and 100% of wages for 1 to 20 February 2019; pay half wages including fringe benefits to the eight women; and pay the 122 workers notice pay, dismissal damages, seniority indemnity, unused annual leave and mental damages, final wages being refused as already covered by the first order. [Para 192][Para 194][Para 195]
On issue 1 the panel read Article 71(11) as allowing suspension only for serious economic or material difficulty, for no more than two months, and only under labour inspectorate supervision, and Article 72(1) as removing the wage obligation only where the suspension itself is lawful. [Para 92][Para 94][Para 96][Para 101][Para 102] Since the shutdown carried no authorisation from the labour inspector, the suspension was unlawful and the employer owed full wages although it had no work to give. [Para 104][Para 111][Para 112] On issue 2 Articles 182 and 183 give 90 days' maternity leave and require the employer to pay half the wage, fringe benefits included, to a woman with at least one year's service; none of the eight had been paid. [Para 114][Para 119][Para 120][Para 124]
On issue 3 the panel held that closing the enterprise does not release the employer from its obligations under the new Article 87, judicial liquidation aside, and that shutting the gates on 20 February 2019 without valid reason or notice was a termination at the employer's initiative. [Para 126][Para 128][Para 141][Para 143] Notice pay follows Articles 75 and 77 by actual seniority, computed on the average of the last twelve months' wages and benefits. [Para 144][Para 146][Para 149] Dismissal damages follow the old Article 89 for seniority earned before the 2018 amendment took effect and the new Article 89, read with Prakas No. 443 of 21 September 2018, from then on, the new provision giving 15 days a year paid twice yearly. [Para 152][Para 160][Para 167][Para 172][Para 173] Unused annual leave is payable under Articles 166 and 167, mental damages under the new Article 91, and final wages under Article 116 are not due again because the issue 1 order already covers wages to 20 February 2019. [Para 178][Para 185][Para 187][Para 189]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of employment contracts under Article 71(11) is lawful only where it is placed under labour inspectorate supervision with prior authorisation; without that authorisation the suspension is unlawful and the employer owes full wages for the period even though it has no work to give. [Para 96][Para 102][Para 112] Where the employer then closes the gates without valid reason or notice, that is a termination at its own initiative, and ceasing operations does not discharge its obligations: notice pay, dismissal damages, seniority indemnity, unused annual leave and mental damages all remain payable, seniority indemnity being split between the old Article 89 for service before the 2018 amendment and the new Article 89 thereafter. [Para 143][Para 167][Para 172]
A woman with one year's service who takes the 90 days' maternity leave under Article 182 is entitled to half her wage, fringe benefits included, from the employer. [Para 119][Para 124]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the employer had itself conceded four heads of termination compensation at the hearing, and that final wages could not be claimed twice over the same period. [Para 131][Para 189]
