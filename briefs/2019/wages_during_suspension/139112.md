# Award No. 063/19 — Siem Reap International Airport (Arbitration Council, 3 September 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — chair ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា, employer-appointed ម៉ារ សំបូរ ណា (dissenting in part), worker-appointed អន ណន · Doc ID 139112

## 1. Facts
The respondent, ក្រុមហ៊ុន អាកាសយានដ្ឋាន អន្តរជាតិ សៀមរាប, provides airport services at Siem Reap and employed 544 workers. The union, the Cambodia Tourism Industry Workers' Union at Siem Reap Airport, raised eight demands; one was settled in conciliation on 11 July 2019 and seven were forwarded to the Council on 19 July 2019 under report No. 064 M.K.B. [Para 19][Para 24][Para 25][Para 82][Para 83]

A hearing was fixed for 30 July 2019, postponed to 1 August at the workers' request, then further postponed to 7 August after the workers objected to one listed arbitrator for conflict of interest — he had trained both parties at the company — and he withdrew, being replaced by ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា. Both parties attended the second hearing. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 29][Para 31]

At that hearing the workers withdrew issues 1, 3 and 6 and reached agreement on issue 2, leaving issues 4, 5 and 7 for adjudication. [Para 32][Para 33]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must contract with Royal Angkor International Hospital to secure staff health and reduce staff mortality? [Para 3]
2. Whether the employer must investigate the circumstances of the death of a worker? [Para 5]
3. Whether the employer must vaccinate all staff against cancer and other serious diseases? [Para 7]
4. Whether the employer must stop requiring fire-safety staff to work more than 8 hours a day? [Para 9]
5. Whether hours worked from 4:00 am to 12:00 noon must be paid in full at the night-shift rate? [Para 11]
6. Whether the employer must always observe the principle of integrity and good faith? [Para 13]
7. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr S to his former post and pay him wages from the date of dismissal to the date of reinstatement? [Para 15]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** fire-safety staff work 24-hour shifts, which exceeds the legal maximum of 8 hours a day and harms their health; Article 144 defines night work as 11 consecutive hours, so a shift running 4:00 am to noon must be paid entirely at the night rate; and Mr S was suspended and then dismissed on 7 September 2018 without valid cause, having produced two negative drug tests of his own. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 100][Para 101][Para 115][Para 116]

**Employer (respondent):** fire-safety staff have only 8 hours of actual work within a 24-hour presence shift, with rest facilities and an additional allowance, and they are not union members and had twice asked the union in writing to drop this demand; Article 144 (new) sets night work at 22:00 to 05:00 and the company already pays 200%, above the statutory 130%; Mr S was found with drugs in a test of 14 September 2017, the civil aviation authority withdrew his airport security pass, and without a pass he cannot work. [Para 10][Para 86][Para 92][Para 98][Para 102][Para 103][Para 119][Para 122][Para 124]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issues 4 and 5 were dismissed, reinstatement on issue 7 was refused, but the employer was ordered to pay Mr S two months' wages in lieu of notice, payment for outstanding annual leave, seniority indemnity of 15 days multiplied by 8, calculated on average wage over the last 12 months, and final wages from 26 October 2017 to 7 September 2018. [Para 275][Para 276][Para 277][Para 278]

On its jurisdiction the panel applied Article 302 and award 10/03 to hold the dispute collective: though only one worker was affected, he was represented by the union holding exclusive representative status, prior strikes had arisen from the same unresolved grievance, and the dispute could disrupt the enterprise. It also found the res judicata conditions unsatisfied, because award 021/18 had declined the same claim only while the contract was suspended, never deciding it on the merits. [Para 194][Para 202][Para 205][Para 218][Para 219][Para 221][Para 222]

On issue 4 the panel declined to consider the merits at all: the fire-safety staff had themselves written on 18 October 2018 and 17 June 2019 asking that the demand be withdrawn, and they are not members of the union, so no worker was a party and the union could not represent them. [Para 156][Para 160][Para 161]

On issue 5 the panel read Article 144 (new) as defining night as 11 consecutive hours that must include 22:00 to 05:00, and held that the 11-hour window does not make every hour inside it night work; night work is only 22:00 to 05:00. The company's split of 22:00–05:00 at the night rate and 05:00–12:00 at the ordinary rate conformed to the law, so the demand failed. [Para 170][Para 171][Para 173][Para 178][Para 179][Para 181][Para 182][Para 183]

On issue 7 the panel first upheld the union's authority to represent Mr S, since the transfer-of-rights letter carried no time limit. On the dismissal, it found the internal rules of 3 May 2017, extended to Siem Reap from 28 September 2017, could not apply to conduct detected on 14 September 2017 — before that extension — and that drug use is not among the Article 83 serious misconducts. The dismissal for serious misconduct was therefore unlawful. But the panel held the employer nonetheless had a proper reason to dismiss, because the civil aviation authority had withdrawn the airport security pass and would not reissue it, so the worker could not lawfully work airside. Accordingly it refused reinstatement but awarded the termination entitlements: notice pay under Articles 74, 75 and 77 for 7 years 11 months and 16 days of service, outstanding annual leave under Articles 166 and 167, seniority indemnity under Article 89, and final wages under Article 116 for the unpaid suspension period. [Para 226][Para 230][Para 242][Para 243][Para 244][Para 245][Para 248][Para 252][Para 253][Para 263][Para 269][Para 273]

The employer-appointed arbitrator dissented on one point only: he considered that no wages were owed for the suspension period, since Article 71(7) permits suspension without pay pending an authority's decision. [Para 282][Para 284][Para 285][Para 290]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The 11-hour definition of night in Article 144 sets the window's length, not its content: only work between 22:00 and 05:00 attracts the night rate, whatever the shift's start and end. And where a dismissal is founded on serious misconduct that does not meet Article 83, but the worker has independently lost a licence without which the job cannot lawfully be done, the dismissal remains justified while the termination entitlements — notice pay, annual leave, seniority indemnity and final wages — still fall due. [Para 179][Para 182][Para 244][Para 248][Para 253]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's reasoning that it could treat a one-worker grievance as a collective dispute because the union held exclusive representative status and prior strikes had occurred was not needed for the outcome, and its remarks on the res judicata test go beyond what the dismissal required. [Para 202][Para 218][Para 221]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is heavily degraded. The chair is named in the header as ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា, yet Para 29 records his appointment as a replacement for a withdrawn arbitrator whose name does not appear anywhere in the text. Two corporate identities are used interchangeably: the respondent as ក្រុមហ៊ុន អាកាសយានដ្ឋាន អន្តរជាតិ សៀមរាប, and the author of the internal rules as Cambodia Airports Management Services / ខេមបូឌា អែរ ផត មែនីជ្ជមែន សឺវីស, with the same company also appearing as "CAMS" and "SCAJCAMS" (Para 43). Dates are frequently wrong or truncated: the first hearing is dated 1 August "ឆ្នាំ ២០១៣" (Para 21), the panel's creation is dated "២០១៩ ២០១៩" (Para 26), a 2017 minute is dated "ឆ្នាំ ១៨" (Para 63), a 2018 letter "ឆ្នាំ ១០១៨" (Para 122), the termination letter is dated 7 September 2019 at Para 143 but 7 September 2018 at Paras 42 and 240, and the suspension is dated 26 October 2019 at Para 272 and 26 October 2017 at Para 273. The statutory year of Article 144 is printed as "ឆ្នាំ ២ ២០០៧" (Para 170). Paragraph 179 contains stray Latin text inside the definition of night work. The dispositive orders repeat the same relief twice (Paras 274 and 278). Individual worker names appear throughout the award but are not reproduced here.
