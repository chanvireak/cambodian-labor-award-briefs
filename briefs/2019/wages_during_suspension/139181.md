# Award No. 85/19 — Chin Yee Garment (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 19 September 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kol Vathana chosen by the employer, An Nan chosen by the workers, Kong Phallar chair) · Doc ID 139181

## 1. Facts
Chin Yee Garment (Cambodia) cuts and sews clothing at Spuean Po, Sethei, Samakki Mean Chey, Kampong Chhnang, with 2,928 workers. [Para 30][Para 31] The employer itself asked the provincial Labour Department on 5 August 2019 to help resolve five worker demands on working conditions; one was settled at conciliation on 27 August and four were referred to the Council. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 14] At the hearing the parties agreed issues 1, 2 and 3, leaving only the demand that wages be paid twice a month on regular dates. [Para 17][Para 18][Para 32]

## 2. Issues in dispute
4. Whether the employer must pay wages twice a month, on fixed dates, to the workers? [Para 32][Para 45]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** payment is not regular — a wage due on the 10th was paid on the 19th and one due on the 25th was paid on the 29th, and in some months wages were paid two or three times; the delays make them late on bank repayments and expose them to bank penalties. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 37][Para 55]
**Employer (respondent):** it intends regular payment as before, but asks to postpone the September, October and November payments and promises regularity from December, because the company faces an economic crisis, has insufficient funds and no reserve, and cannot control when money reaches its bank account, so payment follows the incoming transfer. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40][Para 42][Para 44]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay wages twice a month to the workers on a regular basis. [Para 61][Para 62]

The panel applied Article 116 of the Labour Law, which requires wages to be paid at least twice a month with no more than sixteen days between payments, and Article 1 of Prakas No. 442 of 21 September 2018, which from January 2019 requires two payments a month, the first in the second week and the second in the fourth week, the first being 50% of net monthly wage and the second the remainder together with allowances and other benefits. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47][Para 48] It found the employer's former practice — the 10th and the 25th — consistent with the spirit of Article 116 and Prakas 442, but that the employer no longer fixed clear payment dates and had paid on the 19th or 29th in some months. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 52][Para 54] Following Award 36/11, the panel held that delaying wages by two or three months before paying is improper and contrary to Article 116, and that irregular payment does not conform to the spirit of the Labour Law. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 59] It therefore rejected the employer's request to postpone: an employer cannot defer wages in a way that contradicts Article 116 and Article 1 of Prakas 442, and the panel made no allowance for the three-month grace period the employer sought. [Para 39][Para 60][Para 61]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Article 116 of the Labour Law and Article 1 of Prakas No. 442 of 21 September 2018 impose a duty to pay wages twice a month on fixed dates — the first in the second week and the second in the fourth week — and an employer's cash-flow or banking difficulty does not suspend that duty or justify postponing payment; where the employer has abandoned a compliant date pattern for ad hoc payment on the 19th or 29th, the panel will order restoration of regular twice-monthly payment. [Para 48][Para 52][Para 59][Para 60]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the employer's earlier 10th-and-25th practice already conformed to the spirit of the law, and its reference to the workers' bank-penalty exposure as the practical consequence of delay, were explanatory rather than necessary to the order. [Para 37][Para 52]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The operative order is printed as payment "twice a month in one month February" at [Para 61] and "twice in one month February" at [Para 47], an OCR intrusion of "February" into the recurring-month formula; the reasoning at [Para 48] and [Para 62] states the order without it.
- The panel cites "Article 166" at [Para 52] where Article 116 is plainly meant; the same Article 116 is quoted correctly at [Para 45] and [Para 48].
- The header merges the fax number with a stray date fragment — "09210 71 September 2019: none" — at [Para 1], and the Prakas is printed with stray figures, "ប្រាក់ ៤០ ឈ្នួល" and a truncated "អត្ថ-១៩ ប្រយោជន៍", at [Para 47].
- The signature block at [Para 64] is partly lost; only the employer-party arbitrator's name (Kol Vathana) survives legibly, the worker-party arbitrator's signature is corrupted, and the chair's name appears only in the header.
- The award lists twelve worker representatives from four separate unions at the factory; they are abbreviated here as Mr L, Mr D, Mr T, Mr P, Mr N, Mr M, Mr E, Mr Y, Mr K, Mr M, Mr N and Mr S. One entry, "ឌឿ ភក្តី", carries the company name in place of a role at [Para 1].
