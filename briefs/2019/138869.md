# Award No. 004/19 — Ever Green E-Beryl (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 5 February 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Pen Bunchhea (chair) · Doc ID 138869

## 1. Facts
Ever Green E-Beryl (Cambodia), a Phnom Penh garment factory with 139 workers, suspended the workers' contracts for five days in December 2018 — 18 to 21 December and 28 to 29 December — paying 50% of wages during the suspension. [Para 37][Para 42][Para 48] The workers' three demands (stop unlawful suspensions, repay wages for the suspended days, provide enough work) were merged into a single issue at the hearing. [Para 2][Para 4][Para 22] The employer did not appear; only a GMAC official attended, without any letter delegating authority to represent the company, and the panel heard the case in the employer's absence. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 70]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the suspension of the workers' contracts from 18 to 21 December 2018 and from 28 to 29 December 2018 was carried out in accordance with the procedure of the Labour Law? [Para 90]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer suspended contracts without notice to or authorisation from the Ministry of Labour, paying only 50%, so the suspensions were unlawful and the missing 50% is owed to reach 100% for the days 18–21 and 28–29 December 2018; an employer that hires workers must give them enough work. [Para 42][Para 44][Para 45]

**Employer (respondent):** absent from the hearing; through the GMAC official the company put forward only its position on payment of contract-severance, explaining the suspensions by the absence of buyers and of work, and noting that it had posted notices calling workers in for two days a week. [Para 21][Para 52][Para 54]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer is ordered to carry out contract suspensions in accordance with the Labour Law and to pay a further 50% of wages, completing 100%, to the workers for the suspensions of 18–21 and 28–29 December 2018. [Para 106][Para 107]

On procedure, the panel held it could proceed in the employer's absence under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099: the employer had been properly invited through the GMAC official and gave no valid reason for not attending, so it forfeited its right to respond to the workers' claims. [Para 59][Para 68][Para 101] It also confirmed that the four worker representatives accredited by the Department of Labour Disputes on 2 January 2019 could represent the workforce under Article 1 of Prakas No. 362, since the enterprise had no most-representative union. [Para 80][Para 82][Para 87]

On the merits, Article 71(11) allows suspension only for serious material or economic difficulty, subject to two conditions: the suspension must be under the supervision of the labour inspector and must not exceed two months. [Para 91] Article 72(1) removes the wage obligation during a suspension, but only where the suspension itself was lawful. [Para 94][Para 95] Following Awards 21/03, 01/04, 60/06, 74/07, 27/08, 109/09 and 008/17, the panel held that an unlawful suspension obliges the employer to pay 100% of wages and benefits even where there is no work to give. [Para 97][Para 98] Here the employer produced no written authorisation from the labour inspector, so the suspension was unlawful and the 50% already paid must be topped up. [Para 103][Para 105]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that suspends employment contracts for economic difficulty without prior notice to and authorisation from the labour inspector suspends unlawfully, and must then pay the full 100% of wages and benefits for the suspended days; the exemption from wages in Article 72(1) of the Labour Law is available only where the suspension was itself carried out lawfully. [Para 95][Para 97][Para 103]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that an employer who hires workers should provide them with enough work, and its acceptance of the GMAC official's account that the owner was abroad for a funeral, were not part of the operative reasoning. [Para 21][Para 43]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Dates are printed inconsistently: the conciliation report is said to have reached the Council on 14 January 2017 and to bear the number and date 004 dated 14 January 2017, though the report itself is dated 11 January 2019; the representatives' accreditation letter is dated 2 January 2019 in one place and 2 December 2019 in another. [Para 11][Para 31][Para 38] In stating the issue the panel writes "18 to 12 December" where the claim and the order say 18 to 21 December, and the operative paragraph is cut off mid-date ("29 December 2…"). [Para 90][Para 106][Para 107] The letter number in the GMAC notification is OCR noise ("069 គឺ បរ អត់ 2017 23"). [Para 19] The signature block is largely destroyed, leaving the employer-appointed arbitrator's name only in the header. [Para 1][Para 109] Company name spelling varies (អេវើ ហ្គ្រីន អី ភែរឹល / អ៊ីភែរីល / អេវឿ).
