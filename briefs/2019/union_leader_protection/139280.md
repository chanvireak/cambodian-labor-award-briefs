# Award No. 104/19 — Bou Kir Garment Factory (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 23 December 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun for the employer party, Leav Sovanna for the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 139280

## 1. Facts
Bou Kir Garment Factory (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. is a garment-cutting and sewing enterprise at Pong Tuek, Ang Snuol, Kandal, employing 4,556 workers. [Para 79][Para 81] Workers filed fourteen demands on working conditions on 1 November 2019; conciliation on 18 November 2019 failed entirely, and the file reached the Council on 21 November 2019. [Para 20][Para 23][Para 24] At the hearing the parties settled issues 1, 2, 6, 8, 9, 11, 12, 13 and 14, leaving issues 3, 4, 5, 7 and 10 for the panel. [Para 29][Para 30] The award is expressly **non-binding**. [Para 31][Para 161]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must transfer the union dues deducted from the wages of members of the Cambodia Development Union into that union's bank account? [Para 82]
2. Whether the employer must take back Ms H Sreun as a union leader and repay her wages from the date of dismissal, as Labour Inspector Decision No. 730/19 requires? [Para 87][Para 88]
3. Whether the workers are entitled to their wages and other benefits during the seven-day strike of 8 to 15 July 2019? [Para 94][Para 125]
4. Whether the panel has jurisdiction to order the employer to terminate its security-service contract with VIP Security Company? [Para 142]
5. Whether the employer is obliged to distribute a share of its profits to the workers? [Para 148]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer interfered in the union's internal affairs and colluded with Mr S Sophean, the union president, paying the October dues to him personally while refusing letters from the vice-president and secretary; the security company used violence against Ms H Sreun at the employer's direction; the strike was caused by an administrative officer's improper speech and by cuts to benefits; and other enterprises such as Yong Wa and ACLEDA Bank share profits with staff. [Para 84][Para 85][Para 90][Para 98][Para 100][Para 105]
**Employer (respondent):** it paid the October dues to Mr S Sophean because as union president he alone was entitled to receive and manage them, which is not interference; the Minister of Labour revised the inspector's decision and authorised the dismissal; the security company was contracted for security, not to interfere with the union, and was never instructed to use violence; Article 332 suspends the contract during a strike so no wages are owed; and it already grants benefits above the legal minimum. [Para 86][Para 93][Para 99][Para 102][Para 104][Para 106]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to transfer the union dues deducted from members' wages into the Cambodia Development Union's bank account; the claims on reinstatement, strike wages, the security contract and profit-sharing were dismissed, three of them for want of jurisdiction. [Para 156][Para 157][Para 159][Para 160]

**Issue 3 — union dues.** Article 26(1) of the Trade Union Law requires the employer, once it has the worker's written consent, to deduct dues from that worker's wage and remit them to the union, and Article 2(1) of Prakas No. 300 K.B/Pr.K. of 2 July 2018 requires remittance into the union's bank account and forbids the employer from paying the dues on the worker's behalf. [Para 108][Para 111][Para 112][Para 113] On 17 September 2019 the employer received a request from Mr S Sophean as union president, and paid the October 2019 dues to him in person rather than into the account. [Para 114] That did not comply with Article 2 of Prakas No. 300, so transfer was ordered. [Para 115][Para 116]

**Issue 4 — reinstatement.** The Labour Inspector decided on 24 July 2019 that Ms H Sreun be reinstated with back pay, the employer appealed, and the Minister revised that decision to permit the dismissal. [Para 88][Para 89][Para 118] The panel asked whether it had jurisdiction. [Para 119] Article 87 of the Law on the Organisation of the Courts gives jurisdiction over administrative matters to the civil court of first instance and the civil chamber of the Court of Appeal while no administrative chamber exists, so the panel has no jurisdiction over a challenge to the Minister's unilateral decision; the workers' remedy is to sue in the competent court. [Para 120][Para 122][Para 123][Para 124]

**Issue 5 — strike wages.** Article 332(1) suspends the employment contract during a strike and provides that no wages are paid, and Article 334 obliges the employer to pay striking workers only where it hired replacements. [Para 125][Para 131] The employer hired no replacements. [Para 95][Para 137] Article 324 also requires seven days' prior notice of a strike, which the workers did not show. [Para 134][Para 135][Para 137] The panel therefore held the strike procedurally improper and the workers entitled to no wages or benefits for 8–15 July 2019. [Para 138][Para 139]

**Issue 7 — security contract.** Article 302 defines a collective labour dispute as one about working conditions, exercise of professional-organisation rights, or recognition of such an organisation in the enterprise, and Article 32 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY limits the panel to disputes so defined. [Para 142][Para 143][Para 144] This claim concerns the service contract between the employer and VIP Security Company, not a matter under Article 302, so there was no jurisdiction. [Para 145][Para 146][Para 147]

**Issue 10 — profit share.** Article 2 leaves the employer the right to manage and direct the enterprise, and prior awards hold that an employer may set bonuses and profit shares unilaterally provided the arrangement is lawful and reasonable. [Para 148][Para 149][Para 150][Para 151] No law, regulation or agreement obliged this employer to share profits, so the matter lay within its discretion and the claim was refused. [Para 153][Para 154][Para 155]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Union dues deducted from a member's wage must be remitted into the union's own bank account, never handed to an officer in cash: Article 26(1) of the Trade Union Law and Article 2(1) of Prakas No. 300 make the account transfer mandatory, and paying an individual officer — even the president who requested it — is non-compliance. [Para 111][Para 113][Para 115] Three further rules bound the panel's jurisdiction: it cannot review a ministerial decision revising a labour inspector's order, because Article 87 of the Law on the Organisation of the Courts reserves administrative challenges to the ordinary courts; it cannot hear a claim about the employer's contract with a service provider, which falls outside the Article 302 definition of a collective labour dispute; and it will not order a profit share where no law or agreement requires one. [Para 122][Para 145][Para 153] A strike launched without the seven days' notice required by Article 324 earns no wages, and Article 334 obliges payment only where replacements were hired. [Para 131][Para 137]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that an employer may unilaterally design incentive bonuses to raise output and shorten production time, and that it may attach conditions to a profit share so long as they are reasonable and lawful, are general observations on managerial prerogative rather than holdings. [Para 150][Para 151]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award date is 23 December 2019 on its face [Para 1], but the parties' extension ran only to 24 December 2019 from 16 December 2019 [Para 33]; the reasons close on the extension, not on the printed date.
- The panel's chair is printed "ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈាន" in the composition [Para 1] and "ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា" at the signature [Para 162]; the worker-party arbitrator's signature line at [Para 162] is blank and her name is lost.
- The panel is created on "២៥ ខែវិច្ឆិកា ឆ្នាំ ២០១៩ ២០១៩", the year doubled [Para 26].
- The dismissal letter is dated 25 September 2019 [Para 43], the final-wage notice 7 October 2019 [Para 44] and the suspension letter 5 July 2019 [Para 61]; the strike minute carries two dates, "២ ១០ ខែកក្កដា" [Para 50].
- The employer is repeatedly named "និយោជកម្ពុជា" ("Cambodian employer") where "the employer" is meant, an OCR artefact. [Para 132][Para 139]
- The parties selected a non-binding award [Para 31] but the signature block still carries the standard binding formula; the non-binding text at [Para 161] controls.
