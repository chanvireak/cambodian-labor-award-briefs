# Award No. 266/15 — Sin Hong Yang Faniy Cher (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 28 October 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom chosen by the employer party, An Non chosen by the worker party, Aing Eng Tong chair) · Doc ID 139448

## 1. Facts
A Phnom Penh garment factory employing about 387 workers announced in May 2015 that it would suspend production for shortage of raw materials, paid only 50 per cent of May wages, and then stopped work altogether from 1 June to 31 August 2015 without seeking the labour inspector's approval. [Para 27][Para 37][Para 50][Para 55] Workers who returned on 1 September 2015 found no employer or representative and no notice whether production would resume; the factory had closed. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45] Ninety-two workers thumbprinted an authorisation to the Workers' Development Union Federation on 26 August 2015, but the document carried only identity numbers and thumbprints, no names. [Para 29][Para 58]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay termination compensation to the workers in accordance with the Labour Law? [Para 31][Para 85]

That single unresolved claim was narrowed at the hearing to the two workers who appeared in person, the panel having first decided who the parties were and whether it could hear the case in the employer's absence. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 87]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the factory stopped operating from June 2015 and paid no wages after May; the employer never contacted them or said whether work would resume, so the company has closed its doors and the contracts were cut off by the employer. [Para 36][Para 39][Para 45][Para 92] They claim termination indemnity, notice pay, annual leave and final wages. [Para 34]
**Employer (respondent):** did not attend the hearing, filed no documents and gave no reason for its absence. [Para 13][Para 16][Para 68]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted in part, for two workers only — the panel ordered the employer to pay each of them damages in lieu of notice under Articles 75 and 77, dismissal indemnity under Article 89, indemnity for unused annual leave under Articles 166 and 167, moral damages under Article 91, and full wages for the period of the unlawful suspension, 1 June to 31 August 2015. No riel or USD figures are printed. [Para 152][Para 156][Para 157][Para 161]

The panel first fixed the parties. Under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 a party may appear in person or through a representative holding written authority; the authorisation of 26 August 2015 bore only identity numbers and thumbprints, with no names to link them to individuals, and the workers failed to supply the promised name list by the 19 October 2015 deadline. [Para 56][Para 58][Para 61][Para 63] Following Award No. 230/15, the panel held that such a document does not confer valid written authority, so only the two workers present in person were claimants. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 66][Para 67]

On the employer's absence, Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 lets the panel proceed where a party was properly invited and failed to appear without good reason; the invitation had been served at the factory and signed for by the company's security guard. [Para 69][Para 74][Para 75][Para 76] The panel heard the case in the employer's absence and treated the employer as having waived its right to reply. [Para 78][Para 83][Para 84]

Turning to liability, the panel found the employer had stopped production, stayed away, made no contact and shown no intention of continuing the contracts, and concluded the employer had terminated them. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 93][Para 94] Article 87(3) provides that closing an enterprise does not release the employer from the obligations in Section 3 on termination of contracts, and following Award No. 096/15 the panel held the closure gave no escape from those obligations. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 100]

On the contract type, both workers had signed two successive six-month fixed-duration contracts and were then given no written contract at all; under Article 67(8), a fixed-duration contract of two years or less that expires with neither party expressing an intention continues silently as a contract of unspecified duration, so both held indefinite contracts. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 105][Para 107] Article 74 then required a valid reason relating to the worker's aptitude or conduct or the needs of the enterprise, plus written notice; neither was given. [Para 108][Para 114][Para 120]

Finally, on the suspension, Article 71(11) permits suspension of enterprise activity only for serious economic or material difficulty, for no more than two months, and under the labour inspector's supervision; Article 72(1) removes the wage obligation only where the suspension is lawful. [Para 139][Para 140][Para 143] No approval had been sought for the three-month stoppage. [Para 148] Following a line of earlier awards, the panel held the employer owed 100 per cent of wages for the whole period even though no work was available. [Para 145][Para 149][Para 151]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union's authority to represent workers must name them: a transfer letter carrying only identity numbers and thumbprints, unaccompanied by a name list, is not written authorisation under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099, and the panel will decide only for workers who appear in person. [Para 59][Para 61][Para 66] Where an employer stops production and disappears without lawful suspension, the contracts are treated as terminated by the employer, the closure does not discharge its termination obligations under Article 87(3), and the workers are owed full wages for the whole period of the unlawful stoppage as well as the ordinary termination entitlements. [Para 94][Para 100][Para 149][Para 151]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's reliance on the employer's procedural waiver to accept the workers' account without rebuttal, and its observation that the invitation was validly served on a security guard, are comments on procedure rather than on the substantive rule. [Para 75][Para 83]
