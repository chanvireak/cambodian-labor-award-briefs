# Award No. 066/19 — Im & V Ang Thana Syunnal Manu Fak Cheu Ring L.T.D. (Arbitration Council, 29 August 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Suthy, employer-appointed; Tuon Siphann, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhean, chair appointed by both) · non-binding award · Doc ID 139114

## 1. Facts
The employer runs a garment cutting-and-sewing factory at Trapeang Chheuk, Kampong Chhnang town, with 2,390 workers and no union holding most-representative status in the enterprise. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45] Workers lodged 13 demands on working conditions with the provincial labour department on 4 July 2019; conciliation on 24 July 2019 settled 7 and left 6 unresolved, which were referred to the Council on 26 July 2019 and heard on 7 August 2019. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 16][Para 13] The six surviving claims concern a yearly skill allowance, machine allocation in the computer-automated section, the midday meal allowance, piece rates, a piece-rate cut of 27 April 2019, and holiday pay. [Para 2][Para 3][Para 4][Para 5][Para 6][Para 7]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the 2019 skill allowance (ប្រាក់ជំនាញ) as it did in previous years? [Para 114]
2. Whether the employer must assign fixed-duration contract workers in the computer-automated section to 7 machines, the same as workers on undetermined-duration contracts? [Para 137]
3. Whether the employer must add 1,000 riel a day to the midday meal allowance? [Para 148]
4. Whether the piece rates set by the employer allow a worker of average skill to earn the minimum wage? [Para 171][Para 172]
5. Whether the employer must refrain from cutting piece rates in the ironing, press-check and packing sections? [Para 186]
6. Whether the employer must pay 300% for work on holidays? [Para 200]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The skill allowance of USD 1–5 per worker per month paid every year from 2004 to 2018 became an agreement and cannot be ended because the parent company closed; both contract types do the same work at the same machine speed for the same wage, so the 8-machine/7-machine split is discrimination; prices have risen, piece rates have not been revised since 2016, and more than 80% of workers cannot reach the minimum wage; the cut of 27 April 2019 was made without notice; and other companies pay 300% on holidays. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 63][Para 64][Para 74][Para 78][Para 89][Para 90][Para 94]
**Employer (respondent):** It cannot pay the 2019 allowance because the registered parent at Chak Angre Krom closed for economic reasons, it paid severance to over 2,000 workers there and over 700 at Kampong Chhnang, and it spent the remainder on the provincial factory; 8-machine workers run slower machines and more complex styles and were told at hiring they would hold 8; the cut followed the buyer's order and price after styles were simplified; and it already pays a full day's wage plus USD 2 for holiday work, exceeding the law. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60][Para 66][Para 67][Para 68][Para 91][Para 92][Para 95][Para 210]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 4 alone: the employer was ordered to recalculate the piece rates so that a worker of average skill earns piece-rate pay equal to the minimum wage; issues 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 were dismissed, and the award is non-binding until the 30-day objection period expires. [Para 214][Para 217][Para 218][Para 219][Para 220]

On issue 1 the panel applied the doctrine of past practice (ការអនុវត្ត កន្លងមក), which it grounded in Article 65 paragraphs 2 and 3 of the Labour Law on oral contracts and in Award No. 95/09 (Tak Phat): a practice repeated regularly and accepted by both sides becomes an implied term. The allowance from 2004 to 2018 met that test, but the panel followed 95/09 further to hold that a past practice ends where the parties agree otherwise or where the circumstances founding it change, and it found the closure of the parent company, the severance paid and the reinvestment in Kampong Chhnang were such a change. [Para 115][Para 119][Para 122][Para 125][Para 128][Para 131][Para 133][Para 134][Para 136]

On issue 2 the panel read Article 2 of the Labour Law as conferring the employer's right to organise and manage the enterprise, provided it acts lawfully and reasonably, and found the 8-machine allocation touched neither wage, seniority nor benefits. It placed the burden of proving discrimination on the party alleging it, and held the workers produced no specific evidence that styles and machine speeds were alike. [Para 138][Para 141][Para 143][Para 146][Para 147]

On issue 3 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules or past practice obliging a further 1,000 riel, and characterised the demand as one "better than the law", i.e. an interest dispute. [Para 148][Para 149][Para 150] It then held, under Article 54 of the 2016 Trade Union Law and Prakas No. 099 SKBY, that a Council award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement for one year and so may only be made where the claimant is a most-representative union or a union entitled to conclude one; finding no such status, it declined to consider the claim. [Para 161][Para 163][Para 164][Para 167][Para 168]

On issue 4, Prakas No. 465/19 of 5 October 2018 set the 2019 minimum wage at USD 182 a month and allowed piece-rate workers the greater of actual output pay or that sum; Article 108 of the Labour Law requires piece rates to yield at least the guaranteed minimum. Rates had last been set in 2016, and on the payroll slips filed the panel found more than half of the ironing (pong) section and nearly half of finishing group B below the minimum. It rejected the employer's topping-up as a cure: the rate itself must be recalculated for average skill. [Para 172][Para 175][Para 177][Para 181][Para 182][Para 183][Para 184][Para 185]

On issue 5 the panel accepted that the cut from 5,000 to 4,800 riel and from USD 0.165 to USD 0.15 on style PN 198042 followed the buyer's order after pockets and sleeves were reduced, noted the workers did not contest that explanation, and found no evidence of an unjustified reduction. [Para 191][Para 196][Para 197][Para 198][Para 199]

On issue 6 the panel read Article 4 of Prakas No. 10 SKBY of 4 February 1999, and Article 164 of the Labour Law, as entitling a holiday worker to one additional day's wage, i.e. 200% in total; the employer paid that plus USD 2. Since the 300% demand went beyond the law, it too was an interest dispute, and the panel dismissed it for the same want of a most-representative union. [Para 205][Para 208][Para 210][Para 212][Para 213]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A practice repeated regularly and accepted by both parties becomes an implied term binding the employer, but it lapses where the economic circumstances that founded it have changed and no new practice has replaced it; separately, piece rates must be set so that a worker of average skill working normally reaches the statutory minimum wage, and paying a top-up after the event does not discharge that duty. [Para 125][Para 133][Para 177][Para 184]

**Obiter dicta:** That a demand exceeding the Labour Law is a matter of interest, not right, and that an interest dispute cannot be entertained at all without a most-representative union, were each sufficient to dispose of issues 3 and 6 yet were reasoned generally. [Para 149][Para 164][Para 210]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's name is unstable: the header, the party block and the reasons print at least six renderings of the same Khmer string (e.g. "អ៊ឹម & វ៉េ", "អឹម & វ៉េ", "M&V"), and only the party-block form is used here. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 36][Para 43] The order extending the award date prints both years — "ថ្ងៃទី ១៨ ខែសីហា ឆ្នាំ ២០២៣ ២០១៩" — so the extension runs from 18 August to 29 August 2019, not 2023. [Para 24] The issue-2 summary prints the machine count as "៧២" where the reasons and the dispositive order say 7, and one sentence carries a stray "១៥" before "កំណត់". [Para 137][Para 141][Para 147] The past-practice article is printed "មាត្រា ៦៥០" (Article 650), obviously Article 65. [Para 115] Sections of the reasons are fused where OCR dropped line breaks, e.g. the interview transcript of the worker at para 71 runs into a bare "..។" at para 72. [Para 71][Para 72] The six worker representatives are named in the recognition letter; they are redacted here. [Para 110]
