# Award No. 168/16 — YVP Trading Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 9 August 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Eing Sothy, employer-appointed; On Non, worker-appointed; Tang Tri, presiding) · binding award by agreement of the parties · Doc ID 139068

## 1. Facts
The YVP factory workers' union, affiliated to the Federation of Workers' Unions, brought a collective dispute over nine demands; conciliation on 7 July 2016 left three unresolved, which reached the Council on 13 July 2016. YVP Trading Co., Ltd is a garment manufacturer with about 720 workers, and its local union was registered with the Ministry of Labour on 12 January 2016. The parties agreed at the hearing to a binding award. [Para 15][Para 38][Para 39][Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must supply three sets of work clothing a year? [Para 43]
2. Whether the employer must pay compensation in lieu of annual leave calculated on the average of total wages over the last twelve months, both where the contract has not been terminated and where it has? [Para 86]
3. Whether the employer must add the seniority bonus to the base wage when calculating overtime pay? [Para 112]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the Labour Law obliges the employer to supply work clothing for personal safety and to protect against staining, other factories do so, and the demand rose from two sets to three because the country has developed; compensation in lieu of annual leave must be averaged over twelve months under Article 168, and two former union leaders were paid without that average; the seniority bonus is itself wages, so it belongs in the overtime base, as the Council ordered in Sun Well Shoes. [Para 44][Para 49][Para 54][Para 57][Para 58]
**Employer (respondent):** the company does not require uniforms so it has no capacity to supply clothing; it accepts its past practice of paying compensation in lieu of leave while contracts continued was contrary to law and has stopped it; and it applies the Ministry's annex to the overtime authorisation, which uses the base wage, and labour inspection has never faulted that calculation. [Para 47][Para 52][Para 53][Para 59][Para 61]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim for three sets of clothing was dismissed but the employer was ordered to supply two sets a year free of charge; the claim for average-based compensation while contracts subsist was dismissed, but where the contract is terminated the employer must pay it on the twelve-month average; the claim to include the seniority bonus in the overtime base was dismissed, Arbitrator On Non dissenting. [Para 146][Para 147][Para 148][Para 149][Para 150]

Standing: Article 11 of the Trade Union Law 2016 gives a registered union the rights the law confers, including the right to represent its members before the Council; the local union held its certificate and its president attended, so it was competent to bring the case. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 71][Para 72]

Clothing: Article 6 of Prakas No. 307 of 14 December 2007 requires the employer to give each worker at least two sets a year free of charge, each consisting of at least trousers or a skirt and a shirt. The employer had never supplied any. Under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 the panel may order an unlawful practice stopped and full compensation, so it ordered the practice stopped and two sets supplied; the demand for three exceeded the Prakas and had no legal basis. [Para 73][Para 76][Para 79][Para 80][Para 85]

Compensation in lieu of leave: Article 167(2) makes payment in lieu arise only where the contract is cut short before the worker has taken the leave, and Article 167(3) voids any agreement to waive or forgo annual leave. The panel held that payment in lieu is possible only when the employment relationship ends, and that while it subsists the law prohibits such payment — the employer's former practice was therefore unlawful, and the workers had no right to it. [Para 90][Para 93][Para 94][Para 100]

Where the contract is terminated, Article 168 requires the average of wages, bonuses, benefits and compensation over the twelve months before the leave, so the employer's calculation without that average breached the Article and the claim succeeded to that extent. [Para 102][Para 109][Para 110][Para 111]

Overtime base: Article 139 (new) and Article 5 of Prakas No. 80 of 1 March 1999 fix the 50 per cent and 100 per cent uplifts but neither defines the wage on which they are applied. Article 102 defines wages broadly and Article 103 lists their components, but the list is illustrative and does not oblige every employer to pay every item. No law, Prakas or agreement requires the seniority bonus to be added to the base wage for overtime, and the workers produced no contract or agreement to that effect. [Para 112][Para 114][Para 119][Para 125][Para 132]

The panel further held, on equity under Article 312, that the seniority bonus is already included once in the monthly wage package; adding it again to the overtime base would count the same money twice. The Ministry's annex to Letter No. 3096 uses "base wage" excluding the seniority bonus and is a reasonable formula, though it is not itself a binding legal norm. [Para 135][Para 139][Para 140][Para 143][Para 159]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Compensation in lieu of unused annual leave is payable only when the employment relationship has ended; while the contract subsists the employer must grant the leave itself and any payment in lieu — or any agreement waiving the leave — is void under Article 167, and only on termination is the amount computed on the average of total wages, bonuses and benefits over the preceding twelve months. [Para 90][Para 93][Para 109]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the seniority bonus rewards length of service, experience, effort and loyalty rather than output, and that the Ministry's annex formula is reasonable but not a binding legal norm. [Para 160][Para 161][Para 159]

[Para 4][Para 5] The company's registration certificate number appears as "1 Inv. 1483 E/2009" and elsewhere as 13509, and the two cannot be reconciled. [Para 105][Para 129] The award's signature block leaves the presiding arbitrator's name to the face sheet only, and the date line closing the award is partly overwritten. [Para 152][Para 166]
