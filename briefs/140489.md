# Case No. 27108 — Archid Garment Factory Cambodia Limited v Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (Arbitration Council, 7 March 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 140489

## 1. Facts
Archid Garment Factory Cambodia Limited, a garment factory at Damnak Thom Village, Sangkat Steung Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employs 930 workers. [Para 27] The Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU) and the Local Union of Khmer Youth Trade Union (KYTU) at the factory, with 377 members, brought a collective dispute; the union does not have most-representative status. [Para 28][Para 29] Eleven demands went to conciliation and eight non-conciliation points were referred to the panel, but five were conciliated at the hearing and the union withdrew another, leaving only the wage-for-no-work demand and the fixed-duration contract demand for decision. [Para 21][Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 6) Whether the company must pay workers full wages when it has no work for them to do, where it has not suspended their labour contracts under the legal procedure? [Para 30][Para 43]
2. (Issue 8) Whether a worker whose fixed-duration contract has been renewed many times for a total duration exceeding two years must be converted to an undetermined-duration contract? [Para 32][Para 63]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, KYFTU and local KYTU):** full wages are owed whenever the company has no work and has not lawfully suspended the contracts; they do not seek back pay for past half-wage periods, and press the demand only for the future and only where no suspension procedure is followed. [Para 30][Para 43] On contracts, continuous renewals totalling over two years should convert to undetermined duration. [Para 49]
**Employer (respondent, Archid Garment Factory Cambodia Limited):** in the past it had no work for only two or three days at a time and paid half wages to those who chose to go home, offering full wages to those who stayed and did other tasks; it will continue to use six-month fixed-duration contracts even beyond two years, and sees no bar to doing so. [Para 31][Para 50][Para 61]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to stop its practice of unlawful labour contract suspension immediately and to ensure future suspensions follow the legal procedure, and ordered the employer to convert any worker whose contract has been renewed many times with a total duration exceeding two years from a fixed-duration to an undetermined-duration contract. [Para 49][Para 66]

On Issue 6, the panel read Article 71(1) and Article 71(11) of the Labour Law as permitting suspension of the contract only where the enterprise faces serious economic or material difficulty, and only on notification to and review by the Labour Inspector; Article 72(1) then relieves the employer of wages. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 39] Where that procedure is skipped, full wages remain owed, following Awards 21/03-Loyal Issue 8, 01/04-New Point Issue 1, 46/04-M&A Issue 1, 60/06-New Max Issue 2 and 74/07-Global Apparel Issue 1. [Para 40] The panel found the company never notified the Inspector and instead agreed half wages with the workers. [Para 41][Para 42] It declined to adjudicate the pure future demand, following 10/03-Jacqsintex and Awards 64/04 City New Issue 4, 36/06-Mondotex Issue 5 and 58/07-8 Stars Sportswear Issue 1, because no one can predict when a shortage will occur or whom it will affect. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48] Clause 34 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 nonetheless empowered it to grant any just civil remedy, including an order to cease illegal conduct, which it used. [Para 48][Para 49]

On Issue 8, Article 67(2) caps a fixed-duration contract and its renewals at two years and makes violation convert the contract to undetermined duration; Article 73(5) adds conversion where no notice of expiry or non-renewal is given. [Para 51][Para 53] The panel followed 36/06-Mondotex Issue 2 and 57/06-Evergreen Issue 3 in reading the two years as a total maximum rather than a per-renewal limit, noted the Law's preference for undetermined duration under Article 67(7) and (8), and treated ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982 as a beneficial aid although not binding. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 60][Para 61] It held the employer's contrary position had no legal basis and gave no specific reason. [Para 62][Para 63]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-duration contract that is renewed repeatedly becomes an undetermined-duration contract once the total of its renewals passes two years; an employer cannot preserve fixed duration by issuing successive six-month contracts, because Article 67(2) sets a total maximum and Article 73(5) converts the contract. [Para 63][Para 66] An employer that leaves workers idle without notifying the Labour Inspector and undergoing review under Article 71(11) must pay full wages for the idle period, and the panel may order the unlawful practice stopped for the future even while refusing to decide a demand that is purely anticipatory. [Para 40][Para 43][Para 48][Para 49]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that undetermined-duration contracts promote job security and long-term commitment, and that ILO Recommendation No. 166 is persuasive though non-binding, go beyond what the orders required. [Para 56][Para 60][Para 61]
