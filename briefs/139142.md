# Award No. 029/15 — British American Tobacco Cambodia Limited (Arbitration Council, 2 March 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, chosen by the employer, dissenting; An Non, chosen by the workers; Kong Phallak, chair) · Doc ID 139142

## 1. Facts
British American Tobacco Cambodia Limited (BATC), a cigarette manufacturer registered in 1996, employed about 269 workers at the time of the hearing. [Para 76][Para 77] Fourteen workers, represented by a union federation president, complained that the company had kept them on successive fixed-duration contracts (FDCs) and then cut them off; the employer recognised only seven, because the others' names carried no thumbprints in the copy it received from the Ministry of Labour. [Para 79][Para 82][Para 84][Para 166] The panel found the ministry's list held 14 names and accepted all 14. [Para 176][Para 183] On 31 December 2014 the employer ended every FDC and paid 5% severance plus unused annual leave; on 19 January 2015 it rehired those who passed a fresh test on new FDCs, and two were not rehired. [Para 110][Para 111][Para 112][Para 143]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must recognise all the pre-2015 contracts of the 14 claimants as undetermined-duration contracts (UDCs)? [Para 108][Para 218]
2. Whether the employer must pay back the 13th and 14th month salaries, the additional day of annual leave earned after three years under Article 166, and a share of profits? [Para 126][Para 255]
3. Whether the employer must pay termination compensation under Articles 75, 77, 89, 91 and 166 of the Labour Law to the workers whose contracts it ended? [Para 140][Para 316]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Their first engagement was an oral, open-ended contract later swapped for FDCs without the notice or severance that dissolving a UDC requires, so their total service passed two years and the contracts became UDCs by law; permanent staff received 13th and 14th month pay and profit shares, and those benefits were withheld from them; and the termination was unlawful, so notice, dismissal and moral damages are owed. [Para 114][Para 116][Para 127][Para 145][Para 146][Para 149]
**Employer (respondent):** The complainants are only seven, the union representative has no standing, the dispute is individual rather than collective, and each FDC was under one year and lawfully renewed with 5% severance paid every time, so no conversion occurred; 13th and 14th month pay and profit shares are reserved to permanent and management staff and depend on performance evaluation; the contracts simply expired. [Para 81][Para 94][Para 100][Para 119][Para 130][Para 133][Para 154][Para 327] It also asked for repayment of the 5% severance already paid. [Para 156][Para 358]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — 13 claimants are declared to hold UDCs; the employer must pay termination compensation under Articles 75, 89, 91 and 166, evaluate and pay 13th and 14th month salary for 2012–2014 to those 13, and pay one extra day of annual leave to 11; the profit-share claim and the 14th claimant's claims are dismissed, and the 13 must repay the 5% severance received. [Para 377][Para 382][Para 385][Para 390]
On procedure, Article 312 confines the panel to the issues in the non-conciliation record, and Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 lets a party appear through any person holding its written authority; following Awards 161/09, 43/10 and 144/12 the panel read "help and assist, protect, resolve, claim" as conferring representation, so the employer's objection failed. [Para 172][Para 181][Para 186][Para 188] It upheld jurisdiction: under Article 302 a collective dispute needs several workers against an employer, a subject of working conditions, and a risk of disrupting the enterprise or social peace, and following Award 45/07 it presumed a dispute referred by the labour inspector to be collective unless the employer proves otherwise. [Para 202][Para 204][Para 217]
On the contract type, Article 67(2) caps a genuine FDC at two years but allows renewal so long as each renewal is no longer than two years, and Article 73(5) deems a contract to continue for an equal term, or to become a UDC where the total exceeds the Article 67 limit, when no notice is given. [Para 221][Para 222][Para 224][Para 227][Para 228] Following Award 10/03 and ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982, the panel read the reference to total duration as cumulative, so an FDC chain whose aggregate passes two years converts to a UDC. [Para 224][Para 229][Para 234][Para 235] On the contract tables, 13 claimants had more than two years of FDC service and one, Mr T (01 March 2013 to 31 December 2014), did not, so only 13 converted. [Para 246][Para 248][Para 251]
On the 13th and 14th month salaries, point 3 of Article 5 of the internal rules grants permanent staff an annual bonus of two months' base wage, assessed on performance, plus a discretionary payment if profit targets are met. [Para 134][Para 257] The 13 now hold UDCs and so qualify, but the employer had never evaluated them; the panel ordered an evaluation for 2012 to 2014 and payment according to it, noting that Article 120 bars claims older than three years. [Para 260][Para 270][Para 273] On annual leave, Article 166(4) adds one working day for each three years of service, on the 19-to-23-day scale of Awards 62/04, 68/05, 81/05, 33/07 and 11/09; 11 claimants had over three years, so the extra day was owed to them and not to the two who did not. [Para 278][Para 283][Para 287][Para 290][Para 291]
On the profit share, the panel applied Article 2 of the Labour Law and its earlier awards to hold that an employer may set bonus and profit-share conditions unilaterally provided they are lawful and reasonable; the scheme was confined to management-level UDC staff and was irregular, no law requires it, and no evidence showed ordinary UDC staff ever received it, so the claim failed. [Para 296][Para 301][Para 302][Para 313][Para 315] On termination, Article 74 allows an employer to end a UDC at will on written notice and for a valid reason; the panel found none, since the company rehired workers after the terminations, and held the notice letter of 05 December 2014 ineffective for want of any signature or thumbprint on receipt, its notice period in any case falling short of the one month Article 75 requires for staff of over two years' service. [Para 325][Para 329][Para 330][Para 342][Para 344]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where fixed-duration contracts are renewed in a continuous chain and their combined duration exceeds two years, the relationship converts to an undetermined-duration contract by operation of Articles 67(2) and 73(5), whatever the parties labelled each renewal; and on such a conversion the employer owes the UDC termination package of notice pay, dismissal damages, moral damages and leave, while the worker must return the 5% severance paid at each fixed-term expiry, since the same service cannot be compensated twice. [Para 235][Para 251][Para 361][Para 372][Para 376][Para 390]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remarks that paying out unused annual leave while a worker is still employed offends Article 167(3), and that it would therefore not order repayment of leave money already paid, were not necessary to any order. [Para 366][Para 370]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is heavily corrupted. The number of claimants is printed as 19, 18, 14 and 13 in different places, and the operative paragraphs say "13" while the reasoning several times says "14", including a finding that no valid reason was given for terminating "all 14". [Para 78][Para 158][Para 176][Para 249][Para 329][Para 343] Dates are printed inconsistently: the complaint is received on "31 December 2015" although the letter is dated 30 December 2014 [Para 19]; the panel is constituted on "30 January 2023 2015" [Para 22]; the award date is extended from "20 December 2015" to 2 March 2015 [Para 31]; the termination is dated "31 December 2015" in two places [Para 144][Para 317]; and oral notice of 22 December 2014 sits against a written notice of 05 December 2014. [Para 337][Para 338] The ministry submission says 11 non-conciliated issues while the panel disposes of them in three groups numbered 1–2, 3–5 and 7 with no explanation of the gap. [Para 21][Para 28] The company's name appears in several Khmer transliterations alongside the printed English form. [Para 21][Para 45] The dissent is bound in as an annex signed only by the employer-chosen arbitrator, so the panel was not unanimous. [Para 393][Para 394][Para 411]
