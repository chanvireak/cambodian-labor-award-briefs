# Award No. 239/15 — Cambodia Thorb Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 29 September 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, An Non, chair Kong Phalla) · Doc ID 139432

## 1. Facts
Cambodia Thorb (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូ ខូថប), a garment factory in Pou Senchey district, Phnom Penh, employed about 1,592 workers. [Para 76] On 16 December 2014 it suspended the contracts of five union leaders and activists without wages, saying they had incited and blocked other workers during the stoppages of 08–15 December; on the same day it asked the labour inspector for permission to dismiss them, received no answer, and did not appeal to the Minister. [Para 106] It then sued in the Phnom Penh court on 05 January 2015 for dissolution of the five contracts. [Para 137] Six demands went to conciliation on 24 August 2015; four remained unsettled for the hearing of 10 September 2015. [Para 16]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must convert the weekly target bonus into a daily target bonus and pay it every day? [Para 85]
2. Whether the employer must raise the midday meal allowance from 2,000 to 2,500 riel a day? [Para 97]
3. Whether the employer must pay a total of 5,500 riel in overtime meal allowance, 1,000 riel more per overtime hour? [Para 100]
4. Whether the employer must take back the five union leaders and activists it suspended? [Para 104]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that the weekly target bonus requires four full weeks of attendance, so a worker absent one day through illness loses the whole bonus; that food, goods and rent have risen and the meal and overtime-meal allowances no longer cover them; and that the five were suspended without the inspector's permission and had not caused the disorder the company alleged. [Para 93] [Para 98] [Para 102] [Para 110]
**Employer (respondent):** that the target bonus is calculated as a weekly package and daily payment would make payroll unworkable, and the attendance condition fairly rewards workers who reach the target; that the law requires no midday meal allowance at all and the 2,000 riel is a goodwill payment; that it already pays 3,500 riel for overtime meals, better than the law; and that the five committed serious misconduct under Article 83, with dismissal now before the court. [Para 94] [Para 99] [Para 103] [Para 126]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the three benefit claims were refused and reinstatement was refused, but the employer was ordered to pay 100 per cent of wages to the two claimant leaders for the period of their suspension while the court's decision is awaited. [Para 332] [Para 333] [Para 334]

On issue 1, Article 2 gives the employer the right to organise and manage the enterprise, and the panel had held in earlier awards that an employer may set bonus schemes above the statutory minimum by unilateral decision to encourage productivity; the weekly target bonus, its percentage scale and its attendance conditions were found reasonable and not contrary to Article 2, so the demand to pay it daily failed. [Para 167] [Para 169] [Para 176] [Para 179] [Para 181]

On issues 2 and 3 the panel found no law, collective agreement, internal rule, contract or past practice creating the higher amounts. Notification 041/11 of 07 March 2011 fixes 2,000 riel a day for overtime meals and the employer was paying 3,500 riel, so it had already exceeded the statutory floor. Each demand was therefore an interests claim, and because this union was not the most representative in the enterprise and no letter transferring the workers' rights to it had been filed, the panel declined jurisdiction over those interests claims. [Para 184] [Para 199] [Para 201] [Para 203] [Para 196] [Para 197]

On issue 4, only two of the five were claimants before the panel, so it considered their case alone. Article 293 and Prakas 305 art 3(3) protect election candidates for 45 days before and after the poll, and the panel followed Cases 40/10 (Meng Yan) and 154/13 in holding that protection runs from election day until the union's leadership is recognised. The employer conceded that both were protected persons, that the suspension fell inside the protection period, and that it had been notified of the candidates on 06 October 2014, so all three conditions were met. [Para 208] [Para 232] [Para 235] [Para 241] [Para 245]

Because the employer suspended the two on 16 December 2014 and sought the inspector's permission the same day, received none, and never appealed to the Minister, the suspension was void under Article 295. [Para 271] But the termination claim was already before the Phnom Penh court, and the panel held that an employer may suspend pending a court decision as a reasonable exercise of its management right — while carrying the obligation to pay full wages, since the suspension was unilateral and its ground appears nowhere in Article 71 or Article 332. It therefore refused reinstatement and ordered 100 per cent wages for the suspension period. [Para 319] [Para 324] [Para 325] [Para 328]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer suspends a protected union leader's contract in order to await a court ruling on its own application to terminate him, the suspension is void for want of the labour inspector's approval under Articles 293 and 295, but the panel will not order reinstatement while the termination claim is pending; it orders full wages for the whole suspension instead, because a unilateral suspension resting on no ground listed in Article 71 or 332 shifts the loss of income to the worker. And a bonus or allowance that exceeds the statutory or ministerial minimum is an interests matter on which the panel will not adjudicate at the suit of a union that is not the most representative in the enterprise. [Para 193] [Para 197] [Para 324]

**Obiter dicta:** that the protected-person test has three cumulative conditions — status, suspension within the protection window, and notification of the candidate's name to the employer — and that a bonus above the statutory minimum is a legitimate unilateral management tool. [Para 230] [Para 169]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award prints the respondent's name only in Khmer, in four variants (ខេមបូ ខូថប, ខេមបូ ខូថ ប, ខេមបូ ខូច ប, ខេមបូជ), so the Latin form used in the title is a transliteration. [Para 1] [Para 150] The panel is recorded as constituted on "02 September 2019 2015", a stray year repeated throughout the document. [Para 18] [Para 25] The suspension letters are dated 16 December 2014 in the findings but 16 October 2014 twice in the reasons. [Para 210] [Para 257] [Para 269] The company's registration number is given as 4577 in one certificate and Inv. 0158 E/2012 in the findings, dated 17 January 2012 in one place and 27 April 2015 in another. [Para 27] [Para 75] Paragraphs 326–328 and 329–334 duplicate the same orders in two forms, and the closing paragraph on the effect of the award is garbled past recovery. [Para 326] [Para 335] Individual names are reduced to role or initial per the briefing convention.
