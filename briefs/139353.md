# Award No. 96/04 — Sport Tex Garment Industry (Arbitration Council, 13 December 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach, chosen by the employer party; An Nan, chosen by the worker party; Ang Eng Thong, chair) · Doc ID 139353

## 1. Facts
Sport Tex Industry Company, on National Road No. 5 at Sangkat Kilometer No. 6, Khan Reussey Keo, employed 1200 workers, 646 of them members of the Free Trade Union of Workers at Sport Tex, which had applied for most representative status and had not received it. [Para 20][Para 35][Para 36][Para 37] On 5 November 2004 the Khan Reussey Keo Labour Inspectorate received a telephone complaint asking the company to apply the agreement of 23 December 2003; conciliation on 10 November 2004 settled one of four issues, and the parties later filed minutes dated 2 December 2004 settling the first issue, which the panel therefore did not reconsider. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 25][Para 26] The basic wage was US$45 a month and the company had moved production to a "water-flow" system which workers found harder, without the wage increase they had been promised. [Para 39]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay an overtime-cooperation incentive of US$5 for October 2004 rather than the US$3 it offered? [Para 11]
2. Whether the employer must pay the overtime-cooperation bonus and the daily productivity bonus in accordance with the agreement of 23 December 2003? [Para 12]
3. Whether the workers may work four hours a day on Saturday with a basic salary of US$65 a month? [Para 13]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the agreement of 23 December 2003 is binding as written — the US$5 overtime-cooperation bonus is kept even when workers are too busy to do overtime, the daily productivity bonus is not conditional, and in the past the bonus was paid even in months with fewer than 22 overtime days; they also seek US$65 a month and a shorter week. [Para 12][Para 30][Para 32][Para 39]
**Employer (respondent):** it applies a graduated scale — US$4.50 when a worker misses two hours of overtime, nothing if four hours are missed — and it did not clearly understand the agreement when it signed; the promise of higher wages came from the buyer, not from the company, and it already pays the US$45 minimum wage of Notification No. 017. [Para 11][Para 31][Para 40][Para 44][Para 49][Para 53]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to offer the US$5 monthly overtime-cooperation bonus to employees who work overtime, except a worker who fails to work overtime for at least two hours, and to stop making the daily productivity bonus conditional on overtime; it rejected the demand for US$65 a month and a four-hour Saturday. [Para 64][Para 65]

*First issue.* The parties settled it by minutes dated 2 December 2004 filed during the proceedings, so the panel declined to reconsider it. [Para 25][Para 26]

*Second issue.* The agreement of 23 December 2003, at point 9(3), makes overtime voluntary and fixes the bonus: US$5 a month where the worker does two hours of overtime a day, the same US$5 kept where the worker is busy and cannot do two hours in the month, and the daily productivity bonus still payable where the worker does eight hours without overtime. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 43] The employer said it had not understood what it signed, but the minutes carry the translator's signature and statement that the translation was proper, and the translator was the company's own; the company is responsible for its translator, so the agreement remains valid and must be implemented. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46] Conditioning the daily productivity bonus on overtime work was found to put indirect pressure on employees to work overtime, inconsistent with that agreement, and the company was ordered to correct the practice. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 64]

*Third issue.* The panel treated the wage demand as an interest dispute: Article 43 of Prakas 99/04 provides that an award settling an interest dispute in place of a collective agreement stays in force one year, so an award would bind every worker in the factory. [Para 56][Para 57] Because the union lacked most representative status, such an award would make workers outside the dispute lose their right to strike over the same interest dispute, which the panel held unfair, and a union without that status has no right to bring an interest dispute at all, following Awards 04/03 (Lida), 06/04 (Chou Sing), 24/03 (Top One), 61/04 (Best Honor), 62/04 (Ecent), 31/03 (Hong Wa), 60/04 (United Arts) and 99/04 (AIA). [Para 58][Para 59] On the merits, the promise of a wage increase was the buyer's, not the employer's; Notification No. 017 sets the minimum wage at US$45, which the employer already pays; and the workers gave no reasons or evidence for US$65. [Para 49][Para 51][Para 52][Para 54][Para 61] On hours, the employer may set the schedule provided it does not contravene the Labour Law, and the company's 48-hour week does. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A conciliation agreement that fixes a bonus is binding on the employer as written, and the employer cannot escape it by saying it did not understand the Khmer text: where the employer's own translator signed the minutes and certified the translation, the risk of mistranslation lies with the employer. A productivity bonus made conditional on doing overtime is unlawful indirect pressure, because overtime must remain voluntary. A union without most representative status cannot bring an interest dispute, since an award in place of a collective agreement would bind non-members and cost them their right to strike. [Para 46][Para 48][Para 59]

**Obiter dicta:** the remarks that the workers receive more benefits than other factories [Para 50], and that the employees lack evidence for US$65 [Para 61], support the rejection but are not the ground of it, which is the union's lack of most representative status.

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The governing agreement is dated three ways: 23 December 2003 in the findings and reasons [Para 29][Para 41], 23 December 2004 at [Para 45], and a separate "agreement on some work conditions dated 19 February 2003" at [Para 20] is invoked at [Para 27] for the same bonus; its clause is point 9(3) at [Para 29] but point 19(3) at [Para 41].
- The bonus rate appears as US$5 and as US$4.50 for the same contingency: US$4.50 if a worker is absent from overtime two hours a month [Para 11], US$4.50 when one overtime day is missed [Para 31], US$5 in the agreement and the order [Para 30][Para 64].
- The trigger is stated as three days missed at [Para 11] but more than two days at [Para 31] and [Para 33].
- The settlement of the first issue is reported but its terms are not set out anywhere in the award [Para 25][Para 26].
- Sentences and citations are duplicated by the OCR: the hearing date twice at [Para 19], "employs a total of 1200 workers" repeated at [Para 21], and the list of prior awards repeated at [Para 58]–[Para 60].
- The panel composition is garbled at [Para 18], where the worker-party arbitrator's line runs into the chair's; the award is headed "not bindinq" at [Para 24]; and the employer is variously "Sport Tex Garment Industry", "Sport Tex Industry Company" and "Sportex" [Para 5].
- The award bears only a file number ("Case: 96/04"), no award number; the file number is used here. Union officers appear as initials; no name invented or expanded.
