# Award No. 06/10 — Yak Jin (Cambodia) Inc. (Arbitration Council, 15 February 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirem, Tun Si Phan, Pen Bun Chhea presiding) · Doc ID 140244

## 1. Facts
Yak Jin (Cambodia) Inc., a garment factory operating since 2006 with roughly 3,500 workers, faced sixteen collective demands lodged by the Khmer Youth Union, which claimed 600 members where the company recognised 414. Conciliation at the Kandal provincial labour department on 4 January 2010 failed on all sixteen points and the report was forwarded to the Council on 19 January 2010; the hearing was held on 29 January 2010. At the hearing the panel settled issues 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 8 and 12 and recorded issues 7, 9 and 12 as withdrawn, leaving issues 3, 10, 11, 13, 14 and 15, with 14 and 15 merged into one dispute. [Para 39][Para 43][Para 44][Para 45][Para 54][Para 57]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay compensation in cash in lieu of untaken annual leave twice a year, at Khmer New Year and at Pchum Ben? [Para 59]
2. (Issue 10) Whether the company must pay workers' wages twice in one month rather than once? [Para 69]
3. (Issue 11) Whether the company must remit to the Khmer Youth Union the union dues deducted for February 2009? [Para 76]
4. (Issue 13) Whether the company must pay every worker a lodging or travel allowance of USD 5 per month? [Para 84]
5. (Issues 14 and 15) Whether the company must continue its past practice of evaluating and raising the base wage every six months? [Para 90]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the annual-leave compensation was demanded twice yearly so that workers could afford to travel to their home provinces; twice-monthly payment was said to be required by the Labour Law and by a Ministry notification; the February 2009 dues had been deducted from 414 members but never remitted; the USD 5 allowance was justified by low wages; and the six-monthly wage-scale evaluation had been applied from 2006 to 2008 and should resume. [Para 61][Para 71][Para 82][Para 86][Para 90]

**Employer (respondent):** payment of annual-leave compensation once a year at year-end was an arrangement the workers had accepted since 2007, and continuing it twice yearly was financially impossible; the company could not move to twice-monthly wages; at the date of the deduction request the union was not yet registered; it had no capacity to fund the USD 5 allowance; and the global economic crisis since late 2008 made further evaluations impossible. [Para 66][Para 73][Para 78][Para 89][Para 96]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — on Issue 10 the panel ordered the company to pay workers' wages twice in one month; the claims under Issues 3, 11, 13 and 14–15 were dismissed. [Para 203][Para 204][Para 205][Para 206][Para 207]

On Issue 3 the panel applied Articles 166 and 167 of the Labour Law: annual leave is a paid entitlement of 1.5 working days per month of service, and any agreement to forgo leave or to accept cash in lieu of it is void. Following Awards 45/05, 94/06, 98/07 and 40/09, and Award 08/07 (Seav Qin) holding that leave may be cashed out only on termination of the contract, it held that compensation paid during employment is contrary to the spirit of Article 167 even where the workers consented, and that the company should instead encourage leave-taking. The claim was therefore dismissed. [Para 97][Para 99][Para 102][Para 105][Para 111]

On Issue 10 the panel read Article 116 as requiring wages to be paid at least twice a month at intervals of no more than sixteen days for "workers" (កម្មករ), and only once a month for "employees", a reading confirmed by Circular 1903 of 24 November 2008. Applying Article 6 and Awards 126/09 and 155/09, it found that the claimants were sewing, cutting, finishing, packing, ironing and inspection staff doing mainly manual work under the employer's direction, not office staff, and so were workers entitled to twice-monthly payment; the company was ordered accordingly. [Para 115][Para 120][Para 123][Para 127][Para 130]

On Issue 11 the panel noted that Article 129 and Article 5 of Prakas 305 permit deduction of dues only on the worker's written authorisation, given at least fifteen days before pay day. But on 15 January 2009, when the union wrote requesting the February deduction, it was not yet registered — registration came on 26 February 2009 — so the request had no validity and no obligation arose. The company had in any event already repaid the February 2009 dues to the workers, so nothing remained owed to the union. [Para 132][Para 136][Para 144][Para 146][Para 154][Para 157]

On Issue 13 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law and no agreement obliging the employer to fund lodging or travel, and held that the demand exceeded what the law grants. It added that in interest disputes it considers whether the claimant union is the most representative in the factory, and that the Khmer Youth Union was not, so the claim was declined on that ground as well. [Para 159][Para 160][Para 163][Para 169][Para 170]

On Issues 14 and 15 the panel accepted that six-monthly re-evaluation between 2006 and late 2008 was a past practice, and that under Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 23 of Decree-Law 38 a practice may interpret an unclear contract. Following Award 95/09 it held that a past practice may end where the parties agree, where circumstances change, or where the original basis disappears; the global economic crisis was a genuine and appropriate reason, and no worker protested between late 2008 and November 2009, which signified acceptance of the new arrangement. The claim was dismissed. [Para 174][Para 177][Para 186][Para 196][Para 200][Para 201]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A statutory entitlement cannot be displaced by an established practice or by the workers' consent: where the Labour Law requires manual workers to be paid twice a month, a long-standing once-monthly payroll is no defence. By contrast, a benefit that rests only on past practice may lawfully be withdrawn where the employer proves a genuine change of circumstances and the workers acquiesce by not protesting. [Para 102][Para 115][Para 186][Para 200]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that the company should encourage workers to take their annual leave rather than pay compensation for it, and its statement that a union lacking most-representative status cannot bring an interest dispute to the Council, were observations going beyond what the dismissal of the two claims required. [Para 111][Para 164]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is internally inconsistent about Issue 12, which the panel lists both among the issues settled at the hearing and among those withdrawn, though it is excluded from the residual list either way. The panel composition is printed in the header but the signature block at the end carries no legible name for the worker-selected arbitrator, whose name survives only in the header. The workforce figure at the findings is corrupted by an OCR digit, so the headcount is legible only as "roughly 3,500". The union-dues reasoning states that 2,000 riel per member was deducted for February and March 2009 without authorisation while elsewhere recording that the February portion had already been repaid, and the sums are not reconciled. Several prior-award numbers and the Prakas and Circular references are partly garbled by OCR. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 54][Para 151][Para 154][Para 208]
