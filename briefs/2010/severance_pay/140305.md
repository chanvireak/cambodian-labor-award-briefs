# Order No. 89/10 — Khing Vey Mean New Fack Cherring Ltd v. Prek Treng Garment Workers' Democratic Union (Arbitration Council, 26 August 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, An Non, Run Saray) · Doc ID 140305

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឃីង វេយ៍ មែន ញូហ្វាក់ ឆឺរីង លីមីធីត — Khing Vey Mean New Fack Cherring Ltd — registered with the Ministry of Commerce on 6 May 2010 and began recruiting on 8 May 2010, operating inside premises at Prek Treng, Sitbo commune, Saang district, Kandal, that it rents from the former Prek Treng factory, which had shut its doors on 13 February 2010. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 40] The Prek Treng enterprise union of the Democratic Garment Workers Union of Cambodia (សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា), through the union at សិប្បកម្ម ព្រែក ត្រែង, brought four demands against Khing Vey: reinstatement of 1,200 workers per the Council's order of 10 February 2010, December 2009 wages withheld from 118 workers, full pay from January 2010 for an unlawful suspension, and severance on factory closure. [Para 3][Para 4][Para 5][Para 6] Conciliation on 23 July 2010 settled two of six points, leaving four to be heard on 16 August 2010, when the workers attended and the company did not. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 17]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the panel may hear the case and decide it in the employer's absence, the employer having been properly invited but declining to appear? [Para 44][Para 47]
2. (Issue 2) Whether Khing Vey is liable under Article 87 of the Labour Law for the four demands that the workers of Prek Treng addressed to it as the new occupant of the old factory? [Para 52][Para 63]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), Democratic Garment Workers Union of Cambodia and the Prek Treng enterprise union:** because Khing Vey's lawyer attended the conciliation at the Kandal provincial department and argued the case there, and because Khing Vey knew of the Prek Treng dispute yet still took the premises, the union holds Khing Vey responsible for the four demands originally filed against Prek Treng. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 35] The union adds that Khing Vey kept Prek Treng's documents, that about 70% of its present workforce are former Prek Treng workers, and that its management structure is identical apart from the administration head and assistant. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38]

**Employer (respondent), Khing Vey Mean New Fack Cherring Ltd:** the company states it cannot be responsible for problems belonging to the Prek Treng factory. [Para 7] It did not attend the hearing, giving as its reason that the demands in this case do not concern Khing Vey. [Para 17][Para 47] Its registration certificate shows it to be a new and separate company, distinct from Prek Treng. [Para 42]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused all four demands and held Khing Vey not liable for the obligations of Prek Treng. No sum was awarded. [Para 74][Para 75]

On the procedural question the panel applied Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, which permits it either to continue in the absence of a duly invited party or to terminate by order. [Para 44] Following Awards 53/04 (Kung Hong), 63/04 (San Wel) and 148/07 (Bay He), the panel chose to proceed, since the workers were present and had a right to a decision. [Para 45][Para 46] It held that a company which believes a claim does not concern it must still come and explain that, so Khing Vey's reason for staying away was not valid, and it convened a hearing in the employer's absence. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50]

On the merits the panel set out Article 87 of the Labour Law, under which a change in the employer's legal situation — succession, sale, merger, or conversion into a company — leaves existing employment contracts standing between the new employer and the workers of the old enterprise, and cessation of activity does not release the employer from its obligations except in a case of force majeure, with insolvency and court-ordered liquidation expressly not treated as force majeure. [Para 52][Para 54][Para 55][Para 56] It read Article 87 with Award No. 143/08 (Cham Thich Thai), which held that a change in the employer's legal situation does not affect employment contracts, and with Award No. 27/04 (IM's International), which requires a claimant to show that the company now using the workers on the site is the successor. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 61]

Applying that test, the panel found the workers had proved none of it: the relationship between the two companies appeared to be only that of landlord and tenant, Khing Vey held its own new commercial registration of 6 May 2010, and although many of its staff are former Prek Treng workers, they were recruited through the new company's own announcement rather than carried over. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67][Para 71][Para 72] On that evidence Khing Vey is a genuinely new company and not a successor under Article 87, so the four demands against it failed. [Para 73][Para 74]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A company that merely rents the premises of a closed factory and recruits its own workforce through its own public announcement is not a successor employer under Article 87, even where most of its workers are former employees of the closed operation and its management structure mirrors the old one; the claimant must prove succession by showing the new company took over the old employer's legal position, and the burden lies on the workers. [Para 63][Para 70][Para 72] A respondent's belief that the claim does not concern it is not a valid reason for absenting itself, and the panel may decide the case in its absence. [Para 48][Para 49]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that both companies had dealings with Gold Fame, described as a single large company, a fact it recorded without treating it as evidence of succession. [Para 39]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The hearing time is printed as 08:00 in the procedural summary and as 08:30 in the paragraph convening the session, an unresolved contradiction. [Para 12][Para 16] The employer's name is spelled several ways across the order, including forms that drop or transpose syllables, and one passage replaces "company" with an unrelated word for a district. [Para 1][Para 69] The Article 87 quotation is broken by stray punctuation and a line break in mid-clause, and the passage reciting the union's reasoning ends in an incomplete clause. [Para 52][Para 59] Document list labels are duplicated, with the secretariat's file heading printed twice in succession. [Para 25] In the signature block one arbitrator's name is replaced by an untranslatable Latin-script fragment, and another arbitrator's name is repeated twice; the panel composition on the face of the order is legible. [Para 78]
