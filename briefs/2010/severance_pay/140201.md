# Award No. 58/10 — Chevron (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 July 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chiv Phirom, appointed by the employer; An Vira, appointed by the workers; Kong Phalha, chair) · Doc ID 140201

## 1. Facts
Chevron (Cambodia) Ltd operates in Cambodia under that name since 2008, having entered in 1996 as Caltex Ltd, and employs about 500 people across its Phnom Penh head office and two filling stations. [Para 89][Para 90] Four employees were dismissed on separate dates: two pump attendants on 17 December 2009 after a customer complained of short fuelling, a third attendant on 14 January 2010 for keeping 4.03 USD of excess payment, and a stock clerk on 5 May 2010 for procedural breaches after an earlier one-month suspension over two boxes of sweets. [Para 55][Para 60][Para 103][Para 117][Para 123][Para 125]

The food and service workers' federation filed a demand on 10 May 2010 for the reinstatement of all four or, failing that, statutory severance plus damages. [Para 3][Para 14][Para 134] Conciliation settled one of the two points and the unresolved point reached the panel, which heard the case twice, on 29 June and 8 July 2010. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 12][Para 13]

The panel never reached the merits: it held the dispute was not a collective labour dispute and declined jurisdiction. Whether the four dismissals were lawful, and whether any of them was anti-union, therefore remain undecided. [Para 163][Para 164][Para 165]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether a demand for the reinstatement of four dismissed employees is a collective labour dispute within Article 302, and whether the panel consequently has jurisdiction? [Para 92][Para 138]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — the food and service workers' federation and its local at the company:** the Ministry sent the non-conciliation report to the Council, so the dispute must be a collective one; the dismissals touch the working conditions of all staff because other workers now fear dismissal, and the four were union members, one of them an active complainant about conditions. [Para 98][Para 99][Para 100][Para 114][Para 157]
**Employer (respondent) — Chevron (Cambodia) Ltd:** the four worked in different sections at two different stations, the subject matter is each individual's own interest, nothing about conditions of work or union rights is at stake, and the company continues to operate normally, so Article 302 is not satisfied. [Para 93][Para 95][Para 96][Para 97]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** the claim was refused for want of jurisdiction — the panel held the dispute was not a collective labour dispute and declined to consider the demand for reinstatement or severance. [Para 163][Para 164][Para 165]

The panel quoted Article 302, which defines a collective dispute as one between one or more employers and a number of their employees over working conditions, the exercise of professional rights, recognition of a professional organisation, or relations between employer and workers, and which must be capable of disturbing the orderly running of the enterprise or social peace. [Para 138] Following its earlier award in Case No. 10/03 it broke that definition into three cumulative conditions, the third being the disruption element. [Para 139][Para 140]

It disposed of the first two conditions quickly: four employees against one employer satisfies the "number of employees" element, and a dismissal relates to the employment relationship, so the subject matter falls within the second condition. [Para 151][Para 152] The case turned on the third. The panel found that two of the four worked as pump attendants at one station, one in the convenience store there, and one at the other station; their claims concerned their own rights and interests and were not a condition of work common to the attendants or the store staff. [Para 154] It weighed the four against a workforce of about 500 and found no evidence that other workers supported them beyond the federation's assertion that they feared similar treatment, and the employer's statement that operations continued normally went uncontradicted. [Para 155][Para 156]

Because the third condition failed, the panel held the dispute was not collective and, since Chapter 12 Part 2 gives the Council jurisdiction over collective disputes only, it had none. [Para 160][Para 161][Para 162]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand to reinstate a handful of individually dismissed employees is not a collective labour dispute merely because a federation files it or because the Ministry has issued a non-conciliation report: the third condition of Article 302 requires evidence that the dispute can obstruct the running of the enterprise or disturb social peace, which is not made out where four workers out of some five hundred are dismissed from different sections and no proof of wider worker support is produced. Without that condition the Council has no jurisdiction and reaches no finding on the dismissals themselves. [Para 154][Para 155][Para 160][Para 162]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the first condition was met because four employees count as "a number of employees", and that a dismissal always relates to the employment relationship, dispose of two conditions but decide nothing about the fairness of any dismissal. [Para 151][Para 152]

- The number of dismissed workers is given as three in the employer's argument and as four everywhere else, and the panel's summary of who worked where changes the count of attendants between paragraphs. [Para 95][Para 148][Para 154]
- Article 302 is printed as "ហត្រា ៣០២" instead of "មាត្រា" in the opening of the discussion, and the arbitrator's name is printed as "ឈឺវ ភិរម្យ" in the signature block against "ឈីវ ភិរម្យ" in the header. [Para 92][Para 167]
