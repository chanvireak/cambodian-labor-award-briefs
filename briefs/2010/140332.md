# Award No. 130/10 — Royal Cambodia Phnom Penh Golf Club [Civikha Investment Development Co., Ltd.] and the Local Union of CFITU (Arbitration Council, 21 December 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, An Nan, Pen Bunchhea chairing) · Doc ID 140332

## 1. Facts
Royal Cambodia Phnom Penh Golf Club [Civikha Investment Development Co., Ltd.], a golf facility at Okromdol Village, Dangkor District, employing 240 workers [Para 1][Para 29], faced a seven-point collective demand lodged on 27 October 2010 by the Local Union of CFITU (Cambodian Federation of Independent Trade Unions), whose leadership and second term were recognized on 14 July 2009 [Para 12][Para 24][Para 30]. Four of the seven points were settled in conciliation on 15 November 2010; of the three referred to the panel, the workers withdrew one and settled another — the renewal of four workers' contracts — by agreement at the hearing, leaving only the demand that the employer dismiss Mr C, its deputy director of administration, for improper language [Para 13][Para 14][Para 16]. On 6 October 2010 at 4 p.m. a worker asked Mr C how the female workers of the candy section could cross a path the employer intended to dig up; he replied abruptly with offensive language, adding that he could take the candy-section women outside with him at any time [Para 33]. Eighty-nine female workers thumbprinted a complaint and the union submitted it to the employer on 11 October 2010 [Para 33]. Hearings were held on 6 and 13 December 2010, both parties attending [Para 15][Para 35].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the workers have the right to demand that the employer dismiss its deputy director of administration for using improper language towards the female workers of the candy section? [Para 46]

The withdrawal of the third referred point and the agreed renewal of the four workers' contracts were recorded but not decided on the merits [Para 16].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), Local Union of CFITU:** Mr C used offensive language towards the candy-section women on 6 October 2010, heard by seven named union officers, members and a vendor standing three to four metres away; 89 women thumbprinted a complaint [Para 33][Para 40][Para 41]. They demanded his dismissal as serious misconduct, and observed that a supervisor involved in an altercation with another staff member had himself been dismissed [Para 5][Para 6].

**Employer (respondent), Royal Cambodia Phnom Penh Golf Club [Civikha Investment Development Co., Ltd.]:** it had received no complaint before 11 October 2010, but then met Mr C, who denied the allegation yet admitted using the derogatory phrase (មី នេះ មី នោះ), and it warned him against that language [Para 34]. It promised to punish any staff member who violates the Internal Work Rules, but maintained that it would not dismiss him [Para 35][Para 45].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the workers' demand that the employer dismiss Mr C [Para 55]

The panel began from Article 65, paragraph 1 of the Labour Law and Article 1 of Decree 38 on Contracts and Other Liabilities of 28 October 1988: a labour contract binds only its parties, so only the employer and the worker may terminate it, and a third party is not entitled to do so, following Award 108/08-Hugo [Para 46][Para 47][Para 49]. Prior awards (14/03-Chu Hsing, 17/03 and 18/03-Ho Hing, 87/04-Noble Apparel, 116/07-Grace Sun, 54/08-Zhong Yov) let workers demand a dismissal only where they prove the employee is a dangerous person who should not be allowed in the establishment and whose retention could harm the workplace [Para 49].

On the evidence, the four workers who testified took the oath and signed the witness forms, placed themselves three to four metres from the exchange, and were believed; Mr C denied the allegation and attacked the union president's motives over a car-bidding dispute and two unrelated incidents, without particulars [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41][Para 42][Para 43][Para 44]. The panel therefore found that he did use improper, insulting language towards the candy-section women, but that the remark did not make the workplace unsafe [Para 50]. The demand thus had no valid basis [Para 51].

The panel nevertheless applied Award 29/08-Tai Rong, holding that insults and improper language are inappropriate, cause bad feelings at work, and oblige the company to take appropriate measures to stop them and create a peaceful environment [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53]. It noted that the employer had already warned Mr C and held that the employer should take an appropriate measure to ensure he does not use the language again [Para 54][Para 55].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Workers cannot compel the dismissal of another employee: the right to terminate a labour contract belongs only to its parties, and a demand for dismissal succeeds only if the workers prove that the person is dangerous, should not be allowed on the premises, and that keeping him could harm the workplace; proof of insulting language alone establishes inappropriate conduct but not that threshold, and the appropriate remedy is an order that the employer take measures to stop it [Para 49][Para 51][Para 55].

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that a supervisor who had an altercation with a dismissed staff member fell outside the issue decided, and the panel's general remark that trading insults causes bad feelings in the workplace [Para 6][Para 51].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The document is an unofficial English translation of the authoritative Khmer original, as each page states, so the wording of Article 65 and of the quoted awards is the translator's [Para 1][Para 46]. OCR has systematically dropped the letters "th" throughout, turning "the employer" into "themployer" and "hearing" into "earing", and several passages are duplicated or truncated mid-sentence [Para 33][Para 45]. The issue list is numbered inconsistently — the demand to dismiss the deputy director carries no number in the list although the panel decides it as Issue 2, and the narrative counts seven issues, four resolved and three referred [Para 13][Para 16][Para 46]. Witness testimony was taken at a second hearing after the panel found it needed more evidence [Para 35][Para 36]. Individual employees, witnesses and union officers appear in full in the source and are given initials or roles here.
