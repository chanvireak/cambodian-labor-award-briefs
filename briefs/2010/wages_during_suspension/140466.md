# Award No. 172/09-SD — ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេស ឌី (ខេមបូឌា) ខូអិលធីឌី (SD (Cambodia) Co., Ltd.) v សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ឧស្សាហកម្ម វាយនភ័ណ្ឌ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា (Arbitration Council, 6 January 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Panel: រុន សារ៉ាយ (Chair), អ៉ីង សុទ្ធី, អន ណន · Doc ID 140466

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេស ឌី (ខេមបូឌា) ខូអិលធីឌី, a garment factory at National Road 4, Kandaok commune, Ang Snuol district, Kandal, employs about 612 workers and is the respondent; the claimant is សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ឧស្សាហកម្ម វាយនភ័ណ្ឌ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា, which holds a representative certificate dated 6 April 2009 and counts about 257 members [Para 25][Para 26][Para 28]. On repeated short stoppages — 1 December 2008 and 21 April 2009 (about 562 workers), 4 and 11 July 2009 (about 592), 7 August 2009 (312), 8 August 2009 (all workers) and 10 August 2009 (300 to 400) — the company sent the workers home without pay [Para 33][Para 34][Para 35][Para 36][Para 37]. It paid only 50 per cent of wages in April 2009 for about four or five days, in May 2009 and on 1, 2 and 3 October 2009, and it also charged seven days of annual leave in 2008 and 2009 against no-work days [Para 30][Para 87]. Both parties agree the company never placed these stoppages under Labour Inspector supervision [Para 43][Para 54].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether an employer that sends workers home without a lawful suspension of their contracts must nonetheless pay them 100 per cent of their wages? [Para 49]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the agreement to pay only 50 per cent during stoppages can be applied where the employer did not lawfully suspend the contracts? [Para 49][Para 63]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer may deduct annual leave days in place of the no-work days? [Para 74]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ឧស្សាហកម្ម វាយនភ័ណ្ឌ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា:** the company cut wages by 50 per cent during stoppages under an earlier agreement and announced in advance that annual leave would be used for the no-work days; the workers accepted the 50 per cent only out of sympathy, and because the company then broke even that arrangement by charging annual leave, they now cancel the agreement and demand full wages and repayment of everything deducted [Para 29][Para 32][Para 46][Para 61][Para 62].
**Employer (respondent) — ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេស ឌី (ខេមបូឌា) ខូអិលធីឌី:** the stoppages came from the company's economic decline, and the 50 per cent payment rested on an agreement the workers had accepted and applied; it did not contest the workers' account of the deductions or of the absence of Labour Inspector supervision [Para 31][Para 38][Para 42][Para 44][Para 60].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay 100 per cent of wages for stoppages not preceded by a lawful suspension, to repay the 50 per cent it had deducted for four to five days in April 2009 and three days in October 2009, and to restore the seven days of annual leave deducted in 2008 and 2009 [Para 90][Para 91][Para 92].
On Issue 1 the panel read Article 71 point 11 of the Labour Law as allowing suspension of the contract only where severe economic or material difficulty stops the enterprise's activity, and only under Labour Inspector supervision; Article 72(1) then relieves the employer of the wage obligation solely while such a suspension runs [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51][Para 56]. Since the undisputed facts show no authorisation and no supervisory filing, the suspensions were not lawful and the wage obligation never fell away; the panel followed Awards 51/09, 95/09 and 123/09 on that point [Para 53][Para 55][Para 58][Para 59].
On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 13, under which any arrangement contrary to the Labour Law is void, and its own earlier rulings that a worker may refuse or abandon an agreement that gives less than the law allows [Para 63][Para 65][Para 66]. A 50 per cent payment during a suspension the employer never lawfully effected fell below the statutory 100 per cent, so the agreement could not be applied and the deducted half of the wages had to be repaid [Para 69][Para 70][Para 89].
On Issue 3 the panel relied on Article 170 and on Awards 53/04, 27/05 and 45/05: annual leave may not be set against days when the employer has no work for the workers, and any leave taken outside the Khmer New Year period requires prior discussion and consent and notice to the Labour Inspector [Para 74][Para 77][Para 79][Para 81][Para 83]. Seven leave days had been consumed in this way, so they were ordered restored [Para 87][Para 92]. Finally, Article 120's three-year limitation had not run on any of the deductions [Para 85][Para 88]. The order is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification [Para 92].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A stoppage is a lawful suspension of the labour contract, and so extinguishes the wage obligation, only if the employer both faces the economic or material difficulty described in Article 71 point 11 and places the suspension under Labour Inspector supervision; failing that, the employer owes full wages for the idle days [Para 51][Para 55]. An agreement to pay less than the statutory wage during such a stoppage is void under Article 13 and cannot be pleaded against the workers, and annual leave may never be substituted for idle days [Para 66][Para 70][Para 83].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that even a valid suspension must be reported to the Labour Inspector so that discrimination or bad faith behind it can be checked [Para 51][Para 52].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The scan is badly degraded. Paragraph 9 breaks into a run of meaningless English fragments in the middle of a Khmer sentence, so the conciliation outcome is partly lost [Para 9]. The company's registered name is printed inconsistently (a certificate is attributed to a variant of the name in Latin letters and the registration date appears both as 6 October 1997 and 31 December 2003) [Para 16][Para 27]. The reasons date the deductions to "2019" where every other reference is to 2009 [Para 87]. The length of the April and May stoppages is given as four or five days in one place, three days in another, and the operative order omits May while adding October [Para 30][Para 89][Para 91]. Worker and employer representatives, all named in full in the award, are reduced to roles here, and the panel's own signatures are partly unreadable [Para 93]. A numeral inside the order on annual leave is corrupted as a percentage sign [Para 92].
