# Award No. 12/10 — Blue Sam Sen Zuri (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 1 March 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, An Non, Kong Phalla presiding) · Doc ID 140246

## 1. Facts
Blue Sam Sen Zuri (Cambodia), a garment factory in Takeo province with about 1,500 workers, faced seven collective demands lodged through the Democratic Union of Garment Workers, which had roughly 300 members. Conciliation at the Takeo labour department failed on all seven points on 22 January 2010. At the hearing the union withdrew demands 4, 5 and 6, leaving issues 1, 2, 3 and 7 for decision. Three union activists had resigned and collected their final pay; one worker on a two-month probation contract had been dismissed; the workers claimed a 26 October 2009 written agreement obliged the company to lengthen contracts; and a strike ran from 11 January to 5 February 2010. [Para 17][Para 21][Para 22][Para 62][Para 89]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must take back the three union activists it is said to have pushed out, and give them work again? [Para 63][Para 99]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must reinstate the dismissed worker whose two-month probation contract had run, and re-employ her on a six-month contract? [Para 72][Para 109]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must extend two-month contracts to six months, and six-month contracts to one year, in accordance with the agreement of 26 October 2009? [Para 84][Para 124]
4. (Issue 7) Whether the company must pay wages and other bonuses for the strike period, and refrain from taking reprisals against strikers, because it hired outsiders to replace them? [Para 89][Para 140]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the three activists had been forced out and their resignations were irregular; the dismissed worker had been stopped shortly after her contract expired and had done nothing wrong; the company had signed a binding agreement on 26 October 2009 to lengthen contracts and continued issuing two-month contracts regardless; and because the employer brought in outside workers through the back door at night during the strike, the strike wages and regular attendance bonus were owed. [Para 66][Para 74][Para 85][Para 95][Para 96]

**Employer (respondent):** the three activists resigned voluntarily, collected their wages and severance, and even asked the panel to close the case; the dismissed worker was still within her probation period and her work was poor; the company could not issue long contracts because orders had fallen and there was no work; and since no work was performed during the strike, no wages or bonuses were payable. [Para 63][Para 81][Para 86][Para 98]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all four remaining claims were refused; the award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification if no party objects in writing to the Minister of Labour. [Para 158][Para 159][Para 160][Para 161][Para 162]

On Issue 1 the panel applied the rule that the party making an allegation bears the burden of proving it, following Awards 108/08 and 41/09. The union asserted the resignations were irregular but produced no evidence to that effect and never replied to the employer's documents; the three activists had signed resignation letters and severance receipts on 6 February 2010, did not attend the hearing, and themselves wrote asking the panel to close the file. The claim was dismissed. [Para 101][Para 104][Para 105][Para 107][Para 108]

On Issue 2 the panel found the worker had been on a two-month probation contract from 23 October to 22 December 2009 at USD 45 a month, and was stopped during that period. Article 68 of the Labour Law permits probation of up to three months for employees, two for specialised workers and one for ordinary workers, and Award 178/09 confirms that probation dates must be stated; Award 27/03 holds that an employer need not give full reasons for ending a probation. Since her performance was poor, the termination required no notice and no reinstatement was ordered. [Para 115][Para 116][Para 118][Para 120][Para 122][Para 123]

On Issue 3 the panel accepted that the agreement of 26 October 2009 was valid and binding: under Article 1 of Decree-Law 38 a contract is a voluntary agreement creating obligations, and Award 60/07 holds that parties must perform what they signed. It rejected the employer's defence that falling orders prevented longer contracts — the agreement was signed only days before, and no evidence of any economic downturn or drop in orders was produced. The claim nonetheless failed on proof: the union never showed how many workers were affected or what the practice was before and after the agreement, so there was nothing to measure a breach against. [Para 126][Para 132][Para 133][Para 134][Para 135][Para 139]

On Issue 7 the panel noted Article 332 (the contract is suspended during a strike and wages are not paid) and Article 334 (replacing strikers is prohibited, and any breach obliges the employer to pay the strikers' wages). It found the employer had indeed hired outsiders, since its representative did not deny it. But the protection of Article 334 applies only to a lawful strike: the workers could not state when notice was given, so seven days' notice under Article 324 was not proved, and they never showed they had exhausted conciliation under Article 320. The strike was therefore procedurally unlawful and no wages or bonuses were owed, replacement hiring notwithstanding. [Para 141][Para 144][Para 145][Para 148][Para 153][Para 156]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The wage consequence attached by Article 334 to the hiring of strike replacements arises only where the strike itself was lawful; a strike mounted without the seven days' notice required by Article 324, and without prior exhaustion of conciliation, forfeits the right to pay for the strike period even though the employer breached the replacement prohibition. Separately, a written agreement to lengthen contract terms binds the employer, but a claim under it still fails if the claimant cannot show which workers were affected. [Para 134][Para 153][Para 156]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that an employer's representative was not personally aware of the file and that a party alleging irregularity should ensure the affected workers attend the hearing were observations on conduct, not tests applied to the outcome. [Para 78][Para 107]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The dismissal date of the probation worker is printed two ways: 15 December 2009 in the findings and reasoning, and 30 December 2009 in the summary of the union's case, where it is also said to fall eight days after the contract expired on 22 December; the panel decided on the earlier date and the discrepancy is not resolved. The number of strikers is given as about 300 at the vote and about 700 participants, and the vote itself is recorded as having been taken on signed sheets rather than a ballot box. One worker's identity number appears variously as ZI 1582, 21582 and similar corrupted forms. Names in the evidence schedules and in the signature block are damaged by OCR, one arbitrator's name being rendered in a garbled form in the signature block only. The date of the union's registration certificate is printed as 11 February 2008, which does not sit easily with the 2009 events described. [Para 54][Para 76][Para 93][Para 94][Para 112][Para 163]
