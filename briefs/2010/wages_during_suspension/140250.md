# Award No. 17/10 — Zhong Thich Game Nuve K Chir Ring (Arbitration Council, 5 March 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3 (Ly Tai Seng, employer-appointed; An Nan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhian, president) · Doc ID 140250

## 1. Facts
The factory, which traded as Pheaphek Ta from 2005 and was re-registered in December 2009 as Zhong Thich Game Nuve K Chir Ring with about 330 workers, faced 13 collective demands filed by a union federation on behalf of 322 workers. [Para 39][Para 42][Para 40] Conciliation failed on 9 February 2010 and the case reached the Council on 11 February 2010. [Para 21][Para 22] The workers struck from 5 to 16 February 2010 and the employer dismissed strikers on 10 February 2010, offering re-employment only after a fresh interview and a two-month probation. [Para 89][Para 87] At the hearing the employer did not appear, and the workers withdrew 8 of the 13 demands (issues 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11 and 13). [Para 24][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether workers who perform overtime are entitled to have the overtime meal allowance paid once a week rather than twice a month? [Para 44][Para 105]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer may deduct the regular attendance bonus in proportion to the days of leave the company itself authorised? [Para 137]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the successor company must continue paying the seniority bonus that the previous employer paid, counting service with that predecessor? [Para 163][Para 164]
4. (Issue 10) Whether one toilet-entry card per work group of about 40 workers satisfies Prakas No. 052? [Para 192][Para 198]
5. (Issue 12) Whether the company must allow one hour per day, inside working hours, for a woman with an infant to breastfeed? [Para 212]
6. (Additional issue) Whether the panel may decide the reinstatement demand raised at the hearing, and whether the employer must take the dismissed strikers back on their former conditions? [Para 225][Para 233]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the Federation of Unions for the Promotion of Workers' Rights (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព លើកស្ទួយសិទ្ធិកម្មករ), acting for 322 workers of the factory union and their elected officers under a written mandate of 5 February 2010, argued that the meal allowance for overtime must reach them weekly because they cannot wait for the 5th or 20th of the month to buy food, and that the allowance is wage-like and payable as the law requires; they also said the company's new attendance-bonus rule penalises lawful leave, that the successor employer cannot erase seniority, that a single toilet card per group forces workers to wait until they soil themselves, and that breastfeeding leave is a statutory right. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 47][Para 49][Para 57][Para 79][Para 84]
**Employer (respondent):** Zhong Thich Game Nuve K Chir Ring, the company that took over the Pheaphek Ta factory — absent from the hearing without explanation, so it filed no evidence and answered none of the claims; the panel recorded that no submissions were received from it on any issue. [Para 24][Para 39][Para 50][Para 80]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to pay the overtime meal allowance weekly, to deduct the attendance bonus only in proportion to authorised leave days, to continue the seniority bonus carried over from the old employer, to provide 3 toilet cards per group, to give one hour per day for breastfeeding for one year after birth, and to reinstate the dismissed workers on their previous conditions. [Para 248][Para 249][Para 250][Para 251][Para 252][Para 253]

On Issue 1 the panel held, under Article 116 of the Labour Law, that wages must be paid at least twice a month, and that the meal allowance of 1,000 riel follows Notice No. 017 of 18 July 2000, which links it to overtime actually worked. [Para 107][Para 122][Para 116] The panel distinguished it from overtime pay under Article 103, since the allowance replaces a meal rather than remunerating the overtime hours, but accepted the weekly claim because the notice fixes no payment date and the workers' need to buy food is immediate. [Para 118][Para 128][Para 135]

On Issue 2 the panel applied Articles 103, 71 and 72: the attendance bonus is part of wages, but an authorised absence suspends the contract, so the employer is not obliged to pay for those days; a full $6 for days not worked would be unjust to the employer, while total forfeiture would be unjust to workers who took leave the company allowed. [Para 146][Para 149][Para 157] It therefore ordered proportional deduction, following awards 57/07, 106/07 and 115/07. [Para 159]

On Issue 3 the panel relied on Article 87 (transfer of an enterprise leaves running contracts with the new employer) and on the definition of seniority in award 75/05: continuous service that never stopped. [Para 165][Para 180][Para 183] Because the workforce moved from Pheaphek Ta to the new company without a break, seniority and the bonus calculated on it continued. [Para 189][Para 191]

On Issue 10 the panel read Prakas No. 052 of 10 February 2000, articles 1 and 2, as requiring three toilets for a group of 36 to 55 workers and therefore at least three cards; one card for about 40 workers did not comply and risked workers' health. [Para 195][Para 198][Para 200][Para 209]

On Issue 12 the panel applied Articles 184 and 185: one hour per day, splittable into two half-hours, for a year after childbirth, counted separately from ordinary leave, is both a right of the mother and a duty of the employer, which had never granted it. [Para 213][Para 216][Para 222]

On the additional issue the panel first satisfied itself of jurisdiction under Article 312, because the dismissals of 10 February 2010 followed the 13 demands and were a direct consequence of the dispute; the strike had been peaceful, so under Articles 330 and 332(2) the employer could neither dismiss strikers nor impose a new interview and two-month probation as the price of return. [Para 226][Para 233][Para 238][Para 245]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A change of employer does not interrupt seniority or the contractual benefits built on it: contracts in force at the date of transfer continue with the successor, and a benefit the predecessor paid as of right survives unless a new contract or internal rule replaces it. [Para 167][Para 171][Para 189]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that paying the overtime meal allowance daily is the better practice but not a legal requirement, since a worker who receives it weekly or monthly can still set the money aside for food. [Para 130][Para 132]
