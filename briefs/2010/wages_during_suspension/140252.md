# Award No. 18/10 — Khi Ruyal (Cambodia) Hair-Implantation Factory (Arbitration Council, 29 March 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3 (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Sin Kim Sean, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhian, president) · Doc ID 140252

## 1. Facts
The factory, a hair-implantation (ចាក់សក់) plant operating since 2008 with about 490 workers, faced 19 demands filed by the Democratic Workers' Union federation on 16 January 2010; six were settled in conciliation and 13 were certified unresolved on 18 February 2010. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 66] At the hearing the workers withdrew eight of the 13 (issues 4 to 11), leaving five for decision. [Para 44][Para 45] The dispute concerned a permanent clinic and doctor, the non-renewal of a union vice-president's fixed-term contract, pay during the induction period, the piece-rate incentive scheme, and deduction of the attendance bonus for authorised leave. [Para 70][Para 78][Para 94][Para 105][Para 113] Arbitrator Ing Sothy filed a separate dissenting opinion on Issue 13. [Para 226][Para 262]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether a plant with about 490 workers must maintain a permanent infirmary and a permanently present doctor on site? [Para 70][Para 117]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer's decision not to renew the fixed-term contract of a union vice-president was discrimination on grounds of union membership? [Para 78][Para 138]
3. (Issue 3) Whether workers undergoing three to four days of induction training are apprentices entitled to a wage for that period? [Para 94][Para 172]
4. (Issue 12) Whether the employer's rule granting a $5 bonus for one head braided in 15 days is reasonable, or must be replaced by $5 per head per month? [Para 105][Para 187]
5. (Issue 13) Whether the attendance bonus must be deducted only in proportion to days of absence that the company itself authorised? [Para 113][Para 201]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the union argued that a clinic and resident doctor are a legal duty, that the vice-president's contract was allowed to lapse only because she was elected on 18 October 2009 and the company learned of it the next day, that induction trainees work and must be paid, that the 15-day incentive target is unattainable for beginners, and that the full $5 attendance bonus is lost even for authorised sick leave. [Para 75][Para 87][Para 101][Para 108][Para 113]
**Employer (respondent):** the company said it cannot afford a clinic and relies on a hospital 200 metres away; that the contract simply reached its term and was not renewed, with 68 of 70 braiders renewed; that trainees receive tuition rather than wages; that the incentive scale is its management prerogative; and that the bonus rewards regular attendance only. [Para 76][Para 90][Para 103][Para 110][Para 114]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to set up a permanent infirmary with a resident doctor, to reinstate Ms K with back pay from the date of non-renewal to the date of return, and to deduct the $5 attendance bonus only in proportion to authorised absence days; the claims for training-period wages and for a changed incentive scale were dismissed. [Para 220][Para 221][Para 222][Para 223][Para 224]

On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 242 of the Labour Law together with article 2 of Prakas No. 08 SKA of 19 January 1994 and article 1 of Prakas No. 330 of 6 December 2000: any enterprise employing at least 50 workers must keep a permanent infirmary inside its premises, staffed according to worker numbers. [Para 118][Para 124][Para 126] It found that the person handing out medicine was a dyeing-section supervisor without a medical licence, that the company had no infirmary, and that the nearby hospital did not discharge the duty of first aid at the workplace. [Para 73][Para 130][Para 132]

On Issue 2 the panel accepted that Article 73 lets a fixed-term contract expire by its own terms, but held that Articles 12 and 279 forbid an employer from using union membership or activity as the ground for that decision. [Para 138][Para 146][Para 147] The worker had been elected vice-president on 18 October 2009 and the company was notified the next day through its security guard; only two of 70 braiders were not renewed, and the company still needed braiders. [Para 155][Para 160][Para 162] The panel drew the inference of anti-union discrimination and ordered reinstatement with back pay. [Para 164][Para 167]

On Issue 3 the panel treated the induction period as apprenticeship under Article 8, but found that no written apprenticeship contract existed as Article 52 requires, and that nothing in the contract or in any agreement fixed pay for the training days. [Para 172][Para 174][Para 178][Para 182] Without an agreed rate the employer owed no wage, so the claim failed. [Para 183][Para 184]

On Issue 12 the panel held that fixing incentive bonuses falls within the employer's management right under Article 2, to be exercised lawfully and reasonably, and that a scale tied to output was reasonable where the workers gave no evidence that it was not. [Para 190][Para 196][Para 198]

On Issue 13 the panel applied Articles 103, 71 and 72: the bonus is part of wages, but authorised absence suspends the contract, so a proportional deduction is lawful while total forfeiture is not. [Para 209][Para 210][Para 213][Para 218] Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented, arguing that the workers had asked to keep the bonus intact for authorised leave, not for a pro-rata deduction, so the panel decided a remedy nobody sought. [Para 255][Para 256][Para 257]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Expiry of a fixed-term contract is lawful in itself, but where the employer knew of the worker's election to union office shortly before the decision, renewed almost the whole section, and still needed labour, non-renewal is treated as anti-union discrimination and attracts reinstatement with back pay. [Para 143][Para 164]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that a nearby hospital 200 metres away cannot substitute for on-site first aid goes beyond what Article 242 strictly requires, since that article speaks only of premises. [Para 74][Para 132]
