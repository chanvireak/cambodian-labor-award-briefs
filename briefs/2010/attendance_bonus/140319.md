# Award No. 114/10 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ប៊ី រី អី ភែរីល (ខេមបូឌា) and សហជីព ចលនា កម្មករ ប្រចាំ រោងចក្រ ប៊ី រី (Arbitration Council, 11 November 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mam Sombona, An Vireak, Pen Bun Chhea) · Doc ID 140319

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ប៊ី រី អី ភែរីល (ខេមបូឌា), a factory at Jampu Van village, Chom Chao, Dangkao, operating since 2005 with about 2,900 workers and five unions, faced five unresolved demands out of eleven lodged by សម្ព័ន្ឋ សហជីព ចលនា កម្មករ and its local, which has 208 members. [Para 17][Para 64][Para 65][Para 67]
At the 26 October 2010 hearing Issue 3 was settled by agreement and Issues 2 and 4 were withdrawn, leaving Issues 1 and 5. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24]
Issue 1 concerned the USD 5 attendance bonus when the company bus broke down on the road; Issue 5 concerned paying the overtime meal allowance weekly rather than monthly. [Para 3][Para 11][Para 69][Para 82]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must keep the full USD 5 regular attendance bonus for workers who did not come to work because the bus carrying them broke down? [Para 69][Para 96]
2. (Issue 5) Whether workers who work overtime may require the meal allowance to be paid once a week instead of with the monthly wage? [Para 82][Para 115]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ចលនា កម្មករ and its ប៊ី រី factory local, through Mr F, federation secretary-general:** where the bus breaks down there is no other transport, workers must pay for their own travel, and the employer should not strip the whole bonus; and because they lack money to buy food they cannot wait for the 6th of the following month to receive the overtime meal allowance. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 89][Para 90]
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន ប៊ី រី អី ភែរីល (ខេមបូឌា), through Mr Y and Ms C of administration:** Notice BR 11/08 of 9 June 2008 sets the bonus at USD 5 only for workers attending regularly, an authorised sick absence costing USD 0.19 a day; the company never authorised leave for a broken bus, so the bonus was properly deducted; and the meal allowance is paid monthly through a computerised system that cannot be changed weekly. [Para 73][Para 78][Para 79][Para 91][Para 92]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the Issue 1 demand is refused and, on Issue 5, the employer is ordered to pay the overtime meal allowance once a week to workers who work overtime. [Para 141][Para 142]
On Issue 1 the panel applied point 3 of Notification No. 745 K.K.BV (23 October 2006), which preserves points 3 to 6 of Notification No. 017 SKBY (18 July 2000), under which a worker who works the required days of a month receives a bonus of at least USD 5 — an incentive for full attendance, not a wage. Prior awards (143/08, 152/08, 57/07, 70/07, 53/09) give the bonus pro rata only where the employer authorised the absence. The panel found the company deducted the whole bonus, but also that in October 2010 no bus broke down and that in September, when one did, workers still reached work at 7:15 and kept the bonus. Absence without permission forfeits the bonus, so the demand failed. [Para 98][Para 99][Para 100][Para 102][Para 104][Para 108][Para 111][Para 112]
On Issue 5 the panel noted that Article 116 requires wages to be paid at least twice a month, and that Article 103 makes overtime pay part of wages, but held that the meal allowance is not overtime pay: it substitutes for a meal the employer must otherwise provide, so it may be paid on a separate schedule (following Award 113/09 in this same enterprise). Point 4 of Notification No. 017 gives 1,000 riel a day or one free meal but fixes no payment date, and Award 110/09 required payment within a period that lets workers actually buy food during overtime. A weekly payment was therefore ordered, the employer's computer-system difficulty being no answer. [Para 115][Para 120][Para 124][Para 125][Para 130][Para 134][Para 135][Para 137][Para 140]
The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent written objection to the Minister. [Para 143]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The USD 5 attendance bonus is earned by actually working the month's required days: an absence the employer did not authorise forfeits it, and only an authorised absence reduces it pro rata. [Para 100][Para 104][Para 108]
The overtime meal allowance is not part of overtime pay and may be disbursed separately from wages, but it must be paid often enough — weekly where workers ask — for them to buy food during the overtime worked. [Para 125][Para 130][Para 137][Para 140]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the meal allowance substitutes for a meal the employer is obliged to provide if it does not pay the 1,000 riel, and that computerised payroll is not a justification for withholding it. [Para 126][Para 132][Para 138]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The attendance bonus is printed as USD 5 in the Notice and in the demand, but as "USD 50" at line 100 and as "USD 560" at line 109; the brief uses the USD 5 figure that the parties and the operative order share. [Para 73][Para 99][Para 100][Para 109]
The company's name is spelled differently in the case header, in the party block and in the two prior awards it cites for itself, and the employer-chosen arbitrator's name carries a stray prefix in the signature block; the forms used here are the ones on the face of the award. [Para 1][Para 144]
The demand list is truncated by OCR: the first demand's sentence is cut mid-clause, the numbering runs 1 to 5 while the report records five unresolved points out of eleven, and the union's membership of 208 is reconciled nowhere with the 88 members whose dues were deducted. [Para 3][Para 18][Para 68]
