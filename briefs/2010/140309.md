# Award No. 93/10 — Hai Bun Enterprise (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហាយ ប៊ន អ៉ិន ធើ ប្រាយ) weaving factory (Arbitration Council, 6 September 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kaw Tha Chamroeang for the employer party, An Virak for the worker party, Kong Phalla chair) · Doc ID 140309

## 1. Facts
Hai Bun Enterprise (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហាយ ប៊ន អ៉ិន ធើ ប្រាយ) operates a garment-weaving factory at Village Mol, Sangkat Chom Chao, Khan Dangkao, employing about 760 workers. [Para 39] One union, the Khmer Worker Movement Union at Hai Bun weaving factory (សហជីព ចលនា កម្មករ ខ្មែរ នៃ រោងចក្រ តម្បាញ ហាយ ប៊ន), with roughly 500 members, filed the complaint but had not yet received a most-representative certificate. [Para 40][Para 41] Its letter of 12 July 2010 asked the company to improve working conditions; conciliation on 3 August 2010 settled 4 of 10 demands and sent the remaining 6 to arbitration. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 22] At the hearing the parties settled issues 5 and 6 themselves, so the panel decided only issues 1 to 4. [Para 25][Para 26]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must supply one free meal — rather than a 1,000 riel meal allowance — to workers doing overtime from 4:00 to 6:00 pm? [Para 43][Para 79]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay a travel allowance of USD 8 per month to workers who work overtime until 8:30 pm? [Para 47][Para 91]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must keep the full USD 5 monthly attendance bonus when a worker falls ill at the factory and is sent for treatment at an outside hospital? [Para 55][Para 110]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must spray water on the factory roof at midday in hot weather to reduce the heat? [Para 62][Para 131]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), the Khmer Worker Movement Union at Hai Bun weaving factory (សហជីព ចលនា កម្មករ ខ្មែរ នៃ រោងចក្រ តម្បាញ ហាយ ប៊ន):** 1,000 riel no longer buys a meal because market prices have risen, so the company should provide food itself; the USD 8 travel payment is owed because fares have risen and Notification No. 06 of 3 March 1997 requires it; the USD 5 attendance bonus should survive a factory collapse sent out for outside treatment; and the zinc roof makes the workshop unbearably hot. [Para 44][Para 48][Para 61][Para 63][Para 71]
**Employer (respondent), Hai Bun Enterprise (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហាយ ប៊ន អ៉ិន ធើ ប្រាយ):** it has no capacity to hand out free meals, has already paid the 1,000 riel allowance, cannot afford a travel allowance for the roughly 10 per cent living more than 2 km away, may reduce the attendance bonus where a worker is absent, and cannot spray the roof because there is no state water supply, well water is lime-heavy and would damage the roof, while 300 extra fans were already bought. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 50][Para 52][Para 65][Para 66][Para 69][Para 111]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must give one free meal to workers on overtime from 4:00 to 6:00 pm; the USD 8 monthly travel demand is dismissed; the demand to keep the full attendance bonus is dismissed but the company must immediately stop deducting the whole bonus and deduct it pro rata to the days of sick leave it authorised; and the employer must install equipment or find other means, such as spraying water on the factory roof, to reduce the heat. [Para 147][Para 148][Para 149][Para 150][Para 151]

On issue 1 the panel read point 4 of Notification No. 017 SKBY of 18 July 2000, which says a worker on voluntary overtime receives 1,000 riel a day "or" one free meal, and found it does not say who chooses; following Awards 47/07-Chung Fai and 86/10-Nu Ming Da it held that the substance and purpose of the notification is that the employer must provide a meal, not money. [Para 81][Para 83][Para 86] Because 1,000 riel can no longer buy a meal as it could in 2000, a demand for the meal itself does not depart from the notification. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 89]

On issue 2 the panel applied Article 144 (new) of the Labour Law, which defines night as at least 11 consecutive hours including 22:00 to 05:00, read with Article 6 of Prakas No. 80 SKBY of 1 March 1999 and point 4 of Notification No. 014 SKBY of 6 August 1999, and treated the night period as running 19:00 to 06:00. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 95][Para 97][Para 100] Those texts oblige the employer to provide safe transport home, but the workers asked for money, not transport, and paying a travel allowance is not the main means of securing safety; the demand as framed was therefore refused. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 108]

On issue 3 the panel held that point 3 of Notification No. 745 KKBV of 23 October 2006 preserves the benefits of points 3, 5 and 6 of Notification No. 017, that the attendance bonus is an incentive for regular attendance, and that Article 103 makes a bonus part of wages. [Para 112][Para 113][Para 114][Para 118] Under Article 71(3) and Article 72 the contract is suspended by certified sick absence and the employer owes no wage for those days; following Awards 86/06-Kingland and 62/07-Hong My, the panel ordered the bonus reduced in proportion to the days of authorised sick leave, not paid in full and not forfeited. [Para 119][Para 120][Para 123][Para 124][Para 130]

On issue 4 the panel relied on Article 229, requiring sanitary and healthy working conditions, and on Prakas No. 147 SKBY of 11 June 2002 on the heat environment, whose articles 1 and 3 require the employer to keep heat acceptable and to seek every means of cooling the workplace. [Para 131][Para 133][Para 134] Following Award 94/04-ETN, and although the employer had added 300 fans, the panel found the employer had not fully met that duty and ordered it to arrange equipment or other means. [Para 138][Para 139][Para 145][Para 146]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a notification lets a worker receive either a cash meal allowance or a free meal and the cash sum no longer buys a meal at market prices, the employer's obligation is discharged by providing the meal, not by paying the old sum. Conversely, where the law obliges the employer to provide safe transport after night work, a demand framed as a fixed monthly travel allowance is a different obligation from the one the law creates, and cannot be granted as money. Where a worker is absent with sickness certified and authorised by the employer, the employment contract is suspended, no wage is due for those days, and the monthly attendance bonus must be reduced in proportion to the days of authorised absence rather than withheld in full. [Para 88][Para 106][Para 128]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the union's most-representative certificate was still pending with the Ministry, an observation not needed to decide any of the four issues. It also noted that overtime is heaviest from May to July and declines later in the year, which framed but did not decide the transport question. [Para 41][Para 54]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Line 129 replaces the company's printed name with a bare letter, so the operative paragraph there names the respondent only as a lettered company; the correct name is taken from the header and §1. [Para 129] Line 144 carries an unconnected Latin string embedded mid-sentence, obscuring part of the heat reasoning. [Para 144] The bothersome roof is printed both as "ស័ង្កសី" and as "ស័ង្កសីហនុ", and the meal allowance appears as "១.០០០ រៀល" and "១.000 រៀល" in different passages. [Para 44][Para 71][Para 81] The panel's issue list in the procedural section runs "issue 1, issue 24, issue 3" where issues 2 and 3 have run together in OCR. [Para 26] The 6:00 pm hour is printed in both Khmer and Latin numerals. [Para 147]
