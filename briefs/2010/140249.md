# Award No. 14/10 — MPA International (Security) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 24 February 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Tun Si Phan, Pen Bun Chhea presiding) · Doc ID 140249

## 1. Facts
MPA International, a security-services company in Phnom Penh with nearly 3,000 guards, employed the claimant as a target supervisor from 7 December 1999 on a monthly wage of USD 190 plus USD 25 fuel allowance, with no written contract. On 10 and 11 November 2009 the Khmer Youth Union held elections at the company and he was elected its president; the result was notified to the employer the same day. The company dismissed him on 25 November 2009 without notice, and the union's registration application was lodged with the Ministry only on 20 January 2010. Nine collective demands went to conciliation, three were withdrawn (issues 3, 7 and 9), and the company refused to attend the hearing, filing a defence on 14 February 2010 instead. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67][Para 70][Para 73][Para 76][Para 28][Para 29]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must take back its union president into normal work? [Para 146]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must stop deducting seven paid public-holiday days a year and pay them retroactively? [Para 177]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the company must pay double wages for work done on weekly rest days or public holidays? [Para 203]
4. (Issue 5) Whether the company must keep paying wages during sick leave supported by a proper medical certificate? [Para 220]
5. (Issue 6) Whether the company must pay remaining annual-leave compensation into workers' ACLEDA bank accounts within 48 hours of their departure? [Para 232]
6. (Issue 8) Whether the company must grant annual leave in accordance with the law and allow unused days to be carried into the following year? [Para 256]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissal was union discrimination because the worker had just been elected union president and the company had received notice of the result; he had done nothing wrong and had never been warned; the company had been treating seven paid public holidays as ordinary working days and paying nothing for them, had paid only a single day's wage for work on rest days and holidays, had forced workers to use annual leave when sick, and had held back annual-leave compensation until the 15th of a later month, which many workers never came to collect. [Para 69][Para 77][Para 100][Para 115][Para 131]

**Employer (respondent):** the company did not appear. Its defence of 14 February 2010 said the dismissal rested on repeated misconduct — failing to file work reports, breaching internal rules, not taking responsibility as a supervisor, and distributing security-dues deduction letters of 100 riel per worker per month without the company's knowledge or Ministry authorisation — and that the union was not yet legally registered or recognised. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 95]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the company was ordered to reinstate the union president, to grant paid public holidays in line with the Ministry's Prakas, to pay 100 per cent extra for holiday work and 200 per cent for weekly rest-day work, to apply article 4 of its internal rules on sick pay, to pay outstanding annual-leave compensation by bank transfer within 48 hours of the last working day, and to allow carry-over of unused annual leave with an extra day every three years. It also ordered 100 per cent retroactive pay for 14 holiday days over the previous two years. The award is non-binding. [Para 277][Para 278][Para 279][Para 280][Para 281][Para 282][Para 283]

The panel first confirmed it could hear the case in the employer's absence under article 21 of Prakas 099, having refused an adjournment because the workers did not consent and delay would upset the statutory time limit for issuing the award. [Para 140][Para 144][Para 145]

On Issue 1 the panel applied article 3(3) of Prakas 305, which protects candidates in union leadership elections like worker delegates for 45 days before and after the poll, and article 4(1), which protects founders and members while registration is pending until 30 days after registration. Following Awards 07/06, 09/06 and 148/07 it required three conditions: that the worker belong to a protected category, that the dismissal occur within the protected period, and that the union have notified the employer of the candidacy. All three were satisfied — he was a candidate and elected president, the dismissal on 25 November 2009 fell inside the protected window, and the employer had received the election result on 11 November 2009 through its human-resources head. Article 293 then required the labour inspector's prior approval, which was never sought; the criminal complaint the company had filed against him was unrelated to the dismissal. Reinstatement was ordered. [Para 147][Para 151][Para 154][Para 156][Para 159][Para 167][Para 168][Para 176]

On Issue 2 the panel applied article 161 of the Labour Law and article 1 of Prakas 10 of 4 February 1999: public holidays are paid days, and the company's treatment of seven of them as ordinary working days was unlawful. It ordered recognition of the full holiday list and retroactive payment of seven days per year for the two preceding years. [Para 181][Para 184][Para 202]

On Issue 4 the panel applied article 137 (eight hours a day, forty-eight a week), article 139 as amended (overtime at 50 per cent, and 100 per cent for rest-day or night work) and article 4 of Prakas 10 (holiday work earns one extra day's wage). Work on a weekly rest day is overtime and attracts 200 per cent in total, and holiday work attracts a 100 per cent supplement. [Para 209][Para 210][Para 218]

On Issue 5 the panel found the company's own internal rules of 11 December 2006, article 4, already provide full pay for the first month of certified sick leave, 60 per cent for months two and three, and none thereafter while the post is kept, and simply ordered the company to comply. [Para 225][Para 229][Para 231]

On Issue 6 the panel applied article 116(5) and held that wages and all allowances, including annual-leave compensation, fall due within 48 hours of the last working day, not on the 15th of a following month; paying by bank transfer was an accepted long-standing practice and was allowed. [Para 237][Para 243][Para 253][Para 255]

On Issue 8 the panel applied article 166 (18 working days of annual leave, plus one day for each three years of service) and article 167(4) (unused leave may be carried over for up to three consecutive years, and only beyond twelve days). It ordered leave to be granted by seniority and unused days carried forward. [Para 261][Para 266][Para 269][Para 276]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Protection under articles 3(3) and 4(1) of Prakas 305 is not confined to registered unions: a candidate in a union leadership election, and a founder or member of a union whose registration is still pending, cannot be dismissed within the statutory windows without the labour inspector's prior approval, and the employer's stated grounds for dismissal do not cure that procedural failure. Separately, public holidays are paid days, work on a weekly rest day is overtime payable at 200 per cent, and annual-leave compensation is wages due within 48 hours of the last working day. [Para 151][Para 156][Para 167][Para 184][Para 218][Para 255]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that paying wages by bank transfer is acceptable because it is a long-standing practice, and that a worker's unused leave is not confiscated if not taken in the first year, were explanatory rather than operative. [Para 253][Para 274]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The number of paid public holidays the company must recognise is corrupted by OCR, printed as a run of digits and words that can be read only as seventeen. The Prakas on public holidays is cited with two different dates, 4 February 1999 in the reasoning and 1 February 1999 in the operative passage, and its number appears in several garbled forms. The statutory deadline is written as "48 hours" while the parenthetical Khmer gloss renders it as thirty-eight hours, The retroactive holiday figure appears as 14 days where the reasoning describes seven days a year for two years. The company's name is transliterated inconsistently throughout, and the signature block gives one arbitrator's name in a corrupted form. [Para 180][Para 184][Para 200][Para 237][Para 244][Para 279][Para 285]
