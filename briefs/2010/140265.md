# Award No. 38/10 — Fortune Garment Co., Ltd (ហ្វ័រធ្យូន ហ្គាមេន) (Arbitration Council, 4 June 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach; Vann Pov; chair Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 140265

## 1. Facts
Fortune Garment has operated since 1999 and employs about 1,700 workers; the local union of C.CAWDU has about 1,060 members but its certificate of most-representative status has expired and no replacement had been filed by the date of the award. [Para 36] [Para 37] [Para 39] [Para 40] [Para 42] [Para 136] Work is seasonal: the company has no or insufficient work in January to March and in November and December, and it suspended contracts in those periods without seeking the labour inspector's authorisation. [Para 43] [Para 45] [Para 47] A collective agreement of 28 November 2005, clause 5, provided 40 per cent of wages plus 100 per cent of benefits when work was short, and the company relied on it. [Para 2] [Para 23] [Para 46] Of four demands, one was settled in conciliation on 25 March 2010 and three came to the panel, which held two hearings after finding the parties' first accounts inconsistent with the documents. [Para 14] [Para 15] [Para 19]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay 100 per cent of wages and benefits for the periods when it had no work for the workers, given that it suspended their contracts without the labour inspector's authorisation? [Para 43] [Para 82]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must top up piece-rate workers' earnings (ដេរស៊ីបុង) to the daily minimum wage whenever a day's output falls below it? [Para 56] [Para 104]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must repay the position allowance (ប្រាក់ភារកិច្ច) it deducted when workers took their annual leave? [Para 64] [Para 140]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that they are owed the full 100 per cent of wage and benefits whenever the company has no work, that piece-rate workers should be topped up to the minimum wage day by day, and that the deducted position allowance must be repaid because it was never previously withheld and no agreement allowed the deduction. [Para 43] [Para 52] [Para 59] [Para 68] [Para 71]
**Employer (respondent):** that it applies clause 5 of the 2005 agreement, paying 40 per cent of wages plus 100 per cent of benefits, because the shortage of orders is unavoidable and it too loses money; that its three branches, not small workshops, do the outsourced work; that minimum wage is a monthly, not a daily, guarantee; and that it had stopped deducting the allowance because it was being paid twice, the allowance being folded into the average used to calculate annual-leave pay. [Para 53] [Para 54] [Para 55] [Para 76] [Para 153] [Para 155]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — on Issue 1 the company must pay an additional 60 per cent so that wages reach 100 per cent for the periods when it had no work, because the suspension was not carried out lawfully; on Issues 2 and 3 the workers' demands are refused. [Para 160] [Para 161] [Para 162]

On Issue 1 the panel applied Articles 71 and 72 of the Labour Law: a suspension is permitted only for serious economic or material difficulty, for no more than two months, and must be notified to and supervised by the labour inspector, who must confirm that the difficulty is real. [Para 85] [Para 86] [Para 88] [Para 89] Where the procedure is followed no wages are owed; where it is not, the employer must pay 100 per cent of wages and benefits even though no work is available. [Para 91] [Para 92] The company never requested authorisation. [Para 95] [Para 96] It fell back on the 2005 agreement, and the panel held that under Article 13 of the Labour Law, whose provisions are of public order, any agreement or unilateral decision that gives less than the Law is void; the 40 per cent clause therefore could not justify the shortfall. [Para 98] [Para 99] [Para 100] [Para 102] [Para 103] The benefits were already paid at 100 per cent, so only the wage gap of 60 per cent remained. [Para 83] [Para 103]

On Issue 2 the panel did not reach the merits. It treated the claim as an interest dispute, and under clause 43 of Prakas 99 an award in an interest dispute stands in place of a collective agreement for one year after it becomes final. [Para 126] [Para 129] [Para 130] Following Awards 48/09 and 28/10, the panel held it would not consider an interest dispute brought by a union that no longer holds most-representative status; the certificate had expired and none had been supplied. [Para 131] [Para 137] [Para 138] [Para 139] It also recorded that minimum wage is calculated monthly, not daily, and that the parties had agreed the guaranteed minimum was US$50 a month. [Para 56] [Para 119] [Para 122]

On Issue 3 the panel accepted the company's explanation. Both sides admitted the allowance had been paid monthly without deduction until late 2009, and the panel found the company had a proper reason for the change: it had been counting the allowance twice, once directly and again inside the average used for annual-leave pay, and it offered workers the choice between the allowance and its inclusion in the average. [Para 148] [Para 151] [Para 152] [Para 154] [Para 155] [Para 157]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer suspends contracts for lack of work without notifying the labour inspector and obtaining the inspector's confirmation of genuine economic difficulty, the suspension does not engage Article 72, and a collective agreement or past practice providing only a fraction of wages during such periods is void under Article 13 to the extent it falls below the Labour Law; the employer must pay the balance up to 100 per cent of wages, with benefits already paid at 100 per cent counted against that total. A demand to top up piece-rate earnings to the minimum wage is an interest dispute, and the panel will decline to hear it where the claimant union's certificate of most-representative status has expired and no new certificate has been produced. [Para 92] [Para 100] [Para 103] [Para 130] [Para 139]

**Obiter dicta:** that the minimum wage is a monthly guarantee rather than a daily one, so a worker's good days and bad days average out within the month, and that the company's explanation that it uses three branches rather than nearby sweatshops for outsourced work was not decisive. [Para 53] [Para 119] [Para 122]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is a Khmer original; arbitrator names appear only in Khmer and the Latin forms in the Forum line are transliterations, one of them largely lost at the signature block. [Para 1] [Para 164] Line 8 of the source is a run of orphaned brackets and punctuation with no recoverable text, and duplicate and corrupted passages recur — the Ministry's report is attributed to a differently named company in one line, and the panel is called "the Arbitration Council 2016" in another. [Para 8] [Para 19] [Para 30] The non-conciliation report bears the same number, 203/10, with two different dates, 7 April and 27 March 2010, and reached the Secretariat on 23 April 2010 while the award is dated 4 June 2010. [Para 9] [Para 15] The company's name is printed in at least four forms (ហ្វ័រធ្យូន, ហ្វរ ធ្យូន, ហ្វូរឡូន, ហូរ ឱ្យ នា) and the union's local is sometimes tied to a factory the award does not otherwise name. [Para 30] [Para 31] [Para 137] The amounts of the position allowance are inconsistent: the workers' evidence gives US$38 a month, the reasons give US$20, and total deductions are stated as 5.76 US dollars for five months. [Para 69] [Para 70] [Para 140] The workers' list of affected staff, promised for 7 May 2010, was never filed, leaving the class undefined. [Para 72] [Para 73] Individual workers, union officers and employer representatives are described by role only.
