# Award No. 49/10 — Ving Ying (Cambodia) Garment Factory Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 16 June 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (In Sothy, Liv Sovanna, Kong Phalla presiding) · Doc ID 140269

## 1. Facts
The Khmer Youth Union Federation filed sixteen demands on 26 April 2010 with the Kandal provincial labour department, seeking improved conditions at Ving Ying (Cambodia) Garment Factory Co., Ltd — reinstatement of the union president, shorter hours for pregnant workers, nursing breaks, accident cover, voluntary overtime, fresh shop-steward elections, parking, a nursery, profit sharing, raincoats, leave facilities and a wage top-up [Para 21][Para 2][Para 13]. All sixteen remained unresolved after conciliation on 6 May 2010 [Para 22]. At the hearing of 4 June 2010 the employer was absent and sought a postponement, which the workers opposed, reporting that the employer had dismissed all three union leaders — president, vice-president and secretary — on 3 June 2010 and barred them from the factory [Para 24]. The panel issued an order the same day requiring their reinstatement [Para 27]; on 8 June 2010 the employer wrote refusing to comply, saying the dismissals were lawful [Para 28][Para 29]. Called to explain on 10 June 2010, both sides stayed away and asked for a further adjournment, which the panel refused as no reason had been given in advance [Para 31][Para 33]. The workers then asked the panel to close the file, as in cases where workers strike during arbitration [Para 46][Para 54]. None of the sixteen demands was ever heard on its merits [Para 45].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Sole issue, procedural) Whether the panel should continue the arbitration at all, where the employer had twice failed to appear, had refused to obey the panel's order of 4 June 2010, and had dismissed all three union leaders while the case was pending — and where the workers asked for the file to be closed? [Para 26][Para 55]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer's dismissal of the entire union leadership one day before the hearing, and its refusal to let the three back in, aggravated the dispute and showed contempt for the arbitration process; the panel should therefore close the case rather than arbitrate, as it does where a strike breaks out mid-proceedings [Para 24][Para 46][Para 54]. They also sought an immediate order reinstating the three leaders [Para 25].
**Employer (respondent):** it did not attend either session and asked for postponements; in its letter of 8 June 2010 it stated that it would not implement the panel's order because the dismissals of the three leaders had been carried out "in accordance with the law" [Para 28][Para 29].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** withdrawn — the panel decided not to continue the arbitration in case No. 49/10-Ving Ying, leaving all sixteen demands undetermined and the reinstatement order of 4 June 2010 unenforced [Para 56][Para 57].

The panel applied Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, which obliges parties during arbitration to suspend every dispute measure — strike, lockout or any other step capable of aggravating the dispute — and to attend all meetings to which the panel summons them [Para 47][Para 48]. It found that the employer had neither appeared twice nor suspended its measures, having dismissed all three union leaders mid-proceedings in the very case they were conducting, which the workers said would provoke a strike [Para 50][Para 51][Para 26]. It also recorded that neither party attended the 10 June 2010 information session and that the panel declined a third postponement because no adequate reason had been notified before the appointed time [Para 53][Para 33]. Because the workers themselves asked for closure, and because the hearing could not proceed, the panel made no factual findings on any of the sixteen demands and closed the file [Para 45][Para 55].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
While a case is before the panel, Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 imposes a stay on both parties: any dispute measure that aggravates the situation — including dismissing the union leadership conducting the case — plus non-attendance, defeats the arbitration. Where that stay is broken and the claimant asks for closure, the panel will terminate the proceedings rather than adjudicate, leaving the demands unresolved and any interim reinstatement order unexecuted [Para 49][Para 51].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the workers were contemplating a strike in response to the dismissals, and that this was itself a reason the dispute had worsened, is not part of the operative reasoning, since the file was closed on the workers' own request [Para 26].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's name is printed at least four ways ("រឹង យីង", "វីង យីង", "វឹង", "Ving Ying"), and its province is given as both Kandal and "ខេត្ត កហ្គោល" [Para 1][Para 34]. The three arbitrators' names differ between the header and the signature block, with the employer-appointed member's name spelled with a different final consonant in each place, and the chair's name likewise [Para 1][Para 58]. The invitation letter to the employer is dated "២៨ ខែ ឆ្នាំ ២០១៦ ឧសភា ឆ្នាំ ២០១០", carrying a stray 2016 [Para 41]. Line 45 states that no facts were found because the hearing could not proceed, yet the reasons recite the 3 June dismissals and the 8 June refusal as established — an internal tension the brief reports rather than resolves [Para 45][Para 28].
