# Award No. 180/09-Prek Treng Trading — Prek Treng Trading Co., Ltd. v Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers Democratic Union (C.CAWDU) and Local Union of C.CAWDU (Arbitration Council, 10 February 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Panel: Kong Phallack (Chair), Kao Thach, Sin Kim Sean · Doc ID 140470

## 1. Facts
Prek Treng Trading Co., Ltd., a factory at Prek Treng village, S'ang district, Kandal employing about 1,200 workers, is the respondent; the claimant is the Local Union of C.CAWDU, represented by the Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers Democratic Union (C.CAWDU) [Para 1][Para 12]. On 23 June 2009 the parties recorded an agreement that each worker would be signed for a three-month fixed duration contract, renewable at expiry where performance satisfied the employer, with the union free to consider renewal [Para 13][Para 14]. Both sides accept the agreement was honoured until September 2009, when the company began issuing two-month contracts because it had less work, was supplementing production with work from other enterprises, and had suspended employment for up to two months at a time [Para 14][Para 18][Para 19]. On 30 November 2009 the company terminated the contracts without renewing them; after a strike it renewed for two months only [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24]. The panel heard three demands, but the demand that the employer stop threatening workers into renewing by thumbprint appears nowhere in the reasons or the order and was not decided [Para 5][Para 6][Para 56].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must honour the agreement of 23 June 2009 and renew the workers' fixed duration contracts for three months? [Para 31][Para 32]
2. (Issue 3, first limb) Whether the workers with less than two years' seniority are entitled to severance equal to 5 per cent of their wages at the expiry of each fixed duration contract, while the employment relationship continues? [Para 43][Para 47]
3. (Issue 3, second limb) Whether the fixed duration contracts of workers with more than two years' seniority must be converted to undetermined duration contracts? [Para 54][Para 56]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — Local Union of C.CAWDU and C.CAWDU:** the employer agreed in writing to three-month renewals yet has issued two-month contracts since September 2009, and its excuse that it can suspend the renewed contracts anyway does not excuse the breach; it must honour the agreement [Para 12][Para 25][Para 26]. Separately, 315 workers claim 5 per cent severance on each contract expiry under Article 73, and conversion to undetermined duration contracts for those with more than two years' service [Para 27][Para 29][Para 30].
**Employer (respondent) — Prek Treng Trading Co., Ltd.:** it shortened contracts to two months because the volume of work has fallen and buyers have withdrawn, and it can alternatively suspend contracts while paying only 50 per cent of the severance bonus; it did not threaten workers, and on the severance and conversion demand it declined to respond because the matter was already before the Arbitration Council [Para 4][Para 6][Para 15][Para 20][Para 30].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to renew the contracts for three months as agreed, rejected the demand for 5 per cent severance at each contract expiry, and ordered conversion of the contracts of workers with more than two years' seniority to undetermined duration [Para 56].
On Issue 1 the panel applied Articles 1 and 22 of Decree 38 on Contract and Other Responsibilities, treating the conciliation minute of 23 June 2009 as a legally binding agreement that neither side may revise without the other's consent [Para 33][Para 34][Para 35]. Since the workers never agreed to two-month terms and the company had in fact renewed on that shorter basis, the agreement was breached; the panel ordered three-month renewals [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41][Para 42]. The employer's lack of work did not displace the agreement, because the panel's order was framed as applying where the employer is able to renew at all [Para 39].
On the first limb of Issue 3 the panel read Article 73 paragraph six as making 5 per cent severance payable at the end of the employment relationship, and followed Award 76/06-South Bay for the rule that renewal means the relationship has not ended, so the 5 per cent is not due at each fixed-term expiry [Para 43][Para 45][Para 46][Para 47][Para 48]. Because the company had renewed these workers' contracts and they were still working, their relationships continued and the severance claim failed, consistently with Awards 14/08-Quicksew, 87/08-W & D and 85/09-Nan Kuang [Para 51][Para 52].
On the second limb, Article 67(2) caps a fixed duration contract and its renewals at two years, and the panel followed Awards 10/03-Jacqsintex, 36/06-Mondotex, 57/06-Evergreen and 155/09-USA in holding that renewals pushing total service past two years convert the contract to undetermined duration [Para 54][Para 55]. The employer's refusal to convert was contrary to the Labour Law and was ordered corrected [Para 56].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A conciliation minute recording that fixed duration contracts will be for three months is a binding contract under Decree 38, so an employer that later issues shorter terms without the union's consent breaches it and will be ordered to renew for the agreed period [Para 34][Para 38][Para 41]. Successive renewals of fixed duration contracts that carry total service past two years convert the contract to undetermined duration under Article 67(2), while 5 per cent severance under Article 73 falls due only when the employment relationship actually ends, not at each renewal [Para 47][Para 52][Para 55].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded the employer's alternative proposal to suspend contracts and pay 50 per cent of the severance bonus without ruling on its lawfulness [Para 20].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The whole evidence section is expressly omitted from the English version, so the documents weighed cannot be checked from this file [Para 11]. The OCR repeatedly fuses the article with the following noun, printing "the employer" as "hemployer", "the exact" as "thexact", "the end" as "athe end" and "one or more" as "twor more", which makes the quoted statutory text unreliable [Para 33][Para 43]. Paragraph 31 and paragraph 30 each end with a duplicated clause [Para 30][Para 31]. The award numbers its demands 1, 2 and 3 but the reasons and the order address only Issues 1 and 3, leaving the second demand undecided and unexplained [Para 5][Para 56]. Individual lawyers, employer representatives and union officers are named in full in the award and are reduced to roles here; no name was lost to OCR.
