# Award No. 10/10 — NagaWorld Ltd (Arbitration Council, 16 February 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Tun Si Phan, Pen Bun Chhea presiding) · Doc ID 140247

## 1. Facts
NagaWorld Ltd, a hotel and casino resort in Phnom Penh with about 3,000 staff, dismissed four union office-holders and activists on 26 February 2009 — its union secretary, a worker delegate and two activists — while they were pressing demands for a thirteenth-month bonus and a wage increase. All four signed agreements on 27 February and 2 March 2009 accepting lump-sum termination pay, after which the company stopped providing the monthly meeting room the union had used since 2000 and barred union leaders from entering the premises. The claimant was the Khmer Employees' Rights Union at Naga Resort, affiliated to the Tourism and Services Workers' Union Federation of Cambodia, with about 772 members where it had once had 956. The company refused to attend conciliation or the hearing and argued in letters of 15 and 26 January 2010 that the Council had already decided the same dispute in Award 47/09. [Para 52][Para 61][Para 74][Para 103][Para 107][Para 124]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must stop discriminating against union rights at the workplace and resume cooperating as the law requires, including providing a meeting place? [Para 171]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must take back the four dismissed union leaders and activists in their former positions and pay them back wages from dismissal until reinstatement? [Para 198]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the settlement agreements of 27 February and 2 March 2009 accepting lump-sum termination pay are void? [Para 219]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company had allowed monthly union meetings on the premises, on the 20th of each month between 14:30 and 16:00, until the February 2009 dismissals, and then withdrew the room and barred leaders from entry in retaliation; the four were dismissed without reason or labour inspector approval; and the settlement agreements were signed under duress, with security guards escorting each worker to the HR office, ID cards confiscated, and February wages withheld until they signed. [Para 75][Para 77][Para 107][Para 109][Para 133][Para 134]

**Employer (respondent):** the company did not appear at the hearing. In writing it maintained that the Council had already dealt with the same demands and issued an award in case 47/09, so it had no jurisdiction to hear them again, and that it had already complied with the legal procedure. [Para 15][Para 124][Para 147]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the company was ordered to cooperate with the union and provide meeting space as before and to allow union leaders to enter the premises to carry out their mandate; to reinstate all four in their former positions with back pay from the date of dismissal; and the settlement agreements of 27 February and 2 March 2009 were declared void. The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification if no objection is filed. [Para 250][Para 251][Para 252][Para 253]

As a threshold matter the panel held it could proceed in the employer's absence under article 21 of Prakas 099, which allows the panel to continue or to close the case by award where a properly summoned party fails to appear without good reason. It also addressed the employer's res judicata objection: the principle bars a second decision where the same parties brought the same issue to the same forum and the panel decided the substance, but it applies only where that third condition is met, and since Award 47/09 had not decided the substance, the principle could not be used to shut out the claims. [Para 142][Para 148][Para 151][Para 156][Para 157]

On standing, the panel noted that three of the four had told the prosecutor in July 2009 that they would not pursue the company personally, but held that the claimant here was the union, not the individuals, and that a union may bring a collective dispute to protect its members' interests. The three claims were therefore admissible. [Para 162][Para 164][Para 165][Para 170]

On Issue 1 the panel found no law obliging an employer to provide a union meeting room, following Award 106/06, but held that having granted the facility as a benefit to union activity the employer could not withdraw it because of the workers' union membership or activity: Articles 12 and 279 forbid an employer from using union membership as a ground for decisions on benefits, discipline or dismissal, and the company gave no reason at all for the change of position and offered no evidence to rebut the allegation. The order was made accordingly. [Para 172][Para 179][Para 181][Para 190][Para 197]

On Issue 2 the panel split the four. For the union secretary and the worker delegate, Article 293 requires the labour inspector's approval before a protected worker can be dismissed, and article 4 of Prakas 305 extends that protection to union leaders; no approval had been sought. For the two activists, the dismissals were found to rest on their union activity in pursuing workers' benefits, contrary to Articles 12 and 279. All four were therefore reinstated with back pay. [Para 201][Para 202][Para 206][Para 208][Para 213][Para 218]

On Issue 3 the panel applied Article 6 of Decree-Law 38 (a contract must arise from genuine, free consent) and Article 9 (violence, including physical or moral coercion, is a ground of nullity): the security escort, the confiscated ID cards, the refusal of February wages unless the worker signed, and the absence of any real choice amounted to coercion, so consent was not genuine. It added that Article 13 makes agreements giving less than the Labour Law void, and the lump-sum severance had been miscalculated — one month, or 26 days, for two years' service where Article 89 requires 15 days per year, or 30 days. The agreements were declared void. [Para 220][Para 223][Para 230][Para 234][Para 246][Para 249]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Res judicata blocks a second arbitration only where the earlier panel actually decided the substance of the same dispute; an award that left the merits open does not. A facility granted to union activity, such as a meeting room, may not be withdrawn on account of union membership, and a union leader or worker delegate cannot be dismissed without the labour inspector's prior approval even for an economic reason. A settlement releasing claims for less than the statutory entitlement, obtained by withholding wages and escorting workers to sign, is void for want of free consent. [Para 151][Para 181][Para 206][Para 234]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that no provision of the Labour Law or Prakas 286 obliges an employer to provide a union meeting room in the first place, and that the four individuals' statements to the prosecutor did not bind the union, were observations that framed but did not decide the orders. [Para 172][Para 164]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is internally inconsistent about the earlier case: at one point it records that Award 47/09 of 7 May 2009 already decided the reinstatement demand concerning fourteen people, and at another that the panel in 47/09 had not decided the substance at all — the reasoning on res judicata depends on the second version. The earlier case number is printed both as 47/09 and as a garbled "No. 7/09". The Prakas 305 citation carries two different dates, 22 and 24 November 2001, in adjacent paragraphs. Individual names are reproduced inconsistently across the evidence schedules (one worker appears under three spellings of her given and family names), and the signature block renders one arbitrator's name in a corrupted form. The award speaks at times of two dismissed workers and at times of four, reflecting the split analysis. [Para 56][Para 147][Para 156][Para 202][Para 203][Para 254]
