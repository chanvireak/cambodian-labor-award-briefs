# Order No. 126/10 — PY International Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ភី វ៉ាយ អ៉ិនធើណេសិន ណល) and the workers of PY Factory represented by the Federation of Unions for Workers' Rights (Arbitration Council, 6 December 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (You Soun Ty យូ សួន ទី; Liv Sovanna លីវ សុវណ្ណា; Pen Bun Chhea ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា presiding) · Doc ID 140331

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ភី វ៉ាយ អ៉ិនធើណេសិន ណល (PY International), a factory at Mondol 1, Sangkat 4, Preah Sihanouk City [Para 1], faced a twelve-point collective demand from its workers, presented through the Federation of Unions for Workers' Rights (សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព សិទ្ឋិ ប្រយោជន៍ កម្មករ) [Para 1][Para 2]. The demands ran from reinstatement of a union leader elected on 19 October 2010 and dismissed without fault, through piece-rate overtime and "pong" rates, discipline of Chinese line supervisors, unfair fines, severance, anti-union discrimination, maternity pay, transport after 8 p.m., leave, and toilet and water conditions [Para 2][Para 3][Para 4][Para 8][Para 9][Para 11]. Workers struck from 4 November 2010; the Provincial Department of Labour and Vocational Training failed to conciliate on 5 November 2010 and referred the file on 10 November 2010 [Para 20][Para 21][Para 22]. The hearing was fixed for 19 November 2010 at 14:00 [Para 18][Para 23]. The worker party did not attend; only a federation representative appeared, without any written authority from the workers, and asked for an adjournment [Para 24][Para 35][Para 36][Para 51]. The company attended with counsel and its defence bundle [Para 25][Para 26].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the three conditions for closing a case in a party's absence — that the party was properly notified, that it did not appear or send an authorized representative, and that it gave no valid reason — were satisfied, so that the panel could close case 126/10 by order without reaching the twelve demands? [Para 41][Para 44]

The twelve demands themselves were never reached: the panel held it could find no facts relating to them because the claimant party was absent [Para 35].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), the workers of PY Factory through សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព សិទ្ឋិ ប្រយោជន៍ កម្មករ:** no substantive position was put. Their federation representative, Mr H, appeared only to notify the panel of the union leadership's non-attendance and to seek a postponement, explaining that the union president had been suspended by the company and could not meet travel costs, while the vice-president and secretary had personal business [Para 37][Para 57][Para 58].

**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន ភី វ៉ាយ អ៉ិនធើណេសិន ណល:** the company attended as invited, represented by its deputy director and counsel, and filed a defence statement plus a Preah Sihanouk Provincial Court protective order; it did not seek an adjournment [Para 25][Para 26][Para 28].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel ordered case No. 126/10 (PY International) closed, without deciding the twelve demands, because the worker party was absent from the hearing [Para 62][Para 63]

The panel grounded the power to proceed in the absence of a party on Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council and on Rule 4.7 of its annexed procedural rules, both of which allow the panel either to continue or to terminate by order where a duly invited party fails to appear [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40]. From its own earlier orders the panel extracted three cumulative conditions: proper notification, non-appearance, and absence of a valid reason [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43]. On the first, the Secretariat had issued formal invitations to both parties by the usual procedure and confirmed the date, time and place by letter and telephone, so the condition was met [Para 45][Para 47][Para 48]. On the second, Article 19 of Prakas 099 lets a party appear in person or through a lawyer or another person authorized in writing; Mr H held no such authority and could not represent the claimants, so the workers had not appeared [Para 49][Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 54]. On the third, the panel found the reasons offered insufficient: the president's suspension explained his own absence but not the union's, and the personal business of the vice-president and secretary was never shown to be necessary [Para 55][Para 57][Para 59][Para 60][Para 61]. Because no facts could be established, the panel closed the file rather than deciding the demands [Para 35][Para 62].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a claimant party has been properly notified of the hearing, fails to appear in person or through a representative authorized in writing, and offers no valid reason, the panel may terminate the arbitration by order closing the case without examining the merits; a trade-union federation official who holds no written authority from the workers cannot stand in for them, and the suspension of a union president does not by itself excuse the absence of the union's other officers [Para 52][Para 55][Para 60].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that it could find no facts on any of the twelve demands, which left every demand undetermined rather than rejected [Para 35].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The file is an order (សេចក្តីបង្គាប់) closing proceedings, not a merits award. The number of demands is printed inconsistently: the procedural narrative records a complaint of "10" demands, while the list itself runs to twelve and the conciliation failure is described as covering twelve points [Para 2][Para 12][Para 19][Para 21]. The instrument date in the header (6 December 2010) and the hearing date (19 November 2010) are the only dates given for the order, and no separate signing date appears [Para 1][Para 18]. The union federation's name appears in several corrupt spellings (សហព័ន្ឋ / សហព័ន្ធ / សហព័ន / សហជីប), and the employer's name is printed as ភី វ៉ាយ, ភី វាយ and ភីវ៉ាយ in different places [Para 1][Para 30][Para 32]. A stray year "2015" intrudes into the reasoning, and OCR noise ("the sent", "behba", "marioral") corrupts the signature block [Para 33][Para 56][Para 64]. Individual workers and union officers are identified by initial or role only.
