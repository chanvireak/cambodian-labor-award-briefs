# Award No. 78/10 — NagaWorld Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ណាហ្គាវើល ដ៏ លីមីធីត) (Arbitration Council, 6 September 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of three: សេង វូច ហ៊ុន (employer-nominated), ទូន ស៊ី ផាន់ (worker-nominated), ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា (chair) · Doc ID 140296

## 1. Facts

Two union federations at the NagaWorld hotel and casino in Chamkar Mon, Phnom Penh — the Cambodian Tourism and Service Workers' Federation and the Khmer Employees' and Workers' Rights Union of Naga Resort — brought three collective demands in June 2010 after dismissals that followed a work stoppage on International Women's Day. [Para 22][Para 52] The plant employed about 3,200. [Para 45] Conciliation failed on all three; the first was settled at the hearing, leaving the reinstatement of two dismissed union activists and the correct calculation of termination pay for 20 dismissed employees. [Para 17][Para 18][Para 95] An earlier award, No. 10/10 of 16 February 2010, had already ordered the company to reinstate four union leaders, and the company had written objecting to it. [Para 48][Para 58]

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. (Issue 2) Whether the company must take back Ms S and Ms M, dismissed union activists, and pay them back wages from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 51]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the termination payments to the 20 dismissed employees — 18 on indefinite contracts and 2 on fixed-term contracts — were computed in accordance with the Labour Law? [Para 95][Para 100][Para 199]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant) — សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត វិស័យ ទេសចរណ៍ និងសេវាកម្ម កម្ពុជា and សហជីព ទ្រង់ សិទ្ធិ ការងារ បុគ្គលិក កម្មករ ខ្មែរ នៃ មណ្ឌល កំសាន្ត ណាហ្គា:** the two women were union activists dismissed without notice and without any stated reason, in the middle of a shift, by security escorts, after joining the 8 March 2010 stoppage, so the dismissals were anti-union; on severance, the company averaged daily wage over 30 days instead of 26 and used basic wage alone instead of the full 12-month earnings. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 101][Para 102]

**Employer (respondent) — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ណាហ្គាវើល ដ៏ លីមីធីត:** the dismissals were individual labour disputes outside the panel's jurisdiction; the company in any event paid what the law required and had no cause to state; and the employees signed agreements accepting the sums paid, which the company asked to be upheld. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 74][Para 105]

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** granted — the company was ordered to reinstate Ms S and Ms M with back pay from the date of dismissal; and to recompute severance under Article 89 on the last 12 months' wages and benefits divided by 12 and then by 26 days, to pay moral damages equal to that severance, and to pay notice-in-lieu under Articles 75 and 77; for the two fixed-term workers, termination pay under Article 73. [Para 213][Para 214][Para 216][Para 217][Para 218]

On jurisdiction the panel first tested the company's objection. Under Article 302 a dispute is collective where it is between an employer and a number of workers, concerns working conditions or the exercise of union rights, and is capable of disrupting the enterprise or social peace; all three conditions were met because the claim was filed by a union and two federations and was backed by many workers. [Para 117][Para 119][Para 120][Para 122] The panel added that it ordinarily follows the labour inspector's and the Minister's classification unless there is clear reason not to. [Para 115]

On the dismissals, Article 74 permits termination of an indefinite contract only on written notice and only for a reason concerning the worker's aptitude or conduct or the needs of the enterprise. [Para 129][Para 131] The employer conceded at the hearing that it did not rely on the workers' past errors and dismissed them without cause, and it could state no reason at all. [Para 135][Para 136] Because the dismissals came days after the 8 March stoppage and were executed by security in working hours, the panel found they were connected to union activity, contrary to Articles 12 and 279. [Para 142][Para 144][Para 145]

On severance, Article 89 gives 15 days' wages and benefits per year of service, capped at six months. [Para 153][Para 155] The panel held the daily wage must be found from total earnings over the last 12 months — including overtime, bonuses and allowances — divided by 12 and then by 26 working days, not by 30 and not from basic wage alone. [Para 158][Para 159][Para 176] Taking one worker as the test case, the panel found the company had paid US$1,485 on a US$300 base for nine years, whereas the lawful figure is far higher. [Para 162][Para 165][Para 166] Article 13 makes any agreement giving less than the law void, so the signed settlements could not stand. [Para 170][Para 175]

Moral damages followed: Article 91 allows them where a contract is ended without valid reason, they are distinct from severance and notice pay, and the worker need not prove loss. [Para 178][Para 181] For notice, Article 77 requires pay equal to wages and all benefits for the notice period, and the company had paid wages only. [Para 192][Para 194] For the two fixed-term workers the company had applied the indefinite-contract method; the panel held Article 73 governs instead. [Para 200][Para 201][Para 209]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

Where an employer dismisses without stating any reason and the dismissal follows union activity, the panel will find a dismissal without valid cause under Article 74 and anti-union under Articles 12 and 279, and will order reinstatement with back pay rather than accept the compensation already paid; and an agreement by which a worker accepts less than the statutory severance is void under Article 13 and does not bar a claim. [Para 136][Para 144][Para 170][Para 175]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that it normally defers to the labour inspectorate's classification of a dispute as individual or collective, but will depart from it where the statutory conditions are plainly satisfied. [Para 115]

## 6. Source defects disclosed

The claimant group is described as 20 persons in the issue statement but as 18 in the reasons, the two fixed-term workers being counted separately elsewhere. [Para 96][Para 149] The test-case worker's base wage is printed as US$330 in one line and US$300 in the arithmetic two lines later, and the resulting severance figure is printed as "20.95 dollars", which cannot be the product of the figures given. [Para 163][Para 165][Para 168] The demand list puts the number of employees accepting termination at 42, whereas the panel decided on 20. [Para 4][Para 95] The appendix tables of names, entry dates and payments are destroyed by OCR, and the signature block is unreadable apart from the chair's name. [Para 109][Para 111][Para 220] Article 279 is quoted with a stray year, "2016", inserted mid-sentence. [Para 139] The individual claimants are reduced to initials in this brief; the award prints them in full.
