# Award No. 73/10 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន យូនីសាន់ ហ្គាមេន / Unisun Garment (Arbitration Council, 10 August 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · arbitrators ឈីវ ភិរម្យ, វង្ស វណ្ណា, គង់ ផល្លៈ (president) · Doc ID 140291

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន យូនីសាន់ ហ្គាមេន ("Unisun Garment") employed about 800 workers and had two unions, each with about 70 members [Para 41]. The claimant was សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព លើកតម្កើងជីវភាពកម្មករ និយោជិតខ្មែរ ("Khmer Workers' Livelihood Promotion Federation") and its in-company branch, which had applied for registration but held no certificate because the employer objected that its president had an unresolved labour dispute [Para 42][Para 44][Para 45]. That president, Mr S, had worked since 23 October 2007 on an undetermined-duration contract, was deputy head of the cutting section, and was elected union president on 10 May 2010 with 25 of 30 votes [Para 46][Para 47][Para 50][Para 51]. The employer suspended his contract on 27 May 2010 and asked the Labour Disputes Department to approve his dismissal on 28 May; the union only notified the company of the election result on 29 May [Para 54][Para 52][Para 98]. Fifteen of the seventeen original demands were settled in conciliation and two were referred [Para 12][Para 13].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the dismissal of the union president was lawful under the Labour Law and the company's internal rules, and whether it amounted to union discrimination? [Para 77]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must issue fixed-duration contracts of at least six months to newly hired workers? [Para 107]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimants, សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព លើកតម្កើងជីវភាពកម្មករ និយោជិតខ្មែរ and its Unisun branch):** Mr S should be restored to normal work because his dismissal was unjustified and discriminatory — the company moved against him days after he was elected union president [Para 2][Para 59]. He said he never received a written warning and had only committed minor faults for which he apologised; he had also not been paid his June wages during the suspension [Para 57][Para 58][Para 60]. On contracts, they wanted six-month terms so workers would not lose seniority, maternity leave and termination benefits, and would not be dismissed for union membership [Para 67].
**Employer (respondent, ក្រុមហ៊ុន យូនីសាន់ ហ្គាមេន):** Mr S committed serious misconduct and was repeatedly reprimanded by his supervisor, though the company concedes it never issued a written warning [Para 56][Para 83]. It cannot take him back as union president because he did not respect the internal rules and did not obey the factory head [Para 3]. On contracts, it moved from six months to two and then three months because of the economic crisis and order volumes, pays 5% termination compensation and annual leave, and says unhappy workers may seek work elsewhere [Para 63][Para 64][Para 68][Para 71].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must take Mr S back into employment; the demand for six-month contracts is dismissed [Para 138][Para 139]. The order for reinstatement carries no award of back wages.

On Issue 1 the panel first applied Article 74, since Mr S held an undetermined-duration contract: such a contract may be terminated by either party on written notice, but no dismissal may occur without a valid reason related to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the needs of the enterprise [Para 79][Para 80][Para 81]. It then read the internal rules, which prescribe graduated discipline — oral education recorded in the file, then written warning, and dismissal only after repeated fault — and found the company never warned Mr S in writing at all [Para 82][Para 84][Para 85]. The dismissal therefore breached the company's own disciplinary procedure [Para 86].

On the discrimination question, the panel noted that the burden of proving union discrimination lies on the workers [Para 93]. The election was on 10 May but the union told the administration only on 29 May, after the 27 May suspension and the 28 May dismissal request, and the panel found no evidence that the employer knew of the election, nor any reprisal or disciplinary action after it [Para 98][Para 100][Para 101]. It concluded the dismissal was not union discrimination [Para 102]. Reinstatement was nonetheless ordered under Article 34 of Prakas 099, which empowers the panel to order a dismissed worker back into the former or a suitable post [Para 103][Para 105][Para 106].

On Issue 2 the panel asked first whether the union could represent its members at all. Under Article 268 a professional organisation acquires legal rights and benefits only once registered, and this union had no certificate [Para 107][Para 110][Para 115][Para 116]. Only three workers attended, and no valid written authorisation from other members was produced, so the panel considered the claim only for those three [Para 121][Para 122][Para 123]. On the merits, Article 65 and Articles 1 and 22 of the 1988 contract decree make the contract the law of the parties, and the panel, as a third party, cannot impose a term against either party's will; the three-month practice therefore stands [Para 128][Para 131][Para 136][Para 137].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer's internal rules set a graduated disciplinary procedure, a dismissal that skips the recorded oral warning and the written warning is unlawful even if the worker's conduct was imperfect, and the panel will order reinstatement under Article 34 of Prakas 099 without requiring proof of union discrimination [Para 82][Para 86][Para 103][Para 138].

**Obiter dicta:** the burden of proving union discrimination rests on the worker, and an employer that learns of a union election only after suspending and requesting dismissal cannot be shown to have acted on that knowledge; the panel cannot fix a contract's duration against an employer's will [Para 93][Para 100][Para 136].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
OCR is degraded and the file is short (141 lines), so parts of the reasoning are fragmentary. The individual worker's name is printed in at least four conflicting variants across the face of the award and the evidence list, and is given here by initial only. The union's own name is likewise garbled in the header, with stray characters inside the Khmer text [Para 1]. Dates conflict: the union says it notified the company of the election on 29 May, while the panel elsewhere records the notice as 29 May but the election as 10 May and the suspension as 27 May, which the panel used to find no knowledge [Para 52][Para 98][Para 100]. Article references are partly mangled, including "Prakas 099 of 21 April 2019 2004" and "Article 222" for Article 22 [Para 103][Para 133]. The number of settled demands (15 of 17) is stated once and never reconciled with the two referred issues [Para 12][Para 13].
