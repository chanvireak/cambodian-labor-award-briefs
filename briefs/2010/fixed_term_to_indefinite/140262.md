# Award No. 40/10 — Meng Yan Garment Manufactory & Wet Processing Ltd (Arbitration Council, 11 June 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach; Sin Kimsean; chair Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 140262

## 1. Facts
Meng Yan Garment Manufactory & Wet Processing Ltd runs a garment and wet-processing factory at Svay Rolum, Kandal Province, which began operating in November 2007 and employed 540 workers. [Para 17] [Para 18] [Para 19] A local union of C.CAWDU held its leadership election on 28 March 2010 and notified the employer of the result on 29 March 2010; its registration application was never forwarded by the Department of Labour Disputes, which by letter of 30 April 2010 refused to transmit it because the elected secretary was under the 25-year age of eligibility. [Para 19] [Para 21] [Para 22] [Para 60] Between 31 March and 20 April 2010 the employer suspended or terminated seven union leaders and activists, and the workers demanded reinstatement of all seven with back wages from dismissal to reinstatement. [Para 24] [Para 32] [Para 52] Only four — the president, vice-president, secretary and treasurer — attended or were represented at the hearing; the three activists were absent and had authorised no one. [Para 54] [Para 55] [Para 64]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the unregistered local union of C.CAWDU has legal standing to represent its absent members in the dispute before the panel? [Para 56] [Para 63]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the suspension and termination of the four workers who stood for union leadership complied with the legal procedure required by the Labour Law? [Para 65] [Para 83]
3. (Issue 3) Whether those terminations were in fact based on union discrimination, as the workers alleged, rather than on refusal of the employer's directions and contract expiry? [Para 84] [Para 85]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that the terminations were union discrimination, coming immediately after the election result was notified, and that the head of administration had told the president on 30 March 2010 not to establish the union because he had worked with him for a long time. [Para 24] [Para 25]
**Employer (respondent):** that the president was suspended and the vice-president and secretary terminated for refusing directions from the Head of Production and Head of Section, that the remaining four were terminated because their fixed-duration contracts expired, and that no union discrimination was involved. [Para 31] [Para 32] [Para 33]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer is ordered to reinstate the four workers and to pay them wages from the date of termination to the date of reinstatement; the award is non-binding and becomes binding eight days after notification absent written opposition. [Para 95]

On standing, the panel read Article 268 of the Labour Law as giving a professional organisation the rights and benefits of the law only once the Minister in charge of Labour has registered it, and followed Award 62/06-Quicksew and others in holding that those rights include the right to represent members before the Arbitration Council. [Para 56] [Para 57] [Para 62] [Para 63] Because registration had not been granted, the union could not represent the absent activists, and under clause 19 of Prakas 99 they had authorised no one in writing; the panel therefore confined itself to the four who were present. [Para 60] [Para 61] [Para 63] [Para 64] [Para 65]

On protected status, clause 7 of Prakas 313 limits "union leaders" to the president, vice-president and secretary, so the treasurer, though he stood for office, was not a union leader. [Para 66] [Para 67] Clause 3 paragraph 3 of Prakas 305 nevertheless protects any worker who runs for a leadership position as a shop steward from 45 days before the election, and the panel held that this covers candidates for office whether or not they are elected; the employer knew of the candidacies from the notification of 29 March 2010. [Para 68] [Para 71] [Para 73] [Para 74] Under Article 293 the dismissal of a protected worker requires prior authorisation from the Labour Inspector, and the panel found that none was obtained: the president's suspension was merely requested of the provincial department on 2 April 2010, and the other fixed-duration contracts were cut short without authorisation. [Para 78] [Para 79] [Para 81] [Para 82] The terminations therefore breached the legal procedure. [Para 83]

On discrimination, Articles 12 and 279 forbid an employer to weigh union membership or activity in decisions on hiring and dismissal, and the panel applied Award 10/03-Jaqsintex and 10/10-Nagaworld to the non-renewal and termination of union activists. [Para 85] [Para 86] It rejected the stated reasons: the workers were terminated days after the election notice; the president held an undetermined-duration contract yet was dismissed at once without notice; the vice-president and secretary had their unexpired three-month contracts cut short; and the employer's directional right had been used unreasonably, with repeated transfers between sections, warning letters on refusal, and non-renewal threats. [Para 88] [Para 89] [Para 91] [Para 92] [Para 93] One worker refused a task outside his job description and was warned and terminated for it. [Para 94] The panel concluded that all four terminations rested on union participation. [Para 95]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A local union that has applied for but not obtained registration holds none of the rights conferred by Article 268 of the Labour Law, including standing to represent its members before the panel, and absent members who have given no written authority under clause 19 of Prakas 99 fall outside the award's scope even where they are named in the collective demand. On the merits, a worker who runs for any union leadership post — including one, such as treasurer, that Prakas 313 does not count as a leadership post — is a protected candidate under clause 3 paragraph 3 of Prakas 305 from 45 days before the election, so termination without Labour Inspector authorisation under Article 293 is unlawful; and where the employer's stated ground of refusing directions is preceded by repeated transfers, warnings and renewal threats after the election, the panel will find the true ground to be union activity. [Para 67] [Para 73] [Para 83] [Para 92] [Para 95]

**Obiter dicta:** that Prakas 305 fixes no express end to the protection of a candidate who is elected, and that protection for a candidate who is not elected should run at least 45 days after the election or until the application for registration is filed. [Para 74]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award's chronology is internally impossible: the hearing is dated 12 March 2010, yet the complaint is said to have reached the provincial department on 21 April 2010, with a last conciliation on 29 April 2010, while the non-conciliation report is dated 4 March 2010. [Para 6] [Para 7] [Para 8] The panel writes that "three of them are union leaders" and then lists four names. [Para 54] The president's contract appears as a three-month contract renewed over two years in the facts but as an undetermined-duration contract terminated on 27 March 2010 in the reasons. [Para 35] [Para 87] [Para 88] The termination table at the end has lost its column alignment and several dates. [Para 87] One arbitrator's name is printed "Sin Kimsean" on the panel list and "Sin Kim Sean" at signature. [Para 1] [Para 96] OCR drops the space after "the" throughout ("themployer", "thevidence", "withemployer") and truncates words ("Traing", "Assistanto"). [Para 2] [Para 19] The award is an unofficial English translation; individual workers, activists and employer representatives are reduced to initials and roles here, and two workers' names vary in spelling between passages.
