# Award No. 110/10 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហុង ម៉ី (Hong Mi Co., Ltd) and សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ នៃ រោងចក្រ ហុង ម៉ី (Arbitration Council, 10 November 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Houn Chun Dy, Pen Bun Chhea) · Doc ID 140317

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហុង ម៉ី (Hong Mi Co., Ltd), a garment factory at Suon Canadia, Chom Chao, Dangkao, employing about 450 workers, dismissed 27 workers of its "srei" section on 18 September 2010 after they left at 4 p.m. instead of working overtime on 17 September. [Para 32][Para 41][Para 43][Para 71]
Nineteen were taken back from 21 September; eight remained out, all on indefinite-duration contracts. [Para 35][Para 47]
The union local, សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ នៃ រោងចក្រ ហុង ម៉ី, is the claimant; the employer did not appear at the 25 October 2010 hearing and filed no documents. [Para 13][Para 33][Para 50]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must take the eight dismissed workers back, or, failing that, pay them end-of-contract compensation? [Para 34][Para 58]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា and its ហុង ម៉ី local, through Mr U, federation officer:** the eight were dismissed because they refused overtime on 17 September and were the most active in pressing the workers' claims, so the dismissal was retaliation for union activity and they must be reinstated or compensated. [Para 48][Para 60][Para 71]
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហុង ម៉ី:** in its written conciliation position only, the company said it could not take the 27 back because they incited the 13–15 September 2010 strike; it did not attend the hearing to defend that allegation. [Para 3][Para 49][Para 67]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must take the eight workers back, and the operative order requires it to pay them end-of-contract compensation: notice pay, dismissal damages, moral damages, final wages and unused annual leave. [Para 78][Para 103]
The panel first held it could proceed in the employer's absence: Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY (21 April 2004) allows continuation when a properly summoned party fails to appear without good cause, following Awards 53/04, 63/04, 148/07, 99/09 and 118/09; the panel noted the employer thus abandoned its chance to prove the incitement allegation. [Para 51][Para 53][Para 69][Para 70]
On the dismissal, Article 74 of the Labour Law allows termination at either party's will but forbids dismissal without a valid reason relating to the worker's aptitude or conduct or the running of the enterprise. Point 4 of Prakas No. 80 SKBY (1 March 1999) makes overtime voluntary: an employer may not coerce or discipline a worker who declines it. Dismissing the eight for leaving at 4 p.m. was therefore not a lawful exercise of that right. [Para 61][Para 63][Para 73][Para 75][Para 76][Para 77]
Having found no valid reason, the panel held the employer obliged to reinstate all eight, and went on to fix the alternative in case reinstatement was impossible. [Para 78][Para 79]
Notice pay follows Articles 75 and 77: seven days under six months' service, fifteen days from six months to two years, one month over two years, and payment in lieu where notice was not given. [Para 80][Para 83]
Dismissal damages follow Article 89 — fifteen days of wage and allowances per year of service, capped at six months, with seven days for workers under twelve months — and Article 91 adds moral damages equal to the dismissal damages where termination was without valid reason. [Para 84][Para 86][Para 89][Para 91][Para 94]
Final wages are due within 48 hours under Article 116, and unused annual leave is payable under Articles 166 and 167 only for days actually remaining, which the workers had not evidenced. [Para 95][Para 97][Para 101][Para 102]
The order is not binding; it becomes final eight days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Minister of Labour. [Para 104]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A dismissal whose sole ground is a worker's refusal to work overtime is dismissal without a valid reason, because overtime rests on consent and Prakas No. 80 forbids coercing or disciplining a worker who declines it; the remedy is reinstatement, and where that is not possible the statutory package of notice pay, Article 89 damages, Article 91 moral damages, final wages and unused leave. [Para 73][Para 75][Para 78][Para 103]

**Obiter dicta:** A properly summoned party's unexplained absence lets the panel decide on the claimant's evidence alone, and the absent party bears the loss of its unproven allegations; annual leave compensation was refused for want of any evidence of remaining days. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 101]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The number of affected workers is printed inconsistently: 27 dismissed, 19 reinstated and 8 remaining, yet the damages paragraph opens by saying five or six workers are entitled and then lists eight names, and the final-wage paragraph uses a different numeral again; the brief follows the eight named workers. [Para 47][Para 89][Para 96]
The attendance and notice-period tables are column-scrambled by OCR, with dates duplicated across rows and the entry date of one worker printed as 1970; the individual periods were read from the fifteen-day and seven-day entries only. [Para 36][Para 84]
Worker names are rendered differently across the two tables (one surname appears with two different given names, another with a doubled or single initial consonant, a third with two vowel spellings), so initials are used throughout this brief rather than any of the printed forms. [Para 36][Para 89]
The complaint date's month is unreadable at line 9, the employer's conciliation statement is dated 27 September 2010 while the non-conciliation report is dated 4 October 2010, and the employer-chosen arbitrator's name is spelled differently in the signature block than on the face of the award. [Para 1][Para 9][Para 66][Para 105]
