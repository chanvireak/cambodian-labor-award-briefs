# Award No. 96/10 — K.C. Jason Enterprise (Arbitration Council, 28 September 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Chhiv Phirom (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Aing Eng Thong (chair) · Doc ID 139770

## 1. Facts
K.C. Jason Enterprise (គ.ឈ ហ្សេស៊ិន សហគ្រាស) is a construction company. More than 60 of its employees struck on 16 August 2010 for improved conditions, and the company dismissed workers in successive batches that month, including union leaders; conciliation on 17 August 2010 left all 11 demands unresolved. [Para 2][Para 20][Para 22][Para 181] On 31 August 2010 the panel ordered the strike suspended and the employer to readmit workers not yet dismissed; the workers stopped and dispersed, but the employer refused to let 21 of them back in. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 28][Para 133]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must take back the union leaders and all the workers it dismissed? [Para 3]
2. Whether overtime on normal working days must be paid at the statutory premium, on an eight-hour, six-day week? [Para 4][Para 192]
3. Whether work on Sundays and public holidays must be paid at double the normal rate? [Para 5]
4. Whether paid annual leave must be applied? [Para 6]
5. Whether wages must be paid on the 7th of each month and twice a month? [Para 7]
6. Whether wages must be calculated monthly instead of daily? [Para 7]
7. Whether a meal allowance of 1,000 riel per overtime hour is owed? [Para 8]
8. Whether maternity leave, with 50% of wages and allowances, must be paid? [Para 9][Para 256]
9. Whether the company must supply adequate safety equipment? [Para 10]
10. Whether the company must answer for work accidents and insure against them? [Para 11]
11. Whether the company may deduct wages for lost or damaged property that was not the worker's fault? [Para 12]
12. Whether the 21 workers excluded after the strike ended must be readmitted? [Para 130][Para 294]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissed workers had served for years from 1992 onwards without written contracts, were therefore not temporary, and 26 were dismissed on 13 August 2010 and 12 more on 15 August after protesting; every demand rested on the Labour Law. [Para 81][Para 82][Para 83][Para 87]
**Employer (respondent):** the workers were day-rate, worked intermittently rather than continuously, and were let go only because orders had dried up; it also objected to the worker-appointed arbitrator and, after issue 1 was heard, withdrew from the hearing, so issues 2 to 11 were heard in its absence. [Para 84][Para 88][Para 96][Para 145][Para 148][Para 149]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered priority of re-engagement for the 61 dismissed workers for 2 years, overtime at the 50% premium, 100% premiums for holiday and Sunday work, paid annual leave, at least two wage payments a month, maternity pay at 50%, safety equipment, employer liability for work accidents, reinstatement of the 21 with back pay from dismissal to return, and an end to deductions for property outside the workers' charge; it dismissed the fixed pay date, the monthly-wage, meal-allowance and no-liability claims. [Para 308][Para 313][Para 316][Para 320][Para 322]
Procedurally, the panel first asked whether it could continue at all, the employer having ignored the readmission order. It held that Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 obliges the parties to suspend strikes and lockouts, but does not bar the panel when the party obstructing the process is the employer: refusing to let workers back after they complied was itself a measure aggravating the dispute, so the arbitration continued. [Para 137][Para 139][Para 140][Para 143] It also rejected the objection to its composition for want of adequate grounds. [Para 145][Para 146]
On issue 1, the panel held that unwritten contracts become indefinite-term contracts under Article 67 of the Labour Law, so none of the dismissed workers was a casual hire. [Para 150][Para 152][Para 154] Union leaders, however, earned no special protection: Article 3 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 protects election candidates only where the union notifies the employer by any reliable means, and notice left with a security guard was not such notice. [Para 156][Para 160][Para 166][Para 167] The 61 dismissals in one month were a collective layoff under Article 95, and the employer skipped the required ranking by professional skill, seniority and family burden and never notified the labour inspector; the panel therefore ordered priority of re-engagement for two years under Article 95(5). [Para 180][Para 181][Para 187][Para 191]
Overtime on normal days was payable at 150% of the ordinary wage under Article 139, and the employer had paid only 100%. [Para 194][Para 196][Para 199][Para 200] Holiday work drew a further 100% under Article 164 and Prakas No. 100 of 4 February 1999, and Sunday work the same under Article 139. [Para 201][Para 204][Para 210][Para 212] Annual leave of 18 days a year, or 1.5 days a month, was owed under Articles 166 and 167. [Para 213][Para 217][Para 219] On pay dates, Article 116 fixes a floor, not a day: the employer's managerial prerogative under Article 2 let it choose the date, and the workers had not shown the 7th closed a full month, so that claim failed while the twice-monthly minimum for workers was ordered. [Para 220][Para 222][Para 228][Para 233] Converting to a monthly wage was refused because Article 11 lets the employer pay by month, day or hour. [Para 236][Para 251][Para 252] The meal allowance failed because Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 reaches only textile, garment and footwear workers, not construction. [Para 253][Para 254][Para 255]
Maternity pay was granted under Articles 182 and 183: 90 days' leave and half wages including allowances. [Para 257][Para 259][Para 261] Safety equipment was ordered under Articles 230 and 250 and clause 13 of the internal rules. [Para 262][Para 264][Para 269] Work accidents fell on the employer under Articles 248, 249, 252 and 254, including treatment costs. [Para 272][Para 275][Para 278][Para 279] On damaged property, the panel read Article 127 as allowing deduction only for materials in the worker's charge and use, and barred recovery otherwise. [Para 280][Para 291][Para 293]
Finally, dismissing the 21 workers for striking was unlawful: Article 332(2) restores strikers to their jobs when the strike ends and Article 333 voids sanctions imposed for taking part, so reinstatement and back pay followed. [Para 301][Para 302][Para 305][Para 307]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the workers obey an order suspending a strike and the employer then refuses to readmit them, the refusal is itself a dispute measure that aggravates the conflict, so Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 does not require the panel to stop; the panel continues and may treat dismissals grounded on strike participation as unlawful, ordering reinstatement with back pay from the date of dismissal. [Para 140][Para 143][Para 305][Para 307]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark, following case 49/06, that even participation in an unlawful strike cannot be punished out of proportion to the fault, which went beyond what this award needed. [Para 304]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The worker-appointed arbitrator is printed as An Non (អន ណន) on the face of the award and as An Man (អន មាន) in the signature block. [Para 2][Para 324] The pre-hearing date is given as 26 August 2009 in a case conducted entirely in 2010 [Para 17], and the Article 95 discussion dates the dismissals to August 2009 while the findings date them to August 2010. [Para 168][Para 181] The passage copied from an earlier award into the re-engagement reasoning speaks of 131 workers, though this case concerns 61. [Para 189][Para 191] The conciliation outcome is stated as 11 unresolved points of 11, with "1 point" in the minutes and 11 in the attachment. [Para 20] The employer's name appears as គ.ឈ ហ្សេស៊ិន, គឈ្នះ and KC/ខេស៊ី. [Para 2][Para 55] Page 1 and one passage on maternity pay carry unreadable OCR fragments, including stray Latin text. [Para 1][Para 256] The demand for accident insurance is recorded but never addressed in the disposition. [Para 11][Para 319]
