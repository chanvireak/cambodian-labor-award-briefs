# Award No. 35/10 — Im Way Garment International, Branch 1 (Arbitration Council, 7 May 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (An Nan for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair; the employer-party arbitrator's name is reduced to an OCR smear in the signature block) · Doc ID 140261

## 1. Facts
The Industrial Union of Cambodia and the Independent Union of Cambodia at the Branch 1 factory of Im Way Garment International, which employs about 318 workers on National Road 2 in Phnom Penh, complained on 2 February 2010 about working conditions; conciliation on 19 March 2010 settled four of nine points, and the five it left unresolved came to the Council by report No. 300 of 31 March 2010. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 14][Para 18][Para 60][Para 61]

At the hearing of 20 April 2010 the workers dropped issues 2, 3 and 5 because Award No. 23/08 on the same factory had already decided them, leaving issues 1 and 4. [Para 15][Para 22][Para 23][Para 24][Para 25] Issue 1 concerned the two women in the factory who are paid by output and who take one hour a day to breastfeed, for which the company pays on the USD 50 base wage. [Para 62] Issue 4 concerned Mr S, hired in 1996 and made ironing team leader after four years, then paid USD 90 a month; and Ms D, hired on 26 January 1994 and pressing team leader for about ten years, then paid USD 87 a month, each on an indefinite-duration contract. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74][Para 83][Para 84][Para 85] After three transfer orders each, dated 18, 21 and 28 January 2010 for Mr S and 2, 4 and 5 February 2010 for Ms D, both refused to move from team leader to line worker and were dismissed on 29 January and 6 February 2010 without notice. [Para 76][Para 80][Para 82][Para 87][Para 91][Para 93]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the wage for the one hour a day that a worker paid by output spends breastfeeding must be calculated on her piece-rate earnings rather than on the USD 50 base wage? [Para 62][Para 119]
2. (Issue 4) Whether the company must take Mr S and Ms D back in their former posts at the same wage and benefits, and failing that pay them the compensation due under the Labour Law? [Para 71][Para 132]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the breastfeeding hour must be paid on the piece-rate wage the woman actually earns, not on the base wage, because they lose income otherwise; the two team leaders were demoted to harder line work under a condition that would trigger warnings and dismissal, and should be reinstated or compensated. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 106][Para 108][Para 114]
**Employer (respondent):** piece-rate wages vary from day to day so computing the breastfeeding hour on them would be unworkable, and the company has used the USD 50 base wage for years; the ironing and pressing sections had more team leaders than the 68 and 26 workers required, so the two were moved without any loss of wage or benefit. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 70][Para 100][Para 101]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the breastfeeding hour to be computed on the worker's piece-rate earnings, and ordered the company to pay Mr S and Ms D severance, damages in lieu of notice, unused annual leave, their last unpaid wages and moral damages; the reinstatement itself was not ordered because the company refused it. [Para 212][Para 213]

On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 102, which defines wage as the remuneration owed under the employment contract for work done, and Article 103 on its components, and followed the reasoning of Award No. 82/06 that a worker paid by output is owed what she actually earned. [Para 119][Para 120][Para 121][Para 122][Para 123][Para 124] Since these workers average between USD 2 and USD 3 a day and more than USD 50 a month, paying the breastfeeding hour on the base wage made them lose income, so the panel ordered the piece-rate basis. [Para 126][Para 127][Para 129][Para 130][Para 131]

On Issue 4 the panel first held the additional claim for compensation admissible: Article 312 limits it to matters in the non-conciliation minute or matters arising as a direct consequence, and a claim for compensation is the direct consequence of a claim for reinstatement. [Para 138][Para 139][Para 140][Para 142][Para 144][Para 145] On the merits it accepted that Article 2, paragraph 2 gives the employer the right to organise and transfer, subject to the limits settled in earlier awards — no wage cut, no distant transfer, no shift change, no transfer outside the worker's skill. [Para 146][Para 149][Para 151][Para 153] The wage, place and shift were preserved, but the company attached a condition tying output to the old wage on pain of two warnings and dismissal, and the new post required direct ironing and pressing instead of supervision. [Para 154][Para 155][Para 156][Para 164][Para 165][Para 166] The transfers were therefore unreasonable, and refusing them was not serious misconduct under Article 83, all the more since the internal rules contain no such ground and no written warning was issued. [Para 167][Para 169][Para 170][Para 173][Para 174][Para 175][Para 176] The dismissals being unjustified, the panel applied Article 89 for severance, Article 75 for damages in lieu of notice, Article 167 for leave not taken, Article 116 for the last unpaid wages and Article 91 for moral damages. [Para 180][Para 182][Para 186][Para 195][Para 196][Para 200][Para 203][Para 204][Para 210]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A benefit calculated by reference to the worker's wage must be calculated on the wage the worker actually earns: where a worker is paid by output, the statutory breastfeeding hour is paid on her piece-rate earnings and not on the base wage, since using the base wage would make her lose income. A transfer that keeps the wage but ties output to that wage on pain of dismissal, and changes the worker's rank and manner of work, is unreasonable, so refusing it is not serious misconduct. [Para 129][Para 131][Para 155][Para 167][Para 169]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's ruling that a claim not recorded in the non-conciliation minute is admissible when it is the direct consequence of a recorded claim rests on Article 312 and is not otherwise relied on in the outcome. [Para 139][Para 142][Para 144]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The reasoning at [Para 179] and [Para 180] still carries the full name of the claimant from the companion file (Award No. 34/10) instead of the two claimants here; it was read as referring to Mr S and Ms D.
- Ms D's start date is given as 26 January 1994 at [Para 83] but the contract in the evidence list is dated 26 June 1994 at [Para 33]; the company is named Im Way, IM & Way, M & V and M&V 1 throughout.
- The employer-party arbitrator appears only as "Kau Cha" plus an OCR smear at [Para 1] and [Para 215], and the Prakas on the appointment of arbitrators is dated 2 October 2009 at [Para 12] against 2 September 2009 in the Council's other awards of the same term.
- The prior award is dated 11 March 2009 at [Para 22] although it is cited elsewhere as a 2008 decision; other citations to it give only the case number 23/08.
- Article 89 is quoted with its six-month cap intact at [Para 182], but the surrounding tiers are corrupted at [Para 180]–[Para 181]; the monthly wage of USD 90 for Mr S and USD 87 for Ms D is printed consistently in the findings and in the transfer orders. [Para 74][Para 85][Para 106]
