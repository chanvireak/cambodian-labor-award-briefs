# Order No. 131/10 — Leader In Dasrial Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន លីឌើ អ៉ិន ដាស្ទ្រៀល) and the Federation of Unions for Workers' Rights (Arbitration Council, 31 December 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy អ៉ីង សុទ្ធី, dissenting; Sin Kim Sean ស៊ីន គីម ស៊ាន; Kong Phalla គង់ ផល្លៈ presiding) · Doc ID 140336

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន លីឌើ អ៉ិន ដាស្ទ្រៀល (Leader In Dasrial Co., Ltd), a company at Mondol 1, Preah Sihanouk City, employing about 2,009 workers [Para 1][Para 43], faced a fifteen-point collective demand from the Federation of Unions for Workers' Rights (សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព សិទ្ធិ ប្រយោជន៍ កម្មករ) and its factory branch [Para 1][Para 3]. Thirteen points were settled at the hearing of 16 December 2010, leaving two [Para 26]. The first was the reinstatement of the union's newly elected president, Ms K, a full-rights worker on an indefinite-duration contract who was elected on 19 October 2010 and dismissed on 23 October 2010; the union notified the company of her election on 25 October, two days after the dismissal [Para 3][Para 48][Para 50][Para 51][Para 54]. The second was the charge of USD 3 imposed on workers who lose or damage the electronic attendance card the company issues free at hiring, which the workers asked be cut to USD 2 [Para 14][Para 68][Para 70][Para 72]. The union had no registration at the hearing and was awaiting it [Para 45][Para 46]; 157 workers authorized it in writing [Para 47].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must take Ms K back to work as elected president of the union, given that she was dismissed two days before the union notified the company of her election? [Para 48][Para 78][Para 97]
2. (Issue 14) Whether the employer must reduce from USD 3 to USD 2 the sum charged to a worker who loses or damages the electronic attendance card? [Para 124][Para 144]

The other thirteen demands were agreed at the hearing and were not decided [Para 26].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព សិទ្ធិ ប្រយោជន៍ កម្មករ:** Ms K said she had done nothing wrong: she is a full-rights worker on an indefinite contract who was elected president, and her only prior trouble was being reprimanded for an unexcused absence caused by toothache, for which she never received a written warning [Para 48][Para 55][Para 57][Para 58]. She needs the job and denies fault [Para 58]. On the card, USD 3 is what a worker spends on a week's food, so the charge should be USD 2 [Para 71][Para 72].

**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន លីឌើ អ៉ិន ដាស្ទ្រៀល:** it cannot take her back because she is rude and does not follow the employer's instructions, though it could not say when, how, or how often, and accepts she was never warned in writing [Para 60][Para 61][Para 62][Para 105][Para 106][Para 107]. It says it did not know she was a union president when it dismissed her, and that it applied Article 74 with final wages, severance and notice pay, but no dismissal compensation because her service was under six months [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65]. On the card, it bought each one for USD 5 and charges only USD 3, so the loss is shared and the fault is the worker's [Para 73].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to take Ms K back to work (Issue 1) and rejected the demand to reduce the card charge to USD 2 (Issue 14) [Para 150][Para 151]

On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 293 of the Labour Law and Article 3 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, which protect candidates in a union leadership election for 45 days before the ballot and until 45 days after it, and require the union to notify the employer of the candidacy by any reliable means [Para 78][Para 79][Para 80][Para 81][Para 82]. From Awards 71/09 and 09/10-Jung Hao it drew three cumulative conditions — the worker must be a protected candidate, the dismissal must fall within the protected period, and the union must have notified the employer [Para 84][Para 85]. Conditions one and two were met: she was a candidate, elected on 19 October 2010, and dismissed on 23 October 2010 [Para 87][Para 88][Para 89]. The third failed: notification reached the company on 25 October, two days after the dismissal, so she did not enjoy Article 293 protection [Para 92][Para 93][Para 94][Para 97][Para 98].

The panel then examined the dismissal under Article 74, which permits termination by either party but forbids dismissal without a valid reason related to the worker's capability or conduct or to the needs of the enterprise [Para 100][Para 102][Para 103]. The employer gave no particulars of the rudeness or disobedience, produced no internal rules, and had never issued a written warning [Para 106][Para 107][Para 108]. Article 27 requires disciplinary sanctions to be proportionate to the fault, and prior awards so hold [Para 112][Para 113][Para 114]. The panel found her conduct did not warrant dismissal and that the employer had no valid reason, so it ordered reinstatement under Article 34 of Prakas 099, following Awards 109/07 and 134/08 [Para 115][Para 116][Para 117][Para 118][Para 119][Para 123]. The employer-appointed arbitrator recorded a dissent in an annexed opinion, arguing that Article 74 allows termination at will without reliance on internal rules and that, absent a valid reason, the remedy is compensation under Article 91 rather than reinstatement [Para 153][Para 155][Para 158][Para 161].

On Issue 14 the panel held that the unregistered union could not represent all workers, and considered only the 157 who had authorized it, as Article 268 requires registration for a union to enjoy the rights the law confers [Para 124][Para 126][Para 129][Para 131][Para 133][Para 134]. Under Article 2 the employer may take reasonable measures concerning lost cards to make workers look after them, and the card is the employer's property bought for USD 5, of which the worker bears only USD 3 [Para 137][Para 138][Para 145][Para 146][Para 147]. That charge is a disciplinary measure for negligence, following Award 44/06-Golden Farm, so the demand was rejected [Para 140][Para 147][Para 149][Para 151].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a union fails to notify the employer of a candidate's candidacy before the dismissal, the statutory protection of Article 293 and Prakas 305 does not attach, but the employer must still justify the dismissal by a valid reason under Article 74, supported by warnings and a sanction proportionate to the fault under Article 27; failing that, the dismissal is unjustified and reinstatement follows, and an employer may also charge a worker part of the cost of replacing its own electronic attendance card lost through the worker's negligence [Para 103][Para 114][Para 118][Para 147].

**Obiter dicta:** the dissenting opinion of the employer-appointed arbitrator that Article 74 permits termination at will and that compensation under Article 91, not reinstatement, is the proper remedy [Para 158][Para 161].

## 6. Source defects disclosed

The number of demands is given as fourteen in the procedural narrative even though fifteen points are listed and fifteen were referred [Para 21][Para 22][Para 26]. The company's registration is dated 24 April 2006 in the findings but the attached certificates bear 18 July 2008 and 12 August 2010 [Para 29][Para 30][Para 42]. The annexed dissent is badly damaged, with untranslated English fragments and repeated stray year marks in the middle of the reasoning [Para 159][Para 163]. Individual workers and union officers appear by name in the source and are identified here by role or initial only.
