# Award No. 74/10 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូឌា អ៊ែរ ផត មែនី ជ្ជមែន សឺវីស / Cambodia Airports Management Services (Arbitration Council, 20 August 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · arbitrators សេង វូច ហ៊ុន, អន ណន, សុខ ម៉ាជឿង (president) · Doc ID 140293

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូឌា អ៊ែរ ផត មែនី ជ្ជមែន សឺវីស ("CAMS") provides ground and management services at Siem Reap international airport and employed about 413 workers [Para 63]. The claimants were the airport branch of សហជីព កម្មករ ឧស្សាហកម្ម ទេសចរណ៍ កម្ពុជា ("Cambodia Tourism Industry Workers' Union") and its federation សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត វិស័យ ទេសចរណ៍ សេវាកម្ម កម្ពុជា [Para 1][Para 64]. Two demands survived conciliation: carrying mobile phones into the airside apron area, and a 2010 base-wage increase measured by the 2009 consumer price index [Para 8][Para 13]. A collective agreement registered in 2008 governs the annual base-wage method [Para 39]. The employer is represented by counsel and its operation, HR and legal staff; the union by its federation officers, a lawyer and the branch president [Para 1][Para 2].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether ordinary workers must be allowed to carry mobile phones into the airside apron area (AIRSIDE) during working hours? [Para 65]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must raise the 2010 base wage on the basis of the 2009 consumer price index? [Para 87]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimants, សហជីព កម្មករ ឧស្សាហកម្ម ទេសចរណ៍ កម្ពុជា and សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត វិស័យ ទេសចរណ៍ សេវាកម្ម កម្ពុជា): if phones are barred for safety, the rule must apply to everyone, since supervisors and managers are allowed to carry them [Para 66]. They also argued that family emergencies must be reachable immediately, since parents may fall ill or a wife may give birth [Para 76]. On wages they argued that the National Institute of Statistics bulletin shows the 2009 index rising, not falling, so the 2010 base wage should rise [Para 101][Para 103].
**Employer (respondent, ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូឌា អ៊ែរ ផត មែនី ជ្ជមែន សឺវីស):** the ban protects international aviation security: workers who handle baggage and drive in the apron could cause an accident by using a phone, and managers hold phones only for supervision and do not work directly with equipment [Para 68][Para 71][Para 73]. It provides lockers for phones and an emergency phone line through which managers relay urgent family messages [Para 77][Para 84]. On wages it said the National Institute of Statistics reported a −0.7% change for 2009 against 2008, so the 2010 base wage stayed at the 2009 level [Para 96][Para 97].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the mobile-phone claim is dismissed; on the 2010 base wage the panel ordered the company to compute it on the method already applied, taking the annual average consumer price index of 2009 against that of 2008, which on the figure before the panel produced no increase [Para 146][Para 147].

On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 2 (second paragraph) of the Labour Law, which places the enterprise under the employer's management and direction, and held that this prerogative stands where its exercise is lawful and reasonable [Para 112][Para 115][Para 118]. It accepted that barring phones for workers who handle equipment directly reduces accident risk and is reasonable, and that allowing supervisors phones serves supervision, so the difference in treatment rests on function and not on an arbitrary distinction [Para 123][Para 125][Para 126]. The immediate-contact concern was met by the company's emergency line and locker system, which had operated since 2008 without incident [Para 84][Para 119][Para 127].

On Issue 2 the panel compared the two bodies of evidence. Both sides relied on the same source — the National Institute of Statistics of the Ministry of Planning — and the panel verified the extract on the Institute's website on 9 August 2010 [Para 94][Para 106][Para 136]. The difference lay in method: the employer compared the annual average index of 2009 with 2008 and obtained −0.7%, while the union produced only the 2009 monthly graph, which does not permit comparison with 2008 [Para 139][Para 140]. Since both parties had agreed that the annual base wage is set by comparing the two years' indices, and had used that method in 2009, the panel ordered the same computation for 2010 [Para 141][Para 144][Para 145].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a collective agreement or settled practice fixes the method for adjusting the base wage — here, comparing the annual average consumer price index of the current year with the previous year — the panel will apply that method rather than a party's partial reading of the index, and a single year's monthly index series cannot establish an increase without the comparison year [Para 139][Para 141][Para 145].

**Obiter dicta:** an employer's management prerogative over who may carry a phone in a security-restricted apron area is lawful and reasonable where it tracks each worker's function and an alternative emergency channel exists, and unequal treatment alone does not make it discriminatory [Para 118][Para 123][Para 127].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
OCR is uneven and the employer's name is printed in several forms — including a letter-scrambled version in the case title and the abbreviations "CAMS" and "SCA/CAMS" in the evidence list — so the full registered name cannot be stated with confidence [Para 1][Para 19]. The worker-appointed arbitrator's name appears both as អន ណន and អន មាន, and the presiding arbitrator's as សុខ ម៉ាជឿង and សុខ ម៉ាធ្យូង [Para 1][Para 149]. Dates conflict: the first hearing was set for 28 July 2010, was postponed at the union's request, and was held on 4 August 2010, while the non-settlement report is dated 8 July but recorded as sent on 12 July [Para 8][Para 14][Para 16]. One sentence in the §3 summary of the union's position is unrelated boilerplate about a meal allowance and is not carried into the reasons [Para 76]. The index change is printed both as "−០,៧%" and "−0.7" [Para 96][Para 131].
