# Order No. 128/10 — Jin Ying (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (ជិន អ៊ឹង កម្ពុជា ខូអិលធីឌី) and the Free Workers' Union of Jin Ying Garment Factory (Arbitration Council, 9 December 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy អ៊ីង សុទ្ធី; An Virak អាន វីរៈ; Kong Phalla គង់ ផល្លៈ presiding) · Doc ID 140333

## 1. Facts
ជិន អ៊ឹង កម្ពុជា ខូអិលធីឌី (Jin Ying (Cambodia) Co., Ltd), a garment factory at Toul Sangke, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, operating since 2007 with 350 workers [Para 1][Para 25][Para 26], faced an eleven-point collective demand lodged on 15 November 2010 by the Free Workers' Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia and its factory branch, the Free Workers' Union of Jin Ying Garment Factory (សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ នៃ រោងចក្រ កត់ដេរ ជិន អ៉ីង) [Para 1][Para 12]. Conciliation on 15 November 2010 failed on two of the eleven points [Para 13][Para 14]. Of those two, the reinstatement of four union leaders — the factory union's president, vice-president, another officer and its secretary — was settled by a written agreement of 17 November 2010, and the workers withdrew it, so the panel expressly did not consider it [Para 19][Para 35][Para 36][Para 37]. Only the meal allowance for overtime remained. The employer was invited by letter of 18 November 2010 but did not attend the hearing of 25 November 2010 and gave no valid reason; 238 workers backed the demand, and the workers' four representatives attended [Para 23][Para 24][Para 29][Para 43]. The panel proceeded in the employer's absence [Para 18][Para 44].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay a meal allowance for overtime worked from 16:00 to 18:00 of 2,000 riel, from 18:00 to 20:30 of a further 2,500 riel, and from 20:30 to 22:30 of a further 1,500 riel, as the workers demand, rather than the 1,000 riel daily allowance it has been paying? [Para 31][Para 38]

The reinstatement demand (Issue 2) was withdrawn under the 17 November 2010 agreement and was not decided [Para 36][Para 37].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ នៃ រោងចក្រ ជិន អ៉ីង:** the company has always paid 1,000 riel for overtime from 16:00 to 18:00, 500 riel more from 18:00 to 20:00, and 500 riel more from 20:00 to 22:00, but 2,000 riel no longer buys a sufficient meal [Para 31][Para 32]. No agreement fixes these amounts, and overtime is worked almost every day, including weekly rest days and public holidays, while the company earns large profits and can afford the demand [Para 32][Para 33][Para 34].

**Employer (respondent), ជិន អ៊ឹង កម្ពុជា ខូអិលធីឌី:** it could not grant the demand and asked that the old policy continue to be applied [Para 4]. It did not appear at the hearing to defend that position [Para 43].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider the workers' demand for meal allowance of 2,000 riel, a further 2,500 riel and a further 1,500 riel for overtime [Para 64]

Before the merits the panel asked whether it could hear the case in the employer's absence [Para 38]. Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 allows the panel to continue or to terminate by order where a duly invited party fails to appear without a valid reason, and on that basis, following Orders 53/04-Puong Hong, 63/04-Sap Wel, 148/07-Bay Heur, 06/08 and 99/09-King Lan and 10/10-Naga World, it held that a party absent without valid reason waives its right to defend itself [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40][Para 41]. The employer had been invited in writing and gave no explanation, so the panel heard the case in its absence on the workers' evidence [Para 43][Para 44].

On Issue 1, the panel found that Notification No. 049/10 of 9 July 2010 preserved the benefits workers had received under Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000, points 3 to 6 [Para 46]. Point 4 of Notification 017 provides that a worker who works voluntary overtime at the employer's request receives a meal allowance of 1,000 riel per day or one free meal [Para 47]. The panel therefore held that workers are entitled to only 1,000 riel per day, or the free meal, and that the higher scale demanded rests on no law and no agreement between the parties [Para 48][Para 50][Para 51].

The panel then characterised the claim as an interest dispute (វិវាទ អំពី ប្រយោជន៍) [Para 52]. It recalled that it generally declines to consider interest disputes where the union bringing them is not the most representative in the enterprise, citing Orders 81/04-Evergreen, 98/04-Great Union, 116/09-Sin Tai and 64/10-Sen Tai [Para 53]. Most-representative status is what gives a union the legal capacity to conclude a collective agreement and to bring interest disputes [Para 54]. Under Article 43 of Prakas 099 an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement for one year [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57], and a strike cannot be used to reopen a collective agreement before it expires, following Order 152/08-Wilson [Para 58][Para 59]. Article 277 requires a union to be registered [Para 60]. Here the factory union was neither registered nor most representative, so it lacked the legal standing to represent the workers on a collective interest demand [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that is neither registered nor the most representative in the enterprise cannot bring an interest dispute to the panel, because an award on such a dispute would operate as a collective agreement binding every worker for a year; the panel will therefore decline to consider the demand, leaving the statutory entitlement of 1,000 riel per day or one free meal for voluntary overtime undisturbed [Para 53][Para 57][Para 63].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that a party absent without valid reason waives its right of defence, and its observation that a strike may not be used to reopen a collective agreement before it expires [Para 41][Para 58].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's name is OCR'd in several forms (ជិន អ៊ឹង, ជិន អ៊ីង, ជិន អ៊ីន, ជិន អ៉ីង, ជិន អឹង) across the face and the reasoning [Para 1][Para 12][Para 21][Para 35]. The award states that the factory had no union at all, yet names a factory union with four representatives as the claimant party, a contradiction left unresolved [Para 1][Para 27][Para 28]. The presiding arbitrator's name is corrupted in the signature block, and the worker-appointed arbitrator's signature is illegible [Para 66]. Times and amounts are printed inconsistently (for example 20:30 against "20:300", 22:30 against "22:3000", and 2,500 riel against "2,500 បន្ថែម") [Para 31][Para 45][Para 64]. Stray page furniture and OCR noise intrude into the reasoning, including a broken reference "២១ -៨." and the fragment "Lig ." inside the demand [Para 45][Para 62]. Individual union officers and workers appear by name in the source and are identified here by role only.
