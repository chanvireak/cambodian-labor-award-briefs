# Arbitral Award 177109-JASA — JASA (Japanese Government Team for Safeguarding Angkor / APSARA Authority) v Building and Wood Workers Trade Union Federation of Cambodia (BWTUC) and Worker Union for Maintenance and Protection of Temples (APWU-JSA) (Arbitration Council, 22 January 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Panel: Seng Vuoch Hun (employer nominee), An Nan (worker nominee, dissenting), Pen Bunchhea (chair) · Doc ID 140469

## 1. Facts
The employer, printed on the face of the award as JASA (formerly JSA, the Japanese Government Team for Safeguarding Angkor), runs temple conservation projects at Angkor under cooperation with the APSARA Authority. [Para 1][Para 14] The worker party is BWTUC together with APWU-JSA, the site union holding most-representative status; 42 of the 78 workers originally named in the non-conciliation report remained involved in the case. [Para 2][Para 15][Para 17] The union alleged that when JASA restarted work on its third project in early 2006 it secretly recruited casual and regular workers while excluding union leaders and activists, and it demanded the reinstatement of the 42. [Para 27][Para 18] The employer's representative was absent from the hearing of 11 January 2010 without giving a reason. [Para 1][Para 7]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether JASA must re-employ the 42 former workers, the union having alleged that JASA is the same employer as JSA and that it refused to rehire them because of their union leadership and activism? [Para 17][Para 51]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — BWTUC and APWU-JSA:** JSA and JASA are one organisation with the same management, system and leaders, the only change being the addition of an APSARA Authority co-director; the 42 were union leaders and activists, they applied for work when the site reopened, and their applications were ignored, which proves union discrimination under Article 12 of the Labour Law. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45]
**Employer (respondent) — JASA:** absent from the hearing and filed no evidence or statement, so none of the workers' assertions was answered; the panel proceeded in its absence under Clause 21 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 45][Para 10][Para 48]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the workers' demand that JASA re-employ the 42 former workers was rejected, by non-binding award becoming binding eight days after notification absent written opposition. [Para 81]
On the absent employer, the panel applied Clause 21 of Prakas No. 099 dated 21 April 2004, which allows it to proceed where a duly invited party fails to appear without good cause, and followed Awards 53/04-Kong Hong, 63/04-Shine Well, 148/07-Pay Her and 99/09-Kingsland in continuing on the workers' evidence alone. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50]
On identity of employer, the panel treated the question as the threshold. The workers supplied JASA's management chart but no evidence of JSA's structure; JASA's co-directors are H.E. Ros Borath for APSARA Authority and Prof. Nagawa Tageshi for the Japanese party, and the JSA directors whose signatures appear on the 2004 and 2005 ID cards and employment letter do not appear in that structure. [Para 55][Para 57][Para 58][Para 60][Para 61] The panel therefore could not find that JASA was the former employer of the 42, and held JSA and JASA to be two separate organisations and different employers. [Para 62][Para 70]
On discrimination, Article 12 of the Labour Law prohibits an employer from deciding on hiring on account of union membership or union activities, but the panel held that a worker alleging union discrimination bears the burden of producing evidence, documents or witnesses, following Awards 79/05-Evergreen, 99/06-South Bay (issue 1) and 01/07-Supreme. [Para 64] The workers offered no specific facts beyond the silence that followed their applications; Mr R applied only after noticing the site operating, did not know whether JASA was hiring, and some of the 30 workers recruited for the third project were themselves union members. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67][Para 69] With JSA and JASA separate, past union activity at JSA could not be shown to have affected JASA's recruitment decision. [Para 70][Para 71]
On the contractual point, Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 22 of Decree 38 on Contracts and Other Liabilities were applied: a labour contract rests on consent and no party can force another to accept a contract or a form of contract it has not agreed to. [Para 71][Para 73][Para 75][Para 76] JASA thus retained discretion over the conditions and qualifications for its new project, and the workers had not shown what those were or why jobs should have been offered to them. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 80]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim that a successor entity is the same employer as the one that dismissed the claimants fails unless the claimants produce evidence of the earlier entity's management structure showing the two structures are identical; without that proof the successor is a different employer, and union activity at the predecessor cannot establish discrimination in the successor's hiring. [Para 54][Para 61][Para 70]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the workers were "still seeking employment" and that the 78 in the non-conciliation report had narrowed to 42, neither of which was necessary to the dismissal. [Para 77][Para 18]

## 6. Source defects disclosed

The award states the union's brief statement as dated 14 January 2009 in the reasons but 14 January 2010 in the evidence list.
