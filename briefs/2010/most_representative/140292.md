# Award No. 72/10 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហៃ យ៉ុន / Hai Yun Garment (Arbitration Council, 2 August 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · arbitrators យូ សួន ទី, អាន វីរៈ, ម៉ែ និម្មិត (president) · Doc ID 140292

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហៃ យ៉ុន ("Hai Yun") began operating in 2007 with about 400 workers [Para 36]. The claimant was សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ការងារ កម្មករ កម្ពុជា ("Cambodia Workers' Labour Union Federation") and its in-company branch, which had some 300 members and a registration certificate dated 15 June 2010 but no most-representative status [Para 25][Para 37][Para 38]. Ten demands went to conciliation and none settled; the panel heard the case on 20 July 2010, but the employer did not appear and gave no reason, so the panel proceeded in its absence under Article 21 of Prakas 099 and treated the workers' uncontradicted account as fact [Para 19][Para 22][Para 23][Para 89][Para 104]. At the hearing the workers withdrew Issue 9 and merged Issue 6 into Issue 5 [Para 24]. The remaining claims concerned medicines, sick-leave certificates, annual leave, drinking water, overtime hours, pay timing, leave on termination and union discrimination.

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must stock the medicine box adequately for workers who fall ill or are injured at work? [Para 39]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must accept medical certificates from licensed private clinics and keep the worker's wage during sick leave? [Para 43]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay in cash for annual leave the employer refused to let workers take? [Para 50]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must supply adequate, hygienic drinking water? [Para 56]
5. (Issue 5) Whether overtime must be voluntary and limited to 20:00? [Para 61]
6. (Issue 7) Whether wages must be paid in full by 16:00 on payday? [Para 68]
7. (Issue 8) Whether unused annual leave must be paid for when a worker resigns? [Para 72]
8. (Issue 10) Whether the company must stop discriminating against the union? [Para 76]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimants, សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ការងារ កម្មករ កម្ពុជា and its Hai Yun branch):** the medicine box is empty except when buyers visit, and certificates from private clinics near the factory are rejected so sick workers lose pay [Para 40][Para 43]. They said the company refuses all annual leave and pays nothing in lieu, pays only the last month's wage on resignation, keeps them doing overtime until 21:30 or 22:00 by withholding attendance cards so security will not let them out, and pays wages only after 16:00 [Para 51][Para 63][Para 69][Para 72][Para 83].
**Employer (respondent, ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហៃ យ៉ុន):** absent from the hearing and filed no documents, so it advanced no position and the panel recorded that it had waived its right to contest and defend the claims [Para 42][Para 60][Para 86][Para 184].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must stock the medicine box to the Prakas 330 annex, accept medical certificates from provincial, district, khan and commune public facilities and from licensed private hospitals or clinics while preserving wages, allow workers to take paid annual leave, disclose the source of drinking water and clean up its storage point, stop compelling overtime past 20:00 without consent, and pay wages in full by 16:00; it must also pay in lieu of unused annual leave on termination from now on and pay 21 days to one departing worker. The cash-instead-of-leave claim and the union discrimination claim are refused [Para 212][Para 213][Para 215][Para 217][Para 218][Para 219][Para 221][Para 222].

On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 229 and joint Prakas 330 of 6 December 2000, which requires enterprises with 50 or more workers to maintain a permanent infirmary with the equipment and medicines listed in its annex [Para 95][Para 96][Para 98]. Since the employer was absent and the workers' account was unrebutted, it ordered the box stocked accordingly [Para 104][Para 108]. On Issue 2 it applied Article 71(3), under which a contract is suspended by illness certified by an official doctor, and held that "official" covers state hospitals and health centres as well as private facilities licensed by the Ministry of Health, so the company's refusal of all private certificates was wrong [Para 111][Para 117][Para 124][Para 125].

On Issue 3 the panel applied Article 167(3), which voids any payment in lieu of annual leave and any waiver of the right to leave while employment continues, and therefore refused the cash claim; it ordered the company to let workers actually take their leave, one and a half days per month worked [Para 130][Para 131][Para 140][Para 145]. On Issue 4 it found the water supply sufficient but the storage unsanitary, and ordered disclosure of the supplier and repair of the site under Prakas 054 [Para 151][Para 161][Para 162]. On Issue 5 it applied Prakas 080: overtime rests on consent, and withholding attendance cards to keep workers inside until 21:30 or 22:00 is coercion, so the practice must stop [Para 163][Para 169][Para 171]. On Issue 7 it held under Article 115(3) that wages must be paid within working hours, ordering payment by 16:00 [Para 174][Para 181][Para 186]. On Issue 8 it distinguished termination: Article 167(2) requires payment for leave accrued but untaken when the contract ends, so it ordered that for the future and 21 days for the departing worker, computed on the average wage of the preceding twelve months [Para 188][Para 196][Para 198][Para 221]. On Issue 10 it found the transfer and the 50% suspension never took effect — the president kept his post and full pay — so no discrimination was proved [Para 207][Para 209][Para 210].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer is duly summoned and stays away without excuse, the panel will hear the case in its absence, treat its silence as abandoning the right to contest, and accept the workers' uncontradicted account as established fact before applying the Labour Law and the relevant Prakas [Para 89][Para 104][Para 184].

**Obiter dicta:** private clinics licensed by the Ministry of Health issue "official" certificates for the purposes of Article 71(3); withholding attendance cards to prevent workers leaving is coercion, not voluntary overtime; and pay in lieu of annual leave is unlawful during employment but mandatory on termination [Para 117][Para 169][Para 188].
