# Award No. 98/10 — Hoyea (Cambodia) Garment Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 28 September 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tai Seng, Liv Sovanna, Pen Bun Chha presiding) · Doc ID 140308

## 1. Facts
The respondent is ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហួយៀ (ខេមបូឌា) ហ្គាមេន លីមីធីត — Hoyea (Cambodia) Garment Co Ltd — at Prek Ta Kong, Chak Angre Leu, Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, operating since 2005 with about 989 workers. The claimant is the Khmer Youth Voice Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព សម្លេង យុវជន ខ្មែរ) and its factory branch, which has about 500 members; a second union, the Cambodian Democratic Garment Workers Union, also organises the plant, and neither holds a most-representative certificate. Of eleven demands, three remained unresolved and came before the panel. [Para 27][Para 29][Para 30][Para 14]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must convert the fixed-term contracts of workers whose successive renewals total more than two years into indefinite-term contracts? [Para 32]
2. (Issue 2) Whether workers who volunteer to work on a public holiday or on Sunday are entitled to a meal or 4,000 riel, in addition to what they already receive? [Para 43]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must provide a place where workers can rest and eat at midday? [Para 50]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, Khmer Youth Voice Union):** the employer renews one-year fixed-term contracts indefinitely, so workers who have served many years still face the anxiety of a contract expiring and being left without work; they asked for conversion to indefinite-term contracts, a meal or 4,000 riel for holiday and Sunday work as encouragement, and a proper place to eat at midday because the factory yard, the motorcycle shed and the trees are not adequate. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 45][Para 52]
**Employer (respondent):** it follows a lawful 2-month probation then one-year fixed-term contracts, as it has since 2005, and most contracts are renewed because experienced workers are needed; holiday work is not overtime and it already pays 1,000 riel for meal costs, and it has no space to build a canteen because the premises are cramped and rented. [Para 36][Para 38][Para 47][Para 54]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must convert to indefinite-term contracts the fixed-term contracts of workers whose successive contracts total more than two years, and must provide a free meal to workers who volunteer to work on Sundays; the 4,000 riel meal claims and the canteen claim were refused. [Para 154][Para 157][Para 155][Para 158]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 67(2) of the Labour Law: a fixed-term contract may not run beyond two years, may be renewed once or more provided each renewal does not exceed two years, and performance contrary to that rule converts the contract into one without a specified term. It followed its earlier reasoning in Awards 36/06 and 57/06 that the test is the aggregate duration, and rejected the employer's view that it could keep renewing one-year contracts regardless; ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982, though not binding, supported that reading. [Para 62][Para 65][Para 68][Para 72]

On issue 2 the panel split the claim. Work on a public holiday is not overtime, because the hours stay within the 8-hour day and 48-hour week of Article 137, so Article 164 and Prakas No. 10 give one extra day's wage but no meal allowance, and the 4,000 riel claim failed; the employer's existing 1,000 riel payment was lawful and even more favourable. Sunday, by contrast, is the contractual weekly rest day and not a normal working day, so voluntary Sunday work is overtime and attracts the 1,000 riel meal entitlement under point 4 of Prakas 017 — not 4,000 riel, which no law or agreement requires. [Para 91][Para 95][Para 116][Para 122][Para 140][Para 141]

On issue 3 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law or related regulations making a dining area the employer's burden, and no collective agreement or internal rule in this factory providing one, nor any evidence that its absence injured workers' health; the demand exceeded the law and was an interest dispute, which the panel declined to hear. [Para 144][Para 149][Para 153]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer renews successive fixed-term contracts so that their aggregate duration exceeds two years, the contract becomes one of indefinite duration by operation of Article 67(2), and the employer cannot avoid that result by issuing a fresh one-year contract each time. [Para 73][Para 75]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that a union without most-representative status cannot bring an interest dispute, and that an award on such a dispute would become a one-year collective agreement under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099, was not necessary to the contract claim it granted. [Para 101][Para 107]
