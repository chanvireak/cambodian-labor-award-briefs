# Award No. 84/10 — Ming Da Footwear Industries (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន មីង ដា ហ្វូត វៀ អ៊ីន ដាស់ ស្ទ្រីល កូ អិលធីឌី) (Arbitration Council, 18 August 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of three: អ៉ឹង សុទ្ធី (employer-nominated), អាន វីរៈ (worker-nominated), ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា (chair) · Doc ID 140300

## 1. Facts

The Cambodian Labour Workers' Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ការងារ កម្មករ កម្ពុជា) and its local union at the Ming Da footwear factory in Trapeang Thleung, Choam Chao, Dangkao, Phnom Penh filed 20 collective demands on 2 July 2010. [Para 19] The factory was set up in 1999 and employed 1,600 workers; the claimant union had 562 members. [Para 39][Para 41] Conciliation settled 14; six reached the panel, and four of those — a notice board, cash instead of rice for overtime, a later pay date and reimbursement of medical checks — were settled at the hearing, leaving two demands. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 24]

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay triple wages (2 + 1) plus a 2,000 riel meal allowance to workers who volunteer to work on public holidays? [Para 42][Para 57]
2. (Issue 5) Whether the employer must provide transport home for workers who volunteer to work overtime past 7 p.m.? [Para 46][Para 93]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant) — សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ការងារ កម្មករ កម្ពុជា and សហជីព ការងារ កម្មករ កម្ពុជា នៃ រោងចក្រ ម៉ីង តា:** holiday work is overtime work, so it should attract triple wages and the 2,000 riel meal allowance the employer gives for overtime; and because most workers live about three kilometres away and walk home along dark roads after night overtime, the company should run a vehicle. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 50]

**Employer (respondent) — ក្រុមហ៊ុន មីង ដា ហ្វូត វៀ:** it already pays 200 per cent for holiday work, as Article 164 and Prakas 10 require, and volunteers working a normal eight-hour holiday shift are not working overtime, so no meal allowance is due; it has never provided night transport and has no vehicle to do so. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 54]

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand for triple holiday pay and a 2,000 riel meal allowance was refused, and the employer was ordered to provide transport home for workers doing voluntary overtime from 7 p.m. onwards. [Para 104][Para 105]

On holiday pay the panel worked through Article 137, which caps normal hours at eight a day and forty-eight a week, Article 164, which gives workers who work on a public holiday an additional allowance set by ministerial Prakas, and Prakas 10 of 4 February 1999, which prices that allowance at one day's ordinary wage. [Para 60][Para 62][Para 63][Para 64] A volunteer working a full ordinary day on a holiday therefore stays inside normal hours and earns 200 per cent, not 300 per cent. [Para 61][Para 67] The panel found the company's practice lawful on that point. [Para 68][Para 69]

The 2,000 riel meal allowance was also refused. Notification 017 of 18 July 2000 grants 1,000 riel a day, or one free meal, to workers who volunteer for overtime at the employer's request; where the panel has held that holiday work is not overtime — Awards 115/08 (Thap Vann), 28/10 (Puch Ak Khit) and 64/10 (Sen Tai) — no meal allowance is owed. [Para 70][Para 71][Para 74][Para 76] The panel added that the demand exceeded the law and was therefore an interest dispute, which it will not decide for a union without most-representative status; this union had 562 members out of 1,600 and no certificate. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 88][Para 89]

On transport, Prakas 80 of 1 March 1999 requires an enterprise with night work to give suitable rest facilities and safe transport home at the end of the night shift, and Article 144 (new) defines night work. [Para 93][Para 94] Since a consecutive eleven-hour night period must include 22:00 to 05:00, and darkness in Cambodia falls at about 18:30 or 19:00, the panel followed Awards 116/09 (Sin Tai) and 62/10 (Chong You) in treating 19:00 as the start of night. [Para 97][Para 99][Para 101] The company required overtime until 18:00, 21:00 and sometimes 22:00 without providing transport, which was unlawful. [Para 91][Para 102]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

Work volunteered on a public holiday inside the ordinary eight-hour day is holiday work paid at 200 per cent under Article 164 and Prakas 10, not overtime under Article 137, so the overtime meal allowance and the triple rate do not attach; but once voluntary overtime runs past 19:00 the employer's duty under Prakas 80 to transport the worker home safely is engaged. [Para 61][Para 74][Para 93][Para 101]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel remarked that a Council award resolving an interest dispute would itself operate as a collective agreement for one year under Article 43 of Prakas 099, which was not necessary to its refusal. [Para 83][Para 84]

## 6. Source defects disclosed

The employer appears as មីង ដា ហ្វូត វៀ, មីង ដា ហ្វូត វៀន and ម៉ីង តា across the award, and the union's own letterhead uses the same variants. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 88] The Prakas on night work is cited as No. 80 in one place and No. 800 in another. [Para 93][Para 94] A company document list attributes to the factory a "Khmer Youth Union" certificate, though the union in this case is the Cambodian Labour Workers' Union. [Para 32][Para 33] The conciliation report is dated 26 July 2010 but recorded as transmitted on 27 August 2010. [Para 16] The summary line carries a stray "2015" inside the phrase "multiplied by two plus one". [Para 78] The employer arbitrator's name is printed as អ៉ីង សុទ្ធី on the face and អ៉ីង សុទ្លី in the signature block. [Para 1][Para 107] Individual workers and union officers named in the narrative are reduced to initials here.
