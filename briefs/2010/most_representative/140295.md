# Award No. 79/10 — Global Apparels Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ក្លូប័ល អាបាំ រែលី មីធីត) (Arbitration Council, 20 August 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of three: អ៉ីង សុទ្ធី (employer-nominated), លីវ សុវណ្ណា (worker-nominated), គង់ ជល្លៈ (chair) · Doc ID 140295

## 1. Facts

Three unions at the Global Apparels Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ក្លូប័ល អា ប៉ារែល) garment factory in Trapaing Toul, Ang Snuol, Kandal — the Khmer Union Federation's JUSCA union, the Khmer Workers' and Employees' Union of Global Apparels Ltd, and the Independent and Democratic Union — filed twelve collective demands on working conditions in June 2010. [Para 22] Conciliation at the provincial department of labour settled four; eight reached the panel, and the first was settled at the hearing itself when the employer agreed to fit two cameras at the motorcycle park. [Para 24][Para 48] The plant employed 2,933 workers, of whom the three claimant unions claimed 960 as members. [Para 43][Para 47][Para 117] No union was yet the most representative in the enterprise. [Para 45]

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must enlarge the canteen or build a second one, and install five more fans? [Para 51]
2. (Issue 3) Whether workers in the "modern market" material-supply section must receive US$2 on top of their US$3 bonus? [Para 64]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must cash out employees' unused annual leave the way PCCS does, while their contracts are still running? [Para 73]
4. (Issue 5) Whether the meal allowance must be raised by a further 400 riel on the existing 1,600 riel? [Para 80]
5. (Issue 6) Whether the employer must pay RB replacement workers a stable allowance instead of making them chase group bonuses? [Para 84]
6. (Issue 7) Whether the employer must stop counting days against bonus eligibility on days when it supplies too little work? [Para 91]
7. (Issue 8) Whether the employer must pay every worker US$5 a month for transport? [Para 99]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant) — សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ខ្មែរ, សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត និងបុគ្គលិក ក្រុមហ៊ុន Global Apparels Ltd and សហជីព ឯករាជ្យ និងប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ:** the canteen seated about 800 but up to 1,500 ate there, under a zinc roof with walls blocking airflow and a drying room pumping heat in, so workers ate on the concrete; the sewing section earned up to US$20 a month in bonuses while the supply section earned US$3; the employer had just built another building, so want of land or budget was untrue. [Para 53][Para 55][Para 62][Para 67]

**Employer (respondent) — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ក្លូប័ល អា បាំ រែលី មីធីត:** it had neither the site nor the budget to enlarge the canteen and no law obliged it to; ILO had assessed the canteen and found no problem; leave would be carried forward and paid out only on termination; JIT was a separate registered company, not a branch. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 77][Para 208]

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel refused to order construction or expansion of the canteen but ordered the employer to use every means to reduce the heat inside it; issues 3 to 8 were refused in their entirety. [Para 214][Para 215][Para 216][Para 220]

On the canteen, the panel held that no provision of the Labour Law, notification or regulation requires an employer to provide or enlarge a canteen absent agreement, so the demand was an interest dispute rather than a rights dispute. [Para 109][Para 114] Interest disputes are decided only for the most representative union, and with 960 members out of 2,933 the three claimant unions were not; the demand for a new canteen was therefore refused without examination. [Para 118][Para 121] The fan demand fared differently: Article 229 requires every establishment to keep conditions of hygiene and health, and Prakas 125/01 and 147/02 require adequate air exchange and tolerable heat. [Para 123][Para 129][Para 131] Following Award 106/09 (Jimmy), the panel treated the canteen as part of the establishment, and on photographic evidence that workers sat packed together at midday under a zinc roof, it ordered the employer to reduce the heat. [Para 127][Para 137][Para 138]

The US$2 bonus was refused: Article 2 reserves to the employer the management of the enterprise, which includes setting incentive bonuses, and the sewing and supply sections did no equal work. [Para 140][Para 150][Para 151] No agreement promised the extra US$2, so the claim exceeded the law. [Para 152]

The annual-leave cash-out was refused on Article 167(3), which voids any payment in lieu of annual leave during employment and any waiver of the right to take it; the panel read this as prohibiting cash-out while the contract subsists. [Para 158][Para 161] It added that under Article 167(4) leave may be carried forward for three years, so the employer was told to encourage workers to take their leave rather than buy it. [Para 163][Para 166]

The 400 riel meal increase was refused. Notification 017 of 18 July 2000 sets a 1,000 riel meal allowance for voluntary overtime, and the employer already paid 1,600 riel — above the floor — with no agreement to pay more. [Para 172][Para 175][Para 178] The RB allowance and the bonus-day claim failed on the same management-prerogative reasoning: the RB pool exists to cover absences and its bonus follows days actually worked, and the employer kept full wages on short-work days. [Para 188][Para 202][Para 204] The transport allowance failed for want of any legal basis and for want of evidence that JIT was a branch of the respondent. [Para 209][Para 211]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

A demand that goes beyond what the Labour Law, a Prakas or a collective agreement guarantees is an interest dispute, and an interest dispute is entertained only where the union bringing it is the most representative in the enterprise; a demand that the law does guarantee — clean, ventilated, temperature-controlled premises under Article 229 — is decided on the merits whatever the union's representativity. [Para 114][Para 118][Para 123][Para 134]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted, without relying on it, that the employer had recently built a further building on the site, which bore on its plea of having no room to build. [Para 62]

## 6. Source defects disclosed

The employer's name is printed at least five different ways across the award — ក្លូប័ល អាបាំ រែលី មីធីត in the header, ក្លូប័ល អា ប៉ារែល elsewhere, and once as ក្លូបរ អា ប៉ាវែល. [Para 1][Para 206][Para 210] The chair's name appears as គង់ ជល្លៈ on the face and គង់ ផល្លៈ in the signature block. [Para 222] The meal-allowance total is given as 2,200 riel in the demand list and as 2,000 riel in the issues section. [Para 10][Para 80] The reasons cite years 2019 and 2022 in an award dated 2010, which appears to be an OCR substitution. [Para 156][Para 166] The operative paragraph on issue 4 is partly garbled ("ប្រាក់ ជំនួស ៣០ សម្រាប់"), though its sense is recoverable from the reasoning. [Para 217] The header prints the employer's HR staff and several union officials by full name; this brief does not reproduce them.
