# Award No. 77/10 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ញូ អាក ឃីត ហ្គាមេន / New Arch Kid Garment Factory (Arbitration Council, 20 August 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · arbitrators សេង វូច ហ៊ុន, លីវ សុវណ្ណា, គង់ ផល្លៈ (president) · Doc ID 140294

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ញូ អាក ឃីត ហ្គាមេន ហ្វេក ធើរី លីមីធីត ("New Arch Kid Garment") employed about 3,000 workers at Prek, Ang Snuol, Kandal, having moved there from Boeung Krapeu in Phnom Penh in 2008 [Para 36][Para 39]. The claimant was សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា and its in-company branch, with 974 members, and no union on site held most-representative status [Para 37][Para 38]. Of eleven demands, four settled in conciliation and seven were referred; a previous award, No. 28/10 of 23 April 2010, had already decided several of the same subjects for a different claimant union [Para 22][Para 23][Para 32]. The company offers old workers either company transport from Phnom Penh or rented housing near the factory, plus a meal allowance of 1,000 riel for two hours of overtime and 1,500 riel up to 20:00 [Para 41][Para 42][Para 53].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay US$5 a month to workers who use neither the company bus nor its rented housing? [Para 92]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay 500 riel of meal allowance for each hour of overtime? [Para 111]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must provide tables and chairs for workers to eat at? [Para 127]
4. (Issue 4) Whether building C must have the same working conditions as buildings A and B? [Para 134]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the year-end bonus must be paid at the end of year two rather than year three? [Para 158]
6. (Issue 6) Whether the US$5 attendance bonus must be deducted pro rata for authorised absences? [Para 172]
7. (Issue 7) Whether the company must pay a US$10 monthly allowance to skilled workers, assistant mechanics and team leaders? [Para 186]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimants, សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា and its New Arch Kid branch):** eight company buses carry only about 300–400 workers, so those left out should get US$5 in lieu; old workers who simply went home instead of taking the bus or the rented house got nothing and should be compensated [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47]. On meals they argued that 1,000 riel covers two hours, so each hour is worth 500 riel and longer overtime should multiply [Para 51][Para 52]. They said eating without tables and chairs is very hard, with rice taken on broken stones and rain blowing into the canteen, and that building C workers get fewer benefits than those in A and B though all sew the same [Para 57][Para 58][Para 70][Para 136].
**Employer (respondent, ក្រុមហ៊ុន ញូ អាក ឃីត ហ្គាមេន ហ្វេក ធើរី លីមីធីត):** the transport and housing options were offered only to workers kept on from the Phnom Penh site, and new hires at Kandal were told the terms; it cannot pay US$5 to those who declined both [Para 43][Para 44][Para 49]. It pays 1,000 riel for two hours and 1,500 riel up to 20:00 and says no law requires 500 riel an hour [Para 53][Para 54]. It built a canteen in March 2010 but has no budget yet for tables and chairs, and will provide them when funds allow [Para 55][Para 56]. It does not dock the attendance bonus for properly authorised absences [Para 83].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must deduct the US$5 attendance bonus pro rata to days of authorised absence, and the parties must meet and negotiate within one month on tables and chairs for the canteen; the transport allowance, the 500 riel hourly meal allowance, equality of building C conditions, payment of the year-end bonus in year two and the US$10 skill allowance are all refused [Para 198][Para 199][Para 200][Para 201][Para 202][Para 203][Para 204].

On Issues 1, 2 and 7 the panel found no article, Prakas, agreement or collective agreement requiring the payments, so the demands exceeded the law and were benefits disputes; the claimant union lacked most-representative status, which under the panel's settled practice bars it from obtaining a ruling on such claims [Para 96][Para 99][Para 100][Para 109][Para 110][Para 122][Para 126][Para 193][Para 197]. On Issue 7 it separately addressed jurisdiction: res judicata applies only where the same parties bring the same issue, and since the claimant here is a different union from the one in Award 28/10, the objection failed and the panel examined the claim on its merits before refusing it [Para 187][Para 189][Para 191].

On Issue 3 the panel used Article 34 of Prakas 099, which allows it to order a party to act, and, rather than fixing a date itself, ordered the parties to discuss and negotiate the timing within one month of the award taking effect [Para 131][Para 132][Para 133]. On Issue 4 it applied Article 2 (second paragraph) on the employer's management of the enterprise and held that different arrangements for different buildings fall within that prerogative; the workers produced no evidence of the specific conditions and responsibilities in each building, so the claim failed on proof [Para 140][Para 150][Para 156][Para 157].

On Issue 5 the panel applied Article 167(3), which voids any payment in lieu of annual leave while the worker is still serving, and refused the demand for payment at the end of year two [Para 160][Para 161][Para 171]. On Issue 6 it held that where the employer grants leave, the attendance bonus must be reduced in proportion to the days absent; only where leave was refused may the whole bonus be withheld [Para 183][Para 184][Para 185].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Res judicata bars a second arbitration only where the same parties bring the same issue: where the earlier award was obtained by a different claimant union, the panel retains jurisdiction over an identical demand and will decide it on the merits [Para 187][Para 189][Para 191].

**Obiter dicta:** an attendance bonus may be docked in proportion to authorised absence days but forfeited entirely for unauthorised absence; pay in lieu of annual leave is void while employment continues; and differences between buildings are an exercise of management prerogative where the workers cannot show what the conditions actually are [Para 140][Para 161][Para 185].
