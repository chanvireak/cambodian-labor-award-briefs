# Order No. 117/10-B.I. Peril — B.I. Peril (Cambodia) (ប៊ី រី អ៊ី ភែរឹល), Dangkao, Phnom Penh (Arbitration Council, 30 November 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (គង់ ផល្លះ presiding; មាំរ សំបូរ ណា, employer nominee; លីវ សុវណ្ណា, worker nominee) · Doc ID 140324

## 1. Facts
B.I. Peril (Cambodia) (ប៊ី រី អ៊ី ភែរឹល), a garment factory at Chambou Vorn village, Choam Chao commune, Dangkao district, Phnom Penh, has run since 2005 and employed 2,900 workers under five unions; the claimant was the factory's own union of the Federation of Unions for Workers' Interests (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព សិទ្ធិ ប្រយោជន៍ កម្មករ), created on 20 June 2010 with about 50 members. [Para 1][Para 42][Para 43][Para 45] The federation complained on 11 September 2010 of 16 demands to improve working conditions; conciliation closed on 18 October 2010 with five demands unsettled, and at the hearing the parties agreed issue 3 (a certificate of employment), leaving issues 1, 2, 4 and 5. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 21] The remaining demands train on pregnant and nursing workers, six-monthly health checks for all staff and a share of annual profit. [Para 2][Para 4][Para 6][Para 8][Para 10]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must let pregnant workers leave 15 minutes before the general workforce at each break? [Para 49][Para 90]
2. (Issue 2) Whether nursing mothers may leave the factory to breastfeed at home for one hour a day, rather than only in the factory nursery? [Para 60][Para 105]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the company must send every worker for a special health examination once every six months at its own expense? [Para 72][Para 122]
4. (Issue 5) Whether the company must distribute one share of annual profit to each worker equal to that worker's last wage of the year? [Para 81][Para 134]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព សិទ្ធិ ប្រយោជន៍ កម្មករ និង សហជីព សិទ្ធិ ប្រយោជន៍ កម្មករ ក្រុមហ៊ុន ប៊ឺ រី:** the union asked for all 2,900 workers, members and non-members alike; eight minutes granted to pregnant workers is too short and 15 is needed to avoid the crush at the gate and to let them rest; nursing mothers cannot bring a baby to the factory without spending a second person's time, so they should be allowed to go home during the meal break; six-monthly special health checks would answer their fear of chemical exposure from the cloth; and the company, which keeps opening branches, can afford a one-wage profit share under Article 103. [Para 46][Para 57][Para 58][Para 74][Para 82][Para 117]
**Employer (respondent) — B.I. Peril (Cambodia):** eight minutes has been given since 2006, sometimes ten, because the walk from the work floor to the gate takes only about three minutes and no problem has arisen; the company will not allow mothers to leave for home but requires relatives to bring the infant to the factory nursery, where it keeps carers; it has no policy of six-monthly examinations, provides masks, earplugs, gloves and sewing-machine guards, keeps four doctors on 24-hour duty and pays into the National Social Security Fund; and it cannot pay any profit share. [Para 51][Para 53][Para 57][Para 61][Para 63][Para 75][Para 76][Para 78][Para 79][Para 83]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 2 was granted: the employer must let nursing mothers choose to go home to breastfeed, at times and for periods to be agreed with the employer, subject to discipline if they return late; issues 1, 4 and 5 were refused consideration. [Para 121][Para 146][Para 147][Para 148]
On issue 1 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law or of any agreement requiring 15 minutes' early departure, and the internal rules fix an eight-hour day from 7:00 to 11:00 and 12:00 to 4:00; the demand therefore exceeded what the rules and the law provide and was an interests dispute. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 95][Para 96] For interests disputes the panel asks whether the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise, which alone can legally conclude a collective agreement or bring such a claim; this union had about 50 members among 2,900 workers and did not qualify, so the demand was not entertained. [Para 97][Para 98][Para 102][Para 103][Para 104] The panel followed Orders 71/09-High Tech and 84/10-Mingda Footwear. [Para 100]
On issue 2 the parties agreed on one hour a day but disputed the place. Article 184 gives a mother one hour a day within working hours, splittable into two thirty-minute periods at times she agrees with the employer, or at the midpoint of each shift if there is no agreement, and says nothing about where the feeding must occur. [Para 108][Para 109][Para 111][Para 112] Since the law is silent on place, the panel held the mother may go home for 30 minutes in the morning and 30 in the afternoon, with the employer free to discipline lateness — a course both sides accepted; it followed Orders 75/09 and 17/10. [Para 113][Para 118][Para 119][Para 120] The panel had first limited the claim to union members, since no list of the 2,900 claimants was filed by 15 November 2010. [Para 88][Para 89]
On issue 4 the panel read Article 247 as obliging the Ministry to issue a Prakas on special examinations, not the employer directly, and found no Prakas fixing a six-month interval, following Order 116/09; the demand was therefore also an interests claim outside the law and refused. [Para 122][Para 123][Para 124][Para 128][Para 133] On issue 5 Article 103 merely defines the components of wages, including a share of profit, and imposes no duty to distribute profit; no contract, internal rule or collective agreement did so, so the demand exceeded the law and was refused as an interests dispute. [Para 135][Para 136][Para 138][Para 140][Para 141][Para 145]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that is not the most representative in the enterprise cannot bring an interests dispute — a demand going beyond what the Labour Law, a Prakas, the internal rules or a collective agreement already gives — so the panel will not entertain it; but where a statutory right is silent on how it is exercised, as Article 184 is silent on the place of breastfeeding, the panel will order the employer to accommodate the mother's reasonable choice rather than the employer's preferred arrangement. [Para 98][Para 102][Para 118]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that no worker had yet suffered any health effect from the chemicals in the cloth and that the company is an ILO Better Factories member with four doctors on duty, facts it did not need once it had found the health-check demand outside the law. [Para 59][Para 76][Para 77][Para 128]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's name is printed as "ក្រុមហ៊ុន ប៊ី រី អ៊ី ភែរឹល (ខេមបូឌា)" on the face, "ប៊ឺ រី អី ភែរឹល" in the findings and "ប៊ឺ រី" elsewhere; no Latin form appears anywhere, so the English rendering in the title is a transliteration only. [Para 1][Para 42] The case number is printed "១១៧/១០" without any Latin form. [Para 1] The operative paragraph is corrupted: it repeats issue 4 twice, splices the issue 2 order into it and drops the word for the times to be fixed, so the orders have been taken from the reasoned sections instead. [Para 121][Para 147] The employer's answer on the profit share survives only as scattered fragments around a broken bracket. [Para 83] The header records five unsettled demands, the summary of the hearing says four remained after issue 3 was agreed, and the panel's findings also state that five demands were unsettled — the award does not reconcile the two counts. [Para 17][Para 18][Para 21] The presiding arbitrator's name is printed as "គង់ ផល្លះ" in the header and "គង់ ផល្លៈ" at the foot, and the employer nominee's name is reproduced twice at the foot. [Para 1][Para 150] The individual claimants are described only by number, and no worker is named in this brief.
