# Order No. 87/10 — Bess Tan Garmen Co., Ltd v. Federation of Cambodian Workers' Unions (Arbitration Council, 31 August 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom, Liv Sovanna, Kong Phalla) · Doc ID 140303

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន បេស តាន ហ្គាមេន អិលធីឌី — Bess Tan Garmen Co., Ltd — operates a garment factory on Preah Norodom Boulevard, Phnom Penh, employing some 290 workers, of whom 170 belong to the single union on site. [Para 45][Para 47] The Federation of Cambodian Workers' Unions (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា) and the Bess Tan workers' union filed a collective dispute on 7 July 2010; conciliation on 23 July 2010 left 5 of 13 demands unresolved, and at the hearing two more (demands 3 and 4, on the 5% severance payment and three extra annual leave days) were settled, leaving three. [Para 17][Para 18][Para 22][Para 23] The dispute concerns piece rates, a rice allowance for overtime, and a seven-day suspension imposed on one worker after a transfer. [Para 48][Para 59][Para 64]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must keep the sewing piece rate (តម្លៃឡូតិ៍) for trousers at its former level, including for any future trouser orders? [Para 48][Para 77]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay a 2,000 riel rice allowance to workers doing overtime from 18:00 to 20:00? [Para 59][Para 89]
3. (Issue 5) Whether the employer must pay seven days' wages for an unlawfully imposed suspension and refrain from transferring a cutter to another section? [Para 64][Para 101]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), Federation of Cambodian Workers' Unions and the Bess Tan workers' union:** the piece rate for trousers should stay as it was, expressly for the future, so that if trousers are sewn again the old rate revives; the rice allowance should reach 2,000 riel because market prices are high and the employer pays only 1,000 riel up to 18:00 plus 500 riel thereafter; and the suspension was unlawful because the internal rules require a written warning before suspension, which the employer never issued. [Para 51][Para 58][Para 62][Para 74][Para 75]

**Employer (respondent), Bess Tan Garmen Co., Ltd:** the factory no longer has any trousers to sew, having switched to shirts in late July 2010, and it accepts repeat orders only when another factory is pressed, at prices set by the market, so it cannot promise a fixed rate — though it will top up to the minimum wage if piece work falls short. [Para 49][Para 53][Para 54][Para 57] Its long-standing practice of 1,000 riel plus 500 riel is the correct application of Notice No. 017. [Para 60][Para 63] The suspended worker produced only 30–50% of the target, refused overtime and answered her team leader rudely, so suspension under the internal rules was proper. [Para 69][Para 71][Para 72]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issues 1 and 2 refused; Issue 5 granted by ordering the employer to pay seven days' wages to the suspended worker for 24 to 31 June 2010, from the date the order takes effect. [Para 126][Para 127][Para 128]

On Issue 1 the panel found both sides agreed the demand no longer exists in the present, since no trousers are being sewn, and the workers pressed it only for a hypothetical future order. [Para 77][Para 78][Para 82] Following Award No. 10/03 (Zacksintic) and others, the panel held the Council is established to resolve labour disputes that have actually arisen, not problems not yet in existence, and no one can predict whether trousers will return, at what rate, or to which workers. [Para 80][Para 84][Para 85]

On Issue 2 the panel construed point 4 of Notice No. 017 of 18 July 2000, which grants a 1,000 riel daily rice allowance, or a free meal, to workers who volunteer for overtime at the employer's request. [Para 89] It read "in one day" as a flat daily sum, not an hourly computation, following Award No. 66/06 (Gold Lida). [Para 90][Para 91] Because the employer already paid 1,500 riel for overtime from 16:00 to 20:00, the further 1,500 riel sought exceeded what the law required and was an interest dispute; the panel declined to hear it since the filing union holds no most-representative certificate, citing Awards 84/07 (Yong Va 2), 143/08 (Cham Thich Thai), 152/08 (Wilson) and 48/09 (Rosing). [Para 93][Para 94][Para 98][Para 99]

On Issue 5 the panel accepted that discipline falls within the employer's managerial right under Article 2 of the Labour Law, provided it is reasoned and lawful, and applied Article 10 of the company's internal rules, which grades penalties: light faults attract an oral education, then a written warning, then dismissal; medium faults attract a written warning first and an unpaid suspension of no more than seven days only on the second occasion. [Para 103][Para 105][Para 111][Para 115] The panel interpreted "suspension" as requiring a prior written warning recorded in the personnel file, and found the employer produced no evidence of any such warning before suspending the worker. [Para 121][Para 122] The penalty was therefore not in accordance with the employer's own rules, and under Article 34(b) of Prakas No. 099 the Council may order immediate payment of sums due. [Para 123][Para 124][Para 125]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The Arbitration Council will not decide a demand that has no present subject matter and is pressed only against a state of affairs that may never recur; a contingent future claim must await the event that gives rise to it. [Para 80][Para 84] Where an employer's internal rules grade disciplinary penalties and make a written warning a precondition of unpaid suspension, a suspension imposed without proof of that prior warning is invalid, and the employer must pay the wages withheld for the suspension period. [Para 121][Para 123] A rice allowance fixed by ministerial notice as a flat daily sum may not be re-characterised as an hourly entitlement. [Para 90]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that no one can foresee whether trousers will be sewn again, at what rate, or which workers would be affected, and that the employer would still top up piece workers to the minimum wage. [Para 57][Para 84]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The workforce is given two different figures within the same order — approximately 290 in the findings of fact and approximately 350 in the reasoning on representativeness. [Para 45][Para 98] The suspension period is printed as 24 to 31 June 2010, a date that does not exist, and the same impossible end-date recurs in the operative order; the employer's own account of the suspension period is consistent with it, so the drafting error cannot be resolved from the file. [Para 69][Para 125][Para 128] The worker whose suspension is at issue is named in full throughout, and her surname is also printed in two variant spellings in the same paragraph; this brief uses an initial. [Para 72][Para 101] A sentence on the panel's reasoning breaks into an untranslatable Latin-script fragment, and the union's name is once printed with a transposed syllable in the procedural history. [Para 82][Para 17]
