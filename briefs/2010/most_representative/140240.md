# Award No. 02/10 — Cambodia Air Port Management Services (Arbitration Council, 18 February 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mar Samborana (employer-appointed), Tuon Siphan (worker-appointed), Run Saray (president) · Doc ID 140240

## 1. Facts
The company provides ground-handling and cleaning services at Siem Reap international airport and then employed about 409 workers, of whom roughly 399 held full-time eight-hour contracts and 10 held part-time four-hour contracts in the cleaning section [Para 76][Para 82]. The Cambodia Tourism Industry Workers' Union at the airport, with some 284 members and the most representative status in the plant, filed five demands on 2 December 2009; conciliation failed on 18 December 2009 and the file reached the Council on 6 January 2010 [Para 20][Para 22][Para 79]. At the hearing of 29 January 2010 the union withdrew demands 4 and 5, leaving Issues 1 to 3, and the parties agreed in the hearing itself to a binding award [Para 26][Para 27][Para 29]. Of 33 part-time cleaners previously employed, 17 were moved to full-time contracts in December 2009 and 6 more in January 2010, leaving the 10 who brought this claim [Para 83].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must convert the 10 part-time cleaning workers from four-hour to permanent full-time eight-hour contracts? [Para 80][Para 140]
2. (Issue 2) Whether management has committed fraud or embezzlement in the payment of wages and the meal allowance? [Para 109][Para 191]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must stop the airport security staff from searching workers' private persons without notice and from disregarding the role of the staff delegates and the union? [Para 130][Para 204]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** part-timers earn less in wages, meal allowance and shift premiums, are moved between shifts more often and get worse transport than full-timers, and the collective agreement requires full-time hiring; the refusal amounts to anti-union discrimination [Para 87][Para 96]. Wages were miscalculated and meal money was deducted from nearly 400 workers in May 2007 and never repaid, and one payslip showed a 10,000 riel meal deduction in May 2007 [Para 118][Para 119]. Airport security searched workers' private persons without prior notice, and the company imposed security rules without consulting the staff delegates under Article 284 [Para 130].

**Employer (respondent):** the collective agreement nowhere bans part-time contracts, and clause 7.2 expressly allows a four-hour shift; part-time staff receive the same benefits pro rata and conversions will follow as flight volume requires [Para 100][Para 103][Para 106][Para 175]. Any shortfall was a technical error, already repaid, with an apology made through a substitute staff delegate [Para 116][Para 128]. The searches follow national security rules that every airport must apply, they apply to all staff including managers, and Article 284 does not require consultation before imposing them [Para 131][Para 133][Para 135].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three remaining demands were refused, and the award was declared binding because the parties chose it in the hearing [Para 229][Para 230][Para 233].

On Issue 1 the panel read clause 5, 7.6, 7.7 and 7.2 of the 16 January 2008 collective agreement together with clause 4 of its financial annex. Those clauses fix weekly hours at 40 to 48, allow daily shifts of four to ten hours in the roster, and provide that part-time staff are paid and fed pro rata; none of them forbids part-time contracts, and the claim that they obliged full-time hiring was therefore wrong [Para 142][Para 147][Para 149][Para 153]. Turning to Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 22 of the Civil Code, the panel held a contract to be the agreement of both parties, which neither may alter unilaterally; the workers had a right to ask and the employer a right to refuse, and neither refusal nor request was unlawful [Para 157][Para 163][Para 177][Para 178]. The discrimination allegation failed because the union, which bore the burden of proof, produced nothing beyond assertion [Para 184][Para 187][Para 188].

On Issue 2 the panel found no embezzlement. The company had mis-computed night-shift pay for part-time staff between May 2008 and July 2009 and had repaid it, and in the two meal-allowance cases examined it had over-deducted and then made good the difference, one worker receiving 10,000 riel back on a corrected payslip [Para 114][Para 124][Para 126]. Since no worker had shown more than 2,288 hours worked in a year and the money had been restored, the allegation of fraud was refused; the panel nonetheless observed that the company should try to prevent such errors recurring [Para 113][Para 201][Para 202].

On Issue 3 the panel held that Article 2 paragraph 2 of the Labour Law gives the employer the right to organise and manage the enterprise, exercised lawfully and reasonably, and that the security regime here derived from national rules the government imposes on every airport [Para 205][Para 208][Para 215]. It read Article 284 as confining the staff delegates' consultative role to internal regulations and to layoff plans, not to government security rules that change frequently, so prior consultation was not required [Para 221][Para 226][Para 227]. On the body searches it found no evidence that the security staff acted with bad intent, accepted the company's account that the incident was accidental, but reminded security staff not to exploit checks to wound workers' dignity [Para 217][Para 218].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A collective agreement that permits part-time shifts and fixes pay pro rata does not oblige the employer to convert part-time workers to full-time, and because a contract may be varied only by consent, a demand for conversion is neither granted nor refused unlawfully; a discrimination claim fails where the claimant union offers no evidence beyond assertion [Para 153][Para 163][Para 188]. An employer may impose airport security rules adopted under national regulation without consulting the staff delegates, since Article 284 limits that consultation to internal regulations and layoff plans [Para 226][Para 227].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that management should strive to avoid further payroll errors, and that security staff must not let checks wound workers' dignity, were admonitions not tied to any order [Para 202][Para 217].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The respondent appears under at least four names — Cambodia Air Port Management Services, CAMS, SCA and Airport Management Services SRIA [Para 1][Para 52][Para 76]. Both hearings are dated 2009 in the procedural narrative although the hearing and award are 2010 [Para 23][Para 25]. The collective agreement clauses are set out verbatim twice, at lines 89 to 94 and again at 142 to 148, with the second copy containing OCR damage ("ររនុសញ្ញា") [Para 89][Para 145]. The list of the 10 part-time workers is numbered out of order, running 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 5, 8, 7, 9, 10, and one entry carries a bare "៨" [Para 87]. Stray Latin fragments ("V5", "Var-", "Jezzz", "Shey") interrupt the Khmer in the reasoning and operative sections [Para 152][Para 182][Para 203][Para 230]. Their numbers are also inconsistent: 33 part-timers, 17 plus 6 converted, 23 said to work in baggage handling while the 10 remaining are described as cleaners [Para 83][Para 84][Para 85]. Individuals appear only by role.
