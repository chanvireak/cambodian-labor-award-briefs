# Award No. 112/10 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេស ឌី (ខេមបូឌា) ខូអិលធីឌី / ASD Cambodia Co., Ltd and សហជីព ឧស្សាហកម្ម កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា ASD Cambodia Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 10 November 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chiv Phirom, An Nan, Run Saray) · Doc ID 140320

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេស ឌី (ខេមបូឌា) ខូអិលធីឌី (ASD Cambodia Co., Ltd), a garment factory at Ou Dim, Kandoek, Ang Snuol, Kandal, with about 700 workers, faced nine demands; six were unresolved, of which Issue 1 and Issue 5 were settled at the hearing and Issue 3 was withdrawn, leaving Issues 2, 4 and 6. [Para 20][Para 24][Para 25][Para 26][Para 33]
The claimant is សហជីព ឧស្សាហកម្ម កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា ប្រចាំ រោងចក្រ អេស ឌី with about 396 members, certified most representative by the Ministry under registration No. 011/09, valid to 6 April 2011. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 36]
The demands were USD 6 a month in lieu of a nursery for medical and office staff, the leave and maternity balance of a worker who resigned, and a 1,500 riel meal allowance for each two hours of overtime. [Para 37][Para 45][Para 55]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the USD 6 monthly payment in lieu of building a nursery must also be paid to the medical staff and other office staff? [Para 37][Para 65]
2. (Issue 4) Whether the company must pay the 11 remaining days of annual leave and the outstanding 50% of the living allowance for three months of maternity leave to the worker who resigned? [Para 45][Para 79]
3. (Issue 6) Whether the company must raise the overtime meal allowance to 1,500 riel for every two hours of overtime worked? [Para 55][Para 110]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ជាតិ នៃ ឧស្សាហកម្ម វាយនភ័ណ្ឌ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា and the ASD factory local union, through Mr N and Ms S:** the 2006 agreement promising USD 6 in lieu of a nursery covers all staff, since medical and office staff are also employees under Articles 184–186, and the company has no nursery; the resigned worker is owed 11 days' leave and the full living allowance; and 1,000 riel no longer buys a meal, while nearby factories pay 1,500. [Para 42][Para 47][Para 50][Para 60][Para 61]
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេស ឌី (ខេមបូឌា) ខូអិលធីឌី, through Ms T of accounting and Mr P of administration:** office and medical staff earn from USD 120 and already receive a meal allowance of up to USD 16.67 a month and company transport, so the USD 6 nursery payment is confined to production workers; it paid 8.65 for 9 days of leave and only 50% of the living allowance by established practice; and it cannot match 1,500 riel because it is a subcontractor with no direct buyer. [Para 44][Para 48][Para 54][Para 63][Para 74]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must pay USD 6 a month in lieu of a nursery to the medical staff and other staff (Issue 2), pay the missing 11 days of annual leave and the remaining 50% living allowance for three months of maternity leave (Issue 4), and the 1,500 riel demand is rejected (Issue 6). [Para 138][Para 139][Para 140][Para 141]
On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 186 of the Labour Law, which requires an enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a nursing room and a nursery, and, where no nursery can take children over 18 months, to let mothers place the child elsewhere at the employer's expense. Following Award 62/10, that duty may be discharged by a money payment agreed with the workers. Because Article 186 speaks of employees generally and not of production workers only, the exclusion of medical and office staff was contrary to it, and the panel encouraged the employer to keep the better-than-law benefit for them too. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 70][Para 72][Para 75][Para 77][Para 78]
On Issue 4 the panel applied Articles 167 and 166. With six years' service the worker earned 19 days a year, that is 1.58 days a month; over the seven months from October 2009 to April 2010 she accumulated 11 unused days, and the 8.65 already paid covered only nine, so the shortfall was ordered. [Para 79][Para 87][Para 90][Para 92][Para 93][Para 95][Para 96]
On the maternity point, Notification No. 032/08 of 17 April 2008 fixes a USD 6 living allowance that is not part of base wage and is payable without conditions, and prior awards (119/08, 139/08, 52/10) hold that it is paid in full even where the worker takes leave. Paying it at 50% during maternity leave was therefore wrong and the balance for the three months was ordered. [Para 98][Para 104][Para 105][Para 108][Para 109]
On Issue 6 the panel found the 1,000 riel in point 4 of Notification No. 017 to be the entitlement and no agreement or collective agreement providing 1,500. Although the union is the most representative union and could bring an interest dispute, an award fixing 1,500 would by Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 become a collective agreement binding for a year and would strip the workers of their right to bargain or strike over it later; with no evidence that 1,000 riel cannot buy a meal, the panel declined and urged collective bargaining. [Para 113][Para 114][Para 117][Para 121][Para 126][Para 130][Para 132][Para 136]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The Article 186 duty to provide a nursery, or money in lieu of one, runs to every female employee in the enterprise, not only to production workers, so a scheme confined to workers of one category is contrary to the Labour Law. [Para 72][Para 75]
A most representative union that asks the panel to set a new benefit is asking for an interest award that will operate as a collective agreement for a year; the panel will decline where the amount is already fixed by notification and the claim is unsupported by evidence, leaving the matter to bargaining. [Para 126][Para 130][Para 132]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel encouraged the employer to grant benefits above the legal minimum, and observed that fixing the meal allowance would prevent other workers from later negotiating or striking over it. [Para 76][Para 127][Para 133]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Amounts and percentages are unstable in the file: the maternity allowance demand appears as "500%" at line 8 but as 50% elsewhere, and the operative order on Issue 2 describes the beneficiaries with a stray numeral before the word for medical staff, so the size of that group cannot be read from the order alone. [Para 8][Para 78][Para 138]
The worker at the centre of Issue 4 is named one way in the demand and the reasoning and differently in the wage table; only initials are used here, and her base wage is printed as USD 50 in one place. [Para 27][Para 46]
The union's own certificate of most-representative status is described in one paragraph as an "inventory list" of that status, a mistranslation repeated three times, and line 2 carries a stray 2016 date inside the letterhead block. [Para 2][Para 35][Para 119]
The signature block prints the worker-chosen arbitrator's name in two forms, the second not on the face of the award; the panel composition above follows the face of the award. [Para 1][Para 143]
