# Award No. 107/10 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ជូន តិច ថា យល៍ (Chun Tich Tha Yol) v សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព សម្លេង យុវជន ខ្មែរ (Arbitration Council, 15 October 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Tuon Si Phan, Pen Bun Chhea) · Doc ID 140316

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ជូន តិច ថា យល៍ (Chun Tich Tha Yol), a garment factory in Phnom Penh operating since 1993 with 4,200 workers, faced a nine-point collective demand lodged on 18 August 2010 by the Khmer Youth Workers' Voice Union (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព សម្លេង យុវជន ខ្មែរ) at its B Thmey Neary Cambodia factory local, which has 50 members. [Para 1][Para 35][Para 36][Para 38]
Conciliation at the Ministry of Labour failed on all nine points (report No. 871 K.B/AK/V.K, 13 September 2010). [Para 13][Para 18]
At the hearing the parties settled points 5 to 9, the workers withdrawing point 6, so the panel decided only issues 1 to 4. [Para 20][Para 21]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must add USD 1 per month to the seniority bonus for every year of service beyond the fifth, without limit? [Para 39][Para 66]
2. (Issue 2) Whether maternity leave pay must be computed at 50% of the average monthly wage and allowances of the last 12 months and paid to the worker before she stops work? [Para 43][Para 86]
3. (Issue 3) Whether workers who work eight hours on a public holiday are entitled to a free meal or 4,000 riel in food allowance, on the view that holiday work is overtime? [Para 50][Para 104]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must issue fixed-duration contracts of one year at a time? [Para 59][Para 130]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព សម្លេង យុវជន ខ្មែរ:** Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 caps the seniority bonus at four years without saying so, so a dollar must be added each year thereafter; maternity pay should be a single lump sum before leave because travel to the factory each month is costly; eight hours on a holiday is overtime and attracts 4,000 riel; and the law does not forbid one-year contracts. [Para 40][Para 46][Para 54][Para 62]
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន ជូន តិច ថា យល៍, represented by Mr M:** Notification No. 017 limits the bonus to USD 5 for anyone with four years or more; the company has always paid 50% of base wage monthly on the 5th and allows a relative to collect it; eight hours on a holiday is not overtime so no meal allowance is due; and contracts are the parties' freedom. [Para 42][Para 48][Para 49][Para 56][Para 63]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 2 only: the company must pay 50% of wages and allowances for 90 days of maternity leave before the leave begins, computed on the 12-month average; issues 1, 3 and 4 dismissed. [Para 144][Para 145][Para 147][Para 148]
On issue 1 the panel read point 5 of Notification No. 017 (18 July 2000), preserved by Notification No. 745 K.K.BV (23 October 2006), as fixing USD 5 for four years and saying nothing beyond, and found no collective agreement or past practice supporting more. The demand was therefore a claim to benefits above what the law gives — an interest dispute — and the union was not the most representative union in the enterprise, so the panel declined to consider it, following Awards 81/04, 98/04, 116/09 and 64/10. [Para 69][Para 73][Para 76][Para 78][Para 80][Para 84][Para 145]
On issue 2 the panel applied Articles 182 and 183 of the Labour Law (90 days' leave; half pay including allowances, for women with at least one year's service), then Article 103 to hold that "wage" includes overtime, commissions, bonuses and allowances, so the 50% is computed on total earnings of the last 12 months divided by 12 and multiplied by three, citing Awards 68/04, 18/06 and 33/07. Article 115(3) requires payment before the leave day, following Awards 08/08, 114/08 and 139/08. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 91][Para 94][Para 97][Para 100][Para 102]
On issue 3 the panel held that work on a holiday is treated like a normal working day with one extra day's wage (Article 164; Prakas No. 10 SKBY of 4 February 1999, points 2 and 4), and Articles 137 and 139 do not make eight hours on a holiday overtime; the flat 1,000 riel meal allowance arises only for actual overtime. No agreement provided otherwise, so the demand was also an interest claim and was refused. [Para 110][Para 116][Para 119][Para 121][Para 125][Para 127][Para 147]
On issue 4 the panel held that under Article 65 of the Labour Law contracts sit under general civil law (Civil Code Decree No. 38 KR.Ch, Articles 1 and 22), so duration is fixed by consent; a third party cannot impose terms, following Awards 131/09 and 54/10. [Para 133][Para 136][Para 138][Para 142][Para 148]
Arbitrator Tuon Si Phan dissented on issue 3, reading holiday work as overtime attracting the 100% benefit. [Para 152][Para 156]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that is not the most representative union in the enterprise cannot obtain an award on an interest dispute — a demand for benefits exceeding what statute, a collective agreement or established practice already gives — and the panel will decline to consider it on the merits. [Para 78][Para 80][Para 84]
Separately, maternity pay under Article 183 is half of the 12-month average of total wage including allowances, and must be paid before the leave starts; holiday work of ordinary hours earns one extra day's wage, not the overtime meal allowance. [Para 97][Para 100][Para 121]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that Article 183 does not itself define "wage", which is why it cross-read Article 103, and observed that a worker may authorise a relative to collect maternity pay. [Para 49][Para 90]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The conciliation report is dated 13 September 2010 in the procedural history but 13 September 2016 at line 18; the award otherwise runs on 2010 dates and is signed 15 October 2010. [Para 13][Para 18][Para 165]
Line 94, in the maternity calculation, collapses into unrecoverable OCR (stray digits, Latin fragments and a 2017 date) between the average-wage rule and the "multiply by three" step; the operative paragraph at line 102 restates the rule and was relied on instead. [Para 94][Para 102]
The signature block is garbled and prints the employer-chosen arbitrator's name in two different forms; one form is reported here and the other cannot be read reliably. The company name also appears in several spellings across the file, and the dissent is headed "ឧបសម្ព័ន្ឋ" with a stray page marker. [Para 150][Para 151]
