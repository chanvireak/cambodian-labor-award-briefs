# Order No. 118/10-Menhattan — Sun Industrial Menhattan (សួន ឧស្សាហកម្ម មេនហាតធេន), Kampong Cham (Arbitration Council, 19 November 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (គង់ ផល្លុះ presiding; ឈីវ ភិរម្យ, employer nominee; អាន វីរៈ, worker nominee) · Doc ID 140323

## 1. Facts
Sun Industrial Menhattan (សួន ឧស្សាហកម្ម មេនហាតធេន), a garment factory at Ampil Leu village, Kampong Siem district, Kampong Cham, employed 4,250 workers, 3,825 of them women; the factory branch of the Free Trade Union of Workers of the Kingdom of Cambodia (សហជីពសេរីកម្មករ នៃ ព្រះរាជាណាចក្រកម្ពុជា) claimed about 2,000 members and was not yet the most representative union. [Para 1][Para 24][Para 25] On 5 and 6 October 2010 the workers struck to force payment of September wages before the Pchum Ben holidays; four demands had gone to the Kampong Cham labour department, conciliation settled one, and the remaining three were referred to the Council. [Para 2][Para 10][Para 14] At the hearing the parties agreed issues 1 (wages to be paid by the 5th of the following month) and 3 (reinstatement of a dismissed staff delegate), leaving only issue 2 for decision. [Para 3][Para 18]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether workers who joined the strike of 5 and 6 October 2010 are entitled to their wages and to the regular-attendance bonus for those two days? [Para 26][Para 41]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — សហជីពសេរីកម្មករ នៃ រោងចក្រ កាត់ដេរ មេនហាត ធេន:** the company paid wages on the 10th of the following month, and because 7 to 9 October were Pchum Ben holidays the workers asked for September wages on 6 October; they wrote to the employer to discuss, the discussion failed, and they struck; they nevertheless conceded at the hearing that the strike was called without prior notice to the employer and outside the procedure of the Labour Law. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 33]
**Employer (respondent) — Sun Industrial Menhattan:** payroll could not be computed in time, yet the company still advanced USD 20 per worker, one third of wages, under the Ministry's notification; the strike group forced its way into the new building, broke a glass door and pushed a senior staff member to the ground, injuring him, so this was a violent strike; and no wages or bonus can be paid for the two strike days. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 37][Para 38][Para 40]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the demand that the employer pay wages and the regular-attendance bonus to the workers who joined the strike of 5 and 6 October 2010. [Para 67][Para 68]
On wages the panel applied Article 332(1) of the Labour Law: the employment contract is suspended during a strike, work is not provided in that period and wages are not paid. [Para 42][Para 43] It adopted the reading of prior panels in Orders 49/05 and 27/10-Hat Inter that Article 332 bars wages for strike days whether the strike was lawful or unlawful, and that suspension affects only the principal obligations of the contract — the worker's duty to work and the employer's duty to pay. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 47][Para 48] It read Article 72(1) to the same effect. [Para 46]
On the attendance bonus the panel cited point 3 of Notification No. 017 SKBY of 18 July 2000, which gives a worker who works the required days of a month a bonus of at least USD 5, and followed Orders 110/07, 81/08-Vuopal Apparel and 27/10 in holding that a striker does not attend regularly and therefore loses both wages and the bonus. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51] Article 324 requires at least seven days' notice of a strike, and Article 320(k)(4) allows the right to strike only once peaceful means with the employer are exhausted. [Para 52][Para 55] The panel noted that the workers themselves accepted there had been no notice and no compliance with the Labour Law, that they struck as soon as they learned the employer would not pay by 6 October, and that the conciliation route had not been exhausted, so the strike was unlawful and the attendance bonus forfeited. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63][Para 65][Para 66] The order records that it is not binding and becomes final eight days after notification if no party objects to the Minister of Labour. [Para 69]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The employment contract is suspended for the whole of a strike under Article 332(1) and Article 72(1) of the Labour Law, so an employer owes neither wages nor the regular-attendance bonus for the strike days, whether the strike was lawful or not; independently, a strike called without the seven days' notice of Article 324, before the peaceful means of Article 320(k)(4) are exhausted, is unlawful, and its participants cannot claim the attendance bonus because they did not attend work as required. [Para 44][Para 47][Para 55][Para 65][Para 66]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded the employer's account of a forced entry into the new building, a broken glass door and an injured senior staff member, and the employer's characterisation of the strike as violent, but decided the case on the suspension of the contract and the procedural defects rather than on that alleged violence. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The document is an order (សេចក្តីបង្គាប់) made under Article 313 of the Labour Law, not an award, and says so on its face. [Para 1] The company name is printed three ways: "សួន ឧស្សាហកម្ម មេនហាតធេន" at the head and in the Ministry report title, "សូន ឧស្សាហកម្ម មេនហាត ធេន" in the findings, and "មេន ហាត់ ធេន" in the union description. [Para 1][Para 20][Para 24][Para 25] The hearing is dated 4 November 2010 in the hearing section but 4 October 2010 in the procedural summary, an evident slip for the same sitting. [Para 9][Para 16] The number of strikers is given as "about 3,000" while the workforce is 4,250. [Para 24][Para 27] The foot signature block is partly destroyed: the presiding arbitrator's name appears in the header as printed above and at the foot as a variant with stray Latin OCR strings, and the worker nominee's name is unreadable there. [Para 1][Para 70] The dismissed staff delegate named in demand 3 and the union officials are referred to only by initial and office; they are not reproduced here.
