# Award No. 129/10-SH — SH International Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 27 December 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Kong Chholl, presiding) · Doc ID 140337

## 1. Facts
SH International Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេស អេច អិនធើណេស្យូណល កូ អិលធីឌី), a Phnom Penh garment factory employing about 985 workers, received a complaint lodged on 20 October 2010 by the SH factory workers' union of the Federation of Cambodian Workers' Unions (សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា), demanding better working conditions. [Para 48][Para 27] Conciliation settled three of thirteen demands, leaving ten unresolved points for the panel. [Para 29] The workers' union had no most-representative certificate. [Para 133]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must hold an in-factory party with a lucky draw one day before Khmer New Year and Pchum Ben? [Para 51]
2. (Issue 2) Whether workers may take three days before and three days after those holidays without drawing on annual leave? [Para 56]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the regular attendance bonus must be raised by a further USD 5 per month? [Para 62]
4. (Issue 4) Whether a further 1,000 riel meal allowance is owed whenever overtime reaches four hours? [Para 68]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the employer must contribute USD 10 a month towards travel and lodging? [Para 74]
6. (Issue 6) Whether one additional day of annual leave accrues in each year of service? [Para 79]
7. (Issue 7) Whether special leave must be granted without any deduction from annual leave? [Para 86]
8. (Issue 8) Whether the employer must provide a nursery and a breastfeeding room? [Para 93]
9. (Issue 9) Whether wages and the attendance bonus must be preserved during sick leave covered by a valid medical certificate? [Para 97]
10. (Issue 10) Whether USD 5 a month is owed for breast-milk support? [Para 110]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — SH factory workers' union, Federation of Cambodian Workers' Unions):** the company had long failed to hold holiday gatherings, so the party and draw were due; the extra days around the holidays should not be charged against annual leave, since workers wanted to preserve leave to cash out at year end; and the bonus, meal, travel, leave and nursery demands followed comparators and ministry policy. [Para 52][Para 57][Para 63]
**Employer (respondent — SH International Co., Ltd, represented by its administrative director):** the company had held three such parties since 1997 but could not yet manage another; it already granted three days before and three after the holidays, drawing them from annual leave; bonus and welfare demands exceeded its capacity at a time when it had just raised wages by USD 11; it had a breastfeeding room but no nursery timetable. [Para 54][Para 59][Para 64][Para 94]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 8 alone: the employer was ordered to arrange a suitable nursery (ទារកដ្ឋាន) for its workers; the other nine claims were refused, most because the panel declined to consider them. [Para 244]

On issue 1 the panel asked whether any law, prakas, notification, circular or doctrine obliged an employer to finance a party and draw. It found none, and found no collective agreement or internal rule either; the demand therefore lay outside the law, making the dispute one of interests. [Para 119][Para 121] Issues 2, 10 and the remaining unmade demands travelled the same route: an interest dispute. [Para 147][Para 234]

That classification was decisive, because the panel then examined the union's standing. Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 provides that an award resolving an interest dispute stands in place of a collective agreement for one year. [Para 125] A union therefore needs most-representative status under Article 277 of the Labour Law to bring such a case, and this union held no certificate; the panel followed its earlier awards declining interest disputes from unions lacking that status. [Para 132][Para 133] It recorded about 600 members among 985 workers but still found the threshold unmet. [Para 130][Para 131] Issues 1 and 2 were accordingly refused without any merits assessment. [Para 134][Para 148]

The panel did reach the merits of the legal-entitlement claims. On issue 3, point 3 of Notification No. 049 of 9 July 2010 preserved point 3 of Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000, which guarantees a minimum USD 5 attendance bonus; both sides accepted the USD 5 was paid and no instrument required more, so the claim failed. [Para 150][Para 152][Para 155] On issue 4, point 4 of Notification No. 017 fixes 1,000 riel per day of voluntary overtime, or a free meal; the company paid 1,500 riel, which is more favourable, so nothing further was owed. [Para 160][Para 163][Para 166] On issue 5 no law, internal rule or agreement required travel or lodging money. [Para 168][Para 171]

On issues 6 and 7 the panel construed the Labour Law. Article 166(4) adds one working day of annual leave for each three years of service, not one day per year, and paragraph (ជ) of the internal rules matched that reading; the claim for an annual extra day was refused. [Para 178][Para 186][Para 191] Article 171 and Article 2 of Prakas No. 267 permit special leave to be set against annual leave where leave remains unused, so the demand for deduction-free special leave also failed. [Para 193][Para 196][Para 199]

On issue 9 the panel accepted that wages were preserved but found the pro-rata cut of the attendance bonus for certified sick days defensible, since the worker did not attend; the claim was refused. [Para 215][Para 221] On issue 8, by contrast, Article 186 requires any enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a breastfeeding room and a nursery. Women were 90 per cent of the workforce, the room existed, the nursery did not, and no reason for the omission was given, so the employer was ordered to arrange one. [Para 201][Para 207][Para 208][Para 209] The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification. [Para 247]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that has not obtained a most-representative certificate under Article 277 of the Labour Law cannot bring an interest dispute to the Arbitration Council, because under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 an award in such a dispute would operate for a year as a collective agreement binding every worker in the enterprise, member or not. [Para 125][Para 126][Para 132]

**Obiter dicta:** annual leave is in principle to be taken at Khmer New Year absent a different agreement (Article 170), and special leave may be charged against annual leave only while leave remains unused. [Para 138][Para 197]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award gives two incompatible membership figures for the claimant union: approximately 60 members at one point and approximately 600 at another; both are reported above and neither was reconciled by the panel. [Para 50][Para 130]

Several quantities are corrupted by the scan: a demand for "1 5" dollars, a "19 5" dollar figure, a "2 01" day claim, and the year of Notification No. 017 printed once as "200 0"; the panel's own award makes sense only on the reading that these are USD 5, USD 19.5 or USD 20, 20 days and the year 2000. [Para 149][Para 154][Para 172][Para 151]

Untranslated English or machine noise interrupts the Khmer text in the evidence list and in two reasoning passages, and the presiding arbitrator's name is spelled differently in the header and in the signature block. Individual worker and union-official names in the evidence list are left as roles in this brief.
