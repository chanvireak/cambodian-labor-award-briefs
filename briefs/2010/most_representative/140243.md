# Award No. 03/10 — Yung Wah Industry (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 5 February 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — You Sountheary (employer-appointed), An Nan (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (president) · Doc ID 140243

## 1. Facts
Yung Wah I is a garment factory at Ta Khmau, Kandal, which began operating in 1998 as a branch of Yung Wah and then employed about 2,100 workers; six plant unions exist and none is most representative [Para 1][Para 57]. The plant union of the Federation of Trade Unions of Workers, the Yung Wah 1 Factory Workers' Union, complained to the labour inspector on 7 December 2009; the Kandal labour department conciliated on 29 December 2009 over five demands, settling two and leaving three, which reached the Council on 11 January 2010 [Para 12][Para 13][Para 14]. The union claimed more than 100 members but supplied a dues list of only 90, which the company alone recognises [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61]. The hearing was held on 25 January 2010 [Para 11]. The company pays wages once a month, on the 10th of the following month [Para 62].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay wages twice a month, with no more than 16 days between payments? [Para 62][Para 94][Para 148]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must accept a sick-leave certificate from any properly qualified doctor, rather than only from Chey Chumneas hospital? [Para 77][Para 113][Para 149]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must convene the plant unions and organise a fresh election of staff delegates? [Para 84][Para 127][Para 150]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the Labour Law and a ministerial circular both require wages twice a month, and workers fall into debt for rent and food between payments; if two payments are impossible, pay on the 7th instead [Para 66][Para 67][Para 68]. Many live far from Chey Chumneas hospital and cannot travel there when ill, so certificates from any state hospital should be accepted [Para 79]. The employer has not convened the six unions or held a delegate election for nearly two years [Para 90][Para 91].

**Employer (respondent):** with 2,100 staff, each payroll run costs counted labour, time and materials, and the bank transfers funds only once a month, so two payments are impossible even on the 7th [Para 69][Para 71][Para 72][Para 73]. It accepts only Chey Chumneas certificates because that hospital is one kilometre from the plant and lets it verify genuine illness [Para 81][Para 82]. It missed the election because talks with union representatives at Yung Wah II produced no agreement on candidates and procedure [Para 86][Para 87], but it plans a meeting in February 2010 [Para 89].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — all three demands were upheld: wages twice monthly at intervals of no more than 16 days, acceptance of certificates from any qualified doctor licensed under Ministry of Health standards, and an order to convene all six plant unions within a week and hold the delegate election within two weeks of that meeting; the award is non-binding until eight days pass without objection [Para 148][Para 149][Para 150][Para 151].

On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 116 of the Labour Law, read with Circular No. 1903 KB of 24 November 2008, which requires at least two wage payments a month with a maximum 16-day interval, while allowing the parties to agree another method by a collective agreement registered with the Ministry [Para 94][Para 96][Para 97][Para 98]. Because the article speaks of workers and the circular distinguishes workers from employees, the panel turned to Article 6, which defines a worker as a person performing manual labour under an employer's direction, and to Article 5 on employees [Para 99][Para 100][Para 101]. Following Award 126/09-Amber Garment, it held that garment production workers are workers within Article 6, not office staff, so the twice-monthly rule applied to the 90 claimants and was ordered [Para 102][Para 106][Para 107][Para 108][Para 109].

On Issue 2 the panel read Article 71(3), under which a contract is suspended during absence certified by an official doctor, and defined an official doctor as one trained at a school recognised by the Ministry of Health, holding a valid diploma and a practice licence [Para 113][Para 116]. Adopting Awards 07/08, 72/08-Yung Wah II and 161/09-USA, it held that state hospitals include provincial and municipal referral hospitals, district referral hospitals, khan referral hospitals, and commune and sangkat health centres [Para 117][Para 118][Para 120]. The company's practice of recognising only Chey Chumneas certificates was not authorised by its own internal regulation, which nowhere limits recognition to one hospital, so it was ordered to accept certificates from any licensed doctor in the public or private sector [Para 123][Para 124][Para 125][Para 126].

On Issue 3 the panel addressed jurisdiction first. Article 298 sends disputes about the conduct of a delegate election or the right to stand to the labour court, but this claim was not of that kind; it concerned the employer's failure to organise an election at the end of the delegates' term, which Articles 291 and 292 and Prakas No. 286 make the employer's duty, so the panel had jurisdiction [Para 127][Para 128][Para 130][Para 131][Para 137]. It found the company had let nearly two years pass without convening the unions [Para 143][Para 144][Para 146].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Factory production workers are "workers" under Article 6 of the Labour Law and must be paid at least twice a month with no more than 16 days between payments; cost and banking convenience do not excuse the duty, which only a registered collective agreement can vary [Para 98][Para 106][Para 109]. A sick-leave certificate from any doctor licensed under Ministry of Health standards, in the public or private sector, must be accepted, and an internal rule that names one hospital cannot restrict Article 71(3) [Para 116][Para 125][Para 126]. A claim that the employer failed to hold a delegate election at the end of the term is not an election dispute reserved to the labour court, but a breach of the employer's own duty, over which the panel has jurisdiction [Para 128][Para 130].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's calculation that the union's own dues list showed 90 members rather than the 100 claimed, and its note that the company alone recognises those 90, were recorded but did not affect any order [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer is named variously as Yung Wah I, Yung Wah Industry (Cambodia) Co., Ltd., and Yung Wah 1, and the registration certificate appears under yet another spelling [Para 1][Para 18][Para 57]. The internal regulation is dated 19 February 2002 in one place and "19 February 2019 2002" in another [Para 123]. The payment date is printed as the 10th, then as "the 16th, 10th" of the following month, which is the very interval at issue [Para 62][Para 92]. The employer's representative appears under two different Latin spellings in the party list and the delegation letter [Para 1][Para 20]. More than forty individual workers are named in full, each with a health-centre letter, in the documentary list, and none of those names is reproduced here [Para 21][Para 31][Para 49]. The company name is corrupted to "Yung Wam" and "Yung Ramm" in the ministry report reference [Para 53], and stray Latin fragments of personal names and a garbled signature line interrupt the Khmer in the documentary list and the signature block [Para 46][Para 51][Para 152].
