# Order No. 85/10 — Riel Able South In Das Strell (Cambodia) Co., Ltd v. Federation of Khmer Youth Unions (Arbitration Council, 23 August 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Yu Soun Ty, An Non, Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 140302

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន រីឡាយ អេប៊ល សូស អ៊ិន ដាស ស្ទ្រៀល (ខេមបូឌា) — Riel Able South In Das Strell (Cambodia) Co., Ltd — employed about 1,500 workers at its Phnom Penh factory and recognised two unions on site, the Khmer Youth Union and an independent democratic union holding the most-representative certificate. [Para 50][Para 51] The Federation of Khmer Youth Unions (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ) filed a collective dispute on 8 July 2010 seeking improved working conditions; conciliation left 7 of 10 demands unresolved, and the file reached the Council on 2 August 2010. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 23] Further conciliation at the hearing settled 3 more, leaving the four demands decided here; the remainder — a Category C bonus of USD 52, two extra ping-pong tables, and a demand on heavy work such as dragging cloth into the warehouse — fell away with the settlement and were not decided. [Para 26][Para 27]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must provide lockers with proper locks for workers' food and belongings? [Para 55][Para 97]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must extend the motorbike shed so that all workers' vehicles can park under cover? [Para 70][Para 127]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must issue a work certificate (វិញ្ញាបនប័ត្រការងារ) to a worker when the employment contract ends? [Para 78][Para 139]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the employer may require women workers to drag sewing machines between the production floor and the warehouse when a new garment style is introduced? [Para 85][Para 156]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), Federation of Khmer Youth Unions / Khmer Youth Union at Riel Able:** food brought from home is stored on dining tables and is sometimes lost or blown to the floor, so lockers are needed for hygiene; the shed is too small and machines left in the sun suffer burst tyres; a work certificate lets them show a new employer their experience; and dragging machines about 100 metres over a sloped concrete threshold, three or four women to a machine, causes exhaustion and back pain, aggravated by fear of losing the cooperation bonus. [Para 57][Para 73][Para 92][Para 93] The workers invoke Article 93 of the Labour Law for the certificate and Article 2's internal-rules argument against eating in the workplace. [Para 79][Para 64]

**Employer (respondent), Riel Able South In Das Strell (Cambodia) Co., Ltd:** there is no space, no budget and no manageable way to install lockers, and no worker ever reported lost food even though a security guard sits beside the eating area. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 66] The shed cannot be enlarged because the dining hall abuts one side and a container-truck lane the other, and neither party has any agreement on the subject. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 77] The company has always handed over employment books at departure and disputes that a separate certificate is owed; and machine relocation is voluntary, offered only while women are otherwise idle, with five or six male mechanics unable to finish in time. [Para 81][Para 82][Para 89][Para 90]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issues 1 and 2 refused; Issue 3 granted by ordering the employer to issue a work certificate on the worker's request at termination; Issue 4 granted by ordering the employer to ensure the machines women drag do not exceed the weight the Labour Law protects. No money was awarded. [Para 172][Para 173][Para 174][Para 175]

On Issue 1 the panel read Article 229 of the Labour Law, which requires premises to be kept clean and to show hygiene and well-being conditions, and noted that the Ministry has issued follow-up regulations but none yet addressing food. [Para 100][Para 103] It therefore found no article making the employer liable for every loss of workers' belongings, and held there was no duty to install lockers. [Para 104][Para 105] The internal rules forbidding eating in the workplace were held too unclear to create such a duty, and photographs tendered on 5 August 2010 were not cogent proof of food blown to the ground. [Para 106][Para 110] The panel accepted that the employer had already placed a guard and a complaint procedure beside the dining area, which the workers admitted never using. [Para 107][Para 108]

On Issue 2 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law or related rules dealing with constructing or extending a motorbike shed, and no agreement between the parties requiring it; it followed Award No. 134/09 (Bright Land) on the same point. [Para 130][Para 131][Para 132] Because the demand rested on a benefit the law does not confer, it was characterised as an interest dispute, and the reasoning of Issue 1 was adopted. [Para 133][Para 134] The decisive jurisdictional finding is that the Khmer Youth Union is not the most-representative union in the factory, following Awards 81/04 (Evergreen), 98/04 (Great Union) and 116/09 (Sino Ty), so the panel declined to entertain interest disputes brought by it. [Para 114][Para 123][Para 125] That ground also disposed of Issue 1's refusal. [Para 126]

On Issue 3 the panel applied the final paragraph of Article 73 and Article 93(1)–(2) of the Labour Law, which let a worker require a work certificate on termination, and Prakas No. 414 of 23 March 2005, whose Article 4 requires return of the employment book and Article 5 forbids misuse of it. [Para 139][Para 140][Para 146][Para 147] Because the employer returned only the employment book and never the certificate, that practice was held contrary to those provisions, and the employer was ordered to issue a certificate stating the start and end dates of employment, the job held and its duration — but not salary, which Article 93 does not require. [Para 148][Para 149][Para 150][Para 151]

On Issue 4 the panel accepted the employer's managerial right under Article 2(2) to organise the production line, but held it must be exercised lawfully and reasonably, citing Awards 17/03 and 18/03 (Hohing) and others. [Para 156][Para 159][Para 164] Articles 230 and 250 require safe placement of machinery and precaution against workplace danger, and Article 4 of Prakas No. 124 of 15 June 2001 fixes maximum manual weights. [Para 160][Para 163][Para 168] The order was accordingly not an outright ban on women dragging machines but a duty to keep each machine within the lawful weight limit. [Para 170][Para 171]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that does not hold most-representative status in the enterprise cannot bring an interest dispute — a demand for a benefit the law does not itself confer — to the Arbitration Council; but a demand anchored in a specific article of the Labour Law is a rights dispute and is decided on its merits regardless of which union filed it. [Para 114][Para 124][Para 133] Separately, an employer's managerial prerogative over the production line is lawful only within the weight and safety limits set by Prakas No. 124 and Articles 230 and 250, so a lawful instruction to move machinery must still respect the statutory maximum a woman may lift, push or pull. [Para 164][Para 167][Para 168]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that food is a basic necessity of life and that lockers would also preserve the company's appearance, yet found no legal duty; it noted that Article 229 contemplates ministerial food regulations not yet issued. [Para 99][Para 103]
