# Award No. 135/10 — Tak Fhat Garment (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 December 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mam Somborna, employer-appointed; An Virak, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, presiding) · Doc ID 140340

## 1. Facts
Tak Fhat Garment (Cambodia) Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន តាក ហ្វាត ហ្គាមេន (ខាំបូឌា) អែល ធី ឌី) employs about 1,025 workers at Prek Ta Nu, Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, with seven unions in the plant. [Para 30][Para 31] The claimant is the Free Workers Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia (សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ នៃ ព្រះរាជាណាចក្រ កម្ពុជា) and its Tak Fhat factory union, with roughly 520 members; no union in the factory held a most-representative certificate. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 34] Conciliation left three demands unresolved; at the hearing the parties settled issue 1, and the workers added a further demand for wage protection while attending the hearing. [Para 14][Para 18][Para 19]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether sick-leave wages must be paid on the 10th together with the monthly payroll rather than months later? [Para 35]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the 1,000 riel overtime meal allowance must be paid weekly rather than every two to three weeks? [Para 43]
3. (Additional issue) Whether the panel may hear, and grant, a demand raised for the first time at the hearing that wages be preserved for workers attending it? [Para 56]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — Free Workers Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia and its Tak Fhat factory union):** the company retained wages for authorised sick leave but deducted the sick days from the monthly wage and paid that portion two to three months late, whereas wages were normally paid on the 10th; and the 1,000 riel meal money for overtime, paid weekly from 2000 to 2008, had become erratic, with October and November 2010 still unpaid, so it should return to weekly payment because it buys the day's food. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 46][Para 52]
**Employer (respondent — Tak Fhat Garment (Cambodia) Ltd):** sick-leave pay lies outside the regular payroll because illness is irregular, so it cannot be paid on the same 10th-of-month cycle; the meal allowance now goes out every two to three weeks, not the one to two months alleged. [Para 39][Para 41][Para 50][Para 51]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to pay sick-leave wages together with the monthly wage on the 10th of each month, to pay the 1,000 riel overtime meal allowance weekly, and to preserve the wages and benefits of workers summoned to the hearing. [Para 132][Para 133][Para 134]

On issue 2 the panel held that Article 103 of the Labour Law lists among wages the amounts payable during loss of working capacity and during maternity leave, so payment for leave taken with the company doctor's authorisation is itself wage. [Para 63][Para 65] It found the employer did preserve the wage but deducted the sick days and paid that money at an undetermined later date, generally up to a month after the payroll. [Para 61][Para 62] Since the employer pays once a month, on the 10th, and no other arrangement was agreed, the panel held the delay inconsistent with Article 103 and ordered sick-leave wages into the 10th-of-month payroll. [Para 70][Para 71][Para 77][Para 78]

On issue 3 the panel first asked whether the meal allowance is overtime pay. It held it is not: Article 103(1)(2) makes overtime pay part of wages, but the meal allowance is money replacing a meal, not earnings from extra hours, and if no money is given the employer must instead provide one free meal. [Para 88][Para 91][Para 92] Point 4 of Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 fixes 1,000 riel per day of voluntary overtime or one free meal, but sets no payment date. [Para 95][Para 96][Para 101] Reading that purpose, the panel held the money must reach workers in a reasonable time so they can buy food for the overtime day, that earlier awards requiring daily provision were right in substance, and that weekly payment satisfies the purpose where monthly does not. [Para 100][Para 102][Para 105][Para 108] It ordered weekly payment. [Para 109]

On the additional demand the panel examined its own jurisdiction. Article 312(1) limits it to issues in the non-conciliation minutes and issues arising after those minutes as a direct consequence of the dispute, and Article 33 of Prakas No. 099 says the same. [Para 111][Para 112] It found the wage cut was a direct consequence, because the company cut pay precisely when the workers attended the hearing, and so it had jurisdiction. [Para 116][Para 117] On the merits, Article 306 requires parties to refrain from any measure aggravating the dispute, Article 10 of Prakas No. 305 bars deducting the wages, bonuses or benefits of negotiating representatives, and Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 bars such deductions during arbitration. [Para 118][Para 124][Para 125] The company had not disputed the facts, and the panel ordered the wages and benefits preserved. [Para 58][Para 130][Para 131]

The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent written objection to the Minister of Labour. [Para 135]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Pay for authorised sick leave is wage within Article 103, so it must be paid on the employer's ordinary payday and may not be deferred to a later, undetermined date; and cutting the pay of workers who attend a conciliation or arbitration hearing is a prohibited aggravation that the panel will remedy even when the demand is first raised at the hearing. [Para 65][Para 77][Para 116][Para 125]

**Obiter dicta:** the overtime meal allowance is not a component of overtime pay, and where the employer provides neither the 1,000 riel nor a free meal it is in breach, though the instruments fix no payment date. [Para 91][Para 101]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's name is printed in two forms in the same award — តាក ហ្វាត ហ្គាមេន (ខាំបូឌា) អែល ធី ឌី in the party block and តាក ហ្វាត ហ្គាមិន in the findings and evidence list — and the case heading shortens it to តាកហ្វាត. [Para 1][Para 21][Para 30]

The membership sentence is corrupted into "មាស មាជិក", and the additional demand at first appears to be a claim by "40 workers" before the panel treats it as a union demand, a discrepancy the award never resolves. [Para 33][Para 56] The passage recording the company's objection is also partly destroyed, leaving an English fragment in the middle of the Khmer sentence. [Para 59] In the signature block the worker-side arbitrator's name carries scan noise, though the header names him as An Virak. [Para 136] Union officials and company representatives are given here by office only.
