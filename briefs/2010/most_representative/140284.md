# Award No. 66/10 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ញូ វរ៉យ ដ៏ (ខេមបូឌា) ហ្គាមេន (Nhu Vay Da (Cambodia) Garment) (Arbitration Council, 16 July 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel: អ៊ឹង សុទ្ធី (employer-nominated), ទួន ស៊ី ផាន់ (worker-nominated), កុយ នាម (chair) · Doc ID 140284

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ញូ វរ៉យ ដ៏ (ខេមបូឌា) ហ្គាមេន is a garment factory at Tuol Pongro, Dangkao, Phnom Penh, employing about 1,350 workers. [Para 73] សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ ខ្មែរ, the most-representative union at the plant, concluded a collective agreement with the company which was registered with the Ministry of Labour and which obliged every covered worker to pay, alongside monthly union dues, a further levy the agreement calls "តម្លៃតបស្នង" (a compensation fee for the agreement). [Para 3][Para 79][Para 83][Para 124] The company deducted both from January to March 2010 and then stopped, because 800 workers signed requests to end the deduction and because its new administration took the view that the deduction breached Article 129. [Para 90][Para 92][Para 98] Five demands failed conciliation on 15 June 2010; demands 3 and 4 were settled at the hearing but the union declined to sign and asked for an award. [Para 18][Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must continue deducting workers' wages in order to pay the collective agreement's compensation fee (តម្លៃតបស្នង) to the union, in addition to union dues? [Para 81]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay over the compensation fees it failed to deduct and remit from March 2010 to July 2010? [Para 105]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must allow the union and the federation to meet it regularly so as to monitor implementation of the collective agreement? [Para 111]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must supply copies of the records of the 800 workers who opted out of the agreement? [Para 113]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the company must pay the union 2 million riel a month for four months, 8 million riel in all, for the cost of pursuing the dispute? [Para 114][Para 116]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), represented by សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ ខ្មែរ:** the agreement must be applied in full, including clause 8, which requires every covered worker to pay the compensation fee, and the company had deducted it without objection until its new management arrived; a party that breaches a contract must compensate the other for the cost of enforcing it, and a collective agreement is a contract. [Para 81][Para 89][Para 119]
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន ញូ វរ៉យ ដ៏ (ខេមបូឌា) ហ្គាមេន:** clause 8 speaks only of the workers' duty to pay the union; it nowhere obliges the company to deduct, and deducting wages other than the items permitted by Articles 127 to 129 is unlawful, as the 800 signed refusals show; nothing in law requires it to pay the union's costs of litigation, in which it has spent money and time of its own. [Para 88][Para 91][Para 98][Para 117][Para 122]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claims that the company continue deducting the compensation fee, pay over the arrears from March to July 2010, and pay 8 million riel in damages were refused; the parties were ordered to implement their hearing agreements to hold regular monitoring meetings under clause 5 of the agreement and to let the union inspect the opt-out records and verify thumbprints directly with workers. [Para 171][Para 172][Para 173][Para 174][Para 175]

On Issues 1 and 2 the panel held that clause 8, read with Article 96(4) and clause 4 on coverage, imposes the payment obligation on workers, not on the employer, and that an employer may deduct from wages only the items Articles 127 and 128 allow, plus union dues under Article 129. [Para 127][Para 128][Para 137][Para 142][Para 143] It found that the compensation fee is treated by the parties as something other than dues, that a collective agreement cannot derogate from public-order provisions of the Labour Law, and that the employer therefore has no duty to deduct or to remit arrears. [Para 129][Para 131][Para 138][Para 156] On Issue 5 the panel accepted that Prakas No. 099 SKBY article 34 permits an award of damages for breach, but held that since the employer breached nothing there was no loss to compensate. [Para 165][Para 166][Para 169][Para 170]

Chair កុយ នាម appended a separate opinion arguing that the fee and union dues serve the same function, that clause 8's second and third sentences only make sense if the employer must deduct, and that the practice from January to March 2010 shows how the parties read it; he would have limited the deduction to members of the federation. [Para 183][Para 190][Para 196][Para 219]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may deduct from wages only the items the Labour Law permits — the four exhaustive grounds in Article 127, advances under Article 128, and union dues under Article 129 — so a collective agreement that requires workers to pay an additional levy to their union does not oblige the employer to collect it by payroll deduction, and an employer that stops deducting such a levy breaches nothing and owes no arrears or damages. [Para 137][Para 138][Para 169]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel set out the agreement's coverage clause in full, listing the categories excluded from it — managers and foreign staff, office and accounting staff, tailors and drivers, the company doctor, electricians and builders, and security — though no claim turned on that list. [Para 141][Para 142]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award's operative part describes the order as a binding one made because the parties chose it under the collective agreement, which differs from the non-binding form used in the other awards of this series; the text is also internally inconsistent about whether the agreement is dated 11 December 2009, registered 4 January 2010, or dated 4 April 2010, and the deductions are variously placed in January-March and February-March 2010. [Para 3][Para 33][Para 79][Para 90][Para 176] The employer's name appears in several Khmer spellings and in the signature block the employer-nominated arbitrator's name differs from the header. [Para 1][Para 177] The separate opinion is paginated into the award without a heading separating it from the majority's reasoning. [Para 177][Para 183] The evidence schedule lists 107 and 459 named union members respectively; no individual is named in this brief. [Para 35][Para 36]
