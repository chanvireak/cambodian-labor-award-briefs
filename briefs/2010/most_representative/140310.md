# Award No. 99/10 — Chen Tich Garment (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឆឺន តិច ហ្គាមេន) (Arbitration Council, 30 September 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 140310

## 1. Facts
Chen Tich Garment (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឆឺន តិច ហ្គាមេន), a factory operating since 2005 at Village Kbal Damrey, Sangkat Kap, Khan Dangkao, Phnom Penh, employed about 2,500 workers and housed four unions, none holding a most-representative certificate. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 30] The Cambodian Workers' Union Federation (សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា) and the Chen Tich factory workers' union (សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត រោងចក្រ ឆិន តិច) brought the complaint, the factory union having about 500 members. [Para 31] Of nine demands, conciliation on 20 August 2010 settled six and left three; the panel noted that the employer had already issued Prakas No. 028/2010 of 9 September 2010 granting the USD 11 wage rise claimed first. [Para 14][Para 20][Para 32]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must raise every worker's wage by USD 11 on top of the existing wage? [Para 32]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay each worker USD 7 per month as a housing rent or travel allowance? [Para 35][Para 52]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay the 50 per cent maternity allowance before the woman worker begins her maternity leave, rather than on the monthly payday that follows? [Para 63][Para 65]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), the Cambodian Workers' Union Federation (សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា) and the Chen Tich factory workers' union (សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត រោងចក្រ ឆិន តិច):** the USD 7 allowance is needed because prices have risen and other factories pay it; the maternity allowance must be handed over before leave because the women have no money for the birth, live far from the factory in the provinces and cannot afford to come back for it. [Para 36][Para 48][Para 49]
**Employer (respondent), Chen Tich Garment (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឆឺន តិច ហ្គាមេន):** orders have fallen, there is little work or overtime, and a new USD 11 wage rise is already due, so the USD 7 cannot be paid; maternity pay at 50 per cent of 12 months' average wages is already paid three months in advance, on the 10th payday after the seven-day notice, and the company lets a woman transfer collection to a friend or relative. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 45][Para 46][Para 51]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — on issue 1 the panel declined to consider the USD 11 wage rise because the employer had already granted it by Prakas No. 028/2010 of 9 September 2010; on issue 2 it declined to consider the USD 7 housing or travel allowance; on issue 3 it ordered the employer to pay the maternity allowance before the woman worker begins her maternity leave. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 62][Para 79][Para 80]

On issue 1 the panel recorded that both parties accepted the proclamation of 9 September 2010 granting USD 11 on top of the existing wage to all workers, so no dispute remained and no ruling was made. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 34]

On issue 2 the panel found that the Labour Law and related regulations nowhere require an employer to pay USD 7 a month in housing or travel money, so the claim exceeded what the law gives and was an interests dispute rather than a rights dispute. [Para 53][Para 54] Following Awards 81/04-Evergreen, 980-Grey Union, 116/09-Sin Tai and 64/10-Sen Tey, the panel declined to consider an interests dispute brought by a union without most-representative status, and found this union, with about 500 of 2,500 workers, lacked that legal character. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 60][Para 61]

On issue 3 the panel applied Article 182 (90 days' maternity leave) and Article 183, which entitles a woman to half her wage including allowances, and observed that Article 183 says nothing about when payment falls due. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67][Para 68] It then applied Article 115(3), under which wages may not be paid on a day the worker is entitled to rest and, if the payday falls on such a day, must be paid the day before. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 71] Following Awards 139/08-Super Prime Garment, 109/09-USA and 43/10-Meng Jian, and the Council's practice that maternity pay is handed over before leave begins, it held that making women wait until the 10th of the month was incorrect and ordered payment beforehand. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 75][Para 78][Para 79]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that does not hold a most-representative certificate cannot bring an interests dispute — a demand for a benefit the law does not require, such as a housing or travel allowance — for decision by the panel, although it may bring a rights dispute arising under the Labour Law or a collective agreement. Where the employer has already granted a demanded wage increase by its own proclamation before the hearing, there is no longer a dispute for the panel to decide. Maternity allowance under Article 183 falls due before the woman worker stops work, because Article 115(3) forbids payment on a rest day and requires payment the preceding day. [Para 57][Para 59][Para 34][Para 72]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that four unions operate in the factory, a fact relevant only to the representative question, and that the employer offered payment through a friend or relative where the woman cannot come in person — a practice the panel did not need to rule on. [Para 29][Para 51]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's name is printed at least three ways in the same document — "ឆឺន តិច", "ឆឹន តិច" and "ឆិន តិច" — and one passage inserts an extra Latin syllable before it in the union's own description. [Para 1][Para 27][Para 60] The operative paragraph carries the year "២០១៩" inside an award dated 30 September 2010, an impossible date left uncorrected. [Para 80] The final line of the file is unrecoverable Latin filler with no Khmer content. [Para 83] The header line is partly destroyed, leaving a run of punctuation before the Council's name. [Para 1] Paragraph 26 dates a hearing invitation "06 August 2010" where the surrounding text places it in September. [Para 26]
