# Award No. 34/10 — IM & Way Garment International, Branch 3 (Arbitration Council, 7 May 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (An Nan for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhean chair; the employer-party arbitrator's name survives only as an OCR smear in the signature block) · Doc ID 140260

## 1. Facts
The Industrial Union of Cambodia and the Independent Union of Cambodia at the Branch 3 factory of IM & Way Garment International, which employs about 2,800 workers on National Road 2 in Phnom Penh, complained on 3 February 2010, received on 8 February, seeking better working conditions; conciliation on 19 March 2010 failed on all three points, forwarded by non-conciliation report No. 298 of 30 March 2010. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 10][Para 15][Para 46]

The second demand concerned Mr S, hired on 25 August 1995 as an ironing worker on an indefinite-duration contract, promoted to ironing team leader about eight years before, and paid USD 90 a month, including a USD 6 living allowance and USD 5 attendance and USD 5 seniority bonuses. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63][Para 64] On 28 January 2010 the company ordered his transfer to ironing-line worker at the same wage and benefits; he refused, a second order followed on 1 February and a third on 4 February, and on 4 February 2010 the company dismissed him for serious misconduct. [Para 65][Para 67][Para 69][Para 70]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether annual leave pay for workers paid by output should be calculated on the wage they actually earned each month rather than on the USD 50 base wage? [Para 51][Para 103]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must take Mr S back in his former post at the same wage and benefits, and failing that pay him the compensation due under the Labour Law? [Para 61][Para 119]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer is obliged to provide a place where workers can rest and eat their midday meal? [Para 92][Para 187]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** annual leave must be based on the piece-rate wage actually received, since 1,000 or 1,500 riel no longer reflects earnings; Mr S was moved to harder direct ironing under a productivity condition that would trigger warnings and dismissal; and workers must eat standing among parked motorbikes because no eating place exists. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 59][Para 85][Para 92][Para 94]
**Employer (respondent):** Article 168 requires the base wage for annual leave; Mr S worked in a section with four team leaders for 86 workers, so the post was surplus, and he was moved without any cut in wage or benefits; and the rented site has no free space and no nearby plot could be leased. [Para 56][Para 79][Para 80][Para 96][Para 97]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered annual leave to be computed on the piece-rate wage actually earned, and ordered the company to pay Mr S severance, damages in lieu of notice, unused annual leave, his last unpaid wages and moral damages; it refused to consider the eating-place demand. [Para 206][Para 207][Para 208]

On Issue 1, the panel read Article 166 (1.5 days of paid leave per month of service), Article 102 (the definition of wage) and Article 103 together, and followed Award No. 82/06, which held that for workers paid by output the wage is what they actually earned. [Para 105][Para 107][Para 108][Para 111][Para 112][Para 113] Applying the USD 50 base wage caused loss because these workers earn more than that floor, so the panel ordered the employer to use the monthly piece-rate earnings as the basis. [Para 115][Para 116][Para 118]

On Issue 2, the panel accepted that Article 2, paragraph 2 gives the employer the right to organise and transfer workers, but subject to the limits drawn in earlier awards: no wage cut, no transfer to a distant place, no change of shift, and no transfer outside the worker's skill. [Para 123][Para 125][Para 126] The wage was preserved and the workplace and shift unchanged, but the company attached a condition that if his output did not reach the level of his USD 90 wage he would be warned twice and then dismissed, and the new post required him to iron directly rather than supervise. [Para 127][Para 128][Para 130][Para 139][Para 140] The panel therefore found the transfer unreasonable. [Para 141] Under Article 83 serious misconduct is limited to the listed breaches, and the internal rules contain no clause making three refusals of a transfer a serious breach; no written warning had been issued, and the employer produced no evidence of misconduct. [Para 143][Para 144][Para 145][Para 148][Para 150] The dismissal was consequently unjustified, and the panel applied Article 89 for severance, Article 75 for damages in lieu of notice, Article 167 for leave not taken, Article 116 for the last unpaid wages, and Article 91 for moral damages. [Para 154][Para 160][Para 161][Para 169][Para 170][Para 173][Para 176][Para 183][Para 184]

On Issue 3 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law or of any regulation making an eating area the employer's duty, and no agreement or internal rule providing one. [Para 187][Para 188][Para 191] A demand beyond what the law gives is an interest dispute, and the panel declined to consider it because neither claimant union held a certificate of most-representative status and together their members did not exceed 50 per cent of the 2,800 workers. [Para 192][Para 193][Para 195][Para 196][Para 205]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a transfer preserves the wage but attaches a productivity condition tied to the old wage, and changes the manner of work and the worker's rank, the transfer is unreasonable; refusing an unreasonable transfer is not serious misconduct under Article 83, so a dismissal based on it is unjustified and attracts the statutory payments. A demand for a facility the law does not require is an interest dispute, and a panel will not hear it unless the unions bringing it are most representative in the enterprise. [Para 130][Para 141][Para 144][Para 148][Para 192][Para 195]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's statement that a collective agreement binding on all workers in the factory cannot be renegotiated by strike while it is in force is a general observation on Prakar 43 and is not what disposed of the eating-place demand. [Para 201][Para 203]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The employer-party arbitrator's name is reduced to a smear in the signature block at [Para 210], and the worker-party arbitrator is printed as An Nan at [Para 1] but as a differently spelled name at [Para 210].
- The award calls itself a "subsequent order" rather than an award in the header at [Para 1], and the hearing month is printed as "Besa" instead of April at [Para 12].
- Article 89 is quoted in corrupted form at [Para 154]–[Para 156], where the six-month cap on severance is unreadable; the tiers used in the reasoning are those of the statutory text as partially preserved.
- The panel writes "40" piece-rate wage at [Para 117] where its own reasoning and the operative order use the monthly piece-rate figure, and a stray "2016" interrupts the order at [Para 118].
- The company is named IM & Way, Im Way, "Im and Way" and, in the cited precedent title, "Rim and Way", so earlier awards of the same factory appear under a different spelling at [Para 111] and [Para 113].
- The non-conciliation report number is printed as 298 in three different letter combinations at [Para 10], [Para 15] and [Para 42].
