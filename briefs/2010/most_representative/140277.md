# Award No. 57/10 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឃែន ធើរេន អី ភែរល (ខេមបូឌា) (Arbitration Council, 9 July 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (You Suonty, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Mae Nimith, chair) · Doc ID 140277

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឃែន ធើរេន អី ភែរល (ខេមបូឌា), a garment factory in Chom Chao, Dangkao, Phnom Penh, has operated since 2003 and employs about 1,300 workers; two unions exist there, and សហជីព ឧស្សាហកម្ម កម្ពុជា CIUF-Canteren, the claimant, has 146 members and is not the most representative. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 59][Para 60][Para 61] Of ten demands, three were left unresolved after conciliation on 4 June 2010 and the hearing of 21 June 2010: the deduction of one day's wage and the USD 5 attendance bonus from 180 workers who did not work on Sunday 4 April 2010, which the company had swapped for 12 April 2010; the advance payment of maternity benefits; and the basis for calculating annual-leave indemnity. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 81][Para 107]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company may deduct a day's wage and the regular attendance bonus from workers who did not work on the swapped Sunday and treat 12 April 2010 as a working day, or whether those workers may take 12 April 2010 as paid annual leave? [Para 62][Para 108]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay the 50 per cent wage and allowances for maternity leave in advance rather than month by month during the leave? [Para 88][Para 158]
3. (Issue 3) Whether annual-leave indemnity must be calculated on the average of total wages including bonuses and other benefits over the previous 12 months? [Para 96][Para 179]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ឧស្សាហកម្ម កម្ពុជា and សហជីព ឧស្សាហកម្ម កម្ពុជា CIUF-Canteren:** they never agreed to the day swap, were not invited to the meeting that decided it, and want 12 April 2010 as paid annual leave within the Khmer New Year period; maternity benefits must be paid before the leave starts because they need the money then; and using only the base wage for annual leave strips them of bonuses that form part of their pay. [Para 63][Para 69][Para 87][Para 91][Para 100][Para 171]
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឃែន ធើរេន អី ភែរល:** most workers accepted the swap, 955 attending on 4 April 2010 and only 259 staying away; the delegates and the Free Workers Union signed the agreement, so those who failed to attend were absentees; it pays maternity benefits monthly to protect itself against women who leave without notice; and its long practice is to use the base wage. [Para 80][Para 82][Para 93][Para 98][Para 104]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the company to let the workers take 12 April 2010 as paid annual leave; to repay one day's wage and the USD 5 regular attendance bonus deducted from the 180 claimants; to pay maternity benefits before the three-month leave begins; and to calculate annual-leave pay on the total wages of the last twelve months. [Para 198][Para 199][Para 200][Para 201]

On Issue 1 the panel held that Article 166(1) makes annual leave a right of the worker, not a discretion of the employer, and Article 170 requires it in principle to be taken during the Khmer New Year, with any other timing needing agreement and prior notification to the labour inspector. [Para 109][Para 111][Para 112][Para 115] The swap agreement was signed only by the worker delegates and the Free Workers Union; the claimants were not parties, no union is most representative, and under Articles 96 and 97 and Prakas No. 305 it was therefore not a collective agreement binding all workers in the factory. [Para 137][Para 139][Para 142][Para 154][Para 155] Since the swap could not be imposed, the company had no right to penalise absence on the Sunday, and 4 April 2010 remained a weekly rest day while 12 April 2010 became an ordinary working day available for annual leave. [Para 242][Para 243] The attendance bonus is itself part of wages under Article 103, so it had to be repaid with the day's wage. [Para 127][Para 129][Para 131]

On Issue 2 Articles 182(1) and 183(1) give 90 days of maternity leave on half wages and half allowances, while Article 115(3) forbids payment on rest days and requires it the day before; paying month by month during the leave therefore breached the law, and the panel ordered payment before the leave begins. [Para 158][Para 159][Para 167][Para 173][Para 174]

On Issue 3 Article 168 requires the average of wages, bonuses, benefits and the value of benefits in kind over the twelve months before the leave, yet the company used the base wage alone, which could leave workers worse off than if they had worked; the panel ordered recalculation on total earnings divided by twelve and then by 26. [Para 179][Para 181][Para 190][Para 196]

The arbitrator appointed by the employer, You Suonty, agreed with all three orders but dissented from the reasoning on Issue 1: in his view Article 151 and Prakas No. 100 permitted the employer to suspend the weekly rest for urgent work, but only after obtaining the labour inspector's authorisation, which was never sought, so the agreement was void under Article 13 and the deduction unlawful on that ground. [Para 205][Para 225][Para 228][Para 230][Para 240]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A day swap may not be imposed on workers by an agreement signed only by delegates and one union: where no union is most representative the arrangement is not a collective agreement binding the workforce, and annual leave remains the worker's own right, to be taken at the Khmer New Year unless both parties agree otherwise and the inspector is notified. Where an employer suspends weekly rest under Article 151 without the inspector's prior authorisation required by Prakas No. 100, the deduction is unlawful and the wage and attendance bonus — itself part of wages under Article 103 — must be repaid. [Para 111][Para 115][Para 129][Para 139][Para 155][Para 228]

**Obiter dicta:** the dissent's remarks that the employer's Article 151 right was in principle exercisable but became ineffective for want of authorisation, and the panel's observation that the law intends annual leave to fall at the Khmer New Year so families can gather, were not necessary to the orders made. [Para 119][Para 231]

- The award is headed 9 July 2010 [Para 1], the dissent is dated 29 June 2010 [Para 249], and the panel's own signature page carries a stray year "២០១៦" [Para 203].
- The chair's name appears as ម៉ែ និម្មិត in the header but as ម៉េន ម្ហូត in the signature block, and the employer-appointed arbitrator's signature line there is reduced to Latin fragments [Para 1][Para 203].
- Article 96(2) and Article 97 are quoted at length in corrupted form, and paragraph [Para 142] renders Article 96 as "មាត្រា ៩២", so the statutory chain must be read from context [Para 134][Para 142].
- The number of claimants shifts between 180, 259 and 146 across the findings, the decision and the party list, and the panel resolved only the 180 who had signed [Para 52][Para 80][Para 105][Para 107].
- The claim is dated 11 May 2010 in [Para 13] but appears in the exhibit list as 10 May 2010, and the union's own account of the first meeting gives 27 March 2010 while the company gives 31 March 2010 [Para 51][Para 70][Para 78].
