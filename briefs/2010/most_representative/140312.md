# Award No. 105/10 — Kentheren I Pearl (Cambodia) (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឃែន ធើរេន អី ភែរឹល (ខេមបូឌា)) (Arbitration Council, 30 September 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 140312

## 1. Facts
Kentheren I Pearl (Cambodia) (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឃែន ធើរេន អី ភែរឹល (ខេមបូឌា)), a garment factory operating since 2003 at Village Trapeang Thlang, Sangkat Chom Chao, Khan Dangkao, employed about 1,400 workers and three unions, none certified as most representative. [Para 31][Para 32][Para 33][Para 34] The Khmer Worker Movement Union (សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ចលនា កម្មករ) and its Kentheren factory union complained on 2 August 2010 that the company was not applying the Labour Law; of three unresolved demands, the workers withdrew the demand to lengthen three-month contracts to six months and settled the demand for two cans of milk a month by agreement of 28 September 2010 on daycare, leaving only the pregnancy health-check demand. [Para 14][Para 19][Para 20][Para 21] That union had about 100 members. [Para 35]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must allow a pregnant woman worker one day a month to attend a health check while keeping her full wage and her attendance bonus? [Para 3][Para 37]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), the Khmer Worker Movement Union (សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ចលនា កម្មករ) and its Kentheren factory union (សហជីព ចលនា កម្មករ នៃ រោងចក្រ កាត់ដេរ ឃែន ធើរេន):** pregnant workers need the check, their number is small against the whole workforce, other factories already grant a paid day a month, and the claim asks only for a benefit better than the law rather than anything the law forbids. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 49][Para 50]
**Employer (respondent), Kentheren I Pearl (Cambodia) (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឃែន ធើរេន អី ភែរឹល (ខេមបូឌា)):** the law says nothing about paid time off for a pregnancy check, so no wage can be guaranteed; the company already lets pregnant women go, letting them use paid annual leave, and deducts wage only for the days actually taken. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 43][Para 51][Para 53]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the demand that the company pay a full day's wage for each monthly pregnancy health check; the attendance-bonus element had already been dropped by the workers, and the contract and milk demands were withdrawn and settled. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 63][Para 64]

The panel first recorded what had narrowed. At the hearing the workers accepted that the company lets a pregnant woman take one day a month without cutting the attendance bonus and cutting only the wage, and so they no longer sought the bonus but only the wage. [Para 37][Para 38] On the wage the panel framed the question as whether the employer must pay in full on a day the woman goes for a pregnancy check. [Para 56]

It answered that by Article 71 (new) paragraph 1 point 6 of the Labour Law, under which the employment contract is suspended by absence the employer has authorised by law, collective agreement or individual agreement, and Article 72 (new) paragraph 1, under which suspension affects the essential obligations — the worker's duty to work and the employer's duty to pay. [Para 56][Para 57] Following Awards 119/08-Hong Va Thmey and 83/09-Sun Two World, it held that the wage in Article 72 is a package received only where the worker actually comes to work, so a worker who does not work receives nothing for the suspended period. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60] It added that this health-check absence is not one of the paid leaves the law itself creates. [Para 62] The demand was therefore refused. [Para 63]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A day off that the employer authorises so that a pregnant worker can attend a health check suspends the employment contract under Article 71(6); under Article 72 the employer then owes no wage for that day, because suspension relieves both the duty to work and the duty to pay. A demand for pay on such a day is therefore a request for a benefit beyond the Labour Law, and the panel will refuse it even where other factories grant it, unless a collective agreement, individual agreement or the employer's own practice has already created the entitlement. [Para 57][Para 60][Para 62]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the company lets women use accrued paid annual leave for the check, that only about 10 to 20 women are pregnant at any time, and that other factories pay for the day — none of which altered the legal position. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 46]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
One passage inserts a stray Latin syllable before the word "company" in the middle of the reasoning on the attendance bonus. [Para 54] The employer's name is printed inconsistently, with the first element written "ឃែន" in the header and "សែន" in the description of one union letter. [Para 1][Para 23] Modern years ("2017" and "2016") appear inside a 2010 award — in the party block, at a page marker and in the signature block — and cannot be reconciled with the stated date. [Para 1][Para 12][Para 66] Signature names are duplicated rather than lost, and part of the signature block is unreadable. [Para 66] The panel records issues 2 and 3 as settled at the hearing of 16 September 2010, yet the written agreement on daycare is dated 28 September 2010, twelve days after that hearing. [Para 19][Para 20]
