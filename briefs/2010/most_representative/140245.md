# Award No. 13/10 — T&K Garment Industry (Arbitration Council, 5 March 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Liv Sovanna, Kong Phalla presiding) · Doc ID 140245

## 1. Facts
T&K Garment Industry registered in April 2009 and took over the premises and workforce of Trinunggal Komara, which had run the factory since 2002 and had always let workers leave after seven hours on Saturdays although its contracts and internal rules said eight. The old company terminated its contracts and paid severance, the new company signed fresh contracts and registered new internal rules, and on the workers' request it kept the seven-hour Saturday on a temporary basis until the end of 2009. The Khmer Union and the Free Workers' Union, with 501 and 108 members out of about 980 workers, brought a single collective demand that the seven-hour Saturday be restored. Conciliation failed on 21 January 2010. [Para 74][Para 78][Para 79][Para 80][Para 102]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must keep Saturday work at only seven hours a day as before, rather than the eight hours set by its new contracts and internal rules? [Para 77][Para 92]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the seven-hour Saturday had been applied for years, from 2002 under the old company and continuing after the rename in May 2009; the workers had followed the company to its new site despite the hardship of new rented housing, understood the company's economic difficulty, and asked only to leave one hour early on Saturdays as before. [Para 78][Para 88][Para 89]

**Employer (respondent):** the new contracts and the internal rules registered with the Ministry both provide eight hours a day and six days a week including Saturday; the company wanted to comply with the law and did not have the capacity of the old Trinunggal Komara, being merely a sub-contractor for goods from other firms; the seven-hour Saturday had continued only temporarily, at the workers' request, until the end of 2009. [Para 82][Para 85][Para 86][Para 87]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the claim for a seven-hour Saturday was refused; the award was declared binding and final immediately, the parties having agreed on 16 February 2010 to accept it. [Para 136][Para 137][Para 138]

The panel first asked whether the old company's practice continued to bind the new company. Article 87(1) of the Labour Law provides that where the employer's legal situation changes — by succession, sale, merger or conversion into a company — existing employment contracts continue between the new employer and the workforce, and Award 21/05 (Sinobek) had read this as preserving all prior contracts and agreements unless replaced. The panel found, however, that the old contracts had in fact been terminated with payment of severance, and that the new company had lawfully created a new enterprise, registered new internal rules with the Ministry and signed new contracts with the former workforce. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 100][Para 101]

On that footing the panel held that the new company was not bound by Trinunggal Komara's working-time practice, because its own contracts and internal rules expressly set eight hours a day including Saturday. It then treated the claim as one for a benefit better than the law: Article 137 sets the ceiling of eight hours a day and forty-eight hours a week, and the demand for seven hours exceeded what the statute grants, following Award 162/09. That made the dispute an interest dispute. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 105][Para 106]

On the threshold for interest disputes the panel applied Article 277 and Prakas 305, article 9: where no single union is most representative, unions together representing a majority of the workforce may bring an interest dispute, as held in Awards 19/08 and 20/08 (Quinter Major Enterprise). Neither union here held a most-representative certificate, but their combined 609 members were 62.14 per cent of the roughly 980 workers, so the claim was admissible. [Para 111][Para 113][Para 119][Para 123][Para 124]

On the merits the panel weighed the equities and refused the claim: an eight-hour day is the general practice in most enterprises; a seven-hour Saturday would cost the employer an hour from each of about 980 workers, some 980 lost hours every Saturday; the workers themselves acknowledged the company's economic difficulty; and the old company truly had greater capacity than the new one. It therefore declined to order the practice restored. [Para 131][Para 132][Para 133][Para 134][Para 135]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Article 87 does not carry an old employer's working-time practice into a new company where the old contracts were terminated with severance paid and the new employer has lawfully issued and registered its own contracts and internal rules; those new instruments then govern. A demand for hours shorter than the statutory maximum is an interest claim, admissible only through a union or unions representing most of the workforce, and decided on equity rather than entitlement. [Para 96][Para 103][Para 104][Para 123]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that a party may not strike, lock out or bring a further interest dispute to the Council for one year after an award, and its remark that eight hours a day is the general practice in most enterprises, were contextual statements not necessary to the dismissal. [Para 122][Para 131]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The date on which the old company terminated its contracts is printed two ways: as 30 September 2009 in the list of termination documents, and as 30 June 2009 in the findings, with the new contracts dated 2 October 2009 and the latest ones 1 January 2010; the panel's reasoning rests on the contracts having been terminated, so the discrepancy matters and is not resolved in the text. The company's name is transliterated in at least three different forms across the header, the findings and the file list. The paragraph recording that the new internal rules were registered states expressly that the parties did not know the registration date. The severance figure is rendered only as a parenthesised 5 per cent, without stating what it is 5 per cent of. Names in the signature block are partly destroyed, one arbitrator's name being followed by a garbled OCR string, and worker and union representatives' names throughout the evidence list are recoverable only in corrupted form. [Para 15][Para 18][Para 79][Para 83][Para 84][Para 139]
