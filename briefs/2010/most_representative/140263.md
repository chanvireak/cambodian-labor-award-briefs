# Award No. 36/10 — Sour Bai Enterprise Co., Ltd (សៅរ៍បាយ) and Golden Crown (Cambodia) (ហ្គោលដេនក្រោន) (Arbitration Council, 5 May 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (You Sun Ty; Liv Sovanna; chair Ang Eng Thong) · Doc ID 140263

## 1. Facts
The two respondent companies, Sour Bai Enterprise Co., Ltd (សៅរ៍ បាយ អ៊ិន ធើ ប្រាយស៍ កូ អិលធីឌី, operating since 1997) and Golden Crown (Cambodia) (ហ្គោល ដេន ក្រោន, since 2004), are treated by the panel as a single employer at one site employing about 1,400 workers. [Para 31] [Para 32] [Para 33] The claimant is the factory union of the Cambodian Workers' Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា), with separate local unions for each company, and the panel found it most representative at both. [Para 2] [Para 34] [Para 35] [Para 36] Because orders were short and the factory relocated, the employer suspended the employment contracts of about 1,300 workers from 1 January to 18 April 2010 — a period of 3 months 18 days — paying each worker US$7 per month. [Para 38] [Para 54] [Para 55] Head-of-section staff, union leaders and warehouse workers, about 50 per cent of the workforce, were not suspended. [Para 39] Conciliation on 26 March 2010 settled nothing, and the employer's offer of a US$13 regular-attendance bonus on resumption was not accepted. [Para 2] [Para 12] [Para 13]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay the full 100 per cent of base wages and other benefits — living allowance of US$6, regular-attendance bonus of US$10 and seniority pay by years of service — to the workers whose contracts it suspended from 1 January to 18 April 2010 without authorisation from the labour inspector? [Para 37] [Para 44] [Para 58]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that during a suspension ordered without proper authorisation from the labour inspector the employer must still pay base wages in full together with the living allowance, the attendance bonus and seniority pay, and that the US$7 per month actually paid was inadequate. [Para 37] [Para 41]
**Employer (respondent):** that there were no buyer orders and the factory was relocated, so it suspended about 1,300 contracts from 1 January to 18 April 2010; that the $7 monthly payment was all it could afford; and that supervisors, union leaders and warehouse staff were excluded from the suspension. [Para 3] [Para 38] [Para 39] The workers did not contest the exclusion of those groups. [Para 40]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer is ordered to pay full base wages and other benefits, namely a living allowance of US$6, a regular-attendance bonus of US$10 and seniority pay according to each worker's years of service, for the suspension period from 1 January to 18 April 2010. [Para 58]

The panel began from Article 71 of the Labour Law, which permits suspension of an enterprise's activity only for serious economic or material difficulty and for no more than two months, and requires the suspension to be placed under the supervision of the labour inspector. [Para 45] [Para 46] Reading that provision with earlier awards 51/09 and 95/09, it held that a company must notify the labour inspector and obtain the inspector's examination and confirmation of the difficulty; the inspector must respond to the request. [Para 47] [Para 48] [Para 49] On Article 72 the panel held that suspension suspends the main obligations of the contract — the worker's duty to work and the employer's duty to pay wages — unless a contrary provision applies. [Para 50] Where the statutory procedure is followed with the inspector's authorisation, no wages are owed; where it is not, the employer must pay 100 per cent of wages even though no work is available, following awards 57/08 and 123/09. [Para 51] [Para 52] [Para 53]

On the facts, the panel found that the employer suspended about 1,300 contracts from 1 January to 18 April 2010 and paid each worker US$7 per month, and that it never received authorisation from the labour inspector. [Para 54] [Para 55] Applying Article 71 and its earlier interpretation, the panel held the employer obliged to pay full wages and benefits throughout the 3 months 18 days of suspension. [Para 55] It identified those benefits from the parties' agreed evidence — average base wages of US$55 to US$70 per month, a US$6 living allowance, a US$10 regular-attendance bonus and seniority pay — and ordered them paid for the whole period. [Para 42] [Para 43] [Para 56] [Para 57] [Para 58]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of employment contracts under Article 71 of the Labour Law discharges the employer's duty to pay wages only if the statutory route is actually taken: the enterprise must face serious economic or material difficulty, the labour inspector must be notified and must examine and confirm that difficulty, and the suspension must remain under the inspector's supervision. Where the employer suspends contracts without that authorisation, the suspension is unlawful and Article 72 does not relieve it of the wage obligation; it must pay 100 per cent of base wages plus the allowances and bonuses that formed part of the workers' regular remuneration, for the whole period of the suspension. [Para 47] [Para 50] [Para 52] [Para 55] [Para 57]

**Obiter dicta:** that the inspector has a duty to answer an employer's suspension request and state whether the asserted economic difficulty is genuine, and that a US$7 monthly payment during suspension does not satisfy the wage obligation. [Para 48] [Para 54]
