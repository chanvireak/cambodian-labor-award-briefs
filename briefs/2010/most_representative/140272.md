# Award No. 51/10 — Chin Nanh Garment Manufacturing Ltd (Arbitration Council, 17 June 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (In Sothy, An Vireak, Pen Bun Chhea presiding) · Doc ID 140272

## 1. Facts
Chin Nanh Garment Manufacturing Ltd, operating in Phnom Penh since November 1995 with about 1,147 workers, faced a three-point collective dispute filed on 26 May 2010 by the Free Trade Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia, whose factory branch claimed about 700 members [Para 52][Para 53][Para 55]. All three demands were unresolved at conciliation: an annual incentive bonus of USD 100 after five years' service, USD 80 after four and USD 60 after three, paid half at Khmer New Year and half at Pchum Ben; folding the USD 6 living-cost allowance into the basic wage; and one-year rather than six-month fixed-duration contracts [Para 2][Para 5][Para 7][Para 15]. At the hearing the workers withdrew the third demand, leaving the bonus and the allowance [Para 20][Para 21]. Both parties accepted that the company had paid the bonus in full from 2005 but cut it to 60% in 2009, and that the workers accepted 60% at Khmer New Year 2010 after a meeting [Para 57][Para 58][Para 60].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must restore the service-based incentive bonus of USD 100 / USD 80 / USD 60 in full from October 2010, because it had paid that bonus every year since 2005? [Para 57][Para 62]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must fold the USD 6 monthly living-cost allowance into the basic wage, so that overtime and other payments are calculated on a higher base? [Para 75][Para 124]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the bonus was paid unconditionally and without any agreement limiting it, so it became an established practice binding on the company; the company now has more work, more overtime and even subcontracts from other factories, and the federation's secretary-general was reported saying the economy had recovered, so 100% should be restored [Para 61][Para 62][Para 72][Para 74]. On the allowance, other factories (BN, Mithona, PCCS, Tri Nong Gal Commara) have folded it into the basic wage, and living costs have risen [Para 78][Para 79].
**Employer (respondent):** the economy is not yet recovered, order prices are low and deadlines short, which is why overtime is high; the company closed two branches, in 2006 and in March 2010, and it cannot restore 100% — it will stop the bonus altogether in future [Para 65][Para 66][Para 69][Para 71]. The USD 6 allowance is paid lawfully under Notification No. 032/08 and cannot be merged into the base wage [Para 75][Para 80].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused both the full restoration of the bonus and the merger of the allowance into the basic wage [Para 147][Para 148].

On Issue 1 the panel treated "past practice" as a legal concept, derived from Article 654(2) and (3) of the Labour Law and Article 4 of the Civil Code, and elaborated in Article 23 of the Civil Code, under which an unclear contract is read by the usage of the place where it was made [Para 88][Para 89][Para 90]. A past practice is an established, repeated and mutually accepted way of doing things that need not be written down, and it has been applied to annual leave, wage payment, bonuses and meal allowances in earlier awards [Para 86][Para 92][Para 93]. It found the bonus here clear, stable and accepted by both sides, so it was a past practice [Para 95][Para 96].

The panel then held that a past practice can lawfully be brought to an end in defined circumstances — where a party gives notice to end it, where the parties agree a replacement, or where the circumstances underlying it change — and that an employer may modify or abolish a bonus not required by law if it acts reasonably and in good faith, following Award No. 119/09 [Para 103][Para 105][Para 106]. Weighing the evidence, it accepted that order prices were low, deadlines short and the economy still fragile after the global crisis, and that a general newspaper remark by the GMAC secretary-general could not establish that this particular company had recovered [Para 111][Para 114][Para 116][Para 118]. Past payment, it held, does not create a continuing obligation, so the claim failed [Para 120][Para 122][Para 123].

On Issue 2 the panel first applied point 1 of Notification No. 032/08 of 17 April 2008, which grants the USD 6 monthly allowance and states expressly that it is not to be counted as basic wage [Para 126][Para 127][Para 128]. No other rule or agreement provided for merger [Para 129]. The claim therefore exceeded what the law gives and was a dispute of interests rather than of rights, which the panel hears only where the claimant union holds most-representative status [Para 131][Para 132][Para 133]. On that threshold, Prakas No. 305 Article 6 recognises such status for at least two years, and the certificate relied on had expired with no proof of renewal, so the union was not most-representative and the panel declined to consider the demand [Para 140][Para 142][Para 144][Para 146].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A bonus or allowance paid consistently and accepted by both sides becomes a past practice that the panel will recognise — but a past practice is not immutable: an employer may reduce or abolish a benefit the law does not require, provided it acts for a genuine reason and in good faith, and the panel will measure that claim against the employer's own trading conditions rather than against general economic commentary [Para 103][Para 105][Para 119]. Where the demand goes beyond a statutory entitlement it becomes an interests dispute, which the panel will not hear unless the claimant union still holds a subsisting most-representative certificate [Para 133][Para 144].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the rise and fall of the workforce is normal and that a general press statement cannot be evidence about one company's finances are observations wider than the order required [Para 117][Para 118].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company's name is printed inconsistently ("ចិន ណាន់", "ចិន ល រាន់", "ចិន ណាន់ ហ្គាម៉ីន មែន ព្យួហ្វាក់"), and one passage calls the panel the "National Arbitration Council" [Para 54][Para 27][Para 147]. Two of the three arbitrators' names differ between the header and the signature block, and the employer-appointed member's name is spelled with a different medial consonant in each place [Para 1][Para 150]. The reasons cite "មាត្រា ៦៥៤" of the Labour Law, a number beyond that law's range, apparently meaning Article 65; the point is reported as printed [Para 88]. Stray years ("2016", "2017") are inserted into the paragraph on the allowance demand, and the passage quoting Article 2 of the Labour Law breaks off into fragments [Para 76][Para 97][Para 98]. The workers' headcount appears as about 1,147 in the findings and about 700 union members, with the most-representative certificate's validity dates never printed [Para 53][Para 55][Para 142].
