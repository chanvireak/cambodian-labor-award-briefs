# Award No. 28/10 — New Arch Garment (Arbitration Council, 23 April 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party; the chair's name is printed only as "Pen Bun", its final element lost to OCR) · Doc ID 140258

## 1. Facts
New Arch Garment, registered on 22 August 2008 in Ang Snuol district, Kandal province, employs about 2,800 workers on normal hours of 7:00 to 16:00, Monday to Saturday, eight hours a day, with Sunday as the weekly rest day. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 66] The sewing workers' union at the factory, registered on 18 December 2001 with its second-term leadership recognised by the Ministry on 28 July 2009, complained to the provincial labour department on 1 March 2010; conciliation the same day failed, and the file came to the Council by non-conciliation report of 16 March 2010. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 29][Para 37][Para 38]

At the hearing of 29 March 2010 the workers withdrew points 4 and 5, leaving three demands: a meal allowance, parity for the Building C production line, and a monthly subsidy for skilled workers. [Para 24][Para 25] The employer's head of administration, Mr B, attended without the written authorization of the director even after the panel gave him three days to produce it, so the panel proceeded on the footing that the employer party was absent. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 75][Para 76][Para 77]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay 2,000 riel, or provide a free meal, each time workers voluntarily work on public holidays and Sundays and on overtime from 4:00 to 8:00 in the evening? [Para 45][Para 78]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must organise the Building C production line with the same quotas and bonuses as Buildings A and B, since all three belong to one company? [Para 54][Para 119]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay a subsidy of USD 10 a month, or what it can afford, to workers with practical skills? [Para 62][Para 138]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** 1,000 or 1,500 riel no longer buys a meal, so workers who volunteer for holiday, Sunday or 4:00–8:00 overtime should receive 2,000 riel, and at the hearing they reduced this to a free meal; Building C should enjoy what A and B enjoy because it is the same company; skilled workers deserve USD 10 as encouragement. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 53][Para 61][Para 64]
**Employer (respondent):** it pays 1,000 riel for holiday and Sunday work and 1,500 riel for overtime from 4:00 to 8:00, and it applies its existing policy at Building C, where wages already follow the Labour Law. [Para 5][Para 7][Para 9][Para 47][Para 97]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel refused a free meal for work on public holidays, but ordered the employer to provide a free meal to workers who volunteer for overtime from 4:00 to 8:00 in the evening and for work on Sundays; the Building C and USD 10 subsidy demands were dismissed. [Para 152][Para 153][Para 154][Para 155][Para 156]

On representation the panel applied Prakar 19 of Prakas No. 099, requiring written authorization from the party, following Awards No. 71/08 and No. 161/09, and held that Mr B was not a lawful representative, which left the employer party absent. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 71][Para 73][Para 76] On Issue 1 it first separated holiday work from overtime: Article 137 of the Labour Law caps normal hours at eight a day and 48 a week, and Prakar 2 of Prakas No. 10 of 4 February 1999 lets the employer arrange holiday work by agreement while Prakar 4 gives compensation equal to one normal day's wage. [Para 79][Para 83][Para 84] Following Awards No. 115/08 and No. 109/09 it held that work on a public holiday is not overtime, so no meal is owed for it; and since the company in fact pays 1,000 riel for holiday work, Article 13 preserves that more favourable practice. [Para 90][Para 91][Para 92][Para 98][Para 100][Para 101] For overtime it relied on point 4 of Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000, which entitles a worker who volunteers for overtime to 1,000 riel or a free meal, and on Awards No. 47/07, No. 115/08 and No. 117/08, and found the free-meal demand reasonable for 4:00–8:00 overtime and Sunday work. [Para 107][Para 108][Para 112][Para 113][Para 115][Para 116][Para 117]

On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 22 of the Civil Code: the agreement is the law of the parties and can be changed only by consent. [Para 120][Para 122][Para 124] The workers had accepted the Building C terms when they agreed to work there, the wages paid complied with the law, and they had shown no concrete evidence that comparable work was treated differently, so the demand failed. [Para 128][Para 130][Para 131][Para 134][Para 135][Para 137]

On Issue 3 the panel found no law or agreement requiring the subsidy, and held that a demand exceeding what the law gives is an interest dispute which it will not consider unless the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise, since its award would replace the collective agreement for a year under Prakar 43 of Prakas No. 099; this union was not. [Para 140][Para 141][Para 142][Para 143][Para 146][Para 147][Para 149][Para 152]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim for a benefit the Labour Law does not require is an interest dispute, and a panel will decline to hear it where the union bringing it is not the most representative in the enterprise, because an award on it would become a binding collective agreement for one year. A claim anchored in a Prakas or a Ministry notification, by contrast, is decided on the instrument itself. Separately, a person appearing without written authorization from the party is not a lawful representative, and the party is treated as absent. [Para 70][Para 76][Para 108][Para 143][Para 146]

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that a union's most-representative status carries the legal capacity to conclude collective agreements and to bring interest disputes is general, and is not what decided the meal claim. [Para 146]
