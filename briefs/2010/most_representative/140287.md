# Award No. 70/10 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន មេន បរាត ធេន (Menhattan Garment) (Arbitration Council, 9 August 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel: សេង វូច ហ៊ុន (employer-nominated), អាន វីរៈ (worker-nominated), អាំង អេង ថុង (chair) · Doc ID 140287

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន មេន បរាត ធេន, printed elsewhere as មេនហាតធេន, is a garment factory at Ampil, Kampong Siem district, Kampong Cham, employing about 3,600 workers. [Para 1][Para 22] Its only union, សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ នៃ រោងចក្រ កាត់ដេរ មេនហាតធេន, has about 1,050 members and did not yet hold a most-representative certificate, which it said it was applying for. [Para 23][Para 24] Both sides accepted that roughly 90 per cent of the workforce is on fixed-term contracts, that the first contract lasts two months and later renewals six months, and that a 5 per cent severance payment is made at each expiry before a new six-month contract starts the next day. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 36] The union filed a single demand on 16 June 2010 asking that all fixed-term contracts be converted; conciliation failed on that one point. [Para 9][Para 10]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must convert all fixed-term employment contracts into indefinite-term contracts? [Para 25]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), represented by សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ នៃ រោងចក្រ កាត់ដេរ មេនហាតធេន:** many workers have served far longer than two years on renewed six-month contracts and the law requires conversion; a signature list filed on 22 July 2010 identified 2,235 claimants, of whom 511 had two years' service or more and 1,724 less. [Para 32][Para 34][Para 38][Para 39]
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន មេន បរាត ធេន:** two contract types are used and renewal every six months dates from 2001 or 2002; workers prefer fixed-term contracts because each expiry brings 5 per cent severance and the freedom to move on; orders are unstable, so converting everyone to indefinite contracts would leave the company unable to pay wages; and it objected to every name whose start date precedes 1 January 2010 because severance had already been settled. [Para 25][Para 29][Para 30][Para 40]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to convert the fixed-term contracts of the 511 workers whose successive contracts total more than two years into indefinite-term contracts, and the claim was refused for the 1,724 workers with less than two years. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74][Para 75]

On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 67 §2, under which a fixed-term contract may not run beyond two years and may be renewed only so long as each renewal keeps within that maximum, and Article 73(a) §5 on notice, following Awards 10/03 (Zaksintich), 36/06, 57/06 and 155/09. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 44][Para 47][Para 48] It held that payment of 5 per cent severance at each expiry does not sever the employment relationship and cannot be used to restart the two-year clock, because the same severance is owed in any case on final termination, calculated over all the successive contracts combined. [Para 62][Para 63] It then applied Article 5 of the 1988 Law on Contracts, under which a contract contrary to law is void, to hold that the 2006 agreement prescribing two-month then six-month contracts is void for those workers whose total service exceeds two years. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 71]

The panel rejected the employer's two objections: it had not disputed the numbers, only the reasons, so the count of 2,235 stood; and the employer retains the right to terminate an indefinite-term contract lawfully if orders fall. [Para 51][Para 66]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive renewals of a fixed-term contract become an indefinite-term contract once their combined duration passes two years; the payment of 5 per cent severance at each expiry is a statutory entitlement owed in any event and does not break the employment relationship or reset the two-year period, and a collective practice or agreement that would keep a worker on rolling short contracts beyond that limit is void as contrary to the Labour Law. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 71]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the company would still be free to terminate indefinite-term contracts, provided it did so in accordance with the Labour Law — an answer to the employer's solvency concern rather than a condition of the order. [Para 66]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's name is printed in at least three Khmer variants across the header, the union's name and the evidence list, and the union's own name is inconsistent between the header and the findings. [Para 1][Para 15][Para 23] One sentence in the findings refers to "90 per cent of the total" with a garbled word where the workforce noun should be. [Para 26] Several exhibit numbers in the evidence list are missing or corrupted, and the list of union documents begins mid-word. [Para 17][Para 18] The quoted text of Article 67 §2 in one place says a fixed-term contract cannot exceed two years and in another that renewals must each stay within two years, and the award does not distinguish the two readings. [Para 41][Para 42] The award is non-binding, though it disposes of a pure rights claim. [Para 76]
