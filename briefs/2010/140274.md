# Award No. 53/10 — Sith Garment (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 29 June 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ri, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Run Saray, chair) · Doc ID 140274

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ិដស៍ ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) — Sith Garment (Cambodia) — employed 980 workers in Stung Meanchey, Phnom Penh, where the workers formed a union by election on 21 October 2009 and notified the company of the elected leadership on 23 October 2009; the union had about 480 members. [Para 1][Para 49][Para 50][Para 53] The four officers — president, vice-president, secretary and treasurer — resigned between 28 and 30 November 2009 after a former administration officer of the company offered the president USD 1,300 and each of the other three USD 650. [Para 59][Para 61][Para 76][Para 81] Of the two conciliation issues, only the first was decided: at the hearing the worker party withdrew the second, which asked the company to recognise the union and stop discriminating against it. [Para 4][Para 18]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must take back the four union officers as president, vice-president, secretary and treasurer and pay their wages from the date of termination to the date of reinstatement? [Para 51][Para 92]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា and its factory branch:** the company used money to induce the resignations, then refused to renew the contracts of the union activists on its list while renewing the one activist who agreed to leave the union — a pattern proving an intention to eliminate the union. [Para 55][Para 84][Para 86][Para 87][Para 93]
**Employer (respondent), Sith Garment (Cambodia):** the four left of their own free will for their own separate reasons, without coercion or threat, and the union was not even registered until 19 December 2009, after they had gone; staff turnover is ordinary and there was no discrimination; it could not produce attendance records because they were company confidential. [Para 82][Para 89][Para 91][Para 94]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the company was ordered to take back the four officers in their respective posts and to pay their wages from the date of termination to the date of reinstatement, and the four were ordered to repay the company USD 1,300 and USD 650 each. [Para 118][Para 120][Para 121]

The panel framed the question as whether the company discriminated against the union, and whether accepting money in exchange for resigning and signing resignation letters barred the officers from claiming reinstatement. [Para 95] Article 266 guarantees workers the right to form a union without any distinction and without prior authorisation, and Article 280 prohibits all acts of interference, expressly including the use of financial or other means to place worker organisations under the employer's control. [Para 95][Para 100][Para 102] On the facts the panel found that the company's administration had persuaded the four officers to resign for USD 1,300 to the president and USD 650 to each of the others once the elected leadership had been notified, and that activists on the union's list were not renewed at contract expiry while the one activist who resigned from the union was renewed. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 105] Taken together these acts showed an intention that the union should not exist in the factory, contrary to the law on union freedom. [Para 106][Para 107]

The panel then asked whether the agreement was valid even though the resignations were voluntary in form. Article 13(1) makes the Labour Law's provisions public order, so any rule arising from a unilateral decision, contract or convention that does not respect them is null by operation of law. [Para 108][Para 109][Para 110] The right to form a union is essential to the balance of power between workers and employers and is guaranteed by the Labour Law and the Constitution; an employer act, even a peaceful one, is prohibited if its purpose is to eliminate union freedom, and paying officers more than their statutory severance in exchange for a resignation that touches union activity is itself improper, so the agreement was void. [Para 113][Para 115][Para 116][Para 117]

The arbitrator appointed by the employer, Ouk Ri, dissented, holding that Article 73(4) lets a worker terminate a fixed-duration contract early, that the four resigned of their own will without coercion, that Articles 12 and 279 protect workers who are dismissed, disciplined or whose contracts are severed, not workers who resign, and that the company neither created nor controlled the union, so Article 280 was not engaged; he would have dismissed the claim. [Para 135][Para 136][Para 142][Para 144][Para 146][Para 147] The award is not binding and became final eight days after notification absent an objection to the Minister. [Para 123]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that pays union officers to resign cannot rely on the resulting agreement: because Article 13(1) of the Labour Law is public order, an arrangement that defeats the Article 266 right to organise and the Article 280 prohibition on interference is null, and the absence of overt coercion does not save it. The proper remedy is reinstatement of the officers with wages from termination to return, with the money repaid to the employer. [Para 109][Para 110][Para 117][Para 120][Para 121]

**Obiter dicta:** the general propositions that any employer act, however peaceful, is prohibited when aimed at eliminating union freedom, and that payment above statutory severance in exchange for a union-related resignation is itself improper, were not necessary once the agreement was held void under Article 13(1). The observations on the right to organise as a constitutional guarantee are likewise general. [Para 113][Para 115][Para 116]
