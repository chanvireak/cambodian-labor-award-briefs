# Order No. 123/10 — Joon Tich Tha Yal (ជូន តិច ថា យល៍), Sen Sok, Phnom Penh (Arbitration Council, 13 December 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា presiding; អ៊ឹង សុទ្ធី, employer nominee, dissenting; លីវ សុវណ្ណា, worker nominee) · Doc ID 140326

## 1. Facts
Joon Tich Tha Yal (ជូន តិច ថា យល៍), a garment factory at Boeung 100 Khnong village, Teuk Thla commune, Sen Sok district, Phnom Penh, has operated since 1994 and employs about 4,200 workers under three unions; the claimant was the factory branch of the Workers' Rights Union (សហជីព សិទ្ធិ ប្រយោជន៍ កម្មករ), with about 1,000 members, some 110 of them in Building D. [Para 1][Para 24][Para 25][Para 26][Para 27][Para 28] The demand, which conciliation on 28 October 2010 could not settle, was that the company dismiss Ms I, the head of Building D, who has worked there since 1994 and supervises about 1,000 day- and night-shift workers. [Para 2][Para 29][Para 30][Para 31] On 25 October 2010 workers in Building D stopped work at 2:40 in the afternoon over her conduct and returned at 8:30 that night after the company agreed to move her elsewhere temporarily. [Para 40][Para 41]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the workers have the right to demand that the company dismiss or transfer Ms I, the head of Building D, out of her post? [Para 56][Para 58]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — សហជីព សិទ្ធិ ប្រយោជន៍ កម្មករ:** Ms I habitually used abusive and insulting language towards workers, two or three times a week, and never made their work easier; on 23 October 2010, when a worker's motorcycle was stolen outside the factory and a representative telephoned her, she said she knew nothing, threw the worker's card under the table and shouted insults; on 18 October 2010 she searched three women workers over suspected theft of garments, making one undress to her underwear in a warehouse screened only by cloth, a search the company later admitted was improper; the whole of Building D is dissatisfied with her. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 34][Para 35][Para 36][Para 38][Para 39][Para 44][Para 45][Para 46]
**Employer (respondent) — Joon Tich Tha Yal:** the company cannot dismiss her because she has managed production well, has caught many workers stealing garments and keeps the line fully employed; moving her to another section is difficult because she may clash with another team; her conduct was indeed improper and the company acknowledges it, but it asks that she be given one last chance and says it will dismiss her if the problem recurs; a survey of Building D workers found some satisfied and some not, though the company never produced that survey to the panel by the 26 November 2010 deadline. [Para 3][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 55][Para 57]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to dismiss Ms I or transfer her out of Building D. [Para 71][Para 72]
The panel framed the question as whether workers may demand the removal of another employee, and answered it from Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 1 of Decree No. 38 on contracts and non-contractual liability of 28 October 1988: a contract binds only its parties, so only the employer and the worker concerned may end the employment contract, and a third party has no right to dissolve it. [Para 58][Para 60][Para 61][Para 62] It followed Order 116/10-Viet Hic and a line of earlier orders, including 14/03, 17/03 and 18/03-Ho Hing, 87/04-Noble, 116/07-Grassan, 54/08 and 129/09, for the rule that workers may not order or demand the dismissal or transfer of any other worker unless that person is so dangerous that she cannot be kept in the enterprise, because keeping her would create genuine insecurity at work. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 64][Para 65]
The panel then treated the exception as engaged: it found Ms I had repeatedly used insulting and abusive language, threw a worker's card under the table when a worker came to ask for help, and strip-searched a woman worker down to her underwear in a warehouse with no proper room and only cloth for screening. [Para 66][Para 68] It held that conduct to be improper and immoral, affecting workers' dignity, honour, safety, psychological wellbeing and privacy, and requiring the employer to take immediate measures to keep the workplace orderly and safe; it found her conduct had in fact caused psychological insecurity among all workers in Building D. [Para 69][Para 70] The order is not binding and becomes final eight days after notification if no party objects to the Minister. [Para 73]
The employer nominee, arbitrator អ៊ឹង សុទ្ធី, appended a dissent under Article 37 of Prakas 099: he accepted the fault was real but considered the employer should first apply the disciplinary steps in Prakas 11 and 12 of the internal rules before dismissal or transfer out of Building D. [Para 74][Para 76][Para 78]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Only the parties to an employment contract may terminate it, so workers cannot in principle demand the dismissal or transfer of another employee; but where that employee's proven conduct — here habitual abuse and a strip-search of a woman worker — has destroyed the psychological security of the workplace, the panel will order the employer to remove her from the building, the exception being the dangerousness of the person kept, not the inconvenience of replacing her. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 69][Para 70]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted the employer's claim that a survey showed some Building D workers were content with Ms I's leadership and that she had caught many garment thieves, and that the survey was never produced by the deadline it set — findings not needed once the dangerousness exception was made out. [Para 49][Para 53][Para 55]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award names the supervisor in full throughout, and she is a supervisory employee rather than a claimant, so she is given here as "Ms I"; the source also records that the parties corrected her name at the hearing and that three spellings of it appear in the text. [Para 29][Para 76] The employer's corporate name appears only in Khmer, with no Latin form, and the English rendering in the title is a transliteration. [Para 1] The procedural summary states the complaint was lodged by the company by telephone on 28 October 2010 about the workers' demand, which reverses the parties to the dispute. [Para 9] The operative paragraph is printed twice and the second copy is corrupt, and the hearing left no record of the panel's reconciliation attempt beyond the note that it failed. [Para 14][Para 72] The dissent is signed with a variant spelling of the employer nominee's name that differs from the header, and the panel's question in the reasons opens with a stray "លោក John" before the supervisor's name. [Para 1][Para 58][Para 74]
