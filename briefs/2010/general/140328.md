# Order No. 124/10 — Joon Tich Tha Yal (ជូន តិច ថា យល៍), Sen Sok, Phnom Penh (Arbitration Council, 6 December 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា presiding; អ៊ីង សុទ្ធី, employer nominee; អាន វីរៈ, worker nominee) · Doc ID 140328

## 1. Facts
Joon Tich Tha Yal (ជូន តិច ថា យល៍), a garment factory at Boeung 100 Khnong village, Teuk Thla commune, Sen Sok district, Phnom Penh, has operated since 1994 and employs about 4,500 workers under three unions; the claimant was the factory branch of the Free Trade Union of Workers of the Kingdom of Cambodia (សហជីពសេរីកម្មករ នៃ ព្រះរាជាណាចក្រកម្ពុជា), which claimed more than 2,000 members, about 110 of them among the roughly 300 workers of the cutting section. [Para 1][Para 38][Para 39][Para 40][Para 42][Para 43][Para 44] Conciliation on 28 October 2010 left one demand unsettled: that the company dismiss Mr W, the long-serving head of the cutting section. [Para 2][Para 9][Para 10][Para 11] The complaint had been lodged with the Labour Disputes Department by telephone from the company's side on 28 October 2010. [Para 9]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the workers have the right to demand that the company dismiss Mr W, the head of the cutting section, from his employment? [Para 71][Para 91]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — សហជីពសេរីកម្មករ នៃ រោងចក្រ កាត់ដេរ មិថុនា:** Mr W has repeatedly manipulated lot prices — cutting the rate for a lot priced at USD 0.015 since 1999 down to USD 0.012 in May 2010, and mis-cutting again in September 2010 so that workers learned of it only when wages were paid in October — and they protested in May, June and October; he manages by emotion, scolds without hearing the reason, gives no encouragement, does not replace broken equipment, and answers only when a union or worker representative intervenes; under the internal rules a serious fault warrants dismissal. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 59][Para 60][Para 61]
**Employer (respondent) — Joon Tich Tha Yal:** Mr W cannot be dismissed; he has headed the cutting section since 2005 and reports in a technically precise way that keeps production high; the price cut was a misunderstanding, not intent — the USD 0.012 rate applied where no colour selection or edging was needed, while USD 0.015 was always paid in practice — and the company has already repaid every worker the shortfall; he took no personal benefit; the company will instruct and educate him and will dismiss him only if the confusion recurs. [Para 54][Para 56][Para 57][Para 58][Para 63][Para 64][Para 65][Para 66][Para 67][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70][Para 81]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the demand that the employer remove Mr W from his employment. [Para 90][Para 91]
The panel put the question as whether workers may demand the dismissal of another employee, and answered it from Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 1 of Decree No. 38 on contracts: an employment contract binds its parties, so only the employer and the worker concerned may end it, and a third party has no power to dissolve it. [Para 71][Para 73][Para 74][Para 75] It followed Order 116/10-Viet Hic and the earlier line of 14/03, 17/03 and 18/03-Ho Hing, 87/04-Noble, 116/07-Grassan and 54/08, under which workers may not order or demand the transfer or dismissal of anyone unless they can prove that person is dangerous and cannot be kept in the enterprise. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 78]
It found the price-cutting established, but accepted that the employer had shown it was a misunderstanding rather than intent and had repaid the full shortfall to the workers. [Para 79] It found no worker had suffered harm from his conduct and that his conduct had not caused damage requiring the company to dismiss him. [Para 84][Para 85] It nevertheless held that his habit of scolding team leaders over small faults without asking the cause, and his neglect of broken equipment, could damage the working relationship between him and the workers under his direct control, and that the employer ought to take corrective measures to improve relations at the workplace. [Para 86][Para 88][Para 89] On that basis it refused the dismissal claim and left the employer's own undertaking — to instruct him and dismiss him if the confusion recurred — to stand. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 90][Para 91] The order is not binding and becomes final eight days after notification if no party objects to the Minister. [Para 92]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker or union may not demand that the employer dismiss or transfer another employee, because only the parties to that employee's contract can end it under Article 65 and Article 1 of Decree No. 38; the panel will act only where the worker proves the person is dangerous and cannot be kept in the enterprise, and misconduct that is corrected, compensated and does not harm any worker falls short of that threshold even when it is plainly improper. [Para 75][Para 77][Para 84][Para 89]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that the employer should educate and guide Mr W rather than dismiss him, and its record of the employer's promise to do so, are recommendations that did not form part of the order. [Para 69][Para 80][Para 89]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company is named "ជូន តិច ថា យល៍" in the parties block but "ជូន តិច ថា យ ល័" in the case name, and two paragraphs of the employer's argument refer to it as "ក្រុមហ៊ុន Li", which appears to be a carry-over from another file. [Para 1][Para 9][Para 50] The Ministry's report is numbered twice in one line, "លេខ ២០៤៣ លេខ ១០៤៣", and the panel's reconciliation effort is printed as "០១៧" demands where one was outstanding. [Para 14][Para 34] Decree No. 38 is dated "28 July October 1988", the month being printed twice. [Para 73] The operative paragraph is printed twice with words missing between the copies, and the foot signature block is heavily overlaid with stray Latin OCR fragments. [Para 91][Para 93] The supervisor whose removal was sought is an employee of the company exercising managerial authority and is printed in Latin throughout; he is given here as "Mr W", and the eighteen worker representatives listed in the header are not reproduced. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 45]
