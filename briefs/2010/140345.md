# Award No. 140/10 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន តាក ហ្វាត ហ្គាមិន (ខាំបូឌា) អែល ធី ឌី (Tark Fhat Garmen (Cambodia) Ltd) (Arbitration Council, 17 January 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia (ក្រុមប្រឹក្សាអាជ្ញាកណ្តាល) · 3 arbitrators — មាំ សំបូរ ណា (employer-appointed), អាន វីរៈ (worker-appointed), chair គង់ ផល្លៈ · Doc ID 140345

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន តាក ហ្វាត ហ្គាមិន (ខាំបូឌា) អែល ធី ឌី (Tark Fhat Garmen (Cambodia) Ltd) employs about 1,020 workers and hosts seven unions; the claimant សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ នៃ ព្រះរាជាណាចក្រ កម្ពុជា (Free Workers' Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia), through its factory branch of roughly 520 members, filed on behalf of 20 workers on 17 November 2010 [Para 16][Para 45][Para 47]. Conciliation failed on four of five demands and reached the Council on 15 December 2010; the hearing was held on 29 December 2010 [Para 17][Para 18][Para 15]. The dispute concerns two days of suspended work in June 2010, a wage deduction from the union vice-president, maternity pay for two workers, overtime for three union leaders, and — raised for the first time at the hearing — wages for attending that hearing [Para 22][Para 108].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay 50 per cent of base wage for the two days of suspended work in June 2010? [Para 49]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must repay the half-day wage and USD 5 attendance bonus deducted from the union vice-president for attending the 9 June 2010 conciliation? [Para 59]
3. (Issue 3) Whether two maternity claimants are entitled to half wages, allowances and benefits across 90 days of maternity leave? [Para 70]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must provide overtime work to three union leaders? [Para 82]
5. (Additional issue) Whether the panel may decide, and must order, wages for the union representative's attendance at the 29 December 2010 hearing? [Para 193]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ:** the two-day suspension was imposed by the company alone, without the workers' agreement and without notice to or authorisation from the labour inspector, so 2,500 riel a day is below what the law requires; the union vice-president was invited by the Ministry and should not have lost pay or bonus for attending; the three leaders had overtime before and were denied it only recently [Para 56][Para 57][Para 86].
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន តាក ហ្វាត ហ្គាមិន (ខាំបូឌា) អែល ធី ឌី:** the 2,500 riel payment follows a 2005 agreement with worker representatives covering short suspensions of one or two days; overtime is allocated by team leaders according to production needs and work quality, and the three workers' output had fallen [Para 52][Para 104].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must pay 50 per cent of base wage for the two days without work in June 2010 to the 11 listed workers, repay the wage and USD 5 bonus deducted on 9 June 2010, and pay half wages, allowances and benefits for 90 days of maternity leave to the two claimants; the overtime demand is dismissed, though overtime must resume once output recovers [Para 202][Para 203][Para 204][Para 205][Para 206].
On Issue 1 the panel applied Articles 71(11), 72 and 13 of the Labour Law: a suspension of activity must sit under the labour inspector's supervision, an employer owes no wages during an authorised suspension unless a collective agreement provides otherwise, but owes 100 per cent where the suspension was not authorised [Para 116][Para 122]. The company gave no notice and held no authorisation, so the suspension was unlawful and full wages were due; because the workers claimed only 50 per cent, that is what was ordered [Para 123][Para 124][Para 133][Para 134]. The 2005 agreement was declared void under Article 13 as contrary to the Labour Law [Para 128][Para 129].
On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 306 of the Labour Law and Article 10 of Prakas No. 305: a negotiating representative may not have wages, bonuses or other benefits cut while attending conciliation at the Ministry's invitation [Para 135][Para 138]. The vice-president held an invitation addressed to union leaders, attended with three permitted workers, and the employer did not contest the facts [Para 150][Para 145][Para 64].
On Issue 3 the panel applied Articles 182 and 183: women are entitled to 90 days' maternity leave and to half pay including allowances, after one year of service, and a collective agreement to the contrary is void [Para 157][Para 158][Para 161]. Both claimants had approved requests and were among the 87 workers already covered by an earlier award, so the same half-pay order was made [Para 165][Para 166].
On Issue 4 the panel applied Article 4 of Prakas No. 80, which makes overtime voluntary, and Article 2 of the Labour Law, which confines managerial prerogative to lawful and reasonable exercise [Para 171][Para 172][Para 180]. Output had genuinely fallen, so refusing overtime was reasonable, but the three must be allowed overtime again when demand and quality return [Para 188][Para 192].
On the additional issue the panel applied Article 312(a) khann 1 and Article 33 of Prakas No. 099: jurisdiction is confined to the non-conciliation record and its direct consequences [Para 194][Para 195]. The deduction flowed directly from the representative's role in this case, so jurisdiction existed and the wages were ordered [Para 198][Para 200][Para 201].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that suspends work without notifying the labour inspector and obtaining authorisation suspends unlawfully and owes full wages for the suspended days, and any agreement fixing a flat payment for short suspensions is void as contrary to the Labour Law; separately, a union representative attending conciliation or a hearing on the Ministry's or Council's invitation cannot have pay or bonuses deducted for that attendance.

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that it agreed with prior awards making overtime voluntary and managerial discretion subject to reasonableness, while noting that in this case neither party disputed that principle; it also recorded that no union held most-representative status in the enterprise [Para 174][Para 48].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The conciliation date is printed two ways: 01 November 2010 in the procedural section and 01 December 2010 in the documents list and the Ministry report. The collective agreement underpinning the 2,500 riel payment is dated 30 June 2005 in the parties' positions and 30 June 2010 in the reasons. The respondent is abbreviated "ក្រុមហ៊ុន C" in one reasoning passage although named in full everywhere else, and a former head of government's name appears inside the Issue 4 reasoning, evidently OCR contamination. The hearing demand is said not to appear in the non-conciliation record, though the panel took jurisdiction over it. Individual workers are named in full in the source, including a list of 11; they appear here by role or initial only, and the signature block is partly OCR noise.
