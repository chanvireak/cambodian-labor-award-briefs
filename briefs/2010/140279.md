# Award No. 59/10 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន តាក ហ្វាត ហ្គាមិន (ខាំបូឌា) អែល ធី ឌី (Arbitration Council, 16 July 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Li Tai Seng, appointed by the employer; An Virak, appointed by the workers; Kong Phallha, chair) · Doc ID 140279

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន តាក ហ្វាត ហ្គាមិន (ខាំបូឌា) អែល ធី ឌី — Tack Fat Garment (Cambodia) Ltd — has operated since 1995 at Prek Ta Nu, Chak Angre Leu, Meanchey, Phnom Penh, and now employs about 1,137 workers in seven unions. [Para 24][Para 36][Para 37][Para 38] The claimant is សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ នៃ រោងចក្រ កាត់ដេរ តាក ហ្វាត ហ្គាមិន. [Para 39] Five demands went through failed conciliation on 9 June 2010; the union withdrew Issue 3 at the hearing of 29 June 2010, so the panel decided Issues 1, 2, 4 and 5. [Para 18][Para 22][Para 23] One of those demands was for the maternity benefits and five days of unpaid April wages owed to a packing-section worker, Ms C, who had worked at the factory since 2001. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 58][Para 89]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must let the union vice-president enter the factory to settle members' problems without showing a written authorisation from those members? [Para 40][Para 81]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must stop suspending work, paying half wages, or forcing annual leave in order to push workers into resigning? [Para 47][Para 84]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the company must pay Ms C half wages and benefits for 90 days of maternity leave, and the five days of April wages it never paid her? [Para 50][Para 89]
4. (Issue 5) Whether the company must abandon its plan to move 600 sewing workers from Building 5 to Building 3? [Para 59][Para 107]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ នៃ រោងចក្រ កាត់ដេរ តាក ហ្វាត ហ្គាមិន:** the union president has been refused entry to handle members' grievances even though the company knows each case; the repeated moves between buildings, half-pay suspensions and forced annual leave are devices to make workers accept USD 150 and resign; and Ms C, properly authorised for maternity leave, was paid neither her maternity benefit nor her five days of April work. [Para 2][Para 5][Para 66][Para 71][Para 52]
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន តាក ហ្វាត ហ្គាមិន:** it never barred the vice-president and no longer asks for written authorisations since Award No. 42/10; suspension is an employer right when obstacles arise; no transfer from Building 5 to Building 3 has yet been decided, and the plan responds to a broken lift and to keeping the production chain together; Ms C's payment waits on the outcome for the 87 packing workers. [Para 3][Para 41][Para 49][Para 55][Para 61][Para 77]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to pay Ms C half her wages and allowances for 90 days of maternity leave and the wages for the five days she worked in April 2010, and both parties were ordered to keep their existing practice and behave properly when settling members' problems; the suspension, transfer and annual-leave claims were declined. [Para 115][Para 117][Para 118][Para 119]

On Issue 1 the panel found no live dispute: the company stated that it neither barred the vice-president nor demanded members' written authorisations after Award No. 42/10, and both sides accepted that grievance discussions should be conducted courteously; it therefore ordered the parties to continue their practice rather than impose a new duty. [Para 81][Para 82][Para 83][Para 115]

On Issues 2 and 5 the panel held that both claims were aimed at conduct the company might adopt in future: no suspension had yet injured anyone's rights, and the company had held a meeting on 31 May 2010 about a possible move of 600 workers but had taken no decision and could not say when it would. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 85][Para 108][Para 112] The Council is constituted to settle existing labour disputes and not problems that have not yet arisen, so it declined jurisdiction over both. [Para 86][Para 87][Para 88][Para 109][Para 111][Para 114]

On Issue 4 the company did not contest that it had signed Ms C's maternity-leave request, and both sides agreed she was one of the 87 packing workers reinstated under Award No. 42/10, so she remained an employee entitled to the Labour Law's protections. [Para 51][Para 53][Para 54][Para 56][Para 89][Para 90] Articles 182 and 183 give a woman with at least one year of service 90 days of maternity leave on half wages and half allowances, and the panel found she had worked from 2001 to May 2010, so the benefit was owed. [Para 91][Para 92][Para 95][Para 96][Para 99] Article 102 defines wages as remuneration for work actually done, so the five days she worked in April 2010 had to be paid as well. [Para 101][Para 102][Para 105][Para 106]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The Council will not decide a demand directed at conduct the employer has not yet adopted: a possible transfer of 600 workers discussed but never decided, or a feared suspension, leaves rights untouched and falls outside the panel's jurisdiction, which is confined to existing disputes. By contrast, where the employer has signed a maternity-leave request and the worker has a year's service, Articles 182 and 183 require half wages and half allowances for 90 days, and Article 102 requires the wages for days actually worked, whatever dispute is pending about other workers in the same section. [Para 87][Para 96][Para 105][Para 112]

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that both parties should maintain courteous conduct when discussing workers' problems, and the remark that the earlier award on the 87 packing workers had already held their dismissal unlawful, were background rather than operative reasons. [Para 56][Para 83]

## 6. Source defects disclosed

- Issue 3 is missing from the findings and the decision, and only the withdrawal recorded at [Para 22] explains the gap between the five demands and the four issues decided.
- The decision section at [Para 117] is interleaved: the words "ចំណុច វិវាទ ទី៤" appear in the middle of the sentence describing the maternity order.
- The employer-appointed arbitrator's signature line is reduced to Latin fragments ("Am. Yheed"), and the chair's to "wounce on Groldwe" [Para 121].
- The workforce is given as about 1,137 at [Para 37], and the packing section is put at 87 workers at [Para 51] and [Para 55] without saying how many were dismissed or how many asked for maternity pay.
- Page furniture breaks the text at [Para 36] and [Para 84], and [Para 104] carries a stray page marker inside the sentence.
