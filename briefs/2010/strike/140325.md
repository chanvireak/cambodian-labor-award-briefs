# Order No. 120/10-PCS — PCS Garment Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ភីស៊ី អេស ហ្គាមេន លីមីធីត), Sen Sok, Phnom Penh (Arbitration Council, 15 December 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អាំង អេង ថុង presiding; អ៊ីង សុទ្ធី, employer nominee; អាន វីរៈ, worker nominee) · Doc ID 140325

## 1. Facts
PCS Garment Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ភីស៊ី អេស ហ្គាមេន លីមីធីត), on Russian Federation Boulevard, Teuk Thla commune, Sen Sok district, Phnom Penh, was established in 1998 and employs about 3,334 workers. [Para 1][Para 28] Four factory unions — the Cambodian Workers' Union, the Workers' Union, the Free Workers' Union, the Khmer Union and the Union for Raising Workers' Living Standards — brought the case on behalf of 996 workers, from a complaint of 6 September 2010 pressing three demands, of which conciliation on 20 October 2010 settled two. [Para 2][Para 8][Para 9][Para 29] The single remaining demand was that the company re-erect its PCS signboard. [Para 2][Para 30] The panel heard the case twice, on 11 November and 2 December 2010, after the workers asked for time to negotiate directly with the employer, and no settlement was reached. [Para 8][Para 13][Para 15]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the demand that the company re-erect its PCS Garment signboard is a collective labour dispute within the Council's jurisdiction? [Para 40][Para 53]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — four PCS factory unions representing 996 workers:** the company used to display a proper signboard and has none now, so workers have lost confidence and fear the factory has stopped operating and will abandon them without notice; they want the public to know this is PCS Garment; they conceded that the missing signboard has not affected wages or general working conditions, but some workers wanted to strike over it and the unions told them to wait for the conciliation and arbitration procedure. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 38][Para 39]
**Employer (respondent) — PCS Garment Co., Ltd:** a signboard 1.5 metres wide and 40 metres long stood on top of the building until lightning struck one of its legs and it was taken down; a smaller replacement, 1 metre by 1 metre, was put up and then removed when a strike broke out; the company does intend to re-erect the PCS signboard but will not promise a date, since it must choose the right time and place, perhaps in the coming new year. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 34][Para 35][Para 36][Para 37]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel refused to consider the demand that the employer re-erect the PCS Garment signboard, holding that the dispute is not a collective labour dispute under the Labour Law. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 55]
The panel tested the demand against Article 302, which defines a collective labour dispute by three cumulative conditions: a dispute between a group of workers and one or more employers; a subject concerning working conditions, the exercise of rights conferred on a recognised professional organisation, or relations between employer and workers; and a dispute capable of disrupting the operation of the enterprise or disturbing social peace. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 42]
Condition (a) was met, the dispute being between the four unions representing workers in the factory and the employer. [Para 45] Condition (b) was not: the signboard dispute concerns neither working conditions nor the rights and duties of the employment contract, because the workers suffered no damage and no effect on hours, wages or benefits they had previously received. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49] Condition (c) was met, since the workers intended to strike to force the signboard's re-erection, which could disrupt the company's operations. [Para 50][Para 51] One of the three conditions therefore failed, and the panel held it had no jurisdiction, following Order 61/10-Sin Try. [Para 43][Para 52][Para 53] The order is not binding and becomes final eight days after notification if no party objects to the Minister. [Para 56]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A collective labour dispute requires all three conditions of Article 302 of the Labour Law, and the second fails where the subject matter of the demand touches neither working conditions nor any right or duty under the employment contract — a demand about a corporate signboard, affecting no wage, hour or benefit, is therefore outside the panel's jurisdiction however much disruption a strike over it might cause. [Para 42][Para 48][Para 52][Para 53]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the employer said it intended to re-erect the signboard, without undertaking when, and that the unions had counselled workers against striking while conciliation and arbitration ran their course — neither observation being needed once jurisdiction was refused. [Para 36][Para 39]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The operative paragraph refuses consideration of the demand but does not declare a lack of jurisdiction or order the case removed, although the reasoning does exactly that, so the disposal is recorded in §4 from the reasons as well as the order. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 55] The findings name four unions as claimants but list five union names, and the paragraph testing condition (a) gives their number as "45 unions" where four are meant. [Para 29][Para 45] The same passage states that the demand failed one of "the three conditions" but prints an OCR figure that reads "201 conditions". [Para 52] The lightning damage and replacement signboard are described with dimensions 1.5 by 40 metres and then 1 by 1 metre, and the grounds mention a strike that the summary of the procedure never records. [Para 35] The foot signature block carries stray Latin OCR fragments beside each arbitrator's name; the arbitrators are given here as printed in the header, and no individual worker, union official or employer representative is named in this brief. [Para 1][Para 57]
