# Award No. 60/10 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន គីន តាយ ហ្គាមិន ខូ.អិលធីឌី (Kin Tay Gamine Co., Ltd) (Arbitration Council, 15 July 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel: អ៊ឹង សុទ្ធី (employer-nominated), លីវ សុវណ្ណា (worker-nominated), តង់ ផល្លៈ (chair) · Doc ID 140281

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន គីន តាយ ហ្គាមិន ខូ.អិលធីឌី is a garment factory on National Road 2, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employing about 400 workers, of whom roughly 90 per cent hold six-month fixed-term contracts. [Para 44][Para 74] សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ (Khmer Youth Union Federation), claiming about 120 members at the factory, filed a complaint on 12 May 2010 seeking better working conditions; conciliation failed on 8 of 10 demands and the file reached the Council on 15 June 2010. [Para 22][Para 24][Para 45] The contracts were renewed every six months with a one-day break in which the workers were re-engaged as casual/day labourers (កម្មករបណ្តែត) and paid at the daily rate, so that continuity of service was the factual pivot of most demands. [Para 63][Para 64] The workers withdrew demand 7 at the hearing, and the panel declined to entertain demand 8 (the midday meal allowance) on the ground that the union lacked most-representative status. [Para 27][Para 214]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay 100 per cent of wages for periods when it suspended the workers' contracts without authorisation from the labour inspector? [Para 48]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay the seniority bonus (ប្រាក់រង្វាន់អតីតភាព) to the 172 claiming workers and pay it retroactively for those with three years' service? [Para 125]
3. (Issue 3) Whether maternity allowance must be paid to women with at least one year's service, and paid before the maternity leave begins? [Para 147]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must spray water on the factory roof, or take other measures, to reduce heat when it is extreme? [Para 166]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the company must give prior notice before declining to renew a six-month fixed-term contract? [Para 178]
6. (Issue 6) Whether the company must state a precise end date in the casual (ម្តងម្កាល) employment contracts it issues? [Para 191]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), represented by សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ:** the employer must find work for its workers, so a suspension without the inspector's permission breaches Article 71 and full wages are owed; the 50 per cent practice is a unilateral concession, not a defence; seniority bonus is owed whatever the contract type and however many renewals there were; the one-day casual break does not interrupt service; and clause 9 of every contract promises notice. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 59][Para 90]
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន គីន តាយ ហ្គាមិន ខូ.អិលធីឌី:** paying 50 per cent during suspension already exceeds Article 71, which requires only housing allowance; seniority bonus is withheld because the six-month contracts are discontinuous and 5 per cent is paid at each expiry; the fixed-term contracts genuinely lapsed, so no maternity pay or notice is due; and the heat demand will be considered next year. [Para 56][Para 63][Para 73][Para 87][Para 167]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to pay 100 per cent wages for suspensions made without the labour inspector's authorisation; to pay seniority bonus to all 172 workers and to pay it retroactively for those with three years' service; to pay maternity allowance to women with at least one year's service one day before leave begins; to spray water on the roof or use other means against heat; and to give prior notice under clause 9 — while the casual-contract date claim and the meal-allowance claim were refused. [Para 225][Para 226][Para 227][Para 228][Para 229][Para 230][Para 231]

On Issue 1 the panel read Article 71 §11 as placing any suspension on grounds of economic difficulty under the labour inspector's supervision, and followed Awards 51/09 (Yong Va), 95/09 (Peak Fat) and 123/09 (Sutwe) for the proposition that suspension requires prior notification and authorisation. [Para 111][Para 113][Para 115] Article 72(a) §1 then cuts both ways: a properly authorised suspension extinguishes the wage obligation, but an unauthorised one leaves the employer owing 100 per cent even where no work was given; the company admitted it never sought authorisation, so full wages were ordered. [Para 118][Para 119][Para 123]

On Issue 2 the panel applied point 5 of Notification No. 017 SKBY of 18 July 2000 ($2 monthly at one year, rising to $4 at three years and $5 at four), preserved by point 3 of Notification No. 745 KKBV of 23 October 2006, and held that seniority is measured by continuous service and is unaffected by how many contracts were signed. [Para 127][Para 130][Para 132][Para 139] The one-day casual interlude did not break the employment relationship, so the panel ordered the bonus for the 172 workers whose thumbprints supported the claim. [Para 144][Para 146]

On Issue 3, Article 183 entitles a woman with at least one year's service to half her wages including supplementary pay during maternity leave, and Article 115(3) forbids payment on a rest day, so payment must be made the day before; the panel followed Awards 57/06, 97/06 and 70/09 and rejected the argument that the six-month contracts restarted service. [Para 149][Para 152][Para 157][Para 161][Para 164]

On Issue 4, Article 229 and Prakas No. 147 SKBY of 11 June 2002 (articles 1 and 3) require the employer to keep workplace heat within acceptable limits by fans, extractors or other means; both sides accepted that this year was genuinely hot, so measures were ordered. [Para 168][Para 171][Para 176]

On Issue 5 the panel held that Article 73(5) requires ten days' notice only for contracts longer than six months, so the statute did not assist the workers; but clause 9 of the contract promised notice, and Article 13(2) permits terms more favourable than the law, so the contractual promise was enforced. [Para 180][Para 182][Para 185][Para 189] On Issue 6 the panel refused to compel the employer to insert an end date, because Article 65 and Article 1 of the Civil Code place contracts under the parties' agreement and one party cannot impose terms on the other. [Para 193][Para 196][Para 202]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of employment contracts for economic difficulty is lawful only if notified to, and authorised by, the labour inspector; where that procedure is skipped the employer owes full wages for the suspended period even though no work was performed, and the practice of paying half wages does not cure the defect. [Para 116][Para 119] Separately, seniority is computed from continuous service to the enterprise, so a one-day re-engagement as a casual labourer between successive fixed-term contracts neither breaks seniority nor defeats bonus, maternity and notice entitlements. [Para 139][Para 145]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that Notification No. 017 nowhere conditions the seniority bonus on the type of contract, and that the parties agreed casual workers received the same benefits as permanent ones — observations not needed once continuity was established. [Para 101][Para 133]
