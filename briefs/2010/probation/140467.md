# Award No. 174/09-ឃែនធើរេន — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឃែន ធើរេន អី ភែរឹល (ខេមបូឌា) v សហជីព សេរីកម្មករ នៃ រោងចក្រ កាត់ដេរ ឃែន ធើរេន (Arbitration Council, 5 January 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Panel: គង់ ផល្លៈ (Chair), អ៊ុក រី, អន ណន · Doc ID 140467

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឃែន ធើរេន អី ភែរឹល (ខេមបូឌា), a garment factory on Chom Chao road, Dangkao, Phnom Penh with about 1,300 workers, is the respondent; the claimant is សហជីព សេរីកម្មករ នៃ រោងចក្រ កាត់ដេរ ឃែន ធើរេន, the factory-level union affiliated to សហជីព សេរីកម្មករ នៃ ព្រះរាជាណាចក្រ កម្ពុជា (PTUWKC) [Para 28][Para 29]. Thirty-one workers in the trouser-sewing section, in two teams, thumbprinted the demand [Para 30][Para 31]. They say that from 2004 until the factory relocated in June 2009 the company paid 1 US dollar per 100 pairs sewn, and that the 2009 rate fell to 0.75 US dollars per 100 pairs, which is why they claim 1 US dollar per 100 pairs [Para 33][Para 35]. Both sides concede that the disputed trouser style is no longer being sewn and that the workers have moved to new styles [Para 43][Para 58].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must raise the piece rate for the disputed trouser style to 1 US dollar per 100 pairs, as the workers demand? [Para 32][Para 49]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — សហជីព សេរីកម្មករ នៃ រោងចក្រ កាត់ដេរ ឃែន ធើរេន:** the same style earned 1 US dollar per 100 pairs from 2004 to mid-2009 and the rate must be restored, because the workers are paid by output and press themselves hard without daring to rest, so they should be encouraged with the old rate; they ask for the 1 US dollar figure for future days whenever the company takes that style again [Para 33][Para 44][Para 45][Para 59].
**Employer (respondent) — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឃែន ធើរេន អី ភែរឹល (ខេមបូឌា):** it cannot meet the demand because of the economic downturn, which began in 2008 and not 2009, and because buyers set the price; the 2007 rate was 1.2 US dollars per 100 pairs and the 2008 rate 1 US dollar, while the 2009 rate of 0.75 US dollars merely reflects the lower prices offered by purchasers, and it will raise the rate again when a buyer pays more [Para 35][Para 46][Para 47][Para 60].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider the demand that the company raise the piece rate to 1 US dollar per 100 pairs [Para 67][Para 69].
The panel began from Article 108 of the Labour Law, which requires piece-work wages to be calculated so that a worker of ordinary skill working normal hours earns at least the guaranteed minimum wage, and from Article 137 on normal hours [Para 49][Para 50]. It read Notification No. 745 K.B.V of the Ministry of Labour dated 23 October 2006 as fixing 45 US dollars a month during probation and 50 US dollars a month once the worker is fully entitled, with output pay topping that figure up where output exceeds it [Para 51][Para 52][Para 54][Para 55]. On that basis the panel restated its settled position, drawn from Awards 03/05, 41/05, 44/06 and 69/07, that the employer is free to set and change piece rates provided an averagely skilled worker working no more than eight hours a day or 48 hours a week still receives at least 50 US dollars a month [Para 56][Para 57]. Nothing in the record suggests the new 0.75 US dollar rate breaches that floor, and the workers did not dispute the company's assertion that no worker receives less than the minimum [Para 41][Para 42].
The demand then failed on the facts rather than on the wage principle. Both parties accepted that the disputed style was finished and that the workers were already sewing new styles, so the panel could find no clear basis for holding that the company would or could set the rate at 1 US dollar when the style reappeared; the claim was directed at a hypothetical future order [Para 58][Para 59][Para 61][Para 62]. Following Award 168/09-ទឹកថ្លា ផ្លាហ្សា (issue 2) and its earlier rulings that the Council declines to consider a demand for which there is no sufficient factual basis, and that the Council exists to resolve live labour disputes rather than anticipate problems not yet arisen, the panel refused to rule on the demand [Para 63][Para 65][Para 66]. The order is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification [Para 71].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may fix and vary piece rates at will, but the rate must be set so that a worker of average skill working normal hours earns at least the legal minimum wage, here 50 US dollars a month once fully entitled [Para 57]. Where the style that gave rise to the rate dispute is no longer produced and the claim concerns what the employer might pay on some future order, the panel will not decide it for want of a sufficient factual basis [Para 62][Para 63].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted the employer's own statement that it would restore a higher rate once a buyer offered a higher price, without treating that undertaking as an enforceable order [Para 61].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The Khmer OCR is heavily corrupted. The company's name appears in at least four different spellings across the award, including two variants that drop the first consonant and one that inserts an extra syllable, and a Ministry report attributes the case to yet another variant [Para 18][Para 24][Para 25][Para 30]. The Prakas on the Arbitration Council is cited as No. 199 in one line and No. 099 in the next [Para 4]. Paragraph 70 is a stray duplicated fragment with no content [Para 70]. The panel's signatures are partly destroyed: one arbitrator's printed name survives only as a symbol and the chair's name carries stray Latin characters [Para 72]. The award also attaches a November 2009 payroll naming 22 individual workers, whose names are not reproduced here [Para 17]. Wage figures are printed inconsistently — 45 US dollars is described both as a probation rate and as the monthly floor in one notification recital, and the percentages and dollar signs inside that recital are garbled [Para 51][Para 55].
