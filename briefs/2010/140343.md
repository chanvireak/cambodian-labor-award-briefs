# Award No. 139/10 — Kon Hong Color Printing (Arbitration Council, 5 January 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Uk Ri, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Kong Phalla, presiding) · Doc ID 140343

## 1. Facts
Kon Hong Color Printing (ក្រុមហ៊ុន គ័ន ហុង ខូល័រ ព្រីន ធីង), a shoe-box manufacturer at Trapeang Kong, Samrong Tong, Kampong Speu, employing about 71 workers, ended the employment of its union president and union secretary when their contracts expired at the end of November 2010. [Para 46][Para 1] The claimant is the Khmer Workers Power Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព អំណាច កម្មករ ខ្មែរ) and its Kon Hong factory union, registered on 15 July 2010. [Para 2][Para 38] Of eleven demands, the parties settled ten at the hearing, leaving only the reinstatement claim. [Para 24]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must take back the union president and union secretary in their former posts and pay them from the date of termination to the date of return? [Para 46]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — Khmer Workers Power Union Federation and its Kon Hong factory union):** the two leaders had been employed since 2 February 2009 on successive six-month contracts and were simply told to stop coming in on 2 December 2010, without lawful notice; because their combined service passed two years their contracts should be treated as of indefinite duration, so they must be reinstated with back pay. [Para 46][Para 55][Para 56][Para 57]
**Employer (respondent — Kon Hong Color Printing, represented by its factory head, administration officer and accountant):** written notice of non-renewal went to all affected workers and separately to the union president on 15 November 2010, well beyond the 15 days the law requires; the leaders refused to accept the individual letter and the company could not produce it later, but the timing was lawful. [Para 48][Para 51][Para 53]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to order reinstatement or back pay for the two union leaders, finding the company gave them lawful notice of non-renewal. [Para 99]

The panel first fixed the character of the contracts. Article 67(2) of the Labour Law caps a fixed-duration contract at two years, allowing successive renewals so long as the total does not exceed that ceiling. [Para 66][Para 67] Counting from 2 February 2009 to the end of November 2010, the panel found both leaders had served less than two years in total, so their contracts remained fixed-duration. [Para 68][Para 69] It followed its award in Case No. 100/07 (Hou Yia Cambodia) that such a contract expires of its own force at its term and neither party can compel renewal. [Para 70][Para 73][Para 76]

The union leaders' statutory protection did not alter that. Article 293 requires the labour inspector's agreement before a staff delegate or union leader may be dismissed from their job, and Article 4 of Prakas No. 305 extends that shield to three union leaders. [Para 77][Para 80][Para 82] The panel held, however, that Article 293 guards against dismissal during employment and says nothing about the natural expiry of a fixed-duration contract, which is not a dismissal at all. [Para 83][Para 84]

The case therefore turned on notice. Article 73(5) requires ten days' prior notice of expiry for a contract of more than six months and fifteen days where the contract exceeds one year. [Para 85][Para 88] The leaders had over a year of service, so fifteen days were due. [Para 89][Para 90] The company said it gave notice on 15 November 2010; the workers said they learned of it only on 2 December 2010. [Para 91][Para 92] The panel resolved that conflict from a contemporaneous document: the workers filed notice of a strike on 1 December 2010, lodged with the labour office on 2 December, which they could not have done before learning their leaders were being let go. [Para 93][Para 95] It therefore accepted the company's account as coherent and credible, found fifteen days' notice given, and dismissed. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 98]

The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent written objection to the Minister of Labour. [Para 100]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Article 293's protection of union leaders against dismissal does not apply when a fixed-duration contract simply runs out; the employer owes only the statutory notice under Article 73(5) — ten days beyond six months and fifteen days beyond a year — and an unrenewed contract cannot be forced back to life. [Para 84][Para 88][Para 70]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel relied on the timing of the workers' own strike notice, filed the day before they said they learned of the non-renewal, as the decisive indicator that the employer's notice had come earlier. [Para 93][Para 95]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The dates are internally impossible in places: the leaders are said to have been terminated on 31 November 2011, a date that does not exist and which the reasoning elsewhere corrects to 31 November 2010; one passage also duplicates the year as "ឆ្នាំ ឆ្នាំ ២០០៩". [Para 46][Para 61][Para 89]

An undated passage asserting that the contracts became of indefinite duration and that the employer must take both leaders back appears in the findings without attribution, and it contradicts the panel's eventual holding that the contracts were fixed-duration. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 68]

The quoted text of Article 73(5) carries a stray "២០១៦" inside it. [Para 85] The hearing notice narration also garbles the notice period as "១ ១៥" days. [Para 91] The employer's name appears as ក្រុមហ៊ុន គ័ន ហុង ខូល័រ ព្រីន ធីង in the party block but is shortened to គ័ន ហុង ខូល័រ ព្រីន ធីង or simply គ័ន ហុង elsewhere. [Para 1][Para 26][Para 46] The signature block for the presiding arbitrator is corrupted by scan noise. [Para 101] The two union leaders are identified here by office only.
