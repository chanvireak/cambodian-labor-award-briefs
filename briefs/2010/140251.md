# Award No. 22/10 — Nhu Oak Khit Garment (Arbitration Council, 22 March 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3 (Seng Vouch Hun, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 140251

## 1. Facts
Two unions at the Nhu Oak Khit factory in Ang Snuol district, Kandal, each brought demands: the Khmer Youth Union filed a complaint of 18 points on 10 February 2010, and the Khmer Union, which had previously registered with the factory, added 5 more, producing 23 points of non-conciliation. [Para 39][Para 41][Para 29] Provincial labour officials attempted conciliation on 17 and 18 February 2010; the Youth Union did not attend and could not be contacted, so a non-conciliation report issued on 23 February 2010 and reached the Council on 26 February 2010. [Para 32][Para 35][Para 36] At the hearing of 10 March 2010 the employer appeared through its administration chief, the Khmer Union appeared, and the Khmer Youth Union was again absent. [Para 40][Para 31]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the five demands belonging to the Khmer Union were still before the panel, given that the union withdrew them at the hearing? [Para 42][Para 53]
2. Whether the Khmer Youth Union, the original complainant, abandoned its claims by failing to appear, to appoint a representative or to choose an arbitrator? [Para 61][Para 64]
3. Whether the panel should nevertheless decide the 23 demands on their merits in the absence of the claiming union? [Para 52][Para 66]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the Khmer Union said it did not know why its five points had been merged into the Youth Union's file, asked the panel to reject those five because they had already been through conciliation on 1 March 2010 and were being processed, and otherwise disclaimed any demand in this case. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 54] The Khmer Youth Union sent no representative and stated that the case did not concern it, though its own complaint of 10 February 2010 names it as claimant. [Para 38][Para 39]
**Employer (respondent):** the company agreed with the Khmer Union's position that the five duplicated points should be withdrawn and asked the panel simply to apply the law. [Para 43]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** withdrawn — the panel closed Case No. 22/10 in its entirety without ruling on any of the 23 demands. [Para 70][Para 71]

The panel treated the Khmer Union's statement as a renunciation: a union that tells the panel at the hearing that it makes no claim cannot afterwards be treated as a claimant, so the five points it had contributed were no longer in dispute. [Para 55][Para 56] On the Youth Union, the panel applied Rule 4.7 of the Arbitration Council procedural rules annexed to Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004: where a party fails to appear or to send a representative, the panel may either continue or terminate by issuing an award, but only if that party was properly notified of the date, time and place. [Para 57][Para 58] The record showed repeated contact from 25 February 2010, an invitation to choose an arbitrator that produced no appearance and no representative, and a bare assertion that the case was unrelated to the union. [Para 60][Para 61] Because the original complaint of 10 February 2010 and the non-conciliation report of 23 February 2010 both identify the Khmer Youth Union as the claimant, the panel held that it had standing and that its silence was abandonment. [Para 62][Para 63] The panel added that a complainant carries the burden of giving reasons and evidence before the Council, and that non-appearance deprives it of exactly that opportunity; it read the conduct as showing no genuine intention to have the dispute settled. [Para 65][Para 67][Para 69] Having found no live claim, the panel terminated the file rather than deciding the merits. [Para 70]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A complainant union that is properly notified, declines to appoint an arbitrator, sends no representative and asserts at the hearing that the file does not concern it is taken to have abandoned its claim; the panel then closes the case instead of ruling on the demands. [Para 64][Para 68]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that non-appearance deprives a claimant of the chance to explain and prove its case, and therefore shows a want of good faith, sits outside the operative holding, which rests on abandonment alone. [Para 67][Para 69]
