# Award No. 111/10 — Cambodia Utilities Pte., Ltd. and Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Worker Democratic Unions (C.CAWDU) Local Union (Arbitration Council, 17 November 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry, An Nan, Kong Phallack) · Doc ID 140318

## 1. Facts
Cambodia Utilities Pte., Ltd., an electricity producer at National Road No. 2, Chak Angre Loeu, employing about 140 workers, laid off staff in phases as its sole client, Electricité du Cambodge, cut demand: output fell from 21,861,100 kWh in 2008 to 10,296,800 in 2009 and 60,050 in 2010. [Para 16][Para 32]
On 9 August 2010 it dismissed the worker delegate, Mr S, a plumber since 17 March 1997, and suspended him on 16 August while seeking the Labour Inspector's authorisation; it also forced four staff members into retirement at 55 under Article 12 of its Internal Work Rules. [Para 26][Para 31][Para 38][Para 91][Para 124]
Of nine demands, Issue 1 was withdrawn and Issues 2 and 3 combined, leaving Issues 2–3 and 4. [Para 13][Para 14]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issues 2 and 3) Whether the employer must pay the worker delegate damages under Article 91 of the Labour Law, or reinstate him, when it ended his contract unilaterally? [Para 17][Para 57]
2. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must reinstate the four staff members compelled into retirement at 55, or pay them compensation in lieu of notice, dismissal indemnity and damages under Articles 75, 89, 91, 116 and 167? [Para 40][Para 113]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Worker Democratic Unions (C.CAWDU) and its local union, through Mr O, legal officer:** the dispute is collective because it concerns the dismissal of the workers' elected delegate and could provoke a strike; the delegate's 14 years' service proves his competence; the dismissal was at the employer's will and unauthorised by the Labour Inspector, so damages or reinstatement is owed; and no law fixes retirement at 55. [Para 21][Para 27][Para 29][Para 31][Para 52][Para 113]
**Employer (respondent), Cambodia Utilities Pte., Ltd., through Ms N, head of administration:** the dispute is individual, concerns only one worker and cannot disrupt the enterprise; the dismissal followed Article 95 as a layoff driven by its client's reduced demand; and the four were retired under Article 12 of the Internal Work Rules, which the Ministry registered. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 34][Para 42][Para 116]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demands to reinstate Mr S and to pay him Article 91 damages are rejected, and the employer is ordered to pay the four retired staff compensation in lieu of notice under Articles 75 and 77, dismissal indemnity under Article 89, and damages under Article 91. [Para 154][Para 155]
The panel first took jurisdiction. Article 302 requires a dispute between a number of workers and an employer, about working conditions or relations, capable of jeopardising the enterprise or social peace; the objecting employer bears the burden of showing otherwise, and the panel normally defers to the Labour Inspector's classification. Here the employer's own letter of 13 August 2010 asking the commune chief to intervene against a threatened strike showed condition (c) was met, so the dispute was collective. [Para 57][Para 62][Para 65][Para 73][Para 75][Para 76]
On Issues 2 and 3, Article 293 requires Labour Inspector authorisation before dismissing a shop steward, with a one-month decision period; a request unanswered for more than a month is deemed rejected, and Article 295 suspension is void where there is no serious misconduct, so the procedure failed. [Para 81][Para 89][Para 91][Para 95]
The panel nevertheless found a valid reason: the dismissal formed part of a 33-worker mass layoff under Article 95, ordered by professional qualification, seniority and dependants after consultation, and the delegate's plumbing work was redundant in the new structure. Because a valid reason existed, no Article 91 damages were due and reinstatement was not required. [Para 98][Para 100][Para 109][Para 110][Para 112]
On Issue 4, neither the Labour Law nor the Law on the Social Security Fund fixes a retirement age — the pension at 55 is conditional and has no implementing Prakas — so Article 12 of the Internal Work Rules could not supply one, and retiring the four was termination at will without a reason going to aptitude or conduct under Article 74. [Para 119][Para 120][Para 123][Para 147]
Their claims were within the three-year limitation of Article 120, and each had over ten years' service, entitling them to three months' notice pay, fifteen days of wages per year of service capped at six months, and Article 91 damages, the outstanding wage and leave claims being already paid. [Para 129][Para 131][Para 134][Para 140][Para 150][Para 153]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer cannot retire a worker at 55 merely because its internal rules say so: absent a statutory retirement age, a Prakas or a collective agreement, compulsory retirement is a termination at will and needs a reason relating to the worker's aptitude or the running of the enterprise, failing which notice pay, dismissal indemnity and Article 91 damages follow. [Para 123][Para 147][Para 153]
A shop steward's dismissal still requires Labour Inspector authorisation, and silence beyond one month is a refusal; but where the dismissal sits inside a lawful mass layoff it has a valid reason, so no damages accrue even though the procedure was defective. [Para 89][Para 95][Para 110][Para 112]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the Ministry has enforced only the occupational health and safety chapter of the Social Security Fund Law, and that the pension threshold of 55 with twenty years' registration does not itself set a retirement age. [Para 50][Para 122]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
This is an unofficial English translation of the Khmer original, and the evidence section was omitted from it, so no evidential record could be checked. [Para 15]
A recurring OCR fault deletes the letter "t" before "h" (producing "the employer" as "themployer" and "the" as "the"), and the layoff request is dated 22 July 2012 in two places although every surrounding event is 2010. [Para 35][Para 108]
The table of the four retired staff at line 57 is column-scrambled, with commencement and retirement dates detached from their names and one date reduced to "05/10"; the brief relies on the panel's separate statement that one retired in 2009 and three in 2010. [Para 57][Para 131]
One staff member's surname is spelled two ways ("Kouck" and "Kouch"), another appears as an OCR string with a stray Khmer character appended, and the decision headings for Issues 2 and 3 are merged, which is why the brief numbers them together. [Para 40][Para 140][Para 154]
