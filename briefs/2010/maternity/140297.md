# Award No. 80/10 — ASD (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេស ឌី (ខេមបូឌា)) (Arbitration Council, 19 August 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of three: កៅ ថា ច (employer-nominated), អន ណន (worker-nominated), ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា (chair) · Doc ID 140297

## 1. Facts

The National Federation of Unions of the Cambodian Textile and Garment Industry (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ជាតិ នៃ ឧស្សាហកម្ម វាយនភ័ណ្ឌ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា) and its local union at the ASD (Cambodia) garment factory in Oudim, Ang Snuol, Kandal filed fourteen collective demands on 17 June 2010. [Para 13][Para 33] The factory employed 720 and had been operating since 1998 or 1999. [Para 31][Para 30] Conciliation settled ten; four reached the panel, and demands 3 and 4 — a union meeting room and the removal of a production manager — were settled at the hearing, leaving two demands about maternity entitlements. [Para 14][Para 19][Para 20]

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay the 50 per cent balance of the US$6 monthly living allowance (ប្រាក់ទ្រង់ជីវភាព) it withheld from women on 90 days' maternity leave, and pay it in full from now on? [Para 34][Para 35]
2. (Issue 2) Whether maternity leave pay must be computed on the average of the last twelve months' total wages and benefits, overtime included, rather than on basic wage alone? [Para 44][Para 45]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant) — សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ជាតិ នៃ ឧស្សាហកម្ម វាយនភ័ណ្ឌ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា (and its ASD local, សហជីព ឧស្សាហកម្ម កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា):** the US$6 living allowance is not wage but a separate monthly benefit, so it cannot be halved during maternity leave; and Article 183's "half wage including benefits" must be read with Article 103, which makes overtime part of wage. [Para 36][Para 49][Para 84]

**Employer (respondent) — ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេស ឌី (ខេមបូឌា), trading as ASD (Cambodia) Co., Ltd:** the Labour Law entitles a woman on maternity leave to only half her wage and benefits, and the company has paid US$3 a month since 1998 as other factories do; overtime cannot be added because a woman on leave works no overtime. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 48]

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to pay US$9 in living allowance (US$3 a month over three months) to Ms A and Ms M, and to pay the allowance at 100 per cent, US$6 a month, to every woman on maternity leave; and to compute the 50 per cent maternity wage on total earnings for the last twelve months, overtime included, divided by 12, halved and multiplied by three. [Para 95][Para 96][Para 97][Para 98]

On the living allowance the panel applied Notification No. 032/08 of 17 April 2008, which grants US$6 a month to garment and shoe workers and provides expressly that the sum is not to be counted as basic wage. [Para 58][Para 59] The tripartite minutes of 5 May 2008 halve the allowance only for a new worker in her first month who has worked fewer than 14 days. [Para 60][Para 61] Following Award 119/08 (Hong Wah Tai), the panel read "without any condition" in point 2 of those minutes as reaching unpaid leave authorised by the employer, so a woman on maternity leave keeps the full US$6. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 66] Paying half was therefore contrary to both the Labour Law and the minutes. [Para 71]

On maternity pay, Article 182 gives 90 days' leave and Article 183 gives half wage and benefits to a woman who has at least one year's service. [Para 76][Para 78] Article 183 does not define wage, so the panel turned to Article 103, which lists the components of wage and expressly includes overtime. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 84] Following Awards 68/04 (CT Pool), 18/06 (GHG) and 33/07 (Gold Fame), and Award 06/08 (Kingland), the panel held the base is the last twelve months' actual earnings divided by 12, then halved and multiplied by three for the 90 days. [Para 87][Para 89][Para 92]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

A statutory or ministerial benefit that the instrument says is not part of basic wage cannot be reduced by the employer during maternity leave, because it is not a payment for work performed; and where a statute fixes "half wage including benefits" without defining wage, the panel will read the definition of wage from the same Law, which includes overtime, and average actual earnings over the twelve months before the leave. [Para 59][Para 84][Para 87][Para 92]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that Article 183 conditions the entitlement on at least one year's service, but made its operative order without repeating that condition. [Para 78][Para 96]

## 6. Source defects disclosed

The employer appears as ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេស ឌី (ខេមបូឌា) on the face and as ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេស ឌី in the body, with the Latin form ASD (Cambodia) Co., Ltd used for the registered entity. [Para 1][Para 21] The two claimant unions are named differently in the header and in the findings, the local union appearing as សហជីព ឧស្សាហកម្ម កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា ASD in one place and សហជីព សម្លេង កម្មករ កម្ពុជា in the other. [Para 1][Para 32] The Article 103 list is quoted as including the night-work premium (ប្រាក់បន្ថែមមាំង), while the reasoning and the operative paragraph speak of the overtime premium (ប្រាក់បន្ថែមម៉ោង) in the same position, so the two terms cannot be told apart. [Para 83][Para 85][Para 97] The two claimants' names are printed inconsistently across the demand list and the reasons, and are reduced to initials in this brief; the signature block is largely illegible. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 100] The company's opening year is given as "1998 or 1999". [Para 30]
