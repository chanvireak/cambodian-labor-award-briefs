# Award No. 01/10 — Khen Nitich International (Cambodia) Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 29 January 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tai Seng, Tun Sy Phan; Pen Bun Chhea presiding) · Doc ID 140236

## 1. Facts
The Khmer Youth Federation and its factory local brought twelve collective demands against Khen Nitich International (Cambodia) Co Ltd, of which six remained non-conciliated and reached the panel: full wages for short work stoppages, a 4,000 riel meal or rice allowance for overtime on holidays and Sundays, transport home after overtime, chairs for trimming workers, extended maternity leave, and a USD 10 warehouse allowance. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 3][Para 5][Para 7][Para 8][Para 10][Para 12] The Secretariat issued formal invitations to both parties and also contacted them by telephone as to date, time and venue. [Para 46][Para 48] The worker party did not appear at the hearing of 26 January 2010 and gave no reason; the employer attended. [Para 18][Para 24][Para 54]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the panel could close the file and issue an award without reaching the merits, where the worker party was properly notified, failed to appear, and offered no valid excuse? [Para 45][Para 56][Para 57]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The company should pay 100 per cent of wages and benefits for the short periods of three to five days when there was no work, make up the arrears, and provide a meal or 4,000 riel to workers required to work overtime on holidays and Sundays; the company should also provide safe transport home after overtime and chairs for trimming workers to sit on. [Para 3][Para 5][Para 7][Para 8]
**Employer (respondent):** The company already pays 50 per cent during work shortages under a minutes of 10 September 2007 agreed with worker representatives, cannot give a meal allowance because volunteers on holidays work only eight hours, runs night and day shifts without transport problems, follows the labour health department's Prakas on seating, and applies the 90-day maternity leave under the Labour Law. [Para 4][Para 6][Para 7][Para 9][Para 11]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel closed Case No. 01/10 without determining any of the six demands, the worker party having failed to appear. [Para 56][Para 57]

Because the claimant was absent, the panel first addressed its own power to proceed rather than the merits, applying Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 and Rule 4.7 of the Procedural Rules: where a party has been properly notified and fails to appear without valid reason, the panel may continue in its absence or may terminate the proceedings by issuing an award. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41] It derived from those provisions, and from its earlier awards, three cumulative conditions — proper notification, non-appearance, and absence of a valid excuse. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44]

Each condition was satisfied. On notification, the Secretariat issued formal invitations to both parties under its usual procedure and supplemented them by telephone, so the date, time and venue were conveyed. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49] On appearance, neither the worker party nor any representative of the federation attended the hearing. [Para 52] On excuse, the worker party gave the Secretariat no reason at all for its absence. [Para 54][Para 55] The panel recorded that it therefore could not establish any of the facts underlying the demands, and closed the file. [Para 38][Para 56][Para 57]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the Secretariat has given a party formal written notice of the hearing date, time and place and reinforced it by telephone, and that party neither appears nor offers any valid reason, the panel may exercise its discretion under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 and Rule 4.7 to terminate the arbitration by award without any finding on the merits. [Para 40][Para 48][Para 49][Para 55]

**Obiter dicta:** none identified — the panel expressly declined to make any finding of fact on the six demands because of the absence. [Para 38]
