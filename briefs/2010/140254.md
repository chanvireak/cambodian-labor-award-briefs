# Award No. 24/10 — Rilay Abel Sows On Das Strial (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 8 April 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3 (Seng Vouch Hun, employer-appointed; Soung Sophal, worker-appointed; Ing Phalla, president) · Doc ID 140254

## 1. Facts
The cut-make-trim factory employed about 1,250 workers and hosted two unions: the Khmer Youth Union, with roughly 300 members and the complainant here, and the Independent and Democratic Union, which held the most-representative certificate No. 037/08 of 25 April 2008. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 54] The Khmer Youth Union filed 13 demands on 4 January 2010; conciliation on 18 February 2010 failed on all 13, and at the hearing the parties settled issues 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 10, leaving seven for decision. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 26][Para 27] An earlier award, No. 120/09 of 7 October 2009, had already decided some of the same demands between the same parties. [Para 38][Para 59][Para 112]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 5) Whether the company must distribute $20 per worker per year out of its annual profits? [Para 56][Para 83]
2. (Issue 7) Whether the company must pay 2,000 riel for a meal when workers perform overtime on public holidays and Sundays, when Award No. 120/09 already refused that demand? [Para 59][Para 104]
3. (Issue 8) Whether the company must replace its punch machine with a die-cutting machine because the latter is easier to operate? [Para 64][Para 117]
4. (Issue 9) Whether the company must pay a $10 monthly attendance bonus and a $10 monthly seniority bonus? [Para 67][Para 131][Para 137]
5. (Issue 11) Whether the company must pay a $5 monthly housing allowance to all workers? [Para 76][Para 148]
6. (Issue 12) Whether the company must play music over loudspeakers during working hours? [Para 79][Para 157]
7. (Issue 13) Whether the company must pay a $4 monthly lunch allowance? [Para 81][Para 165]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the union argued that the company made large profits and should share them under Article 103, that holiday overtime deserves a full meal allowance, that the die-cutting machine is easier and safer, that the attendance and seniority bonuses and the housing and lunch allowances are owed as a matter of fairness, and that other factories already play music. [Para 56][Para 76][Para 79][Para 84][Para 149]
**Employer (respondent):** the company said it already pays daily, weekly and monthly productivity bonuses that amount to profit-sharing in substance, that it cannot fund the further allowances, that the punch machine causes no health or safety problem, and that the earlier award already settled the meal and bonus demands. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 116][Para 128][Para 132]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — every remaining demand was refused: profit-sharing, the 2,000 riel holiday meal allowance, the change of machine, the $10 attendance and $10 seniority bonuses, the $5 housing allowance, the playing of music and the $4 lunch allowance. [Para 172][Para 173][Para 174][Para 175][Para 177][Para 178][Para 179]

On Issue 5 the panel read Article 103 as a definition of the components of wages, including a share in profits, and held that it creates no obligation on an employer to distribute profits at year end; it followed awards 70/07, 89/07, 92/09, 116/09 and 165/09. [Para 86][Para 87] Because nothing in law or agreement granted the sum, the demand was an interest dispute, and the panel noted that in interest disputes it always examines whether the bringing union is the most representative. [Para 88][Para 89] The Khmer Youth Union was not, while the certificate holder was a different union that had not complained, so the claim failed on standing as well as on substance. [Para 90][Para 93][Para 101][Para 102]

On Issue 7 the panel applied res judicata: the same demand between the same parties had been decided in Award No. 120/09, and the fact that the workers had appealed against that award did not reopen it before the panel. [Para 107][Para 112][Para 113][Para 114]

On Issue 8 the panel treated the choice of machinery as part of management under Article 2, lawful and reasonable where no health or safety problem is shown; the workers themselves conceded the punch machine had caused no difficulty. [Para 120][Para 127][Para 128][Para 129]

On Issue 9 the attendance-bonus half was refused on res judicata, the same point having been decided in Award No. 120/09, and the seniority-bonus half as an interest dispute outside the law and outside any agreement. [Para 134][Para 136][Para 143][Para 145][Para 147]

On Issues 11, 12 and 13 the panel found that neither the Labour Law nor any agreement required a housing allowance, in-work music or a lunch allowance, so each was an interest dispute carried by a union without representative status and was refused. [Para 152][Para 161][Para 167][Para 169][Para 171]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that does not hold the most-representative certificate cannot bring an interest dispute to the panel: where the demand rests on fairness rather than on a statute, a collective agreement or a prior award, only the certified union may pursue it. [Para 94][Para 99][Para 101]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the company's existing productivity bonuses are profit-sharing "in substance" despite the label used is not necessary to the result, which turns on standing and on Article 103. [Para 58][Para 87]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The scan is heavily degraded and the money claims are unstable. Issue 9 is pleaded as $110 attendance plus $10 seniority in the demand list but as $10 and $10 in the reasoning, and the seniority paragraph contains a stray reference to the year 2023 in a 2010 case. [Para 11][Para 67][Para 72] The company name is rendered in at least five transliterations, including one that drops the country suffix and one that describes the plant as knitting rather than cut-make-trim. [Para 1][Para 52][Para 54] The prior award is cited as No. 120/09 in most places but with a corrupted number in one passage. [Para 38][Para 105] The panel's own operative section prints "Issue 7" twice with different wording and numbers one heading "បំណុច វិវាទ ទី ១២" where the text concerns music during hours. [Para 173][Para 178] Both employer representatives and all union officers are named in the party lists; they are identified here by role only. [Para 1][Para 3]
