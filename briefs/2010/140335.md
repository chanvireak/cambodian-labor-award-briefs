# Order No. 132/10 — Men Hat Then Garment Factory, Soun Osahakam Men Hat Chen (សូន ឧស្សាហកម្ម មេនហាត ចេន) and the Free Workers' Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia (Arbitration Council, 29 December 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Hem Honarith ហែម ហូណារិទ្ធ; An Virak អាន វីរៈ; Sok Mathoeung សុខ ម៉ាធឿង presiding) · Doc ID 140335

## 1. Facts
សូន ឧស្សាហកម្ម មេនហាត ចេន (Soun Osahakam Men Hat Chen), a garment factory at Ampil Leu, Kampong Siem, Kampong Cham, employing 4,250 workers of whom 3,825 are women [Para 1][Para 30], faced a five-point collective demand filed on 17 November 2010 by its single union, the Free Workers' Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia and its factory branch (សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ នៃ រោងចក្រ កាត់ដេរ មេនហាត ធេន) [Para 1][Para 9][Para 31]. Four points were settled in conciliation on 29 November 2010 and one was referred [Para 10]. The surviving demand concerns the end-of-service support paid to workers on indefinite-duration contracts who resign: by point 2 of a reconciliation agreement of 3 June 2010 in case No. 50/10, the company undertook to apply an earlier agreement of 26 January 2008 giving two months' wages of support to such workers when they resign, provided they meet the employer first to discuss the resignation [Para 21][Para 32][Para 33][Para 34][Para 35][Para 36]. The workers say that after that agreement some resigning workers received only one month, and identified three workers who were short-paid [Para 37][Para 38]. The employer admits the two months were not paid to everyone and that some received one month [Para 38][Para 39].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer is obliged to apply point 2 of the agreement of 3 June 2010 by paying two months' wage support to every worker on an indefinite-duration contract who resigns, rather than assessing case by case whether one or two months should be paid? [Para 60][Para 79]

The four other demands were settled in conciliation and were not decided [Para 10].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ នៃ រោងចក្រ កាត់ដេរ មេនហាត ធេន:** the 3 June 2010 agreement binds the company to two months for every indefinite-contract worker who resigns, and the clause requiring a meeting with the employer exists only so that the employer learns the reason for the resignation, not so that it can fix the amount [Para 44][Para 67]. The union was never consulted before the company began paying one month to some and two to others [Para 45][Para 46].

**Employer (respondent), សូន ឧស្សាហកម្ម មេនហាត ចេន:** the agreement requires the worker to meet the general director first, and it is for him to assess how much support each worker should receive [Para 40][Para 41][Para 42][Para 67]. Some workers properly received one month — for instance one who resigned to change residence — while others with two children and a sick mother received two [Para 77].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to apply point 2 of the agreement of 3 June 2010 by providing two months' support to workers on indefinite-duration contracts who resign [Para 81]

The panel first set out the statutory frame. Article 74 of the Labour Law lets either party terminate an indefinite-duration contract with written notice, and Article 89 requires severance only where the employer terminates, except for serious misconduct; Article 89 says nothing about a worker who resigns of his or her own will, and no law or regulation requires this kind of support payment [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 55][Para 56][Para 57]. The obligation therefore had to come from the agreement itself.

Article 13 of the Labour Law then supplied the answer: the Labour Law's provisions are of public order, but a contract or collective agreement granting benefits more favourable than the law is not an obstacle and may be applied, following Order 136/07-Phnom Penh Garden [Para 60][Para 62][Para 63][Para 64]. The panel accordingly held that the Labour Law does not prohibit paying two months' support to resigning indefinite-contract workers [Para 65].

On the construction of the agreement, the panel applied Article 22 of Decree No. 38 of 28 October 1988: a contract is the law of the parties and may be modified only with their consent [Para 71][Para 72][Para 75]. The employer had in fact consented, in point 2 of the 3 June 2010 agreement, to pay two months [Para 66]. Reading the whole clause, the panel found no word or meaning in the requirement that the worker meet the employer that gave the employer a right to evaluate and reduce the amount [Para 68][Para 69]. It therefore held that paying one month to some and two to others did not conform to point 2 [Para 70][Para 76]. It accepted that the employer's individual assessments had some factual basis [Para 77], but held that any change in the way the agreement is applied must first be discussed and agreed with the union [Para 78][Para 80].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A collective reconciliation agreement that grants workers a benefit more favourable than the Labour Law is valid under Article 13 and binds the employer as the law of the parties under Article 22 of Decree 38, so it must be applied uniformly to every worker within its terms; a procedural step in the agreement, such as meeting the employer before resigning, cannot be read as a discretion to reduce the agreed amount, and any variation requires prior discussion and consent with the union [Para 63][Para 69][Para 75][Para 80].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's acceptance that the employer's case-by-case assessments reflected real differences in workers' circumstances, and its observation that Article 89 severance does not arise on resignation [Para 56][Para 77].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's name is printed in at least six forms across the document (មេនហាត ធេន, មេនហាតធេន, មេត ហាធេន, មេ កហាតធេន, មេនហាត ចេន, មេនហាត ធេន) [Para 1][Para 2][Para 25][Para 26][Para 30]. The presiding arbitrator is named សុខ ម៉ាធឿង in the header and សុខ ម៉ាផ្សេង in the signature block, and the employer-chosen arbitrator appears as ហែម ហូណារិទ្ធ and ហែម ហួ ណារិទ្ធ [Para 1][Para 83]. The surviving issue is numbered inconsistently: it is introduced as the fourth point and later referred to as the only remaining issue without a number [Para 32][Para 21]. The hearing time appears as both 14:00 and 02:00 p.m., the number of non-conciliated points as "01" and "017", and the date of the 2008 agreement is given once as 26 January 2008 and elsewhere by a letter dated 24 January 2010 [Para 8][Para 11][Para 13][Para 22]. The three short-paid workers are listed in the source with names and employee numbers; they are identified here only as the three workers named in the union's submission.
