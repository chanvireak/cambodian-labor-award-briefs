# Award No. 27/10 — Hat Inter Brai (Cambodia) Co Ltd Garment Factory (Arbitration Council, 6 April 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3 (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Nan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhian, president) · Doc ID 140255

## 1. Facts
The cut-make-trim factory, operating since 2005 with about 950 workers and three unions, faced a four-point demand; conciliation on 16 March 2010 settled three, leaving one point certified unresolved the next day. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 9][Para 10] Workers stopped work at about 11:45 on 15 March 2010 and struck until 18 March, after the union president was summoned to an administrative meeting that the workers believed was a lock-in. [Para 38][Para 40] The Council issued an urgent order suspending the strike and directing a return to work in shifts from 19 March 2010, which both sides carried out. [Para 11][Para 12] The remaining demand was for wages and the regular attendance bonus covering the strike days. [Para 38][Para 54]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether workers who took part in the strike of 15 to 18 March 2010 are entitled to wages for those days? [Para 55]
2. Whether those workers are entitled to the regular attendance bonus for the strike period? [Para 67]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the free workers' union said the strike was a spontaneous reaction to the company's treatment of its president, that the workers were not at fault, that the company had paid wages and the bonus during earlier strikes, and that they needed the money before the Khmer New Year. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50] It put the number on strike at about 800 of the 950 workforce. [Para 46]
**Employer (respondent):** the company said it never dismissed or locked in the president, that it merely told her she would lose certain benefits after the federation withdrew her recognition, that only about 400 struck while 600 worked, and that it will no longer pay wages or bonuses for procedurally irregular strikes because doing so rewards them and is unfair to those who stayed at work. [Para 43][Para 45][Para 47][Para 52][Para 53]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the claim for wages and the regular attendance bonus for the strike days of 15 to 18 March 2010 was refused in full. [Para 73][Para 74]

On wages the panel applied Article 332(1) of the Labour Law, under which the employment contract is suspended during a strike so that no work and no wage are owed, together with Article 72(1) on suspension of the principal obligations. [Para 55][Para 57] It held expressly that the employer owes neither wages nor bonus for strike days whether the strike was lawful or not, following Award No. 49/05. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60] The panel then tested the strike against Article 324, which requires prior notice, and Article 320(4), which requires exhaustion of conciliation first, and found both unmet: the walkout came without notice and before the Council's conciliation had run its course. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 65]

On the bonus the panel took point 3 of Notice No. 017 of 18 July 2000, which grants at least $5 a month to workers who work the full number of days required, and held that strikers did not attend regularly and so earned nothing; it followed awards 03/05, 63/07, 110/07 and 81/08. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 70][Para 72]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A strike suspends the contract of employment, so strike days carry no wage and no attendance bonus, and that is so whether or not the strike complied with the statutory notice and conciliation requirements; procedural unlawfulness is not the trigger for the loss. [Para 58][Para 72]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's finding that this particular strike breached Articles 324 and 320(4) was not needed to refuse the claim, since the suspension rule applies to lawful strikes as well. [Para 58][Para 65]
