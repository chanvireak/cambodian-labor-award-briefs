# Award No. 52/10 — Ying Dong Sew Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 21 June 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (In Sothy, An Non, Run Saray presiding) · Doc ID 140273

## 1. Facts
Ying Dong Sew Co., Ltd, at Sen Monorom village, Dangkao, Phnom Penh, employed about 550 workers, of whom about 430 belonged to the factory branch of the Cambodian Workers' Union Federation — a union that had applied for registration on 23 April 2010 but had not yet been recognised by the Ministry of Labour [Para 34][Para 35][Para 36][Para 63]. Thirteen demands were filed on 26 April 2010, of which five remained unresolved [Para 17][Para 18]. At the hearing the workers withdrew Issues 1 (three months' maternity wages) and 4 (weekly meal allowance), and the parties settled Issue 3 (facilities for union leaders attending meetings), leaving Issues 2 and 5 [Para 23][Para 38][Para 58][Para 60]. Issue 2 concerned the USD 6 monthly living-cost allowance the company had been deducting pro rata for days of absence, and the position allowance paid to team leaders and translators on the same basis [Para 39][Para 40][Para 51]. Issue 5 concerned three union founders — a translator who was union president, a production section head who was union secretary, and a translator who was union vice-president — dismissed on 26 April 2010, three days after the registration application, without the labour inspector's consent [Para 61][Para 70][Para 71][Para 142].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2 — living-cost allowance) Whether the company must repay the USD 6 living-cost allowance it deducted in past months for days of absence, authorised or not? [Para 104][Para 106]
2. (Issue 2 — position allowance) Whether the company must stop deducting the position allowance of USD 50, USD 25 and USD 30 for absences of team leaders and translators? [Para 122][Para 124]
3. (Issue 5) Whether the company must reinstate the three union founders and pay their wages from the date of dismissal, where it dismissed them without the labour inspector's consent while their union's registration was pending? [Para 132][Para 134]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company deducted the living-cost allowance for every day of absence, lawful or not, although it must be paid in full; and it should stop deducting the position allowance for the same reason [Para 39][Para 57][Para 122]. The three leaders were dismissed because the employer did not want them forming a union — it called them in and told them to leave the union, which they refused [Para 72][Para 73].
**Employer (respondent):** it agreed to stop deducting the living-cost allowance from May 2010 but could not repay past deductions because the company was new and had no document system, so it could not reconstruct the amounts; it denied deducting the position allowance improperly, since that allowance exists only for team leaders and translators and is extra-statutory [Para 43][Para 45][Para 55][Para 123]. It dismissed the three for poor performance and absences, paid wages up to dismissal and offered pay in lieu of notice, but accepted it never sought the inspector's authorisation [Para 79][Para 81][Para 87][Para 93].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must take back all three union founders and pay their wages from 26 April 2010 until they return; both limbs of Issue 2 were refused, the first for want of particulars and the second as a lawful exercise of managerial discretion [Para 145][Para 146][Para 147].

As a threshold, the panel applied Article 268 of the Labour Law and Article 5(1) of Prakas No. 305: a union acquires the rights of representation only once registered and recognised, so an unrecognised union cannot bring a collective claim, and the panel would consider only the workers actually present at the hearing [Para 97][Para 98][Para 99][Para 102][Para 103].

On the living-cost allowance, the panel applied point 1 of Notification No. 032/08 of 17 April 2008 and the tripartite minutes of 5 May 2008, which require the USD 6 to be paid to every worker, new or long-serving, "without setting any condition", and expressly forbid merging it into the basic wage [Para 106][Para 107][Para 110][Para 111]. It read "without any condition" as covering both authorised unpaid leave and unauthorised absence, following Awards Nos 119/08 and 139/08, so the company's pro-rata deductions were wrong in principle [Para 112][Para 113][Para 115][Para 117]. It could not, however, order repayment: the workers never identified who had been deducted from or how much, even after the panel ordered particulars by 7 June 2010, so the claim failed for want of proof of quantum [Para 119][Para 120][Para 121].

On the position allowance, the panel applied Article 2 of the Labour Law, which gives the employer the right to manage the enterprise where the exercise is lawful and reasonable [Para 124][Para 126][Para 128]. Because the allowance is paid only to team leaders and translators and exceeds what the law requires, withholding it pro rata for days absent was a reasonable managerial arrangement [Para 129][Para 130][Para 131].

On Issue 5 the panel applied Article 293 and Article 4(1) of Prakas No. 305: founders and workers who join a union while its registration is pending are protected like shop stewards, and protection continues for 30 days after registration, so dismissal requires the inspector's consent [Para 134][Para 135][Para 136][Para 138]. The union applied on 23 April 2010; the three were founders and were dismissed on 26 April 2010 without authorisation, so the dismissals were procedurally unlawful and reinstatement with back pay followed [Para 142][Para 143][Para 144][Para 147].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Protection under Article 293 and Article 4(1) of Prakas No. 305 attaches from the moment a registration application is filed, not from the moment the union is recognised: a founder or member dismissed without the labour inspector's consent in that window is reinstated with wages from dismissal, however genuine the performance complaint [Para 138][Para 143]. Conversely, a statutory allowance must be paid without deduction even for unauthorised absence, but a claim for arrears still fails if the claimant will not identify the workers and amounts, and an extra-statutory allowance remains subject to lawful managerial adjustment [Para 115][Para 119][Para 130].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that it would look only at the workers present at the hearing, because the union was unregistered, frames the case but does not decide any of the three demands on its own [Para 103].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's name and the union's name are each printed several ways, and the award contains untranslated OCR debris inside a party's submission ("S: among the attaction . Herope was a mediation the firmance") [Para 105][Para 29][Para 34]. The panel chair's name is lost in the signature block to digits and fragments, and the worker-appointed arbitrator's name is partly garbled there [Para 149]. Two of the three dismissed men's ages are given as born in 1986 and on 7 October 1985, with the employer asserting they did not meet the 25-year age requirement for union office, but the award never prints the provision it relies on [Para 66][Para 67]. The award also records that the union had no registration certificate, while elsewhere it records a registration receipt issued on 23 April 2010 — the two are reported as printed rather than reconciled [Para 36][Para 69].
