# Award No. 175/09-APSARAuthority — APSARAuthority v Building and Wood Workers Trade Union Federation of Cambodia (BWTUC) and Water and Forest Conservation Union of APSARAuthority (WFCUA) (Arbitration Council, 5 February 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Panel: Pen Bunchhea (Chair), Seng Vuoch Hun, An Nan · Doc ID 140465

## 1. Facts
APSARAuthority, established by Royal Decree NS/RKT/0295/12 dated 19 February 1995, employs two kinds of worker: civil servants and contractual daily-paid workers [Para 18]. The 14 workers behind this claim were daily-paid cleaners and shrub-clearing workers earning 6,000 riel, or 7,000 to 8,000 riel, per day and holding only verbal contracts [Para 19][Para 27][Para 32]. They were union leaders and members: the Water and Forest Conservation Union of APSARAuthority (WFCUA) is affiliated to the Building and Wood Workers Trade Union Federation of Cambodia (BWTUC), and WFCUA's registration certificate No. 1073 KKBVIVK dated 14 December 2006 lists Mr B as President and Mr Y as Vice-President [Para 20][Para 21]. On 22 December 2006 the employer dismissed them [Para 36]. After the Council ordered a name list, only three individuals pressed the demand — Mr B, Mr Y and Mr N — so the panel confined itself to them [Para 23][Para 24][Para 43]. At the hearing the worker party withdrew its maternity-leave and illness-support demands, leaving paid-holiday back pay and reinstatement [Para 12].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether APSARAuthority must back pay wages for paid holidays it did not provide in the past to its daily-paid workers? [Para 24][Para 47]
2. (Issue 2) Whether APSARAuthority must reinstate Mr B, Mr Y and Mr N, dismissed on 22 December 2006? [Para 33]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — BWTUC and WFCUA:** the employer has never paid wages for public holidays, although daily-paid workers received their monthly pay on the 5th or 6th and worked since 2005, so back pay is owed for the whole period of service [Para 25][Para 26][Para 32][Para 46]. The dismissals were union discrimination: a manager called the workers in and told them there was no more work for union members and that they must resign from the union to keep their jobs, and a former union secretary was reinstated only after resigning [Para 34][Para 35][Para 37][Para 66].
**Employer (respondent) — APSARAuthority:** the Authority runs many projects and hires regular workers, who are paid on holidays, alongside casual daily workers who work only three or four days a week and may send a relative as a substitute; casual workers are therefore not entitled to holiday wages [Para 27][Para 29][Para 31][Para 32]. It denied discrimination, noting that the manager concerned has resigned and that it permitted the union to register, but offered no evidence against the workers' account [Para 38][Para 39][Para 69].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered APSARAuthority to back pay holiday wages to Mr B, Mr Y and Mr N from the date each commenced work until dismissal, and to reinstate all three [Para 72][Para 73].
On Issue 1 the panel split the question. Under Article 120 of the Labour Law the wage claim lapses three years from the date the wage fell due; the Siem Reap labour department received the claim on 27 December 2006, five days after the dismissals and within three years of the 2005 commencement, so the claim was in time [Para 47][Para 49][Para 50]. On the merits, Article 10 puts casual workers under the same rules and gives them the same rights as regular workers, an interpretation the panel had already adopted in Awards 97/08-Eternity Apparel (issue 10) and 108/09-Ying Jang (issue 6) [Para 51][Para 53]. Article 161 paragraph 1 and Prakas No. 10 SKBY dated 4 February 1999 require the employer to grant the paid holidays declared each year [Para 54]. The Authority's practice of paying regular but not casual workers was therefore incorrect, and back pay ran from commencement to dismissal rather than from any shorter limitation window [Para 55].
On Issue 2 the panel treated the two registered leaders and the ordinary member differently but reached the same result. Mr B and Mr Y appear on the registration certificate as President and Vice-President, so they enjoyed the protection of Article 293, which forbids dismissal of a shop steward without Labour Inspector authorisation, read with Clause 4 of Prakas No. 305 SKBY dated 22 November 2001 [Para 58][Para 61][Para 63]. No authorisation was requested before the 22 December 2006 dismissals, so the terminations were procedurally unlawful and reinstatement followed, consistently with Award 149/08-Cambo Handsome (issue 1) [Para 63][Para 64]. For Mr N the panel applied Articles 12 and 279, which forbid using union membership in dismissal decisions, and the established rule that the worker bears the burden of proving discrimination [Para 64][Para 65]. The workers' unopposed account of the 21 December 2006 meeting, the dismissal the next day, the reinstatement of a leader who resigned from the union, and the employer's bare denial with no supporting evidence made the denial not credible; the dismissal of Mr N therefore involved union discrimination and reinstatement was ordered, following Awards 28/07-Dae Kwang, 123/07-E-Garment and 06/08-Kingsland [Para 67][Para 68][Para 71][Para 72]. The award is non-binding and becomes binding eight days after notification unless a party lodges written opposition [Para 74].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Daily-paid or "casual" workers hold the same entitlement to paid public holidays as regular workers under Article 10 of the Labour Law, so an employer that has paid holiday wages only to regular staff must back pay the difference for the whole of each worker's service, cut off only by the three-year limitation in Article 120 [Para 53][Para 55]. Separately, dismissing a registered union leader without prior Labour Inspector authorisation under Article 293 is unlawful on procedure alone, whatever the employer's motive, and reinstatement follows [Para 63].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the employer let the deadline for a written objection pass, leaving the workers' documentary evidence unrebutted [Para 40][Para 41].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The source is an unofficial English translation of the Khmer original and its OCR is faulty throughout: the employer's name is printed as "APSARAuthority", articles and pronouns are fused into words ("themployer", "thearing", "Traing" for Training), so quoted statutory wording is not reliable. The hearing date appears as 19 January 2010 in the procedural heading but as 19 January 2009 in two paragraphs, and one paragraph fixes the first listing at 4 January 2009, a year the events cannot occupy [Para 2][Para 7][Para 9]. The recital of the order on back pay names only two of the three workers, dropping Mr Y's name [Para 55]. Individual workers, the manager and the union officers are reduced to initials here because the award prints them in full; the registration certificate's third office-holder is likewise not reproduced [Para 21]. No Khmer text of the award body survives in the file, so the original party names cannot be given in Khmer.
