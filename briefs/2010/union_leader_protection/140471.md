# Award No. 178/09-Kee Royal — Kee Royal (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. v Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers Democratic Union (C.CAWDU) and Local Union of C.CAWDU (Arbitration Council, 2 February 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Panel: Kong Phallack (Chair), Mar Samborana, Ven Pov · Doc ID 140471

## 1. Facts
Kee Royal (Cambodia) Co., Ltd., a factory on National Road 2, Chak Angre Krom, Meanchey, Phnom Penh, has operated since 2008 and employs 450 workers; two unions exist there, CIUF and the Local Union of C.CAWDU, the latter registered on 25 November 2009 and the claimant here [Para 14][Para 4][Para 18]. The union secretary, Ms N, completed probation from 27 November 2008 to 17 January 2009 and then held two successive six-month fixed duration contracts running to 19 January 2010; she was elected secretary on 18 October 2009 [Para 16][Para 17][Para 18]. On 5 November 2009 the employer discovered that her Khmer identity card was false, suspended her the same day, and on 10 November 2009 asked the Ministry of Labour for authority to dismiss her; no answer had arrived by the hearing and no appeal to the Minister was filed [Para 19][Para 20][Para 21][Para 50]. The union treasurer, Ms S, worked a two-month probation from 20 August to 20 October 2009, was elected on 18 October 2009, and was told on 15 October 2009 that her probationary contract would not be renewed [Para 32][Para 33][Para 59].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer discriminated against the local union and restricted freedom of association by suspending Ms N, and whether she must be reinstated with back pay for the suspension period? [Para 14][Para 41]
2. (Issue 1) Whether the refusal to renew Ms S's probationary contract was union discrimination, and whether she must be reinstated? [Para 52][Para 54]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — Local Union of C.CAWDU and C.CAWDU:** when the employer learned of the 18 October 2009 leadership election it abruptly dismissed the union leaders, suspended the secretary, refused to negotiate on the union's 25 November 2009 letter, and terminated the treasurer's contract, so it restricted freedom of association and must reinstate both with back pay from termination or suspension to reinstatement [Para 2][Para 3][Para 14].
**Employer (respondent) — Kee Royal (Cambodia) Co., Ltd.:** there is no discrimination, since two unions operate at the factory and the union president still works there; the secretary was suspended because she used her sister's identity card and punched in for other workers, and the treasurer was not renewed for lateness and carelessness with fibre, her probation having shown unsatisfactory performance [Para 4][Para 5][Para 6][Para 7][Para 28][Para 29][Para 34].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to reinstate Ms N and back pay her wages from the date of suspension to the date of reinstatement, and rejected the demand to reinstate Ms S [Para 63].
On Ms N the panel applied Article 293 of the Labour Law, under which a shop steward may be dismissed only with Labour Inspector authorisation, read with Clause 4 paragraph 2 of Prakas No. 305 SKBY dated 22 November 2001 extending that protection to union leaders, and followed Award 149/08-Cambo Handsome on the Inspector's duty to decide within one month [Para 41][Para 42]. The request was lodged on 10 November 2009 and remained unanswered at the hearing of 12 January 2010, so by Awards 149/08-Cambo Handsome and 95/09-Tack Fat the request is deemed rejected [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45][Para 46]. Under Article 295 and Article 296, a suspension pending the Inspector's decision falls away if authorisation is refused, so the suspension was null and void [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49]. Reinstatement with back pay was ordered, subject to the Minister's decision if the employer appeals within two months [Para 50].
On Ms S the panel stated the burden rule: a worker alleging union discrimination must prove it, following Awards 56/09-Suntex (issue 2) and 61/09-Cintri (issue 1), under Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law [Para 56][Para 57]. The decisive fact was timing — the employer notified her of non-renewal on 15 October 2009, three days before she was elected, so it could not have acted on her union role [Para 59]. On the alternative ground the panel applied Article 68, which caps probation at three months for regular employees, two for specialised and one for non-specialised workers, and requires the employer to fix and notify the probation dates [Para 61][Para 62]. Following Award 27/03-Standard (issue 1), an employer may decline to renew a probationary contract without giving full reasons where performance is judged unsatisfactory, and her careless use of fibre supported that judgment [Para 63].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer suspends a protected union leader and asks the Labour Inspector for authority to dismiss, silence for more than one month is deemed a refusal, and the suspension thereupon becomes null and void, so reinstatement with back pay follows unless the employer appeals to the Minister in time [Para 45][Para 48][Para 50]. A non-renewal decided before the worker's election to union office cannot be union discrimination, and a probationary contract may be allowed to expire without reasons where performance was unsatisfactory [Para 59][Para 63].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel remarked that an employer's tolerance of two unions and the continued employment of the union president told against a general practice of discrimination, though that observation was not the ground of either ruling [Para 26][Para 27][Para 58].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The evidence section is expressly omitted from the English version, so the documents relied on cannot be checked from this file [Para 13]. The OCR fuses articles with following words throughout ("the employer" as "hemployer", "the evidence" as "thevidence", "at the" as "athe"), and one paragraph carries a duplicated clause about non-renewal [Para 34]. The award gives two different dates for the employer's request for authorisation to dismiss Ms N — 17 November 2009 in the recital of the claim and 10 November 2009 in the reasons, a difference that matters to the one-month computation [Para 3][Para 42]. Ms S is described as treasurer when elected and as secretary later in the same reasoning [Para 33][Para 59]. The two union leaders at the centre of the case are named in full in the award and are reduced to initials here, as are the employer's administration head, interpreter and the union officers.
