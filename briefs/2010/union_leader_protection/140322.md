# Award No. 116/10 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន វាំយ ថិច ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) and សហជីព កម្ពុជា ដើម្បី សិទ្ធិ កម្មករ នៃ រោងចក្រ វាយ ថិច (Arbitration Council, 29 November 2010, corrected 2 December 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Tuon Si Phan, Pen Bun Chhea) · Doc ID 140322

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន វាំយ ថិច ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា), a garment factory at Trapeang Thleng, Chom Chao, Dangkao, with 1,800 workers, saw a strike from 31 August 2010 over the conduct of its head of administration, Mr C. [Para 46][Para 71][Para 76]
On 2 September 2010 the company suspended five union leaders and on 6 and 25 September dismissed or suspended two worker representatives and a group of workers said to be union activists; the union's demand was reinstatement of five leaders, two representatives and 23 workers, plus conversion of casual workers to permanent status. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 89][Para 130]
The employer did not attend the 5 November 2010 hearing; the panel proceeded in its absence and later issued a correction changing 19 workers to 23. [Para 15][Para 17][Para 107][Para 190]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must remove Mr C from the post of head of administration under the agreement of 21 July 2010? [Para 50][Para 108]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must take back the five union leaders, the two worker representatives and the 23 workers it suspended or dismissed after the strike? [Para 130][Para 183]
3. (Issue 3) Whether casual workers who have worked continuously for more than two months must be converted to permanent status with equal benefits? [Para 160][Para 185]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព កម្ពុជា ដើម្បី សិទ្ធិ កម្មករ and the វាយ ថិច factory union, through Mr C.V., its president:** Mr C insulted workers, threw away their food, photographed those who clocked in for absent colleagues and posted the pictures, so the company promised on 21 July 2010 to remove him and must honour that; the suspensions and dismissals were retaliation for the strike and lacked any Labour Inspector authorisation; and casual workers doing eight-hour days for months receive none of the benefits permanent workers get. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 89][Para 95][Para 108]
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន វាំយ ថិច ហ្គាមេន:** by its letters and its summary of 18 September 2010, the company said it would not take the leaders and workers back because they led and incited the 31 August to 18 September strike, and it asked for a postponement of the hearing on grounds of meetings and its director's absence abroad. [Para 90][Para 103]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand to remove the head of administration is refused (Issue 1); the company must take back the five union leaders and, as corrected, the 23 workers as well as the two worker representatives (Issue 2); and casual workers with more than two months' continuous service must be made permanent and given equal benefits (Issue 3). [Para 182][Para 183][Para 184][Para 185][Para 186][Para 206][Para 207]
The panel first held it could sit without the employer: Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 permits continuation where a properly summoned party fails to appear, following Awards 53/04, 63/04, 148/07, 99/09 and 118/09, and the employer's business meetings and director's trip were not a sufficient reason to postpone a dispute already two months old. [Para 100][Para 102][Para 106][Para 107]
On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 1 of Decree No. 38 KR.Ch: a contract binds its parties, a third party cannot terminate it, and the workers produced no evidence that Mr C still held the administrative post, since the company's Notice 005/10 recorded that he had left that department on 20 August 2010 and moved to marketing. The claim failed. [Para 111][Para 114][Para 115][Para 122][Para 128][Para 129]
On Issue 2 the panel applied Articles 293 and 295: dismissal of a shop steward or worker representative requires the Labour Inspector's authorisation, silence beyond one month is a refusal, and suspension pending that decision presupposes serious misconduct. The company suspended the five leaders on 2 September 2010 without ever requesting authorisation, so the suspensions were unlawful. [Para 130][Para 132][Para 134][Para 141][Para 146][Para 147]
As to the 23 workers, the panel held that participating in a strike, lawful or not, is not serious misconduct under Article 83(5), relying on Awards 20/05, 08/05, 76/04, 70/04 and 125/09, and that Articles 332 and 333 require workers to be taken back when a strike ends and forbid any sanction for having struck. Dismissing them after the strike therefore had no valid reason and reinstatement was ordered. [Para 144][Para 145][Para 150][Para 151][Para 153][Para 155][Para 159][Para 206]
On Issue 3 the panel applied Articles 9 and 10, which give casual and permanent workers the same rights and duties, and followed Award 97/08 in holding that the label matters less than the rights: the company's casuals worked eight-hour days for six or seven months without annual leave, seniority pay, five per cent end-of-contract payment or paid holidays, so they were to be converted and equalised. [Para 164][Para 169][Para 171][Para 177][Para 178][Para 179][Para 181][Para 186]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of a union leader or worker representative is lawful only where serious misconduct is alleged and a request for authorisation goes to the Labour Inspector; suspending first and never requesting authorisation makes the suspension void and reinstatement follows. [Para 141][Para 143][Para 146]
Taking part in a strike is never serious misconduct, so dismissal for having struck is unlawful and the workers must be taken back when the strike ends, whatever the strike's procedural regularity. [Para 151][Para 153][Para 159]
Casual and permanent workers hold the same statutory rights; continuous casual service beyond about two months converts into permanent status with the full benefit package. [Para 169][Para 177][Para 181]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that only the parties to an employment contract may end it, and urged the employer and both unions to accept the correction with indulgence. [Para 115][Para 209]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award and its own correction conflict: the operative order takes back 19 workers, and the correction of 2 December 2010 changes that figure to 23 and adds two worker representatives whom the original order had not dealt with. [Para 159][Para 184][Para 190][Para 206][Para 207]
The date the head of administration returned to work is printed as 30 July 2010 in the reasoning but 30 August 2010 in the facts and the recounting of the dismissal letters, and one conciliation minute is dated 13 October 2018. [Para 56][Para 74][Para 117][Para 44]
The company name appears as វាំយ ថិច, វាយ ថិច and វ៉ាយ តិច in different places, the union is at one point called a democratic garment union that does not exist in this case, and the tables of suspended workers are column-scrambled with duplicated rows. [Para 1][Para 46][Para 69][Para 102]
Several individual names survive in the award's lists and in the correction; only initials and role descriptions are used in this brief. [Para 69][Para 205]
