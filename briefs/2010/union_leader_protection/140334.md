# Order No. 127/10 — Mita Bay Omed (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន មី តា បាយ អូមេដ) and the Khmer Youth Union of Mita Bay Omed (Arbitration Council, 24 December 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry អ៊ុក រី; Liv Sovanna លីវ សុវណ្ណា; Kong Phalla គង់ ផល្លៈ presiding) · Doc ID 140334

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន មី តា បាយ អូមេដ (Mita Bay Omed (Cambodia) Co., Ltd), a maker of dental and medical equipment that began operating in 2009 with about 620 workers at Tra Village, Stung Meanchey, Meanchey District [Para 1][Para 66], moved its premises to Chom Chao, Dangkao District, on 11 October 2010 because the lease at the old site had expired and the premises were cramped [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74][Para 75]. It notified all workers in writing on 1 October 2010 and offered a travel allowance of USD 5 for two months [Para 76]. The Khmer Youth Union of the company (សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ), one of two unions in the factory, lodged an eleven-point collective demand on 23 September 2010 [Para 14][Para 67][Para 68]. Seven points were settled in conciliation on 4 November 2010 and four were referred; Issues 2 and 3 were then agreed at the hearing and Issue 4 — reinstatement of the dismissed union president — was withdrawn by the employer's notice of 13 December 2010, leaving only the travel allowance [Para 15][Para 16][Para 19][Para 20][Para 21]. The union was elected on 9 November 2010, applied for registration on 19 November 2010, and had still received no registration when the panel decided [Para 69][Para 70][Para 91]. Forty-four workers thumbprinted an authorization for it [Para 86][Para 96].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay USD 5 a month for five months as travel allowance to the 44 workers who moved from the old premises at Stung Meanchey to the new premises at Chom Chao, rather than the two months it has already paid? [Para 72][Para 79][Para 99]

The three other referred points were settled by agreement or withdrawn and were not decided [Para 19][Para 20][Para 21].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ of ក្រុមហ៊ុន មី តា បាយ អូមេដ:** the new site is about four kilometres away in a remote area where rental housing is hard to find, so five months of allowance is what gives workers time to relocate; the employer earns enough to pay it, and the payment is a legal obligation [Para 78][Para 79][Para 80][Para 81]. On 7 December 2010 they also objected that the thumbprints on the employer's agreement letter signified only acceptance of the USD 65 minimum wage, not of the travel allowance, which had been mentioned only orally [Para 83][Para 84].

**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន មី តា បាយ អូមេដ:** it can pay only two months, because it moved on 11 October 2010 [Para 3]. It gave written notice on 1 October 2010, paid USD 5 for two months, and holds an agreement letter thumbprinted by the workers' representatives and the full-rights delegate [Para 76][Para 82].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the demand of the 44 workers that the company pay USD 5 for five months as travel allowance from the old to the new premises [Para 119]

The panel first asked whether the union could represent the 44 workers at all [Para 87]. Article 268 of the Labour Law provides that a professional organization acquires the rights the Labour Law recognizes only when its statute and list of responsible officers are deposited with the Ministry, and that a complete application unanswered for two months is deemed registered [Para 87][Para 88][Para 89]. On the facts the union was elected on 9 November 2010 and had no registration, so it did not yet enjoy those rights, which prior orders (62/06, 30/08, 31/08, 120/09, 161/09, 40/10) held include the right to represent members before the panel [Para 91][Para 92][Para 93][Para 94]. Article 19 of Prakas 099 nevertheless lets a party appear through a person authorized in writing, and 44 workers had thumbprinted such an authorization, so the panel considered their claim alone [Para 95][Para 96][Para 97].

On the merits the panel applied Article 2 of the Labour Law and the employer's prerogative to organize and manage the enterprise, following Orders 108/06 and 08/09 and Orders 17/03 and 18/03-Ho Hing, which allow relocation provided there is no wage cut, the place is not too distant, shifts are unchanged and skills are not mismatched [Para 99][Para 101][Para 103]. It then followed Order 29/04: the Labour Law does not require an employer to pay daily transport when the enterprise relocates, although an employer may pay an allowance to keep skilled workers, and such payment is not a legal obligation [Para 107][Para 108][Para 109].

Weighing the facts, the panel found the four-kilometre distance could genuinely inconvenience workers living near the old site [Para 112][Para 113]. But the employer had paid USD 5 for two months precisely to give them time to find housing near the new site, and about 80 per cent of the workforce already lived near it, so two months was a reasonable period [Para 114][Para 115][Para 116][Para 117]. The demand for five months accordingly failed [Para 118][Para 119].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that relocates its enterprise owes no statutory travel allowance to workers who move with it: payment is a discretionary measure to retain staff, and where the employer has already funded a transition period that is long enough, judged by the distance and by how many workers have actually resettled, a demand for a longer period will be rejected; and a union not yet registered under Article 268 cannot bring such a claim for its members, only for workers who have authorized it in writing [Para 94][Para 97][Para 109][Para 117].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that the parties disputed only the allowance and not the distance of the move, and its statement that the parties agreed on 29 November 2010 to accept this order as immediately binding [Para 105][Para 120].
