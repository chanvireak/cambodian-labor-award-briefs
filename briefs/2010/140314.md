# Award No. 101/10 — Tripos International National (Cambodia) Co Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ត្រី ប៉ូស អ៊ិនធើណេសិន ណល (ខេមបូឌា) ខូ អិលធីឌី) (Arbitration Council, 28 October 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborna for the employer party, An Virak for the worker party, Kong Phalla chair) · Doc ID 140314

## 1. Facts
Tripos International National (Cambodia) Co Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ត្រី ប៉ូស អ៊ិនធើណេសិន ណល (ខេមបូឌា) ខូ អិលធីឌី) has run a shoe factory at Village Chrok Romeat, Khum Chhuk, Kampong Tralach district, Kampong Chhnang province, since June 2008 with about 2,400 workers. [Para 41][Para 42] The Free Workers' Union of Cambodia (សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ នៃ ព្រះរាជាណាចក្រ កម្ពុជា) and its Tripos shoe factory union filed four demands on 12 August 2010; one was settled in conciliation and three came to the panel, which the union brought without holding most-representative status. [Para 10][Para 11][Para 34][Para 43] From 2 June 2010 the company required a card to enter the toilets, two cards being issued per production group of about 52 workers, and it also required 108 workers who had been hired on other people's identity cards to switch to their own cards and sign fresh contracts with new seniority. [Para 52][Para 54][Para 55][Para 70][Para 75]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must issue five toilet-entry cards per production group instead of the two now issued? [Para 3][Para 44][Para 96]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must preserve the seniority of the 108 workers who changed back to their own identity cards, and pay the seniority bonus on that earlier service? [Para 4][Para 70][Para 71]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay the 5 per cent end-of-contract indemnity each time a six-month fixed-duration contract expired and was renewed? [Para 5][Para 84][Para 91]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), the Free Workers' Union of Cambodia (សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ នៃ ព្រះរាជាណាចក្រ កម្ពុជា) and the Tripos shoe factory union (សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ នៃ រោងចក្រ ស្បែកជើង ត្រី ប៉ូស):** two cards for 52 workers mean a worker who finds both in use must wait for one to come back, so five are needed; the cards also serve for fetching drinking water; seniority must run from first hire because the identity swap was known to the company from the start; and the 5 per cent indemnity is owed for every six-month contract that expired and was renewed. [Para 58][Para 68][Para 69][Para 72][Para 87][Para 90][Para 91]
**Employer (respondent), Tripos International National (Cambodia) Co Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ត្រី ប៉ូស អ៊ិនធើណេសិន ណល (ខេមបូឌា) ខូ អិលធីឌី):** each group has a group leader and an assistant who hold one card each and cover the work of whoever is away, so more cards would need more cover staff and would break the production line; the workers agreed to fresh contracts with new seniority; and the 5 per cent indemnity is payable only where the worker leaves when the contract ends. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 64][Para 75][Para 92]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel refused five toilet cards but ordered the employer to issue three per group of about 52 workers; it ordered the employer to count the seniority of the 108 workers from their first day of work and pay the seniority bonus on that basis; and it refused the 5 per cent end-of-contract indemnity. [Para 169][Para 170][Para 171][Para 172]

On issue 1 the panel applied Prakas No. 052 SKBY of 10 February 2000, article 1 of which requires the employer to provide suitable, hygienic latrines on the premises and article 2 of which fixes the number by headcount — three latrines for between 36 and 55 workers of each sex. [Para 98][Para 99][Para 101] It read that table as implying that three latrines must be usable at the same time, so at least three entry cards are needed for a group of about 52. [Para 102][Para 107][Para 108] The employer's argument that extra cards would disrupt the line was rejected: a company must comply with the law in force to secure workers' welfare in using the latrines. [Para 109][Para 110][Para 111] Five cards, however, had no basis in law or agreement, and in any case five workers could not enter three latrines at once. [Para 113][Para 114]

On issue 2 the panel found that using another person's identity card is a serious fault under article 83(b)(2) of the Labour Law, and that under article 26 (new) paragraph 2 an employer that learns of a serious fault and does not dismiss within seven days loses the right to do so. [Para 119][Para 120][Para 121][Para 122] Here the company did not dismiss the 108 workers but kept them on, signing new contracts said to carry new seniority. [Para 124][Para 125] Since the employment relationship had in fact continued without a break from first hire, seniority continued too, and it ends only when the worker stops working for the enterprise. [Para 126][Para 127][Para 128][Para 130] The agreement to accept new seniority was void under Article 13(1), because it gave the workers less than the law allows, and an agreement less favourable than the law is void by operation of law. [Para 134][Para 135][Para 137][Para 140][Para 141]

On issue 3 the panel applied article 73(a)(6), which requires an end-of-contract indemnity at least equal to 5 per cent of the wages paid during the contract, and found the phrase "when the contract ends" open to more than one reading. [Para 146][Para 148][Para 149][Para 150][Para 151] Following Awards 60/06-Pumek Garment and 76/06, it held that the indemnity is owed only where the employment relationship is severed: where the fixed-duration contract is renewed at its end, the relationship continues and the benefit carries over. [Para 153][Para 155][Para 157][Para 158] Some workers had renewed six-month contracts for over two years, which converted their contracts into indefinite ones, but on either view nothing was severed and nothing was due. [Para 163][Para 164][Para 167][Para 168]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Sanitary rules that fix the number of latrines by headcount also fix the number of workers who must be able to use them at once, so the employer must issue at least that many entry cards; a demand for more cards than there are latrines is not grantable. Where an employer discovers that workers were hired on other people's identity cards, a serious fault under article 83, and does not dismiss within seven days, it waives dismissal, and the employment relationship then runs unbroken — so an agreement by those workers to accept fresh seniority is void under Article 13(1) as less favourable than the law. The 5 per cent end-of-contract indemnity falls due only when the employment relationship is severed, not on each renewal of a fixed-duration contract. [Para 102][Para 122][Para 134][Para 153]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the factory had four buildings with an equal number of toilets, that the latrines themselves were properly arranged, and that the only defect was the number of cards — none of which decided the issue. [Para 46][Para 47]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The demand as restated in the reasoning gives the number of cards sought as "២៥០០០" per group, an impossible figure that contradicts the five requested elsewhere in the same award. [Para 96] The employer's name is printed with several variants of its first element and of the "Co Ltd" suffix, including "ខ្នូ" in the party block. [Para 1][Para 19] Modern years "2016" and "2017" sit inside a 2010 award, at the union's address and before the procedural heading. [Para 2][Para 10] The union's membership figure is lost to a fragment. [Para 43] The evidence section lists individual workers by full name, some in a run-on list of nine or more, and those names are not reproduced here. [Para 31][Para 32][Para 52] Evidence item numbering again runs ១៩ and ១០ for 9 and 10. [Para 24][Para 25]
