# Award No. 147/11 — Hung Wah (Cambodia) Garment Manufacturing Ltd (Arbitration Council, 14 December 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun, Ann Vireak, Kong Phallack presiding) · Doc ID 140091

## 1. Facts
The employer manufactures garments in Phnom Penh with 2,200 workers; the claimant is the FTUWKC local union, registered 14 January 2008, which states it represents 150 workers, while 939 workers signed a list authorising it to act and the workers claimed 1,000 were involved. [Para 17][Para 32] The workers demanded that workers in the Training, Finishing and Quality Control Sections receive the monthly US$ 3 incentive bonus paid to workers in the Sewing Section. [Para 1] The employer says the US$ 3 is not an incentive bonus but part of piece-rate wages for Sewing workers whose wages incorporate a US$ 6 living allowance, and that it now pays a daily 500, 1,000 or 2,000 riel incentive to all sections and plans to extend it to all workers. [Para 18][Para 25][Para 26]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether workers in the Training, Finishing and Quality Control Sections are entitled to the monthly US$ 3 incentive bonus that the employer provides to workers in the Sewing Section? [Para 1][Para 29]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Equal pay for the same work under Article 36, paragraph 2 of the Constitution and Article 106 of the Labour Law; Training workers are multi-skilled and perform all tasks except sewing, Finishing workers trim, iron and pack, Quality Control workers check finished goods, and all receive the same US$ 61 main wage plus seniority and attendance bonuses and work as hard as Sewing workers. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24][Para 37]
**Employer (respondent):** The US$ 3 attaches to piece-rate Sewing wages incorporating the US$ 6 living allowance; a new daily incentive of 500, 1,000 or 2,000 riels is already paid in each section and will be extended to all. [Para 18][Para 25][Para 26]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the demand for a monthly US$ 3 incentive bonus for the Training, Finishing and Quality Control Sections was rejected; the award is binding and final and enforceable under the MoU of 28 September 2010, with no interests dispute determined. [Para 40][Para 42]

The panel first classified the demand as a rights dispute, being a claim to an entitlement in the law, applying Awards 05/11-M&V, 13/11-Gold Kamvimex, 14/11-GHG and 37/11-ASD. [Para 28][Para 29] On standing, it applied clause 19 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, which requires written authorisation, and found that 939 workers had authorised the local union, so it had standing; it declined to examine whether the thumbprints were genuine, a matter it held itself not competent to decide, and the employer did not object to the list. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 32][Para 33][Para 34][Para 35] On the merits, Article 106 of the Labour Law requires equal wages for equal conditions, professional skill and output, and Award 68/04-City New permits a difference in professionalism or productivity to justify a wage difference. [Para 37][Para 38] The panel found that workers in the Training, Finishing and Quality Control Sections have different skills, perform different tasks and achieve different productivity from Sewing workers, so Article 106 did not apply and no entitlement to the US$ 3 arose. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where workers in one section perform different tasks with different professional skill and output from workers in another section, Article 106 of the Labour Law does not require the employer to extend to them an incentive bonus paid to the other section, even where the main wage is identical. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the employer has the right to direct and supervise its enterprise provided it does so lawfully and reasonably, following Awards 06/06-M&V and 108/06-Trinunggal, and noted that the parties had agreed to extend the award due date to 14 December 2011. [Para 12][Para 35]
