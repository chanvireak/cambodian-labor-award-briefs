# Award No. 023/16 — Cambo Kotop Ltd (Arbitration Council of Cambodia, 18 February 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Sok Mathoeung presiding; Ing Sothy appointed by the employer, An Nan appointed by the workers, dissenting) · Doc ID 138976

## 1. Facts
Cambo Kotop Ltd, registered by certificate No. Inv. 0158 E/2012 of 17 January 2012, makes garments and footwear at Kantoak Cheng Village, Kantoak Commune, Por Sen Chey District, Phnom Penh and employs about 1,400 workers. [Para 20][Para 21]
A TUFW local union was registered by the Ministry under certificate No. 3344 of 24 August 2015. The employer already deducted TUFW dues from 32 workers after a September 2015 request; on 1 December 2015 the union wrote asking it to deduct 2,000 riel a month from 210 workers who had joined TUFW and resigned from a rival CUF local union, and the employer refused to accept the letter. [Para 22][Para 27][Para 34]
Both parties attended the hearing of 5 February 2016; the award is non-binding. [Para 16][Para 18]

## 2. Issues in dispute
The panel recorded one issue, taken from conciliation report No. 123 of 27 January 2016, and divided it into parts A and B. [Para 10][Para 26]
1. (Part A) Whether the employer must deduct TUFW local union contribution fees from the wages of the 210 workers said to have become TUFW members? [Para 26][Para 42]
2. (Part B) Whether the employer must stop deducting CUF local union contribution fees from those same workers' wages, and pay back the fees deducted since they withdrew from the CUF? [Para 26][Para 27][Para 57]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the TUFW wrote twice, first with 32 thumbprints in September 2015 and then on 1 December 2015 with the thumbprints of 210 workers who had agreed to join TUFW and resign from CUF, asking for the 2,000 riel monthly deduction to move to TUFW and for CUF deductions to stop; the employer refused to accept the second letter and never answered it. [Para 27]
**Employer (respondent):** three local unions hold duplicate member names, TUFW and CUF have never resolved those overlaps, and some workers do not even know they have been enrolled, so deducting on the strength of the lists could drive workers to resign rather than complain; it deducts only on a proper written request, keeps deducting for the union a worker previously belonged to until a new request arrives, and never received the 1 December 2015 letter, which in any event was addressed to the union and sought membership, not deduction. [Para 28][Para 30][Para 32][Para 34][Para 37]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both demands were rejected: the employer was not ordered to deduct TUFW contribution fees, and not ordered to stop deducting CUF fees or to refund those already deducted. The award carries a dissenting opinion by the worker-appointed arbitrator annexed under clause 37 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 68]
The panel applied Article 129 of the Labour Law, which voids collective agreements permitting other wage deductions but allows a worker to authorise deduction of dues to the union to which he belongs, the authorisation to be in writing and revocable at any time. [Para 40][Para 41]
It read that article with clause 5 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, under which a member may ask the employer in writing at least 15 days ahead for dues to be deducted and the employer must implement the request, following Awards 03/03 (Tong Garment), 05/03 (Top One Garment), 16/05 (New Point), 88/11 and 89/11 (Dai Young Cambodia), 60/05 (Evergreen Apparel), 154/11 (B & N), 230/13 (Everay Textile Garment) and 62/04 (Seratex): the purpose of the writing requirement is to protect each worker from a deduction against his will and to prevent employer error. [Para 41][Para 43][Para 45][Para 47]
On part A the panel accepted that the 210 workers had genuinely agreed to join TUFW and pay dues, but found their letters were addressed to the TUFW and stated only a request to become members; the TUFW's own letter of 1 December 2015 did ask for deduction, but the union was not the party whose request the law requires. The demand therefore failed on form. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 54][Para 56]
On part B the panel found the resignation letters were addressed to the CUF president and said nothing to the employer about stopping deductions; no evidence of any request to the employer existed, so the employer could not be faulted for continuing, and no refund was owed. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 64][Para 66]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A deduction of union dues under Article 129 requires a written authorisation that reaches the employer and expresses the individual worker's consent to that deduction; a letter enrolling in, or resigning from, a union and addressed to the union — even where the union later asks the employer to act — is not such an authorisation, so the employer may neither deduct on it nor be required to stop an existing deduction or refund fees on the strength of it. [Para 46][Para 54][Para 63]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel added that the employer would be obliged to deduct TUFW fees if the union, acting for the workers, demanded the deduction directly, and would be obliged to stop CUF deductions if the 210 workers asked it directly in writing or through their representative — the outcome turned on who wrote and to whom, not on the merits of the members' choice. [Para 56][Para 64][Para 65]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
This is an unofficial English translation and the panel records that the documents, exhibits and other evidence considered were omitted from the English version, so the letters examined at paras 38–39 and 62–63 cannot be checked against the originals. [Para 19][Para 39][Para 67]
The dissent puts the number of resigning workers at "201" where the majority and the rest of the award say 210. [Para 27][Para 56][Para 71]
The text is OCR-damaged throughout, with "h" and "t" dropped at word boundaries ("themployer", "thearing", "Traing", "wit h"), and the employer's name is stable but the arbitration panel's chair appears only in the signature block, not in the running text. Several sentences are visibly duplicated or truncated by the OCR, notably the dissent's account of the employer refusing the letters. [Para 71]
The award refers to "Prakas no. 350" once where the provision applied is Prakas No. 305, and the local union president is printed as "Lor Lim" in the registration facts but "La Lim" in the representatives list. [Para 7][Para 22][Para 56]
Individual workers and union officials are reduced to initials; the arbitrator An Nan's dissent is dated Phnom Penh, 18 February 2016 and signed. [Para 72]
