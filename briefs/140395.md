# Award No. 71/09 — Hytex Apparel (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 16 July 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Hem Hour Naryth (employer-appointed), Tuon Siphann (worker-appointed), Pen Bunchhea (chair) · Doc ID 140395

## 1. Facts
Hytex Apparel (Cambodia) Ltd runs a factory at Trapang Tuol village, Angsnoul district, and employed about 200 workers until a strike on 5 June 2009 at 7:00 a.m., after which 50 remained [Para 22]. The claimant is the Local Union of the Khmer Youth Trade Union Federation (KYFTU), which claims 116 members but held only 32 after the strike and does not have most-representative status [Para 23]. Thirteen demands went to conciliation on 5 June 2009 and none was settled; at the hearing six (issues 2, 4, 7, 8, 9 and 11) were resolved by agreement, leaving seven for decision [Para 16][Para 18]. On 4 June 2009 the employer dismissed the union president, Mr Y N, and vice-president, Mr K V, both of the machine section, one day after the second notice of election results reached it, saying there was no more work [Para 27][Para 31][Para 67].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must reinstate Mr Y N and Mr K V to their positions in the machine section because they are union leaders? [Para 2]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay full wages, with back pay, for periods when it has no work for the workers? [Para 35]
3. (Issue 5) Whether the employer must pay cash in lieu of unused annual leave? [Para 44]
4. (Issue 6) Whether the employer must pay in lieu of notice when it ends fixed-duration contracts for an unspecified reason? [Para 48]
5. (Issue 10) Whether the employer must pay a monthly US$ 5 accommodation or transport allowance? [Para 54]
6. (Issue 12) Whether the employer must pay an additional US$ 5 a month to workers skilled on three machines? [Para 55]
7. (Issue 13) Whether the employer must pay an extra US$ 1 of seniority bonus for each year of service beyond five? [Para 59]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the two dismissals were anti-union discrimination, coming right after the election results were notified, and there is still plenty of work in the machine section [Para 26][Para 29]. On work shortages, the employer must pay full wages because it never suspended contracts lawfully; the 9–11 May 2009 unpaid days were forced on them even though they thumbprinted an agreement [Para 41][Para 43]. Annual leave should be paid in cash as before; notice pay is owed on the two terminated fixed-duration contracts; and the US$ 5 allowances and seniority increase are owed to reward and retain skilled workers [Para 45][Para 49][Para 58].
**Employer (respondent):** there was not enough work, so the two were dismissed for that reason; Mr Y N received 5% of wages and benefits despite early termination and Mr K V was paid under Article 73 [Para 63][Para 68][Para 69]. It acknowledges the second election notice but not the first, and does not recognise the leaders because they do not meet Article 269 [Para 24]. It cannot afford the allowances: orders fell with the global financial crisis, and it stopped the three-machine allowance a year ago [Para 56][Para 57].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to reinstate Mr Y N and Mr K V to their machine-section posts and to back pay 100% of the workers' wages for 9–11 May 2009; the annual-leave and notice-pay claims were rejected, and the three allowance and seniority claims were declined [Para 152][Para 153].

**Issue 1 — discrimination first.** Articles 12 and 279 forbid decisions on hiring, assignment, discipline or dismissal by reference to union membership; the workers carried the burden of proving it, and following Awards 112/06, 148/07, 158/08 and 40/09 the panel found no concrete evidence that anyone else was dismissed alongside them or that the redundancy was a pretext [Para 65][Para 67][Para 72]. **Protection.** Article 293 and clauses 3 and 4 of Prakas No. 305 protect candidates for union leadership from dismissal, subject to three conditions: entitlement, dismissal within the protected period, and notice to the employer by all reliable means [Para 76][Para 79][Para 81]. All three were met — candidacy in the 25 May 2009 election, dismissal on 4 June 2009 inside the window, and notification through the security guard, the second notice admitted [Para 82][Para 83][Para 85]. No authorisation from the labour inspector was shown, so the dismissals breached the Labour Law, and clause 34 of Prakas No. 099 empowered the panel to order reinstatement [Para 86][Para 88].
**Issue 3 — work shortages.** Article 71(11) allows suspension only for serious economic difficulty, for at most two months and under the labour inspector's control; where the employer fails to submit to that control it owes 100% of wages (Awards 74/07, 28/08, 136/08, 51/09) [Para 91][Para 92][Para 95]. The workers' thumbprinted agreement to unpaid leave was void under Article 13(1) because the law is of public order [Para 97][Para 98][Para 99].
**Issues 5 and 6 — rejected.** Article 167(3) voids any payment in lieu of annual leave while the worker is employed, so the employer's former practice was itself unlawful (Awards 45/05, 94/04, 98/07, 40/09) [Para 104][Para 106]. Article 73(5) requires advance notice only for contracts over six months, and the workers filed no evidence of contract type or service by the deadline [Para 117][Para 119].
**Issues 10, 12 and 13 — declined.** Each went beyond the Labour Law and any agreement, making them interests disputes, which a union without most-representative status cannot bring (Awards 57/04, 152/08, 48/09) [Para 132][Para 137][Para 151].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union leader who is a candidate in a union election is protected from dismissal and may be dismissed only with the labour inspector's prior authorisation, and where that authorisation is absent the panel will order reinstatement; and an agreement by workers to take unpaid leave while there is no work is void, so the employer owes full wages for those days unless it notified the labour inspector under Article 71(11) [Para 85][Para 88][Para 99].

**Obiter dicta:** a practice of providing free transport for less than two months is too short to become a binding verbal term of the contract, and payment in lieu of annual leave, though previously made, is contrary to Article 167 [Para 126][Para 106].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
This is an unofficial English translation, and the evidence section was omitted, so the documents weighing on the findings cannot be checked [Para 20]. The employer is styled Hytex Apparel (Cambodia) Ltd in the title and in the case name, but Hytex Garments (Cambodia) Pte. Ltd in the findings [Para 1][Para 21]. The translation drops a leading letter from many words ("themployer", "istipulated"), which corrupts those parts of the statutory quotations [Para 65][Para 66]. Issue 10 is pleaded as a US$ 6 allowance in the list of demands and argued as US$ 5 throughout the reasons [Para 10][Para 54]. The list of demands numbers its paragraphs inconsistently, with two separate paragraphs each marked "4" and one marked "10" twice, so the thirteen claims do not map cleanly onto the thirteen issue numbers used later [Para 4][Para 6][Para 10]. One worker's probationary contract is said to expire on 15 June 2009 but the termination is described as occurring when it expired on 15 May 2009, marked "[sic]" in the text [Para 50].
