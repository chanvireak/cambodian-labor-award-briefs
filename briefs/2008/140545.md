# Award No. 109/08-CD — Chading Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ចាឌីង) (Arbitration Council, 16 September 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ang Eng Thong presiding; Chhiv Phirem, employer nominee; Liv Sovanna, worker nominee) · Doc ID 140545

## 1. Facts
Chading Co., Ltd, at Prey Sala village, Kakab commune, Dangkao district, Phnom Penh, employed about 250 workers, of whom roughly 120 belonged to the Khmer Youth Union Federation at the Chading factory (សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ ប្រចាំ រោងចក្រ ចាឌីង), the claimant party. [Para 1][Para 26] The union's complaint of 9 July 2008 sought improved working conditions on three points; conciliation ended unsettled on all three on 13 August 2008 and the report of non-conciliation No. 922 dated 22 August 2008 reached the Secretariat on 25 August 2008. [Para 11][Para 12][Para 7] At the hearing of 2 September 2008 the employer did not appear, and the workers withdrew demands 2 and 3 — no deduction of seniority for absence, and a USD 6 cost-of-living allowance — because the company had already satisfied them, leaving only demand 1 for adjudication. [Para 10][Para 15][Para 16]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must let workers take special leave by charging it against annual leave days not yet used, while preserving their wage, their regular attendance bonus and their seniority bonus? [Para 27][Para 32]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), the Khmer Youth Union Federation at Chading factory:** special leave should be usable by offsetting it against unused annual leave and must not cost the worker his wage, regular attendance bonus or seniority bonus; the occasions meant were marriage, a spouse's childbirth, a child's marriage, and illness or death of a parent. [Para 2][Para 31]
**Employer (respondent), Chading Co., Ltd:** absent from the hearing and filing no documents, but on the conciliation record it accepts that workers may take special leave; it does so only by deducting wage, regular attendance bonus and seniority bonus, and it grants 18 days of annual leave but pays them out in cash calculated on the base wage instead of letting workers take them. [Para 28][Para 29][Para 30]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to charge special leave against the days of annual leave a worker has not yet used, and ordered workers to repay the company where their annual leave is exhausted or they have no annual-leave entitlement. [Para 46]
On Issue 1 the panel read Article 171 of the Labour Law, which lets an employer grant special leave for events bearing directly on the worker's family, together with Article 2 of Prakas No. 267 SKBY of 11 October 2001 on special leave, which allows the employer to deduct such leave from the worker's annual leave where the worker has not yet taken all of it, or to require repayment where he has. [Para 33][Para 36] It held that special leave may therefore be set against annual leave in the first case, and that in the second the employer may lawfully demand the worker repay the company. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 37][Para 38]
The panel distinguished the practice actually before it: the company was not setting special leave against remaining annual leave at all; it refused annual leave outright and paid money in lieu of it, which the panel, following its own earlier awards, called an application contrary to the law. [Para 39][Para 43] It expressly adopted the interpretation in Award No. 45/05-BN, Award No. 94/04-ENT and Award No. 98/07-Sky Sino, which had rejected cash-in-lieu claims as contrary to the spirit of Article 167 of the 1997 Labour Law. [Para 40][Para 42]
The order was framed in two limbs: the employer must deduct special leave against the annual leave days of any worker who has not used them up, wage included, and the worker must repay the company where he has used all his annual leave or is not yet entitled to annual leave. [Para 46] The award is not binding and becomes final eight days after notification unless a party objects in writing to the Minister of Labour through the Secretariat. [Para 47]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Special leave granted under Article 171 of the Labour Law and Prakas 267 is not a free-standing entitlement: it may be charged against a worker's accrued but unused annual leave, and once that leave is exhausted or not yet earned the employer may require the worker to repay the days taken, but the employer may not instead suppress annual leave and pay cash in lieu of it. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 44][Para 46]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded the workers' own list of qualifying occasions — marriage, a spouse's childbirth, a child's marriage, illness or death of a parent — without deciding the scope of "events bearing directly on the family", which was not necessary to its order. [Para 31][Para 33]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's name is printed ចាឌីង in the case name and header but appears as ថា ឌីង and ថា ឌឹង in the reasons and evidence list, so the company is named two ways. [Para 1][Para 26][Para 30][Para 38] The conciliation minutes in the evidence list are dated 13 July 2008 although the panel records the final conciliation as held on 13 August 2008. [Para 12][Para 19] The decision section repeats its second limb almost verbatim — a duplicated OCR pass — and para 43 carries a stray Latin fragment ("NAST 2017 , who the") and a broken page number, while para 23 begins with "/jh"; the substance of the order is recoverable. [Para 43][Para 46][Para 23] The signature block for the employer-nominated arbitrator is blank although the header names him, and the worker representatives' names in the header survive only partially, so their roles cannot be reliably paired with names; they are not reproduced here. [Para 1][Para 48]
