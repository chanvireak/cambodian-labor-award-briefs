# Award No. 153/08 — Hytex Garment (Cambodia) Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 21 January 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Nan (worker-appointed), Kong Phallack (chair) · Doc ID 140574

## 1. Facts
Hytex Garment (Cambodia) Ltd. operated on National Road 2, Sangkat Chak Angre Leu, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, and employed about 400 workers [Para 27][Para 28]. Its lease of the old premises ran from 1 October 1998 to 1 October 2008, and it moved to new premises opposite the Extraordinary Chambers in Ang Snuol district (Kambol), Kandal province, completing the move on 3 December 2008 [Para 46][Para 47]. The claimant is the local union of C.CAWDU at the factory, acting for 352 workers on undetermined-duration contracts — 182 union members and 170 non-members who thumbprinted a request that the union represent them [Para 30][Para 31]. The employer said 158 of the 352 were not on its payroll but never gave names or ID codes [Para 31][Para 32]. A complaint was lodged on 25 November 2008 and conciliation on 1 December 2008 failed on six issues, which reached the panel on 16 December 2008 [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20].

At the hearings of 22 and 29 December 2008 the workers cut their six demands to four, because the factory had moved [Para 21][Para 22][Para 38]. The four, in the panel's numbering, were: suspension wages from 1 August to 1 October 2008 for 36 workers in cutting and printing under the award of 4 September 2008; suspension wages from 29 August to 29 September 2008 for 380 finishing and sewing workers; wages for a lockout from 2 October to 3 December 2008 for 352 workers; and termination payments for the 352 who refused to move [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay suspension wages from 1 August to 1 October 2008 to 36 cutting and printing workers under the award of 4 September 2008? [Para 41]
2. (Issue 2) Whether it must pay suspension wages from 29 August to 29 September 2008 to 380 finishing and sewing workers? [Para 41]
3. (Issue 3) Whether it must pay wages for the lockout from 2 October to 3 December 2008 to 352 workers? [Para 43]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the 352 workers who declined to move to the new site are entitled to termination payments under Articles 75, 89, 91, 166 and 167 of the Labour Law? [Para 47][Para 48]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the local union of C.CAWDU and the 352 workers say the company suspended them without lawful authority, then locked them out from 2 October 2008, moving equipment out gradually to avoid termination payments, and that the move breached Notification No. 1408 of 6 August 2007 and the agreements of 8 December 2007 and 25 January 2008, so they may refuse to move and claim termination payments [Para 42][Para 43][Para 48][Para 50].

**Employer (respondent):** Hytex Garment (Cambodia) Ltd. says the workers were on strike from 27 August 2008 and locked the gate, that it had applied to the Ministry on 25 August 2008 to suspend contracts for lack of work, that it never intended to dismiss anyone and will take back all who move and pay transport for three to six months, and that Notification No. 1408 binds only the Cambodian Labour Union, not C.CAWDU [Para 41][Para 45][Para 48][Para 49].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel declined jurisdiction over issues 1 to 3 and granted issue 4, ordering the company to pay termination payments to 352 workers comprising dismissal indemnity, damages, payment in lieu of notice, payment in lieu of annual leave and outstanding wages [Para 59][Para 113][Para 114].

On issues 1 to 3 the panel asked only whether it could examine them at all. Article 312 of the Labour Law confines it to the issues in the non-conciliation report or matters arising from later events that are the direct consequence of the dispute [Para 54]. Because the workers conceded the three demands were never raised at the Ministry conciliation, and because each period of suspension or lockout predated that conciliation, the panel held neither condition met and declined jurisdiction, following awards 14/07-Shoe Premier, 42/07-South Bay, 06/08-Kingsland [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58][Para 59].

On issue 4 the employer raised res judicata from award 116/08, which had closed the file when the workers kept striking [Para 59][Para 65]. The panel restated the three conditions — same parties, same issue, and a full decision on the merits — and found the first two met but not the third, because 116/08 closed without any hearing on the merits; the bar therefore did not apply and the issue was decided afresh [Para 62][Para 64][Para 65][Para 66][Para 67]. Award 105/08 and 22/08 were also in evidence [Para 26].

On the merits, Article 2 of the Labour Law was read as giving the employer a management right to transfer workers only if wages are unchanged, shifts are not reversed, skills required are not substantially different, and the destination is not far away [Para 71][Para 72][Para 74][Para 75]. The move from Chak Angre to Ang Snuol in Kandal was a transfer to a far place, so the fourth condition failed [Para 76][Para 77]. Reading Articles 1 and 22 of Decree 38 as making the contract law between the parties, the panel found no clause requiring workers to move, so a unilateral relocation amounted to a breach and to termination by the employer's own will, entitling the workers to terminate and claim damages [Para 77][Para 78][Para 81][Para 83][Para 84].

It then rejected the employer's reading of Notification No. 1408 of 6 August 2007: the text does not limit itself to one union, worker delegates signed it, it speaks of all workers, and the employer had not objected to its application in award 22/08 [Para 86][Para 87][Para 88]. The agreement of 8 December 2007 required two months' notice of any move and none was given, and the agreement of 25 January 2008 said the company would not change location [Para 91][Para 92][Para 92]. Since no prior written notice was given before the equipment was removed on 3 December 2008 and no termination payment was made, the panel quantified the five heads under Articles 89, 91, 75 and 77, 167 and 116, fixing damages equal to the dismissal indemnity because the workers need not prove loss [Para 93][Para 94][Para 96][Para 108][Para 110][Para 112][Para 113].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that moves its factory to a site far from the old one, without the workers' agreement and without the prior notice its own notification and agreements require, breaches the employment contract and is treated as having terminated it at its own will, so the workers who decline to move may claim dismissal indemnity, damages, pay in lieu of notice and annual leave, and outstanding wages, with damages fixed at the indemnity without proof of loss [Para 76][Para 83][Para 106][Para 113].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that it could not find any negotiation or official notification before the move, and that the employer made no proper attempt at a solution, was offered as support for the damages finding rather than as a separate ground [Para 105].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The document is an unofficial English translation of the Khmer original and OCR has dropped "th" throughout, so "the" prints as "he" and "that the" as "thathe", including inside quoted articles [Para 54][Para 71]. Paragraph numbers are absent, so cites here follow the line numbering of the text file. The same agreement is dated 8 December 2007, 8 December 2008 and 8 December 2002008 in different places [Para 50][Para 88][Para 89], and Notification No. 1408 is dated 6 August 2007 and 26 August 2007 in successive sentences [Para 48][Para 49]. The old site is printed as Chak Angre Leu in the findings and Chak Angre Krom in the reasoning [Para 27][Para 67]. The English text of Articles 89 and 91 repeats its own opening clause mid-sentence, which obscures where the quotation ends [Para 94][Para 99].
