# Award No. 136/07 — Phnom Penh Garment City Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 8 January 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry, An Nan, Run Saray) · Doc ID 140668

## 1. Facts
The Free Trade Union of Workers of Kingdom of Cambodia (FTUWKC) at Phnom Penh Garment complained on 5 November 2007 that Phnom Penh Garment City Co., Ltd had stopped paying the monthly food allowance of US$ 6.83 the workers had received since 1998. [Para 12][Para 16][Para 22]
The company, operating since 1996 with 840 workers, changed its practice on 7 July 2007 and now pays 1,000 riels per day only to workers who actually work overtime. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 24]
FTUWKC has 302 members but the demand was made for all workers in the factory. [Para 21]
The allowance had originally been introduced in 1998, when strikes in neighbouring factories were spreading, to keep workers inside the plant at meal times. [Para 25][Para 26]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must keep paying the food allowance of US$ 6.83 per month to all workers, as a practice and contract term, rather than paying 1,000 riels per day only to those who work overtime? [Para 12][Para 23]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** FTUWKC at Phnom Penh Garment argued that the company had applied the US$ 6.83 allowance from the start, so it could not change the practice without the workers' agreement, and that a practice more generous than the law is not unlawful. [Para 31][Para 32]
**Employer (respondent):** Phnom Penh Garment City Co., Ltd said it was merely returning to the law, since paying only overtime workers 1,000 riels matches Notification 017, and that it was unfair to pay workers who do no overtime and risked jealousy among the workforce. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 39]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the company to continue providing workers with a US$ 6.83 meal allowance per month. [Para 51]
Point 3 of Notification 745 dated 23 October 2006 preserved the benefits under Notification 017 dated 18 July 2000, whose Point 4 gives a 1,000 riel daily meal allowance or one free meal only to workers who voluntarily work overtime. [Para 35]
The panel accepted that the 1,000 riel payment complies with Notification 017, but observed that the US$ 6.83 monthly practice was more favourable than the notification. [Para 36][Para 37]
Article 13(2) of the Labour Law allows an employer's unilateral decision, contract, collective agreement or arbitral decision to grant benefits superior to the statute, so a better practice is not barred. [Para 37][Para 38]
The panel then asked whether the practice had become binding, noting the parties both confirmed the practice was agreed and enjoyed for years although nothing was written, and that since 2003 it was tied to taskwork hours. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 53]
Under Article 65(2) and (3) a contract may be verbal and a verbal contract is a tacit agreement between employer and worker, so the practice constituted an oral agreement running from 1998 to 2007. [Para 45][Para 47]
Article 22 of Decree No. 38 makes a contract the law of the parties, amendable only with the approval of both, so the employer could not unilaterally shift the practice. [Para 47][Para 50]
The panel also found the jealousy justification unproven, since no worker complained and no concrete case was shown, and that the new rule applied to all workers without discrimination. [Para 39][Para 41][Para 42]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A long-standing practice more favourable than the statute, applied to the whole workforce and accepted by both sides, is a verbal contract under Article 65(2) and (3) which the employer cannot withdraw unilaterally, Article 13(2) permitting benefits above the statutory floor. [Para 37][Para 47][Para 50]
An employer that justifies a change of practice by workforce jealousy must prove it; unsupported reasons do not displace the contractual practice. [Para 41][Para 42]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel recorded the origin of the allowance in the 1998 strike wave and its purpose of keeping workers from joining neighbouring demonstrations, but decided on contract, not on that motive. [Para 25][Para 26]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The currency is printed both as "US$ 6.83" and "USS 6.83", and the allowance is called "meal allowance" in fused form throughout; the brief keeps the printed figures. [Para 12][Para 22][Para 51]
The employer's name appears as "Phnom Penh Garment City Co., Ltd" in the head, "Phnom Penh Garment Company" in the evidence list and "Phnom Penh Garment City Co,." in the dissent, and the registration certificate relied on is for FTUWKC "at Concept Garment Factory", a different plant. [Para 1][Para 20][Para 52]
Article 65(2) and (3) are quoted as broken fragments, and Article 22 of Decree No. 38 is reproduced in pieces across three paragraphs. [Para 45][Para 47][Para 49]
A dissenting opinion is appended, dated Phnom Penh, January 7th, 2008 — one day before the award date — and it carries no arbitrator's name, only "Signature". [Para 52][Para 61]
