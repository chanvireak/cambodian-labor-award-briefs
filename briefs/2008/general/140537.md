# Award No. 95/08 — Yung Wah Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 12 August 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phyrum, appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna, appointed by the workers; Pen Bunchhea, chair) · Doc ID 140537

## 1. Facts
Yung Wah Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a factory at Thmei village, Takhmao commune, Takhmao district, Kandal province, paid its night-shift workers by piece rate, and the workers claimed that the company had halved the recorded number of items produced without telling them, so that their wages fell below what was due [Para 1][Para 4][Para 5]. The claimant was the Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU) and the local union of the Khmer Youth Trade Union (KYTU) at Yung Wah I, whose representative was recorded as absent [Para 4]. The single non-conciliated issue, referred on 23 July 2008, was the demand that the company, or a Chinese supervisor named in the demand, reimburse the underpaid night-shift wages; that demand was never reached on its merits [Para 4][Para 11][Para 42].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the panel may close the case and terminate the proceedings by award where the claimant union, having been duly invited, fails to appear at the hearing and gives no reason for its absence? [Para 23][Para 27][Para 38]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU) and the local union of the Khmer Youth Trade Union (KYTU) at Yung Wah I asked that the recorded output be restored and the shortfall in night-shift wages reimbursed, asserting that the company halved the recorded items without informing them; its leaders obtained the first postponement to attend the National Election, then wrote that they would not attend the second hearing and gave no reasons despite being asked [Para 4][Para 12][Para 13][Para 15][Para 35].
**Employer (respondent):** Yung Wah Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, through its general affairs manager Mr N, maintained that wages should be calculated on actual output, appeared at the second hearing on 8 August 2008, and left it to the Council to decide whether to continue after the union failed to appear [Para 6][Para 16][Para 17][Para 36].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** withdrawn — the panel treated the union's absence as an abandonment of its claims and closed Case 95/08-Yung Wah I, making no award on the wage claim [Para 44][Para 45]
The panel relied on Rule 4.7 of the Arbitration Council Procedural Rules annexed to Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 and on clause 21 of the same Prakas, both of which allow the panel either to proceed in the absence of a party or to terminate the proceedings by award where a duly invited party fails to appear without showing good cause [Para 23][Para 24]. Following Awards 16/07 (Lotus) and 27/07 (M&V 3), it applied a three-condition test for closing a file: the parties were properly invited, they failed to appear, and they gave no reason for their absence [Para 25][Para 26].
On the first condition, the panel found from the Secretariat's invitation letters of 23 and 31 July 2008 that both parties had received the invitations for both hearing dates [Para 27][Para 28][Para 23]. On the second, it read "appear at the hearing" in clause 21 as requiring both physical presence and participation in the whole hearing, which runs through self-introduction and conflict checks, explanation of the proceedings and clarification of issues, optional re-conciliation, and the hearing of the facts; the union attended none of those stages on 8 August 2008 [Para 28][Para 32][Para 33]. On the third, the panel found that the union had said it would not attend and gave no explanation when the Secretariat asked for one [Para 34][Para 35][Para 37].
All three conditions being met, the panel held it retained power to issue an award even in the union's absence [Para 38]. It noted that the union had been given the opportunity to make claims on behalf of the workers it represented, that a claimant must give reasons and evidence for its claims, and that the union's absence deprived it of that opportunity [Para 39][Para 41][Para 43]. The panel characterised the conduct as an indication that the claims were being rescinded and that the union was unwilling to resolve the dispute before the Council, and closed the case [Para 44][Para 45].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A panel may terminate a case by award without reaching the merits where three conditions are satisfied — the party was properly invited, it failed to appear, and it offered no reason for the absence — and "appear" means attending and taking part in the whole hearing, not merely being present at the door [Para 25][Para 28][Para 38].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that a claimant bears the burden of reasons and evidence, and that absence signals an intention to abandon the claims, are explanatory of its discretion rather than separate holdings [Para 41][Para 44].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
This is an unofficial English translation of a Khmer original, and the typescript systematically fuses "th" across word boundaries, so "the employer", "the hearing" and "the" plus the following word appear run together throughout [Para 4][Para 9][Para 23]. Each representative's name is split so that a bare title ends one line and the personal name begins the next, leaving the representative's full name unrecoverable from the face of the award [Para 1][Para 2][Para 3]. A supervisor is named in two different spellings within the same document, in the demand and in the employer's list of representatives, so it cannot be settled which is correct [Para 3][Para 4]. The exhibit list skips a number and the fourth item of one list is printed inside the translation header line [Para 18][Para 23]. The award date in the header is 12 August 2008 while the operative decision refers to the second hearing of 8 August 2008 without stating a separate date of decision [Para 1][Para 16].
