# Award No. 13/08 — Terratex Knitting & Garment Int'l Factory, Ltd (Arbitration Council, 27 February 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phallack chair; Mar Samborana, employer nominee; Suong Sophal, worker nominee) · Doc ID 140482

## 1. Facts
Terratex Knitting & Garment Int'l Factory, Ltd, on National Road 2, Sangkat Chak Angre Krom, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employs about 500 workers and is faced by the Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Worker Democratic Union (C.CAWDU) and its local union, elected on 28 April 2007 with a president, vice-president and secretary. [Para 1][Para 44][Para 46] The Department of Labour Disputes twice rejected registration of the local union on the company's objection — first because the elected secretary was not on the company's staff list, then because the president's name differed between his marriage certificate and his residence letter — and the correction was never made, so the union remains unregistered. [Para 50][Para 52][Para 54] Conciliation of eight demands on 14 January left seven un-conciliated; at the second hearing C.CAWDU withdrew the six working-condition demands and asked the panel to hear only the reinstatement of one dismissed activist, and the employer, while calling the case an individual dispute, agreed that the panel should proceed. [Para 22][Para 29][Para 31] The company had terminated that worker on 08 December 2007 for forging a document when making his employment contract, which it treats as serious misconduct under Article 83B(2). [Para 64]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the demand of C.CAWDU that the company reinstate the dismissed union activist is a collective labour dispute within the jurisdiction of the Arbitration Council, or an individual dispute it must decline to hear? [Para 61][Para 71][Para 111]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** C.CAWDU and its local union argued that the dismissal of a union activist over variant spellings of his name is a collective dispute because it concerns the exercise of recognised union rights, that the variations were the fault of public officers, caused the company no damage, and that under Article 26 of the Labour Law the company had lost the right to terminate him. [Para 88][Para 93][Para 96]
**Employer (respondent):** Terratex Knitting & Garment Int'l Factory, Ltd, through its assistant director, argued that the dispute involved only that one worker and no other, turned on forgery of his employment contract under Article 83B(2), and had nothing to do with the working conditions of the workforce, so the case was an individual dispute outside the panel's jurisdiction. [Para 73][Para 75][Para 99]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel declined to consider C.CAWDU's demand that the company reinstate the dismissed worker. [Para 136]
The panel began with its own competence. Chapter 12, Section 2 of the Labour Law leaves it to the Labour Inspector and the Ministry to classify a dispute before referral, and the panel will normally respect that classification unless there is explicit reason to object; but where the employer itself objects and pleads that the matter is individual, the panel must decide the point. [Para 111][Para 112][Para 113] Following Award 45/07-Wilson, it held that the employer bears the burden of proving that a dispute listed in the non-conciliation report is individual rather than collective. [Para 114][Para 115]
It then applied Article 302, which defines a collective labour dispute as one between one or more employers and a certain number of employees over working conditions, the exercise of recognised union rights, recognition of professional organisations, or employer-worker relations, where the dispute could jeopardise the effective operation of the enterprise or social peace. [Para 120][Para 121]
Of the three conditions drawn from that article, the panel found condition (a) unmet because the dispute arose between one worker and the company alone, with no other worker involved, and C.CAWDU conceded as much at the hearing; it added that the local union was unregistered and its leaders had resigned. [Para 125][Para 128][Para 130] Condition (b) was met, since the dispute arose over working relations between an employer and a worker. [Para 130] Condition (c) was not met: both parties confirmed the factory had operated normally since the dismissal, and no other workers supported the demand. [Para 103][Para 131] The panel therefore held the dispute was not collective and concerned only that worker, and declined to consider the demand. [Para 132][Para 133][Para 135]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer objects that a matter referred as a collective dispute is in truth individual, the employer bears the burden of proving it, and the panel will find the dispute collective only if all three conditions of Article 302 of the Labour Law are satisfied — a single worker's dismissal, with no other worker involved and no interruption to the enterprise, fails conditions (a) and (c) however strong the union motive alleged. [Para 115][Para 125][Para 131][Para 132]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the local union was not registered and that its president and vice-president had resigned before the second hearing, facts it used to reinforce the absence of any collective dimension rather than as an independent ground. [Para 55][Para 130]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The translation is a mechanical OCR of an unofficial English text that drops the letters "th" throughout — "themployer", "athere", "withexplanation", "Traing" for Training — and in several places splices two sentences together, so quotations of Article 302 and the reasons had to be reconstructed from context. [Para 1][Para 120] The dismissed worker's name is printed in at least six different spellings across the residence book, labour book, medical certificate, marriage announcement, job application and citizen ID card, differing in the middle and final consonants; the award resolves the question by adopting the citizen ID card spelling, and the variants are not reproduced here. [Para 79][Para 83] The minutes date the final conciliation "14 January 2007" although the non-conciliation report and every later reference place it in 2008. [Para 22] The award calls the company "Terratex Knitting Company" in the findings but "Terratex Knitting & Garment Int'l Factory, Ltd" on its face, and the exhibit list carries yet another spelling of the dismissed worker's name. [Para 1][Para 44][Para 33]
