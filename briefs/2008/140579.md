# Award No. 157/08 — Eternal Way (Cambodia) Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេធើណល វ៉េ (ខេមបូឌា) អិលធីឌី) (Arbitration Council, 20 January 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — employer-appointed អ៊ីង សុទ្ធី, worker-appointed លីវ សុវណ្ណា, president គង់ ផល្លៈ · Doc ID 140579

## 1. Facts
The Khmer Youth Union Federation at the Eternal Way factory (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ ប្រចាំ រោងចក្រ អេធើណល វ៉េ) brought a collective dispute against Eternal Way (Cambodia) Ltd, a Phnom Penh garment employer of about 1,600 workers with three factory-level unions [Para 35]. The company announced that the factory would close on 31 December 2008, having completed the closure formalities with the competent authority [Para 17][Para 36]. Four women in the shoulder-inspection section, all on indefinite-duration contracts, had been dismissed on 26 September 2008 after receiving four or five written warnings, and had already been paid their final wages and annual-leave compensation [Para 37][Para 38]. Of the five demands taken to conciliation, the workers withdrew four at the hearing, leaving only the claim for statutory termination payments [Para 18][Para 19].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 3, first question) Whether the company had a lawful ground for dismissing the four women workers? [Para 69]
2. (Issue 3, second question) Whether those four workers were entitled to severance pay, pay in lieu of notice and moral damages? [Para 69]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant union):** The four women were moved out of the shoulder-inspection section and refused the reassignment, relying on an agreement the company had signed with the Khmer Youth Union Federation promising not to change workers' roles or dismiss them over a change of task [Para 55]. Because the dismissal was unlawful they claimed pay in lieu of notice, severance and moral damages equal to severance [Para 40].

**Employer (respondent):** The company said it had acted under its internal rules and the Labour Law, that each of the four had been warned four or five times, and that no such agreement with the union existed [Para 41][Para 56]. It paid nothing beyond final wages and annual leave and left the point to the panel [Para 59][Para 60].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay severance and pay in lieu of notice to the four workers, and refused the claim for moral damages [Para 114][Para 115][Para 116].

On the lawfulness of the dismissal the panel applied Article 74 of the Labour Law, under which an indefinite-duration contract may be terminated on notice and with a ground relating to the worker's capacity or conduct or to the needs of the enterprise [Para 69][Para 71]. It followed its earlier Award No. 07/06 for that test [Para 74]. It found that the company's reassignment of the four women from shoulder inspection to sleeve and front-panel inspection, five metres away with the same hours and the same wages, fell within the employer's right to organise work and breached no condition on wages, distance, shift or skill [Para 78][Para 80][Para 81]. The claimed side agreement was never produced, so the panel gave it no weight [Para 83][Para 84]. Four written warnings had been issued to each worker under Article 10 of the internal rules, which places four warnings at the level of dismissal [Para 63][Para 66][Para 85]. The panel held that warnings under the "light misconduct" limb of Article 10 do not become serious misconduct merely by repetition, so the dismissal was grounded in conduct but not in serious misconduct [Para 90][Para 91][Para 92].

Because the dismissal was not for serious misconduct, Article 89 of the Labour Law entitled the workers to severance, which for service beyond twelve months is fifteen days' wages and allowances per year of service, subject to a six-month cap [Para 93][Para 94][Para 95][Para 98]. The panel also found the company had given no notice at all, dismissing four days after the last warning, so Articles 75 and 77 required two months' wages and benefits in lieu [Para 104][Para 105][Para 106].

Moral damages under Article 91 were refused: although the dismissal lacked a serious-misconduct ground, the employer did have a proper ground under Article 74, and the workers had not proved the damage required for a claim under that article [Para 110][Para 113][Para 114].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer dismisses on repeated warnings that the internal rules classify only as light misconduct, the dismissal is not for serious misconduct, so severance under Article 89 and two months' pay in lieu of notice under Articles 75 and 77 are owed even though the employer had a proper Article 74 ground relating to the worker's conduct; but moral damages under Article 91 are refused in that same case, because a valid Article 74 ground negatives the wrongfulness that article requires [Para 90][Para 93][Para 106][Para 113].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel listed the conditions it treats as limits on the employer's right to reassign — no wage cut, no move to a distant site, no day-to-night shift change, no transfer far outside the worker's skill — though the reassignment before it breached none of them [Para 78][Para 81].
