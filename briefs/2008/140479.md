# Award No. 08/08 — Hi-Tech Garment (Cambodia) Ltd (ហៃ តិច ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា)) (Arbitration Council, 4 February 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Kong Phalla (chair) · Doc ID 140479

## 1. Facts
ហៃ តិច ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) លីមីតធីត (Hi-Tech Garment (Cambodia) Ltd), a garment factory on National Road 2 in Chak Angre Leu, Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employs about 850 workers. [Para 1][Para 31] Workers organised through the Cambodian Democratic Garment Workers' Union (សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា), which had applied for registration but was not yet registered and claimed more than 200 members. [Para 32][Para 34] On 7 November 2007 they filed twelve demands on leave, maternity pay, sick leave, overtime, discrimination, relocation, health-check fees and union facilities; conciliation on 21 December failed entirely. [Para 19][Para 2][Para 13] At the hearing the parties settled eleven points, leaving only the maternity-pay demand, which the panel decided alone. [Para 23][Para 24]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay 50% of three months' maternity wages in advance, before the worker begins her maternity leave? [Para 36][Para 47]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** ភាគី កម្មករ និយោជិត, through the Cambodian Democratic Garment Workers' Union, sought payment of the full 50% of three months' wages before the leave began, because the money would be more useful during the leave and because many workers come from Prey Veng, Svay Rieng, Pursat and Kratie and cannot travel to collect monthly payments, relying on Article 115 of the Labour Law. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 42]
**Employer (respondent):** ហៃ តិច ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា), through its administration staff, answered that it pays maternity wages month by month as usual practice, allows workers to authorise relatives or friends to collect, that a lump-sum advance would disrupt its monthly accounting closing, and that Article 115 applies only to weekly and public-holiday rest days. [Para 37][Para 43][Para 44][Para 45]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay the whole 50% of three months' maternity wages to the worker before her maternity leave begins. [Para 58][Para 59]
The panel applied Article 183 of the Labour Law, which entitles a woman during maternity leave to half her wages including allowances, and found that the article sets the amount but says nothing about when it must be paid. [Para 48][Para 52] It therefore turned to Article 115 §3, which forbids paying wages on a day the worker is entitled to rest and requires payment one day earlier if the due date falls on such a day. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 55]
Following its earlier awards No. 57/06 (Evergreen) and No. 97/06, it held that maternity pay must be handed over before the leave starts, and that the employer's monthly instalment practice did not satisfy that requirement. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57] It noted that both parties agreed the company had never paid the three months' total in advance. [Para 57]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Maternity pay under Article 183 must be paid before the worker's maternity leave begins, not in monthly instalments during it: Article 115 §3 prohibits payment on a rest day and requires payment to be brought forward, and the timing of the entitlement is not left to the employer's accounting cycle. [Para 52][Para 55][Para 58]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel recorded the employer's facility of allowing a relative or friend to collect wages as a convenience that did not answer the legal question. [Para 37][Para 43]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company name is printed variously as ហៃ តិច ហ្គាមេន, ហ៊ីតិច ហ្គាមេន and a garbled "ហៃ តិ ចតួច", and the case name in the header is truncated. [Para 1][Para 26][Para 30] The number of unresolved issues is 12 in the recitals but "224" in the hearing paragraph, where eleven are then listed as settled. [Para 19][Para 23] The date on which the union applied for registration is left blank in the text. [Para 33] The signature block does not match the panel named in the header, printing a different name for the worker-appointed arbitrator. [Para 61] The union is described both as a federation and as a plant-level union, and its name is given in two forms. [Para 2][Para 32] Individual union officers are named in the source; this brief refers to them by role only.
