# Award No. 30/08 — E Garment Co., Ltd v Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers Democratic Union (Arbitration Council, 24 March 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Tuon Siphann for the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 140493

## 1. Facts
E Garment Co., Ltd, at Village Number 3, Svay Rolum Commune, Saang District, Kandal, employs about 2,342 workers. [Para 74] The local union of the Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers Democratic Union (C.CAWDU) notified the company of its founding election on 31 July 2007, held the election on 3 August 2007 with 32 candidates, and gave notice of results listing 44 leaders and activists on 6 August 2007. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 77] It obtained a registration receipt on 15 August 2007 but never received a registration certificate, the Ministry having rejected its documents in case 123/07. [Para 105][Para 109] The company then terminated eight workers said to be on the election list, all holding two-month casual contracts, paying them severance for the full term. [Para 81][Para 113]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must reinstate the eight workers it terminated while they were candidates or members in the union election? [Para 112][Para 142]
2. (Issue 3) Whether workers employed for two months or more must be converted from casual to full-right workers? [Para 162][Para 167]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the company must reinstate the worker it says abandoned his post, and whether that termination was union discrimination? [Para 169][Para 221]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, C.CAWDU and its local union at E Garment):** the eight were dismissed because they stood as union election candidates, the same facts as case 123/07 in which 28 ironing-section workers were reinstated, and the eighth was a union leader whose dismissal was discrimination; they want reinstatement, not severance, and conversion of casuals to regular status. [Para 77][Para 83][Para 144][Para 171]
**Employer (respondent, E Garment Co., Ltd):** the contracts were casual two-month contracts that had expired or were ended early with full severance and no discrimination; it terminated all two-month casuals and will only use them again if buyers return; the eighth worker resigned by walking out without notice. [Para 16][Para 81][Para 82][Para 90][Para 99]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the company to reinstate the eight workers, rejected the demand to convert workers with two months' service to regular workers, and rejected the demand to reinstate the worker found to have abandoned his job. [Para 222][Para 223]

The panel first held that under Article 268 a union enjoys the rights of the Labour Law only once registered and recognised by the Ministry, so C.CAWDU could not bring the dispute for all its members, and could act only for the eight complainants, six of whom appeared. [Para 107][Para 108][Para 109][Para 111][Para 112] On Issue 1, the contracts were fixed-duration under Article 67(7) and could be ended early only by agreement before a Labour Inspector, for serious misconduct or force majeure, with compensation otherwise under Article 73. [Para 117][Para 119][Para 120] Article 293 requires Labour Inspector authorisation to dismiss a steward or candidate, and Prakas No. 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001 extends protection to election candidates for 45 days before and after the ballot and to founders or volunteer members during registration for 30 days. [Para 123][Para 126][Para 129][Para 130] Following Awards 07/06-Dai Young, 09/06-Grand Diamond City and 148/07-Pay Her, the panel applied a three-part test — protected status, termination within the protected period, and notice to the employer — and found seven of the eight satisfied it; the eighth was not a candidate and was dismissed three days before the registration application. [Para 131][Para 132][Para 133][Para 141][Para 142] Because no Inspector authorisation was sought, the terminations of the seven were unlawful, and the dismissal of all eight, drawn from one section on the union list, was union discrimination contrary to Articles 12, 266 and 279, justifying reinstatement under Clause 34 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 142][Para 149][Para 152][Para 154][Para 157][Para 160][Para 161]

On Issue 3 the demand concerned casual workers not yet identified and a practice the company said had ceased; following 10/03-Jacqsintex, 68/04-City New, 36/06-Mondotex and 58/07-8 Stars Sportswear, the panel refused to decide a dispute that had not arisen and that the union had no authorisation to bring for the whole workforce. [Para 166][Para 167][Para 169] On Issue 4, the worker was not on either union list and said he had never worked for the union, so he was not a leader and no discrimination was shown; he left without telling his supervisor, and the Internal Work Rules require warnings before dismissal, but under Article 69(1) he owed the company his working time, so the panel found wilful abandonment. [Para 181][Para 189][Para 211][Para 215][Para 220][Para 221]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union election candidate, and a founder or volunteer member during the registration period, may not be dismissed without prior authorisation from the Labour Inspector under Article 293, read with Prakas No. 305 SKBY; termination without that authorisation is unlawful and the panel will order reinstatement. [Para 123][Para 130][Para 142][Para 161] Ending a fixed-duration contract before its expiry is lawful only by written agreement before a Labour Inspector, for serious misconduct or force majeure, failing which the worker is owed compensation to the end of the term. [Para 119][Para 120] A demand to convert a category of casual workers is a future dispute the panel will not decide where no affected worker is identified. [Para 167][Para 169]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that a union without a registration certificate may still represent individual complainants if it holds their written authorisation under Clause 19 of Prakas No. 099 was not needed, since no such letter existed. [Para 164][Para 165][Para 166]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The same individuals appear under two or three different spellings across the exhibit list, the fact table and the orders, and one vice-president is rendered once with a K and once with a Kh; the version in the termination table does not match the version in the election-result discussion. The eight claimants are given by role here.
- The election notice is dated 31 July 2007 at [Para 75] and [Para 77] but 31 August 2007 at [Para 134] and [Para 139], which matters because the eighth worker's dismissal on 12 August is measured against it.
- The hearing is dated 06 March 2008 in the header at [Para 21] and 6 March 2007 at [Para 22].
- The reasons invoke "Article 67 clause 3" at [Para 120] where the quoted text is Article 73, and attribute the discrimination finding to Article 293 at [Para 160] rather than Articles 12 and 279.
- Prakas No. 305 is dated 22 November 2001 throughout, but the panel also cites Prakas No. 099 as dated 21 July 2004 in the Khmer award series and 21 April 2004 here; only the latter appears in this text.
- Word boundaries are widely lost ("themloyer", "thearing", "whold"), and one fact paragraph is duplicated mid-sentence at [Para 101].
