# Award No. 141/08 — Bloom Them Embroidery Pte Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ប្ល៊ូម ថេម អ៊ឹម ប្រយ ឌីរី ភីធី) and the Free Workers' Union of Bloom Them Factory (Arbitration Council, 11 December 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tai Seng, Tuon Si Phan, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 140568

## 1. Facts
Bloom Them Embroidery, at Trapeang Thleung, Dangkao, Phnom Penh, employed close to 500 workers [Para 74]. On 20 October 2008 the Free Workers' Union complained to the Department of Labour Disputes about four matters: restoration of the former shift hours, retention of unlimited-duration contracts, retention of night-shift wages and benefits, and an end to alleged anti-union discrimination [Para 17][Para 2][Para 4][Para 7][Para 9]. Conciliation failed on 28 October 2008 and non-conciliation report No. 1225 of 5 November 2008 came to the Secretariat the same day [Para 13][Para 18]. The company had begun applying new shift hours on 1 October 2008, before the labour inspector approved the amended internal rules on 25 November 2008 [Para 94][Para 97][Para 107]. Night work ceased altogether on 26 November 2008, and the demand to preserve night-shift pay should it return was treated as a claim about the future and not decided [Para 131][Para 211].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must restore the shift hours worked before 1 October 2008? [Para 80]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the 34 workers who signed six-month fixed-duration contracts must have their unlimited-duration contracts restored? [Para 116][Para 181]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the company discriminated against the union by keeping its two leaders outside the factory while paying them? [Para 134]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), Free Workers' Union of Bloom Them Factory (សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ នៃ រោងចក្រ ប្លូម ថេម):** the union president, Mr S, and the worker representative, Mr K, argued that some 50 to 60 workers had never accepted the new hours, that the change cost them benefits contrary to Article 390 of the Labour Law, and that about 50 to 100 workers who had signed six-month contracts did so without understanding the law and should be restored to unlimited-duration contracts [Para 103][Para 109][Para 121].

**Employer (respondent), Bloom Them Embroidery Pte Ltd:** the company said the change followed a fall in orders, that it polled the workforce in September 2008, that the worker representatives signed an agreement on the new hours, and that the amended internal rules were approved by the labour inspector on 25 November 2008 [Para 98][Para 100][Para 107]. It said the contract conversions were voluntary, compensated with 15 days' wages, and accepted by all but the two union leaders, who kept their unlimited-duration contracts and were paid in full while staying outside the factory [Para 123][Para 124][Para 135].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected all three decided demands, on hours, on contract types, and on anti-union discrimination [Para 230][Para 231][Para 233].

On Issue 1 the panel began from Article 2(2) of the Labour Law, which gives the employer the right to organise and manage the enterprise, and from its own earlier awards restricting that right to lawful and reasonable exercises of it [Para 149][Para 152][Para 156]. It then applied Article 30, under which any change to internal rules must follow the enterprise regulations, and Article 24, which requires consultation with worker representatives and prior approval by the labour inspector [Para 157][Para 159][Para 160][Para 162]. Because the company consulted the 11 worker representatives, two of them union leaders, and the inspector approved the amended rules on 25 November 2008, the procedure was satisfied [Para 162][Para 163]. Article 25, which voids internal-rule provisions that cut rights granted by law or agreement, did not assist the workers, since it does not reach benefits that the old rules gave above the law [Para 164][Para 167]. The new shifts of 6:00 to 14:45 and 14:45 to 23:00 with a 45-minute break stayed within the 8-hour day and 48-hour week of Article 137 [Para 168][Para 170]. Article 390 was held transitional, governing contracts carried over from the 1992 law, and so inapplicable [Para 172][Para 177].

On Issue 2 the panel found the 34 claimants had each freely signed a six-month contract in exchange for 15 days' wages [Para 181][Para 182]. Under Article 65 of the Labour Law and Articles 1 and 22 of Decree-Law No. 38 of 28 October 1988, a contract may be modified only by the parties' consent, and no evidence of coercion was produced [Para 183][Para 187][Para 188][Para 194]. It distinguished the earlier awards that ordered conversion to unlimited-duration contracts, which concerned fixed-duration contracts extended beyond the two-year ceiling in Article 67(2); here the workers had consented to a fresh six-month term, which was valid [Para 198][Para 199][Para 204][Para 206].

On Issue 4 the panel found no evidence of anti-union discrimination: the material filed showed only that a union vice-president had resigned, and the two leaders were paid in full and invited back if they accepted the new hours [Para 224][Para 225][Para 228].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may change shift hours by amending its internal rules provided it consults the worker representatives and obtains the labour inspector's prior approval under Article 24 of the Labour Law, and provided the new schedule stays within the limits of Article 137; Article 25 does not void such a change merely because the old rules granted benefits above the statutory floor, and Article 390 does not preserve them [Para 162][Para 167][Para 170][Para 177]. A worker who genuinely consents to convert an unlimited-duration contract into a six-month fixed-duration contract is bound by it, and Article 67(2)'s two-year ceiling does not invalidate the shorter consented term [Para 194][Para 204][Para 206]. A discrimination claim fails where the only evidence shows how one union officer came to resign rather than any act of the employer against the union [Para 224][Para 225].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel expressly declined to examine the period from 1 October to 25 November 2008 during which the new hours ran before approval, because the workers claimed restoration of the old roster and not repayment of the benefits lost in that interval [Para 147][Para 148].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The two-name party style is printed inconsistently, the factory appearing as "ប្លូម ថេម", "ប្លូម ថេ" and "ប្លូម ថេម អ៊ឹម ប្រយ ឌីរី ភីធី" in different passages [Para 1][Para 74][Para 156]. Shift tables are badly duplicated and interleaved, so the old and new rosters repeat fragments of each other and one line reads only "ក្រុមហ៊ុន ផ្តល់ ប្រាក់ ឈ្នួល ពេញ" [Para 84][Para 85][Para 87]. The award prints 2019 for 2008 in one date in the reasons [Para 147]. The employer's representatives include transliterated names partly in Latin script, and the two union leaders and the 34 claimants are reduced here to initials and role descriptions. The signature block is damaged, one arbitrator's name appearing twice with a Latin OCR fragment before it [Para 235].
