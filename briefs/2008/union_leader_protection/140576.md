# Award No. 154/08 — Fray Ying Dragon (Cambodia) Garment Ltd. / ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្វ្រយ យ៉ីង ដ្រាហ្គន (ខេមបូឌា) ហ្គាមែន លីមីតធីត (Arbitration Council, 6 January 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Kong Phalla (president) · Doc ID 140576

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្វ្រយ យ៉ីង ដ្រាហ្គន (ខេមបូឌា) ហ្គាមែន លីមីតធីត (Fray Ying Dragon (Cambodia) Garment Ltd.) is a garment factory on Chom Chau road, Sangkat Stung Meanchey, Khan Meanchey, Phnom Penh, reported at about 550 workers, though at the hearing the employer said only 91 remained because it had no work [Para 48]. The claimant is សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព-សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ, the C.CAWDU local union at the factory, claiming about 80 members [Para 1][Para 50][Para 51].

The union complained on 12 October 2008 and conciliation on 1 December 2008 failed on six demands, which reached the panel on 16 December 2008 [Para 18][Para 19][Para 21]. At the hearing of 24 December 2008 the workers withdrew issues 1, 2 and 5, so the panel considered only issues 3, 4 and 6 [Para 17][Para 24][Para 25]: reinstatement of the dismissed union president with arrears, payment of sick-leave wages and medical costs, and 100 per cent wages for the months without work [Para 56][Para 74][Para 100].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 3) Whether the company must take back the dismissed union president and pay her wages from the date of dismissal until reinstatement? [Para 56][Para 109]
2. (Issue 4) Whether the company must pay sick-leave wages from 8 August to 8 September 2008 and 3,200,000 riel in treatment costs, the illness being a work accident under Article 248 of the Labour Law? [Para 74][Para 129][Para 146]
3. (Issue 6) Whether the 101 listed workers are entitled to 100 per cent of wages for the period without work in August, September, October and November 2008 instead of the 50 per cent paid under the agreement of 24 May 2008? [Para 100][Para 161][Para 173]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព-សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ says its president was felled by illness at work on 8 August 2008, that the company refused her sick leave and then declared her to have abandoned the job on 4 October 2008, and that the 50 per cent wage for days without work falls below the Labour Law [Para 59][Para 63][Para 70][Para 100].

**Employer (respondent):** ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្វ្រយ យ៉ែង ដ្រាហ្គន (ខេមបូឌា) ហ្គាមែន លីមីតធីត says it cannot take the president back and leaves the point to the panel, that it paid her nothing during sick leave because she never proved a chronic or infectious illness and never filed the internal sick-leave form, that treatment costs are not payable as no work accident occurred, and that it applies the 50 per cent rate fixed by agreement [Para 12][Para 73][Para 100][Para 131].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the company to reinstate the union president and pay her wages from 4 October 2008 once the award takes effect, and to pay 100 per cent wages for the months without work to 101 workers, while refusing the sick-leave wages and the medical costs [Para 179][Para 180][Para 181][Para 182].

On issue 3 the panel applied Article 293 of the Labour Law, read with Article 4 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, which protects three union leaders and requires the Labour Inspector's authorisation before any of them is dismissed [Para 109][Para 116][Para 117]. The company had applied for authorisation on 20 November 2008 and then withdrew the application, and no decision had issued, so the dismissal on 4 October 2008 was unlawful [Para 118][Para 119][Para 120]. Under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 the panel may order full compensation, and awards 10/03, 07/06 and 73/06 were followed [Para 122][Para 124][Para 126][Para 128].

On issue 4 the panel split the claim. For sick-leave wages it held that point 8-6 of the internal rules, approved on 24 April 2008, requires confirmation by the company doctor before full wages are payable, and that the worker carried the burden of showing her illness was chronic or infectious [Para 130][Para 134][Para 135][Para 136]. It found the medical papers partly undated and in a foreign language, and declined to consider them because Article 23 of Prakas No. 099 makes Khmer the language of the proceedings [Para 138][Para 140][Para 141][Para 142][Para 143][Para 145]. On treatment costs it held that Article 248 covers accidents at work, on the commute, and occupational diseases, and that her illnesses fell in none of these categories; no Cambodian text yet defines occupational disease, and Protocol 155 of 2002 has not been ratified, so the cost claim failed [Para 146][Para 149][Para 152][Para 153][Para 156][Para 157][Para 158][Para 159][Para 160].

On issue 6 the panel found the suspension of contracts unlawful: Article 71 (11th) of the Labour Law limits suspension to two months and places it under the Labour Inspector's supervision, and the company never notified the inspector [Para 161][Para 163][Para 168][Para 169]. The agreement of 24 May 2008 giving 50 per cent therefore fell below what the law provides and was void under Article 13, so the 101 listed workers were entitled to the missing 50 per cent, the employer having declined to contest the list [Para 174][Para 176][Para 177][Para 178][Para 182].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union leader within the three posts protected by Article 293 may not be dismissed without the Labour Inspector's authorisation, and an employer that suspends contracts for lack of work beyond two months, or without notifying the Labour Inspector, acts unlawfully, so an agreement paying half wages for such days is void as less favourable than the law [Para 117][Para 163][Para 177].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that no Cambodian instrument yet defines occupational disease, and that Protocol 155 of 2002 cannot bind Cambodia since it is unratified, was not needed once the claim had failed for want of evidence of causation [Para 153][Para 156][Para 157].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is dated 6 January 2008 on its face although the hearing was held on 24 December 2008 and the conciliation report is dated 12 December 2008, so the year is almost certainly a misprint [Para 1][Para 15][Para 17]. The conciliation report is dated 12 December 2008 in one place and 24 November 2008 in the evidence list [Para 15][Para 44]. The company name is spelled five different ways across the face, the operative part and the evidence list [Para 1][Para 25][Para 48]. One paragraph of the reasons begins with a stray figure and the words "A" before the text resumes [Para 86]. The award carries a dissenting appendix by one arbitrator on issue 4(1) that repeats the internal rule in a further, differently corrupted spelling [Para 184][Para 185].
