# Award No. 97/08 — Eternity Apparel Company (Arbitration Council, 26 August 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, An Nan, Kong Phallack) · Doc ID 140538

## 1. Facts
Eternity Apparel Company, on National Road 2, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employed approximately 650 workers [Para 25]. The claimant was the local union of the National Industrial Trade Union of Cambodia (NITUC) at the Eternity factory, represented by the National Industrial Federation Trade Union of Cambodia (NIFTUC) [Para 2][Para 25]. On 3 July 2008 the union complained to the Department of Labour Dispute and put eleven demands for improved working conditions — break time during overtime, moto parking, hygiene, an infirmary, toilets, dust extraction and fans, roof repairs, sun blinds, higher wages for group heads, conversion of casual workers to regular workers, and discipline of the head of security [Para 10][Para 15]. Conciliation failed on all eleven, and the non-conciliation report No. 794 KB/AK/VK of 23 July 2008 went to the Secretariat on 25 July 2008 [Para 15][Para 17]. At the hearing of 11 August 2008 the panel conciliated ten of the eleven demands; only Issue 10, conversion of casual workers, remained for adjudication [Para 21][Para 22].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 10) Whether the company must convert all of its casual workers to regular workers? [Para 25][Para 36]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** NITUC/NIFTUC said there were about 20 to 30 casual workers, some employed since 2006, working 8 to 10 hours a day for 26 days a month, consecutively, and that the company had never converted them; the workers wanted the rights and benefits of regular workers [Para 25][Para 26][Para 27].
**Employer (respondent):** Eternity Apparel did not deny that the workers were casual, and accepted that casual workers existed, but said they worked only 18 days a month; it filed a letter objecting to the reinstatement of one named worker without objecting to his casual status [Para 33][Para 35].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand to convert the casual workers to regular workers was rejected, and the employer was ordered to provide the same rights and benefits to the 6 casual workers as it provides to regular workers [Para 52][Para 53].
On the evidence, the panel ordered the union to supply a name list by 14 August 2008 and received six names, only four of which carried a clear date of employment [Para 29][Para 30]. It also ordered the employer to produce a name and payroll list by the same date; the company filed after the deadline, so the panel refused to consider the document [Para 34]. Because the company raised no objection to the list of workers [Para 38], the panel found that four casual workers were established [Para 37][Para 39].
On the law, the panel set out the Article 9 definition of casual work as work contracted for a short, temporary, intermittent or seasonal period [Para 40]. Reading Article 9 with Articles 166 and 168, and following a long line of its own awards, it held that a worker who works at least 21 days in two consecutive months must be treated as a regular worker whatever the employer calls him or her [Para 40][Para 41]. It then turned to Article 10, which subjects casual workers to the same rules and gives them the same rights as regular workers save for separately stipulated clauses [Para 41][Para 42]. On that basis the panel reasoned that the words "casual" and "regular" in Article 9 describe only the duration and frequency of a worker's employment [Para 49]; that converting a worker from one category to the other produces no legal effect of itself [Para 45]; and that what matters is the benefits the Labour Law attaches to the contract actually performed [Para 46][Para 47]. It distinguished the special protection of Article 293, which depends on holding office as a worker delegate or union leader, not on casual status [Para 48][Para 49]. The employer was therefore obliged to give casual workers the same wages, attendance bonus, seniority bonus where service length qualifies, annual leave and other benefits as regular workers of equal service, including the same termination payment if unfairly dismissed [Para 50][Para 51]. The conversion demand itself was rejected [Para 52]. The award is non-binding and becomes binding eight days after notification unless a party opposes [Para 53].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Because Article 10 already entitles casual workers to the same rights and benefits as regular workers, a demand to re-label casual workers as regular workers secures nothing the law does not already give; the panel will reject the conversion demand and instead order the employer to extend regular-worker rights and benefits — measured by length of service — to the casual workers proved to be working [Para 42][Para 50][Para 52]. A worker shown to have worked 21 days a month over two consecutive months is a regular worker in substance regardless of the label used [Para 40].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observations on Article 293 — that special protection follows from holding office as a worker delegate, union leader, election candidate or founding member, and not from the casual/regular distinction — were not necessary to an order that turned on equal benefits [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49]. Its remark that a seven-month casual worker deserves the termination payment of a seven-month regular worker was illustrative only [Para 51].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The translation is marked unofficial throughout and the OCR has systematically dropped the letter "e" from "the" and from contractions, so many sentences read as misspellings of the original. The statement of the parties' representatives is broken mid-entry, leaving several names split across lines without their roles. One worker's name is printed in two different spellings in the file. One casual worker's start date is printed with a stray digit in the year ("200/8"), so the year is uncertain [Para 32]. Internally the award is inconsistent on numbers: the panel finds four casual workers with clear employment dates [Para 39] but the operative paragraph orders equal benefits for "the 6 casual workers" [Para 53], while the reasoning elsewhere says a list of six names was received of which four were dated [Para 29]. The decision also says "converthe" and "Rejecthe" for "convert the" and "rejects the" [Para 52].
