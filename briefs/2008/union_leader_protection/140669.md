# Award No. 140/07 — Vision Garment (Cambodia) (Vision Company) (Arbitration Council, 9 January 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators: Ing Sothy, An Nan, Kong Phallack (Chair) · Doc ID 140669

## 1. Facts
Vision Garment (Cambodia) (Vision Company), at Toul Pongror Village, Sangkat Chom Chao, Khan Dangkor, Phnom Penh, employed 275 workers; the claimant, the Cambodian Federation for Workers' Rights (CFWR) and the Cambodian Union for Workers' Rights (CUWR), had around 100 members and was registered by certificate No. 1258 dated 3 October 2007 [Para 23][Para 24]. Its vice-president (Mr B) and secretary (Mr T) each held three-month fixed-duration contracts from 1 September to 1 December 2007, paid US$52 and US$51 per month respectively [Para 35][Para 61]. Of 67 contracts expiring on 1 December 2007 the company renewed 63 and did not renew four, including both leaders [Para 52][Para 55]. Both were paid the five percent severance pay and unused leave, but disputed receipt of the seven-day notice payment [Para 37][Para 39][Para 41]. They demanded reinstatement on the ground of union discrimination; the company answered that the contracts had merely expired [Para 42][Para 43].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must reinstate Mr B, union vice-president, and Mr T, union secretary, whose fixed-duration contracts expired on 1 December 2007, because their non-renewal was an act of union discrimination? [Para 31]

Of the twenty demands in the non-conciliation report, seventeen were settled at conciliation and two more (Issues 2 and 3) at the hearing, leaving only Issue 1 for decision [Para 16][Para 19][Para 20].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The two were union leaders whose union mandate had not expired; the company knew of the union since August 2007 yet renewed 63 other contracts while dropping four union members, and neither leader had committed misconduct, so the non-renewal was discrimination [Para 42][Para 52][Para 104].

**Employer (respondent):** The contracts expired by their own terms on 1 December 2007 and the company notified the workers in advance; there was no dismissal and no union discrimination, since several of the renewed workers were also union members, and the two had been kept on for poor performance and indiscipline [Para 43][Para 55][Para 106].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the demand for reinstatement of Mr B and Mr T was rejected, but the panel ordered the employer to allow the two union leaders to perform union activities until the end of the union term in accordance with the union statute [Para 120]

Article 73(1) of the Labour Law provides that a labour contract of specific duration normally terminates at the specified ending date; following Award 100/07 — Ho Yea Cambodia, the panel held that such a contract automatically terminates on that date and neither party may force renewal without consent [Para 89][Para 91]. The contracts ended on 1 December 2007 and both workers signed acknowledgements of expiry, so the obligations of both sides ended [Para 94][Para 100]. On severance, Article 73(7) requires at least five percent of wages paid over the contract term; the company had paid it and the workers confirmed receipt, so that provision was complied with [Para 100][Para 103][Para 104].

On discrimination, the panel held that contract expiry and the term of a union mandate are two distinct things: the employment relationship ends with the contract, whereas the union term governs the relationship between leaders and members, so leaders may continue union activity until the term ends unless the union statute provides otherwise, per Clause 3(4) of Prakas 305 dated 22 November 2001 [Para 107][Para 110][Para 111][Para 112]. Reinstatement had been ordered in earlier cases only where the employer violated Article 12 and Article 279 of the Labour Law by taking union affiliation into account, as in Awards 28/07 — Dae Hwang and E Garment [Para 112][Para 113]. Here there was no dismissal but expiry, and declining to renew was an exercise of the parties' contractual right, not discrimination [Para 114][Para 115]. The burden of proving discrimination lay on the worker party, as held in Awards 90/06 — Evergreen and 112/06 — River Rich, and it was not discharged [Para 116][Para 117][Para 118]. Arbitrator An Nan dissented, finding the non-renewal of the two leaders a violation of Article 12 and would have reinstated them with back pay [Para 141][Para 143].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-duration contract ends on its stated date, and a demand for its renewal must be refused unless the worker shows that the refusal was union discrimination; proof that other union members had their contracts renewed, without more, does not shift the burden to the employer [Para 91][Para 117][Para 118]. Because Article 73 does not touch the union mandate, relief for such workers lies not in reinstatement but in an order permitting them to continue union activities until the end of the union term [Para 110][Para 120].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that the Ministry of Labour did not act on the notification of expiry because expiry is not a dismissal [Para 60][Para 83].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award prints the notification date as "31 November 2007" — a date that does not exist — where the contract is said to end on 1 December 2007 [Para 94]. The worker's birth year is printed as 9 July 2007, which cannot be correct for an adult employee [Para 35]. Case numbers for the Evergreen award are given differently in the award and in the dissent, and the dissent also cites "01/07 Evergreen" against "90/06 — Evergreen" in the majority [Para 117][Para 124]. OCR losses are heavy in this translation: words run together ("themloyer party", "fixeduration"), and the party representative list is split across numbered lines so that some names appear detached from their roles [Para 1][Para 4]. The award is a non-binding award becoming binding eight days after notification absent opposition [Para 120].
