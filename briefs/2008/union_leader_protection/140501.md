# Award No. 43/08-Tai Yang — Tai Yang Enterprise Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 28 April 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-chosen; An Nan, worker-chosen; Pen Bunchhea, chair) · Doc ID 140501

## 1. Facts
Tai Yang Enterprise Co., Ltd, at Thnot Muydeum Village, Bek Chan Commune, Ang Snuol District, Kandal Province, employs approximately 4,000 workers in two buildings under separate administration; the claimant is the local Cambodian Labour Union (CLU) at Tai Yang II, with the Cambodian Labour Union Federation (CLUF) [Para 2][Para 22][Para 23]. Of five demands conciliated on 28 February 2008, one was settled and four reached the Council, which held hearings on 31 March and 4 April 2008 [Para 13][Para 14][Para 15]. The four surviving demands were reinstatement and back pay for two local union leaders who are electricians, deduction of 1,000 riel union dues from the wages of 90 consenting members, payment of wages and bonuses for three union officers who attended earlier dispute-resolution meetings, and recognition of the union registration certificate [Para 5][Para 7][Para 8][Para 9]. The employer's representative told the panel he attended only to comply with the company's request, could make no decision, and left the matter to the Council [Para 18][Para 19].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must reinstate the two local union leaders, both electricians, and pay their back wages, when the same demand was rejected in Case 11/08-Tai Yang? [Para 24][Para 41]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must deduct 1,000 riel monthly from the wages of the 90 members who gave written authorisation, the employer objecting that the union's registered name says Tai Yang II rather than Tai Yang? [Para 7][Para 28][Para 52]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay daily wages and bonuses to the three union officers for the days they attended conciliation at the Department of Labour and hearings at the Council? [Para 8][Para 35]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must recognise union registration certificate No. 1310, dated 21 December 2007 and signed by the Minister of Labour, when it disputes the union's name? [Para 9][Para 39][Para 40]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (Cambodian Labour Union Federation, CLUF, and the local Cambodian Labour Union, CLU, at Tai Yang Enterprise Co., Ltd):** the two elected leaders must be reinstated with back pay; the employer must deduct the 1,000 riel dues authorised in writing by 90 members, since the union registered as the local union at Tai Yang II only to identify the second building, which is separately managed; and the three officers' wages must be paid for the days they attended dispute resolution on the Council's and Department's own invitations [Para 6][Para 29][Para 30][Para 37].
**Employer (Tai Yang Enterprise Co., Ltd):** it does not accept reinstatement; it agrees in principle to deduct dues but will not until the union is renamed to match the company's name, which is Tai Yang and not Tai Yang II; it deducted wages under its Internal Work Rules because workers leaving the premises need written permission; and it recognises only documents it considers correct [Para 6][Para 29][Para 36][Para 40].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel declined to consider Issue 1 (reinstatement), Issue 2 (dues deduction) and Issue 4 (recognition of the certificate), and ordered the company to pay daily wages and bonuses to the three union officers for the period they attended the labour dispute resolution at the Department of Labour and at the Arbitration Council (Issue 3) [Para 80][Para 82][Para 85].
On Issue 1 the panel applied res judicata: the same parties had litigated Case 11/08-Tai Yang, dated 18 February 2008, where issue 2 rejected reinstatement of the same two electricians, and the same issue between the same parties may not be decided twice; it followed Awards 10/06-North Gaiety, 24/06-Fortune, 106/06-Quick Sew, 45/07-Wilson and 14/08-Quick Sew [Para 28][Para 41][Para 44]. On Issue 2 it noted that Article 129(2) lets a worker authorise deduction of union dues in writing, revocably, while Article 281 forbids employers to deduct dues and pay them for workers; reading Article 281 with Article 280 as a protection against employer interference, as in Awards 05/03-Top One, 62/04-Ecent, 94/04-Eternity Apparel, 99/06-AIA and 16/05-New Point World Trade Cambodia, it held that deduction on written authorisation is lawful and that Clause 5 of Prakas 305 of 2001 requires the request fifteen days in advance [Para 46][Para 47][Para 49][Para 50]. It nonetheless declined, because whether the certificate's name is correct falls to the Ministry of Labour, not the Council, and the union must complain there [Para 53][Para 54][Para 55]. Issue 4 fell the same way and was declined on the same ground [Para 77][Para 79]. On Issue 3, Article 306, Clause 20 of Prakas 099 and Clause 10 of Prakas 305 require attendance at conciliation and protect the wages, bonuses and benefits of the representative attending; the company conceded it had received the invitations and had made the deductions, so the panel ordered payment [Para 58][Para 61][Para 63][Para 68][Para 73].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An issue already decided between the same parties will not be reopened by the Council, even in a fresh reference of a different dispute, and the Council will decline to consider it [Para 44][Para 80].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded that in five years of practice the wages and bonuses of worker representatives attending hearings on the Council's invitation are generally not deducted, and observed that the employer had shown no willingness to conciliate while the union had; neither was necessary to the orders [Para 69][Para 73][Para 17].

The certificate number is printed three ways in one document ("No. 1310 KBIVK", "1310 KB/VK", "1310 K.B/V.K"), and the company is called both Tai Yang and Tai Yang II, which is the very dispute before the panel [Para 9][Para 40][Para 85]. Two union officer names differ between the demand and the findings, one appearing with a variant surname in the reasoning [Para 25][Para 26]. Paragraph 22 repeats the word "first hearing" in two invitations both dated 26 and 31 March 2008, so the operative invitation date for the postponed hearing cannot be fixed from the text [Para 22].
