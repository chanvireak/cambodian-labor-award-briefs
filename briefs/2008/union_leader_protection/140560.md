# Award No. 132/08 — J H J (Cambodia) Ltd. (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ជី អេច ជី (ខេមបូឌា)) and the Federation of Trade Unions of Workers at J H J (Arbitration Council, 22 October 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, An Non, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 140560

## 1. Facts
On 7 October 2008 the Department of Labour Disputes received an oral complaint from the federation of trade unions at J H J (Cambodia) Ltd. about a strike in which the workers demanded that the company dismiss the Chinese team leader of Team C (ក្រុម C) for insulting and looking down on workers [Para 9]. Conciliation failed and non-conciliation report No. 1119 dated 9 October 2008 reached the Secretariat on 10 October 2008 [Para 6]. The hearing was set for 17 October 2008 at 08:30; the employer attended, the union and worker party did not, and gave no reason [Para 8][Para 12][Para 13]. The only documents before the panel were a delegation letter from the company's director, the non-conciliation report, the conciliation minutes and the two summonses [Para 14][Para 15][Para 17][Para 18].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether J H J (Cambodia) Ltd. must dismiss the Chinese team leader of Team C for insulting the workers? [Para 2]
2. (Conditions 1–3, Prakas No. 099 Article 21) Whether the panel may close the file in the claimant's absence — that is, whether the party was properly notified, whether it failed to appear, and whether it gave a valid reason? [Para 22][Para 24]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), Federation of Trade Unions of Workers at J H J (សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត នៃ ក្រុមហ៊ុន ជី អេច ជី):** the workers maintained their demand that the company dismiss the Chinese team leader of Team C because he insulted and humiliated them [Para 2]. They filed no documents and put no reasons or evidence before the panel in support of the complaint [Para 15][Para 42].

**Employer (respondent), J H J (Cambodia) Ltd.:** the company stated that it cannot dismiss the Chinese team leader, but would warn or educate him in accordance with its internal rules [Para 3]. It attended the hearing as summoned and produced a letter delegating its director's authority to its administration representative [Para 13][Para 14].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** withdrawn — the panel treated the complaint as abandoned and closed Case No. 132/08 [Para 44][Para 46].

The panel founded its power on Rule 4.7 of the Procedural Rules annexed to Prakas No. 099 dated 21 April 2004, which allows it either to continue in a party's absence or to terminate the proceedings by issuing an award, and on Article 21 of the same Prakas [Para 20][Para 21]. Following Awards 16/07-Lotus and 27/07-IM & W 3, it applied three cumulative conditions: the party must have been properly notified, must have failed to appear, and must have offered no valid reason [Para 22].

Condition 1 was satisfied because the Secretariat's summonses were proved to have reached the party [Para 24][Para 25]. On condition 2 the panel read "appearance" in Article 21 as requiring both presence at the hearing and participation through all four stages of the proceedings — introduction, explanation of procedure and issues, conciliation, and the hearing itself — and found that the union attended none of them [Para 26][Para 27][Para 28]. On condition 3 it found no valid reason: on the hearing day the Secretariat telephoned and was told that the local union president was busy attending a ceremony in the province, and that no other union representative could attend, the party leaving the panel to decide according to procedure [Para 32][Para 33][Para 35]. All three conditions being met, the panel held it had power to issue an award notwithstanding the absence, and concluded that the claimant had abandoned its complaint and had not shown good faith in bringing the dispute for resolution [Para 38][Para 44][Para 45].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a claimant party has been properly summoned and neither appears nor participates at any stage of the proceedings, and offers no valid reason for its absence, the panel may treat the complaint as abandoned and close the file under Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 and Rule 4.7 of the Procedural Rules, its power to award in absentia being discretionary rather than an obligation to hear the claim on the merits [Para 20][Para 21][Para 38][Para 44].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that by choosing not to attend the union gave up the opportunity to present evidence supporting its demand, and that this course did not display genuine good faith in submitting the dispute to arbitration [Para 40][Para 45].
