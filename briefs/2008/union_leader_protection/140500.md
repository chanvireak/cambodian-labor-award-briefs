# Award No. 44/08-Siu Quinh — Siu Quinh Garment (MFG) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 21 April 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phyrum, employer-chosen; Liv Sovanna, worker-chosen; Ang Eng Thong, chair) · Doc ID 140500

## 1. Facts
Siu Quinh Garment (MFG) Ltd, at Kvar Khang Keut Village, Sangkat Dangkor, Khan Dangkor, Phnom Penh, employs approximately 500 workers, of whom about 220 belong to the local Khmer Youth Trade Union (KYTU), the complainant alongside the Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU) [Para 1][Para 15]. In Case 08/07-Siu Quinh the Council had ordered this employer to build a daycare center, and after inspections on 27 November 2007 and 15 January 2008 the labour medical official ordered it finished before 15 February 2008 [Para 15][Para 16][Para 17]. The center opened on 15 February 2008 with one babysitter; three union leaders left work to help with crying children, mothers were called from the line, and on 16 and 18 February mothers of children over three years old were refused entry at the gate [Para 19][Para 21][Para 33][Para 36][Para 72]. The employer deducted about US$ 10 from each of those workers, and the panel twice ordered the union to prove the identities, the days and the exact sums, which it did not do by the deadline [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issues 1 and 3) Whether the employer must maintain wages and the attendance bonus for the three union leaders and for the mothers who stayed at the daycare center instead of working on 15, 16 and 18 February 2008? [Para 17][Para 50]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must admit children aged from eighteen months up to six or seven years to the daycare center, rather than only those aged eighteen months to three years? [Para 48][Para 49][Para 81]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union, KYFTU, and the local Khmer Youth Trade Union, KYTU, at Siu Quinh Garment (MFG) Ltd):** wages and bonus must be maintained for the three union leaders and the mothers, since they were caring for children in the employer's own under-staffed daycare center, and the age ceiling was announced only after mothers had already brought their children; children should be accepted up to six years because mothers cannot take them elsewhere [Para 5][Para 7][Para 28][Para 49].
**Employer (Siu Quinh Garment (MFG) Ltd):** it accepts children only from eighteen months to three years, in line with the Newton Thlay kindergarten rules, and cannot maintain wages for mothers of older children because they do not come to work; on 16 February it told mothers and leaders not to bring children under eighteen months or over three years [Para 7][Para 8][Para 29][Para 50].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel rejected the demand to maintain wages and the attendance bonus for the three union leaders and the mothers (Issues 1 and 3) and rejected the demand to admit children above three years up to seven (Issue 2a), but ordered the employer to stop prohibiting children under eighteen months from attending the daycare center (Issue 2b) [Para 80][Para 81][Para 82].
On Issues 1 and 3 the panel decided on evidence, not on the merits of the caregiving. Articles 312(C) and (D) of the Labour Law and Clauses 24 and 25 of Prakas 099 give it power to require and weigh evidence, and following Awards 63/04-Shine Well, 99/06-South Bay and 33/07-Goldfame it rejects a demand whose claimant supplies no proof [Para 53][Para 54]. The parties agreed the deduction was about US$ 10 each but the union never showed who was deducted, on which days, or whether the sums were equal, although mothers of younger children missed one day and the others three; without that, the claim could not be decided [Para 57][Para 58][Para 59]. The panel nevertheless held that the employer should not have deducted wages from mothers who helped on 15 February, since it had opened an unprepared center and had to call them, nor from mothers of over-three-year-olds on 16 February, since the age change had not been notified in advance; the deduction from 18 February was lawful, and the union leaders' absence was excused because the employer had invited them to negotiate [Para 63][Para 64][Para 69][Para 71][Para 74]. Because no evidence fixed the amounts, no order for repayment issued [Para 55][Para 57]. On Issue 2 the panel read Article 186, which requires a nursing room and daycare center for enterprises with one hundred or more women and sets no upper age, and followed Award 96/06-Wilson to hold that barring children under eighteen months is unlawful [Para 75][Para 76][Para 77]. On the upper limit it followed Case 45/07-Wilson, which fixed three years as reasonable because kindergarten service then becomes available, and found that Cambodian kindergartens generally take children from three to six [Para 77][Para 79][Para 80].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claimant union that cannot identify the workers affected, the days lost and the sums deducted fails for want of evidence, however sympathetic the underlying facts, because the panel will not fix a repayment it cannot compute [Para 54][Para 55][Para 57].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the employer should have planned the center's capacity and babysitter numbers before opening and should have notified any change of the age policy in advance; those remarks did not alter the orders [Para 62][Para 68].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The file is an unofficial English translation with OCR losses throughout, running words together ("theemployer", "Rejecthe", "Non bindingLaward") and dropping letters from "and", "the" and "to" [Para 2][Para 80][Para 82]. The age bracket demanded is printed three ways: eighteen months to seven years in the non-conciliation report, eighteen months to six years in the reasoning, and above three to seven years in the order [Para 6][Para 49][Para 81]. Paragraph 35 breaks off mid-sentence and repeats itself at paragraph 36, losing part of the account of 16 February [Para 35][Para 36]. Internal tension also appears: the reasons state the employer should not have deducted wages on 15 and 16 February, yet the operative order simply rejects the demand, the gap being explained only by the missing evidence [Para 64][Para 71][Para 80]. Individual workers and union officers appear only by role or initial in the body, and the three union leaders are never named in the reasons [Para 19][Para 5].
