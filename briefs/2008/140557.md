# Order No. 121/08 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ីណូ មេក អ៉ិនធើណេស៉ិនណល (ខេមបូឌា) ហ្គាមេន ខូល អិធី ឌី / Sino Mek International (Cambodia) Garment Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 09 October 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (កៅ ថា ច for the employer party, ទួន ស៊ី ផាន់ for the worker party, ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា chair) · Doc ID 140557

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ីណូ មេក អ៉ិនធើណេស៉ិនណល (ខេមបូឌា) ហ្គាមេន ខូល អិធី ឌី (Sino Mek International (Cambodia) Garment Co Ltd), a garment factory at Village 2, Svay Rolum commune, S'ang district, Kandal province, began operating in 2005 and employed about 750 workers. [Para 1][Para 36][Para 37] Two unions brought the case together — សម្ព័ន្ឋ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ នៃ រោងចក្រ ស៊ីណូ មេក with 370 members and សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព ខ្មែរ នៃ រោងចក្រ ស៊ីណូ មេក with 100. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 38][Para 39] Of 19 demands, four were not conciliated; at the hearing the parties settled issue 4 and the workers withdrew issues 1 and 3, leaving only issue 2 — the 5 USD attendance bonus for a worker who is ill with a doctor's certificate. [Para 16][Para 20][Para 21][Para 41]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must keep paying the full 5 USD regular attendance bonus in the case of a worker who is absent sick with a valid medical certificate? [Para 41][Para 56]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), members of សម្ព័ន្ឋ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ នៃ រោងចក្រ ស៊ីណូ មេក and សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព ខ្មែរ នៃ រោងចក្រ ស៊ីណូ មេក:** since April 2008 the parties agreed that a worker with a valid certificate loses no wage, and the bonus is part of wages under Article 103, so the employer may not strip the whole 5 USD even for a half day of certified illness. [Para 42][Para 45][Para 50][Para 51]
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ីណូ មេក អ៉ិនធើណេស៉ិនណល (ខេមបូឌា) ហ្គាមេន ខូល អិធី ឌី:** it kept the wage for certified sick leave but not the attendance bonus, because the bonus requires 26 days' attendance a month and the accounting department misunderstood the April agreement and began deducting the whole bonus from June 2008; the agreement, it says, covered wages only. [Para 41][Para 43][Para 44][Para 47][Para 48]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to deduct the regular attendance bonus only in proportion to the number of days a worker is absent sick with a valid medical certificate, from the date the order takes effect. [Para 80][Para 81]

The panel first framed the question as whether the internal regulations required the bonus for a certified sick absence, noting that the company's rule at clause 3(ង) provides full wages for the first month of certified illness but does not say whether "full wage" includes the attendance bonus, which created the disagreement. [Para 55][Para 57][Para 59] Article 103 of the Labour Law defines wages broadly to include sums paid as bonuses, so "full wage" in the regulations could mean the whole wage package, including the bonus. [Para 62][Para 63] The panel then turned to point 3 of Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000, which grants at least 5 USD a month to a worker who attends the days due in the month, and observed that it does not say what happens where the worker is ill and cannot attend; it followed its earlier reasoning in cases No. 48/05-Manhattan, No. 57/07-Siraphich and No. 62/07-Hong Mi. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 66] It also relied on case No. 31/08-South Bay, where the employer was ordered to deduct the attendance bonus in proportion to days of certified sick leave, and on cases No. 63/04-Sanwell and No. 106/07-IM & V holding that a worker absent with lawful authorisation keeps the bonus in proportion to the days worked. [Para 68][Para 69] The panel reasoned that forfeiting the whole 5 USD for a certified absence would be unjust to the worker, who cannot attend because of illness, but paying the bonus for days not worked would be unjust to the employer; the proportionate deduction reconciles both. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 76][Para 77][Para 79] It noted that the bonus is an incentive for regular attendance, and that the employer must pay it for the days the worker did attend. [Para 70][Para 78]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the internal regulations grant full wages for the first month of certified illness without saying whether the attendance bonus is included, the bonus is not forfeited outright: because it is an incentive for attendance and part of wages under Article 103, it must be reduced in proportion to the certified days absent and paid for the days actually worked. An employer's accounting error in applying an earlier agreement does not justify deducting the whole bonus. [Para 62][Para 74][Para 77][Para 79]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that neither union was the most representative, and that the notification is silent on the sick-leave case, are observations that do not determine the proportional rule it applied. [Para 40][Para 65]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The order decides only issue 2 of 19 demands, issues 1 and 3 having been withdrawn and issue 4 settled at the hearing [Para 20][Para 21], so §2 follows the panel's own numbering rather than renumbering to a single issue.
- The panel records that neither union is most representative [Para 40] but decides the case on the merits without explaining why standing was sufficient.
- The order states that whole-bonus deductions began "from February June 2008" — two months in one phrase — while the agreement is dated April 2008 [Para 42][Para 43].
- The internal regulations are cited as clause 3(ង) [Para 55] and as "ប្រការ៣ ចំណុច (ង)" [Para 57], with the same provision also summarised at [Para 52].
- OCR losses: the company name is broken into "ស៊ីណូ មេ ក" and "ស៊ីណូ បេក" in later paragraphs [Para 23][Para 31], the panel heading is corrupted at [Para 1], and the signature block ends in stray Latin characters [Para 83].
