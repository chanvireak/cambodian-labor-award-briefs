# Award No. 100/08 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្គាវ័ន្ត អី ភែរីល (Arbitration Council, 25 September 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (មាំរ សំបូរ ណា, សុង វណ្ណ ស៊ីន, គង់ ផល្លា) · Doc ID 140541

## 1. Facts
The employer is ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្គាវ័ន្ត អី ភែរីល, a garment factory at Prek Samrong village, Ta Khmau district, Kandal province, with about 800 workers [Para 1][Para 56]. The claimant is the local union of សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ (Khmer Youth Union) at that factory, which put seventeen demands in May 2008 [Para 1][Para 26]. The factory took over the premises, machinery and workforce of the earlier company ប៊ូមីន ខេមបូឌា ហ្គូ មិន (Boomin Cambodia) in 2005, after that company's owner died in a road accident; its shareholders were all former Boomin managers [Para 58][Para 60]. At the hearings of 12 and 15 August 2008 the union withdrew demands 2 to 17 and asked the panel to hear Issue 1 alone [Para 35][Para 36].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must enter on each worker's identity card the date on which that worker first began work, counting service from the time the worker was employed by Boomin Cambodia? [Para 2][Para 55]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The Khmer Youth Union demanded that the company write on every worker's identity card the day, month and year the worker first started work, because the thumbprints taken when the new contracts were signed in 2005 had been used to wipe out their earlier service [Para 55][Para 65]. It said about 600 to 700 Boomin workers had signed new contracts with the new company in 2005, of whom 157 remained, 152 with clear start dates [Para 65][Para 66].
**Employer (respondent):** The company's representative argued that ហ្គាវ័ន្ត អី ភែរីល is an entirely new company, created in 2005, which neither bought nor took any shares from the old Boomin company, so it is not the same employer and owes nothing for earlier service [Para 64][Para 68]. Pressed on how it came to hold Boomin's assets, the representative said he did not know [Para 74].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the company was ordered to recognise and grant seniority (អតីតភាព ការងារ) to the 152 workers named in the annexed list, counted from the date each began working for ប៊ូមីន ខេមបូឌា ហ្គូ មិន, with effect from the day the award takes effect [Para 108][Para 109].
The panel built its reasoning on Article 87 (new) paragraph 1 of the Labour Law, which provides that where the employer's legal status changes — by succession, sale, merger, transfer of funds or incorporation — contracts already in force continue unchanged between the new employer and the workers [Para 75][Para 76]. It read the words "ជាអាទិ៍" ("and the like") as making that list illustrative rather than closed, and held that a change of legal status means a change in the person of the employer, whether that person is natural or legal [Para 77][Para 78][Para 79].
On the facts, the panel found that the new company was indeed registered in 2005 as a new legal entity, but that it had come in place of Boomin and carried on the same garment business at the old factory, on the old premises, using the old machines and the Boomin workforce [Para 88][Para 91][Para 96]. It expressly asked the company's representative whether there was any evidence of severance paid by Boomin to the 152 workers, or of any agreement between the two companies about the assets; the representative could only answer that he did not know [Para 74]. Because the employer produced no such evidence and did not cooperate, the panel found this to be a succession of employers, so the workers' contracts stayed alive against the new company [Para 89][Para 91][Para 92]. Since 152 workers had clear start dates, the panel accepted that list [Para 66][Para 107].
The panel added that under Article 122 (new) paragraphs 1 and 3 of the Labour Law, unpaid wages and the severance and notice payments owed to workers enjoy priority over the debtor employer's assets during the six months before liquidation, and that the new company, having absorbed Boomin's assets before any declaration of insolvency or court-ordered winding up, also took on those liabilities [Para 101][Para 103][Para 104][Para 105]. It noted that Article 87 (new) paragraph 3 does not release an employer from its obligations merely because the enterprise stops operating, except in cases of force majeure [Para 97]. Because the company never proved the 152 workers had been paid off by Boomin, the claim had a sound legal basis [Para 106][Para 107]. The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification unless a party objects in writing [Para 110].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A newly incorporated company that takes over the premises, machinery and workforce of a predecessor and keeps producing without any evidence of severance paid to the old workers is a change in the legal status of the employer under Article 87 (new); the old contracts survive against it, and it must recognise service seniority running from each worker's first day with the predecessor [Para 83][Para 91][Para 92]. Lack of cooperation from the employer on how it acquired the predecessor's assets is held against the employer, not the workers [Para 89][Para 106].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's discussion of the Khmer dictionary meaning of "ជាអាទិ៍" [Para 77], and its account of the priority of wage and severance claims over the employer's assets under Article 122 (new) [Para 101][Para 103], go beyond what was needed to order recognition of seniority.

## 6. Source defects disclosed
This is a Khmer original with heavy OCR damage. The company's name is spelled differently in nearly every mention (five or six competing Khmer renderings), and the predecessor company's name likewise varies, so the two entities can only be matched by context. The annex runs to several pages of a badly mangled table: worker names, identity numbers, section numbers and start dates are interleaved with stray Latin fragments, and a number of dates are visibly incomplete or show impossible years, so the list cannot be counted with confidence beyond the figure of 152 the panel itself states [Para 111][Para 112][Para 113]. The procedure section is internally inconsistent: it says sixteen of seventeen points were conciliated [Para 26], then that none of the seventeen was settled at the hearings and that the union withdrew points 2 to 17 [Para 34][Para 35]. The decision paragraph repeats its own heading and its operative sentence twice [Para 109], and the document closing date is printed in the annex in a form that does not match the award date on the face. Individual workers are named only in the annex and are not reproduced here.
