# Award No. 56/08 — City New Garment Company (Arbitration Council, 20 May 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 140510

## 1. Facts
City New Garment Company operated at Sangkat Srah Chok, Khan Doun Penh, Phnom Penh, with 927 workers covered by the claim and two unions at the factory, the Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU) and the Free Trade Union of Workers of the Kingdom of Cambodia (FTUWKC). [Para 50][Para 51][Para 2][Para 5] In early March 2008 the company notified the workforce that in June 2008 it would move to Preksomrong Village, Takhmao District, Kandal province, 15 kilometres from Phnom Penh, keeping employment structures, seniority and positions unchanged. [Para 16] About 200 workers struck over severance pay for those who would not move; the panel issued an interim order on 23 April 2008 to stop the strike, and the employer made return to work conditional on each worker signing a contract declaring the strike illegal and promising to follow lawful strike procedure in future. [Para 51][Para 23][Para 27]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must provide the workers with benefits according to the Labour Law when the company moves from the old location to the new location? [Para 14]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — KYFTU and FTUWKC):** the workers demanded that City New Garment Company provide benefits under the Labour Law on relocation, and complained that payments to workers who refused to move were not transparent, with three or four workers still unpaid as at 16 May 2008. [Para 14][Para 37][Para 40]
**Employer (respondent — City New Garment Company):** the company refused the demand, saying it had already supplied transport by automobile, or US$ 5 a month to workers using their own vehicles, for three months, that it gave notice in March 2008 of the June move and would preserve structures, seniority and positions, and that it had begun paying workers who did not move from 5 May 2008. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 34]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** withdrawn — the panel closed Case No. 56/08-City New Garment after both parties reported a settlement and asked the Council to close the file, leaving no subject matter to decide. [Para 56][Para 52][Para 54]

On procedure the panel recited its jurisdiction under Chapter XII Section 2B (Articles 309 to 317) of the Labour Law (1997), the Prakas on the Arbitration Council No. 099 of 21 April 2004 and the Prakas on the Appointment of Arbitrators No. 076 of 10 May 2007 (Fifth Term), and recorded that conciliation under Chapter XII Section 2A had failed, the non-conciliation report No. 466 K.B/AK/V.K having been sent to the Secretariat on 22 April 2008. [Para 16][Para 18]

On the strike the panel recorded that its interim order of 23 April 2008 directed the workers back to work and the employer to admit them, that the strike nonetheless continued, that the employer would admit strikers only if they signed a contract declaring the previous strike illegal, and that the panel warned it might discontinue the proceedings because the Labour Law requires both parties to remain peaceful throughout arbitration. [Para 23][Para 27][Para 33]

The panel never reached the substance of the relocation demand. On 8 May 2008 the parties agreed that workers who did not agree to move would be paid instead; the company said payments began on 5 May 2008 and would finish by 12 May 2008, while the unions said the number of workers paid was unclear. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 35] On 16 May 2008 FTUWKC asked the Council to close the case, the company did the same, and although KYFTU reported three or four unpaid workers it too requested closure on 20 May 2008. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40][Para 41] The panel had earlier refused to accept evidence the union filed once the proceedings had stalled. [Para 30]

The case was closed under Rule 4.10 of the Arbitration Procedural Rules annexed to Prakas No. 099 (Clause 31), which provides that settlement through conciliation is always the desirable option and that the parties retain at all times the right to settle on their own terms, including during the course of arbitration. [Para 51] Because the complainant had withdrawn, the subject matter of the dispute no longer existed and there was nothing for the Council to consider; the panel aligned that result with Arbitral Award No. 20/07-Star Knitting. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 55]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the parties to a collective dispute settle the underlying demand between themselves during the arbitration and both ask the Council to close the file, the panel will close the case rather than decide the demand: the withdrawal of the claim removes the subject matter of the dispute, and the parties' right to settle on their own terms at any stage, preserved by Rule 4.10 of the Arbitration Procedural Rules, is not displaced by the fact that a minority of affected workers is not shown to have received the agreed payment. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that it could not accept evidence tendered by the union once proceedings had stalled, and that it could discontinue proceedings where a party breaches the duty of peace, were not applied to any outcome in this case. [Para 30][Para 33]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The case number is printed as "56108- City New Garment" in the header [Para 1] but as "56/08-City New Garment" in the reasons and decision [Para 51][Para 56]; the header form lost the slash in OCR.
- Conciliation dates conflict: the panel says the conciliation hearing took place on 22 April 2008 and the non-conciliation report was submitted that day [Para 18], but also that the final conciliation was conducted on 7 April 2008 [Para 19]; the evidence list dates the departmental record 7 April 2008 and the report 22 April 2008 [Para 44].
- The facts give 927 workers in the claim but 200 workers in the strike [Para 50][Para 51].
- Heavy OCR word-joining runs through the translation ("theemployer", "themployer", "Workerepresentative", "Labour Lawhen", "the lastrike"), and the party-listing block splits each representative's name across line breaks so that given name and surname cannot be assigned with confidence [Para 2][Para 5][Para 27].
- One FTUWKC officer's surname is spelled two different ways in the same document, in the representative list and in the notification record [Para 6][Para 46].
- Individual worker, union-official and employer-representative names are not reproduced in this brief; only the parties and the panel are named.
