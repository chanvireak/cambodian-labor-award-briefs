# Award No. 84/08 — Trinunggal Komara Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 04 August 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tai Seng, appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna, appointed by the workers; Pen Bunhea, chair) · Doc ID 140534

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ទ្រី នុង ហ្គាល (កូមា រាំ (Trinunggal Komara Co., Ltd.), a garment factory operating since 1999 with about 1,500 workers, was told by the owner of its premises on 30 March 2008 that the lease was ended and that it had five months to find a new site [Para 38][Para 40][Para 22]. The company leased a factory at Tuol Pongro, Chaom Chao, Dangkao district, Phnom Penh from 01 July 2008, roughly 8 to 10 kilometres from the old site in Teuk Thla, and told the staff to move there with their work and equipment [Para 47][Para 48][Para 80]. The workers struck from 04 to 07 July 2008 and returned on 08 July after a Council order [Para 50][Para 51]. The claimant union, សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ រោងចក្រ ទ្រី នុង ហ្គាល កូមារ៉ា, representing some 800 workers on open-ended contracts, demanded that the company terminate the contracts and pay all statutory compensation before any move [Para 39][Para 2].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company is entitled to require the workers to work at the new factory site? [Para 71][Para 78]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the workers may require the company to terminate the labour contracts and pay all compensation before they move to the new site? [Para 71][Para 106]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the relocation changes the place of performance written into the contract, which names the old address, and breaches clause (b); workers with five to ten years' service were owed two months' notice, rents near the new site are dearer, and the company should pay under Articles 116, 75, 91, 89 and 77 before re-hiring them at the new site [Para 52][Para 54][Para 55][Para 56].
**Employer (respondent):** the move is not a breach because it follows from the landlord's notice, no vacant site could be found near the old factory, and the new site is better placed near town with rents of USD 25 to USD 30 a month unchanged; the company offered a USD 7 to USD 9 fuel allowance or factory transport, USD 6 added to base wage and USD 5 added to the USD 5 attendance bonus for three months, while guaranteeing wage, position, seniority and benefits [Para 57][Para 58][Para 60][Para 61][Para 62][Para 63][Para 64].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — workers who decline to move may treat the contract as terminated and the company must pay their final wages, severance under Article 89 of the Labour Law and compensation for unused annual leave under Article 167; workers who accept the move are entitled to USD 10 a month or factory transport, USD 6 added to base wage and USD 5 added to the regular USD 5 attendance bonus for 6 months, with wage, position, seniority and benefits preserved [Para 142][Para 143][Para 144][Para 145][Para 146]
The panel began from Article 2 of the Labour Law and its own earlier Award No. 106/06 against the same employer, holding that an employer may redeploy staff from one building to another provided four conditions hold: no reduction in wage, no removal to a workplace far from the original, no shift between day and night, and no effect on essential skills [Para 71][Para 73]. It read the move as a relocation of the company, not merely of a branch, because the production chain at the old site was dismantled and shifted wholesale [Para 77][Para 78], and it held that the contract must be performed at the old factory, so transfer without the worker's consent harms the worker and breaches the contract [Para 80][Para 85][Para 87]. Article 65 of the Labour Law and Articles 22 and 29 of Sub-Decree No. 38 of 28 October 1988 require performance at the agreed place, quantity, quality and duration [Para 81][Para 84].
It then balanced the two sides: the relocation was caused by a landlord's notice outside the employer's will, the distance of 8 to 10 kilometres within Phnom Penh was not excessive, and rents and housing were comparable, so no damages for wrongful termination were due [Para 115][Para 169][Para 117]. The workers therefore had a real choice, and the panel settled the terms of each branch by equity under Article 312 and Prakas No. 099 [Para 88][Para 94][Para 98][Para 101]. Because the employer could not continue at the old site and had offered support, the panel refused the union's request to convert the award from binding to non-binding, holding that the parties had agreed to a binding award at the hearing [Para 136][Para 141][Para 148].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A relocation of the whole production chain to a new site is a change in the place of performance of the contract, and where the move is forced by the landlord, is not excessive in distance and leaves wages, hours and skills intact, it is not a breach by the employer; the worker then chooses either to move on terms that preserve wage, position and seniority with a transitional allowance, or to treat the contract as ended and take the Article 89 severance and Article 167 leave payment [Para 85][Para 108][Para 118][Para 131].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that it was departing from its earlier awards on branch relocations, because this was a relocation of the company itself, explains but does not decide the case [Para 79][Para 80].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award carries a long dissenting opinion by the employer-appointed arbitrator, appended as an annex, which would have held the relocation no breach at all and refused both the severance and the transitional benefits, on the view that the landlord's notice was outside the employer's control and that the workers were exploiting the situation [Para 149][Para 155][Para 177][Para 185]. That dissent is not the award [Para 142][Para 146]. Article 312 is printed once as "Article 312" and once with a corrupted initial character [Para 98][Para 71], and the panel cites Article 89 for severance in one place and the text of Article 90 in another, so the intended article must be read from context [Para 118][Para 108]. The record of the final announcement of the award type is garbled and repeats itself [Para 132][Para 133][Para 147]. Optical recognition left stray Latin words and punctuation in the signature block and in the closing line [Para 149][Para 201]. The new site is named in three slightly different spellings across the award [Para 47][Para 67][Para 80].
