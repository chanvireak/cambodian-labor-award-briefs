# Award No. 71/08 — Riri Ch Tech Style Ltd (Arbitration Council of Cambodia, 10 July 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of three: ម៉ារ សំបូរ ណា (employer-appointed), អន ណន (worker-appointed), ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា (president) · Doc ID 140524

## 1. Facts
The employer, ក្រុមហ៊ុន រី វើរី ច តិច ស្តាយ អិលធីឌី (Riri Ch Tech Style Ltd), runs a garment factory at Prek Ta Pring, Saang, Kandal, employing about 2,500 workers. The workers' side, សម្ព័ន្ឋ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ នៃ រោងចក្រី វើរី ច, brought a collective dispute through the complainant union សហជីព ស៊ី ខៅ ឌូ, which held most-representative status under certificate 003/08 of 9 January 2008 [Para 1][Para 59]. Conciliation at the Kandal labour office settled 2 of 7 points; the 5 unresolved points reached the Council on 20 May 2008 and were heard on 28 May, 9, 17 and 24 June 2008 [Para 16][Para 20]. Before the merits the panel also had to decide whether its own award would bind the parties [Para 145].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay women with at least one year's service 50% of wages, secondary benefits included, across the 90 days of maternity leave, computed on the average of the last 12 months? [Para 61][Para 159]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must notify the piece-rate price of each new series within 7 days of the workers starting it? [Para 67][Para 174]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the US$5 monthly regular-attendance bonus may be deducted pro rata for days of leave the company itself authorised? [Para 75][Para 192]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must take back the three security guards dismissed in November 2007 and pay them back wages? [Para 78][Para 219]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the company must reinstate the five remaining mobile-section workers whose fixed-term contracts were not renewed? [Para 94][Para 242]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** maternity pay must be 50% of the 12-month average of total monthly wages plus all allowances, applied from now on with no arrears claimed for past cases [Para 64][Para 66]; piece rates must be fixed 7 days into a series, because a 25th-of-month announcement leaves workers unable to judge their earnings [Para 71]; the three guards' resignations were extracted under company pressure over union membership [Para 91]; and the five non-renewals were union discrimination [Para 252].

**Employer (respondent):** maternity pay is lawfully computed on base wage, attendance bonus and seniority multiplied by three [Para 161]; the law sets no deadline for publishing piece rates, so the 25th-of-month practice stands and the company tops any shortfall up to US$1.92 a day [Para 74][Para 185]; notification 017 of 18 July 2000 forfeits the whole attendance bonus on any absence [Para 77]; the guards resigned of their own will and refused the statutory compensation [Para 93]; and the five were not renewed for poor work [Para 257].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issues 1, 2, 3 and 4 were granted and issue 5 dismissed; the award was declared non-binding [Para 272]. The company must compute maternity pay as the last 12 months' wages divided by 12, then by 2, then multiplied by 3, from the date the award takes effect [Para 173][Para 267]; notify each series' lot price after 7 days [Para 268]; deduct the US$5 attendance bonus pro rata to authorised leave days [Para 269]; and reinstate the three guards with back pay from the date of dismissal [Para 270].

On issue 1 the panel read Articles 182 and 183 with Article 103: seniority pay, incentive pay and allowances are wage, so the 12-month average including them is the statutory base, following earlier awards on the same point [Para 162][Para 169]. On issue 2, Article 112 obliges the employer to inform workers clearly whenever wage terms change; the statute fixes no number of days, so the 7 days claimed matched the 3-to-7-day range in prior awards and was granted in full [Para 177][Para 188][Para 191]. On issue 3 the panel held the attendance bonus is wage under Article 103, and that authorised absence suspends the contract under Articles 71 and 72, so the bonus accrues for days worked and is reduced, not forfeited, for days authorised absent [Para 204][Para 211]. On issue 4 the panel found the resignations void: the company put its objection to union membership to the guards directly, offered no contrary account, and Article 12 and Article 279 forbid using union membership to decide dismissal, so the guards returned with back pay [Para 230][Para 240][Para 241]. On issue 5 the panel applied Article 73: notice of non-renewal had been given, and the burden of proving discrimination lay on the workers, who showed only bare membership — no union cards, no activism, and 1,559 members still at work [Para 262][Para 263][Para 266].

On the preliminary question the panel held the February 2008 agreements could not found a binding award: one copy carried no signatures of the parties, the other was signed for the company by a person not named in its articles, without the company seal and by photocopy, and no delegation from the registered chairman was produced [Para 154][Para 157]. The award therefore issued as non-binding under Article 313 procedure, one arbitrator dissenting on that point alone [Para 158].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a wage term changes with each production series, the employer's duty to inform under Article 112 is discharged by notifying the piece rate within seven days of the workers starting that series; and an authorised absence reduces the regular-attendance bonus in proportion to the days absent rather than extinguishing it, because the bonus is wage earned on days actually worked [Para 191][Para 211].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that a resignation procured by an employer's pressure to leave the union is void for want of real consent, and that security staff hold the same right to union membership as any other worker [Para 231][Para 237].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The case name on the face of the award is garbled, printing the factory's name in a truncated form that differs from the spelling used throughout the body. The bonus figure is printed two ways: US$56 in the reasoning paragraph that fixes the deduction and US$5 in the operative paragraph. A 2023 date appears in the reasoning where 2008 is meant. Individual worker and guard names are printed in two or three different spellings across sections, so the brief refers to them by role. Latin and machine-noise strings intrude into four paragraphs, and one arbitrator signs a dissent in his own name while the majority is unsigned in the copy.
