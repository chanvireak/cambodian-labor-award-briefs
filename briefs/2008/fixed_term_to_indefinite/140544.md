# Award No. 105/08 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហៃ តិច ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) អិលធីឌី (Arbitration Council, 4 September 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៉ីង សុទ្ធី, អន ណន, អាំង អេង ថុង) · Doc ID 140544

## 1. Facts
The employer is ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហៃ តិច ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) អិលធីឌី (Hai Tech Garment (Cambodia) Ltd), on National Road 2, Sangkat Chak Angre Leu, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employing just over 592 workers in two unions [Para 1][Para 28][Para 29]. The claimant is the Democratic Garment Workers' Union Federation at the factory, printed in the award as ស៊ី ខៅ ឌូ [Para 1][Para 29]. Thirty-six workers in the table-cutting (តុ កាត់) and printing sections demanded 100% of their wages for the period their employment contracts were suspended, on the ground that the suspension was unlawful because the labour inspectorate had not yet replied [Para 2][Para 39]. The company suspended their contracts from 1 August 2008 for two months, notifying the inspectorate on 29 July 2008 and the workers on 30 July 2008, and offered 50% of wages [Para 3][Para 44][Para 45][Para 48].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay the 36 workers in the table-cutting and printing sections 100% of their wages during the suspension of their employment contracts, the labour inspectorate not having authorised it? [Para 2][Para 39]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The Democratic Garment Workers' Union Federation argued that the suspension was not made according to law, since no reply had yet come from the labour inspector, and that the workers were therefore entitled to their full 100% wages for the two months [Para 2][Para 49]. It added that on earlier suspensions the company had always paid 100%, which the company did not deny [Para 36][Para 37].
**Employer (respondent):** ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហៃ តិច said it could pay only 50% of basic wage and the regular attendance, seniority, living and responsibility allowances, because it had already applied to the labour inspector for permission to suspend [Para 3][Para 48]. It said it had no sufficient buyers' orders and could not guarantee work for the two sections after the two-month suspension, and that in any case the inspector had refused to approve the suspension because the union had not agreed [Para 41][Para 47].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to pay 100% of wages to the 36 workers of the table-cutting and printing sections for the period of suspension that had not been authorised, running from 1 August 2008 until the labour inspectorate grants authorisation [Para 69][Para 70].
The panel took its rule from Article 71 (new) paragraph 11 of the Labour Law, which allows an enterprise facing serious economic or material difficulty to suspend activity for no more than two months, under the supervision of the labour inspector [Para 50][Para 55]. It found the two-month limit respected [Para 56]. On supervision, the facts were that the company had notified the inspectorate but the inspector had issued no letter of reply and no authorisation [Para 57].
The panel then asked what "under the supervision of the labour inspector" means, and answered that notification alone cannot make a suspension lawful: if it could, an employer acting in bad faith could suspend contracts indefinitely simply by sending notice, which would empty the words of meaning and defeat Article 72(1)'s protection of the worker's wage [Para 58][Para 63][Para 64]. It read the phrase as requiring a clear, express response of consent from the inspectorate [Para 61][Para 65]. Following Awards 21/03 Loy All and 60/04 United Art, it held that where a suspension does not comply with Article 71(11) the employer must pay 100% of wages for the whole period [Para 66][Para 67][Para 68]. It also noted that the company's own earlier practice was to pay 100% during suspensions [Para 36][Para 37].
One member dissented. The arbitrator chosen by the employer appended a written objection to the award, disputing the recognition of the 36 claimants as members of the union and the award of 100%, arguing that only 28 of them had produced membership cards and that a letter of authorisation was needed for the union to represent non-members [Para 74][Para 79][Para 94][Para 95]. The award itself is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent written opposition to the Minister [Para 71].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of employment contracts under Article 71 (new) paragraph 11 is lawful only once the labour inspectorate has actually authorised it; mere notification of the intended suspension leaves the employer liable for 100% of wages for every day until authorisation issues [Para 63][Para 65][Para 66]. The two-month statutory ceiling limits the period but cannot by itself validate a suspension the inspectorate never approved [Para 55][Para 56][Para 69].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remarks that an employer in bad faith could otherwise suspend indefinitely on notice alone [Para 64], and its reliance on the company's past practice of paying 100% [Para 36], are supporting observations rather than the ground of the order.

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The file as scanned contains the dissenting opinion of the employer-side arbitrator as a long annex after the signatures, drafted in the first person and styled as an objection, so the document reads as two awards in one file; the annex repeats the case number as "105/0" in one place and refers to the union by several different Khmer spellings [Para 97]. The claimant count cannot be fixed: the demand speaks of 36 workers, the union's own verification says 35 of the 36 were members, the membership-card copies produced were 28, and the written complaint carried 34 thumbprints [Para 31][Para 33][Para 74][Para 77][Para 78]. The signature block prints the worker-side arbitrator's name twice and the employer-side arbitrator's name with inconsistent spelling between the award and the annex [Para 72][Para 98]. OCR damage is heavy: whole clauses of Article 71 and Article 72 appear with stray marks, and the phrase quoted from the article is broken mid-sentence in the reasoning [Para 50][Para 58]. Individual workers' names in the annex are withheld here; the award prints one claimant's given name when discussing membership [Para 75].
