# Award No. 60/08 — PCS Garment Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ភីស៊ី អេស ហ្គាម៉ិន លី មីធិត) v Khmer Youth Union Federation at the PCS factory (Arbitration Council, 28 May 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom, employer-appointed; Tuon Si Phan, worker-appointed; Kong Chholla, presiding) · Doc ID 140517

## 1. Facts
PCS Garment Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ភីស៊ី អេស), a Phnom Penh garment factory of about 5,100 workers, faced seven collective demands lodged by the Khmer Youth Union Federation at the factory (សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ នៃ រោងចក្រ ភីស៊ី អេស) [Para 44]. Conciliation failed on all seven, and the Ministry of Labour's report of 17 March 2008 sent them to the Council [Para 18]. At the hearing the union withdrew demands 1 to 5 and 7, leaving only demand 6 — the reinstatement of its president, who had worked at the factory since 2001, resigned in July 2007 over a refused pay rise, was re-engaged on 23 October 2007 at USD 180 a month without any written contract, and was dismissed on 22 January 2008 [Para 22][Para 56][Para 58][Para 61][Para 63].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 6) Whether the employer's termination of the claimant union president on 22 January 2008 was anti-union discrimination rather than a dismissal for incapacity or during probation, and whether he must therefore be taken back into work? [Para 79]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissal came after the union notified the company of its elected leadership on 2 January 2008 and after the company's security post received the election results on 9 January 2008; the company pressed him on 15 or 16 January to give up the union presidency, and he refused [Para 69][Para 70]. He had worked continuously since 2001, was never told he was on probation, and no written contract of any kind exists [Para 92].

**Employer (respondent):** it terminated him for deficient management capacity — workers complained about him constantly — and treated him as a probationary employee under its internal rules, so the dismissal was within its contractual rights [Para 63][Para 64][Para 66]. It also disputed his identity, asserting that no worker of his registered name was on its payroll [Para 50].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to take the claimant union president back into work with effect from the day the award becomes enforceable [Para 109].

On the character of the contract, the panel applied Article 67 (new), paragraph 7, of the Labour Law: a fixed-duration contract must be in writing, and an unwritten one becomes a contract of indefinite duration [Para 81][Para 82]. Since he was re-engaged on 23 October 2007 with no written instrument, the panel held the relationship to be of indefinite duration [Para 83]. Article 74 then governs: such a contract may be terminated at the will of one party, but only by written notice to the other, and never without a valid reason concerning the worker's capability or conduct or the operational needs of the enterprise [Para 84][Para 86]. The employer produced none of that, so Article 74 was held not to have been complied with [Para 90]. The probation defence failed because he had worked for the company from 2004 with only a short break and had returned at the company's own invitation, and because no written contract documented any trial period [Para 93][Para 94]. A 2004 agreement the employer tendered was held unrelated, having predated the events and never having been invoked at the hearing [Para 89].

The claim being one of anti-union discrimination, the panel restated its standing rule that the accusing worker carries the burden of proving the discrimination, citing its earlier awards 112/06 and 01/07 (Sok Prim) [Para 95][Para 96]. On the evidence the burden was discharged: the sequence from the 2 January 2008 notification of union officers, the 9 January receipt of election results, the 14 January objection to his candidacy, the mid-January request that he step down, to the 22 January dismissal was too tight to be coincidence, and the employer offered no proof at all to displace the allegation [Para 98][Para 100][Para 101]. Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law forbid an employer from using union membership or union activity as the basis for hiring, discipline or termination [Para 103][Para 104]. The panel found anti-union discrimination and, following its practice in earlier cases, reinstated rather than awarding damages [Para 102][Para 106].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer dismisses a worker whose contract has become one of indefinite duration for want of a written instrument, without written notice and without proving a reason going to capability, conduct or the needs of the enterprise, and the dismissal follows closely on the employer's learning of the worker's union office, the dismissal is anti-union discrimination under Articles 12 and 279 and the remedy is reinstatement rather than compensation [Para 90][Para 100][Para 106].

**Obiter dicta:** the restatement that the burden of proving anti-union discrimination lies on the accusing worker, and the panel's citations to awards 112/06 and 01/07, were not necessary to a result that turned on the employer's total failure of proof [Para 95][Para 97].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The scan is a Khmer original with patchy OCR: fragments of the typewritten page numbers and letterheads survive mid-sentence, and several statutory and award references are corrupted — the Labour Law article is printed once as "740" and the prior award numbers in the burden-of-proof passage are partly illegible, so only the first of them is usable [Para 90][Para 96]. The award contradicts itself on the union election: the leadership ballot is dated 29 December 2008, a date later than the January 2008 dismissal it is said to have prompted, and the surrounding narrative implies December 2007 [Para 46][Para 63]. The count of unsettled demands is printed as "0700" where seven is meant [Para 21]. The claimant union president's name is printed in several variant spellings through the award and the employer disputed his identity by pointing to a second, workplace name he used; the panel resolved the point from his national identity card and family book, and the name is withheld here [Para 50][Para 75]. The presiding arbitrator's name appears in a different form at the signature block than in the panel composition [Para 111].
