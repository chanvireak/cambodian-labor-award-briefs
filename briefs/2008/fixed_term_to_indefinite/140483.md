# Award No. 111/08 — Tai Yang Enterprises Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 18 February 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ang Eng Thong chair; Ing Sothy, employer nominee; An Nan, worker nominee) · Doc ID 140483

## 1. Facts
Tai Yang Enterprises Co., Ltd, at Tnot Muy Deum village, Bek Chan commune, Ang Snoul district, Kandal, employs 3,648 workers and faces the Local union of Cambodian Labour Union (CLU) at Tai Yang Factory, registered on 21 December 2007. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 19] Of eight demands conciliated on 9 January 2008, six were settled and two referred; the panel conciliated the first of the two itself and heard only the second. [Para 13][Para 14] That issue concerned two electricians who began on 17 March 2004 as white-card workers on two-month contracts, were moved between factories under the same owner, were made regular workers after a year without any written contract, and were told at the end of 2006 that one-year contracts had expired; the company never produced those contracts, the workers said they had not thumb-printed them, and after accepting five per cent severance they signed one-year contracts running from 8 January 2007 to 8 January 2008, which the company then let expire. [Para 27][Para 30][Para 33][Para 37][Para 38]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2(a)) Whether the company must return onto undetermined-duration contracts those workers whose contracts it had changed to fixed duration? [Para 19][Para 47]
2. (Issue 2(b)) Whether the two electricians held undetermined-duration contracts, whether their termination at the end of 2006 was unlawful, and whether the one-year fixed-duration contracts they signed in January 2007 were valid? [Para 51][Para 70][Para 81]
3. (Issue 2(b), union discrimination) Whether the non-renewal of their contracts was discrimination on account of union membership or activity contrary to Article 12 of the Labour Law? [Para 93][Para 94]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the Local union of Cambodian Labour Union at Tai Yang Factory argued that the two men had more than two years' seniority and had never signed written fixed-duration contracts, so they were undetermined-duration employees whose 2006 termination was unlawful; they signed the one-year contracts only because they had no choice and feared losing their jobs, and they asked for reinstatement. [Para 4][Para 40][Para 46]
**Employer (respondent):** Tai Yang Enterprises Co., Ltd, through its general administrative assistant, argued that the two had thumb-printed one-year contracts that expired at the end of 2006, that it terminated them for expiry and notified the Labour Inspector, that it had complied with the law and offered five per cent severance under Article 73, and that it would consider the reinstatement demand. [Para 8][Para 32][Para 44][Para 45]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected both the demand to restore undetermined-duration contracts and the demand to reinstate the two electricians, while holding that the company owed them indemnity for dismissal, notice pay, compensation and paid annual leave for the 2006 termination and payment according to law on the 2008 expiry. [Para 81][Para 90][Para 96]
On Issue 2(a), the union gave no list of the workers affected, promised documents it never supplied, and offered no specific facts, so the panel rejected the demand, following Awards 63/04-Shine Well, 99/06 South Bay and 74/07-Global Apparel. [Para 19][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49]
On the contract type, Article 67 requires a fixed-duration contract to be in writing and otherwise makes it undetermined duration; since no written contract existed, the panel held both men had undetermined-duration contracts from mid-2005. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 67] The company never produced the one-year contracts it said expired at the end of 2006, so it had no valid reason for that termination under Article 74 and gave none of the benefits required by Articles 75, 89, 91 and 167, as explained in Award 97/04-Grace Sun; compensation was therefore owed. [Para 68][Para 73][Para 74][Para 78][Para 80]
The January 2007 contracts stood differently. Conversion from one contract type to another requires the consent of both parties (Awards 69/04-Common Way Fashion and 03/06-Flying Dragon), and although the men said they signed under pressure, they produced no evidence of force, did not protest, and accepted the termination payment for the old contracts; the panel held they should have protested then rather than remain silent until expiry, so the one-year contracts and their expiry in January 2008 were valid. [Para 82][Para 85][Para 87][Para 90] On union discrimination, the worker party neither alleged that union leadership caused the non-renewal nor produced evidence, so the panel found no link to union activity under Article 12. [Para 94][Para 95]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-duration contract that is not in writing becomes an undetermined-duration contract under Article 67, so terminating it needs a valid reason under Article 74 and the statutory payments; but a worker who signs a fresh fixed-duration contract without protest and takes the termination payment for the old one is treated as having consented to the conversion, and the later expiry of that contract is lawful. [Para 65][Para 74][Para 87][Para 90]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the company had notified the Labour Inspector of the terminations, a fact it recorded without treating notification as curing the absence of a valid reason. [Para 45]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The translation is a damaged OCR that drops "th" throughout ("themployer", "athere", "tworkers", "withexplanation") and repeats whole clauses, so that the two remaining issues are introduced twice and the Article 67 and Article 74 quotations each appear twice in slightly different wording. [Para 14][Para 64][Para 71] The case number is printed as "11108" with the slash lost, and the award is dated 18 February 2008 while the hearings ran on 28 January and 11 February 2008. [Para 1][Para 12] The conciliation date is given as 16 January 2008 in the procedural summary but as 9 January 2008 in the next paragraph and in the minutes cited as evidence; both are reported. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 19] The union is described as registered at "Tai Yang II Factory" in the findings and at "Tai Yang Factory" elsewhere, and the two dismissed electricians are named in full throughout the source but are given here by initial and role. [Para 2][Para 27][Para 96]
