# Award No. 78/08 — Unisan Garment Co Ltd (Arbitration Council of Cambodia, 2 July 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of three: មាំរ សំបូរ ណា (employer-appointed), ទួន ស៊ី ផាន់ (worker-appointed), គង់ ផល្លៈ (president) · Doc ID 140529

## 1. Facts
The employer, ក្រុមហ៊ុន យូនីសាន់ ហ្គាមេន ខូអិលធីឌី (Unisan Garment Co Ltd), a factory of about 543 workers at Tuol Pongro, Dangkao, Phnom Penh, faced a collective dispute brought by សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ ប្រចាំ រោងចក្រ យូនីសាន់, registered by certificate 1403 of 14 March 2008 [Para 61][Para 63]. Of 16 demands, conciliation settled 7 and the panel settled 8 more, leaving only issue 4, the reinstatement of two men: the union president Mr C and a worker and union activist Mr T [Para 27][Para 32][Para 33]. The company suspended the president on 4 April 2008 for doing union work in working hours and for pressing it to rehire a worker dismissed for theft, and applied to the labour department for permission to dismiss him on 7 April 2008 [Para 67][Para 71]. It dismissed the activist on 23 April 2008 without notice after he stayed away one day beyond authorised leave [Para 77][Para 81].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 4) Whether the company must reinstate the union president it suspended on 4 April 2008, when the labour inspector never answered its request for permission to dismiss him? [Para 65][Para 98]
2. (Issue 4, second limb) Whether the company must reinstate the union activist dismissed on 23 April 2008 for absence without leave, or whether statutory compensation suffices? [Para 89][Para 112]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** both dismissals were union discrimination, the activist saying the administration told him at the time that the company had intended to dismiss him from the day he joined the union [Para 82]. The president denied nothing but faced dismissal over union work and over his support for a dismissed colleague; the union demanded both men back at work [Para 65].

**Employer (respondent):** the president committed serious misconduct under Article 82 — conducting union business on company time and disrupting the production line, and threatening the company over the reinstatement of a worker dismissed for stealing materials, an incident captured on video [Para 67][Para 68][Para 69]. The activist was dismissed at will for unauthorised absence, having already been warned twice about using working hours for union organising, and it offered him full statutory severance, notice pay, final wages and annual-leave pay [Para 83][Para 87]. It denied discrimination, noting that many members remained employed and union dues were still deducted [Para 84].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must take the union president back until the Minister decides on the dismissal, and must pay the activist full statutory severance; his reinstatement was refused [Para 137][Para 138].

On the president the panel applied Article 293 of the Labour Law: no protected worker, including a union leader, may be dismissed without the labour inspector's agreement [Para 90][Para 97]. The inspector must decide within one month, and where no decision is communicated the request is deemed refused [Para 93][Para 96]. The company applied on 7 April 2008 and the award issued more than 30 days later, so refusal was deemed from 7 May 2008; the company then had two months to appeal to the Minister and did not [Para 100][Para 102][Para 104]. Because Article 295 provides that a summary dismissal is void and its effects erased where the inspector does not agree, the panel held the company could not dismiss the president at all and ordered his reinstatement pending the ministerial decision [Para 107][Para 110][Para 111]. His misconduct was therefore not reached.

On the activist the panel applied Article 74: an indefinite-term contract may be terminated at the employer's will, but only by written notice and only for a reason connected with the worker's aptitude or conduct or the needs of the enterprise [Para 112][Para 114]. The company gave no notice at all, so the termination was procedurally unlawful [Para 117]. In substance, however, the panel accepted that taking working hours for union organising and absenting himself without authorisation were valid grounds, that he had been warned twice, and that union business may not be conducted in working time save in a genuine emergency [Para 122][Para 125][Para 127]. On discrimination it applied the rule that the worker bears the burden of proof, found the company's explanation stronger, and rejected reinstatement while ordering the severance the company had itself offered [Para 132][Para 135][Para 136].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that suspends a protected union leader and asks the labour inspector for permission to dismiss must wait for the inspector's decision and, if refused or deemed refused, for the outcome of its appeal to the Minister; dismissing or withholding the worker's post without that agreement is void and the worker returns to work in the meantime, whatever the merits of the alleged misconduct.

**Obiter dicta:** union officers may not conduct union business during working hours except by lawful authorisation or in a genuine emergency affecting workers' health and safety [Para 122][Para 123].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer-appointed arbitrator's name is printed as មាំរ សំបូរ ណា in the panel heading and as ម៉ារ សំបូរ ណា in the signature block, and the president's signature line is partly unreadable. The company's name is spelled in several different forms, and the union's name is written with the federation and the local union run together in the party heading. The report of the conciliation is dated and numbered inconsistently between the procedural section and the evidence list. One paragraph records the number of unsettled points as a corrupted digit string, and the findings record without resolving that the company says all wages and allowances were paid while the workers say two or three months remain outstanding. Two individual workers are named in full in the findings, including one accused of theft, and those names have been replaced by initials here.
