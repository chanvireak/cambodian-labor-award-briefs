# Order No. 115/08 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ថប វ័ន្ត ហ្គាមេន / Thop Vong Garment (Arbitration Council, 24 October 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (ម៉ារ សំបូរ ណា for the employer party, លីវ សុវណ្ណា for the worker party, ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា chair) · Doc ID 140553

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ថប វ័ន្ត ហ្គាមេន (Thop Vong Garment), a garment factory in Sangkat Svay Pak, Khan Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, employed 742 workers. [Para 1][Para 54] The only union in the factory, សហភາຖ សហជីព សាមគ្គីភាព កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ នៃ រោងចក្រ ថប វ័ន្ត, with about 200 members, filed 15 demands on 05 August 2008 to improve working conditions; nine remained non-conciliated after the last conciliation of 26 August 2008. [Para 1][Para 25][Para 26][Para 55][Para 56] At the hearing the workers withdrew demands 6 and 9, leaving issues 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7 and 8 for decision. [Para 30][Para 31]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer discriminated against pregnant women by not renewing their fixed-duration contracts at expiry? [Para 57][Para 126]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must build a day-care centre (ទារកដ្ឋាន) in the factory for the children of women workers? [Para 74][Para 156]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay 2,500 riel as a meal allowance, or provide one free meal, to workers who voluntarily work overtime from 16:00 to 18:00? [Para 82][Para 176]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must pay 1,500 riel as a meal allowance to workers who voluntarily work on public holidays? [Para 90][Para 206]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the attendance bonus may be deducted in proportion to days of absence that were lawfully authorised by the employer? [Para 96][Para 222]
7. (Issue 7) Whether the employer must reimburse annual leave for workers of four years' service or more whose paid annual leave was previously granted incorrectly? [Para 101][Para 247]
8. (Issue 8) Whether the employer must stop using fixed-duration contracts and convert them into contracts of indefinite duration? [Para 115][Para 271]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), members of សហភាព សហជីព សាមគ្គីភាព កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ នៃ រោងចក្រ ថប វ័ន្ត:** pregnant women on fixed-duration contracts are never renewed although the work continues and new workers are hired in their place; a nursery is needed because the 5 USD substitute cannot pay for outside care; the 1,000 riel meal money no longer buys a meal; and fixed-duration contracts of three or six months leave workers one expiry away from losing their job. [Para 68][Para 75][Para 85][Para 289]
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន ថប វ័ន្ត ហ្គាមេន:** renewal depends on the production line's need, not on pregnancy; the site is leased and too cramped to build a nursery, so the 5 USD paid since an oral agreement at the end of 2007 should continue; the 1,000 riel meal allowance is what Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 requires; and holiday work is already paid at 200 per cent, so no meal money is owed. [Para 72][Para 80][Para 89][Para 92][Para 219]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must build a day-care centre, failing which it must pay the actual invoices of an outside nursery, must provide one free meal to overtime workers from 16:00 to 18:00, and may only deduct the attendance bonus in proportion to authorised days of absence; the discrimination, 2,500 riel, 1,500 riel, annual-leave reimbursement and contract-conversion claims were dismissed. [Para 299][Para 301][Para 303][Para 298][Para 302][Para 304][Para 305]

On Issue 1 the panel held that Article 73 (new) of the Labour Law makes a fixed-duration contract expire by its own terms, following its reasoning in case No. 100/07-Hou Yea Cambodia, so renewal is the parties' choice; Articles 12 and 182 protect women only during maternity leave, and the workers produced no evidence — a promised list of contracts and proof of replacement hiring never arrived by 03 October 2008 — so the discrimination claim failed for want of proof, the burden being on the complainant union. [Para 126][Para 127][Para 133][Para 141][Para 144][Para 149][Para 152] On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 186 of the Labour Law, which requires an enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a nursing room and a nursery, and held that paying 5 USD does not discharge that duty; because the company says it has no land, the order is to build one in the factory and, failing that, to reimburse outside nursery invoices. [Para 156][Para 158][Para 169][Para 171][Para 173] On Issue 3 the panel split the claim: no article or agreement requires 2,500 riel, and Notification No. 017 point 4 — preserved by Notification No. 745 of 23 October 2006 — sets 1,000 riel or one free meal, so the money claim was refused; but since 1,000 riel can no longer buy a meal in 2008, the workers may claim the meal instead, following case No. 47/07-Chung-Fai. [Para 181][Para 182][Para 197][Para 199][Para 204][Para 205] On Issue 4 the panel held that holiday work is not overtime under Article 139 (new) and Prakas No. 80, and that the statutory 200 per cent wage under Prakas No. 10 of 04 February 1999 already covers it; the 1,500 riel claim had no legal basis. [Para 217][Para 220][Para 221] On Issue 5 the panel found the attendance bonus part of wages under Article 103 and held that deducting it for authorised absence penalises the worker for an absence the employer allowed, so deduction must be proportional to days absent. [Para 232][Para 242][Para 246] On Issue 7 the panel accepted Article 166 (18 days, plus one day per three years' service) but dismissed the claim because the union's list lacked each worker's signature, the preparer's name and date, and any reason why the leave was wrongly granted. [Para 253][Para 266][Para 268][Para 269] On Issue 8 the panel held that a contract is a consensual act under Article 65 and Article 22 of the Civil Code, so neither side can force the other to change its type; both the demand and the refusal are lawful. [Para 273][Para 277][Para 291][Para 293][Para 297]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the Labour Law imposes a duty on the employer — such as the nursery required by Article 186 once 100 or more women are employed — a cash payment agreed with the workers cannot buy the employer out of it, although where performance in kind is impossible the employer must bear the actual cost of the equivalent outside service on production of invoices. Conversely, a claim that rests on the employer's discretionary choice (renewing a fixed-duration contract, or converting it to one of indefinite duration) fails, because neither party may impose a contract type on the other, and a claim for arrears fails where the union's supporting list is unsigned and unexplained. [Para 169][Para 171][Para 268][Para 291]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's encouragement to the employer to give priority to renewing pregnant women's contracts, and its remark that clearer evidence might have produced a different result on the annual-leave claim, are observations that do not bind the orders made. [Para 137][Para 270]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- Issue numbers jump: the panel decides 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7 and 8 after the withdrawal of 6 and 9 at [Para 30]; §2 keeps that numbering rather than renumbering to 1–7.
- The 15 original demands are reported as nine non-conciliated points at [Para 26]–[Para 27], and the text never lists all fifteen, so four demands are untraceable.
- Amounts appear two ways: the meal allowance is 2,500 riel at [Para 82] but printed "១០ ១៥០០ រៀល" with a stray digit and then "1,500 riel" at [Para 7]; the nursery payment is "5 dollars" at [Para 4] and 05 dollars at [Para 153].
- The union's name is printed with a corrupted second word at [Para 1] and again with different spelling at [Para 55].
- Numerous OCR breakages in the operative part: "ចំណុច វិ វាទ ទី ៥" loses its mark at [Para 303], and the chair's signature line is reduced to Latin garbage at [Para 307].
