# Award No. 123/07 — E Garment Co, Ltd (Arbitration Council, 14 January 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, An Nan, Kong Phallack chairing) · Doc ID 140659

## 1. Facts
E Garment Co, Ltd (E Garment Company), a Kandal Province factory of 2,000 workers, faced a collective dispute filed by the Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers Democratic Union (C.CAWDU) [Para 34]. The union notified the company of its leadership election on 31 July 2007, held it on 3 August and applied for registration the same day; the Department of Labour Disputes refused registration on 31 August 2007 for insufficient documents [Para 36][Para 38]. Between 18 August and 13 September 2007 the company terminated 28 workers elected as union leaders or activists, all on two-month fixed-duration contracts, and recruited 25 replacements in the ironing unit [Para 41][Para 50]. The parties had signed an agreement to accept a binding award on 19 November 2007, and the employer wrote on 22 November 2007 asking to revert to a non-binding award [Para 65][Para 66].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the 28 workers were dismissed for union discrimination and must be reinstated? [Para 40]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must convert fixed-duration contracts into undetermined-duration contracts? [Para 58]
3. (Additional issue) Whether the employer may change the award from binding to non-binding after agreeing to a binding award? [Para 63]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissals came within weeks of the leadership election, the contracts had always been renewed before, and the company hired 25 new workers to replace them, so the terminations were discrimination on union grounds [Para 49][Para 50].
**Employer (respondent):** the contracts were short-term and expired naturally because there were no purchase orders, the company had the right to end them on notice and had agreed to pay the unfinished terms, and it denied discrimination [Para 55][Para 58][Para 81].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must reinstate the 28 workers; the demand to convert contracts was rejected, as was the employer's request to change the award to a non-binding one, so the award is binding [Para 108][Para 100][Para 107].

On Issue 1, Article 73 allows early termination of a fixed-duration contract only by written agreement before a labour inspector or for serious misconduct or force majeure, and unilateral early termination entitles the worker to damages at least equal to the remaining remuneration [Para 69][Para 70][Para 71]. The workers claimed reinstatement rather than damages, so the panel examined the reason for termination and held the claimant must prove discrimination, following Awards 90/06-Evergreen, 112/06-River Rich and 01/07-Supreme [Para 73][Para 74][Para 75]. The contracts had always been renewed and stopped only when the dispute arose; the company produced no evidence of missing orders and did not answer the allegation that it hired replacements, so the panel inferred there was work and that the dismissals were discriminatory [Para 77][Para 80][Para 81]. Article 12 and Article 279 forbid any account of union affiliation in dismissal, and reinstatement was ordered consistently with Award 28/07-De Fang [Para 84][Para 85].

On Issue 2, Article 67(1) requires a precise finishing date, Article 67(2) caps fixed-duration contracts and renewals at two years, and Article 67(7) requires writing, failing which the contract becomes undetermined [Para 87][Para 90][Para 94][Para 95]. The contracts were written, ran two or six months, and no worker exceeded two years, so the statutory conversion did not apply [Para 93][Para 96]. Article 65 makes the form a matter of the parties' agreement, so no third party could compel a different contract type [Para 97][Para 98]. The demand failed for lack of legal basis [Para 100].

On the additional issue, the panel had explained the award types and both parties signed for a binding award without questions; it followed Award 59/05-Tack Fat that such an agreement cannot be undone without mutual consent [Para 103][Para 104][Para 106].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where fixed-duration contracts were consistently renewed and ceased only when a union election was notified, and the employer offers no evidence of lost work while failing to answer an allegation that it hired replacements, the panel infers the terminations were discriminatory and orders reinstatement under Article 12 and Article 279 [Para 77][Para 81][Para 85].

A fixed-duration contract that is in writing and stays within the two-year cap of Article 67(2) does not convert into an undetermined-duration contract, and the panel will not compel a party to adopt a contract type it never agreed to [Para 93][Para 98].

Once both parties have signed an agreement to accept a binding award, neither may resile unilaterally [Para 106][Para 107].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the union was not yet registered, that it claimed 200 members, and that this did not prevent it from bringing the collective demand [Para 35][Para 36].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The reasoning at Issue 2 cites Article 76(2) where the text it paraphrases is Article 67(2), and the decision text repeats "Issue 2" as a bare heading [Para 92][Para 89][Para 86]. The non-conciliation report is said to name 29 workers although both parties confirmed 28, and the contract table is so badly scrambled by OCR that start, expiry and termination dates cannot be attributed to individual rows [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41]. Two workers' names appear in three different spellings across the evidence list and the findings, and one row records an expiry of 18/12/2007 for a dismissal said to have occurred in September 2007 [Para 29][Para 42]. Names of the 28 individuals are withheld here; they are described by union office only.
