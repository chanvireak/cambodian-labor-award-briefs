# Award No. 87/08 — W&D Cambodia Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 04 August 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ri, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Pen Bunhea, chair) · Doc ID 140536

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ដាប់ ប៊ីល យូ អែន ដ៏ ឌី ខេមបូឌា ខូអិលធីឌី (W&D Cambodia Co., Ltd), a garment factory operating since 1998 at Russey village, Stung Meanchey, Phnom Penh, employed about 1,450 workers, of whom some 1,390 were claimants, and the Free Trade Union at the factory (សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ ប្រចាំ រោងចក្រ W&D Cambodia) with about 100 members brought the claim [Para 1][Para 29][Para 31]. Since 1998 the company had engaged everyone on fixed-duration contracts of one year at a time, and from 2007 it stopped giving notice of expiry, renewing by silence, with the workers continuing to work [Para 32][Para 35]. The workers claimed five per cent severance on each contract that had run out between 1998 and 2006 [Para 36][Para 2]. Of the demands, six points were not conciliated and one came before the panel [Para 10][Para 11].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the workers are entitled to five per cent terminal compensation for the fixed-duration contracts that expired each year from 1998 to 2006, while their employment relationship continues? [Para 45][Para 47]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ ប្រចាំ រោងចក្រ W&D Cambodia asked for five per cent of wages paid on each contract's expiry, on the ground that from 1998 to 2006 the contracts were genuinely fixed-term and the company refused to pay the statutory terminal compensation even when asked, relying on the Council's earlier award in the same company's case no. 05/06 [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38][Para 40].
**Employer (respondent):** W&D Cambodia Co., Ltd replied that since its contracts had been renewed by silence the total duration exceeded two years, so under Article 67(2) of the Labour Law they had already become open-ended, and the law requires no five per cent payment where a fixed-duration contract is simply continued [Para 41][Para 42][Para 46].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the claim for five per cent terminal compensation was rejected, and no sum was ordered; the panel advised the employer to issue written open-ended contracts [Para 71][Para 72][Para 70]
The panel applied Article 67(2) of the Labour Law, which caps a fixed-duration contract and each renewal at two years and provides that a longer succession converts the contract into one of indefinite duration, and it followed its own Awards 10/03 (Zaxin Tech), 36/06 (Mon Dut) and 57/06 (Evergreen Garment) on that conversion [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 51]. On the facts, one-year contracts had been renewed from 1998 through 2008, so total duration far exceeded two years and the contracts were open-ended, with seniority running from the first contract [Para 52][Para 53].
It then construed Article 73(6), which requires terminal compensation of at least five per cent of wages paid during the contract where no collective agreement fixes a rate, and held that the words "on termination of the contract" mean the moment a fixed-duration contract reaches its end date and the employment relationship is actually severed [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57]. Following Awards 60/06 (New Bek Garment) and 76/06 (Sovann Phy), it held that a contract renewed at its end does not sever the employment relationship, so the benefit carries over to the new contract and no five per cent is payable at the mere expiry of a renewed term [Para 61][Para 62][Para 64][Para 66].
Because the workers were still employed and their contracts had already become open-ended, there was no severance of the relationship and the condition for the payment was not met [Para 59][Para 60][Para 69]. The panel also observed that it would be clearer for the workers if the employer issued the open-ended contracts in writing [Para 70].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive one-year contracts whose total duration exceeds two years become a single contract of indefinite duration under Article 67(2), so the five per cent terminal compensation under Article 73(6) is not payable at the expiry of each renewed term; it falls due only when the employment relationship is actually severed [Para 50][Para 52][Para 57][Para 62].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's recommendation that the employer put the now open-ended contracts into written form is advice, not an order [Para 70].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The operative order prints the rate claimed as 50 per cent, while the demand, the reasons and the reasoning throughout say five per cent, so the figure in the order cannot be read literally [Para 72][Para 2][Para 56]. The company name is spelled several different ways across the file, including in the commercial-registration exhibit [Para 1][Para 16][Para 29]. The signature block is badly damaged: two of the three arbitrators' names carry extra syllables or characters not present in the composition line, and placeholder tokens stand where signature images were [Para 74][Para 75]. Article references are printed with a corrupted initial character in the reasons, so "Article" appears in a form that does not resolve [Para 50][Para 55]. The conciliation section states six non-conciliated points against a claim of five points, and only one issue is carried forward, with no explanation of the remainder [Para 10][Para 11]. Optical recognition also spliced stray Latin characters into the middle of the reasoning [Para 43][Para 44].
