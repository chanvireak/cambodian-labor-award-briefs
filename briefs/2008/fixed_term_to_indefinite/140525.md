# Award No. 75/08 — D.Y. Night Thing IMF Jeans Cambodia Co Ltd (Arbitration Council of Cambodia, 23 June 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of three: កៅ ថា ច (employer-appointed), អន ណន (worker-appointed), គង់ ផល្លៈ (president) · Doc ID 140525

## 1. Facts
The employer, ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឌី យូណាយធីត ភ្លឺ ធីង អឹម អេហ្វ ជឺ ខេមបូឌា ខូអិល ធី (D.Y. Night Thing IMF Jeans Cambodia Co Ltd), a garment factory in Mittapheap district, Preah Sihanouk, employing about 302 workers, faced a collective dispute filed by សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ នៃ ព្រះរាជាណាចក្រ កម្ពុជា ប្រចាំ រោងចក្រ ឌី យូណាយធីត [Para 27][Para 28]. The union's complaint of 16 May 2008 raised three demands; conciliation on 27 May 2008 left one unresolved, referred to the Council on 29 May 2008 and listed for hearing on 13 June 2008 [Para 13][Para 14][Para 15]. That single unresolved demand was that the company pay statutory severance and other compensation on closing the factory, or, if it stayed open, provide sufficient work and pay monthly wages regularly; the union also asked the company to confirm that funds were deposited in a bank and not to ship equipment out during the dispute [Para 3][Para 5]. Neither party attended the hearing, and no documents were filed by either side [Para 16][Para 23].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the panel should close the case without examining the single unresolved demand, because the worker party, duly summoned, did not appear or send a representative and gave no valid reason? [Para 34][Para 42]

The substantive demand on severance, work and wages was not reached and is described in §1.

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ នៃ ព្រះរាជាណាចក្រ កម្ពុជា demanded full statutory severance and other payments if the factory closed, sufficient work and regular monthly wages if it did not, a clear statement that money had been banked, and no removal of factory materials while the dispute ran [Para 3][Para 5]. On the day, its representative told the secretariat the union could not attend and gave no reason, then asked the panel to decide the case in its absence according to law [Para 17][Para 18].

**Employer (respondent):** ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឌី យូណាយធីត denied any plan to shut the factory, said it was trying to find suitable work and to pay wages on time each month, and asserted that moving or selling equipment in the factory is the company's own right [Para 6][Para 7]. Its representative did not appear and offered no reason either [Para 19].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel closed case 75/08 without considering the union's one unresolved demand, leaving it undetermined [Para 44].

The panel read Prakas 099 on the Arbitration Council, Article 21, with Rule 4.7 of the annexed procedure: where a party has been properly notified but absents itself without a valid reason, the panel may either continue in that party's absence or terminate the arbitration by issuing an award [Para 31][Para 32]. Following Awards 30/05 and 16/07, it held that termination requires three conditions — proper notification, non-appearance, and no valid reason for it [Para 33]. It then tested each. Notification: the secretariat's invitation letters 375 and 374 of 6 June 2008 reached both sides, so condition one was met [Para 25][Para 34]. Non-appearance: the definition requires both presence at the hearing and participation through to the end; the workers were absent throughout, so condition two was met [Para 36][Para 37]. Valid reason: the union was telephoned on the hearing day and said only that it could not come, offering nothing to the secretariat, so condition three was met [Para 17][Para 40]. Having found all three satisfied, the panel terminated the proceedings under Article 21 and Rule 4.7 rather than deciding the demand in default [Para 43].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A panel may terminate a labour arbitration and leave the demand undetermined, instead of deciding it in default, only where it finds each of three conditions on the record: the party was properly notified, the party neither appeared nor was represented, and the party gave no valid reason for its absence.

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that presence for the purposes of Article 21 means attending the hearing and remaining through the whole proceeding, not merely being summoned [Para 36].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The signature block is largely destroyed: the employer-appointed arbitrator's name is replaced by machine noise, and the president's signature line is unreadable, so only two of the three arbitrators can be named from the face of the award. The company's name is printed in a shortened and differently spelled form in the conciliation and evidence paragraphs than in the header. The Prakas 099 citation carries a stray later year inside the 2004 date in the reasoning paragraph. There is no party evidence at all — both sides filed nothing — so the findings rest entirely on the secretariat's file, and the award records the single demand without any figure for the sums claimed.
