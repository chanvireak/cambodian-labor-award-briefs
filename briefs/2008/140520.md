# Award No. 67/08 — Yong Min Cambodia Apparel (ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉ុង មិន ខេមបូឌា "អាប់ ត្យេរ៉ឹល") and the Union Federation of Workers at the factory (Arbitration Council, 6 June 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Tha Ch, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Kong Chholla, presiding) · Doc ID 140520

## 1. Facts
Yong Min Cambodia Apparel (ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉ុង មិន ខេមបូឌា), a Phnom Penh garment factory at Dangkao with about 890 workers, faced six collective demands filed through the union federation on 18 March 2008 [Para 12][Para 23][Para 24]. Conciliation on 24 April 2008 settled four, leaving two: the employer's practice, begun in late 2007, of reducing the monthly seniority bonus in proportion to days absent, and the failure to pay wages and terminal allowances to workers who had resigned since October 2007 [Para 13][Para 14][Para 30][Para 35]. Only one union exists at the factory, with about 600 members [Para 25]. Both parties agreed at the hearing that the panel should decide the two remaining points [Para 17]. At the panel's direction the union filed a list of 69 named workers who had resigned without final payment; the employer did not contest the list [Para 43][Para 44].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer may lawfully deduct the monthly seniority attendance bonus pro rata to the days a worker is absent, or whether the bonus must be preserved in full? [Para 26][Para 29]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay the wages owed to the 69 workers who resigned since October 2007, and pay any terminal allowances within 48 hours of the ordinary payday? [Para 33][Para 71]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the seniority bonus is support money for workers who stay with the company, guaranteed by Notification No. 017, and the employer's financial difficulties do not entitle it to reduce it; the practice had been applied every day, whether the absence was lawful or not [Para 29][Para 30]. On issue 2 they said the company pays wages on the 10th of each month but had paid nothing to resigning workers since October 2007 [Para 34][Para 35], and they identified between 10 and 20 unpaid workers before later filing 69 names [Para 39][Para 43].

**Employer (respondent):** it began pro-rating the bonus only from late 2007 because of a financial crisis, had never done so between 1999 and 2007, and Notification No. 017 does not forbid the deduction [Para 30][Para 31]. It admitted it had not paid resigning workers since October 2007, blaming the number of departures and the lack of notice, said it could not promise a date, but would ask the owner to settle the arrears [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 1 was dismissed, and on issue 2 the employer was ordered to pay the wages of the 69 listed workers who resigned from October 2007 and to pay terminal allowances within 48 hours of the ordinary payday [Para 85][Para 86].

On issue 1 the panel first established that the seniority bonus is a component of wages, following Awards 41/05 (Violet), 66/06 (Golida) and 96/06 (Wilson) [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57]. It then applied Article 71(k), paragraph 6, of the Labour Law, under which an authorised absence suspends the contract, and Article 72(k), paragraph 1, under which suspension relieves the employer of the duty to pay wages unless a contrary provision applies [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60]. On that basis the employer owes nothing for a day the worker did not work, and a bonus that forms part of wages may be reduced by the ratio of the month's bonus to 26 working days multiplied by the days absent [Para 63][Para 68]. The panel worked the arithmetic: a worker with four years' service on the USD 5 rate absent two days should lose USD 0.385 [Para 69]. Because the deduction tracked Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000, point 5, which fixes the seniority rates at USD 2, 3, 4 and 5 after one to four years, the practice was held not to violate that instrument, and the demand was refused [Para 46][Para 51][Para 67][Para 70].

On issue 2 the panel applied Article 116(k), paragraph 5, of the Labour Law, which requires wages and all allowances to be paid within 48 hours of termination [Para 71][Para 72]. It noted that it had ordered the same in Award 54/07 (Yong Va 1) [Para 74]. The employer filed no contrary evidence on the arrears [Para 75]. The panel also recorded that the union had narrowed its claim at the hearing to the 48-hour timing point and had not specified what terminal allowances were owed, so the claim for allowances at the moment of termination was treated as abandoned, while the 48-hour payment order was granted [Para 80][Para 82][Para 83].

The award is printed as binding, the parties having agreed at the hearing of 26 May 2008 to accept it [Para 87].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A bonus that forms part of wages may be reduced in proportion to days absent, including authorised absences, because an authorised absence suspends the contract under Articles 71 and 72 of the Labour Law and the employer's wage obligation with it; but the reduction must follow the statutory formula of the month's bonus divided by 26 working days and multiplied by the days absent [Para 63][Para 68].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's aside that the employer had never deducted the bonus between 1999 and 2007, and its acceptance that financial difficulty motivated the change, played no part in the holding [Para 30][Para 31].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR is heavily degraded through the fact-finding section: the list of 69 resigning workers is a run of names and identity-card numbers that repeats and truncates, and those individuals are identified here only as the listed workers [Para 43]. Two numbers on that page are lost or ambiguous, and the hearing start time is printed as "130" without hours [Para 11]. The Government Notification is dated 18 July 2000 in one place and "200" in another [Para 52]. The operative paragraphs repeat the heading "Issue 2" inside the Issue 2 order and garble the direction on the 48-hour allowance, so the order has been read together with the reasoning at Paras 77 to 84 [Para 85][Para 86]. The conciliation minutes are transcribed twice in succession [Para 20]. The employer's name appears as Yong Min, Yong Min Cambodia and Yong Min Cambodia Apparel across the file [Para 1][Para 19].
