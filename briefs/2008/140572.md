# Award No. 149/08 — Cambo Hansae Co., Ltd. / ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូ ហាន់ ស៊ែ ខូ.អិលធីឌី (Arbitration Council, 19 December 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Kong Phalla (president) · Doc ID 140572

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូ ហាន់ ស៊ែ ខូ.អិលធីឌី (Cambo Hansae Co., Ltd.) runs a garment factory at Trapeang Chrey village, Sangkat Kab, Khan Dangkao, Phnom Penh, with nearly 770 workers [Para 68]. The claimant is សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ (the Free Workers' Union) at the company, which says it has about 700 members [Para 69][Para 70].

On 30 October 2008 the union put eight demands to the employer; a meeting on 4 November produced no agreement, and at 12:30 on 5 November the workers struck, sitting at their own workstations [Para 71][Para 72][Para 73]. That evening the company suspended the union president, the deputy president and the staff delegate with effect from 6 November, alleging that they had incited the strike and that the strike was procedurally unlawful, and the same day it asked the Labour Dispute Department for authorisation to dismiss them, which was received on 6 November [Para 74][Para 79]. On 7 November 2008 the Phnom Penh First Instance Court issued an injunction ordering all workers back to work except those three [Para 83]. Conciliation on 20 November 2008 failed on all thirteen demands [Para 22]. At the hearing of 8 December 2008 the parties settled twelve of the thirteen — issues 2 to 13 — leaving only issue 1, the reinstatement of the three leaders [Para 26][Para 27].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the three union leaders were entitled to be taken back and allowed to work normally while their dismissal file still sat with the Labour Inspector? [Para 90][Para 111]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ says the three did not incite anyone, that the strike took place inside the factory at the workers' own workstations, and that the employer produced no evidence of incitement, so the three should be reinstated [Para 75][Para 76][Para 77].

**Employer (respondent):** ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូ ហាន់ ស៊ែ ខូ.អិលធីឌី says it cannot take the three back until a court rules whether they were at fault [Para 3]; it never received any decision from the Labour Dispute Department and never appealed to the Minister [Para 80][Para 102]. It also says the workers disobeyed the injunction, that all of them committed serious misconduct, and that it dismissed more than 500 of them and paid them off [Para 88][Para 89].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to take back Mr Y as union president, Mr L as deputy president and Ms P as staff delegate and to let them work normally until the Minister of Labour decides, should the employer lodge an appeal within two months [Para 111][Para 112]. The order is not binding and becomes final eight days after notification if no party objects to the Minister [Para 113].

Article 293 of the Labour Law forbids dismissal of a staff delegate or a candidate without the Labour Inspector's agreement, keeps that protection for three months after the term ends, and — read with Article 4 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 — extends it to three union leaders [Para 90][Para 91][Para 98]. Under Article 293(4) an application left undecided for one month is deemed refused, and the panel found the company's application of 5 November, received on 6 November 2008, still unanswered when the hearing opened, so the month from 6 November to 6 December 2008 had run and refusal was presumed [Para 96][Para 99][Para 100].

Article 295 lets an employer suspend the contract at once pending the Inspector's decision, but provides that if the Inspector refuses, the suspension is void and its effects are erased by operation of law [Para 104][Para 105]. The panel adopted the reading of Award No. 107/08 that the firm may suspend while it waits for the Inspector, and applied it here: because the Inspector never answered within the period, the suspension of the three contracts on 5 November 2008 was void [Para 106][Para 107][Para 109][Para 110]. The employer's failure to appeal to the Minister confirmed that no lawful dismissal existed [Para 102].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that applies to the Labour Inspector for leave to dismiss a protected union leader, and receives no decision within the one month fixed by Article 293(4), is taken to have been refused, and the suspension of that leader's contract under Article 295 is then void by operation of law, so the employer must take the leader back and let him work until the Minister rules on any appeal [Para 100][Para 105][Para 110].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's finding that the suspension letter's accusations of incitement were unproven, since the employer showed no evidence of them and the strike occurred at the workstations, was not necessary once the statutory period had expired [Para 76][Para 77].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The number of demands is printed three ways: thirteen at conciliation, "130" points in the hearing record, and fourteen filed with one withdrawn, which leaves the thirteen actually before the panel [Para 22][Para 26][Para 78]. The company's notice about the strike is dated 5 November 2008 in one clause and 14 November 2008 in the next [Para 47]. The employer's assertion that it dismissed more than 500 workers and paid them off sits uneasily with an award that reinstates three of them without mention of the others [Para 89]. The panel composition is printed with one spelling on the face and another in the signature block, and the worker-appointed arbitrator's name is broken by Latin OCR fragments there [Para 114].
