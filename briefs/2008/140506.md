# Award No. 45/08 — Ei Garment Co. Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អ៉ី ហ្គាមេន ខូអិលធីឌី) and Khmer Youth Federation of Trade Unions (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ) (Arbitration Council, 28 April 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ouk Ri, Toun Si Phan, chair Pen Bunhea · Doc ID 140506

## 1. Facts
Ei Garment Co. Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អ៉ី ហ្គាមេន ខូអិលធីឌី), a garment factory at Svay Rolum, Saang, Kandal with roughly 2,000 workers, hired Mr S. as head of human resources on 8 January 2008 to reform working practices in the administration. [Para 21][Para 32][Para 35] The Khmer Youth Federation of Trade Unions (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ) took up grievances that he used inappropriate language, insulted workers, issued warnings not conforming to the internal rules, and was strict about leave. [Para 36][Para 47] At conciliation on 20 March 2008 point 8 of the minute recorded that, for the common interest, he had agreed to resign; he filed a resignation that day and withdrew it on 22 March 2008. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 27] A further conciliation on 25 March 2008 settled three of four demands, leaving this one unresolved. [Para 28][Para 29]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1, question 1) Whether the workers have the right to require the company to dismiss Mr S. from his post? [Para 48][Para 49]
2. (Issue 1, question 2) Whether the conciliation minute of 20 March 2008 stating that Mr S. agreed to resign obliges the company to dismiss him? [Para 48][Para 68]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (Khmer Youth Federation of Trade Unions):** the union and its factory committee demand that the company dismiss Mr S. because workers dislike his inappropriate speech, his insults, his warnings issued contrary to the internal rules, and his strict refusals of leave, and because the signed and stamped minute of 20 March 2008 records the company's agreement to his departure. [Para 30][Para 36][Para 69]
**Employer (Ei Garment Co. Ltd):** the company never intended to dismiss Mr S.; he resigned of his own accord and the company has taken no step to terminate him, and he was hired precisely to reform administration, which inevitably displeases some workers. [Para 34][Para 70]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the demand that the company dismiss Mr S.; the award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent written objection to the Minister of Labour. [Para 77][Para 78]

On the first question the panel applied Article 65 of the Labour Law, under which the employment contract creates the relationship between worker and employer, and held that only the parties to that contract may end it: hiring and dismissal belong to the employer, and a worker may end only his or her own contract. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51] Following Awards 04/03 Lida, 14/03 Ju Sing, 17/03 and 18/04 Hoh Hing, 06/04 Ju Sing, 15/04 Lucky Season and 16/04 Yada Printing, the panel held it has no power to order an employer to dismiss a worker at the workers' demand, the exception being a person who endangers the health and safety of other workers — which was not established here. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 58][Para 66]

On the second question the panel read the 20 March 2008 minute as leaving the resignation to Mr S. himself and any dismissal to the company, not to the workers' demand; the panel added that his shortcomings as proved did not amount to serious misconduct under Article 83, and that the company should instead apply its internal rules case by case and might dismiss him only if he continued to fail. [Para 61][Para 64][Para 75][Para 76]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that an employer dismiss a manager or official is not a demand the panel can grant: under Article 65 only the parties to the employment contract may terminate it, so the panel will not order a dismissal at the workers' demand unless the person's conduct endangers the health or safety of other workers, and a conciliation minute recording that the person "agreed to resign" leaves the decision with him and with the employer rather than creating an obligation owed to the union. [Para 50][Para 58][Para 75]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the company should discipline Mr S. progressively under its internal rules and that dismissal would be reasonable only if he persisted in non-compliance. [Para 61]

[Para 24][Para 79] The procedural section records conciliation on 20 March 2008 as settling eight points, while the findings record three of four demands settled on 25 March 2008, and the invitation to the 3 April hearing is dated 2007. [Para 11][Para 22][Para 28] The panel is once called by a variant of its own name, and headings carry stray OCR marks. [Para 71] Individual names are not reproduced here.
