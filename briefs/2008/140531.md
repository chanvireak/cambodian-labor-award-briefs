# Award No. 81/08 — Klop La Aparel Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 21 July 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; Sin Kim Sean, appointed by the workers; Pen Bunhea, chair) · Doc ID 140531

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ក្លូប ល អាបាំ រ៉ែ លឺ មីធីត (Klop La Aparel Co., Ltd.), a garment factory employing about 2,450 workers, recognised three unions on site. The claimant was the Khmer Youth Union at the factory (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ នៃ រោងចក្រ ក្លូប ល អាប់ បាំ រ៉ែ ល), which the panel noted was not the most representative union in the enterprise [Para 45][Para 48]. Six demands were referred, all concerning the company's incentive and function-bonus policy; two of them, demand 3 and demand 6, were settled at the hearing and were not decided [Para 25][Para 26]. Some 300 workers in QC, ironing, packing, carton, Scan & Pack and the finished-goods warehouse struck for one and a half days, on the afternoon of Saturday 14 June 2008 and on Monday 16 June 2008, and the company deducted their wages and the regular monthly bonus for that period [Para 49][Para 59].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company may deduct wages for the one and a half days of strike? [Para 3][Para 97]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company may deduct the regular monthly work bonus for the days of strike? [Para 5][Para 97]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the ironing and packing team leaders are entitled to an increase of the incentive rate from USD 2 to USD 3 for grade A teams and from USD 1 to USD 1.50 for grade B teams? [Para 60]
4. (Issue 5) Whether the Scan Pack and finished-goods warehouse team leaders are entitled to the same incentive rate as team leaders in other sections? [Para 84]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company's May 2008 incentive policy gave sewing team leaders, their deputies and sewing workers a productivity bonus but was never applied equally to the heads of QC, ironing, packing, carton, Scan & Pack and the finished-goods warehouse, which is why the strike was called; the ironing and packing leaders also receive less per team than sewing leaders, so their unit rate should rise [Para 50][Para 79].
**Employer (respondent):** the incentive scheme is set section by section according to team size, the number of teams each leader supervises and productivity, and cannot be equalised across sections; the rates are a first trial that the company will adjust if unsuitable, and no wages or regular bonus are owed for strike days [Para 94][Para 96][Para 59].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — every demand of the workers' party that remained in dispute was rejected, and no sum was ordered against the company [Para 129][Para 130][Para 131]
On Issues 1 and 2 the panel applied Article 332 of the Labour Law, under which the labour contract is suspended during a strike and neither work nor wages are provided, together with Article 72(1) and Notification No. 017 S.K.B.Y. of 18 July 2000 [Para 98][Para 101][Para 103]. Following Award No. 22/04 (Raffles le Royal) and Awards 04/03 (Lida), 03/05 (Glowing Dragon), 63/07 (Phnom Penh) and 110/07 (Nouv Khob), it held that the right to wages and to the regular monthly bonus does not arise for strike days whether the strike is lawful or not, because no work was performed; the strike here also failed to follow Articles 320, 324 and 332 [Para 100][Para 106][Para 107]. The deductions for 14 and 16 June 2008 therefore breached neither Article 332, Article 72(1) nor the Notification [Para 109][Para 110].
On Issue 4 the panel found no statutory provision governing incentive bonuses, but the company had its own written policy, and Article 2 of the Labour Law reserves to the employer the management and direction of its workforce [Para 114]. Drawing on Awards 24/05, 1808 (JHG), 20/06 (New Star), 108/06 (Tri Hung Gal Burma) and 116/07 (Gresan), and on 54/07 (Yong Wati), 39/07 (San) and 62/06 (Quick Shu), it held that setting incentive rates falls within that prerogative provided it is exercised lawfully and reasonably [Para 116][Para 118][Para 122]. The differentiation was reasonable because an ironing or packing team leader who supervises a team that misses its target earns nothing for that team, and every leader can still reach at least USD 20 a month by meeting target A [Para 124][Para 125]. Issue 5 was decided on the same reasoning and rejected [Para 127][Para 128].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may set section-specific incentive-bonus rates under its statutory power to manage and direct the enterprise, and such a differentiation is lawful where it is tied to measurable factors such as team size, span of supervision and output, and where it leaves every team leader able to earn the same floor amount on the same target [Para 122][Para 124]. Wages and the regular monthly bonus do not accrue for days on which workers strike, whether the strike is lawful or unlawful, because the labour contract is suspended and no work is performed [Para 100][Para 102].

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that the Khmer Youth Union was not the most representative union in the factory played no part in the decision [Para 48]; nor did the company's statement that the scheme was a first trial subject to later correction [Para 96].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The signature block prints the employer-appointed arbitrator's family name in a different romanisation and spelling from the composition line at the head of the award, so the two do not agree [Para 2][Para 133]. Optical recognition left untranslated artefacts and placeholder tokens in the signature block, and the same block carries stray numerals [Para 133]. Issue 4 is stated twice, once with a duplicated fragment spliced into the middle of the sentence, and the decision section repeats that splice [Para 60][Para 130]. The grade letter for the second bonus tier appears once as a digit rather than as "B" [Para 51]. The two demands settled at the hearing (3 and 6) are described in the demand list but never reasoned, and the panel's own issue numbering therefore runs 1, 2, 4, 5 [Para 26].
