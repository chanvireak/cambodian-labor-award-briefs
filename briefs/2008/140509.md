# Award No. 52/08 — Supreme Garment Pte., Ltd and Cambodian Labour Union of Supreme Garment (Arbitration Council, 7 May 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy, Liv Sovanna, chair Kong Phallack · Doc ID 140509

## 1. Facts
Supreme Garment Pte., Ltd, a factory employing about 665 workers, faced a collective dispute brought by the Cambodian Labour Union of Supreme Garment, which claimed 162 members and did not hold most representative status. [Para 1][Para 24][Para 25] Of thirteen demands, nine were settled in conciliation on 17 March 2008 and, of the four remaining, one was conciliated at the hearing, issue 2 was conciliated, and the 100-riel fee claim and the canteen demand were withdrawn because the panel had decided them before. [Para 15][Para 19][Para 20] What remained was reimbursement of the 12,000 riel medical check fee for January to May 2007 and a covered parking shelter. [Para 21]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must reimburse the 12,000 riel medical check fee paid by workers themselves between January and May 2007? [Para 26][Para 35]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the company must build a parking lot with a shelter protecting workers' vehicles from rain and heat? [Para 31][Para 41]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (Cambodian Labour Union of Supreme Garment):** the company has not reimbursed workers who paid the 12,000 riel check themselves, an obligation arising from the inter-ministerial Prakas on medical check fees, and the existing 50 by 6 metre parking lot is covered only by netting that lets in heat and rain and damages vehicles. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 32][Para 33]
**Employer (Supreme Garment Pte., Ltd):** reimbursement is paid only from May 2007 because that was the director's decision and past periods are already covered by an arbitral award; and a closed shelter cannot be built because it would obstruct vehicles transporting goods, and staff vehicles use the same lot. [Para 8][Para 30][Para 33][Para 34]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to reimburse 12,000 riel to each of the 38 workers on the name list for checks between January and May 2007, and the parking-shelter demand was declined; the award is non-binding for eight days. [Para 52]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 247(a) and (c) of the Labour Law, which requires employers to provide medical examinations at their own expense, together with Clause 2(d) of Inter-Ministerial Prakas 1191 SHV PrK.ChMP of 21 November 2006, which fixes the medical check fee for a Cambodian worker at 12,000 riel. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38] It noted its consistent practice of ordering reimbursement where workers paid for their own checks, citing Awards 02/03 Chu Sing, 21/03 Royal Cambodia, 19/04 Kbal Koh II, 53/04 Kong Hong, 59/05 Tat Fat, 05/06 W&D, 56/07 Golden, 86/07 Target Shoe, 93/07 Global Footwear and 98/07 Sky Sino. [Para 39] A list of 38 workers was filed on 29 April 2008 and sent to the employer, which raised no objection to any name, so the order followed for those 38. [Para 40][Para 41]

On issue 3 the panel found that neither the Labour Law nor any labour regulation requires an employer to build a parking shelter, that no evidence showed vehicles were being damaged, and that the demand therefore exceeded the employer's legal obligations and was an interests dispute. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44] Because the union did not have most representative status under Article 277 and Article 96 paragraph 2(b), read with Prakas 305 clauses 6 and 9, it could not bargain for the whole factory, and an award under Clause 43 of Prakas 099 would stand as a collective agreement for a year and bar other workers from striking on the same subject, so the panel declined to consider it. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 51][Para 52]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer must reimburse the full 12,000 riel medical check fee to any worker who paid for the examination personally, the amount being fixed by the 2006 inter-ministerial Prakas, and an uncontested name list will ground an order worker by worker; a demand for physical amenities such as a covered parking shelter, which no labour rule requires and which no evidence shows to be necessary, is an interests dispute that a union without most representative status cannot bring. [Para 38][Para 43][Para 49]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that an award on the shelter would bind the whole factory as a collective agreement and so deprive non-members of their future right to strike on the point. [Para 52]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The parking demand is numbered Issue 3 in the reasons but appears as Issue 2 in the decisions and orders, and the medical fee appears as 10,100 riel in the non-conciliation demand while the reasons address only 12,000 riel. [Para 7][Para 21][Para 52] Years in the demand are printed with bracketed digits ("200[7]") and the Prakas number is split across lines. [Para 25][Para 28] The employer and union representatives' names are printed in full and inconsistently, and one invitation to the employer is dated 17 March 2008, the day of the conciliation, rather than after it. [Para 1][Para 23] Individual names are not reproduced here.
