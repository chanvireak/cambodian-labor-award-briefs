# Award No. 01/08 — GDM (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ជី ឌី អឹម) (Arbitration Council, 23 January 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Ly Tai Seng (employer-appointed), An Virak (worker-appointed), Kong Phalla (chair) · Doc ID 140475

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ជី ឌី អឹម (GDM), a garment and laundry factory on National Road 5 in Phnom Penh employing about 420 workers, declined to renew the fixed-duration contract of the president of its newly formed Cambodian Labour Union. [Para 37][Para 44] The union held its leadership election on 11 December 2007, notified the employer of the result on 12 December, and applied for registration on 13 December; the company issued its notice of non-renewal on 12 December and says it learned of the union only on 13 December. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 53] The worker had been hired on a two-month probation from 20 April 2007 and then on a six-month contract from 20 June to 20 December 2007, eight months in all, and had not received his 5% severance pay by the hearing date. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49] Of fourteen demands, thirteen were settled in conciliation and only the reinstatement demand was referred. [Para 10][Para 11]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the decision not to renew the union president's employment contract amounted to anti-union discrimination? [Para 71]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the notice of termination of that contract was lawful? [Para 71]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** ភាគី កម្មករ និយោជិត, through the Cambodian Labour Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ការងារ កម្មករ កម្ពុជា), argued that the non-renewal was anti-union discrimination because the worker had just been elected union president, that he wanted to help resolve workers' problems, and that the company had never warned him. [Para 2][Para 50][Para 68]
**Employer (respondent):** ក្រុមហ៊ុន ជី ឌី អឹម, through its administration head, answered that the contract had simply run its term, that notice was given on 12 December before it knew of the union on 13 December, and that the real reason was the steady fall in the worker's productivity, from first to second place in his section. [Para 3][Para 52][Para 55][Para 57]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the demand that the company take the union president back was rejected; the panel nonetheless ordered the company to pay him the outstanding 5% severance pay due on termination. [Para 103][Para 104]
On Issue 1 the panel began from its own line of awards holding that reinstatement follows only where the dismissal is proved to be anti-union discrimination contrary to Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law, and that the burden of proving discrimination lies on the workers. [Para 73][Para 75][Para 76] It weighed the sequence of dates: the union notified its election result on 12 December, the company gave notice the same day, and the company's evidence, unchallenged at the hearing, was that it learned of the union only on 13 December. [Para 81][Para 82][Para 83] The union produced no evidence that the company had received the 12 December notice, and the worker himself offered nothing beyond the fact of his election to show discrimination. [Para 51][Para 84] The panel therefore found no anti-union discrimination. [Para 86]
On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 73 §1 of the Labour Law, that a fixed-duration contract expires by operation of law at its term, and Article 73 §5, that a contract of more than six months requires ten days' notice of expiry or non-renewal, extended to fifteen days for contracts over one year. [Para 87][Para 93][Para 94] It computed the contract at exactly six months, from 20 June to 20 December 2007, so that no advance notice was legally required, and observed that the company had in fact given notice on 12 December, more than the law demanded. [Para 97][Para 98][Para 99] The contract was therefore treated as expired on its stated date and no obligation to reinstate arose. [Para 101][Para 102] The panel added, on the facts it had found, that the unpaid 5% severance must be paid according to law. [Para 103]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A refusal to renew a fixed-duration contract is not anti-union discrimination merely because the worker holds union office: the workers bear the burden of proving that the employer knew of the union activity and acted because of it, and where the notice of non-renewal precedes the employer's knowledge of the union, the claim fails. [Para 76][Para 82][Para 84]
A six-month fixed-duration contract expires at its term without any statutory notice period, and no party can compel its renewal absent agreement. [Para 90][Para 98]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that the employer had given more notice than the law required, a point unnecessary to the outcome since no notice was due at all. [Para 99][Para 100]
