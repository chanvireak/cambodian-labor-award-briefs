# Award No. 62/08 — Pao Da Company (Arbitration Council, 30 May 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phyrum for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Nhean So Munin chair) · Doc ID 140514

## 1. Facts
Pao Da Company, a garment factory at Sangkat Boeung Kork, Khan Tuol Kork, Phnom Penh employing about 300 workers, faced a collective dispute raised by the local branch of the Khmer Youth Trade Union at the factory (KYTU) within the Khmer Youth Trade Union Federation (KYFTU); the union has 246 members and represents 163 workers in the file. [Para 1][Para 16][Para 17][Para 18] Four issues were left unconciliated on 19 March 2008 and referred by report of 24 April 2008. [Para 9][Para 10] The employer did not appear at either hearing: it telephoned minutes before the first hearing on 12 May 2008 to say the General Manager had undergone surgery, obtained a postponement to 22 May 2008, and then failed to appear again with the same excuse and no replacement representative. [Para 11][Para 12][Para 39][Para 41] The panel proceeded under Clause 21 of Prakas No. 099 and held that the employer had given up its right to defend itself. [Para 42][Para 43]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay the seniority bonus to workers entitled to it, where it consistently delays payment by three or four months? [Para 19][Para 44]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must provide an on-duty physician, a clinic and sufficient medicine during working hours? [Para 21][Para 25][Para 51]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must maintain women workers' paid annual leave during their 90 days of maternity leave instead of paying 50 per cent in lieu of that leave? [Para 27][Para 28][Para 57]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must pay the January 2008 wages of eleven pieceworkers whom it underpaid by setting the rate at US$ 1.20 per 100 shirts but paying US$ 1.00? [Para 29][Para 59]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — KYTU local union at Pao Da, within KYFTU):** the company delays the seniority bonus by three or four months for workers who have completed a year; there is no physician on duty, no clinic and insufficient medicine, fainting workers are sent outside slowly because no staff member is responsible for it, and medical supplies that existed about eighteen months earlier were withdrawn; on maternity leave the company pays only 50 per cent of the sum in lieu of annual leave, calculated as one and a half days' wages divided by two and multiplied by three; and eleven named pieceworkers were paid at US$ 1.00 instead of US$ 1.20 per hundred shirts. [Para 19][Para 26][Para 28][Para 29]
**Employer (respondent — Pao Da Company):** no substantive position was put. The employer was absent from both hearings, offered only the General Manager's illness by telephone, and submitted no documents — the evidence section records "N/A" for the employer party. [Para 11][Para 12][Para 14][Para 41]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issue 1 was rejected and the transfer-to-hospital limb of Issue 2 was withdrawn; the employer was ordered to implement Joint Prakas No. 330 SKBY of 6 December 2000 on infirmaries, to maintain women's right to accrue 100 per cent paid annual leave at one and a half days a month during 90 days of maternity leave, and to pay the eleven pieceworkers their January 2008 wages. [Para 68][Para 55][Para 59][Para 69]

On the preliminary question the panel applied Clause 21 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, which allows the panel to proceed when a duly invited party fails to appear without good cause; having postponed once to accommodate the employer and having received no reason why no other representative could attend, it treated the employer as having abandoned its defence. [Para 38][Para 40][Para 42][Para 43]

On Issue 1 the panel accepted that Notification No. 017 SKBY of 18 July 2000, point 5.1, gives workers with more than one year of service a seniority bonus of US$ 2.00 a month, rising to US$ 3, US$ 4 and US$ 5 in the following years, so the right itself was not in doubt. But the union could supply no names, employment dates or amounts, even after being invited to file more evidence and failing to do so by 26 May 2008, so the panel declined to consider the claim, following Award No. 44/06-Goldfame. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50]

On Issue 2 the panel applied Clause 1 and Clause 3 of Joint Prakas No. 330 SKBY of 6 December 2000, under which an enterprise employing 50 or more workers must set up a permanent infirmary with health staff, equipment and medicines at the ratios set in the annexed table. Finding that no physician, nurse or medicine was provided, it ordered proper implementation of the Prakas. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54][Para 55]

On Issue 3 the panel applied Articles 166 and 167 of the Labour Law — 1.5 days of paid annual leave per month of service, and nullity of any agreement compensating or waiving paid leave — and Article 183. It held that paying 50 per cent in lieu of annual leave during maternity leave defeats the right to take leave, since leave may not be converted into cash, and ordered the right maintained for the 90-day period. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58][Para 59]

On Issue 4 the panel first noted the union could not explain its own calculation, but then applied Article 118, under which the burden of proving that wages were paid correctly lies on the employer, provable by the worker's signature or two witnesses in the payroll ledger. Because the employer was absent, that proof could not be produced, and the panel ordered payment to the eleven workers. [Para 59][Para 61][Para 62][Para 64][Para 65][Para 66][Para 67]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a demand rests on a legal entitlement but the claimant supplies no names, dates or amounts and does not answer an invitation to file further evidence, the panel will decline to consider it rather than assess an unquantified claim. By contrast, where the dispute concerns payment of wages, Article 118 of the Labour Law shifts the burden of proof to the employer, and an employer that stays away from the hearing and produces no payroll evidence must pay the wages claimed, whichever way the arithmetic was argued. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 64][Para 65][Para 67]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that the workers had agreed to withdraw the demand about sending fainting workers to outside hospitals, and that it need not decide that part, records a change in the claim rather than a ruling. [Para 25]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The non-conciliation report is numbered 428 KB/AK/VK in the jurisdiction section but 478 KB/AK/VK in the procedural and evidence sections, and is dated 24 April 2008 in one place and 24 May 2008 in another [Para 8][Para 10][Para 16][Para 18].
- The union's complaint letter is dated 31 December 2008 although received on 2 January 2008, while the same letter is dated 31 December 2007 in the evidence list [Para 8][Para 14].
- The workers' wage-loss table is badly corrupted: only about ten names are legible although the award refers to eleven workers, and the stated losses of US$ 30 to US$ 40 do not match the per-worker figures of US$ 1.2, 6.7 and 7 shown in the table [Para 30][Para 33][Para 59].
- The infirmary staffing table is collapsed into a single run of numbers, so the physician and nurse ratios for each size band cannot be read reliably [Para 53][Para 54].
- OCR word-joining is pervasive in this translation ("thearing", "themployer", "torder", "Labur Law"), and the employer's absence is recorded three times in slightly different terms [Para 11][Para 12][Para 41].
- The eleven pieceworkers are not named here; union officers and the employer's management are identified by role only.
