# Award No. 65/08 — Khem Well MFG Co Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមវែល អិម អេហ្វ ជី) and the Free Trade Union of Workers of the Kingdom of Cambodia at the Khem Well factory (Arbitration Council, 3 June 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mam Sombor Na, employer-appointed; An Virak, worker-appointed; Aing Eng Thong, presiding) · Doc ID 140519

## 1. Facts
Khem Well MFG Co Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមវែល អិម អេហ្វ ជី), a Kandal factory of 3,200 workers at Bek Chan, faced 23 collective demands filed on 21 April 2008 by the Free Trade Union of Workers of the Kingdom of Cambodia at the Khem Well factory (សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ នៃ ព្រះរាជាណាចក្រ កម្ពុជា ប្រចាំ រោងចក្រ ខេម វែល) [Para 1][Para 27][Para 30]. A bargaining session on 28 April failed, the union announced a strike for 30 April, and about 1,970 workers stayed out from 7:00 to 9:00 that morning [Para 28][Para 30]. Conciliation at the Kandal labour office settled 21 of the 23 demands; two remained — the USD 2 deduction from the USD 7 monthly attendance bonus imposed on strikers, and sick-leave pay for serious illness [Para 13][Para 26][Para 32]. The parties accepted that the factory had previously operated as Tai Yien and that the Tai Yien conditions and a bonus-deduction agreement concluded there carry over [Para 33][Para 34].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer's deduction of one day's wage and of USD 2 from the USD 7 attendance bonus of the 1,970 workers who struck on 30 April 2008 was unlawful retaliation, given that the strike was called without the statutory conciliation and arbitration steps? [Para 26][Para 37]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay sick-leave wages for serious illness, the union having at the hearing reduced its claim to strict application of Article 4, point 7 of the company's internal rules? [Para 41][Para 78]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company had already docked more than USD 2 in lost daily wage, so the additional USD 2 from the bonus punished them twice; the Tai Yien bonus-deduction agreement covers absences for illness or personal business only and cannot be stretched to strike days; and an ILO document they had seen provides that although strikers lose wages, the attendance bonus is preserved [Para 37][Para 38]. On sick pay they asked in the alternative for the internal rules to be applied, because workers with proper state medical certificates had gone weeks unpaid [Para 42][Para 79].

**Employer (respondent):** the deduction followed the agreement inherited from Tai Yien, the company had already made many concessions, and in truth it should have withheld the entire bonus for the strike period [Para 39]. It did not dispute the evidence that sick workers went unpaid, and left the sick-leave point to the panel to decide by award [Para 43].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim on issue 1 was dismissed, and on issue 2 the employer was ordered to apply Article 4, point 7 of its internal rules and pay half wage, inclusive of allowances, for leave certified by an official doctor, from the date the award becomes enforceable [Para 82][Para 83].

On issue 1 the panel held that whether a strike is lawful falls to the courts alone under Article 337 of the Labour Law, but that it was nonetheless necessary to examine lawfulness because the wage and bonus claim depended on it [Para 50]. It noted Articles 336 and 330 on unlawful and non-peaceful strikes, and Article 321, which allows a strike only once peaceful means of settlement are exhausted [Para 52][Para 53]. Here the union put its demands in writing on 21 April, met the employer once on 28 April, announced the strike on 29 April and struck on 30 April, without passing through conciliation by Ministry officials or through the arbitration procedure [Para 54][Para 56]. The panel therefore found the strike unlawful [Para 61]. Article 333, which bars any sanction for taking part in a strike, protects only participants in a lawful strike; a worker who does not follow Chapter 13 cannot claim its full protection, as the panel had held in Awards 04/03 (Linda) and 16/06 (LA) [Para 57][Para 59][Para 60]. A worker who strikes unlawfully is treated as absent without leave and has no right to the attendance bonus [Para 66][Para 67], so the inherited deduction agreement could lawfully be applied and the USD 2 deduction stood [Para 69][Para 70].

On issue 2 the panel observed that internal rules are general normative instruments governing working conditions under Article 23 of the Labour Law, and that sick leave is ordinarily provided for in them [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74]. Article 4, point 7 of this company's rules certifies half wage, allowances included, for leave ordered by a doctor, and half wage for absence notified with a proper state medical certificate [Para 75][Para 76]. Since the union had narrowed its claim to enforcement of that rule and the employer never answered it, the panel ordered compliance [Para 80][Para 81].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The protection of Article 333 of the Labour Law against sanctions for strike participation attaches only to a strike that has passed through the statutory conciliation and arbitration steps; workers who strike without them are treated as absent without leave, and the employer may withhold their attendance bonus under a pre-existing rules-based deduction scheme [Para 57][Para 60][Para 66].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that only a court may declare a strike lawful or unlawful, offered while explaining why it examined lawfulness anyway, was not necessary to the result [Para 50].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR loses or mangles several figures and citations: the internal rules are called "Article 4, point 70" in one place and "Article 4, point 7" elsewhere, and the rules themselves are once described as "Proclamation 4" [Para 44][Para 75]. The strike announcement is dated 29 April 2009 although every surrounding event is in 2008 [Para 29]. The union's brief is recorded as being lodged at "4" of the 23 demands [Para 27]. The employer's name appears as both Khem Well and Khemwel, and stray characters intrude into the text ("Party 19", "employer Cambodia") in the reasoning [Para 62][Para 70]. The presiding arbitrator's name is printed in one form in the panel composition and another at the signature block [Para 1][Para 85]. No individual worker, witness or union official is named in a way that required redaction beyond the roles used above.
