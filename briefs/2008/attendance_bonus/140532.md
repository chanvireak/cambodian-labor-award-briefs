# Award No. 85/08 — Yung Wah I Enterprise (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 06 August 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; Toun Si Phan, appointed by the workers; Kong Phalla, chair) · Doc ID 140532

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉ុង វាំ អ៉ិន ដាស្ទ្រៀល (ខេមបូឌា) ខូ.អិលធីឌី (Yung Wah I Enterprise (Cambodia) Co., Ltd.) employed about 5,000 workers; the claimant was the Khmer Youth Union at the factory (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ ប្រចាំ ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉ុង វាំ ទី១) [Para 35][Para 36]. Ninety-six night-shift workers in the cutting and sewing groups did not work the full hours of 04 and 05 April 2008; the three team leaders recorded them as refusing to work, and on that record the company deducted the USD 5 regular attendance bonus and the whole of the wages for those two days [Para 39][Para 67][Para 68]. Of five demands, four were settled in conciliation, leaving one issue for the panel [Para 11][Para 12].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the 96 night-shift workers are entitled to the regular attendance bonus of USD 5 and to wages for 04 and 05 April 2008, when they did not work the full hours arranged by the company on those days? [Para 39][Para 75]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company had no work to give from 01 to 03 April 2008 and the workers still attended; on 04 and 05 April they came and worked part of the day and then stopped after wage talks failed, and the attendance lists do not prove refusal, since the internal rules provide no deduction by attendance list [Para 43][Para 53][Para 57][Para 71]. They asked that the USD 5 bonus and two days' wages be repaid by the three team leaders who wrote the lists, and invoked Article 28 of the Labour Law against wage deductions [Para 69][Para 72].
**Employer (respondent):** work was available and the workers refused to take it, sitting and sleeping in the factory after the wage talks failed; the company photographed the workshop floor and issued urgent Notice No. 551/08 dated 04 April 2008 warning that absentees would lose daily wage and the monthly attendance bonus [Para 58][Para 59][Para 49]. It deducted on the team leaders' lists, and it is responsible for its team leaders' acts because they act on its instructions [Para 68][Para 70].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim for the USD 5 regular attendance bonus was rejected, and the employer was ordered to pay the 96 workers wages for 04 and 05 April 2008 proportional to the time each actually worked on each day, the parties to discuss in good faith and fix those hours [Para 118][Para 119]
On the bonus, the panel applied point 3 of Notification No. 017 S.K.B.Y. of 18 July 2000, preserved by point 3 of Notification No. 745 K.K.B.V. of 23 October 2006, which gives the minimum USD 5 monthly bonus only to workers who attend the full number of working days in the month [Para 83][Para 84]. It found as fact that none of the 96 attended fully on 04 and 05 April 2008, so the condition was not met and the bonus claim failed, following Awards 04/03 (Lida), 15/04 (Lucky Seven), 03/05 (Glowing Dragon), 63/07 (Phnom Penh Garment) and 54/08 (Chung Yeu) [Para 85][Para 87][Para 88][Para 89].
On wages, the panel read Article 28 of the Labour Law as barring fines, meaning measures that cut the wage a worker would normally earn by performing the work, and held that withholding pay for time not worked is not such a fine [Para 91][Para 93][Para 96]. It also held Articles 126 to 129 inapplicable because they govern deductions, not non-payment where no work was done [Para 99][Para 100]. Both sides nevertheless agreed that the workers had worked part of each day, and the contract of employment under Articles 65 and 105 obliges the employer to pay for service actually rendered [Para 112][Para 114][Para 105]. Because neither side could give the exact hours, the panel declined to fix a figure and instead ordered payment proportionate to hours worked, leaving the parties to settle the hours [Para 115][Para 116][Para 117].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where workers attend on a working day but perform only part of the hours arranged, the employer may withhold the wage attributable to the time not worked without that being a prohibited fine under Article 28, but it must pay for the part actually worked, in proportion to the hours proved [Para 93][Para 113]. The statutory USD 5 monthly attendance bonus is earned only by workers who attend the full complement of working days in the month [Para 84][Para 87].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the workers directed their money claim at the three team leaders personally rather than at the company, and that the company accepted responsibility for its team leaders' acts; neither point affected the orders [Para 69][Para 70].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award carries a dissenting opinion by the worker-appointed arbitrator, appended as an annex, which would have granted the USD 5 bonus as well and reasoned that Notification No. 017 governs punctuality rather than partial attendance and that an employer which provides insufficient work cannot charge the shortfall to the workers [Para 121][Para 123][Para 125][Para 133]. That dissent is not the award and is not reflected in the orders [Para 118][Para 119]. The group labels for the night-shift teams are printed inconsistently across the file, appearing in several different Latin and Khmer forms for what the award treats as the same two groups [Para 39][Para 56][Para 67]. One date line merges December and April into a single month name where April 2008 is plainly meant [Para 85]. The signature block prints the chair's personal name in a shortened and differently spelled form from the composition line at the head of the award, so the two cannot be matched on their face [Para 2][Para 121]. Optical recognition also left stray fragments and placeholder marks in the decision and signature sections [Para 120][Para 121].
