# Award No. 94/08 — Sharp Casual Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 12 August 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phyrum, appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna, appointed by the workers; Ang Eng Thong, chair) · Doc ID 140535

## 1. Facts
Sharp Casual Co., Ltd, a garment enterprise at Trapang Kol village, Kantok commune, Angsnol district, Kandal province, employed the members of a local union of the Cambodian Labour Union (CLU) affiliated to the Cambodian Labour Union Federation (CLUF), registered at the Ministry of Labour on 21 March 2008 under No. 2006 SSKK [Para 2][Para 20]. The union asked the company to deduct a union contribution fee of 1,000 riels from the wages of members who had agreed to the deduction, and the company declined, saying it had never done so before [Para 6][Para 23]. Of five demands at conciliation, four were settled, and the single non-conciliated issue came to the panel [Para 11][Para 12][Para 13]. By the deadline of 4 August 2008 the union filed with the Council and with the company a list of members who had agreed to the deduction; the company raised no objection to the list [Para 20][Para 22].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company is obliged to deduct the 1,000 riel union contribution fee from the wages of those workers who have authorised the deduction in writing? [Para 13][Para 20]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the Cambodian Labour Union Federation (CLUF) and the local union of the Cambodian Labour Union (CLU) at Sharp Casual Factory relied on their registration at the Ministry of Labour and on their written request, supported by a list of consenting members filed with the Council and the company, and asked for the deduction to be made from each consenting member's wage [Para 20].
**Employer (respondent):** Sharp Casual Co., Ltd, through its representative Mr M, refused on the sole ground that the company had never made such a deduction in the past, and it did not object to the list of consenting members when given the chance to review it [Para 23][Para 22].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the company was ordered to deduct 1,000 riels from the wages of those workers who are members of CLU at Sharp Casual Factory and who had agreed to the deduction, to pay their union contribution fee [Para 28]
The panel set Article 28(1) of the Labour Law, which forbids an employer to deduct union dues from wages and to pay them on the worker's behalf, against Article 129(2), which allows a worker to authorise deductions from wages payable to the trade union to which he belongs, on the condition that the authorisation be in writing and revocable at any time [Para 24][Para 25]. It also applied clause 5 of Prakas No. 305 of 2001 of the Ministry of Social Affairs, Labour, Vocational Training and Youth Rehabilitation, which permits a member to request the deduction in writing at least 15 days in advance in compliance with Article 129 [Para 25].
Reading the two provisions together, the panel held that the purpose of Article 28(1) is to protect workers' freedom of association and to bar employer interference of the kind described in Article 280, and that this purpose is not offended where the employer merely gives effect to a written authorisation freely given [Para 25]. Drawing on Awards 05/03 (Top One), 62/04 (Y Sin), 94/04 (Eternity Apparel), 99/06 (AIA) and 16/05 (New Point World Trade Cambodia), it concluded that the Labour Law does not prohibit an employer from deducting contribution fees where written authorisation exists [Para 25].
It then followed its consistent line in Awards 03/03 (Tonga), 60/07 (Suit Way), 65/07 (Jia Fung) and 61/08 (Focus Footwear), all of which ordered the employer to make the deduction on receipt of a written request from members who had agreed [Para 27]. Because the union had filed the signed list, the company had not objected to it, and no member's authorisation was shown to have been revoked, the order followed [Para 22][Para 28]. The employer's only reason, that it had never done this before, was not a legal ground for refusing [Para 23].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer must deduct a union contribution fee from a member's wage once the member has authorised the deduction in writing, and may not refuse merely because it has not done so in the past; the prohibition in Article 28(1) of the Labour Law is directed at employer interference with union membership, not at implementing a free written authorisation under Article 129(2) and clause 5 of Prakas No. 305 of 2001 [Para 25][Para 26].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the authorisation is revocable at any time was taken from the statute and was not applied to any worker in this case [Para 25].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
This is an unofficial English translation of a Khmer original, and the typescript is riddled with words fused across a lost "th", so that "the employer" appears throughout as a single run-together word and "the hearing" likewise [Para 1][Para 8][Para 20]. A name in the employer's list of representatives is split so that one entry ends with a bare title and the following personal name sits at the head of the next line, leaving the representative's full name unrecoverable from the face of the award [Para 1][Para 2]. The company name is spelled two ways, once as "Sharp Casual" and once as "Sharp Causal", in the certificates listed in evidence [Para 17][Para 18]. The number of workers originally said to have authorised the deduction is given once in the demand and never reconciled with the list later filed [Para 6][Para 20]. The word "Fax" is printed as "NIA" where "N/A" is meant, and the closing sentence of the award is cut off mid-word [Para 1][Para 30].
