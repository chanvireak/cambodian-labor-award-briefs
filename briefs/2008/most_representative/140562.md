# Award No. 134/08 — Chevron (Cambodia) Limited (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឈេហ្វរ៉ុន (ខេមបូឌា) លីមីធីត) (Arbitration Council, 19 November 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · three-member panel: ឈីវ ភិវម្យ៉ (employer-nominated), អន ណន (worker-nominated), ឥង់ ផល្លៈ (president) · Doc ID 140562

## 1. Facts
Chevron (Cambodia) Limited (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឈេហ្វរ៉ុន (ខេមបូឌា) លីមីធីត) operates Caltex fuel stations nationwide, with close to 600 workers of whom about 500 staff the stations and 50 work in management. [Para 59] The claimant federation, សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព-សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ និយោជិត នៃ ក្រុមហ៊ុន កាលំតិច, brought five demands; at the hearing it withdrew Issues 1 and 4 (health cover for pump attendants, and a US$30 wage rise) to await the company's decision. [Para 23][Para 63][Para 81] It also sought restoration of the 13th-month salary, a seniority bonus, and reinstatement of three dismissed employees. [Para 64][Para 77][Para 82] Its union has about 250 members but is not the most representative in the company. [Para 62]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the company must restore the annual bonus equal to one month's salary (the 13th-month salary) that it paid until 2004? [Para 64]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay a seniority bonus of US$3 after one year, US$5 after three years and US$10 after five years? [Para 77]
3. (Issue 5) Whether the three dismissed employees must be reinstated? [Para 82]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), the federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព-សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ និយោជិត នៃ ក្រុមហ៊ុន កាលំតិច):** the 13th month was paid from 1996 and should be restored because the performance bonus reaches only a few staff, and seniority pay is needed as prices rise. [Para 66][Para 78]
**Employer (respondent), Chevron (Cambodia) Limited (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឈេហ្វរ៉ុន (ខេមបូឌា) លីមីធីត):** the 13th month was replaced from 2005 by a Variable Bonus Program rewarding performance; the three employees were dismissed for serious misconduct under internal rule 13 and cannot be taken back. [Para 65][Para 68][Para 104]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issues 2 and 3 were refused as outside the panel's cognisance for want of a most representative union, and the company was ordered to reinstate all three dismissed employees from the date the award takes effect. [Para 195][Para 197]

On Issues 2 and 3 the panel found no provision of labour law, contract, internal rules or collective agreement creating either entitlement; the claims were therefore for benefits beyond what the law gives, which makes them collective-interest demands. [Para 131][Para 147] On such demands the panel always examines whether the union is most representative, because Article 277 requires registration and the prescribed criteria, and Prakas No. 099 Article 43 makes an award operate as a collective agreement for one year — which would strip other workers of their right to strike on the same subject. [Para 136][Para 140][Para 141] This union was not most representative, so the panel declined to consider both claims. [Para 144][Para 149]

On Issue 5 the panel took each dismissal separately. All three held indefinite-duration contracts under Article 67, and Article 74 requires a lawful ground related to the worker's aptitude or conduct or the enterprise's operation. [Para 84][Para 153][Para 155] Mr I was accused of short-pumping and of breaching the company's code of ethics and business conduct, a rule the company never explained; the pump record, the station chief's note and the company's own admission showed he delivered the full US$10 (9.01 litres), and that he bought 3 litres (US$3.33) of his own money to end the dispute with the station chief's agreement. [Para 91][Para 94][Para 97][Para 162] With no proven misconduct, Article 27's requirement that discipline match the fault was not met, and Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 permits reinstatement. [Para 164][Para 166][Para 167]

Mr N admitted losing company phone cards worth US$440, which meets internal rule 13(g) (loss above US$200). [Para 171][Para 174] Ms S admitted losing cards worth US$270, also meeting rule 13(g). [Para 184][Para 185] In both cases, however, Article 26(k) second paragraph required dismissal within seven days of the employer learning of the misconduct; the company learned on 21 March and acted on 1 and 3 April (ten days) for Mr N, and learned on 8 February but decided on 2 April for Ms S. [Para 177][Para 189] The right to dismiss was therefore forfeited and both were reinstated. [Para 179][Para 192]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a demand seeks a benefit the law does not itself provide, it is a collective-interest dispute, and a panel will not decide it unless the claimant union holds most representative status; an award on such a demand would bind all workers as a collective agreement for a year. [Para 133][Para 140] An employer that learns of serious misconduct must dismiss within seven days; if it waits, the right to dismiss is lost and reinstatement follows even where the misconduct is admitted. [Para 176][Para 179]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that a disciplinary penalty must be proportionate to the weight of the fault, and that vague internal standards such as "code of ethics" cannot ground dismissal unless their content is explained. [Para 160][Para 164]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company name is printed in at least three different Khmer spellings across the face, the body and the documents list, and one variant is a plain mis-transliteration. [Para 2][Para 41][Para 43] The employer-nominated arbitrator's name differs between the face of the award and the signature block, which also carries stray years. [Para 1][Para 199] The warning letter to Ms S is dated 31 March 2008 in the documents list and in the reasons but 31 March 2007 where first described. [Para 27][Para 120] Para 177 gives two dates for the same notification (a day in July and 21 March), and Para 135 contains a block of untranslated English OCR noise in place of the reasoning on representativeness. [Para 135][Para 177] The three dismissed workers are printed with full names throughout; they appear here as Mr I, Mr N and Ms S.
