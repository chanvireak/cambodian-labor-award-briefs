# Award No. 80/08 — San Sin Co Ltd (Arbitration Council of Cambodia, 11 July 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of three: សេង វូច ហ៊ុន (employer-appointed), ទួន ស៊ី ផាន់ (worker-appointed), ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា (president) · Doc ID 140530

## 1. Facts
The employer, ក្រុមហ៊ុន សាន់ ស៊ីន ខូ.អិលធីឌី (San Sin Co Ltd), a garment factory of about 1,210 workers at Kambol, Ang Snuol, Kandal, faced a collective dispute brought by សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ ប្រចាំ រោងចក្រ សាន់ ស៊ីន, one of three unions at the plant, none of which held most-representative status [Para 25][Para 27][Para 29]. Of 15 demands, conciliation settled 14, leaving only the wage claim [Para 9][Para 10]. In March 2008 the company had no buyer orders and stopped work for some sewing teams for five to seven days; it posted a notice on 20 March 2008 on the board and by loudspeaker, paid 100 per cent to the 300 workers who came in, and 50 per cent to the 360 workers it had notified and who stayed away [Para 30][Para 33][Para 39]. The company never applied to the labour inspectorate to suspend those employment contracts [Para 45][Para 48].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay the remaining 50 per cent of wages for the March 2008 stoppage, having paid half without any suspension authorised under Article 71(11)? [Para 30][Para 51]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company owes the other half of the wage for March because it never sought the labour inspector's permission to suspend the contracts, which is the only route by which the wage obligation can be set aside [Para 46].

**Employer (respondent):** the stoppage was caused by a genuine shortage of buyer orders, it discussed the matter with the union president and team leaders before issuing the 20 March 2008 notice, the workers accepted 50 per cent, wages were paid in full, and it has applied that arrangement without difficulty until the union reopened the demand in June 2008 [Para 32][Para 42][Para 44]. It paid 100 per cent to those who attended and 50 per cent only to those who stayed home after notice [Para 38][Para 39].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the company must pay the additional 50 per cent of wages for the March 2008 stoppage, within 15 days of the award taking effect [Para 63][Para 64].

The panel applied Article 71(11) of the Labour Law, which permits suspension of an enterprise's activity where it faces serious economic or material difficulty, for no longer than two months, and places that suspension under the supervision of the labour inspector [Para 51][Para 52]. Article 72 provides that suspension removes the main obligations of the contract, including the employer's duty to pay wages, but only where a contrary provision so allows [Para 53]. The panel read those provisions together to mean that the labour inspector decides whether suspension is permitted, on an inspection carried out at the employer's request, and that normally suspension requires notice to and agreement from the inspectorate [Para 54][Para 55].

On the facts it found that the company had paid only half the wage for the stoppage without ever requesting an inspection or a suspension decision [Para 56][Para 61]. It held that the workers' consent to the 50 per cent does not cure the omission, because the statutory procedure must still be followed, and that the employer's obligation was therefore to pay in full [Para 56][Para 59]. It followed Awards 46/05, 01/04, 60/04, 21/03, 46/04, 60/06 and 74/07, which hold that where a suspension is not lawful the workers lose nothing and must receive 100 per cent of wages [Para 57][Para 58][Para 60]. Accordingly it ordered the outstanding half to be paid for the March 2008 period [Para 62][Para 63].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that has no work to give may reduce wages only by suspending the employment contracts through the procedure in Article 71(11) of the Labour Law, which requires the labour inspector's supervision and decision; where it simply pays half and stays silent, the suspension is unlawful and the full wage for the stoppage remains due, whatever the workers agreed at the time [Para 56][Para 62].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that a genuine shortage of buyer orders is capable of grounding a lawful suspension, so the defect in this case was procedural rather than a finding that the company's economic difficulty was invented [Para 47][Para 55].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer-appointed arbitrator's name is printed as សេង វូច ហ៊ុន in the panel heading and as សេង តូច ហ៊ុន in the signature block, and the president's signature line is replaced by machine noise. The union president is named one way in the party list and another way in the reasoning, so the brief refers to her by office. The company is called សាន់ ស៊ីន ខូ.អិលធីឌី in the heading and សាន់ ស៊ីន អិលធីឌី in the findings. A stray year appears inside the paragraph applying Article 72, and the notice of 20 March 2008 is described in one place as issued after a meeting with the union president and in another as issued on the board and by loudspeaker only, so the sequence is reconstructed from both. One paragraph of the reasoning is duplicated almost verbatim from an earlier one, and the number of teams affected differs between the evidence list and the findings.
