# Award No. 54/08 — Zhong Yov Co., Ltd and Khmer Youth Trade Union Federation (KYFTU) / KYTU (Arbitration Council, 16 May 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Lee Tayseng, Liv Sovanna, chair Kong Phallack · Doc ID 140508

## 1. Facts
Zhong Yov Co., Ltd, a factory at Prey Rokar, Angsnoul, Kandal with about 650 workers, faced a dispute brought by the Khmer Youth Trade Union Federation (KYFTU) and its local KYTU union, which was registered but not the most representative union. [Para 4][Para 23] Nine demands went to conciliation on 3 April 2008, four were settled, and the five that remained were heard on 28 April 2008: full pay during work suspensions, the attendance bonus on approved leave, pay for a strike from 14 March to 5 April 2008, reimbursement of bonus deducted from seven workers, and the transfer of an administrator. [Para 18][Para 21] About 500 workers had struck from 14 March to 5 April 2008 without notification or prior conciliation. [Para 33][Para 87]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay 100 per cent of wages and perquisites when there is no work for the workers? [Para 23][Para 60]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the US$5 attendance bonus must be retained in full when workers take approved leave for personal commitments of less than three days? [Para 29][Para 67]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay wages and attendance bonus for the strike period of 14 March to 5 April 2008? [Para 31][Para 81]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must reimburse the attendance bonus withheld from seven workers who took less than four hours' leave? [Para 35][Para 89]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the company must transfer the administrator who fought with the union president? [Para 41][Para 105]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (KYFTU and local KYTU):** the company suspended work without labour-inspector permission and paid only 50 per cent of wages and no duty allowance, so 100 per cent is owed; the bonus should be lost only for absences over three days; pay and bonus are owed for the strike because the company refused to negotiate; and the administrator should be transferred because he struck the union president. [Para 16][Para 26][Para 31][Para 41]
**Employer (Zhong Yov Co., Ltd):** it followed Notification 017 of 18 July 2000 on the attendance bonus and the Labour Law; it did not seek the ministry's permission because a formal request would have left it paying only US$10 for accommodation; and the strike was unlawful, so no wages or bonus are owed. [Para 27][Para 30][Para 34]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel declined to fix future suspension pay, rejected the leave-bonus and strike-pay demands, ordered the company to cease unlawful suspensions and to pay attendance bonus in proportion to days worked, ordered reimbursement of bonus to the seven workers, and rejected the transfer demand; the award is non-binding for eight days. [Para 112][Para 113]

On issue 1 the panel held that Article 71 paragraph 1 point 11 permits suspension only for serious economic or material difficulty, under labour-inspector control, and Article 72 paragraph 1 removes the wage obligation only where suspension is lawful. [Para 51][Para 54] Because the company never sought inspection, its past suspensions were unlawful and 50 per cent pay did not comply; but the workers claimed nothing for the past and sought full pay only for future suspensions, which the panel declines as unforeseeable, following Awards 36/06 Mondotex and 58/07 8 Stars Sportswear — while ordering the practice stopped under Article 34(D) of Prakas 099. [Para 57][Para 61][Para 65]

On issue 2 the panel found that approved absence suspends the contract under Article 71(6), that the attendance bonus is part of wages under Article 103, and that only a proportional deduction is lawful, following Award 57/07 Serratex; the demand to keep the full US$5 for three days' leave was therefore rejected. [Para 68][Para 74][Para 76][Para 78]

On issue 3 the panel applied Article 332 paragraph 1, under which a strike suspends the contract and neither allowance nor salary is paid, and found the strike unlawful because Article 320 paragraph 4 requires all peaceful means to be exhausted first; no wages or bonus were owed. [Para 81][Para 83][Para 88]

On issue 4 the panel interpreted clause 2 of the 9 January 2008 minute, read against the union's November 2007 request, as referring to leave per month, not per day; but because withholding the whole bonus for more than four hours' leave gave less than the proportional deduction the law requires, Article 13 made the minute null and void and the seven workers were entitled to reimbursement. [Para 95][Para 96][Para 98][Para 101] On issue 5 the panel held that under Article 65 only the parties to the contract may end it, and following Awards 73/04 Yen U.N and 87/04 Nobel a transfer can be ordered only where the person is shown to endanger the workplace, which was not proved. [Para 109][Para 110][Para 112]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer suspends work without labour-inspector control the suspension is unlawful and wages and bonus remain payable, but a demand framed only for future suspensions is declined as unforeseeable and met instead with an order to cease the unlawful practice; an attendance bonus may lawfully be deducted only in proportion to approved days absent, so a settlement clause withholding the whole bonus offends Article 13 and is void; and strikers, lawful or not, earn no wage for strike days. [Para 57][Para 65][Para 101][Para 83]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that had the company applied to the ministry it would have had to pay only US$10 for accommodation, and that the workers' chosen remedy for the alleged assault lay with the provincial authority and the court, not the panel. [Para 27][Para 50]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Several individual names are printed in full and inconsistently — the administrator appears with two different surnames, the union president's name is spelled both "Chen" and "Cheun", and seven workers are listed with names and ID numbers; they are not reproduced here. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 104] The OCR duplicates whole phrases (for example the 50 per cent wage sentence), the Issue 5 heading is scrambled into fragments, and the 2007 conciliation minutes in the evidence list are dated variously 12 May, 28 January and 8 July 2007. [Para 21][Para 24][Para 105] One letter is dated 19 January 2007 where the surrounding narrative says 19 November 2007. [Para 35][Para 36]
