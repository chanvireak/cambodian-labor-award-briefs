# Award No. 49/08 — Flying Dragon (Cambodia) Garment and C.CAWDU / local union of C.CAWDU (Arbitration Council, 30 April 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Chhiv Phyrum, An Nan, chair Kong Phallack · Doc ID 140505

## 1. Facts
Flying Dragon (Cambodia) Garment, a factory at Chom Chao, Phnom Penh employing about 1,124 workers, faced a collective dispute brought by C.CAWDU and its local union at the factory on behalf of 442 workers. [Para 1][Para 31] Of eleven demands in the non-conciliation report, one was settled in conciliation, five were withdrawn at the hearing, and three were resolved by agreement there — the piece-rate slip entry, the original contract copy and surgical leave; the panel decided only the three that remained. [Para 22][Para 25][Para 31] Those concerned the piecework production target, wages during work suspensions, and deduction of union dues. [Para 52][Para 72][Para 85]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 4) Whether the company may increase the production target for piece-rate workers, or must leave the target unchanged? [Para 33][Para 52]
2. (Issue 8) Whether the company must pay full wages when it has no work for the workers to do? [Para 39][Para 72]
3. (Issue 9) Whether the company must deduct union contribution fees from the wages of members who hold proper documents? [Para 48][Para 85]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (C.CAWDU and its local union):** since 2007 the company has repeatedly raised piecework targets so that only 40 per cent of workers reach them — for example 400 pairs of trousers a day rising to 490 the next month — leaving workers demotivated and earning less; and when work runs short the company sends workers home on half pay without informing or seeking permission from the Ministry of Labour. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 39][Para 77]
**Employer (Flying Dragon (Cambodia) Garment):** targets are set by the workers' ability and by what the majority produce, with 50–60 per cent of workers reaching them and no punishment or loss of base wage for those who do not; the company relies on its 12 September 2004 agreement with the workers' representatives providing half basic wages on no-work days. [Para 38][Para 40][Para 43]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand on production targets was rejected, the demand for full wages in future suspensions was rejected, the company was ordered to stop suspending work contrary to Article 71 paragraph 11, and the union-dues demand was declined as already decided; the award is non-binding for eight days. [Para 87]

On issue 4 the panel applied Article 2 of the Labour Law, which it has read as conferring a right to manage the enterprise lawfully and reasonably, and found no provision forbidding an employer to raise a piecework target, following Award 10/03 Jacqsintex. [Para 53][Para 54] Articles 104 and 108 require the piece rate to let a worker of average ability earn at least the minimum wage in eight hours, and the parties agreed all workers already receive the minimum wage without a top-up, with no evidence that the increase had cut anyone's wages. [Para 57][Para 59][Para 61][Para 62] The panel found the practice lawful but could not test its reasonableness, held the demand to be an interests dispute, and declined it because the local union of C.CAWDU lacked most representative status under Article 277 and Article 96 paragraph 2. [Para 63][Para 67][Para 70][Para 71]

On issue 8 the panel cited Article 71 paragraph 11 and Article 72 paragraph 1: a suspension requires written notification to the labour inspectorate and inspection certifying the difficulty, failing which wages remain payable. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 75] The company never applied for suspension and its half-wage agreement contradicted Article 71; but the workers sought full wages only for future suspensions, and the panel held it could not decide a demand for something that had not yet occurred, following Awards 10/03 Jacqsintex, 68/04 City New, 36/06 Mondotex and 58/07 8 Stars Sportswear. [Para 77][Para 80][Para 82] It nonetheless used Clause 34 of Prakas 099 to order the company to stop the unlawful practice immediately. [Para 84]

On issue 9 the panel found the demand identical to issue 1 in Award 35/07 Flying Dragon between the same parties, decided on 8 May 2007, and declined it on res judicata. [Para 85][Para 87]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for full wages during a suspension that has not yet happened is anticipatory and cannot be decided for want of identified workers, dates or facts, yet where the employer's existing practice of suspending work without labour-inspector authorisation is unlawful under Article 71 paragraph 11, the panel will order the practice stopped even while rejecting the anticipatory claim; a demand to hold a piecework target fixed is an interests dispute that only a union with most representative status may bring. [Para 71][Para 82][Para 84]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that no provision of the Labour Law prohibits an employer from raising a production target at all, the unlawfulness lying only in a rate that would drop a worker of average ability below the minimum wage. [Para 54][Para 60]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award numbers two different demands as "Issue 4": the piece-rate slip entry settled by agreement, and the production target decided on the merits; issue 8 is likewise introduced twice in the text. [Para 25][Para 33][Para 39] A company document on wage practice arrived on 21 April 2008 in a foreign language and was excluded as untranslatable. [Para 38] The OCR truncates words throughout ("thearing", "themployer", "fixeduration"), the employer representative's surname is spelled two ways, and one party's name appears hyphenated across lines. [Para 1][Para 27] Individual names are not reproduced here.
