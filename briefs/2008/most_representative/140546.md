# Award No. 110/08 — Rith Thych (រីត ធីច) (Arbitration Council, 15 September 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chhea presiding; Li Tai Seng, employer nominee; An Non, worker nominee) · Doc ID 140546

## 1. Facts
The respondent is ក្រុមហ៊ុន រីច ឆឹច ខេមបូឌា ព្រីន ធឹង ហ្វិក ធឹរី អេសធី ឌី, a printing enterprise at house 1019, Phnom Penh Thmei commune, Russey Keo district, employing about 59 workers and, as the panel found, with no union at all. [Para 1][Para 25][Para 26] The claimant was the employee delegate (ប្រតិភូ បុគ្គលិក), acting for eight plate-pulling workers who attended the hearing, and not a union. [Para 1][Para 27][Para 56] Their complaint of 13 August 2008 asked for a USD 5 increase on the base wage; conciliation that same day failed on that single point and the report of non-conciliation No. 926 dated 25 August 2008 reached the Secretariat on 26 August 2008. [Para 9][Para 10][Para 6] Because the strike continued, the Council issued order No. 011 KA of 27 August 2008 ordering an immediate stoppage and a return to work on 28 August 2008, which the workers obeyed; the hearing of 1 September 2008 was attended by both sides. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 8][Para 15]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must raise the base wage by USD 5 a month for the workers, as demanded in the non-conciliation report? [Para 2][Para 28][Para 40]
2. (Issue 1, as pleaded for eight workers) Whether the eight workers who attended the hearing may obtain a USD 3 increase on the base wage over and above the USD 2 the company has already granted? [Para 28][Para 30][Para 38]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), the employee delegate and eight plate-pulling workers:** the company had raised wages by USD 1 a year in the past but gave only USD 2 in 2008 against a demand of USD 5, although goods and house rent have risen by USD 3 a month and the plate-pulling work is heavy; they maintained the USD 3 demand for the eight workers present. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 32][Para 38]
**Employer (respondent), the printing company:** it accepts that plate pulling is heavy and tiring work, but says it has no capacity to meet the demand; it granted only USD 2 this year, has little work at present, and will bring the increase back for discussion when work picks up; its internal rules of 11 September 2000 provide for an annual wage review based on each worker's output, discipline and skill and on the company's capacity. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 35][Para 36][Para 39]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider the demand for a USD 3 increase on the base wage, and made no order for any increase. [Para 57][Para 58]
The panel first tested the demand against the minimum wage. Point 1 of notification No. 745 K.B.V of 23 October 2006 fixes USD 45 a month for workers in the textile, garment and footwear sector during a one-to-three-month probation and USD 50 once the probation ends and the worker is fully entitled. [Para 41][Para 42] It found that none of the eight workers was paid below USD 50, so no minimum-wage breach arose, and that the company had in fact reviewed and raised wages every year. [Para 43][Para 44]
It then characterised the demand as an interest dispute (វិវាទ ផលប្រយោជន៍) rather than a rights dispute. [Para 45] Under the Labour Law and the Council's own jurisprudence, the panel may decide an interest dispute only where it is brought by the union that is most representative in the enterprise, or by several unions together, with more than half the workers as members; Article 277 of the 1997 Labour Law and Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 make such an award a substitute collective agreement for one year. [Para 46][Para 50][Para 51] The panel followed Award No. 81/04-Evergreen, Award No. 98/04-Great Union and Award No. 57/06-Evergreen on that point. [Para 47][Para 49][Para 54]
Because the company has no union, the claimant was not a most representative union but an employee delegate, and the claim covered only the eight workers who appeared, the panel held it could not issue an award that would stand as a collective agreement for the whole enterprise. [Para 26][Para 48][Para 52][Para 56] It therefore declined to consider the claim and made no ruling on the USD 5 figure either. [Para 57][Para 58]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an interest dispute over a wage increase is brought by an employee delegate or by a group of individual workers rather than by a registered union that is the most representative in the enterprise, the panel will not adjudicate the amount of the increase: an award on an interest dispute operates as a collective agreement binding every worker for a year, so it may not be made at the instance of a claimant who represents only some of them. [Para 46][Para 51][Para 56]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that issuing such an award would make non-members lose their right to strike over future interest disputes, which it called unjust to them — a policy reason it did not need once it had found the claimant lacked standing. [Para 52][Para 53]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer is named by a long Khmer corporate name in the header and as រីត ធីច and រឹត ធីច in the findings and order, so three forms of the company name appear. [Para 1][Para 25][Para 26] Para 1 carries a machine-garbage string after the royal arms, para 28 is cut in mid-sentence by a stray English fragment about "the state of the News", and para 39 opens with "cllu .៥--". [Para 1][Para 28][Para 39] Para 30 embeds a wage table whose columns are scrambled and partly duplicated, so individual wage figures cannot be reliably paired with the workers listed; those workers are not named here. [Para 30] The demand is stated as USD 5 in the non-conciliation report but the operative order disposes only of a USD 3 claim, and para 32 runs the two figures together. [Para 2][Para 32][Para 58] The worker-nominated arbitrator is printed as អន ណន in the header and as អន់ លាន in the signature block, and the signature block carries stray Latin characters. [Para 1][Para 60]
