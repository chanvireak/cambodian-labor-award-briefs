# Award No. 139/08 — Sup Prime Garment (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ុប ព្រីម ហ្គាមេន) (Arbitration Council, 27 November 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · three-member panel: អ៉ីង សុទ្ធី (employer-nominated), អន ណន (worker-nominated), គង់ ផល្លៈ (president) · Doc ID 140565

## 1. Facts
Sup Prime Garment (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ុប ព្រីម ហ្គាមេន) runs a garment factory at Daem Mien, Ta Khmao, Kandal, with about 800 workers and a union of about 125 members; no union there holds most representative status. [Para 52][Para 54][Para 55] Thirteen demands were conciliated, five settled, and eight came before the panel; at the hearing Issues 2, 5 and 6 were settled, leaving Issues 1, 3, 4, 7 and 8. [Para 22][Para 26][Para 27] The recurring subject was maternity protection: the US$6 living allowance during 90 days' leave, the rate at which maternity pay is calculated, and the timing of that payment. [Para 56][Para 77][Para 82]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay the full US$6 living allowance to women on 90 days' maternity leave and repay the US$3 shortfall since the allowance took effect? [Para 56]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the company must apply six-month employment contracts to all workers? [Para 61]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the company must raise the meal allowance to 1,500 riel for two hours of overtime? [Para 123]
4. (Issue 7) Whether maternity pay must be calculated on the average of the last 12 months' wage and allowances? [Para 149]
5. (Issue 8) Whether the company must pay women their wages three months before they begin maternity leave? [Para 82]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), the CLUF union at Sup Prime (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា-សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត នៃ ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ុប ព្រីម ហ្គាមេន):** the allowance carries no conditions and must be paid in full; the 2005 agreement promised six-month contracts; 1,000 riel no longer buys a meal; maternity pay should average 12 months. [Para 57][Para 66][Para 71][Para 77]
**Employer (respondent), Sup Prime Garment (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ុប ព្រីម ហ្គាមេន):** the allowance is US$3 only for a partial first month, the 2005 agreement binds only 2005 entrants, market practice is 1,000 riel, and paying three months ahead would disrupt monthly accounts. [Para 60][Para 67][Para 72][Para 83]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must pay the full US$6 living allowance and repay US$3 to women who took maternity leave from April 2008, pay maternity pay on a 12-month average excluding that allowance, and pay women their wages three months before leave; the six-month-contract and 1,500 riel meal claims were refused. [Para 175][Para 176][Para 177][Para 178][Para 179]

On Issue 1 the panel applied point 1 of Notification No. 032/08 of 17 April 2008 and point 2 of the accompanying meeting minute of 5 May 2008, which grant US$6 a month "without any conditions", and followed Award 119/08-Hong Va. [Para 87][Para 91][Para 92] It read that phrase as covering unpaid authorised leave, so maternity leave does not reduce the allowance; the US$3 already paid for April 2008 onwards was therefore a shortfall to be repaid. [Para 93][Para 95][Para 99]

On Issue 3 the panel took Article 65 of the Labour Law and Articles 1 and 22 of the Civil Code (Law No. 38): a contract is the parties' agreement and no one outside it may force a term. [Para 100][Para 102][Para 105] The 2005 agreement was, on its own wording, limited to workers who joined in 2005; the workers could not produce the list of 2005 entrants the panel ordered, so no breach was shown. [Para 117][Para 119][Para 120] The claim was dismissed. [Para 122]

On Issue 4 the panel found that point 4 of Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000, preserved by point 3 of Notification No. 745 KKBV of 23 October 2006, entitles a volunteer overtime worker to 1,000 riel or one free meal, that the company pays 1,000 riel, and that no law requires more. [Para 123][Para 124][Para 127] The demand for 1,500 riel was a claim for a benefit beyond the law — an interest dispute — and, the union not being most representative, the panel declined to consider it, since an award would bind all workers as a collective agreement for a year. [Para 135][Para 143][Para 148]

On Issue 7 the panel applied Article 183 of the Labour Law: half the wage, including allowances, during maternity leave; the average is the total of the last 12 months divided by 12, and the three months are paid at that average halved. [Para 152][Para 155][Para 158] It held the US$6 living allowance is a special allowance under Notification No. 032/08 and must be left out of the average, so the company had erred by including it. [Para 161][Para 163]

On Issue 8 the panel applied Article 115(3): wages may not be paid on a rest day and must be paid a day earlier, which it read with Awards 57/06, 97/06 and 114/08 as requiring maternity pay three months in advance. [Para 167][Para 169][Para 171] The company's accounting objection did not displace a statutory duty. [Para 173]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A statutory living allowance expressed as payable "without any conditions" is not reduced by maternity leave, and any lesser sum already paid must be made up. [Para 93][Para 99] Maternity pay is half the average monthly wage of the last 12 months, calculated without the US$6 living allowance, and must be paid before the leave begins, not during it. [Para 158][Para 173]

**Obiter dicta:** a demand for a meal allowance above the notified rate is an interest dispute that a panel will not reach unless the claimant union is most representative. [Para 135][Para 143]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Para 75 records the panel receiving a letter dated 21 November 2008 on "21 April 2008", and the wage tables are dated only "month 8, 2008" with a stray numeral. [Para 35][Para 75] The company name appears as three different Khmer spellings across the face, the documents list and the operative paragraphs. [Para 1][Para 30][Para 52] The employer-nominated arbitrator's name on the signature block differs from the face of the award, and the president's line carries stray digits. [Para 1][Para 181] Para 152 contains untranslated English noise in the operative reasoning sentence, and Para 51 is a duplicated invitation letter. [Para 51][Para 152] Union officials are printed with full names and are given here by role only. [Para 2]
