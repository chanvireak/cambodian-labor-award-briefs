# Award No. 72/08 — Yong Wang Va Inter Densriel (Cambodia) Co Ltd II (ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉ុង វាំង វ៉ា អ៉ិន ដាស្ទ្រៀល (ខេមបូឌា) ខូ អិលធីឌី ទី២) and the workers' union at the Yong Va 2 factory (Arbitration Council, 12 June 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mam Sombor Na, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Kong Phallak, presiding) · Doc ID 140522

## 1. Facts
Yong Wang Va Inter Densriel (Cambodia) Co Ltd II (ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉ុង វាំង វ៉ា អ៉ិន ដាស្ទ្រៀល (ខេមបូឌា) ខូ អិលធីឌី ទី២), a Kandal factory at Ta Khmao with about 5,000 workers, faced ten collective demands lodged through the Cambodian workers' union federation and the union at the Yong Va 2 factory (សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត រោងចក្រ យ៉ុង វ៉ា ២), which has over 400 members [Para 1][Para 35][Para 36]. Five unions operate at the factory and none is the most representative [Para 37][Para 38]. Conciliation on 12 May 2008 settled four demands and left six [Para 18][Para 19]. At the hearing of 29 May 2008 the parties settled issues 4, 5 and 6, and the union withdrew issue 2 because it was already covered by Award No. 58/08 of 29 May 2008 [Para 22][Para 23]. The panel therefore decided only issues 1 and 3 [Para 24]: payment of unused annual leave in cash, and recognition of medical certificates [Para 39][Para 51].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay workers cash compensation for annual leave not used up during the currency of the employment contract, calculated on the average of the previous twelve months' wages divided by 26 days? [Para 39][Para 65]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the employer may confine itself to medical certificates from the two hospitals it nominates, or must recognise certificates from any doctor trained and licensed under the Ministry of Health framework, as its own internal rules require? [Para 51][Para 95]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company gave 18 days of annual leave a year plus one day for each three years of service, but the demand concerned the method of payment for leave not taken within the year, which should be calculated on the average of the twelve months' wages divided by 26 and multiplied by the days left [Para 39][Para 45]. On sick leave they asked that the company not limit itself to Chhey Chumnit hospital in Kandal and the Kbal Thnal occupational clinic for ordinary illness, or to Preah Kosamak, the Russian hospital and Calmette for traffic accidents, since some workers live too far away to reach the nominated hospitals [Para 56].

**Employer (respondent):** on annual leave it said a worker wanting a day's leave must apply one day in advance, and five days in advance for two days or more, and that unused leave was paid by dividing the basic wage by 26 and multiplying by the remaining days; it left the legal question to the panel and said it would comply with any rule of law [Para 40][Para 42][Para 44][Para 46]. On sick leave it maintained that wages and the regular attendance bonus are preserved for up to 12 days of certified illness a year, but only on certificates from the hospitals it recognises [Para 51][Para 53][Para 54][Para 57].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 1 was dismissed, and on issue 3 the employer was ordered to recognise certificates from doctors trained under the Ministry of Health framework, holding a valid diploma and licensed to practise, as the basis for sick leave under Article 4, point 6 of its internal rules, from the date the award becomes enforceable [Para 105][Para 106].

On issue 1 the panel noted the two competing methods — the company's, which divides the basic wage by 26, and the union's, which divides the average of twelve months' earnings by 26 — and turned to Article 168 of the Labour Law, which fixes the annual-leave indemnity by reference to the average of wages, bonuses, allowances and benefits in kind over the twelve months preceding the leave [Para 66][Para 68]. But the dispositive question was different: Article 167(k), paragraph 3, renders void any agreement providing for compensation in lieu of annual leave or for waiving the right to take it [Para 73]. The panel read that as a prohibition on paying cash for unused annual leave while the worker is still employed, and held the company's annual buy-out of unused days contrary to the spirit of Article 167 [Para 74][Para 75]. It also relied on Article 167(k), paragraph 4, which lets leave be carried over to the end of the contract without that being a waiver, but not for more than three consecutive years [Para 79][Para 80][Para 81]. The employer was accordingly directed to encourage workers to take their leave rather than pay it out, and the claim failed [Para 83][Para 84].

On issue 3 the panel quoted Article 71(k), paragraph 3, under which a contract is suspended by absence certified by an official doctor for up to six months [Para 96][Para 97]. Because the law says only "official doctor", the internal rule confining recognition to state hospitals and the occupational clinic contradicts it [Para 98]. The panel defined an official doctor as one trained at a school recognised by the Ministry of Health, holding a proper diploma and a licence to practise, following Award 102/07 (Te Ra Pichyu) [Para 99][Para 100][Para 101]. It ordered recognition of such certificates, whether from a state or private doctor, and required state certificates to bear the institution's seal and private ones to be accompanied by the clinic's operating licence [Para 102][Para 103][Para 104].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may not pay a serving worker cash in lieu of untaken annual leave: Article 167(k), paragraph 3, voids any such agreement, and the remedy is to require the employer to encourage and schedule the taking of leave, with carry-over permitted but not beyond three consecutive years; by contrast, a worker's absence on a certificate from any licensed doctor counts as an authorised suspension of the contract, and an internal rule narrowing recognition to nominated hospitals is void to that extent [Para 73][Para 81][Para 98].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's recital of the Article 168 averaging formula, which it noted but did not apply because the claim failed on the point of principle, was not necessary to the outcome [Para 66][Para 68].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's name is printed in at least four differing transliterations across the header, the evidence list and the signature block, mixing "Wang Va", "Va In" and "In Dasriel", and the union's address is given as Street 371 in Phnom Penh while the factory address is Ta Khmao in Kandal [Para 1][Para 2][Para 25]. The jurisdiction recital prints the chapter number as "24 12" [Para 14], and the panel's description of the closed demands is garbled at "06 month of issues" [Para 22]. The statutory quotation at Para 66 breaks into untranslatable OCR debris mid-sentence, and Article 167 is printed once as "Article 167 kha paragraph 3" and once as "Article 167(k), paragraph 4" for what the context shows are different limbs [Para 66][Para 73][Para 79]. The internal-rule point is numbered "Cha" (six) and so cited [Para 57][Para 58]. The presiding arbitrator's name appears as គង់ ផល្ហៈ in the composition and គង់ ផល្លៈ at signature [Para 1][Para 108]. No individual worker is named in the surviving text.
