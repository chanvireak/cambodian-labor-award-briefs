# Award No. 40/08 — Soub Prim Garnet PIT Ltd (ស៊ុប ព្រីម ហ្គាមេន ភីអឹធី អិលធីឌី) and Khmer Youth Federation of Trade Unions (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ) (Arbitration Council, 21 April 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators, chair Pen Bunhea · Doc ID 140503

## 1. Facts
Soub Prim Garnet PIT Ltd (ស៊ុប ព្រីម ហ្គាមេន ភីអឹធី អិលធីឌី), a garment enterprise at Ta Khmau, Kandal employing 840 workers, faced a collective dispute filed by the Khmer Youth Federation of Trade Unions (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ), a registered union with 120 members in the factory that was not the most representative union there. [Para 32][Para 34] Conciliation by the Kandal provincial labour office on 13 March 2008 settled four of nine demands; conciliation at the hearing settled points 1 and 5, and the panel noted that only points 2, 3 and 4 remained for decision. [Para 17][Para 21][Para 22] Point 4 concerned reimbursement of health-check fees and was in the end not considered. [Para 97]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the company may issue new worker identity cards bearing an expiry date, or must issue cards free of any expiry date? [Para 36][Para 54]
2. (Issue 3) Whether workers engaged on successive short-term contracts of more than two months must be converted into full-rights workers with the corresponding benefits? [Para 42][Para 65]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant union):** changing a card amounts to severing the employment contract and exposes workers to loss of seniority; workers kept on short-term contracts beyond two months should be integrated as full-rights workers, and the company owes 100 riel per worker for health checks since 1997. [Para 41][Para 47][Para 75]
**Employer (Soub Prim Garnet PIT Ltd):** the expiry date answers a requirement of the company and its buyers, cards are reissued every six months, and the worker's name, entry date and signature are all retained so seniority is guaranteed; the short-term contracts are titled as such and already carry the full wage and benefits. [Para 39][Para 43]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — issue 2 rejected, issue 3 rejected, and issue 4 not considered; the award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification if no party objects in writing to the Minister of Labour. [Para 95][Para 96][Para 98]

On issue 2 the panel relied on Article 2 of the Labour Law, which confers on the employer the right to organise and direct the enterprise, and on its own earlier awards (17/03 and 18/03 Hoh Hing, 28/04 Raffles Grande D'Angkor, 20/06, 17/07, 116/07, 54/07) holding that this managerial right covers access cards and internal procedures so long as they are lawful and reasonable. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 61] The card's expiry date was found not to extinguish seniority, and the workers produced no evidence that any named worker had lost it; the demand therefore had no legal foundation. [Para 62][Para 63]

On issue 3 the panel found that no casual (ម្តងម្កាល) contract existed: the contracts were in writing and titled fixed-duration, and Article 67 caps such contracts at two years, so they were fixed-term rather than occasional engagements. [Para 67][Para 69][Para 70] Because the workers on those contracts already received the 50 USD base wage, attendance bonus, 5 per cent end-of-contract indemnity and annual leave, the demand to convert them asked for a benefit that had not arisen. [Para 71][Para 73]

On issue 4 the panel recorded that Article 247 of the 1997 Labour Law obliges the employer to pay for health checks, and that Award 81/05 Soub Prim Garnet had already covered the whole workforce; because the union did not supply its membership and unpaid-worker lists by 31 March 2008 despite telephone follow-up, the panel declined to consider the claim. [Para 79][Para 83][Para 87][Para 92]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer's Article 2 managerial prerogative extends to the format of factory access cards, and an expiry date on a card does not break continuity of service where the employer retains the worker's name, entry date and signature and no worker proves loss of seniority; likewise a written fixed-duration contract within Article 67's two-year ceiling is not a casual contract, so a demand to convert such workers into permanent staff seeks a benefit that never crystallised. [Para 61][Para 70][Para 73]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that although no new Prakas on health checks had yet been issued by the labour ministry, Article 247 of the Labour Law already supplied a sufficient legal basis for the employer's duty to pay. [Para 79]
