# Award No. 143/08 — Cham Thich Thay (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឆាម ថិច ថា យ) and the Free Workers' Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia (Arbitration Council, 9 December 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tai Seng, An Non, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 140569

## 1. Facts
Cham Thich Thay, a garment factory in Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, has operated since 2004 and employed 975 workers at the time of the award, 721 in the old building and 221 in a new building staffed by workers taken over from Cambo Fashion Co., Ltd. [Para 41][Para 61][Para 63]. The union complained by telephone on 4 November 2008 about twelve matters, of which seven remained in dispute after conciliation, and it withdrew the fourth as duplicative of the second [Para 23][Para 24][Para 29]. The employer has rented buses to carry workers to and from the factory since 2004, but no written agreement on it was ever signed; from December 2008 it proposed to replace the buses with USD 5 a month per worker [Para 48][Para 52]. The hearing was held on 20 November 2008 [Para 22].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must keep renting the buses that carry workers to and from work? [Para 46]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the old-building workers are entitled to the USD 6 attendance bonus and USD 10 living allowance paid in the new building? [Para 133][Para 135]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must raise the housing allowance from USD 5 to USD 10 a month? [Para 183]
4. (Issue 5) Whether wages must be paid during working hours? [Para 190]
5. (Issue 6) Whether the company must add 1,000 riel a day in meal money for overtime from 16:30 to 18:30? [Para 202]
6. (Issue 7) Whether the company may pro-rate the USD 5 attendance bonus by days of authorised absence? [Para 220]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), Free Workers' Union of the Kingdom of Cambodia (សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ នៃ ព្រះរាជាណាចក្រ កម្ពុជា):** the union's officers argued that the buses had run since 2004 and could not simply be bought off with USD 5, since a seat costs USD 10 to USD 12 a month; that the allowances must be levelled up to the new building's rates; that wages were being handed out 15 minutes before the end of the shift and sometimes only at 5:00 or 5:30 p.m.; and that the attendance bonus is wage, so it cannot be withheld for leave the company itself authorised [Para 56][Para 70][Para 90][Para 93].

**Employer (respondent), Cham Thich Thay Company (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឆាម ថិច ថា យ):** the company said it could keep the buses only for November 2008 and would then pay USD 5 a month instead, citing the world economic crisis, and that it could not raise the housing allowance [Para 47][Para 53][Para 78]. It said it had consistently paid the USD 5 attendance bonus and USD 6 living allowance required by Ministerial Notice No. 017, and that for the new building it kept the USD 6 and USD 10 rates those workers already had [Para 138][Para 158][Para 167].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to continue running the buses, to pay wages within working hours, to keep the USD 6 attendance bonus and USD 10 living allowance for the workers transferred from Cambo Fashion, and to pro-rate the USD 5 attendance bonus for authorised absences; it declined to consider the levelling-up, housing-allowance and extra meal-money claims [Para 244][Para 246][Para 248][Para 250].

On Issue 1 the panel noted that no article requires an employer to provide transport, and that Article 13 of the Labour Law allows benefits more favourable than the law to stand [Para 115][Para 116][Para 118]. It found that the practice since 2004, though never written, had become an oral agreement binding on both sides, which the employer could replace only by showing a genuine necessity and the economic impact of not changing [Para 122][Para 123][Para 124]. The employer produced no evidence of the crisis it invoked, and both sides accepted that USD 5 was roughly equal in value to a bus seat, so the panel found the stated reason unsound and ordered the buses continued [Para 127][Para 128][Para 130][Para 131].

On Issues 2, 3 and 6 the panel held the claims to be interest disputes, that is, demands above what the law or an agreement gives, which only the most representative union may bring [Para 141][Para 142][Para 143]. Applying Article 277 of the Labour Law and Article 6 of Prakas No. 305, it found the claimant union held only about 600 of 975 workers and had never notified the company of its membership, so it was not the most representative union and could not bring those claims [Para 43][Para 145][Para 147]. It therefore declined to consider them, and also recalled that an award on an interest dispute substitutes for a collective agreement for one year under Clause 43 of Prakas No. 099 [Para 150][Para 151][Para 154]. The new-building claim was different: under Article 87 the transfer of undertaking preserved those workers' contracts and benefits, and the company had in fact paid USD 6 and USD 10 in November 2008, so the panel ordered the rates maintained until their Cambo Fashion contracts ended or were renewed [Para 168][Para 169][Para 179][Para 181].

On Issue 5 the panel applied Articles 115 and 137 of the Labour Law and clause 4 of the internal rules, which set the working day at 07:30 to 11:30 and 12:30 to 16:30, and held that wages must be handed out and the payment completed inside those hours; it added that if payment runs over by 30 minutes the time must be counted as overtime, which cannot be imposed [Para 192][Para 196][Para 197][Para 199]. On Issue 7 it held that the attendance bonus is part of wage under Article 103, that authorised absence suspends the contract under Articles 71(6) and 72 so no wage is owed for those days, and that the bonus may therefore be reduced rateably [Para 231][Para 235][Para 239][Para 243].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A benefit granted by long and uninterrupted practice, though never written down, is an implied agreement that the employer cannot replace with a cash equivalent unless it proves a genuine necessity and the economic harm of continuing; an unsupported claim of economic crisis is not enough [Para 122][Para 123][Para 127][Para 131]. A union that does not represent more than half the workforce, and has not notified the employer of its membership, is not the most representative union and cannot bring an interest dispute, that is a demand for benefits above the law or an existing agreement, though a right already acquired under a contract or preserved by Article 87 on a transfer of undertaking remains enforceable [Para 145][Para 147][Para 154][Para 181]. Wages must be paid within working hours, with any delay beyond 30 minutes treated as overtime [Para 197][Para 199]. The attendance bonus is wage, but it may be reduced rateably for days of authorised absence, because the contract is suspended and no wage is due for those days [Para 231][Para 239].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that it had consistently refused claims for 2,000 riel meal money where the worker did overtime for two hours, since Notice No. 017 fixes a flat 1,000 riel a day regardless of the length of overtime [Para 210][Para 213].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The letterhead is destroyed, the royal arms replaced by a run of symbols, and many sentences repeat a fragment of themselves, which in places truncates a word mid-clause [Para 1][Para 83]. The award year appears as "ឆ្នាំ ២០០៨" in some recitals and merely "ឆ្នាំ ២០" in others, so the date of the notes and notices cannot always be read [Para 20][Para 135]. The panel's own summary says twelve demands were made and seven remained, while only six are reasoned, the fourth having been withdrawn by agreement without being renumbered, which is why Issue 4 is missing above [Para 24][Para 29]. The employer's figures are printed two ways in the same passage, USD 5 attendance and USD 6 allowance in one place and the reverse in another [Para 6][Para 65]. Individual union officers and worker delegates are reduced here to initials and roles, and the signature block is largely unreadable [Para 252].
