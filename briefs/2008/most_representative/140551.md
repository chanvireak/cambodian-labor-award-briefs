# Award No. 114/08 — Way Tech Garment (Cambodia) (ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ាយ ថិច ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា)) (Arbitration Council, 25 September 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chhea presiding; Mam Sambo Na, employer nominee; Tun Sy Phan, worker nominee) · Doc ID 140551

## 1. Facts
Way Tech Garment (Cambodia) — ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ាយ ថិច ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) — at Trapeang Khlong village, Chom Chao commune, Dangkao district, Phnom Penh, employs 1,114 workers plus about 400 probationary workers, and works Monday to Saturday, eight hours a day, with Sunday as the weekly rest day. [Para 35][Para 69][Para 121] The claimants were two unions acting together, the Khmer Youth Union at the Way Tech factory with more than 300 members and the Cambodian Union for Workers' Rights (សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព.សហជីព កម្ពុជា ដើម្បី សិទ្ធិ កម្មករ) with more than 500, together more than half the workforce though neither is individually most representative. [Para 2][Para 36][Para 37][Para 38] Their complaint of 20 August 2008 followed a strike; conciliation failed on 5 of 19 demands on 21 August 2008 and report No. 941 of 28 August 2008 reached the Secretariat on 2 September 2008; both sides attended the hearing of 12 September 2008. [Para 17][Para 18][Para 14][Para 21]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay 50 percent of wages for the three months before a woman worker takes maternity leave, rather than month by month during it? [Para 39][Para 42][Para 74]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must contribute USD 5 a month towards transport costs or house rent? [Para 49][Para 101]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must raise the regular attendance bonus by a further USD 5 a month, from USD 5 to USD 10? [Para 60][Para 106]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must pay a further USD 5 a month as an overtime cooperation bonus? [Para 64][Para 111]
5. (Issue 5) Whether workers who volunteer to work on Sunday must receive the 1,000 riel meal allowance for each such day? [Para 69][Para 116][Para 119]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), the Khmer Youth Union and the Cambodian Union for Workers' Rights at Way Tech:** the three months of half wage should be paid in one sum before the leave begins, because woman workers live in the provinces and cannot travel each month to collect it, and friends collecting for them have sometimes kept the money; they ask USD 5 towards transport or rent because bus fares rose from USD 5 to USD 12 and rent from USD 40 to USD 50; they also seek USD 5 more attendance bonus, USD 5 more as an overtime cooperation bonus, and 1,000 riel meal money for Sunday work, which they regard as overtime. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 54][Para 55][Para 61][Para 66][Para 71]
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ាយ ថិច ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា):** it applies the Labour Law and pays the half wage month by month, since paying three months in advance would disturb its monthly accounting and the woman might spend the money before giving birth; it cannot afford the allowances, having just paid a USD 6 living allowance under government advice, and it already pays 200 percent for Sunday work, which it says is a rest-day payment and not overtime, with meal money only for overtime on ordinary working days. [Para 3][Para 44][Para 45][Para 46][Para 58][Para 59][Para 72][Para 73]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay the 50 percent maternity wage for the three months before leave begins, dismissed the transport, attendance and overtime-cooperation claims, and ordered 1,000 riel meal money for each Sunday worked. [Para 133][Para 134][Para 135][Para 136][Para 137]
On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 183 of the Labour Law, which requires the employer to pay half wage including benefits to a woman worker on maternity leave, and Article 115(3), which forbids paying wages on a day the worker is entitled to rest and requires payment one day earlier. [Para 74][Para 80][Para 94][Para 95] It read those provisions together with Awards 57/06-Evergreen and 97/06-Neve Max and held that maternity pay, being wage, must be advanced before the leave begins, so the company's monthly practice was wrong. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 98][Para 100]
On Issues 2, 3 and 4 the panel classified each demand as an interest dispute above the statutory minimum, since no law requires a rent allowance, a higher attendance bonus or an overtime cooperation bonus. [Para 102][Para 108][Para 112][Para 113] It held that a group of unions which, though not individually most representative, together represent more than half the workers may bring such a dispute, following Award 81/07-Supreme. [Para 87][Para 89][Para 91] It would apply its equitable jurisdiction under Article 312 bis paragraph 2 only where the parties had exhausted efforts to reach a collective agreement and the claim was shown to be reasonable and affordable, and the union showed neither, so each claim failed. [Para 92][Para 103][Para 104][Para 105]
On Issue 5 the panel held that work on Sunday exceeds the normal 48-hour week and is therefore overtime; point 4 of notification No. 017 SKBY of 18 July 2000 gives 1,000 riel meal money per day to workers who volunteer for overtime at the employer's request, so the allowance was due even though the company also paid 200 percent. [Para 121][Para 124][Para 127][Para 128][Para 131][Para 132]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Maternity pay under Article 183 is wage, so Article 115(3) requires it to be paid before the leave begins rather than monthly during it; and where a group of unions together represents more than half the enterprise, the panel may entertain their interest dispute, but will grant a benefit above the statutory minimum only on proof of a reasonable, affordable claim after real efforts at collective bargaining. [Para 95][Para 98][Para 103][Para 104]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that paying three months at once risks the money being spent before the birth was the employer's argument, not a ground of decision, and the panel did not adopt it. [Para 46]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer appears as ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ាយ ថិច ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) in the header and as វ៉ាយ ថិច, វ៉ាយ ថិជ and វ៉ាយ ថិ in the body, four spellings of one company. [Para 1][Para 24][Para 25][Para 32] The claimant line in the header breaks off mid-word ("សិទ្ធិ កម្ម") and lists a fourth representative from a different federation, the Khmer Youth Union, alongside the two claimant unions, so the parties cannot be cleanly separated there. [Para 2] The transport demand is printed as USD 55 in the list of unsettled issues but as USD 5 everywhere in the findings and order. [Para 4][Para 49][Para 105] The number of probationary workers is corrupted to "កម្ម ររេ បណ្តែត", and the closing page is a machine-garbage line ("the train the track to the 111 @,@ 0 @.@"), with the signature block carrying stray Latin characters. [Para 35][Para 139]
