# Award No. 107/08 — Seratex Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 11 September 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, An Nan, Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 140543

## 1. Facts
Seratex Co., Ltd runs a garment factory at Chamkar Euvleuk village, Kakab commune, Dangkor district, Phnom Penh, with approximately 960 workers [Para 2][Para 46]. The claimant is the Local Union of Free Trade Union of Workers of the Kingdom of Cambodia (FTUWKC) at Seratex Garment Factory, which has 250 members and does not hold most representative status [Para 5][Para 47]. Its complaint of 22 July 2008 raised ten demands; five were conciliated on 12 August 2008 and Issue 4 was conciliated at the hearing, leaving Issues 1, 2, 3 and 5 [Para 25][Para 26][Para 29]. The dispute covers a permission letter required from workers who decline overtime, the transfer and suspension of the local union's vice-president, the overtime meal allowance, and the US$ 5 attendance bonus [Para 9][Para 12][Para 17][Para 20].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must stop requiring a permission letter before workers who do not volunteer for overtime may leave at 4:00 p.m.? [Para 9][Para 94]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must reinstate the local union's vice-president (Mr P) to his former position? [Para 12][Para 102]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay an additional 500 riels per hour as meal allowance for overtime? [Para 17][Para 121]
4. (Issue 5) Whether the company must maintain the US$ 5 attendance bonus when workers take permitted leave for personal commitments of less than three days? [Para 20][Para 125]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** FTUWKC argued that because overtime is voluntary, workers who do not sign the overtime list should simply leave at 4:00 p.m. without a permission letter or any form, and it rejected the company's offer of a special exit card [Para 52][Para 54]. It sought reinstatement of its vice-president, who refused transfer to packing because of an earlier altercation with a worker there [Para 68][Para 72]; 500 riels an hour more meal allowance [Para 89]; and the full US$ 5 attendance bonus even for short permitted absences [Para 92].
**Employer (respondent):** Seratex said the permission letter serves security and order at the gate [Para 51], that the transfer kept the same position, wages and benefits and that the vice-president abandoned his post by refusing it [Para 14][Para 79], that it pays meal allowance in accordance with Notification 017 SKBY of 18 July 2000 [Para 91], and that the same notification allows it to deduct the whole attendance bonus from any worker who is absent, with or without permission [Para 93][Para 94].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to let workers who decline overtime leave at 4:00 p.m. without a permission letter, and to reinstate its union vice-president with back pay from the date of effect of the award; the 500-riel meal demand was declined as an interests dispute, and the demand to keep the full US$ 5 bonus was rejected, the company being ordered to pay the bonus in proportion to days actually worked [Para 143][Para 144][Para 145].
On Issue 1 the panel applied clause 4 of Prakas 80 SKBY of 1 March 1999, which requires overtime to be arranged voluntarily, with no coercion or discipline of workers who decline [Para 96][Para 97]. It found the permission-letter rule an unnecessary additional obligation that burdens the exercise of that right, noted the company could point to no advantage beyond gate security, and ordered the practice ended [Para 100][Para 101].
On Issue 2 the panel treated the vice-president as a protected union leader under Article 293 of the Labour Law and clause 4 of Prakas 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001, so that dismissal required the Labour Inspector's authorisation, with a decision due within one month [Para 105][Para 106][Para 107]. The company had asked for termination on 22 July 2008; more than thirty days had elapsed by the award, so the request was deemed rejected on 22 August 2008 [Para 112][Para 113]. Although Article 295 allowed suspension pending the Inspector's decision, a refusal annuls the suspension and its effects [Para 114][Para 116][Para 117]. The panel accordingly ordered reinstatement when the award takes effect, with back pay until reinstatement, so that the company could still pursue its appeal to the Ministry [Para 119][Para 120][Para 144].
On Issue 3 the panel read clause 4 of Notification 017 SKBY of 18 July 2000 as fixing the overtime meal allowance at 1,000 riels a day regardless of hours, a rate the company was already paying, sometimes twice over for two overtime blocks [Para 121][Para 122]. The extra 500 riels therefore exceeded the law and was an interests demand, which the panel declined to hear from a union without most representative status, holding 250 members out of 960 workers insufficient [Para 123][Para 124][Para 125].
On Issue 5 the panel held that the US$ 5 bonus under point 3 of Notification 017 amounts to part of wages under Article 103 of the Labour Law [Para 126][Para 130][Para 131]. Absence authorised by the employer suspends the contract under Article 71(1)(6), and Article 72(1) then relieves the employer of paying for those days [Para 131][Para 132][Para 133]. The bonus may therefore be reduced, but only in proportion to the days of permitted absence, not forfeited in full [Para 137][Para 138][Para 141][Para 145].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Requiring a worker who declines voluntary overtime to obtain a permission letter before leaving at the normal finishing hour is an unlawful burden on the voluntariness of overtime under Prakas 80 [Para 96][Para 101]. A protected union leader's dismissal that the Labour Inspector leaves undecided for a month is deemed refused, and the suspension that accompanied it falls away, so the leader returns to work with back pay while any appeal runs [Para 113][Para 117]. And a permitted personal absence reduces the attendance bonus proportionally rather than extinguishing it [Para 138][Para 141].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's aside that the company bought food and drink for the few workers who stayed past 9:30 p.m. [Para 89], and its observation that Notification 017 does not define how many days count as regular attendance [Para 128][Para 129], were not necessary to any order.

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The unofficial translation leaves an editorial note in the body of the facts, telling the reader that a passage "should not be included in the findings of fact section" — evidently an instruction from the draft that survived into the published text [Para 56][Para 57]. The OCR splits every individual's name across two lines, so the operative paragraphs read as "reinstate Mr." on one line and the surname on the next; a reader reconstructing the award from line numbers alone cannot tell where a name begins. The same notification is dated 18 July 2000 in the reasons and 18 July 2008 in one finding [Para 126][Para 127]. The attendance-bonus demand is stated as leave "for three days" in the decision but "less than three days" in the analysis [Para 91][Para 139]. One citation gives "Article 72, paragraph 6" for what the preceding sentence calls Article 72(1) [Para 132][Para 133]. Issue 5's order, to pay the bonus in proportion to days worked, does not match the reasoning's measure, days of permitted absence [Para 138][Para 145]. Individual names are withheld and given by role throughout.
