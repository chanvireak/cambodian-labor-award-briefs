# Award No. 64/08 — G Foremost Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 26 May 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Ann Vireak for the worker party, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 140515

## 1. Facts
G Foremost Co., Ltd, a factory at Ta Kae village, Kambol commune, Angsnoul district, Kandal, employing about 813 workers, faced a collective dispute brought by the local branch of the Democratic Trade Union (DTU) within the Democratic Trade Federation Union (DTFU); no union at the factory holds most-representative status, and 40 workers were involved in the demand. [Para 2][Para 4][Para 23][Para 24] The Kandal labour department conciliated 14 points on 29 April 2008, settled 10, and referred four to the Council by non-conciliation report No. 245/08 dated 29 April 2008; at the hearing on 8 May 2008 Issues 1 and 3 were conciliated, leaving Issues 2 and 4 for decision. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 21][Para 22] An interim order No. 007 of 2 May 2008 had been issued before the hearing. [Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must dismiss the administration officer whom workers accuse of taking money from them during recruitment? [Para 24][Para 46]
2. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must maintain the skill bonus it formerly paid to skilled and hardworking workers? [Para 35][Para 59]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — DTU local union at G Foremost, within DTFU):** the company should dismiss the administration officer because he took money from applicants; one union officer stated that he paid US$ 30 in total to a former employee to be hired, and was told US$ 10 of it had to go to that officer, though no evidence showed money reaching him directly; the company should also maintain the skill bonus, which it once paid at US$ 1 to US$ 20 to workers skilled on machines, hardworking, cooperative on overtime and rarely absent, but which it had gradually reduced. [Para 24][Para 28][Para 35][Para 36]
**Employer (respondent — G Foremost Co., Ltd):** the officer denied ever receiving money during recruitment, asked the workers to prove it, and said he had enough money of his own and that his elder sibling, the Vice Director, holds half the shares; the company said it dismissed the former staff involved in taking recruitment money and moved recruitment into an administrative process; on the bonus it said the payment was never a "skill bonus" but a bonus granted by the stated criteria, never deducted, merely lower for workers who did not meet them, and never reduced to writing. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 32][Para 33][Para 36][Para 39][Para 40]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected both the demand to dismiss the administration officer and the demand to maintain the skill bonus. [Para 63][Para 64]

On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 65 of the Labour Law of 1997, under which a labour contract establishes the working relationship and is subject to ordinary law and to the form agreed by the parties, and Article 22 of Decree No. 38 on Contract and Liabilities Outside the Contract of 28 October 1988, under which a contract binds only its parties and may be amended only by consent. From that it concluded that only a contracting party may terminate or amend the contract, that a third party cannot demand it on a party's behalf, and therefore that the employer, as a contracting party, holds the right to hire and dismiss. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49][Para 52][Para 53][Para 54]

The panel followed Award No. 73/04-Genuine, Issue 3, and a line of earlier awards: workers have no right to demand the dismissal of an employee unless they prove that the person is dangerous and cannot be allowed in the factory, so that keeping him would harm the workplace. It found the workers showed no such evidence and only accused the officer of taking recruitment money, the complainants themselves did not attend the hearing, and the file contained complaints but no proof of payment to him. The demand therefore had no legal ground. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 57][Para 58][Para 59]

On Issue 4 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law creating a skill bonus, no agreement and no collective bargaining agreement in the company on the subject, and nothing in the company's internal work rules; it also found the workers could not distinguish whether what they received was a skill bonus or an ordinary bonus. On the employer's statement and on the workers' own examples of fluctuating monthly amounts, it held the payment to be a bonus, still paid, not deducted, and lower only for workers who did not meet the company's requirements. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61][Para 62][Para 63]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Workers have no standing to demand that an employer dismiss a fellow employee: under Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 22 of Decree No. 38 the contract binds only its parties, so only the employer may hire or dismiss, and a collective demand for dismissal succeeds only where the claimant proves the person is dangerous and cannot be allowed in the workplace. Separately, a discretionary bonus paid by published criteria that the employer never agreed to characterise as a fixed skill bonus, and that no law, agreement or internal rule guarantees, is not a right the panel will enforce. [Para 52][Para 54][Para 58][Para 59][Para 62]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the company should expect the workers to confuse the two kinds of bonus because it never put the criteria in writing explains the dispute but was not the ground of decision. [Para 40][Para 41]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The administration officer is identified in the award by a personal name together with an alias, and the alias alone is used in the non-conciliation report; the two forms are not always used consistently [Para 14][Para 24][Para 46].
- The procedural paragraph repeats itself, printing "10 issues were conciliated and four issues" twice, and the count of 14 conciliated points is not reconciled with the four that reached the Council [Para 18][Para 19].
- One conciliation minute in the evidence list is dated 29 April 2007 while every other reference to that conciliation is 29 April 2008 [Para 23][Para 18].
- The workers' bonus figures are scrambled, with amounts and month labels out of order and "US$ 0" entries interleaved, so the pattern of payments cannot be reconstructed from the text [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44].
- The prior-award citations are corrupted and partly duplicated, with the same case numbers appearing twice in successive paragraphs [Para 55][Para 56].
- A former employee accused of taking the recruitment money is named in several paragraphs but the spelling is unstable, so it is not reproduced here [Para 26][Para 28][Para 31].
- Individual workers and union officers are identified by role or initial only; only the company and the panel are named.
