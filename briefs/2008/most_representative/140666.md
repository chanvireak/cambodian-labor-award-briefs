# Award No. 135/07 — Wilson Garment (Cambodia) Co,. Ltd (Arbitration Council, 14 January 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Kong Phallack (Chair), Ing Sothy, Huon Chundy · Doc ID 140666

## 1. Facts
The panel, chaired by Kong Phallack with Ing Sothy (employer nominee) and Huon Chundy (worker nominee), decided a collective dispute between Wilson Garment (Cambodia) Co,. Ltd and the Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU), whose factory branch claimed 400 members among 1,200 workers employed [Para 1] [Para 30]. The employer appeared through an administrator and company representative; the union through its officials, including the branch president, vice-president and secretary [Para 2] [Para 7]. The panel's authority came from Chapter XII Section 2B of the Labour Law (1997), Prakas 099 dated 21 April 2004 and its Procedural Rules, and Prakas 076 dated 10 May 2007 (Fifth Term) [Para 17]. A complaint of 21 October 2007 led to Department of Labour Disputes conciliation, at which four of eight issues were settled; non-conciliation report No. 1298 was submitted on 6 December 2007 [Para 19] [Para 20]. After a first hearing on 17 December 2007, both parties requested postponement to negotiate; conciliation failed again on 4 January 2008, and the worker party withdrew Issue 3 because the repairman concerned had left Cambodia, leaving Issues 1, 2 and 4 for decision [Para 22] [Para 24] [Para 25]. The panel considered company, union, ministry and Secretariat documents, with no witnesses or experts [Para 29].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must provide a US$10 attendance bonus per month rather than the US$5 bonus already paid under Notification 017 dated 18 July 2000? [Para 10] [Para 31]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must provide a 1,000 riel meal allowance for eight hours work rather than the 500 riel allowance paid since 1999? [Para 12]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must take disciplinary action against the sewing machine repairman who was violent toward the worker delegate, per the Internal Work Rules? [Para 13]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must increase the seniority bonus by US$1 per year for workers with more than five years of service? [Para 16]

## 3. Positions of the parties
The union argued the US$5 attendance bonus was insufficient because prices kept rising and the company had operated in Cambodia since 1999, and demanded US$10 per month [Para 31]. The employer answered that it had already implemented Notification 017 dated 18 July 2000, could not afford more, and had fulfilled its obligation [Para 31] [Para 38]. On meals, the union said workers spend 1,500 to 2,000 riels per meal — a bowl of rice costing 500 riels and food 1,000 riels — so 500 riel was inadequate [Para 32] [Para 47]. The company said it had paid 500 riel for eight hours work since 1999, exceeding the Labour Law, and would maintain that practice; the union provided no supporting evidence [Para 33] [Para 34]. On seniority, the union sought US$1 per year for workers with more than five years' service to motivate them, noting Notification 017 does not prohibit an increase; the employer said 700 workers had more than five years' service, the union said 400 of its members did, and the employer could not afford the increase [Para 35] [Para 36] [Para 37].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider all three remaining demands, ordering no payment: the US$10 monthly attendance bonus [Para 45], the 1,000 riel meal allowance for eight hours work [Para 51], and the US$1 per year seniority increase for workers with more than five years' service [Para 55]. The award is non-binding, becoming binding after 8 days from notification unless a party lodges written opposition to the Minister of Labour through the Secretariat [Para 55].

On Issue 1, Point 3 of Notification 745 dated 23 October 2006 preserves benefits under Notification 017 dated 18 July 2000, whose Point 3 requires at least US$5.00 attendance bonus; both parties acknowledged the employer paid US$5, so it had fulfilled its obligation [Para 38]. No labour regulation or agreement required an increase, so the demand had no legal basis and was an interests dispute [Para 39] [Para 40]. Clause 43 of Prakas 099 dated 21 April 2004 makes an award settling an interests dispute a collective bargaining agreement for one year, covering all workers, during which the right to strike cannot be exercised to amend it (Articles 96 and 321(2) of the Labour Law) [Para 40] [Para 42]. The panel considers only unions with most representative status — under Article 277 of the Labour Law and clause 6 of Prakas 305 dated 22 November 2001, more than 50% of workers, registration, and other conditions — may bring interests disputes [Para 43]. The union lacked most representative status and standing under clause 9(1) of Prakas 305, consistent with Awards 81/04 Evergreen, 09/05 Kin Tay, 84/07 Yung Wah and 108/07 8 Star Sportswear [Para 44].

On Issue 2, neither the labour regulations nor any agreement stipulates a 1,000 riel meal allowance, so the demand was an interests demand declined for lack of most representative status [Para 50] [Para 51]. On Issue 4, Notification 017 Clause 5 fixes US$5 per month after more than four years of service; no regulation requires a US$1 annual increase after five years, so the demand was likewise an interests demand declined for lack of most representative status [Para 52] [Para 55].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand exceeding the benefits fixed by law, Prakas or Notification — and not agreed by the parties — is an interests demand, and the panel will decline to consider it where the claiming union lacks most representative status and therefore standing to bargain factory-wide terms [Para 40] [Para 44]. Most representative status requires more than 50% of the workforce, registration at the Ministry of Labour, and satisfaction of the other conditions of Article 277 and Prakas 305 [Para 43]. This is an arbitral panel's reasoning, not binding precedent.

**Obiter dicta:** An award settling an interests dispute takes the place of a collective bargaining agreement for one year, during which the right to strike cannot be used to amend it [Para 41] [Para 42].
