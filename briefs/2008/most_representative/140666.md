# Award No. 135/07 — Wilson Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 14 January 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Huon Chundy, Kong Phallack) · Doc ID 140666

## 1. Facts
The Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU) at Wilson Garment Factory complained on 21 October 2007 over eight working conditions at Wilson Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd., which reports 1,200 workers while the union claims 400 members. [Para 19][Para 30]
Four issues were conciliated at the Department of Labour Disputes; the remaining four reached the panel, and Issue 3 was withdrawn because the repairman concerned had left Cambodia. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 25][Para 27]
The parties had no agreement on the three surviving demands — a US$ 10 attendance bonus, a 1,000 riel meal allowance, and a US$ 1 annual seniority increase. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 34]
The company pays a US$ 5 attendance bonus, a 500 riel meal allowance since 1999, and seniority under Notification 017 dated 18 July 2000. [Para 31][Para 32][Para 34]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay an attendance bonus of US$ 10 per month, rather than the US$ 5 it already pays under Notification 017? [Para 30][Para 39]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay a 1,000 riel meal allowance for eight hours' work, rather than the 500 riel it has paid since 1999? [Para 31][Para 50]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must increase the seniority bonus by US$ 1 per year for workers with more than five years' service? [Para 34][Para 52]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** KYFTU members argued that the US$ 5 attendance bonus is insufficient as prices rise, that a meal now costs 1,500 to 2,000 riels so a further 500 riel was needed, and that Notification 017 does not prohibit a US$ 1 seniority increase. [Para 30][Para 32][Para 34][Para 35]
**Employer (respondent):** Wilson Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. said it already implements Notification 017 dated 18 July 2000, cannot afford any of the three increases, and would keep the 500 riel meal allowance. [Para 31][Para 33][Para 34][Para 37]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider all three demands: the US$ 10 attendance bonus, the 1,000 riel meal allowance, and the US$ 1 annual seniority increase. [Para 55]
On Issue 1, Point 3 of Notification 017 dated 18 July 2000 sets an attendance bonus of at least US$ 5.00 per month and Point 3 of Notification 745 dated 23 October 2006 preserved it; since the company pays US$ 5, the panel found the obligation met. [Para 38]
Nothing in the labour regulations or any agreement required a higher attendance bonus, so the extra US$ 5 was an interests dispute. [Para 39][Para 40]
The panel explained that an award on an interests dispute replaces a collective bargaining agreement for one year under Clause 43 of Prakas 099 dated 21 April 2004, which is why it asks whether the union holds most representative status. [Para 41][Para 42]
Under Article 277 and Clause 6 of Prakas 305 dated 22 November 2001 that status needs more than 50 percent of the factory's workers plus registration, and KYFTU did not have it, so the panel followed Awards 81/04 (Evergreen, Issue 4), 09/05 (Kin Tay, Issue 2), 84/07 (Yung Wah, Issue 1) and 108/07 (8 Star Sportswear) and declined the demand. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45]
On Issue 2, it found neither regulation nor agreement set a 1,000 riel meal allowance, making the demand an interests demand, and declined it for the same lack of representative status. [Para 50][Para 51]
On Issue 4, Clause 5 of Notification 017 dated 18 July 2000 reaches US$ 5 for more than four years but provides no annual increment beyond that, so the US$ 1 demand was again an interests demand and was declined on the same ground. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 55]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for more than a Ministry notification guarantees — a higher attendance bonus, meal allowance or seniority increment — is an interests demand, and the panel will decline it where the union lacks most representative status, because granting it would substitute for a collective bargaining agreement for a year. [Para 40][Para 43][Para 51]
Most representative status requires more than 50 percent of the factory's workers and registration at the Ministry, not merely a large membership. [Para 43][Para 44]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted the company's length of operation since 1999 and its ability to pay only as the employer's assertions, taking neither as a ground of decision. [Para 31][Para 37]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
This is an unofficial English translation of the Khmer original with heavy OCR fusion ("the company", "meal allowance", "per year", "percent"), which I read through without silently correcting the quoted notifications. [Para 9][Para 38]
The second hearing is dated "4 January 2007" in the procedural heading but 4 January 2008 in the body, and the award itself is dated 14 January 2008; both readings are reported as printed. [Para 19][Para 24]
Notification 017 is dated 18 July 2000 throughout but "17 July 2000" once in the reasoning on Issue 1, and Report No. 1298 is dated 5 November 2007 although the conciliation it records took place on 13 November 2007. [Para 30][Para 45]
The operative part labels the seniority ruling "Issue 43" where the reasons discuss Issue 4, and Issue 3 was withdrawn by the union, so it is not briefed here. [Para 25][Para 55]
The repairman named in the withdrawn Issue 3 is an individual and appears here only as a role. [Para 26]
