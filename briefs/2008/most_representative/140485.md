# Award No. 14/08 — Quick Sew (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 19 February 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bunchhea chair; Chhiv Phyrum, employer nominee; Liv Sovanna, worker nominee) · Doc ID 140485

## 1. Facts
Quick Sew (Cambodia) Ltd, at Dei Thmei village, Sangkat Steung Mean Chey, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employs 900 workers and faces the Khmer Youth Trade Union (KYFTU) and its local union at the factory, which claims 100 members and does not hold most representative status. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 21] Of nine demands conciliated on 18 January 2008, four were settled and five referred; the union withdrew the second at the hearing, leaving issues 1, 3, 4 and 5 for decision. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 18] The demands were for one-year instead of three-month contracts, for five per cent severance at the end of every three-month contract, for more dining tables, for pregnant workers to leave 15 minutes early, and for two hours a week of meeting time for union representatives. [Para 6][Para 10][Para 11][Para 24] The parties had settled the annual-leave, table and early-leaving questions before, in agreements of 2005 and 2006 and in Award No. 106/06-Quick Sew. [Para 8][Para 18][Para 34]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1(A)) Whether the company must conclude one-year employment contracts with its workers instead of three-month fixed-duration contracts? [Para 23][Para 39]
2. (Issue 1(B)) Whether the company must pay five per cent severance at the end of each three-month contract even where it renews that contract? [Para 24][Para 44]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must provide more tables for workers to eat at? [Para 29][Para 48]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must allow pregnant workers to leave the factory 15 minutes before the end of the shift? [Para 34][Para 57]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the company must allow union representatives two hours a week for meetings during working time? [Para 36][Para 62]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the Khmer Youth Trade Union and the local union argued that workers should hold one-year contracts, that five per cent severance was due at the end of every three-month contract whether or not it was renewed, that the canteen lacked enough tables, that pregnant workers needed 15 minutes to leave safely, and that union representatives needed two paid hours a week to pursue members' demands. [Para 6][Para 24][Para 34][Para 36]
**Employer (respondent):** Quick Sew (Cambodia) Ltd, through its company advisor and chief of administration, replied that it issues three-month contracts by agreement and pays the five per cent on resignation or final termination, that it pays 18 days of annual leave in cash under a 2006 agreement, that the rented canteen has no space for more tables, that pregnant workers already leave seven minutes early under the 2006 minutes, and that union representatives must respect working hours like any worker. [Para 7][Para 8][Para 10][Para 11][Para 32][Para 38]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — every demand was rejected: one-year contracts, five per cent severance at each renewal, additional tables, 15 minutes' early leave for pregnant workers, and two paid hours a week for union representatives. [Para 67][Para 68]
On Issue 1(A) the panel applied res judicata: the same demand between the same parties had been decided as issue 4 of Award 106/06-Quick Sew, which held that contract duration is fixed by the free will of the parties and that no outsider may impose a different term on each worker, so the issue could not be reconsidered and the demand was rejected. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 42][Para 43]
On Issue 1(B) the panel read Article 73, paragraph 6, which requires compensation at the expiration of a contract of at least five per cent of wages, together with Award 60/06-New Max Garment, issue 6: severance is owed when the employment relationship is severed, and a renewal continues that relationship rather than ending it, so no severance falls due at each renewal. [Para 44][Para 47][Para 48] Since the union itself acknowledged the company pays the five per cent on termination or resignation, the panel found Article 73 complied with and rejected the demand. [Para 25][Para 48]
On Issue 3 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law requiring an employer to supply tables, no agreement to that effect, and no figures from the union on how many tables or workers were involved; it treated the demand as an interests dispute and, because the local union holds only about 100 of 900 workers and is neither representative under Article 277 and Prakas 305 nor entitled to conclude a collective bargaining agreement, declined to grant it. [Para 48][Para 50][Para 52][Para 53][Para 54]
On Issue 4 the panel found no statutory right to leave 15 minutes early, and held that the agreement of 19 July 2006 allowing seven minutes remained in force and had been performed; no pregnant worker had suffered an accident or difficulty leaving. [Para 57][Para 59][Para 60] On Issue 5 it held that Clause 6 of Prakas No. 286 of 05 November 2000 grants two paid hours a week to worker delegates, not to unions, so the demand exceeded the law and, being an interests demand, was rejected for the same lack of representative status. [Para 63][Para 65][Para 66][Para 67]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Severance under Article 73, paragraph 6, of the Labour Law falls due only when a fixed-duration contract ends and with it the employment relationship: where the employer renews the contract, the relationship continues, no severance is payable at that point, and the same demand cannot be relitigated once a prior award between the same parties has decided it. [Para 40][Para 47][Para 48]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel said it would have decided Issue 4 differently had the union shown that the seven-minute arrangement caused specific safety problems or accidents for pregnant workers. [Para 61]
