# Award No. 37/08-Yung Wah I & II — Yung Wah Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 4 April 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, employer-chosen; Tuon Siphann, worker-chosen; Pen Bunchhea, chair) · Doc ID 140498

## 1. Facts
Yung Wah Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd., I & II, at Prek Samrong Village, Ta Khmao Commune, Kandal Province, employs approximately 3,200 workers in Yung Wah 1 and 5,300 in Yung Wah 2, about 8,500 in all, with three unions at the first factory and four at the second [Para 27][Para 28]. The complainant is the Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU), with 1,200 members at Yung Wah 1 and 700 at Yung Wah 2 [Para 30][Para 31]. Of 21 demands conciliated on 16 January 2008, twelve were settled and nine reached the Council; at the hearing issue 7 was conciliated and issues 5 and 8 were withdrawn, leaving issues 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 9 [Para 19][Para 21][Para 22]. The disputed acts are the attendance bonus, the overtime meal allowance, milk formula and babysitting money in lieu of a daycare center, the length of maternity leave and the timing of maternity pay, and the length of the lunch break; an agreement of 11 January 2008 closing case 146/07 was among the exhibits [Para 25][Para 63][Para 81].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the workers may obtain a US$ 20 monthly attendance bonus in place of the US$ 5 set by Notification 017 SKBY? [Para 53]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the workers may obtain a 2,000 riel meal allowance in place of the 1,500 riel the company pays? [Para 58]
3. (Issues 3 and 9) Whether the employer must provide six cans of milk formula monthly for 24 months and US$ 40 monthly for babysitting, or must instead build a daycare center or pay the actual cost of outside care? [Para 63][Para 67]
4. (Issue 4) Whether women workers are entitled to 100 days' maternity leave, and to three months' maternity pay on the day they receive their last wage before leave? [Para 68][Para 75]
5. (Issue 6) Whether the workers are entitled to a one-hour lunch break under Clause 4 of the company's registered Internal Work Rules? [Para 81]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union, KYFTU, and the local Khmer Youth Trade Union, KYTU, at Yung Wah Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd., I & II):** they concede the Labour Law does not provide these benefits but demand them because the market price of goods, food and rice has risen; two cans of milk formula do not feed a baby; 90 days leaves too little post-natal rest; and maternity pay should be handed over with the last wage so the woman need not return to the factory [Para 34][Para 36][Para 41][Para 49][Para 47].
**Employer (Yung Wah Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd., I & II):** the law does not require a US$ 20 attendance bonus, and the company already pays US$ 5 plus 400 riel a day in practice and 1,500 riel for overtime meals; rising commodity prices affect the company as well; milk formula replaced a daycare center because a 2002 survey showed most workers preferred it; and paying maternity money early contradicts its accounting system [Para 35][Para 32][Para 37][Para 38][Para 48].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel declined to consider the attendance bonus (Issue 1), the meal allowance (Issue 2) and 100 days' maternity leave (Issue 4a); it ordered the employer to build a daycare center or pay outside babysitting against actual receipts while rejecting six cans of milk formula and US$ 40 (Issues 3 and 9), ordered the three months' maternity payment at least one day before leave, and ordered a one-hour lunch break, the last two from the date the award enters into effect [Para 85][Para 86][Para 87][Para 88].
On the bonus and meal allowance, the panel held the demands to be interests disputes, not rights disputes, because they claim more than the minimum set by Notification 017 SKBY of 18 July 2000 and Notification 745 KKBV of 23 October 2006 [Para 54][Para 59][Para 61]. Following Awards 81/04-Evergreen, 09/05-Kin Tai, 84/07-Yung Wah II, 08/07-8 Stars Sportswear and 135/07-Wilson, it held that only a union with most representative status may bring an interests dispute, since an award on one would become a collective bargaining agreement binding all workers under Clause 43 of Prakas 099 and would strip other workers of their right to strike [Para 54][Para 57]. Article 277 requires registration and further conditions for that status, and the local KYTU at the two factories did not meet them, so the panel declined to consider both demands [Para 55][Para 56][Para 62]. On milk formula, Articles 186 and 187 oblige an employer of 100 or more women to provide a nursing room and daycare, but do not require formula or payment in lieu; following Awards 63/04-Shine Well and 68/04-City New, and the policy favouring breastfeeding applied in 83/04-June Textile and 24/06-Fortune, the panel rejected the formula and the flat US$ 40 but ordered a daycare center or reimbursement on receipts [Para 66][Para 67][Para 68]. On maternity leave, Article 182 fixes 90 days as a minimum, so 100 days is an interests demand that the same lack of representative status barred [Para 69][Para 70][Para 72]. On the timing of payment, Article 115 paragraph 3 requires payment before a day off, and the panel ordered the three months' pay at least one day before leave, following Awards 57/06-Evergreen and 09/07-Kingland [Para 76][Para 79][Para 80][Para 81]. On the break, Clause 4 of the Internal Work Rules allows one hour, the company allowed 45 minutes, so the panel ordered an hour [Para 84][Para 85].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a benefit above the statutory minimum is an interests dispute, and the Council will decline to consider it unless the claimant union holds most representative status under Article 277, because an award in an interests dispute becomes a collective bargaining agreement binding every worker in the enterprise [Para 54][Para 57].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that government policy encourages breastfeeding over formula, and that the employer may not call a woman back during the 90 days even in a busy period without her agreement; neither observation was necessary to the orders [Para 66][Para 70].
