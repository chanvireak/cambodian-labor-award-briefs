# Award No. 73/08 — Yong Va No. 1 (ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉ុង វ៉ា ទី ១) and the Khmer Youth Union Federation at the Yong Va 1 factory (Arbitration Council, 20 June 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Tuon Si Phan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, presiding) · Doc ID 140523

## 1. Facts
Yong Va No. 1 (ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉ុង វ៉ា ទី ១), a Kandal factory at Prek Samrong in Ta Khmao employing about 3,000 workers, faced 16 collective demands brought by the Khmer Youth Union Federation at the Yong Va 1 factory (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ ប្រចាំ រោងចក្រ យ៉ុង វ៉ា ទី ១), which claims more than 1,101 members there [Para 1][Para 34][Para 38]. Three unions operate at the factory — C.CAWDU, an independent workers' union and the Khmer Youth Union — and none is the most representative [Para 35][Para 36]. Conciliation on 8 May 2008 settled 11 of the 16 demands, leaving five [Para 17][Para 18]. At the hearing of 5 June 2008 the union withdrew issue 2, so the panel decided issues 1, 3, 4 and 5: piece rates, equal base wages for Khmer group leaders, an extra USD 1 of seniority bonus after five years, and itemised payslips for second-category staff [Para 21][Para 22].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer's piece rates (ថ្លៃបុង) must be restored to the levels it applied in earlier years for the same garments and operations? [Para 39][Para 74]
2. (Issue 3 and Issue 4) Whether the panel may consider demands to equalise the base wage of all Khmer group leaders and to add USD 1 of seniority bonus each year after five years' service, given that these are interests claims brought by a union that is not the most representative? [Para 54][Para 94][Para 98]
3. (Issue 5) Whether the employer must issue itemised payslips to second-category staff — office, translation, mechanics, finishing, hygiene, security and packing — as it already does for ordinary production workers? [Para 122][Para 130]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** before setting a piece rate the company used to let a team of 10 to 15 workers trial-sew for five or six days; piece rates fell sharply from 2006, from USD 0.5–0.6 per lot in sewing to USD 0.2, and ironing rates from USD 0.04–0.06 a garment down to USD 0.01–0.03 [Para 42][Para 48][Para 49]. Khmer group leaders should all receive the same base wage whatever their seniority or skill [Para 54], and itemised payslips would let second-category staff see their bonus, overtime and days worked as production workers already can [Para 71][Para 122].

**Employer (respondent):** piece rates are set by reference to the style and the order after discussion with the buyer and production, and are distributed to each team before sewing starts [Para 39][Para 40]; the company tops up any worker who cannot earn the USD 1.92 daily base wage over eight hours [Para 45]. Group leaders' base pay follows each one's own skill and capacity [Para 55][Para 88]. Itemised slips are being prepared by a consultant from Singapore and will take about six months plus testing [Para 72][Para 73].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 1 was dismissed, issues 3 and 4 were not considered, and on issue 5 the employer was ordered to issue second-category staff the same itemised payslips as first-category production workers within one month of the award becoming enforceable [Para 134][Para 135][Para 136][Para 137].

On issue 1 the panel set out Article 108 of the Labour Law, which requires piecework pay to be calculated so that a worker of average skill working normal hours earns at least the guaranteed minimum wage, and Article 137 capping full working time at eight hours a day and 48 a week, together with point 1 of Notification No. 745 of 23 October 2006 setting the garment minimum at USD 45 during probation and USD 50 thereafter [Para 74][Para 75][Para 76][Para 78]. It then held that to decide the claim it needed concrete evidence: whether the trial process really guaranteed USD 1.92 a day to a worker of average skill, what kind of workers took part in the trial, which styles had cut rates, which teams and sections and how many workers were affected, and how many could not reach the base wage [Para 80][Para 51]. Neither side supplied it; the union missed the filing date and the employer's material was in a foreign language and so inadmissible [Para 53][Para 84]. The claim was therefore refused, consistently with Awards 94/07, 74/07 and 41/07 [Para 82][Para 86].

On issues 3 and 4 the panel held that Article 106 requires equal pay only where the work, conditions, professional skill and output are equal, so a demand to flatten group leaders' pay regardless of skill asks for more than the law gives and is an interests dispute [Para 89][Para 91][Para 93]. For interests disputes the panel asks whether the union is most representative, because that status confers the legal capacity to conclude a collective agreement under Article 96(k) and Prakas No. 305, Article 9(k), and the right to bring such a dispute [Para 94]. Article 277 and Prakas 305, Article 6, require at least half of all workers as members and registration with the Ministry [Para 95]. The Khmer Youth Union has about 1,101 members of 3,000 workers and is not most representative, so it cannot conclude a collective agreement for the whole workforce, and the panel declined to consider the two demands, as it had in Awards 81/04, 09/05, 84/07, 108/07, 135/07 and 14/08 [Para 96][Para 97][Para 98][Para 99][Para 105][Para 121]. It also noted that Prakas 099, Article 43, makes an award in an interests dispute stand in place of a collective agreement for one year, which reinforces the need for representativeness [Para 101][Para 102].

On issue 5 the panel applied Article 112(b) of the Labour Law, which obliges the employer to inform workers clearly of all wage components whenever pay changes [Para 123][Para 124]. It reasoned that absences, attendance bonuses, days worked and overtime all make pay vary, so an itemised slip is the ordinary means of meeting that duty and of removing suspicion and recurring protest [Para 126][Para 128]. Because category-2 staff were given only a lump sum, the practice was inconsistent with Article 112, and the consultant's pending system did not excuse compliance in the meantime [Para 130][Para 132].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that exceeds what the Labour Law guarantees — such as equalising pay across workers of different skill — is an interests dispute, and the panel will not consider it when the bringing union holds less than half the workforce and so lacks the most-representative status that Article 96 confers; separately, an employer must give every worker an itemised payslip showing each wage component, and a pending payroll system is no answer to that duty [Para 91][Para 99][Para 128].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that an award in an interests dispute substitutes for a collective agreement for one year under Prakas 099, Article 43, supported but did not decide the representativeness ruling [Para 101][Para 102].
