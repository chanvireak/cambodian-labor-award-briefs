# Award Nos. 55/08 and 57/08 — Sino Mek International (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 20 May 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kol Vaddhana for the employer party, An Non for the worker party, Kong Phallak chair) · Doc ID 140512

## 1. Facts
Sino Mek International (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ីណូ ម៉េក អ៉ិនធើណេស៊ិនណល (ខេមបូឌា) ហ្គាមេន ខូអិលធីឌី), a garment factory at Svay Rolum, Sa'ang, Kandal province with about 700 to 720 workers, faced two collective disputes brought by the Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers Democratic Union (C.CAWDU, សម្ព័ន្ឋ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា). [Para 1][Para 62][Para 63] The union has 550 members but only 230 paying dues, no union at the factory holds most-representative status, and 470 workers supported the claim. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 66] Of 15 conciliation points across the two files, nine were settled; two points of case 57/08 were withdrawn by the workers and one was settled at the hearing, leaving four issues for the panel, which heard the case on 24 April 2008. [Para 19][Para 23][Para 24][Para 25][Para 18] They concerned the non-renewal of a union deputy president's contract, the dismissal of a probation worker, maternity wages and infant milk powder. [Para 67][Para 85][Para 98][Para 133]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Case 55/08, Issue 1) Whether the employer must continue Mr N's employment contract and take him back as deputy president of the C.CAWDU union at the factory? [Para 67][Para 4]
2. (Case 55/08, Issue 2) Whether the employer must preserve Mr N's wage, position and other benefits as at 22 April 2008? [Para 6][Para 25]
3. (Case 57/08, Issue 1) Whether the employer must take back the probation worker Mr K, whom it dismissed without notice before his probation contract ended? [Para 85][Para 87]
4. (Case 57/08, Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay women 50 per cent of their 12-month average wage for the three months before maternity leave, and supply two cans of infant milk powder a month? [Para 98][Para 133]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — C.CAWDU at Sino Mek):** the union asked that Mr N, elected deputy president on 18 January 2008, be retained with his position and benefits because he had taken up workers' problems since his election; it asked that Mr K, a probationary cutter dismissed on 18 February 2008, be reinstated, arguing the company still had work and that he was dismissed for attending union meetings and recruiting members; and it asked for maternity pay computed on the 12-month average and paid before leave, plus milk powder, because mothers face hospital and care costs. [Para 74][Para 84][Para 93][Para 99][Para 100][Para 103]
**Employer (respondent — Sino Mek International (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd):** the company said non-renewal was its prerogative because the last fixed duration contract expired on 12 April 2008, and that it had already sought the labour inspector's ruling, which was still pending; Mr K was a probation worker who worked too slowly and produced less than others, so it could end probation, and it did not know he was a union member; maternity pay was 50 per cent of base wage and the law was unclear on advance payment. [Para 75][Para 77][Para 80][Para 89][Para 90][Para 101][Para 145]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — on case 55/08 the panel refused to consider both demands as premature; on case 57/08 it refused reinstatement of Mr K but ordered the employer to pay him damages equal to the wages he was entitled to up to the end of his contract plus 5 per cent severance, ordered maternity pay at 50 per cent of the 12-month average to be paid before leave for three months, and dismissed the milk-powder demand. [Para 171][Para 172][Para 173][Para 174][Para 175][Para 176]

On case 55/08 the panel found the company had not yet terminated Mr N — it had suspended him pending the labour inspector's decision, and had let him work normally after the interim order stopping the strike. As the dismissal had not occurred and the matter lay within the competence of the Kandal labour department, the dispute was not yet arisen, and the panel consistently refuses to decide future or contingent rights disputes. [Para 104][Para 105][Para 107][Para 108][Para 109][Para 110]

On case 57/08 Issue 1 the panel applied Article 82 (no prior notice is required to end a probation contract), Article 67 (a contract with a definite term must state its end date), and Article 73 (early termination of a fixed duration contract entitles the worker to damages equal to wages to the end of the contract and at least 5 per cent severance). It found the probation contract's clause B allowed 24-hour notice, which is more favourable than the law, that the employer breached the clause by giving none, that the clause itself creates no claim, and that the employer had no adequate reason for its claim of low output; reinstatement was therefore refused but compensation was due. [Para 114][Para 115][Para 117][Para 123][Para 131][Para 132][Para 174] The panel also found no evidence of union discrimination because the employer had not been given the union's membership register, which the union failed to produce when ordered. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 126][Para 127]

On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 182 (90 days' maternity leave), Article 183 paragraph 1 (half wage including allowances during that leave) and Article 115(3) (wages must not be paid on a rest day but one day earlier). It held that paying 50 per cent of base wage alone breached Article 183, that the 12-month average must be divided by 12 and then by two, and that the sum must be paid before the leave begins. [Para 133][Para 136][Para 138][Para 139][Para 146][Para 149][Para 175] On the milk powder the panel found no law or regulation required it, that the demand exceeded the employer's legal duty, and that government policy favours breastfeeding over tinned milk. [Para 151][Para 153][Para 156][Para 157][Para 176]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A probation contract is a fixed duration contract and must state its end date; where an employer ends it early without the notice the contract itself requires, the worker cannot demand reinstatement but is entitled under Article 73 to damages equal to the wages he would have earned to the end of the contract and to at least 5 per cent severance. Separately, maternity pay under Article 183 must be half of the average wage over the 12 months before leave, including allowances, and must be paid before the leave begins rather than on a rest day. [Para 115][Para 119][Para 124][Para 139][Para 146][Para 174][Para 175]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's discussion of most-representative status — that a union without it cannot conclude a collective agreement for the whole workforce, and that a panel award on an interest dispute itself operates as a collective agreement for one year under Prakara 43 of Prakas No. 099 — went beyond the four issues it decided. [Para 158][Para 160][Para 163][Para 165][Para 167]

- The hearing invitation is dated 24 April 2007 in one paragraph and the hearing itself 24 April 2008 [Para 21][Para 18].
- The non-conciliation reports are dated 21 and 22 April 2008 but said to have reached the Secretariat on 21 and 23 April 2008 [Para 16][Para 20].
- Mr N's fourth contract is 12 January to 12 April 2008 in the reasoning but 12 January to 12 October 2008 in the evidence list [Para 73][Para 40][Para 52].
- The employer's name appears as ស៊ីណូ ម៉េក, ស៊ីណូ ម៉ាក, ស៊ឺណូ មេក and ស៊ីណូ មេ ក [Para 1][Para 2][Para 55][Para 62]; the union appears as C.CAWDU and CCAWDUR [Para 4][Para 63].

- Latin OCR debris interrupts the Khmer text at several points ("stager, and she", "Najorkange , and", "Like") [Para 70][Para 106][Para 170].
