# Award No. 53/08 — Yong Va No. 1 (យ៉ុង វាំ ទី១) (Arbitration Council, 30 May 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ri for the employer party, Tuon Si Phan for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 140511

## 1. Facts
Yong Va No. 1 (ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉ុង វាំ ទី ១), a garment factory at Prek Samrong, Ta Khmao, Kandal province employing more than 3,100 workers, faced a collective dispute brought by the Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union at the factory, which the panel recorded does not hold most-representative status. [Para 1][Para 34][Para 35][Para 36] Of seven demands raised in conciliation on 5 April 2008, three were settled and four were referred to the Council, which heard them on 9 May 2008. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 13] On 31 March 2008 the company issued internal notice No. 548/2008 suspending work for five days, 1 to 5 April 2008, at 50 per cent wages, for workers in Groups B, D and F. [Para 37][Para 40] A fifth demand — seniority pay should the factory later move — was raised but the panel declined to decide it as premature. [Para 58][Para 110]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer's suspension of work for five days at 50 per cent wages, agreed by thumbprint, was a lawful application of the Labour Law where the company had no work for the workers? [Para 62][Para 37]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must raise the wages of the five workers in charge of the cutting section (មេកន្ត្រៃយ៍) because a Chinese group leader had promised them a rise? [Para 78][Para 45]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must take back into work the female worker it dismissed after a fight with a co-worker inside the factory during working hours? [Para 97][Para 52]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must pay seniority compensation to workers whose shift was changed from night to day? [Para 115][Para 109]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — Yong Va No. 1 branch of the Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union, សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ ប្រចាំ រោងចក្រ យ៉ុង វាំ ទី១):** the workers demanded 100 per cent wages whenever the company had no work for them, a wage rise for the five section heads — five men paid USD 60 a month while a woman section head received only USD 50 — on the strength of the Chinese group leader's oral promise, and the reinstatement of the dismissed worker; they also asked for seniority pay on any future transfer to a new factory or change of shift. [Para 37][Para 46][Para 47][Para 52][Para 58][Para 60]
**Employer (respondent — Yong Va No. 1):** the company said the five-day suspension followed a signed list and that workers who signed received 50 per cent while those who came to work received 100 per cent, that it could not pay full wages while there was no work, that a group leader may only propose a rise and cannot decide one, that the dismissed worker was guilty of serious misconduct under Article 83, and that a shift change is not a termination of contract. [Para 40][Para 44][Para 80][Para 97][Para 61]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — on Issue 1 the panel ordered the company to pay an additional 50 per cent, topping up to 100 per cent, to workers in Groups B, D and F who had no work from 1 to 5 April 2008; Issues 2, 3 and 4 were dismissed. [Para 140][Para 141][Para 142][Para 143][Para 144]

On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 71 point 6 and Article 71 point 11 of the Labour Law: a contract may be suspended for the absence of work accepted by the employer, and suspension for serious economic or material difficulty may not exceed two months and must be placed under the inspection of the labour inspector. It found the five-day period itself consistent with Article 71(11) but the suspension was never notified to the labour inspector, so it did not fully comply with Article 71; following earlier awards where an employer suspended contracts without notifying the inspectorate the panel had ordered full wages, it held that even a worker's thumbprint cannot validate a suspension contrary to the Law. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70][Para 72][Para 73][Para 75]

On Issue 2 the panel held that fixing wages is an employer prerogative under Article 2 of the Labour Law and clause 5 of the internal rules of 29 May 2006, which ties pay to output, discipline and annual appraisal within the company's capacity; it found no evidence that the section heads had ever been appraised annually, that the group leader's promise was ever put to the company, or that the company decided on it, and dismissed the demand, while observing that the company should adopt a clear appraisal policy. [Para 81][Para 84][Para 85][Para 86][Para 94][Para 96]

On Issue 3 the panel found that the two workers had argued and fought inside the factory during working hours; although clause 10 of the internal rules does not list fighting as serious misconduct, Article 83 of the Labour Law treats fighting between workers as serious misconduct, so the dismissal was lawful and reinstatement was refused. The worker had already accepted USD 101.39 in termination pay. [Para 98][Para 99][Para 106][Para 107][Para 55]

On Issue 4 the panel held that moving a worker from night to day shift exercises the employer's management right under Article 2 and does not terminate the contract, so no seniority or severance was owed; it noted the future-relocation limb of the same demand was a dispute not yet arisen, which the Council cannot decide. [Para 135][Para 137][Para 138][Para 111][Para 114]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that suspends employment contracts for lack of work must place the suspension under the inspection of the labour inspector under Article 71(11) of the Labour Law; where it fails to do so the suspension is unlawful and the employer must pay full wages for the suspended days, and a worker's thumbprint on the employer's list cannot cure the defect because the parties cannot agree to terms contrary to the Law. Separately, a group leader's oral promise of a wage rise does not bind the employer, wage setting remaining the employer's prerogative. [Para 70][Para 72][Para 75][Para 94]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the company should establish a clear written policy for annual appraisal and wage increases, and that a future relocation claim is a dispute not yet arisen, were guidance rather than determinations on the four demands. [Para 86][Para 111]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The employer is printed as យ៉ុង វាំ ទី១, យ៉ុង វ៉ា ទី១ and យ៉ុង រាំ ទី១ in different paragraphs [Para 1][Para 34][Para 95]; all are treated as the same company.
- The operative paragraph orders a top-up "to make 10 per cent" [Para 77] while the decision paragraph orders "100 per cent" [Para 140]; the reasons at [Para 71]-[Para 73] require full wages.
- The internal rules are dated 29 May 2006 in the reasoning [Para 82] but 19 February 2002 in the evidence list [Para 21].
- Group F appears as "IF" in the facts [Para 76] and as "F" in the decision [Para 140].
- The dismissal file is confused: the termination notice, warning and fighting record in the evidence list name a worker whose name does not match the claimant in Issue 3, and badge No. 5170 is attached to different individuals in different paragraphs [Para 24][Para 25][Para 53]; this brief therefore does not reproduce the individuals' names.
- Individual workers, the Chinese group leader and the employer's representatives are identified by role only.
