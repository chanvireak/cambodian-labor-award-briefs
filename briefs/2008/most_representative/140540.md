# Award No. 101/08 — GDM Cambodia Branch (Arbitration Council, 16 September 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kol Vathana, Ann Vireak, Kong Phallack) · Doc ID 140540

## 1. Facts
GDM Cambodia Branch, on National Road No. 5, Sangkat Ruessei Keo, Khan Ruessei Keo, Phnom Penh, employed approximately 700 workers in two unions, the Khmer Youth Trade Union and the Cambodian Workers Labour Union (CWLU) [Para 1][Para 30]. The claimant is the CWLFU local union at the factory, which had about 400 members but had not obtained most representative status [Para 3][Para 30]. Its complaint of 1 August 2008 raised twelve demands; seven were conciliated before the hearing and Issue 4 was settled at the hearing, leaving Issues 1, 2, 3 and 5 [Para 18][Para 19][Para 21]. The dispute arose from a fight on 23 June 2008 between GDM workers and Garuda Security Company guards at the factory gate, in which seven workers were injured [Para 10][Para 31].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issues 1 & 2) Whether the company must compensate workers injured in the 23 June 2008 incident, and whether it must stop using Garuda Security Company guards? [Para 20][Para 57]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay triple wages for work on Sundays and public holidays? [Para 52][Para 79]
3. (Issue 5) Whether the company must pay US$ 20 a month as a milk allowance to women workers who have given birth? [Para 53][Para 99]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The CWLFU demanded damages for workers beaten by Garuda guards on 23 June 2008, and removal of Garuda from the factory because the workers were frightened by the incident [Para 10][Para 11]; it asked 300% for Sunday work and 400% in total for public holidays to motivate volunteers [Para 52]; and US$ 20 a month for one year for milk powder, saying the mothers could not afford it [Para 53].
**Employer (respondent):** GDM said it had already paid the treatment costs required by the labour law on work-related accidents and considered itself a victim of the incident too [Para 51]; it wanted to give Garuda time to settle with the affected workers and said it was bound by a one-year service contract, though it could stop using individual guards [Para 11][Para 76]; it would follow the law on holiday pay [Para 52]; and it could not afford the milk allowance, having already agreed US$ 7 a month for childcare in lieu of a daycare centre in the agreement of 4 December 2006 [Para 54][Para 55].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demands for compensation, for removal of Garuda and for triple holiday pay and the US$ 20 milk allowance were all declined, but the employer was ordered to take measures to ensure the safety and security of workers at the company [Para 115][Para 116][Para 117].
On Issues 1 and 2 the panel split the compensation claim. For the two workers who testified it applied Article 248 of the Labour Law, under which an accident is work-related if it happens at work or during the direct commute [Para 60][Para 61]. Both had finished work at 4 p.m. and were sitting in front of the factory waiting for friends, so the beating was neither during working hours nor on a commute, and the panel held it lacked jurisdiction over their claim under the labour law [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63]. For the four remaining injured workers it found the cause of the fight unproven, the parties having offered no evidence of how or why it began, so there was no basis to characterise the accident as work-related, and it rejected their claim [Para 66][Para 69][Para 70][Para 71].
On the demand to remove Garuda, the panel applied Article 22 of Decree 38 on contract and other liabilities: only the contracting parties may amend or terminate a contract, and the Council will not normally order parties to end contracts with third parties [Para 72][Para 74][Para 75]. The workers had not identified which guard caused the fear, so the demand failed on the evidence [Para 77]. The panel nevertheless noted that the employer itself conceded the incident put workers' safety at risk and intended to change the guards concerned, and on that basis ordered affirmative measures to organise and provide safety and security at the factory [Para 78][Para 79].
On Issue 3 the panel read Article 139 (new) with Article 164 of the Labour Law and clause 4 of Prakas No. 10 SKBY of 4 February 1999, and concluded that workers are entitled to a total augmentation of 200% for work on public or weekly paid holidays — the rate the company already paid [Para 79][Para 82][Para 83]. Because the demand exceeded the law it became an interests dispute, which the panel will only hear from a union with most representative status [Para 84][Para 85]. CWLU's 400 members out of about 600 workers did not give it that status, and it had not registered for it [Para 86][Para 87][Para 88].
On Issue 5 the panel applied Articles 184 and 186 of the Labour Law on breastfeeding hours, nursing rooms and daycare centres, and found the union's demand also went beyond the law and was therefore an interests dispute [Para 100][Para 105][Para 110][Para 111]. The US$ 7 childcare payment under the 4 December 2006 agreement was undisputed, but the panel declined to consider it because the demand itself was outside its jurisdiction [Para 107][Para 109]. Having no most representative union before it, and with the two factory unions not having joined their claims, the panel declined to consider both interests demands [Para 97][Para 98][Para 114]. The award is non-binding and binds after eight days unless opposed [Para 117].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Two rules decide future cases. First, an injury inflicted on workers who have clocked out and are waiting outside the gate is not a work-related accident within Article 248, so the panel has no jurisdiction over a compensation claim founded on it [Para 61][Para 63]. Second, a demand that exceeds a statutory entitlement — holiday pay beyond the 200% augmentation the law fixes, or a milk allowance the Labour Law does not provide — is an interests dispute, and the panel will decline it unless the claimant union holds most representative status or the factory unions have joined their claims [Para 82][Para 84][Para 97].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observations that contracts may only be amended or ended by their parties, and that the Council will not ordinarily order parties to terminate third-party contracts except where a worker endangers others' health and safety [Para 74][Para 75], are general statements wider than the orders required.

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Headcount is printed two ways: approximately 700 workers in the findings and about 600 where the panel measures the union's membership [Para 30][Para 86]. The non-conciliation report is dated 5 August 2008 and the report of the same number is described as sent to the Secretariat on the same day, while the sentence introducing it is duplicated verbatim in the OCR [Para 17]. The translation is marked unofficial and the OCR drops the letter "e" throughout ("themloyer", "Copany", "thissue"), and in places wrecks quoted statutory text so that Article 248 and Article 164 are reproduced twice or cut off mid-sentence [Para 60][Para 80]. The employer appears as "GDM Cambodia Branch", "GDM Company" and "GDM company" without explanation. Individual injured workers are named in the award; they are given as Mr S and Mr D here. The decision heading says "Issue 1&2" and treats the two merged issues as one, while §2 above keeps the panel's own pair numbering [Para 115].
