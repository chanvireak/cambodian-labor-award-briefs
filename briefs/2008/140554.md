# Order No. 119/08 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហុង វ៉ា ថ្មី ខេមបូឌា ហ្គាមិន មែន ញូហ្វាក់ធី រឹង អ៊ែល ធី ឌី / Hong Wah Thmey Cambodia Garment Manufacturing Ltd (Arbitration Council, 15 October 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (ហែម ហូរ ណារិទ្ធ for the employer party, វ៉ែន ពៅ for the worker party, ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា chair) · Doc ID 140554

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហុង វ៉ា ថ្មី ខេមបូឌា ហ្គាមិន មែន ញូហ្វាក់ធី រឹង អ៊ែល ធី ឌី (Hong Wah Thmey Cambodia Garment Manufacturing Ltd) is a garment factory at Trapeang Rumchek village, Sangkat Chom Chao, Khan Dangkao, Phnom Penh, employing 992 workers; the only union there, the factory branch of សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត, has about 500 members and registration No. 1473 of 16 July 2008. [Para 1][Para 34][Para 35][Para 36][Para 37] The federation filed seven demands on 18 August 2008 to improve working conditions; four remained non-conciliated after 04 September 2008, and the parties settled issue 1 (paying wages on the 7th as the old factory had done) at the hearing. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 20][Para 21] Issues 2, 3 and 4 remained for decision. [Para 21]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must raise the piece rate for Poung No. 01 from 0,040 to 0,070 USD a lot and for Poung No. 17 from 0,045 to 0,080 USD a lot? [Para 38][Para 69]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must continue paying the seniority bonus and the attendance allowance to women who take authorised maternity-related leave beyond 90 days? [Para 54][Para 98][Para 100]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must keep wages for sick leave certified by a private hospital as well as by a State hospital? [Para 62][Para 124][Para 126]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), members of សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត at the ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហុង វ៉ា ថ្មី ខេមបូឌា ហ្គាមិន មែន ញូហ្វាក់ធី រឹង អ៊ែល ធី ឌី factory:** most workers sewing Poung 01 and 17 earn below the minimum wage, so the piece rates must rise; women who add a month to their 90 days of maternity leave are still weak and should keep their seniority bonus and attendance allowance; and a sick worker far from home cannot always reach a State hospital, so any properly licensed clinic's certificate should be accepted. [Para 38][Para 56][Para 63][Para 67][Para 88]
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហុង វ៉ា ថ្មី ខេមបូឌា ហ្គាមិន មែន ញូហ្វាក់ធី រឹង អ៊ែល ធី ឌី:** Poung 01 and 17 are easy to sew and need no skill, most workers earn above the base wage and the company tops up anyone below it, so the rates can rise only to 0,045 and 0,060 USD; it granted the extra month of leave but owes no bonus for a month not worked; and because workers have forged certificates from non-State clinics it recognises State certificates only. [Para 39][Para 42][Para 57][Para 58][Para 64]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay the full 6 USD attendance allowance to the women who took the extra month's leave and to recognise medical certificates from provincial, municipal, district referral hospitals and commune or khan health centres; the piece-rate increase and the seniority bonus claims were dismissed. [Para 149][Para 151][Para 147][Para 148]

On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 108 of the Labour Law, which requires piecework to be priced so that a worker of average skill working normal hours earns at least the minimum wage, and Article 137 on the 8-hour day, together with points 1 and 2 of Notification No. 745 of 23 October 2006 fixing 45 USD for probationary and 50 USD for full-status workers. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 76][Para 84] Following case No. 03/05-Fou Hing, the panel accepted that the employer may set and change piece rates but must set them so that an averagely skilled worker reaches the minimum wage. [Para 81][Para 82] On the evidence, the workers produced only four payslips that did not state what each worker actually received, and failed to file the list of under-earning workers the panel ordered by 26 September 2008, so the panel could not tell how many workers were affected and dismissed the claim, noting that clearer evidence from ten workers would have allowed a different result. [Para 49][Para 52][Para 93][Para 94][Para 96][Para 97] On Issue 3 the panel split the claim: the seniority bonus is part of wages under Article 103, but where the contract is suspended for authorised absence under Article 71 (new), Article 72 (new) does not require it, so nothing is owed for the extra month not worked. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 105][Para 108][Para 110][Para 112] The attendance allowance, however, is governed by point 1 of Notification No. 032/08 of 17 April 2008 and the minutes of 05 May 2008, which grant 6 USD "without any condition"; the panel read that phrase as covering unpaid authorised leave, so the allowance is payable in full. [Para 113][Para 115][Para 119][Para 121][Para 122][Para 123] On Issue 4 the panel held that Article 71 (new) clause 3 requires only a certificate from a qualified doctor, not a State doctor, and followed case No. 81/05-Supreme Garment and case No. 47/07-Chung Fai in finding that "State hospital" covers provincial, municipal, district referral hospitals and commune or khan health centres; restricting recognition to State certificates was therefore unlawful. [Para 128][Para 130][Para 131][Para 138][Para 145][Para 146]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a benefit is granted "without any condition", as the 6 USD attendance allowance is under Notification No. 032/08, suspension of the contract for authorised unpaid leave does not defeat it, whereas a seniority bonus — being pay for work done — is not earned in a month the worker does not work. A sick-leave certificate satisfies Article 71 (new) clause 3 if a lawfully qualified doctor issued it, so an employer may not limit recognition to State hospitals; and a piece-rate claim fails unless the claimants identify the workers who actually fell below the minimum wage. [Para 108][Para 119][Para 130][Para 93]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that the case might have been decided differently had ten workers' identities and payslips been produced, and its statement that the employer retains the right to set piece rates, are observations not necessary to the orders. [Para 81][Para 97]
