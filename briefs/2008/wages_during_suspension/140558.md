# Award No. 130/08 — Fu Hing (Arbitration Council, 30 October 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Uok Ri, An Vireak, Kong Phalla (president) · Doc ID 140558

## 1. Facts
About 350 workers signed employment contracts with Fu Hing Garment Factory, located in Tuk Thla, Phnom Penh. [Para 28] They transferred their rights to the president of the national workers' union federation to represent them. [Para 29] Fu Hing and Pada were separately incorporated and separately registered, in 1997 and 1999 respectively, as distinct legal persons. [Para 30] After Fu Hing's owner fled in September 2006, Pada continued supplying materials while Fu Hing's director remained in the management structure. [Para 37] On 3 April 2007, Fu Hing's director, worker representatives and Pada's lawyer signed a four-point agreement: Pada would supply materials until the owner returned, would not touch Fu Hing property, workers would work properly, and Pada would supply sufficient materials. [Para 39] Pada was not responsible for pre-September 2006 service and benefits, but was responsible from September 2006 for wages, allowances and seniority pay. [Para 45] A 29 March 2007 supply contract was treated as void. [Para 47] Pada stopped supplying materials in August 2008, and work was suspended from 20 August to 20 September 2008 with wages of USD 15 per month. [Para 54] Conciliation failed and the unresolved report reached the Council on 8 October 2008. [Para 8] At the hearing both named parties appeared, but Fu Hing could not be contacted and did not attend. [Para 16]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the workers' claim sought enforcement of the 3 April 2007 agreement or termination of their contracts with Fu Hing with statutory compensation? [Para 58]
2. Whether an employment relationship existed between the Fu Hing workers and Pada within the Council's jurisdiction? [Para 65]
3. W. P. was an employer under Article 2 of the Labour Law? [Para 70]
4. W. P. directed and managed the workers under Article 3 of the Labour Law? [Para 73]
5. Whether the Pada–Fu Hing arrangement was a commissionaire contract under Article 45 of the Labour Law? [Para 96]
6. W. P. bore substitute liability under Article 48 of the Labour Law? [Para 98]

## 3. Positions of the parties
The workers said Pada must answer for all Fu Hing workers because it signed the 3 April 2007 settlement and continued supplying work. [Para 3] They argued the agreement included liability for severance pay if employment ended. [Para 48] They said Pada's accountants came to the factory to pay wages, though the parties later agreed wages were received from Fu Hing. [Para 51] They asked that Pada perform the agreement or terminate them with statutory compensation. [Para 58]
Pada denied being the employer, saying wages and allowances were paid by Fu Hing directly. [Para 4] It said the settlement imposed no other statutory benefits. [Para 5] It accepted responsibility only for wages, regular-work bonus and seniority pay under point 4, and said those obligations were completed. [Para 60] The workers accepted receiving those wages. [Para 62] Pada denied liability for termination because it was not the employer. [Para 59] It said the alleged supply arrangement was not in writing. [Para 36]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel refused to consider the workers' claim that Pada implement the 3 April 2007 agreement or terminate their contracts with Fu Hing; no monetary order was made. [Para 108]
The panel treated the employment relationship as the threshold question. [Para 65] Article 1 governs relationships arising from employment contracts. [Para 66] The contracts were between Fu Hing and its workers, not Pada. [Para 67] The dispute therefore fell outside the Council's jurisdiction. [Para 69]
Under Article 2, Pada did not directly employ the workers, who worked at Fu Hing's premises. [Para 71] Article 2 therefore did not apply. [Para 72] Under Article 3, direction and management include control over hiring, discipline, hours, leave, wages, workplace, tools and termination. [Para 74] The workers had contracts with, worked at, used the equipment of, and were managed by Fu Hing. [Para 79] No evidence showed Pada controlled hiring, dismissal, hours, leave, wages or the workplace. [Para 91] There was insufficient evidence that Pada was the employer. [Para 92]
Pada supplied goods; Fu Hing did not provide labour to Pada, so the arrangement was not a commissionaire contract and Article 48 substitute liability did not apply. [Para 105] The 3 April 2007 agreement did not convert Pada into an employer.

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A supplier of materials to an enterprise does not become the employer of that enterprise's workers under Article 2 unless it directly employs them. [Para 72]
Employer status and Council jurisdiction depend on proven direction and management — hiring, dismissal, discipline, hours, leave, wages, workplace and tools — under Article 3. [Para 74]
A supply relationship is not a commissionaire contract under Article 45 unless the enterprise provides labour to perform work or services for the other, in writing. [Para 96]
Substitute liability under Article 48 arises only where such a contract and labour-supply relationship exist. [Para 105]

**Obiter dicta:** The relationship between Pada and Fu Hing might have had a commissionaire character, but the statutory elements were absent. [Para 94]
