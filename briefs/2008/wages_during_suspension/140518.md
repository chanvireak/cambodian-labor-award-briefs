# Award No. 63/08 — Sau Thon Borey and Golden Crown (សៅធន៍ ប៊េរ និង ហ្គោល ដិន ហ្គ្រោន) v Trade Union Federation at the Factory (Arbitration Council, 9 May 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, presiding) · Doc ID 140518

## 1. Facts
Sau Thon Borey and Golden Crown (ក្រុមហ៊ុន សៅធន៍ ប៊េរ និង ហ្គោល ដិន ហ្គ្រោន), a Phnom Penh factory at Chom Chao employing 1,500 workers, faced 14 collective demands presented by the trade-union federation at the factory (សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត ប្រចាំ រោងចក្រ) [Para 21][Para 2]. The demands covered severance and contract renewal, maternity benefits for a named female worker, sick-leave pay, special leave under Prakas 267, a room for worker representatives, milk and medical-check allowances, union-dues deduction, workplace accident compensation, the death of a named worker on 11 March 2008, wage deductions for personal leave, the conduct of the factory doctor, and the opening of the main gate 30 minutes before the shift [Para 2][Para 3][Para 8][Para 11][Para 15]. Conciliation on 25 April 2008 settled nothing: only demand 1 was taken up, it failed amid sharp hostility between the sides, and the parties agreed not to negotiate demands 2 to 14 at all [Para 23]. The workers then struck, and the panel's order of 2 May 2008 directing them back to work on 3 May 2008 was not obeyed [Para 28][Para 29].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Prakas 099, Article 20 — the only question the panel reached) Whether the panel may continue to arbitrate the 14 unsettled demands while the workers remain on strike and have refused to return to work as ordered? [Para 40][Para 42]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the workers did not enter the factory and remained on strike; they did not appear to have any genuine intention of having the collective dispute resolved by the panel, since they neither obeyed its order nor attended to the merits [Para 33][Para 48].

**Employer (respondent):** the company had opened its gates and allowed every worker to return to work, and the workers simply refused to come in [Para 34]. It had also written on 30 April 2008 asking the panel to issue an order stopping the strike [Para 25].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel declined to continue the arbitration and closed case 63/08 without deciding any of the 14 demands, leaving them unresolved [Para 50].

The panel founded itself on Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, which requires the parties to a dispute before the Council to suspend every contentious measure — strike, lockout or anything else that could aggravate the dispute — and to attend all meetings convened by the panel [Para 40][Para 41]. It read that provision, as it had in its earlier order in this very file, to mean that the panel will not carry on with an arbitration while the workers are striking or the employer is locking out [Para 42]. It then surveyed its own practice: in Award 04/04 (IMSI) it had held that a party to a labour dispute has no right to strike during the panel's proceedings, and that it could not decide or issue an award on a collective dispute referred to it in those circumstances [Para 43]; in Award 77/05 (Franco Nith Thing Garment) it had said the same at point 4 [Para 45]; and in Awards 28/06 (GDI) and 34/06 (Golden Frame) it had closed the files outright because the workers kept striking during the proceedings [Para 46][Para 47]. Applying that line, the panel found no genuine intention on the workers' side to have the dispute resolved: the strike continued after the 2 May order, the workers did not return on 3 May, the pre-hearing meeting of 6 May produced only an offer to rotate back in shifts which the workers rejected, and by 9 May the secretariat was told they were still out [Para 29][Para 35][Para 38]. It therefore recorded expressly that it made no findings of fact on the disputed points, because no hearing could be held [Para 39]. The decision was to discontinue, with the file left capable of revival if the strike ended [Para 44][Para 49].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A party that keeps a strike or lockout running after the panel has ordered work to resume disables the panel from arbitrating: under Article 20 of Prakas 099 the panel will discontinue the proceedings and issue no award on the merits, and it may take the dispute up again only once the strike has ended [Para 42][Para 44].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's survey of Awards 04/04, 77/05, 28/06, 34/06 and 97/07 was illustrative of consistent practice rather than necessary to the order, which rested on Article 20 alone [Para 43][Para 47].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel itself records that it made no findings of fact, so the award contains no account of the underlying events beyond the strike chronology [Para 39]. The OCR is uneven and the employer's corporate name is printed in at least five differing forms across the header, the body and the operative paragraph; the case number and the operative order are also repeated twice in Para 50 [Para 1][Para 50]. The numbering of the demands breaks down, and the 14th demand is numbered "4" in the list [Para 15]. The panel's own prior order is cited with a corrupted date, printed as "102 May 2008", and the ministry's conciliation report number carries three different suffix letter-codes across the award [Para 18][Para 39]. Two individual workers named in the demands — a woman on maternity leave and a worker who died on 11 March 2008 — are identified here by role only [Para 3][Para 11].
