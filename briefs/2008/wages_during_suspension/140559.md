# Award No. 128/08 — Vey Hour Garment Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន វេយ ហួ ហ្គាមេន ខូអិលធីឌី) and Vey Hour Workers' Union (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព-សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត ក្រុមហ៊ុន វេយ ហូ) (Arbitration Council, 30 October 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · three-member panel: អ៉ឹង សុទ្ធី (employer-nominated), អន ណន (worker-nominated), ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា (president) · Doc ID 140559

## 1. Facts
Vey Hour Garment Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន វេយ ហួ ហ្គាមេន ខូអិលធីឌី) began operating in 2006 and employed about 600 workers; the only union on site was the claimant workers' union, which applied for registration on 5 September 2008 but held no certificate at the hearing. [Para 36] Of 17 demands raised in conciliation, two remained unresolved and were referred to the panel: the length of fixed-duration contracts and the $5 regular-attendance bonus. [Para 13] The workers had first demanded one-year contracts and withdrew that demand at the hearing in favour of six months. [Para 45] The union held no written mandate from its members, but an elected employee delegate attended. [Para 77]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must convert its three-month fixed-duration employment contracts to six-month contracts? [Para 44]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the $5 regular-attendance bonus must be maintained in full for a worker who takes one day of authorised leave in a month? [Para 122]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), Vey Hour Workers' Union (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព-សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត ក្រុមហ៊ុន វេយ ហូ):** three-month terms are too short and expose members to union discrimination and dismissal at each renewal; the $5 bonus should stay whole whatever the reason for a one-day absence. [Para 46][Para 54]
**Employer (respondent), Vey Hour Garment Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន វេយ ហួ ហ្គាមេន ខូអិលធីឌី):** a contract rests on mutual consent, most workers prefer three months because it eases transfer to new factories, and the bonus is payable only to workers completing 26 working days. [Para 49][Para 56]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — both demands were dismissed, but the employer was ordered to reduce the $5 regular-attendance bonus only in proportion to days actually absent on lawfully authorised leave. [Para 150][Para 152]

On Issue 1 the panel held that Article 65 of the Labour Law leaves the form of a contract to the parties' agreement, and Article 22 of the Civil Code (Law No. 38 on contracts and non-contractual liability) makes a contract the law of the parties, alterable only by consent. No party may impose a term the other has not accepted. [Para 91][Para 95] The workers' fear of union discrimination was assertion only, unsupported by evidence, and they carried the burden of proving it; the panel therefore found no discrimination in the employer's refusal. [Para 118][Para 119] It adopted its reasoning in Awards 56/06-Borik, 79/07-Terantic, 106/07 and 123/07 and dismissed the demand. [Para 100][Para 105][Para 120]

On Issue 2 the panel applied point 3 of Notification No. 745 KKBV of 23 October 2006, which preserves the benefits granted by point 3 of Notification No. 017 SKBY of 18 July 2000 — a minimum $5 monthly attendance bonus. Bonus is part of wage under Article 103, and Articles 71(k) and 72 suspend the wage obligation only on days the employer has authorised absence. [Para 123][Para 135][Para 138] The claim to keep the bonus whole after an unauthorised absence failed, because the condition for the bonus was then unmet. [Para 128] But the panel held it unjust to withhold the whole $5 for an authorised absence, and ordered pro-rata reduction to the days absent, following Awards 57/07-Siratec, 106/07 and 115/07. [Para 144][Para 147][Para 149]

Before the merits the panel dealt with representation: the union was unregistered and held no written transfer of rights, so it could not bring the dispute in its own name under Article 268; but an employee delegate elected under Article 283 may carry workers' grievances to the Council under Article 284 and Prakas No. 313. [Para 71][Para 88]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The length of a fixed-duration contract is a matter of party autonomy: a panel will not rewrite a three-month term into six months merely because workers fear future discrimination, and neither side may force terms on the other. [Para 111] Where a bonus is at once a preserved contractual benefit and part of wage, an employer may reduce it only in proportion to days actually absent with authorisation, never in full. [Para 145]

**Obiter dicta:** refusing the change does not deny workers the right to propose a different contract type, but the employer's consent is still required. [Para 121] Registration is not a precondition for an elected employee delegate to represent workers before the Council. [Para 76]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer-nominated arbitrator's name is printed in three different spellings across the signature block and the annexed dissent, and the forms cannot be reconciled from the text. [Para 154][Para 192] Para 75 dates Prakas No. 099 as both 2019 and 2004 in one sentence; header and body otherwise agree on 21 April 2004. [Para 75] The demand appears in the conciliation report as one-year contracts and at the hearing as six months, explained there as a withdrawal rather than a contradiction. [Para 2][Para 45] Paras 4 and 125 carry stray numerals that leave the bonus conditions partly unreadable.
