# Award No. 136/08 — Su Phethich Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ូ ភើថី ច អិលធីឌី) (Arbitration Council, 28 November 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · three-member panel: អ៉ីង សុទ្ធី (employer-nominated), លីវ សុវណ្ណា (worker-nominated, dissenting), ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា (president) · Doc ID 140564

## 1. Facts
Su Phethich Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ូ ភើថី ច អិលធីឌី) employed about 4,770 workers at a factory on National Road 2. [Para 31] Eight demands went to conciliation and two remained unresolved: reimbursement of pre-employment medical-check fees, and full wages on days when the company had no work to give. [Para 12][Para 48] Forty workers thumb-printed the complaint brought by សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ ប្រចាំ រោងចក្រ ស៊ូ ភើថិច, a union that admitted it held no registration certificate and never produced its application receipt. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 72] The panel therefore confined itself to the 40 named complainants. [Para 73]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must reimburse the medical-examination fees the workers paid themselves before being hired? [Para 74]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay full wages on days when it has no work for the workers, and whether it must apply the statutory suspension procedure instead of paying half? [Para 88]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), the Khmer Youth federation at Su Phethich (សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ ប្រចាំ រោងចក្រ ស៊ូ ភើថិច):** Article 247 obliges the employer to pay for medical checks, the company never paid, and workers were paid only 50 per cent when work ran out. [Para 35][Para 48]
**Employer (respondent), Su Phethich Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស៊ូ ភើថី ច អិលធីឌី):** a pre-hiring health certificate is a condition of recruitment, so the company pays only where it sends a worker for examination; when work runs out it follows its past practice of giving notice a day ahead and paying 50 per cent. [Para 36][Para 52]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim for reimbursement of medical fees was dismissed, and on Issue 2 the company was ordered to stop its unlawful failure to suspend contracts and to apply contract suspension in accordance with the Labour Law; no arrears were ordered. [Para 112][Para 113][Para 114]

On Issue 1 the panel held that Article 247 of the Labour Law and Article 2 of Inter-Ministerial Prakas No. 1191 make the employer responsible for the cost of medical examinations, at a fixed 12,000 riel per Khmer worker. [Para 74][Para 76][Para 77] It confirmed that line of reasoning from Awards 21/03, 19/04, 53/04, 05/06, 56/07, 86/07, 93/07, 98/07, 40/08 and 52/08. [Para 79] The claim nonetheless failed on proof: the workers could not say how many of them had paid, produced no receipts, gave no amounts or dates, and did not file the list of names the panel ordered by 7 November 2008, although a week was ample time. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 84] The panel followed its practice of refusing claims unsupported by evidence and dismissed it. [Para 85][Para 87]

On Issue 2 the panel distinguished lawful from unlawful suspension. Under Articles 71 and 72 of the Labour Law, suspension for economic difficulty must be notified to and monitored by the labour inspector, and only then does the wage obligation fall away. [Para 90][Para 92][Para 96] Both parties agreed the company never suspended the contracts and never notified the inspector; it simply paid 50 per cent by informal arrangement. [Para 99][Para 101] The panel held that this breached Article 72 and that the company owes 100 per cent wages even where there is no work — the rule in Awards 21/03, 01/04, 46/04, 60/06, 74/07, 27/08, 28/08 and 53/08. [Para 98][Para 103] Exercising its power under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099, it ordered the unlawful practice stopped and lawful suspension applied. [Para 104] It declined to rule on the demand for full wages on future no-work days, since a panel resolves disputes that have arisen, not hypothetical ones. [Para 106][Para 111]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer has no work but does not formally suspend contracts with notification to the labour inspector, the wage obligation survives and full wages are owed; only a lawful, inspected suspension under Articles 71 and 72 removes it. [Para 97][Para 103] A claim for medical-examination reimbursement fails without proof of which workers paid, how much and when, even though the legal duty to pay rests on the employer. [Para 82]

**Obiter dicta:** the worker-nominated arbitrator dissented, holding that Article 120's three-year limitation runs from the contract date and that the fees of workers hired between 3 October 2005 and 3 October 2008 should have been repaid. [Para 130][Para 138]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Prakas No. 1191 is dated both 2023 and 2006 in the same citation, and Para 75 is a duplicated, garbled fragment repeating the Article 247(គ) text. [Para 75][Para 76] The company name is printed in four different Khmer spellings across the face, the documents list and the operative paragraphs. [Para 1][Para 24][Para 115] The worker-nominated arbitrator's name is spelled differently on the signature page and in the dissent heading. [Para 116][Para 139] The header line is corrupted by stray digits and dates, and "ក្រុមហ៊ុន មិន បាន ល្អ ព្យួរ" in Para 99 carries an intruding word that obscures the finding. [Para 1][Para 99] The dissent is bound into the same file without a separator, so its reasoning on limitation can be mistaken for the panel's. [Para 129]
