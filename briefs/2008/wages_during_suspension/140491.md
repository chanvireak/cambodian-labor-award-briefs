# Award No. FineGis — FineGis Cambodia Garment Co., Ltd v Free Trade Union of Workers of Kingdom of Cambodia (Arbitration Council, 14 March 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phyrum for the employer party, Ann Vireak for the worker party, Ang Eng Thong chair) · Doc ID 140491

## 1. Facts
FineGis Cambodia Garment Co., Ltd, at Prey Tea Village, Sangkat Chom Chao, Khan Dangkor, Phnom Penh, lost its buyer's orders in December 2007 and suspended operations. [Para 17][Para 27] It notified the Department of Labour Inspection by letter of 25 December 2007, then announced on 31 December 2007 that from 2 January 2008 workers would be suspended, paid US$10 per month, and required to punch in twice weekly on Mondays and Thursdays. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20] The Free Trade Union of Workers of Kingdom of Cambodia (FTUWKC) represented about 500 workers, three of whom appeared at the hearing; the registered local union had accepted the announcement. [Para 16][Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company may require workers to punch in twice a week and pay them US$10 a month during a suspension that the Labour Inspector never approved? [Para 32][Para 39]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay wages on the 10th of each month? [Para 27][Para 50]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, FTUWKC for about 500 FineGis workers):** requiring attendance during a suspension is unfair because many had taken other work or returned to their home provinces, and travelling to punch in costs more than the US$10 paid; they asked for US$20 a month and cited other factories in the area paying part wages without attendance. [Para 22][Para 25][Para 26]
**Employer (respondent, FineGis Cambodia Garment Co., Ltd):** it had no buyer orders and could not pay US$20; the punch-in requirement monitored how many workers remained, and paying absentees would be unfair to the 300 to 600 who did attend; it attributed the November wage delay to the order shortage and promised regular payment. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 53]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay full wages to workers for the two-month suspension conducted without the Labour Inspector's permission, and to pay wages on the 10th of each month as both parties had agreed. [Para 49][Para 54]

The panel held the decisive question to be whether the suspension complied with Article 71(11) of the Labour Law, which permits suspension only on serious economic or material difficulty, for no more than two months, and under the control of the Labour Inspector. [Para 33][Para 35] Following Awards 22/05-Ocean Issue 2, 72/05-North Gaiety Issue 1 and 105/04-United Eternity Issue 1, it read "control" as requiring notification to and written approval from the Inspector, since the control exists to protect workers from serious impact. [Para 37][Para 43][Para 44] It found the company's 25 December 2007 notification drew no response, and held silence means refusal, not approval, because the Inspector has always issued a permission letter when it approves. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 45][Para 46] Article 72(1) therefore did not relieve the employer of wages, and full wages were owed from 1 January 2008 to 29 February 2008. [Para 47] The US$10 punch-in announcement was a unilateral rule less favourable than the law and null under Article 13(1). [Para 48][Para 49] On the pay date, the panel found the employer had met its obligation apart from the November delay paid in December, and recorded the employer's undertaking to pay on the 10th. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 54]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of employment under Article 71(11) is lawful only where the Labour Inspector has actually approved it in writing; the employer's notification alone is not enough, and the Inspector's silence is to be read as refusal. [Para 39][Para 42][Para 46] Where the suspension is unlawful, Article 72(1) does not suspend the wage obligation and the employer owes full wages for the whole suspension period, and a unilateral announcement offering a token monthly sum in exchange for attendance is void under Article 13(1). [Para 47][Para 49]

**Obiter dicta:** the observations that the Inspector's control exists to shield the national economy and workers' benefits, and that a permission letter is always issued in practice when approval is given, are general practice remarks rather than the ground of the order. [Para 44][Para 45]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The issue numbering is not stable: the non-conciliation report lists the punch-in demand with US$20 as point 2 and the pay-date demand as point 3 at [Para 8], the reasons head the pay-date demand "Issue 2" at [Para 27], and the award then calls the same pay-date demand "Issue 3" at [Para 50] and [Para 54]. Section 2 follows the report's numbering, so no Issue 3 heading appears there.
- The suspension is announced as running "until 29 January 2008" at [Para 20] but the panel treats it as a two-month suspension and orders wages through 29 February 2008, although the company's own letter is dated 24 December 2008 at [Para 14] — over a year after the award.
- The panel orders wages "from 1 January 2008" at [Para 47], a day before the announced start of 2 January 2008 at [Para 19].
- The workforce figure is missing from the sentence that introduces it at [Para 15], which stops after "approximately"; the union figure of about 600 members is a separate, unverified assertion by the company.
- Word boundaries are widely lost in this translation ("themloyer", "thearing", "wageshould"), and the employer's internal work rules and one suspension letter number are garbled at [Para 13] and [Para 14].
