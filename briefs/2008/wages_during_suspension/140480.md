# Award No. 05/08 — Tong Garment (ក្រុមហ៊ុន តុង ហ្គាមេន) (Arbitration Council, 31 January 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Mar Samborna (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Ang Eng Thong (chair) · Doc ID 140480

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន តុង ហ្គាមេន (Tong Garment), a factory in Prey Lvea, Chom Chao, Dangkao, Phnom Penh, employs about 900 workers, some 854 of them members of the Tong Garment workers' union. [Para 1][Para 29][Para 30] With no orders from buyers, the company applied to the Labour Inspectorate on 24 December 2007 to suspend contracts for two months, from 1 January to 29 February 2008, offering 50% of base wage plus 50% of seniority, provided workers clocked in daily between 9:00 and 9:30. [Para 16][Para 34][Para 39] The inspector visited on 26 December and signed a minute recording the inquiry, but issued no written authorisation. [Para 17][Para 48][Para 56] Both parties agreed the factory really is in suspension. [Para 32] The single demand referred was for 100% wages during the suspension so long as daily clocking-in is required. [Para 28]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay 100% of wages throughout the suspension of contracts where the suspension was never authorised in writing by the labour inspector and the employer requires workers to clock in daily? [Para 41][Para 43]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** ភាគី កម្មករ និយោជិត, through the Tong Garment workers' union, demanded full wages for the suspension period, arguing that being made to come and thumb-print every day costs them travel time and money, and that 50% is not payable at all if they are not required to attend. [Para 28][Para 38]
**Employer (respondent):** ក្រុមហ៊ុន តុង ហ្គាមេន, through its lawyer and administration, argued that it had notified the inspectorate on 24 December 2007, that inspectors attended and signed the minute of inquiry, and that this signature amounted to approval of the suspension; the daily clock-in was needed to track the workforce for the restart. [Para 35][Para 39][Para 40]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay 100% of wages during the suspension of contracts, from the first day of suspension until it obtains authorisation from the labour inspector. [Para 65][Para 66]
The panel applied Article 71(11) of the Labour Law, which allows suspension of enterprise activity for no more than two months on serious economic or material difficulty and requires the suspension to be placed under the supervision of the labour inspector. [Para 43][Para 44] Following Awards No. 22/05 and No. 72/05, it held that supervision means notice to, and agreement from, the inspector, and that the inspector's response must be clear. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 55]
It found that the inspector's signature on the minutes of inquiry meant only that the participants had said what is recorded there, not that the suspension was approved, and that the employer produced no sufficient evidence of agreement. [Para 50][Para 52][Para 56] The suspension was therefore contrary to Article 71(11). [Para 57]
Turning to Article 72 §1, the panel held that a lawful suspension extinguishes both the duty to work and the duty to pay wages, but that an unlawful one leaves the wage obligation intact. [Para 58][Para 59] Since the suspension was unlawful and the period was still running, the employer owed 100% wages from 1 January 2008 until authorisation is granted. [Para 60][Para 65] It added that Article 13 makes Labour Law provisions public order, so an agreement giving less than the law allows is void. [Para 61][Para 63][Para 64]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of contracts under Article 71(11) is lawful only where the labour inspector has clearly agreed to it; a signature on a minute of inquiry records what was said but is not authorisation, and without authorisation the employer remains bound to pay full wages for the whole suspension period, any lesser arrangement being void under Article 13. [Para 52][Para 55][Para 59][Para 63]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that requiring attendance to clock in daily during a suspension imposes travel time and cost on workers, a factor it weighed in assessing the fairness of the 50% offer. [Para 38]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company's name is printed as តុង ហ្គាមេន, តុង ហ្គា and "តុង ហ្គារ មេន", and the union's name likewise varies. [Para 1][Para 18][Para 20] The conciliation report is dated 8 January 2008 but recorded as sent to the secretariat on 9 January 2007, a year that cannot be right. [Para 5] The award carries no paragraph or issue numbering for the single issue, and the reasoning at paragraphs 45 and 60 is interrupted by OCR debris and a stray "ក្រុម ហ៊ិន". [Para 45][Para 60] An annexed dissenting opinion by the employer-appointed arbitrator, arguing that Article 71(11) requires supervision but not written consent, is appended and is not part of the majority holding. [Para 68][Para 72][Para 81] Individual union officers and company representatives are named in the source; this brief refers to them by role only.
