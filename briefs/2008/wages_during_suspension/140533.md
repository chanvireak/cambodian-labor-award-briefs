# Award No. 83/08 — Le Grand Café Restaurant, Siem Reap (Arbitration Council, 29 July 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Nyan Somonynt, chair) · Doc ID 140533

## 1. Facts
ភោជនីយដ្ឋាន ឡី ហ្គ្រង់ កាហ្វេ (Le Grand Café), a restaurant operating in Siem Reap since mid-2004, passed into the interim management of Ms P, a shareholder and general manager, after the majority shareholder died in March 2008 and the Siem Reap court ordered the business handed to her on 25 April 2008 [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41]. Thirty-four service staff, who had no union, stopped coming to work from 27 April 2008; the shop closed, reopened, and new staff were hired [Para 56][Para 60][Para 65]. Four demands survived conciliation: respect for the labour contracts, reinstatement, April and May 2008 wages, and payment of the staff's tip savings (ប្រាក់ ធីប) [Para 2][Para 86].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the new owner must honour the staff's labour contracts? [Para 66][Para 67]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the staff who stopped work and then struck are entitled to return to their jobs? [Para 75][Para 76]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the staff are entitled to wages for April and for May 2008? [Para 80][Para 138]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the owner must pay out the tip savings (ប្រាក់ ធីប) accumulated for the staff? [Para 85][Para 156]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** their contracts, some fixed-term and some open-ended, remained alive and were continued by agreement; they stayed away only out of fear after Ms P brought armed men to search the restaurant, and they asked to be taken back and paid for April and May 2008 and to receive their tip savings, which they put at 700 (ប្រាំពីរ រយ) United States dollars at the hearing [Para 67][Para 68][Para 81][Para 86].
**Employer (respondent):** ភោជនីយដ្ឋាន ឡី ហ្គ្រង់ កាហ្វេ, through Ms P, denied any threat or coercion, said the fixed-term contracts had already expired on 20 April 2008 and that repeated invitations to return were ignored, that April wages had been paid, that no wage was due for May because nobody worked, and that the tips were never held by her [Para 69][Para 70][Para 71][Para 84][Para 88].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issues 1, 2 and 4 were dismissed, and on Issue 3 the claim for April wages was dismissed except for two named workers, to whom the owner was ordered to pay April wages immediately; the claim for May wages was dismissed [Para 170][Para 171][Para 172][Para 173][Para 174]
On jurisdiction, the panel applied Articles 309 and 312 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 099 and held the dispute collective even though no union existed at the restaurant, because it arose between one employer and most of her staff over working conditions and could disrupt the enterprise and social peace [Para 89][Para 95][Para 97][Para 99]. It accepted the workers' lawyer as their representative because no worker objected, he had acted since the conciliation stage, and the employer raised no challenge [Para 104][Para 105].
On Issues 1 and 2, which it merged, the panel found under Articles 67, 73 and 87 that the twelve-month contracts beginning 20 April 2007 had been extended by silence to 20 April 2009, because no notice of expiry was given and the staff kept working five days past the stated end date [Para 108][Para 113]. The contracts were therefore valid when the staff stopped attending. The panel nevertheless held that staying away for many days without notice or reason, and interfering in the management of the shop, amounted to serious misconduct under Article 83 and to breach of the contract, so termination was lawful [Para 122][Para 127]. What occurred was not a strike within Article 318, since it did not seek a solution to a demand about working conditions, so the protection of Article 332 was unavailable and reinstatement could be refused [Para 132][Para 134][Para 136].
On Issue 3 the panel applied Article 118, which puts the burden of proving payment of wages on the employer: the signed April payroll for the 34 staff was produced and not contested, so the claim was rejected for all but the two workers shown as unpaid, who were to be paid at once [Para 139][Para 142][Para 143][Para 172]. For May, Article 332 suspends the contract and the wage during a strike, and the Article 334 exception for replacement labour did not assist the staff because their contracts had already been lawfully terminated before the stoppage; no wage was due [Para 151][Para 154][Para 155].
On Issue 4 the panel found the tips to be part of wages under clause 8(a) of the contract, which promised an equal share on the 15th of each month; but the staff produced no evidence of the amount held, the period, or the method of division, so the claim failed for want of proof [Para 164][Para 165][Para 167][Para 168].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-term contract of twelve months is converted into an open-ended one where the employer gives no notice of expiry and the worker keeps working past the end date, but that surviving contract may still be terminated for the serious misconduct of prolonged unexplained absence and interference with management, and a stoppage mounted after such a termination is not a strike attracting the wage protection of Article 332 [Para 113][Para 134]. Where tips are contractually pooled and shared, they are part of wages and the employer must hold and divide them, yet the worker still bears the burden of proving the amount outstanding [Para 165][Para 167].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the contracts were written only in English with no Khmer translation, and accepted them because all parties and all three arbitrators could read English [Para 169].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The roster table listing the staff, their contract type and their strike participation is badly corrupted by optical recognition across several pages, with names, columns and figures interleaved and repeated, so the numbers of staff on each contract type cannot be read reliably from it [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63][Para 64]. The award gives the May claim once as month 05 and once with an extra digit appended to the month number [Para 81][Para 82]. A Prakas citation in the jurisdiction section carries extra digits that make the article reference unreadable [Para 89]. The signature block spells the employer-appointed arbitrator's personal name differently from the composition line and leaves the chair's name off altogether, so the panel cannot be matched to its signatures on the face of the document [Para 2][Para 176]. Two workers are named individually in the operative order; their names are not reproduced here [Para 172].
