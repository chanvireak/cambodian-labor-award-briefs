# Award No. 23/08 — M & V International Garment Company 1 v Cambodian Industrial Union Federation (Arbitration Council, 11 March 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 140490

## 1. Facts
M & V International Garment Company 1, a garment factory on National Road 2, Sangkat Chak Angre Krom, Phnom Penh, employs about 193 workers and has two unions, the local CIU with 125 members and a local CITU with 56. [Para 40] The Cambodian Industrial Union Federation (CIUF) and its local union at M & V 1 filed a complaint of 13 November 2007 covering 17 demands on wages, medical checks, leave, maternity pay, pay slips and bonuses; 12 points were not conciliated and were referred, and the union withdrew the two suspension-related demands at the hearing. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 32] The employer refused to discuss five demands it said had never been raised at enterprise level. [Para 34][Para 86]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1 and Issue 11) Whether the company calculated piece-rate and fixed-wage workers' wages correctly, and whether it must back pay wages lost over the last three years? [Para 91][Para 105]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must reimburse the 12,000 riel medical check fee for workers examined from 1 October 2007? [Para 115]
3. (Issue 3) Whether workers are entitled to one additional day of annual leave for every three years of service? [Para 124]
4. (Issue 4 and Issue 10) Whether 90 days of maternity leave means working days excluding holidays and Sundays, whether the payment must be made in advance, and whether mis-calculated payments must be back paid? [Para 135][Para 161][Para 168]
5. (Issue 6) Whether the company must issue pay slips in Khmer rather than English and Chinese? [Para 176]
6. (Issue 7) Whether payment in lieu of annual leave must be based on the average wage of the preceding twelve months? [Para 187]
7. (Issue 9) Whether the attendance bonus must be paid pro rata where leave for personal commitments was authorised? [Para 192]
8. (Issue 12) Whether the panel should decide the five demands the union never raised at enterprise level? [Para 207]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, CIUF and the local CIU at M & V 1):** holiday pay for piece workers must divide actual earnings by days actually worked, not by 26; three-year service earns an extra leave day; 90 days of maternity leave means working days; pay slips must be in Khmer; and the panel has jurisdiction over all issues in the non-conciliation report. [Para 45][Para 129][Para 135][Para 178][Para 207]
**Employer (respondent, M & V International Garment Company 1):** its wage method follows a Ministry of Labour instruction dividing by 26; it pays the medical check only for workers it required to be examined; Article 166 already gives one extra day; Article 182 is satisfied by 90 calendar days paid monthly; and the five new demands were never negotiated at the factory. [Para 47][Para 57][Para 126][Para 73][Para 88]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered correct calculation of monthly and daily wages in months with public holidays (rejecting the three-year back pay), payment of the 12,000 riel medical check fee, one extra annual leave day per three years of service, maternity payments one day before leave begins (rejecting the working-day count and the back pay), Khmer pay slips, annual-leave payments based on the twelve-month average, and pro rata deduction of the attendance bonus; it rejected the issue 12 demands and ordered the parties to negotiate them. [Para 214][Para 216][Para 219][Para 220][Para 221]

On wages, Article 163 entitles piece workers to an indemnity equal to the wage lost on public holidays, following Award 82/06-M&V 3 and Notification 745 KKBV of 23 October 2006. [Para 92][Para 93][Para 94][Para 97] The panel found no evidence of any Ministry instruction and held the daily minimum wage must divide monthly earnings by days actually worked, excluding paid holidays, not by 26. [Para 99][Para 100][Para 101] It found past miscalculation but rejected back pay for want of proof of amounts, Article 120's three-year limitation applying. [Para 107][Para 108][Para 111][Para 113][Para 115]

On the medical check, Clause 2(d) of Inter-Ministerial Prakas No. 1191 of 21 November 2006 fixes 12,000 riels per Cambodian employee, and the obligation applies whether the check precedes or follows commencement. [Para 119][Para 122][Para 123]

On leave, Article 166(4) was read, consistently with Awards 62/04-Ecent, 68/05-Gold Lida, 81/06-Supreme and 33/07-Goldfame, to give one extra day per three years. [Para 125][Para 131][Para 133] Article 168 requires the twelve-month average as the basis for payment in lieu of leave, so the flat US$1.92 was wrong. [Para 187][Para 190][Para 191]

On maternity, the panel rejected the working-day reading of Article 182, ordered advance payment under Article 115 paragraph 3, and excluded suspended months from the average by dividing total wages by months actually worked, while rejecting the back-pay claim on the 17 workers for lack of particulars. [Para 142][Para 166][Para 167][Para 157][Para 174][Para 176] Article 112 and Prakas 269 SKBY of 11 October 2001, with Article 5 of the Constitution, required Khmer pay slips. [Para 176][Para 182][Para 183][Para 186] Under Articles 103, 71(6) and 72(1), authorised personal leave suspends the contract, so the bonus is deducted pro rata. [Para 196][Para 199][Para 206]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a wage depends on a daily rate, the divisor is the number of days the worker actually worked in the month, not a fixed 26; a fixed divisor understates holiday pay for piece workers and breaches Article 163. [Para 101][Para 107][Para 108] A claim for miscalculated wages fails, even where miscalculation is established, if the claimant does not identify the workers, months and amounts, Article 120 barring recovery beyond three years. [Para 111][Para 113][Para 115] Maternity pay must be computed on wages actually received in months worked, advanced before leave begins, and the statutory 90 days are calendar days. [Para 142][Para 157][Para 166]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the working-day-versus-calendar-day presumption is a national and international custom (opinio juris), and its general encouragement of good-faith enterprise-level bargaining, are not necessary to the orders. [Para 140][Para 141][Para 212][Para 213]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The maternity reasoning contradicts itself: at [Para 139] the presumption is said to be working days, at [Para 142] the text says 90 days "is a reference to working days, which means it is inclusive of Sundays and holidays", and the demand is then rejected — the rejection was read as turning on the calendar-day reading, but the sentence is unrecoverable as printed.
- The suspension allowance is US$12 per month at [Para 67] and US$10 at [Para 149] and [Para 152].
- The extra leave day is described as giving "14 days" from the fourth year at [Para 127], against 18 or 19 days elsewhere at [Para 126] and [Para 130].
- The number of referred issues shifts between 12 and 17, and the same hearing is said to concern 17 non-conciliation issues at [Para 30] although 12 were referred.
- Dates inside the evidence list are corrupt ("dated .4 January 2008" at [Para 40]) and whole sentences are duplicated by the OCR at [Para 35], [Para 100], [Para 155] and [Para 176].
- Two individual workers are named in full in the source at [Para 55] and [Para 171]; they are not reproduced here.
