# Award No. 48/08 — Sinohydro Kamchay Hydroelectric Project Co. Ltd and the representative of its workers (Arbitration Council, 29 April 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Hem H. Naryth, Suong Sophal, chair Kong Phallack · Doc ID 140504

## 1. Facts
Sinohydro Kamchay Hydroelectric Project Co. Ltd, a hydroelectric project at Snam Prampi, Kampot, employed approximately 900 workers with no union and no worker delegates. [Para 3][Para 23][Para 24] On 7 March 2008 a quarrel broke out between workers and a group chief; the company terminated three workers on 8 March for fighting, and a strike followed on 9 March against those terminations but not against the group chief. [Para 40] Six workers first demanded reinstatement, but only three — two mechanics and one electrician — still pursued it at the hearing, and only one of them attended in person. [Para 25][Para 32] The workers also complained that the resignation letters put to three of them were written in a foreign language and that only Cambodian, not foreign, workers were dismissed. [Para 10][Para 33][Para 34]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company's termination of the three workers was valid under the Labour Law and whether they must be reinstated? [Para 25][Para 72][Para 89]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the terminations were unreasonable because the group chief started the fight and the two workers only intervened, because only Cambodian workers were dismissed while the group chief was not, and because the resignation letters were in a language the workers did not read. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 37][Para 38]
**Employer (Sinohydro Kamchay):** the three were terminated because they quarrelled with the group chief and destroyed company property, and the company had no other reasons; its representatives said they were not present at the incident and did not know the facts. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 85]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to reinstate Mr R., the one claimant who appeared at the hearing; the award is non-binding and becomes binding eight days after notification absent written opposition to the Minister of Labour. [Para 92][Para 93]

The panel first settled representation. Clause 19 of Prakas 099 SKBY of 21 April 2004 lets a party appear in person, through a lawyer, or through others authorised in writing, and the two workers who described themselves as worker representatives held no authorisation; the panel gave them until 21 April 2008 to file a signed or thumbprinted list of the workers they spoke for, and nothing was filed, so the panel found authority only over Mr R. [Para 60][Para 62][Para 63][Para 66]

On the merits, Mr R. worked without any written contract, so under Article 67 paragraph 7 of the Labour Law his contract was of undetermined duration. [Para 74][Para 75] Article 74 allows such a contract to be terminated at will, but only on prior written notice and for a valid reason relating to the worker's aptitude or behaviour or the requirements of the enterprise. [Para 77][Para 79][Para 80] The company produced no evidence that Mr R. beat the group chief, seems not to have investigated whether he was involved at all, never answered his account that he intervened to separate the fight, and had no internal work rules classifying the alleged conduct as serious, medium or light misconduct. [Para 81][Para 83][Para 84][Para 86] Following Awards 17/07 Charm Textile (issue 1), 35/07 Flying Dragon (issue 2) and 109/07 Kingsland, the party asserting a fact bears the burden of proving it, and here the employer did not. [Para 86][Para 87][Para 88] There was accordingly no valid reason for the termination, and Clause 34 of Prakas 099 empowered the panel to order reinstatement as full relief. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 91]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that terminates an undetermined-duration contract must prove, with evidence and a real investigation, a valid reason going to the worker's aptitude or behaviour; an unsupported allegation of misconduct, unanswered by any inquiry and unclassified by internal work rules, leaves the dismissal without valid reason and reinstatement follows. Separately, a person who appears for a party without written authorisation cannot be treated as that party's representative, and the panel's jurisdiction extends only to those who claim in person. [Para 63][Para 66][Para 88][Para 89]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the employer had no internal work rules visaed by the Ministry of Labour, and that the company's representatives were absent from the incident and could not speak to it. [Para 54][Para 85]
