# Award No. 58/08 — Yong Va Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd No. 2 (Arbitration Council, 29 May 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Somborna for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 140513

## 1. Facts
Yong Va Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd No. 2 (ក្រុមហ៊ុន យ៉ុង វាំ អ៊ិន ដាស្ទ្រៀល (ខេមបូឌា) ខូ អិលធីឌី ទី២), a garment factory at Ta Khmao, Kandal province employing about 5,000 workers, faced a collective dispute brought by the workers' union at the factory, which has 370 members; five unions operate there and none holds most-representative status. [Para 1][Para 20][Para 21][Para 22][Para 23] Conciliation of the single non-conciliated point on 16 January 2008 failed, and non-conciliation report No. 238/08 dated 24 April 2008 reached the Secretariat on 25 April 2008; the hearing was held on 5 May 2008. [Para 10][Para 7][Para 9] The demand concerned the 39 sewing-machine operators in the Khmer sewing section (ជាង ម៉ាស៊ីន ដេរ) paid a base wage of USD 60 a month. [Para 3][Para 24][Para 31] Both parties agreed at the hearing that the award would be binding, and the panel recorded it as such. [Para 14][Para 49]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must raise the wages of, or pay a role allowance to, the 39 sewing-machine operators in the Khmer sewing section who receive USD 60 a month? [Para 3][Para 24][Para 30]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — the workers' union at Yong Va No. 2, សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត យ៉ុង វ៉ា ទី២, within the federation សហព័ន្ធ-សហជីព កម្មករ កម្ពុជា):** the 39 operators are paid a fixed base wage rather than by piece, so they have no chance of earning extra as other workers do; Chinese group leaders had appraised their output and twice filed the paperwork for a rise, which the company never approved; they asked for a wage increase or a role allowance of an amount the union did not fix. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 29][Para 31]
**Employer (respondent — Yong Va Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd No. 2):** the company agreed to pay a role allowance of USD 15 a month, separate from wages, to every Khmer group leader, withdrawable if that leader's performance falls; it refused the same allowance to the 39 sewing-machine operators because they hold no group-leader role, and explained that a group leader may only propose a rise, so silence in reply to a proposal means the company has refused it. [Para 4][Para 16][Para 26][Para 34]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel did not fix a wage increase or allowance for the 39 operators, but ordered the employer to apply clause 5 point A of its internal rules of 29 May 2006 on the setting of wages, bonuses and allowances from the date the award takes effect. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49]

The panel treated the internal rules as the governing standard at enterprise level. Under Article 23 of the Labour Law the internal rules apply the general provisions of the Law and any collective agreement to the enterprise, and cover recruitment conditions, wage payment and allowances, hours, rest and leave, and disciplinary sanctions; the panel read that article as making the internal rules a legal standard inside the company, including for wage increases and allowances. [Para 37][Para 38]

Clause 5 point A of those rules, dated 29 May 2006, provides that the company pays wages according to each worker's output, capacity and results, and reviews wages at the end of every year against output, discipline and performance, within the company's capacity. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41] The panel found that the company had never carried out that annual review for the sewing-machine operators, so clause 5 had not been applied to them. [Para 42]

It also found the practice unequal: some machine operators had received increases of USD 70, 80, 90 and 100 a month while others stayed at USD 60, in a way that was narrow and not transparent, with no explanation to the section of why a handful were raised and the rest were not — a practice combining with rising prices to leave the others dissatisfied and repeatedly claiming. [Para 31][Para 43][Para 44] The panel therefore required the company to publish a clear, general appraisal policy covering output, discipline, attendance, obedience to instructions and skill, and to publicise or explain any decision not to grant a rise under clause 5. [Para 45][Para 46]

The panel accepted that a Chinese group leader performs only an advisory role — he may appraise and propose, but has no power to decide a rise or allowance, which remains the company's. [Para 33] The 15-dollar allowance for Khmer group leaders was recorded as an agreement between the parties rather than a ruling. [Para 15][Para 24]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer's internal rules, made under Article 23 of the Labour Law, promise that wages will be reviewed annually against output, discipline and performance, the panel will order the employer to apply that clause rather than fix a wage increase itself — the amount of a rise being the employer's prerogative — and will require the appraisal criteria and the reasons for refusing a rise to be published so that workers in the same section are treated by a known standard rather than by an unexplained selection. [Para 38][Para 41][Para 42][Para 45][Para 46][Para 47]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that a group leader's proposal can only be answered by the company and that silence operates as refusal, and its observation that a non-response leaves workers unable to learn why a rise was refused, explain the dispute rather than decide the demand. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 36]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The employer's name is printed in several forms — យ៉ុង វាំ អ៊ិន ដាស្ទ្រៀល, យ៉ុង វ៉ា អ៊ិន ដាស្រៀល and យ៉ុង វ៉ា ទី២ អ៉ិន ដាស្ទ្រៀល — and the registration certificate is attributed to the name at [Para 20] with a variant spelling of the company suffix at [Para 17]; the factory is also written យុង វ៉ា ទី២ [Para 1][Para 17][Para 21].
- The demand states that the 39 operators each receive USD 60 a month [Para 24], but the panel's own list shows several at USD 70, 80, 90 and 100 [Para 31][Para 43]; the brief reports both.
- The 39-worker wage table is heavily corrupted: columns collapse and figures interleave with names and dates, so individual rates cannot be reliably attributed [Para 27].
- The internal-rules date is printed with a doubled year, "ឆ្នាំ ឆ្នាំ ២០០៦", in the operative paragraphs [Para 47][Para 48].
- The employer-party arbitrator's name and signature box is blank in the signature block while the other two are filled [Para 50].
- Wage figures of individual workers are not reproduced here; workers, group leaders and the company representative are identified by role only.
