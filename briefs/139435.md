# Award No. 248/15 — Sun Ray Shing Printing & Im Proy Dery (Arbitration Council, 20 October 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chhea presiding; Ing Sothy, employer nominee, dissenting; An Virak, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139435

## 1. Facts
Sun Ray Shing Printing & Im Proy Dery Co., Ltd runs an embroidery and sewing factory at Trapeang Thleung, Chom Chao, Pou Senchey, Phnom Penh, with 320 workers, 237 of whom brought this collective dispute through three union federations. [Para 1][Para 73][Para 76] On 31 August 2015 the employer let the fixed-duration contracts of 33 workers expire without renewal, citing falling orders; among them was Mr R, engaged on rolling three-month contracts since 2013. [Para 3][Para 78][Para 83] The federations complained on 3 September, the Labour Disputes Department conciliated four points without agreement on 4 September, and the file reached the Secretariat on 9 September. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 22] Workers struck in front of the factory from the afternoon of 1 September, and on 4 September, after some had clocked in, supervisors expelled them and the gates were locked for about ten minutes. [Para 104][Para 110][Para 112]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay Mr R compensation on the footing of a contract of unlimited duration, his successive fixed-duration contracts having run beyond two years? [Para 77][Para 134]
2. Whether the panel has jurisdiction over the workers' new demand that fixed-duration contracts of workers with more than two years' service be converted into contracts of unlimited duration, a demand absent from the conciliation record? [Para 198][Para 200][Para 206]
4. Whether the employer must pay 100 per cent of wages for 1 to 4 September 2015, the days of the protest? [Para 103][Para 209]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** three-month contracts renewed beyond two years convert by Article 67 into contracts of unlimited duration, so dismissal without valid reason entitles Mr R to notice pay, dismissal damages, moral damages, annual leave and arrears; and because the employer expelled them and locked the gates it must pay wages for every protest day. [Para 81][Para 90][Para 122][Para 123]
**Employer (respondent):** every contract was a three-month fixed-duration contract with a 5 per cent end-of-contract payment at each expiry, so none converted; the contracts simply ran out and there was no work to give; the strike was unlawful and unpaid; and the 4 September door-closing lasted ten minutes for security reasons. [Para 91][Para 93][Para 102][Para 124][Para 130]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay Mr R one month's wages and benefits in lieu of notice, dismissal damages equal to 30 days' wages plus allowances, unused annual leave, moral damages of the same 30-day amount, and any unpaid final wages, while allowing the employer to recover the 5 per cent payments already made; it dismissed issue 2 for want of jurisdiction, refused wages for 1–3 September, and ordered 100 per cent wages for 4 September alone. [Para 192][Para 193][Para 196][Para 208][Para 263]
On issue 1 the panel read Article 67(2) as capping a genuine fixed-duration contract at two years and Article 73(5) as converting a silent renewal beyond that cap into unlimited duration, following Award 10/03 Zacksintek and holding that the reference is to total cumulative duration rather than to each renewal. [Para 138][Para 140][Para 141][Para 145] Mr R's chain from 1 July 2013 to 31 August 2015 came to about two years and two months, so conversion had occurred and the 5 per cent payments did not undo it. [Para 149][Para 150] The stated reason — expiry of the contract and absence of work — did not relate to his aptitude or conduct or to the needs of the enterprise, and no evidence of lack of work was produced, so the termination breached Article 74; the panel awarded under Articles 75, 77, 89, 91 and 166. [Para 157][Para 158][Para 163][Para 164][Para 177][Para 187]
On issue 2, Article 312(1) and Article 33 of Prakas 099 confine the panel to the points recorded as unsettled at conciliation; the conversion demand surfaced only at the hearing, so the panel declined jurisdiction instead of deciding it. [Para 201][Para 206][Para 207] Issue 3, on change of specialisation, had been withdrawn by the workers before the merits. [Para 27][Para 28]
On issue 4 the panel tested the stoppage against the three elements of a strike in Article 318(1) — concerted cessation of work, inside the enterprise, to obtain a settlement — found all three satisfied, and held the strike lawful in character but carried out without regard to the Labour Law's procedure. [Para 216][Para 224][Para 227][Para 235] Under Article 332 and Article 72(1) the contract is suspended during a strike and wages are not owed, so the claim for 1–3 September failed. [Para 229][Para 231][Para 236][Para 237] On 4 September, however, workers who had already scanned their fingerprints in were expelled; the panel rejected the "unfamiliar faces" explanation and held the expulsion equivalent to a lockout under Article 318(2), effected without the procedure Article 322 requires, so Article 335 obliged the employer to pay wages for that day. [Para 133][Para 239][Para 251][Para 253][Para 254][Para 255] The workers' video was excluded for want of date, place and the guard's identity. [Para 243]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A succession of fixed-duration contracts whose cumulative duration passes two years converts, by Articles 67(2) and 73(5), into a contract of unlimited duration, and the 5 per cent end-of-contract payment does not prevent that conversion; terminating such a contract on the bare ground of expiry, without evidence that the enterprise had no work, is a termination without valid reason attracting notice pay, dismissal damages, unused leave and moral damages. [Para 141][Para 150][Para 164][Para 187] Conversely, a strike suspends the wage obligation so no wages are owed for strike days, but an employer that expels workers who have already clocked in commits a lockout, and a lockout carried out without the statutory procedure obliges it to pay wages for the days lost. [Para 231][Para 251][Para 254]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that ILO Recommendation 166 is not binding but is useful context for reading Article 67, and that repeated fixed-duration contracts benefit the worker while the employer bears the loss — observations the outcome did not depend on. [Para 147][Para 287] Ing Sothy's appended dissent, which would have refused conversion because the 5 per cent was paid at each expiry, is recorded but did not carry. [Para 266][Para 290]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's name is printed four ways: "Sun Ray Shing Printing & Im Proy Dery" on the face of the award, "San Vay Shing ADB Printing" in the conciliation reference of 8 September 2015, and two further variants in the company statute and internal-rules exhibits, so the transliteration used here is the header form. [Para 1][Para 17][Para 34][Para 35] Mr R's start date is 10 July 2013 in his own account but 1 July 2013 in the employer's account and in the panel's finding, a difference that does not alter the two-year point. [Para 83][Para 87][Para 134] The second hearing date survives as "ថ្ងៃ- ១៣០ ខែកញ្ញា" in the procedural summary though the hearing list gives 30 September 2015. [Para 19][Para 24] The extension of the award date is garbled into "1 October 2019 October 2015" for 20 October 2015. [Para 31] Head-count figures conflict: 237 complainants, 198 authorisation letters, 39 workers attending the hearings. [Para 74][Para 75][Para 76] The issue 4 heading claims 1–4 September, while the reasoning divides 1–3 from 4; and the 100 per cent figure degenerates into "4100 per cent" at one point in the panel's question. [Para 209][Para 211][Para 213] Individual worker and union names are not reproduced here.
