# Award No. 286/14 — JSD Tech Style (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 16 December 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Sin Kim Sean, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chea, president) · Doc ID 139564

## 1. Facts
The Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers' Democratic Union (C.CAWDU) brought twenty-four claims on behalf of workers at JSD Tech Style (Cambodia), a garment factory in Takeo province employing about 860 workers, after a strike ran from 29 April to 26 May 2014 and the company barred entry and cut names from the payroll. [Para 93][Para 95][Para 151][Para 154] At the hearing the union withdrew nineteen of the twenty-four points, leaving issues 4, 5, 20, 22 and 23 for decision. [Para 40][Para 97]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must maintain wages and other benefits, including travel and accommodation allowances and the attendance bonus, during the strike from 29 April to 26 May 2014? [Para 108][Para 239]
2. Whether the company must refrain from dismissing or terminating the contracts of workers who took part in the strike? [Para 115][Para 259]
3. Whether the company must take back the twenty-four workers refused entry on 21 May 2014 and pay them back wages from the date of the stoppage? [Para 122][Para 267]
4. Whether the company must withdraw its criminal complaint No. 175 of 24 May 2014 filed at the Takeo Provincial Court? [Para 155][Para 269]
5. Whether the company must take back the thirty-one workers it dismissed during and after the strike and pay them back wages? [Para 160][Para 274]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** travel and accommodation allowances and the attendance bonus are not wages and should not be deducted during a strike; the employer ignored the court's interim protection order, hired about 140 replacements and dismissed workers for striking. [Para 109][Para 111][Para 116]
**Employer (respondent):** it challenged the union's standing, the authenticity of the transfer-of-rights documents and the regularity of the complaint records, and asked the panel to close the file. [Para 103][Para 219][Para 59]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — every remaining claim was refused; no sum in riel or USD was ordered. [Para 276][Para 277][Para 278][Para 279][Para 280]

On standing, the panel held that under Article 268 of the Labour Law a union acquires legal personality and the associated rights only once its statutes and the list of its officers are registered with the Ministry of Labour; the local union at this factory held no such registration, so C.CAWDU could not represent its members. [Para 202][Para 204][Para 207][Para 208] The panel further found the transfer-of-rights instrument of 7 May 2014 defective — fingerprints and staff numbers only, no names — and, when ordered to file a corrected list, the union did not do so. [Para 210][Para 213] The complaint records for issues 20 and 23 were rejected outright because receipt numbers were dated before the documents they purported to receive, an impossibility the panel treated as tainting the whole set. [Para 221][Para 222][Para 230][Para 232] Only the nine workers who were original complainants on issues 4 and 5 were properly represented. [Para 233][Para 238]

On issue 4, Article 332(1) suspends the employment contract during a strike and Article 72(1) suspends the duty to pay wages; following Awards 49/05 and 27/10, the panel held strikers cannot recover wages for strike days whether or not the strike was lawful. [Para 239][Para 241][Para 242][Para 244] Article 334, which obliges an employer that hires replacements to pay the strikers, was read as applying only where the strike itself followed the legal procedure, and the union had neither pleaded nor proved compliance with Articles 319, 320 and 324, nor that replacements were in fact hired. [Para 245][Para 251][Para 256][Para 257]

On issue 5 the union failed to identify the roughly one hundred workers it said were threatened with termination, so the burden of proof was not discharged and the claim was refused. [Para 264][Para 265] On issues 20 and 23 the same lack of representation was fatal. [Para 267][Para 274] On issue 22 the panel held it had no jurisdiction: an order that a party withdraw a criminal complaint is not a labour dispute under Article 309. [Para 271][Para 272]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union with no registration of its statutes and officers at the Ministry of Labour has no standing to bring or continue claims for its members, and a transfer-of-rights document listing only fingerprints and staff numbers, without names, does not cure that defect; separately, wages are not payable for days on which workers strike, and the employer's duty under Article 334 to pay strikers arises only where the strike was conducted according to the procedure laid down by law. [Para 208][Para 213][Para 244][Para 251]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that Article 334's payment duty is confined to lawful strikes is stated as a qualification on a provision the case did not otherwise reach, since no replacements were proved; the general observation that a party asserting a fact bears the burden of proving it is likewise background reasoning. [Para 255][Para 251]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is dated 16 December 2014 in the header and the parties' extension of the deadline to that date is recorded, but the reasons never reconcile the earlier 11 November date. [Para 1][Para 43] The president is named Pen Bun Chea in the header and at [Para 205] signature block, yet a stray line in the same block prints a different name, Thai Bun He; the worker arbitrator appears as Sin Kim Sean throughout. [Para 1][Para 283] The claim count shifts between 23 points in the union's letters, 24 points in the conciliation and 19 withdrawn. [Para 33][Para 62][Para 104] At [Para 207] the panel refers to a union at "Khip Thap Sport Thing Goods (Cambodia)", a factory not party to this case, evidently carried over from a template. Pages are missing from the 24-worker list, leaving entries 14 and 15 as "name not found". [Para 124][Para 138] The claimed numbers are also stated inconsistently, the same grievance being framed as 24 workers at [Para 21] and 31 at [Para 160][Para 274].
