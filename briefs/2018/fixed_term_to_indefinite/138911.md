# Award No. 027/18 — Wai Olet Ei Pheril (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 31 August 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy appointed by the employer, Tuon Si Phan appointed by the workers, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 138911

## 1. Facts
Wai Olet Ei Pheril (Cambodia) Ltd, a garment factory in Sen Sok district, Phnom Penh employing about 1,400 workers, moves workers temporarily to other workstations to cover colleagues on leave, and paid full wages for sickness absence only to take back the social-security daily allowance the worker had received. [Para 60][Para 67][Para 259] A petition signed by 723 workers on 29 May 2018 raised seven demands; four survived conciliation: transfers, the 70 per cent sickness allowance, annual-leave compensation and the IE team's quotas. [Para 15][Para 61][Para 17]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must obtain the worker's agreement before transferring her to another workstation, preserve her wages and benefits, rotate the assignments and fix a clear end date? [Para 2][Para 66][Para 167]
2. Whether the employer must pay the 70 per cent daily allowance under the National Social Security Fund scheme for sickness and employment injury certified by a doctor, in addition to the wages due under the internal regulation? [Para 4][Para 91][Para 219]
3. Whether the employer must recalculate annual-leave compensation correctly and pay the balance for the last three years? [Para 6][Para 128][Para 273]
4. Whether the employer must stop imposing output quotas and involve worker representatives in the IE assessment of the number and price of lots? [Para 8][Para 136][Para 286]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** a transfer must not cost them wages or skill — workers paid by output lose earnings on an unfamiliar model, and some have been left in the substitute post indefinitely, singled out by a team leader who dislikes them. [Para 70][Para 72][Para 76][Para 77] The employer keeps the 70 per cent allowance paid for them although the internal regulation promises full wages in the first month of illness; the IE team sets quotas from the fastest workers and later raises the quota without raising the rate. [Para 97][Para 140][Para 141]
**Employer (respondent):** the transfers are occasional cover on a flow-line, made by picking the most skilled, and most of those moved earn more than before, the minimum wage being topped up where they do not. [Para 78][Para 80][Para 84] The 70 per cent is a Fund benefit and the internal regulation predates the scheme; the IE team alone has the expertise and works from the average, not the fastest. [Para 102][Para 105][Para 145][Para 146]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — on issue 1 wages and benefits must be preserved whenever a worker is moved to cover another, the demand for rotation and a fixed end date being dismissed; on issue 2 the withheld 70 per cent allowance must be repaid to the 24 workers in Annex 2; issues 3 and 4 were dismissed. [Para 310][Para 311][Para 312][Para 313]

On jurisdiction, the employer argued the sickness claim was an individual dispute. Applying Article 302, the panel held that 723 signatories made it collective, the third condition being met because a dispute over a shared condition of work affecting so many workers can disturb the enterprise and social peace. [Para 229][Para 231][Para 237][Para 238] Matching the petition against the Fund's payment list, it found only 24 of the 62 claimants had signed, and decided the issue for those 24 only. [Para 242][Para 247]

On issue 1, Article 2 gives the employer the right to move workers within the enterprise, but only subject to four conditions drawn from earlier awards: no reduction of wages, no day/night shift switch, no transfer to work requiring substantially different skills, and no transfer to a distant location. [Para 177][Para 178] Both parties agreed the moves were occasional cover on a flow-line, not permanent reassignment. [Para 179] The panel found the employer had produced no payroll figures supporting its assertion that most transferred workers earned the same or more, although it carried that burden, and it accepted the workers' evidence that the new models were harder and output-based pay fell. [Para 191][Para 193][Para 196] The first condition was therefore breached and wages and benefits must be preserved during such moves. [Para 199][Para 204] Because no complaint was raised about shift or location the panel did not consider those conditions, and it rejected rotation and a fixed timetable as beyond what the law requires. [Para 181][Para 218]

On issue 2, Prakas No. 184 K.B/Pr.K.B.S of 25 April 2018 gives a worker who has rested on medical advice for more than seven consecutive days 70 per cent of average daily wage, payable by the Fund. [Para 249][Para 250] The internal regulation, however, promises 100 per cent wages for sickness absence in the first month, without exception, and was never amended after the new Prakas. [Para 254][Para 263] The Prakas nowhere says that receiving the Fund allowance extinguishes a more favourable contractual benefit, so paying 100 per cent and then netting off the 70 per cent was unlawful; repayment was ordered under article 34 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 262][Para 264][Para 267]

On issue 3, Article 167(2) allows annual-leave compensation only where the contract is cut short before the leave is taken, and Article 167(3) makes void any agreement to pay compensation in lieu of leave or to waive the right. [Para 274][Para 276] Compensation is payable only when the employment relationship ends, so the employer's yearly payments to serving workers were contrary to Article 167 and nothing was owed for the previous three years. [Para 280][Para 283]

On issue 4, Article 2 lets the employer use an IE team to fix the number and price of lots lawfully and reasonably, and Article 108 requires that a worker of average skill be able to earn at least the minimum wage. [Para 288][Para 290] The panel found trial production preceded the quotas, that they were fixed from the average of the trial workers rather than the fastest, and that quotas already in production were never raised; the workers produced no contrary evidence. [Para 297][Para 303][Para 308]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may move a worker temporarily within the enterprise under Article 2, but the move is unlawful if it reduces the worker's pay: where the work is paid by output and the substitute post is harder, the employer must keep the worker's wages and benefits at their previous level, and the employer — not the worker — bears the burden of proving with payroll evidence that transferred workers earn the same or more. [Para 177][Para 194][Para 204] Where the Fund pays a 70 per cent daily allowance, an employer that has promised full wages for the first month of sickness in an internal regulation still in force may not set that allowance off against the promised wage; the more favourable internal benefit survives. [Para 255][Para 262][Para 266]

**Obiter dicta:** the analysis of Article 302's three conditions, and the observation that agreements waiving annual leave are void under Article 167(3), are general statements of law made while deciding jurisdiction and issue 3. [Para 231][Para 278]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The employer's name appears in at least six forms — "Wai Olet Ei Pheril", "Wai Olet E Pheril", "AN Wai Olet Ei Pheril", "Wai Olet Ei Pherin", "Wai Olet E Kel" — one Khmer string misread by OCR [Para 1][Para 12][Para 59][Para 314]; the case-number form is used above.
- The amending Prakas is cited as No. 184 K.B/Pr.K.B.S of 25 April 2018 amending Prakas No. 109 of 17 June 2016 [Para 34], but at [Para 249] the parent Prakas is dated 17 March 2016. Both are reported, neither corrected.
- The number claiming the sickness allowance is given as 63 by the workers [Para 117][Para 241], 62 on their table [Para 122] and 58 on the employer's list [Para 123]; the panel decided for 24. The order at [Para 267] misprints the rate as "7000 per cent".
- The header names Tuon Si Phan as the worker-appointed arbitrator [Para 1], but the signature block at [Para 314] leaves that line blank.
- Paragraphs [314]–[561] reproduce the original petition forms in badly corrupted form, with names and identity numbers reduced to fragments.
- The award is non-binding, final absent objection within 08 days [Para 313].
