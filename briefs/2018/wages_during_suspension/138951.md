# Award No. 047/17 — Gawon Apparel Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 12 January 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (appointed by the employer), Sin Kim Sean (appointed by the workers), Pen Bun Chhea (president) · Doc ID 138951

## 1. Facts
The respondent is a garment factory in Ta Khmao, Kandal, employing about 940 workers, of whom 943 (828 women) were the subject of a suspension request. Following a strike on 13 November 2017, the employer suspended the workers' contracts from 13 November 2017 to 12 January 2018 and paid each worker a housing allowance of 17 US dollars a month during the suspension. Conciliation on six points failed on 8 December 2017; at the hearing the parties settled issues 1, 5 and 6 by agreement and the workers withdrew issues 3 and 4, leaving only issue 2. The parties agreed the award would be binding. [Para 74][Para 79][Para 80][Para 25][Para 26][Para 27]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay 100% of wages and 100% of other benefits to the workers for the period of the suspension, which the workers say was imposed without authorisation from the labour inspector? [Para 4][Para 79]

That was the sole surviving issue; the panel recorded that the remaining claims had been settled or withdrawn and that it would determine issue 2 only. [Para 25][Para 26]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer suspended the contracts without any authorisation letter from the labour inspector and without the agreement of the workers; the workers were not told of any authorisation at the time of the suspension, the letter was never posted on the notice board, and they learned of it for the first time at the hearing. [Para 81][Para 84][Para 85][Para 86]
**Employer (respondent):** It held meetings with the relevant worker delegates before suspending the contracts, and the labour inspector issued a written authorisation permitting suspension from 13 November 2017 to 12 January 2018; it did not post the letter because it feared workers would wrongly believe everyone's contract had been suspended, whereas suspensions were applied section by section on different dates. [Para 81][Para 87][Para 88]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the claim for 100% of wages and other benefits for 13 November 2017 to 12 January 2018 was rejected, and no sum was ordered. The award is binding on the parties. [Para 131][Para 132]

The panel applied Article 71§11 of the Labour Law, which allows suspension of enterprise activity for serious material or economic difficulty for no more than two months, subject to supervision by the labour inspectorate, and Article 72§1, under which a properly effected suspension relieves the employer of the wage obligation while an irregular one obliges payment of 100% of wages and benefits even where no work is provided. It followed its earlier awards (Nos 58/07, 08/07, 72/05, 22/05, 198/13, 160/14) holding that prior authorisation from the labour inspector is the condition of lawfulness. [Para 113][Para 115][Para 118][Para 121][Para 122]

On the evidence the panel found letter No. 3055 of the Kandal Provincial Department of Labour and Vocational Training dated 10 November 2017, recording receipt of the director's request to suspend 943 workers, 828 of them women, for two months from 13 November 2017 to 12 January 2018. It held the employer had therefore complied with Articles 71 and 72, so no 100% payment was owed. [Para 89][Para 125][Para 127][Para 128]

The panel added that an employer who obtains written authorisation must nevertheless post and publicise that letter so that the workers in the factory are informed of it, and noted that this had not been done. [Para 129][Para 130]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of employment contracts is lawful, and extinguishes the employer's duty to pay wages and benefits, where the labour inspector has given prior written authorisation for a period of no more than two months; the agreement of the workers or of their representatives is not a further condition of lawfulness, so a claim for 100% wages based solely on the absence of worker consent fails. [Para 118][Para 122][Para 127]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's statement that an employer holding a written authorisation must post and publicise it for the workers' information, and its observation that this was not done here, did not affect the outcome, which rested on the existence of the authorisation. [Para 129][Para 130]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award gives three different end-dates for the same suspension: 25 December 2017 at [Para 79], 12 January 2018 at [Para 89] and [Para 125], and "12 January 2017" at [Para 82] — a year earlier than the start date. The suspension memorandum at [Para 38] is dated 8 November 2017 yet covers a period beginning 13 November 2017, two days after the authorisation letter of 10 November 2017, and the sequencing of the two documents cannot be reconciled from the text. The Prakas number is printed as "1099" at [Para 14] where the rest of the document uses 099. The employer-appointed arbitrator's name is lost at [Para 133] to OCR garbage ("manump ... The states"), though Ing Sothy appears on the face of the award at [Para 1]. Worker representatives are identified here by role, not by name; the letter of authorisation at [Para 89] and its quotation at [Para 125] are the same document printed twice with minor differences.
