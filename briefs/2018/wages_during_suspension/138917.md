# Award No. 040/18 — YLT International Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 7 December 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ri, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Koy Neam, president, appointed by both) · Doc ID 138917

## 1. Facts
YLT International Co., Ltd operates a factory at Sathya village, Romchek commune, Preah Sdech district, Prey Veng province, employing 245 workers, 123 of them women [Para 56][Para 57]. Of 12 demands referred after conciliation, 9 were settled and 3 came before the panel [Para 13]. The company had never allowed annual leave between 2015 and 2017, and its contracts with workers were fixed at six months each [Para 82][Para 126]. A strike was suspended by Order No. 003/18 of 26 October 2018, which the workers obeyed [Para 17][Para 18].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay cash in lieu of the annual leave it did not grant from 2015 to 2017, calculated on each worker's seniority? [Para 119]
2. Whether the employer must pay 5% termination indemnity at the expiry of each fixed-term contract? [Para 144]
3. Whether the employer must remove (dismiss) Mr S, its administration manager, from his post? [Para 170]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The Labour Law requires 18 days of annual leave a year, which the employer never granted from 2015 onwards, so it must pay compensation in lieu for each year worked [Para 70][Para 71]. It must also pay 5% at each fixed-term expiry, and dismiss the administration manager for dishonesty, abusive language and threats, for obliging representatives to thumbprint away their demands, for overcharging on workbooks, and for refusing sick leave [Para 83][Para 90][Para 92][Para 94][Para 95].

**Employer (respondent):** Leave for 2015–2017 will be paid in cash according to actual seniority, but the company currently lacks profit and cannot pay immediately [Para 65]. Workers engaged in 2014–2015 were daily-paid, not permanent staff, so no leave is owed for those years [Para 72]. It cannot dismiss the manager, who holds a contract and performs his role properly, and the workbook service charge did not exceed what the Labour Department invoices [Para 96][Para 99].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel refused cash in lieu of leave but ordered the employer to grant the leave days it withheld from 2015 to 2017 so that workers may take them according to actual seniority; it refused to reconsider the 5% claim and refused the demand to dismiss the administration manager [Para 198][Para 199].

On issue 1 the panel applied Articles 166(1) and (4) and 167(1) of the Labour Law: leave accrues at one and a half working days per month of continuous service, plus one day for each three years worked, giving 19 days in years 4 to 6, 20 days in years 7 to 9, and 21 days in years 10 to 12 [Para 120][Para 123]. Having found that no leave was granted from 2015 to 2017, it held that Article 167(2) and (3) permit payment in lieu only when the contract is severed, and that an agreement to forgo leave or to accept cash instead is void [Para 130][Para 131][Para 132]. Paying cash while the employment relationship subsists is contrary to Article 167 even where the employer consents, so the panel refused that head of claim and ordered the days themselves to be restored [Para 138][Para 140]. On issue 2 it applied the res judicata principle the Council has adopted in earlier awards: where the same parties litigated the same issue and the panel decided it fully, the point will not be reheard [Para 150][Para 153]. It found all three conditions met against case No. 012/18, in which the identical 5% claim was refused, the only difference being the identity of the worker representatives, and declined to consider the claim again [Para 162][Para 166][Para 168]. On issue 3 it relied on Article 65 of the Labour Law and Articles 664 and 311 of the Civil Code to hold that only the parties to a contract may vary or extinguish it, so the workers, as outsiders to the manager's contract, cannot demand his dismissal [Para 177][Para 179][Para 183]. It added that Article 2 leaves the employer free to organise and manage the enterprise lawfully and reasonably, that an order to dismiss a manager is an internal management matter, and that the workers had produced no evidence that Mr S was a danger to anyone [Para 188][Para 194][Para 195].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Annual leave is a right to rest, not to money: while the employment contract subsists the employer must grant the accrued days, and neither its own consent nor the worker's can convert them into a cash payment, which Article 167 permits only on severance [Para 132][Para 138]. Where the same parties have already litigated an identical claim and the panel decided it on the merits, the claim will not be reheard; and workers who are strangers to a manager's employment contract cannot require the employer to dismiss him without evidence that he endangers them [Para 168][Para 183][Para 194].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remarks that dismissal of a section or team head is an internal management arrangement best left to the employer were not necessary, since the claim had already failed for want of contractual standing and evidence [Para 189].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel's own leave calculation is internally inconsistent: it states the entitlement as "20, 1.5 days per month or 18 days per year" at [Para 128], whereas Article 166 yields 18 days a year and the graduated scale at [Para 123] runs 19, 20 and 21 days. The company's notice gives its opening date as 30 March 2015 while describing workers engaged from 1 October 2014, and the award repeats both [Para 75]. The company's name is corrupted in places to "YT International" in its own notifications [Para 77]. Worker representative names are printed inconsistently as "Loeng Sophin" and "Tieng Sophin" at [Para 59] and [Para 115]. Long stretches of untranslated OCR debris interrupt the reasoning at [Para 125], [Para 135] and [Para 143], and the operative order at [Para 200] repeats a clause about objections. The factory's product line is lost at [Para 56].
