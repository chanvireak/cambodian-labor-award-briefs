# Award No. 036/18 — Evergreen I Pearl (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 2 October 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; An Non, appointed by the workers; Tang Tri, president, appointed by both) · Doc ID 138916

## 1. Facts
Evergreen I Pearl (Cambodia) is a garment-cutting company at Chong Thnal Khang Cheung village, Tuek Thla commune, Sen Sok district, Phnom Penh, employing about 500 workers [Para 45][Para 46]. Because orders fell away the company suspended the work of several groups from late July 2018 and notified workers that those who resigned would receive a support payment of 100 USD per year, while those who stayed could keep working [Para 51][Para 66]. The workers' demand was one of 200 USD per year, calculated on seniority, for workers who leave [Para 50]. Of the workforce, 337 held indefinite-term contracts with seniority of 2 to 16 years and transferred authority to the six recognised worker representatives [Para 58][Para 59].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to pay 200 USD per year, calculated on seniority, to workers who leave their employment? [Para 89]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The company pays only 5% termination indemnity of about 30 USD per fixed-term contract to workers on three-month contracts, while the 100 USD per year offered on seniority is less than the 5% indemnity those workers are owed [Para 60][Para 61]. The sums the company removed from the premises were merely left behind by workers who had resigned [Para 62].

**Employer (respondent):** It cannot meet the demand: no law provides for 100 USD per year for departing workers, and it pays that amount under agreement with the workers and continues to honour the agreement [Para 63][Para 64]. The machinery taken out belonged to workers who had resigned [Para 62].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider the workers' claim for 200 USD per year on seniority, leaving the company's voluntary payment of 100 USD per year untouched [Para 114][Para 116].

The panel examined the notices of 24 August, 3 September, 4 September and 8 September 2018 and the course of dealing, and found no provision of the Labour Law, a collective agreement, the internal rules, an employment contract, an agreement between the parties, or past practice obliging the employer to pay 200 USD per year on seniority to workers who resign [Para 96]. It characterised the demand as one beyond what the law fixes, and therefore an interest dispute (វិវាទ អំពី ផលប្រយោជន៍) [Para 97]. Applying Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, it reasoned that an award in an interest dispute replaces a collective agreement, applies to all workers in the company, and extinguishes their right to bargain and to strike over the same interest in future [Para 108][Para 109]. Because of that erga omnes effect, the panel held it may adjudicate an interest dispute only where the claimant union is most-representative, or where several local unions together hold the right to conclude a collective agreement under Articles 54 and 72 of the Law on Trade Unions [Para 110]. It confirmed the six representatives had authority to act for the 337 workers, but found the claimant was neither a most-representative union nor a union possessing the qualifying characteristics for bringing an interest dispute [Para 87][Para 113]. It therefore refused to consider the claim, consistent with its earlier awards cited by number [Para 111][Para 112].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a union claims a benefit that no law, collective agreement, internal rule, contract or practice provides, the claim is an interest dispute, and the panel will not adjudicate it at the suit of a union that is not most-representative: an interest award operates as a collective agreement binding every worker in the enterprise, so only a union with the statutory standing to conclude such an agreement may bring the claim [Para 97][Para 110][Para 113].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel set out the Article 54 criteria for most-representative status, including the 30% membership threshold and the fallback to a support vote, but did not apply them to any union, having found the claimant unqualified on other grounds [Para 103][Para 113].

The award is dated 2 October 2018 on its face, yet the parties' agreed extension of the issue date runs from 01 October 2018 to 12 October 2018 in the same document [Para 22]. The company's notices are inconsistent on the amount: 85 USD per year appears in the notice quoted at [Para 67], while the notices at [Para 26], [Para 69] and [Para 94] state 100 USD. The company name is printed in at least six forms across the file ("Ever Green I Pearl", "Evergreen I Pearl", "Evergreen Green I Pearl"), and the claimant description at [Para 10] ends in a stray numeral [Para 10][Para 46]. The workforce is given as about 500 at [Para 46] but 337 workers hold indefinite contracts at [Para 58]; both figures are reported here as printed.
