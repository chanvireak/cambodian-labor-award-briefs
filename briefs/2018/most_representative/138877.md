# Award No. 003/18 — Kennetex International (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 7 February 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Vireak (worker-appointed), Aing Eng Thong (president) · Doc ID 138877

## 1. Facts
The respondent is a garment-cutting and sewing company with 421 workers, 150 of them involved in the dispute. On 21 November 2017 the Khmer Workers' Spirit Union Federation complained of six points; conciliation on 3 January 2018 settled two and left four, which reached the Council on 9 January 2018. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 18][Para 48][Para 51] All workers hold three-month fixed-duration contracts, and in 2017 the employer declined to renew a number of them and suspended contracts for three months (July to September). [Para 55][Para 56][Para 60] The union was registered on 17 August 2017 and its three office-holders were recognised. At the hearing the parties agreed on point 3, leaving points 1, 2 and 4. [Para 49][Para 74][Para 76]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to stop declining to renew workers' fixed-duration employment contracts? [Para 104]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to terminate all workers' contracts at once and pay severance and other benefits under the Labour Law, where it continues to terminate contracts without cause? [Para 124]
4. Whether the employer is obliged to pay a lunch allowance of 3,000 riel per day to workers? [Para 133]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** workers fear losing their jobs because the employer has ended many contracts abnormally, while giving work to outside companies instead; they want all contracts ended at once with statutory severance so that nobody is left exposed. [Para 58][Para 62][Para 69][Para 71]
**Employer (respondent):** orders are few and buyers impose deadlines, so it suspended contracts and reduced staff rather than leave workers idle; it will not close the factory, will pay statutory severance if it terminates, and will take workers back when work returns; anyone who wishes to leave may apply. [Para 59][Para 63][Para 66][Para 72][Para 73]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — points 1 and 2 were refused on the merits and point 4 was not considered at all: no order to renew contracts, no order to terminate all contracts and pay severance, and no 3,000 riel lunch allowance. [Para 156][Para 157][Para 158]

On point 1 the panel applied Article 73(1) of the Labour Law, that a fixed-duration contract expires naturally when its term is reached, and its earlier reading that expiry is automatic and extinguishes both parties' obligations; Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code confirm that a contract is formed, modified or extinguished only by the parties' mutual consent. It followed that neither party may compel the other to continue a contract without agreement, and that the employer, as a party, may decide whether or not to renew a fixed-term contract according to its will. [Para 104][Para 105][Para 106][Para 118][Para 121][Para 122][Para 123]

On point 2 the panel treated the demand as raising the same question of formation, modification and dissolution of contracts, and held that the workers cannot force the employer to dissolve all fixed-term contracts at once and pay statutory severance against its will; the employer had confirmed it did not intend to close, so no such obligation arose. [Para 124][Para 126][Para 128][Para 129]

On point 4 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, employment contract, agreement between the parties or past practice requiring a 3,000 riel daily lunch allowance; the existing practice was 1,500 riel (1,000 riel in 2016). The demand accordingly exceeded what the law fixes and was an interest dispute. [Para 77][Para 78][Para 134][Para 135] Because an award on an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement for one year under Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099, and because no evidence showed the company had a most-representative union, the panel held that the claimants were not a union with the standing to bring such a dispute and declined to consider the merits. [Para 148][Para 150][Para 153][Para 154]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-duration employment contract expires automatically at the end of its term, and neither employer nor worker can be compelled to renew it or to dissolve it early and pay severance in the absence of agreement; a demand for a benefit above what the Labour Law, a collective agreement, internal rules, the contract or established practice provides is an interest dispute, which a panel will not hear unless brought by a most-representative union. [Para 106][Para 122][Para 151]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that the employer had offered to take workers back when orders return and that any worker wishing to leave may apply to the employer directly were not necessary to any of the three refusals. [Para 66][Para 131]
