# Award No. 008/18 — Kos Mo Tech Style Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 13 March 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouchhun, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Kong Phalha, chair) · Doc ID 138882

## 1. Facts
The company runs a garment factory in Ang Snuol district, Kandal province, employing about 701 workers. On 9 February 2018, 326 workers struck; conciliation on 9 issues failed and the case reached the Council on 13 February 2018. At the hearing of 26 February 2018 the workers withdrew issues 2, 3 and 4, the parties settled issues 5–8 and part of issue 1 (the union president and deputy president), leaving two claims: the reinstatement of a worker who was not a claimant, and the removal of two administrative staff. [Para 13][Para 15][Para 16][Para 36][Para 37][Para 39]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the union president, the union deputy president and a worker (all names redacted) and pay their wages from the date they were stopped until the present? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company must provide meal money and a bonus to the workers? [Para 2]
3. Whether the company must pay "skill money" to all workers, whether or not they are skilled? [Para 3]
4. Whether the company must add 3,000 riel to the midday meal allowance? [Para 3]
5. Whether the company must make it easier to obtain leave for personal business? [Para 3]
6. Whether the company must add overtime work for the workers? [Para 4]
7. Whether the company must stop raising the daily production target (ថាហ្គេត)? [Para 5]
8. Whether administrative staff, section heads and team leaders must use appropriate language towards workers when they take leave, when they arrive late and when they ask for medicine? [Para 6]
9. Whether the director must remove two named administrative staff (names redacted) from the company? [Para 7]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** said the two administrative staff had imposed harsh rules — recording how long workers spend in the toilet in order to issue warnings, tightening the paperwork for collection boxes for workers with sick or deceased relatives, and discriminating against the union — and that the dismissed worker had three or four years' service and lost his job because of a traffic accident. [Para 113][Para 140][Para 141][Para 142]
**Employer (respondent):** said the two staff did not apply the rules harshly, that the section head merely reports attendance, that the adviser (Mr M, name redacted) seldom attends the factory, and that decisions are not taken by the head of administration alone; it also objected that the workers present had no authority to represent the non-claimant worker and that the union's leaders had lost their mandate. [Para 100][Para 106][Para 143][Para 171]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider the reinstatement claim and rejected the demand that the two administrative staff be removed; the remaining seven issues having been withdrawn or settled by agreement, no order was made on them. [Para 198][Para 224][Para 225][Para 226]

On the representation objection the panel applied Article 54(1) of the Law on Trade Unions and Prakas No. 362: since no most-representative union existed at the company, workers recognised by the provincial labour department (letter No. 0016 of 12 February 2018) could represent the dispute. It held that no provision makes the absence of two of the three recognised representatives fatal, that 343 workers had signed the request of 9 February 2018 and over 300 still pressed it, and that a withdrawal of confidence by 17 workers did not remove the representatives' mandate for the rest. [Para 167][Para 174][Para 177][Para 181][Para 183][Para 184]

On issue 1, however, the panel applied Article 19 of Prakas No. 099: a party may appear in person or through a person authorised in writing. The worker in question did not appear in the 343-name request, had signed no authorisation, and was absent from the conciliation and the hearing. No one present therefore had a lawful mandate to represent him, and he was not a claimant in the case — so his claim could not be heard. Earlier awards (No. 014/16 Hong Sen Tech Styal, 031/16 Ho Hsin Tay) had refused reinstatement for the same reason. [Para 185][Para 192][Para 194][Para 195][Para 197][Para 198]

On issue 9 the panel applied Articles 65 of the Labour Law and 664 and 311 of the Civil Code: an employment contract binds only its parties, and the workers were third parties to the contracts between the employer and the two staff. Under Article 2 of the Labour Law the employer's right to organise and manage the enterprise stands unless exercised unlawfully or unreasonably; earlier awards (No. 62/06, 108/06, 33/07, 156/13 Pou Yuen, 201/14) held the panel cannot order the removal of a worker or supervisor, that being an internal management matter. Finding no evidence of conduct creating concrete job insecurity, the panel rejected the claim. [Para 206][Para 210][Para 214][Para 215][Para 222][Para 223]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker whose name and fingerprint do not appear in the collective claim, who has given no written authorisation to any representative, and who is absent from the hearing is not a claimant before the panel, and the panel will not hear his individual demand — even where the recognised representatives otherwise have standing for the collective dispute. And workers who are third parties to another person's employment contract cannot obtain an order removing that person: the panel will only intervene where the evidence shows conduct creating concrete insecurity of employment, not merely strict internal rules. [Para 194][Para 195][Para 210][Para 217][Para 223]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the presence of other workers at the hearing does not enlarge the mandate of the recognised representatives; that the head of administration is himself an employee of the employer and acts only with the employer's approval and under the internal rules; and that the worker who claims harsh treatment must show a specific resulting insecurity. [Para 106][Para 174][Para 221][Para 222]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is badly OCR-damaged. The panel's query at para 160 and the recitals of the settlement at paras 130–138 are fragmentary, and Latin-script garbage interrupts paras 86 and 160 ("orye aun again and been greterne iong: abooks"). Numbers do not reconcile: 326 workers struck at para 13, about 200 absent at para 19, about 545 back at work with 100 absent at para 23, and about 50 absent at para 33. The withdrawn-confidence letter is described as signed by 17 workers at para 179 but the panel's reasoning at para 181 prints "១៧២៥" (1,725). Paragraph 160 cites the prior award as both 188/16 and 183/16. The company name appears as ខូស ម៉ូ តិច ស្តាយ and ខូស ម៉ូតូ, rendered here as Kos Mo Tech Style Co., Ltd.; the employer's registration is given as MOC-21156346 (para 43) and as Inv. 1392 E/2008 (para 87). Individual workers and staff are referred to by initial.
