# Award No. 030/18 — Ken Sport Shoe (Arbitration Council, 6 September 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy appointed by the employer, An Nan appointed by the workers, Tang Try chair) · Doc ID 138909

## 1. Facts
Ken Sport Shoe, a shoe factory in Samaki Mean Chey district, Kampong Chhnang province employing about 7,250 workers, changed its practice on sick leave: where from 2015 to 2017 it accepted any doctor's certificate for one paid day off, from 2018 it accepted only certificates stating the number of days of rest and issued by hospitals cooperating with the National Social Security Fund. [Para 38][Para 44][Para 45] The Free Women Workers' Union of Ken Sport Shoe, registered on 26 September 2017 under No. 946 K.B/KCh, raised eight demands on 7 June 2018; three remained unresolved after conciliation and came to the panel — sick-leave certificates, the one-hour breastfeeding entitlement under Article 184, and the timing of maternity leave. [Para 10][Para 11][Para 39]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must accept a medical certificate or a doctor's prescription when a worker is ill? [Para 44][Para 90]
2. Whether the employer must let a woman who has given birth leave work one hour early, from 3:00 to 4:00 p.m., to breastfeed? [Para 54][Para 103]
3. Whether the employer must let a pregnant worker start maternity leave at the time she herself requests? [Para 63][Para 122]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** from 2015 to 2017 any doctor's certificate secured one paid day off, and in 2018 the employer began refusing prescriptions for minor illness such as stomach ache or diarrhoea, which doctors issue without a rest certificate; the employer's own doctor lacks the equipment to examine them, and women cannot bring infants to the factory because they commute by shared van. [Para 44][Para 47][Para 51][Para 58][Para 107]
**Employer (respondent):** sick leave must rest on a certificate bearing a doctor's name, signature and the stamp of a hospital cooperating with the National Social Security Fund; an outside prescription can be shown to the company doctor for assessment; it cannot grant the specific 3:00–4:00 p.m. hour because it must first discuss timing with the other unions, and it cannot fix maternity leave at the worker's request because the law sets no period and late departure risks delivery inside the factory. [Para 48][Para 50][Para 61][Para 68][Para 70]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 1 partly granted, issue 2 partly granted and partly dismissed, issue 3 granted. The panel ordered the employer to apply point 4.2.6 of its own internal regulation and accept every certificate issued by a doctor of a lawful, registered hospital bearing the doctor's name or signature; dismissed the demand for the breastfeeding hour at the end of the working day but ordered one hour within working hours under Article 184; and ordered the employer to stop requiring maternity leave to begin one month before delivery, allowing each worker to request the start date herself. [Para 119][Para 137][Para 138][Para 139][Para 140][Para 141]

On standing, the panel held that the provincial Department of Labour's certification of three worker representatives on 30 July 2018 under Prakas No. 362 gave them authority to act, since both parties accepted the factory has no most-representative union, and Prakas No. 303 article 16 provides that where there is none, collective disputes are handled by worker representatives. [Para 40][Para 43][Para 78][Para 83][Para 88]

On issue 1, the panel found the employer's practice had no basis in the Labour Law and could deter workers from choosing their own health provider, exposing them to loss of wages and of the attendance bonus if their certificate did not come from an NSSF partner hospital. [Para 95][Para 96] Point 4.2.6 of the internal regulation, approved by the Department of Labour on 10 June 2016, recognises certificates from a doctor of any lawful registered hospital bearing the doctor's name or signature, and requires no NSSF link; the employer had therefore departed from its own regulation and was ordered to apply it. [Para 97][Para 99][Para 100][Para 102]

On issue 2, Article 184 gives a woman one hour a day within working hours to breastfeed for one year after birth, split into two thirty-minute periods at times she agrees with the employer, failing which at the midpoint of each work period. [Para 103][Para 104][Para 105] The panel found there was no agreement on timing, that the factory's nursery was an empty room with no carer or equipment, and that the workers' commuting arrangements made bringing an infant impractical — so the choice of where to breastfeed was the woman's, and the law nowhere requires feeding inside the factory. [Para 106][Para 109][Para 113] The demand for the last hour of the day had no legal basis and was dismissed, but the employer was ordered to grant the statutory hour whether or not the child is brought to the factory, since that is a right the law confers. [Para 117][Para 119][Para 121]

On issue 3, Article 182 gives 90 days' maternity leave without fixing when it begins, and point 1.10.5.2 of the internal regulation requires a request fifteen days in advance without fixing a start date either. [Para 122][Para 124][Para 125] The employer's blanket one-month rule left the worker barely a month of rest after delivery, when she most needs it; health differs between women and only the woman herself, advised by her doctor, can say when leave should start. [Para 129][Para 130][Para 131] The panel relied on article 34 of Prakas No. 099 to order the practice stopped. [Para 132][Para 134]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may not narrow the statutory sick-leave entitlement by insisting on certificates from hospitals it has selected, or by requiring a stated number of rest days where its own approved internal regulation requires only a certificate from a doctor of a lawful registered hospital bearing the doctor's name or signature; a practice that departs from the approved internal regulation, and that would expose workers to loss of wages and attendance bonus for choosing another lawful provider, must be discontinued. Likewise, where the law fixes a duration of leave but not its starting date, the employer may not impose a uniform rule on all pregnant workers, because the start of maternity leave depends on each woman's own health and her doctor's advice. [Para 95][Para 100][Para 102][Para 129][Para 130]

**Obiter dicta:** the finding that the factory's nursery was an empty room without a carer or equipment, and the observations on shared-van commuting and on the importance of post-delivery rest, explain why the women's choice of feeding place was respected but are not part of the operative rule. [Para 106][Para 108][Para 131]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The document is headed as case No. 030/18 "Ken Sport Shoe" [Para 1], but one evidence entry names the employer "Sok Sport Shoe" [Para 34] and the union appears as both "Free Women Workers' Union" and "Free Women Cut-Sewing Workers' Union" [Para 30][Para 31]; the face of the award is used.
- The procedural chronology is internally impossible: the deadline to object to documents is given as 24 July 2018 [Para 19], before the hearing of 16 August 2018 [Para 9], and the complaint is said to have been received on 20 July 2018 though it is dated 7 June 2018 [Para 10]; the conciliation is dated 8 August 2018 [Para 11] while the report on it is dated 6 August 2018 and sent on 7 August 2018 [Para 7][Para 12].
- Paragraph [2] is heavily corrupted, carrying fragments of unrelated figures and English ("NO. 42, 511981 092 … 2016 … 2014") in place of the representative list, so the identity of two worker representatives is unreliable; the representative list is recoverable only from [Para 39] and [Para 40], where the names also differ in spelling between passages.
- Paragraph [132] reproduces article 34 of Prakas No. 099 in corrupted form with interspersed text, leaving the wording of the power to order cessation of an unlawful practice partly illegible; the order was read from the cleaner statement of it at [Para 134].
- The award is expressly non-binding and becomes final if no written objection is filed with the Minister within 08 days [Para 142], a figure that may be an OCR doubling. The signature block carries no name for the chair [Para 143].
