# Award No. 021/18 — Siem Reap International Airport (Cambodia Airports Management Services) (Arbitration Council, 31 July 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborna for the employer party, Vong Vanna for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 138897

## 1. Facts
The company operates Siem Reap International Airport through Cambodia Airports Management Services (CAMS) and employs 541 workers; there is no most representative union, and the claimant union is the Cambodia Tourism Industry Workers' Union at Siem Reap Airport, registered with the provincial department on 10 July 2017. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 78][Para 81] A collective agreement for 2014–2016 had raised the base wage by USD 25 (2014), USD 20 (2015) and USD 20 (2016) and set a USD 20 meal allowance; the union formally rejected that agreement on 22 August 2016 and asked for a new one for 2017–2019, which was never concluded. [Para 82][Para 85][Para 86][Para 95] Two workers were suspended indefinitely — Mr V after the Civil Aviation Secretariat withdrew his airport security pass over a camera theft, and Mr S after a urine test showed drugs, though he produced two later negative tests — and both remain suspended. [Para 101][Para 103][Para 120][Para 121] The union also claimed equal wages and benefits for workers supplied under a service contract through the company GSI. [Para 138][Para 139] Seven of ten conciliated points settled; three went to the panel. [Para 16][Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reapply the 2014–2016 collective agreement for a further three years, in respect of the base-wage increase and the meal allowance, or may instead apply the national bargaining council's 12% increase? [Para 2][Para 167]
2. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr V and Mr S at their original workplace and pay their wages from the date of suspension until they resume work? [Para 100][Para 177]
3. Whether the employer must give workers hired through a service contract (ការីយភារី) the same wages and benefits as its own employees? [Para 138][Para 220]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the 2014–2016 agreement stays in force until a new one replaces it and must be read as renewed during the negotiation, and the two suspensions are unjustified — one worker was never charged and the other has two clean drug tests, and neither has been convicted of anything. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 121][Para 135][Para 137]
**Employer (respondent):** the agreement lapsed at the end of 2016 because the union rejected it, and the blockage is the union's own lack of most-representative status; the airport pass withdrawal is not the company's act, so it could only suspend the contracts pending the court and the Civil Aviation Secretariat, and Article 72 means no wages are owed during suspension. [Para 91][Para 93][Para 108][Para 196][Para 198]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three claims were refused: the 2014–2016 agreement was not to be reapplied, and the reinstatement and back-wage claims for both suspended workers and the equal-treatment claim for service-contract workers were declined. [Para 227][Para 228][Para 229]

On issue 1 the panel read the agreement against Article 96(3) of the Labour Law and Article 69 of the Trade Union Law: an agreement concluded for a fixed term runs for at least three years and continues for the same term only where no party gives three months' notice of rejection before expiry. [Para 168][Para 170][Para 172] The union's letter of 22 August 2016 rejected the agreement well before the end of 2016, so the tacit renewal never operated and the agreement lapsed at its term; the panel refused to order it reapplied. [Para 174][Para 175][Para 176] On issue 2 it first answered the employer's jurisdictional objection. Applying Article 302 of the Labour Law and Article 32 of Prakas No. 099, it held the panel is limited to collective disputes, found the suspensions turned on working conditions and could disturb the enterprise, and since the employer offered no evidence that the dispute was purely individual, accepted jurisdiction. [Para 177][Para 187][Para 190][Para 192] On the merits, Article 71(7) and Article 72 permit suspension for a reasonable cause under the internal rules, and Article 19.2.3(b) of the rules of 3 May 2017 allows suspension while an investigation is pending, with wages payable if no fault is found — a rule the panel called more favourable than the law. [Para 194][Para 199][Para 205] For Mr V it found that the prosecution charged only another worker and the summons did not state whether he was a witness or a suspect, but that no court decision had yet excluded him, so the suspension could lawfully continue until the court ruled. [Para 209][Para 211][Para 212][Para 213] For Mr S it noted the two negative tests and that the employer produced no document proving drugs, but held the suspension could continue until the Civil Aviation Secretariat decided the next measure, for airport security. [Para 217][Para 218][Para 219] On issue 3 the panel never reached the merits: Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 requires written authority for anyone representing a party, the service-contract workers had given none, and none attended, so there was no identified claimant. [Para 220][Para 223][Para 225][Para 226]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-term collective agreement does not renew by tacit continuation where a party has given notice rejecting it at least three months before expiry, as Article 96(3) of the Labour Law and Article 69 of the Trade Union Law require; once so rejected it ceases at its term and cannot be ordered back into force while a replacement is negotiated. [Para 170][Para 172][Para 175] Suspension under Article 71(7) and Article 72 of the Labour Law extinguishes the employer's duty to pay wages, and where the suspension flows from a withdrawal of an airport security pass by the civil aviation authority, the employer may keep the contract suspended until that authority or the court decides, even on evidence that the worker was not charged. [Para 196][Para 198][Para 213][Para 218] A claim advanced on behalf of workers who have given no written authorisation to the representative has no claimant and will be refused without consideration of its merits. [Para 221][Para 225]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the internal rules are more favourable than the Labour Law because they restore wages where an investigation clears the worker, and its observations on airport security and passenger safety, are general and did not produce any order. [Para 205][Para 218]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- A corrigendum is printed inside the same document (paragraphs 231–239) correcting the wording of the order on pages 24 and 26, replacing "from the date of dismissal" with "from the date of suspension" and adding "at the original workplace"; the corrected wording is reported above and the corrigendum states it does not affect the other decisions. [Para 233][Para 237][Para 238]
- Paragraph 3 answers the wage claim by saying "CAMS is not a textile factory" and that its base wage is already higher than a garment factory's — reasoning evidently carried over from a garment-sector template and irrelevant to an airport operator.
- The agreement's start date is printed as 1 January 2014 in the findings and 1 January 2015 in its own clause 3.1, and the panel treats it as expiring at the end of 2016 although a three-year term from 1 January 2015 would run to the end of 2017; the panel's premise that expiry fell at end-2016 is unresolved on the face of the award. [Para 82][Para 96][Para 167][Para 174]
- The conciliation report is cited as No. 042 ម.ក.ប in the procedural history and No. 045 ម.ក.ប in the evidence list. [Para 12][Para 69]
- OCR corruption is heavy in the representation reasoning: paragraphs 156–160 are largely unreadable ("ក្រុមប្រឹក្សាអាជ្ញាតណ្តាលឃាត់", "ឧទាហរណ៍ ព្រះសីហនុ"), and paragraph 184 repeats a sentence verbatim without content.
- The claimant workers' names are printed in at least three forms each (វ៉េង សុភារុណ / វែង សុភារុណ / វើង; សុខ សុវណ្ណារី រាសី / សុខ វណ្ណារី រាសី / Sok Vannaryreasey); paragraph 121 refers to blood tests where every other passage refers to urine tests.
- The workers' party address in the header is the Arbitration Council's own secretariat address, and the company appears variously as Cambodia Airports, Cambodia Airport (VINCI/SCA/CAMS) and CAMS.
