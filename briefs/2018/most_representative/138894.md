# Award No. 018/18 — SCG Concrete Roof (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 27 June 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Kong Phalha, presiding) · Doc ID 138894

## 1. Facts
SCG Concrete Roof (Cambodia) Co., Ltd manufactures roof tiles at Chom Chao, Phnom Penh, and employed about 111 workers. [Para 2][Para 49][Para 50] The Khmer Workers' Heart Union at the company (សហជីព បេះដូង កម្មករ ខ្មែរ), registered on 26 September 2017 under certificate No. 361 K.B/V.B/VK, filed nine claims on working conditions. [Para 24][Para 38] Conciliation on 23 May 2018 settled two points and the parties settled a third (point 4, sick leave) at the hearing, leaving six for decision: base wage, lunch, housing allowance, holiday meals, attendance bonus and union-dues deduction. [Para 25][Para 30][Para 31] Both parties accepted that the company has only this one union and that the most-representative certificate it once held expired about three months earlier. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 92][Para 93]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must continue paying workers hired from 2015 a base wage USD 15 above the statutory minimum, as it does for workers hired before 31 December 2014? [Para 55][Para 106][Para 107]
2. Whether the employer must provide one meal, or 4,000 riel, to day-shift workers working 6 a.m. to 4 p.m.? [Para 62][Para 136]
3. Whether the employer must add USD 8 to the existing USD 15 monthly housing allowance? [Para 68][Para 143]
4. Whether the employer must give a meal or USD 1 to workers who work on public holidays and Sundays, beyond the 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance already paid? [Para 71][Para 149]
5. Whether the employer must pay a USD 10 attendance bonus to workers who complete 26 days? [Para 74][Para 155]
6. Whether the employer must deduct union dues from wages before paying wages, rather than collecting them afterwards in cash? [Para 77][Para 161]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Base wages are too low for daily living; neighbouring factories — including the tile maker G B Mong and the garment factories Duher S and Van Sen — provide meals, and other garment factories provide USD 15 housing allowance, so the company should match them; the 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance is not enough to buy food; and collecting dues after payday causes losses because some members do not pay. [Para 60][Para 65][Para 69][Para 72][Para 83]
**Employer (respondent):** It pays base wages according to the annual minimum-wage Prakas, and workers now receive USD 170 a month in 2018; it cannot provide meals because it already pays a 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance as the law requires; the common practice of garment factories is USD 7 housing allowance only; and it cannot change the payroll system, which sits in Bangkok, to deduct dues automatically. [Para 61][Para 66][Para 70][Para 84][Para 85][Para 113]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issues 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 refused outright, and issue 7 refused as to the timing demanded but granted as to completeness: the employer was ordered to deduct the wages of every worker named in the written request for the purpose of paying union dues to the Khmer Workers' Heart Union. [Para 178][Para 179][Para 180][Para 181][Para 182][Para 183][Para 184] The award is non-binding and becomes final 08 days after notification absent a written objection to the Minister. [Para 185]

On representation, the panel applied Article 54(1) of the Law on Trade Unions of 17 May 2016 and Article 72, following Awards 180/16 (Cement Cambodia Chakrey Ting) and 171/16 (Jewelin), that only a most representative union may conclude a collective agreement or settle a collective dispute. [Para 86][Para 89][Para 90][Para 91] It found no most-representative union at the company, since the certificate had lapsed. [Para 92][Para 93] Following Award 180/16, where no such union exists, workers may represent themselves or delegate to a local union or other person, and under point 1 of Prakas No. 362 K.B/P.K of 29 August 2016 representatives recognised by the Labour Department may act even at the Arbitration Council stage; two recognised representatives attended, so the claim was properly before it. [Para 95][Para 97][Para 100][Para 104]

On issue 1 the panel applied Articles 104 and 107(2) of the Labour Law on the guaranteed minimum wage, and found that no minimum-wage Prakas has been issued for the tile-production sector, so the employer's payment of the garment-sector rates is more favourable than the law requires; no law, collective agreement, internal rule, contract or past practice obliged a USD 15 differential, so the claim was one above the legal floor, that is a dispute of interests. [Para 107][Para 108][Para 110][Para 114][Para 115][Para 116] Because the claimant was not a most representative union, the panel declined to consider it, following Award 081/04 (Evergreen) and others. [Para 129][Para 130][Para 133][Para 134]

Issues 2, 3, 5 and 6 were decided the same way: for each, the panel found no provision of law, collective agreement, internal rules, contract, agreement or past practice creating the claimed entitlement, classified the claim as an interest dispute, and declined it for want of a most-representative union. [Para 138][Para 139][Para 144][Para 145][Para 150][Para 151][Para 156][Para 157]

On issue 7 the panel applied Article 129 of the Labour Law, under which deductions outside the permitted cases are void but a worker may authorise deduction of union dues in writing at any time and revoke it, and Article 26(1) of the Law on Trade Unions, which requires the employer to deduct dues for members who consent in writing. [Para 161][Para 162][Para 165][Para 166][Para 173] It found the employer did deduct 2,000 riel per named worker and paid the union directly, so the statutory duty was met whether collection occurred before or after payday; but it also held the employer must deduct for every worker on the written request list, which is what the order requires. [Para 167][Para 168][Para 171][Para 174][Para 175][Para 177]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim for a wage or benefit above the statutory floor is a dispute of interests, and where the enterprise has no union holding a live most-representative certificate the panel will not decide it — recognised worker representatives may bring the case, but that does not supply the standing the interest jurisdiction requires. [Para 129][Para 130][Para 133] By contrast, a claim resting on an existing statutory right is decided: where workers have authorised union-dues deduction in writing, the employer must deduct and remit dues for every worker on that list, and the timing of the deduction relative to payday does not excuse a shortfall. [Para 174][Para 175]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's findings that no minimum-wage Prakas yet covers the tile sector, and that applying the garment-sector minimum is therefore already more favourable than the law, explain why the wage claim failed on its merits as well as on standing. [Para 110][Para 114]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's name is OCR'd as អេសស៊ី ជី ខន គ្រីត រូហ្វ, រូហូ and even "T ស៊ី ជី"; the brief uses "SCG Concrete Roof (Cambodia) Co., Ltd" as on the face of the award. [Para 1][Para 26][Para 45] The union's registration is certificate No. 361 K.B/V.B/VK of 26 September 2017 at para 38 but a bare number "112170215" at para 50, and its most-representative certificate is described only as having expired "about 3 months" earlier, with no date given. [Para 38][Para 50][Para 54] The findings contradict each other on the USD 15 wage differential: para 55 records that the employer paid it to all workers before 2015 and still pays it to those hired before 31 December 2014, while para 106 records that workers hired from 2015 receive only the Prakas rate — both are reported as printed. [Para 55][Para 106] The reasoning section repeats the interest-dispute passage verbatim in issues 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6, and the final decision restates the issue-7 order twice in consecutive paragraphs. [Para 141][Para 183][Para 184]
