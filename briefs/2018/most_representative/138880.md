# Award No. 007/18 — Khen Theron Apparel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 1 March 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Yu Sony, employer-appointed; Sin Kim Sean, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · Doc ID 138880

## 1. Facts
Around 1,060 workers are employed at the company's garment factory. On 2 January 2018 the workers filed a complaint asking the employer to improve several working conditions; conciliation on 29 January 2018 covered 5 issues, of which 1 was settled and 4 were referred to the Arbitration Council by report No. 114 of 6 February 2018. At the hearing of 19 February 2018 the employer, through counsel, challenged the union's right to bring the collective dispute at all, and the workers withdrew three of their four remaining claims, leaving only the demand for weekly payment of the overtime meal allowance. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 22][Para 23][Para 39]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must add USD 10 to the existing USD 6 midday meal allowance, making USD 16 per month? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company must increase the overtime meal allowance by 4,200 riel per hour? [Para 4]
3. Whether the company must pay seniority indemnity by year of service (12 years, 13 years)? [Para 6]
4. Whether the employer must pay the overtime meal allowance once a week, every Saturday? [Para 9]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** pressed all four claims, arguing that the fortnightly payment of the overtime meal allowance forces them to borrow money or bring cash from home in order to eat while working overtime; on representation they relied on the departmental letter naming their three recognised representatives. [Para 44][Para 64][Para 85]
**Employer (respondent):** said it had no capacity to grant the increases, that the Labour Law fixes no particular day for payment, that the workers lose nothing by fortnightly payment and that it needs time to calculate; it also objected that the local union had not met the conditions of Article 54 of the Law on Trade Unions and so could not represent the workers in a collective dispute. [Para 3][Para 47][Para 50]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — on the one issue surviving withdrawal, the panel ordered the employer to pay the overtime meal allowance to the workers once a week, on the Saturday of each week; the claims in issues 1, 2 and 3 were withdrawn by the workers and not decided. [Para 22][Para 88][Para 89]

On representation, the panel applied Article 54(1) of the Law on Trade Unions of 17 May 2016: only the most representative union holds the exclusive right to conclude a collective agreement or resolve a collective dispute, and that right extends to every stage of dispute resolution including the Arbitration Council. Having found no evidence that any union at the company held most-representative status, the panel followed its own awards No. 180/16 (Cement Cambodia Chakrey Ting) and No. 171/16 (Jewelin Co., Ltd): where no most-representative union exists, the affected workers may represent themselves or mandate a local union or other person. It further applied Article 1 of Prakas No. 362 of 29 August 2016, which gives the Labour Dispute Department and provincial labour officials power to issue recognition letters to worker representatives, and held that the two recognised representatives who appeared had a lawful right to represent the workers before the panel. The employer's objection therefore failed. [Para 51][Para 54][Para 56][Para 58][Para 63][Para 64][Para 68]

On the meal allowance, the panel applied point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011: a worker who voluntarily works overtime at the employer's request is entitled to 2,000 riel per day for food, or to one free meal. That notification fixes no time for payment, but the panel reasoned that the allowance exists so that workers can buy food on the day they work overtime, and that a meal is a daily necessity the employer must supply. Earlier awards (No. 4707 Chung Fe and No. 244/12) were followed. Having weighed the workers' evidence of borrowing and the employer's calculation burden, the panel held that weekly payment on Saturday was the reasonable interval and ordered it. [Para 70][Para 72][Para 76][Para 79][Para 84][Para 87][Para 88]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where no union in an enterprise holds most-representative status under Article 54 of the Law on Trade Unions, an employer's objection to the union's standing cannot defeat a collective dispute: workers may be represented by persons recognised in a letter issued under Article 1 of Prakas No. 362, and the panel will hear the merits. The overtime meal allowance of 2,000 riel per day under Notification No. 041/11 must be paid at an interval that lets the worker buy food on the days overtime is worked; weekly payment on Saturday satisfies that requirement even though the notification sets no date. [Para 64][Para 68][Para 76][Para 87]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that a worker who is not paid daily may still arrange the money received weekly, fortnightly or monthly to buy food for overtime days and that the claim therefore depends on the workers' actual necessity; and its statement that the Labour Law does not prescribe any particular payment day. [Para 47][Para 74][Para 81][Para 82]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer's name appears in OCR variants (ឃែន, សែន, យើង បន ធើរេន) and is rendered here as Khen Theron Apparel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. Head-counts conflict: about 1,060 workers employed at para 35 against 340 workers involved in the dispute at para 40, both reported as printed. Claims 1 and 3 contain OCR-damaged figures ("១២២ ល្លារ" at para 6; a stray "១០" in the USD 10/USD 6/USD 16 computation at para 2) and all three were withdrawn in any event. Paragraphs 22, 39, 57–59, 61 and 91 carry heavy corruption ("៣៥០៩", "៤៤", Latin-script fragments in place of the employer arbitrator's signature line). The hearing date is printed as 19 February 2018 at 14:00 in para 15 and as "2 and 00" in the afternoon at para 20. Individual worker and union names are given here as bare initials.
