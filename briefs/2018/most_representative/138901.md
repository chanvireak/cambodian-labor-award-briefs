# Award No. 022/18 — Lan Global Tech Style (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 16 July 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (In Sodhy, employer-appointed, dissenting in part; An Non, worker-appointed; Kong Phalla, president) · Doc ID 138901

## 1. Facts
Lan Global Tech Style (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. is a garment factory with about 226 workers that hired on successive three-month fixed-duration contracts [Para 44][Para 49]. Three union officers — Mr S (a machinist from 7 July 2016), Mr P (pressing, from 7 July 2012) and Mr K (from 3 January 2013) — had their contracts ended at the end of March 2018 along with about 200 other workers, with seven days' notice and 5% end-of-contract indemnity each [Para 51][Para 65][Para 69][Para 70]. They claimed the full indefinite-duration severance package; the company said it had simply declined to renew fixed-duration contracts because it had no orders, and that it would close at the end of July 2018 [Para 4][Para 54][Para 61].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay severance and terminal benefits on the footing of an indefinite-duration contract — notice pay in lieu, indemnity for dismissal, damages (សោហ៊ុយជម្ងឺចិត្ត), payment in lieu of unused annual leave, and final wages — to Mr S, Mr P and Mr K? [Para 93][Para 96]

Issues 2 to 8 (anti-union discrimination, contract length, sufficient work, 100% wages during work shortage, meal allowance, fans, conversion of long-service contracts) were withdrawn at the hearing and not decided [Para 30][Para 31].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** No article of the Labour Law makes a three-month contract a fixed-duration contract; the employer gave seven days' notice, which is the procedure for ending an indefinite-duration contract under Article 75, so the contracts were indefinite-duration and the full severance package is owed [Para 57][Para 58][Para 59].
**Employer (respondent):** The three were on three-month fixed-duration contracts that simply expired, with no work left to give and many others not renewed at the same time; it paid the 5% end-of-contract indemnity, unused leave, final wages, attendance, travel and seniority allowances, and the workers have not come to collect [Para 60][Para 62][Para 63].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim to indefinite-duration severance was rejected for Mr S and the fixed-duration entitlements (5% indemnity, damages equal to wages to the contract's end, unused annual leave, final wages) were ordered instead; for Mr P and Mr K the employer was ordered to pay the indefinite-duration package: notice pay in lieu, indemnity for dismissal, damages, unused annual leave and final wages [Para 220][Para 221][Para 222].

On representation the panel found no union certified as most representative, applied Article 54 of the Law on Unions with Prakas No. 303 and Prakas No. 362, and accepted the four recognised representatives who appeared out of the six named in the Labour Disputes Department's letter of 11 June 2018 [Para 80][Para 81][Para 86][Para 91]. It also recorded that the workers had narrowed their claim from reinstallation plus back pay to severance alone after learning of the closure [Para 94][Para 95].

On the character of the contracts the panel applied Article 67(2) with Article 73(5): a fixed-duration contract exceeding two years in total, including renewals, converts to indefinite duration [Para 97][Para 99][Para 105]. For Mr S it accepted the handwritten last contract running to 30 April 2018, which bore his thumbprint and was not contested on the document-dispute date, so his total service from 7 July 2016 fell short of two years and the contract remained fixed-duration [Para 117][Para 118][Para 119]. But because the employer ended it at the end of March 2018 — before that expiry, for want of work — the panel held this was a dismissal, not a non-renewal, attracting Article 73(3) damages and Article 73(6) indemnity, plus annual leave under Articles 166–167 and final wages under Article 116 [Para 125][Para 126][Para 132][Para 139][Para 144][Para 154]. For Mr P and Mr K, service since 2012 and 2013 exceeded two years, so their contracts were already indefinite-duration and the ending was a dismissal [Para 161][Para 162]. Article 74 requires a valid reason tied to capacity, conduct or the running of the enterprise; the employer gave none, only that there was no work [Para 164][Para 169][Para 170]. The panel declined to apply the amendment law promulgated on 26 June 2018 because the dismissals of 31 March 2018 predated it, and applied Articles 89 and 91 of the 1997 Law for the dismissal indemnity and damages [Para 186][Para 187][Para 193][Para 207].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where successive fixed-duration contracts bring total service past two years the contract is indefinite-duration and can only end by dismissal for a reason Article 74 permits; where total service stays under two years the contract remains fixed-duration and expiry is lawful, but ending it before the printed expiry date is a dismissal that carries Article 73(3) damages and the Article 73(6) indemnity [Para 105][Para 126][Para 162][Para 170].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that a later statute does not govern dismissals that occurred before it came into force, and that the 12-month average wage is the base for both annual-leave payments and dismissal indemnity [Para 187][Para 200]. Arbitrator In Sodhy dissented on the order to convert fixed-duration contracts, arguing the employer had paid the 5% indemnity each time and that conversion defeats the parties' freedom of contract [Para 226][Para 242].
