# Award No. 020/18 — មន្ទីរពេទ្យ អនុស្សាវរីយ៍ ស៊ុន យ៉ា ឃៀល (Arbitration Council, ថ្ងៃទី ១០ ខែកក្កដា ឆ្នាំ ២០១៨)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · យូ សួន ទី / អន ណន / ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា · Doc ID 138824

## 1. Facts
The employer operates a memorial hospital in Kampot province providing health services, and employed about 104 workers. [Para 48] [Para 49] The hospital had no most-representative union. [Para 52] The provincial labour office of Kampot received the workers' request on 28 March 2018 seeking improvement of eight working conditions. [Para 10] Conciliation ended on 10 May 2018 with four points agreed and four unresolved; the unresolved points were sent to the Arbitration Council secretariat on 4 June 2018 with report No. 183. [Para 11] [Para 12] The panel was formed and heard the parties on 14 June 2018; both sides attended, further conciliation failed, and the parties chose a non-binding award. [Para 14] [Para 15] [Para 16] [Para 18] The panel asserted jurisdiction under Chapter XII of the 1997 Labour Law and Prakas No. 099. [Para 6] The hospital appeared through its director, HR head, finance head, lawyers and a legal adviser; the workers through two employee representatives recognised by the provincial labour office on 29 March 2018. [Para 1] [Para 50] The parties extended the award deadline from 27 June 2018 to 10 July 2018. [Para 20]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Preliminary) Whether, absent a most-representative union, the two labour-office-recognised employee representatives could act for the workers before the panel? [Para 52] [Para 122]
2. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay a 5 per cent end-of-contract indemnity when a doctor's two-year fixed-term contract expires and is replaced by an undetermined-term contract? [Para 53]
3. (Issue 2) Whether seniority must be aggregated for employees allegedly moved from an undetermined-term contract to a fixed-term contract? [Para 62]
4. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must conclude fixed-term contracts not exceeding two years, and whether a three-year training/service condition was unlawful? [Para 75] [Para 78]
5. (Issue 4) Whether out-patient doctors must be allowed public holidays under the ministry's Prakas, and whether holiday work must be voluntary? [Para 93] [Para 103]

## 3. Positions of the parties
The workers claimed the 5% indemnity, saying conversion to undetermined-term status erased two years of seniority, and that resignation pay was one month's salary regardless of length of service. [Para 53] [Para 54] They alleged one doctor had been moved from an undetermined-term contract to a fixed-term contract, and alleged coercion or collusion after that doctor confirmed in writing that he had not complained and was satisfied with his contract. [Para 64] [Para 67] [Para 70] [Para 71] They said a three-year clause requiring USD 100 per month repayment for early departure restrained resignation. [Para 74] [Para 79] They sought holiday rest per the ministry notice, said holiday work must be voluntary, and noted eight workers who refused it. [Para 93] [Para 103] [Para 104]

The hospital said inexperienced doctors received one- or two-year fixed-term contracts that continued into undetermined-term contracts without any break in the relationship. [Para 56] [Para 57] It said no 5% was paid, a USD 200 supplement was given on conversion, and one month's salary was paid on departure after more than two years' service. [Para 58] [Para 59] [Para 60] It argued these terms were better than the law, which does not require severance for voluntary resignation. [Para 61] It denied any undetermined-to-fixed conversion and said the doctor was satisfied. [Para 65] [Para 138] It denied fixed terms over two years and said new doctors were now hired on undetermined-term contracts. [Para 76] [Para 81] On holidays, it said all workers except required on-call staff could rest, major festivals saw no general out-patient clinic, schedules were prepared in advance, holiday work was paid plus USD 11 per patient seen, and 13 doctors supported paid holiday clinics. [Para 96] [Para 98] [Para 99] [Para 100] [Para 105]

## 4. Award and reasoning
On standing, Article 54 of the Trade Union Law gives a most-representative union exclusive right to conduct collective disputes, including before the Arbitration Council, but no such union existed at the hospital. [Para 106] [Para 109] [Para 110] [Para 52] Prakas No. 362 empowers the labour dispute department and provincial labour offices to recognise employee representatives for collective disputes. [Para 116] [Para 118] The two recognised representatives therefore had legal standing, and the panel decided the four points for the hospital's workers. [Para 119] [Para 122] [Para 123]

On Issue 1, Article 73 of the Labour Law ties the end-of-contract indemnity to termination and, absent a collective agreement, sets it at least 5% of wages paid during the contract; when an undetermined-term contract replaces a fixed-term one, seniority is aggregated. [Para 124] [Para 125] [Para 126] Because the replacement continued the relationship without interruption, no 5% was due, but the employer must aggregate the total duration of both contracts. [Para 131] [Para 133] [Para 134] [Para 135]

On Issue 2, the party making an allegation bears the burden of proof, and the panel found no evidence of pressure on the doctor and no worker whose undetermined-term contract had been converted to a fixed term. [Para 144] [Para 149] [Para 151] [Para 152] With no identifiable claimant, the panel declined to consider the claim. [Para 153] [Para 157] [Para 158]

On Issue 3, the panel found no fixed-term contract exceeding two years: the trainee doctor's contract had no clear end date and the other document was a training agreement, not an employment contract. [Para 163] [Para 164] [Para 165] The workers produced no supporting contract, and no specific claimant was identified. [Para 166] [Para 167] [Para 168]

On Issue 4, Articles 161 and 164 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 10 require paid holidays and, where work cannot stop, voluntary holiday work with an indemnity equal to ordinary wages. [Para 169] [Para 171] [Para 173] [Para 174] [Para 177] The real dispute was consent, not payment: the employer rostered doctors for holiday work without their request, though some doctors agreed. [Para 178] [Para 179] That practice was unlawful; holiday work must be voluntary and without coercion. [Para 180] [Para 181] [Para 182]

**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel rejected the 5% indemnity claim and declined to consider Issues 2 and 3, and ordered the employer to allow doctors public-holiday leave and to make holiday work voluntary; no monetary award was made. [Para 183] [Para 184] [Para 185] [Para 186]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Under Article 73 of the Labour Law, replacing a fixed-term contract with an undetermined-term contract without breaking the employment relationship does not trigger the 5% end-of-contract indemnity but requires aggregation of seniority. [Para 126] [Para 134] A claimant alleging coercion or a specific breach bears the burden of proof, and an unsupported or unidentified grievance cannot be decided. [Para 144] [Para 146] [Para 167] Under Prakas No. 10, holiday work is lawful only if voluntary and compensated; rostering cannot replace consent. [Para 173] [Para 177] [Para 180] These are the panel's reasons, not binding precedent.
**Obiter dicta:** Where no most-representative union exists, workers recognised by the labour authority may conduct a collective dispute before the panel. [Para 119]
