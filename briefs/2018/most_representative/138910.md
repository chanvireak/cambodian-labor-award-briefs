# Award No. 029/18 — Sang Vo (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 7 September 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy appointed by the employer, Sin Kim Sean appointed by the workers, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 138910

## 1. Facts
Sang Vo (Cambodia) Ltd, a garment factory on National Road 4 in Samrong Tong district, Kampong Speu province, employing about 3,808 workers, pays a monthly attendance bonus of USD 13 and a "skill allowance" in two forms — a position allowance of USD 20 and above, and an individual evaluation allowance of USD 0 to USD 10 — and deducts these on a sliding scale when a worker is absent. [Para 56][Para 60][Para 72][Para 74][Para 76][Para 78] The Democratic Union of Cambodian Garment Workers (C.W.D.) local, registered on 29 May 2017, raised three demands; the first was settled at the hearing, leaving the claim that the attendance bonus may not be deducted for leave the employer has authorised and that the skill allowance must be preserved during lawful leave. [Para 4][Para 5][Para 19][Para 20][Para 40]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must apply the agreement on special leave? (settled at the hearing) [Para 3][Para 19]
2. Whether the employer must pay the attendance bonus in full, without deduction, when a worker takes leave that the employer has authorised? [Para 60][Para 119]
3. Whether the employer must not deduct any part of the skill allowance when a worker takes any kind of lawful leave? [Para 74][Para 169]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** under Article 103 of the Labour Law the attendance bonus is part of wages, so the employer may not deduct all of it when leave has been requested and granted; the leave in question covers attending Pchum Ben, obtaining an identity card, registering immovable property, a friend's or relative's birthday and a relative's wedding. [Para 63][Para 65] The skill allowance is not wages under Article 103 and should be preserved like travel and accommodation expenses, because it exists to keep the production line running. [Para 80][Para 82]
**Employer (respondent):** the deduction scale was agreed with a union and has been applied for years; Notification No. 230 makes the bonus payable only to workers with no absence, and the company pays USD 3 more than the law requires while attaching conditions; the skill allowance is a voluntary extra with no basis in the Labour Law, and not deducting it would be unfair to workers who do attend. [Para 66][Para 68][Para 71][Para 72][Para 83][Para 84]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — both remaining demands rejected; the employer was ordered to do nothing, and the first issue having been settled, no other relief was granted. [Para 168][Para 180][Para 181][Para 182]

On standing, the panel held that the two worker representatives certified by the provincial Department of Labour on 7 June 2018 under letter No. 3548/18 and present at the hearing could represent the claimants, since both parties accepted the factory has no most-representative union. [Para 59][Para 115][Para 117]

On issue 2 the panel adopted the Full Bench decision of 3 October 2017 interpreting Notification No. 230 K.B/S.Ch.N. of 25 July 2012: the employer must pay the attendance bonus at 100 per cent whenever the worker attends on all working days, counting as attendance the days of leave that are a legal right, leave that is a contractual right, and absence compelled by legal obligation; there is no duty to pay for absence beyond those categories unless the employer has adopted a different policy or a separate agreement. [Para 119][Para 123][Para 124] The Full Bench's four categories were then applied: leave as of legal right (weekly rest, paid public holidays, annual leave, sick leave, maternity leave, special leave under article 171 and Prakas No. 267, capped at seven days); leave as a contractual right, which may exceed the statutory minimum under Article 13; leave compelled by legal obligation, such as attending court as a witness under article 538 of the Penal Code; and leave merely permitted by the employer, which carries no entitlement. [Para 126][Para 127][Para 129][Para 141][Para 146][Para 147][Para 158] The panel found that leave for Pchum Ben, an identity card, property registration or a relative's wedding falls in the fourth category, so the bonus may be withheld, and that the employer's own scale — USD 13 for half a day, USD 16 for one to one and a half days, USD 5 for three to three and a half days — is already more favourable than the statutory USD 10 minimum. [Para 162][Para 164][Para 165][Para 166]

On issue 3 the panel held that no provision of law requires an employer to pay a skill allowance at all, that both allowances are extras more favourable than the law, and that the employer's power under Article 2 of the Labour Law to organise and manage the enterprise extends to attaching conditions to them; deducting the allowance for unpaid leave, unauthorised absence and sick leave therefore does not contravene the law. [Para 173][Para 177][Para 178][Para 179][Para 180]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus may lawfully be withheld for leave that the employer merely permits, even where the leave was requested and granted: only leave that is a legal right, leave that is a contractual right, and absence compelled by a legal obligation count as attendance for bonus purposes. Where the employer's own deduction scale is more generous than the statutory minimum in Notification No. 230, the panel will not disturb it. An allowance that the law does not require, such as a position or individual skill allowance, falls within the employer's Article 2 power to manage the enterprise, and conditions attached to it — including deduction during unpaid leave, unauthorised absence and sick leave — stand unless they contravene the law. [Para 124][Para 158][Para 165][Para 178][Para 179]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's survey of the four categories of leave, its observation that parties may agree to extend leave as a contractual right under Article 13, and its remarks on the employer's management prerogative being exercisable only lawfully and reasonably are general propositions drawn from the Full Bench decision rather than findings needed to dismiss these two demands. [Para 141][Para 146][Para 151][Para 173]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The deduction scale is printed two ways: USD 11 for one to one and a half days of leave at [Para 72], but USD 16 for the same band at [Para 164]; a separate passage says a USD 2 deduction applies for absence of one to one and a half days [Para 69]. The employer's stated practice at [Para 68] — USD 3 above the law — cannot be reconciled with a USD 13 bonus against a USD 10 minimum without the same confusion.
- The procedural chronology is internally impossible: the conciliation report is dated 31 July 2018 but said to have been sent on 24 July 2018 [Para 8]; the panel records inviting the parties to a hearing on 23 July 2018 [Para 17] although the hearing was held on 23 August 2018 [Para 10]; and the deadline to object to documents is given as 30 July 2018, before the deadline to file them of 28 August 2018 [Para 22].
- The concession that settled issue 1 is recorded only as a one-line statement at the hearing that the parties agreed on "point 1" [Para 19], with no terms of the agreement reproduced anywhere in the document, so what was conceded cannot be verified from the award.
- The employer's identity and the union's registration number appear in several forms, including certificate No. 105170028 and registration No. 2585/17 and 2586/17 for the same union [Para 40][Para 41][Para 58]; one passage gives the workforce as "4 ប្រហែលជា 3808" [Para 56], leaving the leading digit unclear.
- The demand as recorded at [Para 5] asks that the employer "not deduct the skill allowance", while the reasons and the order address whether the employer must "preserve all of the skill allowance" [Para 169][Para 180][Para 182]; the two formulations are not identical.
