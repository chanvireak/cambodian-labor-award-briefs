# Award No. 025/18 — In Su A I Perl Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 16 August 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Seng Vouch Hun (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Pen Bunhea (president) · non-binding award · Doc ID 138904

## 1. Facts
The company operates a garment factory in Khan Porsenchey, Phnom Penh, employing about 427 workers. [Para 1][Para 59]
On 31 May 2018 at about 1:30 pm a dispute arose inside the factory between Mr T (Thak Sophara), head of the sewing section and elected president of the In Su A Solidarity Union, and the Filipino QA head, over garments of the wrong size. [Para 62][Para 90][Para 101]
The company suspended Mr T by letter of 1 June 2018 pending the labour inspector's decision, then issued a termination letter dated 2 June 2018; he refused the severance payment offered on 1 June 2018. [Para 63][Para 87][Para 95][Para 97]
Of the ten claims filed, three remained unsettled; at the hearing the parties reached agreement on Issues 1 and 3, leaving only the reinstatement claim. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 18][Para 19]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must remove Mr L from the position of factory head, for insulting and demeaning Khmer workers, dismissing workers arbitrarily without cause and forcing them to start work 15 to 20 minutes early each morning and at midday? [Para 3]
2. Whether the company must reinstate Mr T (Thak Sophara), who says he did not strike back at the Filipino QA head but was kicked and had a water bottle thrown at him? [Para 4][Para 62]
3. Whether the company must pay back, as overtime, the 15 to 20 minutes of early work performed each morning and at midday? [Para 6]

Issues 1 and 3 were resolved by agreement of the parties at the hearing and were not adjudicated. [Para 18][Para 19]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr T did not assault the QA head — the QA head kicked him and threw a water bottle at him first, and he merely raised his hands to block a kick; he is the victim, and although a union leader entitled to protection he was dismissed outright, without even receiving the suspension letter. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 71][Para 72]
**Employer (respondent):** Mr T and the QA head fought inside the factory during working hours; the company dismissed both, relying on the security report and on a witness, and cannot reinstate him because fighting at work is serious misconduct under Article 83 of the Labour Law and a breach of the internal rules. [Para 5][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the claim for reinstatement; no sum in riel or USD was ordered. [Para 177][Para 178]

The panel first had to characterise the measure, because the employer's account was internally inconsistent: it first said it had dismissed both Mr T and the QA head, then said it had only suspended Mr T pending the labour inspector's ruling. Since a termination letter issued on 2 June 2018, one day after the suspension letter of 1 June 2018, and the severance payment was tendered, the panel found the measure was a dismissal, not a suspension. [Para 88][Para 125][Para 126][Para 127][Para 130]

It then tested the union-leader protection under Articles 43(1) and 67 of the 2016 Law on Trade Unions, which bar dismissal of a delegate or candidate without the labour inspector's consent and protect candidates for 45 days before and 45 days after the election. Following earlier awards (No. 175/16 Denim Khon Sip, No. 169/16 Morning Glory, No. 170/16 Chai Yu M Real, No. 188/16 Green Summit, No. 001/17 Diamond City), the panel applied a three-condition test: the worker must belong to the protected category, the dismissal must fall inside the protection period, and the union must have notified the employer and copied the Ministry of Labour. [Para 135][Para 137][Para 148][Para 149]

Conditions 1 and 2 were satisfied: Mr T was elected union president on 8 April 2018 and the union's registration application was lodged on 20 April 2018 but not yet granted, which still attracts protection; and the dismissal of 2 June 2018 occurred while registration was pending. [Para 150][Para 152][Para 154][Para 155]

Condition 3 failed. The union's first notification of 19 April 2018 fell on a day the factory was closed, and the second, of 2 July 2018, was sent to an incorrect company address, so the employer did not know of Mr T' candidacy until 2 July — a month after it had already dismissed him. The panel accordingly held he was not a protected worker at the material time. [Para 156][Para 157][Para 158][Para 160][Para 161][Para 162]

On the lawfulness of the dismissal, Article 73(1) and (2) allow a fixed-term contract to be terminated before expiry only by written agreement of both parties before the labour inspector, or for serious misconduct. Article 83(b)(4) treats threatening, insulting or assaulting the employer or another worker as serious misconduct, and clause 10 of the Prakas-approved internal rules of 23 November 2016 provides for dismissal in that case. Weighing the evidence, the panel heard the employer's witness, Ms M (Mok Yan), QC head, who was about three metres away and described Mr T striking the table, pointing at the QA head, saying "what do you want" and seizing her hand; the photograph showed red marks on the QA head's neck and hand. The panel could not conclude from the photograph that Mr T actually squeezed her neck, but found that the two did exchange physical blows. That mutual assault amounted to serious misconduct, so dismissal was lawful and reinstatement was refused. [Para 163][Para 169][Para 174][Para 175][Para 176][Para 177]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Union-leader protection against dismissal is not automatic: a candidate or delegate loses it where the union has not actually notified the employer and copied the Ministry of Labour before the dismissal, so that the employer could not know of the candidacy. Where a worker and a supervisor exchange physical blows at the workplace during working hours, that is serious misconduct under Article 83(b)(4) and justifies dismissal of a fixed-term contract before its expiry. [Para 160][Para 169][Para 177]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that a letter of suspension produced for the first time at the hearing, contradicted by a termination letter issued one day later, cannot establish that the contract was merely suspended; and that where a claim is unsupported by sufficient evidence the party bearing the burden of proof loses it. It also recorded that no most-representative union existed at the company, so the four workers recognised by the Labour Disputes Department could represent the claimants. [Para 119][Para 123][Para 128][Para 130]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The panel's reasoning at Para 128 states that Mr T' suspension "was terminated since 29 June 2018", which conflicts with the termination letter of 2 June 2018 relied on at Para 127 and Para 132; the award does not reconcile the two dates. The three union notifications are dated 19 April, 2 July and 18 July 2018 at Para 84 and Para 156, although the election itself is dated 8 April 2018. The letter transferring representation to the company's accountant is dated 23 July 2018 at Para 23 but 3 July 2018 at Para 51. The company name is rendered variously as In Su A I Perl, In Su Ai, Oun Su A and In Su A throughout. The reasoning at Para 177 is heavily corrupted by OCR, with fragments such as "ទ្វង្វើរ ប្រស់ A" and "មាន ៣ ជន ស្លាប" standing where the Article 83 citation and the conclusion should read; the sense was reconstructed from Para 169 and the operative order at Para 178.
