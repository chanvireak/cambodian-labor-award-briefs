# Award No. 032/18 — Ken Sport Shoe Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 27 September 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; Liv Sovanna, appointed by the workers; Pen Bunhea, president, appointed by both) · Doc ID 138915

## 1. Facts
Ken Sport Shoe Co., Ltd. manufactures shoes at Chamkar Svay village, Sethei commune, Samaki Mean Chey district, Kampong Chhnang province, and employs approximately 7,303 workers [Para 63]. Of 17 demands referred after conciliation on 14 August 2018, 8 remained unresolved and reached the panel [Para 17]. The Khmer Youth Union (សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ) at the company holds a registration certificate from the Provincial Department of Labour dated 24 April 2017 [Para 64]. At the hearing the parties settled issues 1, 2, 3, 5, 7 and 8 by agreement, leaving issues 4 and 6 for decision [Para 21][Para 23].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must stop discriminating against the Khmer Youth Union? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company must install time-card machines in Building M1 covering both the sewing lines and the 13th-floor drainage line, and at the cutting tables? [Para 3]
3. Whether the company must again allow workers to clock in at 06:30 in the morning as before? [Para 4]
4. Whether the employer is obliged to grant half-day or one-day sick leave on the worker's request, as before? [Para 5][Para 128]
5. Whether the company must comply with Article 184 of the Labour Law? [Para 6]
6. Whether the employer is obliged to deduct 2,000 riel per month from the wages of Khmer Youth Union members and pay it to the union? [Para 7][Para 140]
7. Whether the company must stop requiring pregnant workers to take maternity leave before the month in which they are due to give birth? [Para 8]
8. Whether the company must supply sufficient cleaning materials to the FCM section? [Para 9]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The internal rules fix paid sick leave at half a day and upwards, so the employer must grant sick leave for the period requested once a proper medical certificate is produced; a worker who has spent hours travelling to hospital and waiting for the doctor cannot be limited to the hours printed on the certificate [Para 83][Para 88]. On union dues, the employer is bound to deduct once the member consents, and the union submitted a written request with dated consents attached [Para 101][Para 102].

**Employer (respondent):** Sick leave is granted according to the hours of examination recorded on the medical certificate stamped by the National Social Security Fund, travel time being counted but waiting time not, and a worker who merely collects medicine cannot claim a full day [Para 82][Para 86][Para 87]. On dues it does not object to the form of the union's request but requires each worker to lodge it personally at the administration [Para 103][Para 157].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to grant sick leave from half a day upwards in accordance with Article 1.10.2 ("sick leave") of the company's internal rules, and to deduct 2,000 riel per month from the wages of Khmer Youth Union members and pay it to the union whenever the union files a request with dated consent letters bearing signatures or thumbprints [Para 164][Para 165].

On issue 4 the panel read Article 1.10.2 of the internal rules, approved under No. 511 dated 10 June 2016, which fixes sick leave at half a day (0.5 day) with wages and requires a medical certificate whose date matches the leave request [Para 128][Para 130]. Weighing the five medical certificates filed by the workers, it found that nothing in those rules allowed the employer to fix sick leave solely by the hours of examination printed on the certificate, and no provision required a certificate showing half a day or a full day of treatment [Para 94][Para 134][Para 135]. The employer's practice was therefore inconsistent with its own internal rules, and the worker's entitlement runs to the period requested, measured by the actual symptoms [Para 136][Para 137]. On issue 6 the panel applied Article 129 of the Labour Law, Article 26(1) of the Law on Trade Unions (2016), and Articles 1 and 4(2) of Prakas No. 300 of 2 July 2018, which permit a properly registered union to request deduction on ten days' written notice with dated consents attached [Para 141][Para 143][Para 149][Para 152]. It found the Khmer Youth Union duly registered and its request properly documented, and no law requiring the member to lodge the request personally [Para 150][Para 160]. It also confirmed the three worker representatives recognised by the Provincial Department had authority to act [Para 126].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A company's internal rules that fix paid sick leave at half a day and upwards, on production of a dated medical certificate, entitle the worker to sick leave for the period requested; the employer may not cap it at the examination hours printed on the certificate, because the rules nowhere authorise that method of calculation [Para 134][Para 136]. Where a registered union files a compliant deduction request with dated member consents, the employer must deduct the stated dues and may not require each member to appear in person [Para 160][Para 162].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's statement that only a most-representative union holds exclusive rights under Article 54(1) of the Law on Trade Unions was unnecessary here, as it recorded that no most-representative union yet existed at the enterprise [Para 112][Para 115].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The workforce figure is printed as 7,303, likely an OCR corruption of a smaller number [Para 63]. The union's registration number appears as 1041700005 and again as 104170005 [Para 64][Para 150]. The factory operating certificate is dated 28 December 2007 at one place and 28 December 2017 at another [Para 29][Para 62]. The company name is corrupted to "Skane Sport Shoe" in the recital of the conciliation report [Para 17], and the list of new union officers is overwritten by stray digits at [Para 64]. The medical-certificate recital breaks into untranslated fragments ("mongram, The") at [Para 94], and the operative order on issue 6 is duplicated and interleaved with broken clauses at [Para 165]. Issue 2 records an agreement to install machines only if Building M1 holds more than 500 workers, with the figure partly lost [Para 75].
