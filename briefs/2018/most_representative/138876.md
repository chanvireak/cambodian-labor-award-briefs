# Award No. 002/18 — Syung Yin (Cambodia) B Thik Stay Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 6 February 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Kong Phalla (president) · Doc ID 138876

## 1. Facts
The respondent is a garment-cutting and sewing company employing about 1,074 workers. On 5 January 2018 the Labour Department of Kandal Province received a complaint from 200 workers of the auto (loom-watching) section; conciliation failed on all five points, which reached the Council on 8 January 2018. [Para 17][Para 18][Para 19][Para 42] The union party was the Smor Rotei Khmeng Khmer Union at Syung Yin, holder of most-representative status under certificate No. 048/16 of 13 July 2016, valid until 13 July 2018; its three office-holders' terms had expired and the new term had not yet been recognised when the case was heard. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 117] Both sides attended, the employer through counsel. The workers withdrew point 3, leaving points 1, 2 and 5. [Para 25][Para 26]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to pay a meal allowance for overtime to auto-section workers of 6,500 riel for the day shift and 7,500 riel for the night shift? [Para 127]
2. Whether the employer is obliged to pay a machine-handling allowance of 40 USD per month to all auto-section workers? [Para 153]
5. Whether the employer is obliged to reduce loom speed to 75? [Para 158]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** in 2016 a worker supervised 6 looms and now supervises up to 8 at unchanged speed 85, so the present meal allowance is inadequate; a machine allowance of 20 USD was paid in 2016 and then stopped, though other factories pay it; and the added loom makes the work unbearable, about 10 workers having resigned. [Para 59][Para 74][Para 75][Para 83][Para 91]
**Employer (respondent):** the company's finances have been difficult since 2017 with no foreign buyers, so it cannot pay more; clause 9 of the collective agreement is applied as written at 1,000 riel per overtime hour; loom speed 85 has been fixed by technicians since 2013 and cannot be lowered, and workers have continued to cope. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 84][Para 92][Para 164]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all three claims were refused, the panel declining to consider them at all: point 1 no meal-allowance increase, point 2 no 40 USD machine allowance, point 5 no reduction of loom speed to 75. [Para 152][Para 157][Para 174][Para 175][Para 176][Para 177]

The panel first addressed who may bring the claim. Article 54(1) of the Law on Trade Unions reserves exclusive negotiating rights to the most-representative union, and Article 43(1) of Prakas No. 099 provides that an award on an interest dispute stands in place of a collective agreement and binds all workers for one year; the panel reasoned that this substitutes the award for the collective agreement and removes the workers' right to bargain and to strike over that interest, so only a most-representative union, or one qualified under Articles 54 and 72, may bring such a dispute. [Para 100][Para 145][Para 146][Para 147][Para 148] On the facts, the union held valid most-representative status, but its three leaders' mandates had expired and the Labour Department had not yet recognised the new term, so they could not represent the union; the panel held the claimants were therefore not a union with the standing required and did not consider the merits. [Para 103][Para 116][Para 117][Para 150][Para 151]

On point 1 the panel noted that the claim was not for enforcement of clause 9 of the collective agreement — which fixes 1,000 riel per overtime hour — but for an increase above what the employer currently pays, so it was decided on legal grounds rather than on the agreement. [Para 127][Para 130][Para 131] It found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules, employment contract, agreement between the parties or past practice obliging a 6,500/7,500 riel allowance; the demand therefore exceeded the law and was an interest dispute. [Para 137][Para 138] Point 2 was treated identically: the 20 USD allowance paid in 2016 had no basis in any instrument or practice, so the 40 USD demand was likewise an interest dispute. [Para 154][Para 155][Para 156]

On point 5, by contrast, the panel reached the merits through Article 2 of the Labour Law: the employer has the right to organise and manage the enterprise provided that right is exercised lawfully and reasonably. Loom speed had been 85 since 2013 without change, and the workers, who bear the burden of proof, neither identified the workers who could not cope nor showed what the difficulty was or when it arose; the panel therefore could not conclude that 8 looms at speed 85 was truly unbearable or that the employer's exercise of its management right was unlawful or unreasonable. [Para 158][Para 161][Para 167][Para 169][Para 172][Para 173]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that goes beyond what the Labour Law, a collective agreement, internal rules, the employment contract or established practice requires is an interest dispute, and an award on it substitutes for the collective agreement for a year; a panel will therefore refuse to consider it unless the claimant is a most-representative union, which requires an incumbent leadership still holding a recognised mandate — an expired mandate defeats standing even where most-representative status itself remains valid. [Para 117][Para 147][Para 150]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's statement that an interest award extinguishes the workers' right to bargain collectively and to strike over that interest for its duration, and its observation that a claim to enforce the collective agreement would be decided differently from a claim to improve on it, were not necessary to the outcome, which turned on standing. [Para 130][Para 146]
