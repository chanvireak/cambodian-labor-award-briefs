# Award No. 08/10 — Niu Ming Da (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 12 February 2010)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), Tuon Siphan (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (president) · Doc ID 140242

## 1. Facts
Niu Ming Da (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. is a garment factory near Chom Chao, Dangkao, Phnom Penh, which opened in March 2007 and then employed about 1,048 workers; three plant unions exist and none holds most-representative status [Para 1][Para 30][Para 31]. The claimant is the Cambodia Federation of Trade Unions for Workers' Rights, acting with its plant union at Niu Ming Da, which filed six demands on 9 December 2009; conciliation on 4 January 2010 settled four, and two came before the panel, which sat on 5 February 2010 [Para 2][Para 11][Para 12][Para 13][Para 10]. At the hearing the union withdrew Issue 2, a complaint that the company had stopped members wearing union badges inside the plant, leaving only Issue 1 [Para 4][Para 5][Para 16][Para 17]. The claimant claimed 106 members; the parties agreed after checking that only 54 were genuine, and the company deducted their dues on 4 February 2010 [Para 33][Para 34].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay the 1,000 riel meal allowance to workers who voluntarily work on Sunday, the weekly rest day? [Para 35][Para 48][Para 61]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Sunday work is overtime because it falls outside the normal working day and week, so the 1,000 riel meal allowance under Ministry Notice No. 017 of 18 July 2000 and Article 4(3) of the company's internal regulation is owed for each Sunday worked [Para 41][Para 42][Para 55][Para 56].

**Employer (respondent):** it works Monday to Saturday, eight hours a day, with Sunday as the weekly rest day, and it does pay 200 per cent of normal wages for Sunday work; but the meal allowance applies only to overtime on ordinary working days, and the internal regulation and Notice 017 should be read that way [Para 35][Para 39][Para 43][Para 44].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to pay 1,000 riel meal money to every worker who voluntarily works on Sunday, the weekly rest day; the award was issued as non-binding, becoming final after eight days without objection [Para 60][Para 61][Para 62].

The panel set the frame with Articles 137 and 147 of the Labour Law: normal hours may not exceed eight a day or 48 a week, and weekly rest must be at least 24 consecutive hours, in principle on Sunday [Para 48][Para 49][Para 51]. It found that the company's Monday-to-Saturday, eight-hour schedule of exactly 48 hours was the normal working time under Article 137 [Para 52][Para 53]. Work on Sunday, requested by the employer and accepted voluntarily by the workers, therefore fell outside normal hours and was overtime [Para 54].

It then read the two instruments the union relied on. Point 4 of Notice No. 017 provides that a worker who voluntarily works overtime at the employer's request receives 1,000 riel a day in meal money or one free meal [Para 55]; Article 4(3) of the internal regulation of 8 April 2008 repeats the entitlement in the same terms [Para 56]. Neither text limits itself to overtime on ordinary working days, and the employer's reading was accordingly rejected [Para 43][Para 47]. Following Award 114/08-Vay Thich, the panel held that voluntary Sunday work beyond the normal day and week is overtime and carries the 1,000 riel daily meal allowance [Para 57][Para 58][Para 59]. It noted that the 200 per cent wage already paid for Sunday work is a separate entitlement and does not absorb the meal money [Para 39][Para 60].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the normal week is already 48 hours from Monday to Saturday, voluntary work performed on Sunday at the employer's request is overtime, and the 1,000 riel daily meal allowance under Notice No. 017 is owed for it; the 200 per cent overtime wage does not discharge that separate allowance [Para 54][Para 58][Para 60]. An internal regulation that repeats the notice without restricting it to ordinary working days cannot be read as confining the entitlement [Para 56].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that Sunday is in principle the weekly rest day for all workers, and its calculation that the company's schedule reaches exactly 48 hours, were background to the overtime characterisation rather than separate holdings [Para 50][Para 52].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The claimant union's name, and the factory's own name, are printed in several variant spellings across the award, including in the documentary list where the company appears as Nju Ming Da [Para 18][Para 19][Para 31][Para 32]. The hearing is dated 5 February 2010, but the trademark certificate in the documentary list is dated "22 January, 4 days, 2010", an unreadable date [Para 19]. Ministry Notice No. 017 is printed as "2017" in one quotation of the internal regulation [Para 56]. The internal rule is cited both as Article 4, point 3, and as Article 4(3) [Para 42][Para 45]. Stray Latin words ("Xinhua", "The", "mart") interrupt the Khmer in the reasoning question and the signature block [Para 48][Para 59][Para 63], and paragraph 52 contains a garbled phrase where "one day" and "one year" are transposed [Para 52]. Individuals appear only by role.
