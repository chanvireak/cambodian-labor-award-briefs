# Award No. 18/13 — M & V International Manufacturing Ltd (Branch 1) (Arbitration Council, 4 March 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Nan, worker-appointed; Kong Phallack, chair) · rights issues binding, interests issues non-binding · Doc ID 139599

## 1. Facts
M & V International Manufacturing Ltd (Branch 1) is a garment factory of 295 workers. The local union of the Cambodian Industry Union Federation (CIUF) had 35 members and no Most Representative Status (MRS). Twelve demands were referred in non-conciliation report No. 090 dated 18 January 2013 and heard on 8 February 2013; the parties settled issues 4, 7 and 11, leaving nine. The workers sought enforcement of earlier awards (Nos 23 and 35), higher allowances, and the 13th month bonus of the overlocking section head, Ms R (ID K1940002), demoted in March 2011. [Para 2][Para 28][Para 18][Para 30][Para 3]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must implement Award No. 23 dated 11 March 2008 (Issue 9) on the attendance bonus for days of authorised leave? [Para 3]
2. Whether it must implement the same award (Issue 7) on averaging wages over 12 months for payment in lieu of remaining annual leave? [Para 4]
3. Whether it must implement Award No. 35 dated 7 May 2010 (Issue 1) on piece rate wages as the basis for breastfeeding workers' pay? [Para 4]
5. Whether it must pay a 1,000 riel meal allowance per hour of overtime on Sundays and holidays? [Para 7]
6. Whether it must pay a 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance per hour on weekdays? [Para 8]
8. Whether it must add US$1 to the seniority bonus for each year of service after the 11th? [Para 10]
9. Whether it must pay the US$6 living allowance to fixed duration workers hired after 1 October 2010 earning over US$61? [Para 11]
10. Whether it must maintain the full attendance bonus during sick leave certified by a state hospital? [Para 12]
12. Whether it must maintain the 13th month bonus, 50% of one month's base wage, for Ms R? [Para 12]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The earlier awards already decided the attendance bonus, leave averaging and breastfeeding rate, so the employer was bound to apply them; the 1,000 riel weekday meal allowance ought to extend to Sunday and holiday overtime and 2,000 riel was too little against rising prices; seniority past 11 years deserved US$1 a year more; fixed duration workers deserved the same US$6; and the 13th month bonus was withheld from spite after a quarrel with the factory president over a fan. [Para 37][Para 44][Para 48][Para 53][Para 73]
**Employer (respondent):** It complied with Notifications 041/11, 049/10 and 230, paying 2,000 riel a day for Sunday overtime, 2,000 riel for holiday overtime beyond eight hours, and 2,000 riel plus 2,000 more after 6 p.m. on weekdays; it paid US$11 monthly seniority beyond 11 years; the 11 fixed duration workers were not union members and gave no authorisation; and the 13th month bonus rewarded team leaders with staff, so it was not a benefit preserved on demotion. [Para 38][Para 43][Para 47][Para 55][Para 166]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no substantive relief — rights issues 1, 2, 3 and 9 declined, issue 12 rejected, interests issues 5, 6, 8 and 10 declined; Part I binding under the MoU dated 3 October 2012, Part II non-binding. [Para 170][Para 172]

Rights disputes concern enforcement of law or a collective agreement under Article 312 paragraph 2 of the Labour Law and clause 43 of Prakas 099 of 21 April 2004; all else is decided in equity (Awards 05/11, 13/11, 14/11, 31/11, 62/11). [Para 83][Para 84][Para 86]

On issues 1, 2 and 3 the panel held it had no jurisdiction: point 1 of Article 353 of the Code of Civil Procedure requires a court execution ruling before any arbitral award, domestic or foreign, may be executed, so the panel cannot order a party to implement an earlier award. It declined each demand, carrying that reasoning from issue 1 into issues 2 and 3. [Para 88][Para 91][Para 92][Para 93]

On issue 5 the panel split holiday work from Sunday work. Under Articles 137, 139 and 164 of the Labour Law and clauses 2 and 4 of Prakas 10 of 4 February 1999, and following Awards 115/08 (Top One) and 52/12 (Star Sportswear), holiday work is not overtime — eight hours a day, 48 a week, at the ordinary rate plus the holiday indemnity — so the 2,000 riel paid for holiday overtime beyond eight hours satisfied Notification 041/11. Sunday work is overtime, the week running Monday to Saturday (114/08 Whitex Garment), but the employer already paid the 2,000 riel a day the notification requires. Both demands were interests disputes, declined for want of MRS: an interests award replaces a collective agreement and binds all workers, so only a union with MRS, or with more than half the workforce, may bring one (81/04, 98/04, 169/11, 02/11, 66/11); with 35 members in 295, CIUF did not qualify. [Para 103][Para 106][Para 109][Para 113][Para 114][Para 115][Para 122]

On issue 6 the employer's practice beat the notification — 2,000 riel for 4 to 6 p.m. plus a further 2,000 beyond — so the demand exceeded the law. On issue 8, point 3 of Notification 041/11 caps seniority at US$11 monthly beyond 11 years, which was paid, and no rule required a US$1 annual increment. Both were interests disputes and were declined. [Para 128][Para 130][Para 136][Para 137]

On issue 9 the panel found no claimant: the 11 workers named were not union members, filed no authorisation and did not attend. Issue 10 failed likewise — point 2 of Notification 230 of 25 July 2012 conditions the attendance bonus on attendance without absence, and nothing obliged the employer to pay it in full during certified sick leave. [Para 140][Para 144][Para 148]

Issue 12 was a rights dispute, the 13th month bonus being written into the agreement of 2 December 2004. Under Article 2 of the Labour Law the employer may supervise and direct workers and organise teams with or without a hierarchy (62/06 Quicksew, 108/06, 33/07, 119/09), so restructuring the overlocking section to a team without a head was lawful; Ms R did not protest at the 23 March 2011 meeting and gave no evidence of objection, so the panel assumed acceptance. On entitlement, the agreement gave 50 per cent of one month's base wage to team leaders with staff who managed the work well; with no workers left she was not entitled unless the employer agreed otherwise. The minutes preserved "the same benefits", but the term was undefined and the employer said it excluded the 13th month bonus, unopposed, so the panel rejected the demand. [Para 150][Para 157][Para 161][Para 162][Para 163][Para 168]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that an employer be ordered to implement an earlier award is outside the panel's jurisdiction: under Article 353 point 1 of the Code of Civil Procedure only a court execution ruling can enforce an award. A union without MRS, or without more than half the workforce, cannot bring an interests dispute, because such an award substitutes for a collective agreement and binds all workers. And a bonus an agreement ties to holding a post ceases with the lawful removal of that post; a general promise to preserve "benefits" on demotion does not revive it absent the employer's agreement. [Para 91][Para 114][Para 163][Para 168]

**Obiter dicta:** holiday work is not overtime while Sunday work is; the employer's weekday meal allowance practice was "better than" Notification 041/11; and minutes of 20 October 2010 were accepted though two signatories denied agreeing. [Para 106][Para 122][Para 130][Para 53]
