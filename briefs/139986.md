# Award No. 61/12 — Yov Um (Arbitration Council, 9 April 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 139986

## 1. Facts
Yov Um is a garment enterprise at Village M, Norom, Baek Chan commune, Ang Snuol district, Kandal province. Conciliation of the collective dispute on 9 March 2012 settled 10 of 12 demands; the two unresolved demands — a machine-skill allowance and extra pregnancy-related release time — reached the Council secretariat on 16 March 2012. A hearing was fixed for 30 March 2012 at 14:30 and both parties were formally summoned by letter and by telephone. Neither the employer nor the worker party appeared, and neither gave a reason. [Para 1][Para 4][Para 8][Para 10][Para 11][Para 14]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay a machine-skill allowance to sewers — USD 3 for one machine, USD 6.50 for two, USD 10 for three or more? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company must let pregnant workers (from one month of pregnancy) leave 10 minutes before 10:00 and return 10 minutes after working hours, preserving their other benefits? [Para 4]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company should pay the skill allowance on the stated scale, and should allow pregnant workers the two 10-minute adjustments without losing any other benefit. [Para 2][Para 4]
**Employer (respondent):** it refused the skill allowance because setting it is the company's own right, and refused the pregnancy arrangement without giving a reason. [Para 3][Para 5]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** withdrawn — the panel ordered the case closed (បិទ សំណុំរឿង) without ruling on either demand, because both parties were absent from the hearing. [Para 37][Para 39]

The panel first satisfied itself of jurisdiction: the order is issued under Chapter 12, Section 2(B) (Articles 309–317) of the 1997 Labour Law, Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 on the Arbitration Council, its attached procedural rules, and Prakas No. 136 of 7 June 2011 on appointment of Council members (9th mandate). Conciliation had been conducted under Section 2(A) and had failed. [Para 6][Para 7]

It then applied Article 21 of Prakas No. 099, which allows the panel either to continue in a party's absence or to terminate the arbitration by issuing an order where a properly summoned party fails to appear without valid reason. Rule 4.7 of the annexed procedure says the same, subject to the requirement that the parties were correctly notified of the date, time and place. [Para 20][Para 21]

Reading its own prior orders (Nos. 30/05, 16/07, 27/07, 3/VE, 95/08, 132/08, 138/08, 151/08, 09/09, 01/10 and 30/10), the panel distilled three cumulative conditions for closing a case by order: proper notification; non-appearance at the hearing; and no valid reason for that non-appearance. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24]

On condition one, the secretariat had sent formal letters to the employer (No. 331) and to the worker party (No. 332), both dated 21 March 2012, and had also contacted both sides by telephone about the date, time and place. Notification was therefore proper. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 28][Para 29]

On condition two, neither party attended on 30 March 2012 and neither appointed a representative such as a union or a lawyer. Condition three was also met: neither side gave the secretariat any valid reason for not attending. With all three conditions satisfied, the panel closed the case. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 35][Para 36]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a party has been correctly notified of the date, time and place of the arbitration hearing, fails to appear or to send a representative, and offers no valid reason, the panel may terminate the arbitration and close the case by order rather than proceed in absentia. [Para 20][Para 22][Para 25]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that it could equally have continued the proceedings in the parties' absence; termination was a discretion, not a mandatory consequence. [Para 20][Para 34]
