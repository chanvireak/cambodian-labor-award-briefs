# Award No. 050/15 — Van Thi J Rivar Tek Stay Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 24 March 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborna, chosen by the employer; An Non, chosen by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, chair chosen by the two) · employer-chosen arbitrator dissenting in an annex · Doc ID 139152

## 1. Facts
Van Thi J Rivar Tek Stay Co Ltd sews garments at Sangkat 4, Sihanoukville, and employed 439 workers, 395 of them women, at the time of the award. [Para 59][Para 60] Its union branch was elected on 16 November 2014 and was still seeking registration. [Para 61] Ms S, worker No. 082 in sewing group 01 and the union's treasurer, had worked there since 21 November 2013 on an oral contract and was dismissed on 06 February 2015 after a dispute over sewing percentages in which she was accused of spreading false information and of telling management "be careful". [Para 63][Para 65][Para 64][Para 101] About 100 workers struck for 11 and a half days, from 12 February to the morning of 25 February 2015, demanding her reinstatement and performance of a 14-point agreement of 21 January 2015 that the employer had not fully carried out. [Para 128][Para 3]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back Ms S and pay her wages from the date of dismissal until reinstatement? [Para 143]
2. Whether the employer must carry out the agreement of 21 January 2015 — settled at the hearing by the parties' agreement to honour it. [Para 126][Para 127]
3. Whether the employer must pay wages, the 3,000 riel daily meal allowance and the USD 7 monthly transport and accommodation allowance to the workers who struck? [Para 182][Para 128]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Ms S committed no serious misconduct and her dismissal had no valid reason; the employer itself caused the strike by dismissing her unlawfully, so it should keep paying wages, the 3,000 riel meal allowance and the USD 7 during the strike; and it hired seven new workers while the strike was running. [Para 111][Para 133][Para 193]
**Employer (respondent):** Ms S lied and exaggerated information to stir up unrest in the factory, refused the written warning, and used threatening and disparaging words against the company's representatives — serious misconduct under Article 83 of the Labour Law — so it will not take her back; and paying strikers would be unfair to the workers who kept working. [Para 98][Para 101][Para 112][Para 142]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to take back Ms S and to pay her arrears from the date of dismissal until reinstatement, and dismissed the claim for wages and allowances during the strike. [Para 211][Para 212]
On issue 1, Article 83 lists serious misconduct, and following award 107/04 Zaksintex the employer must prove it. [Para 144][Para 147][Para 149] The panel took the two grounds separately. On the shouting, Article 10 point 2 of the internal rules classes "shouting, joking around in the workplace that disturbs others and refusing to stop" as a **medium** fault, punished by a written warning, then unpaid suspension of up to 7 days, and dismissal only on a third occurrence; the conduct could not therefore be a serious fault, and the rules nowhere class the disturbance alleged as one. [Para 121][Para 122][Para 123][Para 124][Para 157][Para 161] The employer did not prove the half hour of interrupted sewing it asserted; the witness present said other workers only looked up for about ten minutes before the group leader told them to carry on. [Para 159][Para 160][Para 119] On the words "be careful", neither the Labour Law nor Article 10 point 4 of the rules defines the scope of a "threat"; the two sides read the phrase differently, and beyond its own characterisation the employer produced no specific evidence of disparagement or threats amounting to dismissal. [Para 165][Para 170][Para 171][Para 172][Para 173][Para 174][Para 176] It concluded Ms S committed no serious misconduct, and under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 SAK BY of 21 April 2004 ordered reinstatement in her old post with back pay, following awards 82/14 Suon Osaha Kanhaphen, 93/14 Teratith and 115/14 Teyang. [Para 178][Para 179][Para 180][Para 181]
On issue 3 the panel applied Article 332(1) and Article 72(1) of the Labour Law: a labour contract is suspended during a strike, work is not performed and wages are not paid. [Para 182][Para 185] Following award 27/10 Hat In The Bay, it held that whether or not a strike is procedurally lawful the employer owes no wages or bonuses for the days struck, and it refused the wage claim. [Para 186][Para 187][Para 191][Para 196] The 3,000 riel meal allowance was owed under no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice; the company pays it in practice, above the statutory floor, but Article 332(1) removed any duty during the strike. [Para 197][Para 198][Para 199][Para 202][Para 203] The USD 7 travel and accommodation allowance, under point 1 of Notice No. 230 K.B/S.J.N of 25 July 2012 and award 81/13 IM V, compensates travel to and from work or accommodation for working, so workers who did not work during the strike had no right to it. [Para 204][Para 205][Para 206][Para 209][Para 210] On Article 334, which forbids hiring replacements during a strike and requires the employer to pay all strikers' wages if it does, the workers filed no evidence and no proof that seven new workers were hired. [Para 192][Para 193][Para 194][Para 195]
The employer-chosen arbitrator, Mar Samborna, dissented in an annex: he agreed there was no serious misconduct under Article 83, but held the dismissal lawful under Articles 74 and 69 as a dismissal on notice for Ms S's conduct and repeated faults, and would have refused reinstatement while ordering severance under Article 89, pay in lieu of notice under Articles 75 and 77, unused annual leave under Article 166 and final wages under Article 116, and refusing moral damages under Article 91. [Para 214][Para 216][Para 217][Para 222][Para 228][Para 231][Para 235][Para 238][Para 243][Para 245]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Conduct that the enterprise's own internal rules classify as a medium fault, carrying a written warning and then a short unpaid suspension before any dismissal, cannot be treated as serious misconduct under Article 83; and where the law and the rules leave a phrase such as "threat" undefined and the employer produces no evidence beyond its own reading of it, the employer has not discharged its burden, so the dismissal is without valid reason and the remedy is reinstatement with back pay. [Para 157][Para 161][Para 170][Para 173][Para 181]
Separately, a strike suspends the contract, and the employer owes neither wages, meal allowance nor the USD 7 travel allowance for strike days — even if the strike was unlawful — unless it breached Article 334 by hiring replacements. [Para 185][Para 187][Para 203][Para 209]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remarks that a disciplinary penalty must be clearly defined and proportioned to the fault, and that employers should apply the internal rules' disciplinary steps before dismissing, state a standard broader than the outcome required. [Para 163][Para 174][Para 175]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is Khmer only and heavily garbled. The incentive paid to group 1 is printed as USD 15 in the company's account and as USD 13 where the workers' account is reported. [Para 66][Para 68][Para 70][Para 71] The hearing is dated 04 March 2015, but the invitations paragraph gives 04 February 2015. [Para 8][Para 13] The case was created on "19 February 2020 2015", both years printed. [Para 12] The company's name appears in at least four spellings (including "ជម្រះ រី" and "វ៉ាន ធី" alone); the Latin form in the heading is a rendering. [Para 1][Para 153] Ms S's name is spelled three ways and she is also called "ចរិយា". [Para 98][Para 100][Para 63] Paragraphs 148, 189 and 207 contain duplicated or emptied sentences, and the signature block mangles the chair's name into "ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ណាស់20277" before printing it correctly. [Para 214] The dissent's quotation of Article 74 is largely unrecoverable OCR ("កម្ពុជា រដ្ឋសភាគ ច្រើន"), so its terms are reported only as the dissent states them. [Para 221] The strike's length is given as 11 and a half days and by its end date, and no compensation figure is computed anywhere, so the sums ordered are unquantified. [Para 128][Para 211]
