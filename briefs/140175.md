# Award No. 177/12 — Sin Chang Sin Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, Phnom Penh (Arbitration Council, 25 October 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phallaph presiding; Chhiv Phirom, employer nominee; Vaen Pov, worker nominee) · Doc ID 140175

## 1. Facts
Sin Chang Sin Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, at Sangkat Tuol Sangkae, Khan Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, is a garment manufacturer employing 920 workers, and the complainant was the factory branch of the Cambodian Democratic Garment Workers' Union (CCAWDU), which held a registration certificate and had 13 members. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40][Para 41] Of twelve demands, five remained unresolved after the final conciliation of 30 August 2012 and came to the panel under non-conciliation report No. 1017 Kb/Ak/Vk of 4 September 2012. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 14] At the hearing of 26 September 2012 Issues 3 and 4 were settled, leaving Issues 1, 2 and 5 for decision, and the parties agreed to postpone the award from 28 September to 25 October 2012. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 25] The two administration assistants who appeared for the employer held no written transfer of authority, and none was filed by the evidence deadline, so the panel decided the case in the employer's absence on that footing. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 73][Para 74] The demands for meals in excess of the statutory allowance, and for milk money beyond twenty dollars, were refused for want of a representative union; only the attendance-bonus claim was ordered, and only for one worker. [Para 83][Para 116][Para 119]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must provide three meals a day to every worker, or a food allowance of USD 3 a day, where neither the Labour Law nor any agreement provides it? [Para 44][Para 47][Para 75]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay USD 20 a month as a milk allowance, for 24 months, to women returning from maternity leave? [Para 48][Para 49][Para 85]
3. (Issue 5) Whether an employer may deduct the whole monthly attendance bonus when a worker takes leave with its permission, or only the pro-rata share of the days absent, and whether the four workers who transferred their rights proved any deduction? [Para 54][Para 89][Para 97]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** prices and rent have risen while wages are low, so three meals a day would both meet their costs and encourage attendance; other factories pay a milk allowance — USD 17 plus three tins at Yong Va, USD 20 for 24 months at Da Rong, USD 15 at Sabrina; and the employer wiped out the whole attendance bonus for May and June 2012 although the absences were authorised, the practice having been a daily bonus of USD 8 before Notification No. 041/11 took effect. [Para 44][Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 54][Para 55]
**Employer (respondent):** it cannot provide three meals or milk money; the Ministry has recently issued notices imposing many allowances, workers are paid by output at USD 200 to 300 a month, and the company's long-standing practice is to deduct the entire attendance bonus whenever a worker is absent, authorised or not. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 56] Its administration assistants appeared in its name without a written mandate, so these contentions were not weighed. [Para 42][Para 74]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demands for three meals and for a USD 20 milk allowance were refused, and the employer was ordered to repay the pro-rata attendance bonus for June 2012 to the single worker with ID A 91; the award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification. [Para 117][Para 118][Para 119][Para 120]
The panel began with representation, holding under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 that a party may appear only in person or through a person authorised in writing, and following Awards 71/08-River Rich and 161/09-Taiyang that a representative without such authority cannot bind the party. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67][Para 69][Para 70] It therefore decided the case in the employer's absence and disregarded the administration assistants' arguments. [Para 71][Para 74]
On Issues 1 and 2 it found no provision of the Labour Law, no collective agreement and no agreement between the parties granting three meals, a USD 3 food allowance or a milk allowance, so both were disputes about interests. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 85][Para 86] Because an interest award substitutes for the collective agreement for a year and strips other workers of the right to strike over the same subject, the panel will entertain such a claim only from the most representative union, or from several unions together representing more than half the workforce. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 80][Para 81] CCAWDU had 13 members among 920 workers, so neither test was met and both demands were refused. [Para 82][Para 83][Para 84][Para 88]
On Issue 5 the panel applied the rule that the party making a claim must prove it, and found that of four workers who transferred their rights only the worker with ID A 91 showed a deduction — in June 2012 — while two showed deductions in March and one named no month at all. [Para 90][Para 91][Para 93][Para 94][Para 95] On the merits it held the attendance bonus to be a wage under Article 103, that leave with the employer's permission suspends the contract under Article 71(6) so that no wage is owed for those days under Article 72, but that this entitles the employer to deduct only the pro-rata share of the days absent and never the whole bonus. [Para 104][Para 105][Para 107][Para 110][Para 111][Para 112][Para 115] It ordered repayment accordingly for the one proved worker. [Para 116][Para 119]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may not withhold the whole monthly attendance bonus because of an authorised absence: leave permitted by the employer suspends the contract, so the bonus is reduced in proportion to the days absent and no further. [Para 110][Para 112][Para 115]
A claimant must prove the deduction it alleges, worker by worker and month by month; where a union transfers the rights of only some workers, the panel decides only the cases the evidence establishes, and dismisses the rest for want of proof. [Para 90][Para 93][Para 95][Para 96]
A party appearing through a representative without a written authority cannot be heard: the panel proceeds in that party's absence and disregards its submissions. [Para 66][Para 70][Para 74]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that an award on interests operates as a collective agreement for one year and would remove other workers' right to strike on the same subject, a consequence it weighed although it refused the claims on standing rather than on merit. [Para 78][Para 79]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The four workers who transferred their rights are named in full, together with employee numbers, across the evidence and the reasoning; they are identified here only by those numbers, and the repetition of the names in the operative order is not reproduced. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 59][Para 60][Para 62][Para 116][Para 119] The employer's name is printed in four forms — "Sin Chang Sin", "Sing Chang Sin", "Xing Chang Xin" and "Sin Chang Xin" — sometimes as a garment enterprise and sometimes as "Garment Co., Ltd", and the union's registration is dated 26 August 2011 in the findings but 26 August 2012 in the exhibit list. [Para 1][Para 32][Para 38][Para 41] Paragraph 10 repeats its own opening line, and the operative order at paragraph 117 is printed twice, the second copy reduced to fragmentary words, so the wording of the first order must be read with care. [Para 10][Para 117] The milk demand is stated as "2 20 dollars" in the order, and the award records that the parties agreed to move the date from 28 September to 25 October 2012 although the hearing was held on 26 September. [Para 25][Para 118] The employer's delegates are named as individuals and are described only by their roles here, and the record does not say whether the employer was notified of the authority requirement before the hearing. [Para 42][Para 73]
