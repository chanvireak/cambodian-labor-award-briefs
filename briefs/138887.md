# Award No. 014/18 — Qun Xing (Cambodia) Garment Factory (Arbitration Council, 25 May 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Seng Vouch Hun (employer-appointed), Liv Sovanna (worker-appointed), Taing Try (president) · Doc ID 138887

## 1. Facts
Roughly 400 workers at Qun Xing (Cambodia) Garment Factory, represented by the Khmer Youth Union of the factory, filed a collective dispute on 12 February 2018 asking the employer to improve working conditions. Conciliation at the Ministry of Labour on 6 April 2018 settled eleven of thirteen points; two — the attendance-bonus deduction and a lunch allowance — were referred to the Arbitration Council and remained unresolved at hearing. [Para 1][Para 12][Para 13][Para 33]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must refrain from deducting the regular attendance bonus (ប្រាក់រង្វាន់ធ្វើការទៀងទាត់) when a worker has requested leave and the employer has authorised it? [Para 38][Para 69]
2. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of 2,000 riel per day to the workers? [Para 49][Para 121]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company deducts the attendance bonus whatever the ground for leave, so the bonus should survive any leave the employer actually grants; other factories such as Queen Menor pay a midday meal allowance, and this is a request for improvement, not a legal demand. [Para 38][Para 44][Para 50]
**Employer (respondent):** it applies the existing company policy in three stages — 5 USD off the bonus for a first absence day, 7.5 USD for a second, and the whole bonus for a third — because workers never state the reason for leave and merely write that they are busy; it has no capacity to pay a meal allowance. [Para 4][Para 39][Para 45][Para 51]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel dismissed the claim that the employer must preserve the bonus whenever it authorises leave, but ordered the employer to retain the whole attendance bonus when a worker is absent on leave that is a legal right, a contractual right, or leave compelled by legal obligation; it declined to consider the lunch-allowance claim at all. [Para 119][Para 120][Para 142][Para 145]

On representation, Article 54(1) of the Union Law gives only the most representative union the exclusive right to conclude a collective agreement and to carry a collective dispute; since both sides confirmed no most representative union exists at the company, the panel followed Award 180/16 (Cement Cambodia Chakrey Ting) and Prakas 362 of 29 August 2016 to hold that workers certified by the Labour Disputes Department may represent the workforce, and that the two certified representatives present at the hearing had standing. [Para 52][Para 55][Para 58][Para 63][Para 67] On the merits, Notification No. 230 K.B/S.J.N of 25 July 2012 requires an attendance bonus of at least 10 USD a month for workers with no absence. [Para 69] The panel adopted the Full Bench decision of 3 October 2017: the bonus is payable at 100% where the worker attended every working day, including leave that is a legal right, contractual leave, and leave due to legal obligation; nothing is owed for absence outside those categories unless the employer's own policy or a separate agreement is more generous. [Para 73][Para 74] Applying that to the evidence, annual leave, special leave under Prakas 267 and sick leave count as presence, so current company practice was not yet correct and the employer must retain the full bonus for the three protected categories. [Para 88][Para 110][Para 112] The wider claim failed because outside those categories the employer keeps discretion, and the three-stage deduction is itself more favourable than the statutory floor. [Para 108][Para 113][Para 114]

On the meal allowance, a demand above the legal minimum is an interest dispute. [Para 123] Under Article 43(1) of Prakas 099 an award in an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement and binds every worker in the enterprise for one year, so the panel may decide such a claim only where the union bringing it is most representative or otherwise entitled under Articles 54 and 72 of the Union Law. [Para 134][Para 137] This union was neither, so the panel refused to consider the claim. [Para 140][Para 141]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus may not be used to penalise the exercise of a leave entitlement: absence on leave that is a legal right, a contractual right, or compelled by legal obligation must be treated as presence and the bonus paid in full, while for any other absence the employer may apply its own progressive deduction policy provided it is no worse than the statutory floor. Separately, a panel will not decide an interest claim brought by a union that is not most representative in the enterprise, because an interest award would bind all workers and extinguish their right to bargain and strike over the same demand. [Para 107][Para 112][Para 137]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that workers did not understand the company's leave-request form and had not been told to state reasons, and said the employer should publicise its policy so workers can complete the form correctly — a recommendation carrying no order. [Para 116][Para 117]
