# Decision 100/04 — Ho Hing Garment (Arbitration Council, 3 December 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · signed by Kong Phallack, An Nan Seng and Vuoch Hun · Doc ID 139363

## 1. Facts
The document is not a reasoned award but a short signed Council decision in case 100/04 concerning Ho Hing Garment, issued in Phnom Penh on 3 December 2004 [Para 1][Para 3]. The factory's operating status was unresolved: the Labour Inspector had still to decide whether it should run or be completely closed, and that fact governed whether the case could proceed [Para 1]. The Council had earlier made an Order in the same case on 24 November 2004, which this decision annulled [Para 3].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the Council may continue to arbitrate case 100/04 while the Labour Inspector has not determined whether the factory will reopen or close permanently? [Para 1]
2. Whether the Council's Order of 24 November 2004 in case 100/04 should stand? [Para 3]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** no position is recorded in the document; the decision refers only to the workers of Ho Hing Garment, who are to apply it from the day of signing [Para 3].
**Employer (respondent):** no position is recorded; the employer's only recorded obligation is to pay the workers' wages according to the Labour Law if the factory is completely closed [Para 2].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no award on the merits — the case was put on hold, the Order of 24 November 2004 was annulled, and the parties were ordered to apply the decision from the day of its signing [Para 1][Para 3].

The panel's reasoning is stated in two propositions only. First, because the Labour Inspector had not yet decided whether the factory should run or be completely closed, arbitration was suspended; if the factory is reopened the case will continue to be resolved at the Council [Para 1]. Second, closure would not extinguish the wage debt: if the factory is completely closed, the employer must pay the workers' wages according to the Labour Law [Para 2]. The earlier Order of 24 November 2004 was annulled without reasons being given, and the decision was made effective from signature rather than after the usual period for opposition [Para 3].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the continued existence of the enterprise is undecided and that fact determines whether the dispute can be arbitrated at all, the Council suspends the case instead of deciding it, and withdraws any interim order it had made; suspension is not a release, because permanent closure leaves the employer bound to pay the workers' wages under the Labour Law [Para 1][Para 2].

**Obiter dicta:** that the case would resume before the Council if the factory reopened — a statement of future procedure, not necessary to the suspension [Para 1].
