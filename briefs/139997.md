# Award No. 20/11 — Sepakor Angkor Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 24 February 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry, employer-appointed; An Nan, worker-appointed; Kong Phallack, chair) · Doc ID 139997

## 1. Facts
Sepakor Angkor Co Ltd, a semi-state company, began operating in 2003 at Viheachin village, Svaydangkom commune, Siem Reap city, and employs about 1,078 workers. [Para 12][Para 13] The Local Union of BWTUC, established in 2007, says it represents 541 workers and collects contributions independently, but it holds no certificate of most-representative status. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 15] The union filed two demands on 4 January 2011 and conciliation on 19 January 2011 failed on both. [Para 5][Para 6] At the hearing the parties settled the second demand, leaving the wage demand. [Para 8][Para 9]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must increase the hourly wages of 499 piece-rate workers who are union members, working in the weaving, silk, wood-sculpture, stone-sculpture, colouring and copper-sculpture sections, by US$0.06 per hour? [Para 15][Para 16]
2. Whether the employer must pay 50% of wages where workers damage a product unintentionally. [Para 1]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer has already raised hourly wages by US$0.03, but the workers want a further US$0.03, a total of US$0.06, because profits have grown: products were sold in large numbers ahead of the buyer schedule, more workers were recruited, and the company arranged a three-day trip for workers. [Para 16] Wages rose by US$0.06–0.08 in 2007, US$0.05 in 2008, US$0.04 in 2010 and US$0.03 in 2011, with 2009 skipped because of the economic crisis. [Para 17][Para 18] Piece-rate workers earn about US$70–150 a month. [Para 22]
**Employer (respondent):** It can only afford US$0.03, which took effect on 1 January 2011 and lifted the average of 503 craftsmen from US$127 to US$142 for January 2011. [Para 26] Raw-material and production costs have risen and the January increase forced a 15% rise in silk prices, after which silk sales fell; stone sculptures and polychromes remain unsold and the factory must be relocated. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 29][Para 30] It says the dispute is an interests dispute with no basis in law or a collective agreement, and that neither side has such an agreement on wages. [Para 30]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** the Council declined to consider the wage demand and held the employer was not obliged to add a further US$0.03. [Para 43][Para 44]
The Council read clause 24 of the Internal Work Rules of March 2008, which ties any wage increase to the employer's good economic situation and to craftsmen's performance, decided in consultation with management, and makes a collective increase depend on the market and the company's situation. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 34][Para 35] It found the employer had assessed its own and the market situation before setting rates, as the rules require. [Para 38] The rules nowhere require an increase of US$0.06, and the workers had no legal basis or agreement to support the claim, so the demand was an interests dispute. [Para 39][Para 40]
For an interests dispute the Council asked whether the union held most-representative status. [Para 41] It followed earlier awards (81/04 Evergreen, 09/05 Kin Tai, 84/07 Yung Wah 2, 108/07 8 Star Sportswear, 135/07 Wilson, 14/08 Quicksew, 101/08 GDM, 42/09 River Rich) holding that status gives a union capacity to negotiate a collective agreement and standing to bring an interests dispute. [Para 41][Para 42] The Local Union of BWTUC held no such certificate, so it had no standing and the Council declined to consider the demand. [Para 42][Para 43]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A wage-increase demand with no basis in law, an employment contract or a collective agreement is an interests dispute, and the Council will decline to consider it when the union bringing it lacks most-representative status. An employer that has weighed its own economic position and the market before fixing rates has discharged its duty under its internal work rules, and the Council will not substitute its own view of the right figure. [Para 39][Para 41][Para 42]

**Obiter dicta:** The Council noted that the union collects contributions independently even without most-representative status, and that the second demand was settled at conciliation on the employer's existing practice of paying 35% of wages for unintentional damage. [Para 1][Para 14]
