# Award No. 61/08 — Foukis Foot Wear (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 28 May 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ri for the employer party, Ven Pov for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhea chair) · Doc ID 140516

## 1. Facts
Foukis Foot Wear (Cambodia) (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្វូភី ស ហ្វូត វៀន (ខេមបូឌៀ)), a shoe factory at Tuol Sangke, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, faced a collective dispute raised by the Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union at the factory (សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ ប្រចាំ រោងចក្រ), which complained on 22 January 2008 and referred two non-conciliated points by report No. 485 dated 24 April 2008. [Para 1][Para 11][Para 8][Para 13] The hearing was held on 12 May 2008, and at it the parties settled Issue 1 — the demand that the company stop discriminating against three union leaders by excluding them from overtime — leaving only the union-dues deduction for decision. [Para 10][Para 16][Para 17] On 20 May 2008 the union filed a fresh list of 817 members who consented to the deduction, and the company was given until 22 May 2008 to object to it. [Para 20][Para 23][Para 32][Para 33]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must deduct 1,000 riel a month from the wages of union members who have voluntarily authorised it, in order to pay their union dues to the Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union at the factory, given that the union is now registered with the Ministry of Labour? [Para 4][Para 37][Para 46]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union at Foukis Foot Wear, សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ ប្រចាំ រោងចក្រ):** the union leaders asked that the company stop discriminating against them by refusing them overtime, and the union asked the company to deduct 1,000 riel a month from each member's wages to pay union dues, on the strength of members' written consent and of registration certificate No. 1227 dated 10 September 2007, which it said made it a lawful organisation. [Para 2][Para 4][Para 29][Para 30]
**Employer (respondent — Foukis Foot Wear (Cambodia)):** the company said the three leaders could not work overtime because they were busy with union work, and that it could not make the deduction because its computerised payroll system was already built and too hard to change — the same difficulty it said prevented it from entering the US$ 6 allowance due from 4 April 2008, which it had to hand out in envelopes instead. [Para 3][Para 5][Para 34][Para 35][Para 36]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to deduct 1,000 riel from the wages of workers who are members of the Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union and have voluntarily authorised the deduction, and to pay that sum to the union as their dues. [Para 60]

The panel first asked whether the union had the legal right to require the deduction. Under Article 268 of the Labour Law a professional organisation must deposit its statutes and membership list with the ministry in charge of labour to obtain registration, and registration is deemed granted if the ministry does not respond within two months. The panel found that this union had been registered by certificate No. 1227 dated 10 September 2007, so it was a lawful organisation in full standing, including the right to require the employer to deduct dues from consenting members' wages. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 42][Para 43][Para 44][Para 45]

The panel distinguished its earlier award in case No. 64/07-Foukis Foot Wear, which concerned the same demand: there the union was still awaiting registration, so it had no full legal right to demand the deduction. Registration having since been granted, that obstacle was gone. [Para 28][Para 38][Para 39]

On the second question the panel applied Article 129 paragraph 2 of the Labour Law, under which workers may authorise deduction from their wages to pay dues to a union they belong to, the authorisation being in writing and revocable at any time, and Article 5 of Prakas No. 305 of 2001, which requires a written request 15 days in advance. It noted that Article 281 forbids an employer to reduce wages in order to pay union dues, or to pay the dues on the workers' behalf, and that Articles 129 and 281 appear to conflict; following a line of earlier awards, it read Article 281 as protecting workers and preventing employer interference and domination of the union under Article 280, not as barring a deduction the worker has requested in writing. The employer was therefore under a duty to deduct. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 55][Para 56]

The panel did not accept the payroll-system objection as a reason to refuse a statutory duty, and the operative order covers only members who consented in writing on the list filed on 20 May 2008. [Para 34][Para 59][Para 60]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union registered under Article 268 of the Labour Law may require an employer to deduct its dues from the wages of members who have authorised the deduction in writing; Article 281's prohibition on employers reducing wages to pay union dues is a protection against employer interference and domination of the union under Article 280, not a bar on a deduction the worker has himself requested, and the employer's administrative inconvenience in its payroll system is no answer to that statutory duty. [Para 44][Para 48][Para 53][Para 55][Para 56]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's comparison with its earlier award in the same factory, where registration was still pending, explains why the same demand succeeded this time but is not a rule for future cases. [Para 28][Para 38]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The employer's name appears in at least five Khmer transliterations across the file, and the arbitration panel's own chair is printed with two different final letters in the header and the signature block [Para 1][Para 22][Para 24][Para 45][Para 62].
- The union's registration certificate is dated 10 September 2007 in the reasoning but 10 September 2008 in the evidence list, which is impossible given the January 2008 complaint [Para 22][Para 29][Para 44].
- In the signature block the worker-party arbitrator's name is printed twice in succession, as is the chair's [Para 62].
- The quoted text of Article 268 and Article 129 is heavily corrupted, with whole phrases displaced, so the statutory wording cannot be reconstructed from the award [Para 40][Para 42][Para 46].
- The reasons refer to a demand as "1,000 riel per month for each member" and elsewhere to a "US$ 6 allowance" paid in envelopes, without reconciling the two payments [Para 4][Para 35].
- The three union leaders named in the original complaint are not identified here; union officers and the employer's administration head are described by role only.
