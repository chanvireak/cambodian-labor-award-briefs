# Award No. 017/22 — V.T.C. Borei Sen (វ៉ាយ ធី ស៊ី ខប ប៉ូរេសិន) (Arbitration Council, 14 June 2022)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ouk Ry (employer-selected), Tuon Si Phan (worker-selected), Pen Bun Chea (president) · Doc ID 140870

## 1. Facts
This was a collective labour dispute at ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ាយ ធី ស៊ី ខប ប៉ូរេសិន, a garment-cutting enterprise employing 2,604 workers [Para 44]. The workers' side (ភាគី តំណាង កម្មករ និយោជិត) filed a complaint on 17 March 2022 raising three demands; conciliation on 31 March 2022 settled two of them, and the single unresolved demand was referred to the Council [Para 11][Para 12]. That demand was that the employer dismiss the head of the cutting section, Mr V, for serious misconduct under Article 83 of the Labour Law, after he repeatedly touched a female worker's thigh [Para 2][Para 46]. The worker had also lodged a criminal complaint with the police, which was still pending at the Kampong Speu provincial court when the panel sat [Para 2][Para 64]. The award is signed with the case number ០១៧/២២ and dated 14 June 2022 [Para 1].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must dismiss the head of the cutting section, Mr V, from employment on the ground that he committed serious misconduct under Article 83 of the Labour Law by harassing a female worker in his section? [Para 152][Para 46]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr V harassed almost every woman in the company and threatened to have anyone who refused him dismissed; the conduct is contempt of the kind named in Article 83 of the Labour Law and in the company's internal rules, so the employer must terminate him [Para 66][Para 68].

**Employer (respondent):** there is no clear evidence against Mr V; the employer is not the adjudicator and will execute whatever the competent authority orders [Para 70]. It suspended his contract for one month to let the police investigate, then let him return to work when nothing came of it [Para 73][Para 59]. The employer also argued the matter was already before the court [Para 152].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the demand that the employer dismiss Mr V, head of the cutting section, under Article 83 of the Labour Law [Para 206].

On jurisdiction, the panel held that the criminal complaint concerned defamation while the claim before it was a labour dispute about the worker's dissatisfaction with a colleague's conduct, so the objects differed and the Council could hear the case [Para 155]. On the merits, Articles 65 of the Labour Law (1997) and 311 of the Civil Code (2007) mean only the parties to a contract may dissolve the obligations it creates, so a third party cannot compel termination of another person's employment contract [Para 160][Para 162]. Article 2 of the Labour Law gives the employer the right to organise and manage the enterprise, exercised lawfully and reasonably [Para 165]; following earlier awards, the panel will not order an employer to transfer or dismiss a section head at the workers' request, but may do so where the person is shown to endanger other workers' health and safety [Para 166][Para 168][Para 171].

Article 83(b)(4) of the Labour Law lists threats, insults and assault on the employer or other workers as serious misconduct [Para 172]; Article 172 forbids sexual harassment in all its forms [Para 174]; Article 10 of the internal rules, filed 27 March 2020, provides for dismissal [Para 178]. The panel noted Mr V admitted an intention to touch the worker's thigh, but found the two employer witnesses inconsistent as to gesture, act and time, and found the worker produced no evidence at all, so the party asserting the claim bore the burden and failed [Para 180][Para 189][Para 192][Para 195][Para 196]. It also weighed that the employer had already responded: a one-month suspension, involvement of the Kampong Speu provincial department of labour, and moving the complainant out of Mr V's supervision [Para 198][Para 199]. The demand was therefore refused [Para 201].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A panel will not order an employer to dismiss a section head at the workers' request unless the misconduct is proved by specific evidence and the person is shown to endanger other workers' health and safety; dismissal remains the employer's managerial decision under Article 2 of the Labour Law, and a third party cannot dissolve an employment contract under Article 65 [Para 171][Para 165][Para 162]. An employer that suspended the individual, sought departmental conciliation and separated the complainant from his supervision has already taken the corrective measures the panel would weigh [Para 199].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed, after refusing the demand, that harassment or sexual harassment at the workplace is a matter employers must attend to, and that the hand-tapping and inappropriate words found here could affect a person's dignity and should attract dismissal under Article 83(b) and Article 10 of the internal rules [Para 202][Para 203][Para 204].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The complainant worker's name is printed in two different Khmer spellings within the same award — one in the statement of the demand and another in the evidence list and findings — so the brief refers to her as Ms K. The signature block at the foot of the award omits the name of the arbitrator selected by the workers, which appears only on the first page [Para 208]. Paragraphs 203–204 state that the employer should dismiss the supervisor under Article 83, while paragraphs 201 and 206 refuse that very demand; §4 and the Outcome above follow the operative decision at paragraph 206, and the inconsistency is reported, not resolved. The police complaint is described as pending before the provincial court at paragraph 2 and as not yet summoned at paragraph 64.
