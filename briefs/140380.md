# Award No. 40/09-Gold Fame — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្គោល ហ្វេម (Arbitration Council, 6 July 2009)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tai Seng, Tuon Si Phan, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 140380

## 1. Facts
The employer, ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្គោល ហ្វេម (Gold Fame), runs a garment factory at Sitbou Village, S'ang District, Kandal Province with roughly 4,500 workers. The claimant is the local union of the Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Worker Democratic Unions (C.CAWDU) at that factory, claiming about 3,700 members. When six fixed-duration contracts expired on 31 December 2008 the company did not renew them; it then announced on 1 June 2009 that five of the six could return, to a new "Auto" section rather than their old posts, and excluded the sixth, an interpreter. Six demands went to conciliation; issue 6 was withdrawn and issues 1 and 3 were merged as one, leaving issues 1, 2, 4 and 5. [Para 1][Para 90][Para 92][Para 35]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must continue the fixed-duration contracts of the five workers who were not returned to their old posts, and whether its refusal to renew the interpreter's contract was union discrimination? [Para 93][Para 201]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the contracts of two warehouse workers must be converted to contracts of undetermined duration because the employer gave nine days' notice of non-renewal instead of ten? [Para 231]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must pay compensation for unused annual leave calculated on the total wages of twelve months? [Para 232]
4. (Issue 5) Whether workers who take authorised leave for ordinary personal reasons still earn the US$5 monthly attendance bonus in full? [Para 255]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, C.CAWDU local union):** the company must continue the five workers' fixed-duration contracts, must take back the seventeen workers it dismissed, must convert the two warehouse contracts to undetermined duration because clause 2.7 of the collective agreement treats a failure to give ten days' notice as converting the contract, must pay out unused annual leave on twelve months' total wages, and must pay the full US$5 attendance bonus despite authorised leave; the interpreter, Mr S, says his contract was not renewed because he joined the union on 10 March 2009. [Para 2][Para 6][Para 151][Para 217]

**Employer (respondent, ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្គោល ហ្វេម):** it re-engaged the five on one-year fixed-duration contracts from 1 June 2009 in the Auto section at unchanged wages and benefits, and cannot put them back in their old section because its head refuses to take them; fixed-duration contracts expire by themselves and one party cannot force renewal; the notice was delivered on 23 December rather than 15 December, nine days before expiry, which it accepts was a technical error but not an absence of notice; and the attendance bonus rewards a full 26-day month, so it cannot be paid in full to workers who were away. [Para 183][Para 199][Para 225][Para 170]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to assign the five workers to new locations where the work is similar to their old jobs, keeping their wages and other benefits unchanged; rejected renewal for Mr S; rejected conversion of the two warehouse contracts; rejected payment of annual leave compensation during employment; and ordered the attendance bonus to be paid in proportion to the days actually worked to workers with authorised leave. [Para 274][Para 275][Para 276][Para 277][Para 278]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 65 of the Labour Law on the nature of the labour contract and Article 22 of the Civil Code, holding that a contract is the law of the parties, may be varied or extinguished only by consent, and cannot be imposed on an unwilling party. Because the company had already re-engaged five workers on similar work at the same pay, and because the old section head refused to receive them, the panel ordered placement in a comparable post rather than the identical one. For Mr S the panel found the total contract period under two years, so Article 67(2) was not breached, and that a fixed-duration contract expires at its term under Article 73, following Award 100/07-Hoya Cambodia. On the discrimination claim under Articles 12 and 279, the panel held the claimant bears the burden and that the timing of union membership alone did not discharge it; the demand was rejected. [Para 175][Para 178][Para 189][Para 192][Para 212]

On issue 2, clause 2.7 of the collective agreement converts a fixed-duration contract to undetermined duration where notice is not given as required by clause 2.6, but clause 2.7 does not cover notice that is merely late. Nine days instead of ten did not deprive the workers of the chance to prepare, and the company had in any event renewed their contracts, so the demand was rejected. [Para 219][Para 221][Para 226][Para 231]

On issue 4 the panel read Articles 166, 167(3) and 168 of the Labour Law as prohibiting cash payment for annual leave while the worker is still employed, permitting only carry-over of up to three years, and allowing payment at termination; the company's practice of buying out leave each year was contrary to Article 167, and the demand was rejected. [Para 239][Para 242][Para 246][Para 253]

On issue 5 the panel found the US$5 attendance bonus part of wages under Article 103 and payable under point 3 of Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000, while authorised absence suspends the contract under Article 71(6) and Article 72(1), lifting the wage obligation for those days only. Following Awards 57/07 and 143/08, it ordered the bonus paid in proportion to days actually worked. [Para 256][Para 263][Para 271][Para 273]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus that forms part of wages must still be paid to a worker who takes authorised leave, but only in proportion to the days actually worked, because authorised absence suspends the contract under Article 71(6) and removes the wage obligation for those days alone; and unused annual leave may not be converted into cash while the employment continues, only carried over for up to three years or paid at termination. [Para 270][Para 246]

**Obiter dicta:** a notice of non-renewal that is late by a day is not the same as no notice for the purposes of a collective agreement clause that converts fixed-duration contracts, and a fixed-duration contract expires automatically at its term so neither party can compel renewal. [Para 223][Para 199]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is a Khmer original of which the OCR is badly degraded: years inside quotations are corrupted, with the annual leave demand dated 2022 and further references to 2014 and 2019 in a 2009 award. The workforce figure is printed as a garbled numeral that reads as 4,500 with stray characters, and the summary of evidence is riddled with bracketed fragments. The employer's name appears in two forms on the face of the award, one with an added Khmer rendering after the company name. The panel members' names are printed twice in the signature block with variant spellings — the employer-nominated arbitrator as both Ly Tai Seng and a differently voweled form, and the worker-nominated arbitrator as both Tuon Si Phan and Nun Si Phan — and one signature block contains a stray untranslated token. The individual workers' names appear in full in the source and are reduced to initials here.
