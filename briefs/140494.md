# Award No. 29/08 — Tairong (Cambodia) Knitting Co., Ltd v Workers' Freedom Union Federation (Arbitration Council, 13 March 2008)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Run Saray chair) · Doc ID 140494

## 1. Facts
Tairong (Cambodia) Knitting Co., Ltd makes socks at Thleuk Village, Peuk Commune, Ang Snuol District, Kandal, employing about 1,000 workers, 650 of them in the weaving and sewing sections. [Para 24][Para 25] The Workers' Freedom Union Federation (WFUF) and its local union, with roughly 200 members and 194 thumbprints on the demand, brought two complaints after five issues were reduced by conciliation. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 19] Since at least mid-2006 the factory has run a rotating weekly day off so that the machinery works 24 hours a day, seven days a week, with the Kandal labour department's approval. [Para 28][Para 31][Para 37] The second complaint concerned the head of administration, who the union said insulted its treasurer over a wage payment request. [Para 41][Para 43]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must schedule the weekly day off on Sunday for all workers? [Para 27][Para 56]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must dismiss, or in the alternative issue a written warning to, the head of administration for insulting a local union officer? [Para 40][Para 87]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, WFUF and its local union at Tairong Company):** Article 147 gives a Sunday day off, and a common Sunday would let the whole workforce rest together; they also asked that the head of administration be dismissed, and in the hearing reduced that to a written warning, relying on the treasurer's account of being called worthless and told to go and study Chinese. [Para 32][Para 40][Para 46][Para 47]
**Employer (respondent, Tairong (Cambodia) Knitting Co., Ltd):** the sock machinery cannot stop without spoiling production, so a rotating day off was agreed in the internal rules and in each employment contract and approved by the labour department; on the second demand it will not warn a manager on the union's word alone, and it denies knowing of the incident. [Para 33][Para 35][Para 37][Para 53][Para 54][Para 80]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the demand for a common Sunday day off and rejected the demand that the company warn the head of administration. [Para 87][Para 88]

On Issue 1, Article 147 requires a weekly rest of at least 24 consecutive hours and in principle on Sunday, but Article 148 allows rest to be rotated where a Sunday rest for all staff would jeopardise the normal operation of the enterprise, subject to authorisation from the ministry in charge of labour. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 61][Para 63] The panel found the weaving and sewing machinery must run continuously or the product deteriorates, that the company requested approval and obtained it by letter No. 036/07 KB/KN of 08 June from the Kandal department (with a monthly reporting requirement), and that Article 149(11) expressly permits rotation in industries where interruption spoils the product. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 67] The internal rules and the contracts also reserved this management prerogative, so the demand had no legal basis. [Para 67][Para 68]

On Issue 2, the panel treated the demand as it was reformulated at the hearing, a written warning. [Para 70][Para 76] Article 2 of the Labour Law and Awards 41/05-Violet Issue 8, 37/07-JRB Issue 3 and 118/07-Jarding Issue 7 place supervision and discipline within the employer's management prerogative, exercised reasonably. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 75] The company refused to act on the union's word without evidence; the insulted officer's account stood alone, the alleged other incidents could not be dated or attributed because those workers had resigned, and the person complained of did not attend. [Para 51][Para 55][Para 56][Para 81] Although the panel called insults inappropriate and urged the company to act to keep the workplace peaceful, it held the demand itself had no legal ground. [Para 84][Para 85][Para 86]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A weekly rest need not fall on Sunday where the enterprise shows that stopping for a common Sunday would jeopardise operations, obtains the labour department's authorisation, and rotates the rest so each worker still receives 24 consecutive hours; the demand for a universal Sunday off then fails. [Para 63][Para 65][Para 67] Discipline, including whether to warn a manager, is the employer's management prerogative, and the panel will not order it on an uncorroborated complaint, though the employer remains free to act on misconduct it establishes. [Para 74][Para 75][Para 86]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that insulting language is inappropriate and that the company should take appropriate measures to create a peaceful workplace is advice, not an order, and did not alter the rejection. [Para 84][Para 85]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The union registration date is given as 08 October 2008 at [Para 27] although the certificate in the exhibit list is dated 08 October 2007 at [Para 22] and the award itself is dated March 2008.
- The labour department's approval letter is dated 08 June 2007 at [Para 22] and [Para 37] but 08 June 2008 at [Para 65], which would post-date the award.
- Workforce figures conflict: about 1,000 workers at [Para 24] and [Para 38], while the approval letter at [Para 37] covers 800 weaving and 400 sewing workers, i.e. about 1,200.
- The procedural note at [Para 19] says five issues were raised, two conciliated, and that "issue 4 and 5 are the same issue", leaving only two — but the text then treats the two surviving demands as Issue 1 and Issue 2, so the original numbering is lost.
- The insult incident is dated 09 January 2008 at [Para 42] with a wage date of the 9th and a Sunday 10th; the calendar does not reconcile with 2008.
- Both parties' representatives, and the head of administration whose conduct is in issue, are named in full in the source; they are given by role here, and the quoted insult is paraphrased rather than reproduced.
