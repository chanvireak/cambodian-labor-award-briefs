# Award No. 01/11 — Pou Yuen (Cambodia) Enterprise Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 26 January 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun, Ven Pov, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 139957

## 1. Facts
Pou Yuen (Cambodia) Enterprise Ltd. began operating in May 2010 and employed 2,200 workers; the Local Union of KYFTU, registered 29 September 2010, was the claimant and represented 250 workers while collecting contributions from only 140 of them. [Para 14] Of ten demands raised in the collective dispute, eight were conciliated and the workers withdrew the second of the two remaining issues at the hearing, leaving only the claim for a daily overtime meal allowance. [Para 7][Para 9]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the overtime meal allowance on each day the workers work overtime? [Para 2]
2. Whether the employer must increase the main wage for skilled workers to US$ 86? [Para 3] (withdrawn by the workers at the hearing [Para 9])

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Notification No. 017 requires the meal allowance to be paid each day rather than once a month, because workers must buy a meal for every overtime shift and some become sick, unconscious or faint from hunger. [Para 16][Para 17]
**Employer (respondent):** It follows existing practice and cannot do otherwise: with 2,200 workers and daily overtime it lacks staff to disburse the allowance daily, has integrated the allowance into monthly payroll in its accounting system, and has received no complaints on the point. [Para 2][Para 19][Para 20][Para 21]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer is ordered to pay the overtime allowance on a daily basis, but only to workers who are members of the Local Union of KYFTU, for each day they volunteer to work overtime. [Para 53]
On the scope of the claim, Clause 19 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY (21 April 2004) requires a representative to be expressly authorised in writing, following Arbitral Award 122/10-Meng Yan; the union had filed no authorisations for non-members, and those workers were absent, so the demand was confined to members. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 26]
Article 116 of the Labour Law fixes only the intervals for paying wages (twice monthly for labourers, once for employees) and does not dictate when an allowance is disbursed. [Para 26][Para 27] Article 103 includes overtime payments in wages, but the panel followed Arbitral Award 17/10-Zongtex in holding the meal allowance is not part of overtime pay: it is given in lieu of a free meal, and where no meal is supplied the employer must instead pay 1,000 riels per day. [Para 30][Para 32][Para 34][Para 36]
Point 4 of Notification No. 049 KB/SCN (9 July 2010) preserves point 4 of Notification No. 017 SKBY (18 July 2000), which the panel read as requiring a meal allowance of 1,000 riels per day, or a free meal, for voluntary overtime. [Para 35] The notification is silent on the payment period [Para 37]; earlier awards (47/07-Chung Fai, 79/07-Terratex, 85/09-Nan Kuang, 17/10-Zongtex) found a meal should be provided for each day of overtime, and the panel held daily, weekly or monthly payment would all be lawful provided workers can manage their expenditure. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40] Here overtime ran daily from 4:00 p.m. to 6:00 p.m. and the workers could not fund meals themselves, so daily payment was ordered; the employer's staffing and accounting burden, though real, was less serious than the workers' difficulty and would be offset by better productivity and cooperation. [Para 44][Para 48][Para 51][Para 52]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A meal allowance owed in lieu of a free meal for voluntary overtime must be paid at intervals that actually let the workers buy food for the overtime they work; where overtime occurs every working day and workers cannot otherwise afford meals, monthly payment through payroll is insufficient and daily payment must be ordered. A union claiming on behalf of the whole workforce without written authorisations from non-members may obtain relief only for its own members. [Para 26][Para 40][Para 52]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observations that resolving the meal problem would raise profitability and motivation and lead to better workplace cooperation, and its statement that weekly or monthly payment would in principle be acceptable. [Para 40][Para 51]
