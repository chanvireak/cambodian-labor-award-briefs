# Award No. 158/11 — B&N Garment (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 30 December 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (S. Vuch Hun, employer-appointed; T. Siphy, worker-appointed; P. Bun Chha, chair) · non-binding award with a dissenting annex · Doc ID 140103

## 1. Facts
The Khmer Workers' Pride Union at B&N Garment (Cambodia), a factory of about 720 workers, held its leadership election at 11:15 on 4 October 2011 during the lunch break and chose a president, a vice-president and a secretary, Mr T, Ms C and Ms S. A federation official tried to deliver the notice of election to the employer that afternoon but company security refused to accept it. The company dismissed all three on 5 October 2011 for poor work and insubordination; the union was registered with the Ministry on 8 November 2011. Of twelve demands, five were settled at the hearing of 13 December 2011 and two withdrawn. [Para 1][Para 40][Para 41][Para 44][Para 45][Para 46][Para 47][Para 24][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Panel issues 1 and 2, merged) Whether the dismissal of the three elected union leaders one day after the election was unlawful for want of the labour inspector's agreement, so that they must be reinstated with back pay and no discrimination? [Para 43][Para 75][Para 109]
2. (Panel issue 3) Whether pregnant workers must be allowed one full day a month for antenatal checks on full pay and benefits? [Para 76][Para 77]
3. (Panel issue 4) Whether the clinic must be moved away from the rubbish heap? [Para 80][Para 82]
4. (Panel issue 5) Whether the company must build a canteen with tables and chairs? [Para 88][Para 91]
5. (Panel issue 10) Whether 4,000 riel meal money is payable for work on Sundays and public holidays, or only the 2,000 riel in the ministerial notification? [Para 100][Para 102][Para 167]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The three leaders were elected on 4 October and dismissed the next day, which shows anti-union discrimination; the election notice was served but security turned it away, and the employer later admitted knowing of the election; the dismissal breached the protection the law gives to candidates. They also pressed the monthly pregnancy check day, removal of the clinic from the rubbish heap, a canteen, and 4,000 riel meal money because Sunday and holiday work is overtime. [Para 46][Para 48][Para 77][Para 102][Para 109]
**Employer (respondent):** The dismissals were for poor performance, not union activity: one leader did not attend to his work, another had a written warning after a dispute with a team leader, and the third dozed during working hours; there was no discrimination against the union. It said a clinic and nurse were already provided and there was no space for a canteen, and that 4,000 riel exceeds the notification. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51][Para 52][Para 59][Para 80][Para 91]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to take back all three union leaders and pay their wages from the date of dismissal to the date of return; to take measures so that the area around the clinic is free of smell and hygienic; and to pay 2,000 riel or one free meal to workers who volunteer for Sunday work; the claims for a full day a month for antenatal checks, for relocation of the clinic, for a canteen, and for 4,000 riel meal money were rejected. [Para 196][Para 197][Para 198][Para 199][Para 200][Para 201][Para 202]

On issues 1 and 2 the panel applied Article 293 of the Labour Law, under which a staff delegate or a candidate may be dismissed only with the labour inspector's agreement, and Article 3(3) of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, which extends that protection to election candidates for 45 days before and 45 days after the ballot, provided the union has notified the employer of the candidacies by any reliable means. The election was on 4 October 2011 and the dismissals on 5 October 2011, inside the protected window; security had in fact received the notice that afternoon and the employer accepted that it knew of the election, even if the hour was closer to 4 p.m. than to the 11:15 ballot. The employer never asked the labour inspector for authorisation, so the dismissal was procedurally unlawful and the panel ordered reinstatement with back pay rather than examining whether the stated grounds were made out. [Para 112][Para 114][Para 115][Para 118][Para 122][Para 123][Para 124][Para 125][Para 127][Para 128][Para 132]

On issue 3 the panel found that no provision of the Labour Law, no agreement and no collective convention gave a full day a month for antenatal checks; a demand above what the law provides is an interest demand, and the claimant union held no certificate of most-representative status at the factory, so the panel declined to consider it. On issue 5 the panel reached the same conclusion on the canteen: no law or internal rule obliged the employer to build one, the workers had not shown any health effect from eating near the fence, and the demand was one of interest. [Para 134][Para 135][Para 142][Para 143][Para 158][Para 162][Para 164][Para 166]

On issue 4 the panel applied Article 229, which requires every workplace to be kept clean and to present hygienic and healthy conditions, and followed Award No. 143/09 (Sinopear): the employer had shown a real willingness to deal with the problem, bagging the waste and having it removed three or four times a week, so relocation was refused, but the employer was ordered to take further measures at once if smell persisted. [Para 146][Para 147][Para 152][Para 154][Para 155]

On issue 10 the panel held that Sunday work beyond the normal 8 hours a day and 48 hours a week under Article 137 is overtime, so point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 gives 2,000 riel or a free meal; public-holiday work, however, is not overtime, following Awards No. 115/08 and 155/09, and only the statutory holiday premium applies, so no meal money is due for holidays. The balance of the 4,000 riel claim was an interest demand and was declined. [Para 173][Para 176][Para 179][Para 180][Para 188][Para 190][Para 195]

The annexed dissent of the worker-appointed arbitrator argues that holiday work is overtime on the dictionary definition and under Articles 137, 139 and Prakas No. 10 of 1999, because the worker is entitled to rest that day and gives it up; holiday work should therefore also attract the 2,000 riel meal allowance. [Para 207][Para 209][Para 212][Para 215][Para 218]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union election candidate is protected against dismissal for 45 days before and after the ballot once the union has notified the employer by any reliable means, and a dismissal made without the labour inspector's agreement is unlawful whatever grounds the employer asserts, the remedy being reinstatement with wages for the intervening period; separately, Sunday work beyond 48 hours a week is overtime attracting the notification's meal allowance, while public-holiday work is not overtime and attracts no meal money. [Para 118][Para 128][Para 132][Para 180][Para 190]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel accepted that the employer knew of the election although the notice reached security late in the afternoon, and declined to decide whether the alleged poor performance would have justified dismissal on the merits. [Para 124][Para 128]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is expressly non-binding and carries a dissenting annex. The date of the election notice is given as 4 October 2011 in one place and 5 October 2011 for the dismissals, while the union's own registration certificate is dated 8 November 2011, after both. The company is named in two forms within the same award, and the workers' three leaders are OCR-garbled in the operative paragraph, one name appearing with an extra element, so initials are used here. Several passages are corrupted into meaningless text, notably at Para 78 and Para 94, and the dissent's pagination markers have been lost.
