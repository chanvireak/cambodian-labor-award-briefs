# Award No. 61/11 — Cambrew Ltd (Arbitration Council, 7 July 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · You Suonty (employer), An Nan (worker), Ang Eng Thong (chair) · Doc ID 140029

## 1. Facts

Cambrew Ltd, a manufacturer and distributor of Angkor Beer, employed 34 workers who had worked since 2000. From 2007 to 2010 the company required them to work on their weekly day off and paid an additional US$ 2 per day. The Cambodian Food and Service Workers Federation (CFSWF) demanded a further 100% of wages for that work and the conversion of the workers' successive one-year fixed-duration contracts to undetermined duration. The dispute was not conciliated and was referred by non-conciliation report No. 547 KB/RK/VK dated 27 May 2011. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 22][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. Whether the company must reimburse an additional 100% of wages to the 34 workers for work on weekly days off from 2007 to 2010 under Articles 118 and 139 of the Labour Law? [Para 5]
2. Whether the company must sign undetermined-duration contracts with those workers who have worked for more than two years? [Para 6]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** Work on the weekly day off was overtime that should be paid at 200% of normal wages, and the flat US$ 2 supplement underpaid them. Their successive fixed-duration contracts exceeded two years, so Article 67 converted them to undetermined duration. [Para 5][Para 31]

**Employer (respondent):** The company objected to the claimant list, saying some listed workers had resigned or belonged to other unions. It maintained that its fixed-duration contracts were lawful and that its US$ 2 payment for weekly day-off work was sufficient. [Para 27][Para 31]

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** Partly granted — the company was ordered to reimburse an additional US$ 2 from 13 November 2007 to 13 December 2010 to the 34 workers, and to convert the 34 workers' contracts to undetermined duration. The award is non-binding and becomes binding eight days after notification unless a party lodges a written opposition. [Para 44][Para 52]

On Issue 1, the panel applied Article 137, which caps normal hours at eight per day and 48 per week, and held that Sunday work beyond those hours was overtime. [Para 32][Para 33] Article 139 requires a 50% increase for overtime and 100% for overtime at night or on the weekly day off; the panel followed Arbitral Awards 114/08-Whitex and 96/10-K.C. Gelson to hold that weekly day-off work is paid at 200% of normal wages. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 37] On a monthly wage of US$ 60, daily pay was US$ 2.30, so 200% was US$ 4.60; the company paid US$ 2 and the panel held a further US$ 2.60 was due. [Para 38][Para 40][Para 42] The workers claimed only US$ 2. On limitation, Article 120(1) gives three years from the date wages were due; the union's negotiation request of 13 December 2010 preserved the claim back to 13 November 2007. [Para 43][Para 44]

On Issue 2, Article 67(2) caps a fixed-duration contract at two years and any renewal beyond that makes it undetermined duration. [Para 44][Para 45] The panel followed Arbitral Award 10/03-Jacqsintex and Awards 36/06-Mondotex and 57/06-Ever Green, and found the 34 workers had worked since 2000, so their total service exceeded two years even though the company used one-year contracts from 2007. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 52]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

Work performed on the weekly day off is overtime payable at 200% of normal wages under Article 139; a flat daily supplement lower than that rate does not discharge the obligation. A fixed-duration contract renewed so that total service exceeds two years becomes an undetermined-duration contract by operation of Article 67(2), whatever the form of the latest renewal. [Para 39][Para 45]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the three-year limitation period runs from the date each wage was due and that the union's written negotiation request stopped time. It also reduced the claimant pool from 38 to 34 after checking the company's list and the 16 withdrawals. [Para 27][Para 43]

## 6. Source defects disclosed

The award states that the company commenced operations in 2009, yet the workers are said to have worked since 2000 and to have worked weekly days off from 2007; the two dates cannot both be right. [Para 21][Para 30] The non-conciliation report is numbered 547 KB/RK/VK in one place and 647 K.B/RK/VK in another. [Para 9][Para 19] The reasoning calculates an additional US$ 2.60 but the order and the workers' demand are for US$ 2. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44] The text is an unofficial English translation with systematic OCR loss of "th" ("thearing", "withemployer", "fixeduration"). [Para 1][Para 32]
