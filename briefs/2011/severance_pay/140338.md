# Award No. 133/10 — Global Footwear (Arbitration Council, 10 January 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouchhun, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Kong Phalla, presiding) · Doc ID 140338

## 1. Facts
The respondent is a shoe factory of about 1,200 workers at Ang Snuol, Kandal, printed in the case name as Global Footwear (គ្លូប៊ល ហ្វូត វែរ) and in the party block as ក្រុមហ៊ុន ធ្នូប៊ល ហ្វូត វែរ អ៊ិន ធើល៍ ខប. [Para 26][Para 1] The claimant is the Khmer Workers Power Trade Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព អំណាច កម្មករ ខ្មែរ) and its union at the factory. [Para 1] Conciliation settled seven of eleven demands, leaving four; at the hearing the workers withdrew issue 1 and accepted the employer's position on issue 3, leaving two live issues. [Para 14][Para 18]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the 5 per cent severance payment on termination of a fixed-duration contract must be calculated on average total monthly wage rather than base wage alone? [Para 27]
2. (Issue 4) Whether 50 per cent of wage plus allowances for 90 days of maternity leave must be paid before the leave begins rather than month by month or after return? [Para 33]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — Khmer Workers Power Trade Union Federation and its Global Footwear factory union):** the company computed the 5 per cent severance on base salary only, whereas Article 168 of the Labour Law required an average of total monthly wage, so the difference was owed; and maternity pay should be handed over before the woman stops work, not afterwards. [Para 27][Para 32][Para 51]
**Employer (respondent — the Global Footwear company, represented by its administrative director):** severance was paid at termination but calculated on base wage because no law required an average-wage computation; maternity money was paid in monthly instalments or after the worker returned, as the practice had always been. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 36]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to compute the 5 per cent severance on all wages from the date of hiring to termination, and to pay 50 per cent of wage for 90 days of maternity leave in advance. [Para 74][Para 75]

On issue 2 the panel read Article 73(6) of the Labour Law: on termination the employer owes a severance equal to the wage proportionate to the contract length, at minimum 5 per cent of the wages the worker received during the contract, unless a collective agreement provides otherwise. [Para 38][Para 39] It followed its award in Case No. 60/06, which held that this entitlement arises only where the fixed-duration contract truly ends and the employment relationship is not renewed, and award No. 107/04, which required 5 per cent of the wages received over the contract period. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 48] Article 103 defines wages to include overtime pay, commission, bonuses, payment in kind and other allowances, so computing the 5 per cent on base salary alone did not satisfy the article. [Para 45][Para 47] The panel therefore ordered the employer to take all wages earned from entry to the date of termination, multiply by 5 and divide by 100, rather than the base-rate calculation the payroll had used. [Para 49][Para 50]

On issue 4 the panel applied Article 183: during maternity leave a woman is entitled to half her wage, including ancillary allowances, paid by the employer. [Para 51][Para 55] Article 183 says nothing about when payment must be made, but Article 115(3) forbids payment on a day the worker is entitled to rest and requires payment one day earlier if the due date falls on such a day. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 60] The panel also followed its prior awards, which held that maternity money is payable before the leave begins and not after the worker returns. [Para 61][Para 63] Article 116, which requires at least two wage payments a month, was held inapplicable to maternity pay. [Para 66][Para 71] The panel found the company had in fact paid one month at a time, or only after the worker returned, and so ordered 90 days of half wage paid up front. [Para 64][Para 73]

The award is non-binding, became final immediately, and was accepted by both parties on 24 December 2010, applying between them alone. [Para 76]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A statutory entitlement expressed as a proportion of "wages" must be computed on the whole Article 103 wage — base pay, overtime, bonuses and allowances — not on base salary alone; and where a periodic payment falls due at the start of a rest period, Article 115 requires it to be paid before that period begins. [Para 45][Para 57]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that many employers pay maternity money after the leave or monthly in reliance on Article 116, and rejected that reading as inapplicable to maternity pay. [Para 65][Para 71]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer is named inconsistently: the case heading reads Global Footwear (គ្លូប៊ល ហ្វូត វែរ) while the party block prints ធ្នូប៊ល ហ្វូត វែរ អ៊ិន ធើល៍ ខប; both are reported and neither was corrected by the panel. [Para 1][Para 26]

The Article 73 citation is printed once as "មាត្រា ៧៣០" in the reasoning and correctly as 73 elsewhere. [Para 43][Para 44] The arithmetic passage setting out the 5 per cent computation is partly destroyed, leaving stray numerals and English fragments in place of the formula. [Para 50] The demands are also corrupted in places, with "២០១៥" inserted between the 50 per cent wage and the allowance figures. [Para 8]

In the signature block the employer-side arbitrator's name and the presiding arbitrator's name are lost to scan noise, although the header names the panel as Seng Vouchhun, Liv Sovanna and Kong Phalla. [Para 77] Union officials and worker names appearing in the evidence and reasoning are given here as roles only.
