# Award No. 113/11 — Yung Wah Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, Branch 2 (Arbitration Council, 18 October 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, employer-appointed; An Vira, worker-appointed; Kong Phalla, chair) · Doc ID 140076

## 1. Facts
Yung Wah Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, Branch 2, a garment factory at Ta Khmao, Kandal, employed 429 workers. A 20-point collective dispute was conciliated on 9 August 2011; six points settled and 14 were referred to the Council by non-conciliation report 383/11 of 24 August 2011. At the hearing four further points settled and three were withdrawn, leaving points 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 and 12. [Para 1][Para 122][Para 34][Para 38][Para 39][Para 40]

## 2. Issues in dispute
5. Whether workers whose fixed-duration contracts were renewed on a public holiday were owed wages for that holiday? [Para 127]
6. Whether the company must pay an overtime meal allowance of 1,500 riel per hour from 17:30 to 20:30? [Para 189]
7. Whether the company must provide a chair to each worker in the QC3 section, section 4? [Para 13]
8. Whether the company must provide chairs to workers in the QI and QA sections? [Para 156]
9. Whether the company must pay a 50% maternity allowance to women with one year's service who take maternity leave? [Para 160]
10. Whether the company must allow pregnant workers one full day per month for antenatal checks while preserving wages and attendance bonus? [Para 166]
12. Whether the company discriminated against pregnant women by not renewing their fixed-duration contracts? [Para 171]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** contracts ran 3 or 6 months and were renewed the day after expiry, so a renewal falling on a holiday should carry holiday wage; the meal allowance is customary; standing eight hours harms health; women with a year's service are entitled to 50% maternity wage; the company refused new contracts to pregnant workers, saying they worked slowly. [Para 127][Para 158][Para 171][Para 172]
**Employer (respondent):** it applies the Labour Law; contracts ended on expiry and were renewed after the holiday, so no contract existed on the holiday; QC inspection requires standing, so no chair; maternity pay was refused because seniority was counted afresh with each fixed contract; the company did not know which workers were pregnant. [Para 130][Para 154][Para 163][Para 175]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — points 7 and 8 granted by orders to install chairs, point 9 granted by an order to pay the 50% maternity allowance, points 5, 6, 10 and 12 dismissed. [Para 269][Para 270][Para 271][Para 272][Para 273]

Point 5: Articles 65 and 102 make the labour contract the source of the employment relation and the wage obligation. Where the contract expired on the holiday and was not renewed on that day, no contract and no employment relation existed, so no wage was owed. [Para 180][Para 185][Para 187][Para 188]

Point 6: the 1,500 riel meal allowance appears in no law, agreement or collective convention, so the claim is an interest dispute rather than a rights dispute and the panel declined to consider it. [Para 189][Para 275]

Points 7 and 8: the panel accepted that QC3, QI and QA staff stand for long hours and that the employer already supplied chairs in ironing and packing; it ordered a chair for each worker in those sections, treating seating as a working-condition entitlement rather than a production question. [Para 154][Para 155][Para 270][Para 271]

Point 9: the employer restarted seniority with each fixed-duration contract. The panel rejected that approach and ordered the 50% maternity allowance for women with one year's service, so successive fixed contracts cannot erase seniority. [Para 163][Para 164][Para 272]

Point 10: the claim sought one paid day a month for antenatal checks; the employer said such absences already used sick, annual and special leave with wages, bonus and benefits preserved. The panel declined the claim. [Para 168][Para 169][Para 276]

Point 12: the workers named two women whose contracts were not renewed after the employer learned of their pregnancies, but supplied no dates of contract start, duration or expiry, nor the stage of pregnancy, and the panel received no evidence on the point. The burden lay on the party asserting the claim. The panel dismissed it. [Para 172][Para 173][Para 174][Para 262][Para 264][Para 266][Para 267][Para 268]

Rights-dispute orders become final immediately; interest-dispute orders become final eight days after notification absent objection. [Para 274][Para 277]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-duration contract that expires on a public holiday and is renewed only on the following working day creates no employment relation on the holiday, so no holiday wage is owed; and seniority for the maternity allowance runs through successive fixed-duration contracts, so an employer cannot reset it at each renewal. A payment found in no law, agreement or collective convention is an interest dispute the panel will not grant as of right; and a worker alleging pregnancy discrimination must prove the contract dates and the timing of the pregnancy. [Para 187][Para 264][Para 272]

**Obiter dicta:** the observation that seating already enjoyed by QC staff in building 2 could not be withdrawn is explanatory rather than necessary to the orders. [Para 155]
