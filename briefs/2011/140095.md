# Award No. 151/11 — Great On Near Tek Sky Fashion Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 December 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambor Nark for the employer party, Sin Kim Sean for the worker party, Kong Phalha chair) · Doc ID 140095

## 1. Facts
Great On Near Tek Sky Fashion Ltd, a garment company in Kandal province renamed from Winner and employing about 1,920 workers, filed this complaint itself, with the factory branch of the Cambodia Democratic Garment Workers' Union as respondent — the reverse of the usual alignment. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 37] The union had roughly 930 members and no most-representative status. [Para 38][Para 39] The dispute began when women in the touch (ប៉ះ) section demanded that the piece rate for lot code 100013 rise from USD 0.30 to USD 0.60 a lot; the company offered only USD 0.05 more, the workers refused to sew the lot, and the company gave it to another section. [Para 2][Para 3][Para 41][Para 62] Conciliation on 15 November 2011 failed on this single point and the case reached the Council on 21 November 2011. [Para 9][Para 10][Para 11] Both parties chose a binding award. [Para 20][Para 84]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the touch-section workers must comply with the employer's assignment of work and accept the piece rate the company set for lot code 100013? [Para 43][Para 59]
2. Whether that assignment was a lawful and reasonable exercise of the employer's management right, given the workers' claim that working the lot alone would leave them below the statutory minimum wage? [Para 63][Para 69]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Employer (claimant):** the company decides which lot goes to which section, the other sections sewed the same lot without complaint, only the touch section refused, and it cannot raise the rate because it has no capacity to do so; a handful of workers, if any, fall below the minimum wage and will be topped up. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51][Para 52]
**Workers (respondent):** the rate for code 100013 is too low — a skilled worker finishes only one and a half to two lots a day, which yields less than the minimum wage — and the model is particularly difficult to sew, so they declined the assignment and asked for USD 0.60. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 55]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the workers to comply with the employer's work assignments in any case where the assignment does not reduce pay below the statutory minimum wage, but refused the employer's demand that the touch section specifically sew lot code 100013, since that lot no longer exists in the production line. [Para 82][Para 83]

The panel first classified the claim as a rights dispute touching the employer's right to direct and manage work under the Labour Law, following its earlier awards 05/11 IM Way and 13/11 Gold Kamivich. [Para 59][Para 60] It then asked whether the employer may allocate work to a particular lot and whether that allocation is lawful and reasonable, reading Article 2(2) as placing the enterprise's staff under the employer's management and direction. [Para 63][Para 66][Para 67]

On the merits it found the company had assigned lot 100013 at USD 0.35 a lot, that the touch section refused and demanded USD 0.60, and that the refusal was a protest over piece rates. [Para 68][Para 69] Article 69(1) requires a worker to perform the work he is hired for, with care, and the internal rules require every worker to observe the company's orders and assignments; a protest over rates therefore does not license refusing an assignment. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 76][Para 77] The panel accordingly held the workers bound to respect the company's direction. [Para 78]

It nevertheless tested the workers' minimum-wage objection against the November payroll the company filed on 5 December 2011. [Para 56][Para 58][Para 70] Of the touch section, 84 workers were listed, 39 earning below and 45 above the minimum wage, and the panel found that none of them had sewn code 100013, so the assignment could not have depressed their pay; a worker who accepted the lot could in fact earn above the minimum. [Para 58][Para 71][Para 72] It concluded the assignment did not affect the statutory minimum wage. [Para 72]

Finally, because the company had already moved lot 100013 to another section and the lot was gone from the production line by the time of the award, the panel held the object of the dispute no longer existed and refused to order the touch section to sew it. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 79][Para 80][Para 81]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may direct which lot and which work a worker performs, and a worker's disagreement over the piece rate for that work is not a lawful ground for refusing the assignment; Article 69(1) and the internal rules require the work to be done. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 77]
That duty is bounded by the minimum wage: the panel will test the assignment against actual payroll evidence, and where accepting the lot would still leave the worker at or above the statutory minimum, the assignment stands. [Para 70][Para 72]
Where the assigned lot has left the production line and been completed by others, the specific demand for compliance has become moot and will be refused, even though the general duty to obey assignments is declared. [Para 79][Para 81]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that other sections sewed the same lot without objection, and that very few workers anywhere in the factory fall below the minimum wage, are background findings that did not determine either order. [Para 49][Para 51]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award date is irreconcilable: the header gives 30 December 2011 [Para 1], while the procedural section says the award was due on 13 December and postponed by agreement to 20 December 2011 [Para 15]. Both are reported.
- The parties are inverted relative to the usual caption: the company is recorded as claimant and the union as respondent [Para 37], and the operative section accordingly "refuses the employer's demand" — a formulation that reads oddly against a caption listing the employer first as complainant. [Para 81][Para 83]
- The rate figures do not reconcile: the demand is to raise USD 0.30 to USD 0.60 [Para 2], the company says it will add only USD 0.05 [Para 3], and the findings record an assignment at USD 0.35 [Para 45][Para 68]. The three numbers are reported as printed.
- Section headcount is given as about 92 workers at [Para 43] and as 84 in the payroll findings [Para 58].
- An exhibit listed as the company's internal rules is in fact the internal rules of "Winner Ne" dated 15 October 2001 [Para 25], the predecessor name, and the award does not address the succession.
- A date is garbled as "3 or 4 December November 2011" [Para 61], and the panel's sentence at [Para 81] orders the workers to comply while appearing to refuse the claim in the same breath.
- The signature block is partly destroyed by scanner noise [Para 85], and the chair's name is printed "Kong Phalha" there against "Kong Phalha" in the caption with a variant spelling in the body. The company's administration chief, its administrative assistant and the union's officer, president, secretary and activist are all named in the source and appear here by role only.
