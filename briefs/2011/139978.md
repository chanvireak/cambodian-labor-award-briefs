# Award No. 15/11 — Golden Gain Shoe Company Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 25 February 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Tuon Siphann; chair Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 139978

## 1. Facts
Golden Gain Shoe Company Ltd., operating since 2006 with 2,700 workers, faced 15 union demands, of which 12 were settled in conciliation on 11 January 2011. The Local Union of KYFTU, representing 740 workers and holding no certificate of most representative status, pressed the remaining three: a higher overtime meal allowance, conversion of probationary workers to permanent status, and repayment or suspension of union dues deducted from 545 workers' wages between October and December 2010 and paid instead to the rival Local Union of CLUF. [Para 9][Para 14][Para 15][Para 16][Para 30]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay an additional 500 riel meal allowance for overtime until 6:00 p.m. and an additional 1,000 riel for overtime from 6:00 p.m. to 8:00 p.m.? [Para 2]
2. Whether the employer must convert probationary workers with over two months of service to permanent workers? [Para 3]
3. Whether the employer must repay three months' union contribution fees deducted from 545 workers' wages from October to December 2010, the workers later revising the demand to temporary suspension of the deductions? [Para 4][Para 29]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Notification No. 017 has stood unchanged for almost ten years while consumer prices rose, so the meal allowance no longer buys a meal; overtime exceeds the legal maximum when workers volunteer; Article 68 entitles specialised workers to a two-month and non-specialised workers to a one-month probation; and the employer redirected 545 workers' dues to CLUF on irregular withdrawal letters without telling KYFTU, infringing union activity. [Para 17][Para 18][Para 21][Para 32][Para 33]
**Employer (respondent):** it already complies with the Labour Law on meal allowances; its three-month probation is "better than the entitlement provided by law" because probationary workers are paid US$ 56 and permanent workers US$ 61; and it paid the dues to CLUF because the 545 workers had withdrawn from KYFTU, joined CLUF and authorised the new deduction in writing. [Para 19][Para 27][Para 30][Para 35]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issue 1 declined for want of standing; issue 2 granted, with the employer ordered to convert specialised workers to permanent status after two months and non-specialised workers after one month; issue 3 rejected as to suspension, but the employer ordered to deduct dues for CLUF from workers whose revocation and authorisation letters are clear and valid, and to review unclear names, ID numbers, work sections or thumbprints within 15 days of receiving resubmitted requests, with worker delegates or union representatives present. [Para 65][Para 99][Para 100]

On the meal allowance, point 4 of Notification No. 049/10 KB/SCN dated 9 July 2010 preserves the benefits under points 3 to 6 of Notification No. 017 SKBY dated 18 July 2000, and point 4 of that notification guarantees 1,000 riel or a free meal per overtime day. Following Award 25/10-High Born, the panel read the clause as fixing the amount but not the duration of overtime. Since the employer already paid 1,000 riel for 4:00–6:00 p.m. and another 1,000 riel for 6:00–8:00 p.m., the demand sought benefits above the law and was an interests dispute. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 46][Para 48]

The union's lack of most representative status was decisive there: an award on an interests dispute stands in place of a collective agreement binding all workers under Clause 43 of Prakas No. 099, so only an MRS union may bring one (Award 42/09-River Rich). KYFTU held no certificate, so the panel declined to consider the demand. [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51]

On probation, the panel first addressed representation: under Clause 19 of Prakas No. 099 a party may be represented only by itself, counsel, or a person expressly authorised in writing, and KYFTU had not proved it represented all 2,700 workers, so the claim was heard for the union and its members only. [Para 52][Para 53] Article 68 paragraph 1 caps probation at what is needed to judge professional worth, and in no case beyond three months for regular, two months for specialised and one month for non-specialised workers. Award 92/08-Cambodian Hoi Fu requires the employer to fix probation by type of work, and Awards 27/03-Standard Garment and 92/08 treat sewing and cutting workers as specialised because the work needs training and skill. The employer's uniform three-month probation for all workers therefore violated Article 68. [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57][Para 60][Para 62][Para 65]

On dues, Article 129 paragraph 2 allows a worker to authorise wage deductions for union dues in writing, revocable at any time, and Clause 5 of Prakas No. 305 requires the employer to comply with a request made 15 days in advance. Article 281 forbids employers to deduct dues, which appears contradictory; following Awards 05/03-Top One, 62/04-Ecent, 94/04-Eternity Apparel, 99/06-South Bay and 16/05-New Point, the panel read Article 281 as protecting workers from interference, not as barring authorised deductions. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 71][Para 72]

Under Awards 60/05-Evergreen, 13/09-June Textile and 06/09-Sangwoo, written authorisation exists to prevent deductions against a worker's will, and an employer facing unclear documents may call workers to clarify, with a delegate or union representative present to prevent coercion. The letters here were mixed: some were clear and valid, some bore overlapping thumbprints, some redirected dues to UFID, and some lacked thumbprints or a union name. Deductions for valid CLUF authorisations were ordered; the rest go to a 15-day review. [Para 74][Para 82][Para 86][Para 94][Para 95][Para 98]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer's probationary practice is unlawful whatever it pays: where the work is specialised, probation may not exceed two months, and a uniform three-month probation applied to all workers breaches Article 68 regardless of the higher wage offered to recruits. [Para 56][Para 62][Para 65]

**Obiter dicta:** that asking workers "why do you join the union, the union or work, which is more important?" is inappropriate interference with the freedom of association in Article 271; and that Articles 129 and 281 appear contradictory but are reconciled by reading Article 281 as an anti-interference rule. [Para 71][Para 84]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The English version omits the evidence section, deferring to the Khmer original, and the translation drops the letter "t" throughout ("themployer", "converthe", "traing"). The award dated 25 February 2011 cites the Prakas on the Appointment of Arbitrators No. 136 dated 7 June 2011 (Ninth Term), a date after the award itself; it is reproduced as printed. Letters of membership withdrawal are described inconsistently — directed sometimes to CLUF and sometimes to "UFID" — and para 91 truncates mid-sentence.
