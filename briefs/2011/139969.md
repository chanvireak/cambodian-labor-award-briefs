# Award No. 09/11 — M & V International Manufacturing Ltd. (Branch 1) (Arbitration Council, 21 January 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 139969

## 1. Facts
M & V International Manufacturing Ltd. (Branch 1) is a garment factory on National Road 2, Chak Angre Krom Commune, Meanchey District, Phnom Penh, with a stitching section at the centre of this case. [Para 1][Para 2] Of the eight demands raised in the unions' complaint, one was conciliated and seven were referred to the panel on 13 January 2011; the Secretariat reported that 51 stitching workers were on strike. [Para 17][Para 18] The workers acknowledged that work was available but sat still in their section insisting the employer meet their demands, and the panel's interim order of 18 January 2011 directing them to resume work was not obeyed. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 27][Para 28]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay for time permitted for breastfeeding and calculate the hourly wage of piece-rate workers on their total wages for that purpose? [Para 2]
2. Whether payments in lieu of annual leave must be calculated on the workers' average wages over the previous 12 months? [Para 3]
3. Whether attendance must be deducted in proportion to the number of days of authorised leave taken? [Para 4]
4. Whether the employer must pay the wages of 25 stitching workers from 15 November 2010 until the dispute is resolved? [Para 5]
5. Whether the employer must allow workers to take leave for personal commitments? [Para 11]
6. Whether the employer must provide sufficient work to workers with seniority rather than assign it to short-term contract workers or outsource it? [Para 12]
7. Whether the employer must refrain from discriminating against workers with many years of seniority? [Para 13]
8. Whether, the workers having remained on strike during the arbitral process, the panel must terminate the process rather than decide issues 1 to 7? [Para 35][Para 42]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** they were not on strike; they had work available but sat still in their section because the employer had not met their demands. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 23]
**Employer (respondent):** it had work for the workers and only 25 of the 45 workers in the stitching section refused to work under its work arrangement; the workers had earlier withdrawn their demands, which suggested an intention to cause disorder and disrupt production. [Para 8][Para 10]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel terminated the arbitral process and closed case 09/11-M & V (Branch 1), leaving all seven non-conciliated issues undecided. [Para 42]

The panel took as its starting point Article 318 of the Labour Law, which defines a strike as a concerted work stoppage by a group of workers for the purpose of obtaining satisfaction of their demands, and held that sitting in the section and not performing work while insisting on those demands was a strike within that article. [Para 35] Clause 20 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 requires the parties to abstain from strikes or lockouts, or any other action likely to aggravate the situation, during the arbitration process. [Para 36] The panel read that clause, consistently with earlier awards (59/06-Sangwoo, 39/08-Zhong Yov, 137 & 138/09-New Point, 103/10-Whitex), as requiring termination of the process where workers remain on strike, and it relied on its notification in 04/04-MSI that a disputant party has no right to strike while arbitration is pending. [Para 36][Para 37] Because the workers continued to sit without working after the interim order and after the Secretariat's reminders, the panel found they had shown no genuine intention to have the dispute resolved by the Council and closed the case. [Para 26][Para 40][Para 41] No evidence on the merits was examined: the award records that no facts were submitted on the issues in the non-conciliation report. [Para 35]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where workers acknowledge that their employer has work for them but remain sitting in their section without performing it in pursuit of unmet demands while arbitration is pending, the panel will treat that conduct as a strike under Article 318 of the Labour Law and will terminate the arbitral process by closing the case, because Clause 20 of Prakas No. 099 obliges the parties to abstain from strikes during that period; non-compliance with an interim order to resume work confirms the termination. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 42]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the workers had "not shown a genuine intention" to have their dispute resolved, and its reliance on a 2004 notification in another case that a disputant party has no right to strike during the arbitral process, are general statements about the panel's practice rather than findings needed for the closure order. [Para 36][Para 40]
