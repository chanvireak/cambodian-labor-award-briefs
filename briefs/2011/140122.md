# Award No. 185/11 — Yong Va Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 30 December 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · three-member panel (Mar Somborana, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Kong Phallha, president) · Doc ID 140122 · individual workers, union officials and company staff identified by role only

## 1. Facts
Yong Va Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd., at Ta Khmao in Kandal province, faced a strike by about 1,900 workers from 26 December 2011, called by four worker organisations — the Labour Union Federation for Cambodia with its factory union, the Federation of Workers' Unions, the Cambodian Workers' Union Federation and the Democratic Union of Cambodian Garment Workers — over two demands: termination pay for all workers with seniority counted from 1998, and payment of 5 per cent of total wages from the date of hiring to the present while preserving that seniority [Para 2][Para 4][Para 11]. Conciliation by the provincial labour department failed and the file reached the Council on 27 December 2011 [Para 12][Para 13]. Because the strike continued, the panel issued Order No. 016/11 on 28 December 2011 requiring the strike to be suspended and all strikers to return to their shifts from 29 December 2011, and on 29 December the secretariat was told by telephone that the workers would not comply [Para 14][Para 15]. At the pre-hearing meeting of 30 December 2011 the unions present withdrew as representatives of their members, one federation had withdrawn the complaint by letter of 28 December, and a third declined to take part [Para 20][Para 21][Para 22].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the panel should continue the arbitration where the workers, having been ordered to suspend the strike and return to work, remain on strike and no union any longer represents them? [Para 31][Para 24]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The four federations and their factory unions pressed the two demands as a collective dispute and the workers maintained the strike even after the panel's order, refusing the explanations of their own unions [Para 2][Para 4][Para 20][Para 24]. The unions then withdrew: the Democratic Union of Cambodian Garment Workers by written letter, the Cambodian Labour Union by telephone, and the two others by asking at the meeting to be discharged as representatives because members were ignoring them [Para 20][Para 21][Para 22].
**Employer (respondent):** The company did not agree with either demand at conciliation and maintained that position before the panel [Para 3][Para 5]. It did not submit any documents; its only contribution was to inform the secretariat that the workers had not returned to work as ordered [Para 15][Para 25].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to continue the arbitration in case 185/11 and closed the file without any decision on the merits of the two demands, so nothing was awarded to either party [Para 43][Para 44]

The panel recorded that it had made no findings of fact, because the hearing could not proceed and the conciliation report contained no evidence on the demands [Para 30]. It framed the only question as whether arbitration should continue at all, and turned to Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, which obliges the parties during arbitration to suspend every contentious measure, including strikes and lockouts under Article 318 of the Labour Law, and to attend all meetings convened by the panel [Para 31][Para 32]. Reading that provision as it had in earlier orders, the panel held that it will not proceed while the worker party is on strike [Para 33][Para 34]. It cited Order No. 04/04 (IMSI) of 29 January 2004, which stated that the Council could not decide a collective dispute while a strike continued and could take the case up again once all unions stopped striking, and the second orders in the hotel case 18/04 and in 77/06, which reserved the right to terminate proceedings where a party breached the order [Para 35][Para 36][Para 37][Para 38][Para 39]. In 28/06 (GDM) the panel had closed the file outright on the same ground [Para 40]. Applying those orders, it found that the workers were still striking, had not obeyed the order to return, and had shown no genuine intention to have the dispute settled by arbitration, especially as no union now spoke for them [Para 41][Para 42]. It accordingly decided not to continue, and made no order on costs or on the two substantive demands [Para 43][Para 44].

**Obiter dicta:** The observation that the case could be taken up again once the strike ended, drawn from Order 04/04, is not part of the operative order and no condition for reopening is fixed in this award [Para 36][Para 44].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a panel has ordered strikers to suspend their strike and return to work and they remain on strike, the panel will not continue the arbitration: Article 20 of Prakas No. 099 makes the suspension of strikes a condition of the proceedings, and the sanction is closure of the file without any ruling on the merits, leaving the dispute unresolved rather than decided against either party [Para 31][Para 40][Para 43].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
No findings of fact were made, by the panel's own statement, so the demands and their amounts rest only on the conciliation report as summarised on the face of the order [Para 30][Para 2][Para 4]. The caption identifies four union parties to the dispute, while the reasoning deals with three that withdrew or refused to appear, and no order records which, if any, remained a party at the date of the decision [Para 1][Para 20][Para 23]. The non-conciliation report is numbered 023/11 with two different reference suffixes in the same document [Para 8][Para 26]. The meeting is described as a pre-hearing meeting, yet the document is titled and numbered as an award and dated the same day as that meeting, and the operative section is headed "decision and order" without the standard division into rights and interest disputes [Para 1][Para 9][Para 44]. Sentences at the start of the reasoning are broken mid-clause by OCR, including the passage that should identify the worker party [Para 41][Para 42].
