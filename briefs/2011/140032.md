# Award No. 64/11 — M & V International Manufacturing Ltd (Branch 3) (Arbitration Council, 15 July 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, An Nan, Kong Phallack) · Doc ID 140032

## 1. Facts
M & V International Manufacturing Ltd (Branch 3) is a garment enterprise at #1670, National Road 2, Phnom Penh, whose workforce includes piece rate workers. The Local Union of CFITU brought eight demands; issues 6, 7 and 8 were withdrawn at the hearing, leaving five. The disputed acts concern how the US$ 7 attendance bonus is reduced for authorised leave, how annual leave and breastfeeding hours are paid for piece rate workers, and whether fixed duration workers receive the US$ 6 living allowance and a seniority bonus. [Para 1][Para 16][Para 17]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must deduct from the attendance bonus in proportion to the number of days of authorised leave taken? [Para 3]
2. Whether the employer must calculate daily wages during annual leave for piece rate workers on the basis of their monthly piece rate payment? [Para 4]
3. Whether the employer must calculate the hourly wage of female piece rate workers who go home to breastfeed on the basis of the piece rate payment? [Para 5]
4. Whether the employer must provide a living allowance of US$ 6 per month to piece rate workers who hold fixed duration contracts? [Para 6]
5. Whether the employer must provide a seniority bonus to workers who hold fixed duration contracts and have one year's service? [Para 7]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Authorised sick and personal-commitment leave should not cost the whole attendance bonus; annual leave and breastfeeding pay for piece rate workers should reflect actual monthly piece rate earnings; the US$ 6 living allowance promised in the 20 October 2010 agreement should continue; and seniority should carry across successive contracts. [Para 33][Para 41][Para 46][Para 50][Para 51]
**Employer (respondent):** The US$ 7 bonus is only for workers who attend regularly, and the US$ 61 main wage is the proper basis for annual leave and breastfeeding hours; the US$ 6 was already incorporated into the main wage under Notification No. 049; workers re-signed after 1 October 2010 are new workers, and seniority restarts with each new contract. [Para 31][Para 43][Para 49][Para 51][Para 52]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — issues 1, 2 and 3 granted; issues 4 and 5 rejected. The panel ordered proportional deduction from the attendance bonus for sick and commitment leave authorised by the employer, calculation of daily annual-leave wages on the monthly piece rate payment, and calculation of the hourly wage of female piece rate workers by dividing average daily wage by hours worked. It rejected the US$ 6 allowance and the seniority bonus for fixed duration workers. [Para 123]

On issue 1, the demand was a rights dispute because it rested on Notification No. 041/11 KB/SCN dated 7 March 2011. Point 1 of that notification does not clearly address authorised leave, the same ambiguity found in Notification No. 017 SKBY dated 18 July 2000. Following Awards 57/07-Seratex, 106/07-M & V 3, 128/08-Wei Hua and 31/11-Quint Major Industrial, the panel held that a worker authorised to take leave should not lose the full bonus. Paid leave — public holidays, annual leave and special leave using annual leave — leaves the bonus intact; only sick and personal-commitment leave attract proportional deduction. [Para 59][Para 64][Para 65][Para 66][Para 69][Para 79]

On issue 2, Article 166 of the Labour Law grants 1.5 days of paid annual leave per month of continuous service, and Article 102 defines wage as remuneration for employment convertible in cash. Notification No. 049 dated 9 July 2010 guarantees piece rate workers at least US$ 61 per month and more where production is higher. Award 82/06-M & V 3 held that wages include actual wages earned. Calculating annual leave on the US$ 61 main wage therefore deprived piece rate workers earning more of part of their wage, so the panel ordered calculation on the monthly piece rate payment. [Para 84][Para 85][Para 86][Para 89][Para 90]

On issue 3, Article 184 gives mothers one hour per day for breastfeeding for one year after delivery. Award 39/11-M & V 1 held that hourly wages for women piece rate workers are calculated by taking average daily wages and dividing by hours worked. The panel ordered that method, rejecting payment based on the main wage. [Para 91][Para 94][Para 95][Para 97]

On issue 4, the 20 October 2010 agreement gave the US$ 6 allowance to piece rate workers commencing before 1 October 2010, but workers whose contracts expired and who re-signed after a one or two week gap had stopped working and were treated as commencing after that date. Under Articles 73(1) and 73(6), the employer had already paid 5% severance on expiry. The panel rejected the demand. [Para 49][Para 99][Para 101][Para 102][Para 108][Para 116]

On issue 5, the same reasoning defeated the seniority claim: seniority is successive service and ends when the worker stops working for the enterprise. Workers whose fixed duration contracts were not consecutively renewed had less than one year of seniority, so the demand was rejected. [Para 120][Para 121][Para 122]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
For piece rate workers, "wage" for annual leave and breastfeeding hours means actual monthly piece rate earnings, not the US$ 61 minimum main wage. An attendance bonus may be reduced proportionally only for authorised sick and personal-commitment leave, not for paid leave. Seniority ends when the employment relationship stops, so a one or two week gap between contracts resets seniority and disqualifies the worker from the US$ 6 allowance and the seniority bonus. [Para 79][Para 90][Para 97][Para 106][Para 121]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the Labour Law favours undetermined duration contracts and that using short-term contracts to avoid statutory benefits conflicts with good faith, though the practice is not prohibited. That observation was not necessary to the outcome. [Para 108][Para 111][Para 114]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR repeatedly runs words together ("themployer", "athearing", "remeaning"), and the union name is printed as "CFITU" while the workplace is "M & V" and "M&V". The source says eight issues were filed but lists issues 1–8 and then treats five as remaining after 6–8 were withdrawn. The notice describes "30% hold three month fixed duration contracts and 10% hold six month" while 60% hold undetermined duration contracts. The award labels the rights-dispute decision "Part I" and the interests-dispute section "Part II" although the interests part is marked non-binding. [Para 16][Para 51][Para 123][Para 125]
