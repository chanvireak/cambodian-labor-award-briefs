# Award No. 34/11 — Lim Line International (Cambodia) Co., Ltd v Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers Democratic Unions (Arbitration Council, 3 May 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuochhun, Sin Kimsean, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 140011

## 1. Facts
Lim Line International (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, registered 16 July 2010 and employing 650 workers, faced a seven-point demand from the local C.CAWDU union covering medicine, rest, needles, safety cords, a day-care centre and canteen, and the reinstatement of Tha Chanthat, a union advisor whose fixed-duration contract was not renewed. Conciliation failed on all seven; five were settled at the hearings, leaving the canteen demand (Issue 6) and the reinstatement demand (Issue 7). [Para 1][Para 12][Para 14][Para 29]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company was obliged to build a canteen for the workers to eat lunch and dinner? [Para 35]
2. Whether the demand to reinstate Tha Chanthat was a collective labour dispute within the panel's jurisdiction and, if so, whether her non-renewal resulted from union discrimination? [Para 47][Para 48]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The Labour Law and Arbitration Council jurisprudence obliged the company to build a canteen; meals were taken on the pedestrian road amid dust and the breaks were too short to go home. The reinstatement demand was collective because 150 workers endorsed it, and the non-renewal was union discrimination since nine of ten contracts in her section were renewed. [Para 41][Para 44][Para 50][Para 53]
**Employer (respondent):** No legal obligation to build a canteen existed and corridor meals were adequate. The dispute was individual under Article 300 because it arose between one worker and the company, and the non-renewal rested on poor performance and unauthorised five-day leave. [Para 45][Para 48][Para 55]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — as a binding rights dispute the panel ordered the company to reinstate Tha Chanthat without wage reimbursement; as a non-binding interests dispute it declined to consider the canteen demand. [Para 118][Para 119]

On standing, the panel accepted the authorisation letter from 150 workers plus the C.CAWDU president's confirmation, holding that Muo Chheang could represent them under Clause 19 of Prakas 099. [Para 62][Para 63] On the canteen, it found no article of the Labour Law or labour regulation imposing the obligation; a canteen is required only by agreement or where its absence could damage workers' health (AA 35/04 Jacqsintex, 81/07 Supreme, 55/07 Siu Quinh). There was no agreement, the Internal Work Rules were silent, and the workers themselves accepted meals along the corridor. The demand exceeded the law and was therefore an interests dispute; because the local union lacked most-representative status, the panel declined to consider it. [Para 67][Para 69][Para 71][Para 73][Para 79]

On reinstatement, the panel treated the non-conciliation report as presumptively collective and placed the burden on the company, which failed to show the dispute was individual. Applying the three conditions from AA 45/07-Wilson, it found the dispute collective because 150 workers endorsed the demand and disruption could follow. [Para 83][Para 84][Para 92][Para 97] The demand was a rights dispute under Article 73. Because her contract ran only six months, prior notice was not required; the panel therefore examined discrimination under Articles 12 and 279. It found the company renewed nine of ten contracts in her section, gave no evidence of poor performance or the alleged unauthorised leave (no warning letter as the Internal Work Rules required), and knew she was a union leadership candidate. Union discrimination was established and reinstatement ordered; wage reimbursement was refused because the company had neither renewed nor terminated the contract. [Para 102][Para 106][Para 111][Para 115][Para 118]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that exceeds what the Labour Law or an agreement requires is an interests dispute, and a union without most-representative status lacks standing to bring it; but a non-renewal of a fixed-duration contract that targets a known union candidate, evidenced by differential renewal and unsupported misconduct allegations, is union discrimination warranting reinstatement. [Para 71][Para 78][Para 117]

**Obiter dicta:** An objection to a non-binding interests award does not affect the parties' obligation to implement the binding rights award under the MoU. [Para 19]
