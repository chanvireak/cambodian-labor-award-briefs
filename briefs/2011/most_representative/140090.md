# Award No. 145/11 — Tropy Kana (Casino) (Arbitration Council, 30 November 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, Tuon Si Phan, Pen Bun Chhea presiding) · Doc ID 140090

## 1. Facts
The Free Trade Union of Workers of Tropy Kana Casino, registered with the Ministry of Labour on 23 August 2011, filed three demands on behalf of workers at the employer's casino in Poipet; the third (exercise of rights under Chapter 11 of the Labour Law) was withdrawn at the hearing, leaving two. [Para 1][Para 19][Para 35] The Ministry's report records 672 workers, while the union asserts approximately 1,080, 80 per cent of them in the casino section; the union claims over 110 members but had not yet given their names to the company. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 36][Para 37] The employer did not appear at the hearing and offered no reason. [Para 17][Para 40]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must provide a suitable place for the union to post photographs, names and functions of its leaders and to publicise union activities? [Para 41][Para 65]
2. Whether the employer must provide a room in the company for the union to meet for one hour every two weeks outside working hours? [Para 53][Para 77]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The union is registered, so it may post its leaders' photographs so that workers recognise and trust their lawful representatives; the company already gave rooms to bodies such as HIV organisations, and should do the same for the union, whose meetings would educate workers on discipline and labour law. [Para 41][Para 51][Para 53][Para 55]
**Employer (respondent):** Absent from the hearing without explanation; no position was presented. [Para 40]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to provide a suitable place for the union to post photographs of its leaders and publicise union activities; the request for a meeting room was rejected. [Para 93][Para 94]

On issue 1, the panel held that Articles 288 and 275 of the Labour Law, on which the union relied, do not deal with posting photographs. [Para 69] It instead applied section 2 of Circular No. 40 SKA of 1 October 1998, which requires owners and managers to provide suitable space for staff representatives and unions to post names, photographs and functions and their activities, following prior Award 106/06-Kwik Su on the same point. [Para 70][Para 71][Para 72] The company's failure to answer the union's letter of 28 September 2011 and its refusal through the head of human resources was therefore improper implementation of the circular, and the order followed. [Para 73][Para 75][Para 76]

On issue 2, the panel found that Article 275 does not require the employer to provide a room, that no provision of the Labour Law does so, that the right is given only to staff representatives under Article 6 of Prakas No. 286 SKBY of 5 November 2000, and that no collective agreement covered the point. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 80][Para 81] The demand was a benefit beyond what the law grants, hence an interest dispute; the panel declines interest disputes where the union is not the most representative in the enterprise, as held in Awards 02/11-Pou Yien and 66/11-In Han Sung. [Para 83][Para 85][Para 86] With 110 members out of at least 672 workers, the union did not meet the more-than-half threshold of Article 277 of the Labour Law and Article 6 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, so the demand was dismissed. [Para 88][Para 90][Para 91][Para 92]

The panel also decided, before the merits, that it could proceed in the employer's absence: Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, Article 21, permits continuation where a duly summoned party fails to appear without valid reason, as done in Awards 53/04-Kuong Hong Kong and 148/07-Bay Hi. [Para 60][Para 62][Para 64]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A registered union is entitled under the 1998 circular to suitable space to post its leaders' names, photographs and functions and its activities, and an employer's silence or refusal breaches that entitlement even where the union is not the most representative; but a demand for a meeting room going beyond what the Labour Law and Prakas grant is an interest dispute that the panel will not examine at the request of a union lacking most-representative status. [Para 70][Para 76][Para 85][Para 91]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that the union has only one representative organisation in the company and that no staff representatives have ever been elected there, observations not necessary to either order. [Para 39][Para 59]
