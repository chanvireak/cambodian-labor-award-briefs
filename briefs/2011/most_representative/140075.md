# Award No. 116/11 — Fortune Garment (Arbitration Council, 24 October 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Stueng Sophal, Kong Phalla) · Doc ID 140075

## 1. Facts
Fortune Garment employed 1,290 workers at its factory in Saang district, Kandal. On 7 September 2011 the Arbitration Council registered a 14-point collective dispute brought by the CCAWDU federation and the factory union after conciliation failed; five points were settled at the hearing, leaving issues 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 12. The parties had signed a Memorandum of Understanding on 28 September 2010 on improving professional relations in the garment industry. [Para 10][Para 44][Para 45][Para 46][Para 69]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must provide sufficient work instead of rotating shifts and sending goods to other factories? [Para 3]
2. Whether the company must pay 100% of wages when it suspends work without the labour inspector's authorisation? [Para 5]
3. Whether the company must stop deducting the attendance bonus for days absent? [Para 7]
4. Whether the company must provide two sets of work clothing per worker each year? [Para 9]
6. Whether the company must convert daily-paid workers into permanent workers? [Para 13]
7. Whether workers are entitled to 1.5 days of annual leave for each month worked? [Para 15]
8. Whether the company must provide a separate meeting room, whiteboard and office supplies for the union and the staff delegates? [Para 17]
9. Whether the company must provide a clean drinking cup to every worker? [Para 19]
12. Whether the company must use a punch clock from 18:00, including Sundays and holidays, for overtime? [Para 25]
Issues 5, 10 and 11 were settled at the hearing. [Para 44]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company failed to provide enough work, suspended work without authorisation, and withheld attendance bonuses, seniority payments, leave and other benefits from daily-paid staff; they seek conversion to permanent status and equal treatment. [Para 3][Para 13][Para 86]
**Employer (respondent):** work volume follows orders across the 12-month cycle and no goods were sent to other factories; the suspension issue was already decided by an earlier award; daily-paid staff received the same pay as permanent staff and none was kept beyond two months. [Para 4][Para 6][Para 14]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — most claims were dismissed as already decided or unsupported; the employer must give casual workers the same rights and duties as permanent workers and must allow annual leave after one year of service. The rights-dispute orders are binding immediately under the 28 September 2010 Memorandum, while the interest-dispute ruling is non-binding. [Para 215][Para 219][Para 220][Para 224][Para 226]

The panel first held that the Memorandum of Understanding remained in force: the case was registered on 7 September 2011, before its expiry, and no formal termination had been recorded, so rights-dispute decisions stayed binding. [Para 104][Para 107][Para 110] Issues 1, 2, 3 and 4 repeated claims already decided in Awards 38/10 (4 June 2010), 94/07 (3 October 2007) and 74/09 (13 July 2009), and res judicata barred reconsideration. [Para 116][Para 124][Para 128][Para 133] On issue 6 the panel applied Articles 9 and 10: a worker averaging at least 21 days over two consecutive months is permanent whatever the payroll label, and the employer's practice of paying daily wages while withholding attendance bonus, seniority bonus and leave breached Article 10; conversion was refused but equal treatment was ordered. [Para 145][Para 154][Para 157] On issue 7, Articles 166 and 167 give 1.5 days per month but only after one year of service, so the employer's deferral of leave to the third year was rejected and leave was ordered from completion of one year. [Para 160][Para 162][Para 165][Para 166] On issue 8, Prakas 286 Article 5 required a meeting room and materials for staff delegates, which the employer had supplied; no law requires a separate room for the union, and the union's most-representative certificate had expired on 8 January 2010, so its claim was an inadmissible interest demand. [Para 168][Para 169][Para 181][Para 187] On issue 9, Prakas 54 Article 4 requires clean drinking facilities only; cups had been provided and lost, so the claim failed except for newly hired workers. [Para 196][Para 198][Para 200] On issue 12, recording overtime hours falls within the employer's management right under Article 2, overtime was paid immediately and no wage loss was shown. [Para 204][Para 210][Para 214]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker who averages at least 21 days of work over two consecutive months is a permanent worker whatever the payroll label, and the employer owes that worker the same statutory rights and duties as permanent staff even where the panel declines to order a formal conversion; a claim already decided between the same parties on the same facts is barred by res judicata. [Para 145][Para 157][Para 116]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that an award on an interest dispute would replace the collective agreement for one year and could strip non-members of the right to strike on future interest claims, which is why it declined to make such an order. [Para 190]
