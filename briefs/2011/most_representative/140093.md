# Award No. 144/11 — Men Hout Then Industrial Garden (Arbitration Council, 13 December 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Ung Sothy, An Vireak, Kong Phally (Chair) · Doc ID 140093

## 1. Facts
The employer, Men Hout Then Industrial Garden, a garment factory with 3,470 workers, was party to a collective dispute with two unions. [Para 1] [Para 55] The unions, representing over 2,000 members but lacking most representative status, filed 11 demands. [Para 19] [Para 55] One demand was settled at conciliation; the remaining 10 were referred to the Arbitration Council. [Para 21] [Para 22] At the hearing, two further issues were settled, leaving eight for adjudication. [Para 25] [Para 26] Under an MOU of 28 September 2010, awards on rights disputes are binding. [Para 27]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must allow workers to wear shoes during work for safety? [Para 3]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must implement clause 4 of the 26 January 2008 agreement, providing a seniority bonus upon renewal of fixed-term contracts without interruption?
3. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must pay the full-attendance bonus under internal regulations (one month’s wage for perfect attendance)?
4. (Issue 5) Whether newly recruited skilled workers must receive wages equal to those of existing workers?
5. (Issues 6 and 7) Whether the employer must honour the 8 September 2011 conciliation agreement to pay wages and benefits upon termination, and whether it may deduct damages from the final pay of worker Ms P?
6. (Issue 8) Whether the employer must set fixed-term contract duration at two years instead of six months?
7. (Issue 10) Whether the employer must pay an end-of-contract indemnity of 15% of wages earned during the contract period?

## 3. Positions of the parties
On Issue 2, the workers sought to wear shoes to prevent electric shocks, citing the internal rule requiring footwear. [Para 57] The employer argued hygiene requirements for medical garments necessitated removing shoes, though it acknowledged the internal rule and that minor shocks occurred. [Para 60]
On Issue 3, the workers demanded the seniority bonus promised in the 2008 agreement for workers whose contracts were renewed without gap. [Para 67] The employer admitted the agreement existed but had never implemented it, claiming the 5% end-of-contract indemnity paid each time extinguished seniority. [Para 69]
On Issue 4, 18 workers claimed the full-attendance bonus. [Para 77] The employer contended it lacked financial capacity and that the workers had not proved they met the criteria of outstanding performance and perfect attendance. [Para 76]
On Issue 5, the workers argued that experienced workers transferred to new sections should receive the full monthly wage of USD 61, not the USD 1 per day apprentice allowance. [Para 88] The employer maintained these workers were apprentices learning new skills under training contracts. [Para 89]
On Issues 6 and 7, the workers sought enforcement of the conciliation agreement requiring payment of wages and benefits upon termination of Ms P. [Para 91] The employer had prepared the payment but insisted Ms P first compensate the company for damages equal to the remaining contract period. [Para 92]
On Issue 8, the workers requested two-year contracts for job stability. [Para 105] The employer refused, wishing to maintain six-month contracts. [Para 107]
On Issue 10, the workers sought a 15% indemnity, arguing the statutory 5% was too low. [Para 109] The employer relied on the 5% rate set by law. [Para 108]

## 4. Award and reasoning
The panel classified Issues 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, and 8 as rights disputes, and Issues 5 (in part) and 10 as interests disputes. [Para 116] [Para 130] [Para 146] [Para 198] [Para 213] [Para 184] [Para 234]
On Issue 2, the employer had a duty under Article 229 of the Labour Law and its own internal rules to ensure workplace safety, including allowing footwear. [Para 118] [Para 121] The hygiene concerns did not override this obligation, as no alternative safety measures were provided. [Para 128] The panel ordered the employer to permit workers to wear shoes. [Para 129]
On Issue 3, seniority continues as long as the employment relationship persists, even if fixed-term contracts are renewed and a 5% indemnity is paid. [Para 135] [Para 139] [Para 140] The panel identified 82 workers whose seniority was undisputed and ordered the employer to pay the seniority bonus under the 2008 agreement and Ministry notification No. 041/11. [Para 74] [Para 145]
On Issue 4, the workers bore the burden of proving they met the conditions for the attendance bonus, including outstanding performance. [Para 154] [Para 157] The list of names submitted was insufficient evidence. [Para 162] The claim was dismissed. [Para 168]
On Issue 5, workers learning a new skill were apprentices under Article 8 of the Labour Law. [Para 170] The demand for USD 61 per month exceeded the legal minimum for apprentices of USD 30 per month (USD 1.15 per day) under Ministry notification No. 06. [Para 176] [Para 177] The panel dismissed the claim for USD 61 as an interests dispute because the union lacked most representative status, but ordered the employer to pay the statutory apprentice wage of USD 1.15 per day. [Para 197]
On Issues 6 and 7, under Article 116 of the Labour Law, the employer must pay all wages and benefits within 48 hours of termination. [Para 204] [Para 205] The employer could not withhold payment pending compensation for alleged damages; its remedy was to sue the worker in court. [Para 209] The panel ordered the employer to pay Ms P’s wages and benefits and to reimburse the damages it had deducted. [Para 210] [Para 211]
On Issue 8, an employment contract requires mutual consent under Articles 65 of the Labour Law and 1 and 22 of Decree-Law No. 38. [Para 215] [Para 216] [Para 217] [Para 218] The panel could not compel the employer to agree to a two-year term. [Para 225] The claim was dismissed. [Para 226]
On Issue 10, the Labour Law requires only a 5% indemnity, and no agreement provided for 15%. [Para 228] [Para 229] [Para 232] The claim was an interests dispute, and the union lacked most representative status, so the panel declined to consider it. [Para 236]
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must allow workers to wear shoes, pay seniority bonus to 82 workers, pay wages and benefits to Ms P and reimburse deducted damages, and pay apprentices USD 1.15 per day; the claims for attendance bonus, two-year contracts, and 15% indemnity are dismissed. [Para 237] [Para 238] [Para 240] [Para 241] [Para 242] [Para 244] [Para 245] [Para 246]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a fixed-term employment contract is renewed without interruption, the worker’s seniority continues to accrue, and the payment of the statutory 5% end-of-contract indemnity does not break the continuity of the employment relationship for seniority purposes. [Para 135] [Para 139] [Para 140]
**Obiter dicta:** A dispute is a rights dispute if it concerns a right set out in law, an agreement, or a collective convention. [Para 113] A union must possess most representative status to bring an interests dispute before the Arbitration Council. [Para 185]
