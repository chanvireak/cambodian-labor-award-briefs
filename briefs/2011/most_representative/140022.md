# Award No. 51/11 — Nugget Textile (Pte) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 31 May 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tayseng for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 140022

## 1. Facts
Nugget Textile (Pte) Ltd, a Phnom Penh garment factory operating since 27 September 2007 with 550 workers, faced a collective dispute brought by the Local Union of CWEFU, which represents about 117 workers and holds no certificate of most representative status. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24] Of seven original demands, two were conciliated and three more were resolved at the hearing, leaving issue 5: the workers' demand that severance pay on termination of fixed duration contracts be raised from 5% to 20% of wages. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 15][Para 16] The employer pays 5% under Article 73 of the Labour Law regardless of whether it renews the contract; about 80 workers hold undetermined duration contracts, 120 hold six-month fixed contracts and the rest three-month fixed contracts. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 29]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay severance equal to 20% of wages when it terminates a fixed duration contract, rather than the 5% it currently pays under Article 73? [Para 24][Para 34]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** consumer prices have risen while wages stayed low, so severance should be 20% of wages to let workers start businesses or save; the demand is made for all workers at the factory. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 34]
**Employer (respondent):** it cannot pay 20% because the practice could set a precedent for other GMAC-member factories, it has already raised wages in accordance with the law, and the demand has no basis in law or agreement. [Para 31][Para 32]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** declined to consider — the panel found it had no jurisdiction over the demand because the claimant union lacked most representative status, and made no order on the merits. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43]

The panel first classified the demand. The workers acknowledged that the 20% figure was not provided by law or by any agreement, and the panel found the demand sought a severance payment higher than the statutory 5%. [Para 33][Para 37] It therefore held the demand gave rise to an interests dispute rather than a rights dispute. [Para 38] Under the Council's consistent approach, an interests dispute may be brought only by a union holding a certificate of most representative status, because such status confers the legal capacity to negotiate a collective agreement and the standing to bring an interests dispute. [Para 39][Para 41] The panel cited Awards 81/04-Evergreen (issue 4), 09/05-Kin Tai (issue 2), 84/07-Yung Wah Branch 2 (issue 1), 108/07-8 Star Sportswear (issue 3), 135/07-Wilson (issue 1), 14/08-Quicksew (issue 3), 101/08-GDM and 42/09-River Rich (issue 2). [Para 40] It found that the Local Union of CWEFU did not hold an MRS certificate under Article 277 of the Labour Law. [Para 41] Accordingly it declined to consider the demand. [Para 42][Para 43] The award was non-binding, becoming binding eight days after notification unless a party lodged a written opposition with the Minister of Labour. [Para 43]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a severance payment above the statutory 5% under Article 73 of the Labour Law, which is not grounded in the law, an agreement or a collective agreement, is an interests dispute; the Arbitration Council will decline to consider it where the union bringing it does not hold a certificate of most representative status under Article 277, because only an MRS union has standing to bring an interests dispute. [Para 38][Para 41][Para 42]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that an arbitral award settling an interests dispute takes the place of a collective agreement for one year, and that the right to strike cannot be used to revise an unexpired collective agreement, were general observations on the consequences of interests arbitration and were not applied to this demand. [Para 41]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The decision is headed "Issue 7" in the DECISION AND ORDER although the reasons and the surviving demand are numbered issue 5; the five non-conciliated issues were numbered 1–5 and the "7" appears to be a surviving original numbering. [Para 13][Para 24][Para 43]
- The text says "two of the seven issues were resolved" at conciliation and "five non-conciliated issues" were referred, but then says issues 1, 2, 3 and 4 were resolved at the hearing, leaving only issue 5 — consistent, but the original seven are never listed in full. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 15]
- The non-conciliation report is dated "17 April 2011" in one place and "27 April 2011" in another; the text at [Para 11] says 17 April and at [Para 13] says 27 April. [Para 11][Para 13]
- The union's acronym is printed as "CWEFU" in the header and facts but "WFUF" once in the reasons at [Para 42]; the same union is intended. [Para 1][Para 42]
- Individual worker and union representative names are partly lost or garbled in the header, and the employer's telephone number is truncated ("012 6896"). [Para 1]
