# Award No. 142/10 — 8 Star Sport Wear (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 25 January 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, An Non, Pen Bun Chha presiding) · Doc ID 140346

## 1. Facts
The respondent is ក្រុមហ៊ុន ៨ ស្តារ ស្ពត វែរ — 8 Star Sport Wear — a garment factory that opened on 26 September 2006 at Boeung Ssii, Stung Mean Chey, and moved in July 2010 to Damnak Thom, Stung Mean Chey, Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, about 3–4 kilometres away, with roughly 100 workers. The move followed an economic downturn in which the owner sold the plant and the landlord terminated the lease. The claimant is សម្ព័ន្ធសហជីព ចលនា កម្មករ (Workers' Movement Union Federation) and its factory branch, formed after the relocation with about 300 members; a second union, the Free Workers Union, had represented the workforce before the move, and neither union holds a most-representative certificate. Workers demand USD 10 a month for transport, or USD 10 a month for rent, across 12 months from the date of relocation. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 50][Para 51]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay the workers USD 10 a month in transport costs, or USD 10 a month in housing rent for 12 months, because it relocated the factory further from where they live? [Para 51][Para 92]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, Workers' Movement Union):** relocation brought them a benefit they should share; most still live near the old site because rents and food are dearer near the new one, and the new factory is 3–4 kilometres further, so travel costs have risen; they ask for 12 months only to give the employer room to negotiate, and they were never told the content of the settlement the Free Workers Union signed. [Para 52][Para 54][Para 68]
**Employer (respondent):** the distance is about one kilometre, not far; the move was forced by economic crisis, sale of the factory and termination of the lease; the company already gave one paid rest day per team and a USD 7 special bonus for occasional traffic jams under the 22 May 2010 settlement, which the free union signed and the workers benefited from. [Para 58][Para 62][Para 65][Para 66]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider the claim for USD 10 a month transport or rent for 12 months. [Para 133]

The panel first dealt with representation: under Prakas No. 099 an applicant may act only personally or through a lawyer or a person authorised in writing, and the union filed its authority late, so the panel disregarded it and considered the case only for the union itself and its own members. [Para 80][Para 86][Para 88]

On the merits the panel applied Article 2 of the Labour Law, which it had read in Awards 17/03 and 18/03 (Hoh Hing) as giving the employer the right to manage and direct the enterprise, including moving workers, provided four conditions are met: no reduction in wage, no posting far from the original workplace, no change from day to night shift or back, and no effect on essential skills. The workers here did not allege any wage, skill or shift impact; 3–4 kilometres was not a distance too far; and the employer had given real consideration in the form of a paid rest day and the USD 7 traffic bonus, while the workers produced no evidence that living costs at the new site were actually higher. [Para 93][Para 95][Para 98][Para 113][Para 115][Para 118]

The panel also examined the 22 May 2010 settlement: it was concluded at the old factory before the move, the Free Workers Union was then the lawful representative of these workers (as they themselves accepted), and the workers had already taken the benefits it provided, so it was lawful and could be accepted. [Para 111][Para 116][Para 117]

Finally, the panel classified the claim as an interest dispute, since it sought a benefit found neither in the Labour Law nor in any agreement; because the applicant union held no most-representative certificate it could not bring such a dispute, and the claim was refused on that ground as well. [Para 120][Para 121][Para 129][Para 131]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may relocate its factory, and require the workforce to move with it, as an exercise of its lawful management right, so long as the move does not cut wages, does not put the workplace far from the original site, does not swap day and night shifts, and does not impair essential skills; a demand for compensation for the move that no law or agreement supports is an interest dispute, which a union lacking most-representative status cannot bring. [Para 95][Para 121]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that an award on an interest dispute would become a one-year collective agreement under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099, and that strikes may not be used to reopen it, were not necessary to the dismissal. [Para 124][Para 126]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The scan is degraded throughout: several exhibit dates and statutory citations are partly unreadable, and the employer is named in two Khmer spellings (ស្ពត វែរ and ស្គុត វែរ). Worker and union-official names in the source, and the employer's director's personal name, are reduced to roles here. The claimed transport figure is printed as USD 10 in the demand and in the order but as USD 100 in one recital of the claim. The workers' distance evidence is stated as 3–4 kilometres while the employer says one kilometre; the panel adopted the workers' figure. The relocation is dated July 2010 in the reasons and the settlement is dated 22 May 2010 in some passages and 8 May 2010 in others. [Para 65][Para 92][Para 119]
