# Award No. 27/11 — Goldfame Enterprises (Int'l) Knitters Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 May 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tayseng, employer-appointed; Ann Vireak, worker-appointed; Pen Bunchhea, chair) · Doc ID 140008

## 1. Facts
Goldfame Enterprises (Int'l) Knitters Ltd has operated an export garment factory since 1997 and employs about 5,800 workers [Para 18]. The Local Union of CLUF, which represents 300 workers but holds no certificate of most representative status (MRS), filed a collective dispute demanding that the employer keep paying a separate US$ 6 living allowance to piece-rate workers [Para 1][Para 19]. Notification No. 049/10 KB/SCN dated 9 July 2010 had already required that allowance to be folded into main wages, lifting the monthly minimum to US$ 61 [Para 20][Para 25].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must maintain the US$ 6 living allowance for piece-rate workers? [Para 1]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the allowance should remain on top of piece rates, because no piece-rate worker earns below US$ 61 and the rate for pre-existing purchase numbers was never increased, so the incorporation gave them no benefit [Para 21][Para 23].
**Employer (respondent):** it has complied with Notification No. 049/10 by incorporating the allowance and topping up any worker below US$ 61, and has raised piece rates for new purchase numbers [Para 2][Para 21].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed (declined to consider) — the panel declined to consider the demand and issued a non-binding award [Para 33].

The panel read Notification No. 049/10 as incorporating the US$ 6 into main wages and requiring a top-up to US$ 61 for regular workers (US$ 56 for probationary workers) [Para 25][Para 26]. It found no provision in the Labour Law, the internal work rules, any agreement or the notification requiring a separate US$ 6 payment; the separate allowance was extinguished when the notification took effect [Para 28][Para 29]. A demand beyond what the law provides is an interests dispute, not a rights dispute [Para 30]. Because the Local Union of CLUF holds no MRS certificate, it lacks legal standing to bring an interests dispute, following AA 46/11-San Lei Fung [Para 32][Para 33].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The panel will decline to consider an interests dispute brought by a union that does not hold a certificate of most representative status, because MRS is what gives a union standing to negotiate a collective agreement and to bring such a dispute [Para 32][Para 33]. A claim for a separate living allowance already incorporated into the statutory minimum wage by notification is an interests dispute, not a rights dispute [Para 29][Para 30].

**Obiter dicta:** the employer did not contest that the local union represented 300 workers, although it collected dues from only about 100 of them [Para 19][Para 20].
