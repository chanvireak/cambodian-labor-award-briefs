# Award No. 06/11 — Sabrina (Cambodia) Garment MFG Corp. (Arbitration Council, 11 February 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Liv Sovanna, Pen Bunchhea chairing) · Doc ID 139964

## 1. Facts
Sabrina (Cambodia) Garment MFG Corp. has operated in Samraong Tong District, Kampong Speu since 1998 and employs about 4,000 workers; the C.CAWDU local holds a certificate of most representative status with over 3,000 members, while the rival RPWFTU local was only established on 3 December 2010 with some 270 members and had no registration certificate at the hearing [Para 16][Para 17][Para 18]. Of thirteen demands, nine were settled in conciliation and issues 2 and 7 were combined, leaving issues 2/7, 10 and 12 [Para 11]. Ms M, a sewing worker on a fixed duration contract since 2 April 2008, was elected Secretary of the RPWFTU local on 3 December 2010 and dismissed on 7 December 2010 after an administrative officer told her the office head could not accept her because she walked back and forth on the factory floor during working hours [Para 21][Para 23][Para 33]. She refused to thumbprint the termination and the employer confirmed her departure to the provincial department by letter the same day [Para 34][Para 35].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issues 2 and 7) Whether the employer must reinstate Ms M, Secretary of the Local Union of RPWFTU, and stop discriminating against the union? [Para 20]
2. (Issue 10) Whether the employer must send workers for medical checks once every six months and pay the medical fees? [Para 41]
3. (Issue 12) Whether the employer must share its profits with the workers in an amount equal to their wages for the last month of the year? [Para 46]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Ms M was dismissed because she was an elected union leader, so the employer discriminated against the union; after notification of the leaders the factory cameras were trained only on union leaders, the union President was dismissed on 3 January 2011, the Vice-President was pressed by his supervisor brother to quit, and Ms M was forbidden to talk to co-workers about her dismissal [Para 36][Para 39][Para 40]. They also relied on Prakas No. 09 of 1994 for six-monthly medical checks and on Article 103 for profit sharing, arguing the employer's expanding profits and branches justified both [Para 43][Para 47][Para 48].
**Employer (respondent):** It never received the election notification, denied that "Channy" was an administrative staff member, denied that the cameras were fixed on union leaders, and refused the reinstatement [Para 25][Para 28][Para 41]. It said it could afford only one medical check at recruitment and that it already pays US$ 10 monthly attendance bonus, base wages of US$ 62 and a US$ 6 seniority bonus for six years' service [Para 44][Para 49].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer is ordered to reinstate Ms M; the demand concerning union discrimination is declined, and the panel declines to consider the demands for six-monthly medical checks (issue 10) and profit sharing (issue 12) [Para 109].
The panel first addressed standing. Under Article 268 a professional organisation enjoys the rights and benefits of the law only once registered, and the RPWFTU local had only an acknowledgement of its 3 December 2010 application, so it could not represent its members before the Council [Para 50][Para 51][Para 52][Para 54]. Clause 19 of Prakas No. 99 SKBY nonetheless allows a party to be represented by any person expressly authorised in writing, and 91 workers had thumbprinted the authorisation, so the panel proceeded to consider their demands [Para 55][Para 56].
On issues 2 and 7 the panel applied the three conditions from Arbitral Award 71/09-Hytex, derived from Article 293 of the Labour Law and Clause 3 paragraph 3 of Prakas No. 305 SKBY [Para 56][Para 61]. Candidacy was satisfied, and because Ms M was also a founder of a union whose registration application was pending, Clause 4 paragraph 1 of the same Prakas gave her delegate-equivalent protection [Para 62][Para 63][Para 64]. The third condition failed: "notification through all reliable means" required the workers to follow the employer's own receipt procedure — hand the letter to a security guard who signs and forwards it to administration — and the signature of "Channy, ID 1065" could not be attributed to any administrative staff member, the workers giving no particulars of the guard or of Channy; the notification was invalid and no special protection applied [Para 66][Para 71][Para 73][Para 74][Para 75].
On discrimination, Articles 12 and 279 forbid any account to be taken of union membership in dismissal, but the burden of proof lies on the workers, and the camera allegations and the later dismissal of the union president were not sufficient to establish it [Para 78][Para 80][Para 81]. Turning to lawfulness under Article 74, the panel found a single act of walking back and forth, two verbal warnings (2008 and 2010) and never a written one, whereas Clause 11 of the Internal Work Rules permits termination only for serious misconduct built up from four minor or two medium instances, and the employer never characterised her act as such; the sanction was disproportionate under Article 27 and there was no valid reason to dismiss [Para 84][Para 88][Para 89][Para 95][Para 96][Para 97]. Clause 34 of Prakas No. 99 SKBY empowered reinstatement [Para 98][Para 99].
Issue 10 failed because Article 247 only empowers the Ministry to issue a Prakas on the frequency of examinations and no such Prakas exists; the demand rested on neither law nor agreement and so raised an interests dispute, which an unregistered union without MRS cannot bring [Para 99][Para 100][Para 101][Para 102][Para 103]. Issue 12 failed on the same basis: Article 103 defines the elements of wage, including profit sharing, but imposes no obligation to distribute profits, so the demand was again an interests dispute [Para 107][Para 108].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer's Internal Work Rules make only serious misconduct dismissable and prescribe an escalating ladder of verbal then written warnings, an immediate dismissal for a single first-time act that the employer never classified as serious is a breach of those Rules and disproportionate under Article 27; the panel will order reinstatement rather than accept that dismissal is the employer's untrammelled right [Para 89][Para 91][Para 95][Para 99].
Notification of union candidacy is effective only if made through the employer's established document-receipt procedure and provable against a person the employer acknowledges [Para 66][Para 73].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that where a special medical check is required by law the fee is the employer's responsibility, although it declined the demand itself [Para 100].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The signature on the notification letter is printed as "Channy, ID 1065" at Para 24 but as "Channy with ID 1064" at Para 69, and the employer's staff IDs are said to begin with "Z". The list of exhibits dates a case receipt "10 January 2010" where the case was filed on 10 January 2011 [Para 14]. The hearing date appears once as "[17 January 2011 at 8:30 a.m" with an unclosed bracket [Para 8]. The sentence introducing the company repeats itself ("commenced its operation in ... 1998" twice) [Para 16], and Ms M's contracts are listed with a 14 December 2007 date though she is found to have started on 2 April 2008. Thirteen demands are numbered in the translation but several numbers were lost, so the panel's own issue numbers (2, 7, 10, 12) are used here.
