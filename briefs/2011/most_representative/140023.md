# Award No. 49/11 — G-Foremost (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 17 June 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tayseng, employer-chosen; Ann Vireak, worker-chosen; Nhean So Munin, chair) · Doc ID 140023

## 1. Facts
G-Foremost (Cambodia) Co., Ltd employed 1,060 workers at a factory with two unions, neither holding most-representative status; the claimant was the CLUF local union representing 200 workers. [Para 14] [Para 15] [Para 16] An eight-point demand was conciliated, six points were resolved, and two non-conciliated issues reached the panel: annual leave and severance pay. [Para 5] [Para 6] [Para 7] The employer had offered fixed-duration contracts to all workers about eighteen months earlier, and workers held contracts of varying lengths. [Para 18] [Para 19]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must allow workers who have exhausted the previous year's annual leave to take leave accrued in the current year after six months of service, rather than waiting a full twelve months? [Para 19] [Para 31]
2. Whether the employer must pay severance equal to 5% of wages and payment in lieu of annual leave each time a fixed-duration contract expires and is renewed? [Para 25] [Para 49]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Workers with more than one year's seniority who had used up prior-year leave should be able to take newly accrued leave six months into the following year; and severance plus leave payments should be made at each contract expiry because short periods are easier to calculate and waiting until the end of the employment relationship is too long. [Para 23] [Para 28] [Para 29]
**Employer (respondent):** Its practice was consistent with the law: workers who chose payment in lieu of prior-year leave had to complete a further twelve months before using newly accrued leave, and severance and leave payments were owed only when the employment relationship actually ended. [Para 2] [Para 24] [Para 25] [Para 26]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to allow workers to use accrued annual leave at their reasonable request without waiting until the end of the subsequent year, and rejected the demand for severance pay and leave indemnity at each contract renewal. [Para 68] [Para 69] The award was non-binding and would become binding eight days after notification absent written opposition. [Para 70]

On issue 1, the panel read Article 167(1) of the Labour Law, which acquires the right to use paid leave after "one year of service", as referring to the first year only. [Para 34] [Para 39] [Para 40] Following Arbitral Award 94/04-Eternity Apparel, it held that workers with one year's seniority may request accrued leave during their second and later years at any time, the number of days depending on the actual work period; the employer's contrary practice was a misapplication of Article 167. [Para 36] [Para 37] [Para 42] Workers with one year's seniority who worked a further six months were entitled to 1.5 days times six months, or nine days. [Para 43] The panel also noted that paying compensation in lieu of leave contradicted Article 167(3), which voids such agreements, but issued no order on that point because neither party disputed it. [Para 46] [Para 47] [Para 48]

On issue 2, the panel interpreted "at the expiration of the contract" in Article 73(6) to mean expiry without renewal or early termination, so severance is owed only where the employment relationship is severed. [Para 57] [Para 58] [Para 59] Following Awards 60/06-New Max Garment, 76/06-South Bay and 101/10-Tripos International, it held that renewal — whether by signing before expiry or tacitly under Article 67(8) — continues the relationship and the accrual of benefits, so no severance arises. [Para 60] [Para 61] [Para 63] The same logic applied to Article 167(2): "terminate" and "expire" both require the relationship to end, and a properly renewed contract is neither. [Para 64] [Para 65] [Para 66] The demand was therefore rejected. [Para 67]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The "one year of service" condition in Article 167(1) of the Labour Law qualifies only the first year of employment; thereafter a worker with one year's seniority may take accrued annual leave at any time in the year, and renewal of a fixed-duration contract does not sever the employment relationship, so neither severance pay under Article 73(6) nor a leave indemnity under Article 167(2) falls due at renewal. [Para 39] [Para 42] [Para 58] [Para 63]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the employer's practice of paying compensation in lieu of annual leave was inconsistent with Article 167(3) and therefore unlawful, but declined to award on it because the issue was not in dispute. [Para 46] [Para 48]
