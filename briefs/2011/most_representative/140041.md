# Award No. 77/11 — Inkyung Wondang Apparel Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 18 July 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phyrum, employer party; An Nan, worker party; Ang Eng Thong, chair) · Doc ID 140041

## 1. Facts
Inkyung Wondang Apparel Co., Ltd, a Phnom Penh garment factory employing 700 workers, faced a collective dispute brought by the Local Union of the Worker Freedom Union Federation (WFUF), which represented 400 workers. The union was established by election on 12 June 2011 and applied for registration with the Ministry of Labour and Vocational Training on 16 June 2011; on 15 June 2011 it notified the employer, through a letter dated 12 June 2011, naming its three leaders and seven standing-committee members. On 25 June 2011 the employer terminated the contracts of the seven standing-committee members, who accepted termination payments; when the three leaders refused to accept payment in the same way, the employer told them not to attend work from Monday 27 June 2011, and on 27 June 2011 dismissed them outright, alleging they had prevented other workers from entering the factory. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 17][Para 19][Para 20][Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate the three union leaders dismissed on 25 June 2011? [Para 14]
2. Whether the employer must provide a monthly accommodation allowance of US$5 to all workers? [Para 31]
3. Whether the employer must pay the overtime meal allowance on a weekly basis? [Para 35]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The three leaders were founding members entitled to special protection during the union-registration period and were dismissed without the Labour Inspector's authorisation; the eight-worker part of the demand was withdrawn, leaving only reinstatement of the leaders. Workers in all sections of the same factory should receive equal treatment on the US$5 accommodation allowance, and the overtime meal allowance should be paid weekly because a monthly payment leaves them without money to buy meals until the 6th of the following month. [Para 14][Para 33][Para 37]
**Employer (respondent):** It issued no written dismissal letters to the three leaders on 25 June 2011 and dismissed them on 27 June 2011 for preventing other workers from entering the factory, producing photographs of the strike as proof. It pays the US$5 accommodation allowance only to sewing workers because sewing labour is hard to recruit and retain, there being no agreement extending it to all workers, and it refuses weekly payment of the overtime allowance because its existing practice is monthly payment with wages. [Para 21][Para 24][Para 32][Para 33][Para 38]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to reinstate Ms T, Ms H and Ms K (the three union leaders), declined to consider the demand for a US$5 monthly accommodation allowance for all workers, and ordered the employer to pay the overtime meal allowance on a weekly basis. [Para 51][Para 59][Para 78]

*Issue 1 — reinstatement.* The panel applied Article 293 of the Labour Law, under which dismissal of a shop steward or candidate requires the Labour Inspector's authorisation, and Clause 4, paragraph 1 of Prakas No. 305 SKBY (22 November 2001), which extends the same protection to founding members and voluntary joiners from the moment the registration application is submitted. Because the union applied on 16 June 2011 and the dismissals occurred on 25 or 27 June 2011, the leaders were protected whichever date applied; the panel held the notification of candidacy sufficient because the employer's security guard, Mao Chanthorn (ID no. 135), acknowledged the letter of 12 June 2011. The employer never sought the Labour Inspector's approval, and the photographs showed only strike action, not obstruction, so the dismissal breached Article 293 and Prakas No. 305. [Para 39][Para 41][Para 45][Para 49][Para 50][Para 51]

*Issue 11 — accommodation allowance.* The panel found no agreement and no provision in the 1997 Labour Law requiring the allowance for all workers, so the demand exceeded legal entitlement and was an interests dispute. Since the Local Union of WFUF held no most-representative-status (MRS) certificate, it lacked standing to bring an interests dispute, and the panel declined to consider the demand, following Awards 71/09-Hytex and 84/10-Ming Da Footwear. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 56][Para 57][Para 59]

*Issue 17 — overtime meal allowance.* The panel followed Awards 85/09-Nan Kuang and 110/09-Berry Apparel in holding that the meal allowance is not part of overtime wages but a substitute for a free meal, and Notification No. 041 (7 March 2011) sets no payment period. Because the law's intention is to fund a meal on each day of overtime, and monthly payment left workers unable to buy meals daily, the panel held weekly payment legitimate and ordered it. [Para 61][Para 64][Para 65][Para 76][Para 77][Para 78]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union's founding members and leaders enjoy the special protection against dismissal conferred by Article 293 of the Labour Law from the moment the registration application is filed, and an employer that dismisses them during the registration period without first obtaining the Labour Inspector's authorisation acts unlawfully and must reinstate them. An interests dispute — a demand exceeding what the law guarantees — may be brought only by a union holding most-representative status. [Para 42][Para 51][Para 58]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that where an employer cannot provide a free meal, it must pay a 2,000 riel meal allowance each day, and that the workers' daily living needs are of paramount importance in fixing the timing of that payment. [Para 68][Para 75]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The English translation is heavily OCR-damaged: "the employer" appears throughout as "themployer", "Training" as "Traing", "meal" as "me", "eight" as "ight", "other" as "ther", and "an" as "n". The dismissal date is printed two ways — 25 June 2011 in the workers' account and 27 June 2011 in the employer's — and the panel expressly noted that no evidence settled the date. The award also alternates between "eight workers" dismissed and "seven members of the standing committee" terminated. The quoted text of Article 293 and Clause 3, paragraph 3 of Prakas No. 305 is garbled at the margins. Individual worker and union names are rendered as bare initials in this brief.
