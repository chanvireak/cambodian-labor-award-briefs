# Award No. 154/11-B&N — B&N Garment (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 27 December 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuth Hun, employer-appointed; Soung Sophal, worker-appointed; Kong Phalha, chair) · non-binding award · Doc ID 140100

## 1. Facts
The claimant is the B&N Garment (Cambodia) chapter of the Federation of Unions for Workers' Rights, with 261 members in a workforce the panel put at 600; its sixteen demands concerned compliance with the Labour Law rather than any single dismissal. Conciliation failed on all sixteen points on 15 November 2011; nine were settled at the hearing of 30 November 2011 and issue 6 was withdrawn, leaving eight for adjudication. [Para 1][Para 25][Para 28][Para 42][Para 44][Para 72]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Panel issue 2) Whether severance and other benefits must be paid within 48 hours of the last working day, with a correct payslip, rather than on the company's monthly pay dates? [Para 50]
2. (Panel issue 4) Whether the 90 days' maternity allowance, at 50% of wages and allowances, must be paid in one sum before leave begins rather than monthly during leave? [Para 62]
3. (Panel issue 7(2)) Whether the attendance bonus must be preserved in full for two days of lawful absence, or may be cut in proportion to the days absent? [Para 73][Para 78]
4. (Panel issue 8(1)) Whether the company must restore an equal attendance bonus to the warehouse, packing, checking and cutting sections? [Para 81]
5. (Panel issue 8(2)) Whether the company must raise skill allowances to USD 15 for team leaders and cutters and pay USD 10 to machine operators? [Para 86]
6. (Panel issue 10) Whether the company must bear the 12,000 riel cost of health checks and arrange special checks for its workers? [Para 95]
7. (Panel issue 15) Whether Sunday and public-holiday work attracts a 4,000 riel meal allowance, or only the 2,000 riel fixed by ministerial notification? [Para 115]
8. (Panel issue 16) Whether the company must stop deducting dues for the rival union and deduct for the claimant union from all 256 signatories? [Para 121]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Terminal benefits came only on the 10th or 12th of the following month, breaking the 48-hour rule, and maternity money came month by month; an equal 500 riel attendance bonus had been paid to all five sections for three years before being withdrawn from four; skill allowances were unequal and machine operators got none; no worker had ever been sent for a health check; and no meal money was paid for Sunday or holiday work. [Para 56][Para 83][Para 86][Para 87][Para 96][Para 259]
**Employer (respondent):** Dismissed workers are paid at once, and those who resign before the 10th are paid on the 10th, those resigning later on the 12th of the next month; monthly maternity payment is its practice, since women just delivered cannot travel for a lump sum; the equal bonus was discontinued years ago; machine operators already earn more; it never sent workers for checks because it hires them whatever the result; and 4,000 riel exceeds the notification. [Para 50][Para 52][Para 89][Para 95][Para 116][Para 120]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to stop its present practice at once and pay all benefits within 48 hours of the last working day with a correct payslip; to pay the 90 days' maternity allowance at least one day before leave begins; to cut the attendance bonus only in proportion to authorised leave; to pay 12,000 riel per worker for health checks and arrange special checks at its own charge; to pay 2,000 riel a day meal money for voluntary Sunday overtime; to stop deducting dues for the other union and deduct for the claimant union from 151 named workers; the claims on the equal attendance bonus, the skill allowance and the 4,000 riel meal rate were rejected. [Para 298][Para 299][Para 300][Para 302][Para 303][Para 304]

On issue 2 the panel held that Article 116, fifth paragraph, requires wages and all terminal payments within 48 hours of the last working day whichever party ended the contract, so an employer cannot substitute its own pay-cycle dates; the company gave no basis for its practice, which was contrary to Article 116 and void under Article 13. [Para 125][Para 128][Para 132][Para 153][Para 159]

On issue 4 the panel held that Articles 182 and 183 give 90 days at 50% of wage and allowances, and that Article 103 treats maternity-period payments as wage, so Article 115 applies: wages may not be paid on a day the worker may rest and, if the date falls on such a day, must be paid a day earlier; monthly instalments during leave therefore breached Article 115. Following Award No. 81/11, it confined the order to women on leave or about to take it, others lacking standing. [Para 166][Para 172][Para 173][Para 180][Para 183][Para 185]

On issue 7(2) "working days in the month" in point 1 of Notification No. 041/11 excludes leave lawfully authorised by the employer, but the deduction must be proportionate: forfeiture of the whole bonus is justified only for absence beyond half a day without authorisation. [Para 189][Para 197][Para 198]

On issue 8(1) the panel found a past practice of paying 500 riel equally to all five sections for about three years, but held it had lapsed: the workers raised no objection for the five years after the bonus was confined to sewing, signifying acceptance of the new practice, so no duty to continue it arose. On issue 8(2) it classified the skill-allowance demand as an interest dispute and, the claimant union not being the most representative union in the factory, declined to consider it. [Para 215][Para 231][Para 232][Para 237][Para 239][Para 249]

On issue 10, Article 247 and point 2(d) of inter-ministerial Prakas No. 1191 of 21 November 2006 place the cost of health checks on the employer at 12,000 riel per worker, and the company's hiring policy could not displace that duty. On issue 15 Sunday work is overtime but holiday work is not, so only the 2,000 riel rate for voluntary overtime applied, and the balance of the 4,000 riel claim, being an interest demand, was declined. [Para 255][Para 256][Para 263][Para 272][Para 277]

On issue 16, Article 129(2) requires written, revocable authorisation for a dues check-off. Of 256 letters, 105 lacked clear thumbprints and correct dates and could not be relied on, leaving 151 valid; the company was ordered to act on those and stop deducting for the other union, and the 105 workers may resubmit proper letters. [Para 291][Para 293][Para 296][Para 297]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Terminal wages and benefits fall due within 48 hours of the last working day whatever the employer's payroll calendar, and an employer practice to the contrary is void under Article 13; the 90-day maternity allowance at 50% must be paid in a single sum at least one day before lawful maternity leave begins, because Article 103 brings it within "wage" and Article 115 forbids payment on a rest day; a "working day" for the attendance bonus excludes leave the employer lawfully authorised, but the bonus may be reduced only in proportion to the days absent; a past practice accepted by silence over several years cannot afterwards be revived; and a union lacking most-representative status cannot bring an interest dispute to the panel. [Para 128][Para 173][Para 198][Para 231][Para 249]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that it has no competence to determine whether a thumbprint is genuine, and that the company had not contested the prints in any event. [Para 294][Para 295]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR is badly degraded in places. The health-check sum is 12,000 riel in the claim and reasoning but "120 riel" at one point; the meal claim appears as 4,000 riel in the demand, "400 riel" in the issue heading of the reasons, and 2,000 riel in the notification relied on; the maternity practice is described as "90 days ... 20 one month at a time", unintelligible as printed. The employer-appointed arbitrator's name is spelled differently at the signature block than on the face of the award. Headings are corrupted by stray characters, and the annexed lists of 151 and 105 worker names are unrecoverable OCR, so no individual is named here. Membership figures vary: 261 members, 256 letters produced, 151 valid. [Para 161][Para 253][Para 260][Para 291][Para 306][Para 307]
