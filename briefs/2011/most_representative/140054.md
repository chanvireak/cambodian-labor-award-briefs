# Award No. 88/11 & 89/11 — Dai Young (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 15 August 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Tuon Siphann, Pen Bunhea presiding) · Doc ID 140054

## 1. Facts
The Confederation of Cambodian Labour Unions (CUW) filed two connected complaints against Dai Young (Cambodia), a Phnom Penh garment factory with 1,050 workers, covering 3 claims in case 88/11 and 13 claims in case 89/11. Conciliation failed on all 16 points, and the two cases were heard together on 22 and 27 July 2011. Six claims in case 89/11 settled at the hearing and three points in 88/11 were merged, leaving ten disputes for the panel. [Para 2][Para 5][Para 24][Para 30][Para 32]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reject the result of the 28 May 2011 election of rival union members and respect CUW's most-representative status? [Para 2][Para 4]
2. Whether the employer must remit union dues withheld from CUW members since March 2011 and continue deducting them? [Para 3][Para 85]
3. Whether the employer must pay severance to workers employed since 2003? [Para 5][Para 94]
4. Whether the employer must raise the milk allowance from 4 to 10 US dollars a month in place of a daycare centre? [Para 107]
5. Whether the employer must pay a 1,000 riel hourly meal allowance for overtime from 6:30 to 8:30 pm? [Para 120]
6. Whether the employer must pay a 2,000 riel meal allowance for work on public holidays? [Para 127]
7. Whether the employer must keep wages while a worker takes leave to care for a wife giving birth, without deducting annual leave? [Para 133]
8. Whether the employer must keep wages during family emergencies without deducting annual leave? [Para 137]
9. Whether the employer must pay a 10 US dollar monthly travel allowance? [Para 141][Para 290]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The 28 May 2011 election of members of two rival unions ignored CUW's most-representative status and should be rejected; the employer froze CUW dues from March 2011 despite a 917-name thumbprint list; workers employed since 2003 were never paid severance; the milk allowance should reach 10 US dollars; meal allowances for overtime and holidays, wage retention for birth and family leave, and a 10 US dollar travel allowance are all owed. [Para 2][Para 78][Para 89][Para 98][Para 107][Para 122][Para 130][Para 133][Para 137][Para 143]

**Employer (respondent):** The election merely identified which workers consented to dues for the two rival unions because the combined lists (668 + 917) exceeded the 1,050 workforce; CUW's most-representative status is respected for collective bargaining; no contract has been terminated, so no severance is due; the parties agreed to 4 US dollars instead of daycare; meal and travel benefits already exceed the law; and it asks to keep its existing leave practice. [Para 80][Para 83][Para 100][Para 109][Para 125][Para 131][Para 136][Para 148]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Partly granted. The panel dismissed the request to reject the election but ordered the employer to keep respecting CUW's most-representative status in collective bargaining, and ordered it to pay CUW the dues withheld since March 2011 and continue deducting for the 917 workers who thumbprinted consent. It dismissed the severance claim, ordered the parties to fix a suitable daycare price within two months (or the employer to pay actual external daycare invoices), dismissed the overtime and holiday meal allowances, dismissed the wage-retention claims but ordered the employer to stop deducting wages once annual leave is exhausted, and dismissed the travel allowance while ordering stronger transport safety. [Para 299][Para 300][Para 301][Para 302][Para 308][Para 313]

On the election, the panel held that Prakas 305 gives the most-representative union the right to bargain, not a veto over the employer dealing with other unions; it therefore refused to annul the 28 May result. [Para 158][Para 159] On dues, Articles 129 and Prakas 305 article 5 require written worker consent before deduction, and the employer must then deduct and remit monthly. The 917-name thumbprint list satisfied this, and the employer's objection that names overlapped was rejected because a worker may join more than one union. [Para 164][Para 173][Para 177][Para 93]

On severance, the panel found no termination: the workers remained employed and had received wages for days worked, so the Article 75, 89 and 91 entitlements were not triggered. [Para 192][Para 197][Para 201] On daycare, the panel treated the demand to raise 4 to 10 US dollars as an interests dispute because the law mandates a daycare facility, not a cash allowance, and applied equity to order bargaining on a suitable rate within two months, failing which the employer pays actual external daycare invoices. [Para 202][Para 216][Para 224][Para 225]

On overtime meals, Prakas 041 requires 2,000 riel a day for voluntary overtime regardless of hours; the employer already paid more, so the 1,000 riel hourly claim failed. [Para 226][Para 236][Para 237] On holiday meals, the panel held that working on a public holiday is not overtime, so the claim was an interests dispute and was dismissed. [Para 256][Para 258][Para 265] On birth and family leave, the panel held the employer may charge special leave against unused annual leave but may not deduct wages once annual leave is exhausted; it dismissed the claim to keep wages without any annual-leave deduction, yet ordered the wage deductions to stop and allowed makeup of lost hours. [Para 269][Para 270][Para 281][Para 283][Para 304] On travel, no law or agreement required an allowance, the employer's buses and remorques were adequate, but the employer was told to strengthen night-time safety. [Para 290][Para 295][Para 298][Para 313]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a statute or Prakas makes a benefit conditional on written worker consent, the employer must act on a valid written consent list and remit the deducted sums; it cannot refuse because workers also hold membership in other unions. And an employer that lets a worker take special family leave may charge it against unused annual leave, but once annual leave is exhausted it may no longer deduct wages for those days. [Para 177][Para 281][Para 304]

**Obiter dicta:** A rival union cannot demand annulment of another union's membership election; the most-representative union's exclusive right is to bargain, not to control the employer's dealings with other unions. Work on a public holiday is not overtime; it carries the statutory holiday wage instead. [Para 158][Para 256]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The worker-selected arbitrator is printed as "ទូន ស៊ី ជាន់" in the header and as "ទួន ស៊ី ផាន់" in the signature block, so the surname is unstable in the OCR. The award date is 15 August 2011 in both the header and para 331. The employer's own figures are internally inconsistent: it reports a 1,050 workforce, 917 CUW consenting members, 668 rival-union names and a combined 1,585; the panel repeats these without reconciling them. Arbitrator Tuon Siphann appended a dissent on holiday meal allowance (paras 317–330), which the majority did not adopt.
