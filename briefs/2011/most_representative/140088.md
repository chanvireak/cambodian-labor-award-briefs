# Award No. 140/11 — Global A Paralle Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 30 November 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Vira, worker-appointed; Kong Polla, president) · Doc ID 140088

## 1. Facts
The Federation of Unions of Workers and two company-level unions at Global A Paralle Co., Ltd. filed 19 demands on 8 September 2011; conciliation left 16 unsettled, and at the hearing of 4 November 2011 six were settled, leaving ten live issues. The company runs 12 unions and employs 1,129 fixed-duration and 1,373 indefinite-duration workers; the two claimant unions claim about 410 members between them. The panel heard both parties, failed to conciliate the remainder, and issued its award on 30 November 2011. [Para 1][Para 40][Para 44][Para 112][Para 116][Para 166]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must raise the regular attendance bonus to USD 10 a month for all workers? [Para 117]
2. Whether the company must pay double wages to its seven drivers for overtime worked on Sundays and public holidays? [Para 119]
3. Whether the company must itemise the "other" entry on the payslip so that every wage deduction is stated clearly? [Para 129]
4. Whether the company must pay a USD 10 transport allowance? [Para 134]
5. Whether the company must pay USD 10 towards housing for workers who rent? [Para 138]
7. Whether the piece-rate incentive for the cutting-table cloth-pulling section must be raised from 50 riel to 200 riel per metre? [Para 146]
8. Whether workers able to operate one-needle and two-needle sewing machines must receive an extra USD 10? [Para 150]
10. Whether seniority pay must be raised by USD 10 for workers with five years' service or more? [Para 156]
13. Whether union leaders must be given two hours a week to meet, as staff delegates are? [Para 162]
14. Whether the company must conclude fixed-duration employment contracts of one year at a time? [Para 165]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** wages are low and long service unrewarded, so the bonus, allowances and piece rates should rise; the law requires double pay for Sunday and holiday overtime; the payslip's "other" column leaves workers unable to know why money was deducted; one-year contracts are needed for job security. [Para 117][Para 128][Para 129][Para 253]
**Employer (respondent):** it already pays a USD 7 attendance bonus, USD 6 per day for Sunday and holiday work plus a USD 20 monthly allowance, and seniority pay under Notification 041/11; it has no buyers, so it cannot pay more; the "other" column has no space and any redesign needs experts in Malaysia; contracts follow the company's needs. [Para 118][Para 123][Para 131][Para 157][Para 167]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — on the rights disputes, the claim for double overtime pay was dismissed and the claim for one-year contracts was dismissed, while the company was ordered to take measures to notify workers clearly whenever their wages change each month; the seven interest disputes were refused consideration. [Para 260][Para 261][Para 262][Para 265]

The panel first classified the ten issues: 2 (overtime pay), 3 (payslip) and 14 (contract type) were rights disputes because they turn on entitlements set by the Labour Law, while 1, 4, 5, 7, 8, 10 and 13 were interest disputes because no law or agreement provides them. [Para 178][Para 179]

On issue 1, Notification 041/11 of 7 March 2011 guarantees a minimum USD 7 attendance bonus and the employer pays it; nothing requires USD 10, so the demand exceeds what the law gives. [Para 180][Para 181][Para 183] On issue 2, the seven drivers did not attend the hearing, are not union members and gave no written authority to the union; under Article 19 of Prakas 99 the union could not represent them, so the claim failed. [Para 193][Para 195][Para 196] On issue 3, Article 112(b) of the Labour Law requires the employer to give clear, easily understood notice of all wages whenever they change; the "other" entry was found unclear, and the employer's technical difficulty in Malaysia did not excuse the duty. [Para 198][Para 199][Para 203][Para 206][Para 207] On issues 4, 5, 7, 8 and 10, no agreement or collective agreement required the transport, housing, piece-rate, skill or seniority increases, so each demand exceeded the law. [Para 217][Para 219][Para 230] On issue 13, no provision of the Labour Law gives unions two hours a week and that entitlement belongs to staff delegates only. [Para 164][Para 235] On issue 14, Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 22 of the Civil Code make the contract the parties' law, and neither side may impose a form the other did not accept. [Para 241][Para 243][Para 246][Para 254] The panel then refused to consider the interest disputes at all, because the two claimant unions were not the most representative in the enterprise. [Para 190][Para 191]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A panel will refuse to consider interest disputes brought by a union that is not the most representative in the enterprise, while still deciding rights disputes that arise under the Labour Law; and an employer cannot escape the Article 112 duty to notify wage changes clearly by pleading administrative or technical difficulty. [Para 186][Para 188][Para 191][Para 206]

**Obiter dicta:** workers who believe union-dues deductions are wrong may complain directly to the administration, without any letter from the company or union [Para 171]; the panel noted that 12 unions operate in the factory and that granting one of them two hours a week would oblige the company to grant the same to the rest [Para 163]. [Para 132]

The hearing date appears as 4 November 2011 and the agreed date for the order as 30 November 2011, the same date as the award. [Para 87][Para 112][Para 190][Para 193]
