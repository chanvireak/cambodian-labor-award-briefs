# Award No. 112/11 — Yong Va Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Branch 1) (Arbitration Council, 4 October 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, employer-appointed; An Vira, worker-appointed; Kong Phally, president) · Doc ID 140072

## 1. Facts
Yong Va Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Branch 1), at Thmei village, Ta Khmao, Kandal, employed about 1,890 workers and recognised five unions; the union bringing the case had 201 members and no union held a certificate of most-representative status. [Para 1][Para 38][Para 40][Para 41] Conciliation of ten collective disputes on 11 August 2011 settled seven points and left three unresolved, which were forwarded to the panel on 29 August 2011. [Para 14][Para 15] The three points concerned maternity pay calculated on base wage only, an extra 2,000 riel meal allowance for overtime, and deduction of the USD 7 monthly attendance bonus; roughly 7 to 10 pregnant women took maternity leave each month. [Para 42][Para 48][Para 49][Para 54]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay 50 per cent of wages and allowances for 90 days' maternity leave, calculated on the average of the 12 months before the leave, including overtime pay and the regular attendance bonus? [Para 42][Para 43]
2. Whether the company must add a further 2,000 riel meal allowance, for a total of 4,000 riel, on each day a worker worked overtime from 5:00 pm to 8:30 pm? [Para 49][Para 52]
3. Whether the company must reduce the USD 7 monthly attendance bonus only in proportion to the days a worker was absent with the employer's permission, rather than forfeiting the whole bonus? [Para 54][Para 57]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Maternity pay must be 50 per cent of total monthly earnings averaged over the last 12 months, including overtime and the regular attendance bonus, under Article 168 of the Labour Law and earlier arbitral awards; the extra meal allowance is owed whenever overtime runs to 8:30 pm, even without three consecutive days. [Para 42][Para 44][Para 52]
**Employer (respondent):** It already pays 50 per cent on base wage plus seniority pay, as the Labour Law does not state the calculation base clearly; it cannot pay the additional meal allowance; and it agrees to apply Prakas/Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 on the attendance bonus. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 53][Para 58]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the company to pay 50 per cent maternity pay for 90 days on the average monthly wage of the 12 months before leave, including overtime pay and the regular attendance bonus (average ÷ 12, then ÷ 2, then × 3); it refused the additional 2,000 riel meal allowance; and it ordered the attendance bonus reduced only in proportion to days of authorised absence. [Para 114][Para 116][Para 113]
On issue 1 the panel treated the claim as a rights dispute because maternity pay is fixed by the Labour Law. [Para 65] Article 183 was found not to define the wage base, so the panel read it with Article 103, which lists overtime pay and sums paid as a bonus within "wage"; the 50 per cent therefore had to be computed on earnings including overtime and the regular attendance bonus, following earlier awards. [Para 70][Para 71][Para 73][Para 74][Para 78] The panel rejected the employer's base-wage-plus-seniority formula as an incomplete base. [Para 64][Para 79]
On issue 2 the panel held the claim was an interest dispute, not a rights dispute, because no law, agreement or collective agreement provided the extra 2,000 riel. [Para 81] The union lacked most-representative status under Article 277, so the panel declined to arbitrate an interest claim that would become a collective agreement binding non-members and remove their right to strike. [Para 83][Para 87][Para 89][Para 91]
On issue 3 the panel found Notification No. 041/11 ambiguous about absence with permission, as the earlier Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 had been, and followed prior awards requiring proportional deduction only. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 98][Para 104] The full forfeiture applied only to absences without proper permission. [Para 104]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Maternity pay under Article 183 is computed on the average monthly wage of the 12 months preceding leave, and that wage includes overtime pay and the regular attendance bonus because Article 103 treats both as wage; an attendance-bonus scheme silent on authorised absence permits deduction only in proportion to the days of absence actually taken with the employer's permission. [Para 71][Para 74][Para 104] An interest claim exceeding the law or a collective agreement cannot be arbitrated where the claimant union is not the most representative in the enterprise. [Para 81][Para 89]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that issuing an award on the meal-allowance point would create a collective agreement extending to non-members and costing them the right to strike over future interest disputes. [Para 91]
