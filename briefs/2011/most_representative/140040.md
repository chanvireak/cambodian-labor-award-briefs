# Award No. 75/11 — Quint Major Industrial Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 July 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun, Liv Sovanna, Tan Try) · Doc ID 140040

## 1. Facts
Quint Major Industrial Co., Ltd employs 3,900 workers in three buildings at Tror Yeoung, Perk, Angsnoul, Kandal, each building holding about 1,000 workers and equipped with three card-reading machines and two three-metre gates [Para 19][Para 30][Para 32]. The gates open at 6:30 a.m. and work starts at 7:00 a.m.; the employer pays a US$ 5 attendance bonus on top of the US$ 7 statutory attendance bonus, conditional on regular and punctual attendance [Para 32][Para 33][Para 34]. About 60 pregnant workers are employed, and on 14 February 2011 one of them, Long Sreytin, was hit while trying to move through the crowd at a gate to punch in [Para 30][Para 37]. The claimant Local Union of KWPFU represents 360 workers and its most representative status has expired [Para 19][Para 20]. Six demands were filed; issues 2 and 3 were withdrawn, leaving issues 1, 4, 5 and 6 [Para 11][Para 15].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must discuss the substitution of normal working days for public holidays and Sundays with the worker delegates and the union, and notify workers one week in advance? [Para 1][Para 24]
4. Whether the employer must allow workers more than one month pregnant to punch in 10 minutes late without losing the attendance bonus? [Para 3][Para 29]
5. Whether the employer must extend the daily one-hour breastfeeding break to 18 months after birth, or pay US$ 15 per month for milk formula? [Para 3][Para 38]
6. Whether the employer must reintroduce technical examinations for all workers to increase the skill bonus? [Para 4][Para 40]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** One day's notice of holiday substitution is too short because they share charged cars with workers from other factories that close on holidays, and they should receive higher wages for holiday work [Para 25][Para 26][Para 27]. Pregnant workers risk their health racing through the crowd to avoid losing the US$ 5 bonus [Para 36][Para 37]. Some children need breastfeeding beyond one year [Para 39]. The employer held weekly technical examinations from 23 October 2008 until September 2009 and should restore them [Para 40].
**Employer (respondent):** Its representatives Peter Pan and Tan Limhong attended but said they would file an authorisation letter later; they never did, and the panel held they had no standing, so their arguments were not considered and the decision was made in the employer's absence [Para 21][Para 41][Para 45][Para 47].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** The advance-notice demand was rejected; the employer must allow pregnant workers to punch in 10 minutes late without losing the US$ 5 bonus unless it adopts appropriate accident-prevention measures; the 18-month breastfeeding or US$ 15 demand was rejected; and the technical-examination demand was rejected [Para 81][Para 82].

On standing, the panel applied Clause 19 of Prakas No. 099 and awards 161/09-Prek Treng and 43/10-Ming Jian: a representative needs express written authorisation, and because none was produced by the deadline, the employer's representatives had no legal standing and their arguments were disregarded [Para 42][Para 45][Para 46].

On Issue 1, the panel held that Article 147 and Prakas No. 010 require holiday work to be voluntary, but no law fixes a notification period; that period is for the parties to agree, and the Prakas does not oblige one week's notice, so the demand was rejected [Para 48][Para 49][Para 55][Para 56]. On Issue 4, the panel held that the right to manage includes setting an attendance bonus (awards 116/07-Grace Sun, 81/08-Global Apparel, 65/11-Sae Han), but that the condition cannot apply to pregnant workers because pregnancy is a natural factor preventing compliance [Para 58][Para 62]. Article 229 obliges the employer to protect workers' health, the acknowledged risk report showed Long Sreytin was hit, and with 3,900 workers and nine machines about 14 or 15 punch in each minute, making pushing unavoidable [Para 65][Para 67][Para 70][Para 71]. On Issue 5, Article 184 gives one hour a day for one year only; the further demand had no legal or contractual basis and was an interests dispute, which the union lacked standing to bring because it holds no MRS [Para 73][Para 74][Para 78]. On Issue 6, the workers produced no agreement, procedure or participation data, and the claimant bears the burden of proof, so the demand failed [Para 79][Para 81].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer's punctuality condition for an attendance bonus cannot be enforced against pregnant workers where the rush to clock in endangers their health, unless the employer puts in place appropriate measures to prevent accidents. [Para 62][Para 71]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that the appropriate notification period for holiday substitution is left to the parties to determine by agreement. [Para 54]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The translation is an unofficial one with widespread OCR fusion ("themployer", "Traing", "remaing") [Para 1][Para 7]. The issue numbering is inconsistent: the demand on advance notice is numbered Issue 1 in the issues section but decided as "Issue 2" in the decision [Para 24][Para 81]. The address line contains a Khmer character string ("Addresនៈ") and the union is called "KFPWFU" in the facts but "KWPFU" elsewhere [Para 1][Para 19]. The source refers to "Building five of the factory" although the facts describe only three buildings [Para 37][Para 30]. The legal attendance bonus is described as "stipulated by law" at US$ 7 without a citation [Para 32].
