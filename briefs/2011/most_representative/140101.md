# Award No. 157/11 — Ing Jie (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 20 December 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Virak, worker-appointed; Kong Phalha, chair) · non-binding award with a dissenting annex · Doc ID 140101

## 1. Facts
Ing Jie (Cambodia) Ltd began operating on 15 February 2011 and employed about 155 workers. The Federation of Unions for Workers' Rights, whose factory union had more than 50 members but no registration certificate from the Ministry, lodged thirteen demands on behalf of 56 workers who thumbprinted a request for improved conditions. Ten points were settled at the hearing of 6 December 2011, leaving three: the rate of meal money for weekday overtime, the attendance bonus during certified sick leave, and meal money for Sunday work. [Para 1][Para 22][Para 25][Para 26][Para 29][Para 34][Para 36][Para 37][Para 42]

## 2. Issues in dispute
0. Whether the Federation may represent the 56 workers who thumbprinted the demand? [Para 58]
1. (Panel issue 2) Whether the company must pay 4,000 riel meal money for overtime worked from 16:00 to 20:00, instead of the 3,000 riel it pays? [Para 44][Para 46]
2. (Panel issue 3) Whether the attendance bonus must be kept in full when a worker takes sick leave supported by a proper certificate from a state hospital? [Para 50][Para 51]
3. (Panel issue 12) Whether the company must pay 4,000 riel meal money for overtime worked on Sundays? [Para 54][Para 91]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The notification fixes 2,000 riel for overtime, so the 4,000 riel demanded is the going rate and the company's 2,000 plus 1,000 falls short; Sunday work is performed on the same 8:00 to 16:00 shift as a normal day, so it is overtime and meal money is owed, though the company pays none; and another factory keeps the attendance bonus during certified sick leave, so the company should not forfeit the whole bonus for an absence that is not the worker's fault. [Para 46][Para 48][Para 52][Para 55][Para 56]
**Employer (respondent):** It already pays 3,000 riel — 2,000 up to 18:00 and a further 1,000 to 20:00 — which is more than the law requires, so it cannot add more; it pays no meal money at all for Sunday overtime; and it declined the bonus claim outright. [Para 45][Para 49][Para 54][Para 57]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the 4,000 riel claims for weekday and Sunday overtime meals were declined as interest demands; the company was ordered to pay 2,000 riel a day meal money to workers who voluntarily work Sunday overtime, and to deduct the attendance bonus in proportion to the days actually worked for workers absent on certified sick leave, rather than forfeiting the whole bonus. The employer-appointed arbitrator dissented on the bonus point. [Para 109][Para 110][Para 111][Para 112][Para 113]

On representation, Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 provides that a disputing party may appear in person or through a representative holding written authority; the thumbprint list signed by 56 workers supplied that authority, so the Federation could act for them. The panel drew a distinction, however, between that procedural capacity and the standing required for an interest dispute: only a union with most-representative status, which requires registration, may bring such a dispute, and this union had none. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60][Para 61][Para 74][Para 76]

On issue 2 the panel found the company paid 3,000 riel for overtime from 16:00 to 20:00 and that no provision of the Labour Law, agreement or collective convention requires 4,000. A demand above what the law fixes is a demand of interest, not of right, and the panel declined it. [Para 64][Para 65][Para 67][Para 77]

On issue 3 the panel found that Notification No. 041/11 requires a bonus of at least USD 7 for regular attendance but says nothing about absence that the law allows. Reading the bonus as an incentive rather than a disciplinary sanction, it held that forfeiting the whole USD 7 for lawful sick leave is unjust to the worker, while paying the full bonus for days not worked is unjust to the employer; following Award No. 63/04 (Sanwell), it ordered deduction in proportion to the days actually worked in the month. [Para 78][Para 80][Para 83][Para 84][Para 85][Para 87][Para 90]

On issue 12 the panel held that Sunday work beyond the normal 8 hours a day and 48 hours a week under Article 137 is overtime, and that point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 entitles a worker who volunteers for overtime at the employer's request to 2,000 riel a day or a free meal, without any hour threshold. The company paid nothing, so the 2,000 riel rate was ordered; the balance of the 4,000 riel claim was an interest demand and was declined. [Para 91][Para 93][Para 97][Para 98][Para 99][Para 100][Para 102][Para 113]

The annexed dissent of the employer-appointed arbitrator reasons from the dictionary and French legal definitions of a bonus: a bonus is conditional, its owner is the employer, and where the condition — attendance for the stipulated number of days — is not met, the worker cannot claim a share of it, so the majority's pro-rata order was wrong. [Para 115][Para 122][Para 124][Para 134]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that is not registered lacks most-representative status and cannot bring an interest dispute to the panel, even though it may appear for named workers who have authorised it in writing; a demand for more than the rate fixed by ministerial notification is an interest demand and is declined; where a notification is silent on lawful absence, an attendance bonus must be reduced in proportion to the days worked rather than forfeited in full for certified sick leave; and Sunday work beyond 48 hours a week is overtime, so the notification's daily meal allowance is payable without any minimum hours. [Para 61][Para 67][Para 90][Para 98][Para 102]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that it has no competence to decide whether a thumbprint is genuine, and that the employer had not challenged the thumbprints in any event. [Para 62][Para 63]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The union is said to have "more than 50" members, but 56 workers demanded and only 20 were said to claim on issue 2. The demand list is described as 13 points in the conciliation report and as 14 points in the thumbprint table. Issue 3 is recorded as settled at Para 25 only as to wage retention and left open as to the bonus at Para 26, so the heading "settled" is misleading as printed. The notification's date appears once as "7 March 2011" and once with a stray digit, "20119". The employer representatives' names are OCR-damaged in the header, and the signature block is partly garbled. The award is expressly non-binding and carries a dissenting annex.
