# Award No. 118/11 — Hen Siven E. Teril Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 6 October 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Kong Phally, president) · Doc ID 140077

## 1. Facts
The Movement of Workers Union at the Hen Siven E. Teril garment factory, which employs about 620 workers, brought a collective dispute claiming 13 points; conciliation settled seven and left six, and further conciliation at the hearing settled four more, leaving issues 4 and 6 [Para 23][Para 27][Para 51]. The employer told Mr S, cutting-table chief and union president, and Ms D, sewing-section chief, deputy union president and staff delegate, orally on 12 August 2011 not to return, days before their fixed-term contracts expired on 13 and 15 August 2011 [Para 62][Para 65][Para 73][Para 76][Para 113].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 4) Whether the employer is obliged to raise the monthly wage of team assistants by USD 10? [Para 54][Para 83]
2. (Issue 6) Whether the dismissal of the two union leaders was lawful, and whether this is a collective labour dispute within the panel's jurisdiction? [Para 101][Para 103]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** team assistants work harder than other workers and other companies pay them the extra USD 10; the two dismissed workers were union leaders, so the dispute is collective [Para 56][Para 59][Para 102].
**Employer (respondent):** assistants already receive the USD 61 minimum wage and the company cannot afford more; the contracts simply ran to their term; the dispute is individual, not collective [Para 57][Para 70][Para 81][Para 101].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — Issue 4 dismissed; Issue 6 granted, ordering the employer to take back Mr S and Ms D and to pay back wages from the date of dismissal until the date of reinstatement. The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent objection [Para 100][Para 125][Para 128].

On Issue 4 the panel found that Prakas (notification) No. 049/10 of 9 July 2010 set the minimum wage at USD 61 per month and that assistants already received it [Para 83][Para 84][Para 85]. No provision of the Labour Law and no collective agreement obliged the employer to add USD 10, so the claim concerned interests beyond what the law prescribes — an interests dispute [Para 86][Para 88]. The panel could adjudicate such a dispute only if the claimant union were the most representative in the enterprise, holding more than half the workers; this union had 214 members out of 620, so it did not qualify, and the claim was refused [Para 89][Para 94][Para 100].

On Issue 6 the panel found the employer summoned both workers and told them orally not to come back, before their contracts expired; that is a dismissal, not the natural expiry of a fixed-term contract [Para 114][Para 115]. As union president and deputy president/staff delegate they were protected under Article 293 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 305, which require the labour inspector's prior approval; the employer admitted it never sought approval [Para 120][Para 122]. The dismissal was therefore procedurally unlawful, and reinstatement with back pay followed [Para 123][Para 124][Para 125].

The panel also held the dispute collective: the employer asserted it was individual but produced no evidence to rebut the presumption, so its burden was not discharged [Para 108][Para 109][Para 110].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A wage claim above the statutory minimum is an interests dispute that the panel may adjudicate only where the claimant union is the most representative in the enterprise, meaning more than half the workers; a minority union cannot obtain a wage increase through arbitration [Para 89][Para 98], and an award on interests would become a collective agreement binding workers who are not members. Dismissal of a protected union leader requires the labour inspector's prior approval under Article 293 and Article 4 of Prakas No. 305, and that approval must be obtained before the dismissal is carried out; its absence makes the dismissal procedurally unlawful [Para 116][Para 119][Para 121].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel ordinarily follows the labour inspector's and the Minister's characterisation of a dispute as individual or collective unless there is a clear reason to differ, and the employer bears the burden of proving that a reported collective dispute is in fact individual [Para 104][Para 107].
