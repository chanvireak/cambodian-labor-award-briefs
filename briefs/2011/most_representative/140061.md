# Award No. 100/11 — Nugget Textile (Cambodia) PTE Ltd (Arbitration Council, 29 August 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ri, Liv Sovanna, Tang Tri) · Doc ID 140061

## 1. Facts
Nugget Textile (Cambodia) PTE Ltd, printed in Khmer as ក្រុមហ៊ុន ណាក់ ហ្គេតេច ស្តាយ (ភី ធី អី) អិលធីឌី, employed 480 workers at the date of the award. Three unions operated in the plant, and the claimant, the Economy Workers Union of Cambodia (សហជីព សេដ្ឋកិច្ច កម្មករ កម្ពុជា), had 200 members; no union in the enterprise held a certificate of most-representative status. The company began paying each worker USD 5 a month in December 2007 because the access road was muddy and hard to travel, and it kept paying after the road was repaired in December 2009, entering the sum in the monthly payroll as an "allowance" (ប្រាក់ ឧបត្ថម្ភ) rather than as a travel cost. From January 2010 newly recruited workers demanded the same USD 5, but the company announced at recruitment that they would not receive it. Conciliation settled 10 of 14 demands, leaving four; at the hearing the parties reported that only three remained, and the panel settled two more, leaving demand 1. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38][Para 39][Para 40][Para 41][Para 42][Para 44][Para 16][Para 20][Para 21]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay USD 5 a month as a travel allowance to all workers, old and new, recruited from 2010? [Para 40][Para 72]
2. Whether the claimant union is entitled to bring an interest dispute on behalf of all the company's workers? [Para 57][Para 71]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Workers recruited before and after 2010 belong to the same employer, so the company may not discriminate in granting this benefit; the company announced the benefits of employment when it recruited, and new workers should receive the same USD 5 as those already receiving it. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 49]
**Employer (respondent):** The payment was never a travel cost but a voluntary allowance, as the payroll shows; the road was repaired in December 2009, and the company announced to every worker recruited from 2010 that the USD 5 would not be paid. The company continued paying existing workers only because it did not withdraw a benefit already granted to them. [Para 43][Para 48][Para 52][Para 53]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider the demand that the employer pay USD 5 a month to workers recruited from 2010. The award was non-binding and would become final eight days after notification unless a party objected in writing to the Minister of Labour and Vocational Training. [Para 72][Para 73]

The panel held that the demand exceeded the law: neither the 1997 Labour Law nor any regulation requires an employer to pay USD 5 a month to workers recruited from 2010. A demand that goes beyond what the law provides is an interest dispute, not a rights dispute. For interest disputes the panel always examines whether the union bringing the case holds most-representative status, because only such a union may conclude a collective agreement and bring an interest dispute to the Arbitration Council. Under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004, an award resolving an interest dispute replaces a collective agreement for one year from the date it becomes final. [Para 54][Para 55][Para 56][Para 57][Para 58][Para 59][Para 60]

The panel followed its earlier awards, citing Wilson 152/08 on point 2, Daiyang 07/06 on point 3, Haytech 71/09 on point 10 and Mingda Fu Wei 84/10 on point 3, for the rule that it declines to consider an interest dispute when the union is not the most representative in the enterprise. Here the claimant union held no certificate of most-representative status, so it lacked the legal standing to represent the collective interests of all 480 workers. The panel therefore refused to consider the demand. [Para 62][Para 64][Para 66][Para 67][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70][Para 71]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that does not hold a certificate of most-representative status cannot bring an interest dispute on behalf of all workers in an enterprise, and the panel will decline to consider its demand; a demand that exceeds what the Labour Law or a regulation requires is an interest dispute, and an award on it becomes a collective agreement for one year. [Para 56][Para 59][Para 70]

**Obiter dicta:** A voluntary allowance paid to existing workers does not by itself oblige the employer to extend it to workers recruited later, and the panel treated the payroll label "allowance" as evidence that the payment was not reimbursement of travel costs. [Para 44][Para 52]
