# Award No. 90/11 — Green Tech Eco Park (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 9 August 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Uk Ri, Liv Sovanna, Pen Bunhea presiding) · Doc ID 140051

## 1. Facts
Green Tech Eco Park (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. runs a plastic-bag factory at Prey Boeung, Dangkao, Phnom Penh, employing about 200 workers. The Khmer Youth Union at the company, which has roughly 92 members, brought 13 demands; 10 were settled in conciliation and 3 were not. At the hearing the union withdrew two of the three, leaving a single demand: a transport and housing allowance of USD 5 per worker per month. [Para 3][Para 15][Para 19][Para 20][Para 21][Para 37]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay each worker a transport and housing allowance of USD 5 per month? [Para 39]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Bus fares and rents have risen, many workers live far from the factory and pay for daily transport or rented rooms, and other factories pay this allowance; the demand is to improve working conditions. [Para 41][Para 42]

**Employer (respondent):** No law requires the company to pay such an allowance, no contract, agreement or collective convention provides for it, and the company is not yet profitable and is short of funds. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 46]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider the claim and ordered that the company need not pay the USD 5 monthly transport and housing allowance. [Para 57][Para 58]

The panel first classified the demand. Because no law, agreement or collective convention provides for a USD 5 monthly transport and housing allowance, the claim is for a benefit better than the statutory floor. [Para 47][Para 48] It is therefore an interests dispute rather than a rights dispute. [Para 49]

For interests disputes the panel examines whether the union bringing the claim is the most representative union in the enterprise. [Para 50] In earlier awards it has refused to consider an interests dispute where the union was not the most representative union in the factory. [Para 51][Para 52] Here the Khmer Youth Union at Green Tech was not certified as most representative: it held no certificate of most-representative status under article 277 of the 1997 Labour Law. [Para 38][Para 55][Para 56] The panel accordingly held that the union had no legal standing to bring an interests dispute before it, and declined to consider the demand. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58]

The order records that it is binding and becomes final immediately, the parties having agreed on 3 August 2011 to accept it. [Para 59][Para 33]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The Arbitration Council will decline to adjudicate an interests dispute — a demand for benefits exceeding what statute, agreement or collective convention requires — where the union bringing it has not been certified as the most representative union in the enterprise under article 277 of the Labour Law. [Para 49][Para 55]

**Obiter dicta:** Most-representative status is what confers legal standing both to conclude a collective bargaining agreement in the enterprise and to bring an interests dispute to the panel. [Para 53] A membership of about 92 out of some 200 workers does not by itself establish that status. [Para 37][Para 38]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company is printed variously as "គ្រីន តិច អេកូ ជា ក", "គ្រីន តិច អេកូ ផាក" and "គ្រីន ថិច"; the union as "សព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ" and "សហជីព យុវជ ខ្មែរ". Arbitrator Liv Sovanna appears as "លីវ សុវណ្ណា" and "លវ សុវណ្ណា". The demand is described both as an allowance for travel and accommodation [Para 3] and as travel costs plus room rent [Para 21]. At [Para 47] the sum is printed as "៥ ដុល្លារ ២០ អាមេរិក", which reads as USD 5 20 and is inconsistent with the USD 5 used everywhere else. The signature block is dated 3 August 2011 [Para 59], the hearing date, while the award itself is dated 9 August 2011 [Para 1]. The order is described as becoming final immediately, yet also as taking effect on the parties' agreement of 3 August [Para 59].
