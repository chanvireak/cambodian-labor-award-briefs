# Award No. 76/11 — Zongtex Garment Manufacturing Ltd (Arbitration Council, 19 July 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer party; Liv Sovanna, worker party; Pen Bunchhea, chair) · Doc ID 140042

## 1. Facts
Zongtex Garment Manufacturing Ltd runs a Phnom Penh garment factory employing 600 workers, where two unions operate: the Local Union of the Rights and Profit Workers Federation of Trade Unions (RPWFTU), representing roughly 250 workers and the claimant here, and the Union for Cambodian Workers (UCW), which alone holds a certificate of most representative status (MRS). Of 12 demands raised in a complaint filed on 6 June 2011, seven were settled in conciliation; the panel took up four unresolved issues, the fifth having been resolved at the hearing. The disputed acts were the size of the monthly attendance bonus, the frequency of overtime meal-allowance payment, the employer's deduction of US$4.50 (about 18,000 riel) or the workers' own payment of 12,000 riel for medical checks during September and October 2010, and the employer's practice of issuing three-month rather than six-month fixed-duration contracts. [Para 7][Para 9][Para 11][Para 17][Para 27]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must increase the attendance bonus by US$3 to reach a total of US$10 per month? [Para 18]
2. Whether the employer must pay the overtime meal allowance on a weekly basis? [Para 23]
3. Whether the employer must repay the US$4.50 deducted from wages or the 12,000 riel paid by workers for medical checks? [Para 26]
4. Whether the employer must offer six-month fixed-duration contracts upon completion of the two-month probationary period? [Para 30]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer previously paid a US$6 attendance bonus against a legal minimum of US$5, and Notification No. 041/11 now requires US$7, so the loss of the extra US$1 and their long service justify an additional US$3, or failing that US$2 or US$1. Weekly overtime allowance payment is needed because some workers lack money to buy meals during overtime, the employer should hire a second accountant to make that possible, the employer is liable for medical-check expenses under Ministry of Labour Prakas, and three-month contracts let the employer terminate without prior notice whereas six-month contracts require notice of expiry and give better security. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 26][Para 29][Para 34]

**Employer (respondent):** It pays the US$7 attendance bonus in compliance with the new notification and refuses any increase; it pays the overtime allowance on the 20th and 5th of each month, once every two weeks, and cannot pay weekly because it employs only one accountant; it refuses to repay medical-check costs because workers must be checked before commencing work and other factories do not pay those expenses; and it refuses six-month contracts for all new workers because its practice matches its production line, though it offers them to workers with one year's seniority and gives seven days' notice of non-renewal. [Para 23][Para 25][Para 26][Para 30][Para 32][Para 35][Para 36]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Partly granted — the panel declined to consider the attendance-bonus demand, ordered the employer to pay the overtime allowance weekly, ordered it to repay the US$4.50 deducted from wages or the 12,000 riel paid by workers for medical checks, and rejected the demand for six-month fixed-duration contracts. [Para 83]

*Issue 1 — attendance bonus.* The panel found the employer was paying US$7 under Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011, which requires the US$7 monthly bonus but does not oblige an increase over the former US$5 minimum by any specified amount. With no agreement or collective agreement on an extra US$1, US$2 or US$3, the demand exceeded legal entitlement and was an interests dispute; because the Local Union of RPWFTU held no MRS certificate, it lacked standing and the panel declined to consider the demand, following Awards 71/09-Hytex and 84/10-Ming Da Footwear. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 45]

*Issue 2 — overtime meal allowance.* Following Awards 85/09-Nan Kuang and 110/09-Berry Apparel, the panel held the meal allowance is not part of overtime wages but a substitute for a free meal, and Notification No. 041 sets no payment period. Since the law's purpose is to fund a meal on each day of overtime and payment every two weeks left workers unable to buy meals daily, the panel found the weekly demand legitimate and ordered it. [Para 49][Para 53][Para 65][Para 66][Para 67]

*Issue 3 — medical-check expenses.* Applying Article 247(a) and (c) of the Labour Law and Clause 2(d) of inter-ministerial Prakas No. 1191 of 21 November 2006, which fixes the employer's fee at 12,000 riel per Cambodian worker, the panel held the employer liable for medical-check expenses, following Awards 02/03-Chu Hsing, 19/04-Kbal Koah II, 59/05-Tack Fat, 05/06-W & D, 98/07-Sky Sino, 52/08-Supreme Garment and 70/09-G.W. On Article 120's three-year limitation, and Award 93/07-Global Footwear, the claim was in time because the checks occurred in September and October 2010. [Para 68][Para 72][Para 73][Para 74]

*Issue 4 — six-month contracts.* Reading Article 65 of the Labour Law with Articles 1 and 22 of Decree 38 on Contracts and Other Liabilities, and Awards 131/09-Medcrest, 54/10-USA and 68/10-USA, the panel held an employment contract requires the employer's consent and the panel cannot impose one without it. The employer's refusal, based on production-line requirements, therefore defeated the demand. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 81][Para 82]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a demand exceeds what the Labour Law or a Prakas guarantees, it is an interests dispute that only a union holding most representative status may bring before the panel; a union without an MRS certificate lacks standing and its demand will not be considered. The employer bears the statutory cost of workers' medical checks at 12,000 riel per Cambodian worker, and the three-year limitation on reclaiming that sum runs from the contract date or the date the worker paid or had the fee deducted. [Para 45][Para 68][Para 72]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the meal allowance must be paid at a time that actually lets workers buy meals, and that the workers' daily living needs are of paramount importance when fixing payment frequency; it also noted that a party cannot be forced into a contract without its consent. [Para 58][Para 64][Para 81]
