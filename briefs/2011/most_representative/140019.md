# Award No. 46/11 — San Lei Fung Garment & Woolen Knitting Factory Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 6 May 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Ang Eng Thong chair) · Doc ID 140019

## 1. Facts
San Lei Fung Garment & Woolen Knitting Factory Ltd., a factory on National Road 2, Phnom Penh, employed 176 workers; the only union was the Local Union of CLUF, the claimant, which represented 19 workers and whose certificate of most representative status (MRS), dated 6 March 2003, had expired. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 25] Of eight demands, five were settled at conciliation and three were referred; issue 1 was resolved at the hearing, leaving issues 2 and 3. [Para 12][Para 13][Para 15][Para 18] Issue 2 sought one-year contracts for casual (floating) workers then engaged on three-month contracts; issue 3 sought an additional US$ 6 per month for six group leaders paid US$ 89. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 29]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must renew the employment contracts of casual workers with contracts of at least one year's duration? [Para 27]
2. Whether the employer must provide an additional US$ 6 of wages per month to group leaders? [Para 28]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer had stopped hiring casual workers; the union sought one-year contracts to secure a long-term employment relationship; and the group leaders received insufficient wages because the employer had not provided enough work for six months. [Para 28][Para 29]
**Employer (respondent):** it could not offer one-year contracts because its practice was to sign three-month contracts, to which the workers had agreed before starting; the group leaders were already paid above the minimum wage, so an increase was not possible. [Para 6][Para 7][Para 29]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the demand for one-year contracts as a rights dispute (binding award) and declined to consider the group-leader wage demand as an interests dispute (non-binding award). [Para 53][Para 54][Para 55]

On issue 2 the dispute arose from a valid and enforceable contract and concerned entitlements under the Labour Law and Decree 38, so it was a rights dispute. [Para 33][Para 34] Article 65 of the Labour Law subjects the labour contract to ordinary law, and Articles 1 and 22 of Decree 38 provide that a contract is formed by the parties' agreement and binds only them. [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38] Prior awards held that no third party can force the parties to enter a contract (AAs 131/09 Medcrest, 54/10 USA, 68/10 USA). [Para 39] The employer did not agree to one-year contracts and no law required it to offer them, so the panel could not compel the employer to sign them. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 42]

On issue 3 the demand had no basis in the Labour Law, a notification, internal work rules, an agreement or a collective agreement, so it was an interests dispute. [Para 46] For an interests dispute the claimant union must hold MRS; the CLUF's certificate dated 6 March 2003 had expired, and Article 277 requires a union to be registered and to fulfil the statutory conditions. [Para 48][Para 50][Para 51] The panel therefore held the union lacked legal standing and declined to consider the demand. [Para 51][Para 52]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The panel cannot order an employer to conclude a labour contract on terms it has not accepted; a demand for longer fixed-duration contracts is a rights dispute but fails where neither the contract nor the law requires renewal on those terms. A union whose MRS certificate has expired lacks legal standing to bring an interests dispute before the panel. [Para 40][Para 42][Para 50][Para 51]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's statement that an MRS union has legal capacity to negotiate a collective agreement and standing to bring an interests dispute is a general description of the standing rules, not a finding on either demand. [Para 48]
