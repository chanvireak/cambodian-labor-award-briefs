# Award No. 96/11 — Top World Garment (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 26 August 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Case No. 96/11-Top World · Panel: Mar Sombona (employer-appointed), Sin Kim Sean (worker-appointed), Kong Chholha (chair, chosen by the two) · Doc ID 140058

## 1. Facts

Top World Garment (Cambodia) Ltd operates a garment factory at Chroy Ampov village, Kbal Koh commune, Meanchey district, Phnom Penh, employing 730 workers [Para 1][Para 96]. The Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers Democratic Union (C.CAWDU) at the factory registered on 23 July 2009 and is the claimant [Para 1][Para 97]. The union filed a complaint on 23 December 2010 seeking improved working conditions; conciliation on 20 July 2011 left 9 of 17 demands unsettled, reported to the Arbitration Council on 28 July 2011 under report No. 851 Kb/Ak/Vk of 27 July 2011 [Para 25][Para 26][Para 91]. At the hearing of 9 August 2011 five of the nine were settled (demands 1, 2, 5, 7 and 8), leaving demands 3, 4, 6 and 9 [Para 24][Para 30].

Both parties signed the Memorandum of Understanding of 28 September 2010 on improving industrial relations in the garment industry, so awards on rights disputes bind them while awards on interest disputes are optional [Para 33][Para 34][Para 225][Para 227].

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. Must the employer build a canteen for workers (demand 3)? [Para 98][Para 222]
2. Must the employer pay wages to workers absent on sick leave with a doctor's certificate (demand 4)? [Para 106][Para 166][Para 223]
3. Must the employer preserve annual leave for union leaders attending seminars (demand 6)? [Para 128][Para 199][Para 224]
4. Must the employer pay a US$7 allowance to material openers (demand 9)? [Para 132][Para 208][Para 226]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** 70 per cent of workers eat inside the factory but there is no proper canteen — only three sheds of about six metres square holding 30 to 40 people, plus about ten canopies — so workers eat under trees, beside the foreign staff building and, in the rainy season, in the rain [Para 98][Para 99][Para 100][Para 101][Para 102]. On sick leave, workers say the company still deducted wages despite medical certificates, citing Sok La (38 days, gastric and intestinal inflammation and kidney stones), Som Nary (over two months, broken thigh from a traffic accident) and Su Poly (14 days, kidney stones) [Para 108][Para 109][Para 111][Para 112][Para 113][Para 114][Para 115][Para 116][Para 117]. Union leaders claim annual leave should be preserved when they attend seminars of one to three days [Para 128][Para 129]. Material openers claim US$7 extra because they do more work than group leaders and fill in on sewing when idle, while group leaders receive a US$15 position allowance [Para 136][Para 137].

**Employer (respondent):** it cannot add rubbish bins because daily emptying disturbs the teams [Para 3]; it cannot add fans because it already uses evaporative cooling and ventilation fans, and will instead close the doors [Para 5]; it is discussing canteen construction with the landowner but cannot fix a completion date [Para 6][Para 103][Para 104]. It recognises only state and labour doctors, so private-clinic certificates are not paid [Para 8][Para 106][Para 118][Para 119]. On annual leave, it allows leaders to attend seminars by deducting annual leave with wages, but will not pay wages if annual leave is exhausted [Para 130][Para 131]. It says no law requires the US$7 allowance [Para 139].

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** partly granted. The panel ordered the employer to build a canteen within six months of the award taking effect [Para 165][Para 222]; ordered it to pay sick-leave wages to workers absent with a proper certificate from a state doctor or a private clinic recognised by the Ministry of Health [Para 198][Para 223]; and dismissed demands 6 and 9 [Para 207][Para 224][Para 221][Para 226].

Canteen: Article 229 of the Labour Law requires employers to ensure health and safety at work [Para 152]. Following Award No. 35/04-Zacksintech, the panel held a missing canteen can harm workers' health and offends Article 229 [Para 151][Para 152]. The employer conceded workers genuinely struggle to eat in the rainy season [Para 153][Para 154][Para 157]. Although the difficulty arises only in the wet season, that season lasts six months — as long as the dry season — so the exposure is prolonged [Para 159]. The employer had promised construction for some time and workers had waited and endured [Para 160][Para 161]. An open-ended discussion period is unacceptable, and building a canteen should not take more than six months [Para 162][Para 163][Para 164].

Sick leave: Articles 71 and 72 of the Labour Law allow suspension of the employment contract for certified illness up to six months, and during suspension the worker need not work and the employer need not pay [Para 167][Para 169][Para 170][Para 171]. But point 4 of the company's internal rules and point 6 of the agreement of 27 November 2009 provide full wages for the first month, 60 per cent for months two and three, and no wages for months four to six while the post is retained [Para 121][Para 122][Para 123][Para 172]. The three workers held proper certificates yet the company paid nothing [Para 173]. "Official doctor" means a doctor trained at a recognised medical school with a certificate and a Ministry of Health practice licence, and includes provincial, district, commune and private clinics [Para 175][Para 178][Para 179]. Sok La's 38 days entitled him to 100 per cent for month one and 60 per cent for month two [Para 182][Para 183][Para 184]; Som Nary's absence of over two months attracted the same [Para 187][Para 188]; Su Poly's 14 days attracted 100 per cent [Para 191]. The company's refusal to recognise only state doctors was therefore wrong [Para 190]. However, the three workers were not named in the conciliation report, and Article 312(1) and Article 33 of Prakas No. 099 limit the panel to issues in that report [Para 192][Para 193][Para 194][Para 197], so the order was framed generically without naming them [Para 197][Para 198].

Annual leave: Article 166 gives 1.5 days' paid leave per month worked [Para 200][Para 201]. The employer permitted leaders to use annual leave for seminars and kept their wages [Para 202][Para 203]. No law or regulation requires wages for seminar attendance, so workers who want wages must use their annual leave [Para 205][Para 206]. The demand was dismissed [Para 207].

US$7 allowance: no law, regulation or agreement requires it [Para 209]; it is an interest dispute [Para 210]. The panel generally declines interest claims where the union lacks most-representative status [Para 211], and found this union did not hold it [Para 219][Para 220], so the claim was dismissed [Para 221].

## 5. Ratio decidendi

Where workers must eat in the rain because no canteen exists, Article 229 obliges the employer to build one, and six months is a reasonable deadline [Para 152][Para 159][Para 164]. Contract suspension under Articles 71 and 72 removes the wage obligation, but more favourable internal rules or collective agreements govern, and "official doctor" includes any MoH-recognised private clinic [Para 170][Para 172][Para 179]. The panel cannot award to named workers absent from the conciliation report, so the remedy must be worded generically [Para 193][Para 197]. An interest claim by a union without most-representative status is dismissed without consideration [Para 211][Para 220].

**Obiter dicta:** an employer's promise to build and workers' patience do not excuse an indefinite timeline [Para 160][Para 161][Para 162]; and using annual leave is the lawful route to paid seminar attendance [Para 205][Para 206].

## 6. Source defects disclosed

The demand list runs to 17 items [Para 26] but only 9 appear in the recital and 4 survive to the award [Para 30]; the recital text at paras 7–19 is heavily garbled and demand 4's wording is truncated [Para 7]. Paragraph 134 is a stray fragment ("ល ។") [Para 134]. The company name alternates between "ថប វើល ដ៏" and "ថបវើលដ៏" and the union between "ស៊ី ខោ ឌូ" and "ស៊ី ខៅ ឌូ" [Para 1][Para 2]. The panel's finding that the union is not most representative [Para 219] sits oddly with its having brought the collective claim, and the basis for that finding is not set out. The arbitrator signature block is largely lost to OCR [Para 228].
