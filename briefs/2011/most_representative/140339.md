# Award No. 138/10 — Whitex Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 5 January 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom, employer-appointed; Tuon Si Phan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, presiding) · Doc ID 140339

## 1. Facts
Whitex Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ាយ ថិច ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា)), a factory operating since 2005 with about 2,000 workers at Trapeang Thleung, Dangkao, faced a demand from the Khmer Youth Trade Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ) and its factory union for a uniform USD 5 wage increase. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 2] Conciliation settled six of eight demands; of the two remaining, the workers withdrew issue 2 (payment of wages twice a month) at the hearing, leaving one issue. [Para 11][Para 15] The union had about 590 members and was not the most representative union in the plant. [Para 32][Para 33]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must raise base wage by USD 5 for every worker, including those whose base wage already exceeded USD 55 a month? [Para 34]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant — Khmer Youth Trade Union Federation and its Whitex factory union):** base wages ran from USD 50 to USD 100; when Notification No. 049 of 9 July 2010 took effect the company raised only the USD 50 group to USD 55 and left the USD 55 group unchanged, which was unfair because those workers had already earned more before the notification, so everyone should receive the additional USD 5. [Para 34][Para 37][Para 39][Para 40]
**Employer (respondent — Whitex Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, represented by its factory and administrative heads):** the company applied Notification No. 049 as written, adding USD 5 only for those who had been on USD 50 and leaving the roughly 30 to 40 workers already on USD 55 untouched; any further increase depended on individual output and supervisor approval, not on a legal duty. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider the demand for a further USD 5 a month for workers whose base wage already exceeded USD 55; the withdrawal of issue 2 disposed of the payment-frequency claim. [Para 69][Para 15]

Point 2 of Notification No. 049 កប/សជណ of 9 July 2010 fixes the minimum wage for the textile, garment and footwear sector at USD 56 a month for workers on probation and USD 61 for full-right workers, that is the prevailing minimum of USD 50 plus USD 5 increase plus USD 6 living allowance. [Para 50][Para 51] The panel read that instrument as guaranteeing a floor of USD 61, not as ordering an increase for workers who were already above the floor, and found the company had raised the USD 50 group to USD 55 and added the USD 6 allowance, so it had complied. [Para 52][Para 54][Para 55]

The panel then looked for a separate source of the claimed increase and found none: no provision of the Labour Law requires a USD 5 rise for a worker whose base wage is already USD 55, and the parties had made no agreement or collective agreement on the point. [Para 56][Para 57] Since the demand therefore exceeded what the law conferred, the panel classified it as an interest dispute rather than a rights dispute. [Para 58]

That classification decided the case. Under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 an award resolving an interest dispute replaces the collective agreement for one year, and a collective agreement binds every worker in the factory. [Para 61][Para 63] Only a union with most-representative status under Article 277 of the Labour Law may bring such a dispute, and the panel found this union, with 590 members among some 2,000 workers, did not have it. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67] It followed a line of earlier awards declining interest disputes brought by unions without that status, and dismissed. [Para 59][Para 68]

The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent a written objection to the Minister of Labour. [Para 70]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a wage increase above the statutory minimum, brought by a union that lacks most-representative status under Article 277, is an interest dispute the Arbitration Council will not hear, because any award would operate as a factory-wide collective agreement for a year under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 67]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that Notification No. 049 sets a minimum of USD 61 for full-right workers and does not by itself require an increase for workers already paid above USD 55, leaving such increases to individual assessment of output. [Para 52][Para 42]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company name is printed in several forms across the award — វ៉ាយ ថិច ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) in the party block, and shortened to វាយ ថិច or វាយ ថិ in the findings and the conciliation records; the English "Whitex Garments (Cambodia) Co., Ltd" also appears in two Social Security letters. [Para 1][Para 26][Para 17]

The invitation letter's date is duplicated by the scan, reading "ឆ្នាំ ២ ឆ្នាំ ២០១០". [Para 27] The operative paragraph on issue 1 repeats its own sentence twice, apparently from a two-column scan, and truncates the phrase "លើសពី ៥៥ ដុល្លារ" at one point. [Para 68][Para 69] One union representative in the appearance list is given a name without any office, and the figure of 590 members sits uneasily against a workforce of 2,000 without any explanation of how representativeness was tested. [Para 2][Para 32]
