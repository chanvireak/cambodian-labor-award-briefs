# Award No. 66/11 — Ing Han Sung Garment Co., Ltd (formerly Dai Young II) (Arbitration Council, 29 June 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phyrum, Liv Sovanna, Sok Mathoeung) · Doc ID 140034

## 1. Facts
Ing Han Sung Garment Co., Ltd (formerly Dai Young II), a small subcontracting garment factory at Angkeo Village, Phnom Penh, employed 430 workers. [Para 12] The Local Union of CLUF represented 110 workers and did not hold most representative status. [Para 12] Of 12 demands, nine were conciliated and issues 2 and 3 were resolved at the hearing, leaving only the demand for a US$10 accommodation and transportation allowance. [Para 6][Para 8]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must provide an accommodation and transportation allowance of US$10 per month to each worker? [Para 1]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Workers live far away, in places such as Takeo and Kompong Speu, and must spend about US$12 a month on transport or US$7 on accommodation including electricity and water. [Para 16][Para 17]
**Employer (respondent):** It has no policy or agreement granting such an allowance, cannot afford it as a small subcontractor, and already rents cars for workers who work overtime after 10:00 p.m. under Prakas No. 80. [Para 2][Para 14][Para 15][Para 18]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel declined to consider the demand for a US$10 accommodation and transportation allowance. [Para 23]
The demand had no basis in an agreement, collective agreement or the Labour Law, so it was an interests dispute. [Para 19] For interests disputes the panel examines whether the union holds most representative status. [Para 20] It followed Arbitral Awards 81/04-Evergreen, 09/05-Kin Tai, 84/07-Yung Wah 2, 108/07-8 Star Sportswear, 135/07-Wilsons, 14/08-Quicksew, 101/08-GDM and 42/09-River Rich. [Para 21] Under Article 277, only an MRS union has legal capacity to negotiate a collective agreement and standing to bring an interests dispute; the Local Union of CLUF represented 110 of 430 workers and lacked MRS. [Para 21][Para 22] It could have standing only by joining other unions to represent more than 50% of the workforce. [Para 21]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that has no basis in law, an agreement or a collective agreement is an interests dispute, and an interests dispute may be brought only by a union with most representative status; a union representing 110 of 430 workers without MRS has no standing unless it joins other unions to represent more than half the workforce. [Para 19][Para 21][Para 22]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted the employer's practice of renting cars for overtime workers after 10:00 p.m. under Prakas No. 80, but treated it as irrelevant to the allowance claim. [Para 2]
