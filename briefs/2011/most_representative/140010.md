# Award No. 31/11 — Quint Major Industrial Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 5 April 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Kong Phallack, chair) · Doc ID 140010

## 1. Facts
Quint Major Industrial Co., Ltd. employs approximately 3,200 workers [Para 33]. The Local Union of KWPFU, which represents 309 workers and holds a registration certificate dated 24 November 2010 but no certificate of most representative status, brought eleven demands [Para 33][Para 34]. Six remained for decision after conciliation and the hearing resolved five: attendance-bonus deductions (issue 3), a US$ 10 transport or accommodation allowance (issue 4), fifteen minutes' early leave for pregnant workers (issue 6), a one-hour breastfeeding break until 24 months or a US$ 15 formula allowance (issue 7), a 1,500 riel daily meal allowance (issue 8), and skill bonuses (issue 9) [Para 23][Para 24].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must deduct attendance bonuses in proportion to authorised leave taken? [Para 35]
2. Whether the employer must pay a monthly US$ 10 transportation or accommodation allowance? [Para 38]
3. Whether the employer must let pregnant workers leave work 15 minutes early? [Para 39]
4. Whether the employer must give a one-hour daily breastfeeding break until the child is 24 months old or pay a US$ 15 monthly formula allowance? [Para 41]
5. Whether the employer must provide a 1,500 riel daily meal allowance? [Para 44]
6. Whether the employer must provide skill bonuses of US$ 3, US$ 6, US$ 10 and US$ 15 per month according to the number of machines handled? [Para 46]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the attendance bonus should be deducted only in proportion to authorised leave taken; the other demands rest on low wages, rising prices, health and safety, and the practice of other employers [Para 37][Para 40][Para 45][Para 49].
**Employer (respondent):** it already maintains the bonus for special and annual leave but deducts the full bonus for other authorised personal leave; it provides no transport, meal or skill allowance and says it complies with the Labour Law and Prakas [Para 37][Para 38][Para 48].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted in part — the employer must deduct the attendance bonus in proportion to authorised leave; the five remaining demands are declined as interests disputes [Para 73][Para 74][Para 75][Para 76][Para 77].

Issue 3 was treated as a rights dispute, because Notification No. 041/11 KB/SCN of 7 March 2011 requires at least US$ 7 per month for regular attendance [Para 54][Para 55]. Following Awards 57/07-Seratex, 106/07-M&V (Branch 3) and 128/08-Wei Hua, the panel held that a worker permitted to take authorised leave should not lose the whole bonus, and ordered proportional deduction [Para 56][Para 57].

Issues 4, 6, 7, 8 and 9 were held to have no basis in the Labour Law, regulations or any agreement, and so were interests disputes [Para 58][Para 62][Para 65][Para 67][Para 70]. Because the Local Union of KWPFU lacks a certificate of most representative status, it has no standing to bring an interests dispute, following Awards 48/09-Roo Hsing and 24/10-Reliable Source and Clause 43 of Prakas No. 99 [Para 60][Para 61]. On issue 7 the panel noted Article 184 gives a one-hour breastfeeding break for one year, so the demand beyond that period was an interests claim [Para 64][Para 65].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer must reduce the attendance bonus in proportion to the days of authorised leave taken, because a worker lawfully permitted to be absent should not forfeit the whole bonus [Para 56][Para 57]. A union without a certificate of most representative status has no legal standing to bring an interests dispute before the panel, because such an award would operate as a collective agreement for all workers [Para 60][Para 61].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the Independent Trade Union held the only MRS certificate at the enterprise and that there was no collective agreement, which explains why no other union could carry an interests claim [Para 34].
