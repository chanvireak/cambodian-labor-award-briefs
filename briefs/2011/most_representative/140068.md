# Award No. 108/11-GHG — GHG (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 3 October 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Eang Sothy, employer-appointed; Tun Si Phan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, president chosen by the two) · Doc ID 140068

## 1. Facts
GHG (Cambodia) is a garment manufacturer at Village Russey, Sangkat Stung Meanchey, Khan Meanchey, Phnom Penh, operating since 2003 with about 1,000 workers. The Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers' Democratic Union (C.CAWDU) filed complaint No. 087/11 S.B.K dated 22 July 2011, received by the labour-dispute department on 28 July 2011, seeking improved working conditions. Conciliation on 17 August 2011 failed on 3 of 5 demands and the non-conciliation report No. 946 Kb/Ak/Vk of 19 August 2011 reached the Council the same day. Hearings were held on 30 August and 21 September 2011, and both parties had signed a memorandum of understanding of 28 September 2010 on improving industrial relations in the garment industry. [Para 1][Para 14][Para 15][Para 16][Para 20]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must raise piece rates by USD 0.20 for workers in every section? [Para 56]
2. Whether the company must raise the meal allowance by 3,000 riel for overtime worked up to 8:00 pm? [Para 72]
3. Whether the company must raise the base wage of the 12 team assistants in the loading section to USD 70 per month? [Para 78]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Piece rates are set so low that an average-skill sewer cannot earn the USD 61 minimum wage; the company pays only 2,000 riel meal money for overtime from 4:00 to 6:00 pm and nothing for the further two hours up to 8:00 pm, although the law limits work to 10 hours a day; and the 12 team assistants, paid USD 61, carry heavy duties and should receive USD 70. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 73][Para 80][Para 81]
**Employer (respondent):** The company has no capacity to pay more. Piece rates follow the prices customers set, so difficult sections are priced a little higher; wages are paid according to each worker's skill and ability; about 20 percent of workers fall below the minimum even with overtime, and the company tops them up to USD 61; overtime is voluntary. [Para 61][Para 69][Para 76][Para 82]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel refused the USD 0.20 piece-rate increase but ordered the employer to have workers trial-sew before fixing rates, and declined to consider the meal-allowance and team-assistant demands. [Para 108][Para 130][Para 131][Para 133][Para 134]
Because the parties are signatories of the 28 September 2010 memorandum, the panel divided the dispute into rights disputes, which are binding, and interest disputes, which are not. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 24]
On issue 1, the panel held the demand to be a rights dispute because it concerns the minimum wage guaranteed by Article 108 of the Labour Law for work paid by output. [Para 83][Para 86][Para 87] Article 108 requires that a worker of average skill working normal hours receive at least the guaranteed minimum wage, and the panel followed its earlier awards holding that piece rates must be tested against that standard. [Para 91][Para 92][Para 93] The payroll records for May, June and July 2011 showed that most piece-rate sewers did not reach USD 61 even including overtime, and the employer had set rates unilaterally without any trial. [Para 96][Para 98][Para 99] The panel therefore refused to impose the USD 0.20 figure, since it could not know whether that figure would bring average workers to the minimum, and instead ordered a trial using slow, medium and fast workers so that rates can be set at or above the minimum. [Para 106][Para 107][Para 108][Para 101][Para 102]
On issue 2, the panel found the employer had complied with Ministry notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 by paying 2,000 riel for voluntary overtime, and treated the further 3,000 riel as a claim for a benefit above what the law requires, that is an interest dispute. [Para 109][Para 112][Para 113][Para 115] On issue 3, no law or collective agreement provides a USD 70 base wage for team assistants, so that demand was also an interest dispute. [Para 125][Para 126] For both, the panel could act only if the union raising them was the most representative in the factory; C.CAWDU was not, so it declined to consider them. [Para 116][Para 118][Para 119][Para 127][Para 129]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand to raise piece rates is a rights dispute, and so within the panel's binding jurisdiction, where it turns on whether a worker of average skill can earn the statutory minimum wage; the panel will not itself fix a figure it cannot verify, but orders the employer to trial-sew with slow, medium and fast workers before setting rates that guarantee the minimum. A demand for a benefit above what the law or a collective agreement requires is an interest dispute, which the panel will not consider unless the union bringing it is the most representative union in the factory. [Para 86][Para 108][Para 116][Para 118]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that piece-rate pay is meant to reward effort with more than the minimum wage, and that where a worker who tries hard still earns less, the rate itself must be reconsidered. [Para 103][Para 104] It also noted that the union claimed about 550 members but had only about 276 after membership-fee deductions. [Para 53]
