# Award No. 136/11 — Cambo Handsome Ltd (Branch 1) (Arbitration Council, 15 November 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (chair Kong Phallab; Ing Sothy for the employer, An Non for the workers; Ing Sothy dissenting) · Doc ID 140086

## 1. Facts
Cambo Handsome Ltd (Branch 1) employs about 2,000 workers; the claimant Friendly Workers' Union of Cambo Handsome Co., Ltd 1 has 117 members, of whom 105 bring this case. [Para 52][Para 54] After conciliation settled two of five demands, three remained: a USD 1 daily attendance bonus for days of lawful leave, the union-dues deduction arrangement, and a USD 15 monthly housing or transport allowance. [Para 19][Para 22][Para 56][Para 67][Para 70] The company pays a USD 10 monthly attendance bonus and deducts USD 3, USD 2 and USD 5 for the first, second and third day of absence. [Para 3][Para 4][Para 5][Para 6]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the attendance bonus pro rata for days of leave lawfully authorised by the employer, rather than deducting for those days? [Para 56][Para 75]
2. Whether the employer must stop deducting union dues from members who resigned from the old union and start deducting dues for the new union? [Para 67]
3. Whether the employer must pay a USD 15 monthly housing or transport allowance? [Para 70][Para 96]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** A worker who has worked a full month should not lose the whole attendance bonus for taking leave with the employer's permission; the bonus is an incentive for effort, and housing rents have risen from USD 25 to USD 35 a month while some factories give extra benefits. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 71]
**Employer (respondent):** The company asks to keep its existing practice because it already provides benefits better than the law requires; it cannot pay USD 15 a month for housing or transport, already paying USD 3, and 95% of workers live near the factory. [Para 10][Para 64][Para 72][Para 73]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to deduct the attendance bonus pro rata to the number of days of leave lawfully authorised by the employer [Para 95][Para 107]; it declined to consider the union-dues issue because the parties agreed to apply Article 129 of the Labour Law [Para 67][Para 69]; and it refused to consider the USD 15 housing or transport claim [Para 106][Para 109].

On issue 1 the panel classified the demand as a rights dispute because the attendance bonus is governed by Ministry Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011, which guarantees at least USD 7 a month to workers who attend regularly. [Para 75][Para 77] It found the notification unclear on absence, as was Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 before it, and followed its earlier awards holding that a worker should not lose the bonus entirely where absence was authorised. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 81] It also adopted the reasoning of Award No. 112/11 (Yong Va 1) that "regular working days" means the days the employer required work, so that lawful and employer-authorised leave days are not counted against the worker. [Para 87][Para 88][Para 93][Para 94]

On issue 3 the panel held the claim was an interest dispute, not a rights dispute, because no law, agreement or collective agreement provides for a USD 15 allowance. [Para 96] It then held that only a union with most-representative status may bring interest disputes, and that with 117 members out of 2,000 workers this union lacks that status, so it declined to consider the claim. [Para 98][Para 102][Para 104][Para 105][Para 106]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a collective agreement or ministerial notification is silent on authorised absence, an employer may not deduct the whole monthly attendance bonus for days of leave it lawfully authorised; the deduction must be proportionate to the days absent. [Para 80][Para 95]

**Obiter dicta:** A union that does not hold most-representative status in the enterprise cannot bring an interest dispute to the panel, even where the individual workers would have standing on a rights claim. [Para 98][Para 102]
