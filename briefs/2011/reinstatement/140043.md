# Award No. 78/11-Mfone — Mfone Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 21 July 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Arbitrators: Chhiv Phyrum (employer), An Nan (workers), Ang Eng Thong (chair) · Doc ID 140043

## 1. Facts
Mfone Co., Ltd., a phone service provider operating since 1995 with 585 workers and no union, dismissed four workers—Deng Samnang, Kim Rim, Pov Kosal, and Mao Sam—on 29 December 2010. The workers had taken 30 litres of gasoline on 8 December 2010 to power generators; 15 litres were consumed, 5 remained in the generator, and the four workers each poured 2.5 litres of the remaining 10 litres into their motorbikes without informing the employer. On 17 December 2010, when requesting more gasoline, they admitted the personal use; the supervisor instructed them to return 20 litres. After apologising on 19 December 2010 and the Head of Administration declining the apology, the Head of Human Resources informed them on 28 December 2010 that the supervisor refused to allow them to return to work because they had committed serious misconduct under Article 83. The employer dismissed them on 29 December 2010 and paid outstanding wages and payment in lieu of annual leave. The workers withdrew demands for Deng Samnang and Pov Kosal after accepting settlement packages, leaving Kim Rim and Mao Sam, who sought reinstatement or termination payments. [Para 1][Para 15][Para 17][Para 18][Para 19][Para 20][Para 23][Para 25][Para 31]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer should reinstate the four workers and provide back pay from the date of dismissal to the date of reinstatement? [Para 2]
2. Whether the employer dismissed the workers all at the same time without informing either them or the Ministry of Labour and Vocational Training? [Para 3]
3. Whether the dispute is collective under Article 302 of the Labour Law, and if not, whether the Arbitration Council has jurisdiction to consider the demand? [Para 3][Para 35]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The dispute is collective because it has been subject to conciliation at the Ministry of Labour and Vocational Training, 23 workers endorse the union in this case, and Kim Rim and Mao Sam are founders of the Local Union of CLC. The workers acknowledged having been at fault but returned double the amount of gasoline used to their supervisor, who agreed to settle the issue; therefore the employer should not have dismissed them. [Para 28][Para 30][Para 31]
**Employer (respondent):** The dispute is an individual dispute because the three conditions set out in Article 302 of the Labour Law have not been satisfied. Although the first condition is satisfied, the second condition relates to working conditions (wages and issues relating to professional organisations), which are irrelevant to the demand, and the third condition is not fulfilled because the dispute does not affect the employer's operation, which continues as usual, or social peace. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 28]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Dismissed — the panel declined to consider the workers' demand that the employer reinstate Kim Rim and Mao Sam and provide them with back pay from the date of dismissal to the date of reinstatement. [Para 47]
The panel first considered the merits and noted that Kim Rim and Mao Sam did not commit other misconduct apart from taking the employer's gasoline for personal use, and that the issue had already been settled between the workers and their supervisor. [Para 32] It found that the employer was not entitled to dismiss them on that basis, citing Article 28 (no double sanctions for the same misconduct) and Article 27 (disciplinary sanction must be proportional to the seriousness of the misconduct). [Para 33] The act of taking 2.5 litres each of the employer's gasoline did not amount to conduct justifying dismissal, and the workers had returned double the amount used without authorisation. [Para 33][Para 34] The panel considered that there was no valid reason for the dismissal and that the employer must pay a legal termination payment to Kim Rim and Mao Sam. [Para 34]
However, the employer argued that the dispute was not collective and therefore the Council did not have jurisdiction. [Para 34] The panel examined Article 302, which defines a collective labour dispute as any dispute between one or more employers and a certain number of their staff over working conditions, the exercise of the recognised rights of professional organisations, the recognition of professional organisations within the enterprise, and issues regarding relations between employers and workers, and this dispute could jeopardise the effective operation of the enterprise or social peace. [Para 40] The panel identified three conditions: (1) a dispute between one or more employers and a number of their workers; (2) the subject relates to working conditions, professional organisations, or relations between employers and workers; and (3) the dispute could jeopardise the effective operation of the enterprise or social peace. [Para 41]
The panel found the first condition met because the dispute involved one employer and its workers. [Para 42] It found the second condition met because the dismissal of Kim Rim and Mao Sam was an issue regarding relations between the employer and workers. [Para 43] On the third condition, the panel noted that the employer had dismissed the workers on 29 December 2010 and paid their outstanding wages and payment in lieu of annual leave; more than six months had passed since the dismissal and the workers had not done anything to disturb the employer's operation, nor was there any sign the dispute could jeopardise its operation in the future, although the Local Union of CLC and some other workers supported Kim Rim and Mao Sam. [Para 44] The panel also found that the dismissal was not the result of union discrimination because the workers were dismissed before the local union was established. [Para 45] Hence the third condition was not met. [Para 46] The panel concluded that the dispute was an individual dispute and declined to consider the workers' demand. [Para 47]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The rule the panel applied is that for a dispute to be collective under Article 302 of the Labour Law, all three conditions must be satisfied, including that the dispute could jeopardise the effective operation of the enterprise or social peace. A dismissal that does not affect the employer's operation or social peace—where more than six months have passed without disturbance and the dismissal occurred before the union was established—does not satisfy the third condition, even if workers claim union support. Consequently, the Arbitration Council lacks jurisdiction to consider such an individual dispute. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 46][Para 47]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observations on the merits—that the workers had returned double the gasoline, that the supervisor had agreed to settle the issue, and that the misconduct did not justify dismissal under Articles 27 and 28—were not necessary to the outcome because the panel ultimately declined jurisdiction on the ground that the dispute was individual. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 34]
