# Award No. 25/11 — MPA International Ltd (Arbitration Council, 18 March 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun for the employer, An Nan for the workers, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 140004

## 1. Facts
MPA International Ltd employs 2,050 workers in Phnom Penh. Three former security guards — Mr H, Mr M and Mr C — brought eight demands; three were conciliated and two more were withdrawn at the hearing, leaving issues 1 and 4. No local union or federation was involved: the three guards made the demand directly. The employer dismissed Mr H for absence from 15 August to 31 August 2010, which it treated as abandonment of work under clause 6, point 3 of its Internal Work Rules, and it agreed to reinstate the other two, who then refused reinstatement. [Para 18][Para 30][Para 31][Para 36]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must introduce an alternating day and night shift rotation at 15-day intervals and pay wages at the night rate in accordance with the Labour Law? [Para 18][Para 57]
2. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr H, Mr M and Mr C and pay them back pay from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 29][Para 45]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** night shifts damage health and leave them exhausted by dawn, so a 15-day rotation would give them time to rest; the demand is collective because it concerns collective benefits and other workers feared to complain; Mr H was replaced at his post without notice and his letter to the Ministry of Labour sought reinstatement. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 26][Para 34][Para 40][Para 43]
**Employer (respondent):** shift rotation is not in its policy; contracts fix eight-hour days and let workers choose their shift; Mr H abandoned his post after being telephoned on 14 August 2010 and refusing to return, and the dispute over him is individual rather than collective; night and day guards are deliberately paid the same. [Para 26][Para 27][Para 36][Para 37][Para 38][Para 47][Para 23]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel declined to consider Mr H's demands for want of jurisdiction, rejected Mr M's rotation demand, and ordered the employer to pay Mr M wages at 130% of day-shift wages, that is 130% of US$ 150, if he agrees to be reinstated; the award is non-binding and becomes binding eight days after notification absent written opposition. [Para 56][Para 69][Para 72][Para 74][Para 75]
The panel took issue 4 first because it governed the employment relationship. Applying Article 302 of the Labour Law and the three conditions stated in Award 10/03-Jacqsintex — a dispute between a number of workers and one or more employers, over working conditions or organisational rights or employer-worker relations, capable of jeopardising the effective operation of the enterprise or social peace — it found the first condition unmet because only one worker was concerned and the third unmet because termination of an individual contract neither affects other workers' benefits nor threatens the enterprise. The dispute was therefore not collective, and under Article 309 of the Labour Law and clause 32 of Prakas No. 099 the panel has no jurisdiction over non-collective disputes, so it declined to consider Mr H's reinstatement. [Para 46][Para 48][Para 50][Para 52][Para 53][Para 54][Para 55][Para 56]
On issue 1 the same reasoning removed Mr H, leaving Mr M, whose reinstatement the employer had accepted. On the rotation, the panel treated the employment contract as a legally binding agreement under Article 65 of the Labour Law and Decree 38 on Contracts and Other Liabilities of 28 October 1988, so that amendments require the consent of both parties; Mr M had known when contracting that he would work nights, and a worker who wants a different shift must seek the employer's agreement. The rotation demand was rejected. [Para 60][Para 64][Para 65][Para 67][Para 68][Para 69]
On the night rate, Article 144 of the Labour Law as amended in 2007 requires night work to be paid at 130% of the daytime wage, following Award 119/07-Royal Crowntex; the panel found the employer paid night guards the same as day guards and ordered the 130% rate for Mr M. [Para 70][Para 71][Para 72]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A dispute is collective, and so within the panel's jurisdiction, only where it arises between a number of workers and one or more employers, concerns working conditions or organisational or employment relations, and could jeopardise the effective operation of the enterprise or social peace; a single worker's reinstatement claim fails the first and third limbs and must be declined for want of jurisdiction. [Para 50][Para 52][Para 55]

**Obiter dicta:** a contract validly concluded fixes the shift a worker may be required to work, and a change of shift requires the employer's consent; the employer bears the burden of proving that a dispute is not collective. [Para 47][Para 68]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The English translation systematically drops "t" and "h" ("themployer", "thearing", "thiss ue"), and several sentences are run together, so wording was reconstructed from context. The hearing date is printed twice, the second time with a stray bracket. The document lists five non-conciliated issues but the panel states that issues 2, 3 and 5 were withdrawn, leaving only issues 1 and 4, whose numbering in the decision section is duplicated. The decision heading for issue 1 in fact carries three separate orders. Exhibit lists are truncated with bare "[" markers, and the award is headed 18 March 2011 while the hearing was held on 24 February 2011. Individual names, including a third party identified only by name and ID No. 340, are redacted here.
