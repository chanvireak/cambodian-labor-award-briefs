# Award No. 119/11 — Van Sing Handicraft Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 5 October 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambor Na, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Aing Aeng Thong, president) · Doc ID 140079

## 1. Facts
The Cambodia Democratic Workers' Union Federation (CCAWDU) at Van Sing Handicraft Co., Ltd., a sweater-knitting enterprise employing about 485 workers, brought nine claims after a strike on 6 September 2011; conciliation settled five and left four [Para 16][Para 17][Para 35][Para 36][Para 38]. The employer did not attend the 14 September 2011 hearing, citing other commitments, and asked the panel to decide in its absence [Para 20]. The remaining issues concerned itemised pay slips and contract length, weekly meal allowance, wages during work suspension, and seniority on renewal [Para 39][Para 48][Para 53][Para 57].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must issue itemised pay slips under the agreement of 14 July 2011, and conclude fixed-term contracts of 6 months to 1 year? [Para 39]
2. Whether the employer must pay the overtime meal allowance weekly, every Saturday? [Para 48]
3. Whether the employer must pay 100 per cent of wages and benefits where it suspended work without the labour inspector's authorisation? [Para 53][Para 128]
4. Whether the employer must preserve workers' seniority when renewing fixed-term contracts? [Para 57][Para 139]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** pay slips are needed to verify wages that vary month to month; short fixed-term contracts are used to erode seniority; meal money must come weekly so workers can buy food when working overtime; suspension without authorisation requires full pay; and seniority should survive short breaks and ID changes [Para 42][Para 47][Para 50][Para 56][Para 68][Para 70].
**Employer (respondent):** it asked to delay implementing the 14 July 2011 agreement until the September 2011 payroll and did not agree to 6-month or 1-year contracts; it also denied discrimination and attributed the work stoppage to a lack of orders [Para 3][Para 4][Para 43][Para 52][Para 105].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — monthly itemised pay slips, weekly overtime meal allowance, and 100 per cent wages for unauthorised suspension were ordered; the claim for 6-month to 1-year contracts was dismissed; seniority was preserved only where renewal was continuous [Para 151][Para 152][Para 153][Para 154][Para 155][Para 156]. The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent objection [Para 157].

On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 112 of the Labour Law, under which the employer must inform workers clearly and understandably of wage conditions before engagement and whenever they change [Para 79][Para 80][Para 81]. Because wages vary with overtime, attendance bonuses, seniority pay and holiday work, itemised slips are the only way workers can check the hours they worked [Para 84][Para 85][Para 86]. The panel ordered monthly pay slips from the date the award takes effect [Para 87][Para 151]. On contract length, Articles 65 of the Labour Law and 22 of the Civil Code make a contract the law of the parties, alterable only by consent [Para 88][Para 91][Para 95]. Duration is the employer's prerogative and a union cannot compel a longer term [Para 98][Para 99][Para 100]. The union showed no anti-union discrimination: about 70 per cent of the workforce held 1–2 month contracts and 30 per cent 2–3 month contracts, applied generally rather than to union members [Para 104][Para 105]. That claim was dismissed [Para 109].

On Issue 2 the panel relied on point 2 of Prakas No. 041 of 7 March 2011, which entitles a worker doing voluntary overtime to a 2,000 riel daily meal allowance or a free meal [Para 111][Para 113]. The employer paid monthly; the workers wanted weekly payment [Para 114][Para 115]. Following earlier awards holding the meal allowance separate from overtime pay, the panel ordered payment every Saturday to workers who volunteer for overtime [Para 117][Para 119][Para 127][Para 153].

On Issue 3 the panel applied Article 71 of the Labour Law, which permits suspension of enterprise activity for serious economic difficulty for no more than two months, under labour inspector supervision [Para 129][Para 130]. Where suspension is carried out without notification and approval, the employer must pay 100 per cent of wages and benefits even with no work available [Para 136][Para 138]. The employer had suspended work from 16 to 30 August 2011 without authorisation, so the order followed [Para 137][Para 154].

On Issue 4 the panel defined seniority (អតីតភាព) as the period of continuous service conferring additional rights, ending when the worker leaves the enterprise [Para 142][Para 143]. Where a contract is renewed without a break, seniority continues [Para 145][Para 149]. Where the contract was terminated, compensation paid and the worker stayed away for one or two weeks, seniority ended [Para 146][Para 147]. The order therefore preserves seniority only for continuous renewals and rejects it for broken service [Para 155][Para 156].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Wages that vary with overtime, bonuses and seniority require itemised monthly pay slips under Article 112 of the Labour Law so that workers can verify their pay [Para 79][Para 86]. The overtime meal allowance under Prakas No. 041 (2,000 riel per day or a free meal) is separate from overtime pay and may be ordered paid weekly [Para 111][Para 117][Para 119]. An employer that suspends work without prior labour inspector notification and authorisation must pay 100 per cent of wages and benefits [Para 136]. Seniority survives renewal of a fixed-term contract where service continues without a real break, but not where the contract was terminated, compensation paid and the worker stayed away [Para 145][Para 146].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel accepted that it may proceed in the employer's absence where the employer was duly summoned and asked for an in-absentia hearing [Para 20][Para 78]; and it observed that the duration of a fixed-term contract is the employer's prerogative, since a contract requires mutual consent [Para 98][Para 99].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award date is 5 October 2011 in the header while the hearing was 14 September 2011; no contradiction, but the header uses "រួមប្រឹក្សា" for "ក្រុមប្រឹក្សា". Line 7 prints "ប្រាក់ ឈ្នួល ៤០ ១០០ ភាគរយ" (40/100 per cent) where the claim is plainly 100 per cent [Para 7]. The employer's name appears as "វ៉ាន ស៊ិង", "វ៉ាន ស៊ីង" and "វៃ ស៊ីន ហ្វេក ធើរី" [Para 22][Para 35][Para 36]. The tables of workers at line 72 are largely unreadable OCR. Lines 73 and 135–136 repeat garbled clauses, and line 159 shows signature digits instead of names.
