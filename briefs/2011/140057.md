# Award No. 95/11 — Sany Ning (Blou Ming) Tech Sty Khob Peresion (Arbitration Council, 30 August 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Yu Soun Ti (employer-appointed), Liv Sovannara (worker-appointed), Pen Bun Chhea (chair, chosen by the two) · Doc ID 140057

## 1. Facts
Sany Ning (Blou Ming) Tech Sty Khob Peresion, a garment factory at Trapeang Thleung, Chom Chao, Dangkao, Phnom Penh, began operating in 2011 with 330 workers [Para 2][Para 62]. The Khmer Youth Union at the factory was registered on 6 June 2011 [Para 63]. Workers worked Monday to Saturday, 7:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., eight hours a day, Sunday being the weekly rest day [Para 64]. When production required Sunday work the company took volunteers and paid 200 per cent wages, but gave no meal or meal allowance [Para 65][Para 66].

## 2. Issues in dispute
The conciliation report listed 15 demands [Para 3]. Thirteen were settled in conciliation and demand 6 (canteen and tables) was settled at the hearing, leaving one live demand [Para 29][Para 30][Para 32]:
1. Whether the employer must provide one free meal or 3,000 riel in meal allowance to workers who voluntarily work on Sunday? [Para 87]
2. Whether that demand is a dispute over rights (វិវាទអំពីសិទ្ធិ) or over interests (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍)? [Para 88]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Sunday work is overtime, so a meal or meal allowance is owed; food prices rose and a packet of rice went from 2,500 to 3,000 riel, and they gave up their rest day without compensation in kind [Para 67][Para 68][Para 69]. They also refused to recognise the administrative director as the company's representative, because the tax registration named Chung Yang Yu as manager, not Liang Yan Ke [Para 76][Para 77].
**Employer (respondent):** it accepted Sunday work as overtime and had already paid 200 per cent wages, so it could not meet the demand; it would comply if the law required it [Para 71][Para 72][Para 73]. It relied on a letter of authority from company president Liang Yan Ke, on company letterhead and seal, describing him as acting director [Para 80].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay 2,000 (two thousand) riel per day or provide one free meal to workers who voluntarily work on Sunday [Para 104][Para 119]; it dismissed the demand for 3,000 riel [Para 118][Para 121].

Representation: under Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY of 21 April 2004 a party may be represented by a person holding written authority [Para 78]. The panel held the letterhead, seal and "acting director" wording sufficed, and that another manager may take over temporarily when the principal is abroad, so the administrative director validly represented the company [Para 81][Para 85][Para 86].

Characterisation: following its earlier awards, the panel held the meal or meal-allowance claim tied to Notification No. 041/11 to be a rights dispute, while the 3,000 riel figure exceeded the notified benefit and was therefore an interest dispute [Para 89][Para 90].

Rights: Article 137 of the Labour Law caps ordinary hours at eight a day and 48 a week [Para 92][Para 96]; the company's Monday-to-Saturday eight-hour week equals 48 hours and Sunday is not an ordinary working day [Para 97], so voluntary Sunday work is overtime [Para 98][Para 99]. Point 2 of Notification No. 041 SKBY of 7 March 2011 entitles a worker who works overtime at the employer's request to 2,000 (two thousand) riel per day or one free meal, without any threshold of hours [Para 100][Para 101]. The employer paid no such allowance [Para 102], so it must pay 2,000 riel per day or provide the meal [Para 103][Para 104].

Interest: the panel could settle an interest dispute only if the claiming union was the most representative in the enterprise [Para 106][Para 108]. The Khmer Youth Union at the factory held no certificate of most-representative status [Para 111][Para 113], so the 3,000 riel demand was dismissed without consideration [Para 118]. An award on it would impose a collective agreement on all workers and strip non-members of the right to strike over future interest claims [Para 115][Para 116].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a notification or collective agreement fixes a meal or meal allowance for overtime, a demand for exactly that benefit is a rights dispute the panel decides on the law, but a demand above the fixed amount is an interest dispute that the panel may decide only if the claimant union holds most-representative status; without that certificate the excess is dismissed [Para 89][Para 90][Para 106][Para 113].

**Obiter dicta:** a company official may represent the employer where the letter of authority bears the company's letterhead and seal and describes the signatory as acting director, even if the tax registration names someone else [Para 81][Para 85]; and an award on an interest claim would extend a collective agreement to all workers and remove non-members' right to strike [Para 115][Para 116].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The demand list contains 15 items [Para 3] but the reasons speak of "all 11 issues" [Para 87]; the conciliation record says 13 of 15 were settled and only 6 and 11 remained [Para 29], and 6 was then settled by agreement [Para 30][Para 32]. The 3,000 riel figure is printed as "១ ៣០ រៀល" in the reasoning [Para 91], and the operative order prints a stray "៤" after "២០០០ (ពីរ ពាន់) រៀល" [Para 104]. The company appears as "ប្ល៊ូ មីង" (Blou Ming) and "សាញ នីង"/"សាញ នឹង" (Sany Ning) in the same document [Para 1][Para 2], while the document title records "Shining (Blooming Textile)". Arbitrator signatures are partly lost to OCR [Para 123].
