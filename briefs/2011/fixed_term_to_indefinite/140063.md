# Award No. 103/11 — Huey Chuen (Cambodia) Corp. Ltd (Arbitration Council, 15 September 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer, An Nan for the workers, Kong Phallack presiding) · Doc ID 140063

## 1. Facts

Huey Chuen (Cambodia) Corp. Ltd runs a Phnom Penh head factory (the "six-storey factory") and a branch at Lot J1649, National Road 2, Tram village, Roka Krau commune, Daun Keo district, Takeo. The company is registered with the Ministry of Commerce under certificate No. 333 dated 24 January 2006, but the Takeo branch holds only a branch-opening certificate from the Ministry of Industry, Mines and Energy and has no separate commercial registration. Both sites answer to one principal, Mr W, who pays the Phnom Penh manager and the Takeo manager alike. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 96][Para 98][Para 109]

The claimant federation C.CAWDU brought the case for four workers recruited by the Takeo branch but working from recruitment in the Phnom Penh warehouse, packing uppers for despatch to Takeo and receiving sewn shoes back for sole attachment. The workers say the Takeo supervisor hired them and controlled their wages and leave, outside the Phnom Penh administration. [Para 1][Para 99][Para 104][Para 106]

On 30 June 2011 the company gave six employees notice to move from the Phnom Penh factory to the Takeo shoe factory with effect from 1 July 2011, relying on Article 188 of the Labour Law and offering USD 15 per person per month as travel or housing cost. The four workers refused to go. [Para 110][Para 113][Para 115][Para 116]

A fifth worker, Ms K, was hired at Phnom Penh on 6 October 2006 on an indefinite contract and was dismissed before the conciliation of 29 July 2011. Her name does not appear in the non-conciliation record, although she signed that record as a workers' representative. [Para 117][Para 120][Para 121]

Conciliation failed on all four demands on 29 July 2011, and non-conciliation report No. 893 Kb/Ak/Vk dated 12 August 2011 reached the Council the same day. Hearings were held on 22 and 25 August 2011. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 16]

At the hearing the parties settled points 3 and 4: the company would pay 50% of wages when there was no work and one day's notice was given, and would not deduct the attendance bonus where workers arrived late as a group because of a flat tyre or traffic jam, while individual lateness of up to 20 minutes a month would still be deducted. Points 1 and 2 were merged as related. [Para 19][Para 20][Para 22][Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. Whether the demand that the four transferred workers be returned to their original Phnom Penh workplace with back wages is a rights dispute, and whether the transfer was lawful and reasonable? [Para 124][Para 142]
2. Whether the panel may consider Ms K's dismissal although she is not named in the non-conciliation report? [Para 159][Para 166]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** The transfer was a disguised response to union activity, and the USD 15 monthly housing allowance did not make relocation reasonable. Ms K's dismissal was a direct consequence of the dispute and should be examined. [Para 116][Para 129][Para 166]

**Employer (respondent):** The four were hired by the Takeo branch and only happened to work at the Phnom Penh warehouse, so the move was a return to their contractual workplace. Phnom Penh and Takeo were separate companies with separate branch certificates and patent tax, and the Phnom Penh company could not take back Takeo staff. [Para 107][Para 109][Para 130][Para 141]

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to take back the four workers and pay their back wages; Ms K's claim for reinstatement and back wages was refused. [Para 158][Para 169][Para 170]

The panel held the reinstatement demand to be a rights dispute, because the right to return to the original workplace is conferred by the Labour Law, and noted that rights disputes bind the parties through the memorandum of understanding of 28 September 2010. [Para 124][Para 126][Para 127]

On the employer's identity, the panel found that the Takeo branch had no separate commercial registration, that both entities were under Mr W, and that the two managers were paid by the same principal. It therefore treated the Phnom Penh company and its Takeo branch as one company and one employer. [Para 135][Para 136][Para 138][Para 140]

On the transfer, the panel applied the four conditions developed in earlier awards — no wage cut, no transfer to a distant place, no shift reversal between day and night, and no change unrelated to the worker's skill — and found the move to Takeo failed them. The USD 15 monthly allowance was not shown to let the workers live comparably to Phnom Penh, since food, transport and daily living costs would all rise. The transfer notice itself showed that the Phnom Penh factory had reorganised and brought in new workers to do the jobs the Takeo workers had done, so the transfer was not caused by a lack of work. [Para 146][Para 147][Para 149][Para 152][Para 155][Para 156][Para 157]

On Ms K, Article 312 of the Labour Law limits the panel to the issues in the non-conciliation record or issues arising after it as a direct consequence of the dispute. Her name was absent from the record and her dismissal predated the conciliation, so the panel declined jurisdiction over her claim. [Para 159][Para 160][Para 165][Para 167][Para 168]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

A branch that holds no separate commercial registration and whose operations answer to the same principal is not a separate employer; the parent company is the employer for reinstatement purposes. An employer's power to transfer staff is lawful only where the transfer satisfies four conditions — no wage cut, no distant relocation, no reversal between day and night shifts, and no change outside the worker's skill — and a flat monthly housing allowance that does not permit living comparably at the new site does not cure a distant transfer. [Para 147][Para 152][Para 157]

The panel will not examine a dismissal that is omitted from the non-conciliation record where the dismissal occurred before conciliation and the dispute therefore predates it. [Para 159][Para 167]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel recorded the workers' explanation that the conciliator forgot to write Ms K's name into the record and that she did not check it, but did not treat that as sufficient to bring her within the record. [Para 121][Para 165]
