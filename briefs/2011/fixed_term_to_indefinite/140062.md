# Award No. 98/11 — First Gawon Apparel (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 7 September 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom, Tuon Siphan, Pen Bun Chhea) · Doc ID 140062

## 1. Facts
First Gawon Apparel (Cambodia) began operating on 26 January 2011 and employed workers in garment cutting and sewing. The Solidarity Union of the Hse Gawon Garment Factory was the claimant, and Mr Ly Sik Karyea was its elected secretary, dismissed on 7 April 2011. Conciliation on 7 July 2011 settled 6 of 17 demands; 3 issues remained and were heard on 15 August 2011. [Para 1][Para 34][Para 21][Para 46]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must stop warning workers with long fingernails and deducting their wages, and stop forcing workers to cut their nails at the workplace? [Para 36]
2. Whether the company must reinstate Mr Ly Sik Karyea, the union secretary, because his dismissal was procedurally unlawful? [Para 46]
3. Whether the company must stop forcing machine operators to lift cartons without their consent? [Para 60]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Women wanted to keep their nails at a moderate length for appearance and asked only for a reasonable allowance; the company had warned of deductions but never actually made them. Mr Ly Sik Karyea was a union secretary and the dismissal was unlawful. Machine operators were being made to lift cartons outside their contracts and were exhausted. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 46][Para 51][Para 65][Para 66]

**Employer (respondent):** Long nails catch garments and pull threads, making the product unsaleable and forcing whole cartons to be reopened, at a cost in time and profit; the company announced the rule orally from the start. Mr Ly Sik Karyea was a probationary worker whose contract was simply not renewed. Machine operators were asked to help only once or twice a month and were paid overtime for work outside hours. [Para 37][Para 41][Para 42][Para 45][Para 57][Para 62][Para 64]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to reinstate Mr Ly Sik Karyea and to stop forcing machine operators to lift cartons without consent, but upheld the nail-length rule and directed the workers to keep their nails short. [Para 92][Para 145][Para 147][Para 148]

On issue 1, the panel applied Article 2 of the Labour Law and its own earlier awards to hold that an employer's right to direct and manage the enterprise is lawful so long as it is exercised lawfully and reasonably. The nail rule was announced orally from the company's opening, served hygiene and product quality, and was reasonable. The panel therefore upheld it and ordered workers to keep their nails short enough not to snag garments. It added two qualifications: the employer should issue the rule in writing and specify the permitted length, and it must not impose fines. Article 28 of the Labour Law forbids disciplinary deductions from wages, and Article 27 requires the penalty to be proportionate to the fault. [Para 72][Para 80][Para 82][Para 84][Para 87][Para 90][Para 92]

On issue 6, the panel examined whether Mr Ly Sik Karyea qualified for the protection against dismissal given to union officials under Prakas 305. Two of the three conditions were met: he was a worker who volunteered for membership during the registration period, and the dismissal on 7 April 2011 fell one day before the union received its registration certificate on 8 April 2011. The third condition failed: the union could not prove that it had notified the employer of its leadership composition in a reliable manner, because there was no signature of receipt. The panel nevertheless held that the dismissal was unlawful on contract grounds. The employer produced no probationary contract with fixed start and end dates, and Article 67(7) treats a fixed-duration contract not reduced to writing as an indefinite contract. The panel therefore presumed an indefinite contract and found the dismissal contrary to Article 74, which permits termination only for a valid reason relating to the worker's capacity or conduct or the needs of the enterprise. It ordered reinstatement under Prakas 099. [Para 95][Para 96][Para 97][Para 108][Para 110][Para 113][Para 118][Para 120][Para 123][Para 126][Para 128]

On issue 7, the panel applied Article 65 and Article 22 of Decree-Law 38 on employment contracts: a contract is the law of the parties and may be changed only by agreement. The machine operators' contracts did not require lifting cartons, and the workers never consented to that added task. The panel ordered the employer to stop forcing machine operators to lift cartons without their consent. [Para 132][Para 135][Para 139][Para 140][Para 143][Para 145]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may enforce reasonable grooming rules under its power to direct and manage the enterprise, but may not back them with wage deductions, because disciplinary fines are prohibited and penalties must be proportionate to the fault. A fixed-duration contract that is not reduced to writing is treated as an indefinite contract, so a dismissal under it needs a valid reason under Article 74. An employer may not add tasks not covered by the employment contract without the worker's consent. [Para 82][Para 87][Para 118][Para 144]

**Obiter dicta:** The employer ought to publish the nail rule in writing and state the permitted length, over and above its oral announcement. The union's failure to prove notification of its leadership cost the worker the special protection of Prakas 305, even though the contract ground sufficed. [Para 84][Para 85][Para 110]
