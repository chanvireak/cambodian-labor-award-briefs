# Award No. 143/10 — GHG (Cambodia) Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 28 January 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mam Samborna, Sin Kim Sean, Pen Bun Chha presiding) · Doc ID 140349

## 1. Facts
The respondent is ក្រុមហ៊ុន ជី អេច ជី (ខេមបូឌា) — GHG (Cambodia) Co Ltd — a garment factory at Boeung Ssii, Stung Mean Chey, Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, operating since 2004 with about 1,100 workers. The claimant is the សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា (Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers Democratic Union, C.CAWDU) factory branch, registered on 5 October 2009, which says it has about 200 members and submitted 160 names for dues deduction while the employer acknowledges only 130. Of seven demands, two were settled at the hearing and three remained: the attendance bonus, a single worker's lost bonus, and seniority. [Para 40][Para 43][Para 44][Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay the USD 5 attendance bonus in proportion to days taken as leave for personal reasons, whether or not the leave was authorised? [Para 45][Para 81]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must restore the USD 5 attendance bonus withheld from one worker for November 2010 after he arrived more than an hour late because of a traffic jam? [Para 54][Para 108]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must maintain each worker's seniority from the first day of hiring, and pay the seniority bonus on that basis, despite successive six-month fixed-term contracts? [Para 68][Para 125]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, C.CAWDU):** the USD 5 bonus is part of wages, so an absence of a day or two should reduce it proportionately rather than wipe it out; the worker penalised for November 2010 was caught in a traffic jam, not absent by choice, and it happened only once; and seniority should run from first hire, because workers have served for years under rolling six-month contracts and some have never received the seniority bonus at all. [Para 47][Para 65][Para 70]
**Employer (respondent):** the bonus rewards a full month of regular attendance of 26 days, so any absence, authorised or not, forfeits it, in line with Notification No. 017; the late worker exceeded the half-hour tolerance the company already grants and did not seek permission; and each six-month contract was closed with a 5% end-of-contract payment and paid annual leave before renewal, so seniority does not carry over — if it must, the workers should repay what they received. [Para 48][Para 60][Para 71][Para 73]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must reduce the USD 5 attendance bonus pro rata for days of authorised absence and must maintain seniority from first hire and pay the seniority bonus under Notification No. 017 to the 264 workers still employed, while the claim for pro-rata treatment of unauthorised absence and the single worker's bonus was rejected. [Para 170][Para 171][Para 172][Para 173]

On issue 2 the panel applied point 3 of Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 and point 4 of Notification No. 049/10 of 9 July 2010, which preserves those benefits, together with Articles 103, 71(6) and 72(1) of the Labour Law: the bonus is part of wage, and authorised absence suspends the contract, so the employer may not simply confiscate the whole bonus but must apportion it to the days actually worked — the solution it had already adopted in Awards 57/07-Seratex, 106/07-M & V and 115/07. For unauthorised absence, by contrast, it followed Awards 54/07, 70/07 and 152/08 and held the employer may withhold the bonus entirely. [Para 83][Para 94][Para 103][Para 106][Para 89]

On issue 3 the panel found the worker arrived over an hour late on 15 November 2010 in a traffic jam without seeking or obtaining permission, while the employer already tolerates lateness under half an hour; lateness beyond the time fixed by the internal rules forfeits the bonus, so the claim failed. [Para 116][Para 117][Para 118][Para 123]

On issue 4 the panel held that seniority is the continuous period of service giving extra rights and ends only when the worker stops working for the enterprise; a change or renewal of contract does not interrupt it. Since successive six-month fixed-term contracts had run beyond two years, they became indefinite-term contracts by operation of Article 67, so the employer must maintain seniority and pay the bonus under point 5 of Notification No. 017, and may recover the 5% end-of-contract payments it had made. [Para 134][Para 138][Para 153][Para 159][Para 162][Para 169]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Seniority runs from the first day of continuous service and is not broken by renewing fixed-term contracts, so an employer that keeps a worker on rolling short contracts for more than two years must treat the contract as indefinite, keep the worker's seniority intact, and pay the seniority bonus for the whole period actually worked. [Para 138][Para 156]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that the employer may reclaim end-of-contract payments already made, once the contracts are recharacterised as indefinite, was an observation on a remedy no party had yet quantified. [Para 162]
