# Award No. 58/11 — United Apparel Cambodia Inc. (Arbitration Council, 21 June 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Mar Samborana (employer), Ven Pov (worker), Kong Phallack (chair) · Doc ID 140030

## 1. Facts

United Apparel Cambodia Inc. is a garment and textile factory established in 2009 employing 3,700 workers. The Local Union of C.CAWDU was not formally registered, though 73 workers authorised it to represent them. Hun Narin, a group leader in the cutting section, began work on 27 December 2008 on successive three-month fixed-duration contracts. His final contract ran from 1 March to 31 May 2011, and on 5 May 2011 the company terminated it prematurely for alleged misconduct by members of his group. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 25][Para 26][Para 27]

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. Whether the employer discriminated against the Local Union and must reinstate Hun Narin to his former position with back pay from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 2]
2. Whether the employer must pay 50% of three months' wages to pregnant workers before they take maternity leave? [Para 4]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** The termination was union discrimination. The union notified the company of its leadership candidates, after which the company met the five candidates and suggested they withdraw their union membership; Hun Narin refused and was dismissed two days later. He was unaware of the incorrect cutting, which the Chinese supervisor and general manager endorsed. [Para 33][Para 37]

**Employer (respondent):** It denied any knowledge of the notification and said Hun Narin was dismissed for poor performance, having led incorrect cutting three times in April and July 2010 and April 2011. It also refused to pay maternity pay in one lump before leave because its accounting system could not make three months' payments at once. [Para 28][Para 35][Para 39]

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** Partly granted — the employer was ordered to reinstate Hun Narin with back pay from the date of dismissal to reinstatement; if it refuses, it must calculate a termination payment under the rules for undetermined-duration contracts. It was also ordered to pay the entire three-month maternity payment before female workers commence maternity leave. The award is binding in Part I. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 78]

On Issue 1, the panel first classified Hun Narin's contract. Article 67(2) caps fixed-duration contracts at two years, and the panel followed Awards 10/03-Jacqsintex, 36/06-Mondotex and 57/06-Evergreen: successive three-month contracts from 27 December 2008 to 28 February 2011 gave two years and one month of service, so the contract was undetermined duration. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46] Under Articles 74 and 49, dismissal at will requires a valid reason and prior notice; the company gave no warning or education about the incorrect cutting, and its internal work rules contained no disciplinary measure for such misconduct. The termination for misconduct was therefore not legitimate. [Para 47][Para 49][Para 50][Para 51] Clause 34 of Prakas No. 099 empowers the panel to order reinstatement, and it did so, adding that any termination payment already paid could be reclaimed or offset. [Para 52][Para 54][Para 55] On union discrimination, Articles 12 and 279 forbid decisions based on union affiliation. The panel found the workers failed to prove the company received the candidacy notification, which was left with a security guard who did not acknowledge receipt, and failed to use other reliable means under Clause 3 of Prakas No. 305. The discrimination claim was rejected for insufficient evidence. [Para 66][Para 69][Para 70]

On Issue 3, Article 183(1) entitles women on maternity leave to half their wage including perquisites, and Article 115(3) forbids payment on a day off. Following Arbitral Award 91/07-J K Forever, the panel held maternity pay must be made before leave begins. The employer's accounting difficulty was no defence. [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74][Para 75]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

A fixed-duration contract renewed successively so that total service exceeds two years is an undetermined-duration contract under Article 67(2); terminating it for misconduct without prior warning or a defined disciplinary measure is not legitimate, and reinstatement with back pay is the remedy. Maternity pay must be paid in full before maternity leave begins, and an employer's internal accounting convenience is not a lawful excuse. [Para 46][Para 51][Para 75]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the timing of the dismissal, two days after the alleged notification, was suspicious, but that the union's failure to prove receipt defeated the discrimination claim. [Para 67][Para 68][Para 69]
