# Arbitral Award 84/11 — Supertex Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 4 August 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · arbitral panel: Mr Seng Vuoch Hun (employer party), Mr An Nan (worker party, per the face of the award; the signature block prints Mr Sin Kimsean), Mr Pen Bunchhea (chair) · Doc ID 140047

## 1. Facts
Supertex Ltd. is a garment factory on National Road 2, Phnom Penh, employing about 1,816 workers; ten local unions and worker delegates are the claimants. [Para 1] Abito (Taiwan) International Inc rented the factory buildings and Supertex produced exclusively to Abito's orders, which Abito withdrew on 31 May 2011. [Para 37] It sold 500 sewing machines to pay May 2011 wages, then suspended the workers' contracts in two periods, 2 to 20 June and 20 June to 20 August 2011. [Para 38] It sought the Inspector's approval only on 28 June 2011, eight days after suspending, and had no response by the hearing. [Para 39] Separately, 20 C.CAWDU members struck on 13–16 September 2010 over a minimum-wage increase, returned on 17 September, and were treated as having abandoned their posts. [Para 53] Conciliation failed; non-conciliation report No. 746 KB/RK/VK, dated 6 July 2011, reached the Secretariat on 7 July; hearings ran 18 and 21 July 2011. [Para 10]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay wages at 100% for the work-suspension period from 2 to 20 June 2011? [Para 41]
2. Whether the employer must terminate the contracts of workers holding undetermined- and fixed-duration contracts and pay termination benefits? [Para 47]
3. Whether the panel should consider the Chinese workers' demand that the employer terminate their contracts, given their absence from the second hearing? [Para 65]
4. Whether the employer must pay wages for 1 to 15 June 2011 to the approximately 450 workers on the third and fourth floors and in the Warehouse Section? [Para 66]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the employer had no work in June 2011 and failed to consult the worker delegates or union leaders and to obtain Ministry approval before suspending. [Para 2] Because it had no work and had sold its machines, the workers demanded termination and outstanding wages. [Para 47] The 20 dismissed workers said the dismissal was illegitimate, that the Government had ordered reinstatement in Notification 1150, and that the strike was lawful. [Para 61] The 450 workers had worked 1 to 15 June 2011 unpaid. [Para 66]
**Employer (respondent):** it paid only 50% of wages for 2 to 20 June 2011 because the workers were informed and 50% payment had been its past practice. [Para 43] It conceded it had not consulted them this time because Abito withdrew its orders immediately. [Para 44] It refused to terminate and would keep the workers on suspension without a clear plan. [Para 49] It called their departure to strike abandonment of work and serious misconduct. [Para 56] On the 450 workers it accepted wages were owed but said responsibility was unclear after Kok Vij gave authority to Peng Ding. [Para 7]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** binding award on the rights disputes — the panel ordered 100% wages for 2–20 June 2011; dismissal indemnity and equal damages, outstanding wages and unused annual leave for undetermined-duration workers; severance of at least 5%, damages equal to remaining contract wages and employment certificates for fixed-duration workers; termination payments and wages to 20 June 2011 for the 20 dismissed workers; wages for 1–15 June 2011 for the 450 workers; and declined to consider the Chinese workers' demand. [Para 154]

The panel treated the wage demand as a rights dispute and turned to Article 71(II), which permits suspension only where the Labour Inspector confirms serious economic difficulty, for no more than two months and under the Inspector's control. [Para 68] It found the employer genuinely in serious economic difficulty after Abito's withdrawal and the suspension substantively warranted, lasting only 19 days. [Para 74] But approval had never been obtained, and was required even where the reasons were proper and the period short; the suspension was contrary to legal procedure. [Para 78] Under Article 72 the employer need not pay during a lawful suspension, but prior awards hold that an employer failing to suspend under Article 71(II) must pay 100% of wages. [Para 81]
On termination, Article 74 allows either party to end an undetermined-duration contract at will, but a worker cannot force the employer to terminate, and the employer wished to keep the workers. [Para 84] The panel nonetheless found the indefinite suspension without a work plan improper, because it affected workers' livelihood, time and accrued benefits and diminished their prospects of new employment. [Para 94] That uncertainty compelled the workers to cancel their contracts individually, so Article 90 entitled them to dismissal indemnity and damages. [Para 96] Compensation in lieu of notice was refused because the employer had not terminated. [Para 97] For fixed-duration contracts, severance of at least 5% under Article 73(6) and damages equal to wages to contract end under Article 73(3) were ordered. [Para 113] Annual-leave payment rested on Articles 166 and 167. [Para 120]
For the 20 dismissed workers the panel held that participation in a strike is not serious misconduct, that the right to strike is protected, and that Article 332(2) requires reinstatement while Article 333 forbids sanctions. [Para 140] The dismissal had no valid reason; the 20 were entitled to the other workers' benefits, plus wages from dismissal to 20 June 2011. [Para 145] The Chinese workers, duly notified and absent without reason, met the three conditions of clause 21 of Prakas 099 and dropped their claim. [Para 148] On the 450 workers, Article 102 defines wages as remuneration for work done, which the employer acknowledged. [Para 153]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension under Article 71(II) requires prior approval of the Labour Inspector; where the employer suspends first and seeks approval afterwards, the suspension is procedurally unlawful and the employer must pay 100% of wages for the no-work period, even if the economic difficulty was genuine and the period short. [Para 81] An employer that cannot supply work but refuses to terminate, leaving workers in indefinite suspension without a plan, pushes them to resign and owes dismissal indemnity and damages under Article 90. [Para 96] Participation in a strike is not serious misconduct and cannot found a dismissal; dismissed workers get the same termination benefits as others and reinstatement under Article 332(2). [Para 144] On expiry of a fixed-duration contract the worker is owed severance of at least 5% of wages and, where the employer ends it early, damages equal to wages to its end. [Para 113]
**Obiter dicta:** the remarks that the suspension was substantively justified by Abito's withdrawal, and that the employer's failure to explain its future work plans showed the suspension was improper, explain the order rather than state independent rules. [Para 94] The observations on the MoU and on the Local Union of VKYUF not being bound are procedural. [Para 17]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The award names the worker-party arbitrator as "An Nan" on the face [Para 1] but as "Sin Kimsean" in the signature block [Para 162]. Both are reported; neither reconciled.
- The date of award appears only as 4 August 2011 in the header [Para 1]; no conflicting date appears later.
- OCR corrupts the text throughout ("themployer", "athe", "withe", "Traing", "fixeduration"). [Para 1]
- The evidence lists at [Para 26] and [Para 30] are cut off mid-entry with "[truncated]" and cannot be reconstructed.
- The second suspension period is left open at [Para 38]; the end date 20 August 2011 is taken from [Para 25] and [Para 50].
- The list of the 20 dismissed workers at [Para 63]–[Para 65] is scrambled, with numbers and names out of order.
- Case references are run together at [Para 71] ("22/05-Ocean ... 72/05-North Gaiety"), so they cannot be separated reliably.
- [Para 143] cites "Articles 33" where Article 332 is meant; [Para 153] places "Issue 3 Decline to consider the Chinese workers' demand" before "Part I"; [Para 159] prints "Issue 43" where Issue 4 is meant.
