# Award No. 37/11 — ASD (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. v National Independent Federation Textile Union of Cambodia Local Union (Arbitration Council, 22 April 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun, An Nan, Ang Eng Thong chair) · Doc ID 140014

## 1. Facts
ASD (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. employs 790 workers and faced a nine-point demand from the Local Union of NIFTUC. Conciliation on 7 March 2011 resolved three issues and the hearing on 24 March 2011 resolved three more, leaving issues 1, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8. The central claims concerned three-month probationary contracts given to 45 new workers, workplace heat, staffing of the infirmary, and the resignation of Meak Kongkea, a nurse with two years and eight months of service. [Para 1][Para 20][Para 22][Para 29]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must implement the agreement made at the Arbitration Council by offering permanent contracts to those with more than two months of service and paying back pay from October 2010? [Para 29]
2. Whether the employer must abide by the agreement to set up a cooling system? [Para 32]
3. Whether the employer must implement the previous agreement by arranging two physicians on standby during working hours? [Para 34]
4. Whether the employer must provide a termination payment to the dismissed worker Meak Kongkea? [Para 38]
5. Whether the employer must consult and obtain the union's consent before modifying previous practices, including transfers? [Para 59]
6. Whether the employer must allow workers to take annual leave for a personal commitment? [Para 62]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The 2008 agreement and Article 68 required permanent contracts after two months, but the employer introduced three-month probation for 45 workers at US$ 56 a month. The workplace was unbearably hot, only one nurse remained for 790 workers, and the employer pushed Meak Kongkea into resigning by stripping her of work and benefits. [Para 30][Para 33][Para 35][Para 40]
**Employer (respondent):** It followed existing practice and Notification No. 049 in using three-month probation; it admitted the heat but pleaded cost; it had one nurse and was seeking a physician; and Meak Kongkea resigned voluntarily after frequent unauthorised leave. [Para 31][Para 34][Para 35][Para 41]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — permanent contracts plus US$ 5 monthly back pay for 45 workers; a cooling system or equivalent; a second nurse on standby; termination payments and benefits to Meak Kongkea; the consultation and annual-leave demands rejected. Binding award. [Para 139][Para 140]

On Issue 1, the panel held the dispute a rights dispute. Article 68 caps probation at three months for regular, two months for specialised and one month for non-specialised workers; garment workers are mostly specialised, so two months applied. The three-month probation contradicted Article 68, and the 2008 agreement remained enforceable. Notification No. 049's phrase "one to three months" was ambiguous and did not authorise a uniform three months. It ordered permanent contracts and US$ 5 monthly back pay to the 45 claimants. [Para 69][Para 72][Para 73][Para 76][Para 79]

On Issue 4, Article 229 and Prakas No. 147 required acceptable workplace temperatures and all means to cool them; both parties agreed it was hot, so the employer had to install a cooling system on the roof or find another means. [Para 87][Para 90][Para 91]

On Issue 5, Prakas No. 330 requires two nurses and one physician on standby for 601–900 workers. With one nurse for 790, the panel ordered a second nurse, stopping short of the two physicians demanded. [Para 97][Para 99]

On Issue 6, Article 90 covers an employer who pushes a worker into ending the contract. The panel found the employer gave her no work, did not inform her of the transfer, withheld wages and transport for three months, and admitted unfair treatment; her resignation was constructive dismissal. It awarded dismissal indemnity and damages under Articles 89–91, US$ 6 monthly in lieu of a day-care centre, transport costs, withheld wages and one and a half days' annual leave. [Para 107][Para 108][Para 113][Para 116][Para 120]

On Issue 7, the panel accepted the employer's managerial prerogative under Article 2 but found no specific transfer proved, so rejected the demand for insufficient evidence. On Issue 8, it held the demand contrary to Articles 166–171 and ordered the parties to discuss annual leave instead. [Para 126][Para 127][Para 137]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A probationary period longer than the statutory maximum for the worker's category is unlawful even where a ministerial notification uses ambiguous ranges, and the workers concerned must be converted to permanent status with back pay; and an employer that strips a worker of work, wages and transport to force resignation commits constructive dismissal under Article 90. [Para 72][Para 79][Para 108]

**Obiter dicta:** Managerial prerogative under Article 2 is protected only where exercised lawfully and reasonably. [Para 126]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The agreement is dated "8 October 2008" in the issue statement but "8 April 2008" in the quoted Point 1. The "Evidence" section is omitted from the English version. OCR losses throughout ("thearing", "fixeduration", "constructive dismissal" passages garbled). The award orders "another half of one and a half months' wages" while the facts refer to "half of her three months' wages". [Para 28][Para 29][Para 58][Para 140]
