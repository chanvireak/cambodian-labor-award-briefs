# Award No. 54/11 — June Textile Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 17 June 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phyrum, employer-chosen; Liv Sovanna, worker-chosen; Kong Phallack, chair) · Doc ID 140024

## 1. Facts
June Textile Co., Ltd, at Russian Federation Boulevard, Sen Sok District, Phnom Penh, employed 4,096 workers, of whom 1,377 held fixed-duration and undetermined-duration contracts and formed the claimant group. [Para 52] [Para 53] On 31 March 2011 a fire began in a ground-floor warehouse of the new building, which housed fabric and thread; the alarms malfunctioned and the building burned down. [Para 56] [Para 60] [Para 63] [Para 66] The company terminated the workers on 1 April 2011 and asserted the fire was an act of god, offering US$ 20 per year of service and payment in lieu of unused annual leave. [Para 69] [Para 70]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay outstanding wages, compensation in lieu of prior notice, payment in lieu of unused annual leave, termination payments for fixed and undetermined-duration contracts, and damages under the Labour Law, the fire having destroyed the factory? [Para 32] [Para 53] [Para 90]
2. Whether the fire of 31 March 2011 was an act of god, so as to release the company from those obligations? [Para 92] [Para 104]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The fire was not an act of god: it was caused by technical negligence in managing the electricity circuit, a fire is a foreseeable consequence of faulty wiring installed in 1997, and the company produced no evidence that the blaze was insurmountable. [Para 81] [Para 83] [Para 84] [Para 85] They also complained that no specific termination notice was given and that the decision was revealed only at the hearing. [Para 86] [Para 87]
**Employer (respondent):** The fire met all three conditions of the Civil Code definition — unintended, unforeseeable and insurmountable — because 30 fire trucks could not enter for lack of face masks; the company therefore had no obligation to pay termination payments, though it offered US$ 20 per year of service and leave indemnity. [Para 69] [Para 71] [Para 76] [Para 78] [Para 79]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — for fixed-duration contracts the company must pay damages at least equal to wages until contract termination and severance of at least 5% of wages, plus payment in lieu of unused annual leave; for undetermined-duration contracts it must pay compensation in lieu of prior notice and dismissal indemnity according to seniority, plus leave indemnity; the demands for outstanding wages and for damages under Article 91 were rejected. [Para 148] [Para 149] [Para 150]

The panel first allocated the burden: the party alleging an act of god must prove it, following Awards 79/05, 101/08, 108/08, 163/09, 168/09 and 115/10. [Para 92] [Para 93] [Para 94] It declined to consider the company's English-language evidence, because clause 23 of Prakas 099 requires Khmer. [Para 95] It also held the Civil Code definition unavailable, the Code not being in force under Article 1305, and instead used the Glossary of the Land Law (2001), which requires an event to be both unforeseeable and insurmountable. [Para 98] [Para 99] [Para 100] [Para 101]

On foreseeability, the panel reasoned that fire is a generally foreseeable risk — the company's own 150 extinguishers, alarms, drills, maintenance team and insurance showed it had anticipated fire — and that the law does not require foresight of the exact date or magnitude. [Para 106] [Para 107] [Para 111] [Para 112] On insurmountability, the fire began small, was not caused by a natural disaster, and the failure to extinguish it was attributable to inadequate equipment rather than to overwhelming scale. [Para 114] [Para 116] [Para 117] Neither condition was met, so the company remained liable. [Para 118]

Applying that finding, premature termination of fixed-duration contracts by the employer's will attracted damages under Article 73(3) and severance under Article 73(6). [Para 119] [Para 120] [Para 121] [Para 124] [Para 127] For undetermined-duration contracts, termination without notice attracted compensation under Articles 75 and 77 and dismissal indemnity under Article 89. [Para 131] [Para 133] [Para 135] [Para 136] Damages under Article 91 were refused because the fire supplied a valid reason under Article 74. [Para 140] [Para 143] [Para 144] Outstanding March wages were already paid and the demand was rejected. [Para 145] [Para 146] Leave indemnity was ordered under Article 167(2). [Para 148]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that closes an enterprise and invokes the act-of-god exception in Article 87(3) of the Labour Law bears the burden of proving both that the event was unforeseeable and that it was insurmountable; a fire in an industrial building that the employer had equipped and insured against fire is foreseeable, and equipment failure in fighting it is not insurmountable, so the exception does not displace the employer's statutory termination obligations. [Para 94] [Para 106] [Para 112] [Para 117]

**Obiter dicta:** Evidence submitted in a language other than Khmer will not be considered, per clause 23 of Prakas 099; and the 2007 Civil Code's act-of-god definition could not be used because the Code was not yet in force. [Para 95] [Para 98] [Para 99]
