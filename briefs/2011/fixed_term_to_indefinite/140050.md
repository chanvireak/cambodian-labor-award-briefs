# Award No. 87/11 — Kin Nitich International (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 12 August 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Sin Kim Sean, Pen Bunhea presiding) · Doc ID 140050

## 1. Facts
Two women, Ms S and Ms C, worked at the Kin Nitich garment factory. Both stood as candidates in a union leadership election held on 9 May 2011 and were elected president and treasurer respectively. On 12 May 2011 the company dismissed them. They claim the dismissals were anti-union discrimination and ask for reinstatement, back pay and conversion of their fixed-duration contracts to contracts of undetermined duration. [Para 57][Para 67][Para 53]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the dismissal of Ms S and Ms C was lawful, given that they were candidates in a union leadership election and the union says it notified the company of their candidacies? [Para 57]
2. Whether the fixed-duration contracts of Ms S and Ms C should be converted to contracts of undetermined duration? [Para 58]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The company knew of the candidacies from a notification letter handed to the gate security guard and signed for by the administrative chief; the dismissals were therefore anti-union discrimination. At the time of dismissal the factory had plenty of work and daily overtime. [Para 77][Para 79][Para 89]

**Employer (respondent):** There was little work, the two women performed poorly and chatted too much during working hours, and the company did not know they were candidates. The signature and date on the notification letter are not those of its administrative chief. [Para 88][Para 83]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim to reinstate Ms S was dismissed and the company was ordered to pay her damages equal to the wages due to the end of her contract; the company was ordered to reinstate Ms C, pay her back wages from 12 May 2011 until reinstatement, and convert her contract to one of undetermined duration. [Para 200][Para 202][Para 204][Para 205]

**Protected-candidate status.** Article 293 of the Labour Law and article 7 of Prakas 313 protect only the three principal union leaders — president, first vice-president and first secretary — so Ms C, as treasurer, fell outside that list. [Para 107][Para 108][Para 110] Article 3 of Prakas 305 protects every candidate in a union leadership election for 45 days before and 45 days after the poll, provided the union notifies the employer of the candidacy by any reliable means. [Para 111][Para 112][Para 113] The first condition was met: Ms S was a candidate and was elected on 9 May 2011. [Para 117] The second was met: the dismissal on 12 May 2011 fell inside the protected window. [Para 120] The third was not: the panel compared the signature on the notification letter with specimens of the administrative chief's signature and found it different, and the union could neither name the security guard nor say when the letter was delivered. [Para 136][Para 137][Para 138][Para 139] Neither woman therefore held protected status. [Para 143]

**Ms S's dismissal.** Her successive fixed-duration contracts totalled 24 months, the last running to 9 July 2011. [Para 145][Para 61] Under article 73 a fixed-duration contract may be cut short before expiry only by agreement of both parties or for serious misconduct or force majeure; otherwise the employer must pay damages at least equal to the wages due to the end of the contract. [Para 147][Para 148][Para 149] The panel found no valid reason: the repeated renewals showed satisfactory performance and she had never been warned. [Para 154] The dismissal came almost two months early; the company tendered payment to the contract end and she refused it, so she is entitled to wages and damages to that date. [Para 152][Para 157][Para 158][Para 200]

**Ms C's contract.** Her successive contracts totalled 29 months, of which 2 years 3 months had run at dismissal. [Para 159][Para 160][Para 63] Article 67(2) caps a fixed-duration contract at two years, caps any renewal at two years, and provides that practice contrary to those limits converts the contract into one of undetermined duration. [Para 161][Para 162][Para 163] Article 73(5) fixes the notice periods. [Para 164][Para 165][Para 166] Following earlier awards, the panel ordered conversion. [Para 168][Para 171]

**Lawfulness of Ms C's dismissal.** Article 74 permits termination of an undetermined-duration contract on notice, but not without a valid reason related to the worker's aptitude or conduct or to the needs of the enterprise. [Para 172][Para 174][Para 175] The company pleaded lack of work and chatting; but on the day of dismissal it was giving daily overtime, and it had issued no warning. [Para 178][Para 179][Para 180][Para 181] The dismissal therefore lacked a valid reason. [Para 186]

**Discrimination.** Articles 12 and 279 forbid an employer from using union membership or activity as a ground of dismissal, and the worker bears the burden of proof. [Para 188][Para 189][Para 190] Because the notification evidence was not credible, the panel could not infer anti-union discrimination. [Para 198][Para 199]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A candidate's protection under article 3 of Prakas 305 depends on proof that the union actually notified the employer of the candidacy by a reliable means before the dismissal. A letter whose receipt signature is not that of the employer's authorised signatory, and whose delivery the union cannot corroborate, does not satisfy that condition. Separately, successive fixed-duration contracts whose total duration exceeds two years convert to a contract of undetermined duration under article 67(2), so that early termination requires a valid reason under article 74. [Para 139][Para 163]

**Obiter dicta:** Even where the employer's internal procedure channels correspondence through gate security, receipt by security can count as valid notification if the workers produce specific evidence that the administration signed for the letter. [Para 134] An employer should issue a warning or apply internal discipline before dismissing for a minor infraction such as chatting. [Para 184]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer is printed as "ខិន នីតិច", "ខិន នឹតិច", "ខឹន នីតិច" and "ខិន នឹង នឹតិច"; the second worker as both "ឆម ស៊ី ផា" and "ឆម ស៊ី ជា"; arbitrator Ing Sothy as "អ៉ីង សុទ្ធី", "អ៉ីង សុទ្លី" and "អ៊ឹង សុទ្លី". The expression "ជម្ងឺ ចិត្ត" appears where the sense is clearly damages ("សំណង"). The union notification letter is dated 6 May 2011 at [Para 38] and 7 May 2011 at [Para 79] and [Para 194], and the panel refers to the candidacy notice under both dates. The award records that the Democratic Union of Khmer Workers had not yet applied for registration [Para 50], yet the panel applies the Prakas 305 candidate protection without addressing that fact. At [Para 202] the text inserts the stray Latin fragment "ក្រុមហ៊ុន Li" where the company is meant.
