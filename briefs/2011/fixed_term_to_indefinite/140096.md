# Award No. 149/11 — Fortune Garment (Arbitration Council, 23 December 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Tuon Siphan, worker-appointed; Pen Bunhea, chair) · Doc ID 140096

## 1. Facts
Fortune Garment employed 1,290 workers at its Prek Khsach factory, 483 of them piece-rate sewers (ដេរ ស៊ីបុង) represented by the C.CAWDU union. The union claimed the company miscalculated overtime for those sewers and asked for correction and back pay under Prakas No. 80 and No. 017. Conciliation failed on one point, which reached the panel. [Para 1][Para 35][Para 37][Para 2]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer's method of calculating overtime pay for piece-rate sewers was lawful and correct under the Labour Law? [Para 38][Para 73]
2. Whether the employer must recalculate overtime and pay back wages wrongly calculated in the past to the affected sewers? [Para 104]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The company's overtime calculation was wrong because it used the monthly piece-rate earnings rather than the base wage to derive the hourly rate, and the pay slips did not show the overtime components clearly. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 49]
**Employer (respondent):** The calculation was rigorously correct, applied the lawful 50% and 100% multipliers, and had been checked by the ILO; the union's formula misread the base. [Para 3][Para 40][Para 76]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to explain its pay-slip table so workers can understand the statutory overtime rates, and dismissed the claim for recalculation and back pay. [Para 105][Para 106]

On the calculation method, the panel applied Article 139 of the amended Labour Law: overtime on ordinary days attracts a 50% increase and night or weekly-rest-day overtime 100%. The company's written formula on the hearing board used 50% for ordinary-day overtime, and the panel accepted that the multiplier matched the statute. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 76][Para 78]

On the base figure, the panel found the company derived the hourly rate from the monthly piece-rate earnings rather than the base wage, and that its computer pay table split the 50% overtime into two unlabelled sections ($2.70 and $2.16), producing $4.86 in a way that confused workers. The panel held the arithmetic was substantially correct but the presentation breached Article 112(b), which requires the employer to inform workers clearly and understandably of all wage components. [Para 43][Para 84][Para 85][Para 87][Para 89]

On back pay, the panel found the workers had not identified which sewers were underpaid or produced evidence of the affected group; the burden therefore fell on the worker party under Article 118, and the claim failed. The order to explain the pay table was the panel's remedy for the presentation defect. [Para 98][Para 99][Para 103][Para 93]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a piece-rate employer applies the statutory overtime multipliers but presents the wage components in a way workers cannot understand, the panel will order clarification of the pay slip under Article 112(b) rather than recompute the wages; a claim for back pay still requires the claimant to identify the affected workers and prove the shortfall. [Para 89][Para 93][Para 103]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the split presentation was generated by the company's computer system and that the computer table was never shown to the workers, which it treated as the source of the confusion rather than as proof of underpayment. [Para 86][Para 47]
