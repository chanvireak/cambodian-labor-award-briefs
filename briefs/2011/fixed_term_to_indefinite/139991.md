# Award No. 17/11 — JRB Action Textile & Clothing Ltd (Arbitration Council, 28 February 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; Ven Pov, worker-appointed; Kong Phallack, chair) · Doc ID 139991

## 1. Facts

JRB Action Textile & Clothing Ltd employs 1,000 workers at Trapang Thleung Village, Chom Chao Commune, Dangkor District, Phnom Penh. The Local Union of C.CAWDU, registered 8 September 2010 and representing about 300 workers, filed four demands on 2 November 2010; conciliation failed on all four and the case reached the panel on 21 January 2011. [Para 1][Para 8][Para 20]

The first demand concerns an electrician, Mr C, hired on a verbal undetermined-duration contract from 1 June 2007 at US$ 130 a month with accommodation and three meals; in September 2010 the employer moved him to eight hours a day and re-drew his payslip as US$ 100 main wage plus a US$ 30 position bonus. The second concerns Mr M, a local union officer on successive six-month contracts from 18 April 2007 whose last contract ran to 30 September 2010 and was not renewed. [Para 22][Para 24][Para 35][Para 40]

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. Whether the employer must incorporate the US$ 30 position bonus into Mr C's main wage, restoring US$ 130? [Para 21]
2. Whether the employer must reinstate Mr M, local union vice-president, with back pay? [Para 34]
3. Whether the fixed-duration contracts of workers with over two years' service must be converted to undetermined-duration contracts? [Para 43]
4. Whether maternity pay must be 50% of wages computed on the last 12 months' wages including overtime? [Para 46]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** Mr C's contract fixed US$ 130 as the main wage and his payslips said so; the US$ 30 deduction was union discrimination after he joined the union. Mr M's contracts ran past two years, so his contract became undetermined-duration and his non-renewal was discriminatory. Maternity pay should follow the last 12 months' wages including overtime. [Para 23][Para 31][Para 40][Para 46]

**Employer (respondent):** The US$ 130 always included the US$ 30 position bonus, so the main wage was US$ 100; the bonus returns if Mr C produces a skill certificate. Mr M's contract expired and was not renewed for staffing reasons in the ironing section. It pays maternity pay on the main wage and denies all discrimination. [Para 23][Para 27][Para 35][Para 48]

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** granted in full — order to pay Mr C US$ 130 monthly and nullify the US$ 30 position bonus; order to reinstate Mr M; order to convert fixed-duration contracts of workers with over two years' service; order to pay maternity pay at 50% of wages for ninety days computed on the previous 12 months' wages. All four issues were rights disputes and the award is binding. [Para 108]

Issue 1: the panel found the demand a rights dispute because it turned on the wage stipulated in the contract. Under Article 65 of the Labour Law the contract is subject to ordinary law, and Decree 38 requires free consent for validity; the payslips showed no US$ 30 bonus before September 2010, and the employer admitted it never told Mr C the bonus would be deducted from his main wage. The deduction was a unilateral act without consent and the main wage stayed US$ 130. The union-discrimination claim failed for want of evidence, the burden being on the claimant. [Para 54][Para 56][Para 59][Para 60][Para 61][Para 64]

Issue 2: Mr M worked 18 April 2007 to 30 September 2010, so his successive renewals exceeded two years. Article 67(2) makes any renewal beyond two years an undetermined-duration contract. The 5% severance paid at each expiry did not sever the relationship, since the relationship continued. Termination was therefore at the employer's will, and as a union leader he enjoyed Article 293 protection: no authorisation from the Labour Inspector was sought, so the dismissal was unlawful. [Para 71][Para 74][Para 78][Para 82]

Issue 3: the panel read Article 67(2) as a cap on total duration, supported by Article 73(5) and ILO Recommendation 166, and held the employer's practice of rolling three- and six-month contracts was not legally founded. [Para 92][Para 95][Para 96]

Issue 4: Article 183 gives half wages including perquisites; Article 103 counts overtime as wages, so maternity pay is 50% of average monthly wages over the prior 12 months times three. [Para 102][Para 105][Para 107]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

A renewal that carries the total term of a fixed-duration contract past two years converts it into an undetermined-duration contract, and severance paid at each expiry does not sever the relationship; terminating such a worker who is a union leader without Labour Inspector authorisation is unlawful. [Para 74][Para 82]

**Obiter dicta:** The Labour Law's bias toward undetermined-duration contracts serves employment security and the employer's interest in long-term commitment; ILO Recommendation 166, though not binding, assists interpretation of Article 67. [Para 91][Para 94]
