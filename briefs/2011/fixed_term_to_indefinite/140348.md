# Award No. 144/10 — Cambodia Airports Management Services / Phnom Penh International Airport (Arbitration Council, 16 February 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouchhun, An Non, Pen Bun Chha presiding) · Doc ID 140348

## 1. Facts
The respondent is ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូឌា អ៊ែរ ផត មែនី ជ្ជមែន សឺវីស — Cambodia Airports Management Services (CAMS), operator of Phnom Penh International Airport, Russian Federation Boulevard, Chom Chao, Dangkao — with about 750 workers. The claimants are the សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត វិស័យទេសចរណ៍ សេវាកម្ម កម្ពុជា (Tourism and Service Workers Union Federation of Cambodia) and the សហជីព កម្មករ និយោជិត ឯករាជ្យ ព្រលានយន្តហោះអន្តរជាតិ (Independent Union of International Airport Workers), which has 400 members. A fire-section employee (Mr H) on an indefinite-term contract since 1995 was suspended from 1 May 2010 and dismissed by letter of 12 May 2010 for serious misconduct: taking an internal grievance outside the company, publicly opposing the top management's closure of gate 5 and the felling of trees, and sending a "request for correction" to the Civil Aviation Secretariat. The second demand was withdrawn at the hearing, leaving one issue. [Para 16][Para 24][Para 47][Para 64][Para 115]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer lawfully dismissed the fire-section employee for serious misconduct, and if not, whether he must be reinstated with back pay or compensated? [Para 100][Para 119]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimants):** the dismissal was union discrimination — the employer neither discussed it with the union delegation nor with the staff representatives, and the employee was punished for speaking up for his colleagues; management's closure of gate 5, which damaged his car, and the tree felling were matters he was entitled to raise; failing reinstatement, he asks for termination pay and the other statutory benefits. [Para 7][Para 114][Para 148]
**Employer (respondent):** the case is not even a collective dispute, since no other worker supports the reinstatement demand; the employee breached the Internal Work Rules (Article 5(2), points 5 and 12) and clause 12.2 of the collective agreement by bypassing the three-day internal complaint procedure and disclosing internal affairs outside the company, damaging its reputation. [Para 96][Para 107][Para 135][Para 136]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must pay compensation in lieu of notice of three months' wages, severance of 240 days' wages and allowances, unpaid annual leave, and moral damages equal to the severance; reinstatement was not ordered. [Para 156][Para 160][Para 167][Para 173][Para 175]

The panel first rejected the employer's objection that this was an individual dispute: under Article 302 of the Labour Law a collective dispute requires a body of workers, a subject touching working conditions or union rights, and the capacity to disturb the enterprise, and all three were met given the union's 400 members and its warning of further action. [Para 109][Para 110][Para 112][Para 118]

On the merits it applied Article 74 (an indefinite-term contract may be terminated by either side with written notice and a valid reason) and Article 83(2)(b)(3) (serious breach of disciplinary rules), and held the employer bears the burden of proving serious misconduct. [Para 121][Para 126][Para 132] The panel found the breaches of Article 5(2) and clause 12.2 proved, but that neither instrument classified them as serious misconduct, and the employer gave no evidence of how far the disclosure harmed its reputation or business — so the threshold for dismissal was not crossed, even though some disciplinary measure would have been proportionate. [Para 137][Para 138][Para 139][Para 141][Para 144]

Because the working relationship was already damaged and would deteriorate further on reinstatement, the panel declined to order reinstatement and instead applied Articles 75, 77, 89, 91 and 166: three months' notice pay for more than ten years' service, severance of 15 days per year for 16 years (240 days), payment for unused annual leave, and moral damages under Article 91 equal to the severance. [Para 146][Para 152][Para 157][Para 168][Para 173]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A breach of internal rules is not serious misconduct permitting dismissal unless the rules or the collective agreement classify it as such and the employer proves the harm it caused; where the misconduct is real but does not reach that threshold, the sanction of dismissal is disproportionate and the worker is entitled to the statutory termination benefits. [Para 139][Para 141][Para 144]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observations that a union with a majority of the workforce can readily disturb the enterprise for the purposes of Article 302 were illustrative rather than determinative of the merits. [Para 115][Para 117]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The scan is heavily degraded: the award-number suffix, the employer's Khmer name and several statutory citations are partly unreadable, and the union federation's name appears in several mangled variants. The dismissed worker's full name is printed throughout the source and is reduced to an initial here. The award is dated 16 February 2011 while the hearing was held on 24 January 2011 and the first listed sitting on 10 January 2011; one exhibit is dated "10 September 2019 2010", printing two years. The award records 750 workers but also states that the claimant union's 400 members are more than half the workforce. [Para 15][Para 19][Para 64][Para 115]
