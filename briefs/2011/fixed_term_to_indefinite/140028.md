# Award No. 59/11 — Ming Jian (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 21 June 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun, employer-chosen; Sin Kim Sean, worker-chosen; Koy Neam, chair, dissenting on issue 2) · Doc ID 140028

## 1. Facts
Ming Jian (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a subcontracting garment enterprise at Trapang Thleung Village, Dangkor District, Phnom Penh, employed about 590 workers; the C.CAWDU local union, registered on 20 January 2010, represented 270 of them. [Para 16] Chuon Vireak had worked since 21 June 2005 as group leader in the cutting section on an undetermined-duration contract at US$ 106 per month, and in October 2010 was given the additional role of group leader in the ironing section without extra pay. [Para 17] [Para 18] After a production error on 2 December 2010 he was warned and signed an undertaking, and after a series of disputes over his duties he was excluded from the factory on 14 March 2011. [Para 26] [Para 36]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate Chuon Vireak and pay his wages from the date of dismissal to the date of reinstatement? [Para 2] [Para 52]
2. Whether the employer must pay full wages to workers when it has no work for them, rather than the 50% it has been paying? [Para 3] [Para 69]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The dismissal was unlawful and Chuon Vireak should be reinstated with back pay; and because the employer suspended work without Labour Inspector authorisation, the workers are entitled to full wages, the 1 March 2011 agreement providing less being null and void under Article 13 — they expressly did not seek implementation of that agreement or damages. [Para 43] [Para 44] [Para 45]
**Employer (respondent):** It dismissed Chuon Vireak for refusing direction, inattention to work, inciting other workers to stop working and violating Clause 10 of the Internal Work Rules; and it paid 75% of wages when there was no afternoon work and 50% when there was no work all day, consistent with the 1 March 2011 agreement, conceding it had not sought Labour Inspector authorisation because suspensions were too short. [Para 37] [Para 40] [Para 46] [Para 47]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted in full — the panel ordered reinstatement of Chuon Vireak with back pay from dismissal to reinstatement, and ordered the employer to pay full wages in cases of unauthorised work suspension. [Para 91] The award was non-binding. [Para 92] Chair Arbitrator Koy Neam dissented on issue 2. [Para 96]

The panel first classified both issues as rights disputes, having a basis in the employment contract and the Labour Law, following Awards 05/11-M&V (Branch 1) and 13/11-Gold Kamvimex. [Para 50] [Para 51]

On issue 1, Article 74 permits termination only for a valid reason relating to aptitude or behaviour. [Para 53] [Para 55] Clause 10 of the Internal Work Rules prescribed escalating warnings for minor and medium misconduct before dismissal, and the employer never characterised the four alleged grounds as minor, medium or serious. [Para 55] [Para 58] [Para 61] Following Award 51/08-ASD, the panel held the employer was bound to follow the Rules and the Law, and found it had failed to do so before dismissing him. [Para 61] [Para 62] [Para 63] Article 27 requires sanctions proportional to the misconduct, as held in Awards 109/07-Kingsland, 134/08-Chevron and 131/10-Leader's Industrial, and the employer had neither warned him nor assessed seriousness beforehand. [Para 64] [Para 65] [Para 66] There was therefore no valid reason under Article 74, and Clause 34 of Prakas 099 empowered the panel to order reinstatement. [Para 67] [Para 68] [Para 69]

On issue 2, Article 71(11) allows suspension for serious economic or material difficulty for up to two months under Labour Inspector control. [Para 71] [Para 72] The panel accepted that subcontracting unpredictability was a material difficulty, but held that the employer had not reported the suspension for inspection, as Awards 51/09-Yung Wah (Branch 1), 95/09-Tack Fat and 123/09-Suit Way require. [Para 73] [Para 76] [Para 77] [Para 78] Under Article 72(1), wages are excused only where the suspension is authorised; otherwise full wages are owed. [Para 80] [Para 81] [Para 82] As the employer paid only 50% during unauthorised suspensions, its practice was invalid, and it must either obtain authorisation or pay full wages. [Para 84] [Para 85] [Para 86] Any agreement to pay less being contrary to Article 13, it was null and void. [Para 89]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer dismissing a worker for misconduct must first apply its own Internal Work Rules' graduated warnings and assess whether the misconduct is minor, medium or serious; failing that, and failing proportionality under Article 27, there is no valid reason under Article 74, and reinstatement with back pay follows. [Para 62] [Para 65] [Para 67]

**Obiter dicta:** Article 71(11) suspension requires reporting to the Labour Inspector; without authorisation, the employer must pay full wages, and any agreement to pay less is void under Article 13. [Para 78] [Para 86] [Para 89]
