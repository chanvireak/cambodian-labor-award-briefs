# Award No. 14/11 — GHG (Cambodia) Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 16 February 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, Sin Kim Sean; chair Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 139977

## 1. Facts
GHG (Cambodia) Ltd., a garment factory operating since 2004 with 1,100 workers, faced four demands from the Local Union of C.CAWDU, registered on 5 October 2009. Two were settled in conciliation at the hearing; the panel was left with the dismissal of Ms L, a cleaning worker employed since 1 June 2006 and dismissed on 3 December 2010 for serious misconduct, and the withholding of Ms K's US$ 5 November 2010 attendance bonus after she arrived 12 minutes late on 15 November and 10 minutes late on 18 November, both attributed to traffic jams. [Para 22][Para 24][Para 26][Para 30][Para 31][Para 32]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay Ms N US$ 12 in wages for sick leave taken in November 2010? [Para 1]
2. Whether the employer must reinstate Ms L and pay her back wages from the date of dismissal to the date of reinstatement? [Para 2]
3. Whether the employer must pay a seniority bonus to workers with more than one year of service under Notification No. 17 SKBY dated 18 July 2000? [Para 3]
4. Whether the employer must pay Ms K the US$ 5 attendance bonus for November 2010? [Para 4]

Issues 1 and 3 were resolved by the parties in conciliation at the hearing and were not decided. [Para 11]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Ms L's dismissal was not for serious misconduct, and the dispute is collective because she is a union leader and member and the matter reached the Council through the Labour Inspector's non-conciliation report; Ms K's lateness was caused by traffic jams, was not intentional, and the employer itself did not deduct the bonus for the earlier of the two incidents. [Para 28][Para 32][Para 54]
**Employer (respondent):** Ms L is not a worker protected by the Labour Law and the dispute is individual — it concerns one worker and disciplinary rules, not collective benefits or working conditions; the attendance bonus is governed by the Internal Work Rules and Notification No. 17 SKBY, and lateness forfeits it. [Para 2][Para 28][Para 32]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction on issue 2 — the panel declined to consider the reinstatement claim as not a collective labour dispute; and dismissed on issue 4 — the demand for Ms K's US$ 5 attendance bonus was rejected. Nothing was ordered. [Para 57][Para 58]

On jurisdiction, the panel noted that the Labour Inspector and Minister have the duty to characterise a dispute before referring it, and that the Council follows that decision absent clear objection by the parties (Awards 10/03-Jacqsintex, 41/04-MiCasa, 07/05-Coca Cola, 45/07-Wilson, 13/09-Terratex Knitting). Award 45/07-Wilson creates a presumption that claims in a non-conciliation report are collective, with the burden on an objecting employer. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 35] Here the Inspector had expressed no view, so the panel tested Article 302 itself: a collective dispute requires (a) a dispute between workers and an employer, (b) a subject concerning working conditions, union rights or employer-worker relations, and (c) a capacity to jeopardise the enterprise's operation or social peace. [Para 36][Para 37] The first two were met — the complaint came from a union and concerned the employment relationship — but the third was not, because both sides agreed the dispute did not threaten the enterprise, and operations continued smoothly after the dismissal. The panel therefore held the dispute was not collective and declined jurisdiction. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40][Para 42][Para 43]

On the attendance bonus, Article 312 paragraph 2 and Clause 43 of Prakas No. 099 frame the rights/interests divide, and Awards 119/09-SL, 86/10-New Mingda and 02/11-Pou Yuen treat a demand as an interests dispute where it does not rest on law, agreement or collective agreement. Because the bonus is stipulated in Notification No. 017 SKBY dated 18 July 2000, the dispute was a rights dispute. [Para 44][Para 46][Para 47][Para 49]

Point 4 of Notification No. 049/10 KB/SCN dated 9 July 2010 preserves the benefits under points 3 to 6 of Notification No. 017, and point 3 guarantees at least US$ 5 per month to workers who attend work regularly. Following Award 44/07-Winner Knitting, "work regularly" means attending on the days set per month, allowing for public holidays and authorised leave; and Awards 94/04-Eternity, 08/07-Siu Quinh, 51/07-Goldfame, 74/09-Fortune, 55/10-Tack Fat and 143/10-GHG hold that workers who do not attend at the times set by the employer forfeit the bonus. [Para 51][Para 52]

The panel accepted that Ms K was late on both dates but found the traffic-jam explanation unsupported by evidence and no legal excuse for breaching the Internal Work Rules; the employer's inconsistent treatment on 15 November did not create an entitlement. The bonus was therefore not owed for November 2010. [Para 53][Para 55][Para 56]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A single worker's disciplinary dismissal, referred by the Labour Inspector without any ruling on its character, is not a collective labour dispute unless it could jeopardise the enterprise's operation or social peace; where both parties agree it could not, the panel has no jurisdiction however strong the union membership of the dismissed worker. [Para 40][Para 42][Para 43]

**Obiter dicta:** that the Inspector and Minister normally decide the collective character of a dispute and the Council defers to them; and that an employer's decision not to deduct a bonus for one instance of lateness does not oblige it to forgo the deduction for another. [Para 33][Para 54]

Para 8 records that one of the four issues was resolved at the 10 January 2011 conciliation, yet the same paragraph refers to "the four non-conciliated issues" and para 11 records issues 1 and 3 as resolved at the hearing; both statements are reproduced as printed. Notification No. 017 is referred to as "Notification No. 17 SKBY" and "Notification No. 017 SKBY" interchangeably.
