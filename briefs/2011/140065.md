# Award No. 105/11 — Goldfame Enterprises (In Ther Breay Sis) (Arbitration Council, 13 September 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Kong Phalla, president) · Doc ID 140065

## 1. Facts
Goldfame Enterprises (In Ther Breay Sis) is a garment factory at Setbo village, Setbo commune, Saang district, Kandal, employing about 5,900 workers. On 22 April 2011 the CCAWDU filed three demands; conciliation on 31 May 2011 failed on all three, and the non-conciliation report No. 257/11 Kb/Kn of 11 August 2011 reached the Council on 16 August 2011. Both parties attended the hearing of 23 August 2011. Because both signed the memorandum of understanding of 28 September 2010 on professional relations in the garment industry, the panel split its decision into rights disputes and interest disputes. [Para 1][Para 10][Para 12][Para 13][Para 16][Para 18]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must take back into employment Ms C Neng, Mr R Phearum and Mr Bo Chit and pay them back wages? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company must pay 5% severance each time a fixed-duration contract expires, for 330 workers, even where the contract is renewed? [Para 4][Para 97][Para 101]
3. Whether 25 workers whose successive fixed-duration contracts total more than two years must be converted to unlimited-duration contracts? [Para 6][Para 104]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The three dismissals were union discrimination against activists; the 5% severance under Article 73 is due at every contract expiry whether or not the worker is rehired; and the 25 long-serving workers must be converted under Article 67. [Para 78][Para 100][Para 102][Para 105]
**Employer (respondent):** Ms C Neng had four disciplinary breaches and her contract simply expired; Mr R Phearum and Mr Bo Chit fought at work, a serious offence; the 5% is paid annually on 31 December to sever contracts during the January–March lull; and no conversion is owed because notice was given and the 5% paid. [Para 75][Para 86][Para 99][Para 111][Para 115][Para 142]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement refused for all three workers; the 5%-per-renewal claim dismissed; the 25 workers converted to unlimited-duration contracts; and the company ordered to pay Mr R Phearum and Mr Bo Chit wages from 15 July 2010 to 31 December 2010, final unpaid wages and 5% severance. [Para 211][Para 213][Para 214][Para 215]
On issue 1 the panel first classified the demand as a rights dispute because reinstatement is regulated by the Labour Law. [Para 117][Para 120] For Ms C Neng, the panel found successive fixed-duration contracts, non-renewal at expiry on 31 December 2010 for four recorded breaches, and no proof that other strikers were treated better; the union-discrimination claim failed. [Para 128][Para 135][Para 136][Para 137]
For Mr R Phearum and Mr Bo Chit, the employer relied on two witness statements taken the same day that contradicted each other; the panel preferred the workers' account that they were playing, held that dismissal was disproportionate to the misconduct under Article 27, but awarded compensation rather than reinstatement. [Para 144][Para 146][Para 147][Para 151][Para 167][Para 168]
On issue 2 the panel read Article 73 as requiring the 5% severance when the employment relationship is cut off, not at each renewal while the worker remains employed; since these workers were still working, the claim failed. [Para 172][Para 178][Para 179][Para 181][Para 185][Para 187]
On issue 3 Article 67(2) caps a fixed-duration contract at two years and successive renewals beyond that convert by operation of law into an unlimited-duration contract; the panel applied its earlier jurisprudence to the 25 continuously renewed workers and ordered conversion. [Para 190][Para 191][Para 196][Para 199][Para 210][Para 215]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where successive fixed-duration contracts are renewed without interruption so that their total duration exceeds two years, the contract converts by operation of law into an unlimited-duration contract under Article 67(2); by contrast, the 5% severance under Article 73 becomes payable only when the employment relationship actually ends, not on each expiry followed by renewal. [Para 181][Para 199]

**Obiter dicta:** A disciplinary dismissal must be proportionate to the misconduct under Article 27; "playing around" at work did not justify termination, although the panel substituted compensation for reinstatement. Arbitrator Ing Sothy filed a dissenting opinion on issue 3, arguing that a renewed fixed-duration contract stays fixed-duration where the employer paid the 5% at each expiry. [Para 151][Para 219][Para 225][Para 230]
