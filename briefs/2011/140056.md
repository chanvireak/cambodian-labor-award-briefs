# Award No. 93/11 — IM & V International Garment (M&V 4, Kampong Chhnang branch) (Arbitration Council, 26 August 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Liv Sovanna, Kong Phalla presiding) · Doc ID 140056

## 1. Facts
The Free Trade Union of Workers of the Kingdom of Cambodia and the Khmer Democratic Labour Union, representing workers at the M&V 4 garment factory at Trapeang Chheuk village, Kampong Chhnang, raised 14 demands covering night-shift rotation, meal money, supervisory conduct, piece rates and the IE production system. Conciliation on 4 July 2011 settled 7; the hearing on 10 August 2011 settled 5 more, leaving two: the night-shift meal allowance and whether the employer must abandon the IE system. The factory employed 4,635 workers, and neither union held a most-representative certificate. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 14][Para 18][Para 19][Para 51][Para 53]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must keep paying the extra 0.12 USD on top of the 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance to night-shift workers? [Para 54][Para 77]
2. Whether the employer must stop using the IE (Industrial Engineering) system at M&V 4 because it has pushed piece rates down? [Para 58][Para 103]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Before Notification 041/11 the company paid two tiers of overtime meal money — 1,000 riel in the first overtime round and 0.12 USD in the second — and it should keep both rather than substitute 2,000 riel for the 0.12 USD. Since IE was introduced in April 2011 most workers in the ironing, checking and sewing sections earn less than the minimum wage, because IE raises output targets while cutting the time allowed. [Para 54][Para 56][Para 58][Para 61]

**Employer (respondent):** It never paid meal money in two tiers; it added 0.12 USD to the 1,000 riel only because 1,000 riel seemed too little. After Notification 041/11 took effect it paid 2,000 riel, which is more and more appropriate, and dropped the 0.12 USD. Only about 2% of workers sew below the minimum wage, and the company tops up to the base wage whenever output falls short. [Para 55][Para 57][Para 62][Para 79][Para 81]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Partly granted. The panel dismissed the claim for the extra 0.12 USD on top of the 2,000 riel meal allowance. It dismissed the demand to abolish IE but ordered the employer to review and adjust the IE system so that workers of average skill, at 60% and above, working normally for the same period, earn at least the legally guaranteed minimum wage. Both orders are rights disputes and became final immediately under the 28 September 2010 Memorandum of Understanding. [Para 108][Para 110][Para 111]

On the meal allowance, the panel classified the claim as a rights dispute because it turned on Notification 041/11 of 7 March 2011, whose item 2 provides 2,000 riel a day or one free meal for workers who volunteer for overtime at the employer's request. It found the employer had applied that notification and that the 2,000 riel was higher and more appropriate than the previous 1,000 riel plus 0.12 USD, so there was no obligation to add the 0.12 USD. [Para 73][Para 76][Para 77][Para 80][Para 81][Para 82]

On IE, the panel accepted that an employer may set or change piece rates, but held that it must set them so a worker of average skill working normally can earn at least the minimum wage — 56 USD a month during probation and 61 USD for full-right workers (50 USD base plus 5 USD increment plus 6 USD living allowance). It weighed the claimant's July 2011 payroll for 122 checking-section workers, showing about 60% sewing below the minimum even including overtime, against the employer's own table for 23 workers showing about 50% below. Because the employer produced no further argument against the claimant's evidence, the panel found the IE rollout unreasonable and unlawful in its management effect, even though the employer topped up the shortfall. It confined the remedy to the checking section, the only section for which evidence was produced. [Para 97][Para 101][Para 103][Para 104][Para 105][Para 106][Para 107][Para 70][Para 71]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may introduce or change a piece-rate or IE system, but the rates must be calibrated so that a worker of average skill working normally earns at least the guaranteed minimum wage; a system under which a majority of workers fall below the minimum even while working overtime is unreasonably managed and will be ordered reviewed, notwithstanding top-up payments. [Para 101][Para 103][Para 105][Para 107]

**Obiter dicta:** Payment of the statutory 2,000 riel overtime meal allowance discharges the obligation in full; an employer that previously paid a higher composite amount may reduce it to the statutory figure without owing the difference. [Para 81][Para 82]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is internally inconsistent on the number of demands: para 14 records 14 claims of which 7 were settled at conciliation and para 15 records 7 unsettled, but the "issues in dispute" section lists only seven numbered demands (paras 2–8). Para 37 dates the company's registered internal rules 4 December 2022, eleven years after the award, which is impossible and is either an OCR error or a document error. The signature block is badly corrupted by OCR: para 113 renders the employer-side signature as "mont", and para 114 gives "2017:555" and "unby" in place of the worker-side and presiding arbitrators' signatures. The employer's name appears variously as "IM & V", "M & V 4" and "M&V4" throughout. Individual workers and union officials are anonymised here per the briefing convention.
