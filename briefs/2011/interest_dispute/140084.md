# Award No. 129/11 — M&V Garment International Co., Ltd (Kampong Chhnang Branch) (Arbitration Council, 4 November 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (chair Pen Bunchhea; Mar Samborna for the employer, Tuon Siphan for the workers) · Doc ID 140084

## 1. Facts
M&V Garment International Co., Ltd runs a Kampong Chhnang branch employing about 5,000 workers and hosting nine factory unions. [Para 118][Para 119] On 1 October 2011 the company stopped employing nine union leaders and activists whose fixed-duration contracts all expired on 30 September 2011; the workers had been hired on two-month probation then rolled over on three-month contracts. [Para 122][Para 123][Para 125] A strike notice of 1 October 2011 raised eight demands; conciliation settled seven, leaving only the demand that the nine be taken back. [Para 15][Para 19]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must reinstate the nine union leaders and activists whose fixed-duration contracts expired on 30 September 2011, or whether their non-renewal was lawful and free of union discrimination? [Para 122]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The nine were dismissed on 1 October 2011 without cause and because of their union role; the union had elected its leadership on 4 September 2011 and notified the company on 7 September 2011, and in past contract cycles the company always told workers to return within 7 or 10 days, whereas this time it would give no date at all. [Para 135][Para 136][Para 137]
**Employer (respondent):** It acknowledged receiving the union notification but denied any link to union activity: there were no purchase orders in October 2011, no work to assign, and the fixed-duration contracts had simply reached their term; it had also suffered garment thefts from containers, which it reported to the National Police. [Para 139][Para 140][Para 180]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the demand that the company take the nine back into employment. [Para 183][Para 184]

The panel first separated the dispute into rights disputes and interest disputes, the parties having chosen a non-binding award on interest. [Para 20][Para 24] On the single live rights issue it examined whether the non-renewal of fixed-duration contracts owed to union membership. Reading Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law together, it held that an employer may not use union membership or union activity as the basis for hiring or job allocation. [Para 172][Para 173][Para 174] It then placed the burden on the workers to prove discrimination, following its own earlier awards. [Para 176]

On the facts the panel found the company's explanation credible: there was no work in October 2011, the contracts had run their term, and the same non-renewal was applied across the board — 29 workers in Section Check 1, of whom 20 accepted severance and only these nine refused, and 14 in Section Check 2, all of whom accepted severance, with no replacements hired. [Para 128][Para 129][Para 130][Para 178][Para 179] The workers did not contest the theft evidence, and the panel found they had not produced enough evidence for an inference of anti-union discrimination. [Para 180][Para 181][Para 182] It therefore dismissed the reinstatement claim. [Para 183]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer declines to renew a fixed-duration contract of a union leader or activist at its expiry, and the non-renewal is applied uniformly across a section because there is no work, the workers bear the burden of proving that union membership caused the decision; absent such proof the non-renewal is lawful and no reinstatement is ordered. [Para 176][Para 181]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that Article 12 and Article 279 bar an employer from basing hiring or job allocation on union membership, and that the Labour Law's requirement to seek labour-inspector authorisation applies only to dismissal, not to the expiry of a fixed-duration contract. [Para 167][Para 174]
