# Award No. 40/11 — Yang Guang (Cambodia) Garment Pte., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 20 April 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 140017

## 1. Facts
Yang Guang (Cambodia) Garment Pte., Ltd, a Phnom Penh garment factory employing over 600 workers, faced a 13-issue collective dispute raised by the Local Union of NIFTUC, which represented about 600 workers. [Para 1][Para 27] Six issues were referred to the Council; four were resolved at the hearing and issue 2 was partly resolved, leaving issues 2 and 6. [Para 16][Para 17] Issue 2 concerned the employer's deduction of 1,000 riels in union contribution fees and its refusal to repay earlier deductions; issue 6 concerned 17 workers holding successively renewed six-month fixed duration contracts whose total service exceeded two years. [Para 28][Para 32][Para 51]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must refrain from deducting 1,000 riels in union contribution fees from the wages of workers who did not agree to the deduction, and repay contribution fees already deducted without authorisation? [Para 28]
2. Whether the employer must convert the fixed duration contracts of the 17 listed workers whose total service exceeds two years to undetermined duration contracts? [Para 51]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the union submitted a list of members who had agreed to the deduction, but the employer had earlier deducted fees from workers not on that list; the 17 workers had successive six-month contracts totalling more than two years and were entitled to conversion. [Para 28][Para 29][Para 30][Para 55]
**Employer (respondent):** it was checking the union's list and had not yet made the new deductions; it received no complaints about past deductions; and it had paid 5% severance on the expiry of each fixed duration contract, so the contracts remained fixed. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 63]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — on issue 2 the panel ordered the employer to deduct union contribution fees in accordance with the list submitted by the Local Union of NIFTUC and rejected the demand to repay earlier deductions; on issue 6 it ordered the employer to convert the contracts of the 17 listed workers to undetermined duration contracts. The award was non-binding. [Para 44][Para 50][Para 57]

On issue 2 the panel applied Article 129(2) of the Labour Law and Clause 5 of Prakas No. 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001: a worker may authorise the employer to deduct union dues, but the authorisation must be in writing and may be revoked at any time. [Para 37][Para 38] Following Awards 03/03-Tonga, 05/03-Top One Garment, 16/05-New Point and 62/04-Ecent, the panel held that the employer must deduct and forward dues once the union supplies an acceptable written list, and must be notified in writing of any change. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 42] Because the employer acknowledged receiving the NIFTUC request and list, the panel ordered deduction on that basis. [Para 43][Para 44] On repayment, the workers' evidence did not identify the dates of the deductions, and the workers admitted they knew of the deductions but did not complain; applying the burden-of-proof rule in Awards 79/05-Evergreen, 77/08-Xing Tai, 101/08-GDM and 108/08-Hugo, the panel found the evidence inconclusive and rejected the repayment demand. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 49][Para 50]

On issue 6 the panel applied Article 67(2) of the Labour Law, which caps a fixed duration contract at two years, and Article 73(5), which requires ten days' notice of expiry for contracts over six months and fifteen days for contracts over one year; without notice the contract is extended for its initial duration or deemed undetermined if its total length exceeds the Article 67 limit. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54][Para 55] Following Awards 36/06-Mondotex and 57/06-Evergreen, and the conversion orders in Awards 10/03-Jacqsintex and 36/06-Mondotex, the panel found the 17 workers had successively renewed six-month contracts totalling over two years. [Para 55] The employer had been ordered to object to the list by 31 March 2011 and failed to do so. [Para 33][Para 56] The panel therefore ordered conversion. [Para 57]

Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented on issue 6. In his view the employer may choose the contract type; each renewal did not exceed two years; the employer paid 5% severance under Article 73(6) on each expiry; and if each renewal stays under two years the contracts remain fixed. [Para 58][Para 60][Para 63][Para 64][Para 65] He warned that conversion would make the employer lose both the 5% severance already paid and the termination payment due on ending an undetermined contract. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 71]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Successive renewals of a fixed duration contract that bring the total term beyond two years convert the relationship into an undetermined duration contract under Article 67(2) and Article 73(5), notwithstanding payment of 5% severance at each expiry; the employer's failure to object to a worker list also leaves the listed service un contradicted. Separately, union contribution fees may be deducted only on the strength of a written authorisation list, and a claim to repay earlier deductions fails where the claimant cannot identify the dates or amounts deducted. [Para 37][Para 49][Para 55][Para 56]

**Obiter dicta:** the dissent's observations on the employer's double loss and on the proper reading of "renewal must not surpass two years" are not adopted by the majority; the discussion of the 28 September 2010 MoU and the binding/non-binding classification of rights and interests disputes is procedural. [Para 17][Para 22][Para 67][Para 76]
