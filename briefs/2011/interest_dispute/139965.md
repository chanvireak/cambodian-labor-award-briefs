# Award No. 05/11 — M & V International Manufacturing Ltd. (Branch 1) (Arbitration Council, 7 February 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, An Nan, Kong Phallack chairing) · Doc ID 139965

## 1. Facts
M & V International Manufacturing Ltd. (Branch 1) employed 294 workers and hosted six unions; the C.CAWDU local, representing 20 workers in the measurement and quality control sections, was registered on 30 December 2010 [Para 23]. Mr L (President) and Mr P (Secretary) were elected at a 12 October 2010 meeting, the union applied for registration that day, and the employer was notified of the result on 16 October 2010; on 21 October 2010 the employer objected to the Ministry that Mr P was not its worker [Para 24][Para 25][Para 26]. Both men worked in the ironing section on fixed duration contracts that expired on 30 November 2010 after seven days' notice of non-renewal, and neither accepted the severance and annual leave payments offered [Para 29][Para 35][Para 36]. Of the 30 contracts expiring that day, seven were renewed and 20 workers were rehired by 8 December 2010, but not the union leaders [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43][Para 44].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issues 1 and 5, combined) Whether the employer must rehire Mr L and Mr P and back pay their wages, because their contracts were not renewed for reasons of union discrimination? [Para 2][Para 10][Para 37]
This being a rights dispute, the panel broke it into two questions: whether the non-renewal of the fixed duration contracts complied with the procedure in the Labour Law, and whether the non-renewal resulted from union discrimination [Para 55].

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer renewed other ironing-section workers but refused to renew the two leaders after the leadership election, so the non-renewal was union discrimination; the leaders had received no warnings for misconduct [Para 30][Para 37].
**Employer (respondent):** The contracts simply expired and there was not enough work, textile factories including M & V having little work between October 2010 and March 2011; the non-renewal was not discriminatory and the two men were never barred from union activity once the union was registered [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40][Para 44].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the demand that the employer renew the two fixed duration contracts and back pay their wages is rejected, but the employer is ordered to submit the two workers to the same recruitment process as other workers, without discrimination, when it recruits [Para 83][Para 84]. The award is binding and immediately enforceable under the Memorandum of Understanding on Improving Industrial Relations in the Garment Industry of 28 September 2010 [Para 11][Para 85].
The panel first classified the dispute: under Article 312 paragraph 2 the Council decides rights disputes by law and others in equity, and a demand not grounded in the Labour Law, an agreement or a collective agreement raises an interests dispute [Para 51][Para 53]. Because non-renewal is governed by the Labour Law, the demand was a rights dispute [Para 55].
On procedure, Article 73 paragraph 1 provides that a fixed duration contract normally terminates at the specified ending date; the panel followed Awards 100/07-Hoyear and 122/10-Meng Yan in holding that such a contract expires automatically, that the parties' obligations end, and that neither party can force renewal without agreement [Para 57][Para 59][Para 60]. It then held that the special protection of Article 293 and Clause 3 paragraph 3 of Prakas No. 305 SKBY covers dismissal only, and cannot apply where a contract runs to its expiry date, following Awards 34/05-Jacqsintex and 10/09-New Wide; renewal therefore needed no Labour Inspector authorisation [Para 60][Para 62][Para 64][Para 65].
On discrimination, Articles 12 and 279 bar any account of union membership, and the panel accepted, following Award 28/07-Dae Kwang, that those articles reach a decision not to renew an expired contract [Para 68]. The burden of proof lay on the workers [Para 68]. On the evidence, the employer's practice was seven days' notice countersigned by the worker, a notice board advertisement and attendance at the production section for recruitment; the two leaders neither signed the notices nor attended the production section as the others did, and the employer's 21 October 2010 objection letter, though showing it knew of the union and was dissatisfied, was not followed by any proven discriminatory act [Para 71][Para 75][Para 76][Para 79]. Recruitment depended on operational requirements, so the demand failed [Para 77][Para 82]. The panel nonetheless ordered that any future recruitment treat the two men like other applicants [Para 81][Para 83].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed duration contract that runs to its stated expiry date ends by operation of the contract, and the special protection of Article 293 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 305 SKBY protects against dismissal during the protected period, not against non-renewal, so no Labour Inspector authorisation is required [Para 62][Para 64].
Articles 12 and 279 nonetheless forbid an employer to let union membership decide whether to renew an expired contract, and the workers bear the burden of proving it; where the employer's own recruitment steps were not followed by the dismissed leaders, the burden is not discharged [Para 68][Para 76].

**Obiter dicta:** Recruitment is a matter of the employer's operational requirements, and a worker whose contract has expired may still apply through the ordinary process [Para 77][Para 80].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Paragraph 66 repeats its own question in garbled form ("Was the non-renewal of the workers" contracts the result of union contracts , the result of union") and omits numbered questions 1 and 2 as separate lines. Paragraph 16 dates a Prime Ministerial recommendation "29 September 2012", two years after the events and after the award. The exhibit list describes a strike "from the 13th to the 16th [month not specified]" [Para 18]. The panel refers at Para 44 and Para 71 to "the three leaders of the union" although only two leaders, Mr L and Mr P, are named and claimed. The hearing date is printed with an unclosed bracket ("[21 January 2011") [Para 5]. Amounts are printed only as a main wage of US$ 61 per month and severance of 5% of wages [Para 29][Para 35].
