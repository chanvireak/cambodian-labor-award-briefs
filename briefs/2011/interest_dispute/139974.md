# Award No. 12/11 — Dai Young Cambodia Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 25 February 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 139974

## 1. Facts
Dai Young Cambodia Co., Ltd. has operated on National Road 5, Russei Keo District, Phnom Penh since 2003 and employs about 950 workers; the claimant is the Local Union of CLUF, which was not formally registered, claimed 717 members and held only an acknowledgement receipt for its registration application. [Para 2][Para 18][Para 19][Para 20] Fifteen demands were referred by non-conciliation report No. 060 KB/RK/VK of 19 January 2011; further conciliation at the hearing resolved nine, leaving issues 3, 5, 6, 7, 11 and 15. [Para 6][Para 14] Because the union lacked registration, the panel considered the demands of the 366 workers who had authorised its president in writing on 29 January 2011. [Para 66][Para 67]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay maternity payment all at once when a worker commences maternity leave? [Para 20]
2. (Issue 5) Whether the employer must provide an additional US$ 10 milk allowance? [Para 29]
3. (Issue 6) Whether the employer must provide a 3,000 riel meal allowance for every hour of overtime work? [Para 36]
4. (Issue 7) Whether the employer must provide a 1,000 riel meal allowance for work on public holidays and an additional 1,000 riel for work on Sundays? [Para 40]
5. (Issue 11) Whether the employer must deduct 1,000 riel from wages for union contribution fees? [Para 45]
6. (Issue 15) Whether the employer must deduct from the attendance bonus in proportion to the number of days of authorised leave taken? [Para 53]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** ninety days of maternity pay must be paid before leave because mothers need the money for childbirth and earlier rulings so require; the US$ 4 milk allowance no longer covers formula; they cannot afford food and night transport on the present overtime allowances; a holiday and Sunday allowance is owed; the employer must honour its promise to deduct union dues; and taking US$ 3 from a US$ 5 bonus for one day's leave out of 26 is unconscionable. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 33][Para 38][Para 44][Para 51][Para 57]
**Employer (respondent):** paying two months before and one month after leave is what brings mothers back on time; it already pays a US$ 4 milk allowance in lieu of a day-care centre since 2007; it already pays 1,000 riel for overtime from 4:00 to 6:00 p.m. and 1,500 riel from 6:00 to 8:00 p.m., and pays above minimum wage; it will deduct dues once the union is registered and thumbprints verified; and the attendance bonus has been conditional since 2003. [Para 21][Para 26][Para 31][Para 39][Para 46][Para 47][Para 54][Para 58]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — under Part I (binding award on rights) the employer was ordered to pay maternity payment all at once when a worker commences maternity leave and to deduct the attendance bonus in proportion to days of authorised absence, while the union dues demand (issue 11) was declined; under Part II (non-binding award on interests) issues 5, 6 and 7 were declined. [Para 152][Para 153][Para 154][Para 155][Para 156]

The panel first held that, under Article 268 of the Labour Law, a professional organisation enjoys the rights recognised by the law only once registered, following 62/06-Quicksew, 30/08-E. Garment, 31/08-South Bay, 161/09-Prek Treng and 40/10-Meng Yan; the local union had no certificate and so could not represent its members, but Clause 19 of Prakas No. 099 allows representation by a person expressly authorised in writing, which limited the case to the 366 signatories. [Para 60][Para 63][Para 66][Para 67] It then applied the Article 312 and Clause 43 distinction between rights disputes, decided on law, and interests disputes, decided in equity, an interests dispute being one with no basis in the law, an agreement or a collective agreement. [Para 68][Para 69] On issue 3, Article 115(3) requires payment a day early where payday falls on a day off, and awards 57/06-Evergreen and 97/06-New Max Garment require maternity pay before leave begins; the employer's split payment was therefore not legitimate and three months' wages were ordered before leave. [Para 72][Para 74][Para 75][Para 76] On issue 5, Articles 184 and 186 provide breastfeeding time, a nursing room and a day-care centre or payment of external fees, but nothing supports an extra US$ 10; the demand was an interests dispute which a union without MRS or registration could not bring. [Para 78][Para 84][Para 87][Para 90] On issue 6, point 4 of Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 gives 1,000 riel a day or a free meal without fixing hours, following 25/10-High Born, and since 2,500 riel was already paid the demand exceeded the law and was declined. [Para 92][Para 93][Para 96][Para 98] On issue 7, work on public holidays is not overtime (115/08-Top One, 109/09-USA), whereas Sunday work is (114/08-Whitex), but the employer already paid the 1,000 riel allowance, so no more was owed. [Para 109][Para 119][Para 120] On issue 11, the panel reconciled Articles 129 and 281 — the latter protecting workers from employer interference — as permitting deduction on written authorisation, but since the union was unregistered the question was a future dispute the panel could not decide. [Para 127][Para 128][Para 139][Para 142] On issue 15, the attendance bonus is wage under Article 103, and absence with permission suspends the contract under Article 71(6) and Article 72(1), so the bonus may be deducted only proportionately, as ordered in 57/07-Seratex, 106/07-M & V 3 and 107/08-Seratex. [Para 144][Para 146][Para 149][Para 151] Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented on issue 15, arguing that a bonus is conditional and that a worker who does not satisfy the employer's condition is entitled to no part of it. [Para 158][Para 163][Para 174]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Maternity pay for the statutory ninety days must be paid in a single sum before the worker commences maternity leave; a practice of paying two months in advance and the third month on return is not lawful, because Article 115(3) requires payment before a day off and the panel's earlier awards require the payment before leave. [Para 72][Para 75][Para 76]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that it cannot know when the union will obtain registration, and so has no facts on which to decide the dues question, and its observation that the workers' distrust of the employer's promise is understandable, are explanatory rather than operative. [Para 136][Para 138][Para 140]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The evidence section is omitted from the English text ("This section has been omitted in the English version of this arbitral award"). [Para 17][Para 18]
- The face of the award announces a dissenting opinion by Arbitrator Ing Sothy, who also sat as the employer-party arbitrator; the dissent runs as an annex and repeats the notification figure as US$ 7 where the body states a US$ 5 bonus. [Para 1][Para 54][Para 174]
- The union's acknowledgement receipt is dated 6 December 2010 in the reasons and 25 January 2011 in the facts. [Para 20][Para 63]
- The complaint is recorded as coming from "C.CAWDU" although the claimant throughout is the Local Union of CLUF. [Para 6]
- Arbitrator Liv Sovanna withdrew for urgent commitments and was replaced by reserve Arbitrator An Nan, whose name is split onto its own numbered line. [Para 10][Para 11]
- Long passages repeat their opening clause ("Dai Young Cambodia Co Ltd commenced operation in 2003 and employs" printed twice) and the OCR drops "e" and "t" after "th" ("themployer", "thearing", "maternity leave" for "maternity leave"). [Para 18][Para 33]
