# Award No. 39/11 — M&V International Manufacturing Ltd. (Branch 1) (Arbitration Council, 11 April 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Tuon Siphann for the worker party, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 140020

## 1. Facts
M&V International Manufacturing Ltd. (Branch 1), a Phnom Penh factory operating since 1993 with 294 workers and four unions, none of which held most representative status (MRS); the claimant Local Union of VKYUF represented 65 workers. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 29][Para 30] Twenty issues were conciliated and five were referred; none was resolved at the hearing. [Para 22][Para 23] The five issues concerned wages for 19 senior stitching workers from 15 November 2010 to 30 January 2011 and alleged discrimination (issues 1 and 4, combined), annual-leave payments (issue 2), a US$ 6 accommodation or transport allowance (issue 3), and breastfeeding-break pay for piece-rate workers (issue 5). [Para 31][Para 48][Para 50][Para 53]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay wages to the 19 senior stitching workers for 15 November 2010 to 30 January 2011 and refrain from discriminating against them? [Para 31]
2. Whether payment in lieu of annual leave must be calculated on the workers' average wages over the last 12 months? [Para 48]
3. Whether the employer must pay US$ 6 per month as an accommodation or transportation allowance? [Para 50]
4. Whether payment for the one-hour breastfeeding break must be calculated on the average wages of piece-rate workers? [Para 53]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** they attended work but the employer gave them no work; they were not on strike; leave pay should use average wages; their low piece-rate wages justified the allowance; and the breastfeeding break should be paid at average piece-rate wages. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46][Para 47][Para 49][Para 50][Para 53]
**Employer (respondent):** the 19 workers refused the assigned purchase number and were on strike; annual leave was paid on the main wage; there was no allowance policy; and the break was paid on the main wage. [Para 37][Para 44][Para 49][Para 50][Para 53]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered payment for pieces completed on 15 November 2010, rejected wages for 16 November 2010 to 30 January 2011, rejected payment in lieu of annual leave and ordered the employer to allow leave, declined the allowance demand, and ordered breastfeeding-break pay calculated on average daily wages divided by working hours. [Para 105]

On 15 November the workers worked the full day but completed different purchase numbers; the employer allowed this and did not stop them, so Article 102 required payment for the completed pieces. [Para 56][Para 60] From 16 November the workers refused the assigned purchase number and stopped work to press their piece-rate demand; that was a strike under Article 318, the contract was suspended under Article 332(1), and no wages were due for the stoppage. [Para 74][Para 75][Para 76] The employer's work assignment was a lawful exercise of its prerogative under Article 2(2) (AAs 62/06 Quicksew, 108/06 Trinunggal, 33/07 Goldfame, 106/07 M&V, 84/08 Trinunggal, 141/08 Bloom Time), and there was no discrimination. [Para 65][Para 68]

On annual leave, Article 168 requires payment based on average wages, bonuses and benefits over the preceding 12 months, and Article 167(3) forbids payment in lieu of leave during employment; the employer's yearly payment practice was contrary to Article 167, so the panel ordered it to allow leave. [Para 77][Para 79][Para 82][Para 86] The US$ 6 allowance had no basis in law or agreement, so it was an interests dispute; because the union lacked MRS, the panel declined to consider it. [Para 86][Para 87][Para 89][Para 93]

On breastfeeding, Notification No. 046 set a minimum wage of US$ 61 for regular piece-rate workers; Article 163 required holiday indemnity, and public holidays counted as working days; the hourly rate was therefore average daily wages divided by working hours. [Para 96][Para 99][Para 102][Para 105]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A refusal to work on the purchase number assigned by the employer, maintained to press a wage demand, is a strike under Article 318; Article 332(1) suspends the labour contract and no wages are owed for the stoppage, even where the workers attended the factory. Work actually completed on unassigned pieces before the stoppage must be paid under Article 102. [Para 60][Para 74][Para 76]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's discussion of the employer's managerial prerogative and the absence of discrimination, and its observation that an award on an interests dispute becomes a one-year collective agreement, provided context for the standing analysis rather than deciding any demand. [Para 65][Para 91]
