# Award No. 180/11 — Whitex (Garment) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 27 January 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · three-member panel (Seng Vouch Hun, employer-appointed; Tuon Si Phan, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 140118

## 1. Facts
Whitex (Garment) Co., Ltd. runs a Phnom Penh factory employing 1,600 workers, 366 of whom belong to the Khmer Youth Union federation that brought this collective dispute [Para 58][Para 59]. The union filed eleven demands on working conditions; conciliation failed on all eleven, and at the hearing of 5 January 2012 the parties settled eight, leaving demands 1, 3 and 9 [Para 18][Para 21][Para 22]. Demand 1 concerns Mr M, a machine operator hired in October 2006 and elected union president, whom the company dismissed on 13 June 2011 for six days' absence without leave after the labour inspector consented [Para 61][Para 62][Para 68]. Demands 3 and 9 concern a daily meal allowance for overtime work and a repeat special health examination at company expense [Para 72][Para 86].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must take Mr M back as union president, and if it does not, whether it must pay termination compensation, notice pay, unused annual leave and outstanding wages? [Para 60][Para 93]
2. Whether the company must pay a meal allowance for overtime, or provide one free meal, on each day that overtime is worked? [Para 72][Para 148]
3. Whether the company must send all workers for a fresh special health examination at its own expense where it already paid for one at hiring? [Para 86][Para 176]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr M was absent because of a road accident on 31 May 2011 that injured his hand; he sent a leave application through a co-worker, which the company denies receiving [Para 63][Para 64]. Reinstatement is the primary claim; in the alternative the company owes severance and final payments under Articles 89, 75, 116 and 166 [Para 60]. On meals, workers buy food daily or borrow money to eat and some faint from insufficient food; they invoke Ministry Notification No. 041 [Para 73][Para 76][Para 77]. On health, a special check was given once at hiring and the law obliges the employer to repeat it [Para 86][Para 87].
**Employer (respondent):** The dismissal followed the labour inspector's approval under Article 293 and the company relies on that decision [Para 71]. It pays the meal allowance twice a month, as it has for about seven years, receives its funds from Malaysia twice monthly and cannot pay daily [Para 78][Para 79][Para 82]. It cannot afford a repeat health check but will comply if the law requires it [Para 89][Para 90].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement refused; the company was ordered to pay Mr M notice pay equal to one month's wages, dismissal compensation, unused annual leave and outstanding final wages (75 days' wages in total for the compensation element), to give a meal allowance of 2,000 riel or one free meal on each overtime day, and the repeat health check claim was dismissed [Para 186][Para 187][Para 188]

On issue 1 the panel first classified the demand as a rights dispute because reinstatement and severance are written in the Labour Law, following its earlier awards 05/11, 13/11 and 14/11 [Para 94][Para 95][Para 98]. Article 293 requires the employer to obtain the labour inspector's agreement before dismissing a union leader; the company applied on 13 June and the inspector agreed on 16 June 2011, having investigated twice without reporting back to the company [Para 67][Para 99][Para 104][Para 105]. The workers' appeal to the Minister of 8 August 2011 drew no reply, and under Article 293 silence for two months operates as refusal, leaving the inspector's decision effective [Para 101][Para 106][Para 107]. The panel departed from Award 91/04 (Haniver Garment), which held that approval strips the union leader of protection but leaves the dismissal reviewable on its merits; it read Article 293 as protecting the leader only up to the point of approval [Para 111][Para 112][Para 114]. Because both parties already held the inspector's decision, the panel declined to reconsider the claim further and refused reinstatement [Para 116][Para 118]. It held that refusal of reinstatement did not extinguish the money claims, and awarded notice pay under Articles 75 and 77, dismissal compensation under Article 89 at 15 days' wages per year of service covering four years seven months, unused annual leave under Article 166 and final wages under Article 116 [Para 119][Para 122][Para 128][Para 132][Para 141]. Moral damages under Article 91 were refused because the dismissal was justified [Para 138][Para 139].

On issue 3 the panel also found a rights dispute, and asked whether meal money forms part of the overtime wage; Article 103 lists overtime pay as wages but not meal money, and the panel held the allowance is a substitute for food, not earnings from extra hours [Para 145][Para 153][Para 157]. Notification No. 041 of 7 March 2011 nevertheless requires 2,000 riel per day or one free meal for volunteers working overtime, without fixing the number of hours, and payment must be daily; paying twice monthly did not comply, so the company was ordered to provide it on each overtime day [Para 160][Para 162][Para 163][Para 169].

On issue 9, Article 247 and Prakas No. 09 of 19 December 1994 require the employer to arrange and pay for special examinations, but the panel found no provision requiring a repeat where one was given at hiring; the company had complied then, so the claim was dismissed even though two workers fainted in 2011 [Para 177][Para 178][Para 182][Para 183][Para 185].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the labour inspector has agreed to the dismissal of a union leader, an appeal to the Minister left unanswered for two months is deemed refused under Article 293, and the panel will not re-examine the merits of that dismissal; the worker's statutory termination entitlements — notice pay, dismissal compensation, annual leave and final wages — survive the refusal of reinstatement. Separately, meal support for overtime must be furnished on each overtime day, and twice-monthly payment does not satisfy Notification No. 041 [Para 107][Para 119][Para 160].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remarks that a free meal supplies additional strength for future overtime and that rice is an unavoidable daily need, and its note that other workers might prefer cash, were not necessary to the order [Para 84][Para 164].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The hire date appears as 25 October 2006 in the facts and 26 October 2006 in the reasons [Para 61][Para 127]; the dismissal date is 13 June 2011 in the facts but printed "13 December June 2011" in the reasons [Para 62][Para 127]. The case number is 180/11 on the face but "10180/11" in the union's objection letter [Para 1][Para 53], and the non-conciliation report is numbered 1426 in one line and 1626 in another [Para 14][Para 18]. Article 89's graduated scale is garbled by OCR, mixing figures that do not belong to a 2011 award, and the worker-side arbitrator's name appears as Tuon Si Phan on the face and Tuon Su Pha at the signature block [Para 123][Para 191]. One worker's name is split mid-word in the reasoning on moral damages [Para 138].
