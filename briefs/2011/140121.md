# Award No. 183/11 — Great On Near Tech Style (Ltd) (Arbitration Council, 31 January 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · three-member panel (Mar Somborana, employer-appointed; Sin Kim Sean, worker-appointed; Kong Phallha, president) · Doc ID 140121

## 1. Facts
Great On Near Tech Style Ltd is a garment factory in Saang district, Kandal province, cutting, sewing, printing and packing socks, sleeves, masks and scarves with about 1,920 workers, whose union is the Democratic Union of Cambodian Garment Workers [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43]. Of eight demands, six were settled in conciliation on 2 December 2011 and a seventh at the hearing, leaving a single dispute about one worker [Para 12][Para 16]. The claimant, a packing worker since 17 July 2000, was elected a staff delegate on 11 August 2011 [Para 46][Para 47]. On 17 October 2011 he placed one shirt too many into a carton; the manager warned him orally, then required him to thumbprint a written warning, and when he refused, administration told him not to come back and sent him off the premises [Para 48][Para 50][Para 51]. The company denies dismissing him, saying he is barred until he acknowledges the fault and that no back pay is owed because he was at fault [Para 56][Para 57].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay the claimant his wages from 17 October 2011, when he was excluded from the workplace, until the day it allows him to work again? [Para 44][Para 61]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Worker (claimant):** He asks to be taken back and paid back wages for the whole period of exclusion, though by the hearing he no longer pressed for reinstatement in the terms of the settlement, which made his return conditional on a written admission of fault [Para 44][Para 45]. His exclusion breached Article 293 of the Labour Law and the internal rules; no rule allows dismissal for refusing to thumbprint a warning; and he had already been warned orally [Para 52]. The extra shirt was not a first offence [Para 53].
**Employer (respondent):** Where a worker packs more than the fixed number, the company issues a written warning or asks for a letter of admission, and it barred him only because he refused both [Para 54][Para 55]. It maintains that it never dismissed him and owes no back pay since he performed no work and admits no fault [Para 56][Para 57].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel declined to award full back wages but ordered the company to pay the claimant half of his base wage from 17 October 2011 until it permits him to return to work, the order taking effect immediately as a binding award [Para 84][Para 85][Para 86]

The panel treated the demand as a rights dispute, since the payment claimed is provided for by the Labour Law, and noted that the settlement of the other demands did not touch it [Para 60]. It began from Article 2(2), which lets an employer organise and direct the enterprise, reading it, as earlier awards did, as authority exercised reasonably and in good faith [Para 64]. Requiring the worker to thumbprint a warning or write an admission was, on that reading, within the employer's disciplinary authority [Para 68][Para 69]. The panel then found that the specific response was not: the internal rules contained no power to send a worker off the premises at once and bar him from returning, and the sanction applied was disproportionate to putting one extra shirt in a carton [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74]. It therefore held that the exercise of managerial authority in this instance was not reasonable [Para 74]. The worker was not blameless either: refusing to acknowledge a fault he had committed was itself a breach of discipline and of the duty to follow the employer's directions on discipline [Para 75][Para 77]. Since his contract was suspended by the employer's own act, the panel accepted that he had done no work and that the general rule gives no wage for no work [Para 79][Para 80]. It reasoned that paying him in full for work not done would be unjust to the employer and to the other workers who attended, while leaving him with nothing would be equally unjust to him [Para 81][Para 82]. Invoking its power under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 to order immediate payment of sums due, it fixed the compensation at one half of the base wage for the whole period of exclusion [Para 83][Para 84].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remark that the employer faced the cost of recruiting a replacement for the excluded worker played no part in the calculation; nor did its observation that he had been warned orally before the written warning was demanded [Para 49][Para 78].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Managerial authority to discipline includes requiring a worker to acknowledge a fault in writing, but a sanction that removes the worker from the workplace indefinitely, where the internal rules provide no such measure and the misconduct was minor, is disproportionate and not a reasonable exercise of that authority. Where the suspension flows from that unjustified order yet the worker also breached discipline by refusing to acknowledge the fault, the panel will apportion the loss and order half the base wage for the period of exclusion rather than full wages or nothing [Para 69][Para 74][Para 84].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is dated 31 January 2012, but one line sets the deadline for issuing it at "31 April 2016 / 2012" and the hearing is recorded as 11 January 2012 with a company letter of 30 December 2011 [Para 1][Para 22][Para 23][Para 10]. The internal rules relied on are those of "Win Ne" company, registered in 2001, not of the respondent, and the factory is named both Win Ne and Great On Near [Para 28][Para 41]. The dispositive text says "half wages (base wage)" without stating whether the half is of the base wage or of total earnings, and the same phrase is repeated in the reasons [Para 84][Para 85]. The company name is transliterated four different ways across the face, the evidence list and the order [Para 1][Para 25][Para 41]. The non-conciliation report number is given as 006/11 with two different reference suffixes [Para 8][Para 13][Para 36]. The page carrying Article 2 of the Labour Law is broken into three fragments, one consisting of a single character [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63].
