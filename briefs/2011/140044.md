# Award No. 82/11-Yakjin — Yakjin (Cambodia) Inc. (Arbitration Council, 27 July 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Arbitrators: Chhiv Phyrum (employer), Liv Sovanna (workers), Ang Eng Thong (chair) · Doc ID 140044

## 1. Facts
Yakjin (Cambodia) Inc. operates a garment factory employing 3,600 workers and holds a collective agreement, No. 285 KB/RK/VK dated 29 March 2011, with the Workers Trade Union. The Local Union of KWPFU, representing 40 workers, demanded that the employer allow workers to leave the factory during lunch breaks without written permission. The employer's existing practice requires workers to obtain written permission from the administration office, and the employer provides a free lunch worth 1,250 riel during the 11:00 a.m. to 12:00 p.m. break. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 11][Para 12][Para 13][Para 14]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer should allow workers to leave the factory during lunch breaks without requiring written permission? [Para 2]
2. Whether the employer's notification process—requiring a form signed by the group leader of line 6 and stamped by the administration office—is lawful and reasonable under the collective agreement? [Para 17][Para 20]
3. Whether workers must state a reason for leaving and leave their ID cards when exiting during lunch breaks? [Para 21][Para 22][Para 40]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Workers have the right to leave the factory during lunch breaks under Article 138 of the Labour Law and Clauses 6 and 20 of the collective agreement. They should only need to notify the administration office near the exit gate, without obtaining the signature of heads of sections, and should not have to leave their ID cards or state a reason for leaving. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 23][Para 24][Para 25]
**Employer (respondent):** The existing practice requires written permission from the administration office, which allows the employer to determine the number of workers for the free lunch and to ensure security. The notification form must be signed by the group leader of line 6 and stamped by the administration office. At the hearing, the employer offered to simplify the process: workers fill in the existing form, submit it to the group leader or assistant for signature, then to the administration office for stamping; the group leader will have a workstation, and illiterate workers will receive help. [Para 3][Para 4][Para 16][Para 20][Para 25][Para 26][Para 27][Para 28]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Partly granted — the panel rejected the workers' demand to notify only the administration office near the exit gate, but ordered the employer to simplify the notification process in accordance with the four points outlined at the hearing and ruled that workers do not have to state a reason for leaving in the notification form. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46]
The panel held that lunch breaks are not subject to the employer's management rights, so workers have the right to have lunch inside or outside the factory without informing the employer. [Para 31][Para 32] However, because the parties have a collective agreement on the provision of a free lunch, the panel considered it reasonable and lawful to require written permission and the signature of the group leader from line 6 before workers leave the factory. [Para 33] The employer needs to know the number of workers who will not eat the free lunch so that it can pay the lunch organiser, and the signature requirement is the employer's strategy to prevent cheating. [Para 34][Para 35] Workers should cooperate by informing the employer when they leave during lunch break to avoid doubt. [Para 36]
The panel acknowledged that the notification process is complicated: workers must state a reason for leaving and secure the group leader's signature, and some workers are illiterate. [Para 37] Workers claimed they did not want to state their reason due to privacy, and the panel noted that leaving during lunch is not within work hours. [Para 38][Para 39] Clause 20 of the collective agreement states that workers will be allowed to leave the premises during lunch break unless they notify their supervisors. [Para 39] Based on the collective agreement, the panel ruled that workers are not required to state a reason for leaving during the lunch period, because the employer only needs to know the number of workers available for the free lunch in order to pay the lunch organiser. [Para 40] The panel also noted the employer's offer at the hearing to simplify the process, including a workstation for the group leader and help for illiterate workers. [Para 41][Para 42] In conclusion, the panel rejected the workers' demand to notify only the administration office near the exit gate. [Para 43] The employer must simplify the notification process in accordance with the four points it outlined at the hearing, and workers do not have to state a reason for leaving in the notification form. [Para 45][Para 46]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The rule the panel applied is that while lunch breaks are not subject to the employer's management rights and workers have the right to leave the factory during lunch, a collective agreement providing a free lunch makes it reasonable and lawful to require written notification and a group leader's signature so the employer can determine the number of workers for the lunch payment and prevent cheating. However, the employer cannot require workers to state a reason for leaving during the lunch break, because the employer only needs to know the number of workers for the free lunch. [Para 31][Para 32][Para 33][Para 40]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the notification process was complicated for workers, particularly illiterate workers, and that workers claimed privacy concerns about stating a reason for leaving; these observations informed the order to simplify the process but were not strictly necessary to the rejection of the workers' demand. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39]
