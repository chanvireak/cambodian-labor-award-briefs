# Award No. 50/11 — Talent Garment Industry (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 26 May 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Ang Eng Thong chair) · Doc ID 140021

## 1. Facts
Talent Garment Industry (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a Phnom Penh garment factory employing 482 workers, 471 of them women, faced a collective dispute raised by the Local Union of KYFTU, which represents 200 workers. [Para 17][Para 18] On average 10 to 11 workers begin maternity leave each month; the employer pays them 90 days' wages including perquisites at 50% of the full rate, but pays monthly on the ordinary wage-payment day rather than in a lump sum before the leave starts. [Para 18][Para 19] The workers demanded payment in full before departure on maternity leave; the employer said the Labour Law did not require advance payment but could cite no article. [Para 19] Of the six original demands, four were conciliated and the second unresolved issue was settled at the hearing, leaving issue 1. [Para 5][Para 6][Para 8]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay pregnant workers three months' wages, including perquisites, at 50% of the full rate in a single payment before they begin maternity leave? [Para 1][Para 18]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the 90 days' wages and perquisites at 50% are a right of the worker and must be paid in full before she leaves to give birth, not in monthly instalments afterwards. [Para 19]
**Employer (respondent):** the Labour Law does not require the whole amount to be paid before the worker departs on leave, and the existing monthly payment on the usual pay day is lawful; it could not identify any article supporting that position. [Para 19]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay workers 90 days' wages including perquisites at 50% of the full rate prior to the commencement of their maternity leave. [Para 25][Para 26]

The panel first classified the demand as a rights dispute, because it concerned an entitlement in the Labour Law rather than a new benefit: wages and perquisites for women taking maternity leave. [Para 20] It applied Article 182(1), which grants 90 days' maternity leave, and Article 183(1), which entitles a woman during that leave to half her wage including perquisites, paid by the employer. [Para 22] The panel found as fact that the employer did pay 90 days at 50% but spread the payments monthly after the leave began. [Para 22][Para 23] It then asked whether the law requires payment in full before the leave commences, and took Article 115(3) — payment must not be made on a day off, and if payday falls on a day off it must be made a day earlier — as the statutory anchor. [Para 24] Reading that with its earlier awards, the panel held that the 90 days' wages at 50% must be paid before the worker starts maternity leave. [Para 25] It followed Awards 57/06-Evergreen (issue 6), 97/06-New Max (issue 1) and 08/08-Hytex Garment (issue 2), which had already ruled that the sum is payable in advance. [Para 25] The award was binding, as the parties had agreed in writing to binding arbitration of the rights dispute under the MoU of 28 September 2010. [Para 10][Para 26]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer owes 90 days' wages including perquisites at 50% of the full rate for maternity leave under Articles 182 and 183 of the Labour Law, that sum must be paid to the worker as a lump sum before she commences maternity leave; paying it in monthly instalments during or after the leave does not discharge the obligation. [Para 22][Para 25]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the employer could not cite any article for its position, and its reliance on the general payday rule in Article 115(3), are explanatory rather than independently operative; the order rests on the advance-payment reading of Articles 182 and 183. [Para 19][Para 24]

- The facts sentence "Talent Garment Industry (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Talent Garment) employs a total of Ltd (Talent Garment) employs a total of Talent Garment Industry (Cambodia) Co 482 workers" is duplicated and garbled by OCR; the figure 482 is clear. [Para 17]
- The panel composition is printed in the header and repeated at the signature block; the employer-party arbitrator's name appears as "Mar Samborana" and the worker-party arbitrator as "Liv Sovanna", both consistent. [Para 1][Para 27][Para 28]
- The award date is 26 May 2011 in the header; the hearing was 6 May 2011 and the non-conciliation report 26 April 2011, submitted 27 April 2011. [Para 1][Para 4][Para 6]
- The text says "four of the six issues were conciliated" but only two non-conciliated issues were referred, so the arithmetic implies two further issues were withdrawn or merged; the award does not explain the discrepancy. [Para 5][Para 6]
