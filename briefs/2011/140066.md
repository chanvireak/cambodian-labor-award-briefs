# Award No. 107/11 — San Nging Texstyle Corp (Chang Long) (Arbitration Council, 9 September 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Yuk Soun, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 140066

## 1. Facts
The company, San Nging Texstyle Corp (Chang Long), employed about 400 workers at Trapeang Thlong, Chom Chao, Dangkao, Phnom Penh. [Para 45] Mr V Sophea was taken on as a machine sewer in February 2011 on an oral trial contract, then signed a three-month fixed-term contract from 8 April to 8 July 2011 at USD 100 a month; he was also president of the Khmer Youth Union at the factory. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49] On 1 July 2011 the employer gave written notice that the contract would not be renewed, which he refused to acknowledge. [Para 50][Para 51] Separately, from April to June 2011 the company had no work and paid only 50% of wages, without agreement and without any suspension authorised by the labour inspector. [Para 68][Para 70]

## 2. Issues in dispute
The collective dispute began with eight demands; four were settled at conciliation and two (medical examination and employee-delegate elections) were withdrawn, leaving two issues. [Para 20][Para 21]
1. Whether the employer must take back Mr V Sophea and let him work normally again as president of the Khmer Youth Union? [Para 2][Para 78]
2. Whether the company must pay wages and other benefits at 100% for the period when it had no work for the workers to do? [Para 3][Para 101]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr V Sophea was a union leader entitled to protection, and his non-renewal was retaliation for that role; the 50% payment during the work shortage was unlawful because the company neither notified them in advance nor obtained a proper suspension from the labour inspector. [Para 66][Para 71]
**Employer (respondent):** The contract simply reached its term; his machine skills were still weak and his intestinal illness, with six days of authorised leave in June 2011, made him unable to do the heavy lifting the job requires. The 50% wage during idle periods was the company's own standing practice, applied sometimes with and sometimes without prior notice. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 70][Para 72]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Partly granted — issue 1 dismissed and issue 2 granted, the panel ordering the employer to pay 100% wages to the workers for the period when the company had no work and had not lawfully suspended the contracts. [Para 100][Para 117][Para 120]

On issue 1 the panel held that Article 293 of the Labour Law protects employee delegates and union leaders against dismissal, but prior awards had already confined it to dismissal and excluded the expiry of a fixed-term contract. [Para 79][Para 80] Non-renewal is not dismissal: dismissal means the employer terminates a contract while it is still running, before its term. [Para 84] Mr V Sophea's contract ran to 8 July 2011 and the employer gave notice on 1 July, seven days ahead, which the panel found more favourable than the statutory requirement; the notice obligation only bites for contracts over six months, and his was three. [Para 87][Para 91][Para 92] The panel accepted the stated reasons — weak machine skills compared with the two remaining skilled sewers, and the physical demands of the job against his illness — and found the non-renewal justified. [Para 93][Para 95][Para 98][Para 99]

On issue 2 the panel applied Article 71(11) of the Labour Law: a suspension of activity must be placed under the supervision of the labour inspector. [Para 103][Para 104] Where the company failed to lodge a suspension request and instead applied its own 50% rule, the suspension was its own unilateral act and unlawful under Article 71. [Para 114][Para 115] The employer therefore owed full wages notwithstanding that no work was available. [Para 116]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that cannot provide work and pays less than full wages must show a suspension approved and supervised by the labour inspector; failing that, it owes 100% of wages for the idle period. [Para 105][Para 113][Para 116] Conversely, the expiry of a fixed-term contract is not a dismissal, so the Article 293 protection of union leaders does not require the labour inspector's consent for non-renewal. [Para 80][Para 84][Para 85]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted the employer's stated willingness to take Mr V Sophea back once he had studied and improved, and treated the seven-day notice as better than the law required. [Para 92][Para 97]
