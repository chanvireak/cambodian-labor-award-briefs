# Award No. 141/11 — Dai Young (Cambodia) Co. (Arbitration Council, 30 November 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Oeng Sothy for the employer party, Toun Si Phan for the worker party — dissenting in part — Run Saray chair) · Doc ID 140089

## 1. Facts
Dai Young (Cambodia) Co., a garment factory on National Road 5 in Phnom Penh employing 1,050 workers, faced a collective complaint from the Cambodia Workers' Union Federation and its factory union over four unresolved meal-allowance and leave demands. [Para 35][Para 1] Of nine demands, five were settled in conciliation on 18 October 2011 and four were referred to the Council on 25 October 2011; the hearing was held on 3 November 2011 and the award issued on 30 November 2011. [Para 16][Para 17][Para 14][Para 26] The filing union held no certificate of most-representative status in the factory. [Para 36] As the parties had signed the memorandum of understanding of 28 September 2010, the panel split its decision into rights disputes (binding at once) and interest disputes (not binding). [Para 21][Para 22][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a 2,500 riel meal allowance for voluntary overtime worked from 18:00 to 20:30, instead of the 2,000 riel it pays? [Para 37][Para 39]
2. Whether the employer must pay a 2,000 riel meal allowance for work performed on public holidays, instead of 1,000 riel? [Para 42][Para 65]
3. Whether the employer must grant seven days' paid special leave to a male worker whose wife is on maternity leave, without deducting wages, allowances or annual leave? [Para 46][Para 75]
4. Whether the employer must pay a 2,000 riel meal allowance for overtime worked on Sundays, instead of 1,000 riel? [Para 52][Para 105]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the extra 500 riel for late overtime and the extra 1,000 riel for holiday and Sunday work are owed because other factories pay them; and a husband should get seven days to care for his wife after childbirth without any deduction from wages, allowances or annual leave. [Para 39][Para 43][Para 49]
**Employer (respondent):** the law requires no more than what is already paid, so the old practice should stand for late overtime and holiday work; three days' special leave are granted with wages and allowances preserved, with anything beyond three days charged against annual leave and then against wages. [Para 3][Para 40][Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 50][Para 54]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demands for 2,500 riel late-overtime allowance and for a 2,000 riel public-holiday allowance were refused; the demand for seven days' special leave was refused, but the employer was ordered to stop deducting wages once the worker's annual leave is exhausted; and the employer was ordered to pay the 2,000 riel Sunday meal allowance. [Para 118][Para 119][Para 114][Para 115][Para 116]

On the two meal-allowance demands the panel applied its settled distinction: a rights dispute concerns an entitlement set by law, agreement or collective convention, an interest dispute a claim beyond what the law fixes. [Para 55] The 2,500 riel claim was held to exceed the legal entitlement, and so an interest dispute. [Para 59][Para 60] The public-holiday claim was treated the same way, the panel following prior awards holding that work on a public holiday is not overtime. [Para 66][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70] For interest disputes the panel examines most-representative status, and this union had no certificate; it further found that a different union holding the certificate had already brought the same demands in awards 88/11 and 89/11 of 15 August 2011. [Para 62][Para 63][Para 72][Para 73] It therefore refused both. [Para 64][Para 74] On the way it addressed res judicata: the principle bars re-hearing where the same party brings the same issue and the earlier decision was complete, but here the claimant union was not the claimant in the earlier case, so the principle did not apply and the panel decided the merits afresh. [Para 77][Para 79][Para 81][Para 82]

On special leave, Article 171 allows an employer to grant special leave for events affecting the worker's family, and Prakas No. 267 of 11 October 2001 permits up to seven paid days a year, including for a wife giving birth, while allowing the employer to charge those days against unused annual leave or to require the hours to be made up. [Para 87][Para 91][Para 92] Following award 99/04 AIA Garment, the panel held the employer may not simply cut wages for such leave, but may deduct it from annual leave, and once annual leave is used up it may require the worker to make up the time rather than docking pay. [Para 93][Para 94][Para 97][Para 101][Para 102] The company's practice of deducting from annual leave was therefore correct; its docking of wages after annual leave ran out was not. [Para 101]

On Sunday work the panel held the claim a rights dispute, because point 2 of Notification No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011 entitles a worker who volunteers for overtime to a 2,000 riel meal allowance or a free meal. [Para 107][Para 108][Para 109] The employer paid only 1,000 riel, so it had not complied, and the panel ordered the full 2,000 riel. [Para 110][Para 111][Para 112][Para 113]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Whether a meal allowance claim is a rights or an interest dispute turns on its source: where a Notification fixes the amount, as Notification 041/11 does for overtime meal allowances, non-payment is a rights dispute the panel will decide and enforce; where the claim exceeds what any instrument fixes, it is an interest dispute which a union lacking most-representative status cannot bring, especially where the certificated union has already claimed it. [Para 55][Para 63][Para 107][Para 109]
Special leave under Article 171 and Prakas 267 may be charged against unused annual leave, and once annual leave is exhausted the employer may require the worker to make up the hours, but it may never simply deduct the worker's wage for taking that leave. [Para 91][Para 92][Para 101][Para 102]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's treatment of res judicata — that the principle requires the same party, the same issue and a complete earlier decision, and so did not bar this claim — was reasoned at length but was not needed, since the interest-dispute claim failed on representative status in any event. [Para 77][Para 79][Para 81]

**Dissent:** the worker-party arbitrator dissented on issue 2, reasoning that work on a public holiday is overtime because the holiday is a statutory rest day, that it requires the worker's voluntary consent, and that a worker so working must receive the 2,000 riel meal allowance or a free meal under Notification 041/11 and Prakas No. 10 of 4 February 1999. [Para 122][Para 124][Para 125][Para 132][Para 135]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The dissenting arbitrator's name is printed as "Toun Si Phan" in the header and signature blocks [Para 1][Para 121] but as "Noun Si Phan" in the body of the dissent [Para 122]; both forms are reported, not reconciled.
- A stray "2016" is inserted mid-sentence in the reasoning on issue 1 [Para 61], and the dissent refers to "issue 2 of 2" in a case with four issues [Para 122].
- The Sunday meal-allowance demand is printed once as "20 riel" [Para 8] and elsewhere as 1,000 and 2,000 riel [Para 52][Para 110]; the 20 riel figure is plainly truncated.
- The claimant is named inconsistently — "Cambodia Workers' Union Federation" in the header [Para 1] and "Cambodia Labour Workers' Union Federation" in the res judicata discussion [Para 81] — and the two are treated here as the same body.
- Union officials, the employer's administration chief and its adviser are named in the source; each is reduced here to role only.
