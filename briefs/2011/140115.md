# Award No. 176/11 — All Souper Inter Bright (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 16 January 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Run Saray (president), Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed) · Doc ID 140115

## 1. Facts
All Souper Inter Bright (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. is a garment-cutting and sewing factory operating since 2005 with 1,750 workers and three unions on site. [Para 35][Para 36] On 14 November 2011 the union federation defending workers' rights filed a collective claim for improved conditions; conciliation on 19 December 2011 settled five of nine demands, and the file reached the Council on 22 December 2011. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 17] Further conciliation at the hearing settled two more, leaving issues 1 and 4, on which both parties chose a non-binding award. [Para 20][Para 21][Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must deduct only USD 1 per day from the USD 7 attendance bonus when a worker is absent sick with a valid public-hospital certificate and the employer's permission (panel's issue 1)? [Para 38][Para 54]
2. Whether the employer must provide two sets of work clothing per year to each worker (panel's issue 4)? [Para 49][Para 80]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The company deducts the whole USD 7 attendance bonus for any absence of a day or more, whether lawful or not, even where a public hospital certificate proves the illness; workers therefore ask that a certified sick day cost only USD 1 of the bonus, a practice they say other factories such as JY and Ing Jia already follow. [Para 39][Para 40][Para 43][Para 44] On clothing, workers recently surveyed said they want two sets of work clothes a year and the employer provides none. [Para 49]

**Employer (respondent):** Notification No. 041 of 7 March 2011 pays the USD 7 only to workers attending the required number of working days, except public holidays with pay and special leave the law grants, so a certified sick day still breaks the condition. [Para 41] On clothing, the employer cannot comply because when the factory opened the workers said they wanted to wear their own clothes and did not want work uniforms. [Para 51]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claim to limit the attendance-bonus deduction to USD 1 a day was dismissed, while the company was ordered to provide at least two sets of work clothing per worker per year free of charge; the award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent written objection. [Para 88][Para 89][Para 90]

On issue 1, the panel first established that the USD 7 attendance bonus is a bonus and therefore a form of wage under Article 103 of the Labour Law, and it adopted the three-case reading of "working days in the month" from Award No. 112/11-Yong Va I and Award No. 64/11-Im Vey: full working months, months reduced by public holidays, and months reduced by leave the employer authorises. [Para 56][Para 61][Para 62][Para 63] It held that certified sick leave falls in the third case and that the bonus should be pro-rated rather than forfeited. [Para 66][Para 67][Para 69] But the workers' specific demand was to cut only USD 1 per sick day, which the panel found lower than the protection the law guarantees: under Article 13 of the Labour Law its provisions are public order, and any rule or agreement giving less is void, so the panel cannot award less than the statutory floor even by consent. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 74][Para 75] The demand was therefore dismissed, not because the deduction was lawful but because granting it would sanction an entitlement below the legal minimum. [Para 77]

On issue 2, the panel applied Article 6 of Prakas No. 307 Kb/BrK of 14 December 2007 on hygiene and safety in garment and shoe factories, which requires the employer to supply each worker free of charge with at least two sets of clothing a year, the first within 15 days of starting work, each set comprising at least trousers or skirt and shirt in a size suited to the worker's body and tasks. [Para 80][Para 81][Para 82][Para 83] It found the company had never supplied any, and rejected the employer's explanation that workers had said they preferred their own clothes as a bare assertion and not a legal reason. [Para 85][Para 86] The order was accordingly made. [Para 87][Para 89]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An attendance bonus is a wage under Article 103, so it may be pro-rated for employer-authorised leave but not forfeited; nonetheless, because Article 13 makes Labour Law provisions public order, the panel will not order a benefit lower than the statutory floor even where the workers themselves ask for it, and will dismiss such a claim rather than grant it. [Para 63][Para 72][Para 77] Prakas No. 307 obliges every garment employer to issue at least two sets of work clothing free of charge each year; a worker preference for ordinary clothes, asserted by the employer without evidence, does not displace that duty. [Para 80][Para 86]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remark that the company's practice of deducting the entire bonus is unlawful, though it could not be remedied because of how the claim was framed, is an observation not necessary to the dismissal. [Para 69][Para 77]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The company name appears in Khmer in several spellings across the face, exhibits and reasons and is given here in a single transliteration; the trade-registration certificate number is also incomplete ("២៩៨៦ ព ២០១១"). [Para 1][Para 27][Para 35]
- The hearing and the notice of hearing are dated 3 January 2011 at para 18 and 3 January 2012 at para 14; the first is plainly a year error in the source and both are reported as found. [Para 14][Para 18]
- The pro-rata figure is given as "20 days" at para 69, while the three-case analysis at paras 57 to 59 speaks of 26 days or 21-22 days in a month. [Para 57][Para 69]
- Several sentences are split across paragraphs by page breaks, leaving fragments such as "the panel finds the employer has an obligation to..." at the end of one paragraph and its object at the start of the next. [Para 83][Para 84]
- Stray numerals from another document ("២០១៧", "២៥") appear inside the reasoning. [Para 56][Para 87]
- The union officer and staff representatives are named in the source; they are reduced to roles here.
