# Award No. 117/11 — Star Knitting & Garment Factory Ltd (Arbitration Council, 19 October 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambona, Tuon Siphan, Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 140074

## 1. Facts
Star Knitting & Garment Factory Ltd employed 320 workers, including a knitting section of 61, when the Khmer Youth Voice Union filed a six-point collective dispute that conciliation failed to settle on 15 August 2011 (report 420/11 of 5 September 2011). On 17 June 2011 the employer declined to renew the fixed-duration contracts of six union committee members before those contracts reached their expiry dates, and the union says the non-renewals were anti-union. [Para 10][Para 42][Para 55][Para 140]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must allow the six named workers to perform overtime by section? [Para 44]
2. Whether the company must allow Mr V, the union president, to return to work? [Para 3]
3. Whether the company discriminated against the Khmer Youth Voice Union? [Para 4]
4. Whether the company must stop terminating the contracts of pregnant workers? [Para 48]
5. Whether the company must stop deducting the attendance bonus when workers arrive 10–15 minutes late because of a broken vehicle or an accident? (settled at hearing) [Para 18]
6. Whether the company must take back the seven workers dismissed without cause on 17 June 2011, six of whom still claimed at the hearing? [Para 55]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the non-renewal of all six committee members, including the union president and secretary, was discrimination against the union, and the workers seek reinstatement, section-by-section overtime and continued contracts for pregnant workers. [Para 62][Para 129][Para 137]
**Employer (respondent):** there was no anti-union motive; Mr V ate in the workplace contrary to internal rules and mismeasured cloth so that his team sewed wrongly, Mr R urged other workers to stop work on 16 June 2011, and the remaining four were not renewed for the same reason. Pregnant workers are re-hired after maternity leave without a new probation period. [Para 52][Para 59][Para 63][Para 70]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement and the overtime claim were dismissed, but the employer must pay each of the six workers the wages still owing to the end of that worker's contract, contractual severance of 5% of wages earned, and compensation for unused annual leave. [Para 150][Para 151][Para 152][Para 153]

On issue 1 the panel held that a demand for future overtime discloses no actual violation and that the panel does not adjudicate hypothetical disputes, so the claim was dismissed. [Para 82][Para 87] On issue 4 the panel applied Article 73, under which a fixed-duration contract expires at its term, and read Article 46 of the Constitution and Articles 12 and 182 of the Labour Law as prohibiting dismissal during employment or maternity leave but not as requiring renewal once the term has run; the claim was dismissed. [Para 99][Para 107][Para 110] On issues 2, 3 and 6 the panel held that the Article 293 protection of the three union leaders (president, vice-president, secretary) requires labour-inspector authorisation for dismissal, not for non-renewal at expiry, and found the workers' evidence of anti-union motive insufficient. [Para 124][Para 139] Because the contracts were nevertheless cut short before their expiry dates, Article 73 paragraph 6 obliged the employer to pay wages to the contract end plus 5% severance, and Article 166 added unused annual leave. [Para 141][Para 143][Para 146]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer declines to renew a fixed-duration contract before its stated expiry date, it owes the worker the wages that would have been earned to that date and statutory severance, even though the Labour Law neither compels renewal of a fixed-term contract nor protects a union leader against non-renewal at expiry. [Para 125][Para 141][Para 143]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that a pregnant worker re-hired after maternity leave need not serve a new two-month probation period, an observation not necessary to the dismissal of the renewal claim. [Para 89]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Issue 6 speaks of seven workers dismissed on 17 June 2011, but the workers confirmed at the hearing that only six remained (paras 7, 55–56). Line 58 dates Mr V's non-renewal to 17 May 2011, whereas the other five are dated 17 June 2011, and the award gives him only seven days' notice when fifteen were required. Individual names survive in variant and partly corrupted forms (Chhay Vat / Thay Vat; Ni Rat / Ni Rak / Ni Sarat; San Manin Rat / San Troap Manin Rat), and some sentences are duplicated by OCR (paras 35, 62, 108). [Para 7][Para 56][Para 58][Para 108]
