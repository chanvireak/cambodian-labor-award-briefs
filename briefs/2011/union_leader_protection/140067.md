# Award No. 106/11 — Fong Chhoeng (Arbitration Council, 7 September 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Yoo Soun Ty, employer-appointed; An Virak, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, president chosen by the two) · Doc ID 140067

## 1. Facts
Fong Chhoeng is a garment factory on Veang Sreng Street, Sangkat Chom Chau, Khan Dangkao, Phnom Penh. On 18 July 2011 the Khmer Workers' Union Federation at the factory filed complaint No. 19/11 with the Ministry of Labour's labour-dispute department, raising ten demands on working conditions. Conciliation on 2 August 2011 failed on all ten points because the company did not attend; the non-conciliation report No. 926 Kb/Ak/Vk of 15 August 2011 reached the Council on 17 August 2011. The hearing was set for 24 August 2011 at 08:30. [Para 1][Para 17][Para 18][Para 19][Para 20]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company should take back the federation's factory-level union leaders into employment? [Para 2]
2. Whether the company should stop deducting USD 10 from each worker's wage? [Para 3]
3. Whether the company should bear responsibility when workers faint or suffer other workplace accidents? [Para 4]
4. Whether the company should allow workers to wear shoes at work? [Para 5]
5. Whether the company should refund the USD 5 deducted from workers who did not come to work on Sunday? [Para 6]
6. Whether factory and Chinese supervisors should treat workers appropriately? [Para 7]
7. Whether the company should stop docking pay for the days workers stopped work unlawfully? [Para 8]
8. Whether the factory should have a medical room and sufficient medicine? [Para 9]
9. Whether the company should pay wages on the 5th of every month? [Para 10]
10. Whether the company should establish a nursery as required by the Labour Law? [Para 11]
The panel treated the operative question as whether the claimant union had abandoned its claim by failing to appear. [Para 37][Para 56]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The factory union did not appear. Two federation officials, Mr M B and Mr L S, attended and said they had received no written transfer of authority and did not know where the three union leaders were; they asked whether they could represent the workers. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24]
**Employer (respondent):** Absent without reason and filed no documents; the panel recorded no submission from the company. [Para 21][Para 26]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel closed case No. 106/11 without ruling on any of the ten demands and ordered no payment. [Para 58][Para 59]
Article 21 of Prakas No. 099 and Rule 4.7 of its annexed arbitration procedure let the panel either continue in a party's absence or terminate the arbitration by issuing an order, provided the parties were properly notified of the date, time and place. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43] The secretariat's invitations No. 598 and 599, both dated 18 August 2011, were served on the employer and the workers and received. [Para 45][Para 46]
The factory union, which alone had standing to represent its member workers, stayed away without reason and gave no written authority to Mr M B and Mr L S. [Para 47][Para 50] Article 19 of Prakas No. 099 requires a representative to hold written authorisation from the party, so the two officials had no legal standing to act for the union or its members. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 51]
A claimant must supply reasons and evidence to support its own complaint. By not appearing and sending no authorised representative, the union lost that opportunity, which the panel treated as abandonment of the claim. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 55][Para 56]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a claimant union, after proper notice, neither appears nor appoints a representative holding written authority from the party, the panel treats the claim as abandoned and closes the case by order without examining the merits; an official of a parent federation who attends without a written transfer of authority cannot cure the default. [Para 47][Para 51][Para 56]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the union's conduct did not show genuine intent to have its labour dispute resolved before the Council. [Para 57]
