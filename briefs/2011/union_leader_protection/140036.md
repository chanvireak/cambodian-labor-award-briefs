# Award No. 65/11 — Sae Han International (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 13 July 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Tuon Siphann, Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 140036

## 1. Facts
Sae Han International (Cambodia) Co., Ltd has operated a garment factory at Trapang Thloeng, Dangkor since 2008 and employs 1,100 workers [Para 15][Para 16]. The claimants are the first and second Local Unions of the Voice Khmer Youth Union Federation (VKYUF), representing 300 and 40 workers and registered on 23 December 2010 and 9 February 2011 respectively [Para 16]. Of nine demands raised in April 2011, seven were conciliated and two were referred to the panel [Para 7][Para 8]. In March 2011 the employer set a daily target of 2,000 to 3,000 pieces for groups 11 and 12, but three group leaders reached only 900 to 1,200 pieces and were denied the US$ 30 monthly incentive bonus; their main wage was US$ 80 [Para 17][Para 18][Para 37]. Neang Sotheara, Secretary of the second local union, worked on successive three-month fixed-duration contracts from 12 October 2010 and was told on 11 April 2011 that his contract would not be renewed [Para 24][Para 25].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must refrain from withholding the US$ 30 incentive bonus from the three group leaders whose groups did not achieve the March 2011 production target? [Para 1][Para 16]
2. Whether the employer must reinstate Neang Sotheara, Secretary of the second Local Union of VKYUF, and pay him back pay from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 3][Para 49]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The three leaders missed the target because their groups were short of workers and new workers were slow; the US$ 30 is part of wages under Article 102, and withholding it is a prohibited deduction under Article 28 [Para 22][Para 23]. Neang Sotheara was a union secretary and his non-renewal was therefore unlawful under Articles 292, 293 and 294 [Para 27][Para 29].
**Employer (respondent):** The bonus is a management-set incentive, not wages, and is payable only when the target is achieved; resigning workers were replaced with skilled workers [Para 2][Para 20][Para 23]. It did not know of Neang Sotheara's position until 19 April 2011, after the election; he was lazy and often absent, there was less work, and he accepted severance pay and agreed to withdraw the demand [Para 25][Para 28][Para 31].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Both demands rejected — no bonus for March 2011 and no reinstatement [Para 62][Para 63].

On Issue 1, the panel held that the right to manage and direct the enterprise includes the right to set an incentive bonus, citing its earlier awards 116/07-Grace Sun and 81/08-Global Apparel [Para 35]. Article 28, which bars fines and deductions from remuneration normally due, did not apply because the bonus is not remuneration normally due: it is conditional extra pay for a group leader who leads members to the target [Para 38][Para 40][Para 44]. Although Articles 102 and 103 treat bonuses as a component of wages, eligibility for a bonus is not the same as entitlement to wages, and the worker must fulfil the conditions the employer lawfully and reasonably sets [Para 42][Para 45][Para 47]. Because the three leaders failed to meet the target, they were not entitled to the US$ 30 for March 2011 [Para 49].

On Issue 2, the panel held that Article 73, paragraph 5 requires advance notice of non-renewal only for contracts longer than six months, and Neang Sotheara held three-month contracts, so no notice was required [Para 49][Para 52][Para 53]. Article 292 concerns the employer's duty to organise worker-delegate elections and did not fit the dispute [Para 55][Para 56]. Article 293 protects shop stewards and union leaders against dismissal, but the panel followed its earlier awards (34/05-Jacqsintex, 10/09-New Wide, 05/11-M&V) in holding that the protection does not extend to the expiry of a fixed-duration contract, because dismissal terminates an unexpired contract [Para 57][Para 58]. The non-renewal was therefore lawful [Para 59]. The panel also found that Neang Sotheara had accepted severance pay and agreed to withdraw the demand, though the workers could not reach him [Para 31][Para 61].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A production incentive bonus is conditional extra remuneration, not wages normally due: where a worker fails to meet a lawful, reasonable target set by the employer, withholding the bonus is neither a deduction nor a fine under Article 28, and Article 293's protection of union leaders does not extend to the non-renewal of a fixed-duration contract. [Para 38][Para 44][Para 57]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that the workers did not know the election date, turnout or vote count for the second local union's officers, and ordered production of the election records. [Para 30][Para 31]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The English translation is an unofficial one with pervasive OCR fusion: "themployer", "thearing", "decling", "Traing" and similar throughout [Para 1][Para 15][Para 49]. The non-conciliation report is cited under three different references — "No. 582 KB/RK/VK dated 6 June 2011", "No. 582 KB/RKIVK dated 6 June 2011" and "No. 582 MKBV/KP dated 6 June 2011" [Para 6][Para 8][Para 14]. The employer's brief lists both Seng Sreyneang and Out Chandara as "leader of group 11", although the groups at issue are 11 and 12 [Para 17]. The source describes the document as a "Dismissal notice" although the facts concern non-renewal of a fixed-duration contract [Para 13].
