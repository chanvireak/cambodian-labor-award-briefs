# Award No. 109/11 — Ming Jian (Cambodia) Garment Factory (Arbitration Council, 20 September 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phirom, employer-appointed; Soung Sophal, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · Doc ID 140069

## 1. Facts
Ming Jian (Cambodia) is a garment factory employing about 500 workers; the claimant union, the Democratic Union of Garment Workers of Cambodia, has roughly 250 members there. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 71] On 14 July 2011 the workers elected three union leaders and notified the employer on 18 July 2011. [Para 80][Para 81] From the evening of 27 to 31 August 2011 the employer suspended 11 cutting-section workers and paid them 50% wages, without any authorisation from the labour inspector. [Para 73][Para 75] Conciliation over 13 demands failed on two points, which came to the panel. [Para 12][Para 13]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer's suspension of the 11 workers from 27 to 31 August 2011 without a labour inspector's authorisation was lawful, and whether 100% wages and benefits are owed? [Para 72][Para 99]
2. Whether the employer must take back the three union leaders and pay their back wages, or whether the dismissal stands? [Para 77][Para 112]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The company had no work for them and never obtained a suspension permit from the labour inspector, so 100% wages are due; they would accept 50% only if a permit existed. [Para 2][Para 72] The three leaders were dismissed after the union election and the alleged misconduct is not serious enough to justify dismissal. [Para 82][Para 92]
**Employer (respondent):** The company applies its old practice of paying 50% whenever there is no work, and the 11 were suspended because there was no work. [Para 3][Para 74] The three leaders cannot be reinstated because they committed serious misconduct under Article 83 of the Labour Law by inciting the 20 July 2011 strike. [Para 5][Para 26]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must pay 100% wages and other benefits to the 11 workers for 27–31 August 2011; the claim for reinstatement of the three union leaders was dismissed without consideration. [Para 141][Para 142]

Issue 1: The panel first classified the demand as a rights dispute, because it turns on entitlements written in the Labour Law and the employment contract. [Para 95][Para 98] Article 71 of the Labour Law allows an enterprise facing serious economic or material difficulty to suspend activity for no more than two months, but the suspension must be placed under the labour inspector's supervision, with notice to the inspectorate and its verification and confirmation. [Para 100][Para 101][Para 102] The employer argued the suspension rested on lack of work and a 50% wage practice, but the panel found no evidence of any notice to, or agreement from, the labour inspector. [Para 108][Para 109] Following its earlier awards in cases 27/08, 105/04, 22/05 and 53/08, the panel held that an unlawful suspension obliges the employer to pay 100% wages and other benefits even where no work was provided. [Para 104][Para 107] It ordered payment for the five days. [Para 110][Para 111]

Issue 2: Article 293 requires the labour inspector's consent before a protected union leader or staff representative can be dismissed, and gives the parties two months to appeal to the Minister, who may reject or amend the inspector's decision. [Para 115][Para 119][Para 120] The employer had sought authorisation and the inspector authorised dismissal on 12 August 2011 with a two-month appeal window; the workers appealed to the Minister and are awaiting the outcome. [Para 125][Para 126][Para 127][Para 128] The panel therefore held that the Article 293 procedure was not complete and that it could take jurisdiction only once it was finished. [Para 138][Para 139] It distinguished case 91/04, where reinstatement was ordered despite authorisation, on the ground that the procedure there had run its course. [Para 130][Para 136] It dismissed the reinstatement claim. [Para 140]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A suspension of employment without notice to, and authorisation from, the labour inspector is unlawful, and the employer must pay the suspended workers 100% of wages and benefits for the suspension period even though no work was given. [Para 107][Para 110] Where a union leader's dismissal is still inside the Article 293 appeal procedure, the panel will not examine the merits of the dismissal until that procedure is exhausted. [Para 139]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel accepted the protective purpose of Article 293 and Prakas 305 — shielding union leaders from discrimination — and agreed in principle with case 91/04 that the panel retains jurisdiction over the lawfulness of a dismissal even after authorisation, while noting that award did not establish whether the procedure had been followed. [Para 131][Para 132][Para 134][Para 135]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company name is printed variously as Ming Jian, Ming Chian and Moeung Chian within the same document. [Para 1][Para 29][Para 45] The exhibit list numbers consecutive items "19" and "10" and then "19" again, so the sequence cannot be relied on. [Para 41][Para 42] Line 63 records a conciliation minute dated 15 August "2016" for a company named Ring Jevon, which cannot belong to a 2011 award. [Para 63] The decision on issue 2 says "both" (ពីរ) and then "three" (បី) persons. [Para 142] Signature blocks and the final enforcement paragraph contain untranscribed OCR fragments. [Para 143][Para 145]
