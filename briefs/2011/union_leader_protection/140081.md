# Award No. 124/11 — Xing Chang Xin (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 19 October 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer, Ven Pov for the workers, Run Saray chair) · Doc ID 140081

## 1. Facts
Xing Chang Xin is a garment factory that began operating in February 2011 with more than 500 workers. A union was registered on 26 August 2011, and Mr H was elected its president. His last fixed-duration contract, running 3 May to 3 August 2011, was not renewed; the employer ended it on 2 August 2011. The union demanded his reinstatement as union leader and back pay from the dismissal date to reinstatement. [Para 27][Para 29][Para 52][Para 53]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take Mr H back as union leader and pay him back wages from the date of dismissal to the date of reinstatement? [Para 30]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer terminated Mr H's contract because of discrimination against union activity, having learned that he was elected to union leadership. [Para 61]
**Employer (respondent):** It cannot take Mr H back; it declined to renew the contract because he did not take sufficient care in his work and did not cooperate with the company. [Para 3][Para 67]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Dismissed — the panel refused the demand for reinstatement as union leader and for back pay from the dismissal date to reinstatement. [Para 70][Para 71]

The panel held that Mr H's fixed-duration contract expired on its own terms, extinguishing the obligations of both parties, so neither side could compel the other to continue the contract or to take him back. [Para 54] It then addressed Article 293(1) of the Labour Law, which requires the Labour Inspector's consent before dismissing a staff representative, and Article 3 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, which extends that protection to candidates for union leadership elections. [Para 56][Para 57] Following Award 10/09-New Way Cambodia, the panel held that a refusal to renew a fixed-duration contract is not a dismissal, so neither notification nor the Inspector's consent was required. [Para 59][Para 60]

On the discrimination claim, the panel noted that the workers produced no evidence of how the employer's conduct amounted to anti-union discrimination beyond the assertion itself. [Para 64] It observed that many workers elected to third-term union leadership, advisory and activist posts were not terminated, except Ms E who resigned voluntarily — reasoning that a genuinely discriminatory employer would have dismissed all of them. [Para 65][Para 66] Mr H did not deny the employer's account of his inattention and lack of cooperation. [Para 68] The panel therefore found insufficient evidence that non-renewal was caused by his union candidacy. [Para 69]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Non-renewal of a fixed-duration contract at its expiry is not a dismissal; the protection of Article 293 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 305 does not require the employer to obtain the Labour Inspector's consent or give notice in that situation. A claim that non-renewal was anti-union discrimination fails where the workers assert it without evidence and the employer shows other union office-holders were retained. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 69]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel reasoned that an employer genuinely hostile to the union would have terminated all the elected union leaders, advisers and activists, not only the claimant. [Para 65][Para 66]
