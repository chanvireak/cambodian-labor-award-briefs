# Award No. 57/11 — Cambo Advertising (Arbitration Council, 24 June 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, employer-chosen; An Nan, worker-chosen; Kong Phallack, chair) · Doc ID 140026

## 1. Facts
Cambo Advertising, at Village No.3, Svay Rolom, Sa Ang District, Kandal Province, employed 130 workers and had no union. [Para 22] On 13 February 2011 twenty-eight workers elected union leaders; the employer was notified on 28 February 2011 and the union applied for registration on 11 March 2011. [Para 22] [Para 23] On 6 March 2011 the employer's representative asked union members to stand aside, and the 55 workers who did so left the workplace after their demands were refused; they were refused entry on 7 March and dismissed on 22 March 2011. [Para 25] [Para 27] [Para 28] [Para 32] [Para 67] The registration was rejected on 28 March 2011 after the employer objected on 25 March 2011. [Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate the 55 workers and provide back pay from the date of dismissal to the date of reinstatement? [Para 1] [Para 23]
2. Whether the employer must comply with the agreement dated 15 March 2006 requiring workers to submit curriculum vitas before working? [Para 1] [Para 24]
3. Whether the employer must refrain from preventing the workers from establishing a union and from discriminating against the union? [Para 2] [Para 66]
4. Whether the employer must provide a seniority bonus to the workers it dismissed? [Para 2]
5. Whether the employer must make contributions to the National Social Security Fund for all workers? [Para 2] [Para 72]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The 55 workers submitted curriculum vitas as agreed and should be reinstated with back pay; failing that they should receive termination payments including notice compensation, damages, dismissal indemnity, leave indemnity and outstanding wages. [Para 61] [Para 62] They alleged the dismissal was motivated by union discrimination and that the employer threatened anyone joining the union with resignation or dismissal. [Para 63] [Para 66]
**Employer (respondent):** It did not dismiss the workers but treated them as having abandoned their work after failing to comply with the 15 March agreement; it denied discrimination, saying it merely would not allow a union to use the company's name or address, and argued Article 279 already protected freedom of association. [Para 63] [Para 69] [Para 70] [Para 72] It also argued the NSSF issue was not collective and fell outside the Council's jurisdiction. [Para 72] [Para 74]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered reinstatement of the 55 workers with back pay from 6 March 2011 to reinstatement and ordered the employer to cease discriminating by preventing the establishment of a union; it declined jurisdiction over the NSSF demand. [Para 106] [Para 114] [Para 118] The printed DECISION AND ORDER, however, records different orders — an overtime allowance for Saturdays and a proportional deduction from the attendance bonus — and does not restate the reinstatement order. [Para 120] [Para 121]

On standing, the panel held the CLC local union was not formally registered, so under Article 268 it could not enjoy the rights and benefits of the Labour Law and lacked standing to bring a dispute; but Clause 19 of Prakas 099 allowed it to represent the 55 workers under their written authorisation of 7 March 2011. [Para 81] [Para 85] [Para 86] [Para 88] [Para 89]

On reinstatement, the panel found the workers wanted to resume work and the employer refused them, so there was no abandonment of work; submitting new curriculum vitas was a condition of rehiring, not evidence of desertion. [Para 92] [Para 93] [Para 94] [Para 95] Because the workers were union founders within the protection of Article 293 and Clause 4 of Prakas 305, and had been dismissed 11 days after registration was applied for — within the 30-day protection period — the employer needed Labour Inspector authorisation before dismissing them. [Para 95] [Para 97] [Para 99] [Para 105] Having failed to obtain it, the employer was ordered to reinstate and pay back wages. [Para 106]

On discrimination, the panel applied Articles 12 and 279 and found the employer had treated union members differently — singling them out on 6 March, requiring only them to resubmit curriculum vitas and dismissing only them — which established discrimination. [Para 107] [Para 111] [Para 112] [Para 113] On NSSF, Article 31 of the Social Security Law and Clause 1 of Prakas 177 vest disputes in the NSSF conciliation committee and then the courts, so the Council had no jurisdiction. [Para 115] [Para 117] [Para 118]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker who is a union founder or member at the time registration is applied for is protected like a shop steward for 30 days after registration; dismissal within that window without prior authorisation from the Labour Inspector is unlawful, and the remedy is reinstatement with back pay from the date of dismissal, whether or not the union itself is registered. [Para 95] [Para 99] [Para 105] [Para 106]

**Obiter dicta:** An unregistered union cannot itself enjoy the rights and benefits conferred by the Labour Law, but it may still represent named workers who authorise it in writing under Clause 19 of Prakas 099. [Para 88] [Para 89]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is internally inconsistent. The DECISION AND ORDER does not match the reasons: it orders an overtime allowance for Saturdays and an attendance-bonus deduction, neither of which appears in the reasoning, and omits the reinstatement and back-pay order the panel made at paragraph 106. [Para 106] [Para 120] [Para 121] The signature block names Ing Sothy, Tuon Siphann and Pen Bunchhea, whereas the header names Mar Samborana, An Nan and Kong Phallack. [Para 1] [Para 124] The agreement is dated 15 March 2006 in the issue list but 15 March 2011 throughout the facts. [Para 1] [Para 24] [Para 38] The evidence section was omitted from the English version. [Para 20] The employer's name appears as "Yem Pov", "Nhem Pov" and "Nop Sambath" for the same individuals, and OCR loses "the" throughout ("themployer", "athe", "thearing", "secondition").
