# Award No. 130/11 — Dik Neathy Nit Ther Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 8 November 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambor Na for the employer, Tuon Si Phan for the workers, Pen Bun Chhea chair, chosen by both arbitrators) · Doc ID 140083

## 1. Facts
Dik Neathy Nit Ther Co., Ltd. is a garment enterprise in Prek Ta Pring village, Sitbo commune, Saang district, Kandal province, which received its certificate of registration in the commercial register in June 2011. It prints and sews clothing, socks, sleeves and scarves, and employs 1,918 workers. [Para 1][Para 41][Para 42]

The Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers' Democratic Union (CCAWDU) filed the complaint in this case and has 1,900 members at the factory. [Para 1][Para 43][Para 44]

Workers' ordinary hours run from 7:00 to 11:00 in the morning and from 12:30 to 16:30 in the afternoon; the employer routinely adds overtime from 16:30 to 18:30. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47] The company fixes piece rates for each style on the 25th day of each month. [Para 49]

The union's grievance concerned piece rates for five style codes. For Mok Pruy style PN 9047-9048 the workers sought US$ 0.95 per lot against the company's US$ 0.85 [Para 2][Para 3]; for the sewing section on the same style US$ 1.20 against US$ 0.89 [Para 4][Para 5]; for Pung Khluen PN 975525 US$ 1.65 against US$ 1.55 [Para 6][Para 7]; for PN 10051 US$ 0.30 against US$ 0.25 [Para 8][Para 9]; and for Pung K PN 9047-9048 US$ 0.80 against US$ 0.78. [Para 9][Para 10]

Conciliation before the Kandal provincial Department of Labour and Vocational Training on 7 October 2011 failed to settle the dispute. [Para 16] The non-conciliation report No. 467/11 Kb/Kn dated 10 October 2011 was sent to the Arbitration Council Secretariat the same day. [Para 13][Para 17] The single unresolved issue was heard on 19 October 2011 at 08:30, with both parties present. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 19] At the hearing the parties reached agreement on the sewing section (PN 9047-9048) and the Pung K section (PN 9047-9048), leaving three styles in dispute: Mok Pruy (PN 9047-9048), Pung Khluen (PN 975525) and PN 10051. [Para 20][Para 21] Both parties agreed to accept a binding award and, at the hearing, agreed to postpone issuance to 8 November 2011. [Para 22][Para 23]

The employer's representatives were Tang Sun Heng (administration/production manager), Zhong Hai (production manager assistant) and Jiang Wei. [Para 1] The worker side was represented by San Sopha (factory union president), Phin Sopha (vice-president) and other union officers and activists. [Para 2]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must raise the piece rates for the Mok Pruy section style PN 9047-9048 to US$ 0.95, for the Pung Khluen section style PN 975525 to US$ 1.65, and for style PN 10051 to US$ 0.30 per lot? [Para 45][Para 50][Para 67]
2. Whether the claim is a dispute over rights, and therefore within the Council's jurisdiction to determine on the law rather than by conciliation? [Para 68][Para 71]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The new rates are lower than those paid for comparable or easier old styles. PN 4085, easier or equivalent to PN 9047-9048, previously attracted US$ 0.93 per lot, yet the company set only US$ 0.85. [Para 55][Para 56] PN 975525 is similar to or easier than the old PN 975526, paid at US$ 1.65, but the company set only US$ 1.55. [Para 57] PN 10051 is harder than the old PN 3007, paid at US$ 0.30. [Para 59] The claim covers only styles already sewn from September to early October 2011, not future production. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54]

**Employer (respondent):** Each style has a different design; PN 975525 differs from PN 975526 in design and stitching and has a shorter hem, so the comparison is not like for like. [Para 58] The employer accepted that the rates it offers are slightly lower than those the workers cite. [Para 60] It also argued that the rates nonetheless allow workers to earn above the base wage within the eight ordinary hours, as shown in the September 2011 payroll submitted to the Council. [Para 61][Para 63] Where a worker sewed below the base wage, the employer topped up the difference. [Para 64] Most workers who fell short had not worked the full 26 ordinary working days. [Para 65] September had many public holidays, so 20 days' attendance was treated as full attendance. [Para 66]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** The claim was rejected. The Council declined to order the employer to raise the piece rates for Mok Pruy PN 9047-9048 to US$ 0.95, Pung Khluen PN 975525 to US$ 1.65 and PN 10051 to US$ 0.30 per lot. [Para 84][Para 85]

The Council first classified the claim. Following its own earlier jurisprudence, a dispute over rights is one concerning an entitlement set out in a law, a collective agreement or a convention, and the claim here concerned the minimum wage payable to workers paid by output. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 70][Para 71] It was therefore a rights dispute, to be decided by the panel and not merely by conciliation. [Para 71]

On the merits, Article 108 of the Labour Law provides that for contract or output-based work, whether performed in a workshop or at home, wages must be calculated so that a worker of average skill working normally in the same period receives at least the guaranteed minimum wage. [Para 72] Prakas No. 049/10 dated 9 July 2010 set the minimum at US$ 56 per month for probationary workers and US$ 61 per month for full-rights workers, and its point 3 provided that workers paid by output receive the wage corresponding to their actual output, with any shortfall topped up by the employer. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 75][Para 76][Para 77]

The Council applied its earlier ruling that while an employer has the right to set or change piece rates, it must do so in a way that lets a worker of average skill working normally earn the minimum wage. [Para 78][Para 79][Para 80] On the September 2011 payroll, 354 of the 452 workers on ordinary hours (78%) earned more than the minimum wage, while 98 (22%) sewed below it — and most of those had not attended the full number of ordinary working days. [Para 81][Para 82] For those who fell short, the employer had already topped up the wage to the minimum. [Para 83]

Because the statutory floor was being met or made up, the Council found no legal basis to compel the rate increase sought and rejected the claim. [Para 84][Para 85]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand to raise piece rates is a dispute over rights where it concerns the minimum wage guaranteed by Article 108 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 049/10. The employer's freedom to set piece rates is lawful provided the rates allow a worker of average skill working normal hours to earn at least the guaranteed minimum, and provided any shortfall is topped up. Where the payroll evidence shows the floor is met or made up, the Council will not substitute its own rate for the employer's. [Para 71][Para 72][Para 78][Para 83][Para 84]
