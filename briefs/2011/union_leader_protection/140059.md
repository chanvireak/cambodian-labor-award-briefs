# Award No. 101/11 — Angkor Village Botanical Resort (Arbitration Council, 30 August 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun, An Nan, Kong Phallack) · Doc ID 140059

## 1. Facts
Angkor Village Botanical Resort began operating in 1995 and employed 142 workers across two licensed branches, Angkor Village Hotel and Angkor Village Resort and Spa, between which staff alternated. The Cambodian Tourism and Service Workers Federation (CTSWF) brought the case for 67 workers. A union election was held on 4 July 2011 and the union applied for registration on 29 July 2011. On 30 July 2011 the employer dismissed Chan Charya over alleged poison remarks, and between 30 July and 6 August 2011 it dismissed 67 workers in total. The employer asserted a temporary shutdown due to falling patron numbers and security concerns. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 29][Para 42]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate the 67 workers who participated in the 2 August 2011 strike and pay their back wages? [Para 1]
2. Whether the dismissals discriminated against activists and leaders of the union at Angkor Village Hotel? [Para 3]
The panel recorded that a third demand, for outstanding annual leave, public holidays and other entitlements, was resolved in conciliation, and that issues 1 and 2 were combined. [Para 10]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The dismissals were union discrimination, contrary to Article 36 of the Constitution and Articles 266, 268 and 279 of the Labour Law and ILO Conventions 87 and 98. The hotel had not shut down; it is renovated every May, when staff are transferred to the Resort and Spa. [Para 52]
**Employer (respondent):** It refused reinstatement because Angkor Village Hotel was shutting down for lack of patrons and lack of security, and it had requested a temporary shutdown from 8 August 2011. [Para 53]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted in substance — the employer was ordered either to reinstate the 67 dismissed workers with back pay from the date of their illegal dismissal, or, if it obtained permission for a business shutdown, to reduce the workforce under the Article 95 mass-layoff procedure. The award was non-binding. [Para 77]
The panel first classified the dismissal dispute as a rights dispute, following its earlier awards, and then applied the special protection for union founders and members. Under Clause 4 of Prakas No. 305 dated 22 November 2001, workers who found or join a union enjoy the same protection as shop stewards for up to 30 days after registration, provided the union notifies the employer by reliable means. The panel found all three conditions met: the 67 workers were union leaders and founders; the dismissals from 30 July to 6 August 2011 fell within the 30-day period after the 29 July application; and the employer had acknowledged the election results notified on 5 July 2011. [Para 56][Para 62][Para 65][Para 67][Para 69]
Because Article 293 of the Labour Law requires Labour Inspector authorisation before dismissing a shop steward or candidate, and the employer had none, the dismissals were unlawful and reinstatement followed. The panel also rejected the shutdown justification: the employer bore the burden of proving a decrease in patrons but submitted no record, and the Ministry of Tourism had not responded to the shutdown request. It therefore treated the business as not officially closed and still having work for the workers. [Para 60][Para 71][Para 72][Para 73][Para 75]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a union has applied for registration and notified the employer of the workers entitled to protection, a dismissal within 30 days of registration without prior Labour Inspector authorisation under Article 293 is unlawful, and the employer must reinstate the workers and pay back wages; an unapproved and unevidenced claim of temporary shutdown does not displace that obligation. [Para 65][Para 71][Para 72]

**Obiter dicta:** If the employer later obtains approval for a temporary shutdown, it may reduce the workforce under Article 95, choosing the order of layoffs by professional qualifications, seniority and family burdens. [Para 76]
