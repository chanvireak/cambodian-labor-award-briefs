# Award No. 30/11 — South Bay Enterprise Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 5 April 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tayseng, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Pen Bunchhea, chair) · Doc ID 140009

## 1. Facts
South Bay Enterprise Co., Ltd. is a garment employer in Kandal Province [Para 1]. Its workers, organised in the Local Union of KWPFU, raised six demands; conciliation resolved one, and the hearing resolved three more, leaving issues 1 and 4 [Para 12][Para 15]. Issue 1 sought reinstatement and back pay for Ms C Ny, the local union president, and Ms Ch Savoeun, its vice-president, both dismissed on 31 January 2011 [Para 3][Para 25][Para 29].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reinstate Ms C Ny and Ms Ch Savoeun and back pay their wages from dismissal to reinstatement? [Para 3]
2. Whether the employer must pay 50% of three months' wages and perquisites, calculated on the average wage of the last 12 months, to pregnant workers before maternity leave? [Para 6]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissals were union discrimination; after the union's registration on 24 January 2011 an administration officer told each leader the union was unlawfully established and asked her to resign, and both were dismissed when they refused [Para 28][Para 32].
**Employer (respondent):** the two were dismissed for serious misconduct under the Internal Work Rules because they falsified documents to register the union; Ms Ch Savoeun lacked one year's seniority and the union secretary did not work at the factory [Para 33][Para 35].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted in part — the employer must reinstate both leaders with back pay, and must pay 50% of 90 days' wages and perquisites to pregnant workers before maternity leave [Para 59][Para 60].

Issue 1: Article 293 of the Labour Law and Clause 4 of Prakas No. 305 require Labour Inspector authorisation before dismissing a worker delegate or union leader. The panel found the union's registration certificate valid and the two women listed as president and vice-president, so they enjoyed special protection regardless of the employer's reasons [Para 45][Para 48][Para 49]. The employer admitted it had not sought the Inspector's approval [Para 36]. Reinstatement and back pay were therefore ordered [Para 50].

Issue 4: Articles 182 and 183 entitle women to 90 days' maternity leave on half pay including perquisites after one year's service [Para 50][Para 51]. Article 115(3) requires payment before a day off, so the panel held the 50% must be paid before leave begins [Para 53][Para 54]. The employer's 2008 agreement with WTU bound only WTU members, and Article 13 makes any agreement contrary to the Labour Law null and void [Para 55][Para 57][Para 58].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union leader whose name appears on a valid registration certificate is entitled to the special protection of Article 293, and dismissal without prior Labour Inspector authorisation is unlawful whatever the employer's substantive reasons, requiring reinstatement and back pay [Para 48][Para 49]. An internal agreement cannot cut down the statutory maternity entitlement; the 50% of 90 days' wages and perquisites must be paid before maternity leave begins [Para 54][Para 57].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the workers advanced no legal arguments to support their discrimination claim, and decided the case on the procedural protection instead [Para 29][Para 33].
