# Award No. 156/11 — Gold Fame Enterprise Bright City Need Textile Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 January 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ung Sothy, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Kong Phalha, chair) · Doc ID 140107

## 1. Facts
The Democratic Khmer Workers' Union at Gold Fame Enterprise Bright City Need Textile Ltd, a garment plant of 5,200 workers, filed nine demands on 9 August 2011 for improved working conditions; conciliation on 26 August 2011 settled one, and the eight remaining points reached the Council on 28 November 2011 under non-conciliation report No. 613/11 of 24 November 2011. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20][Para 105]

At the two hearings (7 and 30 December 2011) points 2, 5 and 6 were settled, leaving issues 1, 3, 4, 7 and 8. Issue 1 was the refusal to re-engage five workers said to have been dismissed after the strike of 13–16 September 2010; the rest concerned a wage rise, rice and accommodation for the union secretary, allowances for a team leader and a QC worker, and a 2009 demotion. [Para 23][Para 108][Para 289][Para 340][Para 372][Para 397]

The union filed no certificate of most-representative status, and the panel struck out the employer's evidence filed a day late and gave no weight to a warning letter issued after the first hearing. [Para 107][Para 140][Para 183]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the five workers' fixed-duration contracts had become contracts of indefinite duration, so that their non-renewal was a dismissal entitling them to reinstatement and back pay? [Para 232][Para 236]
2. Whether the company had to re-engage the five as old workers in priority to new hires under clause 1 of the collective agreement of 1 February 2010? [Para 263][Para 274]
3. Whether the company must raise the union secretary's wage by USD 5 and restore his rice and accommodation as before? [Para 289][Para 309]
4. Whether the company must pay the team leader a USD 5.6 living allowance and USD 6.6 duty pay and raise her wage by USD 13.4 like other team leaders? [Para 340][Para 359]
5. Whether the company must pay the QC worker a USD 18 allowance and a USD 30 incentive for 2011? [Para 374][Para 387]
6. Whether the 2009 demotion of the machine-team leader was a disciplinary measure proportionate to his misconduct? [Para 405][Para 406]

The panel recorded eight issues and settled 2, 5 and 6 [Para 23]; the questions below are the rest. The print says five remain but names four (§6).

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the five had worked continuously for more than two years, so their contracts had become indefinite and their non-renewal was a dismissal tainted by anti-union discrimination and by the company's hiring of new workers instead of them; each individual claimant asserted an entitlement to the pay and benefits he or she had previously received, on the principle of equal pay for equal work. [Para 114][Para 232][Para 233][Para 143][Para 359]

**Employer (respondent):** the five held fixed-duration contracts that simply expired, they were given notice and the 5% termination payment and signed for it, and four of them were re-hired immediately, one in February 2011 among 142 returning workers; the extra payments claimed were personal money from Chinese trainers or discretionary profit-based gifts, and the demotion followed serious misconduct — burning the auto machine. [Para 115][Para 116][Para 127][Para 193][Para 210][Para 379][Para 391]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must re-engage two of the five workers, Mr C M and Mr L V, as a priority in their former sections from the date of the award; the claims of the other three for re-engagement, the back pay of all five, the wage rise, rice and accommodation, living allowance, duty pay, "pov" allowance and incentive were refused; the employer must raise Ms A C D's wage by USD 13.4 to match other team leaders; the demotion claim was refused. [Para 437][Para 438][Para 439][Para 441][Para 442]

On issue 1 the panel treated the character of the contracts as decisive: Article 67 caps fixed-duration contracts at two years, but the claimants produced no continuous chain of contracts, and the record showed gaps of a week or a month between them, so the employment relationship and seniority were broken at each expiry and the contracts never became indefinite. Under Article 73 fixed-duration contracts lapse automatically at the agreed date, notice was given and the 5% payment made and received, so the non-renewal was lawful and not a dismissal; the claim for wages from "dismissal" to reinstatement therefore failed. [Para 237][Para 243][Para 245][Para 259][Para 262][Para 287]

The panel nonetheless held the collective agreement of 1 February 2010 binding as a legal norm for all workers, union members or not. Clause 1(2) requires old workers to be re-engaged in priority over new hires where the section needing labour is one they previously worked in. The company hired 13 new workers in November 2011 in the swaying and ironing sections where Mr C M and Mr L V had worked, gave no reason for passing them over, and filed no contrary evidence, so it breached the agreement as to those two; the other three had worked in dyeing, weaving and label-sewing, sections to which the new hires did not relate. [Para 264][Para 271][Para 274][Para 276][Para 277][Para 284]

On the union secretary the panel split the demand: a wage increase above the legal minimum set by Notification No. 049/10 was an interest dispute, and because the union was not most representative the panel declined jurisdiction over interest disputes, so neither the USD 5 rise nor the living allowance, duty pay, "pov" allowance or incentive could be ordered. On rice and accommodation the panel applied "past practice": the benefit survived the loss of his key-holding duty, but he did not object when it stopped after the September 2010 strike and claimed only in August 2011, which signified acceptance. [Para 294][Para 298][Para 305][Para 306][Para 321][Para 336][Para 383][Para 393]

On the USD 13.4 the panel found a rights dispute under Article 106: equal work, equal skill and equal output require equal pay. The employer admitted the team leader's increase was smaller and asserted poorer performance, but filed no evidence by the final deadline, and a warning letter raised after the first hearing could not retroactively justify a decision already taken, so the increase was ordered. [Para 361][Para 364][Para 369][Para 184][Para 186][Para 370]

On the demotion the panel found the misconduct proved — three internal-rule breaches and the burning of the auto machine — and held that Article 27 requires the sanction to match the gravity of the fault. Although the demotion appeared in no internal rule, Article 26 B(2) and Article 2 leave the employer free to choose a lesser sanction, and a demotion that is provisional and reversible, offering a chance of restoration, is more reasonable than dismissal; the employer's undertaking to promote him again if he improved confirmed it was not permanent. [Para 402][Para 405][Para 418][Para 428][Para 429][Para 432][Para 435]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-duration contract lapses at its stated date where the worker cannot prove an unbroken chain of renewals, so non-renewal is not a dismissal and carries no back pay; but an employer hiring new workers into a section where an old worker served must first offer that worker re-engagement, and a provisional, reversible demotion proportionate to proved serious misconduct is lawful even if the internal rules do not list it. [Para 243][Para 262][Para 274][Para 428][Para 429]

**Obiter dicta:** that a warning letter issued after the hearing cannot supply reasons for a decision already taken. [Para 183]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The team leader's name is printed six different ways across the award (its first element varies between forms read as Ai, Ae, Rai and Chai), so it is reduced to initials. The reasons at one point say the union secretary's wage was raised by USD 5, contradicting the operative order refusing it; the order is followed. The award is dated 30 January 2012 in the header but says the award was due on 20 December 2011 and issued late, and the worker-appointed arbitrator's signature is partly lost ("marmone 4622"), the chair's name appearing as "Kong Phalha" in the body and with a garbled form in the signature block. Several document tables are reduced to runs of dates and digits. [Para 340][Para 339][Para 444][Para 24][Para 449][Para 121]
