# Award No. 21/11-Sun Sky — Sun Sky (Cambodia) Industries Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 4 March 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, An Nan, Kong Phallack) · Doc ID 139999

## 1. Facts
Sun Sky (Cambodia) Industries Co., Ltd, a garment employer at Prey Tea Village, Chom Chao Commune, Dangkor District, Phnom Penh, faced 14 collective demands lodged by the Coalition Union of Movement of Khmer Workers (CUMW) on 10 January 2011, covering union recognition, supervisory language, voluntary overtime, incentive bonus, a 1,500 riel meal allowance for overtime from 4:00 p.m. to 7:30 p.m., leave, day-care, breastfeeding breaks, toilet access and a canteen [Para 1][Para 3]. Conciliation on 31 January 2011 resolved one of the 14 issues, and the remainder went to the Arbitration Council on 9 February 2011 under non-conciliation report No. 163 KB/RK/VK [Para 5][Para 6]. CUMW did not attend the hearing of 28 February 2011 and did not seek a postponement [Para 7][Para 8].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer should recognise the Local Union of CUMW? [Para 1]
2. Whether the employer should instruct supervisors and heads of section to use polite language? [Para 1]
3. Whether workers should be allowed to work overtime on a voluntary basis? [Para 1]
4. Whether the employer should cease requiring workers to sew beyond their capabilities? [Para 1]
5. Whether the employer should resume providing an incentive bonus? [Para 1]
6. Whether the employer should pay an additional meal allowance of 1,500 riel for overtime from 4:00 p.m. to 7:30 p.m.? [Para 1]
7. Whether the employer should allow workers to take leave? [Para 1]
8. Whether the employer should allow workers to leave at 4:00 p.m. on Saturdays? [Para 1]
9. Whether the employer should set up a day-care centre? [Para 1]
10. Whether the employer should grant female workers a one-hour break to breastfeed? [Para 1]
11. Whether the employer should abandon requiring an entry card to use the toilet? [Para 1]
12. Whether the employer forced workers to affix thumbprints to documents? [Para 1]
13. Whether the employer should install more toilets? [Para 1]
14. Whether the employer should build a canteen? [Para 1]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** CUMW filed the 14 demands but attended no stage of the proceedings; contacted by telephone it said no workers were making the demands and it would not attend, and it offered no evidence or argument [Para 8][Para 31].
**Employer (respondent):** The employer attended as summoned and put in its authorisation letter, the collective agreement of 10 April 2008, its internal work rules of 8 June 2005, its commercial registration and its company statute; no substantive rebuttal of the demands is recorded [Para 9][Para 11].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — case 21/11-Sun Sky was closed without adjudication of the 14 demands, none of which was granted or refused on the merits [Para 38].
The panel treated the case as one of non-appearance and applied Clause 21 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 and Rule 4.7 of the Procedural Rules, both of which allow the panel to proceed in the absence of a party or to terminate by award [Para 15]. It adopted the three-condition test drawn from Arbitral Awards 16/07-Lotus and 27/07-M & V (Branch 3): proper notification, non-appearance, and absence of a proper reason [Para 16][Para 17]. On the first condition, the Secretariat had telephoned the worker party and informed it of the hearing, so notification was proper [Para 18][Para 20]. On the second, the panel read "appear before the arbitration panel" as requiring presence and participation in all four steps — introduction and conflict disclosure, explanation of process and issues, conciliation if agreed, and arbitration — and found the worker party attended none of them [Para 21][Para 27][Para 28]. On the third, the union's statement that no workers were making the demands was not a proper reason, while the employer attended as summoned [Para 31][Para 32][Para 33]. Having satisfied itself that it was empowered to issue an award, the panel held that the claimant must argue its claim with reasons and evidence, that the worker party had not done so, and that it had therefore dropped its claim; it closed the case [Para 34][Para 35][Para 37][Para 38].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a party duly notified of an arbitration hearing neither appears nor participates in any of the four procedural steps and gives no proper reason for its absence, the panel may proceed in absentia; and where the absent party is the claimant, its failure to argue and evidence its claim is treated as abandonment, and the case is closed without a merits determination [Para 33][Para 37].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that "appear" comprises both physical presence and participation in the whole process, and that CUMW had been given ample opportunity to present evidence before opting out [Para 21][Para 34].
