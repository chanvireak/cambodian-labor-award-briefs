# Award No. 115/11 — Hong Van (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 5 October 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Vira, worker-appointed; Kong Poly, chair) · Doc ID 140073

## 1. Facts
Hong Van (Cambodia) Co., Ltd., a garment factory employing about 530 workers, hired Mr R Pharith on 16 February 2011 on a contract headed "probationary" at USD 56, rising after probation to USD 61. The employer stopped his work in June 2011 for six consecutive absences and paid severance, then reinstated him on a fresh two-month contract running 1 July to 1 September 2011. On 31 August 2011 the employer notified non-renewal; he refused to sign and did not report on 1 September, when a strike demanding his reinstatement broke out. [Para 1][Para 55][Para 58][Para 60][Para 64][Para 65][Para 68]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take Mr R Pharith back, given that he was a union president and the non-renewal was allegedly a dismissal without cause or lawful procedure? [Para 3][Para 75]
2. Whether the employer must pay an additional 1,500 riel meal allowance to workers on the 18:00–20:30 overtime shift? [Para 5][Para 79]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The non-renewal was anti-union discrimination — Mr R Pharith had just been elected union president — and it was effected without the labour inspector's authorisation; the contract was in substance a full-rights contract because he was paid USD 61. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 84]
**Employer (respondent):** The contract was a fixed-term probationary contract that expired by its own terms; output was low (315 pieces on the Gaspina style against 400–600 for others) and absences were frequent, so non-renewal was justified. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 73][Para 117]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Partly granted — the reinstatement claim was dismissed and the 1,500 riel meal-allowance claim was dismissed; the employer was ordered to pay severance of five (5) per cent of wages for the two-month contract period to Mr R Pharith. [Para 145][Para 146][Para 149]

The panel first classified the claim. Because the demand concerned the non-renewal of a fixed-term contract governed by the Labour Law, it was a "dispute over rights" (វិវាទអំពីសិទ្ធិ), not a benefits dispute. [Para 87][Para 90] It accepted that the contract was labelled probationary but found its substance — a USD 61 wage, the full-rights rate — made Mr R Pharith a full-rights worker, and that its start and end dates made it a fixed-term contract of two months. [Para 76][Para 77][Para 86] Article 293 protects union leaders against dismissal, but the panel held it does not reach the expiry of a fixed-term contract: the dispute was over non-renewal at the contract's term, not dismissal during its performance. [Para 91][Para 95][Para 96] Under Article 73 a fixed-term contract expires automatically at the stated date and neither party can compel renewal without agreement, following Award No. 100/07-Houyia Cambodia. [Para 97][Para 98][Para 100][Para 102] Article 73 paragraph 5 requires ten days' notice only for contracts longer than six months; this contract ran two months, so no notice was required, and the employer's notice of 31 August was a practice more favourable than the law. [Para 104][Para 107][Para 109] On discrimination, the burden lay on the workers, who offered nothing beyond Mr R Pharith's status as union president, while the employer showed low output and dated absences; the panel found the employer's account more rigorous and held the non-renewal was not discriminatory and rested on reasonable grounds. [Para 110][Para 113][Para 117][Para 118][Para 120][Para 121][Para 122] Article 73 paragraph 6 nevertheless entitles a worker on a fixed-term contract to severance of at least five per cent of wages paid over the contract period, so the employer was ordered to pay that amount for the two months. [Para 123][Para 124][Para 126]

On the meal allowance, Circular No. 041 SKBPy of 7 March 2011 requires 2,000 riel per day, or a free meal, for workers who voluntarily work overtime at the employer's request. The employer already paid 2,000 riel for the 16:00–18:00 shift and a further 1,000 riel for 18:00–20:30. Because the demand for 1,500 riel more exceeded what the law provides, it was a benefits dispute, and the panel consistently refuses to consider such a dispute where the union is not the most representative in the factory. The union was properly registered with 260 members but did not show it held that status, so the claim was dismissed. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 128][Para 130][Para 132][Para 134][Para 136][Para 142][Para 144]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Article 293's protection of union leaders applies to dismissal during the performance of a contract, not to an employer's decision not to renew a fixed-term contract at its expiry; such a contract ends automatically under Article 73 and neither party can compel renewal without agreement. [Para 95][Para 96][Para 98]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remark that giving notice on a two-month contract, though not required, was a practice more favourable than the law; and its observation that a contract's label does not determine status where the wage paid is the full-rights rate. [Para 77][Para 109]

[Para 1][Para 56] The severance order at para 126 inserts "in the year 2015" into a 2011 award, an apparent OCR or drafting error; the sum ordered is five per cent of wages for the two-month period. [Para 126] The chair's name appears as Kong Poly in the header and as Kong Phalla in the signature block.
