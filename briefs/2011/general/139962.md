# Award No. 03/11 — Solaris International (Arbitration Council, 14 February 2011)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, An Nan, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 139962

## 1. Facts
Solaris International, established in 2005, ran Apsara Television and Radio Station under a cooperation agreement of 1 June 2006 due to expire on 1 January 2025, employing about 180 workers of whom 80 were Apsara staff. [Para 15] On 2 August 2010 the Phnom Penh Municipal Court issued an order for provisional relief transferring management of Solaris and its equipment to Apsara pending judgment, after which Apsara locked the Solaris office; 30 former staff then claimed unpaid wages, though Solaris recognised only 12 of them — staff with disabilities in the 3D, website and Khmer Rouge Tribunal translation sections — and disputed the claim of a further employee, Yuma Decaux. [Para 15][Para 16][Para 19][Para 21]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether Solaris must pay the outstanding wages of the 12 workers it recognises? [Para 37][Para 38]
2. Whether Solaris must pay the outstanding wages of the 18 workers placed under Apsara's interim management? [Para 47][Para 48]
3. Whether Solaris must pay Yuma Decaux's outstanding wages of US$ 17,500 for February, July, August and September 2010? [Para 33][Para 62]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** They signed their contracts with Solaris, which cannot transfer them to Apsara without their agreement; they accept the employer's financial difficulty but eight months is too long given the cost of living, and three months is enough. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 30]
**Employer (respondent):** It admits owing the 12 workers and asks to pay in instalments over eight months because Apsara controls its expenditure; the other 18 were transferred to Apsara's interim management by the court order and notified of the transfer, and Yuma Decaux resigned and was already paid by the former director. [Para 2][Para 22][Para 27][Para 29][Para 33]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered payment of the 12 workers' outstanding wages in instalments over three months, declined to consider the 18 workers' claim until the Phnom Penh Municipal Court rules on the merits, and ordered payment of Yuma Decaux's wages for February, July, August and September 2010. [Para 71][Para 72]
On the 12, Article 116(2) of the Labour Law requires employees' wages to be paid at least once a month and Solaris had not complied; the arrears flowed from its financial difficulty, which the workers acknowledged, and both sides accepted payment by instalments. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 41] Clause 34 of Prakas No. 099 gives the panel power to order immediate payment, but since the workers sought instalments, three months — not the employer's eight — was held reasonable. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45][Para 46]
On the 18, Article 87 of the Labour Law keeps labour contracts binding on a new employer where the employer's legal status changes by succession, sale, merger or transfer of funds, and the panel followed Awards 98/07-Sky Sino, 83/08-Le Grand Cafe and 100/08-Gawon Apparel in reading "change in legal status" broadly. [Para 49][Para 51][Para 54] The court order did effect such a change, and in principle a successor is liable for the previous operator's debts even where the order does not say so; but because the court had not yet heard the issue, the panel declined to order Solaris to pay these wages before adjudication. [Para 54][Para 56][Para 58]
On Yuma Decaux, Article 118 of the Labour Law puts the burden on the employer to prove payment, following Award 62/08-Pao Da. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61] The employer filed untranslated documents, and Clause 23 of Prakas No. 099 requires Khmer in arbitral proceedings, so those documents were not considered; the Khmer documents it did file, without a signed pay slip, did not prove payment. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 70][Para 71]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An order for provisional relief that transfers management of an employer's business to a third party is a change in the employer's legal status under Article 87, so the contracts survive — but while the underlying case is still pending before the court, the panel will decline to order the former employer to pay the transferred workers' wages, leaving that liability to be fixed on the merits. Separately, an employer sued for unpaid wages must discharge the Article 118 burden with Khmer-language payroll evidence such as a signed pay slip; untranslated documents are inadmissible under Clause 23 of Prakas No. 099 and prove nothing. [Para 58][Para 68][Para 70]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's statements that a successor is in principle liable for the debts of the previous operator even where the court order does not mention that liability, and that the 12 recognised workers were persons with disabilities. [Para 16][Para 56]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
An unofficial English translation with heavy OCR loss ("themployer", "thearing", "witheit", "instalments"), including within quoted statutory text. [Para 1][Para 38][Para 49] The unpaid-wage table is garbled: figures are detached from names, Joachim Loesener's arrears appear as 1,800 while his colleagues' show 180, and Nicolas Bevillard's as 1,500, so the individual amounts cannot be relied on beyond those printed. [Para 21] The non-conciliation report is cited as No. 8039 KB/RK/VK in one place and as No. MKBV/RVK/VK in another, and the claimant is variously "Yuma Decaux" and "Yuma Antoine Decaux", who appears both in the list of 18 and as a separate claim. [Para 6][Para 25][Para 33] The reasons at [Para 63] to [Para 65] are duplicated and cut off mid-sentence. [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65]
