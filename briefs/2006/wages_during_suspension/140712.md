# Award No. 58/06 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន វីល សិន ហ្កាម៉ិន (កម្ពុជា) ខូ. លីមីធិត / Will Sen Garment (Cambodia) Co. Ltd (Arbitration Council, 9 August 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៉ីង សុទ្ធី; អាន វីរៈ; តាំង ទ្រី presiding) · Doc ID 140712

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន វីល សិន ហ្កាម៉ិន (កម្ពុជា) ខូ. លីមីធិត runs a garment factory at Prey Ta, Chom Chao, Dangkao, Phnom Penh, employing 1,113 workers organised in សហជីព ធម្មាធិបតេយ្យ នៃ រោងចក្រ វីល សិន [Para 1][Para 31][Para 32]. At 11:00 on 11 July 2006 the workforce stopped work for two and a half days over the conduct of two managers, without notifying the company or the Ministry of Labour in advance [Para 36][Para 38]. The company posted a notice requiring return by 07:00–07:30 on 12 July, warning that non-return would be treated as serious misconduct, then closed the gates; on 13 July the Phnom Penh Municipal Court ordered the workers back within 48 hours, and they returned on 14 July 2006 [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41][Para 42]. Conciliation on 13 July 2006 settled 13 of 14 demands, leaving two issues: the dismissal of the two managers, and wages and attendance bonus for the strike days [Para 10][Para 44][Para 45].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must dismiss the senior manager and the production manager because of their attitude and language towards the workers? [Para 2]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must maintain the daily wages for the two-and-a-half strike days, 11 to 13 July 2006, and the regular attendance bonus? [Para 4]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), កម្មករ និយោជិត represented by សហជីព ធម្មាធិបតេយ្យ នៃ រោងចក្រ វីល សិន:** the two managers used inappropriate language towards workers and reversed practices that earlier managers had allowed, so they should be removed; wages and the attendance bonus for the strike period should be preserved [Para 37][Para 61][Para 66].
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន វីល សិន ហ្កាម៉ិន (កម្ពុជា) ខូ. លីមីធិត:** the managers will correct their behaviour and the parent company has ordered changes, so dismissal is unnecessary; and Article 332 of the Labour Law bars pay during a strike [Para 3][Para 5][Para 49].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused both the demand that the company dismiss the two managers and the demand that it maintain the daily wages for 11–13 July 2006 and the regular attendance bonus [Para 85][Para 86].

On Issue 1 the panel applied Articles 65 of the Labour Law and 22 of Decree Law No. 38: a labour contract is the law of the parties, may be modified only by their consent, and binds only them, so only a party to the contract may dissolve or terminate it [Para 51][Para 53][Para 54][Para 55][Para 56]. Following Cases 73/04 and 87/04, workers may demand an individual's dismissal only if they prove the person is dangerous and cannot be retained in the enterprise [Para 57][Para 58]; even a worker who has committed serious misconduct under Article 83 need not be dismissed, because the law merely permits dismissal and does not require it [Para 59][Para 60]. Here the company accepted the complaints, the managers promised to change, the parent company ordered corrections, and 13 of the 14 points were settled; the reasons for demanding dismissal were therefore resolved and no longer stood [Para 62][Para 63][Para 64][Para 65].

On Issue 2 the panel held the stoppage was a strike within Article 318 [Para 67][Para 71]. Under Article 332 the labour contract is suspended during a strike, no work is performed and no wages are paid, so the workers could not receive wages for the two and a half days whether the strike was lawful or not [Para 72][Para 74]. The Article 334 exception applies only where a lawful strike is met by replacement hiring, which did not occur [Para 75][Para 76]. On the attendance bonus, Notice No. 017 provides at least USD 5 per month for regular attendance [Para 77], and earlier cases held that a lawful strike does not justify cutting the whole bonus, only a pro-rata part for days not worked [Para 78][Para 79]. The panel found the workers gave no prior notice to the employer or the Ministry, breaching Articles 323 to 327, so the strike was not conducted according to procedure [Para 81][Para 82][Para 83]; the employer was therefore entitled not to pay the full monthly attendance bonus [Para 84].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Workers cannot compel an employer to dismiss a manager or fellow worker unless they prove that person is dangerous and cannot be retained in the enterprise, and the Labour Law's misconduct provisions permit but never require dismissal [Para 57][Para 59]. Wages are not payable for strike days under Article 332, and where the strike failed to follow the notice procedure in Articles 323 to 327 the employer may withhold the attendance bonus in full [Para 74][Para 84].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted the Article 334 exception for replacement labour, while finding no replacement hiring in this case [Para 75][Para 76].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The non-conciliation report is dated 20 July 2009 in the procedural summary but 20 July 2006 elsewhere [Para 8][Para 11]. Paragraph 68 garbles the strike's start and end times, printing "ថ្ងៃទី ២១ ១៤:៣០" where 11 July at 11:00 and 13 July at 14:30 are meant. Paragraph 27 refers to a two-minute work stoppage on 13 March 2006 that has no bearing on the events described, and paragraph 43 contains a stray letter after "ក្រុមហ៊ុន". Paragraphs 33 and 59 carry OCR corruption ("បាន ៤ បញ្ជូន", "អាជីវក្ត្រីក", "Ware"), and the signature block at paragraph 88 is partly illegible.
