# Award No. 15/06 — Xing Tai Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 24 March 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Kao Thach (employer-chosen), Liv Sovanna (worker-chosen), Kong Phallack (chair) · Doc ID 140685

## 1. Facts
The local union of the Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers Democracy Union (C.CAWDU) at Xing Tai Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd complained on 20 December 2005 that the company should improve working conditions on 12 issues [Para 20]. Nine were conciliated on 14 February 2006 and a further two — one-day monthly leave for pregnant workers and reimbursement of unused annual leave — were settled by the parties at the hearing of 7 March 2006, leaving only the first issue for the panel [Para 22][Para 26]. The company sometimes had periods of insufficient work, ranging from one to three days, and paid 50 percent of regular wages during them, requiring the workers to punch in once or twice a day; it never applied to the Labour Inspector for a suspension [Para 39][Para 41]. The union was newly established and had not yet received its registration certificate [Para 38].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay 50 percent of regular wage during periods of insufficient work and allow the workers to punch in only once per week, as demanded in non-conciliation report No. 286 AABVIK/VK? [Para 15][Para 20]
2. (Issue 1, second limb) Whether fixing the punching-in requirement is the right of the employer or the right of the workers? [Para 60]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), C.CAWDU and its local union at Xing Tai Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd:** the company should pay 50 percent of wages when it has no work and need only require a single weekly punch-in; the demand was not for compensation or amendment of past practice but for the future [Para 15][Para 42]. They also sought one day per month of paid leave for pregnant workers and reimbursement of unused annual leave [Para 17][Para 18].
**Employer (respondent), Xing Tai Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd:** it accepted paying 50 percent during periods without work but required two punch-ins a day; it would grant leave to pregnant workers but not preserve their wage and bonus, and would keep its past practice of granting annual leave during annual vacations [Para 16][Para 18][Para 19].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the demand to change the punching-in requirement from twice per day to once per week, adding that the company must nonetheless stop the practice of paying only 50 percent of wages during periods of insufficient work [Para 64][Para 58].

On the wage question the panel applied Article 72(1) of the Labour Law: suspension of the contract affects only the main obligations, the duty to work and the duty to pay [Para 46]. Where workers are not obliged to punch in they need not work and earn no wage; but here the company required a punch-in once or twice a day, so the main obligations were never suspended and the workers remained entitled to full wages even without work [Para 48][Para 49][Para 51]. It followed that a worker sent home for lack of work must still be paid in full [Para 53].

The panel then invoked Article 13, which makes the Labour Law a matter of public order and voids any unilateral rule or agreement below it [Para 54][Para 55]. A practice of paying 50 percent during insufficient work gives benefits below those the law mandates, and the panel ordered the employer to stop it even though the workers had accepted the 50 percent payment [Para 57][Para 58].

On punching in, the panel noted the workers claimed no reimbursement of past wages, only a change going forward [Para 59]. Applying paragraph 2 of Article 2 of the Labour Law, and following Awards 28/04 — Raffles d'Angkor and 49/04 — Ho Hing, it held that the employer has the right and authority to supervise and direct the enterprise, including when workers must punch in [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63]. The demand was therefore rejected [Para 64].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that requires workers to punch in during a period of insufficient work keeps the contract's main obligations alive, so it owes full wages throughout under Article 72(1); a practice of paying 50 percent is void under Article 13 even where the workers consented. The employer alone decides the punching-in requirement, as part of its managerial right to direct the enterprise [Para 57][Para 63].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the workers had not claimed reimbursement of past wages, so only the future practice was addressed [Para 59].

The non-conciliation report number appears both as "286 AABVIAKIVK" and as "286 AABVIAK/VK" [Para 20][Para 34]. "Witnesses and experts: N/A" is OCR'd as "NIA" [Para 28].
