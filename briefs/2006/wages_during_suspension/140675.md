# Award No. 02/06 — Fortune Garment and Woolen Knitting Factory (Arbitration Council, 30 January 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators: OUK Ry, TUON Siphann, PEN Bunchhea (Chair) · Doc ID 140675

## 1. Facts
Fortune Garment and Woolen Knitting Factory, at National Rd. 21, Prek Ksev Village, Saang District, Kandal Province, employed 2,030 workers, of whom 210 worked in the weaving section, about 20 of them group leaders paid by base wage rather than piece rate [Para 1][Para 9][Para 18]. After the company set the price of Lot PN135 at US$3.9 per lot on 28–31 December 2005, the workers asked for US$0.03 more; the company offered an extra 600 riel per lot, the workers asked for 200 riel more, and conciliation produced agreement on that 200 riel on 5 January 2006 [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20][Para 21]. The worker party was C.CAWDU, whose factory president and secretary were its representatives, but neither led the strike; without any union representative and without prior notice, the 210 weaving workers struck for two and a half days from the late afternoon of 3 January to 5 January 2006 [Para 22]. The company had said it would not cut the US$5 attendance bonus if they returned to work; the workers demanded that the bonus be kept for the days actually worked [Para 21][Para 24][Para 25].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the 210 weaving workers are entitled to the US$5 attendance bonus for January 2006, given that they struck without prior notice and before exhausting peaceful dispute settlement? [Para 26]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, C.CAWDU and the 210 weaving workers):** The company should not deduct the US$5 attendance bonus on the ground of a strike, and the workers were willing instead to have their wages deducted according to the number of days of interruption caused by the strike [Para 6][Para 11].

**Employer (respondent):** The workers struck willfully and without notice while the union gave no prior notice of the strike, so the strike was unlawful and the attendance bonus, which rewards regular attendance, was not payable [Para 22][Para 32].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the demand of the 210 weaving workers for payment of the US$5 attendance bonus for January 2006 was rejected on the ground of illegal strike action [Para 37]

Article 324 of the Labour Law requires a strike to be preceded by prior notice of at least seven working days, and the panel found no notice was given to the employer [Para 26][Para 22]. Under paragraph 4 of Article 320, the right to strike may be exercised only once all peaceful methods of settling the dispute with the employer have been exhausted; here efforts at negotiation had not been exhausted and the workers had not yet used Ministry or Arbitration Council dispute resolution when they walked out [Para 27][Para 28][Para 29]. The panel reviewed its own consistent line — Awards 04/03 Lida Garment, 08/04 Wash Concept, 15/04 Lucky Zone, 16/04 Yada Printing, 22/04 Raffles Le Royal, 25/04 Standard Garment, 47/04 Dai Young, 56/04 Hui Ying, 111/04 Sun Shiny, 03/05 Flying Dragon and 12/05 P&E — that a US$5 attendance bonus is payable only where the strike complied with the statutory procedure: if the strike is lawful the bonus is owed pro rata for time worked but not for time on strike, and if it is unlawful no bonus is owed at all because the workers did not attend regularly [Para 30][Para 31][Para 32][Para 33]. It held this strike unlawful and refused the demand [Para 33].

The panel added that Article 332 provides that a strike suspends the labour contract, so no allowance for work and no salary are paid during a strike, and even a lawful strike earns no wage for the strike period, following Award 22/04 Raffles Le Royal [Para 33][Para 34][Para 36].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a strike is called without the seven working days' prior notice required by Article 324 and before the peaceful settlement routes of Article 320(4) are exhausted, the strike is unlawful and the striking workers lose the attendance bonus for that month, because the bonus presupposes regular attendance; entitlement is pro rata to time worked only where the strike itself was lawful [Para 26][Para 28][Para 31][Para 32].

**Obiter dicta:** A strike suspends the labour contract under Article 332, so no wage is payable for strike days even when the strike is lawful [Para 33][Para 36].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award gives the workforce as 2,030 workers in the procedural section and "approximately 2,020" in the facts [Para 9][Para 17]. The strike notification is dated 5 January 2005 in the procedural history and elsewhere placed in January 2006, an apparent one-year error [Para 10]. The employer's representative is named "Long Liang, Coordination Officer" at the head and "Long Heang, Legal Coordination Officer" in the evidence list [Para 3][Para 15]. A striking worker's own account of the December 2005 suspension of about 1,500 workers is printed in the middle of the paragraph describing union notice, so the sequence of events is unclear [Para 22]. The text loses the leading "t" of "the" throughout ("themloyer", "thearing"), and the case list in the reasoning repeats itself [Para 33]. The award does not state any riel amount of the bonus beyond the US$5 figure, and records no figure for back pay, because none was ordered [Para 37].
