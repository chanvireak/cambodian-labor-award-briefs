# Award No. 86/06 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឃីង ឡែន (Khing Lan) (Arbitration Council, 14 November 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៊ុក រី, ស្ទូង សុផល, ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា) · Doc ID 140733

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឃីង ឡែន (Khing Lan) operates a garment factory at No. 1657, National Road 2, Chak Angre Krom, Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employing about 664 workers [Para 27]. The claimant is សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ ប្រចាំ រោងចក្រ ឃីង ឡែន (Khmer Youth Union at the Khing Lan factory) [Para 1]. Five demands remained after conciliation failed entirely [Para 17][Para 18]. They concerned reimbursement of a health-check fee, full pay during work shortages, a USD 5 deposit taken at hiring, equal pay for eight table-cutters, and the USD 5 attendance bonus during lawful sick leave [Para 2][Para 4][Para 6][Para 8][Para 10].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must reimburse the health-check fee paid by workers at the occupational health clinic? [Para 2]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay 100 per cent wages when it has no work for employees to do? [Para 4]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must stop taking a USD 5 deposit at hiring and refund deposits already taken? [Para 6]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must raise the wages of the eight table-cutters to an equal level? [Para 8]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the company must maintain the USD 5 regular-attendance bonus for workers on lawful sick leave? [Para 10]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ ប្រចាំ រោងចក្រ ឃីង ឡែន:** the company should reimburse the health-check fee workers paid themselves; it should pay full wages while workers sit waiting for work, sometimes until evening [Para 2][Para 4]. It should stop deducting USD 5 on hiring, since equipment is the company's burden, and should equalise pay for the eight cutters doing the same work [Para 6][Para 8]. The attendance bonus must survive a lawful sick absence because the team leader granted the leave [Para 10][Para 57].
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឃីង ឡែន:** it refuses to pay the health-check fee for newly hired workers but accepts 1,100 riel for long-serving staff under the 11 April 2006 agreement [Para 3][Para 39]. It pays only 50 per cent, or by the hour, when work is short [Para 5]. The USD 5 is a deposit for machine and tool replacement, refundable on departure once equipment is returned [Para 7]. Wage differences reflect skill, discipline and output, as assessed by team leaders [Para 9][Para 52][Para 53].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to pay the health-check fee of 110,100 riel per worker to 437 listed workers within 30 days [Para 125], to pay 100 per cent wages whenever work is short and the contract was not lawfully suspended [Para 126], and to stop taking the USD 5 deposit and refund it within 30 days [Para 127]; the equal-pay demand was refused [Para 128], and the attendance bonus was ordered pro rata to days actually worked [Para 129].
On issue 1, the panel held that Article 247(c) of the Labour Law obliges the employer to bear health-check costs, and that a contrary agreement is void under Article 13 [Para 64][Para 67][Para 69]. Since the company selected workers after testing and required a medical certificate, the cost fell on it [Para 70][Para 73]. It therefore ordered repayment of 10,100 riel per worker to 437 workers [Para 74].
On issue 2, Article 71(11) permits suspension of the contract for up to two months only with labour-inspection oversight [Para 79][Para 80]. No such suspension was sought, so 100 per cent wages were due even for short stoppages of one to ten days [Para 86][Para 87].
On issue 3, Article 44 forbids the employer to require any cash security [Para 90]. The USD 5 deposit was a condition of obtaining work and so contravened Article 44 [Para 94][Para 95]; the panel ordered it stopped and refunded [Para 96].
On issue 4, Article 106 and Article 12 require equal pay for equal conditions, skill and output [Para 98][Para 99]. The workers did not prove equal skill and output, so only the first and fourth conditions were met and the demand failed [Para 103][Para 106][Para 107].
On issue 5, Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 provides a minimum USD 5 monthly attendance bonus [Para 115]. The panel held it should not penalise lawful sick leave, and awarded the bonus pro rata to days worked [Para 118][Para 122][Para 124].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Health-check costs lie on the employer under Article 247(c), and an agreement shifting them to workers is void [Para 67][Para 65]. An employer that keeps workers idle without a lawful suspension under Article 71(11) owes full wages [Para 85][Para 87]. A cash deposit exacted as a condition of hiring breaches Article 44 and must be refunded [Para 94][Para 96]. An attendance bonus accrues pro rata to days actually worked where absence is lawful sick leave [Para 122][Para 124].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's suggestion that the company publish its wage-evaluation policy so workers understand pay rises and stop demanding equality [Para 112].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company name appears in garbled variants in the header (ឃឹង ឡែន, ឃីង ឡែន) [Para 1]. The health-check sum is stated as 100 riel in the demands, 1,100 riel in the 11 April 2006 agreement, 10,100 riel in the reasons, and 110,100 riel per worker in the operative order [Para 2][Para 39][Para 74][Para 125]. The worker-side arbitrator is printed as ស្ទូង សុផល in the header and សួង សុផល at the signature block [Para 1][Para 131]. Several demand lines are visibly corrupted where OCR has dropped or doubled characters [Para 8][Para 10]. Individual worker names and ID numbers appear in the source and are not reproduced here [Para 100].
