# Award No. 57/06 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេវើ គ្រីន ហ្គាមិន ខូអិលធីឌី / Ever Green Garment Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 18 August 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (លី តៃ សេង; ទូន ស៊ី ផាន់; គង់ ផល្លា presiding) · Doc ID 140713

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេវើ គ្រីន ហ្គាមិន ខូអិលធីឌី operates a garment factory at Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, with about 850 workers and two unions; the claimant is សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា (CAWDU) together with សហជីព កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ ឯករាជ្យ នៃ រោងចក្រ អេវើ គ្រីន, the latter having about 200 members and no most-representative status [Para 1][Para 2][Para 27][Para 29]. Of the workforce, 174 were floating workers, some already over two months' service, and a large share of the permanent staff held successive six-month contracts totalling more than two years [Para 32][Para 54][Para 55]. Conciliation on 12 July 2006 settled six of twelve demands, and the six unresolved demands were heard on 27 July and 4 August 2006 [Para 16][Para 19][Para 20][Para 18].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay floating workers USD 45 a month and convert those with over two months' service into permanent workers? [Para 30]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must reinstate Ms S, or instead pay statutory compensation plus USD 500 for mental illness? [Para 4][Para 34]
3. (Issue 3) Whether fixed-duration contracts whose workers have served more than two years must become indefinite-duration contracts? [Para 54]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must reimburse the health-check fee of 10,100 riel paid by each worker? [Para 61]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the company must deduct union dues from members' wages for the independent union? [Para 65]
6. (Issue 6) Whether the company must pay the 50% maternity wage before the worker's leave rather than on her return? [Para 73]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), កម្មករ និយោជិត represented by CAWDU and the independent Ever Green union:** floating workers should receive USD 45 and permanent status after two months; Ms S was dismissed without notice and should return or be compensated, including USD 500 for mental illness; over-two-year contracts should be indefinite; health-check fees should be refunded; dues should be deducted; and maternity pay is needed in advance because expenses rise [Para 4][Para 34][Para 54][Para 61][Para 65][Para 73][Para 186].
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេវើ គ្រីន ហ្គាមិន ខូអិលធីឌី:** it refused the USD 45 rate and conversion, says Ms S was dismissed for serious misconduct under Article 83 and cannot return, relies on Labour Inspection Letter No. 2540 of 30 September 2004 against conversion, disputes the health fees, sees no duty to deduct dues, and pays maternity wages only on return [Para 3][Para 5][Para 7][Para 42][Para 59][Para 80][Para 157][Para 187].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered conversion of floating workers with over two months' service, conversion of fixed-duration contracts exceeding two years, reimbursement of 10,100 riel in health-check fees, conditional deduction of union dues, and advance payment of the 50% maternity wage, while declining to consider the reinstatement demand for want of jurisdiction [Para 195][Para 196][Para 197][Para 199][Para 201][Para 202].

On Issue 1 the company conceded the USD 45 monthly rate at the hearing, so only conversion remained [Para 81][Para 82]. Articles 166(3) and 68, and earlier awards, treat a worker averaging 21 days a month over two consecutive months as permanently employed [Para 85][Para 89][Para 90][Para 92]; the floating workers worked eight hours a day, 26 days a month [Para 33][Para 91], so the panel ordered their conversion [Para 195].

On Issue 2 the panel tested the dispute against the three conditions of Article 302 and found the third unsatisfied, since no evidence showed the dismissal could disrupt the enterprise or social peace [Para 97][Para 98][Para 101]. Affecting one worker only, it was an individual dispute outside the panel's jurisdiction under Chapter 12 Part 2 [Para 103][Para 104], and the demand was not considered [Para 105][Para 196].

On Issue 3, Article 67(2) caps fixed-duration contracts at two years and Article 73(5) converts them to indefinite where renewal without notice pushes total duration past that cap [Para 107][Para 109][Para 111]. Reading the two-year limit as cumulative duration rather than the length of each renewal [Para 116][Para 122], and following Cases 10/03 and 36/06, the panel ordered conversion [Para 128][Para 130][Para 197].

On Issue 4, Article 247(C) puts health-check costs on the employer and Article 87 preserves labour contracts when the employer changes, so the 2004 sale of the factory did not defeat the claim [Para 133][Para 146][Para 148][Para 149]. The three-year limitation in Article 120 applied [Para 151][Para 154][Para 156], and reimbursement of 10,100 riel per worker was ordered [Para 199][Para 200].

On Issue 5, Article 281 forbids deducting wages for union dues or paying them on the worker's behalf, while Article 129 allows deduction only on written authorisation [Para 158][Para 161][Para 163]; Prakas No. 305/01 Article 5(4) requires a written request at least 15 days ahead [Para 164][Para 166]. No valid member list or thumbprints had been supplied, so the employer was not yet bound, but it must arrange deductions once a proper request is made [Para 171][Para 172][Para 201].

On Issue 6, Articles 182 and 183 grant 90 days' maternity leave on half pay including allowances, and Article 115(3) forbids payment on a rest day, requiring payment the day before [Para 174][Para 177][Para 184][Para 185]. Paying only on return, two to three months later, breached Articles 115 and 116 [Para 182][Para 183]. Departing from Case 94/04 [Para 193][Para 194], the panel ordered the full 50% wage paid before the leave [Para 191][Para 202].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-duration contract whose renewals bring total service past two years becomes an indefinite contract, and floating workers averaging 21 days a month over two consecutive months are permanent [Para 107][Para 128][Para 89][Para 92]. An employer that buys a factory takes it subject to existing labour obligations, including unpaid health-check fees claimed within the three-year limitation of Article 120 [Para 148][Para 151].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel expressly declined to follow Case 94/04, holding that advance maternity pay is a legal entitlement under Articles 115 and 116 rather than an interest dispute outside its competence [Para 193][Para 194].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The Issue 4 heading states 100 riel while the body and the operative order state 10,100 riel [Para 61][Para 199]. A page-break fragment intrudes into the operative list at paragraph 197, splitting the conversion order. Paragraph 9 repeats garbled text on health-check payments, paragraphs 139 and 141 are corrupted, and the worker's name is spelled two different ways at paragraphs 4 and 196.
