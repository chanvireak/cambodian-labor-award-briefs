# Award No. 84/06 — ផាក ស៊ុនី ធីង ហ្វេក ធើរី លីមីធីត (Arbitration Council, 01 November 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · លី តៃ សេង, វង្ស វណ្ណា, គង់ ផល្លៈ (president) · Doc ID 140731

## 1. Facts
The employer is ផាក ស៊ុនី ធីង ហ្វេក ធើរី លីមីធីត, a Phnom Penh factory at lot 786, National Road 2, employing about 1,990 workers [Para 1] [Para 28]. Four unions existed in the factory; one withdrew during the hearing, leaving three: the Free Workers’ Union of Cambodia, the Independent Union of Cambodian Workers and the Democratic Union of Cambodian Garment Workers [Para 29] [Para 30]. The workers, through the unions, sought higher piece rates for weaving PN 332 (USD 4.2 to USD 4.8 per lot) and for mould PN 335, PN 336 and PN 341 (USD 0.48/0.43/0.45 to USD 0.55/0.50/0.50 per lot) [Para 2]. On 03 October 2006 the labour dispute department received a strike complaint; conciliation resolved zero of one points, and the unresolved point was referred to the Council, whose file arrived on 04 October 2006 [Para 8] [Para 9] [Para 10] [Para 5]. The panel issued return-to-work orders on 05 and 12 October 2006; the strike continued, and negotiations on 17 October also failed [Para 11] [Para 17] [Para 20]. At the 23 October 2006 hearing the parties said the strike had ended, though some workers had leave until 01 November 2006 [Para 22]. The panel sat under Chapter 12, section 2 (Articles 309–317) of the 1997 Labour Law and Prakas No. 099 dated 21 April 2004 [Para 3].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the workers were paid below the statutory minimum wage of USD 45 per month? [Para 53]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the demand for higher PN piece rates was a legal entitlement or an interest dispute? [Para 58]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the unions had most-representative status under Prakas No. 305 dated 05 November 2001? [Para 31]
4. (Issue 4) Whether a non-representative union could bring an interest dispute for all workers? [Para 64]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the panel should order the rate increases for PN 332, PN 335, PN 336 and PN 341? [Para 71]

## 3. Positions of the parties
The workers said the rates were too low for their effort and rising goods, rent, transport and fuel costs [Para 49]. They maintained their claims for the weaving and mould product numbers [Para 2]. The Free Workers’ Union’s members were not involved in the weaving claim, but 80 of its members were involved in the mould claims [Para 34]. The Independent Union had over 400 members affected in weaving and none in mould [Para 36]. The Democratic Garment Workers’ Union had 100 members affected in weaving and 190 in mould [Para 38]. The employer said it could not raise rates because work was cheaply priced amid competition, its own costs had risen, some rates had already been increased, and it had lost money since 2000 [Para 50]. After negotiation it offered intermediate rates, recorded in a collapsed table, and the parties agreed no worker received less than USD 45 per month [Para 51]. Weaving wages averaged USD 60 and mould wages USD 70 per month for eight-hour work [Para 52]. The employer allowed strikers home on 06, 07, 12 and 13 October 2006 with 50 per cent pay and paid nothing for other strike days [Para 45] [Para 46]. It accepted written leave requests from 130 knitting workers until 01 November 2006 [Para 48]. The company’s practice was one week of work, then workers proposing an average price, discussed by team leader and department head, with negotiation possible after a lot was finished [Para 40] [Para 42] [Para 43].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider the claim to raise weaving PN 332 and mould PN 335, PN 336 and PN 341 rates, and made no monetary order in riel or USD [Para 72].
By the hearing the lots were completed and wages largely paid, with only 56 per cent of PN 332 wages outstanding with the October pay [Para 39]. No witness or expert outside the parties gave evidence; the panel relied on documents from the parties, the ministry and the secretariat [Para 24] [Para 27]. It found no worker received less than USD 45 per month for normal eight-hour work [Para 57]. It applied point 2 of Notification No. 017 of 2000: piece-rate workers are paid by actual output, the employer topping up any month below USD 45 [Para 53] [Para 54] [Para 56]. The demand was therefore for a benefit exceeding the law — an interest dispute [Para 58] [Para 59]. For interest disputes the panel adopted its established interpretation that most-representative status gives standing to negotiate collective agreements and bring such cases [Para 60]. Article 277 of the 1997 Labour Law requires union registration and other conditions [Para 61]. None of the unions was most representative under Prakas No. 305 dated 05 November 2001 [Para 31] [Para 64]. A non-representative union cannot resolve interest disputes for all workers or conclude collective agreements (Article 96, point 2, and Prakas No. 305, point 9) [Para 65]. Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 would make an interest award replace a collective agreement for one year, and deciding the rates would bind all workers and impair others’ future strike rights; the panel therefore refused to consider the claim [Para 68] [Para 69] [Para 71]. The award is binding and final upon notice [Para 73].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union lacking most-representative status under Article 277 of the 1997 Labour Law and Prakas No. 305 dated 05 November 2001 has no standing to bring an interest dispute over piece rates [Para 61] [Para 64] [Para 65]. Where no worker earns below the USD 45 monthly minimum, a demand for higher piece rates is an interest claim, not enforcement of a legal entitlement [Para 57] [Para 58]. The panel will not make an award operating as a collective agreement for the whole undertaking at a non-representative union’s request, because it would affect unrepresented workers’ future strike rights [Para 65] [Para 68] [Para 69].
**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted prior awards and the one-year substitution effect of an interest award under Article 43 of Prakas No. 099, though not necessary to the refusal [Para 62] [Para 67] [Para 68].
