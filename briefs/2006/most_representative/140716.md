# Award No. 62/06 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឃ្វីក ស៊ូ ខេមបូឌា អិលធីឌី / Kwik Su Cambodia Ltd (Arbitration Council, 18 August 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (ឈីវ ភិរម្យ; ស៊ីន គីម ស៊ាន; ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា presiding) · Doc ID 140716

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឃ្វីក ស៊ូ ខេមបូឌា អិលធីឌី operates a garment factory at Damnak Thom, Stung Meanchey, Meanchey, Phnom Penh, which began operating in August 2005 and employs about 780 workers [Para 26][Para 27]. The workers are represented by សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ នៃ រោងចក្រ ឃ្វីក ស៊ូ [Para 1]. Conciliation on 19 July 2006 settled 12 of 18 demands and left six [Para 19][Para 20]; at the hearings of 8 and 18 August 2006 three more (issues 3, 4 and 6) were settled, leaving issues 1, 2 and 5 [Para 18][Para 23][Para 24].

The company engaged Mr. C.D. as a sewing-machine repair mechanic under a three-month probation contract running from 1 April to 30 June 2006 [Para 29]. A 14 January 2006 agreement between the company and worker representatives dealt with unauthorised absence, paydays and probation [Para 28][Para 75]. On 23 June 2006 the technical department head asked in a Chinese-language letter that Mr. C.D. be dismissed for a weak technical level, "without any fault" [Para 30]; the request was forwarded on 29 June [Para 31] and on 30 June sent to the materials section to recover his repair tools [Para 32]. On 24 June 2006 the Khmer Youth Union Federation held an election for the factory union committee and Mr. C.D. was elected secretary [Para 33][Para 34]; the federation did not notify the company in advance [Para 35], and sent notice of the result only on 26 June 2006 through a security guard [Para 36], which the company denies receiving [Para 37]. The company invited Mr. C.D. on 26 June 2006 at about 08:00 to be told of the dismissal [Para 43].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must take Mr. C.D. back and not dismiss him, the workers alleging discrimination against a union leader? [Para 3][Para 59]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must deduct 1,000 riel from the wages of 45 workers who are union members to pay union dues? [Para 4][Para 45]
3. (Issue 5) Whether the company must open a second security checkpoint, or else let workers leave 10 minutes early, because exit queues cost them time? [Para 10][Para 51]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), កម្មករ និយោជិត represented by សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ នៃ រោងចក្រ ឃ្វីក ស៊ូ:** the dismissal of Mr. C.D. was discrimination against a union leader, and he should be reinstated [Para 3][Para 59]; the company should check wages to collect union dues [Para 4]; and a second checkpoint or an early release is needed because workers lose 15 to 20 minutes at the gate [Para 10][Para 51][Para 54].
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន ឃ្វីក ស៊ូ ខេមបូឌា អិលធីឌី:** it absolutely refuses to take Mr. C.D. back because he was still on probation as a technical worker [Para 3]; it refuses the dues deduction [Para 5][Para 46]; and it has no space for a second checkpoint, workers losing only 10 minutes [Para 11][Para 52][Para 55].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel refused reinstatement but ordered the company to pay Mr. C.D. severance on termination of his contract, and refused the dues-deduction and checkpoint demands [Para 125][Para 126][Para 127].

On Issue 1 the panel first tested discrimination. Article 12 of the Labour Law and Article 279 forbid an employer to use union membership or activity as a ground for discipline or termination [Para 61][Para 62]. Following earlier awards, a worker alleging discrimination must prove it, and discrimination cannot be considered where the employer did not know of the membership [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65]. It found no specific evidence of anti-union motive [Para 66], and found that the company had already decided to dismiss Mr. C.D. before the union was created and before the federation notified the election result [Para 44][Para 67][Para 68].

The panel then examined procedure. Article 68 caps probation at three months for employees, two months for specialised workers and one month for ordinary workers [Para 69]. It held that a sewing-machine repair mechanic is specialised work [Para 72], so the three-month probation was unlawful [Para 73][Para 74][Para 79]; the June month therefore ran past the permitted probation [Para 80]. The contract was nonetheless a valid written fixed-duration contract of three months, within the two-year ceiling [Para 81][Para 82][Para 83][Para 84]. Under Article 73(1) such a contract expires at its term, and under Article 73(6) the employer must pay a severance indemnity on termination, at least 5% of the wages paid over the contract period where the collective agreement is silent [Para 85][Para 86][Para 87]. Because the company terminated the contract, Mr. C.D. was entitled to that payment [Para 88][Para 89].

On Issue 2 the panel held that the union lacked most-representative status: it had applied for registration only on 2 August 2006 and had not received the certificate by the hearing [Para 49][Para 50][Para 116][Para 118]. Under Article 277 only a registered, most-representative union may conclude a collective agreement or bring interest disputes, and an award on such a demand would bind non-members and strip their right to strike [Para 113][Para 115][Para 120][Para 121][Para 122]. The demand was refused [Para 124][Para 126].

On Issue 5 the panel held that scheduling work time and gate arrangements is the employer's exclusive prerogative under Articles 137 and 138 [Para 108][Para 110]; the demand exceeded the law and was an interest demand [Para 111][Para 112], so it was refused [Para 124][Para 127].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Dismissal of a union leader is unlawful discrimination only where the worker proves the employer acted because of union membership or activity; where the dismissal decision predates the union's creation and the employer was never notified of the election, no discrimination is made out [Para 66][Para 67][Para 68]. A probation clause longer than the statutory maximum for the job's category is unlawful, but the contract remains a valid fixed-duration contract, and termination before term entitles the worker to the Article 73(6) severance indemnity of at least 5% of wages paid [Para 74][Para 79][Para 87][Para 88].

**Obiter dicta:** an award granting an interest demand brought by a non-most-representative union would extend to non-members and remove their right to strike, which the panel treats as a reason to decline jurisdiction over such demands [Para 121][Para 122].
