# Award No. 48/06 — Bill Up (Cambodia) Manufacturing Ltd. (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ប៊ីល អាប់ (ខេមបូឌា) មេន នូហ្វេក ឈ័រ រីង លីមីធីត)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators · Doc ID 140706 · award dated 7 July 2006

## 1. Facts

Bill Up (Cambodia) Manufacturing Ltd. (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ប៊ីល អាប់ (ខេមបូឌា) មេន នូហ្វេក ឈ័រ រីង លីមីធីត) ran a garment factory at Ang village, Kantok commune, Ang Snuol district, Kandal, employing about 180 workers [Para 18]. The worker party was the Khmer Youth Union Federation and the Khmer Youth Union at Bill Up, which had 58 members out of 180 workers and was not the most representative union in the factory [Para 24]. The panel was Kaow Thach, chosen by the employer; Liv Sovanna, chosen by the workers; and Run Saray, president chosen by the two arbitrators [Para 1].

Conciliation on 16 June 2006 produced non-conciliation report No. 088 K.B.V/K.N. and left two issues unresolved [Para 6]. The hearing was held on 22 June 2006 from 14:00 to 16:00, with both parties present [Paras 8, 11]. The first issue was the company's practice of deducting the regular attendance bonus when a worker took three days of lawful absence [Para 2]. The second was a demand for USD 1 for each two hours of overtime worked on a normal working day [Para 3].

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. (Issue 1) Whether the company may deduct the regular attendance bonus when a worker is absent for three days with lawful permission? [Para 2]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay USD 1 for two hours of overtime worked on a normal working day? [Para 3]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** They relied on Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000, article 3, arguing that any worker who comes to work every day — even one, two or three hours late — is working regularly and should keep the bonus [Para 25]. They said neighbouring factories do not deduct the bonus for three days of lawful leave, and the employer did not dispute this [Paras 26–27]. On overtime, they said nearby factories pay USD 1 for two extra hours and that the rising cost of goods justified it [Paras 29–30].

**Employer (respondent):** It defined "regular work" as coming to work 26 days a month and eight hours plus four hours a day [Para 23]. It said it would follow the law on overtime, and would pay USD 1 for two hours only if the Ministry of Labour issued a written instruction to that effect [Para 28]. Its internal regulation, article 5(b), provides a USD 5 attendance bonus but does not define "regular work" [Para 35].

## 4. Award and reasoning

On issue 1, the panel read Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000, article 3, as granting a bonus of at least USD 5 to workers who work the number of days required in a month [Para 32]. It found the Notification silent on whether lawful leave preserves the bonus and on how many days count as regular work [Paras 33–34]. It held that a 26-day requirement is only partly correct, because in months containing national holidays workers necessarily work fewer than 26 days yet still earn the bonus [Para 36]. Following awards 45/05-BIN and 63/04-Sun Well, it treated the attendance bonus as an incentive for regular attendance without unjustified absence, not as a penalty for lawful leave [Paras 38–39]. It therefore ordered the employer to pay a pro-rata attendance bonus according to the number of days actually worked in the month to workers who took lawful leave [Paras 42–43, 65].

On issue 2, the panel applied Prakas No. 80 of 1 March 1999, article 5, which sets overtime on normal days at 150% of the normal wage [Para 45], and Article 139 of the Labour Law [Paras 46, 48]. It found that the employer already paid 150% plus the 1,000 riel meal allowance, so two hours of overtime came to 3,594 riel [Paras 31, 46, 49]. The workers' demand for USD 1 (4,000 riel) exceeded that statutory calculation [Para 47]. The panel held the demand was for a benefit above the 1997 Labour Law and Prakas No. 80, and so was an interests demand [Paras 50–51]. Because the Khmer Youth Union was not the most representative union, the panel declined to consider the demand, following awards 07/06-Diamond and 31/03-Hong Van [Paras 52–58, 63–64, 66].

**Outcome:** partly granted — the pro-rata attendance bonus was ordered [Para 65]; the USD 1 overtime demand was refused [Para 66].

## 5. Ratio decidendi

An attendance bonus under Notification No. 017 is an incentive for regular attendance, not a sanction for lawful absence, so it must be reduced pro rata to days actually worked rather than forfeited entirely [Paras 42–43]. A demand for overtime pay above the 150% rate fixed by Prakas No. 80 is an interests demand, and the panel will not decide it where the union bringing the case lacks most-representative status [Paras 51, 58, 64].

**Obiter dicta:** none identified.

## 6. Source defects disclosed

The Khmer OCR is badly degraded in the opening party block and the signature block: brackets, stray Latin characters and digits interrupt the employer's name and the arbitrator signatures [Paras 1, 68]. Paragraph 31 breaks off mid-number and resumes at paragraph 32, so the 3,594 riel figure is split across two lines [Paras 31–32]. Internal regulation article 5(b) is cited but its text is not reproduced [Para 35].
