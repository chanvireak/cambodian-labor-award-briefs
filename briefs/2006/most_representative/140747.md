# Award No. 109/06 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ទ្រី នុង ហ្គាល កូមារា (Trinunggal Komara Garment Industry Co., LTD) (Arbitration Council, 14 December 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (employer-appointed ឈីវ ភិរម្យ; worker-appointed អន ណន; chair អាំង អេង ថុង) · Doc ID 140747

## 1. Facts
The employer, ក្រុមហ៊ុន ទ្រី នុង ហ្គាល កូមារា, runs a garment factory on Russian Federation Boulevard employing 1,500 workers [Para 17]. The Free Workers' Union of the factory (សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ នៃ រោងចក្រ ទ្រី នុង ហ្គាល កូមារា), the most representative union, claimed one extra day's wage for August 2006, when the month had 27 working days although the Labour Law sets 26 [Para 2][Para 18]. The company refused, saying it kept the $45 monthly wage in months of 24 or 25 working days and recovered the difference from 27-day months [Para 3]. The April 2006 wage dispute had already produced meeting minutes [Para 26].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether any law in force gives the workers the right they claim? [Para 36]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the claim is supported by a collective agreement, a contract or company policy? [Para 47]
3. (Issue 3) Whether past practice supports the claim? [Para 58]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The union argued that a written agreement of 21 April 2005 (as it pleaded) together with Article 104 and the 26-day rule entitled workers to an extra day's pay for August 2006 [Para 34].
**Employer (respondent):** The company said it had always paid $45 in months with fewer than 26 working days and always deducted in months with more than 26, so the claim had no basis [Para 35].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the claim for one additional day's wage for August 2006 [Para 73][Para 74].

On Issue 1, the panel read Article 104 of the Labour Law with Declaration (Prakas) 017 of 18 July 2000, which fixes the minimum wage at 45 US dollars per month, and Article 116, which requires payment at least once a month without saying whether the month is a calendar month or 26 days [Para 36][Para 37][Para 38][Para 39]. Article 137 caps hours at eight a day and 48 a week [Para 40], and Prakas 10 of 4 February 1999 uses 26 days, or 48 x 52/12 hours, only as an average divisor for computing a daily or hourly rate [Para 41][Para 42]. The panel held that monthly wage payment does not depend on the actual number of working days in a month and that "one month" means the 1st to month-end, whatever its length [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45]. The claim therefore had no legal support [Para 46]. On Issue 2, the panel found the 21 April 2006 minutes recorded only a conditional offer by the company — keeping $45 in April if it could recover one day in August — which the workers rejected, so no clear agreement was reached [Para 47][Para 48][Para 49][Para 53][Para 54][Para 55]; no collective agreement, contract or policy supported the claim [Para 56][Para 57]. On Issue 3, the panel found the company consistently kept $45 in months under 26 days and deducted in 27-day months, including by carrying February 2006's 24 days against March and May [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60][Para 61]; the practice had run since 2005 and was applied systematically [Para 62][Para 63][Para 64]. Following award 21/06 Seng Yong, it concluded the employer was entitled to set one day from the 27-day August 2006 month against the 25-day April 2006 month [Para 62][Para 72].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A monthly wage of $45 fixed by Article 104 and Declaration 017 is a flat monthly amount; the 26-day figure in Prakas 10 is only an average divisor for calculating daily or hourly pay, so a worker has no right to an extra day's wage merely because a month contains 27 working days [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that the employer had announced the end of the deduction practice during the second round of bargaining in April 2005 yet continued to apply it in 2006 [Para 71][Para 72].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The union's pleading is dated 21 April 2005 in the reasons while the meeting minutes relied on are dated 21 April 2006 [Para 34][Para 26]. The fact-finding cites August 2005 as the month in which an extra day was paid, whereas the demand concerns August 2006 [Para 22][Para 32]. The evidence list includes a company authorisation letter dated 31 March 2013 in a 2006 case, and the printed company name varies between "Trinunggal" and "Trinunngal" [Para 15].
