# Award No. 54/06 — Bright Sky PTE Ltd. (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ប្រាយ ស្កាយ ភីធី អ៉ី អិលធីឌី)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators · Doc ID 140710 · award dated 2 August 2006

## 1. Facts

Bright Sky PTE Ltd. (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ប្រាយ ស្កាយ ភីធី អ៉ី អិលធីឌី) operated a garment factory on Veng Sreng road, Chom Chao, Dangkao, Phnom Penh, employing about 3,300 workers [Paras 1, 19]. The worker party was the Khmer Youth Union Federation and the Khmer Youth Union at Bright Sky (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ និង សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ ក្រុមហ៊ុន ប្រាយ ស្កាយ) [Para 2]. The panel was Kaow Thach, chosen by the employer; Soung Sophal, chosen by the workers; and Pen Bunhea, president chosen by the two arbitrators [Para 1].

The factory began operating in April 2005 with day-shift workers only [Para 20]. From July 2005 it added a night shift, which grew to about 1,500 of the 3,300 workers [Para 21]. Day-shift workers demanded that the company rotate workers between night and day shifts every six months or every year, because night-shift workers receive more benefits, and they wanted wage balance between the shifts [Para 22]. The company said night-shift workers from 6:00 p.m. to 10:00 p.m. receive the normal rate, those from 10:00 p.m. to 5:30 a.m. receive 200%, and only night-shift workers receive a $0.30 travel allowance and $0.23 night-meal allowance [Paras 23–26]. About 20 day-shift workers had been allowed to move to night shift by application when vacancies arose, and the company said it still applied that policy [Paras 27–28]. The workers maintained the demand [Para 29].

The non-conciliation report No. 980 K.B.V/A.K./V.K. was dated 13 July 2006 [Para 6], and the hearing was held on 24 July 2006 [Para 8]. Three unions operated in the factory: the Free Workers' Union, the Khmer Union, and the Khmer Youth Union [Para 30]. The Khmer Youth Union was not the most representative union in the factory [Para 31].

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must rotate day-shift and night-shift workers every six months or every year? [Para 2]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** Day-shift workers demanded shift rotation every six months or annually so that the extra night-shift benefits and wages would be shared equally between day and night workers [Para 22].

**Employer (respondent):** The company said it already paid the statutory night-shift rates — normal pay until 10:00 p.m. and 200% from 10:00 p.m. to 5:30 a.m. — plus travel and meal allowances for night workers [Paras 23–26]. It said it allowed day-shift workers to move to night shift on application when vacancies existed, and still applied that policy [Paras 27–28].

## 4. Award and reasoning

The panel applied Article 2 of the Labour Law, which defines the employer as the person who runs the enterprise and holds authority over it, and held that organising and directing the company, including allocating shifts, is the employer's right and duty under the law [Paras 32–34]. It found the workers' demand required the employer to reorganise shift allocation, and that no article of the Labour Law grants workers the right to organise and direct the enterprise [Para 35]. The demand therefore exceeded what the law provides and was an interests dispute [Para 36].

The panel recalled that for interests disputes it examines whether the union bringing the case has most-representative status, because that status confers legal capacity to negotiate a collective agreement and to bring a dispute to the Council [Para 37]. It cited awards 57/06-Ever Green, 81/04-Ever Green and 98/04-Great Union [Para 38], and Article 277 of the Labour Law, which requires a union to be registered and to meet the other conditions in that article [Para 38]. It found the Khmer Youth Union at Bright Sky was not the most representative union in the factory [Para 39], so it lacked legal capacity to conclude a collective agreement for all workers or to bring an interests dispute on their behalf [Paras 40–42]. Applying Article 43 of Prakas No. 099 and prior awards 31/03-Hong Va, 60/04-Unitex and 99/04-AIA [Paras 43–48], the panel refused to consider the demand [Paras 49–50].

**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider the shift-rotation demand [Paras 50–51].

## 5. Ratio decidendi

Allocating shifts is part of the employer's right to organise and direct the enterprise under Article 2, and a demand that the employer rotate shifts is an interests demand beyond the Labour Law [Paras 34–36]. The Council will not decide an interests dispute brought by a union that lacks most-representative status under Article 277 [Paras 37–42].

**Obiter dicta:** none identified.

## 6. Source defects disclosed
