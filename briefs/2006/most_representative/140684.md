# Award No. 11/06 — Fortune Garment and Woolen Knitting Factory (Arbitration Council, 24 February 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Kao Thach (employer-chosen), Ven Pov (worker-chosen), Pen Bunchhea (chair) · Doc ID 140684

## 1. Facts
Fortune Garment and Woolen Knitting Factory employs about 2,700 workers, of whom roughly 290 work in the weaving section and do not work overtime [Para 30][Para 31]. Those weaving workers demanded a piece-rate increase of US$0.50 for each of the seven models of PN 139, a garment they said was difficult to work on [Para 31][Para 35]. The company refused, saying the PN 139 rate had been fixed after consultation with workers' representatives and group leaders and that a worker could average US$2.28 per day [Para 16][Para 34]. The workers struck over the demand; the panel issued an interim order suspending the strike on 3 February 2006 and the workers returned to work on 9, 10 and 11 February 2006 [Para 23][Para 40]. None of the three unions at the factory represented the workers in this case [Para 32].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must increase the piece rate by US$0.50 for each of the seven models of PN 139, as demanded in non-conciliation report No. 013 KBV/KN? [Para 16][Para 21]
2. (Issue 1, legal test) Whether Fortune Garment set the PN 139 piece rate so that a worker of average skill can earn at least the minimum wage required by law? [Para 48]
3. (Issue 1, legal status) Whether the workers have the legal right to bring this demand as an interests dispute before the Arbitration Council? [Para 61]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), the Workers' Representatives at Weaving Section:** PN 139 is difficult to work on and the rate fixed does not let them earn more than the minimum wage, so the company should add US$0.50 per piece across the seven models; they made the demand collectively for all the workers [Para 16][Para 35][Para 33].
**Employer (respondent), Fortune Garment and Woolen Knitting Factory:** the rate was set on a clear method — a sewing trial by workers' representatives and group leaders of varying skill — and yields on average US$2.28 per day; any worker falling below the minimum wage is topped up to US$45 [Para 16][Para 36][Para 45].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider the workers' demand for an increase of US$0.50 for each of the seven pieces of PN 139 [Para 65].

On the wage question the panel applied Article 108 of the Labour Law, under which piecework must be calculated so that a worker of average ability working normally earns at least the guaranteed minimum wage [Para 47]. Following Award 03/05 — Flying Dragon, it held that once the employer has set a piece rate the panel's task is to test whether that rate delivers the minimum wage to a worker of average skill [Para 47][Para 59].

The panel accepted that PN 139 is difficult to work on, but found that of the 38 workers in the sewing trials, lasting between 33 hours 20 minutes and 113 hours 10 minutes, only one — holder of ID No. T17 — earned below the minimum, at US$0.207 per hour, US$1.656 per day or US$43.056 per month [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51]. The rest earned more than the minimum wage on the piece rate [Para 52]. Because the rate had been fixed with workers' representatives and group leaders and tested on slow, average and fast workers, the panel held the method reasonable and the rate compliant [Para 53][Para 54]. It also relied on the company's top-up: for the roughly 3% of weaving workers who earned less than the minimum in November and December 2005, wages were raised to US$45, as Clause 4 of Notice 017/2000 requires [Para 54][Para 60]. The post-strike calculation — 0.435 pieces per worker per day at US$4.90 per size 16 piece, or US$2.31 per day and US$60.06 per month, a result the workers did not deny — confirmed compliance [Para 56][Para 57][Para 44].

The panel then characterised the demand as an interest dispute, since the company had already met its legal obligation to pay the minimum wage [Para 61]. Applying its consistent line — Awards 04/03 Ly Da, 24/03 Top One, 06/04 Chou Hsing, 61/04 Best Honour, 62/04 Ecent and 09/05 Kin Tai — it held that only a union with most representative status may negotiate a collective bargaining agreement and bring an interest dispute to the Council [Para 62][Para 63]. No such union represented these workers, who had acted collectively without representatives and without evidence of authority to speak for all of them, so the panel found no legal right to bring the dispute and declined to consider it [Para 33][Para 34][Para 64].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand to raise a piece rate that already allows a worker of average skill to earn at least the minimum wage under Article 108 is an interest dispute, which the panel will not settle unless it is brought by a union with most representative status; where the workers have no such union, the demand is declined for want of legal right [Para 61][Para 64].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that PN 139 is difficult to work on yet only one trial worker fell below the minimum, and that about 3% of weaving workers earned under US$45 in November and December 2005 before the company topped them up [Para 49][Para 60].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The date the piece rate was fixed is printed as 16 January 2006 in the issue statement and as 12 January 2006 in the facts, and the brief reports both [Para 16][Para 34]. The size-and-rate table lost a row: five sizes appear with rates from US$4.60 to US$5.00 but one size number is missing [Para 34]. Other awards are punctuated with stray "=" signs in the citation list, and the Prakas on the Arbitration Council is cited both as "99/04" and "099/04" [Para 63][Para 26]. Several sentences are duplicated or truncated mid-phrase by the OCR, including the passage on the 38-worker trial and on the workers presenting no evidence [Para 45][Para 50]. The non-conciliation report number is printed once as "013KBV/KN" without a space [Para 17].
