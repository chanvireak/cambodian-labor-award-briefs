# Award No. 64/06 — Caltex Cambodia Limited (Arbitration Council, 7 September 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sodhy, employer-appointed; Vong Vanna, worker-appointed; Tang Tri, chair) · non-binding award · Doc ID 140720

## 1. Facts
The employer, កាល់ តិច ខេមបូឌា លីមីធីត (Caltex Cambodia Limited, "ក្រុមហ៊ុន កាល់ តិច"), operates petrol stations and Star Mart outlets in Phnom Penh with more than 500 employees. [Para 1][Para 22][Para 23]

The claimant, សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ និយោជិត នៃ ក្រុមហ៊ុន កាល់ តិច, brought five demands; four remained unresolved after conciliation on 21 July 2006 and were referred to the panel on 4 August 2006. [Para 1][Para 15][Para 16]

The company had dismissed two employees for serious misconduct: Mr B, a petrol attendant, found drunk and asleep in the women's toilet during his shift on 9 April 2006, dismissed on 5 May 2006; and Mr E, a station supervisor, accused of decanting about two litres of petrol into his own motorcycle on 4 May 2006, dismissed on 19 May 2006. [Para 24][Para 40][Para 48][Para 56][Para 69]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must take back Mr B and Mr E and let them work again? [Para 2]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay a health care allowance to petrol station staff? [Para 4]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must raise wages by 5 USD for staff with two to five years' service and by 10 USD for staff with more than five years? [Para 6]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must pay equal wages for equal work? [Para 8]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Both dismissals were wrongful and the two employees should be reinstated and allowed to work again. [Para 2][Para 91] Petrol station staff deserve a health care allowance because attendants are exposed daily to petrol fumes, exhaust and extreme heat, unlike Star Mart and senior staff who already receive 200 USD a year. [Para 80][Para 161][Para 163] Long-serving staff should get annual increases of 5 USD and 10 USD, and equal pay should be paid for equal work. [Para 183]

**Employer (respondent):** Both men committed serious misconduct and cannot be taken back. [Para 3] Mr B admitted being too drunk to work; Mr E was seen by two witnesses pouring company petrol into his motorcycle. [Para 94][Para 144][Para 149] There is no law requiring a health care allowance for every worker, and the allowance is not part of wages under Article 103 or Article 106. [Para 164][Para 166][Para 167] Wage increases are a management prerogative under the internal rules, driven by performance and profit rather than seniority. [Para 186][Para 189][Para 190]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to take back Mr B and Mr E and let them work again; the demands for a petrol-staff health allowance, the 5 USD and 10 USD wage increases, and equal pay were all refused. [Para 212][Para 213][Para 214][Para 215]

On issue 1 the panel accepted that both men's conduct was serious misconduct. Mr B's drunkenness made him unable to work and breached the company's Chevron code of business conduct; theft of petrol falls expressly within Article 83(1)(a) of the Labour Law. [Para 114][Para 119][Para 140][Para 150]

But the panel held the dismissals procedurally barred. Article 26 requires dismissal for serious misconduct within seven days of the employer learning of it; otherwise the employer is deemed to have waived the right to dismiss. [Para 121][Para 122][Para 125] The company knew of Mr B's conduct on 9 April 2006 and dismissed him on 4 May 2006, and knew of Mr E's conduct on 4 May 2006 and dismissed him on 19 May 2006 — both outside the period. [Para 126][Para 129][Para 155][Para 157] The company therefore lost the right to dismiss and had to reinstate both. [Para 133][Para 134][Para 158][Para 159]

On issue 2 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law or any Prakas obliging an employer to provide a health care allowance to every worker; Article 103 and Article 106 concern wages only and the allowance falls outside them. [Para 166][Para 167] Internal rule 3 is internally contradictory about who qualifies, so it cannot ground the claim. [Para 170][Para 174][Para 176] The demand was therefore an interests dispute, and since the union had registered but did not hold most-representative status under Prakas 305 of 22 November 2001, it could not bring interests claims to the panel. [Para 178][Para 180][Para 181]

On issue 3 the panel likewise found no statutory rule fixing annual increases, held the level of increase to be a management and economic prerogative, and refused the demand for the same most-representative-union reason. [Para 187][Para 188][Para 194][Para 195]

On issue 4, Article 106 requires equal wages only where working conditions, professional skill and output are equal. [Para 201][Para 202] The panel found no evidence that the company paid less to workers doing equal work; different starting wages between the pre-2002 and post-2002 cohorts were permissible, provided the legal minimum wage was respected. [Para 205][Para 206][Para 209][Para 210][Para 211]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer knows of a worker's serious misconduct but dismisses more than seven days later, Article 26 deems the right of dismissal waived and the worker must be reinstated even though the misconduct was proved; and an unregistered-as-most-representative union cannot bring an interests dispute about allowances or wage increases to the panel. [Para 122][Para 133][Para 158][Para 181][Para 195]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the company's internal rule 3 was internally inconsistent about which employees qualify for health cover, and that the monthly incentive scheme reached only workers on the shift being assessed. [Para 176][Para 192]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award prints the employer both as a full registered name and as the short form "ក្រុមហ៊ុន កាល់ តិច". The two dismissed employees are named throughout in full; their names are redacted here as Mr B and Mr E. One employee's name is printed two ways in the same paragraph, and one dismissal letter's year is printed twice. The panel's discussion of Article 26 refers to a "26-day" period where the article's own text says seven days. Two employee names also appear with inconsistent spelling in the evidence list, and there are OCR fragments such as stray "TY" tokens. [Para 1][Para 26][Para 46][Para 69][Para 131][Para 156]
