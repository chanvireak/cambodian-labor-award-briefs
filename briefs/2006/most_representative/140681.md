# Award No. 07/06 — Dai Young Cambodia Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 17 February 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach for the employer party, Huon Chundy for the worker party, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 140681

## 1. Facts
Dai Young Cambodia Co., Ltd, at Building 388, National Road 5, Sangkat Reusey Keo, Khan Reusey Keo, Phnom Penh, employed the man who became vice-president of its only union in October 2003 as a packer and later assistant supervisor of the packaging section, without any written contract. [Para 5][Para 35][Para 39][Para 41] He was elected union vice-president on 26 November 2005, the union notified the company by letter No. 085/2005 of 8 December 2005, and on 28 December 2005 the company gave him notice of termination for 31 December, ending him in fact on 29 December 2005 without notifying the Ministry of Labour. [Para 42][Para 45][Para 128] The Cambodia Workers Labour Federation of Trade Union (CWLFTU) had filed a twelve-issue petition on 1 January 2006; seven issues were conciliated, one more during the hearings, leaving four. [Para 16][Para 18][Para 24][Para 25]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must reinstate the union vice-president in his position and wage, his dismissal being for alleged work-related misconduct and for union discrimination? [Para 13][Para 45][Para 92]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay triple wages for work on Sundays and holidays? [Para 14][Para 135]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the company must raise wages from US$45 to US$81 per month? [Para 14][Para 146]
4. (Issue 5) Whether the second-term worker delegates must be re-elected because the election followed no legal procedure? [Para 14][Para 171]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissal was vengeance for the union's eleven-issue complaint and for his election, coming weeks after the company learned of both; the monthly wage of US$45 cannot cover accommodation of US$10-US$15, meals of US$15-US$17 and clothes of US$5-US$10, and research puts survival at US$81; holiday work should attract triple pay as at Roo Hsing and Chu Hsing. [Para 45][Para 68][Para 70][Para 135]
**Employer (respondent):** the dismissal rested on incapacity — buyers complained of missing items and wrong size labels in cartons, he was summoned several times — and had nothing to do with the union; on wages and holiday pay the company wished only to comply with the law, and the worker-delegate election failed for want of candidates. [Para 46][Para 48][Para 50][Para 67][Para 75]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to reinstate the union vice-president with back pay from 29 December 2005 and to organise a fresh election of worker delegates; its demands for triple holiday pay and for a wage increase from US$45 to US$81 were refused. [Para 172][Para 173]

On Issue 1 the panel first tested the misconduct ground. Paragraph 1 of Article 26 of the Labour Law forbids disciplinary action for misconduct known to the employer for more than fifteen days, and neither side could date the last summons; Clause 10 of the Internal Work Rules set a termination procedure the company did not follow, so it had renounced its right to dismiss. [Para 85][Para 87][Para 89] On discrimination, the panel found the termination followed on the company's receipt of the election notice of 12 December 2005 and that responsibility for packing lay with quality control and the supervisor, not with him, so the conduct was prohibited by Article 12 and Article 279 of the Labour Law. [Para 93][Para 101][Para 103][Para 113] He was also protected: Article 293 requires Labour Inspector authorisation for dismissing a worker delegate, and Clauses 3 and 4 of Prakas 305/01 extend protection to union candidates and to founders and members for thirty days from registration, the union having been registered on 3 January 2006. [Para 114][Para 115][Para 118][Para 125] Having no written contract, his engagement was of undetermined duration under Article 67(7), terminable only for a valid reason under Article 74, and none existed. [Para 106][Para 109][Para 111][Para 134]

On Issue 3 the panel applied Clause 4 of Prakas No. 10/SKBY of 4 February 1999, which gives a worker who works on a holiday an indemnity of one time the normal wage, i.e. 200 per cent in total; a demand for 300 per cent exceeds the law and is an interests dispute, which a union without most representative status under Article 277 may not bring. [Para 136][Para 137][Para 140][Para 141] It therefore refused to consider the demand, as it did the wage claim on Issue 4, after weighing the company's ability to pay, industry standards, inflation, past practice, state policy and productivity, and noting that Notice 017/00 sets the minimum wage at US$45 per month. [Para 148][Para 152][Para 157][Para 159][Para 160]

On Issue 5 the panel held the demand within its jurisdiction under Article 302 and Article 312, since it concerned the procedure rather than the eligibility or fairness of an election reserved to the courts by Article 298; Article 288 and Clause 2 of Prakas 286/01 require the employer to invite workers' representatives to agree the form, date and seats, and here the company notified only group leaders, held no election and sent sixteen names to the Ministry. [Para 161][Para 162][Para 163][Para 168][Para 169][Para 171]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer loses the right to dismiss for misconduct once fifteen days have passed since it learned of the act, as Article 26 paragraph 1 of the Labour Law provides, and a dismissal that also skips the procedure in its own registered Internal Work Rules is unlawful regardless of the merits. [Para 85][Para 87][Para 89] Dismissing a union leader or candidate inside the protected window, without Labour Inspector authorisation and without Ministry notification, is discrimination prohibited by Article 12 and Article 279 even where the employer alleges poor performance, and it entitles the worker to reinstatement with back pay. [Para 103][Para 113][Para 114][Para 134] A demand that exceeds a statutory entitlement — triple pay where Prakas No. 10/SKBY of 4 February 1999 gives 200 per cent — is an interests dispute that a panel will not decide unless the union holds most representative status. [Para 136][Para 138][Para 141][Para 146]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's six-factor checklist for wage-increase claims, and its remark that an interest award would replace the collective agreement for a year under Clause 43 of Prakas 099/04 and so deprive other workers of the right to strike on that issue, are general guidance rather than holdings. [Para 142][Para 143][Para 148]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The case number is printed "07106-Dai Young" for 07/06 [Para 1], and the non-conciliation report is said to have been submitted "on 26 January 2005" at [Para 15] although the Council received it on 26 January 2006 [Para 18]; its number varies between "130 KKBVIVK" [Para 15] and "130 AABVIAKIVK" [Para 18].
- The last conciliation is dated 18 January 2006 in the procedure [Para 18] but the conciliation minute is dated 19 January 2006 in both evidence lists [Para 28][Para 33].
- The union's election notice is dated 12 December 2006 at [Para 35], a year after the events it describes; the same notice is otherwise dated 8 December 2005 [Para 128].
- The panel finds the dismissal fell within thirty days of union registration [Para 124] and fixes registration at 3 January 2006 [Para 125], although the termination took place on 29 December 2005 [Para 42] — the protection therefore rests on facts the award states out of order.
- OCR damage is heavy and repetitive: sentences are duplicated at [Para 110], [Para 112] and [Para 129], quotations of Article 12 and Article 279 are run together [Para 112], and words lose their first letter throughout ("thearing", "petion", "bargaing").
