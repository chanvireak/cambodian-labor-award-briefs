# Award No. 68/06 — Cambodia Hong Mey Import-Export Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូឌា ហុង ម៉ី អ៉ីម និង អេច)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chiv Phirom, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · heard as non-binding award, 05 September 2006 · Doc ID 140723

## 1. Facts

Cambodia Hong Mey Import-Export Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូឌា ហុង ម៉ី អ៉ីម និង អេច) operates at building 30-31 on South Canadia, Chom Chao, Dangkao, Phnom Penh. [Para 1] The claimants are the Federation of Industrial Unions of Cambodia and its affiliate at the Hong Mey factory (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ឧស្សាហកម្ម កម្ពុជា និង សហជីព ឧស្សាហកម្ម កម្ពុជា ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហុង ម៉ី). [Para 4]

On 05 July 2006 about 70 founding members elected the union leadership, choosing Mr S as president, Mr Y as vice-president and Ms T as secretary. [Para 34][Para 55] The election was held without notifying the employer [Para 55], and the workers gave the company written notice of the union's formation on 06 July 2006 and applied for registration on 07 July 2006 [Para 57][Para 58]. The Ministry of Labour issued the registration certificate on 11 August 2006. [Para 59]

On 06 July 2006, one day after the union was formed, the company dismissed four workers — the president, the vice-president, a founding member who is the president's brother, and a founding member who is the president's wife. [Para 36][Para 47] On 07 July 2006 the remaining workers struck, and the strike continued until 10 July 2006. [Para 36][Para 37] Ms T, the secretary and an elected shop steward, returned on 10 July 2006, joined the strike, and on 11 July 2006 was barred from entering and paid nothing. [Para 52][Para 61][Para 62]

Final conciliation on 14 August 2006 settled none of the 11 demands [Para 20], which were referred by report No. 1162 dated 17 August 2006 [Para 17], and heard on 22 August 2006 [Para 20][Para 22]. At the hearing the company director said he would not take part in any proceeding in which a union participated, and left the room; the panel continued in his absence. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 26]

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. Whether the employer discriminated against the union and must recognise and respect union rights? [Para 5][Para 94]
2. Whether the company must reinstate the five workers dismissed while the union was being formed? [Para 6][Para 46]
3. Whether the company must give shop stewards two hours a week to meet and keep a copy of the Labour Law? [Para 7][Para 134]
4. Whether the company must provide a doctor and first-aid medicine at the factory? [Para 8][Para 145]
5. Whether the company must make overtime voluntary and agreed? [Para 9][Para 157]
6. Whether the company must pay an extra 500 riel an hour for overtime beyond two hours a day? [Para 10][Para 164]
11. Whether the company must discard its past employment contracts because probation and full-status terms were merged? [Para 15][Para 183]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** the company dismissed the union's leaders the day after it was formed and refused to deal with the union at all; it also forced overtime by shutting the gate, denied shop stewards meeting time, provided no doctor, and paid only 1000 riel meal money. [Para 36][Para 65][Para 74][Para 67][Para 72]

**Employer (respondent):** the director would not participate in any conciliation or hearing attended by a union, and withdrew from the proceedings on that basis, so the company filed no substantive response. [Para 24][Para 41][Para 43]

## 4. Award and reasoning

On issue 1 the panel held that Articles 266, 12 and 279 forbid any distinction based on union membership, and that dismissing exactly the union's leaders and founding members, while refusing to recognise the union, was discrimination. [Para 99][Para 100][Para 101][Para 104] Under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 it ordered the company to stop the discrimination at once. [Para 105][Para 106][Para 198]

On issue 2 the claims of four of the five had already been decided in case 53/06 by award of 28 July 2006, against which the union had objected on 31 July 2006; applying Article 40 of Prakas No. 099 and res judicata, the panel declined jurisdiction over them. [Para 107][Para 108][Para 110] For Ms T the panel found an unwritten contract of indefinite duration under Article 65(3) and Article 67(7) [Para 115][Para 116], dismissed on 11 July 2006 without the written notice required by Articles 74 and 75 and without the labour inspector's approval required for a shop steward by Article 293 [Para 118][Para 124][Para 126]. It found the real reason was her union post and ordered her reinstated with wages and benefits from 11 July 2006. [Para 128][Para 129][Para 199]

On issue 3, Article 6 of Prakas No. 286 of 05 November 2000 requires two paid hours a week for shop stewards, and Article 14 of the Labour Law requires at least one copy of the Law at the workplace; both were ordered. [Para 137][Para 139][Para 142][Para 200] On issue 4, Articles 238 and 239 and Articles 1 and 3 of Prakas No. 330 of 06 December 2000 require an infirmary and staff scaled to the workforce, so the company was ordered to provide an occupational doctor and first-aid medicine. [Para 146][Para 148][Para 149][Para 151][Para 201]

On issue 5, Article 4 of Prakas No. 80 of 01 March 1999 makes overtime voluntary and forbids compulsion or discipline for refusing it; the panel found the company compelled overtime and shut the gate to prevent workers leaving, and ordered compliance. [Para 158][Para 161][Para 162][Para 202] On issue 6, point 4 of Notification No. 017 of 18 July 2000 fixes meal money at 1000 riel a day and nothing provides a further 500 riel; the claim was an interests dispute, and the union was not the most representative union under Article 277 and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305, so it was refused. [Para 165][Para 168][Para 169][Para 173][Para 182][Para 203] On issue 11 the panel held it has no power to compel the parties to conclude a contract in any particular form, and refused. [Para 192][Para 196][Para 197][Para 204]

**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to stop union discrimination and recognise union rights, to reinstate Ms T with wages and benefits from 11 July 2006, to give each shop steward two hours a week and keep a Labour Law book, to provide an occupational doctor and first-aid medicine, and to make overtime voluntary; the 500 riel meal claim and the contract demand were refused. [Para 198][Para 199][Para 200][Para 201][Para 202][Para 203][Para 204] The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent written objection. [Para 205]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

Dismissing a union's office-holders and founding members immediately after it is formed, and refusing to recognise the union, is discrimination contrary to Articles 12, 266 and 279, and the Council will order it stopped. [Para 100][Para 104][Para 198] A shop steward may not be dismissed without the labour inspector's approval under Article 293, and an employer's absence does not deprive the Council of jurisdiction. [Para 126][Para 91] Claims that exceed a Prakas minimum are interests disputes that a non-most-representative union cannot bring. [Para 169][Para 173]

## 6. Source defects disclosed

The OCR renders the employer's name in several inconsistent Khmer transliterations across the header and the findings, and corrupts the panel's name in the signature block. [Para 1][Para 206] It also states the number of dismissed founders as four in the narrative but five in the findings, so the count cannot be taken from the printed text alone. [Para 36][Para 95][Para 104]
