# Award No. 66/06 — Golida Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 August 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tai Seng, employer-appointed; Sin Kim Sean, worker-appointed; Tang Tri, chair) · non-binding award · Doc ID 140719

## 1. Facts
The employer, ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហ្គោលី ដា ហ្គាមិន ខូអិលធីឌី (Golida Garment Co., Ltd), runs a garment factory in Phnom Penh employing about 600 workers and operating since late 1999. [Para 1][Para 39][Para 40]

The claimant union, សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ នៃ រោងចក្រ ហ្គោ លី ដា (Democratic Union of Workers at Golida Factory), filed ten demands; five were settled at conciliation on 28 July 2006 and a sixth (code-card absence records) was settled at the hearing. [Para 18][Para 22]

Four demands remained for the panel: reimbursement of the health check fee, correct maternity pay, an extra overtime meal allowance, and conversion of probationary workers with three months' service into full-status employees. [Para 2][Para 4][Para 6][Para 10]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must reimburse each worker the 10,100 riel health check fee? [Para 2]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must calculate maternity leave pay and benefits according to the Labour Law? [Para 4]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must add 500 riel of meal allowance per overtime hour for workers working until 21:00? [Para 6]
4. (Issue 5) Whether the company must apply the 11 November 2005 agreement and convert probationary workers with three months' service to full status? [Para 10]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The company never reimbursed health check fees although the Labour Law places that cost on the employer. [Para 61][Para 62] Maternity pay should be based on the average of total wages over the previous 12 months plus the 50% regular attendance bonus, 100% seniority pay and 100% annual leave. [Para 89] An extra 500 riel per overtime hour is owed for work up to 21:00. [Para 112] The company ignored the 11 November 2005 agreement, leaving 32 probationary workers unconfirmed. [Para 128][Para 129]

**Employer (respondent):** Pre-employment health checks were the worker's own obligation, paid voluntarily before hiring. [Para 49][Para 63] Maternity calculations already included 100% annual leave and 50% seniority; the 5 USD regular attendance bonus was not owed because the women were absent. [Para 87][Para 97] The company had no plan to require overtime to 17:00 or later and had always paid 1,000 riel meal money daily regardless of overtime. [Para 114][Para 115] It accepted the 11 November 2005 agreement but disputed the number of probationary workers on the union list. [Para 130][Para 131]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to reimburse 10,100 riel in health check fees to each worker who paid within three years of 24 July 2006, within 15 days; to pay 50% of wages and benefits for maternity leave, calculated on the 12-month average before leave; and to convert probationary workers who worked at least 21 days a month for two consecutive months to full status. The demand for an extra 500 riel per overtime hour was refused. [Para 150][Para 151][Para 152][Para 153][Para 154]

On issue 1, Article 247(c) of the Labour Law and Prakas 09 of 19 January 1994 require the employer to pay for workers' health checks. [Para 67][Para 68] Prior awards maintained that obligation [Para 69][Para 76], and Article 120's three-year limitation ran from the 24 July 2006 complaint, so earlier payments were time-barred. [Para 81][Para 84]

On issue 2, Article 183 requires 50% of wages including benefits during maternity leave, and the panel held the 12-month average before leave to be the fair basis. [Para 99][Para 109] It rejected the company's withholding of the regular attendance bonus, holding that bonus to be a benefit within Article 103 and preserved by Article 183. [Para 104][Para 105]

On issue 3, Notice 017 of 18 July 2000 sets meal money at 1,000 riel "per day", not per overtime hour, so the 500 riel per hour claim failed. [Para 116][Para 117] Articles 137 and 140 also cap overtime at two hours a day. [Para 119]

On issue 5, the 11 November 2005 agreement did not specify the qualifying period [Para 132], but consistent with Article 166 and its prior awards the panel held that 21 days a month over two consecutive months makes a worker permanent. [Para 136][Para 139][Para 140] Article 13 makes Labour Law rules mandatory, so the agreement could not set a lower standard. [Para 144][Para 145]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer must bear the cost of mandatory pre-employment health checks under Article 247(c); a worker who has averaged 21 working days a month over two consecutive months becomes a permanent employee whatever label the contract uses; and maternity pay is 50% of wages plus benefits calculated on the 12-month average, including the regular attendance bonus. [Para 67][Para 105][Para 139][Para 149]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that the 11 November 2005 agreement left the qualifying period undefined, that the company's health checks were completed before hiring, and that meal money had in practice been paid daily even without overtime. [Para 49][Para 115][Para 132]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company name is printed in the header with a Khmer transliteration of "Garment Co., Ltd" but shortened elsewhere. Prakas 09's date line repeats the word for "year". Notice 017 is dated 18 July 2000 at one point but its year is truncated to "20" in another paragraph. The union's probationary list is counted as 33 at paragraph 28 but 32 at paragraphs 59 and 129. The award date is 30 August 2006, while the non-conciliation report is dated 08 August 2006. [Para 1][Para 14][Para 28][Para 59][Para 67][Para 94][Para 111][Para 129]
