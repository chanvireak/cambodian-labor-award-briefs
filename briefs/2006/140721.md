# Award No. 69/06 — Im Way International Garment Co., Ltd (Branch 3) (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អ៉ឹម វ៉េ កាត់ដេរ អន្តរជាតិ)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · heard as non-binding award, 01 September 2006 · Doc ID 140721

## 1. Facts

Im Way International Garment Co., Ltd, Branch 3 (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អ៉ឹម វ៉េ កាត់ដេរ អន្តរជាតិ (សាខា ទី៣)) operates a garment factory on National Road 2 at Prek Talong village, Chak Angre Krom, Meanchey, Phnom Penh, and employs about 2,959 workers. [Para 1][Para 8] The claimant is the Cambodia Industrial Union at Im Way (សហជីព ឧស្សាហកម្ម កម្ពុជា នៃ ក្រុមហ៊ុន អ៊ឹម វ៉េ). [Para 2]

On 10 August 2006 the Labour Dispute Department received the workers' complaint; final conciliation that day settled one of the two demands. [Para 9][Para 10] The single unresolved demand was referred by report No. 1161 dated 17 August 2006, received on 18 August 2006 [Para 6][Para 11], and the hearing was held on 23 August 2006 [Para 8][Para 12].

About 32 workers in the sewing and finishing section of building C struck on 08, 09 and the morning of 10 August 2006. [Para 19] The 08 August day was settled when the company agreed to keep wages unchanged, leaving only 09 August and the morning of 10 August in dispute. [Para 20][Para 21] The strike followed a misunderstanding created by an interpreter's mistranslation. [Para 22] No advance notice was given [Para 25], and the company did not hire replacement workers during the strike [Para 26].

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. Whether the company must maintain the wages of the striking workers for 09 August and the morning of 10 August 2006? [Para 2][Para 21]

## 3. Positions of the parties

The union said that on 08 August 2006 the section head told the workers that anyone who could not sew one lot a day would not be paid, and that each worker was required to produce two lots a day. [Para 23] The company said the section head asked the interpreter to tell the workers to sew faster and to do their best if they could reach two lots a day. [Para 24] The company refused to pay wages for days on which the workers did not work because they were on strike. [Para 3]

## 4. Award and reasoning

Article 324 of the Labour Law requires at least seven working days' notice before a strike [Para 28], and Article 320(4) permits a strike only after the parties have exhausted peaceful means of settlement [Para 29][Para 30]. The panel found that the workers neither gave notice nor attempted conciliation, so the strike did not comply with the Labour Law. [Para 31][Para 32]

Article 332 provides that the employment contract is suspended during a strike and that no work and no wages are supplied for the strike period [Para 33][Para 34]. Reading that provision, the panel held that an employer has no obligation to pay wages during a strike whether the strike is lawful or unlawful. [Para 35][Para 36] It acknowledged that the dispute began with a mistranslation about the two-lot target, but held that the misunderstanding was not a legal ground for imposing a wage obligation on the employer for strike days. [Para 40] It relied on its earlier awards in cases 22/04, 49/05 and 58/06. [Para 39]

**Outcome:** dismissed — the demand that the company maintain wages for 09 August and the morning of 10 August 2006 was refused. [Para 41][Para 42] The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification if no party objects in writing. [Para 43]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

Wages are not payable for strike days because Article 332 suspends the employment contract for the duration of a strike, and that result does not depend on whether the strike was lawful. [Para 33][Para 36] A strike called without the seven-day notice required by Article 324 and without exhaustion of peaceful settlement under Article 320(4) is unlawful. [Para 28][Para 29][Para 32]
