# Award No. 01/06 — Goldtex Hing Shing (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 23 January 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 140676

## 1. Facts
Goldtex Hing Shing (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a factory at Ang Keo village, Kantok commune, Ang Snoul district, Kandal province, drew about 60 former workers of Goldtex Chak Angrei Le to its new plant on a promise of the same salary plus an accommodation allowance of US$8 per month. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 64] After three workers were said to have been held at the factory for an hour on 26 December 2005, 34 of their colleagues struck in support; the company then announced that it would pay them off, allow only workers physically present at the factory to return, and treat the rest as dismissed. [Para 30][Para 38][Para 39][Para 66] The union, the Union Federation of Independent and Democratic (UFID) of Goldtex Hing Shing, demanded the dismissal of the company's administrator and the reinstatement of the former Chak Angrei Le workers. [Para 3][Para 29][Para 58] Ten demands went to conciliation and eight were settled, leaving these two for the panel. [Para 18][Para 19]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must dismiss its administrator for not knowing the Labour Law, for dismissing 34 workers at his own will, for detaining three workers on 26 December 2005 and for poor health? [Para 9][Para 29][Para 30]
2. (Issue 7) Whether the company must reinstate the 34 former Chak Angrei Le workers it dismissed after the strike of 26 December 2005? [Para 12][Para 58]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the administrator does not know the Labour Law, as shown by his dismissing 34 workers by his own will, by his detaining a worker and her two friends from 11:00 a.m. to 12:00 p.m. on 26 December 2005, and by his poor health; the 34 were dismissed only for striking. [Para 30][Para 37][Para 38]
**Employer (respondent):** the administrator never used violence or injured any worker, did not detain anyone but merely asked them to wait for their piece-rate coupons, and is in good health; the 34 were probationary workers who performed badly, had already been paid under the announcement of 27 December 2005, and had joined the strike. [Para 32][Para 34][Para 36][Para 60]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand that the company dismiss its administrator was rejected, and the company was ordered to reinstate all former workers from Chak Angrei Le once the award takes effect. [Para 68][Para 69]

On the first issue the panel held that it has no power to order an employer to dismiss a worker on the strength of other workers' demands, citing cases 04/03-Lida, 14/03-Chou Sing, 17/03 and 18/03-Ho Hing, 06/04-Chousing, 15/04-Lucky Zone, 16/04-Yada Printing, 32/04-Esin, 34/04-Full Value and 76/05-Global Footwear. [Para 47][Para 48] Article 65 of the 1997 Labour Law makes the employment contract the source of the working relationship, and Article 22 of Decree No. 38 on Contracts and Liabilities of 28 October 1988 leaves alteration and termination to the contracting parties, so hiring and dismissal are the employer's right alone; Article 83 merely permits dismissal for serious misconduct and never requires it. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 55][Para 56] The evidence showed no violence, abuse or detention. [Para 51]

On reinstatement the panel applied Article 332 paragraph 2, under which a worker is reinstated at the end of the strike, and Article 333, which nullifies any sanction imposed for participation in a strike and makes the employer liable to the fine in Article 369. [Para 61][Para 62] No evidence of serious misconduct was produced, so the dismissals were punishment contrary to those articles. [Para 62] The probationary-worker argument failed because the company proved neither poor performance nor probationary status, and most of the 34 had been transferred from its own Chak Angrei Le plant. [Para 65][Para 66] The announcement of 27 December 2005 was discriminatory in restricting return to workers then present at the factory. [Para 68]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A panel will not order an employer to dismiss or transfer a worker merely because other workers demand it: hiring and dismissal belong to the employer as a party to the employment contract under Article 65 of the Labour Law and Article 22 of Decree No. 38, and the power survives only where the worker endangers the health and safety of others. [Para 47][Para 49][Para 52][Para 55] By contrast, workers dismissed for taking part in a strike must be reinstated at the end of the strike under Article 332 paragraph 2, and any sanction for strike participation is null under Article 333, so an employer's probationary label or a cash pay-off announcement cannot defeat reinstatement where it produces no evidence of misconduct or poor performance. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 68]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that it may exceptionally order dismissal or transfer where a worker endangers the health and safety of other workers was not applied, the panel having found no such conduct here. [Para 49][Para 51]
