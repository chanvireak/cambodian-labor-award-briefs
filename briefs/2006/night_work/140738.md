# Award No. 95/06 — ប្រាយ ស្តាយ (Arbitration Council, 14 November 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា, Du យូ សួន ទី and Am Vireak អាន វីរៈ · Doc ID 140738

## 1. Facts
The panel recorded a collective labour dispute concerning the night shift at the employer’s factory, referred after conciliation failed under report No. 1511 dated 16 October 2006 and case file No. 95/06 dated the same day [Para 1]. It listed R. K. No. នស/រកម/០៧០៤/០០១ of 31 July 2004 and R. D. No. នស/រកត/០៧០៤/១២៤ of 15 July 2004 [Para 1]. It relied on the Constitution, the 1997 Labour Law and Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 on the Arbitration Council [Para 1]. The file contained settlement minutes dated 11 October 2006, invitations to both sides dated 19 October 2006, and second-hearing invitations dated 2 November 2006, with the worker party recorded as absent at that hearing [Para 1]. The employer issued a 17 October 2006 notice of temporary closure for night-shift workers and a 20 October 2006 letter on closure of night-shift operations [Para 1]. A 24 October 2006 meeting included Ministry of Labour and Vocational Training representatives, worker representatives and employer representatives, and the panel found that they reached an agreement ending the referred dispute [Para 1]. The award was signed at Phnom Penh on 14 November 2006 [Para 5].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the collective labour dispute over the night shift was properly before the panel after the conciliation process recorded non-settlement [Para 1]?
2. Whether the employer’s 17 October 2006 temporary closure notice and 20 October 2006 letter on closure of night-shift operations raised a live claim for adjudication [Para 1]?
3. Whether the 24 October 2006 meeting, attended by Ministry representatives, worker representatives and employer representatives, produced an agreement covering the referred dispute [Para 1]?
4. Whether the worker party’s absence from the second hearing required a merits ruling, or whether the recorded agreement allowed the panel to close the file without further action [Para 1]?

## 3. Positions of the parties
The worker side’s pleaded claim is not preserved in the readable text; line 2 is almost entirely OCR-collapsed [Para 2]. The employer’s documented position was that night-shift work had to be stopped, first by a temporary closure notice for night-shift workers and then by a letter on closure of night-shift operations [Para 1]. The employer side also participated in the 24 October 2006 tripartite meeting, where worker representatives and employer representatives, with Ministry representatives, reached the agreement the panel treated as decisive [Para 1]. No detailed legal argument, witness statement or union-officer statement from either side survives in the award, so the positions can only be stated from the recitals and documents listed by the panel [Para 2]. The worker party’s non-attendance at the second hearing is recorded, but the award does not state that the employer sought a default ruling or that the worker side withdrew in express terms [Para 1].

## 4. Award and reasoning
The panel first listed its legal and procedural basis: the Constitution, R. K. No. នស/រកម/០៧០៤/០០១ of 31 July 2004, R. D. No. នស/រកត/០៧០៤/១២៤ of 15 July 2004, the 1997 Labour Law and Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 [Para 1]. It then recorded the non-settlement conciliation report No. 1511 and case file No. 95/06, both dated 16 October 2006 [Para 1]. It noted invitations No. 447 and No. 446 dated 19 October 2006 and No. 483 and No. 484 dated 2 November 2006, with the worker party absent at the second hearing [Para 1]. The decisive reasoning was that the 24 October 2006 minutes showed an agreement between workers and employer, ending the issue referred to arbitration [Para 1]. The panel therefore archived case file No. 95/06 and took no further action on the employer [Para 3]. It stated that the decision took effect from the date of signature [Para 4]. The panel issued the award at Phnom Penh on 14 November 2006 [Para 5].
**Outcome:** withdrawn — no monetary order in riel or USD was printed; the panel archived case file No. 95/06 and took no further action [Para 3].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the record shows that the parties have reached an agreement resolving the subject matter of a collective labour dispute, the dispute is ended for arbitration purposes and the panel should not decide the merits [Para 1]. The critical fact is the recorded settlement of the referred dispute, not the employer’s reason for closing a shift [Para 1]. A settlement recorded in tripartite minutes may therefore displace the earlier non-settlement conciliation route and justify closing the file without further relief [Para 1]. This is a panel decision, not binding precedent.

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Line 2 is OCR-collapsed and loses the statement of claim [Para 2]. The employer name is inconsistent: ប្រាយ ស្តាយ appears in the recitals, while ប្រាយ ស្កាយ appears in the operative order [Para 1]. The chronology conflicts because settlement minutes dated 11 October 2006 precede the non-settlement report dated 16 October 2006 [Para 1]. OCR also renders “Council” as COENLY/PABITRATION and garbles parts of the panel names [Para 1].
