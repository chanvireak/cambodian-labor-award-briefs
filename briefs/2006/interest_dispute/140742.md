# Award No. 99/06 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន សៅ ធ៍ បាយ អ៉ិន ធើ ប្រាយ ស៏ កូ អិលធីឌី (Sau Th Bai In The Bright Co., Ltd) (Arbitration Council, 12 December 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tai Seng, Soung Sophal, Kong Phalla) · Doc ID 140742

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន សៅ ធ៍ បាយ អ៉ិន ធើ ប្រាយ ស៏ កូ អិលធីឌី (Sau Th Bai In The Bright Co., Ltd), a garment factory at Suon Vattanak Building E, Chom Chao, Dangkao, employed 650 workers and had two unions, C.CAWDU and សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ (Khmer Youth Union) [Para 32][Para 33]. The Khmer Youth Union registered with the Ministry of Labour on 6 April 2005 and was the claimant [Para 35]. Its newly elected president, Mr H, joined the union and was elected on 9 July 2006, and the company dismissed him on 31 July 2006 for breach of internal rules and Article 83(b)(5) of the Labour Law [Para 45][Para 46]. Of 11 demands, conciliation on 6 October 2006 settled three, and eight unresolved points went to the Council on 17 October 2006 [Para 23][Para 24]. At the second hearing the parties settled points 3 and 4, leaving points 1, 2, 5, 6, 7 and 8 for decision [Para 29][Para 30].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must take Mr H back into work, given that it dismissed him without seeking the labour inspector's consent? [Para 2][Para 107]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must refund the 100-riel health-check fee to the 168 listed workers? [Para 4][Para 72]
3. (Issue 5) Whether the company must deduct union dues from members' wages and pay them directly to the Khmer Youth Union? [Para 9][Para 159]
4. (Issue 6) Whether the company must pay 5% contract-end indemnity each time a fixed-duration contract expires and is renewed? [Para 11][Para 174]
5. (Issue 7) Whether the company has been deducting the workers' base wage? [Para 12][Para 194]
6. (Issue 8) Whether the company must pay 100% wages whenever there is no work in the future? [Para 14][Para 204]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ (Khmer Youth Union):** Mr H was lawfully elected president and the company dismissed him without the labour inspector's consent required by Article 293; it should take him back [Para 2][Para 67]. The company should refund the 100-riel health checks the workers paid themselves, deduct union dues and remit them to the union directly, pay 5% indemnity at the end of each fixed-term contract, stop deducting base wages, and pay 100% wages when there is no work [Para 4][Para 9][Para 11][Para 12][Para 14].
**Employer (respondent) — ក្រុមហ៊ុន សៅ ធ៍ បាយ អ៉ិន ធើ ប្រាយ ស៏ កូ អិលធីឌី (Sau Th Bai In The Bright Co., Ltd):** Mr H committed serious misconduct and was dismissed under Article 83(b)(5) after a prior settlement of 26 June 2006 [Para 3][Para 46][Para 59]. The company does not owe the health-check fee, has no capacity to deduct dues for a second union, pays 5% only when it terminates a contract, never cut base wages, and owes nothing for future work stoppages [Para 4][Para 10][Para 12][Para 15].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to reinstate Mr H and to refund the 100-riel health-check fee to eligible workers, while the union-dues, 5% indemnity, base-wage and future-wage claims were refused [Para 215][Para 216][Para 217][Para 218][Para 219][Para 220].
On Issue 1, Article 293 requires the labour inspector's consent before dismissing a union leader, and Prakas No. 305 extends protection to union founders and members for 30 days after registration [Para 107][Para 109][Para 110]. The panel applied its earlier three-limb test: the worker must hold protected status, the dismissal must fall within the protected period, and the union must have notified the employer of the protected names [Para 113]. It found the third limb unmet: the union never supplied the election notice, receipt or election record it promised, so Mr H's status as president was unproven [Para 44][Para 115][Para 116][Para 117]. Turning to ordinary dismissal law, the panel found the first two disciplinary warnings had been settled by the 26 June 2006 agreement, and only the 27 July 2006 telephone call remained [Para 124][Para 125][Para 126]. The internal rules do not list telephone use as medium or serious misconduct, so dismissal was disproportionate under Article 27, and reinstatement was ordered [Para 127][Para 133][Para 134][Para 135].
On Issue 2, Article 247(k)(4) makes the employer bear the cost of the pre-employment health check [Para 136][Para 138]. The panel ordered repayment of 100 riel, but only to workers whose contract or start date was on or after 4 July 2003, because Article 120 bars wage claims older than three years from the 4 July 2006 complaint [Para 147][Para 155][Para 157][Para 158].
On Issue 5, Article 129 and Prakas No. 305 clause 5 allow deduction only on each member's written request [Para 159][Para 161]. The union produced no request letters or member list, so the company owed no duty to deduct [Para 170][Para 171][Para 172]. On Issue 6, Article 73(6) requires 5% indemnity at the end of a fixed-duration contract, but the panel held that renewal does not sever the employment relationship, so no indemnity is due on renewal [Para 176][Para 182][Para 183]. That claim was also a future-interest dispute the union lacked standing to bring [Para 186][Para 193]. On Issue 7, the evidence showed only the incentive allowance was reduced, not the $45 base wage, so the claim failed [Para 199][Para 202][Para 203]. On Issue 8, the panel declined to rule on a contingent future situation [Para 204][Para 210][Para 214].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union leader claiming the Article 293 protection must prove the union notified the employer of his protected status; absent that proof, his dismissal is tested by ordinary law, and a sanction must be proportionate to the misconduct under Article 27 [Para 113][Para 117][Para 134].
The employer bears the pre-employment health-check cost, but repayment is limited to workers whose claims fall within the three-year period of Article 120, and 5% contract-end indemnity is not payable where a fixed-term contract is merely renewed [Para 138][Para 157][Para 183].
**Obiter dicta:** The panel will not decide rights that may arise in the future [Para 210][Para 214].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company name is printed four different ways — "សៅ ធ៍ បាយ", "សៅរ៍ បាយ", "សៅធិ៍ បាយ" and "សៅធ័រ បាយ" — and once as "ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស្ទឹងត្រែង វ៉ូ" [Para 1][Para 30][Para 88][Para 145]. The health-check sum appears as both 100 riel and 1,000 riel [Para 72][Para 136]. The employer-side arbitrator is "លី តៃ សេង" in the header and "លី តែ សេង" at signature, and the chair is "គង់ ផល្លា" in the header but "គង់ ផល្ល" at signature [Para 1][Para 222]. Paragraph 118 garbles a party name into "កម្ម កូវីនី យោជិ", paragraph 44 inserts a stray "កម្ពុជា បញ្ហា", and paragraph 150 is a bare "។" [Para 44][Para 118][Para 150].
